Intercellular transfer of LncRNA NEAT1 drives post-infarction inflammation by blocking the macrophage IRG1-itaconate metabolic axis
Authors: Jiacheng Ge, Diankui Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Post-infarction inflammation and adverse remodeling remain major therapeutic challenges in ischemic heart disease. However, how specific intercellular communication drives macrophage metabolic maladaptation during this process remains unclear. This study aimed to elucidate the mechanisms by which damaged cardiomyocytes epigenetically reprogram macrophage immunometabolism post-myocardial infarction. Here, we identify a novel inter-organelle and inter-cellular signaling axis wherein hypoxic cardiomyocyte-derived exosomal NEAT1 acts as a pivotal epigenetic rheostat of macrophage immunometabolism. Using single-cell RNA sequencing and molecular tracing in a murine MI model, we demonstrate that exosomal NEAT1, rather than endogenous transcription, accumulates in infiltrating macrophages and interacts with nuclear Sox2. This interaction triggers IRG1 promoter hypermethylation, silencing the biosynthesis of the anti-inflammatory metabolite itaconate, thereby exacerbating pro-inflammatory polarization. To exploit this mechanism therapeutically, we employed an AAV9-mediated, cardiomyocyte-specific shNEAT1 delivery system to intercept this pathological exosomal transfer. This genetic intervention successfully restored macrophage itaconate homeostasis, suppressed the surge of pro-inflammatory cytokines (including MCP-1 and IL-1β), and significantly preserved cardiac contractility (LVEF/LVFS) 28 days post-MI. Our findings delineate the Hypoxic Cardiomyocyte–Exosomal NEAT1–Macrophage Sox2–IRG1 pathway as a critical driver of post-ischemic injury and establish AAV-mediated gene silencing of lncRNAs as a potent translational strategy for cardiac immunometabolic reprogramming.

Introduction

Despite significant advances in reperfusion strategies such as percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), myocardial infarction (MI) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality globally [1]. While acute survival rates have improved, a growing clinical challenge has emerged: a substantial number of survivors suffer from adverse ventricular remodeling, ultimately progressing to heart failure (HF) [2]. This pathological progression is fundamentally driven by uncontrolled inflammation and impaired tissue repair capabilities within the cardiac microenvironment. Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanisms governing inflammation resolution post-MI is critical for preventing maladaptive remodeling and improving patient prognosis.

MI is not merely a localized cardiomyocyte injury but a complex pathological process involving multicellular synergy. Infiltrating cardiac macrophages play a decisive role in clearing necrotic debris and orchestrating tissue repair [3]. Recent advances in immunometabolism have demonstrated that the functional phenotype of macrophages is tightly coupled to their intracellular metabolic state [4]. Specifically, the mitochondrial enzyme immune-responsive gene 1 (IRG1, also known as ACOD1) and its metabolite itaconate have been identified as key endogenous checkpoints that limit excessive inflammation [4]. Itaconate attenuates ischemia-reperfusion injury by inhibiting succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) activity and activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway [[5], [6], [7]]. However, under the pathological conditions of MI, this protective metabolic axis is often suppressed, such as inhibition in Itaconate synthesis, leading to persistent inflammation [8,9]. The upstream regulatory mechanisms—particularly how external signals from damaged cardiomyocytes reshape macrophage metabolic programming—remain poorly understood.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as fine-tuners of gene expression and epigenetic regulation in cardiovascular diseases. Among them, lncRNA NEAT1 has garnered significant attention due to its rapid response to hypoxic stress and its role in nuclear paraspeckle assembly [10]. Although elevated NEAT1 expression in ischemic myocardium correlates with poor prognosis, its specific cellular origin and downstream targets remain unclear [10]. Furthermore, recent studies highlight that damaged cardiomyocytes can transmit bioactive molecules, including ncRNAs, to neighboring cells via exosomes (extracellular vesicles), acting as "danger signals" that drive microenvironmental deterioration [11]. Currently, it remains unknown whether cardiomyocyte-derived exosomal lncRNAs can cross cellular boundaries to epigenetically regulate the macrophage IRG1-itaconate metabolic axis.

Currently, it remains unknown whether cardiomyocyte-derived exosomal lncRNAs can cross cellular boundaries to epigenetically regulate the macrophage IRG1-itaconate metabolic axis.Driven by this knowledge gap, we hypothesized that pathological cardiomyocytes actively orchestrate macrophage metabolic reprogramming via exosomal lncRNA transfer. Accordingly, the primary objective of this study was to delineate the specific intercellular epigenetic networks suppressing the macrophage IRG1-itaconate axis post-MI. Combining single-cell sequencing and functional assays, we reveal a novel intercellular immunometabolic signaling pathway. We demonstrate that under MI conditions, hypoxic cardiomyocytes transfer high levels of lncRNA NEAT1 to cardiac macrophages via exosomes. Upon entering the macrophage nucleus, exogenous NEAT1 acts not through traditional sponging mechanisms, but by physically interacting with the transcription factor Sox2. We show that this interaction abnormally mediates DNA hypermethylation of the IRG1 promoter, transcriptionally silencing IRG1 and blocking itaconate biosynthesis. From a translational perspective, we demonstrated that cardiomyocyte-specific knockdown of NEAT1 via AAV9 successfully restores macrophage itaconate homeostasis and preserves cardiac function caused by MI. Collectively, our findings delineate an exosome-mediated epigenetic mechanism contributing to macrophage metabolic maladaptation post-MI, suggesting that intercepting the intercellular transfer of NEAT1 may represent a potential therapeutic approach to mitigate ischemic injury.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and transfection

RAW264.7 murine macrophages (ATCC, TIB-71, USA) were cultured in DMEM (Thermo fisher, 10564011, Shanghai, China) with 10% FBS (Thermo fisher, A5669701) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Thermo fisher, 15140122) at 37°C, 5% CO2. For assays, plasmid were transfected to cells (1 × 104/well, 96-well plates) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo fisher, L3000008) at 0.2 μg/well. Plasmids: pcDNA3.1-NEAT1, pcDNA3.1-Sox2, and empty vectors. siRNAs (siNEAT1, siSox2, siArnt, siNr1H4, siHIF1A, siSP1, siNC) (10 nM for single dose or concentration gradient) and lncRNA NEAT1 (0.1 μg/well for single dose) and lnc-ctrl were transfected using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Thermo fisher, 13778075) or Lipofectamine MessengerMAX (Thermo fisher, LMRNA008), respectively. Cells were harvested at 48 h. All plasmid, siRNA, lncRNA, sgRNA and primers were synthesized by Genscript (Nanjing, China).

All sequence of siRNAs, sgRNAs and primers were showed in Supplementary Table 1.

Lentiviral transduction

Lentiviral particles (IRG1 promoter-luciferase or CRISPR/Cas9 sgRNAs targeting NEAT1/Sox2) were produced in HEK293T cells. RAW264.7 cells were infected with polybrene (8 μg/mL, Beyotime, C0351, shanghai, China) and selected with puromycin (Thermo fisher, A1113803) (2 μg/mL) for 14 days. Plasmid and sgRNA were Lentiviral particles were produced by transfecting HEK293T cells with the target expression plasmids (IRG1 promoter-luciferase or CRISPR/Cas9 sgRNAs targeting NEAT1/Sox2) along with the packaging plasmids (psPAX2 and pMD2.G) using Lipofectamine 3000. Viral supernatants were harvested at 48 h and 72 h post-transfection, pooled, and filtered through a 0.45 μm polyethersulfone (PES) membrane (Millipore, SLHVR33RS, Shanghai, China). RAW264.7 cells were infected at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 10 in the presence of 8 μg/mL polybrene (Beyotime, C0351, Shanghai, China). Following 24 h of infection, the medium was replaced with fresh culture medium. To generate stable cell lines, infected cells were selected with 2 μg/mL puromycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific, A1113803) for 14 days, with the medium replenished every 2 days. The efficiency of transduction or gene editing was validated by quantitative PCR (qPCR) or Western blot analysis, and the monoclonal cell lines were expanded for subsequent experiments.

AAV9 construction and myocardial infarction model

To achieve cardiomyocyte-specific knockdown of NEAT1, an adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) system was employed. The AAV9-cTnT-shNEAT1 vector, which expresses short hairpin RNA (shRNA) against NEAT1 under the cardiomyocyte-specific cTnT promoter, and its negative control (AAV9-cTnT-shNC) were packaged by Genscript. 6-8 weeks old male C57BL/6 J mice received a single tail vein injection of 5∗10^11 genomic copies per mouse. Fourteen days post-injection, MI was induced by permanent ligation of the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery to allow for stable viral expression. Mice were anesthetized (2% isoflurane (Beyotime, SH6530) induction and 1.5% maintenance), intubated, and ventilated. A left thoracotomy was performed at the 4th intercostal space, and LAD ligation was executed using 7-0 silk sutures, confirmed by anterior wall blanching and ST-segment elevation. Sham mice underwent the same surgical procedure without ligation. Hearts were harvested at Day 4 post-surgery for inflammatory and metabolic analysis, or at Day 28 for functional assessment. Mice were euthanized via intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg, Merck, Y0002194, Shanghai, China).

Echocardiography

Cardiac structure and function were evaluated in mice at Day 28 after sham or MI surgery using a high-resolution small-animal ultrasound imaging system (Vevo 2100, VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada) equipped with a 30-MHz ultrasonic transducer. Mice were anesthetized via 2% isoflurane inhalation and immobilized on a heated echo pad in the supine position to maintain physiological body temperature. Two-dimensional B-mode images were captured in both the parasternal long-axis and short-axis planes. To assess contractile performance, M-mode tracings were recorded at the level of the papillary muscles to measure the left ventricular (LV) end-diastolic diameter (LVEDd) and LV end-systolic diameter (LVESd). Based on these dimensions, the LV fractional shortening (LVFS) and ejection fraction (LVEF) were calculated to quantify the extent of functional recovery or decline. All measurements were performed by an investigator blinded to the experimental groups and represented the average of at least three consecutive cardiac cycles.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

The concentrations of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), keratinocyte chemoattractant (KC), TNF-α, interleukin (IL)-1β, and IL-6, were quantified in myocardial tissue homogenates using specific ELISA kits (R&D Systems, DCP00 for MCP-1, DTA00D for TNF-α, DLB50 for IL-1β, DY206 for IL-6, Shanghai, China; Merck, RAB0117 for KC) homogenized in ice-cold PBS (Beyotime, C0221A) supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail (Merck, P8340). The homogenates were then centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C, and the clear supernatants were collected for cytokine quantification. Samples and standards were processed in strict accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. The absorbance of each sample was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad Laboratories, CA, USA). All cytokine levels in the tissue were normalized to the total protein concentration of the supernatant as determined by the BCA protein assay (Thermo fisher, 23227).

Primary cardiomyocyte isolation and culture

Adult cardiomyocytes were isolated from C57BL/6 N mice [12]. Hearts were perfused retrogradely (6 min) with perfusion buffer (113 mM NaCl (Merck, S9888), 4.7 mM KCl (Merck, P5405), 0.6 mM KH2PO4 (Merck, P0662), 0.6 mM Na2HPO4 (Merck, S9763), 1.2 mM MgSO4 (Merck, M7506), 12 mM NaHCO3 (Merck, S6014), 10 mM KHCO3 (Merck, 237205), 10 mM HEPES (Merck, H3375), 30 mM Taurine (Merck, T0625), 0.1% Glucose (Merck, G8270), 10 mM BDM (Merck, B0753)), followed by digestion (20 min) with Collagenase II (700 U/mL, Thermo fisher,17101015) and CaCl2 (12.5 μM, Thermo fisher, 012316.A1). Ventricles were dissociated, filtered (100 μm), and cardiomyocytes purified by gravity sedimentation (10 min). Cells were plated on laminin-coated dishes in M199 medium (Thermo fisher, 11150059) with 10% FBS.

Hypoxia/Reoxygenation (Hypo/Reoxy) [12]: Cells were incubated in glucose/serum-free medium (0.2% O2, 5% CO2, 94.8% N2, Hypoxia) or (21% O2, 5% CO2, normoxia) for 24 h, then normal medium (21% O2) for 4 h (Reoxygenation), with or without 15 μM GW4869 (MEC, HY-19363, Shanghai, China). Conditioned medium was collected.

Cardiac macrophage isolation

Minced heart tissues were digested with Collagenase IV (600 U/mL, Thermo fisher, 17104019), Collagenase II (60 U/mL), and DNase I (60 U/mL, Thermo fisher, 18047019) in RPMI 1640 (Thermo fisher, 11875093) at 37°C for 45 min [13,14]. Cells were filtered (40-μm) and RBCs lysed (Beyotime, C3702). Cardiac resident macrophages were sorted (FACS canto II, BD, Shanghai, China) as CD45high, CD11bhigh, CD64high, F4/80high, and Ly6Clow/int. FITC anti-mouse CD45 Antibody (1:200, Biolegend, 157214, Shanghai, China), PE anti-mouse CD11b Antibody (1:100, Biolegend, 101208), APC anti-mouse CD64 Antibody (1:50 BD biosciences, 571772, Shanghai, China), PE-Cy7 anti-mouse F4/80 antibody (1:200, BD biosciences, 569223), PerCP-Cy5.5 anti-mouse Ly6C antibody (1:100, BD biosciences, 560525). FACS buffer was contained with 2% FBS in PBS.

Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) measurement of itaconate

Supernatants were extracted with cold methanol (Merck, 34860)/water (5:1) containing Pentanedioic acid-D6 (Merck, BDPH9BCD44B0), centrifuged (15,000×g, 10 min), dried, and derivatized with methoxyamine hydrochloride (Merck, 226904) in pyridine (20 mg/mL, 90 min, 37°C, Merck, 270970) and MSTFA (30 min, 37°C, Merck, 69479). GC-MS (Agilent 7890A/5975C, Shanghai, China) was performed using a DB-35MS column (Agilent, 128-3822E, Germany, Europe) [15]. Temperature program: 100°C (1 min), 8 °C/min to 200°C, 50 °C/min to 325°C (6 min). SIM mode monitored m/z 301.2, 343.2, 358.2 (itaconate) and m/z 235.2, 309.2, 351.3 (internal standard).

Exosome isolation

Conditioned medium (24 h) was centrifuged sequentially at 300×g (10 min), 2000×g (10 min), and 10,000×g (30 min) [16]. Supernatants were ultracentrifuged at 100,000×g for 70 min (Type 60 Ti rotor). Pellets were resuspended in PBS and characterized by Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS), and Western blot (ALIX, TSG101 and Calnexin).

RNA extraction and qPCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted using RNAiso Plus (Takara, 9109, Qingdao, China) and reverse transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript FAST RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, RR092A) within 1ug RNA in 10ul system. For the expression assays, 1 μL of 1:5 diluted cDNA was used as the template per qPCR reaction. Quantitative PCR was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, RR820A) on a LightCycler 96 system (Roche, Shanghai, China). The thermocycling parameters were as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 min, followed by 30 cycles of 95°C for 15 s and 60°C for 32 s. The specificity of the amplification was strictly verified by melting curve analysis. Relative gene expression was normalized to the endogenous control GAPDH and calculated utilizing the 2^-ΔΔ method. All specific primer sequences used in this study are detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

DLS analysis

Exosome hydrodynamic diameter (Z-average) and polydispersity index (PDI) were determined using a Zetasizer Nano ZS (Malvern Panalytical, Malvern, UK). Purified pellets were resuspended in 0.22 μm-filtered PBS and analyzed in disposable microcuvettes (Malvern Panalytical, ZEN0040). Measurements were conducted at 25°C following a 120-s equilibration, utilizing a 173° scattering angle. Data from three technical replicates (10–15 runs of 10 s each) were processed via Zetasizer software (version 7.11) applying the Stokes-Einstein equation.

Western Blot

Total proteins were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013B) and quantified using a BCA assay kit (Thermo Fisher, 23227). To accurately reflect secretion dynamics, distinct loading strategies were applied: whole-cell lysates were loaded at equal protein mass (20 μg/lane), whereas isolated exosomes were loaded by equal volume, normalized to equivalent starting cell numbers and conditioned medium volumes. The prepared samples were then separated by 10% SDS-PAGE (Beyotime, P0052A) and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, 3010040001). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk (Beyotime, P0216) dissolved in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST, Yeasen, 60145ES76, Shanghai, China) for 1 h at room temperature. The membranes were then incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies diluted in TBST. The primary antibodies used were as follows: IRG1 (1:500, Abcam, ab222411), Sox2 (1:1000, Abcam, ab92494), Hmox1 (1:5000, Abcam, ab68477), Nqo1 (1:30000, Abcam, ab80588), Gclc (1:10000, Abcam, ab190685), Cs (1:3000, Abcam, ab129095), Aco2 (1:200, Abcam, ab228923), ALIX (1:1000, Abcam, ab275377), TSG101 (1:5000, Abcam, ab125011), Calnexin (1:2000, Abcam, ab133615), and GAPDH (1:20000, Abcam, ab181602). After three washes with TBST (10 min each), the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:20000, Abcam, ab6721) for 1 h at room temperature. Following three additional TBST washes, protein bands were visualized using an ECL kit (Beyotime, P0018S) and quantified utilizing ImageJ software.

Liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS)

The eluted protein complexes from the RNA pull-down assay were subjected to in-solution tryptic digestion using sequencing-grade modified trypsin (Promega, V5111, Shanghai, China). The resulting desalted peptides were analyzed using an EASY-nLC 1200 system coupled to a Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, Bremen, Germany) operated in data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. The acquired raw mass spectrometric data were searched against the UniProt Mus musculus reference database using MaxQuant software (version 1.6.14.0, Max Planck Institute of Biochemistry, Germany). To ensure high-confidence identifications, the false discovery rate (FDR) for both peptide and protein levels was strictly filtered to < 1%.

RNA-seq and scRNA-seq

RNA-seq and scRNA-seq were performed by Genewiz (Suzhou, China). Briefly, Bulk RNA-seq: Sorted macrophages or heart tissue were lysed (TCL buffer (Thermo fisher, EPX-99999-000) + 1% BME (Merck, M3148)). Libraries were sequenced/analyzed by ImmGen; scRNA-seq: Single-cell suspensions were processed (10X Genomics) and sequenced (Illumina HiSeq 4000, 100k reads/cell). Data were analyzed using Cell Ranger and Seurat. Cells with >5% mitochondrial genes were excluded. Clustering used top 10 principal componentss.

Molecular interaction assays

RNA Pull-Down [17]: Biotin-labeled lncRNA NEAT1/lnc-ctrl (NEAT1 antisense) (Genscript) were incubated with macrophage lysates and streptavidin beads (Thermo fisher, 65001) overnight at 4°C. Proteins were eluted and identified by LC-MS/MS or Western Blot.

RIP [17]: Lysates were incubated with anti-Sox2 (2 μg, Abcam, ab137385) or IgG (1:200, Abcam, ab172730) (overnight, 4°C), then Protein A/G beads (1 h) (0.1 mg, Thermo fisher, 88802). Co-precipitated RNAs were purified and analyzed by RT-qPCR.

Dual-luciferase & methylation assays

Luciferase: IRG1 promoter (−2000 to +500 bp) in pGL3-Basic (Genscript) was co-transfected with pRL-TK(Genscript) and plasmids/siRNAs into RAW264.7 cells overexpressing IRG1 promoter-luciferase fusion gene stably. Activity was measured at 48 h (Promega, E1910, Shanghai, China).

qMSP: Bisulfite-converted DNA (EpiTect Kit, Qiagen, 59104, Shanghai, China) was analyzed using specific primers/probes for methylated IRG1 promoter.

Animals and ethical statement

All animal experiments were conducted in strict accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NIH Publication No. 85-23, revised 1996) and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of The Affiliated Huaian Hospital of XuZhou Medical University. Male C57BL/6 J mice (8–10 weeks old, 22–25 g) were housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) facility under a 12-h light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water. Mice used in this study were randomly assigned to the respective experimental groups (n = 6 per group for each animal experiment).

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± Standard Deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 software. Two-group comparisons were analyzed by unpaired Student's t-test. Multiple group comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc test. A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Given the cellular heterogeneity of cardiac tissue, bulk RNA-seq lacks the resolution to pinpoint the cellular origin of gene expression. To delineate the cell-specific distribution of NEAT1 within the cardiac microenvironment, we performed scRNA-seq on cardiac tissues from both groups. Unsupervised clustering and marker gene identification successfully resolved major cardiac cell populations, including cardiomyocytes, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and immune cells (Fig. 1B). Notably, while previous studies have reported elevated NEAT1 expression in ischemic cardiomyocytes [12] our scRNA-seq data uncovered a previously underappreciated phenomenon: NEAT1 exhibits high abundance in the cardiac macrophage cluster, distinct from the cardiomyocyte population (Fig. 1C). This observation implies a unique role for NEAT1 in the immune-inflammatory response post-MI.

To validate the scRNA-seq findings and quantify NEAT1 dynamics, we utilized flow cytometry to sortCD45highCD11bhighCD64highLy6Clow/intF480highcardiac macrophages from control and MI mice [14]. Subsequent qPCR analysis corroborated that NEAT1 expression was surged approximately 5.5-fold in MI cardiac macrophages compared to controls (Fig. 1D). Collectively, these data definitively confirm a specific and dramatic aberrant upregulation of NEAT1 in cardiac macrophages of MI mice, prompting further investigation into its biological function within this specific cell subset.

To confirm this alteration in the metabolic axis, we systematically profiled the expression of genes associated with the itaconate metabolic pathway at both mRNA and protein levels. The qPCR and Western Blot results showed high consistency: concomitant with the aberrant elevation of NEAT1 in MI cardiac macrophages, the mRNA and protein levels of IRG1 and its downstream metabolic enzymes (including Hmox1, Nqo1, and Gclc) were significantly suppressed. Meanwhile, the expression of the TCA cycle-related enzyme Aco2 showed compensatory upregulation, whereas CS protein levels remained unchanged (Fig. 2C–E). These results suggest that elevated NEAT1 in the MI microenvironment may block itaconate synthesis by suppressing IRG1.

To validate the regulatory role of NEAT1 on itaconate metabolism in vitro, we established NEAT1 overexpression and knockdown models in the RAW264.7 macrophage cell line. Gain-of-function assays demonstrated that transfection with a NEAT1 overexpression plasmid significantly inhibited the expression of IRG1 and related metabolic enzymes (Hmox1, Nqo1, Gclc). Conversely, silencing NEAT1 using siRNA significantly relieved this inhibition, promoting the expression of these genes (Fig. 2F–H). To assess whether these gene expression changes translated into actual metabolic alterations, we employed GC-MS to directly measure itaconate concentrations in the cell supernatant. The results confirmed a strict positive correlation between itaconate production and IRG1 expression levels: NEAT1 overexpression significantly reduced itaconate concentration, whereas NEAT1 knockdown increased it (Fig. 2I). Furthermore, rescue experiments in NEAT1 KO cells generated via CRISPR/Cas9 revealed that transfection with varying concentrations of NEAT1 mRNA suppressed itaconate production in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2J). These comprehensive data confirm that NEAT1 functions as a potent negative regulator of IRG1 expression and itaconate biosynthesis in macrophages.

NEAT1 represses IRG1 transcription by interacting with transcription factor Sox2 and promoting promoter hypermethylation

Given that lncRNAs often function by interacting with proteins, we sought to determine the molecular mechanism underlying NEAT1-mediated suppression of IRG1 transcription. We overexpressed NEAT1 in RAW264.7 cells and performed RNA-pull down followed by LC-MS/MS to screen for potential NEAT1-binding proteins. Concurrently, we utilized the JASPAR transcription factor database to predict the promoter region of IRG1 (−2000∼+500 bp of first base in DNA), identifying 105 potential transcription factors. To identify the specific transcription factor mediating NEAT1 function, we performed an RNA interference screen. Following individual silencing of the five candidates in RAW264.7 cells, qPCR and GC-MS results revealed that only Sox2 knockdown led to the most significant reduction in IRG1 expression and itaconate secretion (Fig. 3C–D). Furthermore, correlation analysis in RAW264.7 cells transfected with varying concentrations of siSox2 showed a significant positive correlation between Sox2 expression levels and both IRG1 and itaconate levels (Fig. 3E). To validate transcriptional regulatory activity, we constructed a dual-luciferase reporter vector containing the IRG1 promoter region. Experimental results indicated that Sox2 overexpression significantly enhanced the luciferase activity of the IRG1 promoter, whereas co-transfection with NEAT1 effectively abrogated this Sox2-mediated transcriptional activation (Fig. 3F). Conversely, reduced Sox2 levels significantly diminished IRG1 promoter luciferase activity, and co-transfection with siNEAT1 failed to restore activity (Fig. 3F), suggesting that NEAT1 acts upstream of Sox2 in regulating the IRG1 promoter. Moreover, genetic rescue experiments based on CRISPR/Cas9 showed that in a Sox2-knockout background, the inhibitory effect of NEAT1 on IRG1 expression and itaconate secretion was completely abolished (Fig. 3G). In contrast, in NEAT1-knockout cells, modulating Sox2 levels remained effective in regulating IRG1 (Fig. 3H). These results establish a clear hierarchical relationship: NEAT1 functions upstream of Sox2 by inhibiting its function.

At the molecular level, Western Blot verification of RNA-pull down products and RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays confirmed a direct physical interaction between NEAT1 and Sox2 protein within the nucleus (Fig. 3I–J). Notably, despite this interaction, qPCR and Western Blot analyses showed that overexpression or knockdown of NEAT1 did not alter Sox2 mRNA or protein levels (Supplementary Fig. 1A–C). Similarly, Sox2 protein levels were comparable between cardiac macrophages from normal and MI mice (Supplementary Fig. 1D–E). These findings rule out the possibility that NEAT1 regulates Sox2 stability or degradation, confirming that NEAT1 influences Sox2 transcriptional activity or DNA binding capacity via direct interaction.

Considering that lncRNAs often act as scaffolds in epigenetic regulation, we further investigated the role of DNA methylation. qMSP revealed that NEAT1 overexpression significantly increased methylation levels at the IRG1 promoter region. Importantly, this epigenetic alteration induced by NEAT1 fluctuation was abolished upon Sox2 knockout (Fig. 3K). Conversely, in NEAT1 knockout cells, changes in Sox2 levels were sufficient to regulate IRG1 promoter methylation even in the absence of NEAT1 (Fig. 3L). In summary, our study reveals a refined molecular mechanism: NEAT1 directly binds to the transcription factor Sox2, impeding its transcriptional activation of target genes and promoting IRG1 promoter hypermethylation, thereby suppressing IRG1 expression at both epigenetic and transcriptional levels.

Cardiomyocyte-derived exosomes transfer NEAT1 to cardiac macrophages during MI

Finally, we sought to identify the fundamental source of the aberrant NEAT1 upregulation in MI cardiac macrophages. According to conventional transcriptional regulation logic, high gene expression is typically associated with promoter hypomethylation. We utilized MethPrimer to predict the CpG islands in the NEAT1 promoter (Fig. 4A). Subsequent qMSP analysis showed that NEAT1 promoter methylation was significantly reduced in MI cardiomyocytes, consistent with its high expression. However, intriguingly, although NEAT1 transcript abundance was significantly elevated in MI cardiac macrophages (Fig. 1), the methylation level of its promoter showed no significant difference compared to the control group (Fig. 4B). To test this hypothesis, we established an in vitro Hypo/Reoxy model to mimic the MI microenvironment. Results showed that Hypo/Reoxy treatment significantly induced NEAT1 upregulation in cardiomyocytes, whereas direct Hypo/Reoxy treatment of macrophages failed to induce endogenous NEAT1 expression (Fig. 4C). Based on this, we hypothesized that damaged cardiomyocytes might transfer NEAT1 as "cargo" to neighboring macrophages via a paracrine pathway. To verify this, we treated RAW264.7 WT and RAW264.7 NEAT1 KO cells with concentrated supernatant from Hypo/Reoxy-treated primary cardiomyocytes. Remarkably, regardless of whether the recipient macrophages possessed the capacity for endogenous NEAT1 production (even in KO cells), the Hypo/Reoxy cardiomyocyte supernatant significantly elevated intracellular NEAT1 levels in macrophages, concomitant with suppressed IRG1 expression and reduced itaconate secretion (Fig. 4D–E). Furthermore, the results revealed that the abundance of NEAT1 and IRG1, as well as the alteration in itaconate secretion capacity induced by exogenous NEAT1, were indistinguishable between WT and NEAT1 KO cells. This further indicates that the presence or absence of the endogenous NEAT1 gene in RAW264.7 cells does not influence the changes in the itaconate pathway induced by exogenous NEAT1. These results strongly confirm the existence of intercellular transfer of NEAT1 and that exogenously derived NEAT1 is sufficient to exert biological functions. Strikingly, pretreatment of cardiomyocytes with the exosome release inhibitor GW4869 almost completely abolished these supernatant-induced alterations in recipient macrophages (Fig. 4D–E), strongly implicating extracellular vesicles as the primary intercellular transfer vehicle.

To definitively confirm the role of exosomes, we isolated extracellular vesicles from the primary cardiomyocyte supernatant via ultracentrifugation and comprehensively characterized them in accordance with the MISEV guidelines. Western blot analysis demonstrated robust enrichment of the exosome-specific markers ALIX and TSG101, while the intracellular organelle marker Calnexin was notably absent, confirming the high purity of our vesicle preparations (Fig. 4F). Furthermore, GW4869 treatment effectively depleted the yield of these exosomal markers in the isolated fractions, validating the pharmacological inhibition of exosome secretion (Fig. 4F). DLS analysis confirmed a size distribution for normoxia- and hypoxia-derived vesicles, respectively, characteristic of standard exosomes (Fig. 4G–H). qPCR analysis revealed that the Hypo/Reoxy-induced cardiomyocyte exosomes were highly enriched with NEAT1 (Fig. 4I).

Subsequently, co-incubation of these NEAT1-rich exosomes with macrophages perfectly recapitulated the effects of the supernatant treatment: exosome treatment significantly elevated intracellular NEAT1 levels, inhibited IRG1 expression, and reduced itaconate production in macrophages. Moreover, the regulation of the itaconate pathway by exosomes occurred independently of endogenous NEAT1 status (Fig. 4J–K). Additionally, qMSP results further revealed that treatment with Hypo/Reoxy-induced exosomes led to a significant increase in IRG1 promoter methylation in RAW264.7 cells, consistent with the mechanism described above (Fig. 4L). Most importantly, this exosome-mediated regulation remained significant in NEAT1 KO macrophages, demonstrating that cardiomyocyte-derived exosomal NEAT1 is the primary functional executor.

To substantiate this paracrine hypothesis and explore its translational relevance in humans, we parsed a public human single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset from patients with acute myocardial infarction (Dataset ID: EGAD50000000927) [18]. Consistently, NEAT1 expression was significantly elevated in human ischemic cardiomyocytes compared to control cells, demonstrating a robust effect size (Cohen's d = 0.439). Given the profound inter-patient heterogeneity inherently characteristic of human clinical biosamples, this substantial effect size provides compelling cross-species evidence that injured cardiomyocytes serve as a primary induction site and reservoir of NEAT1 within the post-ischemic microenvironment, supporting our paracrine transfer model. Notably, due to the technical constraints of gene dropout effects commonly encountered in single-cell transcriptomics for transiently induced metabolic enzymes, IRG1 transcripts within the human cardiac macrophage cluster were below the reliable quantitative threshold in this dataset, precluding a direct intercellular correlation analysis between cardiomyocyte-derived NEAT1 and macrophage IRG1 expression.

Echocardiographic assessment at 28 days post-MI revealed that mice in the shNC group developed severe contractile dysfunction, characterized by the decreased ejection fraction (EF) and systolic fraction (FS) (Fig. 5C–D). In contrast, cardiomyocyte-specific NEAT1 knockdown exerted profound cardioprotective effects, as evidenced by significantly preserved EF and FS (Fig. 5C–D). These physiological data suggest that intercepting the efflux of cardiomyocyte-derived NEAT1 effectively mitigates adverse ventricular remodeling following MI.

We next investigated whether this structural preservation was underpinned by an amelioration of the cardiac inflammatory microenvironment. At the peak of inflammation (day 4 post-MI), analysis of heart homogenates demonstrated that NEAT1 knockdown significantly suppressed the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including MCP-1, KC, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α (Fig. 5E). Crucially, this anti-inflammatory shift was closely coupled with the reactivation of the IRG1-itaconate metabolic axis within cardiac macrophages. Molecular profiling of sorted cardiac macrophages showed that AAV9-mediated NEAT1 depletion successfully rescued the MI-induced suppression of IRG1 expression (Fig. 5F). Consistent with our in vitro findings, this restoration was attributed to the alleviation of IRG1 promoter hypermethylation in macrophages (Fig. 5G). Furthermore, GC-MS quantitative analysis confirmed a concomitant increase in itaconate concentration within the infarcted myocardium of the AAV9-shNEAT1 group (Fig. 5H). Collectively, these in vivo results provide compelling evidence that targeting cardiomyocyte-derived NEAT1 can resolve post-infarction inflammation and attenuate cardiac injury by lifting the epigenetic silencing of the macrophage IRG1 itaconate-producing pathway.

Discussion

LncRNA NEAT1 is widely recognized as a core structural component of nuclear paraspeckles, dynamic subnuclear bodies that regulate gene expression primarily by sequestering RNA-binding proteins and transcription factors [19,20]. For instance, Hirose et al. demonstrated that NEAT1 sequesters the splicing factor SFPQ within paraspeckles, reducing its binding to the ADARB2 gene promoter and thereby inhibiting transcription [20]. Conversely, Imamura et al. reported that under immune stimulation, NEAT1 relocates the repressor protein SFPQ from the IL8 promoter to nuclear paraspeckles, relieving repression and activating the transcription of the inflammatory cytokine IL-8 [21]. Additional studies have reported that NEAT1 regulates cancer-related genes such as P53 and BRCA1 through this classic paraspeckle function, participating in tumorigenesis [22,23].

We found that under physiological conditions, Sox2 binds to the IRG1 promoter, which is essential for maintaining its hypomethylated, active state. However, the influx of excessive exogenous NEAT1 leads to a direct increase in IRG1 promoter methylation via physical binding with Sox2. Therefore, we postulate that the mechanism by which NEAT1 suppresses IRG1 promoter methylation via Sox2 aligns with these classical findings. NEAT1 likely functions as a trap, competitively sequestering Sox2 away from chromatin. This loss of Sox2 deprives the IRG1 promoter of its demethylation protection, resulting in a significant elevation of DNA methylation levels.

Metabolic reprogramming of macrophages is a determinant of cardiac repair outcomes [24]. Among numerous metabolic checkpoints, the IRG1-itaconate axis has emerged as a central negative regulator of inflammation [4]. Itaconate exerts potent anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting SDH and activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway [6,25,26]. However, in the pathological environment of MI, the potential of this protective metabolic axis is often compromised. Previous studies indicate that while inflammatory stimuli typically induce IRG1 expression, endogenous itaconate levels in severely injured hearts are often insufficient to counteract the profound cytokine storm [6,27]. Indeed, Lampropoulou et al. demonstrated that exogenous supplementation with membrane-permeable itaconate derivatives (e.g., 4-octyl itaconate, 4-OI) significantly attenuates ischemia-reperfusion injury and improves cardiac function, implying a relative deficiency or suppression of the endogenous pathway during severe injury [27]. Our study provides a molecular explanation for this phenomenon: the downregulation of IRG1 in MI macrophages is not merely a failure of induction, but an active suppression driven by the accumulation of exosomal NEAT1. By epigenetically silencing IRG1 via the Sox2 interface, exogenous NEAT1 effectively disengages the metabolic brake on inflammation, explaining why macrophages struggle to transition toward a reparative phenotype when inflammation resolution is most critically needed [28].

Furthermore, our findings highlight the pivotal role of exosome-mediated communication between damaged cardiomyocytes and the immune system in MI. Accumulating evidence suggests that the infarcted heart functions as a multicellular syncytium where intercellular communication governs disease progression. Cardiomyocyte-derived exosomes have been identified as key carriers of danger signals, transporting molecules such as miR-155 to resident macrophages to modulate their phenotype [29]. For example, Li et al. confirmed that exosomes derived from ferroptotic cardiomyocytes promote M1 macrophage polarization, thereby aggravating myocardial injury [30]. Our study adds a new dimension to this crosstalk by identifying lncRNA as a functional cargo. Crucially, we observed a significant discordance between NEAT1 expression and its promoter methylation status in macrophages. Combined with our in vitro co-culture data, this confirms that high levels of intracellular NEAT1 in macrophages originate from exogenous input from cardiomyocytes rather than endogenous transcription. This reveals a unidirectional mechanism of detriment, wherein pathological cardiomyocytes transmit pro-inflammatory signals to the immune system, hindering the reparative processes required for survival [31,32].

The therapeutic efficacy observed with the AAV9 system in this study provides critical mechanistic insights into the paracrine crosstalk between cardiomyocytes and macrophages post-MI. Our data demonstrate that the cardiomyocyte-specific silencing of NEAT1 is sufficient to intercept its exosomal efflux, thereby alleviating IRG1 promoter hypermethylation in cardiac macrophages and ultimately preserving left ventricular contractile function. Notably, this cardioprotective effect contrasts with the exacerbated myocardial injury reported in global NEAT1-deficient mice [12]. This discrepancy likely highlights the functional heterogeneity of NEAT1 across different cardiac cell populations. While a constitutive, global deficiency might impair the baseline physiological roles of NEAT1 in maintaining endothelial or fibroblast homeostasis, our targeted approach specifically disrupts the detrimental signaling axis originating from ischemic cardiomyocytes. By rescuing the macrophage itaconate metabolic pathway without perturbing systemic NEAT1 expression, our findings emphasize that the adverse remodeling post-MI is driven by specific intercellular communication rather than a generalized lncRNA effect. This divergence in experimental outcomes further suggests that NEAT1 may exert multifaceted roles in cardiac pathology, reinforcing the importance of cell-type-specific interventions in the development of future therapeutic strategies.

While our study uncovers a novel inter-organelle and intercellular signaling axis post-MI, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, although we corroborated the upregulation of NEAT1 in ischemic cardiomyocytes using public human single-cell RNA sequencing datasets, our functional and mechanistic investigations rely entirely on murine models and cell lines. The absence of direct in vitro or in vivo validation using primary human cardiac macrophages or clinical biosamples restricts the immediate translational application of our findings. Future studies utilizing human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes (hiPSC-CMs) and human primary macrophages are warranted to validate this paracrine crosstalk. Second, interrogating the negative correlation between NEAT1 and IRG1 in human datasets was hindered by the objective barrier: the temporal discrepancy between our acute murine model (Day 4 post-MI) and available human datasets, which are typically sampled during subacute or chronic phases (e.g., during elective surgeries). Finally, while our comprehensive in vitro assays (including the use of exosome inhibitors) clearly demonstrated the exosome-mediated transfer of NEAT1, directly visualizing and quantitatively tracking the real-time transfer of endogenous exosomes from cardiomyocytes specifically to macrophages in vivo remains technologically challenging given the complex, multicellular nature of the ischemic microenvironment.

In conclusion, our study identifies cardiomyocyte-derived exosomal NEAT1 as a pivotal regulator of post-infarction inflammation. We demonstrate that NEAT1, transferred from ischemic cardiomyocytes via exosomes, interacts with Sox2 in macrophages to induce the epigenetic silencing of the IRG1 promoter. This blockade of the itaconate-mediated metabolic brake exacerbates the inflammatory response and promotes adverse ventricular remodeling. By bridging hypoxic myocardial injury, epigenetic regulation, and immunometabolism, our findings establish the "exosomal NEAT1–Sox2–IRG1″ axis as a critical driver of this pathology. Importantly, cardiomyocyte-specific knockdown of NEAT1 via AAV9 successfully restores macrophage itaconate homeostasis and preserves cardiac function, providing a compelling theoretical basis for targeting this axis as a novel therapeutic avenue for myocardial infarction.

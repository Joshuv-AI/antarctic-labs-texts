Lactate metabolism reprogramming through orthogonal tandem catalysis to reverse intervertebral disc degeneration
Authors: Pengfei Xia, Jiancheng Zheng, Zhaopu Han, Yicheng Chen, Linfeng Xu, Jiangming Yu, Xiaojian Ye, Wenguo Cui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lactate significantly accumulates in intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) to promote inflammation storm and nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs) senescence. However, eliminating the lactate efficiently and inhibiting the inflammation storm and NPCs senescence stimulated by lactate remains a challenge. Here, we show a lactate metabolism reprogramming reactor, which converts lactate to the inhibitor of NPCs senescence (alanine) through orthogonal tandem catalysis (OTC) reaction, thereby guiding the lactate metabolism reprogramming. Enzymes and substrates are encapsulated to prepare OTC nanoparticles, which are embedded in hydrogel microspheres to form the reactors. The lactate metabolism reprogramming efficiently performs in vitro and sustainably conducts in vivo to down-regulate lactate to reduce NLRP3 activity and up-regulate alanine to decrease oxidative stress, significantly inhibiting the inflammatory storm and NPCs senescence. The biomechanical function of neo-generated tissues reaches 94% of that of normal tissues, showing clinical potentials in reversing the IVDD.

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is an age-related musculoskeletal disorder disease, which has a massive effect on patients’ life quality. In clinical practice, surgical and conservative treatments are utilized, but the effectiveness is still unsatisfactory1,2. The lactate is considered as a biomarker for IVDD and is closely related to the development of IVDD. Revealing the lactate-related degeneration mechanism is a potential breakthrough point for improving clinical efficacy. With age increasing, nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs) undergo senescence3. The metabolism balance of extracellular matrix (ECM) in intervertebral disc (IVD) tissues is disrupted4. The inflammation reaction occurs to guide the infiltration of macrophages and other immune cells, exacerbating the development of IVDD5. After IVDD, the NPCs undergo anaerobic glycolysis to produce a large amount of pathological lactate6. The local concentration of lactate in IVDD can reach 10 times that in plasma7. Overexpressed lactate activates NLRP3 inflammasome to intensify inflammation reaction and promotes NPCs senescence through upregulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), accelerating the pathological development of IVDD8,9. What’s worse, several studies have revealed that the ability of IVD tissues to clear lactate is limited10. Therefore, it is necessary to design appropriate strategies to remove the accumulated lactate in IVDD, while few studies have effectively cleared lactate so far.

At present, only a few studies have paid attention to the harm of lactate to IVDD. They catalyze lactate to pyruvate by introducing lactate oxidase11,12. However, pyruvate, an intermediate product of anaerobic glycolysis, can still be ultimately converted to lactate by anaerobic glycolysis13, indicating that the currently practiced methods are unable to effectively eliminate lactate. Enzymes that can be used to catalyze lactate mainly include lactate oxidase and lactate dehydrogenase, both of which catalyze lactate to pyruvate14,15, showing that further catalysis of pyruvate is the key to completely eliminating lactate. When determining the further catalysis strategy of pyruvate, it is found that there are many natural metabolic pathways based on lactate and pyruvate in the human body16. For example, the gluconeogenesis in the liver tissue can catalyze lactate to glucose, not only achieving the lactate clearance, but also synthetizing beneficial glucose for the human body17. Inspired by gluconeogenesis, a hypothesis about catalyzing lactate and pyruvate is proposed: after the lactate is catalyzed to pyruvate, further catalysis products of pyruvate should downregulate the expression levels of ROS mediated by lactate, thereby inhibiting the NPCs senescence18,19. If the hypothesis comes true, the inflammatory reaction in IVDD will be suppressed due to the clearance of lactate, and the problem of NPCs senescence will be solved through the intervention of further catalysis products of pyruvate, facilitating the reversing of IVDD.

Many metabolic pathways of pyruvate have been screened in an attempt to find the candidate catalysis product of pyruvate, which could inhibit ROS levels to deal with the senescence of NPCs. It is worth mentioning that the pyruvate could be catalyzed to form alanine through the transamination reaction in the glucose-alanine cycle of human tissues20. The alanine possesses a remarkable advantage in upregulating the activity of heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) to inhibit the levels of ROS, becoming an effective candidate for suppressing the senescence of NPCs21. Based on the above analyzes, a catalysis hypothesis can be specifically designed, and the catalysis reactions involved in the hypothesis respectively exist in the human body. Firstly, the lactate is catalyzed by lactate oxidase or lactate dehydrogenase to produce pyruvate. Secondly, the pyruvate is catalyzed through the transamination reaction to form alanine. The first and the second reaction are mutually orthogonal. The reactant of the second reaction is the product of the first reaction, illustrating that the catalysis hypothesis exhibits a characteristic of tandem. Therefore, the catalysis hypothesis is named as orthogonal tandem catalysis22. It is well expected that the orthogonal tandem catalysis is able to catalyze lactate to alanine specifically and efficiently to realize the lactate metabolism reprogramming, so as to downregulate the level of inflammation and resist the senescence of NPCs, contributing to the reversal of IVDD.

In this work, a catalysis hypothesis called “orthogonal tandem catalysis (OTC)” is developed to realize the lactate metabolism reprogramming for the repair of IVDD. The lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) that could catalyze lactate to pyruvate and the alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and glutamate (Glu) that could catalyze pyruvate to alanine are loaded together into mesoporous silica nanoparticles (NPs) to prepare OTC NPs, which could gather enzymes and substrates of OTC reaction to ensure that the OTC reaction is efficiently performed. To protect the activity of enzymes and delay the loss of OTC NPs, the OTC NPs are encapsulated into hydrogel microspheres (MSs) with a diameter of 200 μm to form OTC MSs. Besides that, in order to compensate for the loss of NPCs after IVDD, stromal cell-derived factor-1α (SDF-1α) that can recruit autologous stem cells from peripheral tissues is loaded into the OTC MSs to form the lactate metabolism reprogramming reactors (OTC MSs@SDF-1α)23. The advantage of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in lactate metabolism reprogramming is assessed. The effects of lactate metabolism reprogramming enabled by OTC MSs@SDF-1α on the inflammation levels and NPCs senescence stimulated by lactate are detected. The IVDD model of SD rats is constructed to evaluate the potential of OTC MSs@SDF-1α to repair the IVDD in vivo. Results

The lactate metabolism reprogramming reactor (OTC MSs@SDF-1α) formed

The mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) possessed the unique advantages in high specific surface area, excellent biocompatibility, and protecting the activity of enzymes24. Therefore, the MSNs were determined to load the enzymes and substrates used for the OTC reaction. Due to the fact that the hydrogen peroxide was the product of lactate oxidase catalyzing lactate, while the hydrogen peroxide did not produce after the LDH catalyzed lactate. Therefore, to avoid the generation of hydrogen peroxide, the LDH was chosen to catalyze the lactate. Meanwhile, to delay the release of LDH, ALT, Glu, and NAD+ from MSNs to ensure the efficiency of lactate metabolism reprogramming, the aldehyde groups-modified alginate (ALG-CHO) was encapsulated on the surface of OTC NPs based on the Schiff base reaction. The chemical structure of ALG-CHO was confirmed in Supplementary Fig. 1. Figure 2A, B shows that the MSNs composed of Si and O elements possessed the obvious mesoporous structures, which could be used to load LDH, ALT, Glu, and NAD+. To realize the encapsulation of ALG-CHO, the MSNs-NH2 were prepared. The amino group concentration of MSNs-NH2 could reach to 1.348 mmol/g (Fig. 2C). The MSNs-NH2 still retained the obvious mesoporous structures (Fig. 2D). The uniform distribution of N element on the surface of MSNs-NH2 further confirmed the modification of amino groups (Fig. 2E). The LDH, ALT, Glu, and NAD+ could be loaded into the MSNs-NH2. The contents of Glu and NAD+ calculated by the standard curves and HPLC curves of Glu and NAD+ (Supplementary Figs. 2–5) were 0.09121 and 0.0156 mmol/g (Fig. 2F). The contents of LDH and ALT were 0.1567 and 0.2285 KU/g (Fig. 2I). The difference of diameters of MSNs, MSNs-NH2 and OTC NPs was not significant, indicating that the preparation of OTC NPs did not affect the structures of MSNs (Fig. 2J). FTIR spectra and zeta potentials were test to further verify the encapsulation effect of ALG-CHO on the OTC NPs. In Fig. 2K, the characteristic absorption bands at 803 cm−1 and 1412 cm−1 were attributed to the amino groups and aldehyde groups. The intensity of characteristic absorption bands of amino groups in OTC NPs was lower than that in MSNs-NH2, and the intensity of characteristic absorption bands of aldehyde groups in OTC NPs was lower than that in ALG-CHO, showing that the Schiff base effect was formed between the MSNs-NH2 and ALG-CHO. In Fig. 2L, the significant differences in zeta potentials also indicated that the Schiff base effect was formed. Therefore, the FTIR spectra and zeta potentials further proved that the OTC NPs had been successfully prepared.

The ability of OTC NPs to protect the activity of LDH and ALT was assessed, which had a great impact on the efficiency of lactate metabolism reprogramming. The SDS-PAGE assay qualitatively determined that the LDH and ALT were loaded in the OTC NPs (Fig. 2M). The activities of free LDH, free ALT, LDH and ALT in OTC NPs at diverse pH and time points were analyzed. The free LDH, free ALT, LDH and ALT in OTC NPs were cultured in phosphate buffer solution at pH 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7, and 7.4 for up to 28 days. At each time point, the relative activities of free LDH and ALT at pH values of 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7, and 7.4 were lower than those of LDH and ALT in OTC NPs at pH values of 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7, and 7.4 (Fig. 2N, O), illustrating that the OTC NPs could protect the activities of LDH and ALT.

The chemical crosslinking structure of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was studied by the rheological assay25. When the strain was 0.01–10% and the frequency was 0.1–10 rad/s, the storage modulus (G′) was larger than loss modulus (G″), illustrating that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α were well crosslinked (Fig. 3D1, D2). When the strain reached to approximately 30%, the G″ was larger than G′, illustrating that the crosslinking structure of OTC MSs@SDF-1α collapsed (Fig. 3D1).

The injectability of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was tested to reflect their ability of minimum invasiveness26. As shown in Fig. 3D3, the viscosity of OTC MSs@SDF-1α decreased with the shear rate increased, exhibiting a typical shear thinning characteristic. Supplementary Fig. 6 also showed that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α retained the full spherical shape after being injected, which proved that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α possessed an excellent injectability to be injected with minimum invasiveness.

The degradation performance of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in vivo was of great importance to the treatment of IVDD27, since the degradation timescale of OTC MSs@SDF-1α greatly affect the effectiveness of lactate metabolism reprogramming and stem cell recruitment in vivo. As shown in Fig. 3E1–E6 and Supplementary Fig. 7, the fluorescence intensities of Cy5-NHS-labeled OTC MSs@SDF-1α gradually decreased, showing that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α could be degraded in vivo. At 4 weeks after degradation in vivo, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α generally disappeared. The degradation timescale of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was 4 weeks.

The lactate metabolism reprogramming could efficiently perform in vitro and sustainably conduct in vivo for 4 weeks

The performance of lactate metabolism reprogramming was crucial for inhibiting the inflammation levels and the senescence of NPCs stimulated by lactate. Therefore, the lactate metabolism reprogramming performance of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was evaluated in vitro and in vivo. The lactate elimination ability of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in vitro was firstly studied. The concentrations of lactate intervened by OTC MSs@SDF-1α were determined based on the standard curve of absolute OD-concentrations of lactate (Fig. 4A). Figure 4B showed that the concentrations of lactate gradually reduced with the intervention time increased, illustrating that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α possessed an excellent ability to eliminate the lactate. The concentrations of lactate and alanine were then analyzed through HPLC to evaluate the lactate metabolism reprogramming performance in vitro. Based on the standard curves and HPLC curves of lactate and alanine (Fig. 4C–E), the concentrations of lactate and alanine could be determined (Fig. 4F, G). The decreased lactate concentration and the increased alanine concentration shown in Fig. 4F, G proved that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α could eliminate the lactate and produce the alanine through the OTC reaction to realize the lactate metabolism reprogramming. The percentage of eliminating the lactate could reach to 95.4% after the lactate was eliminated for 132 h (Fig. 4F). The transformation percentages of OTC reaction were 9.536%, 18.13%, 35.08%, and 68.87% at 60, 84, 108, and 132 h after the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α (Fig. 4H). The OTC MSs@SDF-1α alleviated the inflammation storm and inhibited the NPCs senescence

Based on the excellent lactate metabolism reprogramming performance in vitro and in vivo, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α could eliminate the lactate and produce the alanine efficiently. The effect of downregulated lactate on suppressing NLRP3 activity to alleviate the inflammation reaction, and the influence of upregulated alanine on increasing the expression of HO-1 to decrease the levels of ROS were detected. As shown in Supplementary Figs. 9A, B and 10, the fluorescence intensity of NLRP3 and positive ratio of ROS significantly increased, after the lactate was added, proving that the lactate could activate the NLRP3 and increase the positive ratio of ROS, where the gate strategy was presented in Supplementary Fig. 11. The fluorescence intensity of NLRP3 in the Lactate+MSs@SDF-1α group was generally equal to that in the lactate group, and the positive ratio of ROS in the Lactate+MSs@SDF-1α group was slightly lower than that in the lactate group (Supplementary Figs. 9C and 10), showing that the effect of MSs@SDF-1α on the NLRP3 and positive ratio of ROS was limited. The Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group significantly decreased the fluorescence intensity of NLRP3 and the positive ratio of ROS (Supplementary Figs. 9D and 10). To verify the impact of alanine on the positive ratio of ROS, the HO-1 inhibitor (zinc protoporphyrin) was applied29. In Supplementary Fig. 9E, the positive ratio of ROS increased, compared with the Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group, illustrating that the HO-1 inhibitor inhibited the effect of elevated HO-1 enabled by the alanine. According to Supplementary Fig. 9D–F, the downregulated lactate caused by OTC MSs@SDF-1α could suppress the activity of NLRP3, and the alanine generated by OTC MSs@SDF-1α could inhibit the positive ratio of ROS through increasing the expression of HO-1.

The trends of inflammation levels (TNF-α and IL-1β) in the five groups were consistent with those of NLRP3 and a positive ratio of ROS in the five groups. Compared with the control group, the inflammation level (TNF-α and IL-1β) of lactate group increased by 391%. Compared with lactate group, the inflammation level (TNF-α and IL-1β) of Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group decreased by 78%, illustrating that the improved NLRP3 and ROS stimulated by lactate aggravated the inflammation reaction (Supplementary Fig. 9G), and the reduced NLRP3 and ROS caused by OTC MSs@SDF-1α could alleviate the inflammation reaction (Supplementary Fig. 9G).

Figure 6D exhibits the KEGG analysis based on the profile 5. Among the 20 most significant pathways, three pathways were associated with environmental information processing pathways, illustrating that the differences of gene expression in the control, lactate, and Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups could be explained by the environmental information processing pathways. The enrichment analysis of cell signaling pathways under the environmental information processing pathways was then performed (Fig. 6E). The MTOR, AMPK, FOXO, and NF-kappa B signaling pathways were significantly enriched, where the FOXO signaling pathway was directly related to the ROS, and could regulate the cell senescence. Therefore, the FOXO signaling pathway was chosen for the further analysis.

In Fig. 6F, there were 33 significant genes in KEGG enrichment analysis of FOXO signaling pathway under series test of cluster. There were 14 significant genes in differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis of FOXO signaling pathway between control and lactate groups. There were 10 significant genes in DEGs analysis of FOXO signaling pathway between the lactate and Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups. There were three common genes among the three sets of genes mentioned above. Three common genes were PLK4, MDM2, and SGK3, where the PLK4 could significantly reflect the effect of ROS on cell senescence. Figure 6G–I shows 14 significant differential genes that were directly related to the FOXO signaling pathway in the control and lactate groups, where 12 genes increased in the lactate group, illustrating that the lactate could increase the ROS levels to promote the cell senescence, which was reflected by the upregulated FOXO signaling pathway. Figure 6J–L shows 10 significant differential genes that were directly related to the FOXO signaling pathway in the lactate and Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups, where 10 genes decreased in the Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group, illustrating that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α could decrease the ROS levels to inhibit cell senescence, which was reflected by the downregulated FOXO signaling pathway.

Specifically, according to Fig. 6I and L, the fold changes of senescence level in NPCs could be determined by the significant genes (PLK4, MDM2 and SGK3) in the FOXO signaling pathway, which was directly related to the cell senescence. Compared to the control group, the expression levels of PLK4, MDM2 and SGK3, respectively, rose by 246%, 138% and 208% after lactate stimulation. Compared to the lactate group, the expression levels of PLK4, MDM2 and SGK3, respectively, decreased by 62%, 72% and 57% after the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α. Because the PLK4 was the most representative gene that could show the effect of ROS on cell senescence, the expression levels of PLK4 were selected to reflect the senescence levels of NPCs. After the stimulation of lactate, the senescence level of NPCs increased by 246% (Fig. 6I). After the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α, the senescence level of NPCs decreased by 62% (Fig. 6L).

In summary, based on the lactate metabolism reprogramming performance, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α showed great advantages in eliminating the lactate to downregulate the activity of NLRP3 and generating the alanine to elevate the expression of HO-1 to decrease the ROS levels, thus alleviating the inflammation storm and inhibiting the NPCs senescence.

Discussion

IVDD is a major public health issue worldwide36,37. Lactate, the end-product of anaerobic glycolysis of NPCs, inevitably accumulates in IVDD38. The accumulated lactate activates the NLRP3 inflammasome to secret the proinflammatory factors such as IL-1β to elevate the inflammation levels and increase the expression of ROS to result in mitochondrial dysfunction to trigger the NPCs senescence, exacerbating the pathological development of IVDD39. However, few studies have efficiently eliminated the lactate and inhibited the inflammation storm and NPCs senescence mediated by lactate so far. In response to the problem of inflammation storm and nucleus pulposus senescence caused by lactate, a lactate metabolism reprogramming reactor (OTC MSs@SDF-1α) is developed in this study to eliminate the lactate and generate the alanine based on the OTC reaction to realize the lactate metabolism reprogramming. The lactate metabolism reprogramming performance of OTC MSs@SDF-1α shows great potential in alleviating the inflammation storm by removing lactate and suppressing the NPCs senescence by generating alanine, thus reversing the IVDD.

To realize the lactate metabolism reprogramming, OTC NPs, a kind of NPs that could convert lactate to alanine based on OTC reaction, were prepared (Fig. 2G, H). The Glu (0.09121 mmol/g), NAD+ (0.0156 mmol/g), LDH (0.1567 KU/g), and ALT (0.2285 KU/g) were loaded in the OTC NPs to ensure that the OTC reaction could be efficiently performed (Fig. 2F, I). The OTC NPs could protect the activities of LDH and ALT (Fig. 2N, O). Furthermore, the OTC NPs were encapsulated into the OTC MSs@SDF-1α to delay the loss of OTC NPs (Fig. 3C). After the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α, the percentage of eliminating the lactate in vitro was 95.4% (Fig. 4F). The transformation percentages of OTC reaction to generate the alanine in vitro reached to 68.87% (Fig. 4H), and the reaction efficiency of OTC reaction in vitro retained at approximately 72% (Fig. 4I). MRS assay further confirmed that the concentrations of lactate significantly reduced and the concentrations of alanine significantly increased at 1 day, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 4 weeks after the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in vivo (Fig. 5F1, F2), showing that the lactate metabolism reprogramming could efficiently perform in vitro and could sustainably conduct for 4 weeks in vivo. Comparing the strategy of lactate metabolism reprogramming developed in this study with strategies of consuming lactate reported in the relevant studies, it was found that the relevant studies only introduced the lactate oxidase to catalyze lactate to pyruvate11,12. But the pyruvate was an intermediate product of anaerobic glycolysis. The pyruvate could still be ultimately converted to lactate by anaerobic glycolysis13, indicating that the reported relevant studies were unable to effectively eliminate lactate. Unlike the relevant studies, the lactate metabolism reprogramming strategy proposed in this study could effectively eliminate the lactate based on the OTC reaction mechanism, since the lactate was reprogrammed to alanine instead of pyruvate (Figs. 4 and 5). What’s more, the alanine generated from the lactate could serve as the inhibitor of NPCs senescence (Fig. 6, and Supplementary Fig. 9). Similar studies have not been reported yet.

The effects of lactate metabolism reprogramming performances on the inflammation storm and NPCs senescence were then assessed. Supplementary Fig. 9D–F showed that the downregulated lactate decreased the fluorescence intensity of NLRP3, and the upregulated alanine reduced the positive ratio of ROS through increasing the secretion of HO-1, thus inhibiting the inflammation storm (Supplementary Fig. 9G). Compared to the lactate group, the inflammation levels in the Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group reduced by 78% (Supplementary Fig. 9G). The ability of OTC MSs@SDF-1α to inhibit the NPCs senescence was determined by RNA sequencing analysis. According to the downregulated FOXO signaling pathway (Fig. 6), the OTC MSs@SDF-1α resisted the NPCs senescence through reducing the ROS levels. After the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α, the senescence level of NPCs reduced by 62% (Fig. 6L), proving that the lactate metabolism reprogramming performance of OTC MSs@SDF-1α showed great advances in inhibiting the inflammation storm and NPCs senescence. Comparing the strategy developed in this study with the strategies reported in the relevant studies, it was found that the relevant studies lacked the ability to intervene in the lactate-related inflammation and NPCs senescence pathway mechanisms in IVDD11,12. In this study, the lactate-related inflammation and NPCs senescence pathway mechanisms were well intervened by the lactate metabolism reprogramming strategy (Fig. 6, and Supplementary Fig. 9). So far, the relevant study that focuses on introducing the strategy of lactate metabolism reprogramming through OTC reaction mechanism to inhibit the inflammation storm and NPCs senescence in IVDD has not been reported.

At 1 week, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks after implantation, the radiological observation, histological and immunofluorescent staining, and biomechanical test simultaneously proved that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α could promote the regeneration of nucleus pulposus (Figs. 7–10, and Supplementary Fig. 15). The biomechanical performances of neo-generated nucleus pulposus tissues could reach 94% of those of healthy tissues (Supplementary Fig. 15). The excellent performance of lactate metabolism reprogramming that could inhibit the expression of NLRP3, ROS, TNF-α, and IL-1β to alleviate the inflammation storm and NPCs senescence in vivo and the significant recruitment and differentiation of stem cells caused the regeneration of nucleus pulposus and function reconstruction of IVD tissues in the OTC MSs@SDF-1α group (Figs. 9 and 10, and Supplementary Fig. 15).

The degradation timescale of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in vivo was an important factor that greatly affected the effectiveness of lactate metabolism reprogramming and stem cell recruitment in vivo. The H&E and SO&FG staining in this study showed that the nucleus pulposus of SD rats degenerated in the Puncture+Lactate group at 4 weeks after the healthy nucleus pulposus was partially removed (Fig. 8). A related study also proved that the partially retained nucleus pulposus degenerated in the puncture group at 1 month after the healthy nucleus pulposus was partially removed1. During the 4 weeks of nucleus pulposus degeneration, the lactate metabolism reprogramming needed to sustainably perform to eliminate the lactate overexpressed by the degenerated nucleus pulposus and generate the alanine to inhibit the inflammatory storm and NPCs senescence2. The MRS assay and immunofluorescent staining of NLRP3, ROS, TNF-α, and IL-1β proved that the lactate metabolism reprogramming sustainably performed for 4 weeks in vivo, realizing the suppression of inflammatory storm and NPCs senescence (Figs. 5, 9 and 10). In this study, the degradation timescale of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was 4 weeks (Fig. 3, and Supplementary Fig. 7), which was able to ensure that the lactate metabolism reprogramming could sustainably operate for 4 weeks (Fig. 5). Supplementary Fig. 13 showed that the SDF-1α was fully released within 1 week. The immunofluorescent staining of CD90 in Fig. 9 showed that the released SDF-1α from OTC MSs@SDF-1α could effectively recruit stem cells from cartilage endplate at 1 week after implantation, illustrating that the OTC MSs@SDF-1α with the degradation timescale of 4 weeks could ensure the sufficient recruitment of stem cells. In the first 4 weeks of implantation in vivo, the nucleus pulposus tissue in the OTC MSs@SDF-1α group mainly exhibited degenerative characteristics, while between 4 weeks and 8 weeks of implantation in vivo, the nucleus pulposus tissue in the OTC MSs@SDF-1α group mainly exhibited regenerative characteristics (Figs. 7 and 8). If the degradation timescale of OTC MSs@SDF-1α exceeded 4 weeks, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α might occupy the regeneration space of the nucleus pulposus. If the degradation timescale of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was less than 4 weeks, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α would disappear before nucleus pulposus degeneration, causing the lactate metabolism reprogramming could not sustainably perform for 4 weeks. Therefore, the 4 weeks of degradation timescale could be considered as the optimal degradation timescale for the OTC MSs@SDF-1α.

However, some limitations need to be improved in future research. On the one hand, the LDH and ALT were loaded in the OTC MSs@SDF-1α to ensure the efficient operation of lactate metabolism reprogramming, while the natural enzymes, such as LDH and ALT existed the immunogenicity, which limited the clinical potential of OTC MSs@SDF-1α. Developing the corresponding nanozymes could simulate the activity of LDH and ALT to inhibit the immunogenicity and may possess higher enzyme activities than natural enzymes (LDH and ALT) to improve the performance of lactate metabolism reprogramming40. On the other hand, the lactate metabolism reprogramming showed great advances in reversing the IVDD. But the universal applicability of lactate metabolism reprogramming for other lactate-related inflammatory senescence diseases needs to be validated. It is well expected that the lactate metabolism reprogramming strategy developed in this work can overcome the above limitations in future research and can be translated into clinical practice to treat the lactate-related inflammatory senescence diseases, including the IVDD.

In conclusion, a lactate metabolism reprogramming reactor (OTC MSs@SDF-1α) is developed to guide the lactate metabolism reprogramming through the OTC reaction. Based on the lactate metabolism reprogramming, the lactate accumulated in IVDD can be efficiently eliminated to generate the alanine. The downregulated lactate and upregulated alanine alleviate the inflammatory response and inhibit the NPCs senescence, achieving the excellent goals of the regeneration and function restoration of IVD.

Methods

Ethical statement

The animal experiments were approved and performed by Shanghai Phenotek Biotechnology Corporation (No. AUP-20241129-01). Male and female SD rats (8 weeks) were obtained from Shanghai Phenotek Biotechnology Corporation.

Materials

The mesoporous silica nanoparticles (purity of >99 wt%) were bought from XFNANO Corp. (China). The (3-Aminopropyl) triethoxysilane (APTES) (purity of ≥99%), ethanol (purity of ≥99.8%), alginate (purity of Analytical Reagent), sodium periodate (purity of ≥99.8%), ethylene glycol (purity of ≥99.8%), Glu (purity of ≥99%), chondroitin sulfate methacryloyl (CSMA), paraffin liquid, span 80, phosphotungstic acid (purity of Analytical Reagent), PBS, and so on were purchased from Aladdin Corp. (China). The LDH (purity of ≥300 μ/mg protein), ALT (purity of ≥75 units/mg protein), NAD+ (purity of 98%), lithium Phenyl (2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl) phosphinate (LAP, purity of 98%), deuterium oxide (purity of 99.9 atom % D), isopropanol (purity of ≥99.9%), hydroxylammonium chloride (purity of 99%), 4-hydroxy benzaldehyde (purity of 98%), sodium hydroxide (purity of 99%), lactate (purity of ≥98%), alanine (purity of 99%), and so on were purchased from Macklin Corp. (China). The SDF-1α (purity of ≥98%) was purchased from PeproTech Corp. (USA). The DAPI was purchased from Beyotime Corp. (China). The Cy5-NHS (purity of 98%) and FITC (purity of 98.6%) were purchased from MedChemExpress Corp. (USA).

Synthesis of OTC NPs

Mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs, 0.1 g) were added in ethanol (10 mL) at 75 oC. 0.04 g of APTES was then dropped. After the reaction was performed for 8 h, MSNs with amino groups (MSNs-NH2) were synthetized. No safety hazards were found. The yield was close to 100%.

1 KU of LDH and 1 KU of ALT were added in 1 mL of water. 10 mg of Glu and 10 mg of NAD+ were added in 1 mL of water. 200 μL of LDH and ALT solutions and 200 μL of Glu and NAD+ solutions were absorbed into the pores of MSNs-NH2 by negative pressure. The aldehyde groups-modified alginate (ALG-CHO) reacted with the amino groups of MSNs-NH2 based on the schiff base reaction to encapsulate the LDH, ALT, Glu, and NAD+. The ALG-CHO was prepared as follows. Briefly, the 1 g of alginate was oxidized by 1.1 g of sodium periodate at room temperature in the dark for 3 h. Excess ethylene glycol (2 g) is added to terminate the oxidation reaction. No safety hazards were found. The yield was close to 100%. After the encapsulation of ALG-CHO, the OTC NPs were obtained.

Fabrication of OTC MSs@SDF-1α

The water phase was firstly prepared, where the concentration of CSMA was 10 wt%, the concentration of OTC NPs was 10 mg/mL, the concentration of SDF-1α was 10 μg/mL, and the concentration of LAP was 0.5 wt%. The oil phase was paraffin liquid mixed with 5 vol% span 80. The water phase with a flow velocity of 30 μL/min and oil phase with a flow velocity of 400 μL/min were collected to a microfluidic system (water in oil emulsion) to form microdroplets, which were crosslinked by a UV curing system (UPP1010, UVATA, China) for 15 min to realize the fabrication of OTC MSs@SDF-1α. After being washed by isopropanol, ethanol and deionized water, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α were fabricated.

Characterization of chemical structures

The 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) spectra of ALG and ALG-CHO were obtained by a 1H NMR spectrometer (400 MHz, Bruker, Germany), where the ALG and ALG-CHO were dispersed in deuterium oxide. The chemical structures of MSNs-NH2, OTC NPs and ALG-CHO were tested by a Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectrometer (Nicolet iS20, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Characterization of morphologies

The OTC NPs and so on were captured by a transmission electron microscope (TEM) (Talos F200X, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). In order to visually display the effect of ALG-CHO encapsulation on the surface of OTC NPs, ALG-CHO was stained with phosphotungstic acid.

The morphology of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was observed by an optical microscope (Eclipse E200, Nikon, Japan), a scanning electron microscope (SEM) (GeminiSEM 300, ZEISS, Germany), and a confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM) (SP8, Leica, Germany). For CLSM observation, an OTC MSs@SDF-1α was stained, where the MSs were stained by DAPI, the OTC NPs were stained by Cy5-NHS, and the SDF-1α was labeled by FITC.

Characterization of amino group concentrations on MSNs-NH2

MSNs-NH2 reacted with the aldehyde group of 4-hydroxy benzaldehyde with a known concentration. The concentration of unreacted 4-hydroxy benzaldehyde was calculated by the titration dosage of hydroxylammonium chloride and sodium hydroxide. The amino group concentrations of MSNs-NH2 were equal to those of reacted 4-hydroxy benzaldehyde.

Characterization of contents of Glu and NAD+ in the OTC NPs

Before the ALG-CHO was encapsulated onto the surface of MSNs-NH2 to form the OTC NPs, the loaded Glu and NAD+ were stimulated by ultrasound to be fully released from MSNs-NH2 to determine the contents of Glu and NAD+ in the OTC NPs. The supernatant was collected and tested by a high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (LC-20AT, SHIMADZU, Japan). For the test of Glu, the mobile phase A was sodium 1-octanesulfonate solution (0.865 mg/mL). The mobile phase B was HPLC-grade acetonitrile. The ratio of A to B was 1:1. The isocratic elution was applied. The flow rate was 1 mL/min. For the test of NAD+, the mobile phase A was potassium phosphate monobasic solution (14.97 mg/mL) and potassium phosphate dibasic solution (19.16 mg/mL). The mobile phase B was HPLC-grade methanol. The ratio of A to B was 9:1. The isocratic elution was applied. The flow rate was 1 mL/min. In order to quantify the contents of Glu and NAD+, the standard curves of peak area-concentrations of Glu and NAD+ were drawn.

Characterization of contents of LDH and ALT in the OTC NPs

Before the ALG-CHO was encapsulated onto the surface of MSNs-NH2 to form the OTC NPs, the loaded LDH and ALT were stimulated by ultrasound to be fully released from MSNs-NH2 to determine the contents of LDH and ALT in the OTC NPs. The supernatant was analyzed by LDH and ALT Assay Kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). The assay protocols were carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Characterization of diameters and zeta potentials

The diameters and zeta potentials were determined through the dynamic light scattering (DLS) (Zetasizer Nano ZS90, Malvern Panalytical, UK). The dispersion concentration was 2 mg/mL.

Qualitative characterization of the loading of LDH and ALT

The loading of LDH and ALT in the OTC NPs was qualitatively tested by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) assay (Beyotime, China). 15 mg of OTC NPs, 15 mg of OTC NPs without LDH & ALT, 1 kU/mL of LDH, and 1 kU/mL of ALT were respectively mixed with SDS-PAGE loading buffer (pH = 6.8) and heated in hot water of 100 oC for 15 min. Then, the supernatant (10 μL) of four groups was subjected to electrophoresis in the 10% Tris-Gly SDS-PAGE gels. Bands of samples were stained with Coomassie Blue Fast Staining Solution (Yuanye Biotechnology, China).

Activities of free LDH, free ALT, LDH and ALT in OTC NPs

The enzyme contents of free LDH and LDH in the OTC NPs were set to the same, and the enzyme contents of free ALT and ALT in the OTC NPs were also the same. Subsequently, the effects of pH and time points on enzyme activities were studied. After the free LDH, free ALT, LDH and ALT in OTC NPs were cultured in phosphate buffer solution at pH 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7, and 7.4 for up to 28 days, the enzyme activities were tested by LDH and ALT Assay Kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). The assay protocols were carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The average relative activities of LDH and ALT in OTC NPs at pH 7.4 and 1 day were set as 100%.

Rheological performance of OTC MSs@SDF-1α

The dynamic moduli and shear thinning behaviors of OTC MSs@SDF-1α and MSs with dispersion concentrations of 50 mg/mL were analyzed through a rheometer (Discovery HR-2, TA Instruments, USA).

Injectability of OTC MSs@SDF-1α

The OTC MSs@SDF-1α were injected by a syringe with a needle of 26G. After being injected, the morphologies of OTC MSs@SDF-1α were observed through an optical microscope (Eclipse E200, Nikon, Japan).

Degradation behaviors of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in vivo

Syringes with needles of 18G were punctured into the caudal IVD tissues of male SD rats (8 weeks). n = 5 male rats. The nucleus pulposus was partially removed to make the remaining nucleus pulposus gradually degenerated, mimicking the development of IVDD. Then, the OTC MSs@SDF-1α labeled by Cy5-NHS were injected. At 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, and 28 days after implantation, the fluorescence images and intensities of Cy5-NHS-labeled OTC MSs@SDF-1α were tested by a live imaging system for small animals (ABL-X6, Tanon, China) to show the degradation behavior of OTC MSs@SDF-1α in vivo. The average fluorescence intensity of Cy5-NHS-labeled OTC MSs@SDF-1α at 1 day was set as 100%.

Release behavior of SDF-1α on OTC MSs@SDF-1α

The cumulative release behavior of SDF-1α on OTC MSs@SDF-1α was studied by the SDF-1α ELISA Kit (Elabscience Biotechnology, China). The test protocol was carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Assessment of lactate metabolism reprogramming performance

Assessment in vitro

The lactate elimination ability of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was studied. Briefly, 100 mg of OTC MSs@SDF-1α were immersed in PBS mixed with lactate at 37 oC for 12, 36, 60, 84, 108, and 132 h. The lactate concentrations of supernatant at diverse time points were tested by a lactate assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China). The test protocol was carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

The elimination of lactate and production of alanine were further determined by the HPLC (LC-20AT, SHIMADZU, Japan). For the test of lactate, the mobile phase A was sodium phosphate dibasic solution (50 mM). There was no mobile phase B. The isocratic elution was applied. The flow rate was 1 mL/min. For the test of alanine, the mobile phase A was sodium dihydrogen phosphite solution (0.02 M). The mobile phase B was HPLC-grade methanol. The ratio of A to B was 7:3. The isocratic elution was applied. The flow rate was 1 mL/min. To quantify the concentrations of lactate and alanine, the standard curves of peak area-concentrations of lactate and alanine were drawn. The transformation percentage of OTC reaction (%) = (the concentration of alanine at a time point/theoretical maximum concentration of alanine) × 100%. The reaction efficiency of OTC reaction (%) = (the concentration of newly generated alanine between two time points/the concentration of consumed lactate between the same two time points) × 100%.

Assessment in vivo

Syringes with needles of 18G were punctured into the caudal IVD tissues of male SD rats (8 weeks). n = 5 male rats. The nucleus pulposus was partially removed to make the remaining nucleus pulposus gradually degenerated, mimicking the development of IVDD. 6 mM of lactate (1 μL) was then injected into the IVDD tissues to mimic the real microenvironment of IVDD. The OTC MSs@SDF-1α were then injected to form the treatment group. The group without the intervention of OTC MSs@SDF-1α was set as the Puncture+Lactate group. At 1 day, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 4 weeks after surgery, the male SD rats were anesthetized and then tested by a magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) (BioSpec 94/30 USR, Bruker BioSpin MRI GMBH, Germany) to quantitatively determine the concentrations of lactate and alanine in living IVD tissues. The MRS spectra were obtained based on the nucleus pulposus areas of MRS images. The concentrations of lactate and alanine were proportional to the peak areas of MRS spectra. The relative concentration of lactate in the Puncture+Lactate group at 1 day was set as 100%.

Test of NLRP3 activity, ROS ratio, and inflammation level

The human primary NPCs were purchased from the Shanghai Xuanke Biotechnology Corporation (catalog number: XK-XB4901). The nucleus pulposus tissues were digested with 0.2% collagenase (Type II) at 37 oC for 4–6 h. Then, NPCs were filtered and centrifuged at 800 × g for 5 min. The isolated primary NPCs were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) mixed with 10 vol% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, USA) and 1 vol% penicillin/streptomycin (Sigma, USA) at 37 oC in an atmosphere with 5% CO2. The NPCs at Passage 2 were used in the experiments. The NPCs cultured in DMEM mixed with 10 vol% FBS (Gibco, USA) and 1 vol% penicillin/streptomycin (Sigma, USA) were set as the control group. The NPCs cultured in 150 μM of lactate and DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin for 24 h were set as the lactate group. The NPCs cultured in 150 μM of lactate, DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin, and MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL) for 24 h were set as the Lactate+MSs@SDF-1α group. The NPCs cultured in 150 μM of lactate, DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin, and OTC MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL) for 24 h were set as the Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group. The NPCs cultured in 150 μM of lactate, DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin, OTC MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL), and HO-1 inhibitor (zinc protoporphyrin) for 24 h were set as the Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α + HO-1 inhibitor group. The NPCs (106 cells/sample) in the above groups stained by DAPI (Beyotime, China), phalloidin (Beyotime, China), Anti-NLRP3 antibody (ab283819, 1:200 dilution, Abcam, UK) and the corresponding fluorescent secondary antibody (SeraCare, USA) were observed by the CLSM (SP8, Leica, Germany). The relative fluorescence intensities of NLRP3 were determined by the ImageJ software. The fluorescence intensity of NLRP3 in the control group was set as 100%. The NPCs (104 positive cells/sample) in the above groups were processed by a ROS assay kit (Beyotime, China) to be tested by a flow cytometry (SFLO 3, Powclin, China). The supernatant of NPCs was collected to be analyzed by the TNF-α and IL-1β ELISA Kits (Multi Sciences, China). The assay protocols were carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Analysis of RNA sequencing

The NPCs cultured in DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin for 24 h were set as the control group. The 150 μM of lactate and DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin were cultured with NPCs for 24 h to form the lactate group. The 150 μM of lactate, OTC MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL) and DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin were cultured with NPCs for 24 h to form the Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group.

The RNA from cultured NPCs (2 × 106 cells/sample) in three groups was extracted by TRIzol. The RNA integrity was verified by the bioanalyzer (Agilent 2100, Agilent Technologies, USA). The mRNA was extracted using Oligo (dT) beads, subsequently fragmented, and then converted into cDNA through reverse transcription. Then the second cDNA strand was formed based on the dNTPs, RNase H, and DNA polymerase I. Before analysis with a sequencer (NovaSeq 6000, Illumina, USA), the purified cDNA obtained through QlAQuick PCR Purification Kit underwent the processing such as end-repair modification, adapter ligation and so on. Clean reads of three independent experiments in Control group were 39576860, 48092852, 49279002. Clean reads of three independent experiments in Lactate group were 56256668, 40484744, 59305412. Clean reads of three independent experiments in Lactate+OTC MSs@SDF-1α group were 51922448, 54230290, 46850962. The clean reads were aligned to the reference genome (GRCh38, ensemble release 111) with HISAT2 (v2.2.1).

As for the bioinformatics analysis, the fragments per kilobase million (FPKM) values were firstly calculated to normalize gene expression levels of different gene sets. Then following quality control, the differential expressed genes (DEGs) analysis was performed by the R package DESeq2 (v1.24.0). The series test of cluster and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis was performed on the OmicShare tools platform (www.omicshare.com). The fold change threshold was set as 1.5 or 2, and the p value was set as 0.05.

With regard to the bioinformatics data visualization, the heat maps and violin plots were employed to reflect the effect of quality control among different gene sets. The STEM software was adopted to select appropriate gene sets generated by the series test of cluster. The circle diagrams and bubble diagrams were employed to show significant pathways clustered in KEGG enrichment analysis. The Venn diagrams, heat maps, volcano plots and radar maps were plotted based on DEGs analysis.

The variation degree of NPCs senescence could be quantitatively determined based on the FPKM values in radar maps. The variation degree of NPCs senescence (%) = (A−B)/B×100%, where the A represented the FPKM values of specific FOXO genes on the innermost layer of radar maps, and the B represented the FPKM values of the counterpart on the second-to-last inner layer of radar maps.

The RNA sequencing data generated in this study have been deposited in the Genome Sequence Archive (National Genomics Data Center, China) under accession code HRA012091

Biocompatibility of OTC MSs@SDF-1α

After the NPCs (106 cells/sample) were respectively cultured with MSs (0.1 mL), OTC MSs (0.1 mL), and OTC MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL) in DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin for 7 days, the live/dead staining and F-actin staining were performed to study the biocompatibility of OTC MSs@SDF-1α. The NPCs (106 cells/sample) cultured in DMEM mixed with FBS and penicillin/streptomycin were set as the control group. Relevant reagents such as fluorescein diacetate, propidium iodide, and phalloidin were purchased from Yeasen Biotechnology Corporation (China). The live/dead staining and F-actin staining images were captured by the CLSM (SP8, Leica, Germany).

Cell recruitment performance of OTC MSs@SDF-1α

The primary bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) of SD rats were purchased from stem cell bank of Chinese Academy of Sciences (China) (catalog number: SCSP-402). The primary BMSCs were obtained from bone marrow tissues of SD rats (3–6 weeks) by mechanical separation under the sterile environment. The isolated primary BMSCs were cultured in DMEM mixed with 10 vol% FBS (Gibco, USA) and 1 vol% penicillin/streptomycin (Sigma, USA) at 37 oC in an atmosphere with 5% CO2. The BMSCs at Passage 3 were used in the experiments.

In the wound healing test, the transwell systems (pore sizes of 8 μm) were applied. The BMSCs (106 cells/sample) cultured in DMEM were incubated in the lower chambers of transwell systems. The FBS was not used to avoid the cell proliferation. When BMSCs spread all over the chambers, wounds were constructed by 100 μL of pipette tips and washed by PBS. The dried MSs (0.1 mL), OTC MSs (0.1 mL), and OTC MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL) were dispersed into the upper chambers of transwell systems to form the MSs, OTC MSs, and OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups. The upper chambers of transwell systems without any materials were set as the control group. After the wounds were healed for 24 h, the BMSCs were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde (Servicebio, China) and stained by 0.1% crystal violet staining solution (Beyotime, China). The behaviors of cell migration to heal the wounds were captured by an optical microscope (Eclipse E200, Nikon, Japan). The wound healing percentages were analyzed by the ImageJ software. In the chemotaxis assay, the transwell systems (pore sizes of 8 μm) were also applied. BMSCs (106 cells/sample) cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS were dropped into the upper chambers of transwell systems. The dried MSs (0.1 mL), OTC MSs (0.1 mL), and OTC MSs@SDF-1α (0.1 mL) were dispersed in the lower chambers of transwell systems to form the MSs, OTC MSs, and OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups. The lower chambers without any materials were set as the control group. After culturing for 24 h, the migrated BMSCs were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde (Servicebio, China), stained by 0.1% crystal violet staining solution (Beyotime, China) and captured by the optical microscope (Eclipse E200, Nikon, Japan). The migrated BMSCs were calculated by the ImageJ software.

Construction and treatment of IVDD models

Male and female SD rats participated in this study to determine the effect of sex on the in vivo performance of lactate metabolism reprogramming reactors (OTC MSs@SDF-1α). n = 5 male SD rats (8 weeks) and 5 SD female rats (8 weeks). The overall number of both male and female SD rats was 15. Syringes with needles of 18G were punctured into the seventh, eighth, ninth, and tenth caudal IVD tissues of male and female SD rats. The nucleus pulposus was partially removed to make the remaining nucleus pulposus gradually degenerated, mimicking the development of IVDD. 6 mM of lactate (1 μL) was injected into the seventh, eighth, ninth, and tenth caudal IVDD tissues to mimic the real microenvironment of IVDD. The MSs, MSs@SDF-1α, and OTC MSs@SDF-1α were then injected into the eighth, ninth, and tenth caudal IVDD tissues. The sixth caudal IVD tissues and the seventh caudal IVDD tissues were set as the control group (healthy group) and Puncture+Lactate group, respectively. The control, Puncture+Lactate, MSs, MSs@SDF-1α, and OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups were respectively set on the sixth to tenth caudal IVD tissues. At 1 week, 4 weeks and 8 weeks after implantation, the samples were separately collected.

Radiological evaluation

The control, Puncture+Lactate, MSs, MSs@SDF-1α, and OTC MSs@SDF-1α groups in male and female SD rats at 1 week, 4 weeks and 8 weeks after implantation were observed by the X-ray imaging system (UltraFocus, Faxitron, USA) and the magnetic resonance imaging system (MRI) (BioSpec 94/30 USR, Bruker BioSpin MRI GMBH, Germany). The average gray values of control group in the male and female SD rats at 1 week after implantation were set as 100%.

Histological and immunofluorescent evaluation

After the samples of male SD rats at 1 week, 4 weeks and 8 weeks after implantation were fixed and decalcified, serial sections (thickness of 3–4 μm) were prepared to perform the histological staining of hematoxylin & eosin (H&E) and safranin O & fast green (SO&FG) and immunofluorescent staining of aggrecan (AGG), collagen II (COL II), NLRP3, CA3, CD90, dihydroethidium (DHE), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and Interleukin-1β (IL-1β). Biomechanical performances

The samples of male SD rats were used to perform the biomechanical evaluation. Statistics and reproducibility

All the data were presented as mean ± standard deviation. For the RNA sequencing, the size of independent experiments was 3, and the difference analysis was performed in the R package DESeq2 (v1.24.0). For the series test of cluster, the one-sided hypergeometric distribution test was used without multiple comparisons. For the DEGs analysis, the two-sided negative binomial distribution test was used without multiple comparisons. For all other data that needed statistical analysis, the sizes of independent experiments were 5, and the difference analysis was performed in GraphPad Prism software. Specifically, when the p values of Shapiro–Wilk test were less than 0.05, the Kruskal–Wallis test and Dunn’s multiple comparisons test were performed. When the p values of Shapiro–Wilk test were larger than 0.05, the Brown–Forsythe test was then conducted. When the p values of Brown–Forsythe test were larger than 0.05, the One-Way analysis of variance (One-Way ANOVA) test and Tukey’s multiple comparisons test were conducted. When the p values of Brown–Forsythe test were less than 0.05, the Brown–Forsythe and Welch ANOVA tests and Dunnett’s T3 multiple comparisons test were conducted. *P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. *P ≥ 0.05 (N.S.) meant no significant difference. Statistical tests that were used in the GraphPad Prism software to determine differences among multiple groups were two-sided. Unless otherwise stated in legends of figures, all the statistical tests were conducted as described above.

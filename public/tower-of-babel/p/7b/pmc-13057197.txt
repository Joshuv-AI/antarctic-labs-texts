Dynamic analysis and structural parameters optimization of reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie based on FEM
Authors: Bin Zhang, Fanting Kong, Jicheng Huang, Kunpeng Tian, Senlin Mu, Zhongqiu Mu, Xingsong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn response to the issues of high cutting force and energy consumption during ramie harvesting due to the strong toughness of ramie fibers, research on optimizing the structural parameters of the cutter has been conducted. In order to analyze the variation in cutting force and energy consumption during the operation of the reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie, a finite element model of the ramie-cutter cutting simulations system was established using ANSYS/LS-DYNA software for numerical simulation. The study analyzed the patterns of cutting force and ramie stalk deformation under different structural parameters of the cutter during the cutting process. A three-factor, three-level Box-Behnken simulation experiment was conducted with the cutting angle, blade angle, and cutter thickness were selected as experimental factors, the maximum cutting force and cutting energy consumption as evaluation indicators. The structural parameter combination of the reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie was optimized and validated through bench tests. The results showed that the optimal effect was achieved with a cutting angle of 24.0°, a blade angle of 23°, and a cutter thickness of 2.5 mm. Under these parameters, the measured average maximum cutting force was 163.44 N, and the cutting energy consumption was 1.49 J. The relative errors compared to the simulated theoretical predictions were less than 10%. The findings of this study can provide a reference for the design and optimization of structural parameters in reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie harvesting.Subject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Ramie as a traditional characteristic fiber crop in China, has a planting area and output accounting for about 95% of the world’s total1–3. It is commonly known as “Chinese grass” in the West. Ramie is the second most important staple fiber crop after cotton4–6. At present, the demand for ramie raw materials in both domestic and international markets is increasing day by day. The supply cannot meet the demand, leading the ramie industry to enter a period of rapid development. However, the field production of ramie is still basically at the manual stage, with high labor intensity and low production efficiency7. Therefore, the research and development of ramie harvesting machinery has become an urgent issue that needs to be addressed in ramie production.

The cutting mechanism is one of the critical components of the ramie combine harvester; the quality of cutting directly affects the subsequent conveying performance and ultimately determines the overall harvesting efficiency. Poor cutting quality can cause the rootstock to break off, resulting in a decrease in the germination rate of the rootstock in the following year, which greatly affects the yield and economic benefits of ramie. In the field of stem cutting research, numerous scholars have conducted a large number of related studies. Cutting technology is extensively utilized in the development of harvesting machinery for crops such as rapeseed, rice and wheat, soybeans, and sugarcane. Various cutting tools have also been developed and applied based on the different characteristics of the crop stems being cut. O. Ghahraei et al.8 conducted an optimization design for the single-disc cutting harvesting equipment for jute stems. M. Nazari Galedar et al.9 established a kinematic model for the double-disc cutting blade for sugarcane harvesting and determined the range of conditions for avoiding missed cuts and preventing collisions with the knife discs. Mathanker S K et al.10 conducted cutting tests on a sugarcane stalk platform to optimize the parameters of the harvesting blades for sugar-cane harvesting. Among them, the reciprocating double-action cutter achieves stronger sliding cutting capability and lower cutting resistance thanks to the simultaneous sliding action generated by its upper and lower blades. Its overall performance is significantly superior to single-action cutters, disc cutters, and swing rotary cutters. Based on these advantages, and building on previous research11–13, this paper selects the reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie stems cutting.

The cutting process of ramie stems results from the interaction between the stem and the cutter. The material properties and geometric dimensions of the cutter have a significant impact on the cutting force and energy consumption. The dynamic behaviors during the stem cutting process include collision, contact, and fracture, and exhibit significant multi-scale coupling characteristics. It is difficult to obtain the interaction forces between the ramie stems and the cutter at different stages using traditional theoretical and experimental methods. With the advancement of computer technology, researchers in the field of agricultural machinery have made significant progress in the application of dynamic simulation. The finite element method (FEM) is typically employed to obtain the variation patterns of cutting forces and energy consumption during crop cutting processes. Researchers have employed the finite element method to establish three-dimensional models for coffee tree lateral branches14, cherry tomatoes15, sugarcane16, castor17, spinach18, mulberry trees19, and their corresponding harvesting devices. Through finite element analysis, the stresses within these models were simulated. This approach significantly reduces experimental costs, shortens development cycles, and enables a detailed and quantitative description of the complex interactions between cutter and ramie stem.

Therefore, this paper develops a finite element model for ramie stem and cutter using LS-DYNA software to simulate the dynamic cutting process. It focuses on analyzing the interaction between various structural parameters of the cutter and the stem, and investigates the cutting mechanism of the reciprocating double-action device. The effects of cutting angle, edge angle, and cutter thickness on maximum cutting force and cutting energy consumption are explored. The Box-Behnken experimental method is employed to determine the optimal structural parameters combination of the reciprocating double-action cutting for ramie, followed by experimental validation. These research findings provide a theoretical basis for analyzing the cutting mechanism of ramie stem and for the design and optimization of harvesting device.

Materials and methods

Machine structure and working principles

Working principles

At present, the main cutter used in the ramie harvesting machine include three types: reciprocating cutter, disc cutter, and swinging rotary cutter. Among them, the double-action reciprocating cutter performs a reciprocating motion with the upper and lower cutting blades moving at the same speed, with equal stroke lengths and opposite directions. It cuts the ramie fibers in this way, featuring a high cutting speed. When the stroke lengths are the same, its cutting speed is twice that of the single-action cutter20. The reciprocating motion inertial forces cancel each other out, facilitating the lateral transportation of the rods. Therefore, the ramie harvesting machine designed and researched in this paper adopts a reciprocating double-action cutter. To prevent ramie fibers from entangling the disc shaft cutter and improve cutting quality, this study proposes a reciprocating double-action cutter specifically designed for ramie harvesting.

Currently, double-action cutter drive mechanisms generally fall into two categories: (1) The upper and lower cutting blades drive mechanisms are located on both sides of the header and are independently driven by two crank–linkage mechanisms, requiring two separate transmission systems, which results in structural complexity. (2) The upper and lower cutting blades drive mechanisms are located on the same side of the header and are driven by a single crank–linkage mechanism connected to two parallel rocker arms of equal length, enabling opposite motion of the two cutter blades. However, to achieve the required cutter stroke, the rocker arms must be relatively long. Therefore, both of these double-action cutter configurations are unsuitable for use in ramie harvesters. The cutting transmission system consists of upper and lower cutting blades assemblies and a dual-eccentric crank–linkage mechanism, primarily used for stem cutting. Mechanical analysis

During cutting, the reciprocating double-action cutter clamps the ramie stem between the two cutting blades. The structural parameters of the blades significantly affect their reliability, energy consumption, and cutting performance.

The impact force generated during stem cutting is the primary external excitation acting on the cutter. Taking a pair of adjacent upper and lower cutters as the objects of study, the impact forces they experience during stem shearing are analyzed. The force distribution on the cutters is illustrated in Fig. 3. The two cutters are symmetrically positioned with respect to the stem. Simulation model

The digital model of the ramie stem is a crucial component of the cutting simulation system. The quality of this model directly affects the effectiveness and accuracy of the simulation results, while also enabling clearer observation of the temporal evolution of internal stress, strain, and other responses during the dynamic cutting process. Therefore, when constructing the ramie stem simulation model, both the geometric parameters and the physical-mechanical properties of the material should closely approximate the actual parameters of the lower part of the ramie stem at the harvest stage. During ramie harvesting, the stubble height is generally maintained between 5 cm and 10 cm. The design parameters of the experimental platform must accommodate the cutting of the hardest parts; therefore, strength parameters measured at 10 cm above ground are selected for modeling.

Based on preliminary mechanical test data, the ramie stem can be simplified as a cylindrical structure composed of three coupled parts: the phloem, xylem, and central pith cavity. The proportion and mechanical properties of the epidermis and central pith are negligible compared to the other two components21. Therefore, this study assumes the ramie stem model consists only of the phloem and xylem. The pith is considered to bear negligible load and has a lesser influence on stem sawing than the phloem and xylem, thus it is ignored in the finite element simulation. A 3D simulation model was constructed using SolidWorks, with a stem diameter of 22 mm and a central inner diameter of 4 mm, as shown in Fig. 5. The cutter was designed based on the relevant standards in “Agricultural Machinery- Cutters bars- Part 3: Knife sections, ledger and knife backs "22 selecting the standard Type V movable blade. After creating the simulation model in SolidWorks, the file was saved in STP format and imported into LS-DYNA software for subsequent mesh generation.

Simulation model in LS-DYNA software.

The accuracy of the mesh division directly affects the precision of the analysis results. A finer mesh yields more accurate results but increases the computational load on the computer. Therefore, the mesh around the cutting zone of the ramie stem model is refined to save computational time while ensuring result accuracy in this study. The mesh division generated a total of 215,666 nodes and 165,534 elements. The meshing results for the ramie stem and cutting blades are shown in Fig. 5.

The cutting blade is fabricated from 65Mn steel, modeled as a rigid body. The ramie stem material is nonlinear, heterogeneous, and anisotropic elastic; therefore, its model employs a linear elastic orthotropic material model (MAT_ORTHOTROPIC_ELASTIC). Contact erosion definition and loading

Contact settings are a crucial aspect of LS-DYNA simulations. Proper contact configuration can reduce the model’s solving time and yield more accurate results. The cutting process involves contact between the cutting blade surface and the stem’s fracture surface, accompanied by material failure as the blade penetrates the ramie stem. In this study, the contact type is defined as eroding surface-to-surface contact (ERODING_SURFACE_TO_SURFACE). The key parameters are specified accordingly, with the cutting blade set as the master eroding surface and the ramie stem as the slave contact surface.

Constraints and motions are applied to the simulation model. Since the model is established on a plane, the cutter’s motion is a combination of forward movement along the x-axis and reciprocating motion along the same axis. During the ramie harvesting process, the harvester is in a moving state. Based on the harvester’s operating conditions during ramie harvesting23, the forward velocity of cutter is set to 0.7 m/s, and the reciprocating cutting speed is also set to 0.7 m/s. To ensure realistic simulation results, the motion of the ramie stem is constrained by applying a fixed boundary condition at the stem’s lower end in the SET module, completing the model preprocessing. The termination time is determined by the machine feed speed and cutting stroke. To fully capture the entire sawing process from initiation to complete severance, the termination time is set to 0.5s via the CONTROL TERMINATION keyword, based on actual operating conditions.

Minimum time step and time step factor are set considering both computational performance and the number of model elements. To prevent negative volume issues, the default minimum time step is defined via the CONTROL TIME STEP keyword, with a time step factor of 0.6.

Hourglass control: Hourglass modes are non-physical phenomena characterized by deformation without energy dissipation. ANSYS/LS-DYNA prevents hourglass modes by applying stiffness that resists these modes without affecting rigid body motion or physical deformation, and velocity damping can be added along the hourglass directions. The fourth type of hourglass control is set using the CONTROL_HOURGLASS keyword; it has a short computation time, with the coefficient set to 0.1. To ensure stable computation and accuracy, the quadratic viscosity coefficient is set to 1.5 and the linear viscosity coefficient to 0.06 via the CONTROL_BULK_VISCOSITY keyword.

After setting the simulation parameters in the LS-DYNA module, the contact force keyword must be added during the solution process to obtain the cutting forces for ramie stem. The generated keyword file is imported into LS-PREPOST software, where the *CONTACT_FORCE_TRANSDUCER_PENALTY keyword is added. Output types of simulation results are selected by enabling commands such as *RCFORC, *GLSTAT, and *MATSUM under the *DATABASE_ASCII_OPTION keyword. After modifying the keyword information, the solver is run. The resulting d3plot file is then opened with LS-PREPOST software to visualize the cutting process animation and analyze cutting force data.

Make all the settings properly, save the file in the keyword file format, and then solve it using the software LS-DYNA.

Experimental factors

The structural parameters of the cutter significantly affect its reliability, energy consumption, and cutting performance. Among these, the cutting angle α is a crucial factor in determining the cutter length and influencing clamping stability and cutting force24. Based on prior analysis, this study selects three experimental factors for the cutter structural parameters: cutting angle, blade angle (the included angle of the cutting blade), and cutter thickness, as shown in Fig. 7. The cutting angle is chosen within the typical range of 15° to 25°. A small blade angle results in insufficient blade strength, leading to potential edge damage, while a large blade angle increases cutting resistance. Referring to relevant experimental studies, the blade angle is set between 20° and 40°. The cutter thickness is selected within the usual range of 2 to 4 mm for cutter.

Results

Simulation results

Analysis of cutting stress

The cutting process was simulated using a set of parameters where the cutter thickness was 3 mm, the blade angle was 25°, and the cutting angle was 20°. The motion parameters were set as a forward speed of 0.7 m/s and a cutting speed of 0.7 m/s. The variations in the components of force along the x, y, and z axes, as well as the total stress on the ramie stem, are shown in Fig. 8.

Simulation results of cutting resistance of ramie stem.

The variation of the resultant cutting force during the cutting process is shown in Fig. 8. After contact between the cutter and the ramie stem, the stem is clamped and undergoes elastic deformation, with the cutting force gradually increasing; this stage is characterized by a low slope in the force-displacement curve. As the two blades continue to clamp and shear the ramie stem, the stem begins to exhibit plastic deformation, causing the cutting force to increase rapidly, reflected by a sudden steepening in the force-displacement curve. After the cutter penetrates the cortex and lignin layers of the stem, the cutting force reaches its peak at 0.014 s, with a maximum value of 211.4 N. At this point, the cutting force comprises the shear force causing plastic deformation of the stem fibers, friction between the cutter and the stem surface, and the elastic deformation force from the uncut stem fibers. The sheared ramie stem loses its inertial stability and bends slightly, reducing the cutting area. As the shear force from plastic deformation and friction between the cutter and the stem end surface gradually decrease, the cutting force begins to decline until the end of the cutting process. Throughout the cutting process, fluctuations in contact friction force occur due to the elastic-plastic deformation of the stem fibers pressing against the cutter.

As shown in Fig. 8, the cutting force component along the x-axis mainly arises from internal friction within the ramie stem during cutting and resistance caused by bending. Therefore, the direction of the force changes accordingly, with relatively small magnitude and both positive and negative values. The cutting force component along the y-axis primarily results from the shearing force exerted by the two blades on the ramie stem and friction between the cutter and the stem’s interior; this force is nearly symmetrical and also includes both positive and negative values. The force component along the z-axis is mainly due to the pressure generated by the elastic-plastic deformation of the ramie fibers. Overall, the resultant cutting force is chiefly composed of the cutting forces acting along the x-axis and z-axis directions.

Analysis of stem morphology

Snapshots of the ramie stem cutting animation were captured to obtain the stress and strain variations at different moments during the cutting process. To facilitate observation, the cutter model was hidden, and the effective stress changes in the ramie stem during cutting progress are shown in Fig. 9.

Post-processing of the simulation.

As shown in Fig. 9(a), at the initial stage of cutting, the two cutting blades clamp the ramie stem, causing some compressive-shear failure elements at the edges. Since the stem primarily undergoes elastic deformation, it remains relatively intact, with higher stress concentrations localized at the cutting area. As the blade edge penetrates the ramie stem, illustrated in Fig. 9(b), a region of extremely high stress forms on the stem surface. The stem begins to exhibit plastic deformation, resulting in symmetrical axial deformation and failure. The failure zone is primarily composed of compressive failure elements, with stress predominantly distributed along the central and lateral directions of the cutting region. Concurrently, the already cut portion of the stem experiences frictional stresses from the upper and lower surfaces of the blade, where the stress reaches its peak. With further cutting as shown in Fig. 9(c), as the penetration depth increases, the stem is completely severed. The elastoplastic strain energy decreases and concentrates near the fixed end, causing the stem to buckle. Since the equivalent stress in the stem due to cutting has been released, only the frictional stress between the blade and stem remains, resulting in a relatively low overall stress in the stem at this stage. The fracture stage is shown in Fig. 9(d). Under the shearing action of the two blades, the severed portion of the stem is ejected outward at a certain velocity, while residual stress at the stem’s lower end causes significant bending.

Analysis of the effect of interaction on the performance index

To analyze the interaction effects on the evaluation indices, one factor was fixed at its intermediate level while the interactive effects of the other two factors were investigated. Response surface methodology and contour plots were employed to analyze the influence of cutter thickness, cutting angle, and blade angle on the evaluation indicators.

Figure 10(a) shows the interaction between cutting angle and blade angle on the maximum cutting force when the cutter thickness is at the mid-level. As can be seen from Fig. 10(a) with the rise of the blade angle, the maximum cutting force shows an upward trend, which becomes more pronounced with increasing cutting angle. This is mainly because, under the same cutting conditions, a larger blade angle corresponds to a larger blade radius, resulting in higher localized stress on the stem which deteriorates cutting performance and makes it more difficult to break the physical structure of the ramie stem, thereby increasing the maximum cutting force. Additionally, the tangential displacement of the blade along the ramie stem decreases, as the cutting angle increase, leading to a greater normal cutting force required, while the sliding of the blade edge in the tangential direction increases, causing the cutting force to increase accordingly.

Influence of interactive factors on maximum cutting force.

Figure 10(b) presents the interaction between cutting angle and cutter thickness on the maximum cutting force when the blade angle is at the mid-level. The maximum cutting force significantly increases with the cutting angle, and this trend is amplified as the cutter thickness increases. This is because the cutting angle determines the cutting direction, and the shear sliding formed between the cutting direction and the normal pressure direction has a considerable effect on the maximum cutting force. As the cutting angle increases, the angle between the reciprocating sawing direction and the normal pressure direction also increases, resulting in a greater normal pressure feed distance and thus in-creased cutting resistance. Moreover, when the cutter thickness is larger, the friction caused by the elastic-plastic deformation of the ramie stem during cutting is greater, making the upward trend of the maximum cutting force more pronounced.

Figure 11(a) shows the interaction between cutting angle and blade angle on cutting energy consumption when the cutter thickness is at a mid-level. The blade angle is positively correlated with cutting energy consumption, and this increasing trend becomes more pronounced as the cutting angle increases. This is mainly because, as the blade angle increases, more cutting energy is required to break the ramie stem fibers; additionally, under larger cutting angles, the shear sliding angle also increases, which raises the normal pressure failure threshold during ramie stem cutting, resulting in a greater total cutting energy consumption and a more evident upward trend.

Influence of interactive factors on cutting energy consumption.

Figure 11(b) illustrates the interaction between cutter thickness and blade angle on cutting energy consumption when the cutting angle is fixed. As the cutter thickness increases, cutting energy consumption first rises slowly and then rapidly, while the in-creasing trend tends to level off with increasing blade angle. This is because a larger cutter thickness increases the surface energy due to the elastic-plastic deformation of the stem on the cutter’s surface and sides, leading to higher cutting energy consumption. When the blade angle is small, the stem slides tangentially along the blade edge, reducing local stress under shear sliding and thus lowering cutting energy consumption. Conversely, when the blade angle is large, the rear blade presses against the stem after cutting in, causing further deformation and damage in both the cutting and axial directions, which increases energy consumption. However, the blade angle selection must also take into account its influence on the service life of the cutter.

Discussion

To investigate the variation in cutting force and energy consumption during the operation of the reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie, this paper uses the unit combination method along with ANSYS/LS-DYNA to conduct a numerical simulation analysis of the ramie cutting process. A simulation experimental design was conducted using cutting angle, blade angle, and cutter thickness as experimental factors, with the maximum cutting force and cutting energy consumption as evaluation indicators. And an optimization analysis of structural parameters of the reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie was performed. The experiment results indicate that the optimal parameter combination were achieved with cutting angle of 24.0°, blade angle of 22.79°, and cutter thickness of 2.46 mm, yielding the maximum cutting force of 156.87 N and the cutting energy consumption of 1.39 J. The optimized parameter combination was also verified through bench test. The verification test showed that the maximum cutting force and cutting energy consumption obtained by the simulation method were basically consistent with those obtained by the bench test under the optimized parameter combination. This simulation method can well describe the cutting process of the ramie reciprocating double-action cutter and provides an effective method for further optimizing the structural design parameters of the ramie reciprocating double-action cutter.

Conclusions

To optimize the parameters of the reciprocating cutter for ramie, this study established a model of the reciprocating double-action cutter and ramie stems using the finite element method. The dynamic process of cutting ramie stems under different structural parameters of the cutter was simulated, and the cutting force and energy consumption during the process were obtained. Based on an orthogonal rotational combination experiment, the effects of the cutting angle, edge angle, and cutter thickness on the maxi-mum cutting force and energy consumption were simulated. The optimal design scheme for the cutter structure was proposed and experimentally validated. The following conclusions were drawn:

(1) The results of the multi-factor simulation experiment indicate that The influence of each factor on the maximum cutting force was: cutter thickness > cutting angle > blade angle. The influence of each factor on the cutting energy consumption was: cutter thickness > cutting angle > blade angle. cutter thickness, cutting angle, and blade angle have a highly significant influence on the experiment evaluation indicators. The optimal combination of structural parameters was achieved with cutting angle of 24.0°, blade angle of 22.79°, and cutter thickness of 2.46 mm, yielding the maximum cutting force of 156.87 N and the cutting energy consumption of 1.39 J.

(2) The verification experiment results demonstrate that under the optimized parameter combination conditions, the simulation method exhibits a high degree of agreement with the maximum cutting force and cutting energy obtained from the bench experiment. The simulation method in this paper can effectively describe the cutting process of the reciprocating double-action cutter for ramie.

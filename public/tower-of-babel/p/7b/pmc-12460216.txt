Bioequivalence Study of Two Formulations of Flunarizine Hydrochloride Capsules in Healthy Chinese Subjects Under Fasting and Fed Conditions
Authors: Yinglin Yang, Jiejing Kai, Duo Lv, Huili Zhou, Yan Yu, Jianzhong Shentu, Guolan Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivesFlunarizine, a selective calcium channel blocker with vasodilatory and neuroprotective effects, is a mainstay for migraine prophylaxis and vertigo management. This study aimed to compare the bioequivalence, pharmacokinetics, and safety of test and reference flunarizine hydrochloride capsules after a single oral dose under fasting/fed conditions.MethodsA randomized, open-label, two-formulation, single-dose, two-period crossover bioequivalence study was conducted under fasting and fed conditions. Eligible healthy Chinese subjects received a single 5-mg dose of the test or reference flunarizine hydrochloride capsules, followed by a 21-day washout interval between periods. Blood samples were collected up to 36 h post-dose. Pharmacokinetic parameters were calculated using noncompartmental methods, and bioequivalence was assessed via geometric mean ratios of the test/reference for primary pharmacokinetic parameters, along with 90% confidence intervals. Tolerability was evaluated during the entire study period.ResultsTwenty-four volunteers completed the fasting study, while 42 volunteers completed the fed study. The test formulation demonstrated bioequivalence to the marketed formulation, with 90% confidence intervals for geometric mean ratios of peak plasma concentration (fasting: 97.38–106.57%; fed: 92.71–109.58%), area under the curve from time 0 to 36 h (fasting: 98.20–108.09%; fed: 93.79–100.81%), and AUC from time 0 to infinity (fasting: 97.88–107.30%; fed: 93.63–100.53%), all within equivalence limits of 80.00–125.00%. High-fat meals delayed the time to maximum concentration by 2.5 h and increased exposure by 20%. Both the test and reference formulations were well tolerated, and no serious adverse events related to the study drug were reported during the study.ConclusionsThis study confirmed that test and reference flunarizine hydrochloride capsules were bioequivalent under fasting and fed conditions.Clinical Trial RegistrationChiCTR1900026713.

Key Points

Bioequivalence between test and reference flunarizine hydrochloride capsules was demonstrated following single dosing under fasting and fed conditions in healthy Chinese subjects.The single 5-mg dose of flunarizine hydrochloride capsule was generally safe and well tolerated.

Introduction

Migraine is a neurological disorder characterized by intense debilitating headaches that may last for several hours or even days. It is often accompanied by additional symptoms, such as nausea and vomiting [1]. The 1-year prevalence of migraines is estimated at approximately 15% [2, 3], with the condition potentially developing at any age, including in infants [4]. According to the World Health Organization, migraine is ranked as the sixth most disabling condition globally and the most disabling among neurological disorders [5]. Consequently, migraines impose a significant burden on individuals’ daily quality of life, highlighting the need for effective and timely treatment.

Flunarizine, introduced in the 1980s, is commonly used in the management of migraine [6]. As a non-selective calcium channel blocker, it also antagonizes dopamine D2 receptors and exhibits antihistamine effects [7, 8]. Flunarizine, a medication that works by inhibiting vascular smooth muscle contraction and modulating neuronal excitability, has been shown to reduce both the frequency and intensity of migraine attacks [9]. Flunarizine is commonly prescribed in various regions, including Europe, Japan, and China, for migraine prevention in adults, while its use in pediatric populations is more restricted [10–15]. Numerous national treatment guidelines recommend flunarizine as a first-line prophylactic option for migraine management, supported by level A evidence, with a typical dose range of 5–10 mg [12, 16]. However, common side effects include weight gain, fatigue, mood changes, and drowsiness [12, 15]. To minimize the impact of daytime drowsiness, it is generally recommended that flunarizine be taken at bedtime.

Following oral administration, flunarizine is well absorbed, with peak plasma concentrations occurring 2–4 h post-dose [17]. With consistent daily dosing of 10 mg, plasma concentrations gradually increase, reaching steady-state concentrations after approximately 5–6 weeks of treatment. The large apparent volume of distribution (range = 26.7–79.9 L/kg) observed after oral administration suggests extensive tissue distribution of flunarizine [17]. The elimination half-life (t1/2) of flunarizine after long-term use ranges from 4 to 19 days [17]. It is primarily metabolized in the liver via N-oxidation and aromatic hydroxylation [18]. Follow a single dose, less than 7% of flunarizine and its metabolites were recovered in feces and urine within 48 h [17].

The aim was to evaluate the pharmacokinetics, bioequivalence (BE), and safety of two formulations of flunarizine. The test formulation was developed by Chiatai Qingchunbao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China, while the reference formulation, Sibelium®, was produced by Xi’an Janssen Pharmaceutica Ltd., China. The evaluation was conducted following a single-dose administration of both formulations. This study also aims to provide evidence supporting the approval of generic flunarizine formulations in China.

Materials and Methods

Ethics Statement of Human Rights

With the registration number ChiCTR1900026713, this study was recorded with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry. Approval No. 2018-EC-333 was given after the study protocol and informed consent forms were reviewed and approved by the independent Ethics Committee at the First Affiliated Hospital, School of Medicine, Zhejiang University. The phase I clinical trial unit of The First Affiliated Hospital was the sole site of the clinical procedures. Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki were followed throughout the research. Before taking part in any study procedures, all participants were asked to provide written informed permission.

Study Participants

Healthy volunteers had to be between the ages of 18 and 45 years and have a body mass index between 19 and 26 kg/m2. Male participants were required to be at least 50 kg in weight, while female participants were required to be at least 45 kg. In order to be eligible to participate, individuals had to have a clean bill of health according to the study’s inclusion criteria, which included no major health issues as shown by their medical records, electrocardiograms, clinical laboratory tests, vital signs, or physical exams. In addition, the kidney and liver function laboratory values at baseline were required to be within normal limits. All female participants were required to utilize effective methods of birth control during the study and for a minimum of 6 months after the last dose, and they must not be pregnant when they enrolled. The study did not include any participants who fulfilled any of the exclusion criteria listed in the protocol. Subjects who had anaphylactic reactions to any aspect of the experimental product or had a history of clinical medication allergies were also not allowed to participate. Additionally, subjects could not participate if they tested positive for human immunodeficiency virus or hepatitis B or C. Additional exclusion criteria were being a smoker or user of nicotine products within 3 months before the study, taking any vitamins, herbs, or medications within 2 weeks before the study drug was given, and taking any medications that alter cytochrome P450 enzymes within 30 days before enrolling. Subjects who drank alcohol or ate foods high in caffeine within 2 days of the trial also were not allowed to participate.

Study Drugs

The 5-mg capsules with an active ingredient content of 96.2% were supplied by Chiatai Qingchunbao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. of Hangzhou, China. The batch number is 1903301, and the expiration date is 11 March, 2023. Sibelium®, a 5-mg capsule with the batch number 180902850 serving as the reference formulation, was manufactured in China by Xi’an Janssen Pharmaceutical Ltd.

Study Design

There were two separate parts to this research: the fasting condition and the fed condition. Both studies used a two-sequence, two-period, randomized, open-label, two-formulation, crossover BE design. Researchers used a random number table developed by SAS statistical software (version 9.4) to assign participants to one of two treatment sequences: test-reference or reference-test, with a 1:1 allocation ratio. Each subject took a single oral dose of a flunarizine hydrochloride 5-mg capsule as a test or reference formulation during each treatment period. Between each treatment phase, a washout period of 21 days (± 2 days) was put in place to make sure that the previous dose was cleared out. Two days before each treatment phase, subjects were hospitalized to the phase I clinical trial unit to standardize their nutritional consumption and physical activity levels.

After an overnight fast of at least 10 h, participants in the fasting condition took one 5-mg oral dose of the test or reference formulation with 240 mL of warm water. Thirty minutes before medication delivery, individuals in the fed group ate a standardized high-fat meal (800–1000 kcal), with about 150 kcal coming from protein, 250 kcal from carbohydrates, and 500–600 kcal from fat. Water intake was not permitted from 1 h prior to dosing until 1 h post-dosing, except for 240 mL of water allowed for swallowing the drug. At 4 and 10 h after the medication was administered, standardized meals were given.

Sample Size

Based on clinical pharmacology and biopharmaceutical reviews, flunarizine is not classified as a highly variable drug. Consequently,, the coefficient of intra-individual variation (CVintra) for the maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and the area under the curve (AUC) is presumed to be less than 30%. For the fasting condition, the CVintra for Cmax and AUC was assumed to be 20%. With a one-sided α of 0.05 and a power of 0.8 (β = 0.2), the geometric mean ratio (GMR) between the test and reference formulations was expected to fall between 0.95 and 1.05, with a 90% confidence interval (CI) of 80.00–125.00. Thus, the fasting study required 20 participants. After accounting for potential dropouts, 24 subjects were enrolled for the fasting condition. Given that postprandial conditions could delay the time to Cmax and affect bioavailability, the CVintra for Cmax and AUC under the fed condition was estimated at 29%, with the remaining assumptions unchanged. Therefore, 36 participants were required for the fed condition, and 42 participants were ultimately enrolled after considering the dropout rate.

Biological Sample Collection

Previous studies indicate that flunarizine reaches its Cmax of approximately 20 ng/mL within 1–3 h [18–20]. The terminal t1/2 is significantly influenced by the duration of sampling: a 24-h sampling window yields a t1/2 of 5–7 h, while a 72-h window reveals approximately 31 h [18–20]. By 24 h, mean concentrations decline to ≤ 2 ng/mL, which is less than 10% of Cmax. For immediate-release BE studies, guidelines stipulate that the sampling schedule must include frequent sampling around the predicted time to Cmax (Tmax) to provide a reliable estimate of peak exposure. Additionally, the plasma concentration–time curve should be sufficiently extended to ensure that the AUC from time zero to time t (AUC0–t) encompasses at least 80% of the AUC from time zero to infinity (AUC0–∞). Based on these findings, we have designed a 36-h sample schedule. Within the fasting condition, blood samples were collected at pre-dose (0.0 h) and at 0.5, 1.0, and 1.33 h (1 h 20 min), 1.67 h (1 h 40 min), 2.0 and 2.33 h (2 h 20 min), 2.67 h (2 h 40 min), 3.0 and 3.33 h (3 h 20 min), 3.67 h (3 h 40 min), and 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, 12.0, 24.0, and 36.0 h after dosing. When comparing the fed and fasting settings, the difference in Tmax was about 3.5 h. Here, blood samples were taken at pre-dose (0.0 h) and at 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, and 3.33 h (3 h 20 min), 3.67 h (3 h 40 min), 4.0 and 4.33 h (4 h 20 min), 4.67 h (4 h 40 min), 5.0 and 5.33 h (5 h 20 min), 5.67 h (5 h 40 min), and 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, 12.0, 24.0, and 36.0 h after dosing. The blood samples were centrifuged at 2000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the plasma was stored at − 60 °C until analysis.

Plasma Drug Concentration Analysis

Plasma concentrations of flunarizine were determined at Nanjing Kelitai Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China) using a validated, sensitive, and specific high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry method. The system comprised an LC-20AD™ (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) coupled with an API 4000 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, Framingham, MA, USA). Chromatography was performed using a Kinetex 2.6 μm XB-C18 column (50 × 2.1 mm) equipped with a Phenomenex Security Guard Cartridges C18 (4 × 2.0 mm). The mobile phase consisted of 0.5% formic acid and 10.0 mM of NH4Ac in water (mobile phase A) and acetonitrile (mobile phase B) running at a flow rate of 0.4 mL/min. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–1.7 min, 52%A/48%B; 1.8–2.5 min, 0%A/100%B; 2.6–4.0 min, 52%A/48%B. Quantification was performed using multiple reaction monitoring of the transitions m/z 405.2 → 203.2 for flunarizine and m/z 413.2→203.2 for flunarizine-d8 (internal standard).

The assay exhibited linearity within the concentration range of 0.250–60.0 ng/mL, with a lower limit of quantitation (LLOQ) of 0.250 ng/mL. In the blank matrix, we did not observe any interference at the peak location of flunarizine and internal standard, which indicates that the liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry analysis method was highly selective. No carry-over effect was observed, as the injection of wash following plasma with the highest spiked concentration showed no detectable peaks (< 20 % of the LLOQ) at the retention times of flunarizine or the internal standard. In plasma samples, the intra- and inter-day precision (coefficient of variation) for the LLOQ was below 7.1%. Furthermore, the intra- and inter-day accuracy for LLOQ ranged from 90.8 to 99.2%. The intra- and inter-day precision for nominal concentrations of low (LQC, 0.700 ng/mL), low-medium (MQC1, 8.00 ng/mL), medium (MQC2, 18.0 ng/mL), and high (HQC, 45.0 ng/mL) quality-control samples was below 3.7%. The intra-day accuracy ranged from 95.1 to 102.4%, while the inter-day accuracy ranged from 96.7 to 100.9%. Stability tests indicated that plasma sample decomposition was less than 10% for all conditions examined (19 h at room temperature, five freeze-thaw cycles at − 70 °C, and 107 days of storage at or below −70 °C).

Safety Assessments

A variety of safety parameters were monitored, including electrocardiograms, vital signs, clinical laboratory tests, and adverse events (AEs). The AEs were classified and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.3. The Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities was used for recording these events, with system organ class and preferred term serving as the determining factors. Research physicians and volunteers both reported AEs throughout the trial. Follow-up for participants experiencing AEs continued until their symptoms or corresponding physical and clinical examination results returned to normal or baseline levels, or until the participant chose not to attend further follow-up visits.

Pharmacokinetic and Statistical Analyses

The flunarizine pharmacokinetic (PK) parameters were determined using a non-compartmental model in Phoenix WinNonlin software (version 7.0). The Cmax and Tmax were taken directly from the plasma concentration–time profiles. The AUC from 0 to 36 h (AUC0–36h) was computed using the trapezoidal rule. The AUC0–∞ was calculated by adding the AUC0–36h and the ratio of the last measurable concentration to the elimination rate constant. The elimination rate constant was found by running a log-linear regression analysis on the plasma concentration data during the terminal elimination phase. The elimination t1/2 was then calculated as 0.693/elimination rate constant.

The effects of formulation, treatment period, dosing sequence, and individual individuals were assessed by analysis of variance using a linear mixed model, after Cmax, AUC0–36h, and AUC0–∞ were transformed to their natural logarithmic values. The GMR and associated 90% CI for the test-to-reference formulation were determined using the logarithmically converted major PK parameters. If the 90% CIs for the GMR of the main PK parameters were between 80.00 and 125.00%, it was determined that BE had occurred. The Tmax was compared between the two versions using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Pharmacokinetics and Bioequivalence

Pharmacokinetic parameters were evaluated using the PK concentration set and the PK parameter set. All 24 participants in the fasting group who were randomized completed both treatment periods and were included in the PK parameter set. In period 2, pre-dose plasma concentrations for seven subjects were detected above the LLOQ, range from 0.255 to 0.322 ng/mL. These values, which were substantially lower than 5% of the Cmax (≥ 0.795 ng/mL), were considered acceptable and included in the BE analysis set. Similarly, in the fed group, eight participants had pre-dose plasma concentrations that ranged from 0.260 to 0.426 ng/mL, and these were also included in the BE analysis set. Safety Assessment

Safety and tolerability were assessed using the safety analysis set (SS), which included all subjects who received at least one dose of the study drug. In the fasting study, 24 participants were part of the SS. A total of 11 AEs were reported across 11 subjects, yielding an incidence rate of 45.8% (11/24). Six AEs occurred in six participants receiving the reference product, with an incidence of 6.67% (6/24), while five AEs were reported by five participants in the test product group, corresponding to an incidence rate of 5.63% (5/24). All AEs were classified as grade 1 and resolved spontaneously without specific treatment. Notably, one participant exhibited elevated serum triglyceride levels that did not return to baseline during the follow-up period.

In the fed study, 42 participants were included in the SS. A total of 27 AEs were reported by 16 subjects, resulting in an incidence rate of 38.1% (16/42). The AE incidence was 33.3% (8/42) in the test product group and 41.7% (10/42) in the reference product group. Similar to the fasting study, all AEs were of grade 1 severity and resolved spontaneously without the need for specific intervention. These findings suggest that both the test and reference formulations of flunarizine were well tolerated and safe in healthy volunteers, irrespective of the fasting or fed conditions. Discussion

The study confirmed the BE of the test and reference formulations of flunarizine hydrochloride capsules in healthy Chinese participants, both under fasting and fed conditions. Additionally, the study provided valuable insights into the pharmacokinetics of flunarizine within this cohort. Both formulations were well tolerated, with no severe AEs associated with the study drug.

For most BE studies, a sample size of 18–24 subjects is typically sufficient. In this trial, the CVintra for Cmax and AUC under fasting conditions was 9.12% and 9.71%, respectively. Under fed conditions, the CVintra for Cmax was 23.05%, while AUC had a CVintra of 9.84%. These CVintra values for Cmax were slightly lower than those reported in a previous study, where the values were 14.78% for the fasting condition and 23.65% for the fed condition [20]. In both conditions, the CVintra for all parameters was less than 30%, indicating that the sample size and study design were robust enough to ensure sufficient statistical power for meeting BE criteria.

The study’s 21-day washout period was carefully designed based on the PK profile of flunarizine. Because of its high lipophilicity, flunarizine tends to accumulate in tissues and exhibits prolonged redistribution, which can result in detectable plasma concentrations (> 0.5 ng/mL) even 30 days after administration in a small subset of individuals. To minimize the potential for interference from residual drug concentrations and address inter-individual variability, the washout period was extended to 21 days, exceeding the typical 5 × terminal t1/2 recommendation. After this washout period, residual flunarizine concentrations in 13 subjects (median 0.292 ng/mL, range 0.255–0.426 ng/mL) were confirmed to be well below 5% of the Cmax for each subject during that period. This strategy helped maintain the stability of key physiological markers, ensured operational efficiency with a total study duration of fewer than 28 days, and maximized the inclusion of evaluable PK data for the BE analysis.

When comparing the PK parameters of flunarizine with those reported in previous studies [17–21], the observed values for Cmax and Tmax were consistent with those documented in the Chinese population, thus supporting the validity of our results. However, the Cmax in this study was lower than that reported in healthy Indian volunteers (17.34 vs 32.14 ng/mL) [22]. This discrepancy may be attributed to ethnic differences or genetic polymorphisms. The elimination t1/2 of the reference flunarizine formulation was 8.98 ± 1.50 h under fasting conditions and 9.37 ± 2.55 h following food intake. These values are within the range specified for the labeled reference product (5–15 h) but are shorter than those observed in studies with extended sampling durations [21, 22]. This difference can be explained by the variations in the timing of terminal phase sampling. In our BE study, the elimination t1/2 was estimated using a 36-h post-dose sampling window. Despite the relatively shorter duration, the mean plasma concentration of flunarizine at 36 h post-dose was as low as 1.2 ng/mL, which is well below one tenth of the observed Cmax. This indicates that our sampling schedule sufficiently captured the plasma concentration–time profile, providing a reliable measure of drug exposure and enabling an accurate estimation of AUC0–∞. Our study design resulted in an average AUC0–36h/AUC0–∞ ratio of 92.33% under both fasting and fed conditions. Each individual AUC0–36h/AUC0–∞value is more than 83.96%, surpassing the recommended threshold of 80% [23, 24]. Based on these findings, a sampling period longer than 36 h is not considered necessary for BE studies of immediate-release flunarizine formulations.

Further comparison of the PK data under fasting and fed conditions revealed that food influenced the absorption rate of flunarizine. Specifically, the mean Cmax increased by 37.4%, and the median Tmax was delayed from 2.33 h under fasting conditions to 4.67 h after food intake. As a result, the AUC values for flunarizine were significantly higher when administered with food compared with the fasting state. These findings are consistent with previous reports [20, 21].

Our study presents a significant advantage concerning the sex composition of volunteers. Given that women experience a three-fold higher prevalence of migraine compared with men [25], prior BE research has predominantly focused on male participants (78.4–100%), with limited inclusion of female subjects [18–21]. Our study included 26 female volunteers, constituting 42.4% of the total sample. This substantial female representation enabled a robust comparison of PK exposure between sexes. Our analysis revealed no significant differences in the key PK parameters between male and female participants following the single-dose administration of flunarizine (Table 5). However, as the majority of participants were aged between 18 and 40 years, further research is warranted to explore the pharmacokinetics of flunarizine in older populations. Given that flunarizine is primarily metabolized in the liver, caution is advised when using the drug in patients with severe liver dysfunction. All AEs observed in this study were mild and occurred at low frequencies. Participants were hospitalized for 5 days during each treatment period, and it is plausible that the hypertriglyceridemia observed in some cases could be transient, potentially owing to reduced physical activity during this time. All reported AEs were mild to moderate in nature, with no serious AEs documented. Overall, both the test and reference formulations demonstrated good tolerance among all participants.

Conclusions

The findings of this inquiry demonstrate that the generic flunarizine hydrochloride capsules available in China exhibit BE to the reference formulation (Sibelium®) regarding both the rate and extent of absorption, observed under fasting and fed states. Both formulations exhibited a favorable tolerance profile among the healthy Chinese population and can be regarded as interchangeable for clinical applications, potentially contributing to the reduction in the economic burden faced by patients with migraine in China.

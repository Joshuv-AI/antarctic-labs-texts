Emissive Room-Temperature Ionic Liquids Enabled by Dialkylphospholium-Based Tetracyclic Cores with Bulky Counter Anions
Authors: Masahito Murai, Shohei Kamegai, Keita Andoh, Shigehiro Yamaguchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Metal-free ionic liquids that exhibit intense fluorescence in the visible region of over 500 nm in the fluid state remain scarce. Although small π-conjugated frameworks combining electron-donating and electron-accepting groups represent a promising structural motif, such modifications often lead to fluorescence quenching in polar environments, including neat ionic liquids. Herein, we report fluorescent ionic liquids based on a phospholium containing a tetracyclic core. Incorporation of both an amino group and the phosphonium center in the same benzene ring prevents excessive spatial separation of HOMO and LUMO, thereby suppressing the increase in intramolecular charge-transfer character in the excited state. This push–pull architecture enables the ionic liquids to display bright yellow fluorescence with a large Stokes shift under neat conditions. Furthermore, bridging with a dialkylphosphonium unit effectively suppresses aggregation-induced quenching while lowering the glass transition temperature. The introduction of bulky counter anions, such as a tetrakis­(3,5-bis­(trifluoromethyl)­phenyl)­borate, further decreases the glass transition temperature by hindering π-core proximity and significantly increases the fluorescent quantum yield up to 0.64. A neat film of one derivative, doped with an additional red-emissive dye, exhibited red fluorescence through a Förster resonance energy transfer.

Introduction

Charged compounds that exhibit melting points below 100 °C are classified as ionic liquids (ILs). Owing to their low volatility, high thermal stability, and superior ion conductivity, ILs have been widely utilized as reaction media, electrolytes, and conductive materials. Despite these advantages, the development of ILs that exhibit emission with sufficiently high fluorescence quantum yields remains a formidable challenge due to pronounced fluorescence quenching under neat conditions. A representative work is benzobisimidazolium ions, showing blue fluorescence reported by Bielawski and co-workers in 2007. While several fluorescent ILs have been developed to date, examples of organic dyes that are liquid even at room temperature and emit fluorescence at wavelengths longer than 500 nm remain scarce. Although ILs incorporating transition metals or rare-earth elements have been reported to exhibit phosphorescent emission in the long-wavelength region, it is often necessary to measure at low temperatures to obtain reasonable luminescence quantum yields. Considering concerns regarding toxicity to biological tissues and the high costs of such metals, the development of metal-free ILs that exhibit intense fluorescence even at room temperature remains an ongoing challenge.

In the design of fluorescent ILs that emit in the long-wavelength visible region, it is essential to employ a compact π-conjugated framework with a narrow HOMO–LUMO energy gap. The introduction of electron-donating and electron-accepting moieties into the π-conjugated skeleton is an effective strategy to achieve this purpose. , However, a key challenge lies in suppressing the nonradiative decay process. Donor−π–acceptor-type dyes often form largely polarized excited states, which promote the nonradiative decay pathways and thereby lead to significant fluorescence quenching, especially in polar environments, including neat ionic liquids (Figure a).

To address this challenge, this study employs a compact tetracyclic core incorporating a phosphole ring that contains a phosphonium moiety (i.e., phospholium), , instead of the commonly used nitrogen-based cationic scaffolds. The phospholium-containing ladder-type tetracyclic compounds were originally reported by Baumgartner and co-workers, who investigated their intriguing photophysical properties, liquid crystalline behavior, and the ON/OFF-emission switching via photoinduced-electron transfer (PET) from an electron-donating aryl group attached to the phosphorus center. We anticipated that the introduction of electron-donating groups into this tetracyclic framework would lead to a long-wavelength fluorescence. However, as described above, such structural modifications may compromise the fluorescent quantum yields in polar environments inherent to ILs.

In this study, we demonstrate that introducing an amino group and the phosphonium center in the same benzene ring induces long-wavelength emission without increasing the intramolecular charge-transfer character in the excited state (Figure b). To mitigate fluorescence quenching under neat conditions, we further introduce alkyl groups on the phosphorus center. This structural modification effectively increases the spatial separation between π-conjugated frameworks, thereby reducing the number of intermolecular interactions. Such a reduction in π-core proximity is also expected to facilitate liquefaction of the resulting compound, as the melting point (T m) is strongly influenced by π–π and electrostatic interaction between charged π-skeletons (Figure a). The effectiveness of introducing alkyl groups in achieving both low T m and high fluorescence quantum yields has been previously demonstrated in neutral fluorescent organic liquids, as exemplified by Nakanishi’s emissive anthracene-based liquids.

Herein, we examine the validity of these structural elaboration strategies for developing fluorescent ILs by synthesizing dialkylphospholium salts 1–5, each featuring different substituents on the phosphorus and nitrogen atoms, along with alkylphosphole oxide analogue 6 for comparison (Figure c). We evaluated the quantum yields of these compounds under neat conditions, which were mostly unexplored in previous studies, ,,,− , and found that the emission efficiency is significantly affected not only by the substitution pattern of the cationic π-conjugated skeleton but also by the choice of counter anions (Figure b). These design parameters likewise impact the thermal transition temperatures associated with fluidization, namely, T m and glass transition temperature (T g), which are critical factors for achieving liquefaction. ,,,, Additionally, a theoretical study underscores the importance of positioning the dialkylamino group and the phosphonium center on the same benzene ring, as this arrangement induces intriguing structural relaxation in the excited state without causing HOMO and LUMO separation.

Results and Discussion

A series of dialkylphospholium salts 1 was synthesized using 3-bromo-2-(4-amino-2-bromophenyl)­benzo­[b]­thiophene 7 as a key precursor. While most of the previous phospholium salts have been synthesized by the reaction of dilithiobiaryls with aryldichlorophosphines (ArPCl2), we adopted an alternative strategy for the facile and reliable synthesis of dialkylphospholium salts 1, as shown in Scheme . This approach was chosen to circumvent the use of inherently unstable alkyldichlorophosphines and to allow the introduction of various alkyl groups at the phosphorus center. Thus, a trivalent aminophosphole 8 was first synthesized by sequential treatment of 7 with n-BuLi followed by (Et2N)­PCl2. The reaction with CH3OH, followed by removal of volatiles and subsequent treatment with n-BuLi, introduced a n-butyl group on the phosphorus atom, leading to 9, which was finally converted into 1 OTf via 1 I by using MeI as a methyl source and NaOTf , respectively. Other phospholium salts 1 NNf2 and 1 BArF4 with different counter anions were obtained by treating 1 I with NaNNf2 or NaBAr4 F, respectively (Nf = SO2(n-C4F9), ArF = 3,5-(CF3)2C6H3). Furthermore, replacing MeOTf with n-BuOTf in the final alkylation step of 9 gave 2 OTf , and the use of dichlorophenylphosphine in place of (Et2N)­PCl2 as the phosphorus source resulted in the formation of 3 OTf (see SI for details). Dimethylamino- and diphenylamino-substituted 4 OTf and 5 OTf as well as the corresponding phosphine oxide analogue 6 were also synthesized as reference compounds.

Among the synthesized compounds, 1 OTf , 1 NNf2 , and 1 BArF4 were viscous oils at room temperature, whereas 1 I , 2 OTf , 3 OTf , 4 OTf , and 5 OTf were obtained as solids. All derivatives exhibited excellent stability toward water and air and could be purified by column chromatography without the need for special precautions. No decomposition was observed even after storing these dyes under ambient conditions for more than a year. Good thermal stability was also confirmed by thermogravimetric (TG) analysis. All of the room-temperature ILs, 1 OTf , 1 NNf2 , and 1 BArF4 , exhibited high decomposition temperatures at 5% weight loss (T d5) that exceed 290 °C (Table and Figure S1). These values are higher than those of the corresponding phosphine oxide 6 (T d5 = 247 °C), underscoring the superior thermal robustness of the ionic liquids 1 compared to their charge-neutral analogue.

The effects of the substituents on the phosphorus and nitrogen atoms as well as the counter anions on the T m and T g were assessed by the differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis (Table ). While 2 OTf and 3 OTf , bearing dibutyl or phenyl groups on the phosphorus atoms, exhibited T m values of 99 and 154 °C, respectively, 1 OTf did not display a distinct T m. Instead, it showed a T g of 9 °C, indicating that 1 OTf exists as an IL at room temperature. These results highlight the importance of the unsymmetric dialkylphospholium framework in promoting the liquefaction. A further decrease in T g was observed by introducing bulky counter anions. Among the compounds studied, 1 NNf2 featuring Nf2N– showed the lowest T g value of – 12 °C (Figure S2). These findings suggest that the bulky counter anions, such as TfO–, Nf2N–, and BAr4 F–, effectively hamper the intermolecular interaction of the π-conjugated frameworks. In addition, the fact that 4 OTf and 5 OTf , featuring dimethyl- and diphenylamino groups, respectively, displayed T m values exceeding 100 °C demonstrates the importance of incorporating alkyl chains on both the phosphorus and nitrogen atoms to modulate the thermal properties. Notably, the endothermic transitions corresponding to T m of 2 OTf –5 OTf were observed only during the first heating cycle. This behavior indicates that, once melted, these compounds may not readily reform an ordered structure upon cooling under neat conditions and, instead, form a glassy state.

To gain insight into the structural features of the tetracyclic phospholium salts, the X-ray crystallographic analysis was conducted on single crystals of 2 OTf obtained by vapor diffusion of heptane into a solution in 1-chloropentane at room temperature. The tetracyclic π-conjugated framework is almost planar, with the two n-butyl groups on the phosphorus atom oriented perpendicularly to this plane (Figure a). The intermolecular arrangement of adjacent molecules indicated that these n-butyl groups effectively hinder the stacking of the π-framework. The counter anion, TfO–, was located between two coplanar π-skeletons through electrostatic interactions (Figure b). Furthermore, clustering of the hexyl groups on the amino group of six neighboring molecules formed hydrophobic voids, thereby suppressing close π–π interactions (Figures c and S3). These structural features of 2 OTf , involving the counter anions as well as the alkyl chains on the phosphorus and nitrogen atoms, are likely crucial in lowering the T m and T g even in the unsymmetrically dialkylated 1.

Tetracyclic phospholium salts 1–4 exhibited bright yellow emissions in the diluted solutions. The substituent effects of the electron-accepting phosphonium moiety and the electron-donating amino groups on the photophysical properties were investigated. In the UV–visible absorption spectra of 1 OTf and 3 OTf in CH2Cl2, the longest-wavelength absorption bands appeared with the maximum wavelength (λabs) at 445 and 457 nm, respectively, which were largely red-shifted compared to that of phosphine oxide 6 (λabs = 386 nm). The λabs of these compounds slightly blue-shifted upon increasing the solvent polarity from CH2Cl2 to CH3CN to CH3OH (Figures , S5, and Table ). In the fluorescence spectra recorded in CH2Cl2, 1 OTf exhibited a yellow fluorescence with the maximum wavelength (λem) at 538 nm, which is also red-shifted by 55 nm from 6 (λem = 483 nm). Notably, no significant influence of solvent polarity on the emission wavelength was observed for 1 OTf (Figure ) and, furthermore, the fluorescence quantum yields (ΦFL) of 1 OTf –4 OTf remained high irrespective of the solvent polarity, exceeding 0.72 even in polar CH3OH (Table ). These findings demonstrate that the intramolecular charge-transfer (ICT) character in the excited state is not significant despite the presence of a push–pull structure, and thereby, the fluorescence wavelength and the ΦFL are not dependent on the environment polarity. Nevertheless, 1 OTf exhibited a large Stokes shift of 101 nm (4296 cm–1) in CH3OH, which resulted in a subtle overlap between the absorption and emission bands. This feature is important for effectively preventing self-absorption of the emission and for maintaining the bright emission even under neat conditions.

To elucidate the origin of these notable features in the photophysical properties, i.e., (1) while 1 has the large Stokes shift, (2) it does not show significant solvent effect on the λabs and λem values as well as the ΦFL values; (time-dependent) density functional theory ((TD)-DFT) calculations were performed at the CAM-B3LYP/6–31+G­(d,p) level of theory with the polarizable continuum model (PCM) using CH2Cl2 or CH3OH as solvents (Figures and S9). In these calculations, the butyl and hexyl groups in 1 were replaced with methyl groups in 1′, and the counter anion was not included to reduce computational cost. The structures were optimized in both the ground state (S0) and the lowest singlet excited state (S1). The optimized structure of 1′ in CH3OH in the S0 (Figure a) is in good agreement with that determined by the X-ray crystallographic analysis in terms of the bond lengths and the planarity of the tetracyclic framework (Figure S4). In the optimized structure in the S1, the mode of bond alternation is largely changed compared to that in the S0, where the aminophenyl moiety, combined with the vinyl-phosphonium moiety, adopts a substantial quinoidal geometry denoted as 1′ quinoid in Figure b (Figures a and S13). This geometry change leads to significant energy stabilization in the S1 (Figure b), thereby accounting for the large Stokes shift of the compound, despite the phosphonium and amino moieties being located on the same benzene ring.

On the other hand, the extent of the energy stabilization in the S1 remains nearly unchanged between CH2Cl2 and CH3OH (Figure b), consistent with the experimental observation of negligible solvatochromism in the fluorescence spectra. Indeed, the calculated dipole moments in S0 are comparable between CH2Cl2 and CH3OH (20.4 and 21.4 D, respectively), and those in S1 also show no significant differences (22.3 and 23.4 D, respectively) (Figures S10 and S11). These results explain the lack of solvent dependence in both the absorption and the emission spectra (Figure ). Since both the S0–S1 transition and the S1–S0 transition are mainly attributed to the HOMO–LUMO transition, a key to understanding the negligible solvent effect lies in the nature of the frontier orbital distributions in the optimized geometries of the S0 and S1. Notably, both the HOMO and LUMO are delocalized across the entire four-ring-fused framework, including the amino moiety, despite the substantial geometric changes from the S0 to the S1 (Figures c and S12). This is likely due, at least in part, to the fact that the electron-donating amino group and the electron-withdrawing phosphonium moiety are attached to the same benzene ring, resulting in a relatively short spatial separation. Additionally, the increased oscillator strength in the S1 compared to the S0 should contribute to retaining a high fluorescence quantum yield (Figure S12).

The photophysical properties of 1 OTf –5 OTf under neat conditions were evaluated to elucidate the effects of the substituents on the emission properties in the neat state. Thin films prepared by spin-coating were used for this purpose, as it was confirmed that the fluorescence spectrum of the spin-coated film of 1 was identical to that of the drop-cast sample of the compound on quartz substrates (Figure S7b). The λem values of all derivatives exhibited slight red shifts under neat conditions compared to those in CH2Cl2 (Table ). The shift was slightly smaller for 1 OTf than for 3 OTf and 4 OTf , suggesting that the intermolecular interactions between the tetracyclic π-frameworks are more effectively suppressed in 1 OTf due to the presence of the alkyl groups on the phosphorus and nitrogen atoms. These alkyl groups also influenced the ΦFL values, and the largest value of 0.22 was obtained for 2 OTf , which bears two n-butyl groups on the phosphorus atom. In contrast, 4 OTf and 5 OTf , which possess dimethylamino and diphenylamino groups, respectively, exhibited significantly lower ΦFL values (0.05 and 0.02, respectively). These results indicate that long alkyl chains on the amino groups, as found in 1 OTf and 2 OTf , are also a crucial factor in achieving high ΦFL values under neat conditions.

The size of the counter anions also affects the photophysical properties in the neat state. While the ΦFL value for 1 I under neat conditions was as low as 0.02, those of 1 NNf2 and 1 BArF4 , featuring the bulkier counter anions, increased to 0.28 and 0.64, respectively (Tables , S2, and Figure S7a). This enhancement is likely due to the bulky BAr4 F anion effectively suppressing the formation of nonemissive aggregates. Consequently, 1 BArF4 retains the high ΦFL value even under neat conditions, with only a slight reduction compared to that in solution.

One of the advantageous features of the room-temperature emissive ILs should be their capability to serve as a medium for dissolving other dyes. We envisioned that the Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) could efficiently occur in such media. To examine this possibility, Nile Red (λabs = 540 nm, λem = 615 nm in CHCl3) was selected as a FRET acceptor, considering the overlap between its absorption band and the fluorescence band of 1 BArF4 , which should facilitate the FRET (Figure ). When a spin-coated film prepared from a mixture of 95 wt % of 1 BArF4 and 5 wt % of Nile Red in CHCl3 was excited at 440 nm, where Nile Red does not have absorption, a red emission with the maximum at 650 nm, corresponding to the emission from Nile Red, was observed (Figure ). The excitation spectrum of this film was in good agreement with that of 1 BArF4 , indicating that FRET from 1 BArF4 to the dopant Nile Red occurred efficiently with 5 wt % doping of Nile Red. The fact that the thin film prepared from only Nile Red alone did not show any fluorescence demonstrated the important role of 1 BArF4 as a liquid medium for dispersing the Nile Red. It should be noted that in a chloroform solution containing 1 BArF4 (4.2 × 10–5 M) and Nile Red (2.2 × 10–6 M) with the 1 BArF4 /Nile Red ratio of 95:5, the direct fluorescence from 1 BArF4 was considerably detected together with the emission from Nile Red through the FRET (Figure S8 and Table S1). In contrast, no emission from 1 BArF4 was observed in the aforementioned spin-coated film containing 1 BArF4 and Nile Red, demonstrating that 1 BArF4 is a suitable medium for achieving efficient FRET. It is noteworthy that the fluorescence wavelength (650 nm) of Nile Red detected in the spin-coat film containing 1 BArF4 was longer than that observed in CH3OH (636 nm), despite the presence of several hydrophobic alkyl chains on the phosphorus and nitrogen atoms of 1 BArF4 .

Conclusions

A series of tetracyclic dialkylphospholium-based dyes was synthesized as room-temperature ILs exhibiting a yellow emission. The two alkyl groups on the phosphorus center are arranged orthogonal to the π-conjugated framework, suppressing intermolecular interactions between the π-skeletons, thereby lowering the melting point. The theoretical study provided insights into the push–pull structure composed of an electron-donating amino group and an electron-withdrawing phosphonium moiety. The introduction of these units into the same benzene ring effectively induces long-wavelength emission without significantly biasing the distribution of HOMO and LUMO. Together with the large Stokes shift arising from the structural relaxation based on the push–pull structure of the aminophenylvinyl phosphonium substructure, these features collectively contribute to reduced self-quenching and sustained intense emission even under neat conditions. The incorporation of a bulky counter anion further decreased the T g and significantly increased the fluorescence quantum yield. Notably, one of the derivatives with a BAr4 F– counter anion exhibited bright yellow emission with the ΦFL as high as 0.64, attributed to the effective prevention of intermolecular π–π interactions by surrounding bulky counteranions. Taking advantage of this feature, a wavelength-converting liquid material showing red emission was fabricated by using Nile Red as a FRET acceptor. Overall, the present design of ILs by incorporating dialkylamino and dialkylphosphonium moieties in a push–pull configuration together with employing a large counter anion represents a promising strategy to produce novel emissive liquid materials. These structural features provide enhanced thermal stability and allow tunable thermal properties and quantum yields by varying the counter anion, which adds versatility compared to neutral emissive organic liquids. Further studies aimed at expanding this design strategy to develop ILs with long-wavelength emission are currently ongoing in our laboratory.

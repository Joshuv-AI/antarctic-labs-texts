A unified GRU model for cryptocurrency price prediction and harsh price movement detection using enhanced sentiment analysis
Authors: Mahmood Mohammadi Nezhad, Saeed Rouhani, Navid Mohammadi, Ali Shahedi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPredicting cryptocurrency price movements using social media sentiment remains challenging due to the noisy, heterogeneous, and rapidly evolving nature of online signals. While prior studies commonly combine sentiment analysis with deep learning models, less attention has been given to how sentiment signals are constructed, aggregated, and aligned with price dynamics. This study investigates the impact of sentiment representation and price change labeling on short-term Bitcoin price movement classification. Over 1.1 million Bitcoin-related tweets spanning April to August 2021 are analyzed using a RoBERTa-based sentiment model, incorporating both sentiment probabilities and user-level activity metrics. These features are consolidated via Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and aggregated over time using a decay-weighted scheme to emphasize recent information. Price movements are categorized into discrete regimes using a data-driven K-means clustering approach, with controlled Gaussian noise applied to improve boundary robustness. Multiple predictive models, including a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), LightGBM, and multinomial logistic regression, are evaluated. Although the GRU achieves the highest overall performance, an extensive ablation study demonstrates that the primary performance gains arise from the proposed sentiment construction and labeling framework rather than the forecasting architecture alone. Removing PCA-based aggregation, adaptive clustering, or noise injection leads to substantial degradation, particularly for extreme price movement classes. The findings highlight the importance of sentiment feature design and class definition in cryptocurrency prediction and provide empirical guidance for constructing robust sentiment- driven financial models.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46271-w.Keywords: Cryptocurrency price prediction, Harsh price movement detection, Sentiment analysis, Social media influence, Time-series classificationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Traditional financial markets, such as the stock market, facilitate capital flow and wealth distribution1. They serve as platforms for trading securities, with prices shaped by economic conditions, corporate performance, and investor sentiment2. Often viewed as an economic barometer, shifts in investor confidence can affect businesses and economies3. In contrast, the cryptocurrency market, launched with Bitcoin in 20094, operates on decentralized principles. Driven by speculative trading and technological innovation, it diverges from traditional economic indicators, resulting in significant volatility5. For instance, Bitcoin’s price soared from $1,000 to nearly $20,000 in 2017, then dropped 80% in 2018, revealing speculative bubbles and herd behavior6,7. This unpredictability, fueled by social sentiment and hype, challenges analysts, as traditional econometric models falter8. Additionally, the lack of regulatory oversight enables manipulative practices like pump-and-dump schemes9. Amid these issues, social media platforms such as X, Reddit, and Telegram play a key role in shaping crypto market dynamics. Retail investors use these spaces to share information and opinions, influencing trends10. Indeed, research confirms a link between social media sentiment and crypto price shifts11,12.

Given the cryptocurrency market’s complexity and rapid evolution, researchers have conducted extensive studies to help investors better understand and anticipate market trends. These efforts fall into two main streams: price prediction and anomaly detection. Price prediction research splits into forecasting specific cryptocurrency prices using machine learning and time-series analysis13–15 and predicting market direction with statistical models or sentiment analysis16–18. In contrast, studies on anomaly detection, or specifically pump-and-dump schemes, focus on identifying or anticipating these manipulative practices, vital for market integrity19–21. Many of these studies rely on sentiment analysis to quantify textual data, using two approaches: dictionary-based methods for structured analysis14,15 and advanced machine learning, particularly NLP models, to capture nuanced sentiments13,14,17. Once sentiment scores are derived, they are aggregated to form broader sentiment measures, with most studies opting for a simple averaging of individual text scores13,17,22, while a few explore alternative techniques incorporating text credibility, contextual significance, or weighted calculations to bolster the robustness of the sentiment index23–25.

Despite notable progress in understanding market sentiment and forecasting prices, several research gaps persist. Firstly, the majority of existing models focus either on predicting price fluctuations or identifying abrupt and harsh price changes, rarely addressing both phenomena simultaneously. This separation reduces the models’ comprehensiveness, as focusing on only one aspect, either price prediction or extreme volatility detection, limits their ability to account for the full spectrum of market dynamics. Secondly, the sentiment indices employed in current research typically utilize basic aggregation methods with a narrow set of features. These methods often assume that all sources and market actors exert equal influence, which might ignore the disproportionate impact of key market players.

To tackle these gaps, this study is guided by two research questions:

RQ1: How can a comprehensive method be created to predict cryptocurrency price and spot sudden, harsh price shifts?

RQ2: What novel sentiment analysis framework can be designed to account for the varying influence of market participants in predicting cryptocurrency prices?

To address these questions, the study outlines a comprehensive strategy. For RQ1, it aims to build a model that integrates the forecasting of price direction and magnitude with the detection of harsh price movements, offering a more holistic view of market dynamics compared to existing research, which often examines these aspects in isolation. For RQ2, it plans to break new ground in sentiment indicator creation by employing innovative methods that consider the influence of different market actors, moving beyond traditional data combination techniques. It should be mentioned that recognizing broader timeframes in earlier studies misses rapid market shifts, so this research shifts to shorter intervals. Overall, the study strives to deliver a complete and more flexible framework for cryptocurrency market analysis, supporting investors.

Literature review

Introduction to cryptocurrencies and the crypto market

The history of the cryptocurrency market is rooted in the introduction of Bitcoin, a pivotal moment that marked the beginning of decentralized digital currencies. Bitcoin was launched by an anonymous developer or group known as Satoshi Nakamoto, as detailed in the seminal white paper “Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System”4. This document outlined a decentralized system using blockchain technology, enabling peer-to-peer transactions without intermediaries, challenging traditional financial systems5.

Despite initial skepticism, Bitcoin gained practical use with the first recorded real-world transaction in 2010, where a programmer purchased two pizzas for 10,000 BTC26. The market then expanded with the emergence of alternative cryptocurrencies, or altcoins. Litecoin, launched in 2011, was one of the first, featuring faster transaction times and a different hashing algorithm27. Ethereum, introduced in 2015, brought smart contracts, enabling complex applications beyond simple transactions28,29. Following Ethereum’s launch, the market experienced significant growth and volatility. The year 2017 marked a watershed moment with Bitcoin’s price surging from approximately $1,000 to nearly $20,000 by December, followed by a sharp decline in 2018, with Bitcoin’s value dropping by about 80%, underscoring the market’s speculative nature8. Such a bull run was repeated in 2021, when it reached its all-time high of over $60,000 in April, and the proliferation of meme coins like Dogecoin and Shiba Inu, driven by social media hype30.

Related works

A wealth of studies has delved into the unpredictable swings of cryptocurrency markets, using predictive tools and sentiment insights to unravel their complexities. Research predicts prices or market direction, detects pump-and-dump schemes, and explores how social media-driven investor sentiment shapes price movements.

A significant body of research has focused on predicting cryptocurrency price movements, employing diverse methodologies to address the market’s volatility. Studies targeting price direction include Ortu et al.18, who utilized deep learning algorithms such as MLP, LSTM, AMNN, and CNN to forecast Bitcoin and Ethereum price directions, integrating price data, technical indicators, and social media sentiment via a BERT-based model, and Davoudi et al.17, who developed a network-based model to predict price directions for four decentralized storage cryptocurrencies using market data, technical indicators, and sentiment analysis.

In contrast, efforts to forecast exact cryptocurrency prices have leveraged sophisticated quantitative approaches. Haritha and Sahana15, applied a GRU-based RNN to predict Bitcoin prices by combining price data with Twitter sentiment, while Critien et al.13 used a dual-model strategy involving BiLSTM for direction and CNN for magnitude to estimate Bitcoin price changes, incorporating Twitter sentiment. Yasir22, employed linear regression, SVR, and CNN models to predict prices of five cryptocurrencies, Bitcoin, Litecoin, Dash, Stellar, and Monero, using price data and Twitter sentiment, whereas Smuts31 utilized an LSTM model for Bitcoin and Ethereum price direction, integrating price, volume, Google Trends, and Telegram data with a one-week lag. Such coarse temporal granularity is insufficient for the high-frequency nature of cryptocurrency markets, where ‘hype cycles’ and sharp anomalies often unfold within minutes. Hu et al.32 adopted a BGRU model to forecast Bitcoin prices 48 and 96 h ahead, analyzing Telegram sentiment from February 2020 to May 2022. Complementing these efforts, research into detecting anomalies has utilized distinct analytical techniques. Morgia et al.20 proposed a machine learning model that employs Random Forest and AdaBoost algorithms to detect pump-and-dump schemes on the Binance exchange through the analysis of market order data, specifically identifying rapid spikes in rush orders, while Hu et al.32 employed an SNN architecture to detect such schemes by examining Telegram message sequences and market behavior. Hamrick33, analyzed 952 Discord and 2,469 Telegram pump-and-dump events from January to June 2018 across 2,000 cryptocurrencies and 220 exchanges, though without a predictive model, and Kamps and Kleinberg34, used anomaly detection on hourly price and volume data from April 20 to May 10, 2018, applying moving averages and thresholds to identify manipulations.

While these studies have advanced the understanding of cryptocurrency dynamics, they lack a unified model capable of simultaneously predicting both price movements and extreme market anomalies, highlighting a critical gap in the current research landscape.

Sentiment analysis has emerged as a critical tool in cryptocurrency research, aiming to quantify public sentiment from social media and online platforms to predict market movements. Several studies have employed dictionary-based approaches to analyze sentiment, such as Critien et al.13, who applied the VADER lexicon to Twitter data for Bitcoin price trend predictions, and Smuts31, who used a modified VADER dictionary tailored to cryptocurrency-specific terms for Telegram messages. Yasir22, utilized the Alex Davies word list to classify Twitter sentiment related to major events.

In parallel, machine learning and natural language processing (NLP) techniques have been increasingly adopted for more nuanced sentiment analysis, with Ortu et al.18 employing a BERT-based model to extract sentiment indices from GitHub and Reddit, Davoudi et al.17 using FinBERT on news articles and tweets, Haritha and Sahana15, applying FinBERT to Twitter data for Bitcoin price forecasting, and Hu et al.32 analyzing Telegram messages for anomaly detection. Current cryptocurrency market research has notable limitations. Many studies focus narrowly on specific behaviors, like price prediction (e.g., Rather35 and Oyedele et al.36 or identifying Anomalies (e.g., Dhawan and Putniņš19, without combining these into a cohesive framework. In time series analyses incorporating sentiment data, it is critical to establish sentiment indices for each time step to explore their relationship with the target variable, in this case, Bitcoin price movements. This process entails two key stages: first, determining a composite sentiment score for each tweet, and second, aggregating these scores across each time interval to construct an overall sentiment index.

Each tweet in the dataset includes several attributes, such as sentiment probabilities (positive, negative, neutral) and user-specific metrics, including the number of followers, friends, and favorited tweets. While many previous studies, like Ortu et al.18 and Yasir et al.22, have relied solely on the final sentiment probability as the sentiment index, this approach overlooks the varying influence of tweets based on the author’s social reach41. For instance, a tweet posted by a user with a large following is likely to have a greater impact than an identical message from a user with fewer followers. To address this, the present study incorporates multiple factors to define a more nuanced sentiment index, including:

To consolidate these four factors into a single representative sentiment index per tweet, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was utilized. PCA transforms the original variables into a set of uncorrelated components, ordered by the amount of variance they explain, with the first component capturing the greatest share of information42. Specifically, the PCA was implemented using the PCA class from sklearn. decomposition module of the scikit-learn library, initializing the model to extract four components (n_components = 4) to ensure the preservation of all available variance from the input features. However, as the input variables were not on the same scale, a preliminary step was required: Z-score normalization was first applied to all inputs to ensure a consistent scale before PCA was used to produce the final sentiment index.

After determining PCA components, a loading matrix analysis was conducted to examine the mathematical influence of each feature (sentiment and user metrics) on the PCA components. This provides an “economic meaning” to the signals and helps interpret which social factors drive market-moving predictions.

To generate an overall sentiment index for each time interval, the individual tweet sentiment scores were aggregated using Time-Decaying Aggregation. This method assigns higher weights to more recent data, acknowledging their greater relevance in fast-moving markets such as cryptocurrency, while gradually reducing the influence of older tweets rather than discarding them abruptly. This approach is well-suited to time series data, where recent observations often hold stronger predictive value43. The rationale for adopting this method includes:

Emphasis on recent data: In domains like stock price analysis or behavioral trend prediction, recent data typically offers greater predictive insight.

Classification of price changes

This study organizes price changes into distinct categories, each corresponding to a percentage change in the price of the subsequent candlestick. Unlike traditional methods that rely on manually defined group sizes (e.g., Sable et al.45, this research utilizes an unsupervised machine learning approach, specifically, the K-Means clustering algorithm, to establish optimal intervals for these categories.

K-means clustering is an unsupervised machine learning algorithm that partitions a dataset into k distinct clusters by iteratively assigning each data point to the nearest cluster centroid based on a distance metric, typically Euclidean distance, and then updating the centroids by calculating the mean of all points in each cluster. This process continues until convergence, minimizing the within-cluster variance, and is effective for grouping similar data points, with the optimal k often determined using metrics like the Silhouette Score.

To define these intervals, approximately two months of preprocessed price data, comprising nearly 90,000 data points, were input into the K-Means model. Notably, this dataset is distinct from the one employed for price prediction modeling, ensuring an unbiased classification process.

An initial examination of the price change distribution, to be depicted in the Fig. 6, revealed that most changes hovered around zero, indicative of minor fluctuations.

Price change distribution.

Specifically, the 5th percentile was − 0.0029, and the 95th percentile was 0.0029, highlighting the predominance of small movements. This tight clustering posed a challenge: a model trained on such data might overfit, consistently predicting negligible changes regardless of input.

To mitigate this, Gaussian noise was introduced, a common technique in machine learning and signal processing aimed at enhancing model generalization and reducing overfitting. By adding controlled randomness, this method encourages the model to learn broader, more robust patterns rather than memorizing the training data, as similarly applied by Haritha and Sahana15. It is worth noting that Gaussian noise is defined by two main properties: (1) zero mean, meaning the noise changes symmetrically above and below the original data value (such as equal fluctuations in both directions), and (2) noise intensity (standard deviation), which determines the degree of dispersion of the noise, with larger values resulting in stronger and more noticeable noise.

To guarantee the robustness and stability of the K-Means clustering algorithm, the process was repeated 7 times with different random initializations. This multiple-run approach was essential to mitigate the risk associated with the random initial placement of cluster centroids, ensuring the resulting cluster definitions were reliable and consistent.

Proposed model

This study evaluates four models for classifying price change clusters: a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)-based neural network, a Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), and a Multinomial Logistic Regression (Logit) model. These architectures span sequence-sensitive deep learning (GRU, TCN), ensemble tree-based methods (LightGBM), and traditional statistical techniques (Logit), enabling a robust comparison for time series classification. Each model uses eight principal component features (PCA_Component_1 to PCA_Component_4, their moving averages, and their lags) as input.

Price change classification results

To improve robustness near class boundaries, Gaussian noise was selectively injected into low-magnitude price changes. Specifically, samples with close-to-close percentage changes within the interval [− 0.003, 0.003], corresponding to the neutral regime, were identified. From this subset, 25% of samples were randomly selected and perturbed by adding zero-mean Gaussian noise with a small standard deviation of 0.007 with a standard deviation of 0.007, chosen to be approximately 2.3 times the neutral interval width (0.003), thereby enabling samples to cross cluster boundaries while maintaining financial plausibility. By choosing noise larger than the neutral region, the model pushes samples across boundaries to reduce threshold sensitivity and improve generalization. Random sampling and noise generation were controlled via a fixed random seed (42) to ensure reproducibility. Discussion

While the GRU model achieved a weighted accuracy of 0.786, surpassing LightGBM, Logit, and TCN, its success is primarily due to its ability to handle long-term temporal dependencies. Unlike LightGBM and Logit, which lack sequential processing, the GRU adapts to the dynamic patterns of Bitcoin. This aligns with Haritha and Sahana15, who also found GRU-based architectures superior for sequential data applications.

However, the driver of high accuracy is not just the model, but the sentiment construction. Ablation results demonstrate that the PCA-based weighting and Time-Decaying Aggregation are critical. Performance degradation observed when removing these components confirms that treating all tweets equally (simple averaging) prevents a better understanding of the market reality. By prioritizing recent signals and high-influence users, the model reacts to market Drastic changes faster than traditional methods.

The PCA loading matrix further clarifies why this aggregation strategy is effective. The first component is dominated by user interaction variables (followers, friends, favorites), while sentiment probability contributes more strongly to the second component. This separation indicates that the PCA transformation disentangles sentiment confidence from social reach rather than collapsing them into a single factor. As a result, the sentiment index preserves multiple dimensions of market-relevant information, enabling the predictive models to distinguish between widespread low-impact sentiment and concentrated high-authority signals. This addresses a common limitation in earlier sentiment-based studies, where sentiment intensity and influence are implicitly assumed to be equivalent13,17,31.

Replacing K-means–derived boundaries with fixed thresholds resulted in further degradation, primarily in extreme movement clusters. This outcome highlights the limitations of static labeling schemes in volatile markets, where the scale of price changes shifts over time. Unlike manually defined bins used in prior work (e.g., Sable et al.45, the data-driven clustering approach adapts to empirical distributions, improving sensitivity to rare but economically significant events. The ablation results confirm that this adaptability materially improves the detection of harsh movements.

Finally, removing time-decay weighting produced a measurable performance decline. This suggests that while temporal decay is less critical than sentiment construction, it plays an important role in capturing short-lived hype-driven dynamics, particularly in extreme positive price movements. This aligns with findings by Giachanou and Crestani44, who emphasize the temporal volatility of sentiment relevance in fast-moving domains.

In response to RQ1, the results demonstrate that price prediction and extreme volatility detection can be addressed jointly through a unified labeling and modeling framework. The clustering-based categorization enables the model to distinguish between routine market behavior and anomalous movements, rather than treating volatility as a separate downstream task.

Regarding RQ2, the empirical evidence shows that sentiment impact is more accurately captured when both user influence and temporal relevance are explicitly modeled. The PCA-based aggregation and time-decaying weighting jointly improve predictive performance, particularly for extreme events, validating the assumption that not all sentiment signals contribute equally or persist uniformly over time.

Implications of the study

This study offers valuable insights for cryptocurrency research and practical applications. While the GRU model performs well in capturing sequence patterns, the real breakthrough lies in the sentiment analysis framework, which uses Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to blend user influence metrics, like followers and likes, with inverse time decay to emphasize recent tweets. This approach uniquely captures the impact of key market actors and real-time sentiment shifts, advancing beyond simpler past methods. Practically, the study’s dual focus on price prediction and harsh price anomaly detection deepens market understanding. The GRU’s detection of extreme price clusters (e.g., Cluster 4’s sharp spikes) aids traders in spotting sharp changes versus regular fluctuations, while minute-level sentiment data enhances early anomaly detection. These tools empower traders with better transparency and strategies in a dynamic market.

Limitations

The model’s dependence on Twitter (X) data limits its applicability, as it overlooks other platforms, where distinct user behaviors prevail. Data from these sources often lack essential features, like follower counts or precise timestamps, complicating efforts to apply the PCA-based sentiment weighting beyond Twitter. Moreover, the exclusive focus on Bitcoin leaves the model’s effectiveness untested for other cryptocurrencies, such as Ethereum or Dogecoin, which differ in volatility, liquidity, and social media influence.

Additionally, the use of minute-level data enhances short-term prediction accuracy but obscures longer-term trends, such as market cycles, which unfold over weeks or months. This high-frequency approach may not adequately support strategic, long-term decision-making, suggesting a need for methods that integrate granular insights with wider market dynamics.

Future research directions

Future research could enhance this study by expanding data collection to include platforms such as Reddit and Telegram, adapting the sentiment framework to their distinct data structures and user dynamics. Exploring advanced modeling techniques, like transformer-based architectures or ensemble models, may improve the performance of the model. Additionally, testing the model on a range of cryptocurrencies, from volatile ones like Dogecoin to steadier ones like Ethereum, would help gauge its flexibility and reveal how sentiment impacts different market trends.

Conclusion

The cryptocurrency market’s volatility and sensitivity to social sentiment present substantial challenges, rendering traditional financial models inadequate. This study examined the role of sentiment feature construction and price change labeling in short-term Bitcoin price movement prediction using social media data. Rather than focusing solely on forecasting architectures, the analysis emphasized how sentiment signals are aggregated, weighted, and aligned with market regimes. Using over one million Bitcoin-related tweets and minute-level price data, multiple models were evaluated within a unified framework combining sentiment analysis, dimensionality reduction, temporal aggregation, and data-driven price classification.

Across all experiments, the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) achieved the highest predictive performance; however, empirical evidence from the ablation study demonstrates that model accuracy is driven primarily by the sentiment representation and labeling strategy rather than by the neural architecture itself. Simplifying the sentiment index through naïve averaging resulted in the largest performance degradation, particularly for extreme price movement classes. This confirms that incorporating user-level activity metrics and separating distinct sentiment–influence dimensions via PCA materially improves the quality of market signals. Similarly, adaptive price regime classification using K-means and the introduction of small-magnitude Gaussian noise were shown to stabilize class boundaries and enhance generalization, especially near regime transitions.

The PCA loading analysis further supports the robustness of the sentiment framework by revealing that different components capture complementary aspects of sentiment confidence and user influence, rather than collapsing information into a single dominant factor. This interpretability strengthens confidence in the constructed sentiment features and addresses concerns regarding the transparency of dimensionality reduction techniques. Additionally, the time-decaying aggregation mechanism proved particularly relevant during rapid market expansions, where recent sentiment carries disproportionate predictive value.

Taken together, the results indicate that effective cryptocurrency prediction depends as much on how sentiment and price movements are defined as on which predictive model is used. By empirically validating each component through controlled ablation, this study provides practical guidance for designing robust sentiment-driven forecasting systems.

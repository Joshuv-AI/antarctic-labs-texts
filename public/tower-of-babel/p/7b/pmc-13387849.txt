Sex Differences in Long-term Outcomes Following Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Nationwide Population-Based Propensity Score-Matched Cohort Study
Authors: Suk Kyung Lim, Yong Ho Jeong, Yun Jin Kim, Yongil Cho, Hyo Jun Jang, Yang Hyun Cho, Kiick Sung, Wook Sung Kim, Joon Bum Kim, Hee Jung Kim, Jun Ho Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe relationship between sex differences and clinical outcomes following coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) has been highlighted in several studies. However, these studies were mostly based on single- or multi-center data, and only a few meta-analyses examined long-term outcomes. To address this limitation, we conducted a nationwide population-based cohort study in Korea.MethodsA total of 48,206 adult patients who underwent first-time isolated CABG between January 2005 and December 2021 were enrolled. To address potential confounding, propensity score (PS) matching was conducted. The primary outcome was overall mortality.ResultsAmong the enrolled patients, 12,270 (25.5%) were female. The median age of the overall cohort was 66.0 years (interquartile range [IQR], 59.0–72.0), with female patients (median, 69.0; IQR, 63.0–75.0) being significantly older than male patients (P < 0.001). In the PS-matched cohort, in-hospital mortality did not differ significantly between female and male groups (odds ratio, 1.13; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.78–1.64). However, the female group was a significant predictor of better overall survival (hazard ratio [HR], 0.83; 95% CI, 0.77–0.90) compared to the male group. Cardiac-related mortality was comparable between the two groups (HR, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.65–1.22). The median duration of the follow-up period was 7.8 (IQR, 4.0–12.6) years.ConclusionIn this nationwide population-based PS-matched cohort study, female patients who underwent isolated CABG exhibited significantly lower overall mortality compared to male patients during long-term follow-up, whereas no significant difference was observed in cardiac-related mortality.

INTRODUCTION

Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is a crucial treatment for patients with life-threatening coronary heart disease.1 The relationship between sex differences and clinical outcomes after CABG has been highlighted in several studies.2345678 However, the impact of sex differences on clinical outcomes following CABG remains unclear due to inconsistent findings from previous studies. Moreover, these studies were mostly based on single- or multi-center data, and only a few meta-analyses examined long-term outcomes.23478

In Republic of Korea (South Korea), the mandatory single-payer healthcare system and compulsory registration with the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) enable nationwide cohort studies with accurate diagnostic and interventional codes.9 We analyzed the impact of sex differences on major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events after CABG using the national Korean claims database, which has the strength of including almost the entire population and providing nearly complete follow-up data.

While several previous studies have reported worse short-term outcomes, including higher operative mortality, among female patients undergoing isolated CABG,21011 these differences have been largely attributed to a higher burden of comorbidities in female patients.2410111213 To address potential confounding variables, we performed propensity score (PS) matching by incorporating a comprehensive set of variables. Importantly, unlike previous studies that focused primarily on short-term outcomes,3710 our study aimed to examine long-term outcomes after CABG, providing valuable insights into the effects of sex differences on survival rates.

METHODS

Data sources

The study utilized health claims data from the NHIS in Korea (NHIS-2022-1-306). This database covers 96–97% of the country’s population of 50 million and contains demographic information, diagnoses coded according to the Tenth Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10), surgical or endovascular interventions, hospital identifiers, and mortality records.14

Study population

A total of 61,411 first-time CABG patients (aged 18 years or older) between January 2005 and December 2021 were derived from the NHIS database using unique procedure codes specific to Korea (Supplementary Table 1). Patients who had undergone concomitant or previous cardiovascular surgery other than CABG (n = 12,416) and those who did not subscribe to the national health insurance including the medical aid program (n = 789) were excluded. The final study cohort comprised 48,206 patients (Fig. 1). During the study period, the index date was defined as the admission date for the first-time isolated CABG.

Flow diagram.

CABG = coronary artery bypass grafting.

Definition of covariates

Covariates including comorbidities and previous treatments are presented in Supplementary Table 1. Comorbidity was defined as the presence of two or more of the same ICD-10 codes within three years from the index date. Previous treatment was defined as the presence of one or more of the procedure codes uniquely used in South Korea before the index date.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was overall mortality. Cardiac-related mortality, stroke, myocardial infarction (MI), repeat revascularization (i.e., CABG and percutaneous coronary intervention [PCI]), and new-onset dialysis were among the secondary outcomes. Stroke was defined as the occurrence of I60–I64 codes in patients without preoperative history of stroke. The follow-up period was calculated from the index date to the date of death or December 31, 2022, for living patients. In-hospital mortality, as well as 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year mortality, and overall mortality were assessed. In the NHIS data, it was possible to identify the admission date for CABG; however, it was impossible to determine the exact date of operation. Therefore, the only definable early outcome was in-hospital mortality. Late outcomes were defined as any outcomes occurring at least 1 year after the index date.

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) or frequencies with corresponding proportions. Comparisons of categorical variables between female and male groups were conducted using chi-square and Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Logistic regression analysis was employed to estimate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for assessing the impact of sex differences on in-hospital mortality. To evaluate the association between sex differences and long-term outcomes, Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to calculate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs.

PS matching was conducted to reduce confounding by incorporating variables such as age (modeled only as a continuous variable), comorbidities (including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, acute MI, congestive heart failure, atrial fibrillation or flutter, arrhythmia, peripheral vascular disease, stroke, chronic kidney disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, liver cirrhosis, and cancer), diagnosis on the index date (acute MI), surgical technique (off-pump CABG), number of bypassed coronary arteries, year of CABG, and hospital volume. Surgical technique was defined as off-pump vs. on-pump CABG using NHIS procedure codes and modeled as a binary covariate. This variable was included in the propensity-score model and in all multivariable outcome models (in-hospital mortality via logistic regression, overall mortality via Cox proportional hazards, and nonfatal endpoints via Fine–Gray competing-risk models when applicable). Nearest-neighbor matching without replacement was applied using a caliper width of 0.2 on the PS scale, with a 1:1 matching ratio. Multivariable outcome models additionally adjusted for smoking status and alcohol use together with all variables listed in Table 1.

RESULTS

Characteristics of the study population

Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of patients who underwent isolated CABG in Korea between 2005 and 2021. Data are shown for both the pre-matched cohort (n = 48,206) and the PS-matched cohort (n = 8,086), stratified by sex. In the pre-matched cohort, male patients constituted the majority (n = 35,936), and female patients (n = 12,270) were significantly older (median age 69 vs. 64 years, P < 0.001). Among patients with available lifestyle information, male patients were substantially more likely to smoke and consume alcohol frequently than female ones. In the PS-matched cohort, the proportion of current smokers was 27.1% in male versus 3.6% in female, whereas non-smokers accounted for 39.0% and 94.7%, respectively. Frequent alcohol consumption (≥ 4 times per week) was also markedly higher in male patients (4.2%) than in female ones (0.4%). Similar sex-related disparities were observed in the pre-matched cohort (current smoker, 31.8% vs. 4.3%; frequent alcohol consumption, 5.0% vs. 0.3%). Female patients had higher rates of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidemia, while male patients had higher prevalence of liver cirrhosis and cancer. Following PS matching, most comorbidities and baseline variables were balanced between sexes (P = 0.312). Off-pump CABG was the predominant surgical technique in both cohorts. In the PS-matched cohort, the median duration of the follow-up period was 7.8 (IQR, 4.0–12.6) years.

Overall clinical outcomes

During the study period, the study population in the PS-matched cohort had an overall mortality rate of 29.8% (Table 2). The overall incidence rates of stroke (21.8%), MI (9.3%), repeat revascularization (0.1% for CABG and 8.6% for PCI), and new dialysis (6.9%) were also investigated. The median duration of hospitalization for all patients was 14.0 (IQR, 10.0–19.0) days.

Comparison of clinical outcomes according to sex in the overall cohort

In the pre-matched cohort, after adjustment for preoperative risk factors, the female group demonstrated a significantly higher in-hospital mortality rate compared to the male group (OR, 1.22; 95% CI, 1.08–1.37; Supplementary Fig. 1). Conversely, female patients exhibited significantly lower rates of overall mortality (HR, 0.88; 95% CI, 0.84–0.90) and cardiac-related mortality (HR, 0.94; 95% CI, 0.88–0.99; Supplementary Fig. 1). Kaplan–Meier survival curves for overall mortality, cardiac-related mortality, stroke, MI, repeat revascularization (CABG or PCI), and new dialysis following first-time isolated CABG are shown in Supplementary Fig. 2.

Logistic and Cox proportional hazards regression analyses of factors associated with in-hospital and overall mortality in the PS-matched cohort are summarized in Supplementary Tables 2 and 3, respectively. In the PS-matched cohort, acute MI diagnosed on the index date was independently associated with higher in-hospital mortality (OR, 6.25; 95% CI, 4.31–9.08) and overall mortality (HR, 1.84; 95% CI, 1.65–2.06) in multivariable analyses, whereas off-pump CABG was associated with lower in-hospital mortality (OR, 0.21; 95% CI, 0.14–0.30) and reduced all-cause mortality during follow-up (HR, 0.81; 95% CI, 0.74–0.88). Following PS matching, in-hospital mortality did not differ significantly between female and male groups (OR, 1.13; 95% CI, 0.78–1.64; Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 2). Also, 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year mortality had no significant difference between the two groups. However, the female group was a significant predictor of better overall survival compared to the male group (HR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.77–0.90; Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 3). Cardiac-related mortality was comparable between the two groups (HR, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.65–1.22; Fig. 2).

DISCUSSION

In this nationwide population-based PS-matched cohort study, female patients exhibited significantly better overall survival compared to males during long-term follow-up, whereas cardiac-related and in-hospital mortality did not differ significantly between female and male groups.

Sex-related differences in the clinical presentation and baseline characteristics of patients undergoing CABG have been studied in previous publications.241011121315 It has been reported that compared to male patients, female patients tend to be older and have more comorbidities, such as congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, and stroke.2410111213 Previous reports have investigated whether this higher prevalence of comorbidities in female patients contributes to a higher risk for postoperative complications and early mortality.21011

Previous studies have reported conflicting results regarding long-term survival rates after isolated CABG.48 For instance, Woorst and colleagues demonstrated that lower long-term survival in female patients after isolated CABG was observed in the cohort less than 70 years of age.8 However, this previous study was a single-center retrospective study. Gaudino and coworkers reported that female patients had a higher incidence of MI (HR, 1.30; 95% CI, 1.11–1.52) and repeat revascularization (HR, 1.22; 95% CI, 1.04–1.43); however, no significant difference was found for the all-cause mortality rate between female and male groups.4 Unlike these previous studies, our investigation utilized a real-world, nationwide population-level cohort study with a large sample size, longitudinal follow-up, and PS matching. These methodological strengths help minimize selection bias and enhance the generalizability of our findings.

In this study, the female group was a significant predictor of better long-term survival compared with the male group. Similar results have been reported in several previous studies.11131617 In a series of 3,760 patients undergoing CABG (1,162 females), Toumpoulis et al.13 showed that the female sex was an independent predictor of improved 5-year survival after CABG when adjusted for all independent predictors of long-term mortality (HR, 0.82; 95% CI, 0.71–0.96). Other studies, including those conducted by Kytö et al.11 and Huang et al.,18 have also reported that all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality are higher in male patients. Butt et al.16 conducted a nationwide cohort study in Denmark, demonstrating that male sex was a risk factor for cardiovascular death 1–7 years after CABG (HR, 1.13; 95% CI, 1.02–1.25). The strengths of this previous study include a large sample size of 37,495 patients undergoing isolated CABG in a real-world setting with complete data. In another report from a Korean nationwide cohort of PCI, Lee et al.17 found that the female sex had a lower adjusted 5-year death risk after PCI compared with the male sex (20.2% vs. 26.1%; HR, 0.75; 95% CI, 0.71–0.78), although patients undergoing CABG were not included.

Butt et al.16 have underscored the significance of non-cardiovascular deaths after CABG. According to their study, such deaths were more common in patients who survived the first year following CABG. Among non-cardiovascular causes of death, cancer was the most prevalent. In Korea, cancer was also the primary cause of death.19 The age-standardized incidence rates of cancer were higher in males than females.20 Additionally, cancer mortality rates were consistently lower in females than males.21 In our cohort, we observed a significantly higher prevalence of new-onset cancer in males compared to females after CABG. Malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung was a significant cause (4th most common) of overall mortality in the PS-matched cohort, especially among males (Supplementary Table 5).

The complex mechanisms by which sex differences affect outcomes are influenced by various factors. Smoking, a well-established risk factor in coronary artery disease, has been identified as a significant contributor to increased mortality among male patients.222324 Interestingly, a previous report has shown that female smoking rates are typically lower in Korea compared to western countries.25 Smoking prevalence in males remained unacceptably high, at approximately 49.8% in 2015.25 Alcohol consumption may also be an important factor. Male patients are reported to consume more alcohol and are more susceptible to trauma than female patients.2627 In our cohort, the prevalence of smoking and alcohol consumption was significantly lower in female patients than in male ones. These lifestyle differences may have increased the cardiovascular risk in male patients, resulting in worse late outcomes.

Our study results, which indicate higher long-term mortality in male patients compared to female patients with cardiovascular-related factors, may be partly explained by several previous studies.282930 For instance, Tian et al.28 suggested that male patients with coronary artery disease had larger lipid cores in their coronary non-culprit plaques compared to female patients, which could have contributed to the higher late mortality in male patients. Schreiner et al.29 also found that recurrent MI events are more likely to be lethal in male patients than female patients, which could further explain the higher long-term mortality in male patients. Additionally, the reduction in cardiovascular mortality in female patients could be due to the increased awareness of inherent cardiovascular risks in female patients and the application of female-specific guidelines, as demonstrated by a recent study.30

A noteworthy finding of our study is that off-pump CABG, performed without cardiopulmonary bypass, was associated with lower overall mortality compared to on-pump CABG in the Korean population. This result likely reflects the nationwide preference for off-pump CABG in our country, supported by extensive institutional experience, shorter operative times, reduced hospital stays, and the proactive adoption of this technique across many institutions.31

One of the strengths of this study is the use of the NHIS database, which covers the majority of the Korean population and provides access to comprehensive individual-level data. Furthermore, PS matching was applied to this real-world dataset to minimize confounding factors, and both overall and cardiac-related mortality were analyzed in accordance with the focus of previous studies.3233 In our analysis, the female group emerged as a significant predictor of improved overall survival compared to the male group. While the long-term outcomes observed in our study are consistent with those reported in a previous retrospective study based on a national registry, our investigation has the added strength of including all patients who underwent CABG nationwide, rather than being limited to individuals with acute coronary syndrome.11

There are several limitations to this study. First, perioperative laboratory values or results of imaging studies such as echocardiographic information and coronary angiographic findings could not be investigated. Second, detailed operative techniques, such as the type of bypass grafts and usage of cardiopulmonary bypass, were absent from the study. In South Korea, the surgical coding system for CABG was revised on July 1, 2017, to distinguish between 1, 2, 3, or ≥ 4 bypassed vessels. Prior to this revision, procedures were categorized only as 1 vessel or ≥ 2 vessels. Therefore, given the study period, the number of bypassed coronary arteries in our analysis could only be classified as ‘one’ versus ‘two or more,’ as shown in Table 1. Third, the NHIS claims data do not allow assessment of operative urgency, such as differentiating between same-day and next-day surgery or identifying preoperative extracorporeal membrane oxygenation use, which represents an inherent limitation of this dataset. These limitations could not be avoided because the data were obtained from the NHIS claims database based on the code system.

In conclusion, female patients who underwent isolated CABG exhibited significantly lower overall mortality compared to male patients during long-term follow-up, whereas no significant difference was observed in cardiac-related mortality.

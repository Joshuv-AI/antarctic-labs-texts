Indirect Comparisons of the Efficacy and Safety of Zanubrutinib versus Venetoclax plus Obinutuzumab in Treatment-Naïve Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia/Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma
Authors: Talha Munir, Nicolás Martinez-Calle, Sheng Xu, Keri Yang, Xiaoyun Ge, Ayad K. Ali, Leyla Mohseninejad, Balázs Dobi, Pal Rakonczai, Han Ma, Rhys Williams, Wassim Aldairy, Nicole Lamanna
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCompared with chemoimmunotherapy, both zanubrutinib monotherapy and venetoclax plus obinutuzumab prolong progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). Matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC) can be used to compare the efficacy and safety of different treatment regimens when no head-to-head trial has compared the treatments.MethodsPatient matching was conducted using unanchored MAIC propensity-score weighting to compare PFS, overall survival (OS), tolerability, and adverse events (AEs) of interest (AEIs; grade 3–4 infections, neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, and/or thrombocytopenia and AEs leading to treatment discontinuation) on the basis of data from patients in SEQUOIA for zanubrutinib and aggregate data from CLL14 for venetoclax plus obinutuzumab. Because SEQUOIA occurred during the pandemic, analyses were also conducted to adjust for coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infections.ResultsAfter matching and adjustment, baseline characteristics of the zanubrutinib group in SEQUOIA were well balanced with the CLL14 population (N = 216), with an effective sample size of 163 for the zanubrutinib group. After matching for baseline characteristics, zanubrutinib demonstrated a robust PFS benefit compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab (hazard ratio, 0.66 [95% confidence interval, 0.44–0.97]; P = 0.0351) and higher PFS probability at landmark points (60-month landmarks of 73.9% versus 63%, respectively). OS trended in favor of zanubrutinib. Overall, AEs of any grade over time were comparable in the zanubrutinib safety and venetoclax plus obinutuzumab populations. Zanubrutinib was associated with lower rates of selected AEIs compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab at all time points, except for grade 3–4 infections after 156 weeks. After adjusting for COVID-19, zanubrutinib was associated with a significantly lower incidence of grade 3–4 infections at 104 weeks but similar incidences of grade 3–4 infections versus venetoclax plus obinutuzumab during the overall follow-up period.ConclusionsContinuous treatment with zanubrutinib in treatment-naïve patients with CLL/small lymphocytic lymphoma resulted in prolonged PFS and a favorable safety profile compared with fixed-duration venetoclax plus obinutuzumab.Trial Registration No.SEQUOIA (NCT03336333); CLL14 (NCT02242942).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40487-025-00380-0.

Introduction

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is a low-grade lymphoproliferative neoplasm. When symptoms or signs of worsening appear, patients meeting the 2018 International Workshop for CLL criteria are referred for treatment [1].

The discovery of pathogenic B cell receptor signaling in CLL/small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) has led to the development of novel targeted therapies that have changed the treatment landscape for this disease [2–6]. Novel targeted agents such as the first-in-class Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor ibrutinib and the B cell lymphoma 2 inhibitor venetoclax are active as single agents and significantly improve survival over chemoimmunotherapy (fludarabine, cyclophosphamide, and rituximab or bendamustine plus rituximab for older patients) in patients with CLL/SLL [7–10]; however, when used alone, these therapies rarely lead to disease eradication [4, 11].

Most BTK inhibitors are approved for continuous use as monotherapies or combined with a course of CD20 monoclonal antibody until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [6], while venetoclax combined with obinutuzumab or rituximab is approved for a fixed number of cycles [6, 11–14]. Understanding the benefits of continuous versus fixed-duration regimens is important for the optimal management of patients with CLL/SLL.

The next-generation covalent BTK inhibitor zanubrutinib has greater relative selectivity for BTK versus off-target kinases and an improved safety profile compared with first-generation BTK inhibitors [15–17]. In the global, open-label, phase 3 SEQUOIA trial, treatment-naïve patients with CLL/SLL without deletion of chromosome 17p (del[17p]) were randomized to continuous treatment with zanubrutinib until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity (arm A) or six cycles of bendamustine plus rituximab (arm B) [18]. Because bendamustine plus rituximab was not considered a suitable regimen for patients with del(17p), patients with this mutation were assigned to a separate cohort (arm C), receiving continuous treatment with zanubrutinib until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, and were analyzed separately. The primary endpoint, progression-free survival (PFS) in the randomized arms, was met at the interim analysis after a median follow-up of 26.2 months. The 24-month PFS rate was 85.5% with zanubrutinib and 69.5% with bendamustine plus rituximab. At the 5-year follow-up, the estimated 60-month PFS rates in patients treated with zanubrutinib and bendamustine plus rituximab were 75.8% and 40.1%, respectively, and the median PFS was not yet reached and 44.1 months [19].

The international, open-label, phase 3 CLL14 trial compared 12 cycles of chlorambucil plus obinutuzumab with 12 cycles of venetoclax plus obinutuzumab in treatment-naïve patients with CLL/SLL [20]. Patients with TP53 deletion or mutation were enrolled at the investigator’s discretion. The intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis included 216 patients in each treatment group. After all patients were off treatment for 60 months, the median PFS in the overall patient population was 76.2 months with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab versus 36.4 months with chlorambucil plus obinutuzumab. The 72-month overall survival (OS) estimates were 78.7% and 69.2% in the venetoclax plus obinutuzumab and chlorambucil plus obinutuzumab groups, respectively [21].

While both zanubrutinib monotherapy and venetoclax plus obinutuzumab demonstrated prolonged PFS compared with chemoimmunotherapy, no head-to-head trials comparing these treatment options have been conducted. Matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC) is a statistical method that can be used to compare the efficacy and safety of different treatment regimens when direct comparisons are not available [22] and has been used successfully to compare different regimens across CLL/SLL studies [23, 24].

In this study, we applied MAIC techniques to evaluate PFS, OS, safety, and tolerability, including adverse events (AEs) of interest (AEIs), between treatment-naïve patients treated with zanubrutinib in the SEQUOIA trial and those treated with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab in the CLL14 trial.

Methods

MAIC uses individual patient-level data from one study in conjunction with aggregate-level data from a second study to adjust for differences in baseline characteristics and produce a weighted estimate [22]. Indirect comparisons between zanubrutinib and venetoclax plus obinutuzumab were conducted using both the naïve unmatched ITT population from SEQUOIA and an adjusted population that was balanced by key characteristics to the CLL14 study population. All analyses were performed using R (version 4.0.2). Data were available from multiple follow-up cutoff dates for each study. The study time frames for SEQUOIA differed from CLL14 (Supplementary Fig. S1) as SEQUOIA was conducted during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, while CLL14 was conducted prior to the COVID-19 pandemic. Ethics committee approval was not required for this post hoc analysis, as this article is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

The SEQUOIA (NCT03336333; EudraCT: 2017-001551-31) and CLL14 (NCT02242942; EudraCT: 2014–001810-24) studies were approved by the applicable ethics committees and followed the ethical principles of Good Clinical Practice and International Conference on Harmonization guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki; for both studies, patients provided written informed consent to participate [18, 20].

Matching-Adjusted Indirect Comparison

Individual patient data (IPD) for patients treated with zanubrutinib were extracted from arms A and C of the SEQUOIA trial [18, 19]. Venetoclax plus obinutuzumab data were extracted from CLL14 publications [20, 25]. For efficacy comparisons, aggregate data from CLL14 were extracted from the 5-year results (median follow-up, 65.4 months) [20, 26]. For safety comparisons, data were extracted from two publications, one evaluating patients with a median follow-up of 28.1 months and the other assessing patients who were ≥ 2 years post treatment (median follow-up, 39.7 months) [20, 25].

Population adjustment was conducted using an unanchored MAIC to evaluate comparative efficacy and safety on the basis of data from arms A and C from SEQUOIA for zanubrutinib and the venetoclax plus obinutuzumab arm from CLL14. MAIC patient matching for SEQUOIA and CLL14 was based on prognostic factors or effect modifiers, which were chosen on the basis of data availability and the magnitude of imbalance between populations. Baseline variables used for matching included demographics (age and sex), genetics (immunoglobulin heavy chain variable region [IGHV]), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status, proportion of patients with CLL/SLL, disease stage, β2 microglobulin, creatinine clearance, B symptoms (constitutional symptoms associated with CLL/SLL, including fever, night sweats, and weight loss), and time from diagnosis (Supplementary Table S1). The weighting of individual patients was assumed to follow a logistic regression model, and a method of moments estimator was used to estimate the parameters of the model in accordance with guidance from the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence Decision Support Unit [27]. Sensitivity analyses exploring different sets of matching factors were also conducted to support the robustness of the results (Supplementary Table S1). To mitigate potential bias from the COVID-19 pandemic, which overlapped in timing with SEQUOIA but not CLL14, additional analyses that censored for COVID-19-related deaths were conducted.

Efficacy Outcomes

Efficacy analyses included investigator-assessed PFS (PFSINV) and OS. Pseudo-IPD for venetoclax plus obinutuzumab were constructed from digitized Kaplan–Meier (KM) curves of published PFSINV and OS curves [26]. The digitized reconstruction process for KM curves may introduce bias; hence, the curves were visually analyzed and compared with estimated KM curves, numbers of patients at risk, and survival probabilities versus reported values and versus reported or read-in values. All validation plots supported the accuracy of the pseudo-IPD and ensured that numerical errors were minimal. The hazard ratio (HR), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and P-values for PFSINV and OS were calculated using weighted Cox proportional hazard regression. The ITT population for zanubrutinib included all patients from arms A and C of SEQUOIA. Efficacy subgroup analyses were conducted in patients on the basis of IGHV mutation status. The subgroup analyses were based on the matched full dataset (i.e., SEQUOIA arms A + C IPD were matched to the CLL14 ITT set), and the subgroup outcome comparisons were conducted.

Safety Outcomes

The safety analysis set from CLL14 comprised patients who received venetoclax plus obinutuzumab (n = 212). An overview of the timing and duration of AE reporting in SEQUOIA and CLL14 is provided in Supplementary Table S2. Safety and tolerability assessments included treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) and AEIs, which included grade 3 or 4 infections, neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and AEs leading to treatment discontinuation. To match the timing of venetoclax plus obinutuzumab treatment and follow-up for CLL14, zanubrutinib monotherapy data in arms A and C of the SEQUOIA trial with time windows of 52, 60, 88, 104, and 156 weeks and the overall period (data cutoff date: 30 April 2024) were applied in the analyses of safety results. In this MAIC analysis, data for zanubrutinib were mainly based on two analysis time windows: median treatment durations of 23.9 months (to match the safety follow-up of venetoclax plus obinutuzumab) and 61.2 months. Each set was compared with data for fixed-duration venetoclax plus obinutuzumab from available publications on safety outcomes.

For safety outcomes, the primary analysis was based on the naïve safety population in SEQUOIA, excluding patients who were randomized but not treated; the secondary analysis used the base-case adjusted population for the zanubrutinib groups. Additional analyses were conducted to censor for grade 3–4 COVID-19-related infections and TEAEs leading to discontinuation. Exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs) were used to adjust between continuous and fixed-duration treatments. For AEI incidence, odds ratios with 95% CIs were calculated for zanubrutinib versus venetoclax plus obinutuzumab, and associated P-values were estimated using a logistic regression model.

Efficacy Outcomes

After matching and adjusting for baseline characteristics, zanubrutinib demonstrated significantly longer PFSINV compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab, with an HRPFS-INV of 0.66 (95% CI 0.44–0.97; P = 0.0351) (Fig. 1A) and 60-month landmarks of 73.9% and 63%, respectively. Similar benefits in prolonging PFSINV were seen with zanubrutinib in both the unmatched ITT population and when adjusted for COVID-19 (Fig. 2; Supplementary Fig. S2). Sensitivity analyses controlling for other baseline variables supported the robustness of the PFSINV results (Supplementary Table S3). Zanubrutinib also showed longer PFSINV in patients with unmutated IGHV; these benefits were maintained following adjustments for COVID-19 (Fig. 1B; Supplementary Fig. S2). Safety Outcomes

Serious TEAEs and TEAEs leading to discontinuation or death were proportionately lower at earlier cutoffs with zanubrutinib and comparable at 62.7 months with zanubrutinib versus venetoclax plus obinutuzumab (Table 2). After matching, AEs were still similar between treatments (Supplementary Table S3). EAIRs were lower with zanubrutinib compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab (Table 3). Zanubrutinib was associated with significantly lower rates of the selected AEIs, except for grade 3–4 infections, compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab at all time points (Table 4). After adjusting for grade 3–4 COVID-19 infections, as CLL14 was conducted prior to the COVID-19 pandemic (2014–2017) (Supplementary Fig. S1), zanubrutinib was associated with significantly fewer grade 3–4 infections than venetoclax plus obinutuzumab at 104 weeks but showed similar grade 3–4 infections in the overall follow-up period. In addition, zanubrutinib was associated with fewer grade 3–4 hematologic events, including neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and febrile neutropenia, in both periods (Table 4). Discussion

Understanding the role of continuous versus fixed-duration regimens in CLL/SLL across different patient profiles is critical in optimizing patient outcomes. In this unanchored MAIC between continuous single-agent zanubrutinib and fixed-duration venetoclax plus obinutuzumab in treatment-naïve patients with CLL/SLL, zanubrutinib was associated with prolonged PFSINV compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab at the 60-month landmark, without (HRPFS-INV, 0.66 [95% CI, 0.44–0.97]; P = 0.0351) or with adjustment (HRPFS-INV, 0.58 [95% CI, 0.38–0.88]; P = 0.0095) for the COVID-19 pandemic. The PFSINV advantage with zanubrutinib was also observed in patients with unmutated IGHV, while the PFSINV values were similar between the two treatments in patients with mutated IGHV (HR for zanubrutinib versus venetoclax plus obinutuzumab, 0.99 [95% CI, 0.55–1.81]). The HRs for OS numerically favored the zanubrutinib group.

The incidence of grade 3–4 AEs was lower with zanubrutinib despite its much longer treatment duration (median treatment duration, 61.24 months versus 11.1 months in the venetoclax plus obinutuzumab cohort), especially for grade 3–4 neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and febrile neutropenia. The incidences of serious AEs, TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation, and TEAEs leading to death were lower after a median treatment duration of 35.9 months in the zanubrutinib arm than after a median treatment duration of 11.1 months in the venetoclax plus obinutuzumab arm. EAIRs for AEs were lower after a median treatment exposure of 61.24 months for zanubrutinib versus 11.1 months for venetoclax plus obinutuzumab. After adjusting for the COVID-19 pandemic, the incidence of grade 3–4 infection after 4 years of zanubrutinib treatment was lower than that after 1 year of venetoclax plus obinutuzumab treatment. These data were also supported by sensitivity analyses, which showed similar trends in the MAIC compared with the naïve dataset.

Other indirect-comparison approaches have been used to compare other novel treatments and treatment combinations in patients with CLL/SLL. An unanchored MAIC using IPD from the phase 3 ALPINE study for zanubrutinib and aggregate data from the phase 3 ASCEND trial demonstrated significant PFSINV advantages with zanubrutinib versus acalabrutinib monotherapy in patients with relapsed or refractory CLL [28]. Given the differences in the periods when the two studies were conducted with respect to the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, the analyses were adjusted for the impact of COVID-19 in the ALPINE study. The results of this MAIC suggested a PFS (HR, 0.68 [95% CI, 0.46–0.99]; P = 0.0448) and complete response advantage (HR, 2.90 [95% CI, 1.13–7.43]; P = 0.0270) with zanubrutinib versus acalabrutinib, as well as potentially improved OS (HR, 0.6 [95% CI, 0.35–1.02]; P = 0.0575).

Davids and colleagues also used IPD from ELEVATE-TN for acalabrutinib, with or without obinutuzumab, matched to aggregate baseline characteristics from studies of several comparator treatments in treatment-naïve patients identified through literature review [29, 30]. These included RESONATE-2 (ibrutinib versus chlorambucil) [31], iLLUMINATE (ibrutinib plus obinutuzumab versus chlorambucil plus obinutuzumab) [32], CLL14 (venetoclax plus obinutuzumab versus chlorambucil plus obinutuzumab) [20], ALLIANCE (ibrutinib or ibrutinib plus rituximab versus bendamustine plus rituximab) [33], and CLL11 (chlorambucil plus obinutuzumab versus chlorambucil plus rituximab or chlorambucil monotherapy) [34]. The authors reported prolonged but not significant benefits in PFS with acalabrutinib plus obinutuzumab in all comparisons. Contrary to the statistically significant HR for improved PFSINV seen with zanubrutinib compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab, acalabrutinib monotherapy showed a nonsignificant HR but prolonged PFS versus all comparators except venetoclax plus obinutuzumab. In the analysis of safety, acalabrutinib monotherapy was associated with significantly lower rates of atrial fibrillation and infections compared with ibrutinib and lower rates of grade 3 or 4 AEs, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and infections compared with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab. These results are in line with those observed with zanubrutinib in the ALPINE study, which showed lower rates of atrial fibrillation compared with ibrutinib [16] and lower rates of infection and hematologic AEs in the current MAIC of safety versus venetoclax plus obinutuzumab.

Other indirect analyses have explored the efficacy and safety of other targeted therapies used to treat patients with CLL/SLL. Using a frequentist network meta-analysis for direct and indirect comparisons between different treatments, Wen and colleagues evaluated the efficacy and safety of several first-line therapies [35]. The analysis compared outcomes in various patient subgroups on the basis of age, comorbidities, IGHV status, and cytogenetic abnormalities. The analysis found a PFS advantage with acalabrutinib monotherapy over ibrutinib and venetoclax plus obinutuzumab in patients aged > 65 years and those with unmutated IGHV. Differences in safety profiles were also observed. Lower rates of neutropenia were reported in patients treated with ibrutinib plus venetoclax and ibrutinib plus obinutuzumab than with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab, and fewer infections were associated with ibrutinib plus venetoclax compared with acalabrutinib or acalabrutinib plus obinutuzumab. Rates of hypertension were lower with venetoclax plus obinutuzumab than with ibrutinib plus obinutuzumab.

The current analysis is subject to inherent MAIC limitations, including some differences in trial design and study characteristics (e.g., exposure times, assessments, and length of follow-up) that may limit the generalizability of the interpretation of the results. In addition, subgroup analyses based on IGHV mutation status were conducted post MAIC matching and adjustment and must be interpreted within this framework. The COVID-19 pandemic, which was ongoing during the SEQUOIA trial, could have impacted safety outcomes in ways that may not be easy to discern from direct effects, such as deaths due to COVID-19. MAIC analyses may be limited to confounding and bias effects based on selected effect modifiers; however, sensitivity analyses supported the robustness of the results. The MAIC and naïve comparisons were conducted post hoc and were not designed or powered to detect differences between zanubrutinib and venetoclax plus obinutuzumab, and type 2 errors cannot be excluded.

Findings from this study may help inform clinical treatment sequencing and patient preferences to optimize clinical decision-making and improve health economic modeling. Future studies are important to better understand the roles of fixed versus continuous duration in different patient populations, including sex, race/ethnicity, age, and medical comorbidities. In addition, further research is warranted to explore other outcomes, including quality of life, disability, and cost-effectiveness.

Conclusions

In the absence of head-to-head trials and data from prospectively designed clinical trials, indirect comparisons can provide valuable information regarding the comparative efficacy and safety of treatments. On the basis of the efficacy and safety results of our analyses, continuous therapy with zanubrutinib in treatment-naïve patients with CLL/SLL remains a valuable treatment option compared with fixed-duration venetoclax plus obinutuzumab.

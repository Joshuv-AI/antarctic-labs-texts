Introduction

From the outset, EASL had an underlying mission to “attract mainly young and active investigators to exchange current views and results”.1 This philosophy has been a cornerstone of the Association since its foundation 60 years ago. As a concrete example, EASL has applied age cut-offs within its governance structures, including membership of the Scientific Committee to ensure that the Association remains dynamic and agile, representing the interests of the young and most active members of the community. Such an approach has likely been a key contributor to EASL’s success, as recruiting young and talented individuals and creating space and opportunities for them to grow is essential for ensuring that the organisation remains sustainable and future-oriented.2

Attracting young and talented individuals has become increasingly competitive, and EASL’s offerings must therefore remain highly relevant, attractive, and aligned with the evolving expectations of the next generation. Over the past fifteen years, EASL has made huge strides in achieving balance, diversity, and representation, including the integration of younger voices.3,4 Furthermore, there has been a consistent expansion of a portfolio targeted at young investigators (YIs).

This editorial aims to highlight the vision for YIs within the EASL community, with a specific focus on educational content, congress participation, and funding and fellowship opportunities.

YI representation in EASL and at the EASL congress

EASL has a large and growing early-career base, with YIs representing 33% of the EASL membership in 2025 (Fig. 1A). In parallel, EASL has achieved substantial progress in gender representation as the proportion of women increased from 24% (2009) to 44% (2025) among EASL members, and from 9% to 53% within the Governing Board (Fig. 1B). At the EASL Congress 2025, 7,749 participants were registered, comprising 18% YIs (15% trainees/postdoctoral fellows, and 3% undergraduate students), alongside regular delegates (75%), allied health professionals (3%), nurses (2%) and patient advocates (1%) (Fig. 1C). Within the YI group (n = 1,305), 72% were EASL members (including ∼2% from low-income/lower-middle income economies) (Fig. 1D). The YI Task Force

The origins of the EASL YI Task Force can be traced back to 2011-2012, with the creation of the Young Investigators Concerted Action Group (YI-CAG). Established by the Governing Board, led at that time by Secretary General Prof. Mark Thursz, the YI-CAG was conceived as a representative body to identify and address the needs of young hepatologists in training, particularly in relation to educational and networking activities at the EASL Congress. In July 2011, an election took place with 103 YIs voting. Here, Dr. Vincenzo Cardinale was elected as the first representative and the top five YIs were then invited to create the first YI-CAG. The group promoted community building, dialogue, and dedicated initiatives for YIs, including current EASL activities such as the YI-Networking Event at the EASL Congress and the Masterclass (more details below). Building on this, the YI-CAG evolved in 2018 into the current YI Task Force, reflecting a more structured mandate and an expanded role within EASL governance.

The YI Task Force is currently composed of six members serving a single 3-year term, selected through an open and competitive call. Particular attention is given in the selection process to ensuring that members are dedicated and have ambitions and ideas related to initiatives relevant for young investigators. Therefore, selection criteria prioritise teamwork, communication skills, scientific experience, and the ability to contribute constructively. Attention is also paid to balancing geographical representation and gender, and to ensuring a mix of basic and translational researchers and clinical scientists. This approach ensures that the Task Force reflects the diversity of the EASL community.

The YI Task Force operates with a clear and efficient governance structure. The Task Force meets twice a year, and monthly online meetings are held with the EASL Office. Additional extraordinary meetings are convened when dedicated to specific tasks. In 2025, the structure was further formalised by appointing a chair of the Task Force, who serves as a link from the Task Force to both the EASL Governing Board and EASL Office. Representation across the different EASL Committees is distributed among the Task Force members, enhancing the YIs’ voice when planning new activities and proposals among the whole association. Decisions are taken by consensus or majority vote within the Task Force and are reported through the chair to the EASL Scientific Committee and Governing Board.

The opportunities to contribute to improve possibilities and activities for YIs makes the Task Force highly attractive to motivated EASL members. This is illustrated by more than 140 applications over the last 5 calls for new members of the Task Force.

Vision of the Task Force

In 2025, the current YI Task Force met in Geneva to crystallise a shared mission and vision for the years ahead. We are committed to cultivating a thriving, inclusive, and globally connected community of early- and mid-career hepatology professionals, enabling YIs to grow, excel, and contribute meaningfully to the advancement of liver science and clinical care. Conference opportunities for YIs

The EASL Congress and Summits throughout the year offer a comprehensive set of opportunities designed to support and empower YIs at every stage of their early careers (Fig. 2). New members and first-time attendees at the EASL Congress are warmly welcomed through a dedicated introduction pathway that includes a welcome video and guided tours led by the Task Force, helping participants navigate the congress and integrate quickly into the community. The different workshops and the YI Seminar are carefully prepared every year to promote educational growth, providing practical insights into research translation, career development and excellence, to empower YIs with important skills and knowledge to pursue a successful career and to become the next-generation of leaders. Scientific engagement is further strengthened through dedicated YI-led poster tours, which highlight emerging research and promote discussion with other YIs. Recognition of talent is ensured through the recognition of the yearly EASL Emerging Leader Award, celebrating outstanding contributions and leadership potential. Additionally, interacting with other YIs is the main focus at one of the most celebrated events of the Congress - the YI Networking Event that fosters interpersonal connections, collaboration, mentorship, and peer exchange in a relaxed environment.Fig. 2Opportunities for YIs at EASL Congress 2026.

Overall, these initiatives create an inclusive, supportive environment that enhances visibility, skills development, collaboration, and career advancement for YIs in EASL. In order to expand visibility for YIs, the EASL leadership commits to providing a platform for YIs to take on more faculty tasks as speakers and chairs, often pairing a YI with a more experienced chair.

Beyond the Congress – education, schools, studios, and masterclass

EASL Schools, the EASL–AASLD Masterclass, and Podcasts targeting the YIs form a coherent training pathway that prioritises YI development: Schools build core scientific and clinical competencies. ‘The Talk Liver To Me’ Podcasts provide focused and up to date 30-minute deep dives into hot topics and the latest clinical guidelines. The EASL–AASLD Masterclass adds an intensive, mentored environment where emerging talent can engage directly with senior faculty in small-group formats.

Notably, the EASL–AASLD Masterclass began in 2018 and continues annually, rotating across European and US venues through the EASL–AASLD collaboration. Applicant feedback from recent Masterclasses highlights consistently strong educational value. In 2025, all participants (100%) reported achieving their learning objectives; 75% “strongly agreed” that pre-course instructions enabled adequate preparation, and 100% rated both facilities and breakout sessions as “excellent.” Interaction was also a clear strength, with 100% agreeing the format enabled high-quality exchange with faculty and peers. In 2023, responses similarly emphasized teamwork, networking, and overall organization (often rated “excellent”).

Beyond the Congress – funding and prizes

EASL has a history and prides itself on delivering a wide portfolio of opportunities to support YIs with grants and prizes. The EASL Emerging Leader Award is granted annually to outstanding YIs aged less than 40 years, selected jointly by the YI Task Force and Governing Board in recognition of outstanding research achievements. In parallel, the Daniel Alagille Award, a dedicated fellowship honouring Pr. Daniel Alagille was designed to stimulate biomedical research in paediatric and adult genetic cholestatic diseases. Furthermore, the “EASL Mobility/Research Fellowship Programme” has supported training and mobility of more than 60 researchers since 2014 (formerly known as the Juan Rodés PhD Fellowship, the Andrew Burroughs Short-Term Fellowship, and the Sheila Sherlock Postdoctoral Fellowship).

Future directions

Further alignment and a shared vision for future initiatives for YIs in EASL will be strengthened by ensuring that GB leadership joins YI meetings on a regular basis. Furthermore, the chair of the YI Task Force will attend GB meetings to facilitate joint planning of YI-focused activities.

This will bolster the development of a long-term strategy to attract and retain YIs within EASL. Such close engagement with the next generation will ensure that EASL remains aligned with their interests, expectations, and priorities, keeping EASL relevant to the needs of the members.

In addition, members of the YI Task Force are invited to contribute to several new EASL initiatives. These currently include the Viral HOPE Steering Committee, the development of the EASL Education Core Curriculum, and EASL's podcast “Talk Liver to Me.”

Conclusion

EASL’s sustained commitment and portfolio of opportunities strive to nurture the next generation of hepatology leaders. By embedding YIs into every layer of the organisation, from governance structures to scientific programming, educational activities, and funding streams, EASL has created an ecosystem in which emerging talent can genuinely thrive and provide an infrastructure that will continue to promote leaders for the future.

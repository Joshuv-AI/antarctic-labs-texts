Haemodynamic response to peripheral and inspiratory resistance training in patients with coronary artery disease (HEMODYRET): study protocol of a prospective, single-centre, randomised crossover trial
Authors: Flavia Mazzoli-Rocha, Lena Riemer, Carmen Vikoler, Rebekka Milewski, Christina Niederegger, Uwe Schwan, Hans-Georg Predel, Birna Bjarnason-Wehrens, Thomas Schmidt
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDue to the documented benefits of peripheral resistance training (RT) and inspiratory resistance training, known as inspiratory muscle training (IMT), in patients with cardiovascular disease, both exercise forms are regularly used in cardiac rehabilitation. However, little is known about the haemodynamic responses to different intensities of dynamic RT, isometric RT, and IMT in patients with coronary artery disease (CAD). This study is designed to evaluate and compare the acute haemodynamic responses elicited by different RT and IMT modalities in patients with CAD.Methods and analysisThis study design is a prospective, single-centre, randomised crossover trial. A total of 20 participants with CAD will be included. All participants will undergo four different exercise training interventions: IMT, isometric wall squat training (IWS), dynamic leg press training (DLP) and isometric handgrip training (IHG). For each intervention, participants will perform two intensity modalities in randomised order: IMT (low vs high), IWS (low vs moderate), DLP (low vs high) and IHG (low vs moderate). The primary outcomes are the acute exercise-induced haemodynamic responses (esp. systolic blood pressure, pulse rate, stroke volume, cardiac output) across the different exercise training interventions, as well as the changes in haemodynamic responses during the recovery phase for each intervention. Secondary outcomes include changes in tissue oxygen saturation, oxygen saturation, and perceived dyspnoea and exertion. The study period is planned for 2025.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the German Sport University Cologne (Ethical Approval Code: 209/24). The findings will be disseminated through international peer-reviewed publications. This study is supported by the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation via the partnership with the Coordenação de Aperfeiçoamento de Pessoal de Nível Superior (CAPES)(CAPES-Humboldt Research Fellowship for Experienced Researchers) and by research funding from Edwards Lifesciences LLC (Limited Liability Company).Trial registration numberGerman Clinical Trials Register DRKS00035668.

Introduction

Rationale for the study

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of death worldwide, accounting for approximately 32% of all deaths and more than 17 million fatalities in 2019,1 with a rising trend expected in the coming years. Among all CVDs, coronary artery disease (CAD) represents the leading cause of mortality.1 2

In patients with CAD, meta-analyses confirm the effectiveness of exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (ebCR): participation in ebCR is associated with reductions in overall mortality and hospitalisation rates, as well as improvements in quality of life and exercise capacity, compared with control groups.36 Consequently, international guidelines strongly recommend participation in ebCR following acute coronary syndrome or coronary revascularisation.710

Individually tailored exercise training is a core component of ebCR. While aerobic endurance training has been an integral part of international recommendations for ebCR in CAD patients for more than 40 years, recommendations for peripheral resistance training (RT) were for many years very cautious and hesitant. This caution was predominantly based on the concern that RT could lead to inadequate haemodynamic responses and thus trigger dangerous cardiovascular problems.

In recent decades, the importance of RT in CAD and several other cardiac conditions has received increasing scientific attention. Findings highlight the high prevalence of age-related sarcopenia, disease-related muscle deconditioning, physical limitations and frailty, particularly, but not only, in older patients and thus the need for individually tailored RT to counteract these symptoms.11 Recently, the most important findings on the effectiveness and safety of RT in patients with CAD were summarised in a review, which confirmed the positive effects and safety of moderate RT in patients with CAD.11 The RT is well tolerated by patients with CAD, with very few reports of adverse cardiovascular events.11 It is effective to improve muscle strength, 6 min walking distance (6MWD) as well as exercise capacity, and mobility in CAD patients.

In recent years, also inspiratory resistance training, known as inspiratory muscle training (IMT), has gained increasing attention as a treatment for cardiac patients, as both peripheral and inspiratory muscle weakness are associated with exercise intolerance, disease severity and poor prognosis in patients with CVD.12 In particular, weakness of the inspiratory muscles is associated with reduced lung function and capacity and contributes to dyspnoea and exercise intolerance.13

Dynamic RT

The different guidelines for ebCR recommend a wide and sometimes inconsistent and overlapping range of intensities for lower body dynamic RT in cardiac patients, typically classified as low (eg, 30%–50% of one-repetition maximum [1RM]), moderate (eg, 30%–69% 1RM), high (eg, 50%–84% 1RM) and very high (eg, >85% 1RM).79 10 1417 Despite this variability in exercise prescription, growing evidence suggests that dynamic RT performed at intensities above 70% 1RM is more effective than lower intensities for optimising muscle mass and strength gains.18 The same may also apply to the acute haemodynamic responses, where higher intensities combined with shorter exercise durations might be better tolerated than moderate intensities with prolonged exercise durations.1921 This, in turn, is also relevant from a safety perspective. Nevertheless, the dynamic RT at low-to-moderate intensity (below 69% 1RM) is more studied in current meta-analysis related to ebCR182224 and more used in ebCR routine25 than dynamic RT at high intensity. In addition, it is well-established that a systolic blood pressure (SBP) exceeding 250 mm Hg is a common termination criterion during cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) in healthy individuals. In contrast, blood pressure reaches considerably higher levels during maximal resistance exercise, as the previously reported peaks from 320/250 mm Hg to 480/350 mm Hg.26 Further studies are urgently required to understand the optimal dynamic RT modality for patients with CAD, based on its acute haemodynamic response, to ensure both safety and efficacy in exercise prescription.

Isometric RT

Isometric RT, such as handgrip, wall squat or plank, is a simple form of exercise that can often be performed without the need for expensive equipment or specific space requirements.2731 It has been shown to be effective—and safe—in reducing resting SBP to the same or even greater extent compared with aerobic training and dynamic RT in hypertensive, pre-hypertensive and normotensive adults.283032

Nevertheless, there are currently no specific recommendations for isometric RT in cardiac patients, with dynamic RT being still proposed as the preferred method.79 10 1417 To the best of our knowledge, the acute haemodynamic responses to different isometric resistance training exercises have not yet been investigated in patients with CAD and further research is required.

IMT

Growing evidence suggests that IMT, at both low (30%–45%) and high (70%) intensities, is safe and should be considered as an additional intervention to improve maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP), 6MWD, exercise capacity and quality of life in patients with CAD after coronary revascularisation35 36 and with stable angina.37 Although 2 sets of 30 breaths at 50% MIP or 20–30 min of IMT at 30%–70% MIP have been recommended for patients with heart failure,7 17 38 39 there is no specific recommendation for patients with CAD. Moreover, IMT protocols in CAD patients vary widely, ranging from 30 min at low intensity35 37 to 5 sets of 10 repetitions at moderate to high intensity.36 The acute haemodynamic responses to such protocols remain unclear.

In summary, literature comparing haemodynamic responses and their safety limits across different exercise modalities is scarce for dynamic RT21 40 and virtually non-existent for isometric RT and IMT in CAD patients. Thus, this study aims to evaluate and compare the acute haemodynamic responses of different RT and IMT modalities in patients with CAD, providing a basis for assessing the safety of these training approaches.

Trial design

This study is a prospective, single-centre, randomised crossover trial. The study considers the Declaration of Helsinki41 and the guidelines of good clinical practice.42 This study is reported according to the guidelines of the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT).43

Methods and analysis

Study setting

This trial will be conducted at the Department of Preventive and Rehabilitative Sport and Exercise Medicine, Institute for Cardiology and Sports Medicine, German Sport University Cologne, Germany. Recruitment began on 21 May 2025 and is expected to continue until December 2025.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria are: (1) adult and (2) stable CAD. The exclusion criteria are (in line with current cardiac rehabilitation guidelines44: (1) no signed informed consent, (2) acute infection, (3) clinical instability in the last month (eg, decompensated heart failure), (4) functional class IV of New York Heart Association (NYHA), (5) cardiac interventions less than 6 months before inclusion in this study, (6) hypertension grade 2 (≥160/100 mm Hg at rest), (7) untreated life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias at rest/or during exercise, (8) acute aortic dissection, (9) acute myocarditis/pericarditis, (10) severe hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, (11) abdominal or inguinal hernia, and (12) other clinical or/and functional limitations for the execution of evaluation tests and exercise training interventions. Dropout will be considered in case of withdrawal of the consent by the participant.

Sample size calculation

The primary objective of this study is to compare the acute haemodynamic responses to different RT modalities in patients with CAD. The sample size was calculated using Stata 13.0 (StataCorp LLC, Texas, USA), based on SBP as one of the primary outcomes. Using an effect size of 29.0 mm Hg and a pooled SD of 14.14 mm Hg from the study by Gjøvaag et al21—which evaluated haemodynamic responses to different RT modalities—along with an alpha error of 5% and a beta error of 10% (power=90%), a total of 20 participants will be required to complete the study. This sample size includes 20% increase to compensate for potential dropouts and participants’ refusals.

Recruitment

Patients will be recruited through advertising posters placed in cardiac rehabilitation groups (phase III) from the institute and surrounding areas. Research assistants will introduce and explain the study to potential participants. Participants will be instructed to avoid physical exercise and alcohol for 24 hours prior to the first study day and throughout the study period. A total of 20 participants will be enrolled by December 2025.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

The order of implementation of the modalities will be determined by stratified randomisation (sex) for each exercise training intervention. A sequence will be generated (through Excel Microsoft Office 365 or paper draw) to randomly allocate participants between the two directions of all exercise training modalities (low to high and high to low | low to mod and mod to low) in the ratio of 1:1. The allocation sequence will be generated by the research coordinator, while the enrolment/assignment of participants will be done by research assistants.

Blinding

Due to the difficulty of blinding participants and also exercise therapists who are performing the exercise training, only outcome assessors and statisticians will be blinded to group allocation throughout the entire trial.

Assessments

Baseline assessments

On day 1 of the study protocol, sociodemographic (age, sex, smoke, participation in cardiac rehabilitation groups, previous experience with resistance training exercises) and clinical information (comorbidities, medications, left ventricular ejection fraction, NYHA functional classification, previous surgical and non-surgical procedures) will be obtained from current medical records in order to define participants’ characteristics. Then, haemodynamic (SBP, DBP and PR), SpO2, perception of dyspnoea and effort, and anthropometric variables (weight, height, and waist and hip circumferences) will be assessed. Blood pressure will be measured in both arms using a sphygmomanometer, with patients in a sitting position with legs not crossed. In the case of a high blood pressure (≥140/90 mm Hg) in the first assessment, two measurements will be made to confirm the data (and avoid measurements related to white coat hypertension). SpO2 will be assessed by a finger pulse oximeter (Beurer PO40 Pulsoximeter, Ulm, Germany) placed in the left hand (finger with best signal)). A 6-min walking test (6MWT) will be carried out in accordance with current guideline standards45 46 to assess functional capacity. In addition, maximal exercise tests will also be assessed in order to establish the training intensities. In all maximal exercise tests, participants will do warm-up and familiarisation manoeuvres (maximal effort without recording data), followed by a recovery time of 10 min, and then, tests will be done with appropriate verbal encouragement,4749 as described below.

Inspiratory muscle strength—dynamic manoeuvre: Intensity index (S-Index) will be measured, through dynamic manoeuvres of maximal inspiratory effort, from residual volume to full inspiratory capacity. S-Index test will be done with at least eight forceful manoeuvres, divided into 2–3 series of 2–3 manoeuvres.49 The highest value, respecting a variation of less than 20% among at least three manoeuvres, will determine S-Index value, in a similar way as static manoeuvre.48 Intervals of 30 s between each S-Index set will be done to avoid the fatigue of the musculature involved.

Then, the 1RM will be determined according to the recommendations for 1RM testing in cardiac patients: warm up with 5–10 repetitions at 40%–60% of expected 1RM, followed by a recovery period of 1 min; then, perform 3–5 repetitions at 60%–80% of expected 1RM, followed by a recovery period of 2–3 min; continuous increase in weight to achieve 1RM; after 3–5 attempts, the 1RM should be found.9 If the 1RM has not been achieved after 5 attempts, 1RM will be calculated based on the highest weight that could be moved for a defined number of repetitions (eg, 3RM (three repetition maximum), formula according to the current guidelines).44 The test will be carried out on the training equipment itself.

Interventions

All participants will be submitted to four types of exercise training: IMT, IWS, DLP and IHG. Before all exercise interventions, a specific warm-up will be performed for each training: IMT, DLP and IHG, at 10% of maximum tests; and IWS, participants will assume both positions (135° and 115°) so that foot and wall marks can be set for the training. Following the warm-up, a recovery time of at least 5 min—or until baseline values are reached—will be provided before the training. The number of breaths will be counted during each set/bout. Washout period between each exercise training modality performed in the same day will be 30 min or until key baseline values (SBP, DBP, MAP, PR, RPE) are reached again. Washout period between the intervention days will be 1 to 2 days. For each exercise training, there will be two modalities with different intensities, as described below (figure 2).

Day 2 – IMT and IWS

IMT

Participants will be submitted to the IMT modalities, based on the recommendations for RT.44 Each participant will be asked to perform two different modalities of IMT: 3 sets of 15 repetitions at 40% MIP (IMTLow) vs 3 sets of 8 repetitions at 80% MIP (IMTHigh). IMT will be done with a resistive loading device (Powerbreathe KH2, POWERbreathe International Ltd, Southam, UK). In each IMT repetition, all participants will be encouraged to take a fast and deep inspiration, followed by a slow and deep expiration, in sitting position with knees flexed at 90° and nose clip. After the completion of each set, they will be instructed to take the device out and rest for 90 s until the next set. All participants will perform IMT using the same device, but connected to an individual bacteriological filter, to minimise the risk of infections.

IWS

Participants will be submitted to the IWS modalities, based on the stages proposed by Wiles et al. Each participant will be asked to perform 2 bouts of IWS with a minimal target duration of 60 s (between 60 and 90 s): at 135o (IWSLow) vs 115o (IWSMod). Since, to our knowledge, no prior studies have examined IWS in CAD patients, we selected knee angles representing low (135°) and moderate (115°) intensities to safely initiate investigations in this population. Although Wiles et al55 employed IWS training sessions lasting 2 min, we chose a shorter duration of 60 to 90 s. This decision is based on evidence that the greatest haemodynamic responses occur within this timeframe in healthy individuals. Additionally, CAD patients who are not regularly trained are unlikely to tolerate durations exceeding 90 s due to muscular limitations.

Participants will be required to rest their back against a fixed wall with their feet parallel, shoulder width apart and their hands by their side. Participants will be instructed to lower their back down a solid wall and make small adjustments to their feet position until the required knee joint angle is reached. Knee joint angle will be measured using a modified clinical goniometer and determine the exercise intensity. After the completion of each boat, they will have a rest period of 2 min until the next boat. Verbal encouragement will be given and participants will be informed of the elapsed time. Participants will be instructed to breathe normally to avoid the Valsalva manoeuvres.

Day 3 – DLP and IHG

DLP

Participants will be placed in a semi-recumbent position in the leg press device (Cybex, Germany) and will be instructed to perform two different modalities of RT: 3 sets of 15 repetitions at 40% RM (DLPLow) vs 3 sets of 8 repetitions at 80% 1RM (DLPHigh).44 All sets will be performed at fast speed repetition (concentric and eccentric phases in 1.5 s each), with a rest period of 90 s until the next set.20 To avoid Valsalva manoeuvre, the participants will be asked to exhale on the concentric phase and inhale on the eccentric phase.56

IHG

Each participant will be asked to perform two different modalities of IHG with the right hand: 2 bouts of IHG with a minimal target duration of 60 s (between 60 and 90 s) at 30% HGS (IHGLow) vs 2 bouts of IHG with a minimal target duration of 40 s (between 40 and 60 s) at 60% HGS (IHGMod). Once to our knowledge there is no previous study about IHG at moderate to high intensity in patients with CAD, we choose the loads, according to Javidi et al,57 in which no adverse effects were observed during IHG, at 30% and 60% intensities, in hypertensive patients. Despite the use of IHG lasting 4 sets of 2 min (60% HGS) and 8 sets of 30 s (60% HGS),57 we opted for only 2 bouts with a duration of at least 60 s and maximum 90 s (30% HGS) and at least 40 s and maximum 60 s (60% HGS), to try to guarantee that participants will be able to achieve the goal in a safe way, once the most common IHG in cardiac patients involves 30% HGS during 1–3 min.58 59 Participants will be asked to apply force on the device with position of shoulder in adduction and neutrally rotated and the elbow at 90° flexion. Participants will be instructed to visually control the HGS, targeting the specific load (30% or 60% HGS). After the completion of each boat, they will have a rest period of 2 min until the next boat. Verbal encouragement will be given and participants will be informed of the elapsed time. Participants will be instructed to breathe normally to avoid the Valsalva manoeuvres.

To ensure participant safety, the exercise interventions (IMT, IWS, DLP and IHG) will be immediately discontinued if any of the following criteria are observed: the onset of inappropriate tachycardia, signs of respiratory distress or any other clinically relevant abnormalities. Continuous monitoring will be performed throughout each session to promptly identify these conditions and prevent potential adverse events.

Statistical analysis plan

Data collection plan

The data from baseline assessment and punctually outcome assessments will be collected in specific forms. Continuous outcome assessments will be collected via download of excel sheet from HemoSphere Monitor (Edwards Lifesciences LLC, Irvine, CA, USA).

Data management

The collected patient data are processed pseudonymously, with patient identifying information being replaced with a unique participant identification. All data are linked by the unique participant ID. Only the instructed study team has access to the pseudonymised data. The original documents containing personal data and decoding file will be stored in a lockable cupboard in the study director’s office. The publication of study results always takes place without naming patients’ data such as name, address, etc. A possible subsequent secondary analysis only takes place in sports therapy questions. A possible joint analysis of the data with other medical and scientific partners only takes place under pseudonymised or anonymised conditions. Unmasking data will not be passed on at any time.

On the basis of the research grant received, Edwards Lifescience LLC has the right to receive a copy of the research data in aggregated form in a timely manner after research completion, without any personal identifiers of the research subjects. Edwards must be afforded an opportunity to use the research data after publication or with research organisation’s consent, which shall not be unreasonably withheld, except that any use or disclosure of data, as defined in the European Union and/or local data protection regulation, will be subject to the authorisation provided by the research subject in the informed consent or other authorisation document. Patients are informed about this and asked for their written consent.

Statistical analysis

Demographic, medical history and other baseline characteristics will be analysed for all participants. Continuous variables will be expressed as mean±SD if data are normally distributed; otherwise, data will be reported as the median and IQR (25th and 75th percentile). Categorical variables will be summarised using frequencies and percentages.

To compare changes in primary and secondary outcomes between the different intensity modalities (low vs moderate-to-high) of each exercise training type (IMT, IWS, DLP and IHG), continuous variables will be analysed using either the parametric Student’s t-test or the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be presented as absolute frequencies and percentages and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. For comparisons involving more than two groups, appropriate statistical methods such as a two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) will be applied, depending on data distribution and homogeneity of variances. A p value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation or other appropriate statistical methods.

Monitoring

Composition of data monitoring committee is not needed.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research. Patient satisfaction and feasibility (in terms of accessibility, compliance, training load and safety) of the exercise training modalities have been evaluated in four pilots (three healthy individuals and one CAD patient) (data not published). All of them reported difficulty in performing exercise training in fast speed (1 s concentric and 1 s eccentric), favouring the choice of 1.5 s. No adverse events occurred during the pilots. Each patient who completes the study will receive a printed report on day 3, following the final assessments. This report will include details on baseline haemodynamic variables, maximum strength test results and the training modalities performed. These data can be used to adapt or validate current training recommendations. In addition, all study participants will receive a publication report of each manuscript published by email with the results found.

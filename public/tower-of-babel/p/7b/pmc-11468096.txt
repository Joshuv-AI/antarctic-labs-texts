The outcome of advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients treated with first-line platinum and paclitaxel with or without indication for immune checkpoint inhibitors: the comparative study
Authors: Lan Feng, Qun Shi, Shujuan Wang, Ye Zhao, Haiyan Wu, Lei Wei, Qing Hao, Zhaojun Cui, Lin Wang, Jing Zhang, Dan Zhang, Xinxin Zhan, Jingwen Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveImmune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy activates the immune system to recognize and eliminate cancer cells that have escaped surveillance. This study aimed to compare the treatment outcome of advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients treated with first-line platinum and paclitaxel with or without ICI.MethodsData from 69 advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients treated with first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel (N = 33) or first-line platinum and paclitaxel (N = 36) were reviewed between March 2020 and January 2023 in this retrospective study. Patients chose treatment based on the actual disease condition, patient willingness, and medical advice. Additionally, objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) were calculated, and adverse events were gained.ResultsThere was no difference in baseline data between patients receiving the two different treatments (all P > 0.05). Complete response rate (18.2% vs. 8.3%; P = 0.294), ORR (48.5% vs. 30.6%; P = 0.127), and DCR (81.8% vs. 72.2%; P = 0.345) tended to ascend in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel compared to those treated with platinum and paclitaxel, although there was no statistical significance. In patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, the median PFS was 10.3 months and the median OS was not reached. Meanwhile, the median PFS and OS were 7.7 and 16.9 months in patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel. PFS (P = 0.036) and OS (P = 0.033) were increased in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel versus those treated with platinum and paclitaxel, which was verified by multivariate Cox regression analyses (both P < 0.05). No difference was observed in the occurrence of adverse events between patients receiving the two different treatments (all P > 0.05).ConclusionFirst-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel yields better treatment responses, longer survival, and non-differential adverse events versus first-line platinum and paclitaxel in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-12989-x.

Background

Cervical cancer is the fourth most frequent cancer among females next only to breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and lung cancer in the world [1, 2]. In 2020, there are 109,741 new cervical cancer cases and 59,060 cervical cancer-caused deaths in China [3]. Notably, most cervical cancer patients with early-stage disease achieve favorable survival through surgery or radiotherapy [2]. However, the median overall survival (OS) is only 8.0-16.8 months in advanced cervical cancer patients [4–6]. Platinum-based chemotherapy is the current standard first-line treatment for advanced cervical cancer patients; nevertheless, its treatment efficacy remains inadequate [7]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to search for other first-line treatments to improve the prognosis of advanced cervical cancer patients.

Immunotherapy is a novel treatment strategy that aims to boost or trigger patients’ immune systems to eliminate tumor cells [6]. Notably, immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy is considered a promising treatment that reactivates the immune system to inhibit escape from immune surveillance, thereby killing tumor cells [8–10]. Previous studies have revealed that first-line ICI plus platinum-based chemotherapy shows good efficacy in advanced cervical cancer patients [11–13]. For example, one study suggests that first-line pembrolizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy versus first-line placebo plus platinum-based chemotherapy yields an increased progression-free survival (PFS) and OS in patients with persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer who have positive programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 1 [11]. Another study reveals that first-line camrelizumab plus albumin-binding paclitaxel and carboplatin achieves good treatment response with a good tolerance in patients with metastatic or recurrent cervical cancer [12]. In addition, one research illustrates that first-line pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy exhibits favorable treatment response and survival with controllable safety in patients with persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer [13]. However, these studies all have their respective shortcomings. For example, the KEYNOTE826 study only assessed pembrolizumab and did not assess the efficacy and safety of other types of ICI treatment [11]; other studies had a small sample size [12, 13] or did not include a control group [12]. Thus, the value of first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel in advanced cervical cancer patients should be further verified.

Therefore, this study evaluated three types of ICI treatment (pembrolizumab, camrelizumab, and sintilimab), aiming to assess the efficacy and safety of first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel in comparison with first-line platinum and paclitaxel in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study consecutively enrolled 69 advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients who received first-line treatment with platinum and paclitaxel (36 patients) or ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel (33 patients) between March 2020 and January 2023. The inclusion criteria were: (1) aged ≥ 18 years; (2) pathologically or histologically confirmed as persistent or recurrent cervical cancer, or metastatic cervical cancer [11]; (3) the eastern cooperative oncology group performance status (ECOG PS) score < 2; (4) had complete and accessible clinical data; (5) had at least one follow-up data. The exclusion criteria were: (1) had other malignant diseases; (2) had severe heart, renal, lung, or coagulation disorders; (3) had an active or a history of autoimmune disease. The study gained the approval of the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Xingtai Medical College (approval number: 202309). All patients or their families signed an informed consent form.

Data collection and treatment

The clinical characteristics and treatment information of the patients were obtained from the hospital’s electronic medical information base. The patients used either platinum and paclitaxel or ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel as first-line treatment based on the actual disease condition, patient willingness, and medical advice, which was not intervened by the study. Patients who received platinum and paclitaxel without ICI for PD-L1 CPS negative. Patients selected ICI drugs based on the actual disease condition, patient willingness, and medical advice, which was not intervened by the study. The specific ICI drugs were: pembrolizumab (200 mg/cycle), camrelizumab (200 mg/cycle), or sintilimab (200 mg/cycle). The dose was adjusted based on the actual situation of the patient. The treatment cycle of ICI was three weeks, and ICI was used continuously until the patient’s condition progressed, the patient was intolerant, or the patient died. The specific chemotherapy was: (1) platinum: cisplatin (50 mg/m2/cycle), carboplatin (AUC = 5–6 /cycle); (2) paclitaxel: 175 mg/m2/cycle. The dose was adjusted based on the actual situation of the patient. The treatment cycle of chemotherapy was three weeks, and chemotherapy was recommended to last for 6 cycles, which could be adjusted appropriately depending on the actual situation of the patient. Patients were not limited to a combination with bevacizumab. The dose of bevacizumab was 15 mg/kg/cycle. The dose was adjusted based on the actual situation of the patient. The duration and specific administration of bevacizumab was documented.

Assessment

Imaging assessment information of the patient was obtained from the hospital’s medical electronic information base. Imaging assessments were performed every 3 cycles (about 2 months) at the first year and every 3 months after the first year. The best response of the patients was evaluated based on the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v.1.1. The ORR and the disease control rate (DCR) of the patients were calculated. ORR was calculated as complete response (CR) rate plus partial response (PR) rate. DCR was calculated as CR rate and PR rate plus stable disease (SD) rate.

Follow-up information of the patient was obtained from the hospital’s medical electronic information base. The follow-up range was 5.3–27.2 months. The median follow-up duration was 15.2 months. PFS and OS of the patients were calculated. PFS was defined as the duration from treatment to disease progression or death. OS was defined as the duration from treatment to death. The adverse events that occurred in the patients were also collected, which were assessed according to CTCAE 5.0. Moreover, the monitoring of ICI adverse events was according to National Comprehensive Cancer Network Guidelines [14].

Data analysis

The software applied for the statistical analysis of the data was SPSS 21.0 (IBM Corp., USA). Comparisons between patients were conducted using the t, Mann-Whitney U, Chi-square, and Fisher’s exact tests. Kaplan-Meier curves were used to compare survival between the patients with different treatments, and the method of testing was the log-rank test. Forest plots demonstrated the relationship of factors with PFS or OS by univariable and multivariate Cox regression analyses. In this case, the multivariate Cox regression analysis screened out the factors with a P-value less than 0.05 in the univariate Cox regression analysis, and the “enter” regression method was used. P-values < 0.05 were considered significantly different.

Results

Study flow

Among the included advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients, 33 patients were treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, and 36 patients were treated with platinum and paclitaxel. Next, the clinical characteristics, imaging assessment information, follow-up information, and adverse events were collected in all included patients. Furthermore, best response [complete response (CR), partial response, stable disease, as well as progressive disease], ORR, DCR, PFS, OS, and adverse events were also evaluated (Fig. 1).

Clinical traits of advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

The mean age of 33 patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel was 50.6 ± 8.8 years, and the mean age of 36 patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel was 52.2 ± 8.8 years. There was no discrepancy in clinical traits between patients receiving the two different treatments (all P > 0.05). In patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, 33 (100.0%) patients had PD-L1 CPS positive and 15 (45.5%) patients had PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10. In patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel, all patients had PD-L1 CPS negative. Regarding ICI type, there were 17 (51.5%), 10 (30.3%), and 6 (18.2%) patients who received pembrolizumab, camrelizumab, and sintilimab, respectively. More particular information was provided in Table 1. Moreover, there were 19 patients with persistent or recurrent cervical cancer in patients who were treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, and there were 18 patients with persistent or recurrent cervical cancer in patients who were treated with platinum and paclitaxel.

Best response, CR, ORR, and DCR in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

The best response did not differ between patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel and those treated with platinum and paclitaxel (P = 0.451) (Fig. 2A). What’s more, CR rate (P = 0.294) (Fig. 2B), ORR (P = 0.127) (Fig. 2C), and DCR (P = 0.345) (Fig. 2D) tended to increase in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel compared to those treated with platinum and paclitaxel, although they did not reach statistical significance.

PFS and OS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

The median PFS [95% confidence interval (CI)] was 10.3 (7.7–12.9) months in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, and 7.7 (4.9–10.5) months in patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel. Notably, PFS was ascended in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel compared to those treated with platinum and paclitaxel (P = 0.036) (Fig. 3A). Furthermore, the median OS was not reached in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, and the median (95% CI) OS was 16.9 (13.8–20.0) months in patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel. OS was also increased in patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel compared to those treated with platinum and paclitaxel (P = 0.033) (Fig. 3B).

Factors linked with PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

Univariable model revealed that treatment (ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel vs. platinum and paclitaxel) [hazard ratio (HR) = 0.571, P = 0.044] and bevacizumab combination (yes vs. no) (HR = 0.552, P = 0.036) were correlated with longer PFS, while ECOG PS (1 vs. 0) (HR = 1.856, P = 0.029) and lung metastasis (yes vs. no) (HR = 1.876, P = 0.027) forecasted poorer PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients (Fig. 4A).

Fig. 4Univariable and multivariate models for PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients. The predictors (A) and independent predictors (B) of PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

Univariable and multivariate models for PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients. The predictors (A) and independent predictors (B) of PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

The multivariate model found that treatment (ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel vs. platinum and paclitaxel) (HR = 0.405, P = 0.002) and bevacizumab combination (yes vs. no) (HR = 0.516, P = 0.030) independently predicted favorable PFS, but ECOG PS (1 vs.0) (HR = 2.373, P = 0.003) and lung metastasis (yes vs. no) (HR = 2.137, P = 0.013) independently estimated shortened PFS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients (Fig. 4B).

Factors related to OS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

According to the univariable model, treatment (ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel vs. platinum and paclitaxel) (HR = 0.428, P = 0.038) and bevacizumab combination (yes vs. no) (HR = 0.344, P = 0.015) were related to increased OS, while higher federation international of gynecology and obstetrics (FIGO) stage at initial diagnosis (HR = 1.472, P = 0.016) and lung metastasis (yes vs. no) (HR = 2.317, P = 0.030) were linked with shorter OS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients (Fig. 5A).

The multivariate model showed that treatment (ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel vs. platinum and paclitaxel) (HR = 0.222, P = 0.001) independently forecasted satisfactory OS, but higher FIGO stage at initial diagnosis (HR = 1.450, P = 0.035) independently predicted poorer OS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients (Fig. 5B).

Comparison of the treatment response and survival between patients with or without radical hysterectomy

There were 19 patients with radical hysterectomy among patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel, and there were 12 patients with radical hysterectomy among patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel. In patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel, treatment response did not vary (P = 0.088), while PFS (P = 0.041) and OS (P = 0.001) showed a difference between patients with and without radical hysterectomy. In patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, there was no difference in treatment response, PFS, and OS between patients with and without radical hysterectomy (all P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 1).

Adverse events in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients

Notably, there was no discrepancy in the occurrence of adverse events between patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel and those treated with platinum and paclitaxel (all P > 0.05). In patients treated with ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel, anemia (42.4%), leukopenia (33.3%), fatigue (30.3%), nausea and vomiting (30.3%), and diarrhea (27.3%) were the most frequent adverse events. Meanwhile, in patients treated with platinum and paclitaxel, the most frequent adverse events included anemia (36.1%), fatigue (27.8%), constipation (27.8%), leukopenia (25.0%), as well as nausea and vomiting (25.0%). Most adverse events were of grade 1–2 in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients (Table 2). No difference was found in the incidence of adverse events in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients treated with bevacizumab combined with or without ICI (all P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 2). In addition, there was no difference in the incidence of adverse events among advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients with different ICI drugs (all P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 3).

Discussion

The benefit of advanced cervical cancer patients from classical chemotherapy or radiation therapy is limited, so it is urgent to search for new therapy strategies to improve the management of those patients [15]. Immunotherapy, especially ICI, is considered an attractive strategy for advanced cervical cancer patients [10, 16]. In our study, the ORR and DCR in patients treated with first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel were 48.5% and 81.8%, respectively, which were elevated compared to those treated with first-line platinum and paclitaxel. The possible causes included: (1) ICI targeted programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), which promoted antitumor immune response and activated T cells to eliminate tumor cells [17]. (2) PD-1 inhibitors and chemotherapeutic drugs might have synergistic anti-tumor effects [18]. Therefore, advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients treated with first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel achieved an increased treatment response versus those treated with first-line platinum and paclitaxel. Furthermore, one previous study shows that first-line camrelizumab plus carboplatin and albumin-binding paclitaxel exhibit good efficacy in patients with metastatic or recurrent cervical cancer, with an ORR of 40.0% and DCR of 92.0% [12]. The findings of this previous study were similar to those of our study, which suggested the effect of first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel was favorable [12]. Nevertheless, another study suggests that first-line pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy exhibits an ORR of 77.0% in patients with persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer [13], which was higher than our study. This might be due to the fact that: A substantial portion of patients received bevacizumab in addition to ICI plus chemotherapy, and the numerical proportion in this previous study was higher than that in our study (63.0% vs. 39.4%) [13]. Meanwhile, the effect of bevacizumab in addition to ICI plus chemotherapy might be superior to ICI plus chemotherapy in advanced cervical cancer patients [19]. Thus, the ORR of this previous study was higher than our study.

Our study also preliminarily showed that PFS and OS varied between patients treated with first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel in comparison with those treated with first-line platinum and paclitaxel. However, due to the difference in patient populations (difference in the result of PD-L1 CPS score), future studies were needed to further obtain a solid conclusion. Meanwhile, first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel independently predicted favorable PFS and OS in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients. These findings might be the results of: (1) ICI blocked checkpoint proteins that helped cancer cells escape immune surveillance, which re-enabled the immune system to attack and kill tumor cells [20, 21]. Thus, first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel versus first-line platinum and paclitaxel ascended the survival of advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients. (2) As the described content in our study, first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel yields a better treatment response versus first-line platinum and paclitaxel in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients, which might lead to increased PFS and OS of those patients.

In addition, the safety of first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel in treating advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients is also noteworthy. In our study, no discrepancy was observed in the incidences of adverse events between patients treated with first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel and those treated with first-line platinum and paclitaxel. The above finding suggested a good tolerance of first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel in treating advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients. Notably, it is reported that the application of ICI may cause some adverse events [22]. For example, one study reveals that advanced cervical cancer patients who receive pembrolizumab experience a series of adverse events, such as rash, pyrexia, fatigue, neutropenia, diarrhea, and anemia [23]. Another study also shows that hypothyroidism, decreased appetite, and fatigue are common adverse events in advanced cervical cancer patients who undergo pembrolizumab monotherapy [24]. These adverse events were partly similar to the adverse events in our study. In our study, anemia, leukopenia, fatigue, nausea and vomiting, and diarrhea were the most frequent adverse events in patients treated with first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel. In terms of the severity of adverse events, most of them were of grade 1–2. Meanwhile, there was no new adverse event.

There existed some limitations in our study: (1) Our study reviewed as many patients as possible who met the inclusion criteria and did not meet the exclusion criteria. However, the sample size of our study was still small. Further studies should consider enlarging the sample size to avoid insufficient statistical effects of the results. (2) Our study had a relatively short follow-up period and the survival benefits of first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel could not be fully assessed in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients. (3) Our study was a retrospective study, and prospective, randomized, blind studies were needed for further verification. (4) Due to the fact that the initial treatment of some enrolled patients was not carried out in our hospital, we were unable to trace back the specific method and quality of hysterectomy, as well as the concurrent chemoradiation information in these patients. These factors might influence the results of our study to some extent. (5) In our study, the cohort might be both too heterogeneous and small to provide reliable evidence justifying the use of ICI. Thus, future studies should consider creating a more homogeneous cohort for verification. (6) Our study did not specifically evaluate adverse events caused by ICI or bevacizumab, and future studies should consider this issue for further verification. (7) Our study did not specifically analyze the reasons for adverse events. Future studies should specify adverse events caused by ICI or bevacizumab.

Conclusions

In conclusion, first-line ICI plus platinum and paclitaxel achieves more favorable treatment responses, longer survival, and comparable adverse events especially in patients with PD-L1 CPS positive compared to first-line platinum and paclitaxel in advanced and recurrent cervical cancer patients.

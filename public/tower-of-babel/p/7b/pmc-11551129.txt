Disparities in Kidney Care: Empowering Patients With Data
Authors: Susan Lyon, Barnaby Hole
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

In 2018, Kidney Research UK called for better data describing the barriers to high-quality kidney care faced by people from different age, sex, socioeconomic, and ethnic groups.1 As patients and carers, the UK Patient Council members have direct experience of these barriers, and one of our roles is to work with the UK Kidney Association to produce outputs designed to reduce inequalities in kidney care and improve outcomes for our fellow patients.

Clearly identifying the disparities in health and health care that reflect inequitable treatment is vital; however, the last UK Renal Registry stand-alone description of inequalities in kidney health was published in 2013,2 as a scientific report. Patient Council members have worked with researchers from the UK Renal Registry to produce 4 new reports, using data on the over 70,000 people receiving care across adult and pediatric UK kidney centers. Each report examines a characteristic that may be associated with disparities in health or care: ethnicity, age, male/female sex as assigned at birth, and socioeconomic deprivation. These reports are available on the UK Kidney Association website (https://ukkidney.org/audit-research/disparities-report). In Figure 1 and the Supplementary Table S1, and Figures S1 and S2, we show how readers can compare groups. In publishing these open-access reports, our aim was to produce up-to-date information not as a scientific report, but in a format that would be useful for and readable by patients, family carers, the public, media, charities, and clinicians alike. Our principal aim is to identify and direct action toward inequities in kidney health, where kidney patients themselves will need to drive progress as has been the case in other areas, such as the disability rights movement.3

Persisting barriers to high-quality kidney care have been freshly highlighted by Kidney Research UK.4 Our reports will be valuable in enabling policy makers and health care commissioners to understand the need for improvement. By sharing data with patients, kidney health professionals and patient organizations, we will support them in campaigning for and promoting the changes that will make kidney care more equitable.

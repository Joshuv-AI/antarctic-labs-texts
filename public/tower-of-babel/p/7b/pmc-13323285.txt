Assessment of trace metal contamination in mangrove sediments—a case study from Guadeloupe Island, Caribbean Sea
Authors: Edlyne Colletin, Mélissa Barrast, Olivier Gros, Anniet M. Laverman, Séverine Ferdinand, Maïlie Saint-Hilaire
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Mangroves provide essential ecosystem services, such as shoreline protection, carbon sequestration, and biodiversity conservation. However, these ecosystems face increasing threats from anthropogenic activities, particularly trace metal pollution, which tends to accumulate in sediments. While several studies have investigated trace metal contamination in tropical mangroves worldwide, data from Lesser Antilles remain limited. This study provides the first comprehensive assessment of trace metal concentrations (Al, As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Ni, Pb, Zn) in mangrove sediments across 10 sites in Guadeloupe, covering both environmentally conserved and anthropogenically impacted environments. Results indicate overall low to moderate contamination, consistent with national reference values in conserved sites. In contrast, anthropogenically influenced sites exhibited higher contamination levels and revealed a clear gradient between site types. Localized hotspots of contamination were identified in anthropogenically impacted sites, with high concentrations of Cu, Zn, and As. Notably, Hg and Cd were quantified at very low levels and only at one site. Multivariate analyses further highlighted distinct site groupings and suggested both natural and anthropogenic sources of contamination. These findings provide new insights into the spatial distribution of trace metals in tropical insular mangroves, underscore the influence of human activities on these vulnerable ecosystems, and support future environmental monitoring and risk assessment efforts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10661-026-15600-x.

Introduction

Mangroves provide crucial ecosystem services in tropical coastal environments, including shoreline protection against erosion, carbon sequestration, support for the reproductive cycles of aquatic species and biodiversity conservation (Yan et al., 2017). However, these ecosystems are increasingly vulnerable to anthropogenic pressures, particularly pollution from microplastics, pesticides, persistent organic pollutants (POPs), and trace metals (Al-Tarshi et al., 2024; Dromard et al., 2018; Pescatore et al., 2025). These contaminants originate from agricultural runoff, urban activities, and clinical waste, which can enter coastal ecosystems without treatment (Algül & Beyhan, 2020; Chatterjee et al., 2007). Due to their ability to retain various contaminants, including trace metals, sediments serve as a major reservoir in aquatic ecosystems (Yan et al., 2017). In recent years, marine pollution by trace metals has emerged as a global concern, with increasing concentrations detected in various marine ecosystems around the world (Zhang et al., 2020).

In the Caribbean region, the most frequently detected trace metals are aluminum (Al), lead (Pb), copper (Cu), iron (Fe), manganese (Mn), zinc (Zn), nickel (Ni), cadmium (Cd), chromium (Cr), arsenic (As) and mercury (Hg) (Bernard, 1995; Fernandez et al., 2007; Jaffé et al., 2003). Due to their toxicity and persistence, these trace metals pose significant risks to biodiversity within these environments (Yan et al., 2017).

In Guadeloupe (Lesser Antilles), mangroves cover 34 km2, representing about 22% of the archipelago’s total area and serve as critical habitats for biodiversity, shoreline protection and nutrient cycling (Vaslet et al., 2013). While previous studies have reported generally low concentrations in trace metals, high levels have been detected in coastal sediments from lagoons (Bernard, 1995). In Guadeloupe, high trace metal concentrations are strongly associated with domestic wastewater discharges (Bernard, 1995; Castaing et al., 1986). Whereas Guadeloupean marine mangrove sediments have been studied for contaminants such as pesticides, particularly chlordecone, as well as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) (Bernard et al., 1995; Dromard et al., 2018; Ramdine et al., 2012), data on trace metal contamination generally analyzed only a small subset of trace metals and are limited to studies conducted in sediments from bays (Castaing et al., 1986), seagrass beds, maritime harbors, and lagoons (Bernard, 1995; Bouchon et al., 2016).

This study aims to assess trace metal contamination in mangrove sediments across Guadeloupe by comparing environmentally conserved and anthropogenically impacted sites. It provides new insights into the spatial distribution of trace metals and the influence of human activities on this coastal ecosystem.

Materials and methods

Study area and sediment sampling

Mangrove ecosystems develop in low-energy coastal environments where thick particle deposits accumulate above the bedrock several meters under the sediment surface. Mangrove sediments are predominantly composed of fine-grained particles (silts and clays) originating from river inputs, coastal sediment transport, and in situ organic matter production (Gontharet et al., 2017). Their geochemistry is therefore mainly influenced by organic matter production, redox conditions, and sedimentation processes rather than directly by the underlying bedrock geology. However, Guadeloupe belongs to the Lesser Antilles volcanic arc, formed by the subduction of the American plates beneath the Caribbean plate (Ricci et al., 2017; Verati et al., 2018). The archipelago is characterized by contrasted geological settings, with volcanic formations dominating Basse-Terre and carbonate platforms in Grande-Terre. Volcanic rocks from Basse-Terre are mainly calc-alkaline andesites and basaltic-andesites associated with magmatic and hydrothermal processes (Favier et al., 2021; Ricci et al., 2017). Based on these environmental and geological characteristics, sediment sampling was conducted across mangrove sites subjected to different levels of anthropogenic pressure. A total of 20 sediment samples from 10 sites (Table 1 and Fig. 1) were collected between March and June 2024, coinciding with the dry season. Surface sediment samples (0–10 cm depth) were collected using a manual auger from mangrove areas in Guadeloupe. Samples were taken 5 to 10 m from the shoreline, in direct contact with the roots of Rhizophora mangle, across the selected sites (Fig. 1). Anthropogenically impacted sites were selected according to their distance (< 500 m) to known sources of industrial activities (e.g., energy production and fuel storage facilities, chemical and material storage sites, and agro-industrial installations), as well as urbanized areas and wastewater discharges (Table 1). Environmentally conserved sites were defined using a combination of criteria, including their location within protected areas designated as natural reserve, their greater distance from identified sources of pollution, and the absence of obvious human disturbances. Six anthropogenically impacted sites and four environmentally conserved sites were selected in the bays of Grand Cul-de-Sac Marin and Petit Cul-de-Sac Marin and the surrounding municipalities of Guadeloupe. Collection in the marine protected area was done in accordance with permit No. 2025-026. At several sites, two samples were collected at different dates from the same sampling point. At the Le Moule site, five samples were collected from five different sampling points (sub-sites) to assess the local impact of the wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) on trace metal accumulation. After collection, samples were transferred into sterile containers, transported under refrigerated conditions (4 °C) and then homogenized and stored at − 20 °C until analysis. Chemical reagents, sample preparation, and analysis

Details of chemical reagents are provided in the Supplementary Material. Twenty-five grams of wet sediment samples were dried in an oven (Memmert™, Schwabach, Germany) at 105 °C for 48 h until a constant weight. To prevent moisture absorption, the dried sediments were placed in a desiccator and ground into a fine, homogeneous powder using a ceramic mortar and pestle. The resulting powders were sifted through a 2 mm sieve and stored in amber glass bottles. One hundred milligrams of dried surface sediment sample was digested in 8 mL aqua regia for 6 h at 115 °C. Sample digestion was carried out using a Multicube 48 hot block digestion system (Anton Paar, France). The volume of cooled samples was adjusted to 50 mL with ultrapure water, then centrifuged for 10 min at 4000 rpm. Sediment pellets were formed using a Rottina 380 R centrifuge (Hettich, Hamburg, Germany). The supernatant was diluted with ultrapure water to prevent system saturation and analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (iCAP™ RQ ICP-MS, Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States) to determine the trace metal concentrations (Al, As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Ni, Pb, Zn). Concentrations are expressed in mg/kg dry weight (dw). Instrumental parameters are detailed in the Supplementary Material. It should be noted that aqua regia digestion provides pseudo-total (aqua regia-extractable) metal concentrations rather than total concentrations, as refractory mineral phases are not completely dissolved.

Quality controls in analysis

Quality assurance during the analysis was maintained by the routine inclusion of two proficiency tests (PTs) in each batch: marine sediment (14–0638-0064, BIPEA, France) and river sediment (15–0438-0009, BIPEA, France) provided by BIPEA, a certified organization providing proficiency testing schemes in accordance with ISO/IEC 17043. Accuracy was determined by comparing the measured concentrations with the assigned value of the PTs (n = 5). For all metals, accuracy ranged between 80 and 120%, meeting the acceptable limits of the PTs. Detailed results of the proficiency tests are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

In addition to the PTs, each analytical series included the preparation of a blank sample under the same conditions as the sediments, with the addition of aqua regia. The blank had to be below Limit of Quantification (LOQ)/2 according to the laboratory’s specifications. For each series, quality controls were also performed using doped solutions at concentrations (1, 5, 50, 250 mg/kg dw) corresponding to the various LOQs, prepared under the same conditions as the sediment samples. Internal standards were also used. The recovery rate of internal standards (45Sc, 103Rh, 193Ir) for control solutions was required to range between 70 and 120%.

The linearity of the method for the 11 metals was demonstrated in accordance with the T90-210 specifications. The calibration function was studied using the maximum acceptable deviation approach with five calibration ranges under intermediate accuracy conditions and five concentration levels per range. Linearity was demonstrated for all 11 metals studied.

The LOQ was determined using spiking solutions at different concentrations targeted for each metal. A total of 12 spiking tests per metal were carried out under intermediate accuracy conditions in accordance with the T90-210 norm. By convention, a maximum acceptable deviation of 60% was set at the LOQ. Using this validation at the LOQ level, the laboratory was able to determine the uncertainty (k = 2) for each metal in accordance with the NF ISO 11352 norm. This uncertainty was calculated using the relative standard uncertainties of repeatability and bias. Performance parameters, including LOQ, uncertainty, linearity, repeatability, and intermediate precision, are summarized in Supplementary Table S2. The repeatability and the intermediate precision were less than or equal to 20%, in accordance with the laboratory’s specifications.

Sediment quality guidelines

The trace metal concentrations were compared to the French regulatory reference values (N1 and N2), proposed by the group GÉODE1 and formalized by the French Ministerial Order (JORF n°0162 of July, Acte juridique No. 2020) (Table 2). These thresholds are used to assess environmental risk. Sediments with concentrations below the N1 level are classified as “noncontaminated.” Concentrations between the N1 and N2 levels classify sediments as “contaminated”, while levels exceeding N2 classify as “highly contaminated”; such sediments should not be discharged into the aquatic environment (De Santana et al., 2021; Hurel et al., 2017).

Statistical analysis

A Mann–Whitney test was applied due to the non-normal distribution of the data using GraphPad Prism version 10.2.3. Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to assess the relationship between two trace metals, with p < 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed based on trace metal concentrations to explore associations among metals and to visualize spatial differentiation of the sampling sites according to their contamination profiles. Missing values corresponded to concentrations below the limit of detection (LOD) and were treated as a missing dataset. A regularized iterative PCA algorithm was used for imputation to enable complete multivariate analysis while preserving the structure of the dataset. Given that the proportion of values below LOD exceeded 10% of the total dataset, simple substitution methods (e.g., LOD/2) were not considered appropriate due to the risk of introducing bias. Therefore, an iterative PCA-based imputation was preferred as a more robust approach that accounts for the multivariate structure of the data. Cd and Hg were excluded from multivariate analyses due to the very high proportion of values below LOD, resulting in insufficient variability for reliable statistical interpretation. The suitability of the data for PCA was assessed using Bartlett’s test of sphericity (p < 0.001) and the Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO > 0.6) measure of sampling adequacy. Hierarchical cluster analysis (HCA) was further performed on trace metal concentrations to examine the similarity between sites based on their contamination profiles. The data were standardized to place all variables on the same scale and prevent metals with higher concentrations from dominating the distance calculations. Euclidean distance was used as the dissimilarity measure, and the Ward linkage method (Ward.D2) was applied to construct the dendrogram.

Results

Trace metal concentrations in mangrove sediments

Metal concentrations varied depending on the element and across all sites, with Fe (2,877–71,257 mg/kg dw), Al (3,581–49,152 mg/kg dw), being the major elements of the sediments. The concentration of trace metals was: Zn (< 50–775 mg/kg dw), Mn (58.2–356 mg/kg dw), Cu (< 5–206 mg/kg dw), Pb (< 5–72.6 mg/kg dw), Cr (< 5–35.4 mg/kg dw), As (2.63–53.8 mg/kg dw), Ni (< 5–23.6 mg/kg dw), Cd (< 1–1.10 mg/kg dw), and Hg (< 1–1.00 mg/kg dw). Discussion

Trace metal concentrations in mangrove sediments

Overall, trace metal contamination in mangrove sediments ranged from low to moderate levels, with clear differences between environmentally conserved and anthropogenically impacted sites. This spatial gradient highlights the influence of human activities on sediment contamination.

In this context, several sites showed trace metal concentrations exceeding regulatory thresholds, indicating the possible presence of localized contamination sources. These exceedances involved Zn and Cu, and to a lesser extent As and Hg. Overall high Cu levels observed in five out of six anthropogenically impacted sites were also detected 30 years ago in lagoon sediments (Bernard, 1995) and more recently in seagrass beds within the Grand Cul-de-Sac Marin (Bouchon et al., 2016). Despite spatial differences in the sampling sites, this comparison provides insight into the persistence of Cu contamination over time, showing a global trend toward higher contemporary levels due to accumulation in sediments. As the Petit Cul-de-Sac Marin concentrates a large number of industrial activities (Bouchon & Bouchon-Navaro, 2000), high Cu levels in mangrove sediments may result from multiple sources, including industrial discharges (Rodgers et al., 2020), and nautical activities, particularly the use of antifouling paints applied to ship hulls to prevent the proliferation of aquatic organisms (Direction de la Mer de Guadeloupe, 2023; Dorleon et al., 2025). In addition, the geochemistry of volcanic soils in Guadeloupe, which naturally contain relatively high levels of Cu (Gissol, 2011), is further exacerbated by soil erosion. A clear, decreasing gradient in average Cu concentrations was observed from the anthropogenically impacted site of La Gabarre toward the environmentally conserved sites of Fajou and Perrin (r = −0.98, p < 0.05). This trend likely reflects the influence of marine currents flowing northward through the Rivière Salée.

Pb concentrations remained below regulatory thresholds for sediments consistent with the naturally low Pb levels reported for soils in Guadeloupe and Martinique (Lesser Antilles), which range from 7 to 51 mg/kg dw in the top 30 cm and from 6 to 16 mg/kg dw at greater depths (https://www.notre-environnement.gouv.fr, accessed on 25 April 2025). Elevated Pb levels were reported in lagoon sediments from the Petit-Cul-de-Sac Marin, near the present-day sites of La Gabarre, Jarry, and Sablière, most likely due to soil formation from basaltic rocks in the region (Bernard, 1995). This decline could be related to the ban on leaded gasoline in January 2000, which significantly reduced Pb emissions, particularly in the road transport sector (Directive 98/70/EC of the European Parliament & of the Council, 1998). In addition, improved waste recycling could account for the recent decrease in lead (Pb) emissions from waste incineration.

Spatial patterns, contamination levels, and source attribution of trace metals

Pb generally showed weak correlations with other trace metals, except for a moderate positive correlation with Cr, potentially indicating diffuse or less site-specific Pb inputs. Overall, these correlation patterns support the hypothesis that some metals (e.g., Cu, Zn, Mn, Cr) share common anthropogenic or geochemical origins (Bai et al., 2023), whereas others (e.g., Pb, As) may reflect different sources or environmental processes (Mrunmayee Manjari & Janaki Ballav, 2025).

The PCA grouping and the HCA clusters of conserved sites reflect their relative geochemical homogeneity and limited exposure to anthropogenic inputs, in contrast to anthropogenically impacted sites, which were more dispersed and distant, reflecting multiple and more intense sources of contamination. The Morne Rouge site, showing As contamination, was strongly projected on this axis. Guadeloupe is part of the active Lesser Antilles volcanic arc and hosts several active and fossil hydrothermal systems, including the Soufrière volcanic complex and the Bouillante geothermal field (Allard et al., 2014; Favier et al., 2021). Although arsenic was not specifically investigated in these hydrothermal fluids, volcanic and hydrothermal environments are recognized as potential natural sources of As and associated trace elements in volcanic islands worldwide (McCleskey et al., 2022; Morales-Simfors & Bundschuh, 2022). Therefore, the geological and hydrothermal context of Guadeloupe may contribute to the natural background levels of As observed in sediments, in addition to anthropogenic inputs.

These results highlight the influence of local anthropogenic activities on sediment metal contamination and can guide monitoring and remediation efforts.

Environmentally conserved sites exhibited PLI values below unity (PLI = 0.85), indicating natural background conditions, whereas anthropogenically impacted sites showed PLI values exceeding unity (PLI = 2.64), classifying these sediments as contaminated by trace metals according to the criteria defined by (Tomlinson et al., 1980). These results are consistent with observations reported for other tropical environments characterized by limited industrial activity. For instance, PLI values ranging from 0.09 to 0.45 have been reported in the Meghna estuary in Bangladesh, confirming the absence of significant contamination (PLI < 1) (Siddique et al., 2021). In contrast, mangrove systems subjected to strong anthropogenic pressures, including industrial and agricultural activities and domestic waste inputs, such as the Isaka-Bundu mangrove swamps in Nigeria, display consistently elevated PLI values (> 1), reflecting significant trace metal contamination (Abd Elnabi et al., 2023).

Two contamination hotspots: La Gabarre and Le Moule

Zn concentrations observed at La Gabarre are consistent with previously reported elevated levels in nearby lagoon sediments (Bernard, 1995), suggesting the persistence of contamination in this area. Such contamination likely results from a combination of natural inputs related to the volcanic and geochemical background of Guadeloupean soils characterized by basaltic to andesitic compositions, active weathering processes, and hydrothermal alteration products rich in Fe-, Al- and Ti- bearing phases as well as human activities including industrial activities, atmospheric deposition, and maritime operations such as ship maintenance and harbor activities. In addition, the proximity of a landfill may contribute to Cu contamination through leaching processes, as trace metals can be released from solid waste and transported to adjacent aquatic environments.

These trace metals can migrate through the soil, contaminate groundwater and ultimately reach surface aquatic ecosystems, posing ecological risks (Gautam et al., 2016). Rainfall plays a critical role in these mechanisms; heavy precipitation can increase leachate production and enhance the mobility of metal pollutants (Adamcová et al., 2017). This is especially relevant in the present context, as samples from La Gabarre #1 and La Gabarre #2 were collected at different times, with the latter taken shortly after a heavy rainfall event. Such hydrological conditions may have triggered the release of trace metals into the water column, which could explain the lower concentrations measured in sediments at that time (Koukounari et al., 2020). This highlights the importance of a better understanding of the sources, mobility and accumulation patterns of these trace metals for effective environmental management in a context of climate change where rainfall events will be more frequent and intense.

At Le Moule (WWTP), contamination patterns suggest a strong influence of treated wastewater discharges. Effluents from wastewater treatment plants, even after treatment, may contain residual trace metals originating from urban, healthcare, and industrial sources (Buelow et al., 2020). The spatial distribution observed among the Le Moule sub-sites (A to E) supports this hypothesis, with higher concentrations detected at sites closest to the discharge point (Le Moule E) and progressively lower levels toward more distant sites (e.g., site A). This gradient indicates a localized input of contaminants from WWTP followed by dilution along the coastal continuum.

The detection of Cd and Hg at this site, in contrast to other locations where they remained below quantification limits, further supports the presence of site-specific inputs. These elements are commonly associated with anthropogenic activities such as boating, waste oil disposal, and dredging activities (Bouchon & Bouchon-Navaro, 2000; Rajkumar & Persad, 1994). In Guadeloupe, one recognized source of Cd is the input of waste oils into the marine environment (Bouchon & Bouchon-Navaro, 2000).

Overall, these site-specific contamination patterns highlight not only the local sources of trace metal inputs but also the need to consider their broader environmental fate and potential ecological and human health implications. The presence and accumulation of trace metals in mangrove sediments may also have broader environmental and human health implications. Mangroves act as long-term sinks for contaminants, but trace metals can become bioavailable under changing environmental conditions and enter aquatic food webs (Briffa et al., 2020). Chronic exposure to trace metals such as Cd, Hg, Pb, and As is associated with toxic effects on aquatic organisms and may represent a potential risk to human populations through seafood consumption and environmental exposure pathways (El-Kady & Abdel-Wahhab, 2018).

More broadly, trace metal concentrations in sediments reflect pollutant accumulation (Mîndrescu et al., 2022). However, climatic events may remobilize trace metals into the water column, particularly in more labile forms (Koukounari et al., 2020), increasing their bioavailability and potential toxicity to aquatic organisms (de Freitas et al., 2019). In addition to their direct environmental impact, trace metals may contribute to selective pressures on microbial communities, including the potential co-selection of antimicrobial resistance (Dickinson et al., 2019). However, this aspect remains beyond the scope of the present study and should be considered in future research.

This study has some limitations, including the use of pseudo-total digestion, which may lead to an underestimation of total trace metal concentrations, the absence of geochemical normalization using a reference element (e.g., Al or Fe) to account for grain size and mineralogical variability, and limited temporal sampling. Future work should include the assessment of dissolved trace metal fractions, integration of microbial community analyses, and long-term monitoring to better understand contamination dynamics and associated ecological risks in mangrove ecosystems.

Conclusion

This study contributes to knowledge and data on trace metal concentrations in coastal mangrove sediments at the land-sea continuum. Trace metal concentrations in marine mangrove sediments from environmentally conserved sites were found to comply with environmental reference values. The results identified low to moderate levels of trace metal contamination, notably two hotspots of multiple contamination in marine mangroves that require particular attention. These findings provide a scientific basis for environmental monitoring programs in Guadeloupe mangroves, particularly for the identification, assessment, and prioritization of contaminated hotspots. They also support environmental management and decision-making by local authorities by providing site-specific reference values for trace metal concentrations, which can serve as a baseline for future monitoring and environmental quality assessment in coastal ecosystems.

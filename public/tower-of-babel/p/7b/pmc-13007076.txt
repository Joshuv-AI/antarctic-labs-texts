Nationwide trends in AECOPD care in Italy before, during and after COVID-19: discrepancies between data sources and across regions
Authors: Emanuela Resta, Vitaliano Nicola Quaranta, Silvano Dragonieri, Preethymol Peter, Andrea Portacci, Silvio Tafuri, Paola Pierucci, Giovanna Elisiana Carpagnano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD) are a leading cause of hospital admissions, early mortality and readmissions. Italian data are outdated and fragmented, and the impact of COVID-19 on AECOPD outcomes remains unclear.MethodsWe conducted a nationwide descriptive analysis of aggregated administrative indicators derived from two institutional sources (SDO (Schede di Dimissione Ospedaliera) and AGENAS (Agenzia Nazionale per i Servizi Sanitari Regionali)), reporting national and regional trends in hospitalisations, 30-day postdischarge mortality and 30-day readmissions for AECOPD between 2015 and 2023. Data were compared across pre-COVID (2015–2019), COVID (2020–2021) and post-COVID (2022–2023) periods and stratified by region and health district. Analyses were descriptive and based on aggregated, standardised administrative indicators, with temporal and regional comparisons across pre-COVID, COVID and post-COVID periods.ResultsAECOPD hospitalisations declined by more than 45% during 2020–2021, with only partial recovery by 2023 (−24% vs 2019). Thirty-day postdischarge mortality rose sharply during the pandemic, peaking at 13.6% in 2021 (+47% vs 2019), before returning near baseline in 2023, though excess mortality persisted in several southern regions. Thirty-day readmission rates remained stable at 12%–14% across the study period. Importantly, SDO records captured only one quarter of cases identified by AGENAS, systematically underestimating the true hospitalisation burden.ConclusionsCOVID-19 profoundly disrupted AECOPD care in Italy, exposing entrenched regional inequities and critical limitations in administrative data. These findings call for modernisation of coding systems, strengthening of community-based respiratory care and implementation of structured discharge and follow-up pathways to ensure equity and resilience in COPD management.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is among the leading causes of hospital admissions due to recurrent exacerbations, with a major impact on healthcare expenditures.1 2 Hospitalisations for acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD) accelerate lung function decline, worsen quality of life, increase future exacerbation risk and elevate mortality.3 AECOPD ranks among the conditions with the highest rates of in-hospital death, 30-day mortality and early readmission, comparable to pneumonia and heart failure.4 Preventing early readmissions improves outcomes and reduces costs, thus becoming a global healthcare priority.57

Significant heterogeneity persists due to differences in healthcare systems, patient phenotypes, disease severity, primary care implementation, local guidelines and modifiable factors such as smoking prevalence, resulting in variable data across countries.8 Although several studies analysed hospitalisation rates, mortality and readmissions for AECOPD,3 9 10 its global burden remains incompletely defined.

A recent meta-analysis by Smit et al, including over 65 000 patients, confirmed the substantial burden and variability of AECOPD outcomes worldwide.8 Italian data are limited, outdated and mainly from small or monocentric cohorts.11 The FADOI Epidemiological Study Group (2014–2015) reported marked inter-regional variation in hospitalisation and mortality rates.12 Smit’s meta-analysis also found an estimated 30-day mortality of nearly 30% in Italian AECOPD cases, based on a small, severely ill sample.13

The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted healthcare systems, diverting resources and reducing access to non-COVID care.1416 Hospitalisations for acute conditions, including respiratory diseases, markedly declined,1719 likely worsening outcomes in chronic patients.20 21 Studies reported up to 70% reductions in AECOPD admissions across Europe,112225 confirmed by a meta-analysis including 13 studies from nine countries.26

Despite these findings, comprehensive nationwide analyses of AECOPD hospitalisations during and after the pandemic are lacking. Our recent Apulian study (44 623 cases, 2017–2022) showed a significant COVID-related impact on COPD mortality.11

Therefore, this study aims to do the following:

Evaluate how COVID-19 modified AECOPD hospitalisation, 30-day mortality and readmission.

While DRG 88 (In the Italian Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG) system, DRG 88 corresponds to Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease, grouping hospitalisations where COPD is coded as the principal discharge diagnosis) is commonly used to classify COPD-related admissions, it tends to underestimate the actual burden of AECOPD, as it captures only cases where COPD is coded as the primary diagnosis, often without spirometric confirmation (eg, FEV₁/FVC<70%), and excludes cases complicated by respiratory failure (typically coded as DRG 87). To overcome this limitation, AGENAS incorporates a broader and functionally enriched approach, identifying AECOPD-related admissions using a combination of ICD-9-CM codes:

This broader selection criteria allows for the identification of a more realistic population of patients hospitalised for AECOPD (see detailed coding framework in online supplement B). As previously demonstrated in a regional analysis from Apulia, this approach captured approximately 75 more cases compared with DRG-only classification methods.11

Study design and patient selection

This work is a nationwide descriptive commentary based on secondary analysis of aggregated administrative indicators produced by official institutional sources. No individual-level data were accessed or reanalysed. It investigates temporal and geographic changes in hospitalisations, 30-day postdischarge mortality and 30-day postdischarge readmissions for AECOPD in Italy, using administrative health data from two official institutional sources.

The outcome indicators analysed in this manuscript were produced within the framework of the Italian National Outcomes Programme (Programma Nazionale Esiti, PNE) coordinated by AGENAS. Indicators are derived from routinely collected hospital discharge data using standardised ICD-9-CM-based case definitions, population denominators from official census data and predefined risk-adjustment procedures. For postdischarge outcomes, hospital records are linked to mortality registries according to institutional protocols. The present work reports and interprets these aggregated indicators without performing additional data processing or reanalysis.

Study periods

To capture the evolution of AECOPD care delivery before, during and after the COVID-19 pandemic, we report aggregated indicators across three distinct timeframes, which were selected based on epidemiological and institutional relevance:

Pre-COVID period (1 January 2015–31 December 2019): this 5 year window served as the baseline reference for all three outcomes. It reflects the standard of care prior to any pandemic-related disruptions.

COVID period (1 January 2020–31 December 2021): these 2 years correspond to the first and second waves of the COVID-19 pandemic in Italy, including lockdown phases, healthcare system strain, resource reallocation and altered patient behaviours.

COVID period (1 January 2020–31 December 2021): these 2 years correspond to the first and second waves of the COVID-19 pandemic in Italy, including lockdown phases, healthcare system strain, resource reallocation and altered patient behaviours.

Post-COVID period (1 January 2022–31 December 2023): this biennium represents the immediate recovery phase, with partial normalisation of healthcare services and renewed focus on chronic disease management.

The stratification into pre-COVID, COVID and post-COVID periods was adopted to enhance interpretability and comparability of national indicators across major epidemiological phases. Given the aggregated nature of the data and the absence of longitudinal individual-level linkage, time was analysed as a categorical contextual variable rather than as a continuous covariate within a regression framework.

Hospitalisations were analysed across the entire 9 year period (2015–2023). However, due to availability constraints within the AGENAS outcome data sets, 30-day postdischarge mortality and 30-day readmission rates were analysed from 2018 to 2023, with consistent case definitions and outcome tracking methods applied across years.

These outcomes were selected because they represent complementary and clinically meaningful dimensions of AECOPD care. Hospitalisation rates quantify the overall burden of exacerbations on the healthcare system and reflect access to care, disease incidence and coding practices. Thirty-day postdischarge mortality is a well-established indicator of early vulnerability, quality of in-hospital management and continuity of care. Thirty-day readmission rates capture the effectiveness of discharge planning, follow-up pathways and community-based respiratory services. Together, these three outcomes allow for a comprehensive assessment of AECOPD care delivery across pre-, during- and post-COVID phases and enable the identification of system-wide gaps and regional disparities.

These outcomes correspond to the core indicators routinely used within the Italian National Outcomes Programme (PNE) to monitor quality, safety and continuity of care for AECOPD, and therefore directly inform the descriptive and comparative analyses reported in this study.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted on aggregated administrative indicators provided by AGENAS and the Italian Hospital Discharge Database (SDO). No individual-level or hospital-level raw data were accessed, handled or re-analysed by the investigators.

Descriptive statistics were used to summarise the aggregated indicators. Categorical variables are reported as absolute frequencies (n) and percentages (%), while continuous indicators are presented as medians with fifth–95th percentiles, in accordance with the distributional characteristics of population-level administrative data.

Outcome indicators, including hospitalisation rates, 30-day postdischarge mortality and 30-day readmission rates, were derived and risk-adjusted according to the methodological framework of the Italian National Outcomes Programme (Programma Nazionale Esiti, PNE). For transparency, the variables included in the AGENAS risk-adjustment framework are formally defined below. Age was included as a continuous variable (expressed in years), while sex was included as a binary categorical variable (male/female). The calendar year was treated as a categorical contextual variable, and geographical area (region and health district) was included as a categorical variable for stratified comparisons.

Length of hospital stay was included as a continuous variable (expressed in days). Diagnosis type was modelled as a categorical variable, distinguishing hospitalisations with COPD coded as the primary diagnosis from those with acute respiratory failure or respiratory symptoms as the primary diagnosis and COPD coded as a secondary diagnosis. Major comorbidities were defined a priori according to the Italian National Outcomes Programme (PNE) methodology and identified using ICD-9-CM codes routinely available in administrative data sets. These included cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, malignancy and cerebrovascular disease. Each comorbidity was included as a categorical variable (presence/absence). When available within administrative records, disease severity proxies routinely used by AGENAS were incorporated into the risk-adjustment process. All variables were used exclusively within the standardised AGENAS risk-adjustment procedures to generate aggregated adjusted indicators. The authors did not perform independent regression modelling and report only the final adjusted outcomes produced by AGENAS.

Adjusted indicators were obtained by AGENAS using direct standardisation for age and sex and further accounting for disease severity (when available) and major chronic comorbidities, to ensure comparability across regions and study periods.

Within the Italian National Outcomes Programme (PNE), AGENAS applies standardised risk-adjustment procedures to administrative data in order to generate comparable outcome indicators across regions and districts. These procedures include regression-based adjustment methods implemented by AGENAS at institutional level. The authors did not perform, replicate or modify these models. The present study reports and interprets only the final aggregated and risk-adjusted indicators produced by AGENAS and does not include any original inferential or regression analyses.

Comparisons across geographical areas and time periods were expressed as relative risks (RR), calculated with reference to the national average. RR values greater than 1 indicate excess risk, while values below 1 indicate reduced risk compared with the national reference. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05, as defined within the PNE analytical framework.

Subgroup analyses and trend comparisons were performed using aggregated indicators stratified by study period, geographical area, sex and age group. Temporal and regional patterns were illustrated using bar charts and box plots.

Given the exclusive use of aggregated indicators, multilevel modelling, individual-level longitudinal analyses and hospital-specific effect estimation were not applicable. The analytical approach was therefore designed to describe population-level trends and regional variability, in line with the surveillance and benchmarking objectives of the PNE.

All statistical processing and visualisation were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics V.27 and R V.4.2.0.

All supplementary tables (online supplemental Tables 1–3) and supplementary figures (online supplemental Figures 1–9) are provided together in online supplemental material C.

Results

National trends in AECOPD hospitalisations (SDO vs. AGENAS)

As shown in table 1, AECOPD hospitalisations differed substantially between the two national data sources. In 2018, the Italian Hospital Discharge Registry (SDO, Schede di Dimissione Ospedaliera) recorded 29 645 AECOPD admissions, corresponding to 4.28% of all hospitalisations for major respiratory diseases (MARES). In the same year, the National Agency for Regional Health Services (AGENAS, Agenzia Nazionale per i Servizi Sanitari Regionali) reported 104 862 AECOPD cases (17.54% of MARES), yielding an SDO/AGENAS correspondence ratio of 24.4%.

A similar pattern was observed in 2019, with 25 910 AECOPD admissions reported in SDO and 102 474 in AGENAS (SDO/AGENAS ratio 25.2%).

A marked decline occurred in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic: SDO registered 14 529 admissions (−43.9% vs 2019) and AGENAS 60 640 (−40.8%), while maintaining a comparable SDO/AGENAS ratio (24.0%). The downward trend continued in 2021, when AGENAS reported a 45% reduction relative to prepandemic levels, corresponding to only ~9% of all MARES cases, with a stable SDO/AGENAS reporting ratio (23.6%).

In 2022, both data sets showed a partial rebound—16 386 admissions in SDO and 69 480 in AGENAS—although values remained below those of 2018–2019, and the SDO/AGENAS ratio persisted at 23.6%.

Hospitalisations for AECOPD

National hospitalisation rates for AECOPD showed a consistent temporal pattern between 2019 and 2023 (table 2). Rates decreased from 1.71 per 1000 inhabitants in 2019 to 0.95 in 2020 and 0.92 in 2021, representing nearly a 50% reduction compared with prepandemic levels. Discussion

One of the most unexpected and thought-provoking findings emerging from this nationwide analysis of aggregated institutional indicators is the sharp decline in hospitalisations for AECOPD during the COVID-19 pandemic, despite the concurrent global respiratory crisis. While the pandemic profoundly altered hospital care patterns and strained healthcare systems, our data reveal that admissions for AECOPD dropped dramatically, by over 45% according to AGENAS data, raising important questions about whether this decline reflects a true reduction in disease burden, a shift in management strategies or systemic barriers to care access. This paradoxical trend, observed in multiple regions and districts across Italy, suggests that the behaviour of AECOPD during the pandemic was significantly influenced by both clinical and non-clinical factors. Our observations are consistent with the most recent 2024 meta-analysis, which reported a substantial global decline in hospitalisations during the pandemic, but also demonstrated that this reduction primarily involved mild-to-moderate cases, with severe AECOPD episodes persisting. This shift in case mix likely increased the proportion of hospitalised patients with severe disease, predisposing to worse outcomes.26

Several factors may explain this phenomenon. Public health measures such as lockdowns, mask mandates and reduced pollutant exposure likely contributed to a true reduction in viral-induced exacerbations.29 30 At the same time, fear of nosocomial SARS-CoV-2 infection and hospital reorganisation prioritising COVID-19 likely discouraged emergency visits and admissions for milder cases.11 31 However, this decline in hospitalisations did not improve outcomes. Our analysis revealed a significant rise in 30-day postdischarge mortality, peaking at nearly 14% in 2021—well above prepandemic and global reference values.8 A 2024 meta-analysis likewise reported higher in-hospital mortality for AECOPD during the pandemic, especially in smaller hospitals, supporting the view that system overload and case concentration drove the mortality surge.26

This mismatch between declining admission rates and rising mortality may indicate that only the most severe cases were hospitalised, or that quality and continuity of postdischarge care were compromised during the pandemic. These issues were particularly evident in Southern regions such as Campania, Puglia and Basilicata, where the drop in admissions was more marked and postdischarge mortality notably higher. This regional pattern, as illustrated in online supplemental Figure 3, suggests a structural imbalance in territorial health services and the management of chronic respiratory disease, which may have been exacerbated during the health crisis.

Furthermore, our findings underscore the persistent limitations of administrative data sources. The national SDO reports, which rely heavily on DRG 88 (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), captured only about 23%–24% of the cases identified by the broader AGENAS definitions. As shown in our previous work on Apulia11 and supported by recent surveys in Lombardy,32 a large proportion of AECOPD cases are coded under DRGs related to respiratory failure (DRG 87) or procedures such as mechanical ventilation. These coding discrepancies result in a substantial underestimation of the actual hospitalisation burden, with direct consequences for healthcare planning and policy. This systematic underestimation not only obscures the true burden of AECOPD but also impairs healthcare planning, resource allocation and disease surveillance, particularly in a country where coding practices vary significantly between regions and institutions.

International evidence mirrors the trends observed in Italy. Studies from Spain, the UK and Germany have reported reductions in hospitalisations for AECOPD ranging from 27% to 78%. These declines have been attributed to multiple factors, including changes in viral circulation, environmental conditions and healthcare-seeking behaviour during the pandemic period.232533 The 2024 meta-analysis reinforces these observations, while also highlighting that, despite fewer overall admissions, severe exacerbations remained stable and mortality increased proportionally. Importantly, only three studies in the meta-analysis examined 30-day mortality after discharge, and available data suggest these rates were not higher than 60-day mortality, underscoring the scarcity of focused evidence on early postdischarge outcomes.26

Yet the data remain heterogeneous, and several reviews highlight the lack of consistent information on community-managed exacerbations, especially during the early pandemic phases.3437 In this context, the single-centre UK study by Lawless et al38 offers further insight, showing a marked reduction in AECOPD admissions during the pandemic vs the pre-COVID period, without increases in in-hospital or 30-day mortality. Their findings suggest that the decline largely reflected reduced exposure to common respiratory viruses during lockdown and mask mandates, rather than avoidance of necessary care. While not directly generalisable to our nationwide setting, these results support the hypothesis that public health measures, more than healthcare barriers, contributed to lower hospitalisations. Our population-based approach (2018–2023) provides a broader temporal perspective encompassing pre-, intra- and post-COVID phases. By incorporating 30-day postdischarge mortality and readmission as key endpoints, this study fills an evidence gap, adding population-level context to previous hospital-centred analyses and meta-analyses.26

Interestingly, while hospitalisations decreased, 30-day readmission rates remained stable around 12%–13%, higher than the global average of 7.1% reported by Smit et al.8 This discrepancy highlights enduring gaps in COPD outpatient management in Italy and likely reflects the lack of structured discharge pathways, particularly in under-resourced areas. The underdevelopment of integrated care models for chronic respiratory diseases, despite proven benefits, remains a major challenge. The persistent readmission burden, even amid fewer hospitalisations, underscores the absence of coordinated discharge protocols, community care and pulmonary rehabilitation, especially in southern and rural settings.

Our district-level analysis for 2023 (online supplemental Tables 1–3) revealed striking geographic disparities in hospitalisation, 30-day mortality and readmission rates. Districts in northern and central Italy (eg, Modena, Grosseto, ATS Brianza) showed consistently favourable profiles—lower hospitalisation, mortality and readmission—likely reflecting stronger integration between hospital and territorial services. Conversely, several southern districts were overrepresented among those with both high mortality and readmission rates. These findings indicate that beyond national averages, local healthcare organisation and discharge planning critically influence AECOPD outcomes, underscoring the need for district-level strategies to improve equity in COPD care.

In light of these findings, it becomes clear that the COVID-19 pandemic did not simply reduce the number of AECOPD hospitalisations. Rather, it reshaped the landscape of care, amplifying existing inequities and exposing critical weaknesses in data capture, territorial management and care continuity. Our results, aligned with the 2024 meta-analysis,26 point to a dual challenge: protecting COPD patients from infection while ensuring that severe exacerbations are managed promptly and effectively, regardless of geographic location or hospital size. The evidence presented here advocates strongly for the modernisation of hospital coding practices, the strengthening of community-based respiratory services and the implementation of proactive strategies, including telemedicine and structured postdischarge follow-up, to safeguard vulnerable COPD patients during health emergencies and beyond.

This study has several limitations. First, it relies on administrative data from AGENAS and the Ministry of Health (SDO), which may include coding inaccuracies and inconsistencies across regions and institutions. Second, although adjustments were made for age, sex and comorbidities through direct standardisation, the absence of detailed clinical data (eg, FEV₁, GOLD stage, exacerbation severity, ICU admission) limits full control of confounding. Third, case identification based on ICD codes, though broad and inclusive of respiratory failure with COPD as a secondary diagnosis, may miss atypical or community-managed exacerbations. Fourth, external factors such as regional COVID-19 policies, hospital capacity, ICU resource allocation and patient hesitancy were not fully captured. Fifth, outcomes like cause-specific mortality, therapy, adherence and outpatient access were unavailable. Finally, regional comparisons may have limited statistical power in smaller populations, widening CIs and obscuring trends.

Another important limitation is that the administrative data sets used in this study (SDO and AGENAS) are fully anonymised and aggregated at regional or district level. Hospital-level characteristics—such as size, case-mix, availability of respiratory units, intensity of care and variability in organisational models—are not available for analysis. These factors may contribute to the observed heterogeneity in hospitalisation rates, mortality and readmissions across regions. Future studies using hospital-identified data sets would be valuable to better explore the impact of institutional differences on AECOPD care pathways.

Furthermore, the use of aggregated administrative data precluded the application of individual-level or multilevel statistical models and limited the ability to formally assess patient-level time effects.

Moving forward, several interventions are needed. Hospital discharge coding should be modernised, expanding DRG classifications to include respiratory failure and mechanical ventilation linked to COPD. Community-based respiratory care must be reinforced, particularly in underserved regions. Structured discharge and follow-up pathways, including telemonitoring and pulmonary rehabilitation, should be implemented. Regional audits and outcome tracking should ensure quality and equity. Further research on community-managed AECOPD, especially during crises, is warranted. Finally, real-time integrated data platforms could improve AECOPD monitoring and guide timely clinical and administrative actions.

Conclusion

The COVID-19 pandemic radically disrupted AECOPD care in Italy. Hospitalisations collapsed by over 45% during 2020–2021 and, even by 2023, had not fully recovered—with the steepest and most persistent drops in the South. Alarmingly, national SDO records captured only one quarter of the cases identified by the broader AGENAS definitions, exposing a serious underestimation of the true hospitalisation burden.27 28

Thirty-day postdischarge mortality soared during the pandemic, peaking at +47% in 2021. Although rates fell back near baseline nationally by 2023, southern regions such as Campania, Calabria and parts of Sicily still recorded alarmingly high mortality, in stark contrast to the more favourable outcomes seen in much of the North and Centre.

Thirty-day readmission rates appeared stable at 12%–14%, but this masked persistent territorial disparities: during the pandemic, variability narrowed, yet post-COVID rebounds occurred in several central and northern regions, while some districts consistently exceeded 15% and others stayed below 10%.

COVID-19 acted as both a stress test and a magnifying glass, exposing entrenched inequalities, revealing critical gaps in data reporting and highlighting weaknesses in the continuity of COPD care. Closing these gaps demands urgent action, from modernising coding systems and harmonising case definitions to building stronger, integrated hospital and territory pathways capable of withstanding future health crises.

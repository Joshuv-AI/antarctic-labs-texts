Short term sodium glucose transport protein 2 inhibitors are associated with post contrast acute kidney injury in patients with diabetes
Authors: Jiabin Zang, Jianwen Liang, Xiaocong Zhang, Dan Sang, Xinyue Duan, Zhenyu Wang, Wenbin Wei, Guifu Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Although sodium–glucose transport protein-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors (SGLT2i) do not increase the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) in general, they may pose a risk in patients undergoing angiography. This prospective cohort study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of SGLT2i for post-contrast AKI (PC-AKI) in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Following screening, 306 patients with T2DM selected to undergo coronary arterial angiography with or without percutaneous intervention were enrolled. Patients were divided into the SGLT2i exposure and non-exposure groups. The primary outcome was PC-AKI, defined as an increase in serum creatinine levels > 0.5 mg/dL (44.2 µmol/L), or 25% above the baseline, within 48–72 h after exposure to contrast medium. The incidence of PC-AKI in the overall T2DM population was 5.2% (16/306). Following 1:1 propensity score matching, the incidence of PC-AKI was significantly higher in the SGLT2i group than in the non-SGLT2i group (10.7% vs. 2.9%; P = 0.027), with an odds ratio of 4.5 (95% confidence interval: 1.0–20.2; P = 0.047). Furthermore, PC-AKI occurred at a higher rate among short-term users of SGLT2i than long-term users (20.5% vs. 3.4%, P = 0.018). Thus, our findings suggest an increased risk of PC-AKI associated with short-term SGLT2i therapy in patients with T2DM.

Introduction

Sodium–glucose transport protein-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors (SGLT2i) exert their effects on glucose homeostasis primarily through the kidney; therefore, there is increasing concern regarding renal safety in patients receiving SGLT2i. Although the renoprotective effects of SGLT2i in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) are widely recognised, the potential benefits in acute kidney injury (AKI) remain controversial1–5. The current view is that while SGLT2i do not increase the risk of AKI in general, they may increase the risk of AKI in combination with hypovolaemia or drugs that pose a risk of renal injury3. At present, the clinical question regarding whether to discontinue SGLT2i before angiography is not supported by evidence-based medicine and thus needs to be addressed.

Therefore, this single-centre prospective cohort study was designed to compare the risk of post-contrast AKI (PC-AKI) in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) who underwent angiography with and without exposure to SGLT2i.

Methods

Study population

This study was an investigator-initiated, prospective cohort clinical trial performed in China, designed by the steering committee, and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2000039665).

Patients with T2DM undergoing angiography were eligible. The exclusion criteria were end-stage renal disease (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 30 mL/[min·1.73 m2), maintenance haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis, hypersensitivity to contrast medium, repeated use of contrast medium within a short period of time (48–72 h), rheumatic immune diseases, malignant tumours, and kidney transplantation. T2DM was diagnosed using the criteria of the American Diabetes Association6, and the eGFR was calculated using a formula developed by the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation based on serum creatinine (Scr)7.

Study protocol

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Eighth Affiliated Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University (ID: 2020-023-02). All methods were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. All patients provided written informed consent before enrolment. Patients were asked about SGLT2i medication use in person, while data regarding medication prescriptions were obtained via the inpatient system. Patients who did not use SGLT2i were categorized into the non-exposure group. For SGLT2i users, defined as the exposure group, the type, dose, and time of initiation were recorded; SGLT2i included dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and canagliflozin.

Hydration therapy was standard and administered at the physician’s discretion; it included isotonic saline (0.9% sodium chloride, 1 mL/kg/h), which started 12 h before and continued for 24 h after administering contrast medium. The non-ionic contrast medium, including iopromide (370 mg iodine/mL; Ultravist; Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals LLC, Berlin, Germany) and iodixanol (320 mg iodine/mL; Visipaque; GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, United States), was administered during all procedures.

Blood samples were taken to measure the Scr and cystatin c (Cys-c) concentrations before and within 48–72 h after the administration of contrast medium. The peak post-procedural Scr value was used for the primary endpoint evaluation; we also recorded relevant pre-operative laboratory test results, including haemoglobin (Hb), haematocrit (Hct), glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c), total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, triglycerides, and the urine albumin/creatinine ratio.

Endpoints and definitions

The primary endpoint was the occurrence of PC-AKI, defined as an increase in the concentration of Scr > 0.5 mg/dL, or > 25% above the baseline 48–72 h after exposure to contrast medium8. Secondary endpoints included a post-operative increase in Cys-c levels ≥ 10% from baseline values within 48 h, and the need for renal replacement therapy due to decreased renal function before discharge.

Statistical analysis

The statistical test in this study was a two-sided test with a significance level (α) of 0.05; a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 22; IBM, Armonk, NY, United States).

Quantitative data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and comparisons between groups were performed using the Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Qualitative data were expressed as n (%), and comparisons between groups were performed via Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests.

Based on individual patient data (SGLT2i administration as the dependent variable [Y], and the covariates—with differences including age, sex, Hb, Hct, total cholesterol, triglycerides, vascular intervention, and duration of angiography—as the independent variables [X]), a logistic regression model was developed to estimate the likelihood of each subject receiving the exposure factor. The propensity score (PS) of the study subjects was set to a calliper value of 0.02, and 1:1 PS matching (PSM) was performed between the exposure and non-exposure groups to correct for confounding factors and reduce the possibility of overfitting. The matched baseline values were checked for balance, which allowed for subsequent analyses based on the matched groupings. Since the prerequisite for PSM is to ensure that there are no missing values in the database, this study used the mode to replace missing categorical variables, and the serial mean to replace missing continuous, numerical variables.

In this study, the statistical description of the comparison between groups for each primary and secondary evaluation index was expressed as the frequency and incidence; statistical inference was calculated using logistic regression analysis to determine the PC-AKI odds ratio and 95% confidence interval (CI) between groups. Two logistic regression equations were used to evaluate the association between SGLT2i and PC-AKI: one was a single-factor regression equation, and the other a multi-factor adjusted analysis equation, which was adjusted for confounding factors including age, cardiac function, duration of diabetes, diuretic use, eGFR, HbA1c, Hb, vascular intervention, duration of angiography, iodine contrast medium type and dose, and hydration.

Results

Characteristics of the study cohort

We screened 433 consecutive hospitalized patients with T2DM who underwent angiography between December 2020 and February 2022. Thirty-five patients were excluded according to the study inclusion criteria, including 27 cases of end-stage renal disease, 4 of cancer, 3 of contrast allergy, and 1 case of repeated contrast use within 3 days. A total of 398 patients were enrolled in the study; however, 50 patients discharged early (within 48 h after angiography) without measurement of the post-procedural Scr value and 42 patients with changes in perioperative SGLT2i exposure factors were excluded. Therefore, a total of 306 patients were finally included in the statistical analysis: 149 patients in the exposure group (SGLT2i group) and 157 patients in the non-exposure group (non-SGLT2i group) (Fig. 1).

Angiography

Before PSM, the exposure group had a significantly higher rate of coronary interventions compared with the non-exposure group (55.7% vs. 42.7%; P = 0.023), with a corresponding increase in the duration of angiography (49.6 ± 29.7 vs. 43.4 ± 26.8; P = 0.056). There was no significant difference in contrast dosage between the two groups (P = 0.225). Following PSM, the angiography-related parameter variables were comparable in both groups (Table 2).

Incidence of PC-AKI

The incidence of PC-AKI in the cohort of patients with T2DM was 5.2% (16/306). Before PSM, the proportion of patients with one PC-AKI event in the exposure group was 7.4% (11/149, of whom 10 were dapagliflozin users and 1 was an empagliflozin user). In the non-exposure group, 3.2% (5/157) patients experienced a PC-AKI event. Following PSM, the proportion of patients in the exposure and non-exposure groups who had a PC-AKI event were 10.7% and 2.9%, respectively (P = 0.027, Fig. 2A).

In the cohort of SGLT2i users, the duration of the SGLT2i prescription up until the angiography was used to divide the patients into two groups based on admission: pre-admission (191.5 ± 223.3 days) and post-admission (1.7 ± 1.4 days). We observed a higher rate of PC-AKI among post-admission short-term SGLT2i users than in preadmission long-term users (20.5% vs. 3.4%, P = 0.018, Fig. 2B).

There was a statistically significant decrease in eGFR following angiography in the exposure group compared with non-exposure group (-2.5 ± 1.2 vs. 2.3 ± 1.0; P = 0.002, Table 3). Although no significant difference in the post-angiography increase in Cys-c levels ≥ 10% from the baseline within 48 h (exposure group vs. non-exposure group, 16.5% vs. 11.7%; P = 0.371, Fig. 2C). However, we found that the proportion of patients with a post-angiography increase in Cys-c levels ≥ 10% in the short-term SGLT2i users is significantly higher than that of long-term users (27.3% vs. 8.5%, P = 0.011, Fig. 2D). Furthermore, no patients in this study experienced post-procedural events requiring emergency haemodialysis (Table 3).

Association between sglt2i use and PC-AKI

The logistic regression analysis showed that compared with non-exposure group, the SGLT2i group exhibited a three-fold increase in the risk of developing PC-AKI (OR: 4.0 [95% CI: 1.1–14.7]; P = 0.038); which increased after adjusting for confounders (OR: 4.5 [95% CI: 1.0–20.2]; P = 0.047). There was no significant difference in the risk of a post-operative increase in Cys-c levels ≥ 10% from the baseline within 48 h between the exposure and non-exposure groups (P > 0.05) (Fig. 3).

Adverse events

Among the 433 patients in this cohort, a contrast allergy occurred in three patients: one SGLT2i user and two non-users. No adverse events, such as hypoglycaemia, ketoacidosis, urinary tract infection, or venous thromboembolism, were observed.

Discussion

In this study, we observed that peri-procedural administration of SGLT2i for a short duration was strongly associated with a high incidence of PC-AKI in patients with T2DM, suggesting that a short course of oral SGLT2i may increase the risk of PC-AKI.

Our findings differ from those of previous studies9–12. First, we introduced the concept of PC-AKI, which has previously been referred to as contrast-induced nephropathy, contrast-induced AKI, or contrast-associated AKI. PC-AKI broadly refers to the decline in renal function that occurs following the intravascular administration of a contrast agent; however, the contrast agent is not necessarily the direct perpetrator13. The effect of peri-operative co-administration of drugs, such as SGLT2i, on renal function is the focus of our research observations.

Second, the incidence of PC-AKI in the overall population with T2DM was 5.2% in the present study; however, this has been reported to vary considerably in previous studies from 3 to 11% to 10–20%, or even as high as 50% in high-risk patients14–18. The reasons include different study population characteristics, angiography characteristics, and definitions of PC-AKI. The population enrolled in this study included relatively stable patients with T2DM who underwent elective angiography; patients with end-stage renal disease, emergency interventions, and enhanced computed tomography findings were excluded. The advantage of using the most widely used definition of PC-AKI in this study is that it correlates well with the clinical endpoints, allowing for valid comparisons between trials8,19–25.

Based on the available observational data, the risk of PC-AKI may indeed be diminished in patients administered SGLT2i therapy9,11,12,26. Although the mechanism is complex and not completely understood, it does not seem to depend on any reduction in glycaemia. However, we the data does not suggest that administering an SGLT2i immediately before angiography is beneficial27, as the results of our study showed that the incidence of PC-AKI significantly increased in the T2DM population exposed to SGLT2i. Interestingly, further analysis found that short-term SGLT2i exposure (1.7 days), which is during the period of haemodynamic instability, promoted PC-AKI compared with long-term SGLT2i exposure (191.5 days). Rather than contradicting the current consensus, our study bridges the gap, as previous studies only included patients with long-term SGLT2i exposure of at least 15 days, and even more than 6 months, before angiography, excluding patients with short-term SGLT2i exposure.

The administration of SGLT2i results in a 7% reduction in plasma volume; however, this natriuretic effect disappears 2–3 days after initiating treatment, and sodium and fluid balance are re-established28. The reversible decrease in eGFR levels is an inherent feature after initiating SGLT2i treatment, regardless of the class of SGLT2i; this effect was observed in studies on dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and canagliflozin1,29,30. The difference in mean change in eGFR between the two groups in the present study was 4.8 mL/(min·1.73 m2), which was significantly greater than the value reported in the ‘Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse Outcomes in Chronic Kidney Disease’ trial (0.93 mL/(min·1.73 m2)1. In summary, it is suggested that the risk of PC-AKI increases with SGLT2i therapy, especially upon the initiation of SGLT2i use.

In addition to Scr, we also assessed changes in Cys-c before and after angiography. Cys-c levels can be used to detect renal function with the advantage of the reduced influence of non-renal factors compared to Scr, such as muscle mass, physical activity, and diet31. In the present study, a change in Cys-c ≥ 10% as a secondary endpoint event was found to differ between the two groups, although the difference was not statistically significant. This could be related to the time-point of our blood sampling review. Cys-c starts to rise 6 h after contrast administration, reaching a peak at 24 h; however, the time-point of our review of Cys-c was between 24 and 48 h, thus, the monitored postoperative Cys-c level was not at the peak level, potentially resulting in the observed insignificant changes32.

The main argument in favour of SGLT2i causing renal injury is that inhibition of SGLT2 protein function leads to impaired sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubule, resulting in increased urinary sodium excretion, increased sodium transport to the glomerular para-cellular apparatus, activation of the glomerular feedback system, afferent small artery vasoconstriction, and decreased glomerular intra-capsular pressure; therefore, renal plasma flow thus decreases, resulting in inadequate renal tissue perfusion26. The pathophysiological mechanisms of PC-AKI include direct and indirect effects, as well as haemodynamic changes33,34. The iodine contrast media are not re-absorbed by the tubules after glomerular filtration; its concentration and viscosity significantly increase in the tubules, slowing down the fluid flow and even causing tubular obstruction. The damaging effects of iodine contrast media on the kidney are amplified after SGLT2 inhibition, and renal tubular dynamics and haemodynamics are further disrupted via prolonged exposure of the kidney to the contrast media, including permeable vacuolar-like degeneration of renal tubular epithelial cells, apoptosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and increased tissue oxygenation with reduced reactive oxygen species production5,35–37.

Based on our findings and the possible mechanisms of renal injury, this study highlights the need to take appropriate preventive measures to reduce the risk of PC-AKI. First, in patients with long-term SGLT2i use, it appears to be safe not to discontinue SGLT2i before angiography. Second, in patients who intend to start SGLT2i treatment after admission, it is recommended to delay the start of drug administration until the contrast agent is excreted following angiography. Third, it may be necessary to provide peri-operative intravenous hydration to ensure a certain blood volume for patients with T2DM administered SGLT2i.

This study had some limitations. First, this was a single-centre, small-sample observational study with a low level of evidence-based medicine and a limited sample size that resulted in a wide 95% CI. The current study did not prove a causal relationship between short-term SGLT2i therapy and high risk of PC-AKI. Second, Scr does not always accurately reflect the level of renal function; it would be more convincing if renal tubular injury markers, such as neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin or kidney injury molecule-1, could be included in this study. Third, we did not conduct a long follow-up effort, and outcomes, such as all-cause mortality and cardiovascular death, remain unknown as hard endpoints.

Conclusion

Short-term SGLT2i therapy is associated with an increased risk of PC-AKI in patients with T2DM; however, it may be safe for patients on long-term SGLT2i medication. Therefore, the results of this study provide medical evidence for the renal risk associated with the use of SGLT2i in the peri-operative management of angiography.

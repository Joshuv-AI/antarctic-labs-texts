Assessing the minimal invasiveness of pure single-incision da Vinci SP surgery for right-sided colon cancer: comparative analysis with the da Vinci Xi system using PSM and IPTW
Authors: Sohei Akuta, Yasumitsu Hirano, Yasuhiro Ishiyama, Yume Minagawa, Yusuke Nishi, Hisashi Hayashi, Akihito Nakanishi, Takatsugu Fujii, Hirofumi Sugita, Chikashi Hiranuma, Yusuke Kinugasa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFew studies have compared the invasiveness and safety of pure single-incision robotic surgery using the da Vinci SP system (SP) without an assistant port with those using the da Vinci Xi system (Xi) for right-sided colon cancer. Surgical invasiveness was assessed using the C-reactive protein/albumin (CRP/Alb) ratio and postoperative pain using the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS).MethodsThis single-center retrospective study included 163 patients who underwent robot-assisted surgery for right-sided colon cancer between October 2022 and August 2025 (SP = 60, Xi = 103). Confounding factors were adjusted using propensity score matching and stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting based on body mass index (BMI) ≥ 25 kg/m2, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) ≥ III, tumor size ≥ 40 mm, surgical procedure, and preoperative CRP/Alb ratio ≥ 0.03; short-term outcomes were compared.ResultsAfter adjustment, baseline characteristics were well balanced. The SP approach was associated with a shorter incision length (3.0 vs 5.5 cm, p < 0.01) and lower postoperative pain on day 3 (NRS scores 2 vs. 3, p < 0.01). Operative time, postoperative inflammatory response assessed by CRP/Alb ratio, complications, and pathological outcomes were comparable between the groups.ConclusionsPure single-incision robotic surgery using the da Vinci SP system demonstrated comparable short-term safety and oncological adequacy to the Xi system, with modest advantages in incision length and postoperative pain. No clear benefit in systemic inflammatory response was observed, and further validation in larger, prospective, multicenter studies is warranted.Keywords: Pure single-incision robotic surgery, da Vinci SP system, Right-sided colon cancer, Propensity score matching, Inverse probability of treatment weighting

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most frequently diagnosed malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Robotic-assisted surgery for CRC has gained widespread adoption because it offers superior instrument maneuverability and three-dimensional visualization compared with conventional laparoscopic techniques [2]. Recently, alongside the da Vinci Xi system (Xi), the da Vinci SP system (SP) has been introduced into clinical practice, attracting attention for its potential advantages in minimal invasiveness and cosmetic outcomes. Previous studies have reported that SP surgery provides benefits such as a shorter incision, reduced postoperative pain, and faster recovery, whereas short-term clinical outcomes—including the number of retrieved lymph nodes, hospital stay, and complication rates—are comparable with those of the Xi system [3–5]. Recent systematic reviews and narrative reports have summarized the current applications of the da Vinci SP system across multiple surgical fields, including colorectal surgery, and have demonstrated its overall feasibility and safety. However, these reviews also emphasize several unresolved issues, such as technical constraints related to instrument triangulation, learning-curve effects, and the limited availability of objective data quantifying surgical invasiveness. In particular, evidence focusing on right-sided colon cancer and comparative analyses using standardized inflammatory or pain-related indicators remains scarce [5, 6]. In this context, a recent multicenter matched-cohort study comparing single-port robotic surgery with single-port laparoscopic surgery for colon cancer demonstrated that the robotic approach was associated with less blood loss, lower conversion rates, fewer intraoperative complications, and faster postoperative recovery. These findings suggest that single-port robotic platforms may offer technical and short-term clinical advantages over conventional single-port laparoscopic techniques, further supporting their potential role in minimally invasive colorectal surgery [7].

The Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) is widely used for assessing postoperative pain and serves as a crucial indicator associated with recovery promotion and prevention of postoperative complications [8]. Furthermore, C-reactive protein/albumin (CRP/Alb) ratio has been recognized as a marker of postoperative inflammation and nutritional status, with reported associations with infectious complications and prognosis [9–11]. However, studies directly comparing the Xi and SP systems for right-sided colon cancer—particularly those evaluating NRS and CRP/Alb—remain limited.

Moreover, most previous studies have reported that the SP lacks integrated energy devices and robotic staplers, necessitating the placement of an additional assistant port for suction, energy device use, or stapling [5, 12–15]. In contrast, in this study, all SP procedures were performed as solo surgeries through a single umbilical incision without any additional assistant ports (Fig. 1). Compared with previous reports, the absence of an assistant represents a key distinguishing feature of this study and highlights the potential of the SP as a pure single-port robotic surgical platform.

This study aimed to compare the short-term outcomes of the SP-assisted pure single-incision robotic surgery (Pure SIRS), performed without an additional assistant port, with those of the Xi-assisted surgery for right-sided colon cancer, to evaluate the minimal invasiveness of the SP using postoperative pain and the CRP/Alb ratio as indicators.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was designed as a single-center, retrospective, observational study. Patients who underwent da Vinci–assisted ileocecal resection or right hemicolectomy for right-sided colon cancer—including appendiceal, cecal, ascending, and transverse colon cancer—at Saitama Medical University International Medical Center between October 2022 and August 2025 were included.

Patients were excluded if they had received chemoradiotherapy, had recurrent or multiple cancers, underwent emergency surgery, or had procedures performed by surgeons not certified by the Endoscopic Surgical Skill Qualification System of the Japan Society for Endoscopic Surgery.

Data were retrospectively extracted from electronic medical records, including patient age, sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, history of abdominal surgery, tumor markers (carcinoembryonic antigen [CEA] and carbohydrate antigen 19–9 [CA19-9]), operative time, blood loss, intraoperative transfusion, conversion to laparotomy, incision length, postoperative complications, time to oral intake, time to first flatus and defecation, postoperative hospital stay, 30-day readmission, postoperative pain score (NRS), CRP/Alb, and tumor -node-metastasis (TNM) stage. Postoperative complications were classified according to the Clavien–Dindo system, with grade II or higher considered clinically significant [16, 17]. All patients were managed under the standardized enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocol, including scheduled acetaminophen and on-demand nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs or opioids. Postoperative pain was assessed using the NRS by ward nurses three times daily using a standardized protocol.

Patient demographics, short-term surgical outcomes, and pathological characteristics were compared between the da Vinci SP and da Vinci Xi surgical approaches. Analyses were performed using propensity score matching (PSM) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) to minimize selection bias (Fig. 2).

Surgeon

Eight surgeons participated in this study; all were certified by the Endoscopic Surgical Skill Qualification System of the Japan Society for Endoscopic Surgery [18]. Each surgeon who performed SP procedures had previously completed more than 20 colorectal cancer surgeries using the Xi. The choice between the SP and Xi was primarily determined by the availability of each robotic platform.

Surgical technique

da Vinci SP surgery for right-sided colon cancer

Patients were positioned supine with the left side down and the head slightly tilted downward. A 3-cm umbilical incision was created to insert the SP port, and no additional assistant port was required in any case (Fig. 1). An intracorporeal medial-to-lateral dissection approach was employed. After the duodenum was identified, the superior mesenteric vein (SMV) was exposed, and the ileocolic artery (ICA) and vein (ICV) were ligated, followed by lymph node dissection.

Regarding right hemicolectomy, the dissection was extended cranially along the SMV, followed by division of the middle colic artery (MCA) and accessory right colic vein (ARCV). After lymphadenectomy, the colon is mobilized, most commonly using a complete medial-to-lateral approach. The specimen was extracted through the umbilical incision, and reconstruction was performed extracorporeally using a functional end-to-end anastomosis.

da Vinci Xi surgery for right-sided colon cancer

A 2.5-cm vertical incision was created at the umbilicus, through which an EZ Access device (Hakko Shoji) and a 12-mm laparoscopic port were inserted. Four additional 8-mm da Vinci ports were positioned in the lower and right abdomen, and the procedure was performed with the da Vinci Xi system using a standard five-port configuration (Fig. 1). No additional assistant ports were required; the umbilical laparoscopic port was used for gauze insertion and removal.

An intracorporeal medial approach was employed, and lymph node dissection was performed following the same procedure as in the SP group. The principal difference was the availability of energy devices with the Xi system. Complete medial-to-lateral mobilization of the colon was not feasible; instead, dissection was extended from the retroperitoneum to the hepatic flexure. The specimen was extracted through an umbilical incision, and reconstruction was performed extracorporeally using a functional end-to-end anastomosis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.3.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and EZR version 1.63 (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan) [19]. Continuous variables are expressed as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR, 25th–75th percentiles), while categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages. Unadjusted group comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Propensity score matching: PSM

Analyses were performed using PSM and IPTW to adjust for baseline imbalances and minimize selection bias between the two groups.

The surgical approach (SP group vs. Xi group) was designated as the dependent variable, while five clinically relevant confounders—BMI ≥ 25 vs. < 25 kg/m2, ASA classification (≥ 3 vs. < 3), tumor diameter (≥ 40 vs. < 40 mm), surgical procedure (ileocecal resection vs. right hemicolectomy), and preoperative CRP/Alb ratio (≥ 0.03 vs. < 0.03)—were entered as independent variables in a logistic regression model to calculate the propensity score (PS) for each patient.

The BMI cutoff of 25 kg/m2 was determined according to the Japanese definition of obesity outlined in the national obesity guidelines [20]. The ASA cutoff of 3 was selected because an ASA score ≥ 3 has been associated with an increased risk of postoperative complications [21]. Cutoff values for tumor diameter (≥ 40 mm) and preoperative CRP/Alb ratio (≥ 0.03) were determined based on their respective median.

Covariate balance was evaluated by the standardized mean difference (SMD), with |SMD|< 0.1 indicating adequate balance. The patients were matched 1:1 based on the calculated PS by the nearest-neighbor method with a caliper width of 0.02, and Mahalanobis metric matching was applied to identify the closest pairs. Postmatching group comparisons were conducted using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for continuous variables and McNemar’s test for categorical variables.

Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW)

Stabilized IPTW were generated using the same five covariates as those applied in the PSM model. Extreme values outside the 1st–99th percentile range were trimmed before applying the weights. After weighting, group totals were normalized (82:82) to construct a pseudo-population with balanced baseline characteristics. Covariate balance was evaluated using the SMD, with |SMD|< 0.1 indicating adequate balance.

Weighted Rao–Scott chi-square tests (svychisq) were used for categorical variables, and weighted rank-sum tests (svyranktest) were applied for continuous variables after weighting [22, 23].

Ethical statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Saitama Medical School International Medical Center (Approval No. 2025–160).

Results

Patient characteristics before and after PSM and IPTW

A total of 163 patients who underwent da Vinci–assisted surgery for right-sided colon cancer were included, comprising 60 and 103 patients in the SP and Xi group, respectively. After PSM, 44 matched pairs (88 patients) were included in the comparative analysis. IPTW was also performed using the same five covariates, and stabilized weights were applied after trimming extreme values. A normalized pseudo-population (82:82) was generated (Fig. 2). The discriminative ability of the propensity score model was acceptable, with a c-statistic (area under the curve) of 0.74 (95% confidence interval, 0.663–0.817). Covariate balance improved after both PSM and stabilized IPTW, as demonstrated by reduced SMDs for the five prespecified covariates (Fig. 3, Table 1). Importantly, the results obtained from the IPTW-adjusted cohort were consistent with those from the PSM analysis, indicating robustness of the findings.

Operative outcomes

Operative time did not differ significantly between the SP and Xi groups in either analysis (PSM: median 180 min vs. 179 min, p = 0.41; IPTW: median 193 min vs. 186 min, p = 0.96). Intraoperative blood loss was significantly lower in the SP group in both analyses (PSM: median 5 mL vs. 5 mL, p < 0.01; IPTW: median 5 mL vs. 5 mL, p < 0.01). No conversions to open surgery or intraoperative transfusions occurred in either group.

Incision length was significantly shorter in the SP group (PSM: median 3.0 cm vs 5.5 cm, p < 0.01; IPTW: median 3.0 cm vs 5.5 cm, p < 0.01). Postoperative pain, assessed using the NRS, was significantly lower in the SP group on postoperative day 3 (PSM: median 2 vs. 3, p < 0.01; IPTW: median 2 vs. 3, p < 0.01), whereas no significant differences were observed on postoperative days 1 and 5. Postoperative analgesic use did not differ significantly between the two groups, suggesting that differences in NRS scores reflected pain intensity rather than analgesic consumption. Time to first flatus, oral intake, bowel movement, and length of hospital stay were comparable between the groups.

No significant differences in invasiveness, assessed by the CRP/Alb ratio, were found between the groups on postoperative days 1 and 3 (postoperative day 3 CRP/Alb: PSM: median 1.22 vs 1.12, p = 0.55; IPTW: median 1.42 vs 1.27, p = 0.53).

In the entire cohort, postoperative complications in the SP group included ileus in three patients, pneumonia in two, and arrhythmia in one (Clavien–Dindo grade IIIa). In the Xi group, complications included perianastomotic inflammation in one patient, surgical site infection in one, ileus in two, anastomotic leakage in one (IIIb), anastomotic bleeding in one (IIIa), and duodenal perforation in one (IIIb). The incidence of Clavien–Dindo grade II or higher complications did not differ significantly between the groups (Table 3), and no significant differences were observed after PSM or IPTW adjustment.

Discussion

In this study, we compared the short-term outcomes of the SP and Xi systems for right-sided colon cancer using PSM and IPTW. A distinctive feature of this study is that the SP procedures were performed as Pure SIRS through a single umbilical port without any assistant port. Futhermore invasiveness was comprehensively evaluated by the perioperative CRP/Alb ratio. This design differentiates our study from previous reports.

After adjustment with both PSM and IPTW, operative time, incidence of Clavien–Dindo grade ≥ II complications, and postoperative hospital stay were comparable between the two systems, indicating that True SIRS can be performed safely. However, the SP group demonstrated significantly shorter incision length (PSM: median 3.0 cm vs 5.5 cm, p < 0.01; IPTW: 3.0 cm vs 5.5 cm, p < 0.01) and lower pain scores on postoperative day 3 (PSM: median 2 vs 3, p < 0.01; IPTW: 2 vs 3, p < 0.01), confirming cosmetic and minimally invasive advantages consistent with prior studies. No significant differences were observed in CRP/Alb ratios between the groups, suggesting comparable systemic inflammatory responses.

Although the total operative times were similar (PSM: median 180 min vs 179 min, p = 0.41; IPTW: 193 min vs 186 min, p = 0.96), the time from incision to docking completion was significantly shorter in the SP group (PSM: median 14 min vs 18 min, p < 0.01; IPTW: 15 min vs 17 min, p = 0.05), consistent with previous reports. This difference may be attributed to single-site umbilicus access, which simplifies docking even when redocking is required. The absence of an assistant port further reduces setup time. Conversely, the lack of an assistant port may restrict the use of energy devices and operative exposure, leading to reports recommending the addition of an auxiliary port. However, our study showed no adverse impacts on operative time or postoperative complications, producing outcomes comparable to those of previous studies. Although intraoperative blood loss differed significantly between the groups, both medians were minimal (5 mL), indicating no clinically meaningful difference. Thus, right-sided colectomy using the SP system without an assistant port appears feasible. In addition to clinical outcomes, operative workflow efficiency is an important consideration when introducing new robotic platforms. In the present study, docking time was consistently shorter in the SP group than in the Xi group, reflecting the simplified setup of the single-port system. Although total operative time was comparable between the groups, the reduced docking complexity and the absence of an assistant port might contribute to improved operating room efficiency and workflow standardization. These features could be particularly advantageous in high-volume centers, where incremental reductions in setup time might translate into meaningful gains in resource utilization.

Regarding postoperative pain, the SP group showed significantly lower NRS scores on postoperative day 3 (PSM: median, 2 vs. 3, p < 0.01; IPTW: 2 vs. 3, p < 0.01), likely reflecting reduced wound-related discomfort from the smaller single incision. Previous studies have similarly reported reduced pain with SIRS compared with multiport or conventional laparoscopic surgery, and our results corroborate these findings. However, the absolute difference in NRS score was modest, amounting to only 1 point. Although this difference was statistically significant, it represents a limited clinical effect and should therefore be interpreted with caution. The observed reduction in pain is unlikely to translate into substantial differences in postoperative recovery or other clinical outcomes.

No significant intergroup differences were perioperative CRP/Alb ratio, indicating comparable inflammatory and nutritional responses. The CRP/Alb ratio has been validated as an inflammation-based indicator associated with postoperative complications and prognosis of colorectal surgery, including minimally invasive approaches. Inclusion of preoperative CRP/Alb ratio as a covariate ensured balance in baseline inflammatory and nutritional status between the groups. Postoperative days 1 and 3 were selected for CRP/Alb ratio assessment because serum CRP levels typically peak around postoperative days 2–3, while albumin levels often reach a nadir during the same period due to surgical stress and capillary leakage. Although the SP demonstrated clear advantages in terms of abdominal wall invasiveness, systemic inflammatory responses were comparable between groups (postoperative day 3 CRP/Alb ratio: PSM 1.22 vs 1.12, p = 0.55; IPTW 1.42 vs 1.27, p = 0.53). The absence of a significant difference may reflect the relatively low-risk patient cohort and the overall minimally invasive nature of robotic surgery. Previous studies have reported that robotic surgery using the Xi yields lower postoperative CRP/Alb ratios than single-incision laparoscopic surgery, indicating the minimally invasive potential of robotic platforms and that SIRS or SP procedures may further suppress postoperative CRP elevation. Therefore, larger multicenter studies are required to clarify the inflammatory and immunonutritional implications of these findings.

Furthermore, the distance to the proximal margin was significantly shorter in the SP group (PSM: median, 100 vs. 140 mm, p < 0.01; IPTW: 105 vs. 140 mm, p = 0.03). The shorter proximal bowel margin observed in the SP group may reflect the learning curve during the early phase of pure single-incision robotic surgery. In the initial period, bowel mobilization and traction were performed cautiously to prioritize procedural safety, which may have limited proximal bowel redundancy. Nevertheless, a median proximal margin of 100 mm was considered oncologically adequate, and the extent of lymphadenectomy was deemed sufficient.

A major concern about single-incision surgery is the potential risk of incisional hernia. Since our study focused on short-term outcomes, we could not evaluate the long-term incidence of hernia. Although we prioritized meticulous fascial closure to mitigate this risk, future studies with long-term follow-up are essential for determining whether the cosmetic and pain benefits of SP surgery would outweigh the potential risk of hernia.

This study has some limitations. It was a single-center, retrospective analysis that may have been subject to selection bias. In addition, although we adjusted for five clinically relevant covariates using both PSM and IPTW, residual confounding cannot be completely excluded. Important factors such as prior abdominal surgery, detailed tumor location within the right colon, and surgeon-level clustering were not incorporated into the propensity score model. Given the relatively small sample size and the early-phase experience with the SP system, these unmeasured confounders may have influenced the observed outcomes. However, we minimized confounding factors using both PSM and IPTW with BMI, ASA score, tumor size, surgical procedure, and the CRP/Alb ratio as covariates, and restricted operations to board-certified surgeons. The study period spanned from October 2022 to August 2025, and the da Vinci SP system was introduced in January 2025. Therefore, the SP group mainly consisted of early- to mid-phase cases after platform adoption, and learning-curve effects specific to the SP system were not independently assessed. As such, the observed outcomes may have been influenced by procedural immaturity during the initial SP experience. Although consistency between the results obtained using PSM and IPTW was observed, this agreement should not be interpreted as a formal sensitivity analysis. We did not perform sensitivity analyses using alternative model specifications or excluding early-phase SP cases, and thus the stability of the findings under different assumptions could not be fully evaluated. Formal sensitivity analyses excluding early SP cases were not feasible because of the limited sample size.

In conclusion, pure single-incision robotic surgery with the SP for right-sided colon cancer demonstrated comparable short-term safety and oncological adequacy to the Xi system, with modest advantages in cosmetic outcomes and postoperative pain. However, no clear benefit in systemic inflammatory or nutritional responses was observed. The SP appears to be a feasible minimally invasive option for right-sided colon cancer in experienced hands and warrants further validation in larger, prospective, multicenter studies.

Immune-related cardiotoxicities predict immune checkpoint inhibitors treatment for advanced non-small cell lung cancer: a retrospective multi-center study
Authors: Hengxing Sun, Yini Huang, Hongxia Duan, Mengting Xiong, Yukun Wang, Yingyi Qin, Yanbin Kuang, Xiaoming Li, Siwan Wen, Fengming Ding, Hao Tang, Yang Chen, Junyong Zou, Feng Hu, Caicun Zhou, Changhui Wang, Fenyong Sun, Dachun Xu, Shuanshuan Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy is a cornerstone in the treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), but immune-related cardiotoxicities remain a concern. Whether immune-related cardiotoxicities serve as independent prognostic predictors for NSCLC remains debated. This study aimed to investigate the prognostic value of immune-related cardiotoxicities and develop a predictive model for survival outcomes in NSCLC patients receiving ICI therapy.MethodsThis retrospective multi-center study included NSCLC patients from nine centers who received at least one ICI cycle from 2019 to 2024. Demographic and clinical indicators were analyzed for associations with overall survival (OS). A prognostic model based on Cox regression and a nomogram was developed and validated.ResultsOf 187 patients, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of all participants was 0.903 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.857–0.951], 0.759 (95% CI: 0.666–0.865) and 0.528 (95% CI: 0.361–0.773) respectively and 80 (42.8%) experienced immune-related cardiotoxicities. Kaplan-Meier survival analyses revealed that immune-related cardiotoxicities and immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were not associated with OS (P=0.10; P=0.20). Multivariate Cox regression analyses on OS showed an independent predictive value of platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) [hazard ratio (HR) 1.002, 95% CI: 1.000–1.004, P=0.02], carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) (HR 1.005, 95% CI: 1.002–1.007, P<0.001) and cytokeratin-19 fragment 21-1 (CYFRA21-1) (HR 1.004, 95% CI: 1.001–1.008, P=0.02) for OS in advanced NSCLC patients. A nomogram was constructed and the area under the curve (AUC) was 0.757, 0.741, and 0.815 at 1-, 3-, and 5-year. A prognostic model incorporating PLR, CEA and CYFRA21-1 resulted in an optimal performance to predict the prognosis. Additionally, smoking was associated with the occurrence of immune-related cardiotoxicities.ConclusionsMild irAEs or immune-related cardiotoxicities (grade 1–2) did not extend median OS in NSCLC patients. The prognostic model incorporating PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 could contribute to predicting the treatment efficacy and prognosis of ICI treatment.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), cardiotoxicity, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), immune-related adverse events (irAEs), overall survival (OS)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Mild immune-related adverse events (irAEs) or immune-related cardiotoxicities (grade 1–2) did not extend median overall survival in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients. The prognostic model incorporating platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), cytokeratin-19 fragment 21-1 (CYFRA21-1) could contribute to predicting the treatment efficacy and prognosis of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) treatment.

What is known and what is new?

• ICI therapy is effective in treating advanced NSCLC, but irAEs, including myocardial injuries, are a concern.

• Our study showed evidence that, despite the occurrence of mild irAEs and immune-related cardiotoxicities, the long-term survival of NSCLC patients with ICI therapy was not affected. PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 exhibited predictive significance for the prognosis of patients undergoing ICI treatment.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Clinical indicators can predict adverse immune reactions following ICI treatment, and with prompt management of these reactions, patients exhibit a favorable long-term prognosis.

Introduction

Lung cancer stands as the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, posing a formidable challenge to global healthcare systems (1,2). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of lung cancer (3). A vast majority of advanced NSCLC patients are deemed ineligible for surgical resection (4). Nowadays, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have profoundly revolutionized the treatment for advanced NSCLC (5,6). Numerous clinical trials have demonstrated that ICI monotherapy and its combination with chemotherapy or radiotherapy, when utilized as first-line treatment for advanced NSCLC, yield remarkable clinical benefits (7-9). However, ICIs can also trigger immune-related adverse events (irAEs) that can affect any organ and range from mild side effects to life-threatening complications (10).

Immune-related cardiotoxicity is a rare but severe adverse reaction that is increasingly reported in studies (11-13). Due to the intricate nature of diagnosing immune-related myocarditis and pericarditis, the incidence of ICI-related cardiotoxicities is probably underestimated (14). Emerging studies suggest that the occurrence of irAEs prolongs overall survival (OS), although it may vary in different organs and severity levels (10,15,16). However, there is limited research exploring whether immune-related cardiotoxicities can predict ICI treatment efficacy in advanced NSCLC patients.

Emerging evidence highlights that systemic inflammation and tumor burden profoundly influence ICI responsiveness and irAEs susceptibility (17-20). Peripheral blood biomarkers such as platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and white blood cell count (WBC count) serve as indicators of host inflammatory status, with elevated PLR associated with impaired anti-tumor immunity and poor prognosis in NSCLC (21). Similarly, serum tumor markers including carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and cytokeratin-19 fragment 21-1 (CYFRA21-1) have been validated as reliable surrogates for NSCLC tumor burden and metastatic potential (22). However, whether pretreatment levels of these biomarkers can predict survival outcomes and immune-related cardiotoxicity in ICI-treated NSCLC patients remains unexplored.

Therefore, this multi-center study aimed to investigate the relationship between immune-related cardiotoxicities and survival outcomes after ICI treatment in advanced NSCLC patients. Additionally, we also examined potential prognostic factors, including peripheral blood and serum indicators, to provide references for the evaluation of ICI efficacy. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-374/rc).

Methods

Patients

We conducted a retrospective study of a cohort of 187 patients from nine research centers, aiming to clarify the potential association between immune-related cardiotoxicities and ICI treatment efficacy in advanced NSCLC patients. The eligibility criteria were: (I) diagnosed with NSCLC; (II) tumor node metastasis (TNM) stage III (unresectable) or IV; (III) received at least one complete cycle of ICI treatment [including anti-programmed death-1 (anti-PD-1) agents—pembrolizumab, camrelizumab, tislelizumab, sintilimab, toripalimab, and anti-programmed death ligand-1 (anti-PD-L1) agent—atezolizumab]; (IV) willing to participate in this research. The nine research centers included Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital, Shanghai Chest Hospital, Xinhua Hospital, Tongji Hospital, Shanghai General Hospital, Second Affiliated Hospital of Naval Medical University, Ningbo No. 2 Hospital and Tongren Hospital. Notably, the vast majority of patients underwent a combined regimen of immunotherapy and chemotherapy (Figure 1). Two researchers collected and cross-checked all subject data to ensure the accuracy of the data sources. We employed various means to reach out to the lost participants, encouraging them to resume their participation in the study and minimizing the attrition rate to the greatest extent possible. The study was registered at the Medical Research Registration and Filing Information System (medicalresearch.org.cn) MR-31-24-018801. This multi-center study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) of Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital (approval No. 24KY29). All participating hospitals were informed and agreed to the study. As this is a retrospective cohort study utilizing anonymized data extracted from the hospital’s medical records system, informed consent from individual participants was not required. All patient data used in this study were anonymized prior to analysis to ensure participant privacy.

Definition and assessment of immune-related cardiotoxicities

Immune-related cardiotoxicities mainly include myocarditis, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, pericarditis and myocardial injuries (23). In this study, the diagnosis of immune-related cardiotoxicities was based on a comprehensive assessment of myocardial injury biomarkers, electrocardiogram (ECG) and cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) (if available). In the absence of a gold standard for suspected myocarditis, the case was categorized as immune-related cardiotoxicity based on evidence of myocardial injury markers and exclusion of other potential causes. The severity of irAEs was graded according to the universally recognized Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE). Specifically, reactions of grade 1 and 2 were deemed mild events, whereas those of grade 3 or higher were classified as severe reactions.

Study covariates

Baseline clinical characteristics, including age, sex, pathological classification of tumor, smoking history, and ICI targets, were collected and analyzed. Besides, peripheral blood indicators, including WBC count, PLR, and serum indicators, including immunoglobulin M (IgM), and tumor biomarkers, including CEA, CYFRA21-1, were also added up and analyzed.

Statistical analysis

Summaries of continuous skewed baseline variables were presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for grouped comparisons. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, and the Chi-squared test and Fisher’s exact test compared the two groups. We employed the method of multiple imputation to fill in the missing data.

The OS after ICI treatment was calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method. The prognostic factors for OS were evaluated by the Cox proportional hazards regression model. Nomogram and the calibration curve were utilized to evaluate the performance of the models. The time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to determine the optimal cutoff value for the prognostic index. The exploration of predictive factors related to immune-related cardiotoxicities was achieved through the logistic regression model.

The bootstrap method was used to verify the discrimination and accuracy of the model. Dxy and area under the curve (AUC) served as discriminative indicators, with higher values reflecting superior distinguishing performance. Similarly, lower U and Q values signified enhanced model reliability. R2 was used to measure the overall fitting effect of the model. All numerical comparisons were based solely on their absolute values, and the negative sign only represented the sorting direction.

All the analysis and figures were performed in SPSS (version 26.0) and R software (version 4.4.1). The survival analysis was performed using the R packages “survival” and “survminer”. Nomogram and ROC curve were generated using the R packages “rms” and “ggplot2”, respectively. All significance tests were two-tailed, and P values of <0.05 were used to denote statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics stratified by immune-related cardiotoxicities

A total of 187 participants with TNM stage III–IV NSCLC were enrolled. The baseline demographic and clinical features were detailed in Table 1. The median age of the cohort was 69 years (IQR 65–73 years). The majority of patients were male (n=159, 85.0%), and had a history of smoking (n=110, 58.8%), inclusive of former and current smokers. Regarding the ICI targets, 96.8% (n=181) of patients received anti-PD-1 treatment, while 3.2% (n=6) received anti-PD-L1 treatment. The histological classification distribution revealed that 47.1% (n=88) of NSCLC cases were squamous, and 52.9% (n=99) were non-squamous.

In terms of clinical indicators, the median serum IgM value was 0.84 g/L (IQR 0.57–1.24 g/L), and the median WBC count value was 6.50×109 (IQR 5.26×109–8.45×109). The median PLR stood at 185.1 (IQR 134.3–266.4). For tumor indicators, the median serum CEA value was 3.7 ng/mL (IQR 2.1–10.7 ng/mL), and the median serum CYFRA21-1 value was 3.7 ng/mL (IQR 2.4–8.1 ng/mL).

In this retrospective cohort study, all participants were categorized into two groups: 80 (42.8%) with immune-related cardiotoxicities and 107 (57.2%) without. A significant difference was observed in smoking history (P=0.007), ICI target (P=0.005), IgM (P=0.01), and CYFRA21-1 (P<0.001) levels between the two groups.

Severity and management of immune-related cardiotoxicities

Out of the observed 80 instances of immune-related cardiotoxicities, 72 (90%) were mild adverse events, falling within grades 1–2. Cardiotoxicity onset occurred within a median of 5 weeks (IQR 3–8 weeks) after ICI initiation. All cases underwent rigorous evaluation to exclude alternative etiologies: ischemic causes were ruled out by angiography or computed tomography (CT) in symptomatic patients, infectious myocarditis by viral polymerase chain reaction (PCR), and drug-induced injury by medication history review. These immune-related cardiotoxicities emerged during the initial phases of ICI treatment and were promptly addressed by drug withdrawal and corticosteroid therapy according to the guidelines (24). In the immune-related cardiotoxicities group, 15 patients ultimately died, whereas in the group without immune-related cardiotoxicities, 14 patients died at the conclusion of the follow-up period.

Survival analysis

The 1-, 2-, 3-, and 5-year survival rate of all participants was 0.903 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.857–0.951], 0.808 (95% CI: 0.734–0.889), 0.759 (95% CI: 0.666–0.865) and 0.528 (95% CI: 0.361–0.773), respectively. We conducted survival analyses stratified by immune-related cardiotoxicities and total irAEs. The results indicated that neither the immune-related cardiotoxicities (55 months vs. unreached, P=0.10) nor total irAEs (55 months vs. unreached, P=0.20) were correlated with OS in the enrolled individuals (Figure 2A,2B).

To explore the potential factors influencing the survival of advanced NSCLC patients with ICI treatment, we also conducted a series of survival analyses stratified by various baseline characteristics. For continuous numerical variables, we divided the patients into high- and low-level groups based on the median values. The Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis revealed that no significant associations were observed between sex (P=0.63; Figure 3A), histological (P=0.94; Figure 3B), smoking history (P=0.31; Figure 3C), age (P=0.85; Figure 3D), WBC (P=0.08; Figure 3E), or IgM (P=0.36; Figure 3F) and the median OS. Compared to low-level, high-level of PLR (54 months vs. unreached, P=0.002; Figure 3G) significantly prolonged the median OS. No significant association was found for CEA (P=0.40; Figure 3H), while high CYFRA21-1 levels (55 months vs. unreached, P=0.02; Figure 3I) also showed significant survival benefit. The inclusion of PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 in survival analysis was driven by their established roles in reflecting inflammatory status and tumor burden, both critical to ICI response and immune-related cardiotoxicity susceptibility.

Cox proportional hazards regression analysis and the construction of a prognostic model

To test and validate the potential prognostic factors, Cox proportional hazard regression analysis was employed (Table 2). The univariate analysis yielded compelling evidence that WBC count [hazard ratio (HR) 1.101, 95% CI: 1.023–1.186, P=0.01], PLR (HR 1.003, 95% CI: 1.001–1.004, P=0.001), CEA (HR 1.004, 95% CI: 1.002–1.006, P<0.001), and CYFRA21-1 (HR 1.004, 95% CI: 1.000–1.007, P=0.03) were significantly associated with OS.

Subsequently, we performed multivariate analysis to identify independent prognostic factors. Notably, PLR (HR 1.002, 95% CI: 1.000–1.004, P=0.02), CEA (HR 1.004, 95% CI: 1.002–1.007, P<0.001) and CYFRA21-1 (HR 1.004, 95% CI: 1.001–1.008, P=0.02) emerged as significant and independent predictors of OS.

Based on these findings, a nomogram was developed to evaluate the 1-, 3- and 5-year survival probabilities for NSCLC patients receiving ICI treatment (Figure 4A). Internal validation using the bootstrap method revealed that the nomogram accurately predicted short-term survival, as evidenced by the calibrated plots (Figure 4B). However, predictive performance slightly declined for longer follow-up periods. Moreover, the time-dependent ROC curve analysis indicated excellent discriminative ability, with AUC values of 0.757, 0.741, and 0.815 at 1-, 3-, and 5-year, respectively (Figure 4C).

Furthermore, we applied the optimal cutoff prognostic index to dichotomize patients into high-risk and low-risk categories. The Kaplan-Meier survival curve clearly demonstrated that ICI-treated advanced NSCLC patients with a low prognostic index exhibited significantly longer median OS (unreached vs. 46 months, P<0.001) (Figure 4D), corroborating the findings from the time-dependent ROC analysis.

To comprehensively evaluate the 3-variable (PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1) prognostic model, we augmented the model with additional variables and performed a comparative assessment of various models (Table 3). Compared with other models, this 3-variable prognostic model outperformed other models, exhibiting the optimal balance of accuracy and discrimination.

Logistic regression analysis of immune-related cardiotoxicities

Following the identification of significant prognostic correlations between PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 and survival outcomes in advanced NSCLC patients receiving ICI therapy, we subsequently investigated potential associations between these biomarkers and immune-related cardiotoxicity. Logistic regression analysis of indicators associated with immune-related cardiotoxicities was conducted and summarized in Table 4. Univariate logistic regression analysis reported that smoking was a prominent risk factor for immune-related cardiotoxicities [odds ratio (OR) 2.274, 95% CI: 1.252–4.128, P=0.007] among advanced NSCLC patients treated with ICIs. Notably, our analysis revealed no significant associations between PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 levels and immune-related cardiotoxicity in both univariate and multivariate models adjusted for smoking status (all adjusted P>0.05).

Discussion

This study revealed that immune-related cardiotoxicities did not prolong or shorten OS of advanced NSCLC patients treated with ICIs. The simple prognostic model including PLR, CEA and CYFRA21-1 demonstrated optimal performance in predicting efficacy and OS among ICI-treated advanced NSCLC patients. In addition, smoking may be a significant predictor of immune-related cardiotoxicities in advanced NSCLC patients.

We reported that immune-related cardiotoxicities did not influence ICI treatment efficacy. A single-center study also pointed out that the potential cardiotoxicity associated with immunotherapy may exert minimal influence on the long-term outlook or prognosis for patients with advanced NSCLC (25). Notably, the 2-year OS rate of the cohort (0.808, 95% CI: 0.734–0.889) was consistent with a former study (26), which reported that 2-year survival of NSCLC patients with ICI therapy was about 79–81%. Intriguingly, the median OS of all participants we investigated was longer than existing studies. A previous study has shown that medium to long-term ICI treatment (>2 years) can even prolong the OS of NSCLC patients to over 5–9 years (27). In addition, after about 2 years of treatment with ICI, the median OS of NSCLC patients was reported as approximately 20–30 months (7,8). Besides, a former study in inoperable stage III NSCLC patients who received consolidation pembrolizumab after chemoradiation, likewise found no significant OS or progression-free survival (PFS) differences in patients with and without irAEs (28). But the association between OS and immune-related cardiotoxicities has not been widely reported.

In addition, we also underscored the predictive significance of smoking in the detection of immune-related cardiotoxicities, possibly attributing to the cytokine disruption related to tobacco and its long-term impact on the immune system (29). Actually, the underlying mechanism of immune-related cardiotoxicities remained unclear. Off-targeting of ICIs and the existence of co-antigens between cardiomyocyte and tumor were considered possible causes of immune-related cardiotoxicities (30,31). Furthermore, during inflammatory states, the expression of PD-1 on compromised myocardial cells exerted a protective mechanism, safeguarding them from further harm (32).

This study reported that high pretreatment levels of PLR could not be beneficial for the efficacy of ICI therapy, whereas low levels of CEA and CYFRA21-1 also emerged as favorable prognostic indicators, aligning with previous studies (21,33-35). A large retrospective multi-center investigation also linked a high level of neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and PLR with inferior outcomes with nivolumab (36). Additionally, serum CYFRA21-1 and CEA levels had been proposed as reliable biomarkers for predicting ICI efficacy in NSCLC, which was consistent with our observations (22,37).

The three-variable model, including PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1, demonstrated optimal performance in predicting efficacy and OS among ICI-treated patients. A distinctive feature of the model lied in its simultaneous inclusion of peripheral blood biomarkers and serum tumor markers, distinguishing it from previous models. Lung immuno-oncology prognostic score (LIPS-3), a formerly reported model, also included PLR as an indicator for PD-L1 ≥50% advanced NSCLC patients treated by first-line pembrolizumab (38). Differently, models LIPs-3 and lung immune therapy evaluation (LITE) both incorporated Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG-PS) as a factor, to which we did not pay much attention. Other potential prognostic factors, such as body mass index, serum albumin, and serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), merited further investigation (39,40). While PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 showed no direct correlation with immune-related cardiotoxicity, their prognostic value for OS underscores the multifactorial determinants of ICI therapeutic efficacy. The association between elevated PLR (reflecting systemic inflammation) and tumor-associated antigens (CEA/CYFRA21-1) with both increased immune-related cardiotoxicities and diminished antitumor responses suggests a shared biological pathway warranting mechanistic investigation.

This retrospective multicenter study innovatively discussed the potential therapeutic efficacy of ICI treatment from the perspective of immune-related cardiotoxicities. We established a foundational understanding that could inform the clinical monitoring and management strategies for ICI-related cardio complications, thereby enhancing treatment safety and optimizing outcomes. There are also some limitations in this study. Despite employing multiple imputation, the retrospective nature introduced potential bias. While we had explored the impacts of PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 levels relative to their medians on OS, an optimal cutoff value for prediction remained to be established. Furthermore, the limited number of radiotherapy-treated patients (n=10) precluded robust assessment of its role in immune-related cardiotoxicity. Given the expanding clinical adoption of combined ICI-radiotherapy regimens, future studies with larger cohorts and detailed radiation parameters (e.g., dose, target sites) are warranted to clarify this relationship.

Conclusions

Our multi-center study revealed that, with timely management, mild immune-related cardiotoxicities that occurred in the early stage of treatment did not affect the efficacy and prognosis of ICI therapy. Notably, low levels of PLR, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 were associated with longer median OS and better efficacy. We introduced a prognostic model incorporating these three variables to predict ICI efficacy in advanced NSCLC. Additionally, smoking emerged as a potential predictor for immune-related cardiotoxicities. These findings contribute to the monitoring, management, prognostic evaluation, and development of personalized strategies for ICI treatment in advanced NSCLC patients.

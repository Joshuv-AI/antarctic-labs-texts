Mismatched parameters selection and error sensitivity analysis of easy-off TEC worm drive
Authors: Chongfei Huai, Shenghai Sun, Jingtian Gai, Zifei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper establishes a geometric model of easy-off Toroidal Enveloping Conical (TEC) worm drive that incorporates errors, and based on this, investigates the influence of mismatched parameters on the tooth contact performance and the sensitivity to errors of this type of worm pair. The study introduces an Adaptive Extremum Search (AES) method to efficiently solve complex nonlinear tooth contact equations, facilitating the accurate determination of instantaneous contact points. Additionally, a mismatch design scheme for this worm pair is proposed, focusing on minimizing the number of mismatched parameters. Numerical examples illustrate the impact of varying mismatched design parameters on tooth contact characteristics. The findings reveal that the shaft angle error exerts the most significant impact on the tooth contact characteristics of the ease-off worm gear. The results also demonstrate that a well-designed mismatched worm pair exhibits strong adaptability to errors. However, it is important to note that excessively small values for the mismatch modification parameters can reduce this adaptability.Keywords: Ease-off worm drive, Mismatched parameters, Tooth contact property, AES method, Error sensitivitySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As a recently developed transmission mechanism based on line contact, the TEC worm drive presents a compelling alternative to traditional conical worm drives, particularly in high-performance scenarios demanding large reduction ratios1. Nevertheless, the gear community has unanimously recognized that line-contact gearing is highly susceptible to errors and deformations. These issues can easily lead to edge contact of teeth, curvature interference within the tooth surface couple, and consequently, premature gear unit failure.

To overcome this limitation, a method known as mismatched modification has been introduced2. By imposing a deliberate ease-off modification on the tooth surface, this technique transforms line contact into point contact. The underlying principle is to reduce error sensitivity by introducing additional degrees of freedom to the contact interface.

Subsequently, extensive research has been conducted on the modification of gear transmission misalignment3–9. Of particular note is Litvin’s work, in which he systematically investigated mismatched modification and tooth contact analysis across various gear systems. He has further enriched the local analysis method to evaluate the contact performance and calculate motion errors in point-contact gear pairs, making significant contributions to the field10–13.

In a previous study, the mismatch design of the TEC worm gear transmission was implemented, and a worm gear transmission form with good contact characteristics was obtained14. However, the previous research did not conduct in-depth studies on how the mismatched parameters affect the tooth contact property and sensitivity to errors of the worm gear pair.

Qualitatively, the success of mismatched modification hinges on the existence of a sufficient number of reasonable instantaneous points of contact and the satisfactory performance of tooth surface contacts. Prior to analyzing the contact performance, the identification of the required instantaneous contact point is crucial. Once the instantaneous contact point is accurately determined, the TCA (tooth contact analysis) technique can be employed to delve into the attributes of the mismatched gear drive3,15. Consequently, the precise determination of this point emerges as a fundamental bottleneck in the application of mismatched modification.

However, in the context of worm gearing, particularly conical worm gearing, the determination of instantaneous contact points typically necessitates solving intricate multivariate nonlinear equations. Two pivotal issues arise during this process: (1) how to ascertain the existence of solutions to these complex nonlinear equations; (2) how to furnish reasonable initial values for the iteration. The TEC worm drive poses additional challenges due to the scarcity of relevant computational expertise. There are numerous methods related to solving these problems in the existing literature2,16–20, they have demonstrated excellent performance in addressing specific gear transmission forms. However, due to the more complex tooth surfaces of the TEC worm gear pair, the involved nonlinear equation systems are intricate and contain unknown parameters such as motion parameters and geometric parameters, requiring a more targeted and efficient solution strategy. As a result, the initial research work mainly relied on a large number of repetitive calculations, which significantly reduced the computational efficiency.

To address the above challenges, this paper proposes an adaptive extremum search method to ensure the effective implementation of the mismatch design for the TEC worm drive. Building on this foundation, the influence laws of mismatch design parameters on the contact characteristics and error sensitivity of the worm pair is systematically investigated. Furthermore, to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the mismatched worm drive, extensive tooth contact analysis and error sensitivity analyses are conducted.

This study not only enriches the mathematical foundation of mismatched modification but also facilitates the design of point-contact TEC worm drives with superior performance. Furthermore, the proposed methodology establishes a valuable reference framework for worm gearing in general, enabling the enhancement of meshing characteristics and operational stability in practical engineering applications.

Nevertheless, identifying the minimum points of the multivariate function F poses considerable practical challenges, owing to the inherent complexity of the equations and the nature of the unknown parameters involved.

To address these difficulties, an adaptive extremum search (AES) method is proposed herein for locating the minima of F. In essence, the AES method constitutes an optimization algorithm capable of dynamically adjusting its search strategy in response to interim results, thereby facilitating the pursuit of a global optimum. Instead, only the neighborhood radius needs to be reduced to approximate the real minimum point. To control the step size of change, can be used to replace, and the convergence speed during calculation can be controlled by adjusting the value of . If the function value at the central point is not the minimum among the three points, then the position of the central point needs to be adjusted. Generally, the point with the smallest function value among the above three points can be re-designated as the new central point, and then the other two points will also be reselected based on the predetermined domain radius. Similarly, the calculation process mentioned above will also be followed in the λ2-axis. When it comes to adjusting the domain radius, the initial domain radius value of can be replaced with . Taking into account both computational efficiency and accuracy, and based on a vast number of test calculations, recommended values for k1 and k2 can be obtained as follows: k1 ≈ 0.15 ~ 0.25, and k2 ≈ 0.11 ~ 0.35.

Based on the described computations, a sequence of function values such as F1, F2, F3, … will be obtained. By selecting the minimum value from this series as the initial iterative value for the system of equations, the precise determination of the initial iterative value for complex nonlinear equations can be effectively addressed.

The method is naturally extendable to an n-dimensional setting. With each increment in dimensionality, the requisite additional computational effort is effectively reduced to about 1/n of that in the original dimensional context.

Influence of mismatch design parameters on the tooth contact characteristics of worm gear pairs

In this section, the influence patterns of different mismatch design parameters on the tooth contact characteristics of worm pairs will be uncovered. The analysis is conducted through numerical simulations, wherein results from parameter variations are systematically benchmarked against those of the baseline case E1. Owing to space constraints, only representative numerical examples are presented to illustrate the general trends observed across the extensive simulation dataset.

Influence of process drive ratio on the tooth contact characteristics

Compared with the process transmission ratio in Table 2, the process transmission ratio in this example E2 has decreased by 0.026, i.e., i42 = 50.017.

As shown in Fig. 10, when the modification amount of the process transmission ratio for the worm gear decreases compared to E1, the position of the datum point on the i-flank of the worm pair shifts toward the large-end direction of both the conical worm and conical worm gear. However, it can be observed that the length occupied by the instantaneous contact traces on the helical surface of the worm and the coverage range on the tooth surface of the worm gear do not change significantly. As shown in Fig. 11, the datum point on the e-flank of the worm wheel also shifts toward the large-end direction of the worm and worm wheel compared to Example E1, but the magnitude of this change is smaller than that on the i-flank of the worm pair. Meanwhile, it can be noted that the major axis of the instantaneous contact ellipse at the instantaneous contact points near the small-end of the worm gear on the e-flank increases, indicating a decrease in the relative principal curvature along the first principal direction of the tooth surface at these points and a corresponding improvement in the tooth surface load-bearing capacity.

Influence of outside radius at small end of the conical hob on the tooth contact characteristics

In this example E6, the outside radius of the small end of the conical hob is 25.58 mm, which is 0.08 mm larger than that in the example E1. It was found in the study that to ensure proper meshing of the worm and worm gear, the addendum circle radius at the small end of the conical hob must be greater than that at the small end of the worm.

Meanwhile, it can be observed from these figures that increasing the modification amount of the outside radius at the small end of the conical hob leads to a reduction in the variation range of the angular error of the worm gear on both sides of the worm pair compared to Example E1, which helps mitigate the impact during tooth surface contact of the worm drive.

Therefore, it is advisable to prioritize the control of the shaft angle error in practical production applications of the ease-off TEC worm pair.

Influence of instantaneous contact ellipse on error sensitivity of worm pair

The major axis of the instantaneous contact ellipse on the point-contact tooth surface generally reflects the load-bearing capacity of the worm gear pair. However, in existing studies, the influence of the major axis length of the instantaneous contact ellipse on the tooth surface contact performance has not been sufficiently investigated. In this study, it was found that the ratio of the major axis to the minor axis of the instantaneous contact ellipse also has a significant impact on the error sensitivity of ease-off worm pairs. In the following sections, this phenomenon will be elucidated through two calculation examples, E13 and E14.

As listed in Table 7, the ratio of the minor axis to the major axis of the instantaneous contact ellipse in Example E13 is significantly smaller than the corresponding value in Example E1. Among them, the difference between the mismatched parameters of the former and the standard parameters, that is, the difference in the shape of the tooth surfaces of the corresponding ease-off worm pair compared to the normal line-contact worm, is also significantly smaller than that of the latter. The relevant numerical simulation results are depicted in Fig. 30, where it can be observed that the major axis of the instantaneous contact ellipse in Example E13 is noticeably longer than that in Fig. 8. When an assembly error is applied to the worm gear pair in Example E13, as illustrated in Fig. 31, it is evident that the meshing characteristics undergo significant changes under the influence of the error, leading to a marked decline in the meshing performance of the worm gear pair.

The reason for this phenomenon lies in the fact that as the ratio of the minor axis to the major axis of the instantaneous contact ellipse decreases, the ellipse becomes more elongated, in other words, its shape approaches closer to a line. Under such circumstances, the point-contact worm gear pair tends to behave like a line-contact worm gear pair. These results also indirectly reflect that line-contact worm gear pairs exhibit higher sensitivity to errors, which underscores the significance of mismatched design for toroidal-enveloping cone worm gear drives in this study, namely, it can reduce their sensitivity to errors.

Conclusions

A systematic mathematical model has been established to investigate the error sensitivity of the easy-off TEC worm gearing. To address the challenge of solving the associated complex nonlinear tooth contact equations, an Adaptive Extremum Seeking (AES) method is proposed for efficiently determining solution existence and obtaining potential exact solutions. A more easily implementable mismatched technical solution is presented, and its parametric influence on tooth contact characteristics is investigated. The error sensitivity of mismatched worm pairs is evaluated by introducing four types of assembly errors, with particular attention to the effect of the instantaneous contact ellipse’s axis ratio. The main conclusions drawn are as follows:

A reasonably designed easy-off TEC worm pair exhibits good robustness against assembly errors.

The proposed Adaptive Extremum Seeking (AES) method proves effective in solving the tooth contact equations for the easy-off TEC worm drive.

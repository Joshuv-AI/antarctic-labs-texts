Comment on “Does Salmonella co-infection worsen Histomonas meleagridis infection in Turkeys?”
Authors: Sangameshwar Manikya, Varshini Vadhithala, Rajnish Kumar, Pankaj Nainwal
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the study examining whether Salmonella Typhimurium co-infection modifies the clinical course of Histomonas meleagridis infection in turkey poults (Rafieian-Naeini et al., 2026). The factorial design and simultaneous evaluation of performance indices, lesion severity, colonization dynamics, intestinal morphology, and hepatic apoptosis-related transcripts provide a structured framework to interrogate pathogen interaction under controlled conditions.

The interpretation of absent HM × ST interaction effects warrants cautious consideration from a statistical perspective. Interaction testing in a 2 × 2 factorial model requires greater power than main-effect detection, yet growth performance was analyzed at the pen level with six replicates per treatment and gene-expression outcomes were derived from one bird per pen. Variability in morphometric and colonization measures, as reflected by reported SEM values, suggests that moderate interaction effects of biological relevance may remain undetected (Metzler-Zebeli et al., 2019). In production settings, even subtle synergistic impairments in feed efficiency or mortality can translate into economic impact. Incorporating prospective power calculations for interaction contrasts or expanding replicate numbers in future trials would strengthen inference regarding true biological independence between these pathogens.

The temporal design also influences interpretation. Both pathogens were administered concurrently at day 18, with primary assessments concluding at day 28. Cecal recovery data indicate early robust ST colonization at days 22 and 25, whereas HM-driven tissue damage and mortality manifested progressively. Pathogen interplay within the gut ecosystem is often sequential, shaped by inflammatory cascades and microbiota restructuring over time (Kogut and Enrique, 2023). Evaluating staggered challenge models or extending observation beyond ten days post-inoculation could clarify whether transient synergy emerges during early inflammatory phases that may not persist to the terminal endpoint.

A further mechanistic consideration arises from the significant interaction observed for cecal muscularis thickness, which was greatest in co-infected birds despite unchanged performance and mortality. This structural remodeling suggests additive or synergistic tissue-level responses that were not paralleled by overt clinical deterioration. Without protein-level confirmation of inflammatory mediators, barrier components, or fibrosis-associated pathways, it remains uncertain whether this represents compensatory adaptation, early fibrotic remodeling, or localized inflammatory amplification without systemic consequence. Integrating functional barrier assays or immunohistochemical analyses would enhance mechanistic interpretation of how extracellular protozoal damage interfaces with bacterial colonization (Matteo Cuccato et al., 2022).

Collectively, the findings provide valuable evidence that, within this experimental framework, S. Typhimurium does not substantially intensify Histomoniasis outcomes. Refining statistical power, temporal modeling, and mechanistic correlates may further clarify pathogen interplay relevant to commercial turkey production.

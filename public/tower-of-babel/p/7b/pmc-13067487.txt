Visual outcome of a diffractive trifocal intraocular lens in eyes with short axial length
Authors: Rui Qin, Yiyun Liu, Zihan Shen, Dehai Liu, Zeka Li, Zhengze Sun, Hong Qi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to investigate the visual quality of short-axial length cataractous eyes after bilateral implantation of the trifocal intraocular lens (IOL) AcrySof IQ PanOptix.MethodsThis prospective observational study enrolled bilateral short-axial lengths (≤ 22.5 mm) cataract patients after implantation of IOL AcrySof IQ PanOptix. Monocular and binocular uncorrected distance, corrected distance, uncorrected intermediate (60 cm), distance-corrected intermediate, uncorrected near (40 cm), and distance-corrected near (UDVA, CDVA, UIVA, DCIVA, UNVA and DCNVA) visual acuity were measured with follow-up of three months. Visual disturbances, patient satisfaction and spectacle independence were assessed using a questionnaire.ResultsThe study included 40 patients with a total of 80 eyes, with 80.0% female. The average axial length was 22.05 ± 0.39 mm. Monocular UDVA were 0.05 ± 0.09, CDVA were 0.00 ± 0.07, UIVA were 0.09 ± 0.10, DCIVA were 0.04 ± 0.08, UNVA were 0.11 ± 0.13 and DCNVA were 0.05 ± 0.09. Binocular UDVA were 0.02 ± 0.08, CDVA were − 0.02 ± 0.05, UIVA were 0.03 ± 0.08, DCIVA were 0.00 ± 0.06, UNVA were 0.02 ± 0.06 and DCNVA were 0.02 ± 0.06. Regarding adverse visual phenomena, 37.5% of patients reported significant starbursts and 27.5% reported significant halos. However, overall satisfaction reached 95%, with all patients achieving complete spectacle independence.ConclusionsWith detailed preoperative evaluation, the trifocal IOL provided satisfactory visual outcomes for patients with short-axial length. Since the trifocal IOL can significantly improve the symptoms of hyperopia and presbyopia, patient satisfaction may be higher.

Introduction

Cataract remains a leading cause of blindness worldwide [1, 2]. Age-related cataract is the most prevalent form of cataract among adults, typically beginning in middle to late adulthood [3]. Asia is a primary epicenter for cataract, with a prevalence rate of 42% among individuals aged 60 and older [4]. With advancing age, the incidence of age-related cataracts rises markedly, from 3.23% in individuals aged 45–49 years to 65.78% in those aged 85–89 years [5].

A short axial length (AL) is generally defined as an axial length of ≤ 22.0 mm, with some studies defining it as ≤ 22.5 mm [6–8]. Approximately 9.5% of cataract surgery patients have an AL ≤ 22.5 mm [6]. Short AL can be associated with abnormal ocular anatomy, including microphthalmia, small cornea, shallow anterior chamber, thickened lens, and thickened sclera, increasing the complexity of cataract surgery [9]. Patients with short AL are more likely to have concomitant ocular diseases such as retinal detachment, retinal dysplasia, primary angle-closure glaucoma, and exfoliation syndrome [10–13]. Most patients with short AL are hyperopic, experience presbyopia earlier, and have a more pressing need for near vision.

Refractive cataract surgery can improve visual acuity, reduce the incidence of primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG), correct presbyopia and refractive errors in patients with cataract combined short AL [14, 15]. Therefore, conducting refractive cataract surgery in individuals with short AL is particularly important. Bilateral implantation of trifocal intraocular lens (IOL) is an effective strategy for refractive cataract surgery, providing patients with better vision and correcting presbyopia and refractive errors [16]. Although many patients with short axial length cataract without concomitant ocular complications choose bilateral implantation of trifocal IOLs, there is currently a lack of prospective observational studies evaluating the visual quality, especially the correction of presbyopia, of trifocal IOLs in patients with short axial length. We evaluated the efficacy, predictability and safety of bilateral trifocal IOL implantation in patients with short AL at 3 months postoperatively to provide guidance for improving vision and correcting presbyopia in patients with short AL.

Methods

Patients

This is a prospective observational study. We enrolled 40 patients who underwent bilateral implantation of trifocal intraocular lenses TNFT00 (Alcon Laboratories, Fort Worth, TX) at the Department of Ophthalmology, Peking University Third Hospital, Beijing, China between January 2023 and September 2024. This study was consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki for the use of human participants in biomedical research and received the approval of the Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital (M2022756). An informed consent for surgical procedure and participation in research was obtained from every patient. G-Power was used to analyze the required sample size in the study, with the SE (spherical equivalent) as the primary efficacy analysis. The medium effect size = 0.25, 1 − β = 0.95, and significance level α = 0.05 were prespecified. The sample size was 35 or more cases. Accounting for a 10% lost-to-follow-up rate, a total of 40 patients were ultimately enrolled in the study.

Monocular and binocular visual acuity and subjective visual quality were assessed at 3 months postoperatively. For the assessment of monocular visual quality, one eye of each patient was randomly selected. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) any gender; (2) age between 45 and 80 years; (3) axial length ≤ 22.5 mm; (4) expected postoperative corneal astigmatism ≤ 1.5 D; (5) corneal spherical aberration and high-order aberrations ≤ 0.5 μm within a 4 mm pupil. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) occurrence of severe intraoperative complications; (2) history of other ocular diseases such as glaucoma, retinopathy, optic nerve diseases; (3) history of cornea refractive surgery; (4) inability to complete 3-month follow-up.

Intraocular lens

The AcrySof IQ PanOptix is a trifocal IOL with a combination of diffractive and refractive design on its anterior surface. It is single-piece designed, foldable, preloaded, ultraviolet (UV) and blue light filtering, and made of a hydrophobic material acrylate/methacrylate copolymer [17]. This IOL has a 6.0 mm biconvex optic with an inner 4.5 mm diffractive zone and an outer refractive zone, a total diameter of 13.0 mm and a 2 open-loop haptics. Its edge is designed to be 360-degree square to prevent posterior capsule opacification. The posterior lens surface is spherical, and the anterior surface is aspheric with a -0.18 spherical aberration. The IOL is available in a diopter (D) range of + 6.0 to + 30.0 D (0.5 D increments) and + 31.0 D to + 34.0 D (1.0 D increments). The IOL divides the incoming light to create an intermediate addition power of + 2.17 D (60 cm) and a + 3.25 D (40 cm) near add power.

Preoperative examinations

Thorough preoperative examination was performed in all patients, including the examination of uncorrected and corrected visual acuity (UDVA, CDVA), tonometry, slitlamp examination, manifest refraction, biometric evaluation (IOLMaster 700, Carl Zeiss Meditec AG), corneal topography and aberrations (Pentacam HR, Oculus Optikgerate GmbH), dilated fundoscopy and retinal optical coherence tomography (Cirrus 4000, Carl Zeiss Meditec AG). Visual acuity was recorded in the form of logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR). The Barret Universal II formula carried by IOLMaster 700 was applied to calculate the IOL power. All patients received eye drops 3 days before surgery, including 1 drop of levofloxacin and 1 drop of pranoprofen 4 times a day.

Surgical technique

All surgeries were performed by the same experienced surgeon using topical or retrobulbar anesthesia. For eyes with regular corneal astigmatism less than 0.50 D, surgeon used a primary incision at 135° and an auxiliary incision at 45°. For the other eyes with higher corneal astigmatism, the primary incision was loc ated at the steep meridian. After the anterior capsulorhexis with a 5.5–6.0 mm diameter, the phacoemulsification and IOL implantation were then performed. The Lumera 700 live microscope (Carl Zeiss Meditec) and the Callisto Eye System (Carl Zeiss Meditec) provided real-time location and eye tracking technique to improve the accuracy of all aspects of surgery. Postoperative medication regimens were consistent for all patients, including 1 drop each of levofloxacin, prednisolone acetate and pranoprofen 4 times a day for 1 month. The frequency decreased by 1 time a week.

Postoperative examinations

Patients would undergo routine examinations on the first postoperative day and one week later, including visual acuity, intraocular pressure, and slit-lamp examination. After 1 and 3 months, patients would undergo examinations including tonometry, slit lamp examination, manifest refraction, monocular and bilateral uncorrected and corrected distance (5 m) visual acuity (UDVA, CDVA), uncorrected and distance-corrected intermediate (60 cm) and near (40 cm) visual acuity (UIVA, DCIVA, UNVA and DCNVA). After 3 months patients would also undergo examinations including monocular and bilateral defocus curve and contrast sensitivity (CS), high-order aberrations and questionnaire.

Using OPTEC 6500 Vision Tester (Stereo Optical Co., Inc.), the CS was evaluated in four conditions [photopic (85 cd/m2), mesopic (3 cd/m2), photopic with glare and mesopic with glare] and at five spatial frequencies [3, 6, 12, and 18 cycles per degree (cpd)], respectively. The high-order aberrations measured by iTrace system (Tracey Technologies Corp. Houston, TX) included total high-order aberration, spherical aberration, coma aberration and trefoil aberration in 3 mm pupil diameter.

As for the questionnaire, its first part evaluated the spectacle independence at different distances, the incidence of several adverse photic phenomena including glare, halo and starburst, and the overall satisfaction at different distances [18]. Adverse photic phenomena were graded on a scale of 1 to 5. (1: None; 2: Little; 3: Mild; 4: Moderate; 5: Severe). Satisfaction were also rated on a scale of 1 to 5. (1: Very dissatisfied; 2: Dissatisfied; 3: Neutral; 4: Satisfied; 5: Very Satisfied).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were recorded as means and standard deviations (mean ± SD), range, median and interquartile range, and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Statistical analysis was performed with SPSS Statistics for Windows software (version 24.0, IBM Corp.). For examination results, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to verify the normal distribution. Then one-way ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis H test was used to compare the means. For the correlation between two variables, Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used if both variables were continuous and normally distributed and Spearman’s correlation coefficient was used at least one variable was non-normally distributed. The paired t-tests were used for pre–post comparisons. Multiple comparisons were corrected by using Bonferroni method. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Safety

Preoperative monocular CDVA was 0.18 ± 0.15 LogMAR, which significantly improved to 0.02 ± 0.03 LogMAR postoperatively (p < 0.05). No significant complications were observed during the 3-month follow-up. As of September 2024, no patient experienced severe IOL decentration or posterior capsule opacification.

Discussion

A previous study from our group assessed the visual outcomes after implantation of trifocal IOLs (AT LISA tri 839MP, Carl Zeiss, Germany) in patients with short axial length [18]. Although postoperative visual acuity improved significantly, the sample size was small. Nevertheless, the evaluation of visual outcomes for all types of trifocal and extended depth-of-focus IOLs requires continuous advancement.

Among the short-AL cataract patients included in our study, 32 were female (80.0%). This aligns with the gender distribution among short-AL cataract patients in another study conducted by our research group (75.0%) [18]. 36 (90.0%) of the patients we included were hypermetropic or emmetropic preoperatively, while four exhibited low myopia. Notably, none of these four patients had a history of wearing nearsighted glasses, which we attribute to age-related cataracts. Progressing nuclear and posterior capsular cataracts could induce myopia secondary to the lens [19].

For efficacy, three months postoperatively, all patients achieved better than 10/20 uncorrected visual acuity for distance, intermediate, and near vision. Notably, distance visual acuity was better than that of intermediate and near. In the visual acuity below 16/20, the highest proportion of patients exhibited uncorrected near visual acuity. Ayoung Choi et al. reported that six months postoperatively, short-AL patients achieved better UNVA (0.02 ± 0.05 LogMAR) and UDVA (0.02 ± 0.06 LogMAR) compared to our results [20]. We attribute this difference to our shorter follow-up period of three months. Junya Kawamura et al. reported a large-scale study, although not all patients were short AL, the three-month postoperative UNVA (0.19 ± 0.11 LogMAR) and UIVA (0.11 ± 0.09 LogMAR) were similar to our findings [21]. In 95% of eyes in our study, UDVA, UIVA and UNVA reached 16/20 or better at three months postoperatively. Only two patients had corrected intermediate visual acuity less than 16/20. Preoperatively, due to lens opacification, fundus examination revealed blurred optic discs. Subsequent fundus evaluation postoperatively revealed abnormal cup-to-disc ratios in these patients, although visual field tests showed no significant abnormalities. Despite no reported history of acute PACG, we suspect glaucomatous optic neuropathy in these two patients. Glaucoma-induced optic neuropathy can impair visual acuity, especially at low-luminance foci, and may necessitate spectacle correction [22, 23]. Defocus curves demonstrated good visual performance across the range of + 0.5D to -2.50D, both monocularly and binocularly, although intermediate and near vision were inferior to distance vision. These findings are similar to those previously reported in patients with normal and long AL [24–26].

For predictability, while the mean postoperative SE for patients with short AL were close to 0, the mean postoperative sphere is 0.22 D and the mean postoperative cylinder is -0.40 D. Mohsen Farvardin et al. did not classify patients based on axial length. In their study, the mean postoperative sphere was 0.35 ± 0.12 D, the mean cylinder was − 0.89 ± 0.17 D, and the mean SE was 0.19 ± 0.29 D [27]. Ayoung Choi et al. reported a mean SE of -0.04 ± 0.39 D 6-months postoperatively without specifying sphere and cylinder [20]. In our study, the median ME was 0.128 D, although Melles’ large-scale analysis suggested a higher likelihood of myopic shift in patients with short AL [6]. The mean MAE in our study was 0.33 ± 0.23 D, and the Barrett universal II formula demonstrated relatively good predictability. Nevertheless, in the study by Ayoung Choi et al., the Barrett universal II formula resulted in a mean MAE of 0.44 ± 0.30 D for patients with short AL, while the Holladay 1 and Hoffer Q formulas resulted in lower MAE (0.32 ± 0.22 D and 0.32 ± 0.21 D, respectively) [20]. Hyunggoo Kwon et al. suggested that implanting a capsular tension ring (CTR) could reduce the posterior bowing of the Panoptix IOL within the capsular bag, thereby decreasing the MAE [28]. In our study, 33 patients (82.5%) exhibited a postoperative MAE of less than 0.50 D. The remaining seven patients (17.5%) had a postoperative MAE of 0.50 D or greater, which might be attributable to the incomplete stabilization of refractive outcomes at the 3-month postoperative assessment.

For safety, postoperative patients demonstrated a significant improvement in CDVA. No notable complications were observed postoperatively, and posterior capsule opacification was absent. Hyunggoo Kwon reported that six patients (20%) underwent posterior capsulotomy 3-months postoperatively [29].

For CS, postoperative patients exhibited satisfactory performance in photopic conditions. However, a marked decline was observed in mesopic conditions, especially at high spatial frequencies. In Satish Modi’s study, patients demonstrated lower CS in mesopic conditions but only experienced a decrease at spatial frequencies above 6 cycles per degree [30]. Conversely, Virgilio Galvis reported lower CS in mesopic conditions across all spatial frequencies [24]. High-order aberrations are the primary cause of the patients’ adverse photic phenomena. The patient exhibited a predominantly trefoil aberration in total eye, while spherical aberration was the primary high-order aberration intraocularly. Shen Jiying et al. reported similar findings in eyes with long AL, however, the corneal diameter at the time of measurement was not specified in their study [26].

The patients mentioned starbursts and halos as the primary adverse photic phenomena, with only six patients mentioning mild glare. Study by Satish Modi et al. on emmetropic eyes and Jiying Shen et al. on eyes with normal to long AL both indicated that halos and starbursts were the primary adverse photic phenomena [26, 30]. Some patients reported experiencing glare, particularly in the temporal visual field, which we attribute to potential IOL tilt. In Mariska Ackerman’s study, adverse events primarily manifested as glare and halos [31]. However, our study did not assess the severity of patients’ disturbance. Comparing the outcomes in normal AL eyes, the short AL patients mentioned a relatively high rate of halos and starbursts. A high-power IOL is inherently a thicker lens with steeper curvature. This can introduce higher levels of spherical aberration and other higher-order aberrations. When these inherent IOL aberrations combine with corneal aberrations, the overall optical quality degrades, making disruptive visual phenomena from point light sources more pronounced, especially under mesopic conditions when the pupil is dilated. However, these symptoms decreased over time or remained stable at 3 months.

Regarding visual satisfaction, no patients expressed dissatisfaction with their post-operative distance vision due to its significant improvement. Nonetheless, some (32.5% and 30.0%) patients mentioned concerns about intermediate and near visual acuity.

Although most patients achieved a distance visual acuity of 1.0, they were not entirely satisfied, primarily due to the interference of adverse photic phenomena. All patients expressed satisfaction with their overall vision. Notably, none of the patients in our study required nearsighted or farsighted glasses post-operatively. These findings were similar with previous research [29, 32, 33].

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. (1) We assessed outcomes at three months post-operatively, and the refractive outcomes at six months may be more accurate. Neuroadaptation to trifocal optics and stability of refraction may continue to improve up to 6 months. (2) When measuring high-order aberrations, we chose to measure within a 3 mm zone due to insufficient pupil diameter in some patients. (3) Additionally, while we evaluated the severity and incidence of adverse visual phenomena, we did not assess the degree of patient disturbance caused by these symptoms. (4) The subjective nature of the questionnaire did not have a validated quality-of-life instrument. (5) The absence of a control group with normal axial length is another limitation of our study. In future studies, we will add a control group and conduct a cohort study.

Conclusions

This study systematically evaluated the presbyopia-correcting performance, visual quality and subjective satisfaction of eyes with short AL after the implantation of the AcrySof IQ PanOptix. With detailed preoperative evaluation and postoperative follow-up, the trifocal IOL provided stable and satisfactory visual outcomes for patients with short AL. Since the trifocal IOL can significantly treat the symptoms of hyperopia and presbyopia, patients’ satisfaction may be higher.

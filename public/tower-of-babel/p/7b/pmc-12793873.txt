Reply
Authors: Tjitske D Groenveld, Esther HW Breunissen, Dominique VM Verhaert, Harry van Goor, Peter Damman
Published: 2025

We would like to thank the authors for their thoughtful and constructive feedback on our study.1 We appreciate the interest in our work and the relevant points you have raised regarding both the use of biomarkers and the potential for intraprocedural virtual reality (VR) application.

The authors suggest to include physiological and biochemical markers such as cortisol to complement patient-reported outcome measures. We agree that such biomarkers may offer valuable insights into the acute stress response. However, these biomarkers do not always correspond with subjective patient experiences, as individuals may report high stress levels without exhibiting corresponding physiological changes.2 Since our primary aim was to improve patient comfort using VR, we deemed patient-reported outcome measures the most appropriate outcome. Repeated blood sampling required to measure dynamic biomarkers might have delayed routine workflow and induced stress and discomfort potentially influencing our main outcome. Nevertheless, we acknowledge the added value of biomarkers to enable more mechanistic studies, particularly combining both subjective and objective outcome measures.3 This integrated approach could offer a more comprehensive understanding of the mechanism of action of VR therapy as well as patients’ stress response.

The authors also suggest extending VR exposure into the procedural phase. We appreciate the interest in this promising direction. In our initial study, we opted to limit the VR use to the preprocedural period for 2 main reasons. First, anxiety levels are typically the highest preprocedure. Second, we were concerned that the VR use throughout the procedure may interfere with communication between the interventional cardiologist and the patient, potentially disrupting the procedural workflow. In 2 cases, VR was continued throughout the entire procedure at the patients’ request and with the cardiologist’s approval. Both patients and cardiologists reported a highly positive experience. These cases were included in the analysis, as they were not expected to significantly influence the primary outcome (T3) which was assess before the extended VR use began.4

The VRinCard outcomes have encouraged us to design a new study investigating the use of VR during electrophysiological procedures (VR inEP) including device-implantations and ablations. Primary aim of this study is to evaluate feasibility, patient comfort, and patient and provider satisfaction of VR therapy during electrophysical procedures which are generally performed with local anesthesia. The use of biomarkers and extended VR exposure during the procedure will be incorporated in this protocol. This might give further insight into the mechanism of action of VR therapy and patients’ stress response. Furthermore, extending the use of VR therapy throughout the entire procedure may enhance and broaden its effect.

Anion-engineered CsInTiS₄₋ₓOₓ (x = 0.8) quantum dots for enhanced nonlinear photonics and optoelectronics
Authors: M. S. El-Bana, Abdullah Alsulami, M. A. M. El-Mansy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Computational modelling of anion-substituted quantum dots reveals that controlled oxygen incorporation into CsInTiS₄ nanostructures induces profound modifications in electronic structure and optical response. Therefore, in our study, we examined 20% anion-site oxygen incorporation into CsInTiS4 (i.e., CsInTiS3.2O0.8 QDs) using methods based on density functional theory and dielectric function optical modelling. Our results regarding structural relaxation and simulated X-ray diffraction indicate that sulfur’s substitution with oxygen induces lattice contraction and structural reorganization. Introduction

The demand for semiconductors whose band structures can be modified while preserving essential light coupling increases as optoelectronics advances toward longer wavelengths into the infrared (IR). Of all such semiconductors, quantum dots (QDs) are unique candidates for tuning optoelectronic properties because their electronic structures can be modified according to various tuning parameters at different scales. This includes modifications in scale from the nanoscale to the macroscale and in structure from the meso- to the atomic level. Such changes allow modulating the absorption edge, dielectric response, and carrier dynamics over ranges inaccessible in bulk solids1–4. That intrinsic chemical and electronic tunability of QDs explains why many of the known QD materials have found widespread use in displays, imaging, photovoltaics, sensors, and why ongoing compositional development continues in the quest for materials for IR detection and nonlinear photonics2–5.

The need for tunable narrow-gap semiconductors also makes chalcogenide nanomaterials highly competitive candidates for light–matter interaction due to their typically large polarizability and intrinsic covalent bonding. Most oxides of titanates—that is, classical titanates such as BaTiO3 and SrTiO3—are well-known to have robust structural stability due to the corner-shared TiO6 perovskite network, along with rich dielectric properties. However, their wide band gap limits their use for longer wavelength optoelectronic applications6,7. That dynamic suggests that anion engineering could be a valuable approach for deriving optical semiconductors from types of titanate structures if their anion chemistry is expanded from simple oxides to softer, more polarizable chalcogenide compositions.

While conventional cation substitution has been extensively utilized to tune the electronic properties of semiconductors, it primarily perturbs the conduction or valence band edges via gradual shifts in metal d- or s-orbital energy levels, often yielding limited modulation of the fundamental bandgap and minimal disruption to lattice symmetry. In stark contrast, anion substitution (anion engineering) directly reconstructs the valence band maximum, which is predominantly governed by the anion p-orbitals, and profoundly alters the metal-anion bond covalency and orbital hybridization8,9. Recent landmark studies on heteroanionic materials have demonstrated that strategically replacing a fraction of the anion sublattice (e.g., O²⁻ for S²⁻) can induce dramatic structural reorganizations, including massive lattice contractions and fundamental space group transitions, which are rarely achievable through cation doping alone10. Furthermore, anion engineering provides an unprecedented lever to drastically narrow the bandgap and amplify the dielectric screening and optical transition probabilities, thereby unlocking extreme optoelectronic and nonlinear optical responses that lie beyond the reach of traditional cation-substituted counterparts11. Motivated by these profound distinctions, our study strategically targets the anion site to exploit the unique degrees of freedom offered by hetero-anionic solid solutions.

From that perspective, because valence states typically derive from oxygen or chalcogenide p orbitals, chalcogenide oxides or any mixed-anion composition readily present a new avenue for chemically tuning band structures and optical properties. Such materials have only recently become recognized as versatile platforms for tuning properties due to their compositional freedom, which can lead to the new local coordination, symmetry, and covalency of bonds and hence dispersion characteristics not realized by mere cation substitution8,12. Among mixed-anion materials, oxychalcogenides stand out for their strategic coexistence of hard \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{O}}^{2-}$$\end{document} and soft \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{S}}^{2-}$$\end{document} anions that provide two independent knobs to manipulate lattice stiffness and electronic polarizability. Furthermore, modifications in lattice stiffness and polarizability may well influence band gap, dielectric screening, and optical transition strengths in complex ways. Indeed, according to the emerging literature on hetero-anionic solids, the stability of phases and the electronic structure heavily depend on anion ordering, and for that reason, the partial substitution of oxygen for sulfur is far beyond elementary intuition, given the end members and simple arguments concerning electronegativity10,11.

The quest for anion-engineered narrow-gap semiconductors CsInTiS4, which can also be regarded as a boundary composition, on the one side, between titanate-derived materials and, on the other, more standard chalcogenide materials. First-principles calculations have recently shown that, unlike typical titanate oxides, the parent material does not have any obvious band gap shortcomings but exhibits all the essential features needed for a chemically controlled band structure engineering platform13. However, because the parent sulfide represents only one point in a wide chemical space, it remains unclear whether the electronic structure will respond to the targeted anion substitution and, if so, then how sensitively. Beyond general chemical intuition, because oxygen is more electronegative than sulfur, it also remains unclear what its oxygen structure-retaining or symmetry-breaking character will be and how that substitution is manifested in terms of gap size, transition character, dielectric response, and optoelectronic properties.

Beyond linear optoelectronic tuning, recent advancements in mixed-anion and chalcogenide-based nanostructures have underscored their exceptional third-order nonlinear optical (NLO) properties, positioning them as highly viable candidates for advanced photonic applications such as optical limiting, ultrafast switching, and frequency conversion. For instance, recent investigations into Cu-based rare-earth oxytellurides have demonstrated that the synergistic hybridization of metal d-orbitals with chalcogen p-orbitals, coupled with defect-assisted charge transfer, significantly enhances the third-order nonlinear susceptibility \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\chi\:}^{\left(3\right)}\:$$\end{document}and promotes robust reverse saturable absorption (RSA) behavior14. Similarly, studies on transition-metal chalcogenides and their doped variants, such as Ni-doped VS4 and La-doped VTe2, have revealed that targeted compositional engineering and rare-earth incorporation can effectively modulate the nonlinear refractive index (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{n}_{2}$$\end{document}) and nonlinear absorption coefficient (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\beta\:$$\end{document}). These enhancements are frequently governed by mechanisms such as two-photon absorption (TPA) and thermally induced excited-state absorption under continuous-wave laser excitation15,16. These collective findings underscore the profound potential of anion substitution and compositional tuning within chalcogenide frameworks to not only tailor linear electronic structures but also to drastically amplify light–matter interactions and nonlinear optical responses.

Within that framework, in our study, we investigated the oxygen incorporation in the CsInTiS4 host lattice and its effect on the structural relaxation, electronic structure, and optical properties of the parent sulfide using DFT and dielectric function optical modeling. We especially focused on anion substitution at the S site, CsInTiS₃.₂O₀.₈, to clarify whether a previously less explored route—anion substitution—may be effective in tailoring structural relaxations, crystallographic symmetry, electronic states, band gap character, and linear and nonlinear optical properties in chalcogenide QD nanostructures based on the CsInTiS4 host lattice. Our findings stand to illuminate new pathways toward rational band structure design for infrared photodetection and nanophotonic applications.

Computational details

The Generalized Gradient Approximation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left(GGA\right)$$\end{document} method via Perdew-Wang 1991 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left(PW91\right)$$\end{document}17,18 is one of the levels of density functional theory (DFT) that is most used for calculating the electronic and optical properties of nanostructures as well as quantum dots. In the present study, GGA-PW91 is implemented via the CASTEP19,20 module to model CsInTiS₄ quantum dots that have been deliberately modified through 20% anion substitution, wherein oxygen atoms replace one-fifth of the S sites. This strategic compositional engineering is intended to tailor the electronic band landscape and amplify optoelectronic functionality.

During the structural optimization phase, both lattice vectors and internal atomic coordinates were fully relaxed to their equilibrium configurations. Norm-conserving pseudopotentials were adopted to streamline core–electron interactions while maintaining high fidelity in valence-state descriptions. By explicitly accounting for electron density gradients in the exchange–correlation term, the GGA-PW91 scheme corrects the systematic limitations of the local density approximation (LDA)21,22, proving particularly advantageous for systems where charge distribution and spatial confinement critically govern physical behaviour.

Computations were conducted utilizing a plane-wave basis formalism with a kinetic energy cutoff set to 770 eV, alongside a Monkhorst–Pack k-point sampling mesh of 7 × 5 × 3. Geometry optimizations were executed via the BFGS minimization scheme, adhering to strict convergence criteria: residual forces not exceeding 0.03 eV/Å, stress components below 0.05 GPa, and self-consistent field energy changes within 5 × 10–7 eV, with a maximum limit of 100 ionic iterations. Van der Waals interactions were accounted for through the Grimme D2 empirical dispersion correction, and ultrasoft pseudopotentials were adopted to model core–valence electron interactions. To aid convergence of the electronic self-consistency cycle, Methfessel–Paxton smearing with a broadening parameter of 0.2 eV was implemented. Dielectric and optical response functions were evaluated over a photon energy window spanning 0 to 20 eV, sampled at intervals of 0.05 eV. Furthermore, simulated X-ray diffraction profiles for CsInTiS₃.₂O₀.₈ nanostructure were generated using the Reflex module, applying conventional powder diffraction settings to enable direct comparison with experimental characterization data. Consequently, this methodology provides a rigorous platform for evaluating how controlled oxygen incorporation influences quantum confinement, dielectric screening, and the linear and nonlinear optical signatures of the resulting engineered CsInTiS₃.₂O₀.₈ nanostructure.

From a practical perspective, such bandgap engineering makes the material useful for not only visible to near-infrared photovoltaic applications but also specialized mid-infrared optoelectronic functions, including thermal imaging, environmental gas sensing, and long-wavelength photodetection. Given that potential, it may increase its relevance to next-generation narrow-gap semiconductor technologies.

Conclusion

A successful computational protocol via CASTEP software to perform a 20% sulfur-to-oxygen anion-substitution has been achieved to form CsInTiS₃.₂O₀.₈ QDs. This compositional modulation induced a profound structural reorganization, characterized by a substantial 68.52% lattice volume contraction (from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:972.3\:A^{{^\circ 3}}$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:306.06\:A^{{^\circ 3}}$$\end{document}) and a critical symmetry transition from the non-centrosymmetric \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{P}_{21}$$\end{document} in pristine CsInTiS₄ to the centrosymmetric P2/m space group in CsInTiS₃.₂O₀.₈. Consequently, the electronic band structure underwent a dramatic transformation, exhibiting a 73.98% reduction in the fundamental bandgap from 2.552 eV (direct) to 0.664 eV (indirect). This substantial contraction significantly boosts optoelectronic performance, evidenced by a ~ 4.76% rise in the static refractive index (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${n_ \circ } = 2.35$$\end{document}), exceptional mid-IR transparency, and a high Wemple–DiDomenico dispersion energy (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{E}_{d}$$\end{document}) of 45.27 eV alongside an oscillator strength of 455 eV². Furthermore, carrier dynamics were profoundly amplified, characterized by an ultrafast 1440-fold reduction in the optical relaxation time to 1.22 × 10⁻¹⁶ s, and a 74% augmentation in the optical carrier concentration-to-effective mass ratio (reaching 1.08 × 10⁵² kg⁻¹ m⁻³). These highly quantified findings suggest that CsInTiS₃.₂O₀.₈ QDs could be a promising candidate for IR photodetectors, ultrafast optical modulators, and low-power nonlinear switches. Fundamentally, this work demonstrates that unlike conventional cation doping, targeted anion substitution uniquely drives simultaneous macroscopic symmetry breaking, a drastic direct-to-indirect bandgap collapse, and ultrafast carrier relaxation, establishing hetero-anionic engineering as a powerful and independent strategy for advanced optoelectronics. Ultimately, the synergistic combination of a deeply narrowed mid-infrared bandgap, exceptional third-order nonlinear susceptibility, and ultrafast carrier dynamics provides distinct practical advantages, positioning this framework as a highly competitive platform for broadband photodetectors, high-speed all-optical modulators, and low-power nonlinear photonic switches. In addition, these findings provide a rigorous theoretical blueprint for the targeted experimental synthesis of mixed-anion quantum dots, directly guiding ongoing research in thermal imaging systems and ultrafast photonic architectures. Nevertheless, bridging these theoretical predictions into functional devices still requires essential experimental validation.

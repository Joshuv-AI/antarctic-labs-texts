Complete genome sequence of the halophilic archaeon Haloferax volcanii PC0224, isolated from a solar saltern in Thailand
Authors: Manassanan Phatcharaharikarn, Pattarawan Ruangsuj, Thunwarat Songngamsuk, Parweenuch Santaweesuk, Marut Tangwattanachuleeporn, Kanokporn Srisucharitpanit, Prapimpun Wongchitrat, Montri Yasawong

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesHaloferax volcanii is an extreme halophile belonging to the Haloferacaceae family that thrives in hypersaline environments. This study presents the complete genome sequence of the H. volcanii strain PC0224 isolated from a Thai solar saltern. Genomic data will enhance our understanding of circadian rhythm mechanisms and their evolutionary significance in extremophilic archaea.Data descriptionThe H. volcanii PC0224 genome comprises four circular sequences containing 3,773,977 bp and 66.16% GC content. Sequenced using Illumina and PacBio technologies and assembled with Hybracter, the genome contained 3,731 CDS, 6 rRNAs, 54 tRNAs, 2 ncRNAs, and 4 CRISPR arrays. This dataset enables the investigation of circadian rhythm regulatory systems and comparative genomic studies of temporal adaptation mechanisms in extremophilic archaea.Keywords: Haloferax volcanii, Clock gene, Circadian, CirA, CirB, CirC, CirD, Microcin

Objective

Halophilic archaea have evolved sophisticated adaptations for survival in extreme saline environments. Although the reference genome of H. volcanii DS2 has been established [1], genomic diversity among geographical isolates remains underexplored. Our study contributes to this knowledge gap by presenting the complete genome sequence of the H. volcanii strain PC0224 isolated from Thailand solar salterns, expanding our understanding of strain-level variation within this species.

The discovery that some haloarchaeal possess circadian rhythm mechanisms challenges our understanding of these regulatory systems. Researchers have identified four genes (cirA, cirB, cirC, and cirD) in H. volcanii that regulate cellular processes in harmony with daily environmental cycles [2, 3]. By decoding the complete genome of H. volcanii PC0224, we open new avenues for exploring circadian rhythm conservation and temporal regulatory mechanisms across geographical isolates. This genomic resource will facilitate comparative studies of circadian adaptation in extremophilic archaea and their potential biotechnological applications.

Data description

PC0224 was isolated from a solar saltern soil sample collected in Phetchaburi Province, Thailand (13°09’00.7"N, 100°03’03.9"E) using Hv-YPC agar [4] with 25% NaCl at 35 °C. Genomic DNA was extracted using DNeasy® PowerSoil® Pro Kit (QIAGEN, Germany) from cultures grown in Hv-YPC broth (20% NaCl). Short-read libraries were prepared using the TruSeq Nano DNA Kit and sequenced on the NextSeq 550, generating 150-bp paired-end reads. These high-quality reads, available in the SRA under accession number SRR33612293 (Table 1, DataFile 1), underwent quality-trimming with fastp v0.23.4 [5]. Libraries for long-read sequencing were prepared using the PacBio Microbial Library Protocol (v3.0) and sequenced on the Revio platform. These high-quality reads were deposited in the SRA under accession number SRR33612292 (Table 1, DataFile 2). They were then systematically processed with fastplong v0.2.0 [6]. A hybrid assembly integrating both short and long reads (Table 1, DataFile 1 and 2) was performed using Hybracter v0.11.1 [7]. The assembled genome was functionally annotated using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) [8]. Strain PC0224 possesses a multi-replicon genome architecture, characterised by an exceptional sequencing depth of 1,132-fold average coverage. This comprehensive genomic structure comprises a 2,821,581-bp chromosome along with three distinct plasmids: pHA1 (563,288 bp), pHA2 (291,869 bp), and pHA3 (97,239 bp). The complete genome sequence of strain PC0224 is publicly available in the GenBank database under the accession numbers CP191799 (Table 1, DataFile 3), CP191800 (Table 1, DataFile 4), CP191801 (Table 1, DataFile 5), and CP191802 (Table 1, DataFile 6). Circular genome maps depicting the chromosomal and three plasmid replicons of PC0224 were generated using Bakta v1.10.4 visualisation module, illustrating key genomic features and their distributions (Table 1, DataFiles 7–10). Genome completeness was validated using CheckM2 v1.0.1 [9], revealing 99.88% completeness and 0.3% contamination (Table 1, DataFile 11). JSpeciesWS [10] analysis comparing PC0224 with the closest relative Haloferax type species revealed high average nucleotide identity (ANI) between PC0224 and H. volcanii (ANIb: 97.91% (Table 1, DataFile 12), ANIm: 98.03% (Table 1, DataFile 13)), confirming PC0224 as H. volcanii. Comprehensive genomic annotation using prokka v1.14.6 [11] identified a complete circadian rhythm regulatory system in the PC0224 genome, comprising four core clock genes: cirA (Table 1, DataFile 14), cirB (Table 1, DataFile 15), cirC (Table 1, DataFile 16), and cirD (Table 1, DataFile 17). As determined by pairwise alignment analysis using the Sequence Manipulation Suite [12], these genes displayed high sequence conservation compared with their homologs in the reference strain H. volcanii DS2, with detailed amino acid sequence identity/similarity metrics presented in DataFile 18 (Table 1). A gene encoding a putative microcin antimicrobial peptide (Table 1, DataFile 19) was identified in the PC0224 genome through systematic functional annotation using Prokka v1.14.6 [11]. A comprehensive prophage detection analysis using VirSorter2 v2.2.4 [13] revealed no evidence of integrated viral elements within the PC0224 genome, suggesting an absence of prophage sequences in this H. volcanii strain. The 16 S rRNA gene phylogeny was reconstructed using Bayesian inference (MrBayes v3.2.7a) with the GTR + I + G model through two million generations [14]. The resulting evolutionary tree (Table 1, DataFile 20) was visualised using FigTree v1.4.4 (Table 1, DataFile 21) [15]. All bioinformatics workflows and analytical scripts used in this study are available in DataFile 22 (Table 1).

Limitations

Although this study provides a complete genome sequence of the H. volcanii strain PC0224, several limitations exist. The identification of functional genes, including circadian rhythm elements and microcin-encoding genes, was based solely on computational prediction. The actual expression and roles of these predicted genes require experimental validation using biochemical approaches. Future studies should focus on verifying predicted gene functions to confirm their activity and biological significance.

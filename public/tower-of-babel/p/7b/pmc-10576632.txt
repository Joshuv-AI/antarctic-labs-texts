Early aortic valve neocuspidization failure due to xenograft leaflet tear in a young adult
Authors: Kwok Fai Lucius Lee, Ka Lun Tony Mak, Qiang Chen, Nicholson Yam
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAortic valve neocuspidization, initially described by Ozaki and colleagues, has become an increasingly popular treatment approach for aortic valve disease in both adult and paediatric populations. We report a case of an 18-year-old man with severe aortic regurgitation who underwent the Ozaki procedure using photo-oxidized bovine pericardium. Around 5 weeks after the surgery, he developed early symptomatic valve failure due to multiple leaflet tears requiring urgent reoperation.Keywords: Ozaki procedure, Neocuspidization, Aortic valve reconstruction, Early failure

INTRODUCTION

The high reproducibility, comparable midterm durability to valvular replacement, favourable haemodynamic profile and warfarin-sparing nature of the Ozaki procedure have promoted its widespread adoption in both adult and paediatric populations. However, recurrent aortic regurgitation (AR) due to infective endocarditis and structural valve degeneration remains the most frequent cause for reoperation. This report describes a case of early structural valve degeneration after photo-oxidized bovine pericardial neocuspidization.

CASE REPORT

This study was approved by the central institutional review board, the Hospital Authority, on 2 May 2023 (reference number: PAED-2023-023) with written consent given by the patient.

Our case involves an 18-year-old gentleman, weighing 74 kg and a body surface area of 1.9 m2, who had a history of perimembranous ventricular septal defect with surgical autologous pericardial patch repair performed during childhood. He developed progressively worsening AR and had New York Heart Association class II symptoms. The diameter of the ascending aorta and sinotubular junction were 3.5 and 4 cm, respectively. Aortic valve neocuspidization was performed as described by Ozaki et al. [1]. Considering the insufficient autologous pericardium, photo-oxidized bovine pericardium was used. Cusps were sized using a single-use kit supplied by the Japanese Organization for Medical Device Development Inc. (Tokyo, Japan). Equal tricuspidization with three size 27 leaflets was reconstructed using running 4–0 Polypropylene sutures for the asymmetrical coronary cusps [right coronary cusp (RCC) size 31; left coronary cusp (LCC) size 23; noncoronary cusp size 27]. A tiny residual ventricular septal defect was identified on preoperative doppler echocardiogram. However, it was not found during the operation. Postoperatively, the newly reconstructed valve functioned well (Fig. 1A and 1B). He was discharged uneventfully on postoperative day 7. His blood pressure was under well controlled with angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor.

Transoesophageal echocardiogram showed satisfactory neoleaflets reconstruction with no significant aortic regurgitation in both midesophageal aortic valve (A) long-axis and (B) short-axis views immediately after the Ozaki procedure. (C) Transthoracic echocardiogram (parasternal long-axis view) done 1 month after discharge which showed an apparent gap over the right coronary cusp with severe aortic regurgitation. (D) During urgent reoperation, transoesophageal echocardiogram (midesophageal aortic valve short-axis view) showed perforations of both right and left coronary cusps.

Around 1 month after discharge, the patient complained of a throbbing headache, but he denied any heart failure symptoms. Reassessment echocardiography showed severe AR with suspected perforations in both the RCC and LCC (Fig. 1C and 1D). As such, early reoperation was scheduled. However, given that his condition deteriorated with the onset of acute pulmonary oedema, which required mechanical ventilation and inotropic support, urgent reoperation was performed. After aortotomy, leaflet tears were found at the LCC and RCC (Fig. 2). Annular stitches were all in place. No vegetation or positive tissue culture existed to suggest the presence of infective endocarditis. The valve was replaced with a mechanical prosthesis. The patient recovered satisfactorily and was discharged on heart failure medications.

DISCUSSION

According to a recent meta-analysis, only 8.7% of the paediatric patients who underwent the Ozaki procedure required reoperation [2]. However, the detail of valve failure has yet to be characterized and leaflet tear is shown to be exceedingly rare. In fact, the largest adult cohort analysed by Ozaki et al. [1] showed only one case of cusp tear occurring 5 weeks after surgery. Makino et al. [3] reported another case of leaflet tear 27 months after the Ozaki procedure in an 85-year-old lady. An autologous pericardium was used in both cases. Wiggins et al. [4] reported a case of bovine pericardial cusp perforation occurring 2.8 months after surgery; however, they performed single leaflet reconstruction and not the Ozaki procedure. The causes for such an early failure remain uncertain.

In our reported case, although technical flaws should always be considered, we were unable to identify any deviation from the well-described technique. Imperfect sizing and suboptimal equal tricuspidization leading to abnormal shear stress on the leaflet may be one of the contributing factors. Hofferberth et al. [5] demonstrated that photo-oxidized bovine pericardium stretched more at a given strain when compared to 10-min glutaraldehyde fixed autologous pericardium and recommended downsizing of the neocusps. The choice of leaflet material may be an issue; however, no single material has been proven superior to any another. Hence, the optimal leaflet material remains to be confirmed.

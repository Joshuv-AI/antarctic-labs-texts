Effect of HA380 hemoadsorption on postoperative delirium in elderly cardiac surgery patients: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Min Chen, Yanlin Wu, Siling Zou, Meiyan Chen, Qiaoping Liu, Lihua Lei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the efficacy of HA380 hemoadsorption during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) in reducing postoperative delirium (POD) and improving clinical outcomes in elderly cardiac surgery patients. A prospective, single-center, evaluator-blinded, randomized controlled trial. Single institution, tertiary university hospital. 130 patients were randomized (HA380 n = 65; control n = 65), with 128 included in the complete-case primary analysis (64 per group). Eligible patients were aged ≥ 65 years and scheduled for cardiac surgery under CPB with anticipated duration > 2 h. The intervention group received HA380 hemoadsorption integrated into the CPB circuit; the control group underwent standard CPB. Outcome assessors remained blinded to group allocation. The primary endpoint was POD incidence within 7 days postoperatively, assessed using the confusion assessment method. Secondary outcomes included inflammatory biomarkers (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-10), liver function markers (ALT, AST, CRP), renal function (creatinine, procalcitonin), and postoperative complications. The primary unadjusted analysis showed a significantly lower incidence of delirium in the HA380 group versus controls (28.1% [18/64] vs 51.6% [33/64]; unadjusted OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.18–0.81; P = 0.012). Multivariable adjustment (primary analysis per locked SAP v1.0 Model I: age and hypertension) yielded an adjusted OR of 0.42 (95% CI 0.19–0.91; P = 0.028), with consistent results in sensitivity analysis additionally adjusting for coronary artery disease (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.18–0.89; P = 0.025). Results were robust in random-forest multiple-imputation sensitivity analyses. HA380 significantly attenuated inflammatory markers (permutation P < 0.01 for IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α at surgery-end/24 h) and significantly reduced ALT levels (permutation P = 0.015). However, no significant differences were observed for AST, CRP, creatinine, procalcitonin, mechanical ventilation duration, ICU length of stay, total hospital stay, or postoperative complications. Exploratory analyses suggested shorter delirium duration among patients who developed POD in the HA380 group (median 3 [2–3] vs 4 [3–5] days, P = 0.021). In this single-center, preliminary efficacy trial, HA380 hemoadsorption during CPB significantly reduced POD incidence in elderly cardiac surgery patients. These findings require confirmation in larger, multicenter, adequately powered trials with pre-specified secondary hierarchies and long-term cognitive follow-up.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46907-x.

Introduction

Postoperative delirium (POD), an acute and clinically significant cognitive syndrome, represents a prevalent and severe complication following cardiac surgery, with incidence rates ranging from 26 to 52% in systematic reviews1. A subsequent landmark prospective cohort study by Brown et al., comprising 142 patients assessed with rigorous CAM protocols, reported an incidence as high as 53.5%2. POD is not only associated with prolonged hospital stays and elevated healthcare expenditures1 but is also strongly correlated with long-term cognitive deterioration2, profoundly impacting the clinical outcomes and quality of life of elderly cardiac surgery patients.

The underlying pathogenesis of POD is multifactorial, with systemic inflammation, ischemia–reperfusion injury, and subsequent blood–brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction playing pivotal roles3,4. During CPB, surgical trauma activates the complement system and coagulation cascade, resulting in a marked surge of pro-inflammatory mediators (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α) that exacerbate endothelial dysfunction. This inflammatory cascade disrupts BBB integrity and induces glial cell injury5, facilitating neuroinflammation and neuronal dysfunction. Importantly, IL-10, a key anti-inflammatory cytokine critical for resolving inflammation and maintaining immune homeostasis6, was also assessed in this study. While IL-10 serves protective immunoregulatory functions6, its measurement alongside pro-inflammatory markers allows comprehensive characterization of the inflammatory balance and evaluation of whether HA380 hemoadsorption exerts broad-spectrum cytokine modulation or selectively targets pro-inflammatory mediators.

Cytokine removal via extracorporeal blood hemoadsorption has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy to interrupt this pathological cascade. The HA380 hemoadsorption device incorporates biocompatible neutral macroporous resin microbeads that selectively adsorb inflammatory mediators with molecular weights ranging from 10 to 60 kDa7. Preclinical and clinical studies have demonstrated the biocompatibility and safety of the HA380 device8. A randomized controlled trial revealed that HA380 significantly reduced systemic levels of inflammatory cytokines during heart valve surgery9. Furthermore, an observational study suggested that HA380 may mitigate the risk of acute kidney injury and respiratory dysfunction following acute type A aortic dissection repair10. However, the potential of HA380 to attenuate the inflammatory response and reduce POD incidence when integrated into the CPB circuit during cardiac surgery remains unexplored.

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of HA380 hemoadsorption during CPB in reducing the incidence of POD among elderly patients undergoing cardiac surgery, while elucidating the underlying mechanisms through a rigorously designed randomized controlled trial. We hypothesize that HA380-mediated attenuation of the systemic inflammatory response may significantly decrease the occurrence of POD, thereby improving postoperative outcomes in this vulnerable patient population.

Methods

Study design and ethical considerations

This prospective, single-center, evaluator-blinded randomized clinical trial was conducted at Fujian Provincial Hospital from December 2022 to December 2023. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Fujian Provincial Hospital on 28 October 2022 (Approval No. K2022-10-012), before patient enrollment commenced in December 2022. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Although trial registration was completed retrospectively on 12 June 2024 (ChiCTR2400085575) after all 130 participants had been enrolled and the database locked (December 2023), the core statistical analysis plan (version 1.0, dated 1 September 2022) was finalized and locked before the first patient was enrolled, specifying all primary and secondary endpoints (including those subsequently not measured). Study population

Eligible participants were aged ≥ 65 years and scheduled to undergo cardiac surgery (including valve replacement/repair and ascending aortic surgery) under CPB with an anticipated CPB duration exceeding 2 h. Exclusion criteria included: (1) pre-existing cognitive dysfunction, defined as a score < 24 on the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) administered during screening; (2) a history of psychiatric disorders; (3) severe hepatic or renal insufficiency (serum aminotransferases > 2 times the upper limit of normal or estimated glomerular filtration rate < 60 mL/min/1.73m2); (4) a history of cerebrovascular disease; (5) use of medications that could interfere with cognitive assessments (e.g., antipsychotics, high-dose corticosteroids); and (6) severe hearing or language comprehension impairments that would preclude accurate cognitive evaluation.

Randomization and blinding

Randomization was performed using computer-generated sequences (SPSS version 25.0), with block randomization (block size 4) and patients allocated in a 1:1 ratio to either the standard CPB group (control group) or the CPB plus hemoadsorption group (intervention group). The randomization sequences were generated by an independent statistician uninvolved in clinical care and were securely stored in consecutively numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. Allocation concealment was maintained until the day of surgery, when a dedicated research assistant opened the envelopes to assign patients to their respective groups. While patients were informed of the possibility of receiving either standard CPB or CPB with HA380 hemoadsorption therapy, they remained blinded to their specific group assignment. It was not feasible to blind the surgical/CPB team to device use, but outcome assessors and laboratory personnel remained blinded to group allocation throughout the study. For the statistical team, group allocation remained concealed until completion of the blinded review meeting; subsequent data analyses were conducted after unblinding.

Interventions

All patients received standardized anesthesia protocols: induction with midazolam (0.05 mg/kg), etomidate (0.2 mg/kg), or propofol (1–2 mg/kg), rocuronium bromide (0.6 mg/kg), and sufentanil (1–2 μg/kg); maintenance was achieved with continuous infusions of propofol (1–3 mg/kg/h), rocuronium bromide (0.5 mg/kg/h), and sufentanil (1–2 μg/kg/h), targeting a bispectral index (BIS) value of 40–60.

In the intervention group (HA380 group), the HA380 hemoadsorption device (Jianfan Biotechnology Group Co., Ltd.) was integrated into the CPB circuit, operating at a blood flow rate of 150–200 mL/min. The control group received standard CPB without hemoadsorption. Both groups maintained a perfusion flow rate of 60–80 mL/kg/min, mean arterial pressure of 50–80 mmHg, hematocrit levels of 22–30%, and an activated clotting time (ACT) > 480 s. Postoperative care followed a standardized enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocol.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was the incidence of in-hospital delirium within 7 days postoperatively. Delirium was assessed twice daily (8:00 AM–10:00 AM and 6:00 PM–8:00 PM) by independent, trained, and blinded evaluators using the confusion assessment method (CAM) for non-intubated patients and the Confusion Assessment method for the intensive care unit (CAM-ICU) for intubated patients. Prior to each assessment, depth of sedation was evaluated using the Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS), with assessments deferred for patients scoring -4 or -5. The CAM evaluates four key features: (1) acute onset and fluctuating course, (2) inattention, (3) disorganized thinking, and (4) altered level of consciousness.

Secondary outcomes included levels of inflammatory markers (IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α). Systematic venous blood sampling was performed via central venous catheterization utilizing EDTA-anticoagulated collection tubes at five predefined perioperative time points: (T0) at baseline after anesthesia induction but before CPB onset, (T1) within 3 min after the onset of CPB, (T2) within 3 min after CPB termination, (T3) at skin closure, and (T4) at 24 h postoperatively. Plasma was separated by centrifugation at 4 °C and stored at -80 °C for subsequent analysis. Cytokine concentrations were quantified using a multiplex cytokine assay kit (Jiangxi Sage Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) on a flow cytometry platform. Additional outcomes included measurements of C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin, creatinine, and transaminases on both the preoperative day (D0) and the first postoperative day (D1); total hospital length of stay; ICU length of stay (time from admission to transfer); postoperative length of stay (time from surgery completion to discharge); and duration of mechanical ventilation. All clinical outcomes were recorded by blinded personnel.

Prespecified outcomes not reported herein. While S100β protein and neuron-specific enolase (NSE) were prespecified secondary neuroinjury biomarkers in the trial registry (ChiCTR2400085575), they were not measured in the final study due to logistical constraints and resource limitations following protocol amendments. Comprehensive analyses of blood gas dynamics are planned as a separate publication. Similarly, detailed longitudinal cognitive assessments (including Mini-Mental State Examination [MMSE] and Montreal Cognitive Assessment [MoCA]) will be reported separately to allow adequate methodological discussion of these distinct mechanistic pathways. The MMSE was administered preoperatively only for screening purposes (exclusion criterion: score < 24) and was not analyzed as a postoperative efficacy outcome.

Statistical methods

Sample size calculation

Based on preliminary data from a pilot study conducted at our center (n = 30), the incidence of delirium within 7 days postoperatively was 56.25% (9/16) in the control group and 26.7% (4/15) in the HA380 group. To account for potential variability in effect size, a conservative assumption was adopted that hemoadsorption therapy would reduce delirium incidence to 30%. With a two-sided α of 0.05 and 80% power, PASS software estimated that 58 participants were required per group. Allowing for a 20% dropout rate, 65 participants were planned for each group, yielding a total sample size of 130. Of the 130 randomized participants, 128 were included in the primary complete-case analysis (64 per group), as 2 patients (1 per group) had missing primary outcome data (unassessable due to death and excessive sedation). All 130 randomized patients were retained in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population according to original assignment regardless of protocol deviations (e.g., actual CPB duration < 2 h in one control patient). This patient contributed to the primary outcome analysis but had missing inflammatory biomarker data (IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α at all timepoints), resulting in available-case sample sizes of n = 127 for cytokine analyses. The study was powered exclusively for the primary endpoint (POD incidence). No formal power calculations were performed for secondary clinical outcomes (ventilation duration, ICU stay, hospital stay) or subgroup analyses; these are considered exploratory and may be underpowered to detect medium-effect differences.

Following database lock and prior to unblinding, a blinded review meeting was conducted per SAP Sect. 6.2 to confirm the primary analytical approach. The meeting verified that the primary endpoint met all four criteria for complete-case (CC) analysis: (1) relative missing rate 1.5% (< 10%); (2) absolute missing number 2 (< 10 cases); (3) between-group missing difference 0 (≤ 3 cases); and (4) Little’s MCAR test P > 0.05. Thus, CC analysis (n = 128) was designated as primary, with random-forest multiple imputation (m = 5) as sensitivity analysis, strictly adhering to the locked SAP v1.0.

Treatment allocation was subsequently unblinded on 24 January 2024, and all statistical analyses were performed after unblinding of group assignment.

Descriptive statistics

Continuous variables are presented as mean (standard deviation) and categorical variables as number (percentage). Baseline characteristics were compared using Student’s t test or the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

Primary endpoint analysis

The 7-day postoperative delirium incidence was the sole prespecified primary endpoint. As prespecified in the locked statistical analysis plan (version 1.0, dated 1 September 2022), Model I (adjusting for age and hypertension; CAD excluded due to EPV < 10) was the primary analysis, with Model II (additionally adjusting for CAD) as sensitivity analysis. Complete-case (CC) analysis (n = 128) was primary. Unadjusted analysis showed significantly lower delirium incidence in the HA380 group (28.1% vs 51.6%; unadjusted OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.18–0.81; P = 0.012). As prespecified, Model I (adjusting for age and hypertension) was the primary adjusted analysis (OR 0.42, 95% CI 0.19–0.91; P = 0.028). A sensitivity model additionally adjusting for coronary artery disease (Model II) yielded similar results (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.18–0.89; P = 0.025).

No multiplicity adjustment was applied to the primary endpoint

RF-MI (m = 5) sensitivity analysis yielded consistent results (OR 0.42, 95% CI 0.28–0.63). Linearity of continuous covariates was assessed via Box-Tidwell test (age × ln[age] interaction P = 0.994, supporting linearity; Table 3).

Secondary endpoints analysis

Inflammatory markers

Available-case (AC) analysis with Brunner–Munzel nonparametric test11 for between-group comparisons at each timepoint (T0–T4), reporting Location-shift Delta (LD) with 95% confidence intervals (Table 2). Longitudinal trajectories were analyzed using generalized additive mixed models (GAMM)12, selected for their flexibility in modeling non-linear time effects and unbalanced repeated-measures data, configured as follows: Gaussian distribution with identity link function, random intercept for patient, time treated as categorical factor (T0–T4), log1p-transformed cytokine concentrations as dependent variables, and group × time interaction term (observed data, MI = 0; full coefficients in Supplementary Table S2). Model fit was assessed via residual diagnostics (normality, homoscedasticity). RF-MI sensitivity in Supplementary Table S3 (pooled GAMM estimates).

Biochemical markers

AC analysis with Brunner–Munzel nonparametric test11 for between-group comparisons at each timepoint (D0 vs D1), reporting LD and permutation-adjusted P values13,14. For CRP specifically (6.2% missing at D1), RF-MI (missForest, m = 5) was the primary analysis, with estimates pooled using Rubin’s rules15; AC served as sensitivity.

Clinical outcomes and complications

AC analysis using Brunner–Munzel test, reporting LD and permutation-adjusted P values13,14.

Exploratory analyses: delirium duration

Mann–Whitney U test among patients with delirium only (n = 52), reported as median (Q1–Q3) (Supplementary Table S7).

Post hoc exploratory analyses

Per SAP Sect. 5.4 closed procedure, post-hoc exploratory subgroup analysis by age was conducted using a closed candidate set {65, 70, 75} years; the cut-off of 70 years was selected by an independent statistician based on baseline age distribution (median 68.9 years) blinded to efficacy outcomes, ensuring n ≥ 20 per subgroup (21 HA380 vs 27 control in the ≥ 70 group). Analysis was performed using CC data (n = 128) with likelihood-ratio interaction test (Table 5). This analysis was conducted after observing the overall treatment effect and should be interpreted with extreme caution. Although the interaction test reached nominal significance (P = 0.010), the ≥ 70 subgroup had limited sample size (n = 48 total) with wide confidence intervals (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.14–1.93, P = 0.331). The apparent trend toward larger benefit in older patients represents a non-significant, hypothesis-generating observation only, subject to inflated type I error due to multiple testing and small subgroup sizes. This finding requires confirmation in adequately powered, pre-specified stratified trials before any clinical inference regarding differential treatment effects by age.

Effect size estimation

Effect sizes for secondary endpoints were quantified using Location-shift Delta (LD) with 95% confidence intervals from the Brunner–Munzel test, interpreted per Cohen’s d thresholds (0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large) (Table 2).

Multiple comparisons adjustment

To control the type I error rate associated with multiple comparisons, permutation-based P values (5,000 random exchanges)13,14 were computed within prespecified outcome families: (1) inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α), (2) laboratory parameters (CRP, PCT, creatinine, ALT, AST), (3) clinical outcomes (hospital stay, postoperative stay, ICU stay, ventilation), and (4) postoperative complications, analyzed as three separate families: (4a) respiratory (pneumonia, ARDS), (4b) organ dysfunction (liver dysfunction, AKI), and (4c) cardiac events (single endpoint, no adjustment) (Table 2). Only permutation P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant for inference within each family; nominal P values are provided for transparency. For cardiac events (Family 4c, single endpoint), the nominal P value was used for inference without further adjustment. No adjustment was required for the primary endpoint.

Missing data handling

A hierarchical strategy was adopted, strictly adhering to pre-specified guidelines in the SAP.

Missing data overview

Baseline characteristics had no missing data (0%, n = 130). For the primary endpoint (7-day POD), missingness was minimal at 1.5% (2/130), yielding complete-case n = 128 (64 per group). Among secondary biochemical indicators, CRP D1 exhibited the highest missing rate (6.2%, 8/130), with others ranging from 0.8% to 3.1%. Inflammatory markers had ≤ 1.5% missing per timepoint. Notably, prespecified neuroinjury biomarkers (S100β protein and neuron-specific enolase [NSE]) were not measured due to logistical constraints and resource limitations following protocol amendments, and are absent from the analysis. Detailed missingness patterns are presented in Supplementary Table S6 and Table 2 footnotes.

Details of Multiple Imputation (MI): Implemented using random forest imputation via the missForest package in R16, with m = 5 imputations. Missing data patterns included: complete data (n = 117), isolated CRP D1 missing (n = 6), CRP D0 missing (n = 1), combined CRP/PCT missing (n = 2), and one patient with complete inflammatory marker missingness. Predictors included treatment group, baseline demographics (age, sex, BMI), surgical characteristics (CPB time, cross-clamp time, operation time), preoperative comorbidities (hypertension, coronary artery disease), and concurrent outcomes (delirium status, biomarker levels at other timepoints). Continuous variables were log1p-transformed prior to imputation. FMI for CRP_D1 was estimated < 0.3, validating m = 5 sufficiency. Rubin’s rules15 were applied to pool estimates across imputations.

Results

Patient enrollment and baseline characteristics

During the study period, 463 patients were screened, with 130 ultimately completing randomization: 65 were assigned to the HA380 group and 65 to the control group. All 130 randomized patients were retained in the modified intention-to-treat population. For the primary endpoint, 2 patients (1 per group) had missing outcome data and were excluded from the complete-case (CC) analysis, yielding n = 128 (64 per group). Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups (Table 1).

Demographic characteristics

Demographic characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups (Table 1). The mean age of patients in the HA380 and control groups was 68.63 (3.59) years and 69.31 (3.85) years, respectively (P = 0.302). No significant differences were observed in body mass index (23.21 [3.19] kg/m2 vs. 23.19 [3.32] kg/m2, P = 0.971) or gender distribution (male proportion: 49.2 vs. 52.3%, P = 0.726).

Surgery-related characteristics

Surgery-related characteristics did not differ significantly between the two groups (Table 1). Key surgical parameters, including cardiopulmonary bypass time (172.35 [42.45] min vs. 179.86 [53.67] min, P = 0.378), aortic cross-clamp time (114.92 [35.97] min vs. 119.62 [36.39] min, P = 0.461), and total surgery duration (308.57 [71.57] min vs. 329.08 [84.96] min, P = 0.139), were comparable. Surgical complexity distributions were also similar (P = 0.901).

Preoperative comorbidities

Preoperative comorbidities were similar between the two groups (Table 1). The prevalence of hypertension (40.0 vs. 52.3%, P = 0.159), coronary artery disease (43.1 vs. 44.6%, P = 0.860), atrial fibrillation (47.7 vs. 35.4%, P = 0.154), and heart failure (43.1 vs. 47.7%, P = 0.597) did not differ significantly. Cardiac function grading was also comparable (P = 0.626). All standardized differences were below 0.3, confirming a balanced distribution of baseline characteristics.

Prespecified primary outcomes

In the complete-case analysis (n = 128), the unadjusted analysis showed a significantly lower incidence of delirium in the HA380 group compared to the control group (28.1% [18/64] vs. 51.6% [33/64]; unadjusted OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.18–0.81; P = 0.012) (Table 2). Multivariable adjustment (primary analysis per locked SAP v1.0 Model I: age [continuous] and hypertension; CAD excluded due to EPV < 10 in pilot data) yielded an adjusted OR of 0.42 (95% CI 0.19–0.91; P = 0.028). Sensitivity analysis additionally adjusting for coronary artery disease (Model II) showed consistent results (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.18–0.89; P = 0.025) (Table 3). Prespecified secondary outcomes

Prespecified secondary outcomes demonstrated that HA380 hemoadsorption significantly modulated the inflammatory response. Specifically: TNF-α levels were significantly reduced at the termination of CPB and at the end of surgery (both permutation-adjusted P < 0.001). IL-10 levels were significantly reduced at the termination of CPB (permutation-adjusted P = 0.007) and at the end of surgery (permutation-adjusted P = 0.009). IL-6 levels were significantly reduced at the end of surgery (permutation-adjusted P = 0.002) and at 24 h postoperatively (permutation-adjusted P = 0.012) (Table 2).

GAMM with repeated measures demonstrated a significant time-dependent effect on inflammatory factor levels. Dynamic profiles of inflammatory markers differed significantly between the HA380 and control groups, with IL-10 exhibiting the largest effect size at 24 h (β = –0.91, 95% CI: –1.16 to –0.66, P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table S2).

Biochemical analysis demonstrated a high rate of data completeness. Following multiple comparison correction, HA380 hemoadsorption significantly modulated biochemical indices. Postoperatively at 24 h, ALT levels exhibited a significant reduction (β = –0.38, 95% CI –0.64 to –0.11, P = 0.006), while AST reduction did not reach significance (β = –0.09, 95% CI –0.31 to 0.14, P = 0.460) (Table 4). PCT and creatinine showed numerical reductions but did not differ significantly (permutation P = 0.507 and P = 0.389, respectively) (Table 2). Clinical outcomes revealed no statistically significant differences between the HA380 hemoadsorption group and the control group in terms of total hospital stay (21.74 ± 13.82 vs. 27.26 ± 23.40 days, permutation P = 0.263), postoperative stay (12.62 ± 9.64 vs. 16.40 ± 16.56 days, permutation P = 0.292), ICU length of stay (56.31 ± 54.69 vs. 75.23 ± 78.51 h, permutation P = 0.291), or duration of mechanical ventilation (22.18 ± 35.60 vs. 34.98 ± 58.50 h, permutation P = 0.342) (Table 2). These null findings are consistent with the trial being under-powered to detect medium-effect differences in these secondary clinical outcomes.

Additionally, the incidence of postoperative complications did not differ significantly between the groups, including pneumonia (32.3 vs. 41.5%, permutation P = 0.497), acute respiratory distress syndrome (10.8 vs. 20.0%, permutation P = 0.318), liver dysfunction (9.2 vs. 15.4%, permutation P = 0.473), and acute kidney injury (18.5 vs. 27.7%, permutation P = 0.411) (Table 2). The incidence of cardiac events (10.8 [7/65] vs. 24.6% [16/65], nominally P = 0.039, but as a single endpoint without family-level adjustment, this difference was not considered statistically significant) also showed no significant difference between groups.

Exploratory analysis related to delirium

Per SAP Sect. 5.4 closed procedure, post-hoc exploratory subgroup analysis by age was conducted using a closed candidate set {65, 70, 75} years; the cut-off of 70 years was selected by an independent statistician based on baseline age distribution (median 68.9 years) blinded to efficacy outcomes, ensuring n ≥ 20 per subgroup (21 HA380 vs 27 control in the ≥ 70 group). Multivariable logistic regression models demonstrated a robust independent association between HA380 intervention and reduced delirium risk in the overall population (Table 3). Linearity of continuous covariate age in the logit was confirmed by the Box-Tidwell test (P = 0.994), supporting its inclusion as a continuous term.

Post hoc exploratory subgroup analysis by age revealed a nominal interaction between intervention and age (interaction P = 0.010). In patients aged < 70 years, no significant difference in delirium risk was observed between the HA380 (n = 43) and control (n = 36) groups (OR = 0.90, 95% CI 0.33–2.45, P = 0.843). Among patients aged ≥ 70 years, the HA380 group showed a non-significant trend toward lower delirium risk compared to controls (OR = 0.52, 95% CI 0.14–1.93, P = 0.331) (Table 5). Sensitivity analyses using multiple imputation suggested a stronger effect in the ≥ 70 subgroup (OR = 0.11, 95% CI 0.06–0.20); however, given the small sample size (n = 48 total in ≥ 70 group), wide confidence intervals, and exploratory nature of this post-hoc assessment, this finding is hypothesis-generating only and requires cautious interpretation. Discussion

Overview of principal findings

This prospective, single-center, evaluator-blinded, randomized controlled trial represents the first investigation into the efficacy of HA380 hemoadsorption during CPB in reducing the incidence of POD among elderly cardiac surgery patients. In the complete-case analysis (n = 128), the primary adjusted analysis (Model I per locked SAP v1.0: adjusted for age and hypertension; CAD excluded due to EPV < 10) showed a significantly lower incidence of delirium in the hemoadsorption group compared with the control group (28.1% [18/64] vs. 51.6% [33/64]), with an adjusted odds ratio of 0.42 (95% CI: 0.19–0.91; P = 0.028) (Table 3). Sensitivity analysis additionally adjusting for CAD (Model II) yielded consistent results (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.18–0.89; P = 0.025). However, these findings should be interpreted as preliminary efficacy signals requiring confirmation in larger, multicenter trials before considering routine clinical application.

Mechanistic considerations: inflammatory modulation

The pathophysiology of POD is multifactorial, with systemic inflammation and surgical trauma-induced blood–brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction playing pivotal roles3. Surgical trauma triggers the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), activating macrophages and monocytes to secrete inflammatory mediators such as IL-6 and TNF-α6. These cytokines increase endothelial permeability and BBB disruption via activation of ERK1/2 and p38 MAPK signaling pathways17, facilitating neuroinflammation and neuronal dysfunction. Intraoperative hemoadsorption offers a plausible mechanistic strategy to interrupt this cascade by selective removal of circulating cytokines (molecular weight 10–60 kDa)7.

In the present study, HA380 hemoadsorption was associated with significant attenuation of inflammatory markers. Specifically, TNF-α levels were significantly lower at CPB termination (T2) and surgery end (T3) (both permutation-adjusted P < 0.001), while IL-6 was significantly reduced at surgery end (T3) and 24 h postoperatively (T4) (permutation-adjusted P = 0.002 and 0.012, respectively) (Table 2). Generalized additive mixed models (GAMM) revealed significant treatment-by-time interactions, with IL-10 demonstrating the largest effect size at 24 h (β = − 0.91, 95% CI: − 1.16 to − 0.66; P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table S2). However, the implications of IL-10 reduction are biologically ambiguous. While lower IL-10 concentrations may reflect successful adsorption by the HA380 cartridge, IL-10 serves critical immunoregulatory and anti-inflammatory functions6. Whether this reduction represents beneficial modulation or potential impairment of inflammation resolution remains unclear. These cytokine trajectories should be viewed as neutral, hypothesis-generating observations rather than evidence of mechanistic causality, and they do not alter the interpretation of delirium outcomes as the primary clinical endpoint. In particular, this trial was not designed or powered to establish mediation between cytokine modulation and POD reduction.

Clinically important outcomes and lack of translation

Notably, despite the reduction in POD incidence and inflammatory markers, no statistically significant differences were observed for hard clinical outcomes, including mechanical ventilation duration (22.18 ± 35.60 vs. 34.98 ± 58.50 h; permutation P = 0.342), ICU length of stay (56.31 ± 54.69 vs. 75.23 ± 78.51 h; permutation P = 0.291), or total hospital stay (21.74 ± 13.82 vs. 27.26 ± 23.40 days; permutation P = 0.263) (Table 2). Several factors may explain this discrepancy. First, the trial was powered exclusively for the primary endpoint of POD incidence and was underpowered to detect medium-effect differences in secondary clinical outcomes (including these resource utilization measures). Second, in this specific cohort of elderly cardiac surgery patients with relatively preserved baseline organ function (exclusion of severe hepatic or renal impairment), the impact of POD on resource utilization may be less direct than in more heterogeneous populations. Third, delirium duration (median 3.0 [IQR 2.0–3.0] days in the HA380 group vs. 4.0 [IQR 3.0–5.0] days in controls; P = 0.021 among those with delirium) (Supplementary Table S7), while statistically significant, may be of insufficient magnitude to translate into measurable differences in length of stay when analyzed across the entire cohort. Furthermore, no significant differences were observed for creatinine, procalcitonin, or postoperative complications (all permutation P > 0.05) (Table 2).

Relationship to previous literature

Hemoadsorption can modulate inflammatory responses during cardiac surgery, potentially attenuating systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) triggered by CPB9,10,18. He et al.9 and Wang et al. 10 reported reductions in inflammatory mediators with HA380 use in cardiac surgery and acute type A aortic dissection repair, respectively. The SIRAKI-02 trial demonstrated that blood purification reduces TNF-α levels post-CPB18. Our findings align with these reports, showing attenuation of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-10 trajectories alongside a reduction in POD incidence. However, unlike these prior studies, we observed no differences in ventilation duration, ICU length of stay, or perioperative complications, and no causal relationship between cytokine modulation and POD reduction can be inferred from our data.

Methodological considerations

This study has several methodological strengths, including a prospective randomized controlled design, rigorous randomization with allocation concealment, blinded outcome assessment using validated delirium screening tools (CAM/CAM-ICU), and comprehensive perioperative biomarker monitoring. The use of GAMM for longitudinal cytokine analysis and random-forest multiple imputation for sensitivity analyses enhances the robustness of our findings.

However, significant limitations must be acknowledged. First, the trial was registered retrospectively (June 2024) after enrollment had commenced (December 2022). While the analysis plan was pre-specified and followed without deviation, retrospective registration introduces potential risk for selective outcome reporting and violates prospective registration principles. Second, the analysis represents a modified intention-to-treat approach (complete-case n = 128), as two patients with missing primary outcome data were excluded; however, sensitivity analyses including these patients via multiple imputation yielded consistent results. Third, the single-center design limits external validity, and the exclusion of patients with pre-existing cognitive impairment, psychiatric disease, cerebrovascular disease, or significant hepatic/renal dysfunction resulted in a highly selected, lower-risk cohort than the real-world elderly cardiac surgery population. Fourth, the sample size (n = 128 for primary analysis) provides limited power for secondary clinical endpoints and subgroup analyses, increasing the risk of both type I and type II errors with multiple testing. Fifth, while neuroinjury biomarkers (S100β protein and neuron-specific enolase [NSE]) were prespecified as secondary outcomes in the trial registry, they were ultimately not measured due to logistical constraints and resource limitations following protocol amendments, reducing the comprehensiveness of mechanistic insights. Sixth, comprehensive blood gas dynamics and detailed longitudinal cognitive assessments (including MMSE and MoCA) were prespecified but are planned as separate publications; these data are not reported herein. Seventh, outcome assessors were blinded, but the CPB team could not be blinded to device use, potentially introducing performance bias in intraoperative management (e.g., hemodynamic targets, transfusion thresholds). Eighth, hemoadsorption may non-specifically adsorb medications (e.g., antibiotics, sedatives) or other bioactive molecules; we did not systematically quantify drug levels or adsorption capacity, and the absence of observed adverse events in this small cohort does not rule out clinically relevant drug loss during larger-scale application. Ninth, delirium assessment was restricted to the first 7 postoperative days, precluding evaluation of delirium severity, duration beyond 7 days, and long-term cognitive outcomes (e.g., persistent cognitive impairment at 3 or 12 months), which represent critical patient-centered endpoints2. Finally, exploratory subgroup analyses are subject to inflated Type I error and should be considered hypothesis-generating only; details are provided in Supplementary Materials.

Conclusions

In this single-center randomized trial evaluating preliminary efficacy, intraoperative HA380 hemoadsorption was associated with a statistically significant reduction in the incidence of postoperative delirium in elderly patients undergoing cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass. The intervention was associated with modulation of inflammatory markers (IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α) without detectable improvements in ventilation duration, ICU stay, hospital length of stay, or other perioperative complications. However, these findings should be viewed as preliminary evidence requiring validation in larger, multicenter, adequately powered trials with pre-specified secondary hierarchies and long-term cognitive follow-up before routine prophylactic application can be recommended. Future research should prioritize multicenter validation, optimization of hemoadsorption protocols, evaluation of long-term cognitive outcomes (including persistent cognitive impairment), and cost-effectiveness analyses to determine the appropriate place of this therapy in perioperative care.

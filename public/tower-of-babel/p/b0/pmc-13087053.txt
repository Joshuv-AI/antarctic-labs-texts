Smart wastewater management in hydro-technical systems using digital twin technology
Authors: Tariq Ahamed Ahanger, Zhanuzak Abdibayev, Saule Sagnayeva, Ainur Zhumadillayeva, Kanagat Dyussekeyev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOperational inefficiencies in wastewater management systems increasingly lead to treatment delays, hydraulic overloads, resource wastage, and contamination events. Digital Twin (DT) technology provides a systematic approach to addressing these challenges through continuous physical–virtual synchronization, predictive analytics, and scenario-based decision support. This study presents a DT-assisted, real-time monitoring and forecasting framework for wastewater infrastructure, integrating IoT-enabled sensing, EPANET–MATLAB based hydraulic simulation, hybrid ANFIS-driven predictive intelligence, and a consortium blockchain for secure state attestation and auditability. The proposed framework was evaluated using two real-world time-series datasets obtained from Kazhydromet and affiliated municipal wastewater facilities, comprising a total of 80,114 samples that capture both internal operational telemetry and external environmental drivers. Experimental results demonstrate stable physical–virtual alignment with bounded synchronization errors and no cumulative drift over extended operation. The control-path end-to-end latency was measured at approximately 9.51 s for operational data and 11.01 s for environmental data, confirming near-real-time responsiveness suitable for supervisory wastewater control. When security-path operations are considered, the worst-case secure latency remains bounded below 18 s, with blockchain consensus executed asynchronously to avoid interference with time-critical decision loops. From a diagnostic perspective, the proposed DT–ANFIS model achieved consistently superior performance compared to generic machine learning baselines and process-rule based simulators. Across operational datasets, the framework attained a precision of 89.3%, sensitivity of 88.1%, specificity of 90.2%, and an F-measure of 88.7%, with statistically significant improvements () and narrow 95% confidence intervals. Introduction

Rapid urbanization, population growth, and expanding industrial activities have significantly increased the volume and complexity of wastewater, placing substantial stress on existing wastewater management infrastructure1,2. Conventional systems often struggle with inefficiencies such as treatment delays, energy-intensive operations, undetected leakages, and poor handling of fluctuating inflow rates3,4. Moreover, the absence of real-time monitoring and intelligent control mechanisms limits the responsiveness and adaptability of these systems under dynamic operational conditions5. To address these challenges, the integration of advanced digital technologies has become increasingly vital. Digital Twin (DT) technology offers a promising solution by creating real-time virtual representations of physical wastewater networks6,7. These digital counterparts allow for continuous monitoring, performance simulation, and scenario-based planning for treatment plants, sewer systems, and drainage infrastructures8. When combined with fuzzy logic, the framework can effectively manage uncertainties inherent in environmental and operational parameters—such as flow variability, chemical imbalances, and inconsistent water quality measurements, thus enabling more flexible and intelligent decision-making9. This paper proposes a DT-assisted, Fuzzy Logic-inspired intelligent framework for wastewater management, designed to improve operational efficiency, resource optimization, and predictive maintenance. The framework leverages data from embedded system sensors to model real-time conditions. Fuzzy logic is employed to interpret imprecise inputs and provide adaptive control decisions, while Artificial Intelligence (AI) techniques enhance forecasting and system optimization. Additionally, blockchain-based data handling ensures transparency and integrity in the monitoring and reporting processes. An experimental simulation is conducted to validate the proposed framework, demonstrating its high predictive accuracy and practical applicability in managing complex wastewater systems. Related work

Recent advancements in IoT and DT technologies have prompted extensive research for efficient wastewater management and optimization of hydro technical infrastructure. Numerous studies have demonstrated the potential of these technologies to transform conventional systems through enhanced monitoring, predictive analytics, and intelligent automation. Brad et al.19 proposed the use of the MQTT protocol for transmitting environmental sensor data efficiently, which holds relevance for wastewater infrastructure, where continuous real-time data is essential for process control. Their study compared the protocol’s performance with traditional data relay systems, highlighting its low-latency and energy-efficient communication benefits. However, they also identified limitations in scalability and fault tolerance when deployed across distributed networks. Khan et al.20 suggested an IoT-based real-time monitoring system for hydraulic structures like dams and canals, focusing on critical parameters such as water flow, level, rainfall, and temperature. When adapted for wastewater systems, such monitoring aids in detecting abnormal flow rates, potential leakages, and early signs of treatment inefficiencies. Despite the benefits, their approach faced challenges in sensor calibration, power consumption in remote installations, and limited interoperability with legacy infrastructure. Hu et al.21 introduced a decentralized sensor network to detect anomalies in water utility operations and issue early warnings. This approach offers promise for wastewater plants in identifying sudden inflow surges, equipment faults, or contaminant spikes. However, issues like data inconsistency, false positives, and the absence of centralized control mechanisms were noted as key limitations. Wang et al.22 designed a hybrid cloud-edge architecture using IoT for predictive maintenance in water treatment facilities. Their framework emphasizes localized decision-making at the edge level, reducing latency and enhancing system responsiveness. In the context of wastewater management, this allows for real-time feedback control and operational tuning. Nevertheless, limitations such as data synchronization between cloud and edge devices, high deployment cost, and cybersecurity risks were highlighted. Narayan et al.23 presented a model that employed IoT devices for remote monitoring and logistic coordination in sanitation-related health systems. Their approach can be extended to wastewater settings for tasks like automated chemical dosing, sludge tracking, and sanitation compliance. However, implementing such systems requires robust infrastructure, standardized protocols, and high levels of stakeholder coordination, which are not always readily available. Lai et al.24 proposed an intelligent wastewater monitoring system that integrates big data analytics and high-performance computing (HPC) with IoT platforms. This solution provides real-time insights into system behavior, identifies inefficiencies, and supports adaptive resource allocation. Despite its advantages, such solutions are computationally intensive, require specialized technical expertise, and are not easily deployable in resource-constrained municipalities. Vargas et al.25 conceptualized a DT-based simulation platform for utility optimization, planning, and control. When applied to wastewater infrastructure, such a platform enables real-time scenario testing, predictive modeling of treatment outcomes, and digital performance benchmarking. However, constructing accurate DT models for complex, distributed wastewater systems involves high initial data acquisition costs, advanced modeling capabilities, and significant IT infrastructure investment. Liu et al.26 introduced the concept of a Wastewater DT that integrates AI, IoT, and simulation to streamline operation, maintenance, and compliance in urban water systems. Similarly, DT Smart Cities are being developed to automatically track spatial and temporal changes in wastewater generation, reuse, and treatment efficiency27. While these systems hold immense promise, they face persistent barriers, including heterogeneous data sources, standardization issues, the need for continuous model updates, and organizational reluctance to adopt digital transformation at scale. Such approaches often lack continuous temporal synchronization, adaptive intelligence, and secure DT state management, which limits their applicability for real-time operational decision support. Once the pattern synthesis and integration phase is completed, the harmonized sensor data is transferred to the cloud computing layer for predictive analysis and intelligent decision-making. Wastewater risk index (WRI) estimation

The WRI is a composite predictive indicator derived using an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) to quantify the likelihood of hydraulic overload, overflow events, or degradation in effluent quality within municipal wastewater infrastructure. Elevated WRI values correspond to increased operational stress and are used to trigger preventive control actions by supervisory systems. ANFIS was selected in preference to deep sequence-learning models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformer architectures for three principal reasons.

Wastewater treatment processes exhibit strong nonlinearity and regime-dependent behavior, while also operating under limited and moderately sized datasets with heterogeneous sampling intervals. ANFIS performs robustly under such conditions by combining rule-based fuzzy inference with data-driven parameter adaptation, whereas deep learning models typically require substantially larger, fully stationary datasets to generalize reliably.

Interpretability is a critical requirement for risk assessment in safety- and regulation-sensitive infrastructure systems. ANFIS produces transparent linguistic rules (e.g., high inflow and low dissolved oxygen implies high risk), enabling domain experts to validate, audit, and refine risk logic. In contrast, LSTM and Transformer models function as high-dimensional black-box predictors, offering limited explanatory insight into causal risk factors.

Data preprocessing and feature engineering

Before model training and DT integration, a standardized preprocessing pipeline was applied. Missing values (not exceeding 2.1% across all variables) were handled using time-aware linear interpolation for short temporal gaps and median imputation for isolated outliers. All continuous variables were normalized using z-score normalization to ensure numerical stability during ANFIS learning. Outliers exceeding three standard deviations were detected via interquartile range (IQR) analysis and retained only if physically plausible, thereby avoiding distortion of rare but valid hydraulic events. To mitigate sensor drift, rolling-window detrending was applied to long-duration measurements of flow rate and dissolved oxygen concentration.

Data partitioning and validation strategy

To prevent data leakage and overfitting, the datasets were partitioned using a temporal hold-out strategy. Specifically, 70% of the earliest samples were used for training, 15% for validation, and the remaining 15% for testing. This chronological split preserves causal consistency and reflects realistic deployment conditions. In addition, five-fold time-series cross-validation was employed during hyperparameter tuning of the ANFIS module to ensure robustness across varying operational regimes.

Computational and deployment infrastructure

All data processing, training, and simulation tasks were executed on Amazon EC2 t3.large instances (2 vCPUs, 8 GB RAM), while scalable object storage was provided via Amazon S3. Feature extraction and statistical preprocessing were conducted using the STATA statistical toolbox, enabling reproducible transformation of raw sensor streams into DT-compatible state variables. To ensure data integrity and traceability, a permissioned blockchain ledger was implemented using Hyperledger Fabric, recording validated anomalies, DT state updates, and predictive alerts. Furthermore, edge-level inference was supported through a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (8 GB RAM), enabling low-latency local assessment and decentralized alert generation before cloud synchronization. All datasets used in this study were obtained from authoritative public-sector sources, including Kazhydromet and affiliated municipal wastewater utilities. Due to regulatory and privacy constraints, raw sensor data cannot be publicly redistributed. However, detailed descriptions of data variables, units, sampling frequencies, preprocessing steps, and validation protocols are provided to ensure methodological transparency.

Dataset representativeness and operational coverage

The employed datasets span a continuous monitoring period exceeding two months, thereby capturing a broad range of seasonal, hydraulic, and environmental variability. Specifically, the environmental dataset includes winter freeze conditions, monsoon rainfall events, and high-temperature summer periods, enabling evaluation of inflow surges, dilution effects, and temperature-dependent process sensitivity. Operationally, the wastewater dataset reflects heterogeneous loading conditions, including dry-weather base flow, wet-weather peak inflows, and transient stress events associated with rainfall-induced combined sewer contributions. Variability in dissolved oxygen and sludge volume index further captures changes in aeration efficiency and biomass settleability, which are critical indicators of short-term process stability. While rare catastrophic failures were not explicitly labeled, the dataset includes multiple abnormal operational regimes characterized by flow spikes, low DO excursions, and pH fluctuations, which were treated as anomaly-prone conditions during DT-based risk assessment.

Wastewater parameters

The present study focuses on a hydraulic operational DT rather than a full biochemical process simulator. Consequently, the selected parameters emphasize flow dynamics, oxygen availability, temperature effects, and sludge settleability, which are directly observable through online sensors and are essential for real-time DT synchronization and operational decision support. Advanced biochemical parameters such as ammonium (), nitrite (), nitrate (), chemical oxygen demand (COD), and total suspended solids (TSS) were not included due to their limited availability as high-frequency online measurements in the studied facilities. These variables typically rely on laboratory-based analysis with low temporal resolution, making them unsuitable for continuous DT synchronization in the targeted deployment scenario. Nevertheless, the proposed DT architecture is modular by design and can be readily extended to incorporate ASM-based biochemical state variables when such measurements or simulation modules are available. This ensures compatibility with future extensions toward fully process-resolved wastewater DTs.

Evaluation of the blockchain

While blockchain is introduced as a trust-enabling component within the proposed DT framework, its practical impact on system performance must be quantitatively assessed. This subsection evaluates the latency overhead, scalability behavior, computational cost, and security rationale of the blockchain layer, and justifies the selection of a consortium blockchain over conventional secure database architectures.

Threat model and security objectives

The blockchain layer is designed to protect the integrity and auditability of DT state transitions rather than to replace high-throughput operational data stores. The assumed threat model includes:

This design minimizes storage overhead while ensuring non-repudiation and traceability of system decisions.

Scalability assessment

Scalability was evaluated by increasing the frequency of DT state commits and the number of participating peers. While throughput decreases with network size, the observed performance remains sufficient for event-driven logging (e.g., WRI threshold crossings), which occur at significantly lower rates than raw sensor sampling. This confirms that the consortium blockchain scales appropriately for supervisory auditing rather than high-rate telemetry.

Justification for consortium blockchain

A conventional secure database can enforce access control and encryption, but cannot guarantee immutability, distributed trust, or verifiable consensus across independent stakeholders. In contrast, the consortium blockchain offers:

Tamper-evident DT state histories through cryptographic hash chaining,

Decentralized trust without reliance on a single administrative authority,

Decentralized trust without reliance on a single administrative authority,

Non-repudiation of operational decisions, enabling regulatory audits and post-incident forensics,

Fine-grained control over participation and consensus membership, avoiding the overhead of public blockchains.

The consortium model, therefore, represents a balanced trade-off between security guarantees and operational efficiency, making it more suitable than both public blockchains and centralized databases for safety-critical wastewater infrastructure. Although the blockchain layer strengthens trust and accountability, it is not intended to handle high-frequency sensor data or real-time control loops. Its role is explicitly limited to event-driven DT state attestation. Future work will explore lightweight consensus variants and selective state aggregation to further reduce overhead in large-scale deployments.

Conclusion

Operational inefficiencies in wastewater management systems pose significant risks in the form of treatment delays, hydraulic overloads, resource wastage, and contamination events. This study presented a DT–assisted, real-time monitoring and forecasting framework that integrates IoT-enabled sensing, EPANET–MATLAB based hydraulic simulation, hybrid ANFIS-driven predictive intelligence, and a consortium blockchain for secure state attestation. The framework was designed to move beyond conceptual DT integration by enabling continuous physical–virtual synchronization, scenario-based risk analysis, and predictive decision support under realistic operational constraints. The proposed framework was rigorously evaluated using two real-world time-series datasets obtained from Kazhydromet and affiliated municipal wastewater facilities, comprising a total of 80,114 samples that capture both internal operational telemetry and external environmental drivers. Experimental validation demonstrates that the DT maintains tight physical–virtual alignment, with bounded synchronization errors and no evidence of cumulative drift during extended operation. The control-path end-to-end latency was measured at approximately 9.51 s for operational data and 11.01 s for environmental data, confirming near-real-time responsiveness suitable for supervisory wastewater control. When security-path operations are included, the worst-case secure latency remains bounded below 18 s, while blockchain consensus is executed asynchronously to avoid interference with time-critical decision loops. From a diagnostic perspective, the proposed DT–ANFIS model achieved consistently superior performance compared to both generic machine learning baselines and process-rule based simulators. Across operational datasets, the framework attained a precision of 89.3%, sensitivity of 88.1%, specificity of 90.2%, and an F-measure of 88.7%, with statistically significant improvements () and narrow 95% confidence intervals. Predictive evaluation further confirmed strong but realistic forecasting capability, achieving a Pearson correlation coefficient of up to , alongside reduced absolute and squared prediction errors relative to recent wastewater modeling studies. Stability analysis based on the Mean Absolute Shift (MAS) metric demonstrated controlled deviations under disturbance conditions, validating the robustness of the DT under stochastic inflow, environmental variability, and sensor noise. The integration of EPANET–MATLAB simulation enabled physically grounded analysis of flow redistribution, water-level dynamics, and surcharge behavior under multiple stress scenarios, including rainfall surges, industrial load spikes, and pump degradation. Scenario comparison and anomaly propagation analysis confirmed the DT’s ability to anticipate cascading effects and provide early warning before system-wide escalation. An ablation study further demonstrated that performance gains arise from architectural integration—particularly hydraulic modeling and bidirectional synchronization—rather than from standalone data-driven inference.

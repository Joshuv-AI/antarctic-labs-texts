A practical protocol of processing mineralized tissue for Visium spatial transcriptomics
Authors: Jixing Miao, Mingjie Ma, Yuhan Guo, Ling Qin, Lutian Yao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Spatial transcriptomics analysis of mineralized tissues faces significant challenges due to lengthy decalcification procedures that severely compromise RNA integrity and subsequent gene detection. This protocol details an optimized workflow to process bone and fracture callus samples for 10x Genomics Visium spatial transcriptomics. The key innovation involves replacing conventional EDTA decalcification with Morse's solution, enabling rapid decalcification (<24 ​h) while preserving RNA quality, as evidenced by a favorable DV200 value. Additionally, the protocol emphasizes the critical use of SCHOTT NEXTERION® Slide H (3-D hydrogel-coated) to maximize adherence of fragile decalcified bone sections during processing, preventing detachment and preserving morphological integrity. We applied this optimized method to intact and fractured mouse femurs. The results demonstrate a substantial improvement in transcript capture efficiency: Visium V2 yielded an average of 5639 genes per spot, while Visium HD achieved an average of 170 genes per 8 ​μm bin (equivalent to ∼4100 genes per 55 ​μm spot). This step-by-step protocol overcomes major pre-analytical hurdles, enabling high-resolution spatial transcriptomic profiling of mineralized tissues with significantly enhanced data quality.

Introduction

In the past decade, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has drastically transformed the way research is performed in the musculoskeletal field. To date, this revolutionary approach has uncovered substantial heterogeneity among bone cells at various skeletal sites under healthy and diseased conditions, identified novel stem and progenitor subpopulations in the mesenchymal and other lineages, predicted their differentiation trajectories, and revealed previously unrecognized intercellular cross-talks and regulatory mechanisms.1 However, while truly paradigm-shifting, this technique still possesses several inherent limitations. The most profound one is the loss of spatial positioning information, which is essential for positioning cells and understanding their interaction with their local environment. In addition, due to the incomplete dissociation of dense bone tissue and multiple processing steps to collect single cells, some special cell types, such as osteocytes, adipocytes, and hypertrophic chondrocytes, are often missed or under-represented in the scRNA-seq dataset.

Recently emerged, spatial transcriptomics offers crucial complementary information to address the aforementioned limitations.2,3 This new technique delivers unprecedented insights into cellular spatial distribution, gene expression patterns, and intercellular interaction networks within the microenvironments of diverse organs and tissues.4, 5, 6 Based on the signaling detection strategy, it can be broadly categorized into imaging-based approaches (e.g., Xenium, MERFISH, seqFISH/seqFISH+) and sequencing-based approaches (e.g., Visium, Stereo-seq); the former utilizes predefined panels targeting up to 5000 genes and achieves single-molecule resolution through fluorescent probe hybridization and in situ imaging, whereas the latter employs spatially barcoded RNA capture on tissue sections followed by high-throughput sequencing for unbiased broad or whole-transcriptome profiling.7 These two approaches have their own pros and limitations and thus should be selected based on experimental purposes. In general, the former is more suitable for a hypothesis-driven study examining known markers, and the latter is better utilized for a discovery study.

The overall workflow of Visium spatial transcriptomics from 10x Genomics is depicted in Fig. 1. This approach allows analyzing fresh-frozen (FF) or formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens. Since decalcification is required, FFPE process is the only option for mineralized tissue. After sectioning, tissue samples are mounted on standard glass slides. They are first subjected to high-resolution H&E staining to document tissue morphology, followed by cell permeabilization to enable mRNA release. The mRNA is captured by spatially barcoded oligonucleotides on the slide surface, reverse-transcribed into cDNA, and used to construct sequencing libraries. Following sequencing, Space Ranger is utilized for data processing. The prototype, Visium V1, supports multispecies samples via poly-A capture to enable the detection of unannotated transcripts. Its sequencing area contains hexagonally-arranged barcoded spots that are 55 ​μm in diameter, with a distance of 100 ​μm between the centers of each spot. It requires high RNA quality (RNA Integrity Number, RIN, ≥6), and exhibits high off-target rates. Visium V2 maintains the same resolution as Visium V1 while enhancing compatibility with FFPE samples, relaxing the quality control criteria (RIN ≥4), and providing either a 6.5 ​mm2 or an 11 ​mm2 capture area—compared to Visium V1's single 6.5 ​mm2 option. By replacing poly-A capture with targeted ligation probes, Visium V2 reduces dependence on RNA integrity. Species-specific probe panels significantly improve detection rates in low-quality samples while maintaining an off-target rate of 4.13 ​%.8 Similar to V2, the newly released Visium HD (with WT Panel) uses mouse or human probe panels covering ∼19000 genes with a subcellular, gapless resolution of 2 ​μm ​× ​2 ​μm barcoded spot array,9 leading to 11 million spots per capture area. Compared with the Visium V2 platform, this technique exhibits a significantly lower off-target rate, averaging only 0.70 ​%.8 Another product, Visium HD (with whole transcriptome), does not apply to bone tissue because it is currently for FF specimens only. This article primarily focuses on Visium V2 and Visium HD based on our experience of analyzing mouse bone tissues.

A critical outcome for evaluating Visium data is the number of genes detected by sequencing per spot or per bin. This number is high in soft tissues prepared by FFPE. For example, the average number of genes detected in a 55 ​μm spot is 2055 in liver,10 4179 in brain,11 and 3626 in kidney.12 However, this number is extremely low in bone tissue. Previous studies employing Visium to analyze FFPE processed mineralized tissues revealed that the average number of genes in a similar spot is 264 in adult human femoral head,13 233 in adult mouse bone marrow, 172 in mouse cortical bone, 194 in mouse trabecular bone,14 and 310 in mouse intervertebral disc.15 One possible reason for these low yields is that those studies use the conventional EDTA decalcification method, which requires extensive time. This approach not only impairs RNA integrity but also disrupts cellular morphological integrity, thereby substantially diminishing the signal-to-noise ratio in subsequent spatial transcriptomic analyses.16 Following conventional decalcification protocols, decalcified bone tissue exhibits a significant reduction in gene capture efficiency compared to soft tissue.13 Therefore, optimizing the pre-analytical workflow for mineralized specimens—particularly by balancing decalcification efficiency with RNA integrity preservation—has emerged as a fundamental prerequisite for enhancing data quality of spatial transcriptomics.

We recently applied Visium V2 to mouse femurs collected on day 5 and day 15 post fracture and Visium HD to intact mouse femurs using an optimized decalcification method. A major change is replacing EDTA with Morse's solution,17 leading to a much shorter decalcification (less than 24 ​h) step. Morse's solution has been successfully utilized for RNA FISH assay on bone samples, indicating its ability to preserve RNA integrity in bone. Another feature of our experiment is adopting SCHOTT NEXTERION® Slide H (3-D hydrogel–coated) to maximize section adherence during the Visium CytAssist FFPE workflow. As a result, we obtained an average of 5639 genes per spot in the V2 dataset and an average of 170 genes per 8 ​μm bin (equivalent to ∼4100 genes per 55 ​μm spot) in the HD dataset.18 This article presents a detailed, step-by-step optimized Visium protocol for sample preparation in spatial transcriptomics analysis for mineralized tissues.

Methods

To mitigate RNA degradation, it is essential to rapidly dissect tissues, maintain RNase-free work surfaces, and pretreat all instruments with an RNase decontamination solution (RNaseZap™). This solution should be applied to laboratory surfaces and equipment, including glassware, plastics, workbenches, and pipettes, to eliminate residual RNases, followed by thorough rinsing with RNase-free water. Contamination from personnel must also be prevented.18, 19, 20

Animal study

Mice are acquired from Cyagen Biosciences and maintained in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) environment. The animal experiments in this study employ 12-week-old male C57BL/6J mice. In the fracture group, a closed transverse fracture is induced on the right femur using a blunt guillotine following pre-insertion of an intramedullary nail for stabilization.

Prognostic value of alkaline phosphatase-to-albumin ratio in critically ill patients with atrial fibrillation: A MIMIC-IV retrospective study
Authors: Zhenyu Yuan, Lei Zhao, Hong Geng, Qiang Ji, Kun Zuo, Yuanzhi Luo, Guanli Liang, Gengxu He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective: Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a prevalent arrhythmia associated with elevated risks of cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality. Identifying reliable prognostic biomarkers is essential for outcome assessment in AF patients. The alkaline phosphatase-to-albumin ratio (APAR), which integrates inflammation, vascular calcification, and nutritional status, has shown independent prognostic value in various conditions, yet its role in AF remains underexplored.Methods: This retrospective cohort study utilized the MIMIC-IV database and included 676 adult (≥18 years) AF patients. Participants were divided into two groups based on their APAR threshold inflection point: APAR <4 and APAR ≥4. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression and Cox proportional hazards models assessed the association between APAR and mortality at 60, 365, and 1460 days. Predictive performance was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the corresponding area under the curve (AUC), net reclassification improvement (NRI), and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI).Results: RCS analysis revealed a nonlinear association between APAR and mortality in AF patients. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve shows that it has some predictive value, with AUC values of 0.6331 (60 days), 0.6477 (365 days), and 0.6424 (1460 days). Kaplan-Meier survival curves showed that the survival rate of the APAR ≥ 4 group was significantly lower than that of the APAR < 4 group at all three time points. Cox regression confirmed APAR as an independent predictor of mid-to long-term mortality, with sustained prognostic significance at 365-day and 1460-day follow-up.Conclusion: In conclusion, APAR represents an easily obtainable independent prognostic biomarker for mid- to long-term mortality in critically ill patients with AF, displaying a nonlinear association with mortality. These findings suggest the potential clinical utility of APAR in guiding patient management.Keywords: MIMIC-IV database, atrial fibrillation, alkaline phosphatase to serum albumin ratio, mortality, retrospective cohort study

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia worldwide, affecting millions of patients and closely associated with substantially increased risks of stroke, heart failure, and all-cause mortality. 1 Furthermore, AF contributes to a markedly elevated global burden of heart failure, with this impact particularly pronounced among older adults, women aged 55 years and above, and populations in high Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) regions. 2 Numerous studies have demonstrated that existing scoring systems play a central role in the assessment of stroke risk in patients with AF. Increasing evidence suggests that the incorporation of biomarkers reflecting systemic inflammation, nutritional status, and metabolic conditions may provide additional information for the long-term prediction of all-cause mortality risk. 3 Therefore, the primary objective of this study is to identify novel biomarkers that can offer supplementary prognostic information.

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) is an enzyme involved in bone metabolism and liver function. Elevated ALP levels are associated with cardiovascular diseases, including AF, as well as adverse outcomes such as heart failure and mortality.4–6 High ALP levels are significantly associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular events (particularly heart failure hospitalization) in patients with AF. 4 Similarly, elevated ALP levels may predict the risk of symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation in AF-related ischemic stroke. 5 On the other hand, serum albumin serves as a marker of nutritional status and inflammation. Reduced serum albumin levels are associated with the occurrence, recurrence, and increased risk of cardiovascular events in AF.6,7 Epidemiological and Mendelian randomization studies have shown a linear, independent inverse association between serum albumin levels and incident AF. 6 Furthermore, albumin levels may predict the risk of recurrence after cryoballoon ablation in patients with AF. 7

The alkaline phosphatase-to-albumin ratio, as a composite index integrating the biological information of ALP and albumin, has been demonstrated to possess independent prognostic value in various diseases. For example, in patients with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, APAR serves as an independent prognostic indicator following radical resection. 8 In patients with sepsis and chronic kidney disease, elevated APAR levels are associated with increased mortality risk and adverse outcomes.9,10 Additionally, a perioperative study has further confirmed the prognostic value of APAR in patients with solid tumors. 11 However, the role of APAR in the prognosis of patients with AF has not been adequately investigated. This study used data from the MIMIC-IV database to explore the relationship between APAR levels and all-cause mortality in AF patients admitted to the intensive care unit. This retrospective cohort study aims to fill this knowledge gap and is of great significance for understanding the prognostic factors of AF.

Methods

Data sources and study population

This study adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, originally adopted in 1975 and revised in 2024. It utilized publicly available, de-identified patient data from version 3.0 of the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) database. MIMIC-IV is a large, de-identified dataset comprising information on patients admitted to the emergency department or intensive care units at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, Massachusetts. Author Zhenyu Yuan obtained valid access to these data after completing the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Protecting Human Research Participants training and examination (certification number: 68594079).

The manuscript was prepared and edited in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines for observational studies. 12 The corresponding STROBE checklist is provided as supplementary material at the end of the article.

Patients diagnosed with AF were identified using International Classification of Diseases (ICD) codes (ICD-9: 427.31; ICD-10: I48). The following exclusion criteria were applied: (1) patients younger than 18 years at the time of first admission; (2) patients with an intensive care unit (ICU) stay of less than 1 day; (3) only data from the first ICU admission were extracted; (4) patients lacking alkaline phosphatase, albumin, or other serological data on the first day of ICU admission; (5) patients with completely missing comorbidity data.

Variable extraction

Extracted clinical data included demographic data, comorbidities, laboratory indicators, vital signs, disease severity scores, treatment interventions, and discharge outcomes. All laboratory indicators and disease severity scores were collected within 24 hours of ICU admission. APAR was calculated as the ratio of alkaline phosphatase (U/L) to albumin (g/L). The inflection point was determined using a data-driven maximum likelihood method based on piecewise Cox regression. Participants were categorized into two groups based on the inflection point of APAR: APAR < 4 and APAR ≥ 4.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was all-cause mortality at 365 days. Secondary outcomes included ICU mortality as well as all-cause mortality at 60 days and 1460 days after admission.

Statistical analysis

Time zero was defined as the time of ICU admission. The endpoint was all-cause mortality. For the 60-day, 365-day, and 1460-day analyses, patients who remained alive beyond each respective time point were administratively right-censored at that time point. Survival time was calculated in days.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.1). To avoid the influence of multicollinearity on the regression models, the variance inflation factor (VIF) was used for collinearity diagnostics among candidate variables. Variables with VIF > 5 were considered to exhibit severe multicollinearity and were excluded.

Continuous variables were described according to their distribution. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD), and comparisons between groups were performed using the independent samples t-test (unpaired). Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range) [Median (IQR)], with group comparisons conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages (n, %), and intergroup comparisons were performed using the chi-square test.

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was conducted to explore the association between APAR and mortality at 60 days, 365 days, and 1460 days. The predictive performance of the alkaline phosphatase-to-albumin ratio for mortality at these time points was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, along with the calculation of the NRI and IDI. The discriminatory ability was quantified by the area under the ROC curve.

We used the Schoenfeld residuals to test the proportional hazards assumption of the Cox regression model. The APAR group met the proportional hazards assumption for all endpoints: 60 days (P = 0.70), 365 days (P = 0.53), and 1460 days (P = 0.94).

Cox proportional hazards models were employed to quantify the association between APAR and mortality risk, yielding hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Model 1 was adjusted for age, gender, body mass index, heart rate, and systolic blood pressure. Model 2 was further adjusted for white blood cell count (WBC), platelets, anion gap, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, chloride, potassium, glucose (GLU), sodium, bilirubin, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), neutrophils, and international normalized ratio (INR) on the basis of Model 1. In the fully adjusted Model 3, additional adjustments were made for stroke, hypertension, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, mild liver disease, major surgery, severe sepsis, trauma, Warfarin, Beta-blocker, Aspirin, SOFA(Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) score, CHA2DS2-VASc score, and HAS-BLED score. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were constructed, and differences between groups were assessed using the log-rank test. Subgroup analyses were performed to evaluate heterogeneity of outcomes across different demographic characteristics and other clinical strata.

A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Figure 1 illustrates the participant selection process. From the MIMIC-IV database, a total of 19,880 cases of AF were initially identified as meeting the criteria for first ICU admission. After applying the exclusion criteria, the final cohort included 676 patients. Among the enrolled patients, there were 420 males (62.1%) and 256 females (37.9%).

Mortality outcomes according to the APAR group

We compared the clinical outcomes of patients with APAR <4 and APAR ≥4 (Table 3). The ICU mortality rate, 60-day mortality rate, 365-day mortality rate, and 1460-day mortality rate were significantly higher in the APAR ≥4 group than in the APAR <4 group (all P < 0.01). These results suggest that elevated APAR is associated with increased short-term and long-term mortality, supporting its value as a prognostic biomarker for critically ill patients with AF.

Nonlinear relationship between APAR and mortality

We employed restricted cubic spline (RCS) curves to evaluate the association between APAR and mortality in patients with AF. The analysis revealed a nonlinear L-shaped relationship between APAR and mortality at 60 days, 365 days, and 1460 days, with both the overall nonlinear P value and the nonlinearity test P value < 0.01 (Figure 2). The spline curves demonstrated a positive association, such that higher APAR levels corresponded to progressively increased mortality risk.

The effect of constrained cubic spline curves of APAR on mortality in patients with atrial fibrillation.(A) 60-day mortality; (B) 365-day mortality; (C) 1460-day mortality.

Association between APAR and mortality

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that at 60, 365, and 1460 days post-admission, patients with APAR ≥ 4 had significantly lower survival rates than those with APAR < 4 (all P < 0.001). Cox proportional hazards regression analysis showed that APAR ≥ 4 was significantly associated with an increased risk of death in both unadjusted and partially adjusted models. After multivariate full adjustment, the association between APAR ≥ 4 and mortality risk varied with follow-up time points: (1) 60-day mortality: APAR < 4 = 1.00 (reference group), APAR ≥ 4 = 1.05 (0.80–1.38), P = 0.72 (not statistically significant); (2) 365-day mortality: APAR < 4 = 1.00 (reference group), APAR ≥ 4 = 1.28 (1.01–1.62), P = 0.04; (3) For 1460-day mortality: APAR < 4 = 1.00 (reference value), APAR ≥ 4 = 1.27 (1.01–1.59), P = 0.04 (Table 4). Overall, these results indicate that, after full adjustment, APAR ≥ 4 remains an independent predictor of 365-day and 1460-day mortality.(Figure 3)

Predictive significance of APAR for mortality in AF patients

ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the predictive ability of APAR for 60-day, 365-day, and 1460-day mortality in patients with AF, and the area under the ROC Curve (AUC) was quantified. The observed AUC values ​​were 0.6331 at 60 days, 0.6477 at 365 days, and 0.6424 at 1460 days, with the highest predictive value at 365 days (Figure 4).

The ROC curve demonstrates the predictive performance of APAR in patients with atrial fibrillation.(A) 60-day mortality; (B) 365-day mortality; (C) 1460-day mortality.

In addition, using a baseline clinical model incorporating age, sex, SOFA score, heart failure, severe sepsis, chronic kidney disease, and CHA2DS2-VASc(Congestive heart failure, Hypertension, Age ≥75 years, Diabetes mellitus, Stroke/transient ischemic attack, Vascular disease, Age 65–74 years, Sex category)and HAS-BLED(Hypertension, Abnormal renal/liver function, Stroke, Bleeding history or predisposition, Labile international normalized ratio, Elderly, Drugs/alcohol)scores as a reference, NRI and IDI were calculated to assess the incremental predictive value of APAR inclusion. Categorical NRI did not show significant improvement at any time point (all P > 0.05). Conversely, continuous NRI significantly improved at 60, 365, and 1460 days (0.1576, 0.2245, and 0.1949, respectively; all P < 0.05), and IDI also improved at 365 and 1460 days (both P < 0.05). These results confirm that, in addition to routine clinical risk factors, APAR can provide some predictive value for mid- to long-term mortality in critically ill atrial fibrillation patients (Table 5).

Discussion

This retrospective analysis, based on the large intensive care database MIMIC-IV, systematically investigated the association between APAR levels and mortality risk in 676 patients with atrial fibrillation. The results suggest that higher APAR levels may be associated with an increased medium- to long-term all-cause mortality risk in AF patients.

ALP has recently become an important biomarker associated with cardiovascular disease. ALP plays a role in inflammation regulation by dephosphorylating lipopolysaccharide and other inflammatory molecules, thereby attenuating the activation of the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway and reducing the inflammatory response.13,14 However, persistently elevated ALP levels in chronic disease states often indicate an underlying pathological process. Tissue nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (TNAP) plays a crucial role in vascular calcification by hydrolyzing pyrophosphate, promoting osteogenic differentiation of vascular smooth muscle cells, and thus accelerating vascular calcification and arterial stiffness.15,16 Multiple studies have confirmed that elevated ALP levels are significantly associated with increased risk of cardiovascular events and increased all-cause mortality.16,17

On the other hand, serum albumin is a key biomarker for systemic inflammation and nutritional status. In addition to maintaining plasma colloid osmotic pressure, albumin also has antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antiplatelet aggregation effects.18,19 Experimental evidence suggests that albumin can scavenge reactive oxygen species and inhibit oxidative stress, thereby helping to maintain endothelial function. 19 Hypoalbuminemia is common in chronic inflammatory and malnourishing states and is closely associated with poor prognosis in various cardiovascular diseases.20,21 Furthermore, malnutrition and low albumin levels may lead to impaired immune function, weakness, and reduced muscle mass, further increasing the risk of adverse clinical outcomes.22,23

Compared to single biomarkers, APAR integrates the different pathophysiological information reflected by ALP and albumin. Elevated APAR generally indicates increased inflammatory burden, enhanced oxidative stress, and reduced nutritional reserves. Therefore, this ratio may partially reflect the imbalance between pro-inflammatory and protective factors in the body.16,24 This multidimensional integration may explain why APAR is superior to single biomarkers in predicting clinical outcomes. In patients with atrial fibrillation, inflammation and oxidative stress are considered key drivers of disease progression. Inflammatory responses may exacerbate atrial structural remodeling and fibrosis. 25 Furthermore, endothelial dysfunction is common in AF and is closely associated with oxidative stress and inflammation, potentially impairing vascular homeostasis by reducing the bioavailability of nitric oxide.26,27 These processes are all associated with increased cardiovascular events and mortality risk.

Recent studies have also highlighted the role of metabolic remodeling in the occurrence and development of AF. AF may induce mitochondrial dysfunction and abnormal glucose and lipid metabolism, thereby impairing myocardial energy supply and cardiac function.28,29 Given that alkaline phosphatase and albumin are both closely related to metabolic and nutritional status, APAR may partially reflect the overall metabolic stress level in patients with AF. Therefore, elevated APAR may indicate more severe systemic metabolic disturbances and decreased physiological reserves, which are associated with a higher risk of death.

In summary, as a comprehensive biomarker, the association between APAR and all-cause mortality in patients with atrial fibrillation may reflect the overall degree of dysregulation across multiple key pathophysiological axes, including inflammation, oxidative stress, vascular function, nutritional status, and metabolic remodeling. High levels of APAR may predict a more severe systemic inflammatory burden and/or deteriorating nutritional status, thereby increasing the risk of cardiovascular events and ultimately leading to higher all-cause mortality.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective cohort study, the data were obtained from the MIMIC-IV database, and patient inclusion was based on pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria, rather than prospective consecutive enrollment. This design may introduce selection bias. This study used APAR values ​​first measured within 24 hours of ICU admission to predict 60-day, 365-day, and 1460-day all-cause mortality, without monitoring the dynamic trajectory of APAR levels over time. Due to the extremely high missing rate of C-reactive protein (high-sensitivity CRP) values, we chose neutrophil count as a surrogate indicator, which has a relatively low missing rate. Furthermore, the MIMIC-IV database primarily includes Caucasian patients and lacks representativeness of other races/ethnicities or geographic regions; therefore, the applicability of the results to non-Western populations requires further validation.

Furthermore, as an observational study, this analysis only confirmed a statistical association between APAR and long-term mortality in patients with atrial fibrillation but could not establish a causal relationship. Notably, the AUC values for predicting 60-day, 365-day, and 1460-day mortality using APAR ranged from only 0.633 to 0.647, indicating that APAR alone has limited independent predictive ability for mortality risk in this patient population. In addition, we adopted conventional ROC analyses based on fixed-time binary mortality status for each endpoint, rather than time-dependent ROC, which cannot fully reflect the predictive performance across the entire follow-up period. Moreover, the categorical NRI was not statistically significant, and the improvements in continuous NRI and IDI were modest, suggesting that the incremental predictive value of adding APAR to existing clinical models is limited and should not be overstated. Therefore, it may be necessary to use it in combination with other clinical risk factors or biomarkers to improve its overall predictive accuracy for long-term mortality in patients with atrial fibrillation. Future multicenter prospective studies are recommended to validate the prognostic threshold of APAR for predicting mortality in patients with atrial fibrillation and to further explore the potential mechanisms underlying the observed association.

Conclusion

APAR exhibits a positive association with mortality in patients with atrial fibrillation. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis has confirmed the presence of a nonlinear relationship between APAR and mortality. As an easily obtainable biomarker in clinical practice, APAR holds potential value in the management of patients with atrial fibrillation. However, given the inherent limitations of this retrospective study, further multicenter prospective studies are required to validate these findings.

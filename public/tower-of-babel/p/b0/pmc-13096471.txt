Application of quick group search optimizer with passive congregation algorithm in cable force optimization of completed bridge of cable-stayed bridge
Authors: Guang Qin, Liangbo Wang, Zhan Wu, Tianyang Zeng, Hupeng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study introduces the Quick Group Search Optimizer with Passive Congregation (QGSOPC) coupled with the influence-matrix method to optimize cable forces in a completed 1 070 m, five-span, twin-tower cable-stayed bridge. Compared with the original design, QGSOPC reduces maximum tower-top displacement by 83.8% (84.1 → 13.6 mm), girder deflection by 41.9% (236.7 → 137.5 mm) and peak bending moment by 11% (118 078 → 105 120 kN·m), while lowering the composite objective function by 40.7%. A comparative analysis using GSO confirms the enhanced performance of the proposed algorithm. The results demonstrate that QGSOPC offers a practical, efficient tool for achieving the “straight-tower & level-beam” completion state of long-span cable-stayed bridges.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42581-1.

Introduction

Cable-stayed bridges must satisfy the “straight tower - level beam” criterion under permanent loads. Identifying a set of cable forces that simultaneously limits tower displacement, girder deflection and bending energy is therefore essential. Although traditional techniques (zero-displacement, rigid-support, minimum-bending-energy and influence-matrix methods) are widely adopted, they either ignore tower stresses or require extensive manual iteration. Recent advances in swarm-intelligence algorithms offer an alternative, yet comparative studies that demonstrate their relative merits on actual bridges are scarce.

This paper establishes a transparent, fully reproducible optimization framework that couples the influence-matrix method with two algorithms: the Quick Group Search Optimizer with Passive Congregation (QGSOPC) and the Group Search Optimizer (GSO). A high-fidelity finite-element model assembled in MIDAS Civil generates the displacement and internal-force influence matrices, while both algorithms—QGSOPC and GSO—are coded in MATLAB to independently search for the optimal set of cable forces. The comparative efficacy of the two optimisers is demonstrated on a 1070-m, five-span, twin-tower cable-stayed bridge crossing the Xijiang River in Foshan, Guangdong Province, China. Compared with the original design, the cable-force set refined by QGSOPC reduces the maximum tower-top displacement by 83.8%, the girder deflection by 41.9% and the peak bending moment by 11%. In contrast, the GSO optimized solution increases the tower-top displacement by 56.8% relative to the design basis, reduces the girder vertical displacement by 43.7%, yet raises the maximum bending moment by 14.6%. These comparative metrics demonstrate that QGSOPC is markedly more effective for completion-stage cable-force optimization of cable-stayed bridges.

Literature review

Classical methods

Zero-displacement approaches1–7 enforce zero vertical displacement at cable–girder joints, often resulting in large tower forces. Rigid-support continuous-beam analogies8,9 match girder moments to those of a continuous beam on rigid supports, but neglect tower behaviour. Minimum-bending-energy methods10,11 minimize the sum of flexural and axial strain energy; however, uniform cable-force distribution is not guaranteed.

Influence matrix based optimization

The influence matrix method12 linearly relates cable-force increments to structural responses. When combined with mathematical programming13–15 or meta-heuristics16–20 it can respect multiple constraints. Genetic algorithms (GA)21, Ant Colony Algorithm (AC)22 and Particle Swarm Optimizer Algorithm (PSO)23 have been employed. Also these intelligent optimization algorithms are combined with structural calculation software, they can realize optimized design24,25 for different structures26,27.

Group search optimizer

Group Search Optimizer Algorithm (GSO)28 emulates producer–scrounger–ranger behaviour. Quick Group Search Optimizer with Passive Congregation (QGSOPC)29 introduces passive congregation and a harmony-search-based boundary operator, improving global exploration while guaranteeing feasibility. Table 1 summarizes a comparative study. QGSOPC consistently attains lower mean objective values than GSO, demonstrating its superior suitability for cable-force optimization of cable-stayed bridges at their completion state.

Optimization objective

The rational completed state of a cable-stayed bridge was that under the action of dead loads after completion, the response values (internal forces and displacements) of the bridge’s main components (tower, girder, etc.) were uniformly distributed and reasonably small, and the cable forces could uniformly increase with the growth of cable length. When the structural form and dead load distribution of a cable-stayed bridge were determined, the optimization of cable force values became the key factor in achieving a rational completed state. Therefore, to achieve the goal of “straight towers and level girders” in the displacement of the cable-stayed bridge, the horizontal displacements of the control nodes of the tower, the vertical displacements of the control nodes of the girder, and the bending moments of the girder were selected as optimization objectives30. Optimization constraints

As mentioned previously, the corresponding constraints mainly involved the value ranges of various variables, such as the displacement value range and cable force value range. Additionally, if only the value ranges were constrained, the resulting cable forces may be non-uniform. Therefore, it was also necessary to impose constraints on the uniformity of cable force values.

In addition, dealing with optimization constraints’ problems, penalty function was a commonly used method. The basic principle was to add a penalty term to the objective function. When the optimization combination value violates the constraints during the iteration process, the objective function value under the current combination value was artificially assigned a corresponding penalty number (coefficient in the penalty term), thereby transforming the constrained optimization problem into an unconstrained optimization problem with a penalty term. In this paper, when the cable force combination value exceeds the constraints, a reasonable value was artificially assigned to the current cable force combination value to keep it within the constraint range.

Optimization model of the bridge

A MIDAS CIVIL finite element model with truss element was established for the aforementioned cable-stayed bridge (shown in Fig. 4). Beam elements were used to simulate the main girder and tower of the cable-stayed bridge, while truss elements were employed for the stay cables. The bridge was completely symmetrical on both sides, so a total of 26 pairs of cables were set as optimization variables, denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${{X=(x}_{1}+{x}_{2}+\dots{+x}_{i}+\dots+{x}_{26})}^{T}$$\end{document}.

In the finite element model, stay cables are simulated using tension‑only truss elements. This choice is based on the following considerations: (1) In the completed bridge state, cables are under high tension (typically > 1000 kN), which significantly reduces sag effects, making the linear truss approximation reasonable. (2) The influence‑matrix method for cable force optimization relies on linear superposition, which requires linear elements. (3) To account for cable sag, the equivalent modulus concept (Ernst formula) is automatically applied in MIDAS Civil when truss elements are used with initial tension. This captures the primary nonlinear effect without sacrificing computational efficiency. (4) This approach is widely adopted in engineering practice for completed‑bridge analysis and has been validated to provide sufficient accuracy for displacement and internal‑force predictions33. The accuracy of truss elements for this purpose is considered acceptable because the cable stress levels are high, and the analysis focuses on the global structural response under dead load.

The above formula mean that when the difference between two adjacent cable force values exceeded 10%, their average value was returned.

Conclusion

This paper presents a comparative optimization analysis of cable forces for a twin-tower, double-cable-plane cable-stayed bridge during its completion stage, utilizing both the Quick Group Search Optimizer with Passive Congregation (QGSOPC) and the Group Search Optimizer (GSO) algorithms. Both algorithms were integrated with the influence matrix method to systematically evaluate and refine the cable force distribution, aiming to achieve a rational completed state characterized by minimal structural displacement and balanced internal forces.

The comparative results demonstrate that QGSOPC significantly outperforms GSO in terms of optimization quality and structural35 performance. Specifically, QGSOPC achieved a 40.7% reduction in the composite objective function, surpassing the 40.3% reduction attained by GSO. More importantly, QGSOPC led to a 83.8% reduction in maximum tower top displacement (from 84.1 mm to 13.6 mm), while GSO increased the tower displacement by 56.8%, indicating a less favorable structural response. In terms of girder deflection, both algorithms were effective, with QGSOPC and GSO reducing the maximum vertical displacement by 41.9% and 43.7%, respectively. However, QGSOPC also achieved a 11% reduction in the maximum bending moment of the main girder, whereas GSO increased it by 14.6%, further highlighting the superior stability and rationality of the QGSOPC optimized solution. The QGSOPC algorithm, with its adaptive parameter switching and passive congregation mechanism, demonstrated stronger global exploration capabilities and convergence stability compared to the standard GSO for this optimization problem.

The cable forces optimized by QGSOPC are generally larger than the original design values, with increases ranging from 0.2% to 59.3% across individual cables. This increase may require larger jacking forces during construction, potentially adding to construction complexity and equipment demands. However, stress checks confirm that all optimized cable forces remain below the nominal ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the cables, preserving customary safety margins. The increased forces do not necessitate resizing of the superstructure sections, indicating that the optimized solution is practically feasible without major design changes. Nevertheless, the higher cable forces may lead to slightly larger compression in the tower and girder, which should be verified in detailed member checks during final design.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the optimization considers only the completed bridge state under dead load; time-dependent effects such as concrete creep, shrinkage, and steel relaxation are not included. These effects may alter the long-term distribution of cable forces and structural displacements. Second, the analysis assumes linear elastic behavior and uses truss elements with Ernst-equivalent modulus to approximate cable sag. While sufficient for the high stress levels in the completed bridge, this linear approximation may become less accurate for very long cables or during construction stages where stresses are lower. Third, the objective function combines displacement terms and a bending moment term with a fixed weight coefficient (ω = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵), which was determined through trial calculations. The sensitivity of the optimal solution to this weighting choice has not been systematically investigated. Fourth, although a qualitative comparison with genetic algorithms (GA) and particle swarm optimization (PSO) from the literature suggests that QGSOPC achieves competitive results, a direct quantitative benchmark on the same bridge model using these algorithms has not been performed. Such a systematic comparison would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the relative performance of QGSOPC against established metaheuristics.

Future research may extend the current framework in several directions: (1) incorporating time-dependent effects (creep, shrinkage, temperature variations) into the optimization to obtain cable forces that remain optimal over the bridge’s service life; (2) considering multiple load cases (e.g., live load, wind, seismic) and serviceability constraints; (3) developing multi-objective formulations that explicitly trade off displacement, bending moment, and cable force uniformity; and (4) systematic sensitivity analysis of the weighting coefficients in the objective function.

In summary, the proposed framework—combining influence matrix-based finite element modeling with intelligent optimization algorithms—offers a robust and efficient approach for cable force optimization in cable‑stayed bridges. The inclusion of GSO as a benchmark highlights the enhanced performance of QGSOPC and validates its applicability in real‑world bridge engineering scenarios. With the extensions outlined above, this methodology has the potential to become a practical tool for bridge design and construction control.

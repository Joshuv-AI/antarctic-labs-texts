Baseline serum PINP is associated with early lumbar spine bone mineral density gains during twice-weekly teriparatide treatment: the OASIS cohort study
Authors: Rui Niimi, Tomonori Kobayakawa, Yuki Etani, Takaaki Noguchi, Atsushi Sugimoto, Ken Nakata, Seiji Okada, Masahiro Hasegawa, Kosuke Ebina
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBaseline serum PINP was significantly and independently associated with achieving a ≥ 8% increase in lumbar spine (LS) bone mineral density (BMD) after 12 months of twice-weekly teriparatide therapy.PurposeEarly predictors of the BMD response to twice-weekly teriparatide remain poorly defined. This study aimed to identify clinical and biochemical determinants of early LS BMD improvement under this regimen.MethodIn this retrospective multicenter cohort study, 242 osteoporosis patients at high fracture risk completed 12 months of twice-weekly teriparatide (28.2 μg). At baseline, 88.0% were female, mean age was 80.0 years, 83.5% had a history of osteoporotic fracture, and 72.3% were treatment-naïve. Multivariate regression was performed to identify independent predictors of LS BMD change, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was used to determine the optimal cutoff value for predicting an LS BMD increase ≥ 8% at 12 months.ResultsThe percent change (mean ± standard deviation) in LS BMD was 7.0 ± 8.6%, and 41.3% of patients achieved an LS BMD increase ≥ 8% at 12 months. In the multivariate model, baseline total procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (PINP) was significantly and independently associated with percent LS BMD change (standardized coefficient = 2.73, p < 0.001). ROC analysis identified a baseline PINP cutoff of 53.1 μg/L for predicting ≥ 8% LS BMD increase (sensitivity 74.4%, specificity 60.5%, area under the curve = 0.67).ConclusionBaseline serum PINP represents one of the clinically relevant independent predictors of early LS BMD response to twice-weekly teriparatide.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00198-026-07985-9.

Introduction

Fractures and the subsequent deterioration in quality of life impose a substantial burden on patients with osteoporosis. In recent years, novel therapeutic options—particularly bone anabolic agents—have become available [1–5], and the 2024 position statement by the American Society for Bone and Mineral Research (ASBMR) and the Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation (BHOF; formerly the National Osteoporosis Foundation) has further emphasized their clinical importance [6]. In Japan, a twice-weekly self-injectable teriparatide formulation (2/W-TPTD) was introduced in November 2018. The regimen consists of a 28.2-μg subcutaneous self-injection administered twice weekly for up to 24 months at home via an auto-injector (Teribone®, Asahi Kasei Pharma Co., Tokyo, Japan), making it particularly suitable for elderly patients who may struggle with daily injections. Moreover, both 2/W-TPTD and once-weekly TPTD exert a unique dual action—simultaneously stimulating bone formation and suppressing bone resorption [5]—partly through reductions in circulating sclerostin levels [7]. Notably, this characteristic is not observed with the once-daily formulation (D-TPTD). However, because 2/W-TPTD is approved only in select countries, real-world clinical evidence remains limited.

In postmenopausal patients, the risk of secondary fracture is highest within the first year following an initial fracture [8]. Thus, early gains in bone mineral density (BMD) are considered crucial for timely fracture-risk reduction [9]. Although a ≥ 3% increase in total hip (TH) BMD is widely adopted as a therapeutic benchmark [10], evidence from TPTD therapy indicates only a modest correlation between TH BMD increases and fracture-risk reduction [9], suggesting that TH BMD-based assessment may underestimate the antifracture efficacy of TPTD.

In contrast, meta-regression analyses showed that estimated fracture-risk reduction per unit BMD improvement by osteoporosis treatment is comparable between a 6% increase in TH (66% in vertebral fracture, 40% in hip fracture, and 21% in nonvertebral fracture) and an 8% increase in LS BMD (62% in vertebral fracture, 38% in hip fracture, and 21% in nonvertebral fracture) [9]. Previous studies have reported that 12 months of 2/W-TPTD treatment increases LS BMD by approximately 6% [11] to 7.3% [5] in treatment-naïve or washed-out patients. Taken together, these findings suggest that an early increase of ≥8% in LS BMD during 2/W-TPTD therapy may represent a clinically meaningful therapeutic target for achieving sufficient fracture-risk reduction.

Several baseline factors have been reported as predictors of the BMD response to daily TPTD, including prior osteoporosis medication [12, 13], baseline bone turnover marker levels, and baseline BMD [12–14]. We have previously shown that baseline PINP levels significantly predict LS BMD increase with D-TPTD [14] and TH BMD increases with romosozumab (ROMO) [15]. Building on this knowledge, the present study aimed to determine whether baseline serum PINP levels could predict early treatment response to 2/W-TPTD, particularly the increase in LS BMD at 12 months.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

This multicenter retrospective cohort study utilized data from the Osteoporosis Aging Surveillance and Interventions (OASIS) Cohort, a nationwide, multi-institutional observational project designed to monitor osteoporosis in Japan. Eligible participants were postmenopausal females and males diagnosed as osteoporosis with a high fracture risk, according to the World Health Organization’s 1998 criteria or the 2011 Japanese Guidelines for Prevention and Treatment of Osteoporosis [16]. High fracture risk was defined by the presence of at least one of the following: (1) a BMD T-score ≤ −2.5 with ≥ 1 fragility fracture, (2) a LS BMD T-score of < −3.3, (3) ≥ 2 vertebral fractures, or (4) a semiquantitative (SQ) grade 3 vertebral fracture [17].

Exclusion criteria comprised patients with bone disorders such as parathyroid disorders, those receiving hormone replacement therapy; osteomalacia; malignancy under skeletal radiation therapy; severe renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2); serious hepatic or cardiovascular dysfunction; or autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus, systemic sclerosis, and vasculitis [18]. Individuals with baseline serum calcium (> 10.5 mg/dL) at baseline were also excluded. All antiresorptive agents were discontinued before initiation of 2/W-TPTD. Calcium (200–1200 mg/day) and vitamin D (active form; 0.125–1 μg/day) supplementation was administered at the discretion of the treating physicians [18]. Medication adherence was assessed at each visit; patients were considered adherent if ≥ 80% of planned injections were completed.

Bone mineral density assessment

LS (L1–L4) and TH BMD were measured using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) at baseline and every 6 months thereafter with Horizon (Hologic, Inc., Marlborough, MA, USA), PRODIGY (GE Healthcare, Tokyo, Japan), or ALPHYS LF (FUJIFILM Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The coefficient of variation for L2–L4 DXA was 0.63% (Horizon) and 0.41% (PRODIGY). BMD values were standardized to Japanese population reference data and corrected according to the Japan Osteoporosis Society and the International Society for Clinical Densitometry Guidance [19]. Vertebral levels affected by severe sclerosis, degenerative changes, vertebral fractures, or surgical sites were excluded from analysis [18]. All DXA measurements in individual patients were performed using the same device.

Biochemical markers

Overnight fasting morning blood samples were obtained at baseline and at follow-up visits. Samples were obtained using serum separator tubes, allowed to clot at room temperature, and subsequently centrifuged. The separated serum was stored at − 80 °C, and then analyzed within a week after collection. Total procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (PINP; interassay coefficient of variation ≤ 5.0%; Roche Diagnostics, Basel, Switzerland) was measured as a bone formation marker, and isoform 5b of tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRACP-5b; interassay coefficient of variation ≤ 9.0%; Nittobo Medical Co. Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) as a bone resorption marker. TRACP-5b provides greater sensitivity and a superior signal-to-noise ratio compared to serum cross-linked C-telopeptide of type I collagen (CTX) [20]. Serum 25-hydroxycholecalciferol (25(OH)D) was measured using the Elecsys system (Roche Diagnostics, Basel, Switzerland).

Radiographs

Spinal radiographs were obtained at baseline and at 6-month intervals after treatment initiation. Vertebral fractures (SQ grade ≥ 1) were identified using the semiquantitative method [17]. Unscheduled radiographs were additionally performed when patients presented with symptoms suggestive of clinical vertebral or nonvertebral fractures, and images were assessed by the treating physicians.

Determinants

Candidate determinants for BMD response to 2/W-TPTD were evaluated using univariate analysis and included the following: age, sex, body mass index (BMI), prior osteoporotic treatment, history of osteoporotic fractures, baseline LS BMD, baseline corrected serum calcium, baseline serum total PINP, and baseline TRACP-5b. These factors were selected based on previous literature indicating their potential relevance to anabolic treatment response [21, 22].

Statistical analysis

To identify determinants of BMD response, univariate analyses were conducted using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients and the Mann–Whitney U test. Subsequently, multiple regression analysis was performed to evaluate independent predictors of 12-month LS BMD changes, incorporating variables previously reported to influence treatment response—age, BMI, prior bisphosphonate (BP) or denosumab (DMAb) treatment, baseline BMD, and baseline bone turnover markers [21]. Because baseline PINP and TRACP-5b were strongly correlated (r = 0.57, p < 0.001) and baseline PINP demonstrated a stronger association with BMD response in the univariate analyses, baseline PINP was selected as the representative bone turnover marker in the multivariate model.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to determine the optimal baseline PINP threshold discriminating BMD responders and non-responders (≥ 8% LS BMD increase or ≥ 3% TH BMD increase at 12 months). The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated to assess discriminative performance, and optimal cutoff values were identified using both the point closest to sensitivity = 1.0 and specificity = 1.0 and Youden’s index [15].

All analyses were performed using EZR software (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University), a graphical user interface for R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) [23]. Numerical data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Ethical statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional ethical review board of The University of Osaka Graduate School of Medicine (approval no. 18258) as well as the ethics committees of all participating institutions. Written informed consent was obtained from the patients and opt-out information was made available on the hospital’s homepage.

The ROC curve for predicting an ≥8% increase in LS BMD is shown in Fig. 3. The optimal baseline PINP cutoff value was 53.1 μg/L, yielding 74.4% sensitivity and 60.5% specificity (AUC = 0.66; 95% confidence interval (CI), 0.59–0.74). In contrast, ROC analysis for predicting a ≥ 3% increase in TH BMD (Supplementary Fig. 3) identified a cutoff of 76.4 μg/L, with 87.1% sensitivity but only 29.3% specificity (AUC = 0.57; 95% CI, 0.49–0.65), demonstrating substantially lower discriminative performance compared with LS BMD. These findings suggest that baseline PINP is a more reliable predictor of early BMD gain at the LS than at TH.

Incidence of fragility fractures

During the 12-month observation period, 31 patients (12.8%) experienced fragility fractures. A total of 21 vertebral, 11 rib, 3 patella, 2 distal radius, 2 hip, 2 humerus, 1 clavicle, 1 scapula, and 1 sternum fractures were recorded.

Discussion

This study evaluated baseline determinants of LS BMD gains after 12 months of 2/W-TPTD therapy and demonstrated that baseline serum PINP concentration is a strong and independent predictor of early LS BMD increase, irrespective of prior antiresorptive treatment.

For once-daily teriparatide (D-TPTD), older age, treatment-naïve status, and lower baseline LS BMD have been reported to enhance the BMD response [13], and higher baseline PINP has been shown to predict greater percent and absolute LS BMD increases [24]. The present findings extend these observations to the twice-weekly formulation, supporting the concept that baseline osteogenic activity modulates responsiveness to TPTD therapy.

Higher baseline PINP is thought to reflect a preserved osteoblastic pool capable of rapid recruitment by osteoanabolic agents through PTH1R activation, promoting osteoblast differentiation and survival [25]. Conversely, low baseline PINP may indicate suppressed bone formation due to aging or inflammation and may blunt anabolic responsiveness. Furthermore, baseline bone turnover may reflect the number of active remodeling sites and basic multicellular units (BMUs) available during therapy [26]. In this context, PINP may reflect the level of ongoing bone formation and the activity of BMUs.

The association between baseline PINP and BMD gain has been reported with other osteoporosis agents—including both osteoanabolic and antiresorptive therapies. Baseline PINP independently predicted TH BMD gain during ROMO treatment [15]. Liao et al. reported that baseline BMI, PINP, and serum 25(OH)D levels predicted BMD gain during alendronate and vitamin D3 combination therapy [27]. Bønløkke et al. similarly showed that higher baseline PINP and CTX were associated with greater BMD improvement with antiresorptive agents [28]. Moreover, we recently reported that baseline PINP may differentially influence BMD response to TPTD, ROMO, and denosumab, suggesting the emerging concept of PINP-guided treatment selection [29]. Collectively, these observations support the idea that baseline PINP reflects both osteogenic capacity and total bone turnover, enabling prediction of BMD responses arising from either anabolic stimulation or turnover suppression.

Considering the difference between D-TPTD and 2/W-TPTD, our previous study using D-TPTD demonstrated similar associations between baseline PINP and LS BMD increase, consistent with the present findings [14]. Although D-TPTD (20 μg/day; 140 μg/week) and 2/W-TPTD (28.2 μg twice weekly; 56.4 μg/week) differ in total weekly dose and pharmacodynamics, both formulations increase LS BMD. Phase 3 studies have shown that D-TPTD induces a rapid and sustained rise in PINP followed by a delayed but persistent CTX elevation [30]. In contrast, 2/W-TPTD produces a modest but sustained PINP increase with continuous CTX suppression [5]. As a result, LS BMD increases by 7.3% with 2/W-TPTD [5] and 10.0% with D-TPTD [30]. Although head-to-head comparisons are not possible due to differences in patient characteristics, the consistency of PINP–BMD associations across formulations underscores the central role of baseline bone formation status in anabolic responsiveness.

Although baseline PINP is well validated as a predictor of LS BMD response to TPTD, evidence for the proximal femur remains limited. Consistent with this, baseline PINP demonstrated lower predictive accuracy for TH BMD gain than for LS BMD in our cohort. This discrepancy may be attributable to the cortical-rich architecture of the proximal femur and the complex sequence of events triggered by TPTD—including periosteal modeling, transient increases in cortical porosity during remodeling, and delayed mineral deposition [31]—which together may blur linear relationships between turnover markers and net BMD change. Clarification of these mechanisms will require further mechanistic and imaging-based studies. In addition, the stronger correlation between LS BMD and PINP than between TH BMD and PINP may be related to differences in detectability of BMD changes. Although the coefficient of variation of DXA measurements is similar at LS and TH, treatment-induced BMD increases are typically greater at LS. Consequently, the signal-to-noise ratio is higher at LS, enabling more precise detection of treatment effects and a stronger association with PINP.

This study has several limitations. First, the impact of BMD increases on fracture events should ideally be evaluated in a larger cohort with long-term follow-up, including subsequent sequential osteoporosis therapies. Second, bone turnover markers may be affected in patients initiating treatment soon after fracture, although precise data regarding the timing of fractures were difficult to obtain. Third, 11.6% of the patients had a baseline eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, a range in which total PINP may be overestimated. Finally, the precise biological mechanism linking baseline PINP to LS BMD improvement remains to be elucidated.

In conclusion, the principal strength of this study is that, to our knowledge, it provides the first real-world evidence that LS BMD gains with 2/W-TPTD correlate with baseline serum PINP. These findings highlight baseline PINP as one of the clinically relevant biomarkers and support its potential utility in estimating the expected speed and magnitude of treatment response by 2/W-TPTD rather than serving as a solely treatment selection criterion in osteoporosis management.

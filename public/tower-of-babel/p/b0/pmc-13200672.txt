The Regulatory Mechanism and Value in Early Diagnosis and Prognosis Assessment of miR-4448 in Breast Cancer
Authors: Zhenyu Zhang, Zhengrui Liu, Chao Li, Fukai Wang, Zhaokun Tian, Xiaoyu Liu, Zhiyong Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeBreast cancer (BC) is heterogeneous malignancy and the most common cancer among women globally. It emphasized the continuous need for effective strategies for early diagnosis and treatment. This study aimed to elucidate the regulatory mechanism of miR-4448 in BC and evaluate its value in early diagnosis and prognosis.Patients and MethodsThis analysis included 68 patients with BC. The expression levels of miR-4448 and thiamin pyrophosphokinase 1 (TPK1) were measured using RT-qPCR experiments. The diagnostic value of miR-4448 for BC was assessed via ROC analysis. The association between miR-4448 and patient survival was estimated using Multivariate Cox regression analysis and Kaplan-Meier (K-M) analysis. The effects of miR-4448 and TPK1 on the migration, invasion and proliferation of MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells were determined by CCK8 and Transwell experiments. Dual luciferase reporter assay was performed to validate TPK1 as a direct target of miR-4448, and the correlation between their expression was confirmed by Pearson analysis.ResultsCompared with the healthy controls, the level of TPK1 in the serum of BC patients was elevated, while miR-4448 levels was significantly reduced. MiR-4448 demonstrated substantial diagnostic value for BC. Low miR-4448 expression was associated with poorer survival outcomes. Inhibition of miR-4448 promoted the migration, invasion and proliferation abilities of BC cells. TPK1 was confirmed as a direct target of miR-4448, with their expression showing a significant negative correlation. Importantly, silencing TPK1 effectively reversed the protumorigenic effects induced by miR-4448 inhibition.ConclusionThe study identifies miR-4448 as a potential biomarker for the early diagnosis and prognosis of BC. Inhibiting miR-4448 can promote the development of BC by negatively regulating TPK1.Keywords: MiR-4448, TPK1, breast cancer, biomarker

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is a common malignant tumor among women worldwide.1,2 Approximately one-tenth of BC patients will develop metastatic BC, and their five-year survival rate is less than 30%.3 In fact, the metastasis of important organs such as the brain, which leads to limited treatment options, is regarded as the main cause of the high mortality rate of BC.4,5 On the psychological level, the diagnosis and treatment of BC have had a considerable impact on patients, including long-term anxiety, depression and other mental disorders;6 while on the physiological level, BC brain metastasis can also cause brain damage, resulting in headaches, cognitive dysfunction or limb movement disorders.7 However, the underlying molecular mechanism remains unclear to this day. Based on this, it is of great significance to deeply explore new diagnostic targets and the pathogenesis of the disease for the subsequent treatment of BC.

The history of cancer treatment development is marked by the emergence of molecular targeted cancer therapy. The earlier treatment methods included surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy; later, targeted drugs, immune checkpoint inhibitors and other approaches were developed; and in the future, the emerging methods such as allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and cancer treatment drugs related to microRNAs (miRNAs) are highly anticipated.8 BC poses challenges in treatment due to its heterogeneity and metastatic nature. For instance, targeted therapy and novel immunotherapy approaches such as oncolytic viruses have shown insufficient efficacy in treating patients with BC metastasis, and their prognosis remains poor.4,9 However, the clinical application potential of miRNA in BC has already attracted attention.10

It is well known that non-coding RNA (ncRNA) plays a crucial role in BC and has the potential to serve as a biomarker and therapeutic target for this disease.11 Among them, miRNA can perform post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression, thereby participating in the occurrence and development of BC.12 Not only that, miRNA can also be transported through exosomes and penetrate various microenvironments such as the blood-brain barrier, thereby affecting the entire body.10 Previous studies have shown that miR-126 participates in tumor tissue metastasis through complex molecular mechanisms and is a potential biomarker for BC.13 Similarly, the MiR-200 family also has a high diagnostic value for BC, and its dysregulation is closely related to the molecular subtypes, occurrence and development, and drug resistance of BC.10,14 Emerging evidence has highlighted the clinical significance of miR-4448 in multiple cancer types. In BC, the expression of miR-4448 is markedly downregulated and correlates with poorer prognosis.15,16 Functions as a tumor suppressor, miR-4448 can inhibit malignant progression in gastric cancer and liver cancer.17 Additionally, overexpression of miR-4448 suppresses tumor growth in small cell lung cancer (SCLC)18 and reduces the proliferation and metastasis of A549 cells, suggesting its potential as a biomarker for adenocarcinoma metastasis.19 Collectively, these research results all indirectly indicate that miR-4448 has the potential to be involved in the occurrence and development of BC.

This study is designed to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying miR-4448-mediated regulation in BC. Ultimately, we seek to validate the diagnostic value of miR-4448, which may offer a new avenue for improving early BC detection.

Materials and Methods

Baseline Data

Baseline clinical data were obtained from 68 female patients with primary BC and 40 female healthy volunteers from Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute. Collected variables included age, tumor size, TNM stage, lymph node metastasis status, etc. A total of 108 blood samples were collected and centrifuged at 4°C, and the supernatant was stored at −80°C for subsequent experiments. Additionally, a 5-year follow-up was conducted on 68 BC patients. The methods included in-person follow-ups and telephone interviews to document survival outcomes. Exclusion criteria were as follows: 1. Patients who had previously undergone surgical, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy treatments. 2. Individuals with autoimmune diseases or other serious illnesses. 3. Pregnant or lactating women.

This study had been approved by the Ethics Committee of Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute (No. 2019–0048) and had received the informed consent of the research participants. All procedures performed in studies were in accordance with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and later versions.

Cell Culture

The human breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231 was obtained from Procell (Wuhan, China) and cultured in Leibovitz’s L-15 medium containing 10% FBS and 1% PS. The culture condition was 37°C. MCF-7 cells were purchased from Sunncell (Wuhan, China) and maintained in EMEM (MEM + NEAA) medium containing 10% FBS and 1% PS, and 10 μg/mL Insulin, at 37°C in a cell incubator with 5% CO2.

Cell Transfection

Negative control (NC) inhibitor/mimic, miR-4448 inhibitor/mimic, si-NC/thiamin pyrophosphokinase 1 (TPK1), and TPK1-WT/MUT plasmids were synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). Cells at the logarithmic growth phase were transfected according to the experimental grouping using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from the serum and the transfected cells respectively using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Waltham, Massachusetts). To prevent RNA degradation, reverse transcription was performed immediately. The relative expression levels of miR-4448 and TPK1 were quantified using the SYBR Green I Master Mix kit (Invitrogen, Waltham, Massachusetts).

CCK8 Assays

The cells were cultured in a 96-well plate. Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) reagent. Before the detection, 10 μL of the reagent was added to each well. After incubation at 37°C for an appropriate duration, the absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate detector to evaluate the cell proliferation ability.

Transwell Assays

Following serum starvation, cells were digested and resuspended them in a serum-free medium to prepare a single-cell suspension of the appropriate concentration. For invasion assays, the matrix gel was melted in an ice bath, diluted to the appropriate concentration, and then added to the Transwell chamber. Then, it was allowed to solidify at 37°C. The cell suspensions were added to the different Transwell chambers in sequence according to the groups, and then they were placed into 24-well plate containing complete medium. After 48 hours of incubation, non-migrated or non-invaded cells on the upper chamber of the Transwell were wiped off. Both the Transwell chambers containing matrix gel and those without matrix gel were fixed, stained with crystal violet, and photographed. Migrated or invaded cell were counted under a microscope.

Dual-Luciferase Gene Reporter Assay

To validate the targeting relationship between miR-4448 and TPK1, dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed. The miR-4448/NC inhibitor/mimic and TPK1-WT/MUT reporter gene plasmids were respectively co-transfected into logarithmic growth phase cells. After 48 h, cells were lysed, and then the relative luciferase activity (RLA) was calculated by microplate reader.

Statistics Analysis

This study employed SPSS 23.0 software and Graph Pad-Prism 7.0 software for statistics analysis. All data were determined for normality and homogeneity of variance through Shapiro–Wilk test and Levene test. Categorical variables are described by frequency. The comparison between the two data groups was conducted using the T-test or Chi-square test, and the comparison among multiple groups was performed using variance analysis. In addition, the study conducted survival analysis using the Kaplan-Meier (K-M) curve, and employed a multivariate COX regression model to assess the risk factors for poor prognosis in BC patients. An ROC curve was established to evaluate the diagnostic value of miR-4448 for BC, and Pearson analysis was conducted to verify the correlation between the expression level of miR-4448 and TPK1. When p < 0.05, there was a significant difference.

Results

Baseline Data and Survival Status Analysis

Comparative analysis of the clinical indicators — including age, tumor size, and hormone receptor status — was performed in 68 female patients with primary BC. Among all variables evaluated, the TNM stage and lymphatic metastasis were significantly correlated with circulating miR-4448 expression levels (p < 0.05, Table 1). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further revealed that the TNM stage and lymphatic metastasis were strongly associated with the poor prognosis of BC patients, while the low miR-4448 expression was an independent risk factor for mortality in BC patients (p < 0.05, Table 2).

The Role of miR-4448 in BC

Compared with the healthy control group, the circulating miR-4448 levels of BC patients was significantly downregulated (p < 0.05, Figure 1A). The ROC analysis demonstrated that miR-4448 could effectively distinguish BC patients from healthy volunteers (AUC = 0.9022), with a detection sensitivity of 73.53% and a specificity of 92.50% (Figure 1B). The K-M analysis revealed that patients with the low miR-4448 expression exhibited significantly poorer survival outcomes compared to those with high expression (p = 0.006, Figure 1C).

The diagnostic value of miR-4448 in BC. (A) The expression level of miR-4448 in the serum. (B) ROC curve was used to evaluate the diagnostic value of miR-4448 for BC. (C) The K-M analysis estimated the impact of miR-4448 expression level on the survival rate of BC patients. ***P < 0.001.

Effect of miR-4448 on MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 Cells

Transfection with miR-4448 inhibitor effectively reduced, whereas the mimic significantly increased, miR-4448 expression levels in both MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells (p < 0.05, Figure 2A). Functional assays demonstrated that miR-4448 inhibition markedly enhanced the cell migration and invasion capabilities (p < 0.05, Figure 2B and C) and promoted cell proliferative activity (p < 0.05, Figure 2D and E). Conversely, miR-4448 overexpression exerted the suppressive effect in the experiment.

The Target Gene of miR-4448 Is TPK1

The TargetScan Database predicted that the target gene of miR-4448 was TPK1, and the specific binding site was shown in Figure 3A. Dual-luciferase assay confirmed direct interaction between miR-4448 and the 3’UTR region of TPK1 (p < 0.05, Figure 3B and C). Analysis of serum samples revealed that the expression level of TPK1 in BC patients was significantly elevated than healthy control group (p < 0.05, Figure 3D). The Pearson analysis indicated a strong negative correlation between miR-4448 and TPK1 expression levels (r = −0.7284, p < 0.001, Figure 3E).

Compensation Experiment

In both MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells, inhibiting miR-4448 could significantly upregulated the expression level of TPK1, while transfection with si-TPK1 could effectively weaken this effect (p < 0.05, Figure 4A). Importantly, inhibiting TPK1 successfully attenuated the enhanced cell migration and invasion caused by miR-4448 downregulation (p < 0.05, Figure 4B and C), as well as the cell proliferation (p < 0.05, Figure 4D and E).

Discussion

The global incidence and mortality rates of BC have been continuously rising in recent years.20 As a highly heterogeneous malignancy, BC encompasses diverse molecular subtypes and distinct histopathological characteristics, which collectively contribute to significant differences in diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis.21,22 This inherent heterogeneity underscores the current lack of universal diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for BC.22 Presently, the gold standard for BC diagnosis remains the tissue biopsy and imaging methods, which have obvious drawbacks.23 Technological advances have expanded the diagnostic armamentarium to include mammography, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and ultrasound.24 However, the need for minimally invasive, readily accessible biomarkers persists. Accumulating studies have shown that miRNAs and other non-invasive and minimally invasive biomarkers hold considerable promise for early screening of BC and other tumors, providing timely feedback on subsequent treatment outcomes and thereby improving survival rates. They have broad prospects in early screening and treatment evaluation.23,25 In fact, liquid biopsy technology has emerged as a pivotal tool in oncological diagnosis.26 Numerous basic studies have been continuously exploring and enriching the biological markers related to BC. For instance, studies have shown that serum exosomal miR-200c is a potential biomarker for the diagnosis of BC.27 Nevertheless, the diagnosis of BC remains challenging and requires ongoing extensive research to further identify sufficiently sensitive and specific early diagnostic markers.28 In this context, this study analyzed the baseline clinical data from BC patients and identified a significant correlation between circulating miR-4448 expression level and both TNM stage and lymphatic metastasis of the patients. Multivariate Cox regression analysis further established low miR-4448 expression as an independent risk factor for increased mortality.

MiRNA is a class of non-coding single-stranded RNA that plays a pivotal role in regulating diverse physiological and pathological processes.29 Recent studies have elucidated that miRNAs can regulate gene expression at the molecular and epigenetic levels. Their dysregulation can activate oncogenic pathways and affect various oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes, thereby playing a role in the clinical diagnosis and treatment of BC.10 Not only that, in the complex tumor microenvironment, extracellular vesicles30 can affect tumor invasion and metastasis through the transport of miRNAs for intercellular communication.31 For instance, miR-4448 can be packaged into EVs and exert its effects outside the initiating cells, potentially serving as a diagnostic biomarker for lung adenocarcinoma metastasis.19 This study demonstrated significantly reduced serum miR-4448 expression in BC patients compared to healthy controls, making it a potential biomarker for BC diagnosis. Moreover, low miR-4448 expression was also significantly associated with a high mortality rate in BC patients.

To elucidate the functional role of miR-4448 in BC, this study conducted in vitro cell experiments using MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells. MDA-MB-231 cells, representative of triple-negative BC (TNBC), and the cells in this line do not express estrogen receptors (ER) or other related molecules. Patients with this type of cancer often have a poor prognosis.32 Conversely, MCF-7 cells can express large amounts of ER protein, playing a significant role in the study of BC invasiveness.33 Both cell lines are therefore widely employed in BC molecular research. For instance, they have been utilized to investigate the effect of miR-518a-5p on the activity of BC cells.34 In the present study, transfection with miR-4448 inhibitor significantly enhanced the migration, invasion, and proliferation abilities of both MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells, confirming the tumor-suppressive role of miR-4448 in breast cancer.

Mechanistic investigations identified TPK1 as a direct target gene of miR-4448. Previous studies have reported that, compared with normal cells, the expression level of TPK1 in BC cells is significantly increased.35 Not only that, the upregulation of TPK1 is also closely correlated with the poor prognosis of BC patients.36 It has been proposed that tumor cells may promote malignant progression through the adaptive upregulation of TPK1.37 In this study, the expression level of TPK1 in the serum of BC patients was elevated, and it exhibited a significant negative correlation with miR-4448 expression. Critically, compensation experiment results demonstrated that TPK1 silencing effectively reversed the enhanced migration, invasion and proliferation abilities of BC cells induced by miR-4448 inhibition, confirming that TPK1 mediates the oncogenic effects associated with miR-4448 downregulation in BC cells.

This study has the limitations of small sample size and being a single-center study. In the future, it is necessary to conduct studies with larger sample sizes and more diverse research cohorts to verify these conclusions and further enhance the general applicability of it. This study mainly conducted preliminary exploration of the tumor-suppressing effect of miR-4448 on BC through in vitro cell experiments and blood samples, by directly targeting TPK1. Therefore, future research can focus on the perspective of structural biology to conduct molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to clarify and verify the binding of miR-4448 to TPK1 mRNA; use more reliable cancer tissue samples or establish BC xenograft or metastasis animal models, especially by injecting into the common carotid artery to construct a brain metastasis model, to verify the biological function and therapeutic potential of this signaling axis in a complex physiological environment. Furthermore, this study only focused on the regulatory mechanism of miR-4448 within BC cells, and failed to delve into whether it can be packaged into exosomes to play a role in cell-to-cell communication and thereby promote the metastasis of BC. This limitation has pointed out the direction for our future research: We will isolate and identify the exosomes derived from BC cells, measure the abundance of miR-4448 within them, and, in light of the clinical background of this study, focus on their role in intercellular communication and the process of brain metastasis of BC.

Although our data indicate that miR-4448 has potential diagnostic and prognostic value in BC, its clinical application still faces multiple challenges. Firstly, the results of this study need to be externally validated in independent multi-center cohorts to confirm their generalizability. Secondly, prospective longitudinal studies are a necessary condition for evaluating the predictive value of this biomarker in real clinical scenarios, including whether it can guide treatment decisions or monitor treatment responses. Before obtaining these validation data, caution should be exercised in the clinical application of miR-4448.

Conclusion

In summary, this study shows that miR-4448 is underexpressed in the serum of BC patients, and it is expected to become a potential biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of BC. Inhibiting miR-4448 can effectively enhance the migration, invasion and proliferation abilities of BC cells by negatively regulate TPK1, thereby promoting the development of BC.

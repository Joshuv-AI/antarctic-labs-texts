Genetic variants in IGF2BP family genes are associated with glioma risk in Chinese children
Authors: Yuling Su, Yuxiang Liao, Li Yuan, Linqian Wang, Jiabin Liu, Haixia Zhou, Tianyou Wang, Jing He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGlioma is a highly prevalent malignant tumor of the central nervous system in children and is driven by complex genetic and environmental factors. IGF2BP family genes (IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, and IGF2BP3) encode critical RNA epigenetic “readers” that participate in posttranscriptional gene regulation and modulate various cellular processes. However, the contributions of these gene variants to glioma risk remain unclear.MethodsA multicenter case–control study was conducted, enrolling 360 patients with glioma and 547 cancer-free controls. Genotyping of 11 potentially functional polymorphisms within IGF2BP family genes was performed using the TaqMan assay. Unconditional logistic regression models were employed to estimate odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Furthermore, expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) clinical database analyses were conducted to investigate the potential regulatory mechanisms and clinical significance of the identified variants.ResultsWe found that the IGF2BP1 rs2270575 polymorphism was significantly associated with a decreased risk of glioma. Conversely, the IGF2BP2 rs17289925 and rs7646419 polymorphisms were linked to increased glioma risk. Stratification and cumulative effect analyses revealed that harboring multiple risk genotypes of IGF2BP1 or IGF2BP2 substantially increased glioma susceptibility. This cumulative risk was especially notable among males, younger children (< 60 months), and patients diagnosed with early-stage (I + II) tumors. Additionally, eQTL and TCGA analyses revealed that the rs2270575 and rs7646419 alleles correlated significantly with altered mRNA expression levels of CALCOCO2 and AC099661.1 (ENSG00000286086), respectively, which further correlated with favorable molecular subtypes (IDH mutation status) and WHO tumor grades.ConclusionGenetic polymorphisms within IGF2BP family genes significantly modulate pediatric glioma susceptibility and have cumulative, subtype-specific, and age-dependent effects; thus, these genes may serve as promising noninvasive biomarkers for early risk stratification of childhood glioma.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s10020-026-01527-z.Keywords: Glioma, IGF2BP, RNA binding protein, Genetic susceptibility, Polymorphism

Introduction

Glioma is a prevalent primary neoplasm of the central nervous system (CNS) and accounts for a considerable fraction of cancer-related morbidity and mortality in children (Udaka and Packer 2018; Ostrom et al. 2022; Shen et al. 2025). According to the World Health Organization (WHO) classification, gliomas are stratified into low-grade (WHO grades I–II) and high-grade (WHO grades III–IV) lesions. Low-grade gliomas are generally benign with a favorable prognosis, whereas high-grade gliomas progress rapidly; the most aggressive forms are often lethal within months (Louis et al. 2016, 2021; Ostrom et al. 2014). Epidemiological data indicate that the annual incidence of primary brain tumors among children under 19 years of age in the United States is 6.14 per 100,000. Malignant gliomas constitute 11.9% of these cases, with high-grade variants accounting for one-third of all childhood gliomas and representing the leading cause of cancer-related mortality in this population (Bale and Rosenblum 2022; LaBelle et al. 2025). Despite advances in multi-modal treatments—including maximal safe resection, radiation, and chemotherapy—the clinical outcomes for glioma remain highly heterogeneous (Douw et al. 2026; Bent et al. 2023). The etiology of glioma is poorly understood. Given the lack of clearly defined environmental or lifestyle risk factors beyond rare exposure to ionizing radiation, accumulating evidence underscores the critical role of genetic predisposition in disease susceptibility (Le Rhun et al. 2019; Ostrom et al. 2021). Thus, identifying robust genetic biomarkers is imperative for early risk stratification, personalized diagnosis, and the establishment of personalized therapeutic strategies.

Insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins (IGF2BP) family, which comprises three highly conserved members (IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, and IGF2BP3), plays a central role in posttranscriptional gene regulation (Shao et al. 2022; Lederer et al. 2014; Li et al. 2025). Structurally, IGF2BPs contain multiple RNA-binding domains, including RNA recognition motifs and K-homology domains, which mediate the specific recognition of target transcripts (Wachter et al. 2013; Huang et al. 2018b). Notably, IGF2BPs have recently emerged as critical “readers” of N6-methyladenosine (m6A) and internal N7-methylguanosine (m7G) modifications. By regulating the stability, subcellular localization, translation, and degradation of target mRNAs, RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) function as molecular receptors that critically influence cell fate decisions (Hentze et al. 2018; Duan et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024). Under physiological conditions, the IGF2BP family is essential for embryonic development and neurogenesis (Dai et al. 2015; Tybl et al. 2011). In contrast, during oncogenesis, these members are frequently re-expressed as oncofetal proteins (Muller et al. 2020). Their dysregulation contributes to multiple canonical hallmarks of cancer, including accelerated proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and chemoresistance. In various types of malignant tumors, such as pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and renal cell carcinoma, high IGF2BP expression is strongly correlated with poor clinical prognosis and immune evasion (Qiu et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2026; Bell et al. 2013; Elcheva et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2018a). Emerging evidence underscores the pivotal oncogenic role of these genes in glioma. For instance, IGF2BP2-mediated stabilization of let-7 is crucial for preserving glioblastoma stem cells (Degrauwe et al. 2016), whereas IGF2BP3 accelerates glioma progression by remodeling the immunosuppressive microenvironment and modulating tumor ferroptosis (Deng et al. 2024; Dai et al. 2024). While the molecular mechanisms and prognostic value of abnormal IGF2BP expression in glioma are well established, genetic variants remain poorly understood.

Genome-wide association studies (GWASs) indicate that single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within regulatory regions can critically alter gene transcription, thereby modifying individual susceptibility to malignancies (Hawkes et al. 2026). Building upon our previous findings that variants in RNA modification core genes contribute to pediatric glioma risk (He et al. 2021; Liao et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2023), we hypothesized that functional SNPs within RNA “reader” genes may similarly contribute to tumorigenesis. Therefore, we systematically evaluated 11 potentially functional SNPs in IGF2BP1-3 in Chinese children with gliomas. By integrating epidemiological data with eQTL analyses and clinical–molecular data from TCGA, this study aims to provide a detailed genetic landscape of IGF2BP variants in pediatric glioma.

Materials and methods

Study populations

We conducted a multi-hospital, retrospective case–control study in China. Briefly, a total of 360 patients with primary gliomas and 547 glioma-free control children (Table S1) were recruited from three hospitals (Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, Xiangya Hospital, and The Second Affiliated Hospital and Yuying Children’s Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University) derived from three provinces across China. Epidemiological data and case inclusion criteria for gliomas and glioma-free controls are detailed in our previous publication (He et al. 2021). The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center (Approval No. 2026164A01). Detailed information regarding the sample selection process is provided in our previous publication (He et al. 2021).

SNP selection and genotyping

Candidate SNPs within the IGF2BP family genes were systematically identified using the NCBI dbSNP (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp) and SNPinfo (https://snpinfo.niehs.nih.gov/snpinfo/snpfunc.html). We ultimately selected 11 potentially functional SNPs on the basis of three criteria: a minor allele frequency (MAF) > 5% in the Han Chinese population, localization within putative regulatory or coding regions, and low linkage disequilibrium (R2 < 0.8). Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Valencia, CA, USA). Genotyping was performed using the TaqMan allelic discrimination assay on an ABI Q6 system (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) (Yin et al. 2024; Deng et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025). All laboratory personnel were blinded to the clinical status of the samples. For quality assurance, 10% of the samples were randomly selected for duplicate genotyping and analyzed alongside negative controls, yielding a 100% genotypic concordance rate.

eQTL and clinical association analysis

To investigate the putative regulatory mechanisms underlying the identified susceptibility loci, expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) assessment was performed using the publicly accessible Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) Portal database (https://www.gtexportal.org/home/). Specifically, we evaluated the correlations between the significant risk variants and the transcriptional abundance of their adjacent genes across relevant normal tissues, particularly the brain and cultured fibroblasts. Because the GTEx repository is predominantly derived from nonneoplastic adult biospecimens rather than from pediatric neural tissues or primary glioma tumors, these computational evaluations were interpreted as exploratory annotations. Consequently, they are intended to establish preliminary biological plausibility for our epidemiological findings rather than to serve as conclusive mechanistic validation.

Furthermore, RNA-seq data (FPKM format) and corresponding clinical profiles for glioma cohorts were acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). After normal tissues, duplicate records, and cases with incomplete clinical data were excluded, the expression levels of CALCOCO2 and AC099661.1 were normalized via log2 (value + 1) transformation for subsequent clinical association analyses. All the statistical analyses were performed using R software (v4.2.1). Data visualizations were generated using the ggplot2 package.

Statistical analysis

The Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) for each selected SNP in the controls was evaluated using a goodness-of-fit χ2 test. Differences in baseline demographic and clinical variables between cases and frequency-matched controls were assessed using Student’s t test (for continuous data) or the two-sided χ2 test (for categorical data). To estimate the magnitude of the associations between IGF2BP family gene polymorphisms and glioma susceptibility, unconditional multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to calculate the odds ratios (ORs) and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). In these regression models, patient age and sex were systematically included as covariates to adjust for fundamental demographic confounding effects. Genetic effects were systematically evaluated under dominant, recessive, and additive genetic models. The Bonferroni correction was applied to correct for multiple testing in the primary analyses. The stringent threshold for statistical significance was adjusted to P < 0.0015 (0.05/33). Subgroup stratifications and combined risk-genotype (cumulative effect) analyses were conducted as secondary exploratory evaluations. Differences across clinicopathological subgroups were evaluated using Wilcoxon rank sum test. Furthermore, functional annotations of the identified significant variants were performed via eQTL analysis using public data from the GTEx portal, based on linear regression. All the epidemiological statistical procedures were performed using SAS software (version 10.0; SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Bioinformatics analyses and data visualizations (using the ggplot2 package) were executed using R software (version 4.2.1). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered to indicate nominal statistical significance.

Results

Population characteristics

The demographic and clinical characteristics of the 360 patients with glioma and 547 cancer-free controls are detailed in Table S1. The mean age was 70.09 months in the case group and 69.14 months in the control group. The case and control groups showed no significant differences in age distribution (P = 0.500) or sex (P = 0.410), indicating adequate frequency matching. Among the 360 glioma cases, the predominant histological subtype was astrocytic tumors (n = 242, 67.22%), followed by ependymomas (n = 73, 20.28%), neuronal and mixed neuronal–glial tumors (n = 31, 8.61%), and embryonal tumors (n = 14, 3.89%). According to the WHO grading system, nearly half of the gliomas were classified as grade I (n = 179, 49.72%), while grades II, III, and IV accounted for 22.22% (n = 80), 11.94% (n = 43), and 16.11% (n = 58) of the cases, respectively.

IGF2BP1 and IGF2BP2 variants are significantly associated with altered pediatric glioma susceptibility

The associations between 11 SNPs in the IGF2BP family genes and glioma risk in Chinese children are summarized in Table 1. The genotype distributions for all selected SNPs in the control group were consistent with the HWE (all P > 0.05). Following adjustment for age and sex, our logistic regression analysis identified notable associations for SNPs across various genetic models. Specifically, the IGF2BP1 rs2270575 polymorphism was associated with reduced pediatric glioma risk. Carrying the homozygous variant genotype was associated with a significantly reduced susceptibility to developing glioma under the recessive model (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] = 0.57, 95% CI = 0.40–0.82, P = 0.003) (Table 1). Conversely, two variants in the IGF2BP2 gene were shown to elevate the risk. The rs17289925 variant conferred an elevated risk of glioma in both the dominant (AOR = 1.67, 95% CI = 1.25–2.24, P = 0.0005) and the additive (AOR = 1.57, 95% CI = 1.22–2.02, P = 0.0005) models. This effect remained significant after Bonferroni correction. Additionally, the risk effect of the IGF2BP2 rs7646419 polymorphism was significant according to the recessive model (AOR = 1.71, 95% CI = 1.08–2.71, P = 0.021). None of the remaining polymorphisms assessed in the IGF2BP family genes showed a statistically significant link to glioma susceptibility.

As detailed in Table 2, the IGF2BP1 rs2270575 variant was consistently linked to a lower risk of glioma across nearly all the stratified cohorts, including those stratified by age, sex, histological subtype, and clinical stage. Notably, this protective effect was particularly profound in highly malignant subtypes, demonstrating a substantially decreased susceptibility to ependymoma (AOR = 0.35, 95% CI = 0.15–0.83, P = 0.018) and a markedly lower likelihood of developing WHO grade IV tumors (AOR = 0.34, 95% CI = 0.13–0.87, P = 0.025). Conversely, the IGF2BP1 rs3744085 variant acted as a risk factor, specifically for patients with astrocytic tumors (AOR = 1.46, 95% CI = 1.08–1.99, P = 0.016), stage I (AOR = 1.42, 95% CI = 1.01–2.00, P = 0.043), stage II (AOR = 1.62, 95% CI = 1.01–2.59, P = 0.046) and early-stage (I + II) gliomas (AOR = 1.48, 95% CI = 1.10–1.99, P = 0.010). Furthermore, a cumulative risk model revealed that the presence of 4–6 risk genotypes in the IGF2BP1 gene significantly increased disease risk, particularly in younger children (< 60 months: AOR = 1.96, 95% CI = 1.18–3.27, P = 0.010), males (AOR = 1.66, 95% CI = 1.06–2.59, P = 0.025), stage I patients (AOR = 1.88, 95% CI = 1.21–2.92, P = 0.005) and stage I + II patients (AOR = 1.72, 95% CI = 1.18–2.49, P = 0.005).

Subsequent analysis of the IGF2BP2 gene revealed broad risk-elevating effects (Table 3). The rs17289925 polymorphism conferred increased glioma risk across multiple strata, with statistically significant associations observed in both age groups, males, astrocytic and ependymal tumors, and WHO stages I, II, III and I + II. Most strikingly, it conferred an increased risk in males (AOR = 1.85, 95% CI = 1.26–2.71, P = 0.002), and a substantial increased risk for WHO stage III tumors (AOR = 2.32, 95% CI = 1.21–4.45, P = 0.011). Similarly, the rs7646419 variant has been linked to an increased risk for tumors in astrocytes and stage I and I + II tumors. Notably, evaluation of the combined risk profile revealed that carrying 3–4 risk genotypes for IGF2BP2 substantially increased glioma risk across nearly all the evaluated subgroups, including age, sex, tumor subtype, and clinical stage (Table 3).

With respect to the IGF2BP3 gene (Table 4), the rs156414 polymorphism was significantly correlated with increased glioma susceptibility; however, this result was restricted to specific subgroups, primarily younger children (< 60 months: AOR = 1.55, 95% CI = 1.01–2.37, P = 0.043, patients with grade II (AOR = 1.94, 95% CI = 1.13–3.31, P = 0.016) and grade I + II tumors (AOR = 1.48, 95% CI = 1.08–2.03, P = 0.016).

Collectively, these stratified analyses revealed that genetic variants within the IGF2BP family genes strongly affect glioma susceptibility in a subtype specific, and age-dependent manner. Notably, the cumulative risk burden associated with harboring multiple risk alleles is predominantly driven by highly specific, vulnerable patient populations, thereby underscoring the complex genetic architecture underlying childhood glioma.

eQTL analysis reveals the potential regulatory effects of IGF2BP SNPs on downstream target genes

To investigate the potential functional significance of the IGF2BP1 rs2270575 and IGF2BP2 rs7646419 variants, we conducted an eQTL analysis using data from the GTEx portal. Compared with those with the C genotype, the CALCOCO2 mRNA levels in cell-cultured fibroblasts from samples with the rs2270575 A allele were significantly greater (Fig. 1A). Additionally, the rs7646419 A allele was demonstrated a marked correlation with considerably lower mRNA levels of ENSG00000286086 (AC099661.1) in the brain tissues (Fig. 1B).

Discussion

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive evaluation of polymorphisms in IGF2BP family genes and reported that functional variants in IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, and IGF2BP3 significantly modulate the risk of glioma in children. Our findings reveal that the IGF2BP1 rs2270575 variant has a significant protective effect, whereas variants in IGF2BP2 (rs17289925, rs7646419) and IGF2BP3 (rs156414) are associated with increased susceptibility. After strict correction by the Bonferroni method, IGF2BP2 rs17289925 still noteworthy. These genetic associations, supported by eQTL analysis and corroborated by TCGA clinical data, suggest that genetic variations in RNA epigenetic “readers” are pivotal determinants of pediatric glioma etiology.

IGF2BP1 is a prominent m6A reader that typically stabilizes oncogenic mRNAs (Qiu et al. 2025). However, our results demonstrate that the IGF2BP1 rs2270575 variant significantly reduces glioma risk. This protective effect was revealed by our eQTL analysis, which showed that the rs2270575 A allele is associated with increased expression of the adjacent gene CALCOCO2. CALCOCO2 is a highly selective autophagy receptor critical for maintaining cellular homeostasis (Yang et al. 2026; He et al. 2026; Mattioni et al. 2025). In cancer progression and treatment, autophagy, mediated by CALCOCO2, frequently acts as a tumor-suppressive mechanism by clearing damaged organelles and oncogenic proteins (Liao et al. 2018; Zeng et al. 2023). Crucially, our TCGA analysis confirmed that CALCOCO2 expression is significantly elevated in IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted gliomas—molecular subgroups definitively associated with a favorable prognosis—and is inversely correlated with WHO tumor grade (grade 4). We postulate that the rs2270575 variant may alter the local regulatory landscape, upregulating CALCOCO2 to enhance autophagic defense mechanisms against malignant transformation in neural progenitor cells.

Unlike IGF2BP1, the IGF2BP2 variants exerted broad, risk-increasing effects across multiple strata. IGF2BP2 is uniquely recognized for its role in glioblastoma stem cell maintenance and metabolic reprogramming, preserving tumor pluripotency by controlling the let-7/HMGA2 axis (Degrauwe et al. 2016). We found that the IGF2BP2 rs7646419 risk allele is associated with the significant downregulation of AC099661.1 in brain tissues. Our study showed that AC099661.1 expression is positively correlated with favorable IDH mutation status and inversely associated with advanced WHO grade. The suppression of this potentially tumor-suppressive transcript by the rs7646419 risk variant implies a mechanism of cis-regulatory disruption, which perturbs the balanced regulatory network required to prevent oncogenesis.

Furthermore, the risk association of IGF2BP3 rs156414 was notably restricted to specific subgroups, particularly children aged < 60 months. Considering that IGF2BP3 is a critical m7G reader that promotes mRNA degradation during rapid cellular transitions (Liu et al. 2024) and modulates glioma ferroptosis (Deng et al. 2024), this pronounced age-specific effect may reflect its specialized vulnerability during highly sensitive windows of early neurodevelopment.

Because cancer is a polygenic disease, individual SNPs typically confer modest effects, making the cumulative allele dosage model particularly valuable. Our cumulative risk model demonstrated that harboring multiple risk alleles for IGF2BP1 or IGF2BP2 substantially increased glioma susceptibility. This dose-dependent pattern was most pronounced among younger children, males, and patients with early-stage (I + II) tumors. The observed sex disparity aligns with the epidemiological consensus that pediatric brain tumors frequently exhibit male predominance, potentially reflecting the intricate crosstalk between genetic predispositions and sex-specific epigenetic or hormonal environments during early brain development.

Despite the clinical relevance of our findings, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, due to the retrospective nature of this study relying on historical medical records, detailed information regarding other potential confounding factors was unavailable. Consequently, the potential impact of these unmeasured confounders could not be adjusted for in our current models. Furthermore, while our study utilized a multi-hospital design, the reliance on a Chinese cohort may restrict the generalizability of our findings to other geographic regions or diverse ethnic populations. Independent validations in large-scale and prospective multinational cohorts are highly warranted. Second, although the overall sample size was substantial for pediatric glioma, the retrospective case–control design may introduce selection bias, and the statistical power was diminished in certain subgroup stratifications. Consequently, these subgroup analysis results might be susceptible to random variation and the inflation of false positives associated with multiple comparisons. Therefore, these stratified findings should be interpreted with caution as preliminary and hypothesis-generating, warranting rigorous verification in future large-scale studies. Third, the in silico eQTL functional annotations provided by the GTEx and TCGA datasets are predominantly derived from adult tissues. We encountered a critical scarcity of publicly accessible databases featuring simultaneous, paired SNP and RNA-seq datasets specifically for pediatric gliomas. Finally, the fundamental nature of this observational epidemiological study strictly limits our findings to statistical associations rather than definitive biological causality. Exploring the exact causal mechanisms requires rigorous in vitro and in vivo functional assays. The lack of experimental validation using advanced pediatric glioma models, such as patient-derived organoids or CRISPR/Cas9 gene-editing technologies, remains a key limitation. Future comprehensive studies integrating pediatric-specific dual-omics data and robust functional disease models are essential to accurately characterize the underlying molecular mechanisms of these IGF2BP susceptibility variants.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study reveals the first epidemiological evidence that functional variants within the IGF2BP gene family significantly modulate susceptibility to pediatric glioma. These variants, characterized by profound cumulative, age dependent, and subtype-specific effects, hold great promise as noninvasive biomarkers for individualized risk assessment in pediatric populations.

Dual-metric Bayesian optimization of B-spline mesh size for 4DCT lung registration
Authors: Liang Tan, Liyuan Chen, Huanli Luo, Xin Yang, Bin Feng, Fu Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesWe aim to optimize the patient-specific mesh size (N) in the B-spline deformable image registration method, enhancing the computational efficiency of 4DCT lung image registration.MethodsThis study included 37 subjects (10 from the DIRLAB public dataset and 27 from a private 4DCT cohort), each consisting of 10 respiratory phases. A Bayesian optimization (BO) framework was proposed to determine patient-specific N within [2, 50]. Registration accuracy was evaluated using the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) and Hausdorff Distance (HD). To further validate registration robustness, extreme-phase registrations were additionally tested, and inverse consistency error (ICE) was calculated to assess deformation invertibility. A global evaluation approach was also applied across the full respiratory cycle, and the computational cost of the traditional grid search (GS) was analyzed for comparison.ResultsBO efficiently determined patient-specific N, with optimal values ranging from 6 to 15 (overall mean = 10.4 ± 2.6), achieving DSC = 0.976 and HD = 0.814. In the extreme-phase tests, registration performance remained stable between forward and reverse directions, with DSC > 0.94, HD95< 3 mm, and small ICE differences (ICE95 = 0.467 ± 0.230 mm), indicating strong inverse consistency and deformation stability. Compared with GS, BO achieved 50.7 %–99.4 % time savings, while GS showed a power-law increase in runtime (exponent = 2.53).ConclusionsThe proposed BO framework efficiently optimized patient-specific mesh sizes, achieving high registration accuracy and significantly reduced computation time, thereby offering a promising tool to improve efficiency in adaptive radiotherapy and motion-compensated treatment planning.

Introduction

Accurate lung image registration in Four-Dimensional Computed Tomography (4DCT) is critical for radiation therapy planning, tumor motion tracking, and adaptive treatment strategies [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. Precise alignment of respiratory phases enables dose accumulation analysis and motion-compensated treatment delivery, ultimately improving clinical outcomes [6], [7], [8]. However, lung tissue undergoes complex, large-deformation motion due to respiration, making robust and accurate registration particularly challenging [2], [9], [10], [11]. Among deformable image registration (DIR) techniques, B-spline-based DIR is widely used for modeling smooth, continuous deformations while balancing flexibility and regularization [12], [13], [14], [15]. Despite its advantages, optimizing the B-spline mesh size (N) remains a critical challenge, as improper selection can lead to loss of fine details or overfitting, affecting both accuracy and computational efficiency [16]. Accurate 4DCT lung registration also plays a vital role in improving diagnostic reliability and treatment precision by compensating for respiratory motion and ensuring spatial consistency across all breathing phases, which is essential for precise target delineation in thoracic imaging.

Existing B-spline mesh optimization approaches typically rely on empirical tuning or grid search, which are inefficient and lack patient specificity. A coarse mesh may fail to capture subtle deformations, whereas an overly fine mesh introduces noise, increases computational cost, and risks overfitting [17], [18]. Additionally, current evaluation methods such as Extreme Phase Evaluation (EPE) and Phase-to-Phase Evaluation (PPE) provide limited insight into accumulated registration errors across the full respiratory cycle [6], [19], [20], [21], [22]. Most studies further rely on single-metric assessments, such as the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) for volumetric overlap or Hausdorff Distance (HD) for boundary discrepancies, each with inherent limitations [23], [24]. Previous studies have shown that motion-induced misalignment in 4DCT can cause uncertainties in anatomical localization and dose calculation, indirectly influencing diagnostic reliability and treatment accuracy [6], [8], [21]. These factors hinder the adaptability of B-spline registration in clinical settings, where rapid, accurate, and patient-specific optimization is required.

To address these limitations, this study introduces a novel 4DCT lung image registration framework incorporating three key innovations. First, a Global Evaluation (GE) framework quantifies registration accuracy across all 4DCT phases, mitigating phase-dependent biases. Second, a dual-metric evaluation integrates DSC and HD to provide a standardized assessment of both volumetric overlap and boundary accuracy. Third, Bayesian Optimization (BO) replaces conventional grid search for B-spline mesh selection, enabling patient-specific optimization that enhances accuracy and computational efficiency [25], [26], [27]. Through comprehensive evaluation on DIRLAB and a private 4DCT dataset, this study demonstrates the superiority of the proposed framework over conventional approaches, underscoring its potential for clinical implementation in radiation oncology. Therefore, the primary objective of this study is to establish a dual-metric Bayesian optimization framework for patient-specific B-spline mesh size selection in 4DCT lung registration, enabling accurate and efficient parameter determination tailored to individual patients.

Datasets

This retrospective computational study included two datasets acquired using Four Dimensional Computed Tomography (4DCT): a public dataset, DIRLAB [28], [29] (http://www.dir-lab.com/), containing 10 sets of 4DCT data, and a private dataset (PRIVATE) from the Chongqing University Cancer Hospital, consisting of 27 sets of 4DCT data. Each 4DCT dataset includes 10 respiratory phases, denoted as P1, P2, …, P10. Specifically, P1 = end-inhale phase and P6 = end-exhale phase. Adjacent respiratory phases are paired as (Pf, Pm), where for f < 10, m = f + 1; when f = 10, m = 1, resulting in 10 pairs of per case. In each image pair, the former is considered the fixed image (Pf), and the latter is the moving image (Pm). For the private 4DCT dataset, only scans with sufficient image quality and complete thoracic coverage were included, while cases with severe motion artifacts or incomplete data were excluded. Deformation image registration

To accurately capture lung deformation, we employed the Free Form Deformation (FFD) method, a non-rigid, control-grid-based image registration algorithm [30]. A specific form of FFD, the B-spline registration method, was used due to its smoothness and continuity, which allows for the preservation of smooth image deformations during spatial transformations. In the B-spline registration method, we set the N as an optimization parameter, ranging from [2, 50] with a step size of 1. The grid spacing in the in-plane direction, measured in pixels, is calculated as:(1)Grid Spacing = Dimension / N,where the mesh size is equal along all three axes (N × N × N).

The registration process uses the mean square function as the similarity metric, with random sampling set at 1 % of lung voxels. The lung region is selected for sampling using automatically generated masks. To enhance robustness and computational efficiency, a multi-resolution framework is implemented, with shrink factors set to [4, 2, 1] and smoothing sigma set to [2, 1, 0]. The optimizer type was selected as LBFGSB, with a gradient convergence tolerance of 1e-5 and a maximum number of iterations set to 100. As the analysis was fully automated and quantitatively evaluated, no manual image interpretation or blinded reading was performed. This study did not involve diagnostic evaluation or pathological confirmation, as the objective was algorithmic optimization rather than clinical diagnosis.

The goal of the registration process is to determine the optimal transformation T that minimizes the following energy function:(2)MLung(f,m∘T)=1|MLung|∑(x,y,z)∈MLung(m(x,y,z)−f(x,y,z;T))2.Where MLung quantifies the degree of alignment between the moving image (f) and the fixed image (m), m(x,y,z) is the image value of the target image at position (x,y,z), f(x,y,z;T) is the image value of the transformed image at position (x,y,z), obtained by the transformation T, |MLung| is the volume of the lung region.

Evaluation metrics

After registration, the transformed image TN(f) is evaluated against the fixed image using two key metrics: the Dice similarity coefficient and Hausdorff distances, which are formulated as two functions, DSC(N, f, m) and HD(N, f, m), respectively.(3)DSC(N,f,m)=2TN(f)∩mTN(f)+m,(4)HD(N,f,m)=max(maxp∈TN(f)minq∈m‖p-q‖,maxq∈mminp∈TN(f)‖p-q‖).Where p and q represent points from TN(f) and the fixed image, respectively, and ||p - q|| denotes the Euclidean distance between these two points.

Bayesian optimization

Bayesian optimization is a probabilistic model-based optimization technique [26], [31]. Considering the temporal and global nature of 4DCT data, we designed a cost function based on all breathing phases within the 4DCT dataset, incorporating the DSC and HD for the region of interest (lung). Equal weights (0.5 each) were assigned to these two metrics. Additionally, due to the dimensional inconsistency between HD and DSC, the HD was normalized, specifically, normalized_HD = transformed_HD / initial_HD. The normalized Hausdorff distance (normalized_hd) is calculated by dividing the transformed Hausdorff distance by the initial Hausdorff distance, thereby standardizing the HD to a relative scale.

The mathematical expression of the cost function (Best score) is presented below.(5)minN∈[2,50]∑(Pf,Pm)∈imagepairs(wdice⋅(1−Dice(N,f,m)+whd⋅HD(N,f,m))),where wdice and whd represent the weight coefficients (both set to 1) for the DSC and HD, respectively.

Further parameter configurations are as follows: the goal is to minimize the cost function, with the sampler utilizing the TPE (Tree-structured Parzen Estimator) algorithm [32], [33]. Initially, 10 calculations of N are performed, which are treated as prior knowledge. Subsequently, the N is optimized through 10 iterative steps based on observed data to identify the optimal mesh size.

Extreme-phase and inverse consistency evaluation

Beyond consecutive-phase matching, we additionally evaluated registration performance between the extreme respiratory phases P6 and P1 to test the algorithm under large deformations. Specifically, we registered P6→P1 (forward) and P1→P6 (reverse) for all 37 patients. Accuracy was quantified on lung masks using the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95), reported per patient and per direction.

To assess invertibility, we computed the inverse consistency error (ICE). Let T6→1 denote the forward deformation from P6 to P1 and T1→6 the reverse deformation. For each voxel x∈Ω (lung domain),(6)ICE(x)=‖T1→6(T6→1(x))−x‖.

ICE was summarized by mean, median, 95th percentile (ICE95), and maximum, evaluated in both P6 and P1 spaces. To characterize symmetry between directions, we further computed absolute and relative forward–reverse differences for six metrics (DSC, HD95, ICEmean, ICEmedian, ICE95, ICEmax) across all patients.

Validation and Implementation

This study also compares the Bayesian optimization method with the traditional grid search approach on two datasets. A total of 37 subjects (10 from the DIRLAB dataset and 27 from the private 4DCT cohort) were analyzed, each with ten respiratory phases, yielding 370 phase-pair registrations in total. The comparison is made by evaluating the optimal N values and the computational time required within different ranges of N: [2,15], [2,30], and [2,50]. The objective is to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of Bayesian optimization in identifying the optimal mesh size, as well as its computational advantage over the traditional method.

The registration algorithm is implemented using the SimpleITK package, while the Bayesian optimization is carried out in Python using PyCharm Professional Edition (Version 2022.2.2). The experiments were conducted on a personal computer with a 12th Gen Intel® Core™ i9–12900KF processor (3.19 GHz) and 64.0 GB of RAM.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that patient-specific optimization of B-spline N via Bayesian optimization improves 4DCT lung image registration, with N ranging from 6 to 15 and mean values of 9.2 ± 2.1 (DIRLAB dataset) and 10.8 ± 2.7 (Private dataset). This difference may be attributed to variations in patient-specific anatomical characteristics, image quality, size, or resolution [3], [9], which is consistent with previous findings on inter-patient variability in lung image registration performance. The observed variability in optimal N underscores the importance of personalized registration over fixed parameter selection. Future research should explore further image preprocessing (e.g., noise reduction) and standardization techniques (e.g., uniform image sizing or resampling) to enhance registration consistency and robustness [34], [35].

The radar plots in Fig. 3 illustrate that the optimized cost function values (Best score) range from [7.3, 9.8] for DIRLAB and [6.7, 9.9] for the Private dataset, with average values across two breathing phases of 0.837 (Best score), 0.976 (DSC), and 0.814 (HD), indicating high registration accuracy. Notably, equal weighting (coefficient = 1) was assigned to both DSC and HD [6], which directly influences the Best score and indirectly impacts the determination of the optimal mesh size. Therefore, balancing DSC and HD weights based on clinical considerations is crucial for optimizing registration performance. Additionally, to further refine registration evaluation, additional metrics such as Target Registration Error (TRE) [9], [36], [37], which relies on manually labeled anatomical landmarks, could be incorporated to provide a more comprehensive assessment.

We evaluated the performance of GS for deformable image registration across two breathing phases. The results indicate that registration accuracy, measured by DSC and HD, does not exhibit a strictly monotonic relationship with the B-spline N. Specifically, as N increases, the overall trend initially declines, then improves, with noticeable local fluctuations. Notably, when N exceeds 20, the DSC deteriorates to levels even worse than pre-registration values. Moreover, registration time increases significantly with N, following a power-law relationship with an exponent of 2.53. In contrast, Bayesian optimization (BO) substantially enhances computational efficiency compared to GS, reducing runtime by 50.7 % (N ≤ 15), 95.8 % (N ≤ 30), and 99.4 % (N ≤ 50). Clinically, this acceleration in deformable image registration is critical, enabling real-time or near-real-time applications in adaptive radiotherapy, improving workflow efficiency and patient outcomes. The proposed framework may assist clinicians and medical physicists by improving registration efficiency and ensuring more reliable motion-compensated dose delivery.

Future studies should focus on multi-parameter optimization, extending beyond mesh size to include regularization weights and similarity metrics for more comprehensive performance improvements. Expanding the dataset to diverse patient populations and imaging modalities (e.g., MRI-based lung motion tracking) will further validate the robustness of the approach. Given the current single-center and relatively small sample size, multi-center validation with larger and more heterogeneous datasets is essential to confirm generalizability. Additionally, integrating deep learning-based priors with Bayesian optimization could refine parameter selection and accelerate convergence. Finally, exploring adaptive search space refinement strategies and hybrid BO-deep learning frameworks may enhance both efficiency and registration accuracy in large-scale clinical applications.

In conclusion，we demonstrated that Bayesian optimization provides a quantitative and efficient framework for personalized B-spline mesh size selection, significantly improving 4DCT lung image registration accuracy and computational efficiency compared to traditional grid search. By tailoring parameters to individual patients, the proposed approach enhances registration reliability, which may contribute to more efficient adaptive radiotherapy workflows. Results show that Bayesian optimization not only reduces computational costs by up to 99.4 % but also maintains high DSC (0.976) and low HD (0.814), ensuring robust registration performance.

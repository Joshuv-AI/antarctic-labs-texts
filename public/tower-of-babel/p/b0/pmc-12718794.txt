The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on dyslipidemia in peritoneal dialysis patients
Authors: Fan-Yu Chen, Shu-En Li, Jen-En Tsai, Po-Yen Lu, Yu-Hsuan Li, Ann Charis Tan, Szu-Yuan Li, Yi-Fang Chuang, Chyong-Mei Chen, Chih-Ching Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic has had a profound impacted on various aspects of society, including the healthcare system and patient care. In this context, this study aimed to evaluate the impact of COVID-19 control strategies on the lipid profile and blood sugar levels of peritoneal dialysis (PD) patients in Taiwan, a crucial focus for understanding the pandemic’s influence on individuals with chronic kidney disease (CKD).Methods:A retrospective cohort study was conducted, analyzing data from the medical records of 170 PD patients who visited the nephrology division of Taipei Veterans General Hospital in 2021. The generalized estimating equations method was used to analyze the longitudinal data and assess the changes in biomarker levels over different periods. Covariates were taken into consideration in the statistical models.Results:The study enrolled 70 (41%) males and 100 (59%) females, with an average age of 56 years old. Over 12 months in 2021, from the first period (January to April: pre-COVID-19) to the second period (May to August: COVID-19 surge), there was a notable decline in both high-density lipoprotein (HDL) and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels, and a significant surge in triglyceride (TG) levels. However, total cholesterol (TC) and hemoglobin (HbA1c) levels remained stable. Furthermore, the TG to HDL, TG to LDL, TC to HDL, and TC to LDL ratios were analyzed, revealing a pronounced increase during the second period.Conclusion:Our findings underscore the significant impact of COVID-19 pandemic-related disruptions in the healthcare system and the subsequent management strategies on dyslipidemia in PD patients while not affecting dysglycemia. These results provide valuable insights for healthcare professionals to enhance their strategies and interventions for CKD patients undergoing PD during a pandemic.Keywords: COVID-19, Dyslipidemia, Peritoneal dialysis

1. INTRODUCTION

The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic has far-reaching consequences worldwide, profoundly impacting society, health, and the global economy.1,2 This unprecedented crisis has led to a worldwide public health emergency, with millions of individuals contracting the virus and experiencing illness and hospitalization.3 The healthcare system worldwide has been severely strained as a result. Within this context, managing individuals with chronic kidney disease (CKD), particularly those undergoing maintenance dialysis, has posed unique challenges. CKD is a prevalent health concern both globally and locally in Taiwan, affecting a substantial number of individuals.4–11 Previous studies have indicated that the COVID-19 pandemic has resulted in significant biochemical changes in the general population, particularly impacting lipid profiles and glucose metabolism.12,13 Many COVID-19 patients have dyslipidemia, marked by reduced total cholesterol (TC) and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels, suggesting a potential association between lipid metabolism and COVID-19 outcomes. Patients with low levels of high-density lipoprotein (HDL) and elevated blood triglycerides (TG) tend to experience more severe disease progression. In addition, uncontrolled diabetes mellitus (DM) is acknowledged as an independent risk factor for poor clinical outcomes and increased mortality rates. However, the relationship between dyslipidemia and COVID-19 remains unclear, as previous studies had small sample sizes and produced inconsistent findings. Moreover, there is a significant gap in research specifically investigating these biochemical changes in those with end-stage renal disease (ESRD).

Understanding the influence of the COVID-19 pandemic on these metabolic parameters is crucial, as disruptions in the healthcare system during the pandemic could potentially contribute to significant changes. Therefore, this study aims to assess the impact of COVID-19 pandemic control strategies on the lipid profile and blood sugar levels of PD patients. By examining the effects of pandemic control strategies, such as lockdowns, social distancing measures, and changes in healthcare access, on the lipid profile and blood sugar levels, valuable insights can be gained regarding the broader health implications of the pandemic. These findings may inform healthcare professionals to enhance their strategies and interventions to mitigate the impact of the pandemic on the metabolic health of PD patients.

2. METHODS

2.1. Study design

The retrospective cohort study explored the alterations in lipid profile and blood sugar levels of PD patients from January 1 to December 31, 2021. The data were collected and analyzed from the medical records of PD patients who visited the nephrology division of Taipei Veterans General Hospital during that year.

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria of participants

This study focused on adult patients (older than 18) with ESRD who had been stable and on maintenance PD for at least 3 months. Patients who passed away, transferred to another hospital, shifted from PD to HD, or received a kidney transplant during the study period were excluded from the analysis—the final sample number for analysis comprised 170 patients, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

Patient enrollment flowchart. ESRD = end-stage renal disease; HD = hemodialysis; PD = peritoneal dialysis.

2.3. Variables

2.3.1. Pandemic fluctuations during the three periods

The exposure variable for this study was the COVID-19 period. The study period was divided into three periods: January to April as the first period, May to August as the second period, and September to December as the third period. The first period represented the period before the effects of the pandemic were observed. The second period represented the period when the number of confirmed cases and deaths in Taiwan increased and pandemic control strategies were implemented. The third period represented the period when the number of cases and deaths started to decline, and the control measures gradually relaxed. Data on COVID-19 cases and deaths were obtained from the Taiwan Centers for Disease Control,14 while the periods of control measures were determined based on information from Taiwan’s Ministry of Health website.15

2.3.2. Outcome variables

The outcome variables of interest were the changes in dyslipidemia and dysglycemia biomarkers. Dyslipidemia biomarkers included TC, HDL, LDL, and triglyceride (TG). The dysglycemia biomarker was glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c). The TG to HDL, TG to LDL, TC to HDL, and TC to LDL ratios were calculated as additional outcome variables. These biomarker levels were measured and obtained from hospital records during routine checkups, which occurred at least once every 3 months during the study period.

2.3.3. Covariates

The analysis of changes in TC, HDL, LDL, TG, and HbA1c levels included several covariates. The covariates were sex and age, as well as comorbidities such as DM, hypertension, and dyslipidemia. Sex was incorporated into the models using females as the reference category, and age was included as a continuous variable. The comorbidities were dichotomized into “yes” (indicating the presence of the disease) or “no” (indicating the absence of the disease). The “no” category was used as the reference for the “yes” category in the statistical models.

2.4. Statistical methods

For continuous variables, mean and standard deviation were calculated if the variable was normally distributed. If the variable was not normally distributed, median and interquartile range were calculated. For binomial or discrete data, the number and percentage were calculated. The normal distribution of all variables was tested using the Shapiro-Wilk test at α = 0.05. The main objective of this study was to examine the changes in biomarkers in PD patients across different pandemic periods. The generalized estimating equation (GEE) models for linear regression were used to analyze longitudinal data. The GEE analysis calculated population average effects covariates with the working correlation matrix, which modeled the correlation between observations. The GEE models demonstrated robustness against the misspecification of the correlation structure.16 The choice of correlation structures aimed to accurately represent how COVID-19 management affected the alteration of lipid profile and blood sugar levels during the specified periods. The period variable was modeled as a dummy variable, with the first period serving as the reference. Five main outcome variables (CHOL, LDL, HDL, TG, and HbA1c) and additional biomarker ratios (TG/HDL, TG/LDL, TC/HDL, and TC/LDL) were analyzed using a simple regression model and three additional multiple regression models for adjusting the potential covariates. The multiple regression models gradually incorporated covariates in the regression, starting from sex and age in the first model, then adding diabetes and hypertension in the second model, and finally including dyslipidemia in the third model. Most biomarkers in the GEE analysis utilized the exchangeable correlation structure, which assumed an equal correlation between all pairs of responses in a subject. However, the unstructured correlation structure was used for HbA1c due to the lack of fit when implementing the exchangeable correlation. The unstructured correlation allowed the data to vary freely.

3. RESULTS

3.1. Patient baseline characteristics

The baseline characteristics of the patients are illustrated in Table 1. The average age of the patients was 56 ± 12.69 years old. The median duration of PD for the patients was 3.69 (1.88-6.87) years. A significant number of patients had comorbidities with 73% having hypertension, 24% having DM, and 65% having dyslipidemia. The biomarker levels at baseline were as follows: median cholesterol level of 156 mg/dL (135-128), HDL level of 39 mg/dL (30-55), LDL level of 86 mg/dL (69-112), TG level of 115 mg/dL (80-174), and HbA1c level of 5.3 % (5.0-5.8).

3.2.Changes in COVID-19 confirmed cases, deaths, biomarker levels, and the population average of biomarkers in patients based on the GEE approach

Fig. 2 illustrates the monthly average of biomarker values in the patient population juxtaposed with the confirmed and fatal cases in Taiwan for 2021. A sharp surge in confirmed cases was observed between April and May, which tapered off until August (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Fig. 1, http://links.lww.com/JCMA/A282). Fatalities began to climb post-May, peaking in June, and subsequently decreased until August (Fig. 2B). Throughout this year-long span, biomarker levels displayed notable variations. The outcomes derived from the GEE models are detailed in Table 2. In the simple regression analysis (model 1), the second period didn’t show a marked drop in TC levels when compared to the first period (p: 0.36; 95% CI, −4.79 to 1.78). However, a borderline significant uptick was evident in the third period (p: 0.05; 95% CI, −0.26 to 17.10). The second period revealed a pronounced decrease in HDL levels by 2.07 (p: 0.004; 95% CI, −3.49 to −0.65) and LDL levels by 4.99 (p: 0.005; 95% CI, −8.49 to −1.50). Conversely, TG levels surged by 15.73 (p: 0.002; 95% CI, 5.51-25.9) during the second period. HbA1c levels remained relatively stable across both periods. After adjusting for covariates, models 2, 3, and 4 mirrored these trends, reinforcing the consistency of the findings. Neither cholesterol nor HbA1c exhibited significant shifts in any period across all models. 4. DISCUSSION

The primary aim of this study is to investigate the potential correlation between the specific periods of the COVID-19 pandemic in Taiwan and the precise changes in lipid profile and blood sugar biomarkers among patients undergoing PD. Through the use of simple regression analysis, we compared the second period (May to August) with the first period (January to April). The results revealed a statistically significant decrease in HDL and LDL levels, coupled with a noteworthy increase in TG levels. Conversely, TC and HbA1c levels exhibited a non-significant change during the study period.

The pandemic significantly impacted daily life during the second period, primarily due to the government’s implementation of alert levels 2 and 3 on May 11 and May 15, respectively,15 aimed at curbing the COVID-19 spread (Figs. 2A, B), which led to a notable decline in social and outdoor activities, such as work, doctor visits, classes, and shopping. Several factors likely influenced the observed changes in lipids and blood sugar levels. Lockdown measures probably affected dietary habits and physical activity, which may impact these biomarkers.17,18 Altered dietary patterns and reduced physical activity have been linked to lipid and blood sugar changes.19–21 Extended periods of inactivity can increase TG levels and decrease HDL levels, affecting lipid metabolism.22 Reduced physical activity may also worsen blood sugar control, especially in diabetes patients.21 Pandemic-related disruptions to schedules and healthcare access may have hindered lipid and blood sugar management.23 Delayed medical checkups and medication refills could contribute to suboptimal control. Dietary and emotional factors likely played roles24,25 wherein uncertainties due to the pandemic and the resulting stress of facing such uncertainties may alter eating behavior, affecting these biomarkers.26–28

Based on Fig. 3A and Table 2, the study observed a decrease in TC levels during the second period compared to the first period (p: 0.36; 95% CI, −4.79 to 1.78). This finding contradicts another study that reported increased TC, LDL, and serum glucose levels after 3 months of lockdown,29 suggesting intricate pandemic impacts on lipid and blood sugar levels that need further clarification. The results (Fig. 3B, C, and Table 2) indicated a significant decrease in LDL (p: 0.005; 95% CI, −8.49 to −1.50) and HDL (p: 0.004; 95% CI, −3.49 to −0.65) levels during the second period contrary to a typical LDL rise with reduced activity.30 The only significant increase observed in the study was the TG levels (p: 0.002; 95% CI, 5.51-25.9) (Fig. 3D, Table 2). Regular physical activity is known to positively affect HDL levels,19 while insufficient exercise can result in reduced metabolism and clearance of TG. Therefore, the restrictions during the pandemic may present limited opportunities for physical activity for patients, leading to a decline in HDL levels and higher TG levels.19,20 The dietary habits, food availability, and eating behaviors of PD patients may have been influenced by the pandemic and lockdown measures.18,24,25,31,32 Altered diets due to limited restaurant access may have increased fresh and healthy food consumption, contributing to lower LDL levels.33 However, unbalanced diets might explain HDL and TG fluctuations.31 In addition, because the liver transports endogenous TG to peripheral tissues within the central structure of very LDL (VLDL), the dimensions of which closely mirror their TG concentration,34 an elevation in TG levels may stem from both heightened hepatic VLDL production and secretion, as well as a reduction in the clearance of lipoproteins rich in TG.35 The Friedewald formula can estimate the VLDL value as TG/5.36 With the increase in VLDL, TG may also increase due to the lockdown. Peritoneal dialysis patients may also have experienced heightened awareness of the importance of medication adherence during the pandemic. This increased awareness could have reinforced the significance of consistently taking cholesterol-lowering medications, such as statins, which are known to reduce LDL levels effectively. Statins work by slowing down cholesterol production in the liver and have been shown to reduce the risk of vascular events.37–39 There was a 6% increase in statin usage among PD patients from 56% to 62%, which may serve as a possible explanation for the reduction in LDL levels during the second period. Chronic stress also has the potential to decrease HDL levels.27,32 Therefore, this may have resulted in the observed decrease in HDL levels.

The TG to HDL ratio is vital for assessing cardiovascular health and lipid metabolism and predicting coronary heart disease.40 Similarly, the TG to LDL ratio predicts small, dense LDL.41 TG to HDL ratio significantly increased (p: 0.01; 95% CI, 0.205-1.20) in the second period (Fig. 4A, Table 2). TG to LDL ratio also increased significantly (p: 0.001; 95% CI, 0.141-0.640) (Fig. 4B, Table 2). The TC to HDL ratio, also an essential cardiovascular indicator,42,43 showed a significant increase (p: 0.027; 95% CI, 0.023-0.40), as did the TC to LDL ratio (p: 0.001; 95% CI, 0.048-0.20) in the second period (Figs. 4C, D and Table 2).

The analysis of lipid-lowering agents used by PD patients revealed no significant changes over the study period. Statin use remained stable at 49.4% in the first and third periods and slightly increased to 50.0% in the second period. Similarly, fibrate use showed no significant variation, declining from 4.7% in T1 to 2.9% in the second and third periods. These findings suggest consistent lipid management strategies in the study cohort (Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/JCMA/A283).

The absence of a statistically significant HbA1c level change during the pandemic (Fig. 3E, Table 2) suggests stable blood glucose control. Unlike lipid monitoring, which requires regular hospital visits, finger glucose levels can be home-monitored with glucometers, potentially promoting consistent tracking. The heightened awareness allowed for quick adjustments to lifestyle and eating habits in response to fluctuations in blood sugar levels. Some studies on the effect of COVID-19 lockdown on glycemic control in type 2 diabetes patients have also shown no significant changes.44,45

This study has several limitations. First, the study was conducted at a single center in Taiwan, which may limit the generalizability of the results to other regions or populations with different healthcare systems, cultural backgrounds, and pandemic control strategies. The lack of a control group is a notable limitation of the study. This absence hinders our ability to draw definitive causal inferences from our findings. Future research should incorporate control groups to validate the observed effects and enhance the reliability of the conclusion. While some covariates were considered, the absence of direct measurements of dietary intake, physical activity levels, psychological status, socioeconomic factors, and access to healthcare services may hinder our understanding of the influence on the observed changes in lipid profiles. Moreover, the low number of COVID-19 infections in the cohort, with only one patient testing positive during the study period, limits our ability to conclude the direct impact of COVID-19 on biomarkers in PD patients.

In conclusion, this study presents compelling evidence that COVID-19 pandemic control strategies significantly impacted the lipid profile of PD patients, namely increased TG levels and decreased LDL and HDL levels, while showing relatively stable changes in HbA1c levels during the lockdown period. It is of the utmost importance to closely monitor and manage these biomarkers to optimize patient care and improve outcomes. The findings provide valuable insights into the wider health implications of the pandemic and hold promise for refining healthcare strategies and interventions for CKD patients undergoing PD.

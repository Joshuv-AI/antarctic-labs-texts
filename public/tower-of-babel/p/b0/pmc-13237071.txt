FusionLSTM-CNF: a confidence-calibrated multi-modal late fusion framework for robust stock movement prediction under uncertainty
Authors: Tian Wen Wang, Zaffar Ahmed Shaikh, Sook Lu Yong, Hela Elmannai, Lip Yee Por
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Financial markets exhibit complex, non-linear dynamics characterized by high volatility and uncertainty, making accurate stock movement prediction a challenging task. This study introduces FusionLSTM-CNF, a hybrid deep learning framework that integrates multi-modal data fusion, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and confidence calibration for stock movement prediction under uncertainty. Our model leverages a late fusion architecture, combining the outputs of three parallel LSTM sub-models trained on technical indicators, textual sentiment from financial news, and cross-asset correlation signals. A confidence-aware neural fusion (CNF) layer adaptively reweights modality contributions based on learned uncertainty estimates. We validate our model across multiple financial indices including S&P 500, NASDAQ, and FTSE 100. Experimental results show a 12.3% relative improvement in prediction accuracy over single-modal LSTM baselines and 23.7% reduction in prediction variance. Compared to recent state-of-the-art hybrid architectures, improvements are more modest (1.1–1.8% absolute accuracy gain) but statistically significant (Diebold-Mariano test, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p < 0.05$$\end{document}). The framework provides calibrated confidence estimates (Expected Calibration Error: 0.031) that enable confidence-filtered trading strategies with improved risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe ratio: 0.267 for top-40% confidence trades versus 0.131 unfiltered). This work contributes to financial artificial intelligence by demonstrating the practical benefits of unifying uncertainty quantification with heterogeneous time-series fusion, providing practitioners with both predictions and confidence estimates for risk-aware decision-making.

Introduction

Financial market prediction remains challenging due to inherent volatility, non-linear dependencies, and external shocks1–3. While deep learning approaches particularly LSTM networks have shown promise4,5, existing methods face three critical limitations: (1) reliance on single-modal data that ignores alternative sources such as sentiment and cross-asset correlations6,7; (2) point predictions without uncertainty quantification essential for risk management8,9; and (3) limited interpretability for regulatory compliance10,11.

This paper introduces FusionLSTM-CNF to address these limitations through: (1) parallel LSTM sub-networks preserving modality-specific temporal patterns, (2) a confidence-aware neural fusion layer enabling adaptive modality weighting, and (3) an uncertainty quantification module distinguishing epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty. Related work

The application of deep learning techniques to financial market prediction has evolved significantly over the past decade, with researchers exploring various neural network architectures and data fusion strategies to improve prediction accuracy and robustness. Early approaches in this domain focused primarily on single-modal data processing using traditional machine learning algorithms, but recent developments have shown the superiority of deep learning methods, particularly LSTM-based architectures for financial forecasting.

LSTM-based financial prediction models

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks have emerged as the dominant architecture for financial time series prediction due to their ability to capture long-term dependencies and handle sequential data effectively. Harbaoui and Elhadjamor1 proposed an LSTM-RNN fusion model that combines the collective potential of LSTM and RNN architectures to address the intricate dynamics of financial market forecasting. Their approach demonstrated improved performance through the strategic fusion of features extracted from both LSTM and RNN layers.

Yang et al.2 developed a Multi-Sensor Temporal Fusion Transformer that incorporates an adaptive Sharpe ratio approach for stock performance prediction. Their work highlighted that while LSTM performs comparatively well for shorter prediction horizons, it falls behind transformer-based approaches for longer-term forecasting. Similarly, Sivakumar3 introduced an HMM-LSTM fusion model for economic forecasting, comparing traditional ARIMA models with LSTM networks on various financial indices including IXIC, GSPC, and DJI12.

The integration of LSTM with other deep learning architectures has shown promising results. Raju et al.5 proposed a CNN-LSTM fusion approach for mutual fund performance analysis, incorporating explainable AI techniques to improve the interpretability of financial forecasting models. Pan et al.13 developed a MULTI-GARCH-LSTM mixed model that integrates GARCH family models with LSTM to more effectively capture volatility clustering effects in financial data.

Multi-modal fusion approaches

The incorporation of multiple data modalities has become increasingly important in financial prediction systems. Chen14 developed a deep learning-based feature fusion and prediction approach that handles non-stationary time series data through sophisticated preprocessing and fusion procedures. Their Deep LSTM-MLP architecture demonstrated superior performance compared to traditional single-modal approaches.

Chen et al.6 proposed a multimodal deep fusion model that combines financial news analysis with LSTM-based temporal pattern recognition. Their approach uses separate branches for processing textual information from financial news and temporal patterns in multivariate data, achieving improved prediction accuracy through late fusion strategies.

Graph-based approaches have also gained attention for incorporating relational information. Zhang et al.7 developed a graph-based stock prediction model with multisource information and relational data fusion. Their method separately processes behavioral finance information, transaction data, and textual data, realizing early fusion of technical indicators and textual data through tensor operations. Shi et al.15 further advanced this direction with integrated GCN-LSTM approaches for stock price movement prediction based on knowledge-incorporated graph construction.

Multi-feature fusion models have shown significant promise in stock price forecasting. Li et al.16 proposed the MLBGK model as a novel feature fusion approach, while Wu et al.17 developed futures price prediction models based on multi-feature fusion techniques. Wang18 explored improved feedforward neural networks with multimodal fusion for enhanced stock prediction capabilities.

Uncertainty quantification in financial models

The quantification of prediction uncertainty has become crucial for practical financial applications. Xu et al.19 proposed a hybrid LSTM-GARCH framework for financial market volatility risk prediction, providing solid financial theoretical explanations while effectively fitting nonlinear characteristics in the data. Their fusion approach significantly improved prediction reliability by incorporating uncertainty estimates.

Wei et al.20 demonstrated the importance of integrated data and transformer-based deep learning for financial risk analysis. Their study showed that bidirectional LSTM can effectively capture dependencies in financial data while providing uncertainty estimates through ensemble methods. Sheng21 further explored temporal dependency modeling in loan default prediction using hybrid LSTM-GRU architectures with uncertainty quantification capabilities.

Advanced approaches to financial crisis prediction have incorporated uncertainty modeling. Liu et al.22 developed hybrid CNN-LSTM models for financial crisis prediction, while Dong23 investigated financial crisis prediction models based on the fusion of financial and non-financial data with uncertainty-aware algorithms.

Advanced fusion architectures

Recent developments have focused on more sophisticated fusion mechanisms. Friday et al.8 introduced a multi-modal approach using hybrid Vision Transformer and Temporal Fusion Transformer models for stock price movement classification. Their architecture processes vast quantities of textual data from financial articles and company reports while handling trading data through LSTM models.

Zhang et al.9 developed a SentiStack Network based on stacking LSTM architecture for Bitcoin forecasting, fusing sentiment and market signals. Their approach combined stock price data with sentiment signals extracted from financial news using multimodal deep reinforcement learning principles.

Pei et al.11 proposed a cross-modal temporal fusion mechanism for financial market forecasting that dynamically weighs different data modalities. Their LSTM-based sequential processing with cross-modal attention demonstrated superior performance on real-world financial datasets.

Enhanced ensemble modeling approaches have also gained traction. Liu et al.26 developed optimized ensemble modeling using multi-source heterogeneous data with LSTM attention mechanisms. Cai et al.27 explored fusion k-means clustering with multi-head self-attention mechanisms for multivariate time prediction models with feature selection capabilities.

Specialized applications and domains

Several studies have focused on specific financial applications. Qiu et al.24 developed a two-stage deep fusion integration framework for gold price forecasting, employing BP-CNN-LSTM fusion models with feature fusion and residual correction mechanisms. Yao et al.25 created a hybrid CNN-LSTM model for bond default risk prediction, combining convolutional feature extraction with temporal modeling.

For cryptocurrency markets, Chen et al.28 proposed a multi-source data fusion method based on CSTNet for Bitcoin price prediction using social media data. Their approach effectively integrates diverse data sources through novel Transformer-enhanced architectures combined with LSTM and regression components. Peik et al.29 leveraged time series categorization and temporal fusion transformers to improve cryptocurrency price forecasting performance.

Sentiment analysis integration has become increasingly important. Gu et al.30 developed FinBERT-LSTM approaches for integrating news sentiment analysis with stock price prediction. Wang et al.31 explored automatic generation of critical audit matters using LSTM-MacBERT-based dual-stream transfer learning.

Sector-specific applications have also emerged. Wang et al.32 focused on multimodal market information fusion for stock price trend prediction in the pharmaceutical sector. Dong and Hao33 proposed stock prediction methods based on multidimensional and multilevel feature dynamic fusion. Hu and Qu34 investigated Trans-LSTM based arbitrage strategies for science and technology boards.

Advanced hybrid architectures and optimization techniques

Recent advances in financial prediction have witnessed the emergence of sophisticated hybrid architectures that combine multiple deep learning paradigms with evolutionary optimization techniques. These approaches aim to leverage the complementary strengths of different neural network architectures while optimizing model configurations through meta-heuristic algorithms.

Multi-level fusion strategies have gained significant attention for their ability to extract hierarchical feature representations. Hybrid frameworks combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for spatial feature extraction with Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) for temporal dependency modeling have demonstrated improved performance in capturing both local patterns and long-range dependencies in financial time series. For instance, recent work on stable technical feature extraction with GRU-CNN-GA fusion35 proposed an integrated architecture that employs genetic algorithms (GA) for adaptive feature selection and hyperparameter optimization, achieving enhanced stability under volatile market conditions. This approach highlights the importance of combining architectural innovations with intelligent optimization techniques.

Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) have emerged as competitive alternatives to recurrent architectures due to their parallelizable structure and ability to capture long-range dependencies through dilated causal convolutions. The BiMT-TCN framework36 introduced a bidirectional multi-scale temporal convolutional architecture specifically designed for stock price prediction, demonstrating superior performance in capturing multi-resolution temporal patterns. Such approaches underscore the evolving landscape of sequence modeling in financial applications.

Bayesian optimization and adaptive weighting mechanisms have also been explored for dynamic model configuration. Unlike static fusion weights, Bayesian adaptive approaches can automatically adjust the contribution of different model components based on uncertainty estimates and historical performance, providing a principled framework for handling non-stationary financial data. Our proposed confidence-aware neural fusion (CNF) mechanism shares this philosophy of adaptive weighting but distinguishes itself by explicitly modeling both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties through a unified neural architecture, enabling end-to-end training without requiring separate optimization procedures. Despite these advances, existing hybrid approaches typically focus on either architectural innovations or optimization techniques in isolation, without jointly addressing the critical challenge of uncertainty quantification. Furthermore, most multi-level fusion strategies employ early or intermediate fusion, which may compromise modality-specific temporal patterns. Our FusionLSTM-CNF framework addresses these limitations by combining late fusion architecture with confidence-aware adaptive weighting, providing both improved prediction accuracy and calibrated uncertainty estimates essential for risk-aware financial decision-making.

Current limitations and research gaps

Despite these advances, existing approaches face several limitations that motivate our proposed FusionLSTM-CNF framework. First, most multi-modal approaches employ early fusion strategies that may lose important modal-specific temporal patterns by combining features too early in the processing pipeline16,18. Second, current uncertainty quantification methods often focus on either epistemic or aleatoric uncertainty in isolation, without considering their joint modeling and interaction effects21,22. Third, the lack of adaptive fusion mechanisms that can dynamically adjust the contribution of different modalities based on data quality and uncertainty estimates represents a significant gap in current literature29,33.

Our proposed FusionLSTM-CNF framework addresses these limitations by introducing a late fusion architecture with confidence-aware weighting mechanisms that can effectively handle multi-modal financial data while providing calibrated uncertainty estimates for robust decision-making.

Proposed methodology

The FusionLSTM-CNF framework is designed to address the challenges of multi-modal financial prediction by leveraging the complementary strengths of different data modalities while providing reliable uncertainty estimates. The architecture consists of three main components: parallel LSTM sub-networks for modality-specific feature extraction, a confidence-aware neural fusion layer for adaptive combination, and an uncertainty quantification module for epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty estimation.

As shown in Fig. 2, the framework processes three distinct data modalities through separate LSTM networks to preserve the temporal characteristics of each data stream. The technical indicators modality includes price-based features such as moving averages, relative strength index (RSI), and Bollinger bands. The sentiment modality incorporates textual features extracted from financial news articles and social media posts using pre-trained language models. The cross-asset correlation modality captures inter-market relationships and sector-specific dynamics through correlation matrices and volatility spillover effects.Fig. 2Detailed architecture of the FusionLSTM-CNF framework showing parallel LSTM processing, confidence-aware fusion, and uncertainty quantification components.

Detailed architecture of the FusionLSTM-CNF framework showing parallel LSTM processing, confidence-aware fusion, and uncertainty quantification components.

The parallel LSTM sub-networks are designed to capture modality-specific temporal patterns without interference from other data streams. Each LSTM network consists of multiple layers with dropout regularization and batch normalization to prevent overfitting and improve training stability. The hidden states from each LSTM network are concatenated and passed through fully connected layers to generate modality-specific predictions and uncertainty estimates.

The confidence-aware neural fusion (CNF) layer represents the core innovation of our framework, as detailed in Fig. 3. This component dynamically adjusts the contribution of each modality based on the uncertainty estimates and historical performance of individual sub-networks. The fusion weights are computed using a dedicated neural network that takes as input the uncertainty estimates from each modality and outputs normalized attention weights.Fig. 3Confidence-aware neural fusion mechanism showing uncertainty-based weight computation and adaptive modality combination.

Confidence-aware neural fusion mechanism showing uncertainty-based weight computation and adaptive modality combination.

The uncertainty quantification module is designed to capture both epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty) and aleatoric uncertainty (data uncertainty) through a combination of Monte Carlo dropout and learned variance estimation. Epistemic uncertainty reflects the model’s confidence in its parameters and can be reduced with more training data, while aleatoric uncertainty captures the inherent noise in the data that cannot be reduced through additional observations.

For epistemic uncertainty estimation, we perform \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K = 50$$\end{document} stochastic forward passes with dropout enabled (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p = 0.3$$\end{document}) during inference. The final prediction is the mean across passes (Eq. 12), and epistemic variance follows Eq. 13. We verified that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K = 50$$\end{document} provides stable estimates: the coefficient of variation of epistemic uncertainty is below 5% across 100 repeated inference runs. Increasing to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K = 100$$\end{document} improved stability by only 0.3% while doubling inference time.

Post-hoc temperature scaling

After training, we apply temperature scaling to further improve calibration. A single temperature parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{cal}$$\end{document} is optimized on the validation set by minimizing negative log-likelihood:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} T_{cal}^* = \arg \min _{T} -\sum _{i=1}^{N_{val}} \log p(y_i | \hat{y}_i / T) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}The optimal value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{cal}^* = 1.12$$\end{document} is applied during test inference. This reduces ECE from 0.038 (before scaling) to 0.031 (after scaling).

Computational environment and reproducibility

All experiments use fixed random seeds (PyTorch: 42, NumPy: 42, Python: 42) with CUDA deterministic mode enabled. We conducted 5 independent runs with different weight initializations and report the results from the best-performing run based on validation set performance. Training time for the full model is approximately 28.4 minutes per epoch on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. Total training time including hyperparameter optimization is approximately 72 GPU-hours.

Dataset construction and preprocessing

We conducted comprehensive experiments to evaluate the performance of the FusionLSTM-CNF framework across multiple financial datasets. To address the concern about data specificity and sample size, we carefully constructed our datasets as follows:

For the S&P 500 dataset, we selected the top 30 stocks by market capitalization to ensure data quality and trading volume consistency, including companies such as Apple (AAPL), Microsoft (MSFT), Amazon (AMZN), and Google (GOOGL). To ensure the integrity of our experimental evaluation and eliminate any potential look-ahead bias or temporal data leakage, we implemented a strict chronological data splitting protocol. Specifically, we employed a walk-forward validation strategy where the training, validation, and test sets are partitioned based on temporal order rather than random sampling. For each dataset, the first 60% of the time series (January 2020 to April 2023) was used exclusively for training, the subsequent 20% (May 2023 to February 2024) for validation and hyperparameter tuning, and the final 20% (March 2024 to December 2024) for out-of-sample testing. This chronological partitioning ensures that the model never observes future information during training or validation phases.

Furthermore, we implemented several safeguards against data leakage during feature engineering and preprocessing. First, all technical indicators (moving averages, RSI, Bollinger Bands) were computed using only historical data available up to time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t-1$$\end{document} when predicting the movement at time t. Second, sentiment scores were derived exclusively from news articles published before market close on day \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t-1$$\end{document} to predict day t movements, with a strict timestamp verification protocol. Third, cross-asset correlation features were calculated using rolling windows that exclude any future observations. Fourth, all normalization parameters (mean, standard deviation) for feature scaling were computed solely on the training set and subsequently applied to validation and test sets to prevent information leakage through preprocessing statistics. The sliding window approach with 60-day lookback periods was implemented such that each prediction window \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$[t-59, t]$$\end{document} only contains information strictly preceding the prediction target at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t+1$$\end{document}.

Sentiment score extraction from FinBERT

To clarify the FinBERT sentiment score extraction process, we employed a sophisticated multi-step approach. FinBERT outputs probability distributions over three sentiment classes: positive, negative, and neutral. Additionally, we incorporate sentiment intensity by calculating a confidence-weighted score:21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \text {Final Score} = \text {Sentiment Score} \times \max (P_{\text {positive}}, P_{\text {neutral}}, P_{\text {negative}}) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}This approach ensures that high-confidence predictions have stronger influence while maintaining the continuous nature required for LSTM processing. To provide a comprehensive comparison against state-of-the-art hybrid architectures, we implemented and evaluated three additional advanced baselines. Overfitting assessment

To rigorously assess overfitting risk, we conducted multiple diagnostic analyses:

Generalization gap: the difference between training accuracy (65.4%) and test accuracy (61.2%) yields a generalization gap of 4.2%, comparable to simpler baselines (LSTM: 3.8%, CNN-LSTM: 4.5%) and lower than more complex models (BiMT-TCN: 5.1%, Transformer: 6.3%). This indicates our regularization strategy (dropout, weight decay, early stopping) effectively controls overfitting despite the model’s complexity.

Temporal cross-validation: we performed 5-fold temporal cross-validation with expanding windows, obtaining consistent results: accuracy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$61.2\% \pm 1.8\%$$\end{document}, F1-score \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$61.4\% \pm 2.1\%$$\end{document}, AUC \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.671 \pm 0.023$$\end{document}. The low standard deviations indicate stable performance across different temporal splits.

Extended out-of-sample evaluation: we evaluated on a held-out period (January–March 2025) not used during any training or hyperparameter tuning, achieving 59.8% accuracy and 0.658 AUC. The modest degradation (1.4% accuracy drop) confirms that performance generalizes to truly unseen future data.

Learning curve analysis: training with 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of data yielded test accuracies of 55.2%, 58.1%, 60.3%, and 61.2% respectively. The continued improvement with more data suggests the model has not saturated and would benefit from larger datasets, indicating genuine learning rather than memorization.

Deployment considerations

For practical deployment, FusionLSTM-CNF has inference latency of 6.7ms per prediction (single forward pass) or 335ms with MC dropout (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K=50$$\end{document}). Both are suitable for daily trading applications. Memory footprint is 2.8GB, manageable on standard hardware. For latency-sensitive applications, we provide an ONNX-exported model with 4.2ms inference time. The model can be deployed as a REST API; we include Docker containers and deployment scripts in the code repository.

A key deployment advantage is graceful degradation under missing data. When sentiment data is unavailable (e.g., market holidays, data provider outages), the CNF layer automatically increases weights on technical and correlation modalities. We simulated 10% random sentiment dropout during testing, observing only 1.3% accuracy degradation (61.2% \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rightarrow$$\end{document} 60.4%), demonstrating robustness to partial data availability.

Discussion

The experimental results demonstrate that FusionLSTM-CNF achieves consistent improvements across multiple metrics and market conditions, though the magnitude of gains varies by baseline complexity. We discuss the key findings and their implications.

Late fusion effectiveness: the late fusion strategy outperforms early fusion approaches18,33 by preserving modality-specific temporal patterns. The ablation study confirms that technical indicators, sentiment, and correlations exhibit distinct temporal characteristics that early fusion conflates. This is particularly evident in the 2.5 percentage point accuracy gain from adding the CNF layer to multi-modal fusion without confidence weighting (58.7% \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\rightarrow$$\end{document} 61.2%).

Uncertainty calibration value: the CNF mechanism’s primary contribution is enabling uncertainty-aware decision-making rather than maximizing raw accuracy. The ECE of 0.031 indicates well-calibrated confidence estimates, allowing practitioners to selectively act on high-confidence predictions. The confidence-filtered strategy (Table 9) demonstrates substantial practical value: trading on top-40% confidence predictions improves Sharpe ratio from 0.131 to 0.267 while reducing maximum drawdown from 12.4% to 6.9%.

Performance in context: against sophisticated hybrid baselines (GRU-CNN-GA, BiMT-TCN), improvements are modest (1.1–1.8% accuracy) but statistically significant. This suggests that architectural innovations alone face diminishing returns in financial prediction, and the marginal value increasingly lies in complementary capabilities such as uncertainty quantification and adaptive fusion.

Market regime robustness: performance varies across market conditions (Table 7), with strongest results during stable growth (64.1% accuracy) and weakest during bear markets (58.9%). The CNF layer partially compensates by increasing technical indicator weights during high-volatility periods when sentiment becomes less reliable. However, the 4.5% accuracy gap between best and worst regimes indicates room for improvement in regime-adaptive modeling.

Limitations: our framework has several constraints. The computational cost of MC dropout inference limits high-frequency applications. Performance on illiquid or emerging market securities remains untested. The English-language FinBERT dependency restricts direct application to non-English markets. Finally, the 5-year evaluation period, while covering diverse market conditions, may not capture longer-term regime shifts.

Conclusion

This paper introduces FusionLSTM-CNF, a confidence-calibrated multi-modal late fusion framework for stock movement prediction under uncertainty. The proposed approach integrates heterogeneous data modalities through parallel LSTM networks while providing calibrated uncertainty estimates through a confidence-aware neural fusion mechanism. Our experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements over baseline methods across multiple financial datasets. Compared to single-modal LSTM baselines, FusionLSTM-CNF achieves 12.3% relative improvement in prediction accuracy and 23.7% reduction in prediction variance. Against recent state-of-the-art hybrid architectures including GRU-CNN-GA and BiMT-TCN, the improvements are more modest (1.1–1.8% absolute accuracy gain) but remain statistically significant as confirmed by Diebold-Mariano tests (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p < 0.05$$\end{document}). The framework’s primary practical contribution lies in its uncertainty calibration: the ECE of 0.031 enables confidence-filtered trading strategies that achieve substantially higher risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe ratio 0.267 for top-40% confidence trades) compared to unfiltered approaches (Sharpe ratio 0.131), even under realistic 20 basis point transaction costs. We acknowledge several limitations. First, while statistically significant, performance improvements over sophisticated hybrid baselines are incremental rather than transformative suggesting that the primary value lies in uncertainty quantification rather than raw predictive accuracy. Second, our evaluation focuses on major liquid indices; performance on smaller-cap, emerging market, or illiquid securities may differ substantially. Third, the reliance on FinBERT assumes English-language financial news availability, limiting direct applicability to non-English markets without model adaptation. Fourth, the computational overhead of MC dropout inference (335ms for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K=50$$\end{document} samples) may constrain deployment in latency-critical high-frequency trading contexts, though it remains suitable for daily rebalancing strategies. Despite these limitations, we believe the principled integration of multi-modal fusion with calibrated uncertainty quantification addresses a genuine practical need. The ability to distinguish high-confidence from low-confidence predictions and to trade selectively based on this distinction represents meaningful value for risk-aware financial applications. Future research directions include extending the framework to incorporate additional data modalities (e.g., options flow, satellite imagery, alternative data), developing online learning capabilities for non-stationary market dynamics, investigating transfer learning for data-scarce emerging markets, and exploring applications to portfolio optimization and systematic risk management.

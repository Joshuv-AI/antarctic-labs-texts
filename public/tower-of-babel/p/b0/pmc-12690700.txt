PDNO-14 SURVIVAL OUTCOMES OF PEDIATRIC LOW-GRADE GLIOMAS IN A MIDDLE-INCOME COUNTRY: EXPERIENCE FROM COTE D’IVOIRE
Authors: Landry Drogba, Grâce Djonde, Marc Gbazi, Raissa Diaby, Joel Brou, Landry Konan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPediatric low-grade gliomas, including glial and glioneuronal tumors, are the most common pediatric brain tumors. While outcomes are favorable in high-income countries, data from middle-income settings remain limited.ObjectiveTo evaluate survival outcomes and identify prognostic factors associated with tumor progression and mortality in children treated for Low-grade gliomas in Cote d’Ivoire.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study including 15 pediatric patients treated for histologically confirmed low-grade gliomas, by the neuro-oncology multidisciplinary team of Côte d’Ivoire. Data on clinical presentation, tumor location, histology, diagnostic delays, treatment modalities, and outcomes were analyzed. Kaplan-Meier curves were used to assess survival. Prognostic factors were tested using Fisher’s exact test.ResultsThe median age at diagnosis was 7 years (range :3-15), with 9 males and 6 females. Sixty percent of tumors were supratentorial and 73% were astrocytic. Ten patients had total resection; five received adjuvant radiotherapy. Median follow-up was 48 months for progression -free survival and 36 months for overall survival. Overall survival remained at 93.3% up to 72 months of follow-up, with one death (oligodendroglioma and oligoastrocytoma). Progression-free survival remained stable at 86.7% through 48 months of follow-up. Histological subtype showed a trend toward association with progression (p = 0.15) and extent of resection (p = 0.067). No other clinical factors were significantly associated with outcomes.ConclusionPediatric low-grade gliomas are associated with encouraging survival outcomes, despite resource-limited settings. These results highlight the potential for delivering quality neuro-oncology care in middle-income countries through multidisciplinary collaboration and growing neurosurgical expertise.

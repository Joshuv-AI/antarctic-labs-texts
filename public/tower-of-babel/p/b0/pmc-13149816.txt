Diagnosis and management of a severe ALK-positive pneumonia-type lung adenocarcinoma: a case report
Authors: Ting Su, Qiaoli Zhang, Mengyun Wang, Qifan Jing, Jiani C Yin, Kun Wang, Jian He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPneumonia-type lung adenocarcinoma (PLADC) exhibits clinical symptoms and radiographic features that mimic those of pneumonia. This often leads to misdiagnosis, which can delay appropriate treatment and result in poor outcomes, particularly in severe cases. Effective management of PLADC requires a timely and accurate diagnosis through tissue biopsy, allowing for detailed pathological examination and genetic profiling, followed by targeted therapy tailored to the patient’s condition. While mutations in EGFR are commonly associated with PLADC, ALK fusions are rare and typically detected at advanced stages, with limited reports on treatment effectiveness.Case presentationIn this report, we present a case involving a young female patient with severe stage IV ALK-positive PLADC. Prior to receiving the accurate diagnosis, the patient suffered from severe complications, including respiratory failure, cardiac tamponade, invasive mechanical ventilation, and disseminated intravascular coagulation, all posing a high risk of mortality. Remarkably, within five days of initiating alectinib therapy, the patient’s condition significantly recovered. She was successfully weaned from mechanical ventilation, and her Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status score improved from 4 to 2 within eight days, leading to her discharge in much stabilized state.ConclusionThis case highlights the importance of genetic profiling and rapid and substantial efficacy of alectinib in treating ALK-positive PLADC, reinforcing the role of ALK inhibitors in managing severe cases and offering valuable insights for clinical strategies.Keywords: Pneumonia-type lung adenocarcinoma (PLADC), Rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE), ALK fusion, Alectinib, Case report

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer incidence and mortality worldwide [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of lung cancers, with adenocarcinoma comprising about 55% of these cases [2]. In contrast to NSCLC, pneumonia-type lung adenocarcinoma (PLADC) poses unique diagnostic challenges due to its atypical clinical manifestations. PLADC often presents symptoms such as cough, sputum production, fever, and difficulty breathing. Radiological imaging typically features infiltrative consolidation or patchy ground-glass opacities that mimic pneumonia [3]. Despite receiving antimicrobial therapy, patients typically experience no symptom relief, and their lung abnormalities either remain unchanged or progress, ultimately leading to a pathological diagnosis of PLADC. This subtype is more prevalent among women, non-smokers, and Asian populations.

The atypical clinical features of PLADC often result in misdiagnosis and inappropriate treatment, largely due to a lack of awareness among healthcare providers. Consequently, many patients are diagnosed at advanced stages (III or IV) with a high-performance status (PS) score. This limits their ability to undergo conventional radiotherapy and chemotherapy, ultimately increasing their risk of mortality [4]. For many PLADC patients, targeted therapy has become the primary treatment option, highlighting the importance of genetic testing. While extensive efforts have been made to uncover driver genes, such as EGFR and ALK, in NSCLC, our understanding of the mutational landscape in PLADC is rather limited. A study of 85 PLADC cases identified EGFR mutations as the primary driver gene, present in 71.2% of the tested cases [5]. However, little is known about the prevalence of other driver gene alterations like ALK, and the efficacy of associated targeted therapies in PLADC remains less well characterized.

According to the international consensus, severe lung cancer is defined as a distinct entity of critically ill lung cancer patients with treatable traits and salvage value, differentiating them from terminal-stage cases. These patients typically present with a PS score of 2–4 due to rapid tumor progression, comorbidities, or treatment-related adverse events, yet maintain a high probability of survival benefit and PS improvement following precise etiology-based supportive and anti-tumor therapy [6].

In this study, we report a severe case of PLADC harboring ALK fusion. The patient was treated with alectinib, which has been approved by the FDA as a first-line treatment for ALK-positive NSCLC patients [7]. Alectinib is known for its efficacy against brain metastasis and its low incidence of adverse events, highlighting its potential as a viable treatment option for PLADC cases with ALK fusion. This case underscores the importance of comprehensive genetic testing to identify actionable mutations and optimize treatment strategies for PLADC patients.

Case presentation

A 37-year-old non-smoking female patient was admitted to the central ICU of our hospital (Day 1) with severe pneumonia and respiratory failure. She had experienced a recurrent cough for over two months, dyspnea for one week, and worsening symptoms accompanied by fever over the last two days. Physical examination indicated that the patient was in acute distress, with a body temperature of 38.1 °C, blood pressure of 75/45 mmHg, severe dyspnea with a seated posture, coarse breath sounds in both lungs, wheezing, moist rales, and a heart rate of 130 beats per minute. Computed tomography (CT) scans showed multiple patchy infiltrates and diffuse interlobular septal thickening in both lungs, consolidation with atelectasis in the left upper lung, right pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, and enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes (Fig. 1A-D). An arterial blood gas test indicated type I respiratory failure with a PaO2/FiO2 ratio of 98 mmHg. The complete blood count showed a white blood cell count (WBC) of 18.84 × 10⁹/L, and a granulocyte ratio of 85.4%. Hemoglobin was at 125 g/L, while platelets were 333 × 10⁹/L. In addition, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) level was measured at 48.7 mg/L, and procalcitonin (PCT) was at 0.14 ng/L. The patient was initially treated with broad-spectrum antibiotics and antivirals. Despite these efforts, the patient’s respiratory distress progressively worsened, and her PaO2/FiO2 ratio dropped to 60 mmHg. Consequently, endotracheal intubation and invasive mechanical ventilation were required. Subsequent adjustments to the anti-infective regimen and supportive nutritional therapy. However, the patient’s condition continued to deteriorate.

Following a multidisciplinary consultation, the patient’s condition was re-evaluated. Given the poor response to anti-infective therapy and the review of CT findings, which revealed specific signs of malignancy including stenosis of the left main bronchus, diffuse interlobular septal thickening, coalesced mediastinal lymph nodes, and massive pericardial effusion, the multidisciplinary team raised a high suspicion of severe PLADC with extensive metastasis. Consequently, the patient was transferred to the Department of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine on Day 7. Upon transfer, an immediate bedside electronic bronchoscopy was performed under intravenous anesthesia (Fig. 1E-H). The bronchoscopy revealed a widened carina, stenosis of the left main bronchus, diffusely thickened and edematous bronchial walls that bled easily on contact, and severe extrinsic compression-type stenosis of the left upper lobe bronchus, with an increased interbronchial ridge. A lung biopsy was conducted in the left upper lobe, and endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration was performed on the 4R lymph nodes. Rapid on-site evaluation (ROSE) cytology indicated adenocarcinoma (Fig. 2A). Based on these findings from the bronchoscopy and pathological biopsy, including the ROSE examination, the diagnosis was confirmed as PLADC with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) score of 4. Tissue biopsy was sent for pathological examination and tumor genetic testing, and the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid was sent for targeted next-generation sequencing (tNGS) to detect potential infectious agents.

Pathological examinations confirming the diagnosis of pneumonia-type lung adenocarcinoma. (A) Rapid on-site evaluation using Diff-Quik stain at 400×magnification shows clusters of atypical cells, suggesting adenocarcinoma. (B) Hematoxylin and eosin stain of the biopsy specimen from the left upper lobe of the lung at 200×magnification confirms the presence of adenocarcinoma. (C) Immunohistochemistry at 200×magnification demonstrates positive staining for TTF-1, supporting a pulmonary origin of the adenocarcinoma. (D) Next-generation sequencing of the tumor biopsy reveals the presence of EML4-ALK fusion (V3) and TP53 alterations.

On Day 8, the patient began oral administration of gefitinib 0.25 g QD and discontinued levofloxacin. Gefitinib was chosen because EGFR exon 19 deletion and L858R point mutations are commonly observed in Asian non-smoking female patients with lung adenocarcinoma. The patient was in an urgent condition, initiating gefitinib therapy was appropriate to provide prompt treatment targeting these likely mutations. A Doppler ultrasound of the lower extremities indicated deep vein thrombosis in both legs, with progressively rising D-dimer levels (Fig. 3D). Anticoagulation therapy with enoxaparin was subsequently initiated. On Day 9, the patient experienced a drop in blood pressure. Although septic shock was initially considered due to the infection-like presentation, the lack of response to antibiotics and the presence of pericardial effusion pointed towards an obstructive cause. An ultrasound-guided emergency pericardiocentesis was performed, extracting 300 ml of hemorrhagic pericardial effusion, which resulted in the normalization of the patient’s blood pressure, confirming that the hemodynamic instability was primarily caused by cardiac tamponade rather than septic shock. Analysis of the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid using tNGS revealed the presence of Pneumocystis jiroveci, which was considered contamination based on the patient’s clinical symptoms and auxiliary examinations, including blood tests. On Day 10, the patient had a WBC of 20.05 × 10⁹/L, with an absolute neutrophil count of 17.87 × 10⁹/L, and a granulocyte ratio of 89.3%. The hs-CRP level was measured at 40.8 mg/L, and PCT was at 0.10 ng/ml. As a result, cefoperazone/sulbactam was discontinued, and the treatment was adjusted to meropenem at 1 g every 8 h for infection management.

On Day 12, pathological examination (Fig. 2B and C) and pericardial effusion exfoliative cytology both confirmed the presence of adenocarcinoma. On Day 14, the patient exhibited soy sauce-colored urine and coagulation dysfunction, evidenced by a prothrombin time of 62.8 s, an activated partial thromboplastin time of 53.9 s, and a D-dimer level of 63.71 mg/L. Enoxaparin treatment was discontinued and plasma transfusion was initiated. The patient then exhibited a WBC of 24.03 × 10⁹/L, with an absolute neutrophil count of 21.41 × 10⁹/L, a granulocyte ratio of 89.1%. The level of hs-CRP was 88.4 mg/L, and PCT was 0.16 ng/ml. Elevated levels of infection markers suggested that the anti-infection treatment was ineffective (Fig. 3A-D). On Day 16, genetic testing of the tumor samples revealed the presence of EML4-ALK fusion variant V3 and TP53 mutations (Fig. 2D). Immunohistochemistry showed positive PD-L1 expression with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 20%. Consequently, diagnosis was revised to: (1) lung adenocarcinoma, cT4N3M1c (according to the 8th edition IASLC TNM classification [8]; with extensive metastasis to the lungs, pericardium, pleura, and mediastinal lymph nodes), stage IV, ECOG PS score of 4, harboring EML4-ALK V3; (2) lymphangitic carcinoma; (3) acute respiratory distress syndrome; (4) cardiac tamponade. Gefitinib was immediately discontinued and replaced with an ALK inhibitor, alectinib, administered at 600 mg twice daily. In addition, local injections of a vascular endothelial inhibitor were given at a dose of 30 mg every other day into the pericardial cavity, along with a daily dosage of methylprednisolone of 40 mg.

After two days of alectinib treatment, the patient’s breathing problems began to improve, and the PaO2/FiO2 ratio increased. Five days into treatment, the PaO2/FiO2 ratio further increased to 193 mmHg. The patient’s coagulation function normalized, with the D-dimer level reduced to 7.57 mg/L (Fig. 3D). The WBC was decreased to 11 × 10⁹/L (Fig. 3A) and the granulocyte ratio was 88.1%. In addition, the hs-CRP level was reduced to 5.5 mg/L and PCT was 0.03 ng/L (Fig. 3B-C). On the same day, the tracheal tube was removed, and mechanical ventilation was discontinued. Eight days later, CT scans indicated substantial absorption and amelioration of the lung lesions (Fig. 4A-D), with ECOG PS score improving from 4 to 2, demonstrating significant recovery. The patient was discharged in a stable condition. A follow-up CT scan was performed one month later revealed a marked reduction in the shadows, consolidation areas, and diffuse interlobular septal thickening in the lungs, along with resolution of the right pleural effusion and a reduction of the pericardial effusion (Fig. 4E-H). The patient continued alectinib therapy and maintained clinical stability. At the three-month follow-up, her ECOG PS score further improved to 1, and chest CT confirmed sustained disease control. Regrettably, she was subsequently lost to follow-up due to relocation to another province.

Discussion

PLADC represents a significant clinical challenge due to its overlapping presentation with pneumonia. Despite its low incidence rate, the aggressive nature of PLADC and the high potential for misdiagnosis underscore the importance of awareness and timely intervention. In this case, we described a young non-smoking female with severe stage IV ALK-positive PLADC. The severity of her condition was driven by the rapid progression and dissemination of the malignancy itself, which manifested as severe pneumonia-like consolidation, bronchial stenosis, extensive lymphangitic carcinomatosis, and cardiac tamponade. Her condition dramatically improved following the administration of alectinib, highlighting the transformative potential of targeted therapies.

As summarized in Table 1, a comparison with typical PLADC characteristics reveals that while our patient fits the demographic profile of female non-smokers [9], she presents a distinct clinical picture with a notably younger age of onset and ALK-positive status. The delay in diagnosis and treatment associated with PLADC often contributes to high mortality, as noted in the literature comparisons (Table 1). However, in contrast to this generally poor prognosis, our case demonstrates that accurate diagnosis and targeted therapy can yield dramatic recovery. This emphasizes the urgent need to speed up the diagnostic process to extend survival time and improve outcomes. Our multidisciplinary approach illustrates the effectiveness of a rapid, coordinated response involving immediate critical care support and expedited genetic testing. Under active and effective critical respiratory support treatment, complications were managed proactively to gain the maximum survival time for the patient. Employing ROSE significantly reduced the diagnostic timeline, allowing for the swift initiation of targeted therapy.

Upon reviewing the literature, there are few reports of successful treatment with ALK-TKIs in patients who have ALK fusion, a PS score of 4, and accompanying severe organ dysfunction [10, 11]. In selecting the appropriate ALK inhibitor for our patient, we considered several factors. NGS results revealed that the patient had an EML4-ALK V3 fusion and concurrent TP53 mutations, which are associated with a poorer prognosis and more aggressive disease behavior [12]. Indeed, recent research has further characterized ALK-rearranged lung adenocarcinoma as an aggressive disease entity with a specific tropism for lymph nodes and a high risk of recurrence, underscoring the need for comprehensive management strategies [13]. Given these challenges, alectinib, a second-generation ALK inhibitor, was chosen for its proven efficacy and favorable safety profile [12, 14, 15]. The BFAST study has demonstrated the therapeutic advantage of alectinib in patients with ALK fusions, particularly the V3 variant, confirming its effectiveness in this high-risk subgroup [16]. In addition to its efficacy, alectinib has an outstanding safety profile with fewer adverse reactions compared to first-generation ALK inhibitors such as crizotinib [14, 15]. Its high selectivity and ability to penetrate the blood-brain barrier make it a superior option [17, 18].

Indeed, we observed a rapid and marked improvement of the patient’s tumor burden and overall conditions, from a compromised ECOG PS score of 4 to 2, within a short timeframe after initiating alectinib. Notably, the medication was administered through the nasogastric tube initially as the patient was intubated. However, this approach did not compromise the overall efficacy of alectinib. The success of using alectinib in our case reinforces the necessity of personalized treatment strategies based on molecular profiling. Moreover, this case highlights that even patients at the brink of organ failure can recover substantially with the right targeted approach.

It is crucial for clinicians to maintain a high index of suspicion for PLADC in patients presenting with pneumonia-like symptoms, especially when there is no response to conventional antimicrobial therapies. Early tissue biopsy and genetic profiling are imperative to differentiate PLADC from infectious conditions and to guide subsequent treatment strategies effectively.

In conclusion, the successful management of this severe lung cancer patient not only emphasizes the importance of genetic testing and targeted therapy in PLADC but also exemplifies the potential for recovery even in dire clinical circumstances. This case serves as a valuable reference point for clinicians managing similar complex cases, underscoring the critical role of personalized medicine in lung cancer treatment.

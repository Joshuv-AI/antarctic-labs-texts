A heterobifunctional IRAK4-targeting degrader impairs myddosome signaling in acute kidney injury and ameliorates kidney fibrosis
Authors: Marta Lopez-Marfil, Xichi Wang, Diana Ofelia Lopez-Cantu, Leonardo Gael Pelayo-Minero, Andres Ebergenyi-Camarena, Jocelynn Colunga-Minutti, Antonio Esquivel-Herrera, Xiaozhang Zheng, Veronica Campbell, Matthew Weiss, Nello Mainolfi, Anthony Slavin, Dario Ruben Lemos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Heterobifunctional degraders emerge as a novel pharmacologic strategy to treat inflammatory conditions. Previous data have indicated that inflammatory signaling via the myddosome complex is critical for kidney injury and fibrosis development. Within the myddosome, modulation of interleukin-1 receptor associated kinase 4 (IRAK4) by small molecule kinase inhibitors has been shown to reduce fibrosis, but has limited anti-inflammatory effect. Here we used a novel strategy to abrogate IRAK4 by means of targeted ubiquitination triggering proteasomal degradation. We tested the antifibrotic and anti-inflammatory activity of the small molecule IRAK4 degrader KTX-545 in vitro and in vivo in a model of acute kidney injury. Our results indicated that incubation with degrader resulted in abrogation of IRAK4 protein levels, and reduced formation of myddosome complex in primary kidney fibrogenic cells. In human kidney organoids, IRAK4 degradation reduced both extracellular matrix deposition, as well as fibrogenic gene expression caused by tubular damage. Further, the treatment resulted in inhibition of NF-kB activation and downstream inflammatory cytokine expression. In vivo, KTX-545 showed increased efficacy compared to the small molecule IRAK4 kinase inhibitor CA-4948 in ameliorating fibrosis following ischemia/reperfusion injury. Collectively, our results indicate that targeted degradation of IRAK4 is a new promising therapeutic approach for the treatment of renal fibrosing disorders.

Introduction

Targeted protein degradation by means of target-specific degrader molecules has recently emerged as an effective therapeutic strategy for abrogation of protein activity1–3. Such degrader molecules are heterobifunctional compounds linked to a ubiquitin E3 ligase that bind a therapeutic target, resulting in ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of the target protein4,5. Various degraders targeting IRAK4 have been independently developed and are undergoing clinical trial for the treatment of inflammatory diseases6. Based on the efficacy that targeted IRAK4 degradation shows in different contexts, we hypothesized that this therapeutic approach might be effective for preventing inflammation and fibrosis in the context of tissue injury.

Tubulointerstitial fibrosis is a hallmark of kidney malrepair and it is considered to be a major factor contributing to end-stage renal disease7. Currently there is no effective treatment for kidney fibrosis. The fibrosing process is driven by stromal cells, including pericytes and fibroblasts residing in the interstitial space of the kidney8,9. In recent years, work by us and by others have shown a critical role for Toll-like receptor (TLR)/interleukin-1 receptor (IL-1R) signaling via the stromal cell myddosome in driving the activation and differentiation of kidney stromal fibrogenic cells into extracellular-matrix-secreting myofibroblasts10–12. Fibrosis triggered by acute kidney injury (AKI) is significantly impaired in mice lacking myddosome activity in kidney fibrogenic stromal cells (kFSCs) as a result of genetic ablation of MyD8810. Those results highlighted the interleukin-1 receptor associated kinase 4 (IRAK4) as a potential therapeutic target using pharmacologic approaches. A selective small molecule inhibitor of IRAK4 kinase activity showed significant anti-fibrotic activity in a model of ischemia/reperfusion injury (IRI)10. Kinase inhibition, however did not significantly reduce local inflammatory cytokine production, suggesting that this pharmacologic strategy did not alter the pervasive effect of inflammatory cytokines on the nephron epithelium10. Further, this result was in concurrence with previous studies reporting that IRAK4 plays two independent roles, one as a kinase and another one scaffolding protein supporting myddosome-mediated activation of the master pro-inflammatory transcription factor NF-kB13,14. Therefore, inhibition of the kinase function does not significantly affect IRAK4 pro-inflammatory activity13–15.

Here we tested KTX-545, a small molecule degrader with a structure comprising an IRAK4 binder molecule linked to an E3 ligase. KTX-545 has a DC50 of 0.9 nM determined in in vitro assays with human monocytes16–18. In selectivity assays, IRAK4 was the only protein degraded of over 10,000 different proteins in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) incubated with a concentration of KTX-545 ten-fold over its DC9016–18. A related analog, KLT-474, recently completed a Phase I clinical trial in which similar safety, PK and PD in patients with hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) and atopic dermatitis (AD), and in healthy volunteers was observed. The treatment resulted in robust IRAK4 degradation in blood and skin, consistent with the observed systemic anti-inflammatory effect (clinical trial number NCT04772885).

Our results indicate that KTX-545 significantly impairs myddosome assembly in kFSCs, reducing renal fibrogenesis and inflammatory signaling both in vitro and in vivo. Our data therefore support the notion that the myddosome scaffold function is critical for acute innate inflammation and tubular damage in the context of AKI.

Concise methods

IRAK4 degrader

The IRAK4 degrader molecule KTX-545 was supplied by Kymera Therapeutics. For its in vitro usage, a stock of drug was prepared in dimethyl-sulfoxide (DMSO), and the final dilution used in the experiments was prepared in the indicated cell culture medium. For in vivo usage, a modified bioavailable form of KTX-545 was dissolved in 20% 2-hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HPBCD).

Mouse kidney stromal fibrogenic cell purification and culture

Mouse kFSCs were purified from adult C57BL/6 mouse kidneys obtained by means of magnetic immunopurification as previously described19,20. Briefly a single cell preparation, excluding capsule and glomeruli was generated by digestion with a collagenase cocktail (Liberase, Roche). Epithelial cells were depleted using anti-EpCAM and Lotus-lectin conjugated beads in magnetic columns (Dynabeads). The remaining of the sample was used to prospectively purify Pdgfrβ+ FSCs using anti-Pdgfrβ antibody-conjugated (ThermoFisher Scientific, clone APB5) beads in magnetic columns (Dynabeads). Mouse kFSCs (P3–P5) were cultured in 6-well gelatin-coated plates at a density of 4 ×105 cells/ml for 24 hours in pericyte medium (Lonza). For cytokine stimulation experiments, cells were washed 5 times with PBS and cultured overnight in serum-free medium. IL-1β was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. The Irak4 degrader KTX-545 was prepared following manufacturer recommendation, namely diluted in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at a stock concentration of 1 mM. The stock solution was diluted in culture medium to the concentration indicated in each in vitro experiment.

Generation of human kidney organoids and IL-1β stimulation

Kidney organoids were generated from H9 human embryonic stem cells21,22. H9 was maintained in mTESR1 medium (Stem cell technologies). When cells reached 40-50% confluency, differentiation was initiated CHIR (TOCRIS) as previously reported21. After 4 days of CHIR treatment, cells were differentiated with Activin for 3 days. Then, the medium was changed to one supplemented with FGF9, and cells were cultured for 1 ~ 2 days. After that, cells were dissociated with Accutase (Stem cell technologies) and plated onto 96-well plates with U-shaped bottom (Corning) at 100,000 cells/well in a differentiation medium supplemented with CHIR and FGF9. After 2 days, the medium was changed to one supplemented with only FGF9, and cells were cultured for 3 ~ 4 days. FGF9 was removed after that, and kidney organoids were formed without any additional growth factors in Advanced RPMI + 1X L-GlutaMAX. Organoids were allowed to mature until day 21, when 50 ng/ml human IL-1β (Sigma-Aldrich # H6291) was added for 96 h11. The IL-1β-containing medium was replaced every 48 h. Additionally, KTX-545 was added to the media at the final concentrations indicated throughout the text. Organoids were collected and fixed in 4% PFA for 30 minutes, followed by cryopreservation in 20% sucrose overnight. Ten μm organoid sections were prepared by cryosectioning and incubated with antibodies for immunofluorescence, including α-SMA (Sigma Aldrich) and Kim-1 (1:300)23. Images were captured by confocal microscopy, using a Nikon C1 microscope running EZ-C1 software.

Animal experiments

WT mice used in acute kidney injury experiments were 8- to 10-week-old C57BL/6 males purchased from Charles River Laboratories. Mice were housed in the animal unit of the Harvard Institutes of Medicine. Kidney IRI surgical procedures were performed as previously described on 8- to 10-week-old mice. Briefly, For the unilateral ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI), the left kidney was exposed through a flank incision and subjected to ischemia by clamping the renal pedicle with non-traumatic microaneurysm

clamps (Roboz, Rockville, MD) for 30 min. Reperfusion was confirmed by visual color change. Body temperatures were controlled at 36.5–37.5°C throughout the procedure. Daily doses of 25 mg/kg or 50 mg/kg of the IRAK4 degrader molecule KTX-545, or 75 mg/Kg of CA-4948 bis in die (BID) (Selleckchem) were delivered intraperitoneally. Mice were euthanized using CO2. We have complied with all relevant ethical regulations for animal use. Animals are observed constantly during the procedure and for 3-4 hours post surgery by the surgeon. They were then observed twice a day for 4 days by the investigator, and then observed once every 48 h until euthanasia at the end point. The care was documented post-operative including administration of analgesics and overall animal wellbeing. All experiments were performed under protocols approved by the IACUC at Brigham and Women’s Hospital.

Mouse tissue preparation and immunofluorescence

Mouse tissues were collected and stained as previously described8. Briefly, cryosectioned samples (12μm) were fixed in paraformaldehyde-lysine-periodate (PLP) solution for 2 h and then were washed in 20% sucrose solution overnight to ensure cryopreservation. Primary antibodies used in this study were α-SMA Sigma-Aldrich (#F3777, clone 1A4, 1:2000), Cd11b (BioRad M1/70.15) and Kim-1 (in-house raised,1:300)23. Tissue sections were mounted in ProLong Gold Antifade Reagent with DAPI (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Images were captured by confocal (Intelligent Imaging Innovation or Zeiss) or standard fluorescent microscope (Nikon) as previously described8

Q-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells or whole tissue using RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (Qiagen). Purity was determined by A260 to A280. cDNA was synthesized using oligo(dT) and random primers (iScript Reverse Transcription Supermix, Biorad). Quantitative PCR was performed using QuantStudio™ 7 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Life Technologies) using the /react-text TaqMan® react-text: 3672 Gene Expression Assays (Life Technologies). The specific primer pairs used in qPCR are listed in Supplementary Table 1 (Supplementary Information).

Western blot and co-immunoprecipitation

For kFSC protein analysis, 100,000 cells were seeded on 0.2% gelatin-coated 24-well plates and cultured overnight. Cells were serum starved for 4 h and stimulated with IL1β for the duration of the experiment. Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer containing Halt Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (Thermo Fisher Scientific) on ice. Following protein quantification, SDS PAGE and Western blotting was performed as described11. The antibodies used for western blotting were against IRAK4 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA5-15883) and MyD88 (Thermo Fisher Scientific PA5-19919). Immunoprecipitation experiments were performed using an amine-reactive resin-based method (Pierce, #26149), using an anti-MyD88 (R&D Systems; AF3109). Anti-mouse Irak4 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA5-1588) and Irak2 (Abcam, ab62419) antibodies were used to detect Irak4 and Irak2 in the immunoprecipitates. Primary antibodies were detected with peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse or anti-rabbit IgG (1:3000) and visualized with SuperSignal West Femto Substrate (ThermoFisher Scientific #34094). Blots were quantified using ImageJ.

Statistics and reproducibility

Statistical evaluation was carried out using the One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey post-test using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software). A P value < 0.05 was considered to be significant. Error bars indicate standard error of mean. Samples sizes and number of replicates are indicated in the respective figure legend. Sample randomization was performed using randomizer software available at https://www.randomizer.org.

Results

IRAK4 degradation blocks IL1R-driven inflammatory and fibrogenic activity in kFSCs

While the causal role of kFSCs, including pericytes and fibroblasts, in renal tubulointerstitial fibrosis has been demonstrated8,9, the contribution of those cells to the process of local inflammation is less well-understood. Previous work has shown that in vitro stimulation of human kSFCs with histones or with interleukin -1β (IL-1β) results in significant upregulation of NF-kB-target genes via a stromal cell myddosome signaling mechanism involving IRAK4 kinase activity10. To investigate the contribution of IRAK4 scaffold activity, here we tested the inhibitory activity of a preclinical IRAK4 heterobifunctional degrader molecule termed KTX-545. KTX-545 specifically links the Cereblon (CRBN) domain binding the E3 ligase to IRAK4, triggering its ubiquitination and degradation by the 26S proteasome with half maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) of 60 nM (CRBN) and 11 nM (IRAK4) (Fig. 1A, B)16–18.Fig. 1Targeted degradation of IRAK4 abolishes IL-1R signaling in kidney fibrogenic stromal cells.A Mechanism of targeted IRAK4 degradation by the 28S proteasome upon ubiquitination driven by the bifunctional degrader molecule linking an E3 ligase to IRAK4. Stimulation of isolated primary mouse kSFCs with interleukin-1β (IL-1β) increased myddosome assembly, indicated by formation of Myd88/Irak4/Irak2 complexes (Fig. 1C) determined by immunoprecipitation of Myd88 and co-immunoprecipitation of Irak4 and Irak2, using a previously reported strategy13,24. IL-1R signaling also resulted in significant upregulation of Nf-κB-target genes, including Il1b, Il6, Ccl2, p65, p50, Ikba and Ikka (Fig. 1D). Co-incubation with KTX-545 resulted in impairment of myddosome formation and a significant reduction of Nf-kB-driven gene expression (Fig. 1C, D). In these experiments, no bands for MyD88, Irak4 or Irak2 were observed when c-Myc was immunoprecipitated with a specific antibody. Additionally, degradation of IRAK4 significantly reduced the expression of the myofibroblast marker gene Acta2 encoding myofibroblast smooth muscle actin (Sma), the extracellular matrix protein encoding genes Col1a1, Fn1 and Fbn1 and the cytokines Tgfb and Ctgf (Fig. 1E), consistent with the effect observed in previous work, when IRAK4 kinase activity is inhibited10.

We next assessed the effect of targeted IRAK4 degradation on human pluripotent stem cell-derived kidney organoids. To that end, we exploited our model of acute tubulointerstitial organoid injury induced by IL-1β (Fig. 2A, Supplementary Fig. 1)11. This experimental strategy allows us to study stromal cell-intrinsic mechanisms of inflammation and fibrosis in the absence of immune cells11. We found that degradation of IRAK4 following 48 hours of incubation with KTX-545 (Fig. 2B) prevented loss of proximal tubule brush borders that is observed upon incubation with IL-1β for 96 h (Fig. 2C). The treatment effectively blocked phosphorylation of P65, necessary for NF-kB activation, and which was prominently observed in organoid interstitial PDGFRβ+ stromal cells, and in a small number of nephron tubule cells in which phospho-P65 was detected (Fig. 2D, E). Gene-expression analysis indicated that, similar to the effect observed in isolated kFSCs, the compound significantly reduced the expression of NF-κB target genes in whole organoids (Fig. 2F). In addition to its anti-inflammatory activity, KTX-545 significantly reduced fibrogenic gene expression, accumulation of differentiated kFSC-derived alpha smooth muscle actin (αSMA)-expressing myofibroblasts, and interstitial deposition of Collagen 1A1 (COL1A1) that is synthesized by kFSCs7,25,26. Taken together, our in vitro results with isolated kFSCs and human kidney organoids confirmed mechanistically that KTX-545 effectively drives IRAK4 degradation, and impairs NF-κB activity, providing evidence for an anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effect of KTX-545 in the renal tubulointerstitial compartment.

Targeted degradation of mouse Irak4 in vivo reduces inflammation triggered by AKI

Our in vitro results prompted us to test the efficacy of KTX-545 in vivo, in a model of AKI. To this end, we performed unilateral ischemia reperfusion injury (UIRI) in kidneys of C57/BL6 mice received KTX-545 intraperitoneally. We initially tested two doses, namely 25 mg/Kg, 50 mg/Kg that were delivered daily, over the course of fourteen days (Fig. 3A). Our results indicated that, both 25 mg/Kg, 50 mg/Kg, KTX-545 were well-tolerated and did not affect survival compared to the groups of control non-damaged mice and mice that were subjected to AKI and received vehicle (Supplementary Fig. 2A). The treatment did not affect kidney weight significantly post-injury, albeit a trend was observed toward larger organ size (Supplementary Fig. 2B). Mechanistically, KTX-545 induced Irak4 degradation in a dose-dependent manner, three days after treatment initiation (Fig. 3B). Downstream the myddosome, KTX-545 treatment impaired Nf-κB activation in a dose-dependent manner in isolated Pdgfrβ+ kFSCs as well as in infiltrating Cd11b+ monocytes/macrophages from injured treated animals, after three days of treatment (Fig. 3C). Those results were consistent with the inflammatory cytokine-encoding gene expression profile in whole kidney extracts, showing a dose-dependent decrease in the mRNA levels of Il-1β, Il-6, Tnfa, and Il-18 (Fig. 3D). Collectively, our results show good tolerability to the doses of KTX-545 tested, and a strong anti-inflammatory effect for the drug in the context of AKI, validating the previously-established efficacy of this molecule to inhibit Nf-kB signaling in vivo.

Irak4 degradation reduces nephron injury and interstitial fibrosis in AKI

To determine the joint contribution of Irak4 kinase and myddosome scaffold activities to kidney fibrosis, we next assessed the effect of targeted Irak4 degradation on tubulointerstitial injury and fibrogenesis in AKI. Given its increased efficacy to block injury-induced inflammation, the 50 mg/Kg KTX-545 dose was selected. At that dose, KTX-545 significantly reduced injury-induced cell proliferation (Fig. 4A). In addition, the treatment reduced tubule injury, as indicated by the expression of proximal and distal tubule injury markers Kim1 and Ngal, respectively, fourteen days after injury was induced (Fig. 4B, C). These results were coherent with our histologic results showing reduced Sirius red staining indicative of ECM collagen in the interstitium of mice treated with 50 mg/Kg KTX-545, compared to CA-4948 treatment (Fig. 5B). Similarly, myofibroblast content, determined by SMA signal in the interstitium, was significantly lower in the group treated with KTX-545 (Fig. 5C).

Collectively, our results indicate that targeted degradation of IRAK4 effectively suppresses fibrosis driven by AKI. The data further support a model in which abrogation of IRAK 4 scaffold and kinase activities is a more effective antifibrotic therapeutic strategy than kinase inhibition alone (Fig. 5D).

Discussion

Here we tested targeted IRAK4 degradation as a novel prospective therapy for the dual treatment of acute inflammation and renal fibrosis in AKI. Previous work by us and others had shown the relevance of TLR/IL-1R/myddosome signaling in kidney fibrosis in the context of acute injury. One of the strategies previously employed to inhibit the myddosome was the use of high-affinity small molecule inhibitors to block the kinase activity of IRAK410. However, the kinase activity and the scaffold role of IRAK4 independently contribute different signaling pathways13,14. This was confirmed by previous work showing that while the IRAK4 kinase function was critical for TLR/IL-1R fibrogenic activity, its role in inflammatory signaling appears to be context-dependent and in certain cases dispensable for downstream NF-kB activation13,28,29, as indicated by data showing its partial contribution to IL-1-induced NF-κB signaling and cytokine production15. Loss of IRAK4 scaffold function, on the other hand, has been shown to be essential for IL-1–induced signaling and cytokine production15. Based on those results, and given that inflammation elicited by AKI has a strong deleterious effect on the nephron epithelium and a pro-fibrogenic effect on stromal cells30,31, targeting both kinase and scaffold roles of IRAK4 emerges as potentially more effective therapeutic strategy.

Our results with the degrader molecule KTX-545 confirmed that degradation of IRAK4 was effective at reducing fibrogenesis in kidney stromal cells in isolation as well as in the context of renal tissue microenvironment that is present in kidney organoids. From a mechanistic standpoint, loss of IRAK4 prevented myddosome formation, preventing NF-kB-driven expression of inflammatory genes in response to IL1R signaling. In kidney organoids, abrogation of IL1R signaling resulted in improved proximal tubule integrity and prevented myofibroblast accumulation. Both results are in keep with our previous observation that IL1R signaling simultaneously induces proximal tubule cell stress and activation and differentiation of fibrogenic stromal cells11.

Currently, a number of target-specific degraders are undergoing clinical testing to explore their pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD), associated clinical biomarkers and safety (NCT03888612, NCT04072952, NCT05080842 among others). Further, KTX-545 (KT-474) has recently completed testing in a dose escalation phase 1 clinical trial (NCT04772885). The results from single ascending dose and multiple ascending dose portions of the trial demonstrated near complete IRAK4 degradation in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) and skin, and a favorable safety profile (NCT04772885)6,32.

Our data supports the idea that targeted IRAK4 degradation is a prospective therapy to be tested for the amelioration of AKI-driven nephron damage and interstitial fibrosis. Our in vivo data confirm the safety and tolerability to 25 mg/Kg and 50 mg/Kg KTX-545. Additionally, in our model of AKI the drug showed good anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic efficacy, the latter being superior to treatment with the selective IRAK4 kinase inhibitor CA-4948 which has received orphan drug designation for the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML, NCT04278768 and EU CT Number 2023-505828-58-00)27. These results therefore outline the rationale for testing IRAK4-targeting degraders in conditions of kidney damage associated with increased TLR/IL-1R signaling activation, including end-stage renal failure, acute tubulointerstitial nephritis, acute renal transplant rejection and delayed allograft function33.

In summary, our experimental studies support targeted IRAK4 degradation as a prospective therapeutic approach to ameliorate acute kidney injury. It is important to consider, however, that our efforts have been focused on inhibiting fibrosis driven by the acute inflammatory response that occurs at early stages of AKI11. Consequently, further efforts are still needed to assess the renoprotective and antifibrotic effect of inducing IRAK4 degradation at later stages of AKI, as well as in experimental models of chronic kidney disease (CKD).

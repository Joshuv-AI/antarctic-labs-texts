Discussion

Cutaneous manifestations show multiple brownish discrete papules distributed symmetrically over the face, chest, trunk, and extremities (Figure 1). The patient also complained of occasional itching during increased sweating. The differential diagnosis considered was eruptive lichen planus, eruptive syringomas, and xanthogranuloma. Hematoxylin and eosin stained sections show a fibrocollagenous dermis with a focus of proliferation of epithelial cells forming ductule, nest, and cord in a background of fibrotic stroma (Figure 2). Cells are cuboidal and double layered, showing eosinophilic secretion within the lumen (Figure 3). Thus, the diagnosis is generalized eruptive syringomas. The patient was advised for laser ablation.

Syringomas are benign adnexal tumors of eccrine origin, which show differentiation of the intra‐epidermal portion of the eccrine duct [1]. Syringomas can be classified into four types: local, eruptive type, Down syndrome associated, and familial. The local type is the most common variant of syringoma, characterized by symmetrical papules around the eyes [2]. Generalized eruptive syringoma is a rare variant of syringoma, which is more frequently observed in patients with Down's syndrome, Ehlers‐Danlos syndrome, Nicolau Balus syndrome, and Brooke Spiegler syndrome [3]. Eruptive syringoma is common in pubertal or postpubertal females. Although the exact etiology is not known, hormonal factors, autoimmune disorders, genetics, immunosuppression, and inflammation around sweat ducts can play a role in the etiopathogenesis of eruptive syringoma [2]. Patients usually present with generalized symmetrical small, flesh‐colored papules appearing in successive crops associated with pruritus and spare palms, soles, and mucosa [1]. Histopathological examination reveals sweat gland proliferation lined by two layers of cuboidal cells with eosinophilic cuticle lining the sweat duct and amorphous sweat within the ductal lumen. Eruptive syringomas lack a universally agreed first line treatment; however, laser ablation is often considered a preferable choice in patients with cosmetic‐related concerns. Over time, eruptive syringoma may regress without intervention in contrast with the more typical presentation [3].

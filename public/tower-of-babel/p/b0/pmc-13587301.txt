Trem1 exacerbates myocardial infarction-induced heart failure by inactivating the Rap1 pathway
Authors: Jing Liu, Zhen Kou, Peng Cao, Tingting Chen, Wanlin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Heart failure (HF) after myocardial infarction (MI) involves adverse cardiac remodeling. Trem1 may be a potential therapeutic target for MI, but its role in the HF process remains unclear. Our work aimed to investigate the effect of Trem1 on myocardial remodeling in MI-induced HF and the downstream molecular mechanism. The HF mouse model was established by left anterior descending (LAD) ligation, and cardiac function, hypertrophy markers, and fibrosis were measured. Additionally, Mouse cardiac fibroblasts (MCFs) were stimulated with angiotensin II (Ang II), and cell phenotypes, including proliferation and fibrosis, were detected. The regulation of Trem1 on the Rap1 pathway was evaluated using bioinformatics and western blot. We found that Trem1 was upregulated in HF mice (mean fold-change at RNA level was 4.68 and at protein level was 4.43) and AngII-treated MCFs (mean fold-change at RNA level was 6.28 and at protein level was 4.01). Trem1 knockdown improved cardiac function, as indicated by an increase in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) from 36.45% to 58.62% and left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS) from 18.78% to 30.19%, reduced hypertrophy (heart weight/body weight fold reduced from 6.40 to 4.85 and ANP and BNP mRNA expression was downregulated), and reduced collagen volume fraction from 30.47% to 14.33% in vivo. In AngII-stimulated MCFs, Trem1 silencing decreased cell viability by 48.76%, reduced EdU-positive cells by 46.92%, and downregulated fibrosis-related marker expression. Mechanistically, silencing of Trem1 promoted Rap1 pathway activation, manifested as an increase of 3.79 folds in Epac1, 1.90 folds in active Pap1, and 5.56 folds in Rac1 protein levels. Inhibition of Rap1 caused by GGTI298 partly reversed the effects of Trem1 knockdown both in vivo and in vitro. In conclusion, Trem1 promotes myocardial remodeling in post-MI HF by suppressing the Rap1 pathway, suggesting Trem1 is a potential therapeutic target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04470-6.

Introduction

Myocardial infarction (MI) is a globally prevalent coronary event with high morbidity and mortality [1]. Despite advances in reperfusion therapies, many patients still progress to chronic HF due to maladaptive repair processes. Thus, MI is a leading cause of heart failure (HF), which is considered to be the decisive factor for poor prognosis after MI [2]. The incidence of HF after MI remains high worldwide. Epidemiological research suggests that about 2.5 million patients experience an MI each year, with 25–40% subsequently developing HF [3]. Evidence has shown that adverse cardiac remodeling occurs after MI, which is caused by changes in the shape and function of the left ventricle, subsequently leading to pathological changes of myocardial fibrosis, finally resulting in HF [4]. Therefore, focusing on the regulatory mechanisms of cardiac remodeling may provide strategies for the prevention and treatment of HF after MI.

Triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 1 (Trem1) is a pro-inflammatory receptor initially identified in innate immune cells such as neutrophils, monocytes, and macrophages, where it amplifies inflammatory responses by initiating downstream pathways [5, 6]. Consequently, Trem1 plays a critical role in infection and inflammatory diseases. Recent studies have revealed that Trem1 is expressed in non-immune cells such as cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts [7, 8]. Recent evidence shows that Trem1 plays a critical role in cardiovascular diseases, such as MI, ischemic stroke, atherosclerosis, and sepsis-induced cardiomyopathy [9]. It has been reported that deficiency of Trem1 inhibits myocardial inflammation and improves cardiac remodeling after MI, suggesting it is a promising target for the treatment of MI [10, 11]. Moreover, knockdown of Trem1 enhances cardiac function after MI and reduced fibrotic remodeling [12]. However, the role of Trem1 in HF post-MI remains unclear.

The Ras-associated protein 1 (Rap1) is a 21 kDa monomeric G-protein that regulates cellular functions such as neuronal survival, platelet activation, and cardiac excitation-contraction [13]. Particularly, the deficiency or overactivation of Rap1 contributes to the progression of cardiovascular diseases [14]. Moreover, Rap1-related pathways involved in MI have been revealed, which regulate cardiomyocyte death and cardiac function [15, 16]. However, whether Rap1 interacts with Trem1 in the context of post-MI remodeling has not been investigated.

Here, we identified Trem1 as an upregulated gene in HF using bioinformatic analysis. Thus, we evaluated the role of Trem1 in MI-induced HF using in vitro and in vivo models. Moreover, we demonstrated the regulation of Trem1 on the Rap1 pathway. Our findings reveal a previously unrecognized Trem1/Rap1 axis in post-MI cardiac remodeling, providing a potential therapeutic target to mitigate HF progression.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatic analysis

The RNA expression profile of GSE180065 was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. This dataset included the change of gene expression in a mouse model of HF using RNA sequencing. Differentially expressed genes were analyzed using the R package. The criteria were |log2(fold change)| > 1 and adjusted P value less than 0.05. Genes correlated with Trem1 were analyzed using the Pearson correlation coefficient. The negatively correlated genes of Trem1 were subjected to Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis to assess pathways.

In vivo study

C57BL/6J mice (male, 8-10-week-old, 20–22 g) were purchased from Beijing Vital River (Beijing, China) and housed in a specific pathogen free environment with free access to water and food. MI was induced by left anterior descending (LAD) ligation. Briefly, mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane inhalation. After thoracotomy, the LAD coronary artery was ligated using a 7-0 nylon suture. Ligation was maintained for 14 days to induce HF. The control group mice underwent anesthesia and thoracotomy without LAD ligation.

To investigate the role of Trem1, short hairpin RNA targeting Trem1 (shTrem1) and the negative control (shNC) were packaged into lentiviruses, termed Lv-shTrem1 and Lv-shNC. Lv-shTrem1 and Lv-shNC were injected into mice via the tail vein 48 h before LAD ligation. The lentiviruses were injected every 7 days.

To investigate the role of Rap1 signaling, the Rap1 inhibitor GGTI298 (MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) at a concentration of 25 µM/kg was intraperitoneally injected into mice 30 min before LAD ligation. GGTI298 was injected once every day.

Cell culture

Mouse cardiac fibroblasts (MCFs) were purchased from Procell (Wuhan, China) and grown in the specific complete medium (Procell) at 37℃ with 5% CO2.

To induce a cell injury model in vitro, MCFs were treated with 1 µM angiotensin II (Ang II; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 48 h.

To explore the effect of the Rap1 pathway on cellular processes, MCFs were treated with 10 µM GGTI298 for 24 h before AngII treatment.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was isolated from heart tissues and MCFs using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). After evaluating RNA concentration, cDNA was synthesized using the Transcriptor first strand cDNA synthesis kit (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Subsequently, quantitative PCR was conducted to measure mRNA expression using the SYBR Green qPCR master mix (MedChemExpress). β-actin served as the internal control. Results were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blot

MCFs and heart tissues were lysed using RIPA lysis buffer, and the lysate was collected after centrifuging at 12,000 g for 15 min. Protein concentration of the lysate was measured using a bicinchoninic acid kit (Sigma-Aldrich). The lysate containing equal amounts of protein was subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis for separation, followed by transfer to PVDF membranes. Following blocking with 5% skim milk, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against Trem1, β-actin, α-SMA, FN, Col1a1, Col3a1, Epac1, Rap1, and Rac1 overnight at 4℃, and subsequently incubated with a secondary antibody labeled by horseradish peroxidase. Finally, the bands were visualized using the Immobilon ECL Ultra Western HRP Substrate (Sigma-Aldrich) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Rap1 activation assay

Activated Rap1 (Rap1-GTP) levels were measured using the Active Rap1 pull-down and detection kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Briefly, MCFs were lysed and the supernatant was collected after centrifuging. After determining protein concentration, cell lysate containing 500 µg of protein was incubated with 20 µg of GST-RalGDS-RBD in a spin cup containing the glutathione resin at 4℃ for 1 h. Following centrifuging at 6000 ×g for 30 s, the resin was collected and washed with lysis buffer. After eluting, western blot was performed to measure Rap1-GTP levels.

Echocardiography

Cardiac function was analyzed using echocardiography before euthanasia. Mice were anesthetized with isoflurane and placed on a heating panel at 37 °C. Mice were shaved, and echocardiography was conducted using the Vevo2100 high-resolution imaging system (VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada) with a 10-MHz high-frequency ultrasound probe. Two-dimensional M-mode images of the heart were obtained. Left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV), left ventricular internal diameter at diastole (LVIDd), and systole (LVIDs) were measured. Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS) were calculated. Heart weight (HW), measured as left ventricular weight (LVW), was also detected using echocardiography. The HW/body weight (BW) ratio was calculated.

Masson’s trichrome staining assay

After euthanasia by inhalation of 5% isoflurane, the hearts were collected and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. Following dehydration and transparency, the hearts were embedded with paraffin. Paraffin sections were cut to a 5 μm thickness. The sections were dewaxed and rehydrated, and then stained using the Masson’s trichrome stain kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China) in line with the manufacturer’s protocols. The stained sections were observed under a light microscope.

Cell transfection

MCFs were seeded into 6-well plates 24 h before transfection. Transient transfection of MCFs with shTrem1 and shNC was performed using the Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, transfected cells were harvested.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8)

MCFs were suspended using the complete medium, and 100 µL cell suspension was added to each well of the 96-well plates and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. CCK-8 solution from the CCK-8 cell proliferation and cytotoxicity assay kit (Solarbio) in a volume of 10 µL was added to each well to incubate with MCFs for 4 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate.

EdU (5-Ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine) assay

MCFs were seeded into 96-well plates at a concentration of 5 × 104 cells/well and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. The cells were incubated in the complete medium with 100 µL 50 µM EdU for 2 h. After washing with PBS, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, and the fixation was stopped with glycine. MCFs were subsequently incubated with 0.5%TritonX-100 for 10 min, followed by incubation with Apollo stain solution for 30 min in the dark. DNA was stained with DAPI for 15 min in the dark. Following washing, the images were captured using a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

Results were shown as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The difference between the two groups was evaluated by an unpaired two-tailed t-test, and the difference among multiple groups was analyzed using one-way ANOVA. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using the GraphPad Prism 8 software.

Results

Trem1 is highly expressed in in vivo and in vitro models of HF

Microarray analysis was performed to explore which gene was involved in HF. There were 238 upregulated genes and 269 downregulated genes in HF, compared with the control (Fig. 1A). An upregulated gene, Trem1, was chosen. We then measured Trem1 expression in mice and cells. Mice were subjected to LAD ligation to induce HF. qRT-PCR and western blot were conducted to measure Trem1 expression in the hearts of mice. The results showed that Trem1 expression was elevated in the hearts of HF mice, compared with the control group (Fig. 1B-D). Additionally, cardiac fibroblasts were exposed to AngII to generate an in vitro model. The results of qRT-PCR and western blot indicated that compared with the control group, AngII treatment increased Trem1 expression in fibroblasts (Fig. 1E-G). High expression of Trem1 in LAD ligation-induced HF mice and AngII-induced cardiac fibroblasts suggests that Trem1 may be involved in HF by affecting fibroblast processes.

Knockdown of Trem1 attenuates HF in vivo

To explore the role of Trem1 in HF progression, Lv-shTrem1 and Lv-shNC were injected, followed by establishing an HF mouse model. Cardiac function was evaluated. The left ventricular regional wall motion was weakened in HF mice, accompanied by a decrease in LVEF and LVFS, compared with the control group, while Trem1 knockdown enhanced LVEF and LVFS in the mouse model (Fig. 2A-C). The HW/BW ratio was increased in HF mice, compared with the control group, which was reduced by Trem1 silencing (Fig. 2D). Next, ANP and BNP levels were both elevated in HF mice, and their levels were reduced by Trem1 knockdown (Fig. 2E and F). Moreover, the mice after LAD ligation showed more severe fibrosis, whereas knockdown of Trem1 alleviates fibrosis in HF mice (Fig. 2G and H). The findings suggest that knockdown of Trem1 decelerates cardiac remodeling in HF mice.

Silencing of Trem1 inhibits proliferation and fibrosis of AngII-induced MCFs

Subsequently, we performed in vitro experiments to evaluate the impact of Trem1 on the biological behaviors of MCFs. The cells were transfected with shNC and shTrem1, and the expression of Trem1 was found to be downregulated in shTrem1-transfected MCFs (Fig. 3A). MCFs were stimulated by AngII to establish a damaged cell model. The results of CCK-8 showed that AngII promoted cell viability, which was abrogated by Trem1 knockdown (Fig. 3B). In addition, AngII induced an increase in EdU-positive cells, while Trem1 knockdown reduced these cells (Fig. 3C and D). Protein levels of α-SMA, FN, Col1a1, and Col3a1 were elevated in AngII-induced MCFs, which was counteracted by Trem1 silencing (Fig. 3E-I). These results demonstrate that AngII promotes cell proliferation and fibrosis, whereas knockdown of Trem1 suppresses proliferation and fibrosis of the AngII-induced cell model.

Trem1 inhibits the activation of the Rap1 signaling pathway

To evaluate the underlying molecular mechanism, Pearson correlation coefficient was performed to evaluate genes correlated with Trem1. A total of 688 positively correlated genes and 548 negatively correlated genes were found (Fig. 4A). These negatively correlated genes were subjected to KEGG enrichment analysis. Several pathways were predicted, including the Rap1 signaling pathway (Fig. 4B). Then, we conducted western blot to verify whether this pathway was mediated by Trem1. As shown in Fig. 4C-F, AngII treatment induced the downregulation of Epac1, Rap1, and Rac1 expression, as well as suppressed the activation of Rap1, whereas knockdown of Trem1 elevated their levels and activated Rap1 in AngII-treated MCFs. The results indicate that the Rap1 signaling pathway was suppressed by Trem1.

Knockdown of Trem1 attenuates HF via targeting the Rap1 signaling pathway

To evaluate the role of the Rap1 signaling pathway in vivo, GGTI298 was injected to inhibit Rap1 before HF mouse model establishment. We found that the improvement of left ventricular regional wall motion and the increase of LVEF and LVFS caused by Trem1 knockdown were reduced by GGTI298 in HF mice (Fig. 5A-C). The decrease of HW/BW induced by Trem1 silencing was reversed by GGTI298 in the mouse model (Fig. 5D). Next, knockdown of Trem1 decreased the expression of ANP and BNP, while GGTI298 reversed this decrease (Fig. 5E and F). Moreover, the fibrosis in the heart inhibited by Trem1 knockdown in HF mice was counteracted by GGTI298 (Fig. 5G and H). We also knocked down Rap1 expression in mice using Lv-shRap1 injection. The results revealed that silencing of Rap1 counteracted the impact of Trem1 knockdown on the improvement of ventricular regional wall motion (Fig. S1A), the increase of LVEF and LVFS (Fig. S1B and C), the reduction of HW/BW (Fig. S1D), the downregulation of ANP and BNP (Fig. S1E and F), and the inhibition of fibrosis (Fig. S1G and H). The results demonstrate that knockdown of Trem1 attenuates cardiac remodeling by activating the Rap1 signaling pathway.

Silencing of Trem1 inhibits proliferation and fibrosis of AngII-induced MCFs by targeting the Rap1 signaling pathway

To evaluate the role of the Rap1 signaling pathway in vitro, Rap1 was inhibited in MCFs using GGTI298 stimulation or shRap1 transfection. Rap1 expression was decreased after shRap1 transfection (Fig. S2A). Cellular processes were analyzed. We found that cell viability suppressed by Trem1 knockdown was reversed by GGTI298 treatment or silencing of Rap1 (Fig. 6A; Fig. S2B). Additionally, GGTI298 or Rap1 knockdown counteracted the reduction of EdU-positive cells caused by Trem1 silencing (Fig. 6B and C; Fig. S2C and D). Furthermore, Trem1 knockdown decreased α-SMA, FN, Col1a1, and Col3a1 levels, which could be abrogated by GGTI298 or Rap1 knockdown (Fig. 6D-H; Fig. S2E-I). The results demonstrate that silencing of Trem1 inhibits proliferation and fibrosis by activating the Rap1 signaling pathway.

Discussion

Our study demonstrates that Trem1 drives myocardial remodeling in MI-induced HF mice, and we found the involvement of the Rap1 pathway in this process. The results offer new insights into the pathogenesis of MI-induced HF.

Trem1 regulates pathophysiological processes in multiple diseases, including MI [17]. It acts as a pro-inflammatory receptor and contributes to myocardial inflammation after MI [11, 18]. Besides, it is involved in MI progression. For example, Trem1, targeted by miR-206, regulates oxidative stress and endothelial cell senescence to participate in MI [19]. Additionally, inhibition of Trem1 suppresses cardiac dysfunction, reduces infarct size, and alleviates organ damage such as the lung, kidney, and liver [20]. Importantly, Trem1 is associated with cardiac remodeling post-MI. The expression of Trem1 is increased in ischemic myocardium. Blockade of Trem1 improves cardiac function and limits ventricular remodeling [11]. MI usually leads to HF, but whether Trem1 regulates cardiac remodeling in MI-induced HF has not been clarified. In the present study, we used LAD ligation to establish the HF model. We found that Trem1 expression was increased in HF mice. Moreover, knockdown of Trem1 increased LVEF, LVFS, as well as reduced HW/BW, ANP, and BNP expression, and attenuated fibrosis, suggesting it ameliorates cardiac function, cardiac hypertrophy, and fibrosis. The findings indicate that Trem1 promotes adverse myocardial remodeling in HF mice after MI.

Fibroblasts are considered essential targets for the prevention of myocardial fibrosis and remodeling [21]. Fibroblast proliferation, in particular, is key to promoting fibrosis and damage in the heart with HF [22]. Thus, in our work, we studied the effect of Trem1 on fibroblast phenotypes. The results demonstrated that Trem1 expression was increased in AngII-induced MCFs, and silencing of Trem1 inhibited the proliferation and fibrosis of these cells. The findings suggest that Trem1 facilitates cardiac remodeling by promoting fibroblast proliferation and fibrosis.

Rap1 is associated with MI progression. For example, gCTRP9 protects against MI via the inhibition of apoptosis and fibrosis by activating the Rap1/MEK/ERK pathway [23]. In addition, activating Epac-Rap1 pharmacologically enhances heart function by improving the survival, adhesion, and differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells, which helps restore functional myocardium [16]. Our bioinformatic analysis results showed that Trem1-negatively correlated genes were enriched in the Rap1 pathway. We further found that knockdown of Trem1 activated the Rap1 pathway, manifested by up-regulation of Epac1, Rap1, and Rac1 expression and enhanced Rap1 activation. Epac1, a protein with several domains, serves as an exchange factor activated by cAMP for the small G-proteins Rap1 and Rap2 [24]. Then, the activation of Rap1 modulates the activity and accumulation of Rac1 [25]. However, whether the Epac1/Rap1/Rac1 pathway is involved in cardiac remodeling remains unknown. We found that inhibition of Rap1 induced by GGTI298 or knockdown of Rap1 reversed the effect of Trem1 knockdown on cardiac remodeling improvement and the inhibition of MCF proliferation, suggesting that the Rap1 pathway protects against cardiac remodeling by inhibiting fibroblast proliferation, similar to previous findings [26].

We are aware of several limitations of this study. Although we established the Trem1-Rap1 axis, whether Trem1 directly interacts with Rap1 or acts via intermediate regulators remains unclear. We speculate that Trem1 can activate Rap1 by regulating Epac1, but it cannot be ruled out that Trem1 can partially bind to Epac1. Future research will further explore how Trem1 regulates the Rap1 signaling pathway. Additionally, we used MCFs for the study in our in vitro experiments. Whether the Trem1-Rap1 axis plays a role in targeting fibroblasts in vivo needs further investigation.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that silencing of Trem1 improves cardiac function, hypertrophy, and fibrosis in MI-induced HF mice by suppressing the activation of MCFs. Mechanistically, Trem1 knockdown activates the Epac1/Rap1/Rac1 pathway. These findings identify Trem1 as a key regulator of post-MI cardiac remodeling and elucidate a novel Trem1-Rap1 axis in HF progression, suggesting that targeting this axis could be a promising strategy to prevent adverse remodeling in MI-induced HF progression.

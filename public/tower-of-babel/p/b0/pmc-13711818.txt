Transcription factor FOXA1 suppresses ferroptosis of cervical cancer cells by upregulating SLC7A11 expression
Authors: Fei Gao, Yan Zeng, Li Jia, Jun He, Huarong He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ferroptosis, a unique modality of cell death, possesses critical activity in tumorigenesis. This study defined the role of transcription factor (TF) forkhead box A1 (FOXA1) in modulating ferroptosis of cervical cancer (CC), a prevalent disease with high mortality rates in females. mRNA analysis was done by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), and protein expression was analyzed by immunoblotting and immunohistochemistry (IHC). Colony formation and EdU proliferation assays were used to evaluate cell growth. Apoptosis was detected by flow cytometry. The contents of SOD, MDA, GSH, ROS and Fe2+ were measured by assay kits. Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR and luciferase assays were applied to analyzed the binding of FOXA1 to the SLC7A11 promoter. FOXA1 was an overexpressed factor in CC, which was related to prognosis of human CC. FOXA1 downregulation impaired CC cell in vitro proliferation, promoted apoptosis, and induced ferroptosis. FOXA1 was validated to bind to the promoter of SLC7A11 and enhance SLC7A11 transcription. Furthermore, the FOXA1/SLC7A11 cascade affected proliferation, apoptosis and ferroptosis of CC cells. In addition, FOXA1 downregulation impeded the in vivo tumorigenesis of C33A CC cells. Our study has identified FOXA1 as a crucial repressor of CC cell ferroptosis depending on its activation of SLC7A11 transcription. Developing inhibitors of FOXA1 is a promising way of harnessing ferroptosis for CC treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13008-026-00182-4.

Introduction

In spite of the reducing effect of HPV vaccine on its incidence, cervical cancer (CC), primarily caused by human papillomavirus (HPV) 16 and 18, is still a prevalent disease resulting in high mortality rates in females all over the world [1, 2]. Ferroptosis, a unique and distinct modality of cell death within a metabolism framework, possesses critical activity in biological contexts by both sustaining normal physiological development and participating in tumorigenesis [3]. Ferroptosis has a potential therapeutic effect on combating various cancers, including CC [4]. The mechanism by which molecular players modulate CC development and ferroptosis is poorly understood. It is believed that unveiling the complex role of these modulators is essential for possible clinical interventions of CC [5].

It has been well demonstrated that forkhead box A1 (FOXA1) is critical to chromatin access, alternative splicing, and transcriptional switch during carcinogenic process [6]. Elevated FOXA1 expression contributes to cancer resistance development of chemotherapy, immunotherapy and endocrine therapy [7]. As an activated oncogenic transcription factor (TF), FOXA1 accelerates human carcinogenesis by controlling a large panel of oncogenes [7–9]. Emerging data support the notion that FOXA1 contributes to tumor development in CC by enhancing proliferation and impeding apoptosis [10]. FOXA1 can activate the transcription of SIX4 to aggravate chemoresistance and progression of CC [10].

Dysregulated FOXA1 contributes to reprogramming of metabolism, and FOXA1 has been established as a lipid metabolic control factor [11–13]. Considering the fundamental role of lipid metabolism in ferroptosis [3], Gan and colleagues proved that exogenous upregulation of FOXA1 alleviates ferroptosis of HK-2 cells exposed to high glucose by enhancing CPT1A expression [11]. Despite intensive research, the role of FOXA1 in modulating cancer cell ferroptosis remains indistinct.

As a cystine transporter, SLC7A11 is upregulated in various types of cancers and is capable of working as a pro-tumorigenic factor partially by driving resistance to ferroptosis [14]. Through the ferroptosis-dependent function, SLC7A11 exacerbates the progression of CC [15, 16]. Numerous TFs, such as ATF4 and SOX2, has been found to activate SLC7A11 transcription and induce its upregulation during tumorigenesis [17, 18]. Nonetheless, whether FOXA1 drives the transcription of SLC7A11 is unknown.

Considering the control of FOXA1 in lipid metabolism [11] and cervical tumorigenesis [10], researchers hypothesized that FOXA1 might function as a regulator of CC cell ferroptosis. Considering the pro-tumorigenic roles of FOXA1 and the ferroptosis repressor SLC7A11 in CC [15, 16], researchers hypothesized that FOXA1 might contribute to CC development by facilitating growth and repressing ferroptosis via SLC7A11 based on their potential relationship predicted by a computerized algorithm.

Results

Identification of FOXA1 as an overexpressed factor related to prognosis of human CC

Based on published findings regarding the oncogenic role of TF FOXA1 in CC [10], this study focused on its exact functions and related mechanisms in the present work. Using GEPIA 2 database for the expression profile of FOXA1 in CC, the significant upregulation of FOXA1 was observed in CC tumors compared with neighboring normal cervical samples (Fig. 1A). This study then analyzed the expression of FOXA1 in clinical cervical tumors (n = 35) from patients with CC to validate its upregulation and observe its association with diagnosis and prognosis of CC. qRT-PCR confirmed the high expression of FOXA1 mRNA in human CC tissues versus neighboring healthy cervical samples (Fig. 1B). Interestingly, FOXA1 displayed a favorable performance in detecting CC because of the high sensitivity and specificity and the AUC of 0.8224 (Fig. 1C). These patients were also divided into high or low FOXA1 expression groups on the basis of median of FOXA1 level. Patients with low FOXA1 expression had a longer survival time than subjects with high FOXA1 levels (Fig. 1D), suggesting the potential prognostic value of FOXA1 in human CC. In addition, all of patients had remarkably low FOXA1 levels after operation in their serum specimens (Fig. 1E). Immunoblotting and IHC analyses demonstrated that FOXA1 protein levels in CC samples were much higher than those of neighboring healthy controls (Fig. 1F and G). Consistent data that both mRNA and protein levels of FOXA1 markedly increased in CC cell lines were obtained by qRT-PCR and immunoblotting (Fig. 1H and I). These data suggest a tight relation of FOXA1 overexpression with CC diagnosis and prognosis.

Inhibition of FOXA1 impairs CC cell in vitro proliferation and promotes apoptosis

Considering the important implication of FOXA1 in CC prognosis, this study then examined its exact functions in C33A and HeLa CC cells. To resolve this, FOXA1 levels in the two CC cell lines were knocked down using lentiviral vector-mediated FOXA1-shRNA (sh-FOXA1). sh-FOXA1, which induced a striking downregulation efficacy, was applied in the study (Fig. 2A). Depletion of FOXA1 by sh-FOXA1 introduction led to a considerable suppressive impact on in vitro cell colony formation and proliferation of C33A and HeLa CC cells (Fig. 2B and C). Downregulation of FOXA1 also induced potent enhancement on apoptosis of the two CC cell lines (Fig. 2D). It is believed that Bax (a strong anti-apoptotic factor) and Bcl-2 (a well-known protein in blocking apoptosis) are two essential players in controlling CC cell apoptosis [19, 20]. The immunoblotting results of sh-FOXA1- or sh-introduced CC cells showed that downregulation of FOXA1 augmented Bax expression but diminished Bcl-2 levels (Fig. 2E and F), supporting the promotion of FOXA1 depletion in CC cell apoptosis. Therefore, in agreement with previous documents [10], inhibition of FOXA1 in CC cells exhibits a strong anti-cancer effect by impairing significant growth and inducing strong apoptosis.

Inhibition of FOXA1 induces in vitro ferroptosis in CC cells

Ferroptosis, a unique and distinct form of cell death within a framework of lipid metabolism, has a potential therapeutic effect in combating CC [4]. FOXA1 has been established as a lipid metabolic control factor [11–13]. Our study thus examined the impacts of FOXA1 on modulating CC cell ferroptosis. Biology of SOD, MDA, GSH, ROS and iron is implicated in ferroptosis process [3]. Indeed, FOXA1 downregulation resulted in a reduced SOD production, an increased MDA expression, and decreased content of GSH in C33A and HeLa CC cells compared to the sh-NC control group (Fig. 3A and C). ROS defenses and iron regulation are believed to together modulate ferroptosis, and ROS accumulation and Fe2+ augmentation contribute to ferroptosis [3]. Moreover, content analyses of ROS and Fe2+ also supported that FOXA1 plays a critical role in controlling CC cell ferroptosis, as evidenced by enhanced levels of ROS and Fe2+ in FOXA1-depleted C33A and HeLa CC cells (Fig. 3D and E). In conclusion, these results suggest the key function of FOXA1 in repressing ferroptosis.

FOXA1 is demonstrated to bind to the promoter of SLC7A11, a potent repressor of ferroptosis, and enhance SLC7A11 expression

FOXA1 is a well-known pivotal TF that has established a key role in cancer biology depending on its control in gene transcription [21]. Therefore, this study identified its targets in order to elucidate the mechanism of how FOXA1 affects cervical tumorigenesis and CC cell ferroptosis. SLC7A11, a well-known repressor of ferroptosis, has been identified as a potent oncogenic promoter in CC [15, 16]. Our qRT-PCR data validated the striking upregulation of SLC77A11 mRNA in clinical cervical tumors (n = 35) relative to neighboring controls (Fig. 4A). More interestingly, in these clinical tumors, a significant and positive expression association of SLC77A11 mRNA and FOXA1 was observed (Fig. 4B). Consistent with FOXA1 expression, both mRNA and protein levels of SLC77A11 strongly elevated in CC tumors and cell lines (Fig. 4C and F). From these, SLC7A11 might be responsible for the modulation of FOXA1 in CC.

Using Jaspar2024 tool, the predominant motif was displayed (Fig. 5A). When using this motif and set the relative profile score threshold of 90%, only two binding sites (S1: -204~-194 and S2: -980~-970) were found within the SLC7A11 promoter (-2000 bp ~ 0 bp) for FOXA1 (Fig. 5B). To demonstrate this, ChIP experiments were performed by incubating DNA fragments of C33A and HeLa CC cells with the antibody to FOXA1 (anti-FOXA1). The fragment of the SLC7A11 promoter harboring the S2 site, but not the S1 site fragment, was distinctly enriched in anti-FOXA1 immunoprecipitates (Fig. 5C and D), demonstrating the direct FOXA1/SLC7A11 binding action via the S2 site (TATTTACTTTG). Our study next tested whether the binding of FOXA1 to the SLC7A11 promoter via the S2 site is required for its influence on SLC7A11 transcription. Wild-type (WT) pGL3-basic SLC7A11 reporter (SLC7A11-WT) carrying the fragment of the SLC7A11 promoter harboring the S2 site or the mutant (TATTTACTTTG mutated to ATAAATGAAAC, Fig. 5B) construct (SLC7A11-MUT) was introduced into C33A and HeLa CC cells. Exogenous upregulation of FOXA1 by pcDNA-FOXA1 transfection significantly enhanced the luciferase activity of SLC7A11-WT, but not SLC7A11-MUT (Fig. 5E and F). Furthermore, FOXA1 inhibition by introduction of sh-FOXA1 clearly diminished SLC7A11 protein expression in both C33A and HeLa cell lines (Fig. 5G). Collectively, TF FOXA1 modulates SLC7A11 expression by pairing to the SLC7A11 promoter via the “TATTTACTTTG” site.

The FOXA1/SLC7A11 cascade is functionally associated with proliferation, apoptosis and ferroptosis of CC cells

As FOXA1 and SLC7A11 are oncogenic factors in CC [15, 16] and FOXA1 is demonstrated to enhance SLC7A11 transcription, this study then explored whether the cascade is functional for CC cell growth, apoptosis and ferroptosis. Rescue experiments were carried out by restoring SLC7A11 expression in FOXA1-donwregulated C33A and HeLa CC cells and subsequent checked cell functional behaviors. The results showed that SLC7A11 protein expression of FOXA1-donwregulated cells was remarkably increased by SLC7A11 plasmid co-introduction (Fig. 6A). Suppression in cell colony formation as well as proliferation and enhancement in apoptosis of FOXA1-donwregulated cells were reversed by co-introducing SLC7A11 plasmid (Fig. 6B and D). In addition, FOXA1 downregulation-triggered Bax increase and Bcl-2 inhibition were abated by restored SLC7A11 levels (Fig. 6E and F). More importantly, restoration of SLC7A11 levels led to a striking repression in CC cell ferroptosis, as indicated by elevated SOD level, reduced MDA production, enhanced GSH expression, as well as downregulated ROS and Fe2+ contents in sh-FOXA1 + SLC7A11 transfected cells compared with sh-FOXA1 + pcDNA controls (Fig. 7A and E). All these findings suggest the key function of FOXA1 in affecting CC cell growth, apoptosis, and ferroptosis through SLC7A11.

Inhibition of FOXA1 impedes the in vivo tumorigenesis of C33A CC cells

To further demonstrate the induction of FOXA1 depletion on an in vitro growth disadvantage, our study also analyzed the impact of decreased FOXA1 expression on in vivo xenograft growth of C33A CC cells. In nude mouse models of subcutaneous implantation, the nude mice injected with sh-FOXA1 lentivirus-transduced C33A CC cells were found to have much smaller tumor volume and weight than those injected with C33A CC cells after infection by sh-NC lentivirus (Fig. 8A and B). Immunoblotting data validated the downregulation of FOXA1 in sh-FOXA1 lentivirus-transduced C33A CC xenografts, and downregulation of FOXA1 resulted in a decreased SLC7A11 protein expression in the subcutaneous xenografts (Fig. 8C), which were also confirmed by IHC assay of the xenografts (Fig. 8D). Consistent with the finding that FOXA1 depletion impairs the proliferation of C33A CC cells by SLC7A11, downregulation of FOXA1 greatly hinders the in vivo tumorigenesis of C33A CC cells partially due to its regulation in SLC7A11.

Discussion

As a distinct modality of cell death, ferroptosis has been proposed to possess a potential therapeutic benefit in combating human cancers [3]. This study shows that downregulation of FOXA1, a lipid metabolic control TF, induces ferroptosis of CC cells. Mechanistically, our findings have demonstrated that FOXA1-induced upregulation of SLC7A11, a well-known pivotal repressor of ferroptosis [14], is functionally associated with proliferation, apoptosis and ferroptosis of CC cells. Our work suggests that inhibiting FOXA1 could be a novel avenue of harnessing ferroptosis for CC treatment.

Dysregulation of the pioneer TF FOXA1 is known to be linked to multiple cancers, including CC, by mediating transcriptional switch by opening chromatin conformation [6, 10]. By activating the transcription of the oncogenic factor SIX4, FOXA1 has also been identified as the pro-tumorigenic player in CC, as evidenced by enhanced invasion, growth and resistance to drug [10]. In the present paper, via gene expression profiling in a cohort of CC cases, FOXA1 is found to be a promising diagnostic marker and prognostic predictor of CC. Through functional confirmation in two CC cell lines, FOXA1 is identified as an essential promoter of CC growth, in line with earlier findings [10]. FOXA1 is capable of controlling metabolism reprogramming and has been established as a lipid metabolic control factor [11–13]. Considering the implication of lipid metabolism in ferroptosis [3], researchers hypothesized that FOXA1 might operate as a repressor of CC cell ferroptosis, which was strongly proved by our detection of contents of ferroptosis-related factors (SOD, MDA, GSH, ROS and Fe2+) [3]. Similarly, recent work demonstrates the ferroptosis-inhibitory activity of FOXA1 in HK-2 cells exposed to high glucose [11].

One intriguing question is how FOXA1 affects cervical tumorigenesis and CC cell ferroptosis. FOXA1, an oncogenic TF, has been revealed to accelerate human carcinogenesis by upregulating a large panel of oncogenes [8, 16]. In addition to regulation in growth and apoptosis, our data have discovered, for the first time, the potent ferroptosis-inhibitory function of FOXA1 in CC cell lines. Therefore, researchers considered the carcinogenic ferroptosis suppressors for elucidation of the mechanism governing FOXA1’s oncogenic role in CC. Among these suppressors, SLC7A11 is very noticeable due to its potent pro-tumorigenic role in CC [15, 16]. Enhanced SLC7A11 expression is found in CC, which is in agreement with the data of TCGA database reported by Ning et al. [22]. Combination of propofol and paclitaxel presents anti-cancer activity in CC by inducing ferroptosis by altering SLC7A11 pathway [16]. Furthermore, the protein CNIH4 can promote SLC7A11-mediated cystine import to lead to suppressed in vitro ferroptosis of CC cells [15]. With these reported findings, SLC7A11-mediated ferroptosis resistance contributes to CC development. Through a computerized algorithm (Jaspar2024) and observations of the FOXA1/SLC7A11 interaction, our data have confirmed that FOXA1 binds to the SLC7A11 promoter and enhances its expression in CC cells. Our work is the first report of the FOXA1-mediated SLC7A11 upregulation by activating SLC7A11 transcription. Furthermore, our study has also demonstrated the functional association of the FOXA1/SLC7A11 cascade with CC cell growth and ferroptosis. Similarly, SLC7A11 activation mediated by some TFs, such as SOX2 and NF-κB p65, confers ferroptosis resistance to contribute to human tumorigenesis [18, 23]. Hence, inhibition of FOXA1 could influence CC cell ferroptosis via SLC7A11, which is a possible opportunity for combating CC. The role study of FOXA1 in two CC cell lines is limited, and FOXA1 downregulation-mediated CC suppression will be further defined in different CC cellular and animal models.

Collectively, this study has identified FOXA1, an oncogenic TF, as a crucial repressor of CC cell ferroptosis depending on its activation of SLC7A11 transcription. Designing inhibitors of FOXA1 is a promising avenue of harnessing ferroptosis for CC treatment.

Materials and methods

Specimens of CC patients

With adequate informed consent of the subjects, peripheral blood specimens from clinical patients with CC (n = 35) before operation (pre-operation) and after operation (post-operation), as well as clinical cervical tumors (n = 35) and paired neighboring healthy cervical samples (n = 35) were collected from these patients. These tissue specimens and serum samples isolated from peripheral blood were used for expression analyses of FOXA1 and SLC7A11. These subjects were monitored for survival analysis through 5-year follow-up by outpatient and telephone interviews. Human research in this work was approved by the Third Hospital of Mianyang (Sichuan Mental Health Center) Research ethics committee.

Cell culture

The current study used C33A (Cat#CL-0045, Procell, Wuhan, China) and HeLa (Cat#CL-0101, Procell) CC cell lines and the HPV-immortalized H8 cervical cell line (HonSun Biological Technology, Shanghai, China) in the present research. As control, the H8 cell line was reproduced using 10% (v/v) FBS DMEM (Procell).

C33A and HeLa CC cells propagated in 10% (v/v) FBS MEM containing 1% (v/v) Streptomycin/Penicillin (all from Procell) were introduced with FOXA1-shRNA (sh-FOXA1, for FOXA1 depletion), a nontarget shRNA vector (sh-NC), sh-FOXA1 along with pcDNA-SLC7A11 plasmid (for SLC7A11 upregulation), or sh-FOXA1 and control plasmid pcDNA, all of which were procured from Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China), using RFect plasmid transfection reagent as suggested by the producer (Biogenerating Biotechnology, Changzhou, China).

Xenograft generation and handling of mice

For mouse xenograft experiments, the lentivirus particles coding sh-FOXA1 or sh-NC from Genostarbio (Suzhou, China) were procured and used in female athymic BALB/c mice at 7 weeks of age (Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology, Beijing, China) following the approved protocols by the Third Hospital of Mianyang (Sichuan Mental Health Center) Animal Research Committee and National Guidelines. C33A CC cells infected with sh-FOXA1 or sh-NC lentivirus (8.0 × 106 cells per mouse) were re-suspended in 200 µL non-serum MEM, followed by subcutaneous implantation into the flanks of mice to generate C33A CC xenografts. For evaluation of xenograft growth, tumor volume was monitored with the method: volume = (width2 × length)/2. After 28 days, C33A CC xenografts were harvested from the sacrificial mice.

mRNA analysis of FOXA1 and SLC7A11

For RNA preparation, the Qiagen RNeasy Mini Kit was utilized to isolate total RNA from human specimens and transfected C33A and HeLa CC cells, in accordance to the producer’s protocols (Qiagen, Courtaboeuf, France). After determination of concentration by a spectrophotometer, 1 µg RNA was randomly primed to generate cDNA using QuantiTect RT Kit and protocols (Qiagen). For mRNA analysis of FOXA1 and SLC7A11, quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR, a 25 µL reaction volume) was conducted on the Rotor Q (Qiagen) using synthesized cDNA at a 20-fold dilution as the template, primer sets (Supplementary Table 1), and SYBR Green (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Relative FOXA1 and SLC7A11 mRNA levels were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method after normalization with the amount of β-actin.

Immunoblotting

For protein analysis as described [15], protein extracts were first prepared from human specimens, cultured CC cells, and mouse xenografts under the application of Protein Isolation Kit and concomitant guidelines (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Protein (15 µg) was loaded per lane of a 10% SDS gel, electrophoresed, and subjected to transfer membrane to nitrocellulose (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Under the blocking buffer, the following antibodies were applied: anti-FOXA1 rabbit pAb (Cat#PA5-27157, 1:2000, Invitrogen, Hemel Hempstead, UK), anti-SLC7A11 rabbit pAb (Cat#26864-1-AP, 1:1500, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), anti-Bax mouse mAb (Cat#GB12690, 1:1000, Servicebio), Bcl-2 (Cat#GB113375, 1:800, Servicebio), and anti-β-actin mouse mAb (Cat#660009-1-Ig, 1:50000, Proteintech).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Paraffin-embedded tissues, including human specimens and mouse xenografts, were deparaffinized and subjected to IHC processing under standard protocols [24]. Probing of FOXA1 or SLC7A11 was conducted using anti-FOXA1 rabbit pAb (Cat#PA5-27157, 1:500, Invitrogen) or anti-SLC7A11 rabbit pAb (Cat#26864-1-AP, 1:200, Proteintech), respectively. After incubation of the secondary antibody, sections were visualized by the DAB kit (Beyotime).

Evaluation of cell growth ability

The growth of C33A and HeLa CC cells after the different transfection was evaluated using colony formation and EdU proliferation experiments (BeyoClick™ EdU-488, Beyotime). In colony formation analysis, approximately 200 transfected CC cells were seeded in each well of a 6-well plate and grown for 12 days at 37 °C, which was followed by crystal violet staining. In EdU analysis, transfected CC cells grown in a 96-well plate were subjected to EdU incubation, Azide 488 staining (showing a green fluorescence), and DAPI staining (showing a blue fluorescence). The SZH10 Stereo microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was subsequently used for image acquisition.

Flow cytometry

Under the use of the apoptosis detection kit and protocols (Beyotime), C33A and HeLa CC cells introduced with sh-FOXA1, sh-NC, sh-FOXA1 + SLC7A11, or sh-FOXA1 + pcDNA were stained with PI and Annexin V-FITC. Within 30 min, the apoptotic rate was analyzed via the FACSCanto (BD Biosciences, Heidelberg, Germany).

Detection of SOD, MDA, GSH, and Fe2+ contents

For these analyses, C33A and HeLa CC cells were harvested after introduction by sh-FOXA1, sh-NC, sh-FOXA1 + SLC7A11, or sh-FOXA1 + pcDNA. The commercial SOD Assay Kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China), Ferrous Iron Colorimetric Assay Kit (Elabscience), GSH Assay Kit (Enzyme-linked Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), and MDA Assay Kit (Beyotime) were applied as per the producers’ instructions.

ROS detection

C33A and HeLa CC cells after sh-FOXA1, sh-NC, sh-FOXA1 + SLC7A11, or sh-FOXA1 + pcDNA transfection were harvested to assess the amount of ROS using the ROS Assay Kit, which was based on the DCFH-DA probes, as recommended by the vendor (Beyotime). Data analysis was performed using the Olympus SZH10 Stereo microscope.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR assay

To validate the direct binding of FOXA1 to the SLC7A11 promoter, ChIP experiments were carried out using EZ-Magna ChIP™ A/G Kit, in accordance to the accompanying suggestion (Millipore, Shanghai, China), and anti-FOXA1 rabbit pAb (Cat#PA5-27157, 1:500, Invitrogen) or rabbit IgG pAb control (Cat#30000-0-AP, Proteintech). After cellular DNA fragmentation, incubation of the relevant antibody and magnetic beads was conducted overnight at 4 °C. The co-precipitated DNA was processed by qPCR for assessment of the enrichment level of the SLC7A11 promoter fragment. The specific primer sets (Supplementary Table 1) were used: the S1 fragment, producing a product of 134 bp, and the S2 fragment, which produced a product of 129 bp.

Luciferase assay

The SLC7A11 promoter fragment harboring the predicted binding site (TATTTACTTTG) or the mutant (TATTTACTTTG mutated to ATAAATGAAAC) was cloned into the pGL3-basic plasmid to produce the SLC7A11 promoter reporter constructs (SLC7A11-WT and SLC7A11-MUT). C33A and HeLa CC cells propagated in 10% (v/v) FBS MEM were introduced with SLC7A11-WT or SLC7A11-MUT and pcDNA-FOXA1 (for FOXA1 upregulation) or nontarget control pcDNA-NC (both from Sangon Biotech). After 48 h, the luciferase activity was gauged under the application of Dual-Luc Reporter Gene Assay Kit (Beyotime).

Bioinformatics and statistics

The GEPIA 2 database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index) was utilized for the expression profiling of FOXA1 in TCGA-cervical squamous cell carcinoma (CESC) dataset. The computerized algorithm Jaspar2024 tool was applied to observe the binding of FOXA1 and the SLC7A11 promoter. The ROC curve was utilized to evaluate the diagnostic ability of FOXA1 in CC, with the area under the curve (AUC). The Kaplan-Meier estimator was used for survival analysis, followed by significance assessment by long-rank analysis. For statistical significance (P < 0.05), non-parametric Mann-Whitney test, Student’s t-test (unpaired or paired), and one-way ANOVA were used.

Astragalus polysaccharide ameliorates bone marrow haematopoietic failure in mice with aplastic anaemia via the Hippo/TERT signalling pathway
Authors: XinYu Tang, RuiRong Xu, Yan Wang, Kui Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo study the effects of astragalus polysaccharide (APS) on aplastic anaemia (AA) in mice through modulation of the upstream and downstream effectors of the Hippo pathway. Further, we investigated the underlying mechanisms of APS in the treatment of AA to provide possible therapeutic strategies and drugs for clinical use.MethodsBALB/c mice were subjected to 4 Gy X-ray irradiation and subsequently injected with lymphocytes from DBA/2 donor mice by using the tail vein to establish an AA mouse model. Next BALB/c mice were randomly divided into five groups: control (untreated), AA (model), and three APS remedy groups - LA (low-dose APS, 200 mg/kg), MA (medium-dose APS, 400 mg/kg), and HA (high-dose APS, 800 mg/kg). The mice were sacrificed after 14 d of continuous gavage with different doses of APS or saline, and the bone marrow, spleen and liver were obtained. The haematopoietic condition, apoptosis levels, and the expression of Large Tumour Suppressor 1/2 (LATS 1/2), transcriptional co-activator of activation (TCA) Yes-associated protein (YesAP), transcriptional enhancer of adherence domain (TEAD) protein, and Telomerase Reverse Transcriptase (TERT), were analysed.ResultsCompared with control mice, mice in the AA group exhibited a reduction in peripheral blood cell counts, a significant decrease in the haematopoietic area, increased adipocyte infiltration, and overall haematopoietic failure, mirroring the clinical presentation of AA; After 14 days of treatment with different doses of APS, the APS-treated group, in comparison to the untreated AA group, showed an increase in peripheral blood cell counts (P < 0.05), an expansionof the haematopoietic area in the bone marrow, restoration of haematopoietic function (P < 0.05), and a marked reduction in adipocyte infiltration within the bone marrow(P < 0.05). Cell infiltration into the bone marrow was also reduced. Further experiments revealed that the expression levels of TERT, LATS 1/2, YAP, and TEAD were elevated in the treated mice (P < 0.05).ConclusionX-ray irradiation combined with T-lymphocyte infusion successfully established an AA mouse model, and APS administration modulated the expression of TERT and the Hippo pathway effectors LATS 1/2, YAP, and TEAD, which are implicated in the pathogenesis of AA. This modulation resulted in the restoration of bone marrow haematopoietic function in AA mice, suggesting APS as a promising therapeutic agent for the treatment of AA.Keywords: Aplastic anaemia, Astragalus polysaccharide, Hippo signalling pathway, Telomerase reverse transcriptase

Introduction

Aplastic anaemia (AA) is a common disorder of bone marrow (BM) failure that affects the blood system. It is characterised by a severe depletion of haematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), leading to a decrease in the number of all blood cell lineages. However, the pathogenesis of AA remains unclear Recent studies on telomere dynamics in AA have revealed contribute to leading to the development of AA. In our previous study, we that patients exhibit significant alterations in telomere length and telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) expression. TERT is a critical determinant of telomerase activity, and these changes may play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of HSC senescence, apoptosis, and accelerated telomere shortening [1], Maintaining stable telomere length is crucial for protecting cell survival, and the loss of telomerase function can lead to stem cell self-renewal disorders [2].

The Hippo signalling pathway adjusts cell homeostasis and growth, maintains normal stem cell function, and is important for tissue regeneration and cancer development [3]. At the core of this signalling pathway is a kinase cascade involving the large tumour suppressor factor 1/2 (LATS 1/2) protein kinases, which phosphorylate and inhibit the Yes-associated protein (YAP) and the PDZ binding motif (TAZ). The kinase chain phosphorylates and inhibits the transcriptional coactivators YAP and TAZ, thereby regulating the expression of target genes [4]. When the Hippo signalling pathway is activated, upstream factors such as LATS 1/2 are activated and the expression level of YAP is reduced, leading to cell apoptosis. Conversely, when the Hippo pathway is inactivated, the expression of YAP is increased, leading to cell proliferation [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. YAP/TAZ regulates self-renewal of stem cells [9] These transcriptional coactivators regulate the differentiation of mouse embryonic stem cells and are activated in induced pluripotent stem cells. Down regulation of YAP expression leads to the loss of stem cell pluripotency [10]. Additionally, YAP functions as a momentous effector molecule downstream of the Hippo signalling pathway in tumour cells. Transcription-enhanced association domains (TEADs) act as effectors located downstream of the Hippo signalling pathway and function by binding to YAP. Together, YAP and TEAD can co-localize at the TERT promoter, regulating the expression levels of TERT. These research endorsed that the Hippo pathway can also play a function in the development of AA [11,12]. Building on our previous research, we further investigated the pathogenesis of AA.

Huangqi, also known as Mianqi or Mianhuangqi, is the dried root of perennial herbaceous plants in the legume family, such as Mongu Huangqi or Membranous Astragalus. As a type of tonic in Chinese medicine, Astragalus is known for its efficacy in tonifying qi and generating yang. It supports the body's natural defences by expelling pus, stabilizing the surface, and stopping sweating. It can help patients with AA recover from HSC transplantation, alleviate the symptoms of radiotherapy, and prolong the survival period. Consequently, it is widely integrated into the comprehensive treatment approach for AA [13]. Modern pharmacological studies have already discovered that Huangqi contains various effective ingredients such as polysaccharides, saponins, flavonoids, and amino acids. Among these. Astragalus polysaccharide (APS) is particularly significant APS plays an important role in haematopoiesis by not only promoting and protecting haematopoietic function, protecting against bone marrow suppression, and decreasing apoptosis of haematopoietic cells [14], but also by maintaining telomerase activity and promoting telomere lengthening [15]. These effects are expected to contribute to the therapy of AA. However, little detailed research has been executed on the mechanism through which APS affects AA, and the application of APS remains inadequately supported by theoretical research.

In this study, we focused on TERT, a marker of stem cell senescence and apoptosis, and the Hippo pathway, which is closely interrelated to AA. We investigated the effects of APS on AA by examining its impact on the effectors of the Hippo pathway involved in the pathogenesis of AA. We devoted to reveal the pathological mechanisms of AA-related haematopoietic failure and to identify new targets for the development of new-type therapeutic drugs.

Animals and experimental protocols

Beijing Viton Lever (Beijing, China) provided 90 specific pathogen-free (SPF)-grade healthy male BALB/c mice and DBA/2 mice, each weighing between 18 and 22 g and aged between 6 and 8 weeks. Among them, 40 DBA/2 mice were lymphocyte donors and 50 BALB/c mice were lymphocyte recipients. The animals had been emplaced in a warm, noiseless surroundings with freedom to obtain to meals and water. They were acclimatised and fed for one week before the start of the experiment. All animal procedures were authorized by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of the Shandong University of TCM (Animal Quality Certificate Licence No. SCXK [Beijing] 2021-0006). Whole operations were implemented in the light of the corresponding guidelines and regulations.

Establishment of an animal model of recalcitrance

Fifty BALB/c mice were stochasticly separated into several groups: control, AA, LA (low APS treatment), MA (medium APS treatment), and HA (high APS treatment), with 10 mice in each group. Preliminary pre-tests revealed that BALB/c mice illuminated with 5 Gy X-rays and subsequently infused with lymphocytes exhibited symptoms of depression, loss of appetite, decreased mobility, and rough fur on the second day, and died one after another in the following two weeks. In contrast, the mice irradiated with 3 Gy X-rays showed minimal differences in appearance, mobility, and peripheral blood cell counts compared to normal mice. These mice did not reach the AA severity required for the experiment. In contrast, while 4 Gy X-ray irradiation did not lead to rapid death of mice in the short term, it produced a more pronounced immune-damaging effects.

Consequently, our AA mouse model was established based on specific pre-experimental results, with reference to a previous study [16]. The specific pre-experimental results were as follows: Briefly, BALB/c mice were irradiated with 4 Gy X-rays, and 5 × 106 lymphocytes/ml from DBA/2 mice were injected into BALB/c mice via the tail vein within 4h of irradiation. The control mice were sham-irradiated (lead-brick shielding) and injected with an equal volumes of saline. Immediately after irradiation, the mice were moved into a SPF-grade sterile laminar flow chamber and fed autoclaved water containing enrofloxacin. The mice were closely monitored and their mental status, activity level, water intake, food intake, urination, and defaecation were systematically recorded.

The day of T-lymphocyte injection was designated as day 0 of the experiment. On the 3rd, 7th, 11th, and 15th days, mice from each group were anaesthetised with isoflurane gas, and blood samples were collected via the infraorbital vein to determine the changes in the amount of white blood cells (WBC), red blood cells (RBC), haemoglobin (Hb), and platelets (PLT) in the peripheral blood. On the 15th day, the femurs of mice in each group were harvested for bone marrow (BM) sectioning, while the sternum and tibia were collected to prepare BM cell suspension. These suspensions were used to create bone marrow smears, which were then examined to verify whether the model was successfully established.

Medicated feeding interventions

Drug administration was initiated after successfully establishing the model. Mice in the treatment group were gavaged daily with APS dissolved in saline at 65 °C, at doses of 200, 400, and 800 mg/kg/day in the LA, MA, and HA groups, respectively, based on previous studies. Mice in the control group and AA group had been given an equal quantity of saline by way of gavage daily for 14 d. Throughout the course of the study, all mice were fed a standard diet and provided with autoclaved water supplemented with enrofloxacin.

Evaluation of the AA mouse model and experimental data testing

Mouse weight and survival recordings

Starting on day 0, we measured the weights of all mice every five days. Additionally, the survival of mice in each group was observed, and the data were used to plot curves showing changes in body weight and survival rates.

Routine measurement of mouse peripheral blood

After 14 days of APS or saline treatment, all mice were anaesthetised and euthanised on day 14. Collected blood from mice through the orbital vein prior euthanasia. WBC, RBC, HGB, and PLT counts were measured for each group of mice, and the differences in peripheral blood counts were compared between the groups.

BM smear and section testing

Immediately after the death of all animals, the femur was excised, and BM from the femur was rinsed by using PBS buffer and suspended the flushed BM in erythrocyte lysis to obtain isolated bone marrow single nucleated cells (BMMNCs). These cells were then suspended in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10 % foetal bovine serum (FBS), and the counts were determined using a haematological cell counter, with observations made under a light microscope. In addition, the left femur of each mouse was fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde in 0.1 M PBS (pH 7.4), embedded in paraffin, sectioned and stained with haematoxylin and eosin (HE), and then the slides were analysed by light microscopy.

Detection of apoptosis levels

The degree of apoptosis in newly collected BMMNCs from various groups of mice was quantified by flow cytometry using a membrane-bound protein V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)/propidium iodide (PI) double-staining kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. Mouse femurs from each group were collected, and the BM was extracted using PBS buffer, centrifuged at 1000 g for 5 min, then discarded the supernatant. RPMI-1640 medium was mixed to the pellet, and 1-5 × 105 cells were inoculated into six-well plates. Then we washed the cells twice by using cold PBS, suspended the cells in 500 μl of Binding Buffer, and mixed 5 μl of Annexin V-FITC and Propidium lodide. The mixture was gently mixed and reacted at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. Lastly, we analysed samples using flow cytometry (Beckman Navios EX 3L10C, USA) to detect the level of cellular regeneration.

Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) analysis

After 14 d of treatment, samples were collected from each group of mice, including HSC suspensions, liver tissue, and other BM tissues. RNA was abstracted from these samples and reverse transcribed into cDNA using reverse transcription primers. Then resulting cDNA was then expanded by PCR primers. The reaction system and reaction parameters were set according to the instruction manual, and the expression of TERT, LATS 1/2, YAP, and TEAD was detected in each group of mice.

Western blot analysis

After 14 days of treatment, HSC cell suspensions were collected from the BM of each group of mice.Quantified the extracted protein by BCA method. Separated 40 μg of protein in each sample by 10 % SDS-PAGE electrophoresis. Then transferred the protein electrophoresis to a PVDF membrane, incubated the membrane with the primary antibodies against TERT, LATS 1/2, YAP, and TEAD whole night (4 °C), next incubated the membrane with the secondary antibody. Then washed, developed, and analysed the membranes for band intensity. The results were recorded, and the expression levels of TERT, LATS 1/2, YAP, and TEAD in mouse HSCs from each group were analysed.

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed operating SPSS 25.0. Data were denoted as the mean ± sd. A t-test was used to contrast differences between treatment groups. Statistical analyses and plotting were implemented by using GraphPad Prism 10. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for examining the importance of discrepancy between groups, with factorial ANOVA applied to analyse the effects of different treatment groups, when P < 0.05, the results were considered as having statistical significance.

APS improves BM haematopoiesis in AA mice

HE staining of bone marrow smears and femoral tissues of mice was performed to assess the potential effects of APS on AA mice. The results revealed a significant decrease in blood cell counts, haematopoietic area, megakaryocytes, and the proportion of haematopoietic cells, alongside a marked increase in non-haematopoietic cells (adipocytes) in femoral bone marrow smears of mice in the AA group contrasted to the control group(Fig. 4 [a – j]). As the dose of APS increased(200, 400, and 800 mg/kg), there was a progressive rise in both BM cell and peripheral blood cell counts, as observed in blood smears as well as BM histology. Additionally, there was an increase in the haematopoietic area and the proportion of haematopoietic cells.

APS restores the increased rate of apoptosis in AA mice

The degree of apoptosis of BMMNCs in each group of mice was measured 2 weeks after the completion of APS treatment. The results showed a significantly increase in apoptosis among BMMNCs in mice with AA in contrast with the control group (P < 0.01). Conversely, the degree of apoptosis in BMMNCs was prominently lower in the APS-treated groups than in the AA group, indicating a protective effect of APS against apoptosis (P < 0.01). Discussion

Previous studies have confirmed that AA may be triggered by haematopoietic failure, a decrease in the number of HSCs, immune system abnormalities, and telomere shortening. However, the specific pathogenesis of AA remains unclear, and there is a lack of research on the number and function of HSCs and the regulatory mechanisms underlying telomere depletion. Our preliminary research focused on exploring the pathogenesis of AA [1.2] and found that AA mice exhibited abnormalities in various key areas, such as bone marrow haematopoiesis, cell apoptosis, and cytokine expression. Multiple funded research projects and clinical applications have substantiated the efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine in AA. Furthermore, our findings indicate that Astragalus polysaccharides play a crucial role in mediating these effects.

As a major part of the tonic TCM Astragalus membranaceus, APS possesses immune-modulating and antitumour functions [17], and the extract is often used clinically in the treatment of haematological tumours to improve immune function and regulate the level of apoptosis [18]. Currently, studies on the use of APS for the treatment of AA are relatively rare, and the underlying mechanisms of action lack experimental validation. Our experiments investigated the intrinsic regulatory mechanisms contributing to the development of AA in mice, focusing on the effects on HSC states—both resting and activated—as well as the Hippo signalling pathway involved in regulating apoptosis.

Building on previous studies and the results of preliminary experiments, we exposed BALB/c mice to 4 Gy X-ray irradiation. Four hours later, we administered 5 × 106 lymphocytes/ml from DBA/2 donor mice via the tail vein into the irradiated BALB/c mice, and the control mice were sham-irradiated (shielded with lead bricks) and injected with an equal volume of physiological saline. We monitored the external manifestations of the mice, along with WBC, RBC, PLT counts, Hb levels, as well as BM sections and smears, were observed to establish a mouse model of AA that reflects clinical features and laboratory indices. This form of AA may be caused by abnormal activation of the donor-derived immune cells, which attack foreign lymphocytes introduced into the recipient's body, resulting in a reduction in the number of functional HSCs and telomere depletion. Treatment with different doses of APS restored impaired haematopoietic function and reversed telomere depletion. This study suggests that APS can affect the functional quantity and telomere length of HSCs, regulate apoptosis, and may serve as an effective therapeutic component for the treatment of AA.

To investigate the intrinsic mechanisms underlying the treatment of AA by APS, we investigated the levels of cellular factor upstream and downstream of the Hippo pathway at the molecular level. Our findings revealed significantly higher levels of LATS 1/2 and lower levels of YAP, TEAD, and TERT in the AA group contrasted to the control group. After intervention with APS at various concentrations, the expression of LATS 1/2 in the APS-treated mice was notably reduced, while the expression levels of YAP, TEAD, and TERT were obviously elevated when compared to the AA group.

The Hippo is an important signalling pathway that affects the resting and activated states of HSCs and mediates apoptosis. When this signalling pathway is abnormally activated, it disrupts the expression of its upstream and downstream factors, leading to dysregulated apoptosis, altered resting and activation states of HSCs, and excessive telomere depletion, ultimately leading to AA.Forepassed reasearch has indicated that the relationship between the pathogenesis of AA and the Hippo signalling pathway remains poorly understood, which complicates further exploration of the underlying mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets. Our AA mouse model exhibited severe BM haematopoietic failure, reduced HSCs, and reduced apoptosis. Through molecular studies, we confirmed that these abnormal changes could be ameliorated by APS treatment. In AA mice, aberrant activation of the Hippo pathway resulted in increased downstream expression of LATS 1/2 and decreased expression of YAP. This imbalance prompted apoptosis, resulting in a reduced the number of HSCs and decreased TERT expression, ultimately causing shortened telomere length. After intervention with APS, the expression levels of related factors in mice approached normalization, haematopoietic function was restored, and telomere length was extended. These results indicate that APS can modulate the resting and activation states of HSCs and regulate apoptosis by influencing the expression of effectors of the Hippo pathway in AA mice. Therapeutic targets that maintain the expression of these effectors are likely to be important players in the treatment of AA.

Currently, research on the application of APS to treat AA is scarce, and the underlying mechanisms of action lack experimental verification. Our research builds on previous studies and elucidates the relationship between the pathogenesis of AA and the Hippo signalling pathway. The results of this study may furnish new targets and directions for the clinical therapy of AA. However, owing to possible deficiencies in the feeding environment, feeding techniques, experimental procedures, and other factors, our study has certain limitations. Next, we plan to conduct long-term, multi-batch research to further validate the relevant mechanisms and targets.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrated that injecting mice with lymphocytes from donors within 4 h after whole-body irradiation with 4 Gy X-rays induced AA. This effect may be attributed to the attack of donor lymphocytes by recipient T lymphocytes, resulting in reduced activity and numbers of HSCs and failure of BM haematopoiesis. Hippo pathway is important for stem cell activity. The results showed that APS restored HSC activity, corrected immune abnormalities, and normalized apoptosis levels by regulating the expression of molecules both upstream and downstream of the Hippo pathway. The restorative influence of APS on blood routine counts in AA mice may also be attributed to its immunomodulatory and haemopoietic-protective properties. These results suggest that APS promotes and protects haematopoietic function, reduces haematopoietic cell apoptosis, and may offer potential therapeutic benefits for AA. Radiation affects a wide range of people, with haematopoietic failure leading to immune abnormalities and abnormal activation of T lymphocytes in vivo—emerging as a significant concern for global health. Consequently, our conclusions will be useful for the prevention and therapy of haematopoietic hypoplasia and subsequent AA. More exploration is still required to clarify the functional properties and intrinsic regulatory mechanisms of APS.

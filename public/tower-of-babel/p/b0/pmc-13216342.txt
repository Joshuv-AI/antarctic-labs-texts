A unified multimodal GenAI platform integrating GraphRAG multi-agent systems and custom language models for intelligent document processing and knowledge synthesis
Authors: Bharat Singh Parihar, Tarun Kumar Singh, Govind Gonnade, Bhupesh Kumar Dewangan, Nilesh Shelke, Amit Pimpalkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRetrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) based enterprise systems on document reasoning tasks are getting to grow more based on Large Language Models (LLMs); however, non-vector-based RAG pipelines continue to show inadequate relational consistency, multi-document aggregation, and task-specific decision support. This work presents a unified Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) platform that integrates a multi-agent system with Graph-based RAG (GraphRAG) to support complex, multi-task reasoning over structured and unstructured data. The framework combines knowledge graph construction, dense retrieval, and a custom language model to enable accurate and context-aware responses across tasks such as document question answering, entity extraction, text-to-SQL generation, and fact verification. A modular pipeline is designed for task classification, agent routing, retrieval-oriented reasoning, and task-specific execution, including ATS resume evaluation. The suggested platform consists of 5 conceptual layers and may be applied to solve multi-PDF questions and analyze resumes in an automated Applicant Tracking System (ATS). The platform has a set of six custom-trained LLMs, the largest of which is a 175-billion-parameter foundation model that trained on 2.5 trillion tokens, is more domain flexible and less reliant on external API-based solutions. Experimental analysis of document question answering depicts that GraphRAG performs better by 23% and 46% in exact-match accuracy and multi-hop reasoning accuracy in comparison to baselines with only a slight augment in mean latency of 45 ms. The accuracy on complex queries of a schema-aware T2S model containing safety checks at execution of 94.2% is significantly greater compared to direct prompting on Trustworthy Language Model (TLM). The multi-agent model achieves 96.8% correspondence with professional recruiter guesses on 500 resumes and mean error of absolute lesser than that of uni-agent benchmarks. The independent research assistant saves approximately 65% time utilized in manual research and the level of accuracy is 98%.Keywords: Large language models, Graph retrieval-augmented generation, Multi-agent systems, Knowledge graphs, Natural language processing, Semantic search, T2S, Document intelligence, Transfer learning, Microservices architectureSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The unstructured text is one of the main bottlenecks in the modern businesses and organizations as they end up creating thousands of documents reports, resumes, research, and communications are generated each day but searching, summarizing, and utilizing these documents in making decisions is still inefficient. The existing tools are fragmented and ineffective and thus there is clear and urgent need to create more effective and reliable tools that would help to unify all the data effectively. The traditional document management systems are on top of the keyword-based searching systems that lack in semantic dependencies and contextual relations in natural language. It is a disadvantage particularly sharp in cases when users require synthesized answers in a series of documents or when they are required to understand complicated relationships between an entity within a knowledge bank. This has led to promising performances of the recent developments in LLMs, especially natural language understanding and language generation, e.g. GPT-4, Claude and PaLM1–3 although even the general-purpose models have significant drawbacks to more specialized fields.

On the one hand, they are exposed to the ages of knowledge disintegration, which renders them unable to assimilate the new information or proprietary information unless they are directly encouraged to consider it. Second, they exhibit a hallucination tendency in that they do give reasonable but rather false information when it involves questions that are not in their training distribution4. Thirdly, they rely on parametric memory that deprives them of the chances of providing correct citation and determinate reasoning pathways. Fourth, the models are API-dependent, including operational costs and data privacy, and depending on a third-party service. To address these limitations, RAG has emerged as a possible paradigm to combine parametric knowledge in language models and non-parametric knowledge base retrieval5. The classical RAG designs decode the semantically similar document fragments by referencing to dense representations of vectors of words or characters6 and integrate them to the context window of the language model to produce. Though there is a lot of contribution towards the factual accuracy by such approach and there are such benefits as ability to consider the latest information, the approach has some drawbacks. The strategies of the vector-only based retrieval models have no means of defining multi-hop links between entities, cannot support queries that require synthesis between sections of document, and provide no direct way of representing structural knowledge, such as hierarchies, taxonomies and causal relationships7,8.

GraphRAG has proven to be superior when dealing with complex reasoning tasks, more particularly with accuracy of multi-hop reasoning or making sense of the relationship of objects across document boundaries. However, the existing versions of the GraphRAG algorithm are largely confined to research editions and there has been little knowledge on the application of the algorithm in actual production systems to suit the diverse applications. Other advancements that have been realised in multi-agent systems demonstrate the utility of task breakdown and specialised interaction between agents in solving complex problems9. Multi-agent structures rather than relying on a single and optimized, monolithic model to accomplish multi-faceted multi-agent problems assign the agents specific tasks which are tuned to accomplish subtasks. It has been found that such paradigm can be effective in fields where a variety of knowledge is required e.g. in the code generation, scientific reasoning and document analysis. The coordination mechanisms which these agents use to share information, distributing intermediate products and introducing in the end products are dynamic study areas with grave practical implications10. The resumes submitted to large corporations are automatically filtered away by search engines so that only three-quarters of them are read by their staff, often due to formatting errors, mismatched keywords, or other structural issues that would not otherwise matter when it comes to qualification of the applicants by a human recruitment department11. The current resume optimization schemes present generic suggestions following simple rule of thumb to evade the degree of evaluation requirements of the automated systems and human. The collective solution assumes simultaneous study of various factors that include how high the availability and concentration of keywords, how strictly the structural formatting is maintained, how successful the quality of the material, role-based matching, and rules of the industry. Language models may be quite efficient in content summarizing and synthesis, however, with no independence in the choice of the research strategy, various multi-step information retrieval processes, credibility of source verification, and citation accuracy, such models limit their applicability into practice12. In an effective research assistant, it is required not only to be natural language understanding, but also to be metacognitive planning of tasks, iterative refining and quality assessment13. One of the challenges that continue to be felt in the domain of data-driven decision making is the accessibility barrier between the domain experts, as well as the organizational databases. There are frequent situations when business analysts, research, and managers require specific information of corporate databases and lack the required technical expertise to compose SQL queries and navigate through the complex database structures. The existing natural language interfaces to databases perform well on the simple queries and not well on the more complicated tasks involving multiple joins, nested subqueries and compound aggregations14,15.

This study proposes only one GenAI platform that addresses such intricate problems by offering one centralized architecture that has five dedicated modules that are powered by custom-trained LLMs specialized in a specific task. Related work

RAG and dense retrieval

Recent literature on RAG and knowledge graph shows that the results were enhanced indicating that they encounter problems of high accuracy and reliable reasoning. Combining structured knowledge graphs with retrieval pipelines leads to enhanced consistency and semantic although the general performance improvements are incremental with a couple of benchmarks indicating only moderate changes in question answering activities. Wang et al.49 examine the combination of RAG and knowledge graphs, showing a small improvement, yet still, they provide the Natural Questions and PubMedQA scores predominantly within 55–70% which implies that the existing approaches to RAG are not effective enough. Designing a cloud-based framework, Sanyal et al.50 suggest RAGMail aimed at minimising the number of hallucinations in the output of LLM; nevertheless, their results of comparison between the generic and resume, as well as FEWL setups, reveal only minor enhancement. The study by Lewis et al. introduced RAG as knowledge-intensive natural language processing task and showed that enhancing the language models with passages retrieved significantly increases the performance in open domain question answering5. Their architecture consists of a dense passage retriever that is founded on the BERT-based architecture 35 that extracts a relevant context of a large corpus and feeds it to the BART-based generator19. Subsequent research has also created dense retrieval approaches that include Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) in which Karpukhin et al. trained the encoder on queries and passages through contrastive learning2. DPR demonstrated significant improvements to standard BM25 retrieval and bettered lexical-based methods by a range of nine to nineteen of the percentage on several open-domain question answering data sets6. The paper has been following with the work on the topic; Gao et al. investigated the issue of accurate zero-shot dense retrieval without relevance labels and offered the Hypothetical Document Embeddings (HyDE) technique20. HyDE uses language model to generate hypothetical relevant documents instead of query encoding which is accomplished by encoding the documents to be retrieved. The algorithm manages distributional incompatibility of query and document that is likely to weaken dense retrieval performance. Recent literature21,22 has been discussing other developments on the basic RAG paradigm. Iterative processes of retrieval involve multiple retrieval and generation and cycles and abandoned the model to make changes in its information needs due to initial results. The encoder is used to decode one passage then another and the representations are combined within the decoder, permitting more usefully brought multivariate evidence to be used. Self-RAG introduces self-reflection mechanism whereby models are given a chance to condemn and would improve the self-retrieval/generation decisions through learning to utilize critique and refinement tokens.

Graph-based knowledge representation and GraphRAG

Knowledge graphs is a type of structured information representation, of which the well-known examples include Freebase, Wikidata, and domain ontologies. The first attempts to use knowledge graphs in neural networks focused on knowledge graph embeddings such as TransE, DistMult and ComplEx that can learn continuous vectors of both entities and relations that can be graph structured. The combination of graphs and language models was already a significant field of study that sought to bridge the symbolic structured knowledge and the natural language knowledge. ERNIE23 was enhanced by adding entities of knowledge graphs; entity embeddings of TransE were used as inputs in pre-training. K-BERT enhanced knowledge injection three times when implementing sentence encoding, and visible matrices were used to regulate the scope of knowledge integration. In KagNet24, commonsense knowledge graphs were entered into Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN)25 to make more reasoning on CommonsenseQA. More recent attempts have done more fine integration between language models and knowledge graphs. QA-GNN represented a single reasoning system that aimed to influence the strengths of providing text representations and graph representations on the ConceptNet and performed state-of-the-art on CommonsenseQA through the combination of unstructured text comprehension with structured relational reasoning. Samir et al.26 use the graphs neural networks with contextual embeddings as queries of the knowledge-based QA but get an average coverage of approximately 65 and that sparse subgraphs contribute of the high percentage errors which suggests that there is a problem of graph completeness and reasoning depth. In recent times, Edge et al. introduced ideas as the GraphRAG paradigm that essentially represents a re-conceptualization of RAG7. To retrieve entities, like the semantic similarity, GraphRAG will initially generate a knowledge graph by identifying entities and categorizing the relationships based on documents used to construct it and thereafter, semantic community detection to discover hierarchies in a graph. The semantic similarity as well as graph proximity are employed to extract relevant subgraphs on a query time that provides richness of the relational context that the multi-hop reasoning can be carried out on a higher level. However, the work on GraphRAG is quite recent and little experimentation has been undertaken on the production deployment, scaling of large document corpus and interaction with downstream applications8. Peng et al.51 concentrate on refined extraction methods to build knowledge graphs using LLM but got a false positive rate of 7.4% in low confidence triples indicating quality problems with graph outputs. Although the current methods have improved, they are still limited by inefficiencies of retrieval, limitations of understanding, and challenges in data quality and quality, which means much to be done to develop effective methods of knowledge integration.

Multi-agent systems and collaborative AI

The multi-agent systems lie around distributive AI whereby two or more autonomous agents are employed in solving problems that could not be solved alone. The focus on the use of multi-agent approaches in the context of LLMs is explained by the realization that task decomposition and model specialization can overcome the shortcomings of monolithic models. GenAI agents suggested by Park et al.9 demonstrated emergent social behaviours and also employed advanced architecture that enabled agents to produce relationships, organize actions and present patterns of complex actions with comparatively simple elements of architecture. Hong et al., MetaGPT, the collaborative framework works with multi-agent where characters of a software development are specialized. MetaGPT could compute more successfully on the code generation benchmarks by adjusting complex tasks into dedicated subtasks that fitted the processes of software engineering10. The system of creating a multi-agent conversation system by Wu et al.,11, AutoGen, provides the general format of assembling this type of systems. They are highly customizable in pattern of conversation, are flexible in their definition of agents, and can integrate external tools and API. AutoGen assists in the production of sophisticated workflows through the coordination of the agents, and its functionality is demonstrated in various applications which include solving mathematical problems, web automation and completion of multi-turn tasks. Multi-agent architectures that are more specific to domains have more recently been investigated. Although there have been these successes most of the research done in the multi-agent field has been in the form of code generation, game playing, or simulated environments and little is known about the potential application in document analysis, resume evaluation or research automation. The practical areas that we have demonstrated the effectiveness of the specialized agent interaction in knowledge work are used to apply the multi-agent paradigms. Klang et al.53 demonstrates that multi agent systems organized in an orchestrated way are more accurate compared to single agents when the clinical workload is limited; but the tests only restrict to specific retrieval and extraction tasks as the actual performance in more comprehensive clinical reasoning is not yet being evaluated. Bevara et al. (2025) propose a plug-in Resume2Vec 52 which encodes resume and job description, which in turn enhance candidate matching in ATS. Although it also enhances ranking, profit is slight, making nDCG gain only 4.44% over conventional systems, so it makes little influence in some types of jobs.

Natural language interfaces to databases

Machine learning techniques have been adopted at the expense of text-to-SQL (T2S), which is a traditional rule-based and template-based techniques. Attention-based sequence-to-sequence models were created due to the progress of deep learning with moderate achievements in the case of simplified datasets15. Still, more complex questions involving more than one join, more than one sub query and more complex aggregations were still an issue. Yu et al.14 produced a model called Spider benchmark, which was used to determine a rigorous system of evaluation of cross-domain T2S. T2S functionality has been substantially enhanced by most of the latest pre-trained language models. In RAT-SQL, relational-aware self-attention was introduced as a way of better encoding database schemas. BRIDGE suggested an encodification of questions and database schemas. The state-of-the-art results were captured by the RESDSQL that was suggested by Li et al.,11, and it uses a decoupling technique that can link the schema and parsing the skeleton independently. This decomposition could go up to 84.1% accuracy when it came to the implementation of Spider. DIN-SQL went still further with self-correcting in-context learning which was decomposed13. Their scheme breaks down SQL generation into sub problems, and each step is self-validated by calling separate language models and intermediate self-validating. This decomposition as well improved accuracy and interpretability of the generation process. Even though this has been done, a significant amount of the literature on T2S dwells on the theoretical standards with not much reflection on practical deployment problems of query validation through safety, production of the explanations of results, or design the user interface. These are the empirical issues that are addressed in our practice, and which is still competitive in a complex querying.

Domain-specific language model adaptation

Language models domain adaptation and transfer learning have been broadly researched. Premilitary findings revealed that trained models could be trained on domain-specific data to achieve improved performance on special tasks. Other helpful fine-tuning methods that were also suggested by ULMFiT include discriminative learning rates, and gradual unfreezing27. Domain-adaptive pre-training, i.e. pre-training then domain-specific tasks have been observed to be equally beneficial across domains. State-of-the-art parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods have enabled the adaptation of very large models without updating the parameters. LoRA applies low-rank transformation matrices to modify the behaviour of models without modifying original parameters, making it much less expensive to train as well as to store18. Other ways to effective adaptation utilization encompass prefix tuning, adapter layers and prompt tuning. Fields There are domain-specific language models that have been developed in various fields. BioBERT, SciBERT and PubMedBERT are specialized in biomedical and scientific ones. CaseLaw and LegalBERT models address the legal document understanding. FinBERT is text-based oriented financial analysis. During domain specific tasks, these domain specific models will outperform the general-purpose ones. Nonetheless, most studies consider encoder-only models such as BERT variations or small generative models. It is uncommon to perform large-scale domain-specific generative model training in-the-large because doing so is expensive16,17.

Methodology

System architecture

Our GenAI platform employs a microservices architecture designed for scalability, maintainability, and independent component evolution. Figure 1 outlines of unified GenAI platform architecture comprises five logical layers: frontend presentation, API gateway, service layer, AI engine layer, and data persistence layer. It is a system with 5-layer microservice policy comprising of (1) Frontend Layer, which contains react based user interfaces in each module, (2) API Layer, at which request routing and authentication services are coordinated by the Express API Gateway, (3) Service Layer, which contains five independent micro services (File Service, PDF Service, ATS Service, SQL Service and Research Service), (4) Business Logic Layer, in which core AI features, including PDF Processor, GraphRAG Engine, Agentic RAG System, NL-to-SQL Processor, The interacting layer implements a one-page application on the top of React and Type-script, Type-safety, Tailwind CSS, which is the responsive design. Code splitting is employed in the application to reduce initial load times, and lazy loading is employed, each major feature module is in its own package. Live communication with the backend services is carried out with the assistance of the Server-Sent Events and simplify to stream language model replies and present a token-by-token perspective, which enhances user experience in generation. There is the API gateway layer that is introduced in the Express.js on the top of Node.js that provides an API gateway with a single point of entry to the client requests. The authentication of the gateway is based on the combination of JSON web tokens, cookies to handle the sessions and put as cookies as an httpOnly due to rate limiting of 100 requests per minute to individual users who are authenticated. The service layer is comprised of five autonomous microservices that correspond to features of the platform, such as PDF Service (document upload, processing and chat), ATS Service (resume analysis and feedback generation), Research Service (autonomous execution of research tasks), SQL Service (natural language database queries), and Authentication Service (user management and sessions handling).

Unified GenAI platform architecture.

The services include a RESTful API and database schema, which is deployable and scalable individually. The inter-service interaction works based on the direct calls to the HTTP when the synchronous operation is performed and on the message queues when the asynchronous work is conducted. AI engine layer includes an abstraction that brings together heterogenous custom models and is realised as an independent FastAPI-based service that is optimised to be inference-performant. Models, batch and cache responses are loaded using this layer. NVIDIA Triton Inference Server supports the management of assets of GPUs and provides optimistic serving of models compiled with TensorRT. The layer of abstraction enables the support of many variants of models at once, which enables A/B testing and progressive application of model amendments. PostgreSQL version 15 is the primary relational database applied in the data persistence layer and implemented logical separation through the provision of a schema to each microservice. Embedding of documents is stored in custom vector database, which is provided by FAISS with HNSW indexing, the semantic search is carried out16. Redis is applied in caching common data and user sessions along with intermediate calculation results. Object storage, which is S3 compatible interface, is used to store files that are uploaded through lifecycle policies and to store files that are not accessed very often.

Custom language model development

GenAI-LLM foundation model

Our model has been initiated on transformer architecture plus several extensions that have been done to improve its performance and efficiency. The model contains 175 billion parameters, which are distributed into 96 transformer layers; there are multi-head self-attention and 96 attention heads on the feed-forward networks with an intermediate dimension of 49,152. The training data consists of 2.5 trillion tokens of which curated data can be found in the different sources. It includes 40% filtered web text whose quality classifiers are trained on human curated examples, 25% books and long-form text which is curated based on narrative coherence, 20% scholarly papers and technical documentation utilized in different scientific domains, 10% high quality code found in open-source repositories, and 5% curated conversational data. Data preprocessing involved deduplication of data by MinHash LSH on the levels of document level and paragraph level, the language filtering involved choosing the English language and maintaining the best scoring and selection with the classifier and removal of personally identifiable information 32 33. Training a model including several optimizations with AdamW optimizer had a learning rate of 6e-5, linear warming up of 2000 steps and decaying linearly by a factor of 10 to the original. Mixed precision training and float-16 training and float-32 master weights were experienced. Gradient clipping with norm 1.0 globally provided the stability in the training. The model was trained on 1.2 million steps using a batch size 4 million tokens on H100 GPUs which took about a month. The model parallelism in terms of within-layer distribution using tensor parallelism degree 8, pipeline parallelism degree-4, and layer distribution between GPUs, and batch distribution using data parallelism degree 32. The alignment after training was done in two steps. To begin with, fine-tuning on 50,000 high-quality demonstrations of instruction-following of a variety of tasks, format and domain16. Second, human feedback with reinforcement learning by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm17. We gathered preference comparisons on 100,000 model outputs, where each of the 3 independent annotators rated the comparisons. DPO training optimized directly on preference data with learning rate 5e-7. GenAI-embed-V3 embedding model

The quality of semantic search basically depends on the incorporation of model performance. We had our model trained based on contrastive learning37,38. It is made up of 12-layer transformer encoder with 768 hidden dimensions and 12 head attention and produces output embeddings of 1536 dimensions which is learned via a projection layer. The training data consisted of 1 billion pairs of sentences that were constructed using several sources. The positive pairs were made of two sequent sentences of the documents, translation data paraphrases, query, and its answers, query and its clicked document, and paraphrases synthetically made through the back-translation. In-batch negatives were done using hard negatives, Okapi best matching 25 was used to retrieve document and model-mined was done using the examples produced by the model itself which is trying to detect challenging negative. The training objective was to use InfoNCE contrastive loss each example had one positive pair and N -1 negative pairs. We had 0.05 temperature parameter (τ) and in-batch negatives of 8192. The model was optimized with the AdamW, learning rate 2e-5 and cosine decay schedule on 1 million steps. The final model achieved 92.1% mean average precision on the massive text embedding benchmark (MTEB), which is similar the state-of-the-art embedding models and is tuned to technical and academic styles of material typical of our applications.

Hybrid retrieval strategy

The query time system employs hybrid retrieval mode that combines the similarity in vectors and graph closeness45. Based on a user query we view the embedding of the query then select 20 best document chunks based on the cosine similarity. To compute the similarity of two non-zero vectors (A and B) with each other, the cosine of the angle between vectors A and B is computed, which has a value between − 1 and 1.

Simultaneously with this, we are extracting the entities according to the query using the exact extraction model as documents. We re-traverse all the extracted query entities and search their local neighbourhood to 2 hops of the node, using its nodes of the relevant graph of knowledge. The recovered subgraph normally contains 10 to 50 entities and the linking relationships. To rank our subgraph nodes, we use a weighted average of centrality in subgraph, similarity to query entities and their frequency of occurrence in the better ranked document chunks. The last context provided to the language model is the combination of top 5 document chunk with the assistance of the similarity of the vectors and the top 10 graph node with their direct relationships. This is a hybrid environment which enables the model to use semantic similarity as well as structural knowledge and be more precise at those questions which require multi-hop inference or entity relationship knowledge. Schema-aware query generation and safety validation

Our schema serialization converts the metadata on database into a text representation that is comprehensible by language models. We serialise the name of the table, the names of the columns with data types, relationships between the primary keys and foreign keys and sample values of categorical database columns in the case of each table to display value space. The model takes a natural language query and schema context to pose few-shot SQL queries based on illustrations of query patterns of every schema type. The examples are common patterns, including simple selection with filtering, multi-table joins based on foreign key relationships, aggregation by the GROUP BY clauses, subqueries and common table expressions and window functions, when used in the case of an analytical query46,47. It is grounded on a decoding strategy that is constrained in a way that supports syntactically sound SQL. This likelihood of the tokens to complete valid SQL constructs is tuned to advantage tokens and reduce the likelihood of syntax errors. The process of creation offers the SQL statements and the natural language description of the logic of the query and the structure of the anticipated result.

The releasing up to production databases of natural languages demands a strong safety mechanism that prevents destructive operations. Firstly, SQL parsing and ATS analysis involves the use of SQL parsing library which is a library used to interpret the generated SQL into abstract syntax trees through which the query structure is analyzed whether the query is to be executed or not. The parser also identifies the type of statements with SELECT statements being safe and INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, and DROP statements being potentially dangerous. Table and column reference validity work out that referenced entities exist in the schema and table names hallucinated do not exist. Second, the SELECT statements will be the only statements accepted in allowlist validation and by default, every query containing data alteration operation or a schema alteration operation will be denied. Other constraints that we place include limit on result size to make sure that the amount of rows that we have retrieved is 1000; query time limit where we terminate all queries that take more than 30 s and connection privilege where we use database read only privileges that cannot in any way make any changes even when they are used to do it. Third, sensitive database approval processes have human-based approval workflow in which generated queries are forwarded to human operators prior to acting on them. These substitute security-sensitive environment, and utility of natural language interfaces in aiding in the development of queries. These alternative supports security-sensitive settings, and utility of natural language interfaces in helping to formulate queries. Any query that does not pass validation will generate an error information indicating the safety issue and recommending changes to make it a safe query. Such transparency assists users to learn how to create the right queries by getting to know the constraints of the systems.

Discussion

Key findings and contributions

We have already established several crucial findings in our research on a viable implementation of the current AI systems to automatize knowledge work. Firstly, GraphRAG integration has shown important benefits over all-vector-based retrieval of multi-document reasoning problems with complicated models, and more to the point on the type of query that involves understanding of entity networks and multi-hop reasoning. The additional engineering intricacy and calculation charge of the knowledge graph building and preservation are compensated by the 23% to 46% accuracy constructions on several benchmarks. Second, multi-agent architectures successfully divide complex analysis activities such as resume evaluation into tasks, and the agents are making a calculable contribution. The results of the ablation experiment on the impacts of removing single agents are that overall system performance is affected and the architectural choice is worth the issue. Paralleled execution where possible by the dependencies of the dependencies coupled with the low coordination overhead has been maintained by efficient inter-agent communication protocols. Third, great-purpose models cannot compete with distinctively trained LLMs on special uses. Our domain-specific fine-tuning has achieved a range of tasks, 8–15% performance gain, compared to base performance. Fourth, T2S synthesis that is sensitive to schema has a production quality accuracy with sound safety checks. Our 94.2% success on complicated queries indicates that natural language database interfaces are now no longer immature to be utilized in practice and drastically lowers the barriers to the data access by non-technical users. Fifth, AI assistants can successfully automate most of literature review and synthesis procedures with human judgment potentially applicable in cumbersome interpretation and critical analysis. The 65%-time saves will enable the researchers to explore more questions space and prototype faster than normal which will change the process of research.

Some system architectural decisions were highly significant to success. With the diverse resource requirements of document processing, model inference and database operations the microservices architecture offers the benefit of scaling and deployment of individual component parts of the system. Quality of services can be isolated using separate databases and APIs to further encourage maintainability, as well as to optimize constituent parts. Both the PostgreSQL and the object storage are the hybrid storage architecture which adapts itself to the disparate access patterns or data attributes to the vector databases. A good database that can process structured transactional data and whose queries have a complex scope is the postgreSQL. Similarity search at scale Vector-based data which provides similarity search with low latency. Document storage is an effective means of storage of archived documents which are seldom used. The performance and cost effectiveness of such isolation of concerns is excellent compared to monolithic storage means. It is known that multi-layer caching is essential to acceptable performance. Caching, authentication token caching, and general results of lots of query’s caching can reduce downstream service load by 60.8%. The level 1 and 2 layers of the cache with a lower TTL of different types of data versus tuning in versus actual performance. The Triton and TensorRT optimized model of inference in the infrastructure cuts model latency by approximately 3 times compared to the naive PyTorch 34 inference. Dynamic batching is an algorithm used to combine the concurrently active requests and to employ the GPUs in a better manner without impacting the latency of the requests 36.

We proposed an integrated GenAI platform which represents a significant progress in integrating the LLMs to the enterprise knowledge workflow. We have presented a new retrieval system, which provides an explicit representation of the entity relationships, which leads to the complex reasoning tasks obtaining a 20–30% boost in using only our proposed system as opposed to just vectors.

The work contributes to the current literature of AI systems that may make positive contributions to the human knowledge work conducted with a well-thought design and implementation. While the proposed GraphRAG-driven GenAI platform demonstrates strong performance across multi-document reasoning, ATS evaluation, and hybrid retrieval, certain challenges remain. The system performance is influenced by the quality of knowledge graph construction and the scalability of multi-hop reasoning over large graph structures. Additionally, maintaining optimal latency under high concurrency and dynamic workloads requires further optimization. In our opinion, this work will capture the interest in further research and development of such a field as beneficence of AI in order can benefit human knowledge work. Future work will focus on adaptive retrieval mechanisms, efficient graph pruning strategies, and lightweight model optimization to improve scalability and response efficiency. Extending the framework to domain-specific knowledge graphs and incorporating continual learning mechanisms can further enhance system adaptability. These directions provide a foundation for advancing large-scale, RAG systems and can support ongoing research in GraphRAG-based architectures.

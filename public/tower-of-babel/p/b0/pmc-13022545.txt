MicroRNA networks in rice seeds: unveiling key regulators of development, germination, and rice resilience under climate changes
Authors: Alessandra Boccaccini, Mara Cucinotta, Michela Molinari, Raheleh Mirdar Mansuri, Laura De Gara, Sara Cimini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractClimate change threatens global food security by compromising the growth and development of major crops like rice, a staple food for over half of the world’s population. Ensuring sustainable agricultural production in increasingly challenging environments requires a comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms regulating plant stress responses. Given the central role of seed physiology in crop establishment and yield, investigating the key regulators of seed reproduction, development, and germination offers opportunities to enhance agricultural productivity and strengthen global food security. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as crucial post-transcriptional regulators, controlling fundamental developmental processes and orchestrating plant adaptation to environmental challenges. Recent studies highlight their role in rice seed development and germination, where they modulate gene expression in signaling pathways of hormones, including auxin and abscisic acid, as well as cell proliferation. Understanding these regulatory networks is essential for advancing rice seed biology, enhancing rice resilience, optimizing yield, and mitigating environmental stress. This review explores miRNA-mediated regulation of rice seed physiology, from reproduction to germination, focusing on the role of miRNAs in integrating environmental and hormonal signals. By highlighting the potential role of miRNAs in optimizing key agronomic traits, this review aims to support ongoing efforts to develop targeted strategies to improve crop adaptation to climate change.

Introduction

The acceleration of climate change poses a serious threat to ecosystem sustainability and food security in the 21st century. While the global population continues to grow, thus increasing food demand, agriculture faces severe pressure due to climate change, which alters the productivity of food crops (United Nations, 2019; Kumar et al., 2022).

Rice, as a staple food, is indispensable for maintaining both food and nutritional security in many countries (Mohidem et al., 2022). However, despite considerable gains in rice production over the past decade, the surge in consumption has outpaced improvements, resulting in a broadening gap between supply and demand (Rezvi et al., 2023). Abiotic stresses, such as extreme temperatures, flooding, drought, salinity, and nutrient imbalances, further threaten rice productivity and have emerged as major constraints on global rice production (Dhankher and Foyer, 2018; Fiaz et al., 2021; van Dijk et al., 2021). These environmental constraints are intensified by climate change, which increases the vulnerabilities of rice cultivation and highlights the urgent need for effective strategies aimed at mitigating the impacts of abiotic factors. Enhancing rice resilience to these stressors is crucial for ensuring long-term agricultural sustainability.

Plants have evolved a wide range of mechanisms to sense and respond to abiotic stresses, including physiological adjustments, metabolic shifts, and epigenetic modifications. These adaptive responses help to maintain cellular function and plant survival in adverse environments. Various post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms to maintain cellular homeostasis under abiotic stress have been described (Floris et al., 2009). Among these, the regulatory activities exerted by microRNAs (miRNAs) have garnered significant attention due to their role in modulating the expression of stress-responsive genes, such as those encoding MYB, NAC, and WRKY transcription factors. These small non-coding RNAs, typically 20–22 nucleotides in length, repress target gene expression through translational inhibition or mRNA cleavage (Voinnet, 2009; Zhang et al., 2022). Several miRNAs are known to influence a wide range of traits related to abiotic stress tolerance and agricultural productivity, many of which are linked to seed physiology, including seed yield and germination (Abbas et al., 2022).

Recent studies have revealed that miRNA activity is not only stress-specific but also strongly depends on the developmental stage at which plants experience stress (Pagano et al., 2021). Furthermore, miRNAs are involved in mediating hormonal crosstalk during stress responses, interacting with pathways governed by auxin, abscisic acid (ABA), and other key hormones that coordinate stress adaptation processes (Pagano et al., 2021). The regulatory influence of miRNAs on stress-related pathways positions miRNAs as a new promising target for improving tolerance to environmental fluctuations (Abbas et al., 2022).

In rice, despite significant advances in miRNA research, much remains to be uncovered regarding the full scope of miRNA–target interactions and their tissue-specific roles across different developmental stages. A deeper understanding of these regulatory networks will be essential for utilizing miRNAs in breeding programs aimed at enhancing stress tolerance in rice.

In this context, this review examines the role of miRNAs as key post-transcriptional regulators of gene expression in rice seeds across different developmental stages, including reproduction, seed development, and germination (Table 1). It focuses on their role in modulating genetic programs that govern seed formation, viability, vigor, and early seedling establishment, as well as on the hormonal pathways regulated by miRNAs in rice seeds.

Given the global importance of rice as a staple crop for human nutrition, understanding the regulatory functions of miRNAs in seed biology is essential. Their involvement in controlling gene expression under both optimal and stress conditions highlights their potential impact on crop performance and resilience. By elucidating these regulatory mechanisms, this study contributes to advancing strategies aimed at improving rice productivity and resilience, thereby addressing key challenges in global food security.

Small RNA-mediated regulation of reproductive development and stress responses in rice

Exploring miRNA-target modules in rice gametogenesis

Angiosperms have a life cycle that alternates between a gametophytic and a sporophytic generation. Sporophytes produce two types of spores, microspores and megaspores, which give rise to pollen grains and female gametophytes, respectively. The pollen grains develop in the anther and undergo microsporogenesis to form the sperm cells, while the ovule, housed within the ovary, undergoes megasporogenesis to form the female gametophyte. Detailed descriptions of ovule and pollen developmental stages have been provided in earlier studies (Raghavan, 1988; Lopez-Dee et al., 1999). The role of miRNAs in plant sexual reproduction has been recognized for approximately 20 years (Chen, 2004). Recent reviews (Petrella et al., 2021; You et al., 2022; Nie et al., 2023) have increasingly emphasized the importance of the small RNA (sRNA)-dependent silencing pathway in regulating reproductive processes in both model plants and crop species.

In rice, sexual reproduction begins with the transition from the vegetative to the reproductive phase, regulated by environmental factors such as day length and temperature, along with hormonal signals like gibberellins (GA). This transition triggers flowering and the development of reproductive structures through a sequential shift from the inflorescence meristem to the spikelet meristem and finally to the flower meristem, where essential floral organs are formed (Ikeda et al., 2004; Itoh et al., 2005). According to Ikeda et al. (2004), floral organ differentiation begins at stage 7 of inflorescence development and completes its maturation at stage 11, culminating in anthesis, fertilization, and subsequent seed development.

Several studies have explored miRNA expression during anther and ovule development in rice, identifying hundreds of known and novel miRNAs and their respective targets (Wu et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2021). For instance, a specific miRNA, miR5506, was found to significantly influence floral organ development and female gametophyte formation, and its potential target genes include REPRODUCTIVE MERISTEM (REM) transcription factors (Chen et al., 2021). Another miRNA differentially expressed during young inflorescence development is miR5488 (Chen et al., 2021), which targets AUXIN RESPONSIVE FACTOR 25 (ARF25), a transcription factor belonging to the ARF family, known regulators of the auxin signaling pathway. Overexpression of miR5488 leads to abnormal embryo sac development, thus affecting the seed-setting rate (Guo et al., 2023) (Fig. 1).

Similarly, Yang et al. (2017) and Liu et al. (2019) investigated the regulatory roles of miRNAs and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in rice female gametophyte abortion, comparing the miRNA transcriptome in ovules of a highly female-sterile line (fsv1) and a wild type line (Gui 99). Among them, miR5521, miR5179, and miR444d.3 target MADS98 and MADS57, belonging to MADS-box family, which have the potential to play a role in the formation of fertile female gametes (Yang et al., 2017).

miRNA-mediated stress responses in reproductive tissues

A further step forward has been made by studies that have sought to integrate miRNA regulatory networks in reproductive organs under conditions of drought, heat, or salinity stress. This is of great importance since drought stress hinders flowering and grain filling, while heat stress disrupts pollen viability and spikelet fertility, ultimately leading to reduced grain formation (Barnabás et al., 2008; Resentini et al., 2023).

Research on miRNA-mediated drought responses has identified a multi-tiered regulatory network in Nagina 22, a drought-tolerant rice genotype. sRNA sequencing of spikelet tissue identified a set of cultivar-specific drought-responsive miRNAs, including miR159f, miR1871, miR398b, miR408-3p, miR2878-5p, miR528-5p, and miR397a (Balyan et al., 2017). Among these, miR528 negatively regulates pollen fertility and influences seed-setting rate in rice. It has been demonstrated that miR528 targets the gene encoding UCLACYANIN-LIKE PROTEIN 23 (UCL23) involved in the deposition of pollen intine (Zhang et al., 2020) (Fig. 1).

More recently, Kumar et al. (2024) studied the role of miRNAs in drought-sensitive Swarna, a high-yielding but drought-susceptible rice variety. Drought stress was imposed before booting and during panicle emergence, revealing key miRNA–transcript modules under drought stress, including miR172d, miR169a, miR1876, miR171b/f, miR397a, and miR530.

Among these, miR172, which has been recently also associated with rice salt response (Cheng et al., 2021), is well known for controlling AP2-like transcription factors (Chen, 2004), regulating the transition to the reproductive stage and floral organ identity in Arabidopsis (Fig. 1). This function is conserved in both dicots and monocots (Zhu et al., 2009; Zhu and Helliwell, 2011). The miR172 acts in tandem with miR156 to coordinate key developmental transitions, including the shift from juvenile to adult phases and the timing of flowering. A wealth of genetic and molecular studies have shown that as plants age, miR156 levels gradually decline, leading to the de-repression of its direct targets, the SQUAMOSA PROMOTER-BINDING PROTEIN-LIKE (SPL) transcription factors. These SPLs, in turn, promote miR172 expression and activate downstream genes involved in flowering. This miR156–SPL–miR172 regulatory cascade is a cornerstone of our current understanding of flowering time control in Arabidopsis (Wu et al., 2009).

Studies suggest that miR156 is up-regulated in response to drought stress, potentially delaying developmental transitions. As the plant enters the reproductive phase, miR172 expression appears to be regulated by the miR156–SPL module, highlighting a coordinated interplay between these sRNAs in response to environmental cues and developmental timing (Giri et al., 2021). miR156 is also involved in seed development and dormancy, processes that will be discussed in the next paragraphs.

Another interesting miRNA that is expressed in panicles and associated with drought stress is miR530. In the Kasalath background, changes in miR530 resulted in lower seed yield per plant, fewer filled seeds per panicle, and smaller seeds (Li et al., 2021). Alteration of miR530 has also been shown to significantly affect seed yield in the Nipponbare background, by targeting OsPL3, which encodes a PLUS3 domain-containing protein (Sun et al., 2020). It was also demonstrated that miR530 is directly regulated by PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTOR-LIKE 15 (PIL15), which binds to G-box elements in the miR530 promoter (Sun et al., 2020). PIL15 belongs to the bHLH subfamily of transcription factors, which act as key regulators of development and stress responses in rice (Q. Li et al., 2022).

tasiRNA and phasiRNAs in reproductive stress adaptation

The trans-acting siRNA (tasiRNA) pathway, triggered by miR390, plays a central role in plant development and stress adaptation by regulating AUXIN RESPONSE FACTOR (ARF) genes. In this pathway, miR390 binds to TRANS-ACTING siRNA 3 (TAS3) precursor transcripts, which are stabilized by SUPPRESSOR OF GENE SILENCING 3 (SGS3) and converted into double-stranded RNA by RDR6, leading to phased tasiRNA production (Peragine et al., 2004). These tasiRNAs fine-tune auxin signaling, crucial for both organ patterning and reproductive development. In Arabidopsis, the role of the tasiRNA pathway, especially the TAS3–ARF module, has been thoroughly investigated in the context of female germline establishment, where it contributes to the spatial restriction of reproductive cell fate in ovules by modulating auxin distribution (Su et al., 2017).

In rice, recent work by Gu et al. (2023) revealed that the OsSGS3–tasiRNA–OsARF3 module orchestrates a critical trade-off between abiotic and biotic stress responses. The ossgs3a mutant displays a pronounced thermosensitive phenotype during female floret development under high temperatures, including floral abnormalities and reduced fertility, due to impaired tasiRNA accumulation and deregulated ARF3 expression. Interestingly, while OsSGS3a/b enhance thermotolerance through tasiRNA biogenesis, their loss results in increased resistance to bacterial and fungal pathogens, highlighting the role of SGS3 as a molecular switch between heat stress resilience and immune activation. Phased small interfering RNAs (phasiRNAs) are a class of plant-specific sRNAs derived from long RNA precursors that are first cleaved by specific 22 nt microRNAs. This cleavage triggers the synthesis of double-stranded RNA by RDR6, which is then processed by Dicer-like enzymes in a phased, regular pattern (Fei et al., 2013). In reproductive tissues, especially in monocots, 21 nt and 24 nt phasiRNAs are abundantly produced and have been implicated in male fertility and meiotic progression (Arikit et al., 2013; Zhai et al., 2015). More specifically, miR2118 and miR2275 are triggers of 21 nt and 24 nt reproductive phasiRNAs in rice (Song et al., 2012). Mutation of miR2118 leads to complete male and female sterility, confirming the important role for phasiRNAs in rice reproduction (Araki et al., 2020). Interestingly, this study also implicated specific Argonaute proteins, AGO1b/d, in this process, and mutation in AGO1 or in reproductive 21 nt phasiRNA-generating loci produces thermo-sensitive male sterility in rice (Shi et al., 2024). Thus, researchers are now exploring the potential of miR2118 manipulation to enhance heat tolerance in rice breeding programs.

tasiRNA and phasiRNA biosynthesis are strictly interconnected since they both depend on RDR6, which plays a crucial role in meiosis, as demonstrated by the Osrdr6-mei mutant described by Liu et al. (2020). This mutant exhibits complete male and female sterility due to severe meiotic defects, including impaired double-strand break formation, failure of homologous chromosome pairing, and arrest at the pachytene stage. These defects correlate with the loss in anthers of 21 nt reproductive phasiRNAs, which are required to regulate genes involved in meiosis.

Moreover, evidence suggests that the biogenesis of 24 nt phasiRNAs, which play a crucial role in chromatin modifications and transposon silencing, is particularly sensitive to elevated temperatures. Heat stress can disrupt their production, potentially leading to genome instability in developing pollen meiosis and reduced fertility (Fei et al., 2013). Moreover, emerging research indicates that 24 nt phasiRNAs may contribute to epigenetic heat stress memory, enabling plants to retain information about past heat exposure and potentially enhancing their resilience to future stress conditions (Molnar et al., 2010). Overall, phasiRNAs are becoming increasingly recognized as key regulatory elements in plant genomes, with multifaceted roles in stress adaptation, reproductive development, and epigenetic regulation (Hemenway and Gehring, 2023).

Molecular and environmental crosstalk in seed development

Seed development is a complex and highly regulated process comprising several stages, each crucial for the formation of a viable seed capable of germination. This process can be divided into two main phases: embryogenesis and seed maturation, which both contribute to final grain size and quality.

Environmental stress conditions may disrupt the finely tuned regulatory networks essential for seed development, thus negatively affecting crop performance. Hence, this section focuses on miRNAs that are primarily involved in seed development but also play roles in plant response to environmental stresses. By highlighting these dual-function miRNAs, we aim to underscore the strong interdependence and molecular crosstalk between developmental and stress-response pathways, reflecting the integrated nature of growth and defense programs in plants.

miRNA-dependent seed growth regulation and morphological traits

Some miRNAs modulate developmental regulators that control panicle architecture and spikelet morphology, which indirectly affect seed traits. The members of the miR396 family are general regulators of rice development targeting different GROWTH-REGULATING FACTORS (GRFs) and GRF-INTERACTING FACTORS (GIFs) genes to modulate spikelet and panicle morphology and leaf development (Omidbakhshfard et al., 2015). In particular, miR396 plays a crucial role in seed maturation in rice by regulating the expression of the transcription factor GRF4, which is encoded by the semi-dominant QTL for seed size and weight, GS2. The expression patterns of GRF4 and miR396 are complementary during panicle and seed development, indicating a precise temporal and spatial regulatory relationship (Duan et al., 2016). A 2 bp substitution mutation of GRF4 disrupts the miR396-mediated regulation, resulting in larger seeds with increased embryo and endosperm size, as well as significantly larger epidermal cells in the spikelet hull compared with wild type plants (Duan et al., 2016). Moreover, it has been shown that GRF4 promotes and integrates nitrogen assimilation and carbon fixation, to increase growth and photosynthesis (Li et al., 2018), offering potential targets for crop improvement. Notably, the miR396–GRFs module also directly influences the expression of miR408 (J. P. Zhang et al., 2017), a conserved miRNA known for balancing plant growth and stress tolerance. miR408 targets genes encoding blue copper proteins, such as UCLACYANIN-LIKE8 (UCL8) in the regulation of rice yield and photosynthesis (J. P. Zhang et al., 2017), as well as LACCASE (LAC) genes in crop response to drought (Hong et al., 2024). For its dual role in enhancing both yield and stress tolerance in different plant species (Balyan et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024), miR408 has been proposed as an optimal candidate for crop improvement.

Seed morphology encompasses both size and shape, the latter being regulated by specific miRNA–SPL networks, which control cell proliferation within the spikelet hulls by the transcriptional regulation of the transition from G1 to S phase of the cell cycle (Wang et al., 2012; Si et al., 2016). SPL13 and SPL16, which have been found to be associated with two QTLs that determine seed shape, are both targeted by miR156 (Xie et al., 2006; Wang et al., 2012; Si et al., 2016). Accordingly, CRISPR–Cas9 mutants of several members belonging to the mi156 family have longer seeds than wild type plants (Miao et al., 2019). Similarly to miR156, miR535 controls the expression of SPL7, SPL12, and SPL16 in the panicles, affecting length/width ratio and seed weight (Sun et al., 2019). Notably, miR535 expression is regulated by abiotic stress, such as drought and high salinity, and rice plants overexpressing miR535 have a reduced survival rate in the presence of drought stress (Yue et al., 2020), suggesting a dual role of miR535 in regulating seed development and the activation of tolerance programs to abiotic stress.

miRNA-regulated sugar metabolism during seed development

Grain filling is a critical phase of seed development because it contributes to the final seed size and quality, which are two important agronomic traits, determining the value of the cereal variety on the market. Grain filling consists of the accumulation of starch, along with a small portion of storage proteins, in the endosperm and it is controlled by a complex crosstalk between the seed and the mother plant (Ma et al., 2023). The starch accumulated in the endosperm derives, in fact, from the sugar produced during photosynthesis in the leaves of the mother plant. However, non-foliar photosynthetic tissues, including parts of the panicle, also contribute to grain filling, particularly during the later stages of seed development (Chen et al., 2020).

Transcriptomic analysis in developing inflorescence and young panicles led to the identification of the rice-specific miR5519 (Fei et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2021), whose expression is up-regulated by heat stress (Li et al., 2015). The overexpression of miR5519 causes smaller and lighter seeds, which is due to a reduced content of starch in the endosperm and a smaller spikelet size. This phenotype is accompanied by lower sugar levels in leaves, underlining the critical role of the maternal source strength in grain filling (Guo et al., 2024). In agreement with the role of miR5519 in regulating sugar metabolism in rice, a predicted target of miR5519 is RSUS2, encoding RICE SUCROSE SYNTHASE 2, whose overexpression enhances seed yield (Fan et al., 2019). However, further analyses will be needed to understand how the miR5519–RSUS2 module is involved in sugar metabolism, controlling seed development.

miRNA–hormonal crosstalk during seed development

Among the molecular factors contributing to the success of seed development, hormonal signaling is emerging as a central regulatory hub that integrates seed developmental cues with environmental signals. In this context, miRNAs have been shown to fine-tune hormonal signaling pathways, acting as key post-transcriptional regulators that coordinate hormone homeostasis and responsiveness during seed development.

Recently, auxin, a major hormone in reproductive development, has emerged as a crucial factor for endosperm formation and grain filling, affecting both sugar and protein content, and consequently, seed size (Wu et al., 2024). Two miRNA families, miR160 and miR167, known to modulate auxin signaling through the regulation of ARFs in many plant development processes (Mallory et al., 2005; Wu et al., 2006; Gutierrez et al., 2009; Yao et al., 2019; Cai et al., 2025), play also an important roles during the rice grain filling stage (Fig. 2A). In particular, the miR160-resistant version of ARF18 causes smaller seeds because of a reduced accumulation of starch in the endosperm (Huang et al., 2016). Conversely, suppression of miR167 activity using target mimicry and short tandem target mimic (STTM) technologies leads to increased seed size and weight by enhancing the grain filling rate (Zhao et al., 2023). This occurs because miR167 down-regulates ARF12, whose expression positively correlates with seed weight. Among the lines overexpressing different ARFs, the ones overexpressing ARF12 showed the most robust and consistent increase in seed weight. This supports a pivotal role for ARF12 in the stage of grain filling, which seems to be carried out by the promotion of CYCLIN-DEPENDENT KINASE F;2 (CDKF;2) expression. Further genetic analyses confirmed that the activity of miR167 on ARF12 causes the increase of the parenchyma cell numbers in spikelet hulls, by modulating CDKF;2 expression (Zhao et al., 2023). Overall, these data proposes the miR167 family as a negative regulator of seed size. However, Qiao et al. (2021) showed that the overexpression of miR167a increased seed length because of the down-regulation of ARF6. In turn, ARF6 activates the expression of the auxin carrier protein AUX3, which drives the redistribution of auxin, determining the longitudinal expansion of the epidermal cells of the glume and, hence, of the developing seed (Qiao et al., 2021). The apparent contradiction of the results obtained by the down-regulation of the whole miR167 family and the overexpression of miR167a could be because of the diverse function and tissue expression pattern during the seed development of the miR167 family members, as well as of the targeted ARF transcription factors. Moreover, miR167 members could have divergent targets besides the ARF genes, controlling different molecular pathways at specific seed developmental stages and in distinct tissues. Hence, a deeper analysis focuses on specific miR167-ARFs pairs, as well as a characterization of other potential miR167 targets that are necessary to unravel the miR167-mediated molecular mechanism controlling seed maturation.

Besides the role of miR160 and miR167 families in seed development, several members of both these families were found deregulated in rice seedlings exposed to abiotic stresses (Nguyen et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2024), for examples high salinity and drought, suggesting a potential role for these miRNAs in orchestrating plant development also under environmental stress.

Another miRNA implicated in modulating seed development through hormonal pathways, and also modulated under stress conditions, is miR1432 (Fig. 2A). The expression of this miRNA increases at the later stages of seed development and is associated with a reduced grain filling rate. The expression of miR1432 increases at the last stages of seed development (30–35 d after fertilization) and negatively correlates with the filling rate, which is higher 10–15 d after fertilization (Peng et al., 2014; Zhao et al., 2019). The selective down-regulation of miR1432 by STTM in endosperm causes larger and heavier seeds than wild type, while overexpressing lines show an opposite behavior, suggesting a negative role for miR1432 in regulating rice seed size. The miR1432-mediated regulation of seed size occurs by the down-regulation of the target gene ACOT, encoding an Acyl-CoA thioesterase, whose overexpression phenocopies the seed with reduced levels of miR1432 (Zhao et al., 2019). Transcriptomic data showed that the down-regulation of miR1432 or the overexpression of its target, ACOT, affects the expression of genes of the auxin and ABA biosynthetic and signaling pathways, causing an increase in auxin and ABA levels in the seeds (Zhao et al., 2019).

Besides auxin and ABA, other phytohormones are also integrated into the miRNA-mediated regulation of seed development. In particular, brassinosteroids (BR) and GA play a pivotal role in controlling seed size by modulating cell division and elongation (Boccaccini et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024). Several miRNAs have been identified as key regulators of these hormonal pathways, contributing to the fine-tuning of grain filling and maturation under both optimal and stress conditions.

miR159 is a positive regulator of seed size in rice by targeting the transcript encoding the transcription factor GAMYBL2, which participates in the signaling pathways of BR and GA, as well as in the auxin pathway, by regulating the expression of the already mentioned miR167 (Zhao et al., 2017; Gao et al., 2018) (Fig. 2A). The miR159–GAMYBL2 responds to variations of BR levels to repress the expression of BRASSINOSTEROID UPREGULATED 1 (BU1), a BR-responsive gene considered a positive regulator of seed size (Tanaka et al., 2009), and ENT-COPALYL DIPHOSPHATE SYNTHASE 1 (CPS1) and GA3 BETA-HYDROXYLASE2 (GA3ox2), which encode enzymes of the GA biosynthesis pathway. The involvement of miR159 in regulating seed size has also been shown in other plant cereals, such as maize (Wang et al., 2023).

Another miRNA involved in the BR-mediated regulation of seed size is miR397 (Zhang et al., 2013). MiR397 plays a crucial role in rice development and maturation by targeting LAC genes, including LAC13 and LAC3 (Kumar et al., 2024). miR397 is predominantly expressed in young panicles and seeds, with expression levels declining during post-embryonic development. Overexpression of miR397 in rice has been shown to enhance panicle branching, resulting in an increased number of seeds per panicle and larger seeds. In japonica rice, transgenic lines overexpressing miR397 exhibit a 13.4% increase in 1000-seed weight, with improvements in seed length, width, and thickness compared with wild type plants. In contrast, overexpression of its target gene, LAC13, results in reduced panicle branching and lower seed yield, highlighting the antagonistic effects between miR397 and LAC13 on yield-related traits (Zhang et al., 2013; Zhong et al., 2020). Recently, Yu et al. (2024) showed that LAC protein interacts with TRANSTHYRETIN-LIKE (TTL) protein to prevent their turnover. In turn, TTL negatively regulates BR signaling pathway through the binding with the BRASSINOSTEROID INSENSITIVE1 (BRI1) receptor (Yu et al., 2024). Interestingly, the effects of miR397 appear to be conserved across species, as overexpression of miR397 in Arabidopsis also leads to an increase in seed size, indicating an evolutionarily conserved mechanism across monocots and dicots (Wang et al., 2014). Beyond its role in yield, miR397 is also involved in the plant response to abiotic stress; in fact it has been found deregulated in response to drought, high salinity, and high temperature (Zhou et al., 2010; Jeong et al., 2011), suggesting that miR397 plays a dual role in both developmental regulation and stress resilience.

miRNA-mediated control of redox homeostasis in developing seeds

One of the most common effects of exposure to environmental stress conditions is the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which results in oxidative stress. This oxidative imbalance can disrupt cellular homeostasis and impair vital physiological processes. Therefore, the ability to maintain a proper redox balance is crucial not only for mounting effective responses to stress but also for supporting energy-intensive developmental processes. Among these, seed development represents a particularly demanding phase, during which a tightly regulated redox homeostasis is essential to ensure cellular integrity, metabolic coordination, and successful progression of developmental programs.

A critical component of stress-linked seed quality control is miR398. miR398 plays a role in balancing seed development and stress responses by regulating genes that encode COPPER/ZINC SUPEROXIDE DISMUTASES (CSDs), such as CSD1 and CSD2, as well as COPPER CHAPERONES LIKE (CCS). This regulation helps in maintaining cellular redox homeostasis, especially during grain filling and maturation, which is a highly demanding metabolic process that increases ROS accumulation. To manage this oxidative stress, the miR398-mediated regulation of CSD enzymes allows ROS scavenging, securing a proper seed development (J. Li et al., 2022). In addition to its role in oxidative stress regulation, miR398 influences copper homeostasis by modulating the expression of copper-binding proteins, which affects copper distribution (J. Li et al., 2022). The importance of miR398 in seed development is indicated by genetic analyses showing that the down-regulation of miR398 by STTM leads to a significant decrease in seed length and width, resulting in a reduction in seed weight (H. Zhang et al., 2017).

miRNA-mediated regulation of seed dormancy

Seed dormancy, a crucial adaptive trait in plants, is the inherent ability of mature seeds to temporarily suspend germination even under favorable environmental conditions (Sajeev et al., 2024). This mechanism is vital for plants to synchronize their germination with optimal growth conditions, ensuring the survival and successful establishment of the next generation.

Dormancy is regulated by environmental cues and intrinsic factors. Abiotic stresses play a crucial role in the onset, maintenance, and breaking of seed dormancy. High ABA and low GA levels, often induced by abiotic stresses, promote dormancy to ensure seeds only germinate when conditions are adequate for seedling development (Shu et al., 2018). Seed dormancy is established during seed maturation, the phase following zygotic embryogenesis, and the embryo plays a more significant role in seed dormancy than the endosperm, which primarily functions in providing nutrients and maintaining seed shape and quality.

In a study on Oryza sativa cv. Nipponbare, embryos and endosperms were sampled at 30, 45, and 60 d after heading (DAH) to investigate seed dormancy. The highest number of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and miRNAs was observed between 30 and 60 DAH, suggesting that gradual molecular changes during this period play a key role in dormancy release. Hormone-related DEGs were most enriched in the embryo, energy and stress-related DEGs in the endosperm (Park et al., 2024).

Moreover, the study highlights, among the differentially expressed miRNAs, two potential miRNA-target modules implicated in seed dormancy regulation: miR531b–AMINOCYCLOPROPANE-1-CARBOXYLIC ACID OXIDASE 4 (ACO4) and miR390-5p–MULTIDRUG AND TOXIC COMPOUND EXTRUSION PROTEIN 19 (MATE19). MiR531b targets ACO4, an ethylene biosynthesis gene. The study suggests that the down-regulation of miR531b at 60 DAH may contribute to the increased expression of ACO4, potentially promoting ethylene biosynthesis and reducing ABA sensitivity, leading to increased pre-harvest sprouting (PHS) (Park et al., 2024). It is noteworthy that miR531b is a drought-responding miRNA in rice seedlings, suggesting that this miRNA could play a role in ABA and ethylene pathways, also to regulate stress response (Kumar et al., 2023).

MiR390-5p potentially targets MATE19, encoding an ABA exporter. Increased miR390-5p levels in the embryo might contribute to the down-regulation of MATE19, affecting ABA transport and potentially influencing dormancy levels in relation to abiotic stresses (Park et al., 2024).

The inability to control seed dormancy before harvest can lead to PHS, which is detrimental to crop yield and quality, especially in the face of changing climates (Tai et al., 2021). Park and co-authors investigated the roles of genes and miRNAs in affecting PHS in rice by comparing two japonica rice accessions with contrasting PHS susceptibilities: Gopum (PHS susceptible) and Jowoon (PHS resistant) (Park et al., 2021). The study identified two candidate miRNAs, miR5827 and miR1862h, with higher expression levels in Jowoon compared with Gopum at 30 DAH. The high expression of these miRNAs, particularly miR1862h, might be associated with the lower PHS rate observed in Jowoon (Park et al., 2021).

A fundamental role in seed dormancy and PHS is played by Group II miR156 whose function was experimentally validated by CRISPR–Cas9 approach. Indeed, mutations in this specific subfamily of miR156 genes (MIR156a–MIR156c, MIR156k, and MIR156l), significantly enhanced seed dormancy and suppressed PHS without compromising seed yield (Miao et al., 2019). This effect is mediated through the suppression of the GA pathway via the up-regulation of the miR156 target gene IDEAL PLANT ARCHITECTURE 1 (IPA1), which directly regulates multiple genes involved in GA biosynthesis, signaling, and deactivation. This suggests that targeting Group II MIR156 genes could be an effective strategy for developing PHS-resistant rice varieties also in suboptimal environmental conditions (Miao et al., 2019).

miRNA controlling seed vigor and longevity

Seed vigor is an important agronomic trait that refers to the capacity of the seed to germinate and establish strong and healthy seedlings, also under unfavorable conditions. It reaches its peak in the late stages of maturation, and it declines during prolonged storage due to aging processes that affect seed longevity (Rajjou et al., 2012). Seed vigor is crucial for sustainable and profitable agriculture, especially in environments in which seeds face stress during germination and early growth. Moreover, the development of varieties that preserve their vitality over a long period of seed storage, also under unfavorable conditions, is a key point for the preservation of the germplasms and ensuring food security.

Transcriptome data in artificially aged seeds identified miR168a and miR164c as potential regulators of seed longevity (Zhou et al., 2020). Functional analyses of rice transgenic lines with a reduced or enhanced expression of miR164c indicated a negative role for this miRNA in regulating longevity of artificially aged seeds (Y. Zhou et al., 2020; S. Zhou et al., 2022). Targets of miR164 belong to the NAM, ATAF1/2, and CUC2 (NAC) gene family, which encodes the largest transcription factor family in plants, that participate in many plant growth processes, including leaf development, seed germination, and stress responses (Wang et al., 2021; Fuertes-Aguilar and Matilla, 2024). In rice, the miR164 family targets the coding sequence of the ORYZA MIR164-TARGETED NAC (OMTN) genes, downstream of their NAC domain, to regulate the plant response to drought at the reproductive stage (Fang et al., 2014). Among these, OMTN2 appears to regulate rice seed longevity downstream of miR164c, since its expression declines during seed aging as a consequence of the increase of miR164c expression (Zhou et al., 2022). Another miR164c target in aged seeds is the PHYTOSULFOKINE 5 (PSK5) gene (Huang et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2022), encoding a poorly studied phytosulfokine precursor that has been shown to regulate plant growth in response to drought (Stührwohldt et al., 2021). Taken together, these results suggest that miR164 mediates plant responses to multiple stress conditions by regulating different genes depending on the developmental stage.

In contrast, miR168a seems to play a role in preserving the capacity of seeds to germinate after a long period of storage. miR168a expression declines during artificial aging, while the expression of the two target genes, AGO1 and PEPTIDE TRANSPORTER 2 (PTR2), encoding a nitrate transporter, increases (Zhou et al., 2020). The positive role of miR168a in seed longevity was further confirmed by functional analyses showing that seeds overexpressing this miRNA have a better germination rate than wild type seeds undergoing artificial aging. However, further analyses will be needed to fully elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the aging processes regulated by miR168a and miR164a.

miRNA-target modules in rice germination under stress conditions

Seed germination marks the transition from seed to seedling stage and ensures the establishment of new seedlings for the next generation. This process is shaped by a dynamic interaction between the embryo and the endosperm. The endosperm acts as an environmental sensor, regulating embryo growth, while the embryo orchestrates endosperm degradation to mobilize the stored nutrients. Germination starts with the uptake of water by the quiescent dry seed, and it is considered complete when the radicle emerges from the seed coat (Chahtane et al., 2017). Rice seeds typically germinate within 2 d upon water imbibition in the presence of a temperature range between 21 °C and 36 °C. Extreme environmental conditions such as heat, flooding, drought, and nutrient deficiencies pose significant challenges to rice germination and early seedling growth (Jeyasri et al., 2021; Reed et al., 2022).

The role of miRNAs in regulating rice germination under stress conditions has been increasingly characterized, particularly in response to flooding and drought, two opposing extreme events associated with climate change that significantly impact seed imbibition (Bailey-Serres et al., 2012). Unlike other cereals, rice can germinate under flooded conditions, because it manages starch degradation even in the absence of oxygen, which is instead typically necessary for the GA-mediated α-amylase activity (Gómez-Álvarez and Pucciariello, 2022). Hence, in submerged conditions characterized by low oxygen, the coleoptile emerges rapidly and elongates searching for the water surface, followed by root emergence. In contrast, under aerobic conditions, root emergence precedes coleoptile elongation, as observed in most other cereal species. However, prolonged submergence, even in rice, should be recognized as a stress factor that affects the rooting process. miR393a and miR167 have been identified as regulators of seedling establishment depending on water and oxygen availability, by targeting different regulators of the auxin signaling pathway (Fig. 2B).

MiR393a affects auxin signaling, targeting the transcripts for auxin receptors TRANSPORT INHIBITOR RESPONSE 1 (TIR1) and its paralogue AUXIN SIGNALING F-BOX2 (AFB2) (Bian et al., 2012; Xia et al., 2012; Lu et al., 2018). These receptors mediate the auxin-mediated degradation of the proteins Aux/IAA, which are the negative regulators of the ARF transcription factors and, hence, of auxin-mediated transcriptional regulation. During germination in normal oxygen conditions, miR393a promotes primary root elongation, down-regulating the expression of TIR1 and AFB2, while under submerged situations, miR393a is down-regulated in the growing coleoptile to allow the expression of its targets. Moreover, miR393a is up-regulated by ABA to repress auxin-mediated lateral root formation depending on the environmental conditions (Belin et al., 2009; Guo et al., 2016; Lorrai et al., 2018). And, in this ABA and auxin crosstalk, miR393s act synergistically with the already mentioned miR390 (Lu et al., 2018), which controls auxin signaling in developing roots by affecting the expression of TAS3 (Lu et al., 2018) (Fig. 2B).

Submergence conditions also repress miR167, to allow the expression of the ARF6, ARF12, ARF17, and ARF25 genes and, indirectly, the transcript of the auxin conjugation enzyme GRETCHEN HAGEN3-type (GH3) (Lee et al., 2023). This regulation is central for maintaining the optimal concentration of free auxin during seedling establishment upon germination.

The opposite of submergence conditions, drought represents another pressing challenge for rice growth and productivity, which demands immediate attention in the current climate change era (Farooq et al., 2009). Water availability controls the imbibition phase, during which water uptake is needed to reactivate metabolic pathways and allow germination. Drought limits rice seed imbibition and is reported to impair the synthesis of endogenous plant hormones and the balance of ROS, ultimately reducing the plant capacity for development and growth (Yang et al., 2022).

Among the miRNAs involved in the rice response to drought stress during germination and seedling growth, recent studies have shown that the already mentioned miR1432 affects rice germination under drought conditions by targeting the CALMODULIN-LIKE2 (CaML2) transcript (Lin et al., 2021). CaML2 encodes a protein belonging to the family of calcium-binding messenger proteins that play a critical role in various processes by translating calcium signals into cellular responses. Both mutation (CRISPR) and suppression (STTM) of miR1432 significantly impair seed germination and seedling growth under drought-stress conditions. Instead, miR1432 overexpressing lines showed no significant changes in terms of tolerance to drought stress compared with wild type lines (Luo et al., 2024).

Transcriptome analyses showed that the suppression of miR1432 specifically affects the expression of genes involved in the MITOGEN-ACTIVATED PROTEIN KINASE KINASE (MAPKK) pathway. The mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascade is a major signal transducer and plays an essential role in regulating plant drought stress responses via crosstalk with hormone signaling pathways (Lin et al., 2021). It is known that miR1432 serves as an upstream regulator of the MAPK cascade by suppressing the expression of CaML2. However, the direct targets of CaML2 remain unknown (Luo et al., 2024).

Another miRNA recognized for its role in rice germination under adverse environmental conditions is miR529. Studies indicate that miR529 is able to avoid the accumulation of ROS in germinating seeds, helping the plants to manage and mitigate the damaging effects in seeds induced by ROS under stress conditions (Yue et al., 2017a). This is particularly relevant in this phase of plant development, since redox metabolites with antioxidant properties, such as ascorbate and glutathione, are present in very low amounts at the beginning of seed germination (De Gara et al., 1997). miR529a achieves this by down-regulating the expression of SPL genes (Yue et al., 2017a). Among SPL, SPL2 and SPL14 are the genes down-regulated by miR529a to mediate the response to oxidative stress. The enhanced efficiency of germination and early seedling development under oxidative stress of plants overexpressing miR529a is associated with the repression of its targets, which leads to an increase of ROS-responsive genes like superoxide dismutase and peroxidase (Yue et al., 2017b). This mechanism is mediated by the expression of H2O2-responsive genes, including the nucleoporin CONSTITUTIVE EXPRESSER OF PATHOGENESIS RELATED GENES 5, a mitochondrial import inner membrane translocase (Os10g0519700), and VACUOLAR PROCESSING ENZYME 3. The up-regulation of these genes occurs due to the repression of miR529a target genes. This condition may, in turn, trigger the increased expression of DELTA1-PYRROLINE-5-CARBOXYLATE SYNTHETASE and PEROXIDASE 59. As a result, superoxide dismutase and peroxidase enzyme activity increase, ultimately enhancing the plant tolerance to oxidative stress (Yue et al., 2017b).

Emerging trends and future directions

This review underscores significant progress in understanding the role of sRNAs, particularly miRNAs, in regulating rice seed reproduction, development, and germination, especially under abiotic stress. It emphasizes a critical evolution within the field of molecular plant research, transitioning from the initial discovery of miRNAs to a more advanced exploration of their functional roles and intricate regulatory networks and their potential applications in improving crop performance. Given the recognition of miRNAs as central regulators in environmental stress responses as well as in plant development, this transition represents a significant milestone for harnessing miRNA biology to address the dual challenge of enhancing stress tolerance while maintaining or improving seed quality. Since the discovery of plant miRNAs in 2002, research has primarily focused on identifying both conserved and novel miRNAs and studying their expression patterns also thanks to the development of next-generation deep sequencing technology (Fig. 3). Numerous studies report miRNA expression data in rice roots or leaves under adverse conditions such as drought, salinity stress, or pathogen attacks (Zhou et al., 2010; Balyan et al., 2017; Xia et al., 2020; Nguyen et al., 2023). A promising research direction is to further explore this area to identify miRNAs that could serve as early biomarkers of plant stress. Indeed, while the use of miRNAs as biomarkers is more established in the animal kingdom (Chakrabortty et al., 2023), it can also be applied to rice and other crops to detect early stress indicators at critical phenological stages, ultimately contributing to productivity improvement. In this context, miRNAs can be used as objective indicators of a plant’s cellular state in response to abiotic and biotic stress factors, and they can be used in crop breeding and engineering to produce stress-tolerant crop species. For instance, Parmar et al. (2020) identified and characterized salt-responsive miRNAs in both susceptible and tolerant rice varieties, providing insights into miRNA-mediated regulatory networks that can inform breeding strategies for enhanced salinity tolerance. They observed that the up-regulation of specific miRNAs enhanced salinity tolerance by inducing the activity of some transcription factors, such as NAC and AP2/EREBP and L-ascorbate oxidase, thus suggesting possible biomarkers for rice breeding.

Emerging trends and future directions for miRNAs. To improve the knowledge of miRNA-based regulatory mechanisms, a multidisciplinary approach is necessary, and it involves several computational and functional analyses. These include advanced bioinformatics tools for target prediction, high-throughput sequencing for expression profiling, and gene editing techniques for functional validation. Integrating these approaches will enhance our understanding of miRNA roles in plant stress responses and development, paving the way for novel biotechnological applications. GO, gene ontology; NGS, next-generation sequencing.

In recent years, spatial transcriptomic techniques have been increasingly adapted to study miRNAs by combining spatial resolution with sRNA detection. While originally developed for mRNAs, both sequencing-based methods like spatial smRNA-seq, padlock probe-based in situ sequencing, and image-based techniques like multiplexed fluorescence in situ hybridization now allow detection of miRNAs in their native tissue context. These approaches are well established in animal systems, where they have revealed spatial patterns of miRNA expression in development and disease (Robles-Remacho et al., 2025). Although they are not yet used in plants, their application is rapidly emerging and holds strong potential to uncover cell-specific miRNA functions in processes such as organogenesis, floral transition, and stress responses. The integration of spatial transcriptomic approaches for miRNA analysis in plants may provide unprecedented insights into the spatiotemporal dynamics of stress-responsive miRNAs, ultimately enabling the identification of cell-type-specific regulatory modules that can be harnessed to guide targeted breeding strategies for improved stress resilience.

In parallel, the growing understanding of miRNA biology has been greatly enhanced by advancements in computational tools and bioinformatic approaches (Fig. 3). Increasingly sophisticated algorithms for target prediction, such as TargetFinder (Bo and Wang, 2005), psRNATarget (Dai et al., 2018), and TAPIR (Bonnet et al., 2010), have been developed. These tools employ diverse algorithms to identify potential miRNA–mRNA interactions, providing complementary insights when used in combination. A multi-tool approach integrating predictions from four different algorithms was applied in a recent study, demonstrating enhanced prediction accuracy (Suddal et al., 2024). Accurate miRNA target prediction is of fundamental importance, as experimental validation remains a critical yet complex, time-consuming, and labor-intensive process. The more precise the preliminary predictions, the more efficiently miRNA targets can be identified, facilitating a deeper understanding of the regulatory pathways in which they are involved. Recently, machine learning-based algorithms have been introduced alongside classical software tools to further enhance the accuracy of miRNA target prediction. As an example, RFMiRTarget (Mendoza et al., 2013), MultiMiTar (Mitra and Bandyopadhyay, 2011), NBmiRTar (Yousef et al., 2007), MiRAW (Pla et al., 2018), DeepMiRTar (Wen et al., 2018), and MiRDTL (Cheng et al., 2016) have emerged as promising strategies to refine predictions by incorporating complex biological datasets and improving reliability (Fig. 3). Following the application of bioinformatics tools for target prediction, molecular techniques such as degradome sequencing and RNA immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing (RIP-seq) can experimentally validate predicted miRNA–mRNA target interactions (Fig. 3). Degradome sequencing captures and sequences the cleaved products of miRNA-guided mRNA degradation, enabling the identification of direct targets. RIP-seq, on the other hand, isolates RNA molecules bound to AGO proteins, key components of the RNA-induced silencing complex, allowing the detection of miRNA–mRNA associations in vivo.

Nevertheless, much of the current research is descriptive and lacks mechanistic insights. To make meaningful progress, functional approaches are essential for elucidating the specific roles of miRNAs and the cellular pathways in which they are involved. These studies are crucial, for example, in understanding how miRNAs regulate reproduction, seed dormancy and germination, as well as rice responses to abiotic stress, providing insights into the underlying molecular mechanisms and potential applications for crop improvement. Recently, Hong et al. (2024) overexpressed three copper-miRNAs (Cu-miRNAs; miR397, miR408, and miR528) in three major food crops, rice, maize, and wheat. Cu-miRNAs are a subclass of plant miRNAs whose expression is regulated by copper (Cu) availability. They primarily function in response to Cu deficiency, helping the plant to remodel gene expression and maintain Cu homeostasis (Pilon, 2017). In that study, the overexpression of the Cu-miRNAs simultaneously silenced several target laccase genes, resulting in reduced lignin content, increased flavonoid metabolites, and an overall higher abiotic stress tolerance.

In this context, genome editing through CRISPR and CRISPR–Cas is increasingly recognized as a tool not only for trait improvement but also for functional genomics studies (Fig. 3). Among CRISPR-based systems, Cas9 and Cas12a (also known as Cpf1; Zetsche et al., 2015) have been the most extensively utilized with their applications being the most advanced. While CRISPR–Cas9 remains the predominant tool for plant genome modification, CRISPR–Cas12a is gaining traction for its flexibility, features, and broader range of applications. These include the use of a small guide RNA, the ability to target T-rich genomic regions unsuitable for Cas9, intrinsic RNA processing that facilitates multiplexing, and the generation of double-strand breaks with staggered ends (Bandyopadhyay et al., 2020). Cas12a-mediated genome editing has been successfully implemented in rice with efficient production of monoallelic (Begemann et al., 2017) and biallelic (Li et al., 2020) gene targeting. A recent study in rice provides a clear example of how CRISPR technology can be used to directly target microRNA genes in plants. Using the Cas12a system, Zheng et al. (2025) knocked out nine OsMIRNA loci with distinct spatiotemporal expression patterns. The method achieved editing efficiencies of up to 100% and generated large deletions, likely resulting in complete loss-of-function alleles. Transcriptome and phenotypic analyses of the edited lines revealed roles for these miRNAs in regulating seed development and grain quality, highlighting the value of CRISPR tools for functional studies of post-transcriptional regulation in crops. However, research is ongoing to improve its efficiency in plants, including identifying new variants of Cas12 or engineered Cas12 variants with flexible PAM sequences.

It is important to keep in mind that all these approaches, whether aimed at understanding functional mechanisms or identifying potential applications, often consider miRNAs as isolated molecules. However, recent findings emphasize the need to investigate not only how miRNAs regulate their mRNA targets but also the broader regulatory networks in which they operate, including interactions with other non-coding RNAs such as lncRNAs and circular RNAs, and with shorter peptides, called miPEPs (Chu et al., 2020; Babaei et al., 2022). LncRNAs can act as endogenous target mimics of miRNAs, thereby inhibiting miRNA activity (Meng et al., 2021). The first identified endogenous target mimic was observed in Arabidopsis, where a lncRNA binds to complementary sequences of miR399, preventing its function and consequently promoting the accumulation of PHOSPHATE 2, a well-known miR399 target (Franco-Zorrilla et al., 2007). In rice, the role of lncRNAs as miRNA mimics remains poorly explored, especially in the plant adaptation field, and warrants further investigation due to its potential impact on plant development and productivity in the context of climate change (Cho and Paszkowski, 2017; Meng et al., 2021). Moreover, considering the complexity of miRNA interaction networks, the development of comprehensive miRNA interaction maps offers valuable insights into their role in regulating gene expression in rice under environmental conditions (Fig. 3).

Finally, integrative approaches that combine the whole set of molecular interactions and data from transcriptomics, sRNA sequencing, proteomics, and metabolomics will allow researchers to construct detailed models of miRNA-mediated regulation, providing a holistic understanding of its functions. As highlighted in previous studies, such an approach holds immense potential for enhancing rice productivity by providing crucial insights into seed reproduction, development, and germination, while also improving stress resilience, thereby contributing to global food security (Fig. 3). For example, an integrated transcriptomics and miRNAomics approach has recently been proposed to investigate the regulatory networks associated with coleoptile senescence in rice. This study generated a network by integrating the high-confidence miRNA–RNA target pairs with the protein–protein interaction dataset. A distinguished hub of miR164a/d targeting copper-containing proteins, laccases, and plastocyanin, indicated that copper and ROS homeostasis regulate coleoptile senescence in rice. Overall, the study reports a comprehensive atlas of the expression dynamics of genes, miRNA regulators, and their interactions and provides insights into the fundamental pathways regulating coleoptile senescence (Sasi et al., 2022).

By advancing and integrating these approaches within the field of miRNA research, it is possible to move beyond fundamental discoveries to elucidate the complex roles of miRNAs in seed biology. This knowledge holds substantial potential for enhancing crop productivity, improving stress resilience, and contributing to global food security.

Conclusion

Elucidating the role of miRNAs in rice germination, seed development, and reproduction is essential for understanding the intricate regulatory networks that control these processes. As key post-transcriptional regulators, miRNAs modulate gene expression in response to developmental cues and environmental stimuli, influencing seed viability, dormancy, and early seedling establishment.

In the context of climate change, where rising temperatures, water scarcity, and soil salinity increasingly threaten crop productivity, miRNAs emerge as critical components of stress adaptation mechanisms. Their ability to fine-tune gene expression enables plants to optimize resource allocation, enhance stress resilience, and maintain reproductive success under adverse conditions.

Future research integrating multi-omics approaches, including transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and spatial transcriptomics, will be instrumental in constructing comprehensive models of miRNA-mediated regulation. Improving the knowledge of miRNA-based regulatory mechanisms offers promising avenues for the development of climate-resilient rice varieties. Thanks to this knowledge, it may be possible to enhance seed quality, improve germination rates under suboptimal conditions, and secure stable yields, contributing to global food security in an era of unprecedented environmental challenges.

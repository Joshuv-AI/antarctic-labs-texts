Andy Gump deformity: a rare benign jaw tumor sequela
Authors: Atanwo Nita Lise Dongmo, Zilefac Brian Ngokwe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importance:Andy Gump deformity, rarely associated with benign jaw tumors, is characterized by an almost absent lower lip and chin. It is mainly linked to malignant jaw tumors or trauma.Case presentation:In this case report, we present a 24-year-old female presenting with an Andy Gump deformity as a sequelae from a benign jaw tumor.Clinical discussion:The association of a late clinical presentation and an aggressive benign jaw tumor led to the benign jaw tumor sequela.Conclusion:Early diagnosis of benign jaw tumors will lead to early and better management, and hence, fewer sequelae, especially in resource-limited settings.

Odontogenic benign tumors, including ameloblastomas, have been reported to be more frequent in the mandible than in the maxilla[1]. These benign jaw tumors are usually asymptomatic, can be discovered fortuitously in the early stages, and are rarely associated with complications such as Andy Gump deformity, motivating this case presentation.

A 24-year-old otherwise healthy female presented with a facial tumor sequela, having previously been operated on 8 years ago for a benign jaw tumor, specifically an ameloblastoma (Fig. 1). The advanced presentation of the tumor lesion at that time warranted a radical approach. Specifically, a mandibulectomy was carried out with disarticulation. No rehabilitation has been carried out to date, most likely due to financial limitations. The lack of surgical rehabilitation resulted in a backward placement of the lower jaw with respect to the upper, alongside an almost absent chin and lower lip, which is referred to as Andy Gump deformity. This deformity has significant aesthetic and functional repercussions for the patient. Andy Gump deformity is usually associated with malignant tumors[3] as well as trauma such as gunshot wounds[4] but very rarely with benign jaw tumors. Benign jaw tumors are insidious; however, in developing countries, relatively late presentations have been reported[5]. In addition, benign jaw tumors represent a wide array of lesions, with some being more aggressive than others[6] Ameloblastomas, despite being odontogenic benign jaw tumors, are locally invasive[7].

Hence, the association of late clinical presentations and aggressive benign jaw tumors could lead to a radical surgical approach and, hence, important sequelae such as Andy Gump deformity.

Discussion

The current mainstay treatment for ameloblastoma, a benign epithelial odontogenic tumor, is surgery, which often entails a safety bony margin following the tumor resection to help prevent recurrence of this aggressive lesion[7]. However, this leads to significant tissue loss, which is accentuated in late tumor presentation, leading to defects such as the Andy Gump deformity, an anterior mandibular defect associated with mandibular retrognathia and the appearance of an absent lower lip and chin[3].

Basic oral activities like elocution, mastication, and convection (breathing) are dependent on the mandible, the only movable bone in the skull other than the middle ear ossicles. Therefore, anterior mandibular defects entail a high morbidity rate because they hinder oral feeding and understandable speech[8], increase the risk of excessive drooling and a high risk of airway compromise[3] as well as the aesthetic effects, particularly in patients who are not receiving rehabilitation.

The therapeutic options for an Andy Gump deformity should cater to both the aesthetic and functional needs of the patient, and this treatment could either be immediate or delayed. The immediate reconstruction would require substantial preoperative preparation to clearly account for the dental implantation as well as the 3D remodeling.

One other therapeutic option could be to use a vascularized bony flap alongside a firm titanium surgical reconstruction plate. Indeed, several vascularized flaps can be used, including the fibular osteocutaneous flap, radial forearm osteocutaneous flap, scapular osteocutaneous flap, and the iliac crest osteocutaneous flap. The fibula flap, among these options, offers the highest bone volume with up to 25 cm bone length[7], which is important for significant defects like the Andy Gump, where it can serve as a good support for dental implantation, further improving patient function. This fibula flap also has a good blood supply from the peroneal vessels[7].

Conclusion

Early diagnosis of benign jaw tumors will lead to early and better management, and hence, fewer sequelae, especially in tropical settings with limited resources.

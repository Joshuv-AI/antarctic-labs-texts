Cicatricial Alopecia Over Scalp
Authors: Dharmesh Parmar, Rutoo Polra, Pragya A. Nair
Published: 2024

A 54-year-old male presented with complaints of loss of hair from his scalp for almost 2 years. The history of hair fall was gradual over a period of 2 years. No itching or pain over the lesions was present except for some discomfort and burning at times on sun exposure. The patient was hypertensive for 8 years and on medications. Routine investigations were normal. Liver function tests, renal function tests, thyroid stimulating hormone, and antinuclear antibody (ANA) profile were within normal range. Cutaneous examination showed a well-defined patch of cicatricial alopecia measuring around 7 × 4 cm in size with depigmentation in the center and erythema at the periphery affecting the frontal area up to the vertex of the scalp [Figure 1]. No other parts of the body were affected. Nails and oral cavity were normal.

A well-defined patch of cicatricial alopecia with depigmentation in the center and erythema at the periphery affecting the frontal area up to the vertex of the scalp

Dermoscopy (Dermalite DL 4) showed multiple arborizing vessels, a few scattered and a few arranged in groups, pink white background, fibrotic white dots, and absence of follicular opening [Figure 2]. Histopathology showed epidermal atrophy with vacuolar degeneration in the basal layer. Dense perivascular and periadnexal lymphocytic infiltration with focal lichenoid band-like infiltration of lymphocytes. Papillary dermal edema was seen [Figure 3]. Changes were suggestive of discoid lupus erythematosus.

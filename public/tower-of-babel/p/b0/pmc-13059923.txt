Intervention effects of optimised carbohydrate diet in patients with type 2 diabetes: study protocol for a randomised controlled crossover trial
Authors: Yuwei LU, Ruiqi Zhang, Jingyi Yang, Dan Liu, Qian Wu, Xiaoxue Long, Di Cheng, Jingyi Guo, Qian Li, Ying Zhang, Piao Kang, Qinyi Wang, Xiaojing Gao, Rong Zeng, Mingliang Zhang, Qichen Fang, Weiping Jia, Yueqiong Ni, Huating Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDietary intervention is fundamental for the management of type 2 diabetes (T2D), playing a crucial role in stabilising blood glucose levels and improving quality of life. As the major contributor to daily energy intake, the quality of carbohydrates can directly influence the glycaemic stability. Therefore, we aim to explore whether adjusting and optimising the composition of dietary carbohydrates, specifically starch, can provide multiple metabolic benefits for patients with T2D.Methods and analysisThis multicentre randomised crossover clinical trial will include 150 participants with T2D. Participants will be assigned to either a conventional diet (CD) following the guidelines for T2D or an optimised carbohydrate diet (OCD) focused on increasing resistant starch intake to 40 g/day and decreasing rapidly digestible starch intake for 12 weeks. This will be followed by a 6-week wash-out period, after which participants will crossover to the alternate diet with equal energy and consistent energy proportion of the three macronutrients for another 12 weeks. The primary outcome is the difference in the change of postprandial glycaemia (changes in the average incremental area under the blood glucose curve (iAUC)) induced by OCD and CD interventions. Secondary outcomes include changes in other glucose and lipid metabolism-related parameters and cognitive function, as well as psychological, behavioural and physiological factors. Exploratory outcomes include changes in the iAUCs for each of the three meals, appetite-related hormone levels, degree of hepatic steatosis, serum cytokines, immune functions and multiomics parameters.Ethics and disseminationThe protocol has received approval from the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital (Approval No. 2025–018; Protocol V.4.1, 20250112) and has been registered with the ClinicalTrials.gov Registry. The findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journal publications, conference presentations and media releases.Trial registration numberNCT06936657.Keywords: Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2; Clinical Trial; Diabetes & endocrinology

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes (T2D) has emerged as a global health crisis, with its prevalence rising steadily in all regions of the world. Over the past three decades, the number of adults with diabetes has increased by more than three times,1 with T2D accounting for 90%.2 However, the proportion of patients who achieve guideline-recommended treatment targets remains generally low, with certain regions below 40%.3,6 Poor glycaemic control can increase the risk of cognitive impairment7 8 and cardiovascular events,9 10 underscoring the need for more effective and sustainable interventions. Lifestyle interventions are the cornerstone of diabetes management, and dietary modification is integral.11 Mediterranean diet, which emphasises high-quality fat via encouraging extra-virgin olive oil, nuts and fish intake, has been shown to reduce cardiovascular risk among individuals with T2D.12 Given that diabetes is characterised by an abnormality in processing glucose, lowering intake of carbohydrate, which can be absorbed as glucose, and enhancing carbohydrate quality have also been suggested to improve glycaemic control.13,16 Despite the recognition of low-carbohydrate dietary patterns for short-term metabolic benefits, some research showed their adverse effects on long-term health,17 18 with evidence indicating increased risk of depression and mortality associated with low percentages of carbohydrate diets.19 20 Notably, evidence suggests that the quality of carbohydrates plays a more significant role in metabolic benefits than restricting total carbohydrate intake.21 22 High-quality carbohydrates, mainly whole grains, are commonly recommended by international diabetes guidelines,23 24 while low-quality carbohydrates, such as sugar-sweetened beverages and white bread, should be limited. However, in practice, the specific characteristics of different whole grain varieties, as well as their processing methods, may induce different glycaemic responses.25 26 Therefore, recommendations of carbohydrate intake in individuals with diabetes require further refinement.

Carbohydrates that differ in their digestive characteristics elicit distinct glycaemic responses. According to the digestion and absorption rates within the gastrointestinal tract, starch, a major carbohydrate in the diet, can be further subdivided into rapidly digestible starch (RDS), slowly digestible starch (SDS) and resistant starch (RS).27 While RDS can be quickly hydrolysed, resulting in rapid spikes in blood glucose levels,28 RS, a type of starch with fibre properties, resists hydrolysis in the small intestine and is fermented by colonic microbiota to produce beneficial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids,29 30 which can attenuate postprandial glycaemic fluctuations and improve glycaemic control.31 Through two randomised, placebo-controlled clinical trials, we have confirmed that taking 40 g/day of RS can effectively improve insulin resistance, facilitate weight loss and decrease intrahepatic triglycerides in individuals at risk for diabetes. The effects of RS are mediated by alterations in gut microbiota, restoration of intestinal barrier integrity, improved inflammation and alterations in metabolites.32 33 Despite these benefits, advances in modern milling and food-processing technologies in the industrialised society have profoundly decreased RS consumption. It has been reported that medieval Europeans consumed roughly 50–100 g RS daily,34 while contemporary populations in Western countries and China have daily intakes of only 3–8 g and 14.9 g RS, respectively.35 36 Additionally, intake of free sugars, another rapidly digested and absorbed carbohydrate, has been climbing globally in recent decades and is identified as a contributor to the global rise in obesity and diabetes prevalence.37 Starch predominant dietary patterns are widespread globally, with particularly high rice intakes in South Asia and Southeast Asia,38 and increasing consumers in Western countries.39 Thus, adjusting the composition of starch intake may offer clinical benefits for individuals with T2D without restricting overall carbohydrate intake, which still needs further investigation. Given the metabolic benefits derived from RS consumption, we propose optimising the carbohydrate composition in the staple food by increasing the daily intake of RS to 40 g/day, reducing the consumption of RDS and limiting the consumption of free sugar to develop a new dietary pattern for patients with T2D. Our study will evaluate the effects of this new dietary pattern on glycaemic control, cognitive function and psychological, behavioural and physiological factors.

Objectives

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of the optimised carbohydrate diet (OCD) for glycaemic control and its impacts on the cognitive function, other metabolic parameters, immune function, psychological, behavioural and physiological factors as well as multiomics landscape in patients with T2D.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a multicentre, randomised, crossover open-label clinical trial in patients with T2D. The study protocol was developed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials checklist40 (see online supplemental file 1). Each participant will undergo two 12-week dietary intervention phases, separated by a 6-week washout period, for a total study duration of 30 weeks. Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to one of two intervention sequences: (1) OCD-washout-conventional diabetes diet or (2) conventional diet (CD)-washout-OCD. The OCD will adhere to a diet with adjusted carbohydrate composition and proportions, while the CD will follow an isocaloric diet in accordance with diabetes dietary guidelines.41 After the initial 12-week intervention phase, participants will undergo a 6-week washout period, followed by a crossover to the alternative dietary intervention for an additional 12 weeks, resulting in a total study duration of 30 weeks. Participants will receive visits 0–11, as shown in figure 1. At V0, V1, V4, V6, V7 and V10, participants will wear a continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) device to monitor glucose levels at real time and assess postprandial glucose changes. At V1, V2, V3, V5, V7, V8, V9 and V11, saliva, urine, stool and blood samples will be collected, and assessments will be conducted for anthropometric measures, body composition, glucose and lipid metabolism-related indicators and other clinical biochemical indicators. At V1, V5, V7 and V11, additional assessments will be conducted for cognitive function, psychological status, sleep quality, bowel habits and other related factors. Additionally, subgroups of participants in both groups will undergo functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) to assess cognitive function by capturing brain region activity alterations and cardiopulmonary exercise testing to assess cardiopulmonary function. The study was registered with NCT06936657 in April 2025 and is expected to begin in October 2025 and be finalised in June 2027.

Participants and recruitment

Adults of either sex, aged 35–70 years, with a confirmed diagnosis of T2D who are not receiving insulin therapy will be included. Detailed eligibility criteria are listed in table 1. Participants will be recruited by posing recruitment advertisements in the physical examination centre and outpatient hall of each trial centre (Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital, Haikou Orthopaedic and Diabetes Hospital, Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, Peking University Shenzhen Hospital and the Second Affiliated Hospital of the Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen).

The medical history and health status of those who are interested in participating in this clinical trial will be documented. Before any screening procedures, all candidates will receive full information on the study’s potential benefits and risks, and written informed consent (see online supplemental file 2) will be obtained. Participants will be notified and reassured that their involvement is entirely voluntary, and they have the right to discontinue participation at any time without risk of penalties or negative repercussions. If, at any point during the intervention, the investigators or treating specialists determine that initiation of insulin or other prescription therapy likely to alter glycaemic control is essential, the participant will be withdrawn from further study participation. Participants will likewise be withdrawn if any condition arises that poses an unacceptable risk to safety, including life.

Physicians will then carry out an initial clinical assessment, including anthropometric measurements (height, weight, body mass index and waist, hip and thigh circumferences), blood pressure, biochemical test, personal details (age, sex, menopausal status, education, occupation) and questionnaire data on lifestyle, family history, current diseases and medications. Candidates who satisfy every criterion will enter a run-in phase, and those who complete this phase successfully will subsequently be randomised.

Interventions

From the run-in period through the end of the trial, all screened participants will receive dietary guidance and supervision in accordance with ‘Chinese Diabetes Medical Nutrition Therapy Guidelines (2022)42’ and ‘Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents (2021)43’. The individual daily energy intake will be calculated as [(height (cm)−105) kg* 25 kcal/kg]. For both dietary interventions, carbohydrate intake will constitute 50%–60% of the total energy intake, with fat contributing 25%–30% and protein 15%–20%. Furthermore, the total energy intake will be distributed across meals as follows: approximately 25%–30% for breakfast, 30%–40% for lunch and 30%–35% for dinner. Participants will receive a daily menu via the custom mobile study application. Carb-staple foods for all three daily meals during the two intervention phases are readily producible and procurable items and will be produced and provided to participants on a regular cycle. A non-repeating 14day menu will be provided to reduce monotony, alongside a standardised participant handbook. Except for the difference in carb-based staple foods, all other components in the menus provided to participants will be the same during the two dietary intervention phases. Participants will be required to upload their meal details and eating times each day so that investigators can monitor and manage dietary compliance.

Optimised carbohydrate diet

During the OCD intervention, participants will follow a modified diet with adjusted carbohydrate composition and proportions for 12 weeks. For breakfast, participants will consume carb-based staple foods made by partially replacing wheat flour with RS (Hi-Maize 260 RS, 22 000B00, Ingredion, USA), such as bread, dumplings and noodles (Qingmei Green Food Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China), without additional free sugars, except for the ingredients themselves. At lunch and dinner, participants will consume parboiled rice rich in RS44 (Anhui Parboiled Rice Food Technology Co., Ltd, Anhui, China). The starch composition of the provided carbohydrate-based staple foods is displayed in table 2. During this intervention period, participants will be prohibited from consuming starchy vegetables. In this diet, RS will be ensured to constitute 15%–20% of daily starch intake (approximately 40 g/day), SDS accounts for 5% and RDS makes up 75%–80%. Under such circumstances, when following the menus provided by the investigators, dietary fibre (including RS) intake will be maintained at 20–40 g per 1000 kcal. Additionally, free sugars will contribute less than 5% of total energy intake according to the recommendation of WHO45 (table 3).

Washout period

To minimise carryover effects as much as possible, we included a 6-week washout period between the two intervention phases.46 During the washout period, participants will receive the same dietary guidance as during the run-in period. No staple foods will be provided, and participants will prepare their own meals to approximate their baseline dietary habits. Participants will continue to record daily intake so investigators can verify that the washout diet aligns with the run-in (baseline) pattern.

Medication management

During the intervention, participants will be required to maintain their original medication type, dosage and frequency unless any of the following conditions occur: (1) if any blood glucose reading falls below 4 mmol/L; (2) if any blood glucose reading exceeds 20 mmol/L or if fasting blood glucose levels remain above 10 mmol/L. In either case, physicians who are blinded to the study group will assess participant compliance and adjust the medication dosage as needed. At each follow-up visit, the actual medication type, dosage and frequency of each participant will be recorded, and any changes in medication will be quantified using the medication effect score. Antihyperglycaemic medication management will be conducted by physicians blinded to group allocation, following established clinical guidelines for diabetes care, to guarantee consistent and high-quality treatment for all participants.

Compliance assessment

The adherence to the intervention will be assessed based on the participant’s dietary record uploaded to the custom mobile study application, the dietary questionnaire and face-to-face interview at each visit. The number and percentage of meals that meet the intervention requirements during the participants’ intervention period will be calculated. In addition, during the OCD and CD intervention, we will provide carbohydrate-based staple foods for the three daily meals on a fixed cycle. At the end of each cycle, any uneaten staples and empty packaging of consumed staples will be required to be returned to the study centres and reconciled against distribution logs to estimate actual consumption and provide an objective cross-check of adherence.

To ensure adherence to the intervention and visit protocols, the following measures will be implemented: (1) Visits will be arranged based on the convenience of the participants. (2) Trained dieticians and physicians will provide daily reminders for participants to upload their dietary records which will be reviewed and summarised on a weekly basis. Participants will be required to complete a dietary questionnaire at every follow-up visit to evaluate adherence as well. Based on the online records and the dietary questionnaires, tailored dietary advice will be provided to participants to enhance the general adherence to the dietary guidance.

Process evaluation

The process evaluation will gather data regarding the implementation of the intervention, contextual influences and participant motivation. We will conduct interviews with any participant who decides to withdraw from the trial to explore the reasons behind their choice to discontinue their involvement. To assess the fidelity of implementation and participant engagement, we will use the self-assessment questionnaire and the quality control checklist. All participants will complete these questionnaires. The quality control checklist will be filled out by trained dieticians.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome will be the change in the average incremental area under the curve (iAUC) of the 3-hour postprandial blood glucose curve, calculated from the 2-week CGM data collected before the intervention and during the final 2 weeks of the intervention.

Secondary outcomes

Metabolic-related parameters

Metabolic-related parameters will include the following: (1) other glycaemic parameters assessed by CGM, such as time in range, time above range, time below range and mean glucose; (2) glucose metabolic indicators tested by laboratories, such as glycated haemoglobin type A1C (HbA1c), fasting and postprandial blood glucose, insulin and C-peptide; (3) lipid profile; (4) anthropometric and body composition parameters, such as waist circumference, hip circumference, thigh circumference, weight and body composition; (5) other clinical biochemical indicators, such as liver and kidney function, vitamins and trace elements.

Brain health-related parameters

Cognitive function-related indices will be assessed with the following methods: (1) behavioural experiments involving n-back and flanker tasks; (2) resting-state fMRI and task-based fMRI; (3) various cognitive assessment scales and tests, including the mini-mental state examination (MMSE), Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA, Beijing version), repeatable battery for the assessment of neuropsychological status (RBANS), trail making test and stroop colour and word test.

Psychological, behavioural and physiological factors

Psychological, behavioural and physiological factors will include the following: (1) psychological status, such as depression and anxiety, will be assessed by the patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) depression screening scale, the problem areas in diabetes (PAID) questionnaire and other relevant scales; (2) sleep quality will be assessed by the Pittsburgh sleep quality index (PSQI) questionnaire and other methods; (3) bowel habits will be assessed by the Bristol stool scale questionnaire and other relevant scales; (4) appetite will be measured by visual analogue scales (VAS) and other relevant scales; (5) cardiopulmonary function will be assessed by cardiopulmonary exercise test.

Exploratory outcomes

To evaluate the impact of the OCD intervention on postprandial blood glucose changes at each meal and the secretion of appetite hormones, we will compare the changes in the iAUC of postprandial blood glucose over 3 hours after each meal assessed by CGM and analyse the differences in appetite-related hormones (ghrelin, peptide YY, glucagon-likepeptide-1, etc). Given that individuals with T2D often have visceral fat accumulation,47 which is associated with the progression of diabetes,48 we will also examine the changes in intrahepatic fat content in participants using Fibroscan to explore the effects of the OCD intervention. Furthermore, to investigate the underlying mechanisms, we will study the changes in cytokines (fibroblast growth factor 21 [FGF 21], adiponectin, fatty acid-binding protein 4, and lipocalin 2), immune function (isolation and analysis of peripheral blood mononuclear cells) and multiomics features (metagenomics, metabolomics, genomics, lipidomics, proteomics, etc).

Outcome assessments

All assessments and respective time points are listed in online supplemental table 1.

Continuous glucose monitoring

CGM will be performed using SIBIONICS@. The CGM will be mounted on the back of the upper dominant arm according to the user manual. All blood glucose data will be transmitted via the SIBIONICS@ app software, with each CGM lasting 14 days.

Height measurement

Participants will be asked to remove shoes and socks before measuring. During measurement, participants will be required to straighten bodies, keep hands close to the base of thighs and stand naturally with feet on the base of the measuring tape, with legs, heels, backs and heads as close to the tape as possible. The measuring tape will be flattened against the participants’ hair, and the reading will be accurate to one decimal place in cm.

Weight and body composition measurement

Prior to the measurement, participants will be required to remove all external metal items, keep their hands and feet dry and stand quietly in the centre of the analyser and remain quiet. Once the measurement is complete and the readings have stabilised, the results, including weight, whole-body fat mass, body fat percentage, lean mass and lean mass percentage, will be recorded.

Blood pressure and heart rate measurement

Participants will be required to avoid strenuous exercise before measurement and sit still for 5–10 min in a quiet environment with appropriate temperature before measurement. The arm to be measured (usually the right upper arm) should be exposed at the same level as the heart, and the cuff of the sphygmomanometer should be placed close to the brachial artery at the elbow socket, with the lower edge of the cuff 3–4 cm from the elbow socket. Blood pressure and heart rate will be measured twice and recorded, with an interval of at least 2 min between measurements.

Waist, hip and thigh measurement

To minimise measurement variability, all assessments will be conducted by a dedicated healthcare professional using a non-stretchable measuring tape (resolution: 0.1 cm). Participants will be instructed to stand upright in a relaxed position with feet together and arms at sides during measurements. In order to minimise the error caused by manual measurement, a fixed physician at each clinical centre will be arranged to conduct the measurement. Participants will be asked to remain relaxed with their legs together in an upright position during measurement. Waist circumference will be measured above the navel, at the midpoint between the lower edge of the costal arch and the upper edge of the ilium; hip circumference will be measured horizontally around the most prominent point of the buttocks; thigh circumference will be measured at the midpoint of the line between the anterior superior iliac spine and the upper edge of the patella, and readings will be accurate to single digits in centimetre.

Cognitive function and functional magnetic resonance

Participants will be asked to complete the following questionnaires: MMSE, MoCA (Beijing version), RBANS, trail making test, stroop colour and word test and other scales to assess cognitive function. fMRI will be performed using a 3.0T MRI scanner (Ingenia, Philips Medical Systems, The Netherlands) integrated with a brain functional audiovisual stimulation system (SA-9939, Shenzhen Sinorad Medical Electronics Co., Ltd, Guangdong, China). In collaboration with psychiatrists, fMRI will be used to assess cognitive function changes following intervention. Participants will fast for ≥6 hours prior to the experiment. A trained researcher will conduct a prescan practice session to familiarise participants and adjust the scanning parameters. Participants will be randomly assigned a task number, and the screens and answer buttons in the MRI chamber will be checked prior to the experiment to ensure that the equipment is functioning properly. Participants will enter the MRI scanning chamber in a supine position with the head immobilised by a vacuum-retained pillow and a restraining band to minimise head movements. Brain function will be assessed using both resting-state fMRI and task-state fMRI modalities. Resting-state fMRI records brain neural activity in the baseline state. Task-state fMRI consists of three parts, namely, the detection time task (assessing reaction speed), the n-back task (assessing working memory) and the lateral inhibition task (assessing cognitive control and conflict inhibition).

Meal tolerance test and appetite assessment

Participants will be instructed to commence an overnight fast from 22:00 on the preceding day. On arrival at the clinical research centre the following morning, a fasting venous blood sample will be obtained for fasting index measurement (eg, blood glucose, insulin and C-peptide). Subsequently, participants will be required to consume a standardised test meal (instant noodles: 1566 kJ, containing 68.4 g carbohydrates and 10.4 g protein) in 5 min. Venous blood samples will be collected at predefined postprandial time points—30, 60, 90 and 120 min—to quantify the corresponding postprandial metabolic indicators (blood glucose, insulin, C-peptide, etc). Before and after the meal tolerance test, appetite-related subjective feelings of participants, such as hunger and satiety, will be quantified by using a 100 mm VAS.

Cardiopulmonary exercise test

As individuals with T2D are at higher risk of heart failure,49 which is closely associated with cardiopulmonary function,50 we plan to evaluate the effects of the intervention on participants’ cardiopulmonary function. A symptom-limited incremental cardiopulmonary exercise test will be performed on an electromagnetically braked cycle ergometer using an individualised linear ramp of approximately 10–25 W/min. Breath-by-breath respiratory gas exchange will be continuously measured with a Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing System (SensorMedics, Yorba Linda, CA, USA).

Other questionnaires

Participants will be asked to complete the following questionnaires at each visit: PHQ-9 depression screening scale, PAID questionnaires, generalized anxiety disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7), Pittsburgh sleep quality index, Bristol stool scores, international physical activity questionnaire, etc, to assess psychological status, sleep quality, appetite, bowel habits and exercise.

Fibroscan

Transient elastography will be conducted using the FibroScan device (EchoSens, Paris, France) to evaluate the severity of hepatic steatosis and fibrosis. Participants will be positioned in a supine posture with the right arm fully abducted to facilitate optimal access to the right hepatic lobe through the intercostal space. Prior to elastography, a two-dimensional ultrasound-guided localisation will be performed to identify a suitable liver region free from major vascular structures, anatomical landmarks (eg, gallbladder and superior border of the right kidney) and other potential obstructions. The selection of the probe (M probe or XL probe) will adhere to the EASL-ALEH Clinical Practice Guidelines. Liver stiffness measurements and controlled attenuation parameter will be obtained by an experienced sonographer who is blinded to the participant group allocation. For each participant, 10 valid measurements will be taken, and the median value will be recorded for subsequent statistical analysis.

Blood, stool, urine and saliva samples collection and preservation

Fasting and postprandial blood samples will be collected from participants before and after consumption of a standardised test meal. Blood draws will be performed at predefined time points: 0 min (fasting), 30 min, 60 min, 90 min and 120 min relative to meal ingestion. Collected blood samples will be centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C to isolate serum. Biochemical parameters (blood glucose, insulin, C-peptide, etc) will be analysed immediately using an automated biochemical analyser (Hitachi 7600–020, Tokyo, Japan). Serum aliquots intended for cytokine profiling will be stored at −80°C in an ultra-low temperature freezer until further analysis. Serum concentrations of cytokines and metabolic markers (fibroblast growth factor 21 [FGF21], adiponectin, fatty acid-binding protein 4, lipocalin 2, etc) will be quantified using ELISAs and other methods following manufacturer protocols. The blood will be collected using BD Vacutainer CPT cell preparation tube with sodium citrate (REF 362761) to isolate human peripheral blood mononuclear cells. Participants will be instructed to collect approximately 5 g of stool using commercial tubes containing DNA stabiliser (STRATEC Molecular, Berlin, Germany) and sterile sample collection tubes and deliver the samples to the hospital within 30 min to 1 hour. If immediate delivery is not feasible, samples may be temporarily stored in the freezer compartment of a refrigerator until transfer. All stool samples will ultimately be stored at −80°C in an ultra-low temperature freezer for subsequent analysis. Morning urine will be collected on the days of visits; 10 mL of the first-morning urine will be collected into a separate centrifuge tube for metabolomic analysis. All urine samples will be centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. The supernatant will be collected and stored at −80°C in an ultra-low temperature freezer. Saliva samples from each participant will be collected in the morning, prior to tooth brushing. When collecting saliva samples, participants will be instructed to tilt their heads forward so that saliva accumulates at the floor of the mouth, and to spit the saliva into a sterile sample collection tube once per minute. Approximately 5 mL of saliva will be obtained from each individual. The samples will be aliquoted and stored at −80°C in an ultra-low temperature freezer.

Adverse events

Adverse events (AEs) refer to any unfavourable or unintended occurrences in a participant that are temporally associated with the clinical trial intervention, regardless of whether they are causally related. AEs may manifest as symptoms, including gastrointestinal symptoms (eg, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, bloating and constipation), palpitations and other discomforts, hypoglycaemia events, signs (eg, tachycardia and hepatomegaly) or abnormal test results (eg, laboratory findings and ECG abnormalities). A serious adverse event (SAE) is defined as any unexpected and medically significant event that occurs during the trial, which requires hospitalisation, prolongs existing hospitalisation, causes disability, impairs work capacity, threatens life, results in death or leads to congenital anomalies. In the study, occurrence of T2D-related AEs, including major cardiovascular events (non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke, cardiovascular death), acute metabolic events (diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state, severe hypoglycaemia requiring assistance) and microvascular/diabetes‐related complications (new or worsening diabetic foot ulcer, lower‐extremity infection/osteomyelitis, amputation, sight‐threatening retinopathy interventions, hospitalisation for nephropathy), will be closely monitored. On the occurrence of any AE (including SAEs), detailed information must be recorded in the case report form, including the time of onset, clinical manifestations, treatment measures, duration, outcomes and the relationship to the intervention. For laboratory abnormalities, participants must be followed up until test results return to normal, revert to pretreatment levels or are determined to be unrelated to the intervention. Any SAEs must be documented in the SAE form and reported to the sponsor, the Ethics Committee, the Safety Supervision Department of the China Food and Drug Administration and the relevant health administration department within 24 hours. Once a qualified physician determines that a participant’s health condition is no longer suitable for continued intervention, the intervention will be discontinued.

Sample size

Based on the results of the pilot trial, the sample size is calculated by PASS 21.0.3 statistical software. The anticipated difference in changes of iAUC between the two groups after the 12-week dietary intervention is 55.8 mmol−1 min−1 with a SD of 92.1. With a two-sided α level of 0.05 and a power of 90%, the calculated minimum sample size is 118. Accounting for a potential 20% loss to follow-up, the required sample size is 148 participants. Therefore, we plan to enrol a total of 150 participants in this study.

Randomisation and blinding

Randomisation will be stratified by clinical trial centre and gender. Participants with T2D will be allocated to either intervention order in a 1:1 ratio (equal number of participants in each intervention order) using a web-based central randomisation system. Participants will be enrolled on a competitive basis to minimise inter-centre variability. Due to the obvious differences in texture and taste of carbohydrate-based staple foods provided during OCD and CD intervention, blinding of the participants to treatment is not feasible. However, the food will be packaged uniformly, and participants will not be informed about which dietary intervention corresponds to the carbohydrate-based staple foods they received. All investigators responsible for clinical assessments and data collection and analyses will remain blinded to group allocation. Furthermore, participants will not have access to the glucose data measured by their CGM devices. Any data collection and analyses potentially susceptible to investigator bias will be conducted without knowledge of intervention groups.

Data management and analysis

Data management

In this trial, participant data will be collected via electronic case report forms completed by trained study personnel. To protect confidentiality, personal information will be anonymised and participants identified only by study IDs. Data access will be restricted to authorised investigators. All data will be stored in a central database with strict access controls and encryption, and both transmission and storage will adhere to relevant information security and privacy regulations. Biological samples will be securely stored in the research biobank at Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital and identified solely by study IDs.

Statistical methods

This study will apply the intention-to-treat principle to evaluate the effectiveness of the OCD on blood glucose management in participants with T2D. Statistical analyses will be conducted using SPSS V.25.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), SAS V.9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA), STATA V.18.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA) and R V.4.3.0 (or above). Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation. Continuous variables will be presented as mean±SD deviation or mean (95% CI) for normally distributed data. For non-normally distributed data, they will be expressed as median with IQR. Categorical variables will be reported as percentages. The normality of the data will be assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. All statistical tests will be two-tailed, with a significance α level at 0.05. Comparisons of baseline characteristics before OCD and CD intervention will be performed using a linear mixed model adjusted by clinical trial centre and intervention order. Longitudinal analysis of the OCD or CD intervention effects will be conducted using one-way repeated measures analysis of variance, a linear mixed model adjusted by categorical time points represented by visits, clinical trial centre and intervention order or other methods to compare differences between baseline and follow-up time points. The intervention’s effects on outcomes assessed only before and after the intervention will be analysed with analysis of covariance, adjusting for the clinical trial centre, intervention order and baseline characteristics. The intervention effects on outcomes measured multiple times during the intervention will be evaluated using linear mixed models. Fixed effects will include baseline values of the assessed variables, treatment, categorical time points represented by visits, the interaction term of visit×treatment and intervention order. Random effects will include clinical trial centre. For each outcome, we will adjust for the corresponding baseline value by excluding any systematic main effect of group at baseline. In the multiomics analysis, differences in the abundance of microbial species and functional pathways, proteins, lipids, metabolites and other multiomics features before and after OCD or CD intervention will be assessed with the one-way repeated measures analysis of variance, Student’s paired t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test or other appropriate methods. Differences in multiomics features between OCD or CD intervention will be assessed using Student’s unpaired t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, MaAsLin2 and other methods. To further investigate the association between multiomics features and between multiomics features and clinical indicators, Spearman correlation analysis, partial Spearman correlation analysis and other methods will be performed while controlling for potential confounding factors.

Monitoring

This study will be overseen by an independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) composed of experts not involved in the research. The DMC will comprise a chair (senior clinician with T2D trial experience), an independent biostatistician with DMC experience, an endocrinologist and a bioethicist. Scheduled DMC meetings will occur monthly during active recruitment and follow-up. In the event of a SAE occurring during the study, it must be immediately reported to the DMC, the principal investigator (PI) and the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital. The DMC will review the trial process and data and, in collaboration with the attending physician, determine whether the participant should continue or discontinue the intervention. In cases of medical emergencies at the clinical site, researchers will provide all necessary supportive medical care under the guidance of the PI or designated personnel.

Stoppage rules

Trial stoppage decisions will be made based on prespecified criteria below: three or more SAEs occurring within any 30-day window in the same arm; or a materially higher SAE rate in OCD intervention versus CD intervention.

Patients and public involvement

Patients and public will not be involved in the design, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

This study involving human participants was registered with the ClinicalTrials.gov and approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital (Approval No. 2025–018). Participants will be provided with written informed consent in this study. Any subsequent modifications required to the study protocol will be submitted to the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital as required and will be implemented and disseminated in accordance with their recommendations. The PI has access to the final trial dataset, and data sharing requires approval from the PI and the signing of a data use agreement. Presentation of study results will include peer-reviewed publications, conference reports and media releases.

Discussion

Carbohydrates have consistently served as the principal source of energy for the human body, owing to their widespread availability and efficient conversion into metabolically usable energy. Globally, 70.5% of the total energy available per person per day comes from carbohydrates.51 Despite some evidence demonstrating that carbohydrate restriction is metabolically beneficial for individuals with T2D,52 53 the prevailing pattern of carbohydrate consumption indicates that populations accustomed to long-term carbohydrate-rich diets may have limited adaptability to sustained low-carbohydrate diets, thereby partly accounting for the low adherence observed with such dietary interventions.54 55 In addition, carbohydrate consumption can enhance the brain’s absorption of tryptophan, thereby boosting serotonin production56 and improving mood. A lower intake of carbohydrate has been shown to be associated with higher risk of depression.20 Therefore, long-term reduction of carbohydrate intake may negatively affect the emotion of individuals with diabetes and decrease their quality of life. Accordingly, in this study, we develop an OCD that focuses on improving carbohydrate quality rather than reducing total carbohydrate consumption, with the aim of promoting sustainable metabolic improvements in individuals with T2D. We will collect multidimensional phenotypic data to evaluate the effects of OCD intervention on glucose metabolism, cognitive function and psychological, behavioural and physiological factors in individuals with T2D, while also integrating multiomics data analysis to explore the underlying mechanisms.

As T2D is a chronic metabolic disorder marked by insulin resistance and impaired β-cell function,57 postprandial hyperglycaemia is common in T2D. Historically, postprandial hyperglycaemia emerged as the first additional factor in glycaemic control evaluated alongside HbA1c.58 It has been discovered to associate with oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction and identified as an independent risk factor for cardiovascular diseases.59 In addition, postprandial hyperglycaemia is associated with regional white matter hyperintensity and brain atrophy, indicating that controlling postprandial blood glucose may help prevent diabetes-related neurodegenerative changes.60 These findings underscore the importance of postprandial blood glucose control in diabetes management. Therefore, in this study, the change in postprandial iAUC was designated as the primary endpoint to evaluate the postprandial blood glucose responses of participants with T2D under OCD intervention.

Notably, patients with diabetes often experience neurodegenerative61 and neurovascular processes,62 manifesting in varying degrees of cognitive dysfunction.63 Furthermore, the progression of diabetes is frequently accompanied by a range of psychological and physiological health issues, including mental health disorders such as depression64 and physiological problems such as impaired sleep quality.65 These issues are associated with poor glycaemic control and can exacerbate the clinical manifestations of diabetes and deteriorate the overall health of patients. In light of this, this study will also evaluate multidimensional information, including cognitive function, psychological status, sleep quality, appetite and other aspects, in addition to metabolic-related indicators, to assess the impact of the OCD diet on glycaemic control and overall health conditions in patients with T2D. Especially in the cognitive function assessment module, this study will integrate multiple scales and testing methods for a collaborative evaluation that comprehensively covers cognitive domains. We will assess multiple cognitive dimensions, including memory, attention, executive function, abstract thinking and language ability, thereby accurately capturing the dynamic changes in participants’ cognitive function during the intervention.

To further investigate the mechanisms underlying the effects of OCD intervention, we will conduct longitudinal collection of biological samples for multiomics profiling and integrative analysis. Existing research indicates that dysbiosis of the gut microbiota is commonly observed in patients with diabetes.66 This dysbiosis may lead to disturbances in the body’s metabolic and neuroendocrine systems, thereby impacting physiological and psychological health, manifesting as changes in appetite,67 mood,68 sleep69 and other related aspects. Furthermore, an imbalance in the gut microbiota may exacerbate cognitive decline and neurological dysfunction.70 Studies have shown that diet-microbiota interactions are important in metabolism71 and dietary modification can significantly improve the metabolic status of patients with diabetes via modulating the gut microbiota.72 However, current evidence does not fully elucidate whether dietary interventions can influence the psychological status and cognitive function of individuals with T2D. The roles of which important microbial communities and multiomics effect molecules are involved remain to be further explored as well.

In addition, to ensure the feasibility of the intervention, staple foods for all three daily meals are readily producible and procurable items and will be produced and distributed on a regular cycle. Participants will receive portable weighing tools to support accurate quantification of intake and improve the quality of dietary records. Dietitians are involved throughout the study to provide specialised nutritional guidance and assessment. Therefore, participants will receive a high-quality intervention throughout the clinical trial and, on its completion, will be able to implement the OCD intervention in daily life. Although the intervention duration of this study is insufficient to provide evidence of long-term intervention effects, we believe it provides a methodological and empirical foundation to inform subsequent longer-duration trials.

In conclusion, the ultimate goal of this study is to establish an optimised carbohydrate dietary pattern that facilitates blood glucose control and overall health improvement in patients with T2D, providing patients with a practicable and sustainable dietary option to improve metabolic health and enhance quality of life.

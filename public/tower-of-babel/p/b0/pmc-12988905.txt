b-Hurwitz numbers from refined topological recursion
Authors: Nitin Kumar Chidambaram, Maciej Dołęga, Kento Osuga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe prove that single G-weighted b-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces are computed by refined topological recursion on a rational spectral curve, for certain rational weights G. Consequently, the b-Hurwitz generating function analytically continues to a rational curve. In particular, our results cover the cases of b-monotone Hurwitz numbers, and the enumeration of maps and bipartite maps (with internal faces) on non-oriented surfaces. As an application, we prove that the correlators of the Gaussian, Jacobi and Laguerre β-ensembles are computed by refined topological recursion.

Introduction

Motivated by topological string theory, Bouchard and Mariño [22] conjectured that simple Hurwitz numbers can be computed using the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion formalism [27, 38]. Subsequently proved in [37], this conjecture sparked an explosion of activity relating Hurwitz theory and topological recursion [16, 31, 32, 35, 47] culminating with the following recent result of [1, 14]: whenever the weight G is a rational function times an exponential, G-weighted Hurwitz numbers can be computed by topological recursion on an associated spectral curve. The existence of topological recursion is a key result that controls the structure of weighted Hurwitz numbers. Particular important consequences include the existence of a rational parametrization for their generating functions, existence of quantum curves and determinantal formulas, and ELSV-type formulas generalizing [36].

b-deformation

Motivated in part by the β-deformation of matrix models, one-parameter deformations of both Hurwitz theory and topological recursion have been studied intensely in the last few years. When b=0, Jack functions reduce to Schur functions, only orientable contributions survive, and τG(0) becomes the generating function of G-weighted Hurwitz numbers, which is known to be a Kadomtsev–Petviashvili tau function [44].

Refined topological recursion, defined by [46, 53] building on [26], is a one-parameter deformation of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion whose underlying (global) symmetry is the Virasoro algebra at central charge c=1-6b2. Refined topological recursion takes as input a refined spectral curve, denoted Sμ, which consists of the data (Σ,x,y,P+,{μa}a∈P+) — Σ is a compact Riemann surface; x, y are two non-constant meromorphic functions on Σ such that x:Σ→P1 is of degree two; P+ is a subset of the set of the zeroes and poles of ydx (possibly with some shift); and μa∈C is a complex number for every such a∈P+.

The data of a refined spectral curve defines the unstable correlators ω0,1,ω0,2, and ω12,1, and the refined topological recursion is a recursive (on the integer (2g-2+n)) construction of the stable correlators ωg,n for (g,n)∈12Z≥0×Z≥1 such that (2g-2+n)>0. When Σ=P1 which is the case we are interested in, the ωg,n are proven to be symmetric meromorphic n-differentials on Σ that are polynomials in the parameter b of degree at most 2g (see Section 3 for their definition). Upon setting b=0, we recover the usual Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion correlators.

Inserting internal faces

Our second main result is an extension of Theorem 1.1 to the case of b-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces. The terminology “internal faces" stems from the interpretation of τG(b) as a generating function for colored monotone Hurwitz maps, for rational weight G [12, 54]. The expansion coefficient Fg,n[μ1,…,μn] is (up to an automorphism factor) a weighted count of colored monotone Hurwitz maps on non-oriented surfaces of genus g with n faces of degrees μ1,⋯,μn. In this interpretation, the dependence on b is through the factor (-αb)ρ(M), where ρ(M) is the so-called measure of non-orientability of a map M – see Section 2.2 for precise statements.

We are interested in counting colored monotone Hurwitz maps of genus g with n marked boundary faces of degrees μ1,⋯,μn, where we allow the maps to also have arbitrarily many internal faces of degree at most D∈Z>0. Context and future work

To prove Theorem 1.1, we use Virasoro-type constraints found in [25] that uniquely determine the tau function τG(b). We turn these constraints into formal loop equations for the generating functions of the Fg,n and prove that the refined topological recursion correlators ωg,n uniquely solve these loop equations using the results of [46]. Our result extends the result of [1, 14] to arbitrary b for the restricted choice of weights G(z) in equation (2). However, the results of loc. cit. rely heavily on KP integrability, which is unknown in the b-deformed setting and hence we cannot use their methods to prove our result.

The strategy of our proof applies to any rational weight G(z), and indeed can be used to show that the generating function of rationally weighted b-Hurwitz numbers analytically continues to a rational curve Σ=P1 (extending the result of [13] to arbitrary b). The only obstruction is that the refined topological recursion formalism is yet to be defined when the associated branched covering x:Σ→P1 is of degree greater than 2. We will return to this question in the future.

The key idea in extending Theorem 1.1 to Theorem 1.3 for b-Hurwitz numbers with internal faces is that inserting internal faces in the combinatorial models can be interpreted as the application of the variational formula in topological recursion. This technique was employed by [10] in the b=0 setting, and we use the refined variational formula proved in [52] to extend the result to arbitrary b.

In his list of unsolved problems in map enumeration [17], Bender posed a question regarding the origin of a universal pattern that has been discovered and proven for various models of maps over the years. This universal pattern matches physics predictions from the so-called double scaling limits in quantum gravity. Eynard explained in [39, Chapter 5] how the structural properties of the topological recursion correlators can be used to prove these predictions. In particular, the recent results in the b=0 setting on topological recursion applied to weighted Hurwitz numbers with internal faces provide a general answer to Bender’s question in the case of orientable surfaces. However, in the non-orientable case, this asymptotic pattern is proved only for some special models, and remains conjectural in general. We plan to use refined topological recursion to tackle this problem in future work.

In the following we collect the colored monotone Hurwitz maps by the generating series that will additionally “measure their non-orientability” by the following algorithm.

Definition 2.4

Let M be a monotone Hurwitz map. We iteratively remove either the vertex of maximum label (if it is isolated) or the edge of maximal label. Doing so we also collect a weight 1 or -αb whenever we remove an edge e, by the following rules. Let M′ be the map obtained from M by removing e. Then:

(i)if e joins two active corners in the same face of M′, the weight is 1 if e splits the face into two, and -αb otherwise;

if e joins two active corners in the same face of M′, the weight is 1 if e splits the face into two, and -αb otherwise;

(ii)if e joins two active corners in different faces of M′, the weight is 1 if the local orientations of the active corners are compatible, and -αb otherwise.

if e joins two active corners in different faces of M′, the weight is 1 if the local orientations of the active corners are compatible, and -αb otherwise.

The product of all the collected weights is necessarily of the form (-αb)ρ(M) for some non-negative integer ρ(M), which is called the measure of non-orientability of M.

β Brézin–Gross–Witten integral and b-monotone Hurwitz numbers

When G=1v-z, the tau function τG(b) interpolates between the partition functions for the unitary (at b=0) and orthogonal (at b=2-2-1) Brézin–Gross–Witten integrals, so that it can be interpreted as their natural β deformation whose topological expansion was found in terms of b-monotone Hurwitz maps (described in Theorem 2.6; see [12] and references therein). Thus, Theorem 5.11 shows that b-monotone Hurwitz numbers are computed by refined topological recursion.

It is worth noting that a slight variant of τG(b) is known to be the Gaiotto state of 4d N=2 pure SU(2) gauge theory in arbitrary Ω-background [3, 50, 56] (see [25, Remark 4.9]). Thus, the Gaiotto state can also be computed by refined topological recursion, but this study is beyond the scope of this paper.

Maps and bipartite maps with internal faces on arbitrary surface

The main motivation to study the extension to internal faces in Sect. 5 comes from the fact that the associated ωg,n produces generating functions of special interest in enumerative combinatorics and quantum gravity, as we will explain below.

Recall that ϕg,nD(X1,…,Xn) defined in Sect. 5 is the generating series for colored monotone Hurwitz numbers of genus g with n marked boundaries and with internal faces of degree i weighted by ϵpi. It was proved in [24] that for two special cases of G-weighted b-Hurwitz numbers the function ϕg,nD(X1,…,Xn) posses a different, particularly simple, combinatorial interpretation that turns into two classical generating functions for b=0 and b=2-1-2.

Recall that a map is a 2-cell embedding of a multigraph on a compact surface (orientable or not). We say that a map M is bipartite if one can partition its vertex set V(M)=V∘(M)∪V∙(M) into disjoint subsets such that no edge links two vertices of the same subset. The degree of a face f of a map M is the number of corners inside f that are adjacent to V∘(M). By convention, we set V(M)=V∘(M) if we work with ordinary (not bipartite) maps. We are interested in enumerating maps with n marked faces of degrees k1,⋯,kn, that we will call boundaries and arbitrarily many faces of degree at most D that we will call internal faces, as in Sect. 2.3. As in Sect. 2.3, each boundary has a distinguished corner that “marks” this face as a boundary. See Fig. 2 for an illustration of a bipartite map on the torus with internal faces of degree at most 3.

A bipartite map on the torus with three boundaries of degrees 4, 9, 9, and with internal faces of degree at most 3, so that it contributes to the coefficient of [X1-5X2-10X3-10] in ϕ1,3bip,3. The marked corners are indicated by blue arrows, and the root is indicated by the yellow oriented arrow

Finally, we will be interested in generating series for rooted maps, which means that maps will have a distinguished and oriented corner called the root. This can be any corner in M, including the marked corners lying on boundaries. For a rooted map M, one can use the root to define an order on the set of edges. Then one can define the measure of non-orientability of M by successively removing the edges from M and collecting a weight 1 or -αb as in Definition 2.4, so that the final weight is of the form (-αb)ρ(M) for some non-negative integer ρ(M). We will not need this specific algorithm here, so we refer the reader to [24, Section 3] for more details. In the special cases of b=0 and b=2-1-2, the role of the root is not important any more, and instead of counting rooted maps weighted by the inverse of the number of its edges, we could equivalently count unrooted maps weighted by the inverse of the size of their automorphism group.

Corollary 6.4

For 2g-2+n≥1 the formal series ϕg,nD(X(z1),…,X(zn))from Theorem 6.3, for both maps and bipartite maps, can be written as a rational multidifferential in z1,⋯,zn.

While the above corollary is a straightforward consequence of refined topological recursion, the problem of rational parametrization of generating series in enumerative combinatorics of maps has a rich history of studies employing algebraic, analytic, and bijective methods [8, 9, 21, 23, 28–30, 42]. In the special case b=0, which corresponds to the generating series of orientable maps/bipartite maps, the rational parametrization of generating series was known before. It was recently reproved using topological recursion in [10, 15] for a much wider class of maps than the ones studied here. See also [19] for an efficient way of computing bipartite maps from topological recursion in the unrefined setting

On the other hand, the rational parametrization even in the special case of b=2-1-2, which corresponds to the generating series of non-oriented maps/bipartite maps, is a new result obtained here. In fact, while the rational parametrization manifests itself through topological recursion in both the refined and unrefined setting, the analytic structure of the correlators in the refined setting differs substantially. Indeed, the RTR correlators have poles along the “anti-diagonals” zi=σ(zj) and at zi∈σ(P+), for any i,j∈[n].

It was noticed in the past that the structure of the generating series of non-oriented maps is not always as simple as their orientable counterparts [2, 11, 34]. We plan to address this problem in future work, especially from the point of view of a conjectural universal pattern in asymptotic enumeration that so far was explained in the orientable case by the structural properties of TR [39], but in the non-orientable case remains elusive.

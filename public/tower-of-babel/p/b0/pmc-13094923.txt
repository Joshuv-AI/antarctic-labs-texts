Education 5.0: Technological transformations and health perspectives for sustainable development
Authors: Karuna Nikum, Pooja Singh, Sunita Pachori, Kavita Bani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:This article discusses the potential of frontier technologies to boost education in the context of Education 5.0, along with analyzing educational issues, their effects on students’ health, and how to overcome these issues. Technologies like IoT, AR, ML, VR, CC, DS, and AI have the potential to revolutionize the education sector, each playing its own distinct role to maximize learning, teaching, and institutional management.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Two web-based surveys were administered in an Engineering college: one of 313 students and 67 educators to determine comfort with the use of digital technology, and a second of 115 students to examine academic pressures and stressors.RESULTS:Results focus on the two-sided effect of digital technologies: while they offer possibilities for more customized and interactive learning, they also increase stress, sleep disturbance, and workload issues, which lead to poor mental health.CONCLUSION:The convergence of new technologies in Education 5.0 has the potential to transform teaching and learning, but long-term adoption needs alignment with health policy standards. Approaches like WHO’s Health policies framework and UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3: Good Health and Well-Being, and SDG 4: Quality Education) emphasize the convergence of innovation with students’ well-being. Embedding these benchmarks into institutional planning ensures that Education 5.0 supports both academic excellence and the long-term health of learners.

Introduction

The educational landscape is rapidly evolving with the advent of Education 5.0, an era where advanced technologies such as the IoT, AR, ML, VR, CC, DS, and AI are reshaping how education is delivered and experienced. These emerging technologies hold the potential to revolutionize traditional learning environments, making them more personalized, interactive, and data-driven. However, the full integration of these technologies into the existing education system presents a unique set of challenges.

One of the most pressing questions educators, administrators, and policymakers face is how to incorporate these transformative technologies smoothly and effectively, ensuring they enhance the educational process without overwhelming existing structures. These technologies promise a more dynamic approach to teaching, learning, and assessment, but their implementation requires careful consideration of technological infrastructure, teacher training, data security, and curriculum adaptation. This paper aims to explore the role of these advanced technologies, highlighting the benefits and the challenges that need to be addressed for their effective adoption. Through this exploration, we aim to provide a comprehensive understanding of how these tools can be harnessed to foster a more efficient, accessible, and future-ready educational system. The integration of digital technologies into education systems has sparked extensive research, revealing complex interactions between technology, pedagogy, policy, and societal demands. Recent reviews highlight the transformative potential of digital technologies in fostering sustainable and inclusive learning environments, emphasizing applications such as e-learning platforms and AI-driven personalized education.[1] Technological infrastructure and smart learning environments play a very important role to bridge the gap between theory and practical.[2] Generational differences have influenced how educational strategies address learners’ digital fluency.[3] Frameworks uniting technology, pedagogy, and content knowledge are extensively used to advance curriculum design.[4] The importance of ICT strategies that align education reform with economic and social development objectives.[5] For deeper reflection on social inequalities and the digital divide in educational technology adoption, more focus on equitable access and lifelong learning is key to twenty-first-century education.[67] Holistic approaches to integrating technology and pedagogy stress managing educational change across multiple levels of the system.[89] Critical perspectives caution against overestimating technology’s classroom impact, pointing out limited and superficial use despite wide availability.[10] Fostering interdisciplinary curricula, digital skill development, and innovation-driven learning is a very important aspect.[1112] The worldwide scenario for digital education has witnessed rapid transformation, especially in the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic, which accelerated the adoption of online and blended learning models across all education levels. According to UNESCO and the OECD, over 190 countries shifted to remote learning in 2020, prompting massive investments in digital infrastructure, platforms, and teacher training. High-income countries have seen widespread integration of LMS, AI-based tutoring, and cloud-based content delivery, while low- and middle-income countries face challenges such as limited internet access, device shortages, and teacher preparedness. Despite these gaps, global initiatives—like UNESCO’s Global Education Coalition and various EdTech partnerships—are working toward inclusive and equitable digital education, aligning with Sustainable Development Goal 4 (Quality Education). The focus now lies on ensuring digital literacy, data privacy, and reducing the digital divide to make education accessible and effective for all learners worldwide. The rapid integration of new technologies into education is not merely an issue of academic reform but also one of public health significance. Global initiatives like the World Health Organization’s (WHO) Health in all Policies strategy and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3: Good Health and Well-Being, and SDG 4: Quality Education) highlight that digitalization in education needs to be harmonized with the encouragement of mental, physical, and social well-being. Hence, all advancement towards Education 5.0 needs to be measured against health policy standards so that development in technology does not erode student health but rather encourages resilience, equity, and sustainable learning achievements.

Educational technology and its effect on students health

While educational technology provides learning advantages, its negative effects on physical health include eye strain, posture problems, disturbed sleep, reduced physical activity, hearing issues, and fatigue. The academic pressure also affects physical, mental health, and social life of the students as shown in Figure 5. Academic pressure refers to the stress and burden students feel to perform well in their studies and meet high expectations set by themselves, parents, teachers, and society. In today’s fast-changing, competitive world, this pressure has increased significantly due to various factors such as high expectations, competition, technology distraction, etc.

Discussion/Impact of Technologies in Education

While new technologies pose enormous prospects for individualized and inclusive learning, their application needs to be led by health-based benchmarks as well. Reforms in education under Education 5.0 need to be measured by quantifiable parameters like a decline in technology-caused stress, better sleep quality, avoiding digital addiction, and equal access to mental health services. Although the study employed a scale-based survey, which may restrict the depth of qualitative insights, it offers the advantage of systematically quantifying perceptions across a larger sample. Future studies may complement this approach with qualitative methods to gain deeper contextual understanding, thereby enriching the findings. Aligning technology use with such health policy standards assures institutions of promoting not just academic achievement but also healthier living, thus fulfilling international obligations under SDG 3 and SDG 4. Conclusion

As the globe becomes more digitized, the education sector has a responsibility to adapt to the expectations of a technology-based future. Education 5.0, fueled by new technologies, promises a lot in terms of teaching, learning, and institutional effectiveness. Yet, its deployment cannot be considered separately from the well-being of learners and teachers. In a bid to ensure sustainability, digital education adoption needs to be harmonized with health policy standards at both national and international levels. Frameworks like the WHO’s Health in All Policies strategy and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3: Good Health and well-being, and SDG 4: Quality Education) emphasize the need to strike a balance between technological advancements and outcomes for mental, physical, and social well-being. The conclusions drawn from this research—especially regarding academic stress, sleep disturbance, and electronic overload—underline the need for education reforms to be benchmarked in terms of measurable health parameters like stress levels, ergonomics, digital detox mechanisms, and balanced access to wellness resources. As outlined, aligning Education 5.0 with setting health policy standards guarantees that technological innovation at the level of education prioritizes not just academic achievement but also long-term student well-being. Education and health policy thus pull together in tandem to develop an education ecosystem that is resilient, inclusive, and future-ready.

Practical implications

The conclusions of the study offer immediate guidance for educational institutions, policymakers, and health authorities. The results can be useful to institutions by incorporating digital wellness programs into academic courses, utilizing health benchmarks to track student stress and well-being, and encouraging appropriate technology use through organized guidelines. These results can also be adopted by policymakers to further develop country-level education and healthcare policies so that integration of Education 5.0 not only enables digital transformation but also the long-term physical and mental well-being of students.

Future scope

Subsequent studies must aim at building holistic frameworks that incorporate Education 5.0 with health policy indicators, allowing institutions to measure not just academic achievement but also student well-being metrics like stress levels, sleeping habits, and balance of digital engagement. Longitudinal studies in various educational settings may be able to give greater insights into how new technologies influence mental, physical, and social well-being over time. In addition, collaborative studies among teachers, policymakers, and health professionals could assist in creating evidence-based digital wellness approaches integrated into curricula. Taking this initiative to national and global scales would also enable cross-policy evaluations of how various policy climates affect the long-term realization of Education 5.0 and thus assist in establishing a worldwide model of tech-facilitated, health-oriented education systems.

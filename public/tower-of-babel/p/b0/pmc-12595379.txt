Comparison of Acute Kidney Disease Staging by Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate and by Serum Creatinine Ratio in Patients With Dialysis-requiring Acute Kidney Injury: A National Cohort Study From Taiwan
Authors: Szu-Yu Pan, Nai-Chi Teng, Thomas Tao-Min Huang, Chih-Hsiang Chang, Heng-Chih Pan, Ming-Yen Lin, Shi-Heng Wang, Chih-Cheng Hsu, Shuei-Liong Lin, Vin-Cent Wu, Likwang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rationale & ObjectiveThe comparative performance of 2 staging systems for acute kidney disease (AKD), based on estimated glomerular filtration rate (AKDeGFR) or serum creatinine ratio (AKDsCr), remains uncertain. Our objective is to assess the predictive ability of these staging systems concerning outcomes.Study DesignPopulation-based retrospective observational cohort study.Setting & ParticipantsIn total, 40,849 hospitalized patients with acute kidney injury requiring dialysis between July 1, 2015 and June 30, 2022, in the Taiwan National Health Insurance Research Database.Tests ComparedAKD stages were defined according to the Acute Dialysis Quality Initiative 16 Workgroup (AKDsCr) and 2021 Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes Consensus (AKDeGFR). Cox proportional hazard models were constructed to examine associations between AKD stages and outcomes.OutcomesMortality and sustained kidney recovery.ResultsThe AKDeGFR staging system predicted both outcomes better than the AKDsCr staging system. Hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals for mortality across increasing AKDeGFR stages were 1.29 (1.19-1.41), 1.78 (1.65-1.92), 2.68 (2.49-2.87), 3.11 (2.84-3.42), and 6.34 (6.07-6.63). Conversely, within the AKDsCr staging system, hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals for mortality across ascending stages were 0.84 (0.75-0.94), 0.90 (0.77-1.04), 0.93 (0.75-1.16), and 4.32 (4.18-4.47). Subgroup and sensitivity analyses yielded consistent results.LimitationsRetrospective analysis based on a claims database.ConclusionsWe conclude that the AKDeGFR staging system performs better than the AKDsCr staging system in patients with acute kidney injury requiring dialysis regarding all-cause mortality and kidney recovery. Future investigations are warranted to evaluate the comparative efficacy in patients with acute kidney injury not requiring dialysis.

Introduction

Acute kidney disease (AKD) is defined as newly onset abnormal kidney function, presenting as an increase in serum creatinine (sCr) level, a decrease in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), or development of albuminuria, for less than 3 months.1, 2, 3 AKD can be viewed as a transitional period between acute kidney injury (AKI) and chronic kidney disease (CKD). Accurate outcome prediction and effective treatment during the AKD period are considered critical for managing patients with AKD, although evidence is still accumulating.4

Several studies have proved that the development of AKD, regardless of preceding AKI, is associated with worse outcomes including mortality and major adverse kidney events (MAKEs).5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 However, whether an AKD staging system accurately predicts outcomes in AKD patients remains controversial. The 2012 Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) Clinical Practice Guideline for Acute Kidney Injury defined AKI staging but not AKD staging. The 2017 Acute Dialysis Quality Initiative (ADQI) 16 Workgroup proposed an sCr-based AKD staging system (referred to as the AKDsCr staging system in this article) congruent to the KDIGO AKI staging system.2 Because of difficulties in determining accurate baseline serum creatinine, a 2021 KDIGO Consensus Conference proposed a GFR-based AKD staging system (referred to as the AKDeGFR staging system in this article).3 This system aligns with the KDIGO CKD stages and is intended for use when the initial AKI insult, if present, has stabilized.12,13 Studies by others and us on the performance of the AKDsCr staging system showed conflicting results.14, 15, 16 Furthermore, few if any studies have validated the AKDeGFR staging system to predict outcomes.

In this study, we aim to compare the ability of the AKDsCr and AKDeGFR staging systems to predict mortality and kidney recovery in a national cohort of patients with dialysis-requiring AKI from Taiwan.

Methods

Study Population

This study used the Taiwan National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) through the Applied Health Research Data Integration Service from Taiwan’s National Health Insurance Administration.17 This retrospective cohort study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of National Health Research Institutes, Taiwan (EC1091109-E). The individual information in the NHIRD was encrypted, therefore the requirement to obtain informed consent was waived. The analysis of NHIRD was consistent with our previous studies.16,18 The NHIRD, ranked among the largest databases globally, encompasses nearly all (99%) inpatient and outpatient claims for a population surpassing 22 million.19, 20, 21, 22 As an integral facet of standard protocols aimed at detecting possible instances of insurance fraud, the National Health Insurance Administration conducts regular audits of data and documentation submitted by health care establishments and practitioners.23, 24, 25, 26

We used the data between January 1, 2015 and December 31, 2022, in the database to identify patients receiving in-hospital acute hemodialysis and having AKI. The cohort entry period was established from July 1, 2015 to June 30, 2022, ensuring a minimum observation span of 6 months both before and after cohort entry. The index hospitalization was defined as the first hospitalization for acute hemodialysis. This was characterized by hemodialysis conducted by a catheter placed during the index hospitalization in patients with a baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) exceeding 5 mL/min/1.73 m2, without prior maintenance dialysis therapy, permanent vascular access, or kidney transplantation. AKI was defined by the ratio of peak serum creatinine level during the index hospitalization over the baseline serum creatinine level of more than 1.5 times. The index date was designated as the earliest date during the index hospitalization when the serum creatinine level surged to more than 1.5 times the baseline level. The baseline serum creatinine level was defined as the median of the recorded serum creatinine levels within the 180 days preceding the index date. The baseline eGFR was estimated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) formula.27 To mitigate potential coding discrepancies commonly encountered in database analyses, we delineated dialysis therapy using procedure codes directly associated with reimbursement in the National Health Insurance system, thereby minimizing the likelihood of misclassification. The procedural and diagnostic codes employed in this investigation have been detailed in our previous publication.18

Definition of AKD Stages

The methods for defining AKD stages are summarized in (Fig S1). We defined the time periods with regard to the index date as baseline period (Day D-180 to Day-1), AKI period (Day 0 to Day 7), AKD period (Day 8 to Day 90), and CKD period (beyond Day 91) according to the ADQI 16 Workgroup.2 During the AKD period, patients were classified as “under maintenance dialysis” or “not under maintenance dialysis,” regardless of whether death occurred during the AKD period or afterward. “Not under maintenance dialysis” included “not under dialysis” and “weaning from dialysis.” Patients who had not received any dialysis treatment during the AKD period were defined as “not under dialysis.” For patients who had previously undergone dialysis, if they had been off dialysis for more than 30 consecutive days during the AKD period, they were defined as “weaning from dialysis.” Those who received dialysis therapy without achieving the state of weaning, including 8 patients who died in the AKI period, were defined as “under maintenance dialysis” and were assigned to the AKD stages labeled “dialysis” in both the AKDeGFR and AKDsCr staging systems. Patients who were “not under maintenance dialysis” during the AKD period were assigned AKD stages based on the eGFR (AKDeGFR) and AKD ratio (AKDsCr).

In this study, AKDeGFR stages were defined as stage 0 (eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m2), stage 3A (eGFR 45-60 mL/min/1.73 m2), stage 3B (eGFR 30-45 mL/min/1.73 m2), stage 4 (eGFR 15-30 mL/min/1.73 m2), stage 5 (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m2), and stage “dialysis.” Albuminuria was not used for staging because of a high percentage of missing data. AKDsCr stages were defined as stage 0 (AKD ratio <1.5), stage 1 (AKD ratio 1.5-2), stage 2 (AKD ratio 2-3), stage 3 (AKD ratio ≥3), and stage “Dialysis.” The eGFR level was estimated by the CKD-EPI formula based on the lowest serum creatinine level during the AKD period. The AKD ratio was calculated by the ratio of the lowest serum creatinine level during the AKD period over the baseline serum creatinine level.

Outcome

The primary outcomes of interest were death and sustained renal recovery. Sustained renal recovery was defined, for patients who had received dialysis treatment, as being off dialysis for at least 30 consecutive days from the last dialysis treatment to the end of the observation period. In other words, the duration between the last dialysis session and the end of the observation period was more than 30 days. The cohort was censored at the occurrence of the following events: withdrawal from the National Health Insurance, kidney transplantation after the index date, or the arrival of December 31, 2022.

The methods for covariate, subgroup analysis, sensitivity analysis, and statistical analysis are described in the Supplementary Material: Item S1.

Results

The Patients, Death, and Recovery From Dialysis

We identified 40,849 patients with AKI requiring dialysis and available AKD stages during the index hospitalization (Fig S2). A Sankey diagram illustrated the changes in the proportion of the 40,849 patients in different states (death, alive without maintenance dialysis, or maintenance dialysis) during the AKD and CKD periods (Fig S3). The baseline CKD stages and the proportion of patients receiving dialysis during the AKI periods were also shown. Among the 40,849 patients, most (69%) had a baseline eGFR of less than 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, and 79% received dialysis therapy during the AKI period. During the AKD period, 37% of the patients died, 33% remained alive without requiring maintenance dialysis, and 30% were under maintenance dialysis. The proportions of patients in the 3 different states were similar during the CKD period as during the AKD period.

These results indicate that, in this cohort of patients with dialysis-requiring AKI, a sizable number of deaths occurred, and considerable recoveries from dialysis were also observed during the AKD period.

Distribution of Patient Characteristics Across Different AKD Stages

AKD stages were assigned for the 40,849 patients regardless of whether death occurred. Patients under maintenance dialysis during the AKD period were defined as at the AKD stage of dialysis (n = 27,146), whereas patients weaned from dialysis or not under dialysis (n = 13,703) were assigned AKD stages based on eGFR (AKDeGFR staging) and serum creatinine levels (AKDsCr staging) according to the consensus from the KDIGO Consensus Conference and the ADQI 16 Workgroup, respectively.2,3 Among the 13,703 patients, 6,707 (49%), 1,682 (12%), 1,879 (14%), 2,137 (16%), and 1,298 (9%) were classified as having AKDeGFR stages 0, 3A, 3B, 4, and 5, respectively. In terms of AKDsCr staging, 12,415 (91%), 760 (6%), 359 (3%), and 169 (1%) patients were classified as stages 0, 1, 2, and 3, respectively (Table 1). The severity of AKDsCr stages correlated poorly with AKDeGFR stages, especially in patients with baseline eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, whereas the correlation improved in patients with baseline eGFR > 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 (Table S1). The clinical characteristics were unbalanced across different AKD stages in both the AKDeGFR and AKDsCr staging systems. Interestingly, among the 13,703 patients not under maintenance dialysis during the AKD period, a higher AKDeGFR stage was associated with a lower baseline eGFR level, a higher baseline albumin level, shorter hospitalization duration before the index hospitalization, and fewer unstable clinical conditions during the index hospitalization (including cardiopulmonary resuscitation, cardiac mechanical support, respiratory failure, and vasopressor use). Association between AKD stages and outcomes

We analyzed the association between the 2 different AKD staging systems and outcomes of death and sustained kidney recovery. Sustained kidney recovery was defined as being off dialysis treatment for at least 30 consecutive days from the last dialysis treatment to the end of the observation period in patients who had previously received dialysis treatment. A total of 26,246 (64.3%) deaths and 11,554 (28.3%) sustained kidney recovery occurred among the 40,849 patients (Table S2). In the unadjusted analyses, both a higher AKDeGFR stage and a higher AKDsCr stage were associated with a higher crude mortality rate and a lower chance of sustained kidney recovery (P for trend < 0.001 by Cochran–Armitage test). However, concerning the association between AKD stages and sustained kidney recovery in the 13,703 patients not under maintenance dialysis, the trend was less discernible in the AKDsCr than in the AKDeGFR staging system. Kaplan–Meier plots for survival (Fig S4) and sustained kidney recovery (Fig S5) also showed similar results.

Because several factors associated with morality or sustained kidney recovery were not balanced in patients of different AKD stages (Table 1), we generated multivariable Cox models adjusted for age, sex, frailty, comorbid conditions, medications, serum albumin level, baseline eGFR level, and clinical conditions during the index hospitalization to verify the associations between AKD stages and outcomes. Importantly, in the adjusted models the AKDeGFR stages were all significantly associated with mortality, but the AKDsCr stages were not (Fig 1A and Table S3). A higher AKD stage was significantly associated with a higher mortality hazard in both the AKDeGFR staging system and the AKDsCr staging system (Fig 1A, P for trend < 0.001). Importantly, the AKDeGFR staging system had a higher Harrell’s C-index than the AKDsCr staging system (AKDeGFR 0.786 vs AKDsCr 0.783), a higher continuous integrated discrimination improvement value (AKDeGFR 0.0787 vs AKDsCr 0.0641), and a higher continuous net reclassification improvement value (AKDeGFR 0.240 vs AKDsCr 0.214), indicating a better overall model fit. In terms of recovery from dialysis, both AKDeGFR and AKDsCr stages were significantly associated with sustained kidney recovery in the multivariable Cox models (Fig 1b and Table S4). Both AKD staging systems showed significant trends for the association between increasing AKD stages and decreasing sustained kidney recovery (Fig 1b, P for trend <0.001). However, the point and error estimates of hazard ratios for AKD stage 1 and stage 2 almost overlapped in the AKDsCr staging system. Discussion

In our cohort of patients with dialysis-requiring AKI, we found that the eGFR-based AKD staging system (AKDeGFR) predicts mortality and sustained kidney recovery better than the sCr-based AKD staging system (AKDsCr). However, significant heterogeneities in the ability of either the AKDeGFR or the AKDsCr staging system to predict outcomes exist across several baseline characteristics, especially in the AKD stage of “dialysis.” In addition, modifying the definition of the AKD stage of “dialysis” changes the ability of both staging systems to predict outcomes.

The AKDeGFR Staging System Performed Better Than the AKDsCr Staging System

In a recent study, we found that the AKDsCr staging system was not associated with outcomes of mortality, MAKEs, or major adverse cardiac events in a cohort of patients with dialysis-requiring AKI.16 In the current study, we extended the scope to include the AKDeGFR staging system, aiming to compare its performance with that of the AKDsCr staging system. Despite differences in patient inclusion criteria between the 2 studies, both demonstrated that the AKDsCr staging system does not reliably predict mortality in patients with AKI requiring dialysis. On the contrary, a higher AKD stage in the AKDeGFR staging system is strongly associated with a higher risk for either mortality or nonrecovery of kidney function.

The primary distinction between the AKDsCr and AKDeGFR staging systems lies in their criteria for assigning a higher AKD stage. In the AKDsCr staging system, a stage is denoted based on changes in serum creatinine level relative to the baseline (AKD ratio). In the AKDeGFR staging system, a stage is defined by the eGFR level during the AKD period, regardless of the baseline eGFR level.

The AKDsCr Staging System is Limited in Patients with CKD

In the AKDsCr staging system, given a similar change in absolute serum creatinine levels, a lower AKD ratio (and a lower AKD stage) is expected when the baseline serum creatinine level is elevated. As a result, in patients with significant baseline CKD, the sensitivity of the AKDsCr staging system to detect changes in kidney function is reduced. For example, in a 60-year-old male patient with a baseline serum creatinine level of 2.0 mg/dL (corresponding to an eGFR of 38 mL/min/1.73 m2) based on the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation, a creatinine level of 2.9 mg/dL (an eGFR of 24 mL/min/1.73 m2) during the AKD period results in discordant AKD stages in both staging systems (AKDsCr stage 0 and AKDeGFR stage 4).27 In our cohort with 47% of patients having a baseline eGFR of <30 mL/min/1.73 m2, the ability of the AKDsCr staging system to identify patients with high AKDsCr stages is limited, reflecting the small proportion of patients (169 patients, 0.4%) having AKDsCr stage 3 in our cohort. Notably, when the analysis was restricted to patients without CKD (eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m2), the AKDsCr and AKDeGFR staging systems were highly correlated (Table S1) and their performance in predicting mortality and sustained kidney recovery was comparable (Figs 2, 3, S6, and S7). Interestingly, in a study analyzing hospitalized patients with AKI not requiring dialysis, the positive associations between a higher AKDsCr stage and a higher risk for MAKEs were more discernible in patients without CKD than in patients with CKD.14 These results highlighted the limitation of the AKDsCr staging system in patients with baseline CKD.

The Importance of Including the AKD Stage of “Dialysis” in the Analysis

In this study, we included the AKD stage of “dialysis” in both the AKDsCr and AKDeGFR staging systems for the prediction of outcomes. The AKD stage of “dialysis” was assigned to patients receiving maintenance dialysis without being off dialysis treatment for more than 30 days during the AKD period. As expected, the patients in the AKD stage of “dialysis” were associated with the worst survival and sustained kidney recovery. Importantly, more than half of the cohort (27,146 patients, 66.5%) was assigned the AKD stage of “dialysis” in our cohort, and significant heterogeneity existed within this population (Figs 2, 3, S6, and S7). In addition, changing the definition of this stage also changes the association between AKD stages and outcomes (Tables 2 and 3). These results indicate the importance of including this population in the analysis.

Study Limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, only patients hospitalized for acute hemodialysis and having AKI were included in the analysis. Whether the results can be generalized to patients with AKI not requiring dialysis or community-acquired AKI is unknown. Second, the definition of sustained renal recovery in patients with AKI requiring dialysis in our study may be different from other studies. The ADQI 16 Workgroup suggested a 14-day interval of dialysis independence to define renal recovery in patients with AKI requiring acute dialysis (Consensus Statement 3A).2 However, because the reimbursement of peritoneal dialysis is declared monthly in Taiwan, we define sustained renal recovery as being off dialysis for at least 30 days before the end of the observation period. Third, we did not have sufficient data of proteinuria during the AKD period in our cohort. Whether the incorporation of proteinuria (as suggested by the KDIGO consensus) will further enhance the performance of the AKDeGFR staging system warrants further investigation.3 Fourth, the lowest serum creatinine level used to define AKD stages in our study might be affected by dialysis treatment because patients were classified as “not under maintenance dialysis” and assigned a nondialysis AKD stage if they had been off dialysis for at least 30 days during the AKD period. In addition, the most representative timing of serum creatinine measurement to define the AKD stage is yet to be answered. However, using the serum creatinine level closest to D90 instead of the lowest serum creatinine level to define AKD stages yielded consistent results (Tables 2 and 3), indicating that our approach to defining AKD stages is reasonable. Future studies may be needed to investigate using measured GFR or other biomarkers such as cystatin C to predict residual renal function and AKD stages in patients under dialysis.28 Fifth, the retrospective analysis using a claim database might introduce biases. However, our rigorous subgroup and sensitivity analyses provide robust support for the conclusion that the AKDeGFR staging system predicts outcomes better than the AKDsCr staging system, especially in patients with CKD.

In conclusion, in a national cohort of patients with hospitalized AKI requiring dialysis from Taiwan, the AKDeGFR staging system outperformed the AKDsCr staging system in predicting mortality and sustained renal recovery. Future studies are warranted to investigate the comparative performance of the AKDeGFR and AKDsCr staging systems in patients with non-dialysis-requiring AKI.

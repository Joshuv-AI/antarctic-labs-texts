Prediction of postoperative complications following transanal total mesorectal excision in middle and low rectal cancer: development and internal validation of a clinical prediction model
Authors: Zhu Jingtao, Wu Bin, Bai Haoyu, Lin Hexin, Yu Xuejun, Wang Tinghao, Xu Zhiwen, You Jun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe objective of this study is to develop a nomogram for the personalized prediction of postoperative complication risks in patients with middle and low rectal cancer who are undergoing transanal total mesorectal excision (taTME). This tool aims to assist clinicians in early identification of high-risk patients and in addressing preoperative risk factors to enhance surgical safety.MethodsIn this case–control study, 207 patients diagnosed with middle and low rectal cancer and undergoing taTME between February 2018 and November 2023 at The First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University were included. Independent risk factors for postoperative complications were analyzed using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression and multifactorial logistic regression models. A predictive nomogram was constructed using R Studio.ResultsAmong the 207 patients, 57 (27.5%) experienced postoperative complications. The LASSO and multifactorial logistic regression analyses identified operation time (OR = 1.010, P = 0.007), smoking history (OR = 9.693, P < 0.001), anastomotic technique (OR = 0.260, P = 0.004), and ASA score (OR = 9.077, P = 0.051) as significant predictors. These factors were integrated into the nomogram. The model's accuracy was validated through receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration curves, consistency indices, and decision curve analysis.ConclusionThe developed nomogram, incorporating operation time, smoking history, anastomotic technique, and ASA score, effectively forecasts postoperative complication risks in taTME procedures. It is a valuable tool for clinicians to identify patients at heightened risk and initiate timely interventions, ultimately improving patient outcomes.

Introduction

The gold standard for rectal cancer surgery is total mesorectal excision (TME), as popularized by Heald. The integrity of rectal mesenteric specimens is a crucial criterion in assessing surgical quality and predicting local tumor recurrence [1–3]. In patients with a narrow pelvis, obesity, or large tumors, transanal total mesorectal excision (taTME) provides unique advantages [4, 5]. This technique overcomes the limitations of the field of view in a narrow pelvis, enabling better visualization of the distal rectum and precise determination of the distal resection margin, potentially increasing the likelihood of radical resection. A meta-analysis of 14 clinical studies comparing laparoscopic TME (laTME) and taTME revealed that the taTME group outperformed the laTME group in terms of secondary surgery rate within 30 days, postoperative hospital stay, overall complication rate, serious complication rate, and anastomotic leakage rate [6]. However, the technical challenges of taTME have led to high complication and local recurrence rates in some studies [7–10], contributing to controversy and hindering its widespread adoption for treating middle and low rectal cancer.

Identifying patients at high risk of complications may assist in selecting risk-adapted surgical approaches and perioperative interventions to mitigate complications and bolster surgeon confidence. Consequently, various scoring systems, such as the Estimation of Physiologic Ability and Surgical Stress (E-PASS) and the Physiological and Operative Severity Score for the Enumeration of Mortality and Morbidity (POSSUM), have been developed to predict postoperative complication risk [11, 12]. Nevertheless, these algorithms lack specificity for taTME, and no clinical prediction models currently exist for anticipating perioperative complication rates specific to taTME. To address this gap, our study aimed to identify principal predictors of postoperative complications in taTME through the application of the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression and multifactorial logistic regression and develop a Nomogram for the personalized prediction of postoperative complication risks in patients with middle and low rectal cancer who are undergoing taTME. This tool aims to aid clinicians in the early identification of high-risk patients and in addressing preoperative risk factors to improve surgical safety.

Patients and methods

Study subjects

This retrospective case–control study included patients who underwent taTME from February 2018 to November 2023 at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University. Inclusion criteria were: 1) pathologically confirmed rectal adenocarcinoma before surgery; 2) tumor located below the level of the peritoneal reflection; 3) patients who underwent taTME with or without intersphincteric resection (ISR). Exclusion criteria included: 1) patients with evidence of preoperative distant organ metastases; 2) patients requiring multi-organ resection; 3) absolute contraindications to general anesthesia or prolonged pneumoperitoneum;4)emergency surgery due to bleeding, bowel obstruction, and perforation; 5) patients with incomplete clinical or pathological data.

The study has received approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University (approval no.XMYY-2024KY034), and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

This study adheres to the STROBE reporting recommendations of the Equator Network.

Surgical technique

The taTME procedure adheres to the consensus and operating guidelines of Chinese experts on transanal endoscopic surgery [13]. The same team performed all surgical procedures in this study. In this context, taTME refers to laparoscopic-assisted taTME. The surgeries were conducted concurrently by two surgical teams: one performing the transanal procedures and the other the transabdominal procedures, both experienced with over 200 laparoscopic rectal resections.All patients underwent preoperative mechanical intestinal preparation the day before surgery and received prophylactic intravenous antibiotics 60 min prior to surgery.

Variables and definitions

The study included 207 patients, with data collected by two independent researchers. The data encompassed sex, age, body mass index (BMI), comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes), previous abdominal surgery, smoking history, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, preoperative neoadjuvant therapy, and tumor edge distance from the anal verge (measured via MRI or colonoscopy/CT if MRI was not possible). Preoperative serum albumin (ALB), total protein (TP), and hemoglobin (HB) levels were assessed from blood samples taken a week before surgery. Intraoperative variables included total operation time (transabdominal and transanal), anastomotic technique (manual or stapled), combined intersphincteric resection (ISR), creation of a diverting ileostomy, and intraoperative blood loss. Pathological characteristics evaluated were tumor diameter and stage.

The primary outcome of this study was the incidence of complications within 30 days postoperatively.Complications were classified according to the Clavien-Dindo (CD) grading system: Grade I (complications managed with routine measures such as antipyretics or analgesics and not requiring medication treatment), Grade II (complications necessitating treatment beyond routine measures), Grade III (complications requiring surgical or endoscopic intervention), Grade IV (life-threatening complications such as cerebral hemorrhage), and Grade V (patient death) [14].

Statistical analysis

Data grouping

Data were divided into a training set and a validation set using the 'caret' function in R, with patients randomly allocated to either the training set (70%) or the validation set (30%). The training set was used to develop a risk prediction model, and the validation set was employed to assess the model's accuracy.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous data following normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data were expressed as median and interquartile ranges (IQR). Categorical data were reported as frequencies and percentages. As appropriate, differences between patient groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test, Mann–Whitney U test, or Chi-square test. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Model Development

(1) Independent risk factors were identified using the LASSO regression, employing the 'glmnet' package in R. The LASSO regression, a penalized regression method, estimates regression coefficients by maximizing the log-likelihood function while constraining the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients [15], thereby eliminating non-essential covariates and reducing high-dimensional data. Compared to traditional linear regression methods, it avoids overfitting by applying penalty terms to the magnitude of model coefficients, thus providing better predictive performance and model interpretability. (2) Following the selection of predictor variables via LASSO regression, those with a P-value < 0.1 were utilized in building the risk prediction model using binary logistic regression with the 'glm' function in R. (3) To illustrate the relative importance of each predictor and enhance the model's practicality in clinical settings, a nomogram was constructed using the 'rms' package in R, based on the results from the multivariable logistic model.

Model evaluation and validation

The model's performance was assessed regarding its discriminative ability, calibration, and clinical utility. Harrell’s C-index was used to gauge the predictive accuracy of the nomogram [16], with values ranging from 0.5 (indicating no predictive value) to 1.0 (perfect discrimination). Generally, a C-index > 0.7 indicates good discriminative ability. The model's fit was evaluated using a calibration curve, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was comparable to the C-index. Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) also quantified the net benefits across various threshold probabilities to ascertain the model's clinical usefulness [17]. A calibration curve evaluates the consistency between actual and predicted results.

Results

Clinical characteristics of patients

The cohort comprised 135 males (65.2%) with an average age of 59.7 ± 11.4 years. The mean Body Mass Index (BMI) was 23.1 ± 3.0 kg/m2. Preoperative neoadjuvant therapy was administered to 61 patients (29.5%). Surgical procedures were concurrently performed by two teams, one managing transabdominal and the other transanal approaches. Predictors screening

Using the "glmnet" package in R software, All independent variables were screened using LASSO regression, and the adjustment parameter λ was validated using the ten-fold crossover method. Conversely, if the regression coefficient is not zero, it indicates that the variable is strongly associated with the occurrence of complications in patients after taTME. Of the 22 variables analyzed, 9 had nonzero coefficients (Fig. 1), including operation time, smoking history, BMI, gender, diabetes, anastomotic technique, ASA score, combined ISR, and TNM stage. Multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed statistical significance in operation time, smoking history, anastomotic technique, and ASA score between groups. Discussion

The proximity of the tumor to the anus presents significant challenges in traditional laTME, especially when conducting delicate procedures in the pelvic region and perirectal mesentery. These challenges are more pronounced in patients who are obese men, those with an enlarged prostate, and individuals with pelvic stenosis. Transanal total mesorectal excision was developed to overcome these difficulties, specifically addressing limited exposure in the surgical field, navigating complex anatomical planes, and managing the intricacies of anastomosis. The principal advantage of taTME is the improved access it provides to the distal rectum, potentially enhancing visualization and facilitating a more complete resection of the rectum and mesentery. Several studies have shown that taTME results in lower rates of positive pathological circumferential margins than conventional laTME surgery [6, 18, 19]. However, taTME's requirement for specialized instruments and operation within a confined space entails technical challenges, leading to a prolonged learning curve and initially higher rates of surgical complications [20]. The current study reports a 27.5% complication rate within 30 days post-surgery, aligning with other taTME research findings [21–23]. Previous research indicates that mastering taTME takes about 40 cases, after which the postoperative complication rate significantly declines [20]. This study compared postoperative complication rates between the initial 40 cases and a subsequent set of 167 cases, finding a reduction in the latter group. However, the difference was not statistically significant (35.0% vs. 25.7%, P = 0.239).

Numerous studies have focused on identifying risk factors for anastomotic fistula following taTME [8, 21, 24]. However, there is a lack of research exploring risk factors for overall postoperative complications post-taTME and establishing a clinical prediction model. This study introduces and validates a novel nomogram, which incorporates operative time, smoking history, anastomotic technique, and ASA score, utilizing the LASSO regression and multifactorial logistic analysis. These four variables effectively predict post-taTME complication probabilities. Prior research indicates longer colorectal surgery durations correlate with increased 30-day postoperative complications [25]. Consistently, our findings reveal that extended surgical durations elevate the risk of bacterial contamination and postoperative pulmonary infections due to prolonged exposure and increased effects of mechanical ventilation and anesthetic drugs. Additionally, longer surgeries may intensify surgical team fatigue, potentially leading to technical errors. Factors influencing surgery duration include operation complexity and surgeon skill, yet standardized criteria for assessing surgical complexity still need to be improved. Therefore, operation time serves as an objective assessment metric. In our study, smoking history emerged as a significant independent risk factor for post-taTME complications. Smoking-induced nicotine and carbon monoxide constrict blood vessels, impair tissue perfusion, induce cellular hypoxia, and hinder tissue healing [26]. Smoking also damages respiratory mucosa epithelial cells, reduces ciliary motion in the trachea and bronchi, and weakens alveolar macrophages' phagocytic and anti-inflammatory functions, thus escalating postoperative pulmonary complication risks [27]. The impact of anastomosis type on related complications needs to be better defined. While Cong et al. [28] reported that stapled anastomosis decreases anastomotic leakage and stenosis compared to manual anastomosis, a comprehensive international taTME registry study did not observe a significant association between anastomosis type and anastomotic fistula occurrence [21, 29]. This study identified manual anastomosis as an independent risk factor following taTME. We hypothesize a significant correlation between the use of manual anastomosis and two factors: tumor edge distance from the anal verge and the integration with ISR. Consequently, manual anastomosis typically indicates a lower tumor position and greater depth of tumor infiltration. These factors likely increase surgical complexity, extend operation duration, and elevate the risk of associated complications. Lastly, the ASA score is increasingly utilized in perioperative patient management and is strongly linked to postoperative complication and mortality rates [30, 31]. We found that higher ASA scores correlate with increased postoperative complications.

This study developed a nomogram to predict postoperative complication rates in patients undergoing taTME. The model, utilizing a nomogram approach, offers enhanced visualization, simplicity, and ease of understanding compared to traditional logistic regression methods. It calculates the likelihood of complications by assigning scores to various risk factors, thereby serving as an effective clinical tool for early postoperative complication prediction in taTME cases.

However, the study is subject to certain limitations. Primarily, as a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size and exclusion of patients with incomplete data, it is susceptible to selection bias. Additionally, the study reflects our center's initial experience with taTME, potentially underrepresenting cases still within the surgeon's learning curve. Moreover, a notable shortcoming is the lack of a robust external validation group to assess the model's predictive efficacy.

The study presents a prediction model based on operative time, smoking history, anastomotic technique, and ASA score. This model, leveraging readily available clinical data, offers relative accuracy. It aids in the early identification and management of high-risk patients, enhancing surgical safety, improving patient outcomes, and potentially broadening taTME's clinical application.

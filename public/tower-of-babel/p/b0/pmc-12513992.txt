Utilization of sequential model-based optimizer integrated machine learning models in correlation of famotidine solubility in supercritical carbon dioxide
Authors: Hadil Faris Alotaibi, Chou-Yi Hsu, Fadhil Faez Sead, Anupam Yadav, Renuka S. Jyothi, Swati Mishra, Bilakshan Purohit, Egambergan Khudaynazarov, Murodjon Yaxshimuratov, Ashish Singh Chauhan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We investigated solubility variations of a medication in supercritical carbon dioxide with an insight into preparation of nanomedicines with improved aqueous solubility. As the case study, the solubility of famotidine (FAM) medicine in sc-CO2 (supercritical carbon dioxide) was computed as a function of temperature and pressure, with a particular focus on modeling and predicting solubility and sc-CO2 density. Three regression models with machine learning behavior including Quadratic Polynomial Regression (QPR), Weighted Least Squares (WLS), and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) were employed to analyze the data, and Sequential Model-Based Optimization (SMBO) was utilized for hyper-parameter tuning. Among these models, the best-performing model for predicting FAM solubility was the QPR model, with an impressive coefficient of determination (R2) of 0.95858 for all sets including training and validation. Additionally, QPR exhibited low MAPE of 1.64278E + 00, RMSE of 9.6833E-02, and a maximum error of 1.49480E-01, while exhibiting a higher maximum error of 18.99 kg/m³ for density predictions, indicating areas for potential improvement. These results highlight the accuracy and precision of the QPR model in predicting FAM solubility in sc-CO2. For the prediction of sc-CO2 density, QPR again proved to be the most effective model with a remarkable R2 score of 0.99733. This model achieved a low MAPE of 1.06004E-02, RMSE of 8.4072E + 00, and a maximum error of 1.89894E + 01. The QPR model demonstrates its exceptional capability in accurately predicting sc-CO2 density in terms of temperature and pressure.

Introduction

Machine learning (ML) has revolutionized our ability to extract valuable insights from complex datasets, making it an indispensable tool in various scientific and engineering domains. One of the fundamental tasks in ML is regression analysis, which aims to model the relationship between input variables and output variables1–3. In pharmaceutical area, machine learning can be exploited in a wide range from drug discovery to estimation of thermodynamic properties of APIs (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients), while these models rely on the collection of data and training ML algorithms along with optimizers4–7. In fact, ML has been recognized as a useful and powerful tool in developing and solving the problems in the pharmaceutical industry.

Considering that continuous manufacturing (CM) and poor solubility of medications are major drawbacks and challenges in the pharmaceutical industry, some ML based tools have been recently developed and customized for addressing these challenges. A number of ML regressive models have been proposed for description and modeling continuous pharmaceutical granulation and crystallization where the critical quality attributes of pharmaceutical materials are estimated using ML8–10. For solving the poor solubility of medications, ML techniques can be used for estimation of drugs solubility in different solvents for production of nanomedicines. The solubility for nanomedicines is higher than normal drugs because of increased surface area. Indeed, the ML models are great tools for estimation of solubility for preparation of drugs nanoparticles with improved solubility11–13. Different methods can be adopted for production of drugs nanoparticles such as chemical and mechanical methods.

Supercritical carbon dioxide (sc-CO2) is an environmentally friendly and versatile solvent used in numerous industrial processes, including pharmaceuticals. Understanding its solvation behavior, particularly in relation to the solubility of specific compounds like small molecules, is of paramount importance in the pharmaceutical industry, where sc-CO2 is employed in various extraction and purification processes. The method of supercritical solvent processing is an attractive method especially with CO2 as the solvent owing to its attractive supercritical properties and low price of solvent. CO2 is also suitable for medical applications and is regarded as green and safe solvent. From the sustainability point of view, CO2 is a great choice as a solvent unlike the organic solvents which are used for drug processing. At the pressure higher than 7.38 MPa and temperature above 304.2 K, CO2 is considered as supercritical (sc-CO2) and can be used as solvent for dissolution of different medications14–16. The solute can be easily dissolved in the solvent at the supercritical point and then recovered at the end of process by lowering the pressure17. In this method, solubility is an important factor, and the drug should have an acceptable level of solubility in sc-CO2 for consideration of drug in this process. Thus, the solubility needs to be measured and determined for applying drug in this process.

Thermodynamic models have been applied for estimation of phase equilibria and solubility of medicines in sc-CO2, however applying these models for many drug molecules would be time consuming and might have errors in the calculations. Instead, machine learning models are preferred which have been used with greater accuracy than those thermodynamic models for solubility of medications in sc-CO218–20. So, these models can be further explored in pharmaceutical solubility studies for diverse drugs to make a better prediction of medications solubility with diverse regressive models and optimizers.

In the context of this study, we delve into the application of ML regression methods to predict two critical parameters in the solubility of famotidine (FAM) in sc-CO2 namely, the density of sc-CO2 and the solubility of FAM as small-molecule drug. This research employs three distinct ML regression models: Quadratic Polynomial Regression (QPR), Weighted Least Squares (WLS), and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) for solubility estimation. The models were developed and evaluated utilizing a dataset in which temperature and pressure served as the input parameters, while the outputs comprised the density of supercritical CO₂ and the solubility of FAM.

QPR model fits a quadratic polynomial equation to the data, capturing potential non-linear relationships between the input and output variables. WLS introduces weighted coefficients to the least squares regression, accounting for heteroscedasticity in the data. This method is valuable when the variance of the errors is not constant across different data points. OMP is a feature selection technique that identifies the most relevant input variables for predicting the output variables. It can provide insights into the most influential factors affecting solubility.

This research provides multiple significant advancements within the domains of chemical and pharmaceutical engineering. This research serves as a valuable contribution to the ongoing efforts in the pharmaceutical and chemical engineering communities to harness the power of ML for optimizing processes involving sc-CO2. The accurate prediction of solubility and density under varying conditions can potentially lead to more efficient and sustainable industrial practices. These ML models and the optimizer are used for the first time in analysis of FAM solubility.

Data for models

In this study, the dataset utilized for analysis was sourced from reference21 which contains solubility information concerning Famotidine (FAM) within a supercritical carbon dioxide (sc-CO2) environment. The dataset encompasses a spectrum of temperature and pressure conditions which are withing the supercritical range for CO2. The distributions between input and output variables are analyzed prior to the modeling, and both density of sc-CO2 and API solubility have been regarded as the outputs.

Method

The methodology followed in this study (Fig. 1) begins with data preprocessing, during which normalization was performed using the min–max scaling technique. Subsequently, the dataset is divided into separate training and testing subsets, adhering to an 80 − 20 ratio. Following this, predictive models are constructed and tested using the FAM solubility dataset. During this phase, hyperparameter tuning is conducted to attain optimal performance using SMBO. In the evaluation phase, the most suitable model is chosen for each specific output, which forms the basis for the final analysis. All simulations were implemented in Python 3.8 (https://www.python.org). To ensure reproducibility, the following key library versions were used: NumPy (v1.21.0), pandas (v1.3.0), scikit-learn (v0.24.2), TensorFlow (v2.6.0) [or PyTorch if used], and Matplotlib (v3.4.2).

Sequential Model-Based optimization (SMBO)

SMBO is a powerful approach that utilizes Bayesian optimization and model-based strategies to optimize hyperparameter of machine learning models20,22,23. Hyperparameters are model configurations that influence the accuracy of a ML algorithm. In contrast to model parameters—which are estimated directly from the training data—hyperparameters are determined prior to the learning process and are not inferred during model fitting20,24.

SMBO use Bayesian optimization techniques to iteratively assess and refine a surrogate model, while in each iteration, SMBO determines the subsequent hyperparameter configuration to assess by balancing exploration and exploitation. Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) is a sparse regression method designed for high-dimensional datasets, particularly when the number of predictors substantially exceeds the number of observations. OMP iteratively selects a subset of predictors that are most relevant to the response variable, making it useful for feature selection and dimensionality reduction.

The OMP algorithm aims to find the best-fitting model by iteratively adding predictors while ensuring orthogonality between selected predictors. Conclusion

The solubility of famotidine (FAM) in sc-CO2 as a function of temperature and pressure is investigated in this study, with a particular emphasis on modeling and predicting solubility and sc-CO2 density. To analyze the data, three regression models were used: Quadratic Polynomial Regression (QPR), Weighted Least Squares (WLS), and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP), with Sequential Model-Based Optimization (SMBO) used for hyper-parameter tuning. The Quadratic Polynomial Regression (QPR) model performed the best for predicting solubility, with an impressive coefficient of determination (R2) score of 0.95858. Furthermore, QPR had a low Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of 1.64278E + 00, an RMSE of 9.6833E-02, and a maximum error of 1.49480E-01. These findings highlight the QPR model’s accuracy and precision in predicting FAM solubility in sc-CO2. QPR was once again the most effective model for predicting sc-CO2 density, with a remarkable R2 score of 0.99733. The MAPE for this model was 1.06004E-02, the RMSE was 8.4072E + 00, and the maximum error was 1.89894E + 01. Overall, the model provides reliable predictions of sc-CO₂ density, with errors generally within 2–9% of reference values. While this accuracy is sufficient for many engineering applications, the results indicate that caution is warranted in scenarios requiring very high precision. The relatively lower performance of WLS and OMP can be attributed to the fact that, although they account for heteroscedasticity and sparsity, they are less effective at capturing the nonlinear interactions between pressure, temperature, and solubility compared to QPR, which better accommodates such complexity.

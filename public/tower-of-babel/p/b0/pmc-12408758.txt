Letter to the editor
Authors: Karlijn J P van Wessem, Kim E M Benders, Luke P H Leenen, Falco Hietbrink
Published: 2025

Dear editor,

First of all, we would like to thank Dr. Khan et al. for their thoughtful and constructive comments regarding our manuscript. We appreciate the opportunity to clarify and expand on several key aspects of our study.

We acknowledge that clinical practices inevitably evolve over an extended study period. However, the overarching treatment philosophy for severely injured patients remained consistent throughout. The observed trends—namely, a gradual decrease in crystalloid use (approximately 205 mL per year) and an increase in FFP administration (approximately 0.35 units per year)—were central to the research question and represent the key exposure variables under investigation. While TXA was introduced into our trauma system before the study began, its usage increased over time. To account for this, TXA was included in the multivariable analysis assessing thromboembolic complications. It did not emerge as an independent predictor. Aside from this, we did not identify other significant systematic changes that would have meaningfully confounded our findings.

Further, we agree that our findings are specific to a distinct population—severely injured patients admitted to the ICU in an urban Dutch Level I trauma center. The exclusion of patients with isolated TBI, burns, drowning, or asphyxiation, as well as the local care protocols, do limit generalizability to other trauma systems, particularly those with higher rates of penetrating trauma or different logistical constraints (e.g., rural or military environments). However, cohort studies are inherently designed to provide in-depth insight into a defined patient group under a specific system of care. Our intention was to describe outcomes within this context, recognizing these limitations. The findings can nonetheless serve as a reference point for comparison or hypothesis generation in broader, multi-center studies.

With regard to viscoelastic testing, its omission was based on prior institutional research which demonstrated that it did not contribute additional value in guiding resuscitation in our population [1].

Additionally, we concur that the low incidence of ARDS limited our ability to draw strong conclusions regarding associations with resuscitation strategies. Anticipating this limitation, we made an a priori decision to exclude ARDS from multivariable analyses and transparently acknowledged this in the methods and results sections of the manuscript. Based on standard assumptions, a sample size of approximately 1300–1500 patients would have been required to achieve 80% power for ARDS-related outcomes—well beyond the size of our cohort.

We thank Dr. Khan and colleagues once again for their detailed and constructive feedback, and we believe they contribute meaningfully to the ongoing debate in trauma resuscitation research.

Sincerely,

Karlijn J.P. van Wessem, Kim E.M. Benders, Luke P.H. Leenen, and Falco Hietbrink.

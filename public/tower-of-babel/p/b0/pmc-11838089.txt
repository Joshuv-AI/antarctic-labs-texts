Apple recognition in complex environments based on FC-DETR
Authors: Lijun Hu, Xu Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aims to address the challenges of apple recognition in complex environments by proposing a solution based on the FC-DETR model. Apple cultivation is an important part of agriculture in Xinjiang, but the increasing shortage of labor has driven the demand for automated harvesting technologies. Therefore, research and development of apple recognition technology in complex environments have become crucial. While CNN architecture models can accurately identify apples in conventional settings, their inherent receptive field limitations prevent them from fully capturing global features in noisy and complex environments, making it difficult to achieve the accuracy and robustness required for practical applications. To solve this problem, this study proposes a real-time object detection model, FC-DETR. This model incorporates the innovative FEMA-BasicBlock residual module, the CAFM cross-scale adaptive feature fusion module, and the novel Inner-WIoU loss function, thereby enhancing feature processing, multi-scale feature selection and integration, and detection accuracy. Experimental results show that the FC-DETR model achieves an apple recognition accuracy of 87 % and a recall rate of 82 % in complex backgrounds while maintaining a lightweight design. This study not only makes significant advances in automated apple harvesting technology but also contributes to improving the efficiency and sustainability of the apple industry in Xinjiang and globally.

Introduction

Apple is one of the most popular and economically valuable fruits worldwide. In the Xinjiang region of China, particularly the Aksu apples, are highly favored by consumers for their unique quality and taste. In 2023, China's apple production reached 47.5718 million tons, ranking first in the world, with Xinjiang's apple production accounting for 2.1596 million tons. This vast production volume underscores the substantial labor demand, particularly during harvest time. Traditional manual harvesting methods are insufficient to meet market needs, highlighting the necessity of orchard automation through machine vision as a critical technological advancement.

As image acquisition equipment becomes more affordable and convenient, researchers globally have increasingly applied deep learning-based object detection methods to apple recognition. For instance, Sun et al. utilized FBoT-Net for green apple recognition, enhancing feature discrimination by modifying elements of the ResNet-50 architecture [1]. Zhao et al. effectively detected apples on trees and the ground using YOLOv8 via transfer learning [2]. Praveen improved the YOLOv5 architecture with attention modules and refined loss functions to enhance the accuracy and quality of small object detection [3]. Kang introduced LedNet, which boosted model detection performance through feature pyramid networks and elastic spatial pyramid pooling [4]. Ma et al. optimized YOLOv8n′s network perception by incorporating ShuffleNetv2 and C2f modules with SE attention mechanisms, dynamically adjusting anchor box quality thresholds and gradient gain allocation by replacing the Wiou loss function [5].

Despite these advancements, convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures have predominantly been applied to apple recognition tasks in ideal [6,7], conventional settings. In the complex and variable conditions of real-world orchards, especially under extreme weather and significant occlusions between fruits and leaves in the Aksu region, the limitations of CNN architectures become evident. Their inherent receptive field constraints hinder comprehensive global feature capture, particularly in high-noise environments, resulting in compromised recognition performance and failing to meet the accuracy and robustness needed for practical applications.

On the other hand, traditional Transformer architecture models, although showing advantages in handling complex scenarios and noise, face the bottleneck of high computational costs, limiting their widespread deployment in real-time detection.

Therefore, this paper innovatively proposes the FC-DETR model, aiming to improve the accuracy and real-time performance of apple recognition in complex environments. Firstly, we designed a lightweight cross-scale adaptive feature module (CAFM), which significantly enhances the model's feature extraction capability in complex orchard environments. Through cross-scale fusion and adaptive learning, it effectively addresses issues such as fruit occlusion and varying lighting conditions. To further reduce the computational burden, we developed a lightweight residual module (FEMA-BasicBlock), which significantly reduces computational complexity while ensuring model performance, laying the foundation for real-time detection.

To address the severe occlusions caused by the dense growth of apples in the Aksu region, we proposed the Inner-WIoU loss function [8,9]. This loss mechanism guides the model to focus on medium-quality anchor boxes, providing a more accurate evaluation of overlapping regions and significantly accelerating model convergence, ensuring efficient recognition in complex occlusion scenarios.

Compared to existing methods, FC-DETR effectively addresses complex noise and occlusion problems in orchard environments while retaining the advantages of real-time detection. By combining CAFM and FEMA-BasicBlock, the FC-DETR model not only demonstrates stronger feature extraction capabilities and robustness in complex environments but also significantly reduces computational complexity, ensuring efficient performance in practical deployments.

Materials and methods

Data collection and construction

This study collected 2240 high-quality apple images in Aral City, Xinjiang. The images were captured using a Canon 800D camera from October 10 to 27, 2023, at 4 p.m. and 10 p.m. daily. The image resolution is 6000 × 4000 pixels. During the collection process, we meticulously recorded the variations of apples at different maturity stages, under various lighting conditions, and from different shooting angles and distances, thoroughly capturing the colors, sizes, and their relative positions and occlusions with other fruits or leaves. We also took into account the impact of different weather conditions on apple recognition. This data provides rich feature information for the subsequent algorithm development. Fig. 1 shows some cropped image samples, reflecting the visual performance of the fruits under different environmental conditions.Fig. 1Partial presentation of the dataset (including images of apples at different maturity stages, with different levels of shading, various weather noise conditions, and different lighting conditions).Fig. 1

Partial presentation of the dataset (including images of apples at different maturity stages, with different levels of shading, various weather noise conditions, and different lighting conditions).

By processing the captured images, we eliminated highly similar images and ultimately obtained 2240 high-quality images. The dataset includes 1640 images under normal lighting conditions and 600 images under shaded conditions (shaded images refer to those where part or all of the apple is covered by shadows due to lighting angles, obstructions, etc.). These images were precisely annotated using Labelme software. To validate the model's generalization performance, the dataset was randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets in a ratio of 7:2:1, as shown in Fig. 2(a). Conducting data division beforehand ensures the independence of the training and test sets, avoiding data leakage and thus accurately evaluating the model's performance. Additionally, this method better controls the data augmentation ratio, ensuring consistent proportions of original images, strong light, rain, and heavy fog in both the training and test sets. Specifically, the training set contains 1568 images, the validation set contains 448 images, and the test set contains 224 images.Fig. 2Data distribution. To further simulate the complex environments brought about by different weather conditions, we used the Albumentations library to process the data [10], adding noise from solar flares, rain, and heavy fog. During data augmentation, each image was subjected to only one type of weather noise at a time, and these noises were added to the images with equal probability. Ultimately, we obtained 3136 training images, 896 validation images, and 448 test images, ensuring that the proportion of original images, solar flares, rain, and heavy fog in the training, validation, and test sets was approximately 3:1:1:1, as shown in Fig. 2(b). This ratio ensures the diversity and balance of the dataset, allowing the model to learn under various weather conditions while preserving the importance of the original images, thereby improving the model's generalization ability and robustness in complex environments.

Research background and objectives

With the advancement of agricultural automation, the technology for automated apple picking has gained increasing attention. However, complex environmental conditions, such as variable weather and lighting changes, pose significant challenges to apple recognition technology. This study aims to develop a model capable of efficiently recognizing apples in complex environments, thereby improving the accuracy and stability of automated apple picking [11].

Apple recognition method in complex environments using FC-DETR

This study developed the FC-DETR model, based on RT-DETR (Real-Time DETR, an end-to-end real-time object detection model designed for fast and efficient object detection) [12], specifically for apple recognition in complex environments. The structure is shown in Fig. 3.Fig. 3FC-DETR model structure. The main innovative modules of this study are the FEMA-BasicBlock in the Backbone and the CAFM in the encoder.Fig. 3

FC-DETR model structure. The main innovative modules of this study are the FEMA-BasicBlock in the Backbone and the CAFM in the encoder.

The model first processes input images through the Backbone network, extracting multi-scale feature maps S3, S4, and S5. The innovative FEMA-BasicBlock module within the Backbone optimizes feature spatial mixing and channel expansion and compression processes, significantly enhancing the model's performance and generalization ability. The extracted features S3, S4, and S5 are fed into the encoder, where S5 is processed through the Attention-based Intra-scale Feature Interaction (AIFI) module, and then fused with S3 and S4 through the innovative Cross-scale Adaptive Feature Fusion Module (CAFM). The CAFM integrates channel and spatial feature selection mechanisms, using dynamically learned attention weights to enhance the representation of input data features, thereby greatly improving the model's ability to recognize apples in high-noise environments.

To further improve the model's apple recognition accuracy in high-noise environments, this study introduces the innovative Inner-WIoU loss function. This loss function reduces background interference by focusing on medium-quality anchor boxes, thereby enhancing overall recognition performance. Subsequently, the model employs IoU-aware query selection to select a certain number of image features from the encoder output sequence as the initial object queries for the decoder. Finally, the decoder, equipped with auxiliary prediction heads, iteratively optimizes the object queries to generate bounding boxes and confidence scores.

Through the combination of these innovative modules and methods, the FC-DETR model demonstrates excellent performance in apple recognition under complex environments, showcasing its potential and advantages in practical applications.

FEMA-BasicBlock: A Lighter and faster residual basic block

FEMA-BasicBlock (Feature Enhanced Multi Aspect BasicBlock) is an efficient residual basic block designed to enhance the model's performance in apple recognition under complex environments. As shown in Fig. 4(c), this module first extracts input features through a convolution layer and then processes them further with the FEM-Block. The FEM-Block is a key module designed to strengthen feature representation capabilities. It combines features processed by the second convolution layer with shortcut path features, constructing a residual learning framework through an additive approach, and finally outputs features with rich representation capabilities through a nonlinear activation function.Fig. 4Lighter and faster residual base block FEMA-BasicBlock architecture Diagram.(a)Partial convolution (PConv).(b)Efficient multi-scale Attention (EMA).(c)FEMA-BasicBlock.Fig. 4

Perform a 3 × 3 convolution operation on X1, maintaining the spatial dimensions of the data. The processed X1′ is then recombined with the unchanged X2 to obtain the final output X′. Inner-WIoU

To improve the original model's loss function's sensitivity to occlusion and overlap in high-noise apple recognition, as well as its inaccuracy in localizing small targets, this study innovatively combines Inner-IoU and Wise-IoU to propose the Inner-WIoU loss function [27,28]. Inner-IoU reduces background interference by narrowing the actual and predicted boxes, focusing on the overlapping region inside the target. At the same time, leveraging the advantages of Wise-IoU, this loss function pays more attention to targets within generally good quality anchor boxes. This not only enhances the model's detection performance in complex environments but also improves the accuracy of bounding box regression. The principle is as follows:

Training parameter settings

The graphics card used for the experiment is an RTX 4060. The operating system is Windows 10. The model was built using the Pytorch deep learning framework. CUDA version 11.8 and CuDNN version 8.7.0 were used. We have improved on Yolov8n by selecting images with a size of 640x640 as the input. The number of epochs is set to 300, with a batch size of 4. Discussion

The FC-DETR model proposed in this study aims to address the challenges of apple recognition in complex environments by innovatively introducing the FEMA-BasicBlock and CAFM modules, significantly enhancing the model's capabilities in feature extraction and fusion. The discussion will focus on four aspects: research results, research extensions, limitations, and research conclusions.

Firstly, experimental results show that the FC-DETR model performs excellently under various complex weather conditions, maintaining efficient recognition without the need for transfer learning. Although Praveen and Ma have made progress in small object detection and lightweight design, their methods still exhibit limited robustness in complex environments. Our results demonstrate that the FEMA-BasicBlock module enhances multi-scale feature extraction by improving the residual block structure, leading to superior performance when handling variations in apple sizes. Additionally, the CAFM module introduces a feature selection mechanism that better focuses on important features during feature fusion, thereby improving the model's robustness and detection accuracy in complex backgrounds.

Secondly, regarding research extensions, this study explores the technical advantages of FC-DETR. The FEMA-BasicBlock module optimizes the residual block structure to enhance feature extraction capability, while the CAFM module, through effective feature fusion strategies, further improves the model's performance in handling complex backgrounds and multi-scale targets. This design is not only innovative theoretically but also significantly enhances the model's performance in practice. Future research directions may include combining infrared images with RGB images to achieve multi-modal fusion, further enhancing FC-DETR's recognition performance in high-noise environments.

Despite the significant achievements, there are still some limitations in this study. Although FC-DETR achieves a certain degree of lightweighting, the computational complexity of the Transformer architecture remains high, which may pose challenges in terms of resources and time during actual deployment. Future research could consider further optimizing FC-DETR to reduce computational complexity and enhance lightweight performance. These improvements will help lower the deployment and maintenance costs of the model, making it more efficient and feasible in practical applications.

Finally, with further improvements and optimizations, FC-DETR is expected to play a greater role in the field of automated apple picking, promoting the development and application of agricultural technology. We believe that through continuous technological innovation and optimization, the FC-DETR model will not only achieve more breakthroughs in academic research but also demonstrate broad application prospects in practical use.

Conclusion

This study significantly enhanced the RT-DETR model's ability to automatically recognize apples in complex environments by introducing three innovative techniques: FEMA-BasicBlock, CAFM, and Inner-WIoU. These innovations not only optimized the model's performance in varying conditions but also improved processing efficiency and accuracy, as evidenced by significant increases in mAP50 and recall rate and efficient real-time processing capability. FEMA-BasicBlock notably reduced the model's computational complexity while significantly improving recognition accuracy in visually obstructed environments by enhancing feature extraction capabilities. CAFM improved the model's adaptability and stability in multi-scale and complex background conditions through multi-level feature fusion. Additionally, the novel Inner-WIoU loss function optimized loss calculation and accelerated training convergence, further enhancing the model's generalization ability and environmental adaptability. Comparative experimental results demonstrated that the improved RT-DETR model outperformed existing advanced models such as Deformable-DETR, YOLOv5, and the YOLOv8 series under harsh weather conditions, including rain, fog, and strong sunlight. Particularly in complex environments, our proposed model exhibited higher recognition accuracy while ensuring efficient real-time processing and significantly reducing false recognition rates. In summary, the results of this study provide an efficient and reliable technical solution for apple recognition in complex backgrounds and variable lighting conditions, demonstrating significant practical application value. This advancement not only promotes the development of agricultural automation technology but also provides strong technical support for further research and solutions to similar machine vision recognition problems. Future work will continue to explore optimization opportunities to further enhance the model's stability and accuracy in a wider range of environmental conditions.

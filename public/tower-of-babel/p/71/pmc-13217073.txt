Gender disparities in the burden of vision impairment and regional environmental susceptibility among Chinese children and adolescents
Authors: Haojie Tong, Caiyun Cao, Shang Shi, Xiaoling Liu, Tingting Li, Shaojun Xu, Xiaoyan Wu, Shuman Tao, Fang-Biao Tao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGender disparities in vision impairment (VI) among Chinese children and adolescents persist over time. Assessment and prediction of secular trends and the disease burden associated with this disparity can optimise vision health practices.MethodsWe analysed data from the 2005, 2010, 2014 and 2019 Chinese National Surveys on Students Constitution and Health (CNSSCH), which included 874 640 students aged 7–18 years. Regional environmental factors comprised the Price Constant Index (PCI), health worker density, greenery coverage, intensity of light at night, annual patent grants of three kinds, population density, climate and air quality. Trends in VI burden were assessed and predicted using Chinese standard population and WHO population estimates. Mixed-effects models were employed to evaluate sex-based disparities in regional environmental factors and VI prevalence.ResultsMale VI patients have surpassed females, with the gender disparity increasing from −8797.9 thousand in 2005 to 5941.9 thousand in 2030. The PCI (β=0, p=0), greenery coverage (β=0, p=0), intensity of light at night (β=0, p=0) and annual patent grants of three kinds (β=0, p<0) were positively associated with VI prevalence. Stronger associations were observed between VI in male and both PCI (male: β=0, p=0.; female: β=0, p=0) and annual patent grants of three kinds (male: β=0, p<0; female: β=0, p<0).ConclusionsIn China, VI is more prevalent in females, yet the absolute number of patients is larger in males, with this disparity progressively increasing. Males demonstrate heightened susceptibility to regional environmental factors. These findings support incorporating sex-specific prevention measures into current vision protection policies.

Introduction

Vision impairment (VI), primarily attributed to myopia, is one of the most common health conditions in children and adolescents globally, presenting a major public health challenge. Over the past 40 years, VI prevalence among Chinese children and adolescents has risen markedly, currently affecting 61.3% of students.1 2

The patients with VI face dual burdens: increased risk of ocular complications and mental health disorders,3 coupled with the lifelong economic costs of refractive correction.4 Myopic patients are at elevated risk for various ocular conditions, including nuclear cataract, posterior subcapsular cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic macular degeneration and retinal detachment.5 The Chinese government has been prioritising children’s vision health through national initiatives such as the 2018 Comprehensive Plan to Prevent Nearsightedness among Children and Teenagers, but it has been strongly hindered by the COVID-19 pandemic.6 7

Gender disparities in VI constitute a significant but under-researched dimension of paediatric vision health. Epidemiological evidence consistently demonstrates higher VI prevalence among female than male children and adolescents, potentially attributable to sex differences in behavioural patterns and biological factors.1 8 Specifically, females’ greater engagement in near-work activities and pubertal hormonal variations may elevate VI risk.9 10 Although gender disparities in VI are prevalent in China, the trends and causes remain unclear and sex-specific measures are inadequately reflected in vision protection practices. This knowledge gap may compromise intervention effectiveness and perpetuate health inequalities. Quantifying the evolving disease burden of VI and its sex-specific patterns could inform targeted strategy refinement and population health improvement. Our study has two primary objectives: first, we characterise and forecast VI prevalence trends among Chinese children and adolescents from 2005 to 2030, with particular focus on gender disparities in disease burden, hypothesising persistent overall increases with widening sex-based differences in disease burden; second, we examine population-level VI associations with regional environmental factors, emphasising sex-specific environmental sensitivities, to provide evidence-based guidance, for optimising current vision care systems, with the hypothesis that VI correlates with economic development and environmental degradation while demonstrating gender-specific associations. By examining gender disparities in VI burden and the differential impact of environmental measures on vision health at the regional level, our findings will provide policy-relevant evidence for optimising vision care systems and promoting health equity.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study used data from the Chinese National Surveys on Students’ Constitution and Health (CNSSCH) surveys conducted in 2005, 2010, 2014 and 2019. As a nationwide repeated cross-sectional survey, CNSSCH covered 30 provincial-level regions (excluding Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau and Tibet). The study was conducted from September to November of each survey year and adopted a stratified random cluster sampling method to select several schools (including urban and rural areas) from three regions with different economic levels (high, middle and low) in each province and municipality and further stratified by grade and conducted at the class. Detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria have been previously published,1 and we believe that the enrolled study population meets the required standards. Our analysis included 874 640 Han Chinese students aged 7–18 years, representing China’s predominant ethnic group (accounting for 92% of the total population). The project is considered exempt from review and consent requirements by the School of Public Health ethics committee at Anhui Medical University because it uses publicly available deidentified datasets.

Measuring VI

Visual acuity (VA) examinations were performed by ophthalmologists and related professionals using a retro illuminated logMAR chart with tumbling-E optotypes at approximately 500 lux illumination and a distance of 5 m.1 In this study, VI was defined as unaided distance VA in the worst eye less than 6/6 (20/20 Snellen equivalent), while mild VI, moderate VI and severe VI were defined as VA equal to 6/7.5 (20/25 Snellen equivalent), 6/15 to 6/9.5 (20/50 to 20/32 Snellen equivalent) and 6/19 or worse (20/63 Snellen equivalent), respectively. In previous studies, VI was interchangeably termed reduced visual acuity and poor vision. All VA examinations, supervision and data uploading procedures were conducted by specifically trained personnel.

Other data

To ensure population comparability and minimise errors caused, the VI prevalence was adjusted based on the national and provincial population structure (including sex and age) obtained from China’s Sixth National Population Census.11 Subsequently, we projected the VI disease burden for 2025 and 2030 by applying WHO World Population Prospects demographic projections.12

To evaluate regional environmental exposures, we incorporated multiple regional-level environmental indicators. Per capita gross domestic product and the consumer price index for each province were obtained from statistical yearbooks published by national and local statistical bureaus,11 with constant price indices calculated based on 2010 consumption levels to ensure comparability.1 Data on population density, number of health workers per 1000 people (reflecting regional healthcare capacity), annual patent grants of three kinds (reflecting regional technological development), annual mean temperature and precipitation (reflecting regional climate conditions) and annual mean concentrations of particulate matter (PM10), sulfur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) (reflecting regional air pollution) were sourced from China Statistical Yearbook China Statistical Yearbook published by National Bureau of Statistics11 and Ecological and Environmental Condition Bulletins published by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment of the People’s Republic of China.13 Night-time light exposure has emerged as a recognised visual health determinant. Given the extensive area of nighttime light pollution in China, it is necessary to incorporate nighttime light pollution into the category of regional environmental factors.14 15 Night-time light data were derived from satellite remote sensing observations released by the Global Change Research Data Publishing & Repository.16 All regional environmental exposure data corresponded to the years of the CHNSSCH. However, due to data unavailability in earlier periods, we only included regional environmental factors from 2010, 2014 and 2019 in our analyses.

Statistical analysis

Participant characteristics from 2005 to 2019 are summarised. Continuous variables were expressed as median (IQR) and categorical variables as percentages (%). Differences in VI prevalence between sexes are reported in percentage points (PP). Trends in VI prevalence across different characteristics were analysed using trend χ² tests, and data were visualised using maps. To minimise errors, regional environmental factors were normalised to eliminate scale interference. Given the unsuitability of linear regression for analysing percentage data ranging from 0 to 1,17 we employed a mixed-effects model with a β distribution to analyse the relationship between regional environmental factors and VI prevalence. Non-linear relationships were estimated using restricted cubic splines curves. Subgroup analyses were conducted to explore gender differences in sensitivity to VI and regional environmental factors. Finally, based on linear regression predictions (online supplemental figure S1) and WHO’s World Population Prospects, we estimated and projected the number of VI patients among Chinese children and adolescents. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS V.29.0 and R V.4.4.2, with p value <0.05 considered statistically significant in two-sided tests.

Results

Trends in VI prevalence from 2005 to 2019

A total of 874 640 participants from four repeated cross-sectional surveys were included in the analysis (online supplemental table S1 in Supplementary). The selected schools in each survey were broadly consistent,1 and the proportion of gender, residence and age subgroups was evenly distributed in each survey year. Table 1 displays the prevalence of VI (defined as VA <6/6 (20/20 Snellen equivalent)) across population subgroups. Among participants aged 7–18 years, VI prevalence increased from 51.9% (51.7–52.1%) in 2005 to 70.3% (70.1–70.5%) in 2019. Male participants demonstrated a rise from 47.4% (47.2–47.7%) to 66.6% (66.3–66.9%), while female participants showed a greater escalation from 57.0% (56.7–57.3%) to 74.5% (74.3–74.8%). Nearly all provinces exhibited upward VI trends (online supplemental figure S2 in Supplementary), with consistent increases observed across different residence and age groups (table 1).

Among VI patients, the proportion of mild VI (males: −2.35 PP; females: −2.57 PP) and moderate VI (males: −7.12 PP; females: −7.97 PP) declined, whereas severe VI increased significantly (males: 9.47 PP; females: 10.54 PP;figure 1B). The association between VI prevalence among different genders and regional environmental factors

Table 2 presents adjusted model analyses accounting for population density, climate (mean annual temperature, precipitation) and survey year. VI among children and adolescents was positively associated with Price Constant Index (PCI) (β 0.58; 95% CI 0.07 to 1.10), greenery coverage (β 0.42, 95% CI 0.09 to 0.), intensity of light at night (β 0.004, 95% CI 0.001 to 0) and annual patent grants of three kinds (β 0.63, 95% CI 0.21 to 1.04), but not with the number of health workers per 1000 population, annual mean concentrations of PM10, SO2 and NO2. Estimation and prediction of disease burden of VI by sex

Based on China’s historical population structure and WHO projected demographics, although females exhibited higher VI prevalence, the number of male VI patients surpassed that of females after adjusting for sex and age composition, with this disparity widening since 2010 (table 3, online supplemental table S2 and in online supplemental table S3). Male VI patients increased from 62 195.8 thousand (K) in 2005 to 65 498.3K in 2019, 82 229.1K in 2025 and 81 176.2K in 2030. Female VI patients rose from 70 393.7K in 2005 to 63 075.3K in 2019, 78 256.5K in 2025 and 75 234.3K in 2030. The gender gap shifted from −8197.9K in 2005 to 2423.0K in 2019 and 5941.9K in 2030. Males consistently outnumbered females in mild VI (2005: −177.9K; 2030: 18 079.0K) and moderate VI (2005: −1039.7K; 2030: 54 202.3K), with these disparities expanding over time. Females maintained a higher burden of severe VI, although this gap narrowed (2005: −6980.3K; 2030: −912.6K).

Discussion

This study reveals that the VI prevalence among Chinese children and adolescents increased from 51.9% in 2005 to 70.3% in 2019, emerging as a major public health concern. Since 2018, China has implemented nationwide interventions, including increased outdoor activities, reduced academic workload, improved visual environments, enhanced health education and strengthened screening-diagnosis-treatment protocols, to mitigate this trend.6 However, these efforts face challenges related to high academic pressure and lifestyle changes, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic, when policies with population movement restrictions have accelerated digital education and recreation and curtailed outdoor exposure, potentially counteracting prevention efforts. Notably, a persistent gender disparity was observed. While females consistently exhibited higher VI prevalence, males experienced a steeper increase (male: 47.4–66.6%; female: 57.0–74.5%), with the male patients surpassing females and the gap widening. Population-level analyses are steeper. These findings highlight the escalating burden of VI and suggest that sex differences may reflect divergent environmental sensitivities, underscoring the need to develop gender-based prevention strategies alongside stricter population-wide measures to advance health equity.

Consistent with prior studies, we observed higher overall VI prevalence among females than males across all surveyed years and provinces in China, though notable variations emerged when stratified by severity. During 2005–2019, gender disparities were minimal for mild (0.60 to −0.20 PP) and moderate VI (1.81 to 0.32 PP), with male prevalence gradually surpassing females, whereas marked disparities persisted for severe VI (7.11 to 8.18 PP), particularly in older adolescents (13–18 years). These findings reveal critical gender disparity trends in VI burden, warranting targeted interventions for females while addressing the rising disease burden in males. Multiple factors may contribute to these disparities. In traditional Chinese culture, the labour style of females participating in household chores and males working outside is highly respected. This has subconsciously shaped the value orientation that encourages females to engage in activities that involve prolonged sitting and near work, potentially leading to a higher VI prevalence among females.1 8 Additionally, evidence suggests that female students are more likely to exhibit inappropriate eye use behaviour, such as incorrect reading and writing posture.18 Furthermore, the relatively lower rate of glasses wearing among female students may also exacerbate the disparity.19 Biological factors may be another reason.9 The drastic changes in hormone levels during puberty might be associated with VI. Besides, the onset of puberty in females is earlier than in males, which may increase the risk of VI in females.20 Notably, after adjusting for the standard population structure, male VI patients exceeded females, with this gap projected to widen to 5.94 million by 2030, highlighting unique manifestations of sex inequality in paediatric VI. Regional analyses revealed stronger associations between male VI and environmental factors (PCI and annual patent grants of three kinds), suggesting heightened male susceptibility to urbanisation-driven lifestyle transitions from outdoor to sedentary activities, though direct evidence remains lacking. This trend may exacerbate healthcare resource mismatches, leaving high-risk groups underserved. Further investigation is warranted to assess sex-specific disparities in associated public health and economic burdens. Current vision health policies lack sex-specific strategies, creating a critical gap between existing measures and epidemiological needs.

The association between regional environmental factors and VI prevalence warrants focused discussion. Our findings demonstrate positive correlations between VI prevalence and PCI (reflecting economic development), intensity of light at night and annual patent grants of three kinds (indicating technological advancement) among Chinese children and adolescents, consistent with previous research.21 Several possible explanations may account for these associations. first, economic development has been accompanied by the popularisation of information technology, which has changed traditional learning methods and recreational activities, increasing their near-work activities.22 Second, the intensive education system is one of the significant factors contributing to the high VI prevalence in China. The traditional examination system inherited from the imperial examination system, as well as monotonous talent cultivation approaches, more importantly, creates a social atmosphere where exam scores are most important, resulting in many children being crowded into schools. With the increasing academic pressure, sedentary activities such as doing homework have increased, while outdoor activities have decreased. This may contribute to developing chronic diseases such as VI and obesity.23 24 Third, urbanisation has exacerbated environmental pollution and nighttime light exposure,25 increasing visual fatigue and the probability of exposure to harmful substances.26 In summary, productivity development has altered children’s living environments and activity patterns, increased sedentary near-work behaviours while diminishing outdoor activities, ultimately driving VI prevalence upward. We observed an unexpected positive correlation between greenery coverage and VI, which contradicts previous studies.27 This paradox may arise because children rarely access green spaces beyond school and residential areas, making this indicator more likely to be correlated with economic development than a true reflection of green space-VI associations. Notably, economic and environmental disparities among subregional areas within provinces and the long time span may introduce confounding when assessing associations between regional environmental factors and school-aged children’s VI.

This study carries several critical public health implications. First, the demonstrated upward trend in VI prevalence among Chinese children and adolescents suggests the need to continuously implement and strengthen existing preventive and interventional measures. Second, the observed gender disparity in VI burden calls for sex-specific prevention strategies that account for physiological and psychological differences between boys and girls to optimise current public health approaches. For instance, optimising physical education curricula with sex-tailored activities could promote outdoor time and sustainable exercise habits, while enhancing school-family partnerships may improve targeted eye health education, addressing sex-specific lifestyle factors (eg, near-work patterns, use of electronic devices) and correcting harmful visual behaviours. Notably, given China’s demographic profile, male VI patients now outnumber females, and considering males’ heightened sensitivity to regional environmental factors, vigilance is warranted against potential widening of this gender gap in disease burden. Importantly, VI represents a complex public health challenge requiring coordinated multisectoral engagement from government, healthcare systems, educational institutions, families and individuals. Comprehensive actions should include reducing academic pressure, diversifying development pathways, ensuring adequate outdoor activity time and establishing lifelong vision healthcare systems for children.

This study leverages data from large-scale national surveys, providing a gender disparity in the disease burden of VI in Chinese children and adolescents. However, several limitations should be noted. First, as the CNSSCH was school-based and only Han Chinese students were included in this study, the generalisability of the findings in minority areas, where minority populations are concentrated, needs to be further studied. However, given China’s universal 9-year compulsory education policy and the Han demographic predominance (about 92% of the national population), this study still provides valuable insights for policymaking and public health interventions. second, the data used in this study is based on multiple cross-sectional surveys rather than a cohort study. Therefore, it cannot prove causality. Third, while this population-based study provides valuable epidemiological evidence, potential ecological fallacies should be acknowledged. Behaviours such as individual outdoor activities and near-work may lead to potential biases. Future studies incorporating larger-scale individual-level data would strengthen these findings. Finally, we evaluated VI prevalence based on multiple repetitive national datasets, which are still ongoing. Future long-term follow-up or repetitive cross-sectional studies may provide a more objective evaluation of the gender disparities in VI. Notably, social factors, such as changes in lifestyle caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, may influence VI trends. While challenging to assess using historical national data, evaluating these social factors could optimise public health strategies for childhood VI.

Conclusion

The results of this study support previous conclusions that the prevalence of VI in Chinese children and adolescents is increasing, with females exhibiting higher VI prevalence than males, while highlighting the need for vigilance regarding rapidly increasing gender disparities in VI burden. Additionally, we observed that the number of VI patients surpassed that of females, with males showing greater sensitivity to regional environmental factors, reflecting their heightened risk of VI burden. These results underscore the critical role of sex in childhood VI care and advocate for the integration of sex-specific prevention and intervention strategies into existing vision care policies to promote gender equity. The findings advance the current paradigms of child vision protection and provide valuable insights for other countries with similar backgrounds.

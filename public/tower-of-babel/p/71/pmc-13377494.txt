Ecological and functional succession of the microbial community during pit mud maturation in Nongxiangxing Baijiu
Authors: Lei Tian, Ji Qin, Yue Deng, Liling Liu, Shouqin Wang, Mei Zhang, Tongwei Guan, Yan Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPit mud (PM) microbiota play a vital role in Baijiu flavor formation, yet its ecological and functional succession during maturation remains incompletely elucidated. Here, physicochemical profiling, amplicon sequencing, and metagenomics were integrated to investigate 5-, 15-, and 30-year PM of Sichuan Tang Dynasty Laojiao cellars. Bacteria dominated the community (82.59%), followed by Archaea (16.99%), with Lactobacillus acetotolerans, Ruminococcaceae CPB6, and Methanobacterium paludis as major species. Discrepancies between sequencing methods were reflected in fungal taxa which had low-abundance. The 15-year PM exhibited distinct community and functional features, indicating a critical transitional stage. Functional analysis revealed that fermentation-relevant functions were mainly contributed by 7 key genera and 5 species. Physicochemical properties changed with pit age, characterized by increased moisture as well as decreased acidity and humic substance levels. Moisture, ammonium nitrogen (NH₄⁺–N), available phosphorus, and age were identified as key drivers shaping microbial composition and function. Moisture was identified as the most central mediator, establishing a three-tier cascade causal chain from microorganisms to nutrient accumulation. Functionally, 5-year PM sustained a simple, Lactobacillus-dominated, growth-oriented community; 15-year PM shifted toward aromatic compound degradation, nitrogen utilization, flavor-precursor synthesis; and 30-year PM developed into a stable, flavor-optimized ecosystem enriched in caproic-acid-producing Ruminococcaceae CPB6. Overall, PM maturation is driven by microbiome functional evolution, and the 15-year represented a pivotal period. This study provides a theoretical foundation for scientific PM management and targeted microbial regulation in Baijiu production.Keywords: Metagenomics, Amplicon sequencing, Pit mud, Microbial structure and function, Physicochemical property

Introduction

Nongxiangxing Baijiu (NXXB) is a representative of traditional distilled spirits in China (Xia et al., 2023). Its fermentation relies on pit mud (PM), a unique anaerobic soil environment (Chai et al., 2021). The age of PM critically influences Baijiu fermentation by shaping the physicochemical conditions and microbial communities for flavor formation (Xia et al., 2023). The physicochemical properties also impact the survival and composition of microbial flora. PM undergoes prolonged use during Baijiu production and gradually matures over time, forming a highly diverse and functional microbial ecosystem (Wang et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020). Studies showed that PM at around ten years of age represented a transitional stage with unstable community structures (Tao et al., 2014a). By about 25 years, microbial succession approaches maturity, characterized by stabilized functional taxa such as Clostridium spp. and Methanogens. Full maturation occurred after about 30 years, when PM develops a stable microbial community and significantly enhances Baijiu quality (Tao et al., 2014; Ren et al., 2025). Understanding microbial dynamics and driving factors before full maturation is key to elucidating the formation and maturation mechanisms of PM (Chai et al., 2021).

Luzhou is one of the core Baijiu-producing regions of China, where the distinctive climate and geology shape unique PM microbiota and complex aroma profile of Baijiu (Zhang et al., 2021). Existing research primarily focused on Luzhou Laojiao and its century-old PM (Liu et al., 2017a; Liu et al., 2017b). However, the Tang Dynasty Laojiao (TDL)—the other of Luzhou's two historic cellars, with a legacy of over 160 years—remains scientifically unexplored. The PM of TDL is made from local Wuduxi yellow clay, which is both impermeable and conductive to microbial growth. It was applied to bamboo-reinforced cellar walls, forming unique bamboo-woven PM. As production expands, transitioning from empirical knowledge to scientifically guided PM maturation is essential to address the challenge of accelerating cellar development.

Current PM studies primarily use amplicon sequencing (AS) (Chai et al., 2021; Guo et al., 2019; Tao et al., 2017a), with limited taxonomic and function resolution, typically at the genus level. And results vary substantially depending on the selected hypervariable regions (e.g., V3-V4 or V4-V5) and primer design (Wang et al., 2020). Some investigations focused exclusively on bacterial and fungal communities (Xia et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2021a), others examined only bacterial and archaeal (Chai et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020; Tao et al., 2014b). Comprehensive analyses encompassing all microbial domains remain scarce, and methodological variations hinder cross-study comparisons. Metagenomics offers deeper resolution, not only in species-level classification but also in functional gene annotation and metabolic pathway analysis (Xue et al., 2023). Yet this approach has seldom been applied to PM research, and some investigations even lack clear documentation of pit age, further limiting comparability.

Notably, existing research on Luzhou PM has exclusively focused on aged microbial communities (>50 years), while species-level distributions and function during succession and early maturation stages remain uncharacterized. Furthermore, functional genes and potentials in 15-year PM from other production regions remain uncharacterized. Previous studies suggested that Luzhou PM exhibited higher microbial diversity and a greater proportion of unique genes compared to other Sichuan regions (Guo et al., 2023). Younger PM also contained more unique functional genes (Guo et al., 2023), which contribute to its superior quality. Once fully matured, microbial communities stabilize and exhibit minimal structural change with age (Tao et al., 2014). Therefore, studying the microbial ecology of new and developing PM is critical for identifying key functional microorganisms and guiding scientific maturation strategies. Integrating metagenomics with 16S rRNA sequencing offers a more comprehensive and accurate approach to elucidate microbial composition, diversity, and metabolic potential, thereby advancing our understanding of microbial ecology in Baijiu fermentation.

This study presents the first investigation of 15-year PM, and the first ecological survey of PM from the TDL cellar. By integrating metagenomic and AS analyses, we characterized the microbial community structure, functional potential, and physicochemical properties of PM aged 5, 15, and 30 years. The study aims to elucidate microbial succession and its drivers during critical maturation stages, providing a scientific basis for targeted PM optimization and maintenance.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

PM samples were collected from cellars of Sichuan Tang Dynasty Laojiao (Group) Co., Ltd., a NXXB distillery in Luzhou, Sichuan province, China. The cellars had been used continuously for 5, 15, and 30 years. PM was collected using the five-point sampling method (Liu et al., 2021). Three replicates were randomly selected for each age group. All samples were homogenously mixed and stored at −80 °C until analysis.

Analysis of physicochemical properties

The physicochemical properties of PM—including moisture, total acidity, pH, available phosphorus (AP), humic substances, and ammonium nitrogen (NH4+-N) levels—were assessed as previously described (Wang et al., 2021a; Wang et al., 2020). All experiments were performed in triplicate.

DNA extraction, PCR, and AS

Total DNA of PM samples was extracted using the FastDNA™ Spin Kit for Soil (MP Biomedicals, USA), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Bacterial 16S rRNA, archaeal 16S rRNA, and fungal ITS genes were amplified with primer pairs 338F/806R, 524F10extF/Arch958RmodR, and ITS4F/ITS3R, respectively (Table S1). Libraries were constructed using the NextFlex Rapid DNA-Seq Kit (Bioo Scientific, Austin, TX, USA) and sequenced on the Illumina MiSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Raw FASTQ files were quality-filtered and clustered into operational taxonomic units (OTUs) at a 97% similarity threshold using UPARSE (v7.1). To minimize the effects of sequencing depth on alpha and beta diversity measure, the number of 16S rRNA gene sequences from each sample were rarefied to 36973. Bacterial/Archaeal 16S rRNA and Fungal ITS sequences were annotated against the SILVA 138 database and UNITE database respectively.

DNA extraction and sequencing of metagenomic

Total genomic DNA was extracted from PM samples using FastDNA™ Spin Kit for Soil (MP Biomedicals, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The DNA was sheared to an average size of approximately 400 bp using the Covaris M220 system (Gene Company Limited, China). A paired-end library was constructed using NEXTFLEX Rapid DNA-Seq (Bioo Scientific, Austin, TX, USA) and sequenced on Illumina NovaSeq (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) with the NovaSeq 6000 S4 Reagent Kit v1.5. Metagenomic sequencing was performed using single-end 150 bp reads. PhiX Library was spiked into the final library pool prior to improve base balance and cluster identification.

Raw metagenomic reads were quality-filtered with fastp (v0.20.0) to remove low-quality sequences (length < 50 bp, quality score < 20, or containing N bases). Residual adapter sequences at both the 3′ and 5′ ends were detected and trimmed. Clean reads were assembled into contigs using MEGAHIT (v1.1.2) (Li et al., 2015). Clean contigs and predicted ORFs were annotated against the NCBI NR database using DIAMOND v2.0.13. Gene prediction was performed on assembled contigs (>300 bp) (Li et al., 2015, Tian et al., 2024) with MetaGeneMark (version 3.38) (Noguchi et al., 2006), and a non-redundant gene set (Unigenes) was generated using CD-HIT (v4.6.1) at 90 % identity and coverage thresholds. Gene abundance was calculated with SOAPaligner (v2.21) (Li et al., 2009). The relative abundance of microbial taxonomic was calculated based on annotated ORF number and sequence coverage. Metagenomic sequences were annotated using eggNOG, KEGG, and CAZy databases to characterize microbial functions and metabolic potential.

Statistical analysis

Kruskal-Wallis H test as the initial parametric multi-group test to determine whether significant differences existed among groups. Following a significant result (p < 0.05), False Discovery Rate (FDR) method was applied for multiple comparison correction. Dunn's multiple comparisons test was conducted for pairwise comparisons. Alpha diversity indices were calculated using Mothur v1.30.2, with inter-group comparisons conducted via the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. For functional annotation, non-redundant gene sequences were aligned against reference databases using Diamond v0.8.35 for taxonomic, eggNOG, CAZy and KEGG annotations. Metabolic pathway differences between groups were visualized and analyzed using iPath 2.0. The linear discriminant analysis (LDA) effect size (LEfSe) (http://huttenhower.sph.harvard.edu/LEfSe) was performed to identify the significantly microbial taxa and functions among the different groups (LDA score > 2, P < 0.05). Microbial correlation network was constructed using Python Networkx v1.11 package. Spearman correlation analysis was used for network construction between microbial genera, with screening criteria of |r| > 0.5 and P < 0.05. Mantel tests were conducted in R (v4.2.1) using Bray-Curtis distance for microbial community matrices and Euclidean distance for environmental factor matrices, with Spearman rank correlation (9,999 permutations, P < 0.05) to assess associations among microbial species, functions, and physicochemical factors. Mediation effects analyses were performed using R packages (lavaan). The significance of indirect effects was estimated using the bootstrap method with 5,000 resamples; a 95% confidence interval not containing zero was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

Physiochemical properties of PM across different ages

Moisture content was significantly lower in 5-year PM than in 15-year samples, with minimal difference between 15- and 30-year samples (Fig. 1A). This indicated that microbial metabolic activity varies less in more mature PM, whereas the difference is more pronounced between new and mature PM. Our findings align with previous reports indicating that PM moisture increases with pit age (Wu et al., 2022). All PM samples exhibited moisture contents exceeding 55%, significantly surpassing the high‑quality PM (38%-42%) (Deng et al., 2024). Humic substances in 30-year PM were lower than in 5- and 15-year samples (Fig. 1D), consistent with aging-related declines (Bi et al., 2022). All samples maintained levels above 49%, far exceeding the high-quality range of 11%–18% (Deng et al., 2024), suggesting sustained nutrient richness and superior water-holding capacity (Wu et al., 2022).

The physicochemical properties of PM samples from different-aged (5, 15, and 30 years). (A) Moisture content. (B) pH. (C) Available phosphorous. (D) Humic substances. (E) NH4+-N. (F) Total acidity. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). * p < 0.05, Kruskal-Wallis H analysis followed by Dunn's multiple comparisons test.

The pH of 15-year PM was significantly lower than that of 30-year PM, and the 30-year sample displayed the highest value (Fig. 1B), approaching the neutral range characteristic of premium aged PM. This favorable pH likely enhances fermentation performance and aroma precursor formation (Wu et al., 2022). Previous studies have demonstrated that new PM with a lower pH, which typically increases with pit aging (Tao et al., 2014b; Wu et al., 2022). Notably, the pH of 30-year PM in this study was significantly higher than reported values (Bi et al., 2022), indicating superior quality. Conversely, total acidity decreased with increasing pit age, with 30-year PM showing significantly lower levels than 5-year samples (Fig. 1F). Despite this decline, acidity remained higher than reported Luzhou PM (Zhong et al., 2022). High acidity in PM promotes the enrichment of acid-producing Bacteria that may contribute to NXXB flavor development.

NH₄⁺-N peaked in the 5-year PM (Fig. 1E), contrasting with the trend observed by Tao et al (2014a) and suggesting unique nitrogen metabolism during succession. AP increased consistently with pit age (Fig. 1C), which aligns with the finding of Wu et al. (2022). AP levels across all samples were considerably higher than the premium range (Zhao et al., 2012), reflecting robust nutrient availability for microbial growth.

Overall, the 5- and 30-year PM samples exhibited considerably higher moisture, humic substances, NH₄⁺-N, and AP levels than those previously reported for Luzhou PM (Wang et al., 2020; Chai et al., 2021). Even when compared with PM of similar or greater age, our samples exhibited markedly higher moisture, pH, and NH₄⁺-N levels (Xia et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2020b). Comprehensive assessment of all physicochemical parameters indicated that the 5- and 15-year PM displayed superior quality characteristics typically associated with much older, high-quality pits (Chai et al., 2021; Bi et al., 2022). These superior physicochemical properties are likely attributed to the Wuduxi yellow clay used in the Tang Dynasty Laojiao. Its fine texture, high density, and mineral richness (Fe, Ca, and P) provide an ideal matrix for water retention and microbial activity, distinguishing it from other production regions.

Microbial community analysis based on AS

Microbial diversity analysis of PM at different ages

Beta-diversity analysis (Fig. 2A-C) showed clear age-dependent separation among PM samples, indicating distinct distribution in microbial community structure. Venn diagrams further showed that 8 archaeal genera (Methanosaeta, unclassified_f__Methanobacteriaceae, Methanomassiliicoccus, Methanoculleus, Methanobacterium, Methanosarcina, Methanobrevibacter, RumEn M2), 103 bacterial genera, and 34 fungal genera were shared across all pit ages (Fig. 2D–F), accounting for 9.2%, 18.5%, and 22.1% of their respective total genera. The 15-year PM contained the greatest number of unique archaeal (8) and bacterial taxa (34), whereas the 5-year PM contained the most unique fungal genera (33). Overall, these results demonstrate pronounced age-related divergence in microbial composition, with 15-year PM exhibiting the highest microbial diversity.

Analysis of community structure succession across PM ages

At the phylum level, archaeal communities were dominated by Euryarchaeota and Halobacterota, with Halobacterota reaching its highest proportion (38.59%) in 15-year PM (Fig. 3A). Bacterial communities were primarily composed of Firmicutes and Bacteroidota, with Firmicutes abundance increased dramatically in mature PM (Fig. 3B). Fungal communities were dominated by Ascomycota (Fig. 3C), with Basidiomycota gradually decreased with pit age (Fig. 3B). Although relative abundances varied with pit age, the core taxa remained stable.

Microbial community characteristics analysis of different PM based on amplicon sequencing. Bar plots display the relative abundance of the archaeal, bacterial and fungal communities at the phylum level (A–C) and genus level (D–F).

At the genus level, the key methanogens Methanobrevibacter, Methanobacterium, and Methanosarcina were abundant across all pit ages (Fig. 3E), but did not exhibit a progressive increase with age as reported in previous studies (Gao et al., 2023). Methanobacterium and Methanosarcina showed the highest proportion in 15-year PM (Fig. 3D), which have been reported to potentially play an active role in the maturation process of PM (Xu et al., 2020). Methanosaeta, enriched in 15-year PM (Fig. 4A), further indicated active syntrophic cooperation and acetate-driven methanogenesis at this transitional stage (Rotaru et al., 2014).

The LEfSe analysis of PM during different ages based on 16S rRNA sequencing at (A) archaeal, (B) bacterial and (C) fungal communities.

Bacterial community analysis showed clear age-associated differentiation at genus level. Lactobacillus absolutely dominated in 15-year PM (64.79%), while Clostridium sensu stricto 12 and Caproiciproducens were the most abundant in 5-year (20.48%) and 30-year (40.45%) PM, respectively (Fig. 3B). LEfSe analysis identified 27 significantly enriched bacterial genera across different pit ages (Fig. 4B), represented by Aminobacterium (5-year), Lactobacillus (15-year), and Caproiciproducens (30-year). Aminobacterium has been reported to metabolize amino acids to acetic and butyric acids (Baena et al., 2000). Enrichment of Lactobacillus likely contributes to acidic environment that selects for beneficial microbes (Tao et al., 2014), whereas the rise of caproic acid–producing Bacteria (e.g., Caproiciproducens) in mature PM reflects enhanced fatty acid synthesis capacity, which is essential for ethyl caproate formation (Sun et al., 2022). Studies demonstrated that Caproiciproducens was more stable in microbial community structures of mature PM (30-year), with higher functional microbe abundance than in transitional stages (10-year) (Kim et al., 2015). These microorganisms play indispensable roles in PM and are dominant in improving the physicochemical properties and volatile components of PM (Wu et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2020).

Fungal communities were dominated by Aspergillus, peaking in 15-year PM (78.74%) (Fig. 3F), reflecting its strong hydrolytic potential during transition (Hu et al., 2019). Thirty-year PM showed significant enrichment of Millerozyma, Dekkera, and Lichtheimia (Fig. 4C), suggesting enhanced potential for ester formation and starch degradation in mature pit ecosystems (Niu et al., 2022). Specifically, Millerozyma correlates positively with ester production (Li et al., 2024), while Lichtheimia—a common thermotolerant fungus from Daqu - serves as a vital source of hydrolytic enzymes for starch saccharification and ethanol production (Zhu et al., 2023).

Interestingly, the 15-year PM displayed the most distinct microbial characteristics. PM may undergo a critical ecological shift at around 15 years. To date, research on 15-year PM remains scarce (Xia et al., 2024), our findings updates previous understanding. This unique transitional feature likely results from both stage-dependent microbial succession and the traditional bamboo-woven cellar structure. Bamboo enhances aeration, water retention, and nutrient availability (e.g., cellulose), creating a differentiated habitat that shapes community structure and accelerates maturation processes. Unlike immature 5-year PM with simple microbial communities and mature 30-year PM with highly stable climax ecosystems, the successional microbial community of 15-year PM is highly sensitive to such microenvironmental variations. Consequently, the bamboo-induced habitat differentiation specifically remodels the 15-year microbial assembly, drives unique successional trajectories, and eventually forms the distinct microbial and fermentation characteristics of this transitional PM.

Correlation between microbial communities and environmental physicochemical factors

Canonical correspondence analysis (CCA) revealed significant correlations between dominant microbial genera and physicochemical properties in PM (adjusted p values < 0.05, cumulative explained variation: 91.53%) (Fig. 5A), indicating that physicochemical factors play a critical role in shaping microbial communities across pit ages. pH was the primary driver of bacterial and archaeal communities, whereas fungal communities were mainly influenced by moisture. Total acidity showed the strongest correlation with archaeal taxa. Key genera displayed distinct environmental preferences. Methanosarcina, Methanobacterium, Proteiniphilum, Methanoculleus, and Methanothermobacter were positively correlated with both pH and NH₄⁺-N. Caproicibacterium, Clostridium, and Atopobium showed positive correlations with AP and pH. Lactobacillus and Acetilactobacillus were positively associated with moisture, humic substances, and total acidity. These patterns are consistent with previous findings that Clostridium-driven acid production promotes the proliferation of Caproicibacterium, Clostridium, and Atopobium, enhancing protein and organic acid synthesis (Chai et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2022).

The correlation between 16S_OTU, ITS_OTU, Archaea_OTU, and physicochemical properties was further analyzed using the Mantel test (Fig. 5B). Fungi showed positive correlation with moisture (r = 0.78, p < 0.05), but negative correlation with NH4+-N (r = -0.77, p < 0.05). Archaeal communities were positively correlated with pH (r = 0.72, p < 0.05), and this finding corroborates the CCA results. While archaeal communities was positively correlated with pH (r = 0.72, p < 0.05), corroborating the CCA findings (Fig. 5A). In contrast, physicochemical factors exerted minor effects on bacterial communities. The result showed physicochemical indicators only impose limited community-shaping effects on Bacteria: moisture and acidity mainly suppress microbial physiological activity instead of drastically restructuring community composition.

In addition, significant correlations were observed among various physicochemical factors in PM. Total acidity was negatively correlated with AP (p < 0.01), NH4+-N was negatively correlated with moisture (p < 0.01), and pH was negatively correlated with humic substances (Fig. 5B). These interrelationships may alter the fermentation microenvironment, thereby influencing microbial succession (Mei et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2017a). Collectively, appropriate regulation of pH, NH₄⁺–N, and moisture is essential for maintaining microbial stability and optimizing PM function.

Metagenomic sequencing analysis

Statistics of metagenomic data

Metagenomic sequencing generated 66.21 Gbp of raw data in PM samples (Table S2). After quality control filtering, 64.45 Gbp of clean data were retained for further analysis, averaging 7.16 Gbp per sample (Table S2). A total of 2023082 genes were assembled into 817264 non-redundant gene sets (average length: 560.65 bp; N50: 1188.22 bp) (Table S2). These metrics indicated that the sequencing depth and assembly quality were sufficient for subsequent analyses.

Microbial diversity of PM at different ages

Microbial richness increased with pit age, with significant differences in the Ace, Sobs, Shannon, and Simpson indices among the three age groups (Fig. 6A–D). The 15-year PM exhibited the highest evenness and diversity, followed by 30- and 5-year samples, reflecting a clear successional pattern during PM maturation. It has been found that during the succession period (1 to 25 years), the microbial abundance in PM gradually increases with pit age (Tao et al., 2014). In our study, younger pits (5–15 years) showed progressive community development, while the 30-year pit displayed a distinct diversity pattern, likely resulting from long-term fermentation cycles that promote community stabilization and the formation of flavor-related metabolites (Wu et al., 2022).

Community composition across pit ages

Taxonomic annotation identified 187 phyla, 3,429 genera, and 14,538 species across all samples. Bacteria dominated total gene abundance (82.59%), followed by Archaea (16.99%), whose proportion rose sharply from only 0.09% in 5-year PM to 34.07% in 15-year PM and 23.09% in 30-year PM. Hierarchical clustering further confirmed clear separation between young and mature PM communities (Fig. 6E, 6F, 6G). 5-year samples forming a distinct cluster, exhibiting a simpler microbial structure, while the other two older PM groups showed greater similarity in community composition. Community differentiation intensified with pit age, accompanied by increasing numbers of unique phyla and species (Fig. 6H, 6I, 6J).

At the phylum level, 5-year PM was overwhelmingly dominated by Firmicutes (99.66%), whereas 15-year PM exhibited a balanced relative abundance among Firmicutes (27.40%), Euryarchaeota (33.79%), and Bacteroidetes (27.97%). In contrast, the 30-year PM exhibited a lower proportion of Firmicutes (64.19%), accompanied by a substantial increase in Euryarchaeota (22.91%) (Fig. 6D). Statistical analysis identified 96 phyla that differed significantly across pit ages (Fig. S1A). Specifically, Actinobacteria declined continuously with pit age, while Bacteroidetes, Synergistetes, and Chloroflexi peaked at 15 years. These phyla are known to participate in sulfide transformation, amino acid degradation, and organic acid metabolism, thereby facilitating PM maturation and aroma generation (Zhang et al., 2011; Tang, 2020; Ren et al., 2024).

At the genus level, compositional complexity increased markedly with pit age (Mei et al., 2023; Fig. 6E). Lactobacillus dominated 5-year PM (94.88%) but decreased significantly with aging (Fig. S1B), serving as a potential biomarker for young PM (De et al., 2024). Methanogens (Methanobacterium and Methanosarcina) were enriched in 15-year PM (12.39% and 10.98%, respectively), while unclassified_f_Oscillospiraceae became the most abundant genus in 30-year PM (30.84%). This taxon is capable of fermenting multiple carbon substrates (Ekanayake et al., 2024). Among the differentially abundant genera, unclassified_f_Oscillospiraceae, Atopobium, Caproicibacterium, and Clostridium increased with pit age, while most remaining differential genera peaked at 15 years (Fig. S1B). Notably, Clostridium and Caproicibacterium are key caproic acid producers that govern PM flavor metabolism (Tao et al., 2014b; De et al., 2024). However, contrary to the findings of Zheng et al. (2020), methanogen abundance did not increase persistently with pit age in our study.

At the species level, the number of unique species increased with pit maturation (Mei et al., 2023; Fig. 6J). Lactobacillus acetotolerans dominated 5-year PM (93.18%) and declined significantly over time; its outstanding acid resistance supports early-stage fermentation (Zhao et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2023; Fig. S1C). Methanosarcina sp. peaked in 15-year samples, whereas the caproate-producing Ruminococcaceae bacterium CPB6 dominated 30-year PM (17.22%). Most key functional differential species—including Ruminococcaceae bacterium CPB6, Oscillospiraceae bacterium, Atopobium sp., and Caproicibacterium lactatifermentans—gradually increased in abundance and reached their maximum at 15 years. These strains convert lactate into medium-chain fatty acids and play a central role in Baijiu aroma formation (Tao et al., 2017b; Liu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021b; Mei et al., 2023).

Metagenomic Functional Profiling across PM

To comprehensively characterize the functional potential of PM microbial communities, three complementary annotation databases were employed with distinct objectives: EggNOG for general functional categorization (COG), KEGG for specific metabolic pathway mapping, and CAZy for carbohydrate-active enzyme profiling.

COG functional annotation

EggNOG analysis identified 25 shared COG categories. The most abundant functions were J (Translation, ribosomal structure and biogenesis: 8.19-10.38%), G (Carbohydrate transport and metabolism: 7.21-9.66%), and E (Amino acid transport and metabolism: 6.32-8.93%) (Fig. 7A). These findings confirmed the crucial roles of carbohydrate and nitrogen metabolism in Baijiu fermentation. Functions with the largest age-related differences included J, E, and M (Cell wall/membrane/envelope biogenesis) (Fig. 7A). Notably, the abundance of E gradually increased with pit age, while M declined (Fig. 7A), reflecting a transition from structural growth to metabolic specialization during maturation. The enhanced metabolic capacity may be driven by long-term substrate input from fermented grains and yellow water. Previous studies have shown that mature PM exhibits higher relative abundances of carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism compared with new PM (Zhou et al., 2022). Consistent with this, our results also revealed an age-dependent increase in amino acid transport and metabolism.

KEGG functional annotation

KEGG analysis identified 6 level-1 and 25 level-2 pathways across the three PM age groups. At the KEGG level-2, global overview maps (24.44–28.92%), carbohydrate metabolism (8.81–10.64%), and amino acid metabolism (4.47–8.00%) dominated (Fig. 7B). Twenty-three KEGG functions exhibited significant differences among the groups, with the most prominent being global and overview maps, amino acid metabolism, membrane transport, and metabolism of cofactors and vitamins (Fig. S2B). Notably, cellular community-prokaryotes and cell motility functions increased with pit age (Fig. S2B), suggesting enhanced microbial interactions and environmental adaptation in mature PM.

Potential enzymes associated with substrate degradation and flavor formation pathways that showed significant differences among PM of different ages were selected. We focused on key functional enzymes involved in organic acid synthesis and metabolism, ethanol synthesis and metabolism, carbohydrate metabolism, nucleotide synthesis and metabolism, and amino acid synthesis and metabolism (Fig. 7C).

LEfSe analysis clearly revealed distinct functional biomarkers in microbial communities across PM ages (Fig. S2C). Five-year PM was enriched in enzymes supporting rapid synthesis of cellular components and energy metabolism. Unique functions were concentrated on building cellular structures (e.g., peptidoglycan and lipopolysaccharide), synthesizing genetic material (e.g., EC 1.3.5.4), and establishing basic energy supply (ion transport) (EC 7.2.2.21, EC 7.2.2.12, EC 7.2.2.14). These adaptations support the high demands of rapid microbial growth.

Functional profiles of 15-year PM were most distinct and complex, primarily involving environmental stress response mediation (e.g., EC 2.7.11.1), complex substances degradation (e.g., EC 3.2.1.23), unique anaerobic respiration facilitation (EC 1.2.7.3 and EC 1.2.7.11), alongside genetic defense and regulation (EC 2.1.1.72, EC 3.1.21.3). These functional traits reflect a phase of intense environmental pressure and metabolic transition.

Functional priorities of 30-year PM shifted toward transmembrane transport (EC 7.4.2.8, EC 7.2.2.10, EC 7.5.2.7), sulfur metabolism (EC 2.8.1.7), and precise internal metabolism (EC 3.1.3.16). These signature enzymes demonstrate systemic stabilization and a functional shift toward refined transport and metabolism, ensuring the consistent production of complex flavor compounds under steady-state conditions.

KEGG-based metabolic network analysis showed that PM of different ages shared highly interconnected pathways dominated by carbohydrate, energy, cofactor/vitamin, and nucleotide metabolism. Functional divergence increased with pit age (Fig. 10). The 5-year PM exhibited unique pathways associated with cellular construction (e.g., map00550, map00562), stress response (map00430), and pollutant degradation (map00440, map00622) (Fig. 10A), reflecting an ecosystem undergoing rapid establishment and environmental adaptation.

Metabolic pathway profiles analysis across different PM. (A) Comparison analysis between 5 years and 15 years, (B) Comparison analysis between 15 years and 30 years.

In contrast, 15-year PM possessed substantially more unique pathways, particularly those involved in aromatic compound degradation (e.g., map00362, map00627), nitrogen utilization (map00230), carbohydrate and energy metabolism shift (map00473), complex substrate degradation (map00550) (Fig. 10A). This indicated a metabolic shift towards efficient material cycling and flavor precursor formation.

From 15 to 30 years, metabolic functions shifted from active growth and competition to enhanced degradation of aromatic compounds (e.g., phenylacetic acid), glutathione-based redox regulation (map00480), refined energy coordination (map00030), and specialized synthesis of secondary metabolites such as terpenoids (map00909) and alkaloids (map00960) (Fig. 10B). These features collectively underpin the superior flavor-forming capacity of mature PM.

CAZy Functional annotation

A total of 450 CAZy genes were assigned to six categories. Glycoside hydrolases (GHs: 38.30-40.80%) and glycosyl transferases (GTs: 34.89-40.46%) were most abundant (Fig. 7D), highlighting strong carbohydrate-degrading and glycosylation capabilities (Zhou et al., 2023). Significant age-related differences were detected in carbohydrate esterases (higher in 5-year PM) and carbohydrate-binding modules (lower in 5-year PM) (Fig. S2D), indicating distinct substrate utilization strategies in young ecosystems.

Functional contribution of key microbial taxa

Correlation analysis revealed that 7 genera (Petrimonas, Lactobacillus, Methanosarcina, Caproicibacterium, Proteiniphilum, Methanobacterium, Methanosarcina) and 5 species (Lactobacillus acetotolerans, Methanosarcina sp., Caproicibacterium amylolyticum, Petrimonas sp._IBARAKI, Methanobacterium paludis) were the major contributors to fermentation-relevant functions (Fig. 8). Their contributions to carbohydrate and amino acid metabolism (COG-G/E, KEGG) (Fig. 8A, 8D), membrane transport (Fig. 8B, 8E), and CAZy enzymes families (GH, GT, CE) (Fig. 8C, 8F) increased with pit age, demonstrating the gradual establishment of a mature, fermentation-efficient microbiome.

Microbial interaction networks

Network analysis showed extensive positive correlations among taxa. Lactobacillus, Methanosarcina, and Methanobacterium exhibited extensive connections with other genera (Fig. 9A). Most archaeal genera showed positive correlations, indicating cooperative interactions in the PM ecosystem. Lactobacillus displayed strong negative correlations with Methanosarcina and Methanobacterium, while Methanosarcina showed positive correlations with both Methanobacterium and Atopobium (Fig. 9A). Under anaerobic conditions, Clostridium and Caproiciproducens produce caproic acid and hydrogen gas via enzymatic catalysis (Tao et al. 2014a),. However, hydrogen accumulation can partially inhibit caproic acid synthesis (Hu et al. 2019). Chai et al. (2021) proposed that the interaction between methanogenic Archaea and acid-producing Bacteria relieves hydrogen inhibition, thereby enhancing acid accumulation and promoting the formation of the characteristic flavor of NXXB (Sun et al. 2022).

At the species level, Methanosarcina sp., Lactobacillus acetotolerans, Methanobacterium paludis, Methanobacteriaceae archaeon, Bacteroidales bacterium, Clostridiales bacterium, and Porphyromonadaceae bacterium exhibited extensive microbial associations with a variety of microorganisms (Fig. 9B). A total of 20 positive correlation pairs were identified, with Methanosarcina sp. demonstrating the most associations, being significantly positively correlated with six bacterial and archaeal species (Methanobacterium paludis, Methanobacteriaceae archaeon, Clostridiales bacterium, Bacteroidales bacterium, Atopobium sp., and Porphyromonadaceae bacterium) (Fig. 9B). Lactobacillus acetotolerans showed significant negative correlations with six microbial species (Porphyromonadaceae bacterium, Clostridiales bacterium, Methanobacterium paludis, Bacteroidales bacterium, Methanobacteriaceae archaeon, and Methanosarcina sp.) (Fig. S3B).This aligns with previous findings that Lactobacillus dominates in young PM but declines with age as methanogens increase (Tao et al. 2014a). These inhibitory interactions likely result from lactic acid production and antimicrobial compounds, which help regulate archaeal overgrowth and maintain microbial homeostasis.

Environmental drivers of microbial community structure and function

Mantel tests revealed that moisture, NH₄⁺-N, AP, and pit age significantly influenced both the composition and function potential of the PM microbiota (Fig. S3). Moisture exerted the strongest influence on overall variation. Functional profiles were negatively correlated with moisture, AP, and pit age, but positively associated with NH₄⁺-N. Microbial community structure exhibited analogous correlation patterns. Interrelationships among physicochemical factors were pronounced: AP and moisture were positively correlated with pit age, whereas total acidity and humic matter content were negatively correlated. This suggests that optimizing the PM microenvironment—particularly through moisture and acidity regulation—is critical for promoting functional taxa.

Mediation effects analysis

To investigate the indirect mechanisms by which microorganisms (species or functions) affect physicochemical factors, mediation models were constructed. Microbial variables (species relative abundance from amplicon sequencing or functional gene relative abundance from metagenomic annotation) were set as independent variables (X); physicochemical factors (moisture, pH, available phosphorus, NH₄⁺-N, humic matters) were set as mediators (M) and outcome variables (Y) in sequence. The mediation analysis results were integrated to reveal more complex causal relationships (Table S3, Table S4).

Moisture was the dominant mediator, mediating 23 species-level pathways (67.6%) and 25 function-level pathways (83.3%). Among moisture-mediated pathways, key species including Lactobacillus acetotolerans, Oscillospiraceae bacterium, Methanobacterium paludis, Clostridiales bacterium, Bacteroidales bacterium, and Porphyromonadaceae bacterium regulated the accumulation of NH₄⁺-N, humic matters, and available phosphorus by influencing PM water-holding capacity. At the functional level, multiple KEGG enzymes (e.g., 3.6.4.12, 2.4.1.227, 5.1.3.14, 3.6.5.4, 6.2.1.30), CAZy families (carbohydrate esterases, auxiliary activities, carbohydrate-binding modules, polysaccharide lyases, cellulosome modules), and COG categories (J, E, K, H, L, C, R, M) all used moisture as a mediator to affect NH₄⁺-N, with additional effects on humic matters (5.1.3.14) and available phosphorus (3.6.5.4). Furthermore, the KEGG pathways "Cellular community – prokaryotes" and "Cell motility" influenced pH via moisture. pH and available phosphorus served as second-tier mediators. pH mediated 9 species-level pathways (26.5%) and 2 function-level pathways, primarily involving the effects of Methanobacterium paludis, Bacteroidales bacterium, and Porphyromonadaceae bacterium on available phosphorus and humic matters, as well as the regulation of available phosphorus and humic matters by COG-R. Available phosphorus mediated 5 species-level pathways (14.7%) and 2 function-level pathways, mainly linking Lactobacillus acetotolerans, Ruminococcaceae bacterium CPB6, and Oscillospiraceae bacterium to NH₄⁺-N and humic matters, and connecting COG-G and COG-M to humic matters.

Together, these results reveal a "moisture-centric, multi-tier cascade" causal model for microbe-driven nutrient cycling in PM ecosystems, providing evidence for microbe-environment interaction mechanisms.

Comparison between AS and metagenomic results

Both AS and metagenomics consistently identified the highest microbial diversity in 15-year PM. However, the trends for the 5- and 30-year PM were different. Differences between methods were mainly reflected in fungi (e.g., Ascomycota, Aspergillus), which exhibited extremely low abundance (< 1%). The dominant taxa identified through metagenomics aligned with those from AS. Notably, fungal taxa (e.g., Methanosaeta) showing significant differences in AS were not detected in metagenomic datasets. This is a predictable outcome given the inherent methodological differences between amplicon and metagenomic sequencing: AS targets a specific hypervariable region and is therefore more sensitive for detecting rare taxa, whereas metagenomic sequencing distributes reads across the entire genomic content, reducing sensitivity for low-abundance community members. Consequently, the absence of these taxa in metagenomes does not necessarily indicate biological absence but rather reflects differences in detection limits. Importantly, this complementarity—AS providing higher taxonomic resolution for rare biosphere members and metagenomics enabling functional characterization—supports the value of combining both approaches to study microbial succession and functional maturation in PM ecosystems. In addition, due to the adoption of the 300 bp gene prediction threshold, some genes may exist as incomplete fragments, which may slightly impact on functional annotation. Future research can combine long-read sequencing technologies (such as PacBio or Nanopore) to obtain more complete gene structures for further verification of the conclusions of this study.

Conclusions

By integrating AS and metagenomics, this study systematically elucidates the microbial and functional succession of PM across three key ages (5, 15, and 30 years) during maturation at the Sichuan Tang Dynasty Laojiao distillery. Discrepancies between the two methods were mainly observed in low-abundance fungal taxa. Microbial communities exhibited increasing taxonomic distinctiveness and amino acid metabolic functions with pit age. The 15-year PM represents a critical metabolic transition stage, characterized by the highest microbial diversity and functional diversification. PM follows a clear successional trajectory: the 5-year PM is Lactobacillus-dominated and focused on cellular construction; the 15-year PM transitions to complex carbon utilization and flavor-precursor biosynthesis; and the 30-year PM matures into a stable, flavor-optimized ecosystem enriched in caproic-acid-producing Ruminococcaceae CPB6. Notably, the PM from all three ages groups exhibited superior physicochemical properties compared to previous reports. Moisture, NH₄⁺–N, available phosphorus, and pit age significantly shape both microbial community structure and function, with moisture exerting the strongest influence. pH is the primary driver of archaeal communities, while moisture predominantly shapes fungal communities. Mechanistically, microorganisms causally drive nutrient cycling by first altering moisture status, which subsequently regulates pH and available phosphorus, ultimately leading to the accumulation of NH₄⁺-N and humic matters. This establishes a "moisture‑centric, multi‑tier cascade" causal network. The identified connections between key physicochemical parameters and microbial taxa provide valuable insights for scientific PM management. Overall, PM maturation is driven by coordinated environmental selection and functional optimization, ultimately forming a stable and efficient microecosystem for high-quality Baijiu production. This study provides a scientific basis for microbiome-guided strategies to optimize Baijiu fermentation processes, while also offering methodological guidance for future PM research.

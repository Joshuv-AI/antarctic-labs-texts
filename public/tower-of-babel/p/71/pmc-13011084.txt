Reconstruction of extensive craniofacial defect following paraganglioma excision using a chimeric anterolateral thigh flap: a case report
Authors: Phan Tuấn Nghĩa, Đặng Phương Nam, Trần Thiết Sơn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractExtended radical excision of aggressive tumors, such as the carotid paraganglioma involving the ethmoid, maxillary and intracranial spaces, results in massive composite craniofacial defects. Successful reconstruction requires simultaneously achieving effective dead space refilling and creating a stable anophthalmic orbital socket. This report highlights the utility of the chimeric anterolateral thigh (ALT) flap in addressing this reconstructive challenge. A 28-year-old man presented with a complex craniofacial defect following the wide excision of an extensive carotid paraganglioma. A chimeric ALT musculocutaneous flap (22 × 10 cm) was harvested, containing two independent skin paddles and a part of the vastus lateralis muscle. One musculocutaneous flap (8 × 14 cm) was de-epithelialized to augment the dead space after total maxillectomy and orbital exenteration. The fasciocutaneous flap (6 × 8 cm) was thinned and molded into a double-layer flap, which was rolled into a socket configuration for orbital reconstruction. Microvascular anastomosis was performed end-to-end to the facial vessels. Intraoperatively, perfusion of all flap components was excellent. The flap remained well perfused postoperatively without congestion or ischemia. At the 25-month follow-up, the patient demonstrated stable healing, a well-contoured midface profile, and adequate socket depth for prosthetic fitting. The chimeric ALT flap offers a highly reliable and versatile solution for the complex reconstruction of massive composite craniofacial defects following extensive tumor resection. This chimeric approach effectively meets the simultaneous requirements for dead-space obliteration and stable orbital socket formation using a single donor site.

Introduction

Radical removal of extensive tumors, such as carotid paraganglioma involving the ethmoid, maxillary and intracranial spaces, creates large complex craniofacial defects that require both bone and soft tissue reconstruction [1,2]. The main challenge in reconstruction is simultaneously ensuring complete filling of the dead space in the maxillary and orbital cavities while accurately recreating the delicate orbital socket. Although the anterolateral thigh (ALT) flap is a versatile option for soft-tissue coverage, its use in orbital reconstruction has been limited due to its perceived bulkiness. However, recent advances in techniques and the ability to design the flap as a chimeric type have overcome these limitations. This chimeric design offers independent components: a muscular, bulky part to fill the large dead space, and a flexible skin paddle that can be shaped into a functional orbital socket. The multi-component design of the chimeric ALT flap is therefore ideally suited for managing these needs. We present a successful case of reconstructing a large craniofacial defect after extended carotid paraganglioma resection using a chimeric ALT flap. This report demonstrates the effectiveness and versatility of this technique in managing large tumor-related defects, ensuring stable healing, and providing.

Case report

A 28-year-old man presented with a 4-year history of a facial mass. Imaging showed a carotid paraganglioma involving the ethmoid and maxillary sinuses, the retrobulbar space, and extending intracranially into the right frontotemporal region. Preoperative embolization of the tumor’s feeding vessels was performed, followed by complete excision by the neurosurgery team (Figure 1). One week later, the patient was referred for reconstruction of the composite craniofacial defect involving both bony and soft tissue components. The patient was managed and followed by the neurosurgery team; no adjuvant radiotherapy or chemotherapy was required postoperatively as the tumor was benign and completely resected.

Reconstructive procedure

An ALT musculocutaneous flap measuring 22 × 10 cm was harvested based on two perforators originating from the descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery. The flap was elevated and divided into two separate skin islands and a portion of the vastus lateralis muscle. The larger muscle-skin component (8 × 14 cm) was de-epithelialized to fill the dead space after total maxillectomy and orbital exenteration. The second skin paddle (6 × 8 cm) was folded into a double-layer flap for orbital socket reconstruction. It was rolled into a socket-like shape and precisely inset to the remnants of the upper and lower eyelids using interrupted absorbable sutures to minimize tension (Figure 2). A conformer was placed to maintain socket depth and prevent contracture. Microvascular anastomosis was performed in an end-to-end manner between the flap pedicle and the facial vessels. Intraoperatively, perfusion of both skin paddles and the muscle component was excellent. The donor site was closed primarily with minimal morbidity. Postoperatively, the flap remained well perfused without congestion or ischemia, and the patient reported satisfactory functional outcomes. Specifically, speech and mastication remained unaffected, and nasal airflow remained unobstructed. At 25 months, the patient demonstrated stable healing, adequate socket depth for prosthesis fitting, and a well-contoured midfacial profile (Figure 3).

Discussion

Paragangliomas are a fascinating and challenging group of neuroendocrine tumors that develop from extra-adrenal paraganglionic tissue spread along the autonomic nervous system. Managing aggressive tumors like carotid paragangliomas involving the intracranial, ethmoid and maxillary sinus regions requires extensive radical excision to achieve oncological control. This procedure usually results in a large, complex defect, often classified as a large orbital-maxillary defect (e.g. Brown classification type IV) [3,4]. Such defects involve total maxillectomy and orbital exenteration, leading to: (a) a significant volumetric loss (dead space); (b) exposure of the cranial base, which demands a durable dural seal; and (c) the creation of a stable anophthalmic socket for prosthetic rehabilitation. Reconstruction after paraganglioma removal aims to restore both appearance and function while reducing morbidity. The specific reconstruction needs depend on the tumor’s location, the extent of resection, and the structures sacrificed during removal. Nonetheless, the literature offers limited descriptions of specific free-flap or local-flap protocols typically used after paraganglioma excision [1,2].

In these situations, using grafts or local/regional flaps is insufficient due to the large volume needed and the compromised recipient bed, often consisting of exposed orbital bone or tissue damaged by previous treatments (although not relevant in this case, it is a crucial factor). Therefore, vascularized free flaps are more appropriate, offering dependable tissue that supports wound healing and reduces the risks of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leakage, infection and flap contracture [5,6]. The key step in reconstructing large maxillofacial and anterior skull base defects is effectively filling dead space to prevent seroma formation, fluid buildup, infection and CSF leakage. The ALT musculocutaneous flap is an advantageous choice for volume management compared to purely fasciocutaneous flaps, as it causes minimal donor-site morbidity and offers great versatility. The decision to use the ALT flap over the latissimus dorsi myocutaneous flap (LDMF), another reliable musculocutaneous option, was based on several strategic benefits. While the LDMF provides ample, reliable muscle and skin, the ALT flap offers flexibility in chimeric design. For large-volume, multi-component repairs, the chimeric flap is preferable because it can replace each component as needed. Additionally, each part of the chimeric flap is separate, making molding and folding more convenient. In our case, the chimeric flap, consisting of a segment of the vastus lateralis muscle and the de-epithelialized, bulky skin paddle, was essential. This musculocutaneous element provided the necessary mass and bulk to tightly fill the extensive maxillary and orbital cavities, effectively obliterating dead space and providing structural support to the midface contour. Studies endorse the use of the ALT flap, especially its musculocutaneous or chimeric variations, as a reliable choice for large skull base and cavity reconstruction [7–10]. This approach ensures a reliable, vascularized fill, reducing the risk of postoperative volume loss and related complications. The secondary, possibly most delicate challenge is creating a functional orbital socket with adequate depth and flexibility to hold an ocular prosthesis without future contracture [11]. Traditionally, the fasciocutaneous ALT flap was limited in orbital reconstruction due to its thickness. However, advances in thinning techniques now allow the skin paddle to be safely reduced to about 8 mm. This refinement transforms the flap from a bulky mass into a pliable lining suitable for delicate three-dimensional structures such as the orbit. It allows for simultaneous reconstruction of deep (maxilla/bulk) and superficial (socket lining) structures through a single vascular pedicle. This method ensures a sturdy, non-contracting socket lining, which is crucial for stable prosthetic fitting and a pleasing aesthetic result. While the use of ALT flaps for craniofacial defects is well documented, this case is unique due to the specific nature of the defect following excision of a massive vascular paraganglioma.

Conclusions

The chimeric ALT flap is an advanced, feasible and versatile option for reconstructing complex, massive craniofacial defects after extensive tumor removal. It effectively fills large dead spaces and provides the delicate detail necessary for stable orbital socket reconstruction.

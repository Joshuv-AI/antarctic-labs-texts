LncPVT1 modulates decidualization and links to recurrent spontaneous abortions via glucose and lipid metabolic regulation
Authors: Chen Du, ZhiMin Wang, Terigele Dai, Yuan Yuan, Jie Zhao, Ya Tuo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Recurrent spontaneous abortion (RSA), defined by the occurrence of two or more consecutive pregnancy losses, significantly affects women’s quality of life. However, the intricate and complex mechanisms still pose bottlenecks in the diagnosis and treatment of RSA. Transcriptome analysis of decidual tissue, along with pathway analysis of differentially expressed genes, revealed that the most significant differences were observed in pathways related to linoleic acid metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, glycerophospholipid metabolism, and arachidonic acid metabolism, all of which are integral to glucose and lipid metabolism. Through upstream mechanistic studies, it has been found that long non-coding plasmacytoma variant translocation 1 (lncPVT1) plays a crucial regulatory role in modulating these pathways. The present study aimed to verify the hypothesis that lncPVT1 modulates the biological functions of decidual tissue in RSA and to investigate the upstream mechanism involved. It was found that lncPVT1 was overexpressed in individuals with RSA. Furthermore, knockdown of lncPVT1 enhanced the proliferation, migration, and invasion of human endometrial stromal cells while decreasing apoptosis. In vitro experiments demonstrated that lncPVT1 knockdown influences the expression of key enzymes in the glycolytic pathway, including glucose transporter 1 (Glut1), glucose transporter 4 (Glut4), hexokinase 2 (HK2), and lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA), as well as the expression of TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) in the lipid metabolism and RSA. Overall, our study demonstrated that lncPVT1 knockdown disrupts glycolysis and lipid metabolism, subsequently impacting the incidence of RSA. This study provides novel insights into understanding the molecular pathogenesis of RSA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47103-7.

Introduction

Recurrent spontaneous abortion (RSA), defined by the occurrence of two or more consecutive pregnancy losses, is a relatively common condition affecting 1–2% of women of reproductive age1. The etiological factors contributing to RSA are varied and encompass chromosomal abnormalities, endocrine dysfunctions, immune imbalances, anatomical anomalies, environmental influences, among others, all of which may play a role in the manifestation of RSA2–5. Nonetheless, approximately 50% of RSA cases remain idiopathic, indicating that the condition is multifactorial and characterized by numerous inconsistencies and ambiguous relationships in its clinical diagnosis and management. Consequently, the mechanisms underlying RSA necessitate further research and investigation.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), defined as RNA molecules exceeding 200 nucleotides in length without protein-coding potential6, are capable of regulating the expression of downstream proteins. Specifically, lncRNAs can act as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) by interacting with microRNAs (miRNAs), thereby modulating the expression of downstream genes7. These properties of lncRNAs present new opportunities and perspectives for the diagnosis and treatment of disease8. A multitude of lncRNAs have been demonstrated to play critical regulatory roles in the development and progression of RSA. For example, the lncRNA amine oxidase copper containing 4, pseudogene (AOC4P), which facilitates the recruitment of TNF-receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) to regulate enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) ubiquitination, is involved in trophoblast glycolysis and M2 macrophage polarization, potentially contributing to the onset and progression of RSA9. Similarly, another lncRNA, long non-coding RNA maternally expressed gene 3(lncMEG3), modulates the expression of RAS p21 protein activator 1(RASA1), thereby promoting proliferation and invasion while inhibiting apoptosis in the primary trophoblasts of RSA patients10. However, there is a paucity of research regarding the impact of the decidualization process on RSA.

Decidualization, a process marked by a series of metabolic, biochemical, vascular, and immunological changes within the endometrial stroma, is essential for regulating embryonic implantation11. Defects or impairments, in decidualization can lead to abnormalities at the maternal-fetal interface, significantly affecting normal embryonic development and successful implantation. In cases of RSA, deficiencies in this process, which impede the embryo’s capacity for stable growth and successful implantation, are frequently identified as contributing factors to miscarriage.

Given the pivotal role and intricate nature of the decidualization metabolic process in RSA, it is imperative for us to concentrate our research efforts on this specific metabolic pathway. In light of the critical function of lncRNAs in modulating these metabolic processes, we will accordingly carry out targeted screening of lncRNAs within both the metabolic pathways and their associated regulatory networks, aiming to elucidate this regulatory interplay.

To investigate the potential scenario, we conducted transcriptomic profiling on decidual tissues obtained from RSA cases and healthy controls, with a particular focus on KEGG pathways. Following this, we constructed competing endogenous RNA(ceRNA) networks to pinpoint and rank candidate regulatory factors. Additionally, we conducted both loss-of-function and gain-of-function experiments in human endometrial stromal cells, complemented by studies using an abortion-susceptible mouse model, to assess the phenotypes and metabolic alterations linked to decidualization.

To examine this possibility, we carried out transcriptomic profiling of decidual tissues from RSA cases and controls, constructed ceRNA networks to prioritize candidate regulators, and performed loss- and gain-of-function studies in human endometrial stromal cells, together with an abortion-prone mouse model, to evaluate decidualization-associated phenotypes and metabolic readouts.

Materials and methods

Ethics declarations

All human (using decidual tissue) and animal experimental procedures have received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University (Ethics No. 2023048). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to sample collection. The human study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All animal experiments were performed in accordance with the relevant institutional guidelines and regulations, and all methods are reported in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

Participants

The study cohort comprised five patients diagnosed with RSA and five healthy controls who voluntarily elected to terminate their pregnancies, recruited from the Affiliated Hospital of Inner Mongolia Medical University between January 2022 and December 2022. Decidual tissue samples were collected by reproductive clinical specialists.

Sample collection and processing

The decidual tissue was rinsed with physiological saline, rapidly frozen by exposure to nitrogen, and subsequently stored at −80℃ until RNA extraction was conducted.

RNA-sequencing analysis

Total RNA was isolated from the decidual tissues utilizing Trizol (Invitrogen, MA, USA). The RNA concentration was determined using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo, MA, USA). The integrity and quantity of the RNA were evaluated using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA) and a NanoDrop, respectively. Five micrograms of RNA from each sample served as the initial material for RNA preparation. Ribosomal RNA was removed from the total RNA samples using the Ribo-Zero™ rRNA Removal Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), following the manufacturer ‘s protocal. The RNA-seq library was prepared utilizing the NEBNext Ultra Directional RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, MA, USA), in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. The resulting library was quantified using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer and sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq 4000 platform (Illumina, CA, USA) with paired-end reads of 2 ⋅ 150 bp. Subsequent, bioinformatics analysis commenced with the preprocessing of raw data using Cutadapt (v1.10) to eliminate adapter sequences and exclude low-quality reads. The resulting high-quality sequencing data were then aligned to the reference genome using Hisat2 (v2.0.4). Transcript assembly and quantification of transcript abundance for all genes were conducted using StringTie (v1.0.4). Transcripts of lncRNA and mRNA were identified by aligning them with their respective standard databases, specifically GENCODE (v27, ftp://ftp.sanger.ac.uk/pub/gencode/Gencode_human/release_27/gencode.v27.lncRNA_transcripts.fa.gz) and Ensembl (v101,ftp://ftp.ensembl.org/pub/release-101/fasta/homo_sapiens/dna/). Gene expression levels were quantified using the Reads Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads (RPKM).

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis of decidual tissue between RSA group and the normal group was performed using the “edgeR” package for both lncRNA and mRNA, applying a filtering criterion of P<0.05 and |log2(FC)| ≥1. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were visually represented through a volcano plot and a heatmap, generated using the “ggplot2” and “heatmap” packages, respectively.

Construction of ceRNA networks

The lncRNAs and mRNAs associated with expressed miRNAs across all samples were predicted using the ENCORI software platform(https://rnasysu.com/encori/). To ensure the robustness of the ceRNA network, a Venn diagram intersection analysis was performed on the miRNAs predicted to mediate regulatory interactions between lncRNAs and miRNAs, as well as mRNAs and miRNAs. The central role of miRNA within the regulatory network facilitates the deduction of their interactions with lncRNAs and downstream mRNAs, thereby establishing a lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory axis.

Functional annotation analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway12 analyses were conducted on the differentially expressed mRNAs (DEmRNAs) using the “clusterProfiler” package in R.

Real-time quantitative PCR analysis

This investigation was centered specifically on exploring the regulatory role of lncRNAs in RSA, thereby paving the way for subsequent research focused on the identification of potential lncRNAs. A total of 70 ng of RNA was extracted from decidual tissue and subsequently reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) assays were performed using the ABI 7900 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The expression levels of the target RNAs were normalized to glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences specifically designed for RT-qPCR amplification of the selected target RNAs were detailed in Table S1.

Cell culture, and transfection, and treatment

Human endometrial stromal cells (HESCs) were procured from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA, Cat. No. SCRC-2002) and cultured in Dulbecco ‘s modified Eagle medium/F12 (DMEM/F12, Thermo Fisher Scientific), supplemented with 2.5% charcoal/dextran-treated fetal bovine serum, 100 IU/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin, at 37 ℃ in a 5% CO2 atmosphere. For in vitro decidualization, HESCs were exposed to 0.5 mM 8-Br-cAMP (Sigma-Aldrich) and 1 µM methoxyprogesterone acetate (Sigma-Aldrich) for 48 h. LncPVT1 knockdown and overexpression vectors were constructed and transfected into the cells under optimal growth conditions using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlbad, CA, USA), followed by a 48 h incubation. Post-incubation, the medium was replaced, and the intervention effect of the target gene were analyzed using RT-qPCR. During the development of cell models, the respective cell lines undergo specific treatment protocols to facilitate the construction of these models.

CCK-8 cell viability assay

Post-transfection, the cells were incubated for intervals of 0, 24, 48, and 72 h. Subsequently, 100 µL of DMEM (Corning, NY, USA) supplemented with 10% cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was added to each well. The plate was then incubated in the dark for 2 h, following which the absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a pre-warmed microplate reader.

Apoptosis assay

The transfected cells were washed with binding buffer and stained with 5 µL of Annexin V conjugated with allophycocyanin (Annexin V-APC) and Propidium Iodide (PI) (KeyGEN BioTECH, Nanjing, China) for 10 min at room temperature. The apoptosis rate was evaluated using an Attune NxT flow cytometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

Transwell invasion assay

To assess the invasive potential of HESCs, a transwell invasion assay was meticulously performed. In the preparatory phase, the upper chamber of the transwell setup was coated with 60 µL of Matrigel, which was prepared at a 1:2 ratio with DMEM lacking phenol red, and subsequently incubated for one hour at 37 ℃. HESCs were then seeded into these upper chambers at a density of 103 cells per well. The lower chambers were supplemented with DMEM devoid of phenol red and enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum. After a 48-hour incubation period, cells in the upper chamber were meticulously removed. The transwell filters were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, followed by two washes with phosphate-buffered saline. Staining was performed using 0.5% hematoxylin for 5 min. Invasive cells were counted in three distinct fields using a Leica Upright Metallurgical Microscope (Wetzlar, Germany). To quantify invasion, absorbance at 550 nm was measured with a spectrophotometric plate reader. To ensure the experimental reliability, this entire procedure was conducted in triplicate.

Scratch wound assay

Cell lines (1 ⋅ 105) were cultured in 24-well plates until reaching confluence. Upon achieving confluence, a uniform scratch was made across the center of each well using a 200 µL pipette tip. The wells were subsequently rinsed twice with serum-free medium to remove any non-adherent cells and residual growth media. The cells were then maintained in serum-free medium for 48 h. The wound areas were documented at both initial and final time points to assess cellular migration into the scratched region. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate and repeated independently three times.

F-actin staining

Following treatment, the cell lines were washed with 1 ⋅ phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and fixed with 3% paraformaldehyde for 20 min. The cells were then washed with 1 ⋅ PBS and permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 5 min at room temperature. After two additional washes with 1⋅ PBS, the cells were incubated with 1.5 mg/mL rhodamine phalloidin (ab235138; Abcam, United Kingdom) and goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) Fluor 594-conjugated (S0005; Affinity Biosciences, China) for 30 min at room temperature to stain F-actin filaments. The cytoskeletal structure was visualized and captured using an inverted Nikon Eclipse TE200 microscope.

Validation assays on glucose metabolism abnormalities in cell lines

Prolactin analysis

Prolactin concentrations were measured in duplicate utilizing a commercial human ELISA kit (Abnova, Denmark) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

Analysis of lactate dehydrogenase activity

The activity of lactate dehydrogenase in treated cell lines was evaluated using the Lactate Dehydrogenase Activity Assay (Solarbio, Beijing, China).

Glucose oxidase assay (GOD-POD)

Post-treatment, the remaining culture medium was collected for glucose oxidase assay (mlbio, China). Experimental wells were supplemented with 300 µL of GOD-POD working solution and 3 µL of culture solution per well. Standard wells received 300 µL of GOD-POD working solution and 3 µL of GOD-POD standard solution, while blank wells were supplemented with 300 µL of GOD-POD working solution and 3 µL of distilled water (ddH2O) per well. The plates were gently agitated and incubated at 37 ℃ for 15 min prior to absorbance measurement at 505 nm using a Microplate Reader (SpectraMax190, Molecular Devices, USA). The absorbance of the experimental wells was denoted as Aexperiment, and the absorbance of the standard wells was denoted as Astandard. The glucose concentration (mmol/L) was calculated using the formula: Glu= Aexperiment/Astandard ⋅ 5 to calculate the glucose concentration (mmol/L).

RT-qPCR of enzymes associated with glycolysis of LDHA, PKM2, HK2, PDK1, Glut1 and Glut4

Total RNA was extracted from treated cells using the TRIzol® reagent. Subsequently, 3 µg of RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA utilizing the Transcriptor cDNA Synthesis Kit (Roche Diagnostics). RT-qPCR was conducted with SYBR Green (Soyetin Biohech) under the following thermal cycling conditions: an initial denaturation at 95 ℃ for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 3 s and 60 ℃ for 30 s. GAPDH was served as the normalization control, and the primers for the respective genes were listed in Table S2.

Validation assays on lipid metabolism abnormalities in cell lines

Sample preparation

The knockdown and control cell samples were precisely transferred into 2 mL centrifuge tube. Subsequently, 1 mL of a chloroform-methanol (2:1) solution and 100 mg of glass beads were added. The samples were then subjected to a high-throughput tissue grinder after being frozen for 2–3 min, with shaking at 60 Hz for 1 min, repeated twice. The supernatant was collected into a 15 mL centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 ℃. Subsequently, 2 mL of a 1% sulfuric acid methanol solution was added, and the mixture was vortexed thoroughly for 1 min. After esterification in a water bath at 80℃ for 30 min, 1 mL of n-hexane was added. Following vortexing and a 5 min standing period, 5 mL of water at 4 ℃ was added for washing. Centrifuge the sample at 3500 rpm for 10 min at 4 ℃. Subsequently, transfer 700 µL of the supernatant into a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and add 100 mg of anhydrous sodium sulfate powder to eliminate excess water. After vortexing and centrifugation, pipette 300 µL of the supernatant into a new 2 mL centrifuge tube. Add 15 µL of methyl salicylate (500 ppm) as an internal standard to the supernatant. Vortex the mixture prior to gas chromatography-mass spectrum (GC-MS) analysis.

Gas chromatography-mass spectrum conditions

The GC analysis was conducted using a Trace 1300 gas chromatograph (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with a Thermo TG-FAME capillary column (50 m ⋅ 0.25 mm ID ⋅ 0.20 μm). Helium served as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 0.63 mL/min. Samples were injected in split mode at a ratio of 8:1, using an injection volume of 1µL and an injector temperature of 250 ℃. The ion source and transfer line temperatures were set at 300 ℃ and 280 ℃, respectively. The column temperature was programmed to increase from an initial temperature of 80 ℃, held for 1 min, to 160 ℃ at a rate of 20 ℃/min, maintained for 1.5 min, then to 196 ℃ at a rate of 3 ℃/min, held for 8.5 min, and finally to 250 ℃ at a rate of 20 ℃/min, where it was maintained for 3 min.

Fatty acid quantification

A mixed standard stock solution containing 51 fatty acids at a concentration of 4000 µg/mL was serially diluted to create ten calibration points. The calibration curve was established by adding N-hexane, covering a concentration range from 1 to 2000 µg/mL (specifically at 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1000, and 2000 µg/mL) to facilitate the quantification of the sample.

Animal experiments

To elucidate the impact of lipid and glucose metabolism on the progression of RSA through the regulation of metabolic pathways, an animal model of RSA was developed. Concurrently, metabolic pathway inhibitors were administered to assess embryo survival. CBA/J (stock #000656), BALB/c (stock #000651), and DBA/2J (stock #000671) mice, aged 8 weeks, were procured from Jackson Laboratories13. For the control group mice, 6 female CBA/J mice were mated with 3 male BALB/c mice. The model group was established by mating 6 female CBA/J mice with male 3 DBA/2J mice. In the model treatment group, 6 female CBA/J mice were mated with 3 DBA/2J mice, and the inhibitor 2-deoxy-D-glucose(2-DG) was administered via intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 5 mmol/kg. The details of mice used in this experimental are presented in Table S3. The presence of a vaginal plug was checked the day following mating, which was designated as the first day of pregnancy. On the 14th day post-gestation, the mice were anesthetized with a pentobarbital sodium solution at a dose of 50 mg/kg, and then humanely euthanized using the cervical dislocation method and their embryos were extracted for analysis. Because fetal loss in this model is typically observed at mid-gestation (GD12–14), tissues were collected on GD14 to score resorption sites and quantify the resorption rate13. The total number of embryos, viable embryos, and aborted embryos were quantified, and the rate of embryo loss was calculated using the formula: Embryo loss rate (%) = (Number of lost embryos/(Number of lost embryos + Number of viable embryos)) ⋅ 100%.

HE staining

Decidua tissue samples were appropriately fixed, dehydrated, and embedded in paraffin wax for sectioning. Following deparaffinization and rehydration, 5 μm longitudinal sections were stained with hematoxylin for 5 min, subjected to 5 dips in 1% acid ethanol (1% HCl in 70% ethanol), and rinsed with distilled water. Subsequently, the sections were stained with eosin for 3 min, followed by dehydration using graded alcohols and clearing in xylene. The prepared slides were then examined and photographed using an Olympus BX51 fluorescence microscope (Tokyo, Japan).

RT-qPCR analysis of lncPVT1 in uterine tissue

A total of 70 ng of RNA was isolated from uterine tissue and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. RT-qPCR assays were performed using the ABI 7900 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The expression levels of lncPVT1 were normalized to GAPDH using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences specifically designed for the Real-Time PCR amplification of lncPVT1 were provided in Table S1.

Western blotting assay

Protein samples were separated using a 10% polyacrylamide gel and subsequently transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). The membranes were initially blocked for 1 h with a Tris Buffered Saline and Tween 20 solution containing 5% skim milk. They were then incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with antibodies, including TRAF6 (Abcam Inc, Shanghai, China) and GAPDH (Affinity Bioscience, Jiangsu, China). Following washing, the membranes were incubated with a secondary antibody for 1 h. Color development was performed using the Immobilon Western Chemiluminescent HRP Substrate kit (Millipore Corp). Finally, the immunoreactive bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Rockford, IL, USA).

Immunohistochemistry

For the immunohistochemical quantification of TRAF6, 4 μm thick paraffin-embedded tissue sections were prepared and subjected to standard deparaffinization and rehydration protocols. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched by immersing the sections in 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min. Subsequently, the sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a polyclonal rabbit anti-TRAF6 antibody (Abcam, ab40675, 1:1000 dilution). This was followed by a 30 min incubation at room temperature with a goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (S0001, Affinity Biosciences, China) specific for rabbit immunoglobulins. Visualization was complished using diaminobenzidine (DAB) staining, and sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, and mounted under coverslips. Imaging was conducted using a Leica Upright Metallurgical Microscope (Wetzlar, Germany).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 19.0 (SPSS, Inc.). The GraphPad Prism 6.0 software (GraphPad Software, Inc.) was employed for the generation of histograms and scatter plots. The independent samples t-test was utilized to assess the statistical significance between groups. P<0.05 was deemed indicative of a statistically significant difference.

Results

Identification and clinical characterization of lncPVT1

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) revealed two distinct distribution patterns of decidual tissues between individuals diagnosed with RSA and healthy controls who voluntarily terminated their pregnancies (Fig. 1a). To identify aberrantly expressed transcripts associated with RSA, we conducted a differential expression analysis of transcripts in decidual tissues between RSA patients and healthy controls. Through threshold screening, we identified 477 differentially expressed lncRNAs, comprising 151 that were upregulated and 326 that were downregulated (Fig. 1b). Additionally, a total of 430 differentially expressed mRNAs were identified, including 130 upregulated and 300 downregulated (Fig. 1c). To investigate the underlying pathological mechanisms of RSA, we conducted a biological function enrichment analysis of the DEmRNAs. GO analysis indicated that the terms “regulation of erythrocyte differentiation”, “positive regulation of caspase activity in apoptosis”, and “embryonic skeletal system morphogenesis” were enriched within the biological processes (BP) category. In terms of cellular components (CC), the terms “CAK-ERCC2 complex”, “dynactin complex”, and “HFE-transferrin receptor complex” were significantly enriched. Major representatives of molecular functions (MF) included small molecule binding, mismatch base pair DNA N-glycosylase activity, and aryl sulfotransferase activity (Fig. 1d). Additionally, 10 KEGG pathway analysis terms were evaluated, including “glycolysis/gluconeogenesis”, “PI3K-Akt signaling pathway”, “TGF-beta signaling pathway”, “linoleic acid metabolism”, “glycerophospholipid metabolism”, and “metabolic pathways” (Fig. 1e). Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that these pathways are predominantly enriched in abnormalities related to glucose and lipid metabolism. To further investigate the regulatory role of lncRNA in these pathways, we constructed ceRNA regulatory networks (Fig. 1f). Within the ceRNA network, genes associated with glucose metabolic disorders are centered around LDHA, pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), hexokinase 2 (HK2), pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase isozyme 1 (PDK1), glucose transporter 1 (Glut1), and glucose transporter 4 (Glut4), while TRAF6 is linked to lipid metabolic disorders. Given our focus on glucose and lipid metabolism, lncPVT1, which regulate these genes, were selected for further study. To further substantiate the association between the candidate genes lncPVT1 with the occurrence and progression of RSA, RT-qPCR analyses were performed to measure the expression levels of lncPVT1 (Fig. 1g).

Effects of lncPVT1 on the construction of HESCs and the relation with the decidualization

To investigate the potential role of lncPVT1 in the pathogenesis of RSA, the knockdown and overexpression (OE) models of lncPVT1 were developed in HESCs. In the overexpression model group, transfection with lncPVT1-OE plasmid led to a significant increase in lncPVT1 mRNA levels in HESCs (Fig. 2a), whereas transfection with lncPVT1 short hairpin RNA (shRNA) resulted a in substantial decrease in lncPVT1 mRNA levels in HESCs (Fig. 2b). To confirm the relationship between lncPVT1 and the process of decidualization, as well as its link to RSA, an in vitro decidualization cell model was established using HESCs induced with medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) and 8-Bromoadenosine 3’,5’-cyclic monophosphate (8-Br-cAMP) (Fig. 2c). Our study examined the expression patterns of lncPVT1 across various induction durations of decidualization in this cellular model. The study identified the 48-hour induction point as optimal for peak lncPVT1 expression during decidualization. Decidualization was associated with reduced lncPVT1 levels (Fig. 2d), highlighting their interaction. Further analysis using CCK-8, showed that knockdown of lncPVT1 increased HESCs viability (Fig. 2e). Flow cytometry was conducted to assess cell apoptosis, revealing that lncPVT1 knockdown significantly increased the number of HESCs (Fig. 2f). Similarly, we also detected a cell apoptosis-related protein expression using Western blotting (WB) that after lncPVT1 knockdown, Bcl-2 associated X protein (Bax), caspase 3 and C-caspase 3 expression were reduced, whereas B-cell CLL/lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2) was elevated. The above experiments showed same results after decidualization (Fig. 2g, Fig. S1). The data indicated that lncPVT1 knockdown enhanced migration and invasion capabilities of HESCs (Fig. 2h and i). During decidualization, immunofluorescence staining of F-actin reveals morphological changes in which cells adopt a multipolar configuration with numerous cellular projections. (Fig. 2j). Additionally, ELISA assays revealed that lncPVT1 knockdown increased expression of the decidualization marker prolactin (PRL), thereby confirming its influence on the decidualization process (Fig. 2k). Collectively, these findings indicate that lncPVT1 knockdown promotes decidualization by enhancing HESCs proliferation and migration, suppressing apoptosis, and upregulating F-actin and PRL expression. These findings highlight a pivotal regulatory role for lncPVT1 in endometrial decidualization, providing novel insights into the molecular mechanisms of associated reproductive disorders.

The association between knockdown of lncPVT1 and glucose metabolism

RNA sequencing of decidual tissues revealed that differentially expressed genes are predominantly enriched in pathways related to glucose metabolism. In the context of assessing metabolic dysregulation in RSA, three critical indicators-lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity, glycolysis levels, and glucose uptake capacity are of considerable importance. Variations in these indicators have significant implications for embryonic implantation and development, closely associating them with the onset and progression of RSA. To elucidate the mechanism by which lncPVT1 expression leads to impaired decidualization, we conducted evaluations to assess abnormalities in glucose metabolism.

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), a crucial enzyme in the final step of glycolysis, serves as an indicator of anaerobic glycolytic capacity, reflecting compensatory mechanisms that may arise due to mitochondrial dysfunction or alterations in metabolic pathways. The expression of LDH in shlncPVT1 group was lower than in the shCtrl group, suggesting that the knockdown of lncPVT1 may inhibit LDH activity in HESCs (Fig. 3a). However, following treatment with decidualization, LDH activity decreased further compared to the shlncPVT1 group (P < 0.001), implying that decidualization may inhibit LDH activity and lead to altered glycolysis levels. The glucose oxidase-peroxidase (GOD-POD) method was employed to assess glucose levels in each cell group, thereby indirectly evaluating glucose availability. As illustrated in Fig. 3b, glucose uptake in the shlncPVT1 group was higher than that in the shCtrl group (P < 0.001), suggesting that lncPVT1 knockdown may enhance glucose metabolism in HESCs. The glucose concentration in the shlncPVT1 group was significantly lower than that observed in the shlncPVT1 + DE group (P < 0.001), indicating that decidualization enhances glucose metabolism in HESCs. We also detected a dysregulation in glycolysis-related protein expression after knockdown lncPVT1 by RT-qPCR (Fig. 3c).

To elucidate the mechanism by which lipid metabolism contributes to RSA, fatty acid analyses were performed using in vitro assays. Lipid metabolism, a complex biochemical process within organisms, involves the synthesis, degradation, and transformation of various lipid molecules. Fatty acids, as fundamental components of lipids, play a critical role in lipid metabolism. As illustrated in Fig. 4a, PCA was conducted on the lncPVT1 and NC groups, utilizing the expression levels of 52 fatty acids. The analysis revealed a clear separation trend between the two groups. A subsequent heatmap analysis of the 52 fatty acids demonstrated varying degrees of alteration between the groups, further supporting the influence of lncPVT1 knockdown on lipid metabolism (Fig. 4b). Notably, significant differences were observed in fatty acids such as palmitoleic acid, heptadecanoic acid, and oleic acid, as depicted in Fig. 4c.

In vivo studies were performed using models of normal pregnancy and abortion in mice to assess the impact of glycolysis and lipids on pregnancy outcomes. Following the intraperitoneal administration of 2-DG to inhibit glycolysis in model mice, a reduction in embryonic loss rate was observed in the treated group, further substantiating the association between glycolysis and RSA (Fig. 5b). Compared with the control group, the expression of TRAF6 was increased in the RSA group, and treatment with 2-DG could reduce the RSA-induced increase in TRAF6 (Fig. 5c). To further elucidate the relationship between lncPVT1 and glucose metabolism, a quantitative analysis of lncPVT1 mRNA was conducted across three groups, and the findings revealed that lncPVT1 expression was elevated in the model mice compared to the control group. Discussion

LncRNAs are RNA molecules transcribed from the genome that are primarily located in the nucleus and cytoplasm. Due to their extensive lengths and complex secondary structures, lncRNAs exhibit resistance to degradation by RNA enzymes, which confers stability and potential regulatory roles within the cell. Recent advancements in sequencing technology have significantly enhanced our understanding of lncRNAs. LncRNAs can function as sponges to regulate the activity of miRNAs14,15. Additionally, lncRNAs have been implicated in regulating diverse processes during pregnancy, including decidualization and trophoblast development16–18. Due to the intricate interplay of regulatory genes and processes, there have been relatively few studies examining the relationship between lncRNAs and RSA, a condition characterized by this complex regulatory network. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify novel lncRNAs that could serve as potential diagnostic biomarkers for RSA.

In our study, we analyzed sequencing data from RSA decidual tissues and validated our findings using RT-qPCR, leading to the identification of lncPVT1, which is upregulated in decidual tissues. Our results suggest that lncPVT1 may represent a diagnostic target for RSA. To elucidate its biological role, we conducted a series of functional assays. In vitro experiments demonstrated that knockdown of lncPVT1 increased cell invasion, growth, and metastatic potential while reducing apoptosis. We subsequently investigated the mechanisms through which lncPVT1 facilitates the occurrence of RSA. Our exploration of the decidualization process associated with RSA revealed that lncPVT1 is involved in the regulation abnormalities of glucose and lipid metabolism abnormalities, which contribute to the pathogenesis of RSA.

Pregnancy loss frequently occurs during the peri-implantation period, a critical phase characterized by an increased demand for glucose to support embryonic development and endometrial decidualization18. Glucose is a fundamental component of uterine fluid, essential for these processes. The endometrium is unable to synthesize glucose, necessitating its transport into the uterine lumen via glucose transporters, specifically Gluts19. The initial step in glucose utilization involves its uptake into cells. In this study, the primary glucose transporters examined are Glut1 and Glut4. Glut1 was the first member of the Glut family identified in the endometrium, with its mRNA abundantly expressed in embryonic stem cells (ESCs) of both humans and rodents. During pregnancy, elevated levels of Glut1 are detected in the decidua, suggesting its critical role in sustaining pregnancy and facilitating fetal development20,21. Additionally, it has been demonstrated that during decidualization, Glut1 protein levels significantly increase, reaching up to ten folds. This elevation is accompanied by an increase in glucose in vivo, further elucidating the alterations that occur during this physiological transformation19,20. It has been documented that the upregulation of Glut1 occurs in decidualized human embryonic stem cells22. Conversely, the downregulation of Glut1 in embryonic stem cells leads to decreased glucose uptake and inhibited decidualization23. Unlike Glut1, Glut4 is an insulin-dependent protein, and plays a critical role in glucose transport, particularly in the context of insulin resistance. Therefore, in patients with RSA who exhibit insulin resistance or hyperglycemic conditions, Glut4 dysfunction may be partially implicated in these pathophysiological states. In this study, both the knockdown of lncPVT1 and the induction of decidualization were observed to result in the upregulation of Glut1 and Glut4, corroborating previous research findings23. This supports the notion that glucose transport has a positive regulatory effect on decidualization, thereby facilitating embryonic implantation. Decidualization constitutes a critical physiological transformation within the endometrium during early pregnancy, characterized by rapid cellular proliferation and differentiation. A reduction in proliferation, an increase in apoptosis, and insufficient trophoblast invasive capacity can hinder the remodeling of uterine spiral arteries, leading to shallow placental implantation, and a spectrum of pregnancy-related complications24.

Rapidly proliferating cells require substantial energy to sustain their proliferative and differentiative functions, underscoring the pivotal role of energy metabolism in the process of decidualization. Glycolysis, a fundamental pathway in glucose metabolism, involves the breakdown of glucose into pyruvate, releasing energy in the form of ATP. During decidualization, glycolysis is upregulated, facilitating ATP production and providing the necessary energy for rapid cellular proliferation25. This glycolytic process not only generates ATP but also produces intermediate metabolites that are utilized by the cells. These metabolites play an active role in the biosynthesis of nucleic acids and proteins, thereby facilitating rapid cellular proliferation26. Studies have shown that reduced expression levels of phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK1) and phosphoglycerate kinase 1, pseudogene 2 (PGK1P2) can impair decidualization, leading to complications such as inadequate trophoblast cell invasion and placental hypoplasia27. These issues may be linked to insufficient energy metabolism, as a disrupted glycolytic pathway affects ATP production, thus failing to satisfy the energetic requirements of the decidualization process. HK2, LDHA, and PKM2 are critical enzymes in the glycolytic pathway, similar to PGK128. HK2 catalyzes the phosphorylation of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate, initiating glycolysis29. Conversely, LDHA converts pyruvate to lactate, constituting the terminal step of glycolysis30.Additionally, PKM2, an isoform of pyruvate kinase, is involved in the ATP-generating conversion of phospyruvate during glycolysis31. Although PDK1 is not directly involved in glycolysis, it plays a crucial regulatory role in the glycolytic process32. As a pivotal regulatory element in glycolysis, it exerts direct influence on the rate at which pyruvate enters the tricarboxylic acid cycle, thereby modulating both the velocity and directionality of the entire glycolytic pathway. In this study, following the knockdown of lncPVT1 and the induction of decidualization, an increasing trend was observed in the levels of HK2, LDHA, and PKM2, suggesting that the organism augments its glycolytic capacity to sustain optimal implantation competence. Simultaneously, the rise in PDK1 levels appears to function as a regulatory mechanism to restrain excessively rapid glycolysis, thereby ensuring a balanced metabolic rate.

TRAF6 plays a crucial role in intracellular signal transduction33. Upon activation, it facilitates the formation of short ubiquitin chains on itself and other proteins, thereby activating or modulating downstream signaling pathways. These signaling cascades encompass a range of processes including adaptive immunity, innate immunity, as well as cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis34. Research has suggested that TRAF6 plays a role in regulating both the differentiation and function of adipocytes, as well as the inflammatory responses within adipose tissue. These processes are potentially linked to disruptions in lipid metabolism, which may subsequently influence the organism’s overall metabolic state35. The involvement of TRAF6 in these regulatory pathways highlights its importance in modulating the metabolic environment. Lipid metabolism includes various components, such as fatty acid synthesis, catabolism, oxidation, and cholesterol metabolism. Disruptions in lipid metabolism can lead to increased inflammation and oxidative stress, exerting biological effects that may be detrimental to pregnancy36. For example, oxidative stress can cause damage and apoptosis in trophoblast cells, thereby affecting embryo implantation and development37. Additionally, abnormalities in lipid metabolism may impair endometrial receptivity, thereby increasing the risk of miscarriage. In vitro experiments have revealed significant differences in fatty acid levels between the lncPVT1 knockdown group and the control group, highlighting the regulatory role of lncPVT1 in lipid metabolism. Validation through in vivo experiments using mouse models confirmed an elevated expression of TRAF6 in the RSA group, as evidenced by WB and IHC analyses. These findings further emphasize the contributory role of increased TRAF6 expression in the pathogenesis of RSA.

Given the fact that lncPVT1 exerts an influence on the onset and progression of RSA through the regulation of glucose and lipid metabolism, from a therapeutic standpoint, it presents enormous potential as a therapeutic target for the management of RSA.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study identifies a novel lncRNA, lncPVT1, which is markedly overexpressed in RSA, suggesting a potential association between lncPVT1 and the occurrence of RSA. Our research also demonstrates that lncPVT1 regulates the expression of downstream glucose and lipid metabolism during the process of decidualization, thereby influencing abnormal embryonic implantation. Furthermore, we have validated the impact of this regulatory mechanism on RSA through both in vitro and in vivo experiments. Consequently, lncPVT1 may represent a candidate biomarker and a potential therapeutic target for RSA, pending validation in larger cohorts and pregnancy-relevant preclinical studies. Certainly, this study is not without its limitations. Firstly, the sample size utilized in this investigation is relatively small, which may introduce bias in the selection of candidate genes due to the heterogeneity present among individual samples. Future research will aim to increase the sample size to facilitate the identification of more reliable genes. Secondly, constraints in sequencing depth may have resulted in the omission of genes with low-level expression. The KEGG pathways selected based on differential gene enrichment were primarily focused on glucose and lipid metabolism, from which only the lncPVT1 was identified among the upstream non-coding RNAs. This offers merely a preliminary insight into the comprehensive exploration of the pathogenic mechanisms underlying RSA.

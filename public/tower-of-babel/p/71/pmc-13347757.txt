Multifocal and Multicentric Breast Cancer: Evaluation of Clinicopathological Features and Survival Outcomes
Authors: Busra Burcu, Isik Cetinoglu, Aziz Sener, Zeynep Gul Demircioglu, Zerin Sengul, Esma Cerekci, Ramazan Ucak, Cemal Kaya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe reported incidence of multifocal breast cancer (MFBC) and multicentric breast cancer (MCBC) varies widely in the literature. This study aimed to compare the clinicopathological characteristics, treatment approaches, and survival outcomes between patients with MFBC/MCBC and those with unifocal breast cancer (UFBC).MethodsData from 666 female patients aged >18 years who were histopathologically diagnosed with breast cancer and followed up at the Department of General Surgery of a tertiary hospital between January 2018 and January 2025 were retrospectively reviewed. MF and MC were defined based on tumor localization within breast quadrants and the distance between foci. Clinical, pathological, and treatment-related parameters were compared between the groups.ResultsOf all patients, 83.5% (n=556) had UFBC and 16.5% (n=110) had MF/MCBC. The mean age was 54.89±12.17 years. Axillary lymph node positivity was significantly higher in the MF/MCBC group (p=0.022). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) identified MF/MCBC in 71% of cases, demonstrating greater sensitivity than mammography (MMG) and ultrasonography (USG). Mastectomy was performed in 85.4% of patients with MF/MCBC compared with 45% of those in the UFBC group. The rate of neoadjuvant chemotherapy was also significantly higher among patients with MF/MCBC (p=0.008). There were no significant differences between the groups regarding local recurrence, distant metastasis, or overall survival.ConclusionPatients with MF/MCBC exhibited higher rates of axillary involvement and more frequent use of neoadjuvant therapy. However, overall survival was comparable to that of patients with UFBC. A multidisciplinary approach remains essential for determining the optimal management strategy in this subgroup.

The incidence of multifocal (MF) and multicentric (MC) breast cancer varies between 6% and 60%, depending on the imaging modalities used.[1] This wide range can be attributed to differences in lesion definitions and variations in imaging sensitivity.[2,3] Studies have reported that MF/MC breast cancers occur more frequently at younger ages and during the premenopausal period compared with unifocal breast cancer (UFBC).[4,5] Tod et al.[6] reported that approximately 60% of all breast cancers are multifocal and histologically heterogeneous.

The present study aimed to evaluate the demographic, pathological, and treatment characteristics of MF/MC breast cancer cases diagnosed and managed in our clinic and to compare these findings with those of UFBC cases.

Methods

This retrospective study included 666 female patients diagnosed with breast cancer between January 2018 and January 2025 at the Department of General Surgery of a tertiary hospital. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, and the study was approved by the institutional ethics committee (protocol no. 4845). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Patients who had undergone surgery at another center or had incomplete follow-up data were excluded.

Multifocal (MF) disease was defined as the presence of two or more tumor foci within the same quadrant, whereas multicentric (MC) disease was defined as tumor foci located in different quadrants or at least 5 cm apart. Surgical decisions were made by a multidisciplinary tumor board consisting of surgeons, radiologists, pathologists, and medical and radiation oncologists.

Histopathological evaluations were performed according to the WHO classification for histological subtype, the TNM system for staging, and the Elston–Ellis grading system. The presence of lymphovascular invasion was also recorded. Postoperative follow-up visits were scheduled at 3, 6, 12, 18, and 24 months after surgery. Disease-free survival (DFS), local recurrence (LR), overall survival (OS), and mortality rates were assessed.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using NCSS 2020 software. Data distribution was tested using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For normally distributed variables, Student’s t-test was used; for non-normally distributed data, the Mann–Whitney U test was applied. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Of the 666 patients included, 83.5% (n=556) had unifocal breast cancer (UFBC), whereas 16.5% (n=110) had multifocal or multicentric breast cancer (MF/MCBC). The mean age was 54.89±12.17 years, with no significant difference between the groups. The most common histological subtype was invasive ductal carcinoma (75.7%).

In the MF/MCBC group, ductal histology was identified in 80.9% of cases, whereas lobular carcinoma was less frequent compared with UFBC. The mean tumor diameter was 2.36±1.73 cm. The incidence of ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) was significantly higher in the MF/MCBC group (p=0.014). Axillary lymph node positivity was observed in 60% of MF/MCBC cases (n=66) and 48% of UFBC cases (n=267), showing a statistically significant difference (p=0.022); however, the total number of positive nodes did not differ between the groups (Table 1). The median number of positive lymph nodes was 2 (range: 1–17) in the UFBC group and 2 (range: 1–24) in the MF/MCBC group, with no statistically significant difference between the groups (p=0.153).

Regarding molecular subtypes, 79.9% (n=88) of MF/MCBC cases were luminal A or B, 12.7% (n=14) were triple-negative, and 7.27% (n=8) were HER2-positive, with no significant difference compared with UFBC.

MRI demonstrated the highest diagnostic sensitivity for MF/MCBC (71%), followed by ultrasonography (47%) and mammography (19.1%). Mastectomy was performed in 85.4% of patients with MF/MCBC and 45% of patients with UFBC. According to the type of surgery, the most frequently performed procedure was breast-conserving surgery with SLNB (34.7%, n=231), followed by modified radical mastectomy (31.2%, n=208), breast-conserving surgery with axillary dissection (18.5%, n=123), and mastectomy with SLNB (15.6%, n=104).

Postoperative radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and hormone therapy were administered in 66.2%, 59.5%, and 79.3% of all cases, respectively. In the UFBC group, 69.1% (n=384) of patients received radiotherapy, 58.2% (n=323) received chemotherapy, 79.4% (n=439) received hormone therapy, and 16.1% (n=88) received anti-HER2 therapy. Among patients in the MF/MCBC group, radiotherapy was administered to 51.4% (n=56), chemotherapy to 66.4% (n=73), hormone therapy to 79.1% (n=87), and anti-HER2 therapy to 21.1% (n=23). The rate of neoadjuvant chemotherapy was significantly higher among patients with MF/MCBC (p=0.008).

Local recurrence occurred in 2.5% of UFBC cases and in none of the MF/MCBC cases. A total of 14 patients in the UFBC group developed recurrence. Among these, 8 patients experienced ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence following breast-conserving surgery, 3 developed axillary recurrence after sentinel lymph node biopsy, and 3 had recurrence within the mastectomy bed. Patients with ipsilateral breast recurrence underwent completion mastectomy, whereas axillary recurrence was managed with axillary lymph node dissection. The median time to recurrence was 1.9 years (range: 1–5 years).

No significant differences were observed between the groups in terms of metastasis or overall survival (p>0.05) (Table 2). The total follow-up duration in this retrospectively analyzed cohort was 9 years.

Discussion

Multifocal breast cancer (MFBC) is defined as the presence of two or more tumor foci within the ductal system, whereas multicentric breast cancer (MCBC) refers to the occurrence of multiple primary tumors within the same breast.[1]

Two main mechanisms have been proposed to explain the development of breast cancer (BC): (i) intramammary local metastasis originating from a single primary lesion and (ii) the independent emergence of multiple primary (polyclonal) tumors.[2,7]

MF/MCBC represents a distinct clinical subgroup with specific diagnostic and therapeutic implications. In the present study, the incidence of MF/MCBC was 16.5%, consistent with the rates reported in the literature, which range between 10% and 25%.[5,8]

The higher rate of axillary lymph node positivity observed in patients with MF/MCBC may be attributed to increased tumor heterogeneity and biological aggressiveness.[9,10] Similarly, Zhang et al.[11] reported that MFBC was associated with higher HER2 positivity and more frequent lymphovascular invasion compared with UFBC.

Although molecular subtypes were similar between the groups in our series, the MCBC group showed a significantly higher rate of axillary node involvement (p=0.022), whereas the mean number of positive nodes was comparable. The more frequent administration of neoadjuvant therapy in the MF/MCBC group likely contributed to the absence of a difference in the number of positive axillary lymph nodes between the two groups.

Among imaging modalities, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated the highest sensitivity for detecting multifocal and multicentric lesions.[9,12] This finding is consistent with the results of Kanumuri et al. [5] and Iacconi et al., [13] who also emphasized the superior diagnostic performance of MRI in MF/MCBC cases. In the present study, consistent with the existing literature, MRI demonstrated high sensitivity and enabled diagnosis in 71% of patients in the MF/MCBC group.

From a surgical standpoint, mastectomy was preferred in the majority of patients with MF/MCBC (85.4%), which can be explained by the increased risk of multifocality and positive surgical margins.[14]

The significantly higher rate of neoadjuvant therapy in the MF/MCBC group (p=0.008) may be related to greater tumor burden and the extent of multifocal disease.[15]

Nevertheless, the absence of a significant difference in survival outcomes between MF/MCBC and UFBC supports the effectiveness of multidisciplinary treatment strategies currently in use.[14,16]

Although some studies have suggested that MF/MCBC negatively affects prognosis, our results demonstrated comparable overall survival between the two groups. [17,18] This may reflect advances in diagnostic imaging, earlier detection, and improvements in multimodal therapy.

The higher tendency toward mastectomy observed among patients with MF/MCBC in the present study may account for the absence of local recurrence in this group. Additionally, the predominance of luminal tumors as the molecular subtype in both groups differs from what has been reported in the literature and may have contributed to the observed prognostic outcomes.

Therefore, individualized treatment planning, ensuring negative surgical margins, and optimizing adjuvant therapies remain essential for achieving favorable outcomes in patients with MF/MCBC.

Conclusion

Patients with MF/MCBC exhibited higher rates of axillary lymph node involvement and a greater need for neoadjuvant therapy compared with those with unifocal breast cancer. However, overall survival rates were similar, indicating that tumor multifocality alone may not represent an independent prognostic factor.

Early detection, optimal surgical management, and a comprehensive multidisciplinary treatment approach are key determinants of successful outcomes in this patient group.

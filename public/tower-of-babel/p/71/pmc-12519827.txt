Identification of lipid metabolism-related signature in nonalcoholic fatty liver: evidences from transcriptomics and single cell RNA-sequencing analysis
Authors: Yanqing Sun, Minjie Hu, Jin Yang, Dong Jia, Chun Gao, Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Background

Non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a progressive liver disease caused by excessive lipid accumulation in the liver, which included a series of liver conditions from simple steatosis to steatohepatitis, advanced fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [1]. Globally, the incidence and prevalence are rising rapidly [1]. It is evaluated that the worldwide prevalence of NAFLD is approximately 25% recently [1]. Notably, in China, the prevalence of NAFLD nearly doubled between 2010 and 2020, rising from 18 to 29% [2]. NAFLD represents one of the most important risk factors for HCC, and NAFLD-associated HCC primarily affects elderly patients, unlike HCC from other causes, resulting in a delayed diagnosis and poor survival time [2, 3]. Therefore, it is an urgent need for the medical field to explore crucial biomarkers in NAFLD for precise diagnosis and therapeutic development.

NAFLD is driven by metabolic syndrome and closely associated with obesity, insulin resistance (IR), and hyperlipidemia [4, 5]. Obesity and IR contribute to chronic inflammation, disrupt lipid metabolism, and promote oncogenic phenotypes, ultimately facilitating HCC development [6–9]. Some experts even proposed changing the definition of NAFLD to metabolically associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) [10]. Considering the complex and close-knit relationship between NAFLD and the metabolic status [11, 12], lipid metabolism-related biomarkers and the underlying mechanism should be clarified in NAFLD. Liu et al. exploited a lipid metabolism-related molecular cluster for predicting NAFLD and investigated the correlation of the lipid metabolism-related signature and the immune inflammatory feature [13]. Furthermore, the latest publication conducted by Zhang et al. demonstrated the crucial roles of sphingolipid metabolism-related genes CD37 and CXCL9 in NAFLD [14]. Although these findings emphasized the complex relationship between metabolism-related biomarkers and NAFLD, elucidation of the underlying mechanisms of metabolism-related biomarkers is still absent.

Single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) technology represents a powerful tool to discover disease-associated changes in the specific enriched cell subtypes [15]. Various studies utilize scRNA-seq to identify different signatures related to oxidative stress and immune cells in NAFLD [16, 17]. However, little investigation focuses on metabolism-related differences in NAFLD. In the present study, Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) and three algorithm methods were performed for the selection of the important lipid metabolism-related genes (LMGs) in NAFLD. Furthermore, a nomogram model based on the key genes was established for the clinical prediction of patients with NAFLD; scRNA-seq analysis was performed to explore the underlying mechanisms. Examining the changes in gene expression and cell composition in the pathological microenvironment during NAFLD development could help identify appropriate prognostic and therapeutic targets and develop personalized therapeutic regimens for patients with NAFLD.

Methods

Data source

All data analyzed in this study were retrieved from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Bulk RNA-seq data of NAFLD were obtained from GSE48452 (18 NAFLD and 14 control), GSE63067 (nine NAFLDs and seven controls), GSE89632 (19 NAFLDs and 24 controls), and GSE72756 (five NAFLDs and five controls) datasets. GSE48452, GSE63067, and GSE89632 were served as training datasets. GSE72756 was served as the validation dataset.

scRNA data were retrieved from the GSE159977 dataset, with three NAFLDs and four controls.

LMGs were downloaded from a previous publication [18].

Data preprocessing

To integrate the data in the three training datasets into a reliable training dataset, the batch effects were eliminated using the ComBat method. Specifically, data correction and standardization were conducted for each dataset to make the data distribution in different datasets more consistent, including data normalization using the normalizeBetweenArrays function in the limma package and log2 transformation. Then, the ComBat function was utilized to correct the batch effect on the merged gene expression matrix using a Bayesian method. In the ComBat function, the dataset source served as the variable of batch effect, and the sample grouping information served as the variable of the model matrix. Finally, principal component analysis (PCA) analysis was adopted to evaluate the removal effect of the batch effect.

Single sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) was used to calculate the enrichment score of the LMGs using the ssgsea function in GSVA package based on the 776 LMGs. The differences of LMGs scores in NAFLD and control groups were compared.

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis was adopted to identify genes with statistically significant expression changes between NAFLD and control samples. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between disease and control groups were selected using the limma package, with the threshold of P < 0.05 and |log2 Fold change (FC)|> 0.5. P value was corrected using the Benjamini and Hochberg (BH) method.

Consensus cluster analysis

The consensus cluster analysis was conducted using the ConsensusClusterPlus package to classify all the NAFLD samples into different subgroups based on the differential LMGs. The immune scores and pathway scores in different subtypes were compared among different subgroups.

WGCNA

By focusing on the modules related to the phenotype of NAFLD (such as whether there is a NAFLD sample), disease-related gene clusters were identified by using R package WGCNA (version 1.72-5) [19]. Briefly, the appropriate power value was selected to make the connections between genes in the network following scale-free networks. Next, based on clustering and dynamic pruning methods, parameters (min ModuleSize = 100: each module contains at least 100 genes; softPower = 9; deepSplit = 2 and MEDissThres = 0.4) were set to cluster highly relevant genes into modules. Finally, by calculating the correlation between modules and phenotypes (disease or not), genes in modules closely related to diseases (|cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05) were selected as disease-related genes.

Identification of the differential LMGs

To obtain differentially expressed LMGs, we performed intersection analysis of DEGs, module genes, and predefined LMGs, defining the overlapping genes as NAFLDs-related differential LMGs. For functional annotation of these LMGs, we conducted Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses using the clusterProfiler package (version 4.4.4) [20], which revealed their biological roles and metabolic pathways. The GO analysis contained three categories: cellular component (CC); molecular function (MF); and biological process (BP). Machine learning

To further narrow down the differential LMGs, three machine learning methods were performed: Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO, glmnet version 4.1-8) [21], Best Subsets Regression (BSR, leaps version 3.1) [22], and Boruta (version 8.0.0) [23]. The three machine learning models give priority to the genes with the strongest predictive ability for NAFLD, ensuring the robustness of the model and avoiding overfitting and biological redundancy. In the LASSO, the parameters were set as: LASSO: nfolds = 10; family = “binomial”; alpha = 1. The parameters in BSR and Boruta were set as default parameters. The overlapped genes from the three machine learning methods were considered as the key LMGs in NAFLDs. GSE72756 was used to extract the expression values of candidate key genes in each sample, and the candidate key genes whose expression was consistent with the training dataset were defined as key genes.

Construction of prognostic nomogram model

To investigate the clinical prognostic ability of the key genes, a nomogram model was developed using rms package (version 6.7-1) [24] based on the key ERGs. Furthermore, calibration curves, decision curve analysis (DCA, ggDCA version 1.2), clinical impact curve (CIC, rmda version 1.6), and receiver operator characteristic (ROC) were used to detect the stability and reliability of the nomogram model.

Immune infiltration analysis

The immunedeconv package was used based on the ABIS-seq deconvolution to assess the number of immune cells infiltrated from the NAFLD gene expression profile. Immune infiltration abundance and proportion for each sample was represented using the ggplot2 package. Wilcoxon test was used to compare the proportion of 22 kinds of immune cells between NAFLD samples and control samples. Spearman rank correlation coefficient was used to analyze the correlation between key gene expression and infiltrating immune cell content.

scRNA-seq analysis

For scRNA-seq analysis data, Seurat package (version 4.4.0) was conducted [25]. Firstly, low-quality cells with low expression and high mitochondria were removed. The mitochondrial gene expression ratio less than 5%, nFeature_RNA > 300, nFeature_RNA < 4000, and nCount_RNA < 15000 were retained. Next, dimensionality reduction and clustering were processed. NormalizeData function was used to standardize the data. The FindVariableFeatures function was performed to extract genes with relatively large inter-cell variation coefficients, and the first 2000 highly variable genes with obvious fluctuations were displayed. The ScaleData function was used to scale the scRNA-seq data. JackStrawPlot function was used to determine the principal components with statistically significance. The principal components and the first 2000 high-variant genes were involved in principal component analysis (PCA) using RunPCA function. The top 20 principal components with statistical significance in PCA analysis were selected for subsequent analysis. Small cell clusters were identified for subsequent analysis by FindNeighbors and FindClusters function using the Seurat package, with the resolution parameter of 0.5. Finally, FindAllMarkers was performed for identifying the cell clusters, with thresholds of |log2FC|> 0.5 and P < 0.05). CellMarker (http://xteam.xbio.top/CellMarker/) and related literatures [26, 27] were used for the auxiliary annotation. The expression levels of marker genes used for the annotation were visualized in a bubble diagram. UMAP was used to visualize the annotated cell types.

In vitro expression validation

Human hepatocyte line HepG2 cells (CL-0103, Procell Biotech) were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 U/mL streptomycin at 5% CO2 and 37 ℃. HepG2 cells were treated with 1 mM free fatty acid (FFA, containing oleic acid and palmitic acid, with a volume ratio of 2:1) for 24 h to induce an in vitro NAFLD cell model.

qRT-PCR

qRT-PCR was used to detect the mRNA expression levels of PRKAA2 and ME1 in cells. The total RNA was extracted using a Trizol reagent (15596018, Invitrogen). The 2–ΔΔCt method was employed to quantify gene expression, which was normalized to GAPDH. The primer information is shown in Table S1.

Western blotting

Western blotting was used to detect the protein levels of PRKAA2 and ME1 cells. Proteins were extracted from the cells and treated with radioimmuno-precipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (P0013B, Beyotime), and then the protein concentration was quantified using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) kit (P0010S, Beyotime). GAPDH served as the internal control. Primary antibodies used in the study were: PRKAA2 antibody (1:1000, DF8173, Affinity) and ME1 antibody (1:1000, DF4217, Affinity).

Statistical analysis

All the bioinformatics analyses and the statistical analysis were conducted in R software version 4.2.3. Before the statistical test, the Shapiro–Wilk test method was performed for a normality test, and Levene test was adopted for the homogeneity test of variance. When the data satisfied a normal distribution and homogeneity of variance, parametric test methods, such as t-test and analysis of variance (ANOVA) were adopted. When the data did not satisfy normal distribution or homogeneity of variance, or the sample size was small, non-parametric test methods, including the Wilcoxon (for two independent samples), Mann–Whitney U test (for two independent samples), or Kruskal–Wallis H test (for multiple independent samples), were adopted. Spearman rank correlation was adopted for the correlation analysis. P value below 0.05 was set as significance.

Results

Identification of differential LMGs

The baseline characteristics of samples from GSE48452 and GSE89632 are listed in Table S2, with no detailed information provided in GSE63067 dataset. The analysis indicated that there were no significant differences in the age and gender between NAFLD and control groups (P > 0.05). Following batch effect correlation, the combination of data in GSE48452, GSE63067, and GSE89632 datasets was served as the training dataset. The PCA result indicated that the differences of the corrected data among different datasets were significantly reduced (Fig. 1A), indicating an effective elimination of the batch effect and an improved reliability of the data. A total of 295 DEGs, including 142 upregulated and 153 downregulated, were observed between NAFLD and control groups in the generated training dataset (Fig. 1B). Moreover, based on the 776 LMGs, ssGSRA analysis found significantly higher LMGs score in the NAFLD group than that in the control group (Fig. 1C), indicating that LMGs might be important in the progression of NAFLD. Moreover, these LMGs were significantly correlated with functions and pathways like lipid metabolism, inflammation, bile acid metabolism, and fatty acid metabolism in NAFLD (Fig. 1D). Through systematic intersection of the 295 DEGs and 776 LMGs, 24 high-confidence differential LMGs were captured (Table S3).

Subtype analysis

To investigate the classification potential of our identified differential LMGs in NAFLD, the comprehensive subtype analysis was conducted. First, based on the 24 differential LMGs, the ConsensusClusterPlus package was used to perform unsupervised cluster analysis on 46 NAFLD samples in the training dataset, and the samples were categorized according to cumulative distribution function (CDF) curve. The results showed that the cluster effect was most appropriate when all the samples were split into two categories: cluster 1 and cluster 2 (Fig. 2A). Next, the PCA analysis showed that the two categories can be separated well under this classification method (Fig. 2B). The expression levels of LMGs between the two categories were also significantly different (Fig. 2C). GSVA analysis revealed 10 upregulated and 10 downregulated pathways in cluster 1 (Fig. 2D). For example, fatty acid metabolism and bile acid metabolism were inhibited in cluster 1; inflammatory response was activated in cluster 1 when compared with cluster 2. Immune infiltration analysis suggested five significantly different immune cells between the two categories: macrophages M2, mast cells activated, mast cells resting, monocytes, and neutrophils (Fig. 2E). To investigate the biological functions of the differential LMGs between cluster 1 and cluster 2, GO and KEGG analysis were performed, and the results further demonstrate that these genes were mainly related to functions like leukocyte cell–cell adhesion, fat cell differentiation, cholesterol metabolic process, and pathways like TNF signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, JAK–STAT signaling pathway (Fig. 2F).

WGCNA for selection of the NAFLD-related genes

All the samples in the training dataset were clustered according to the similarity of gene expression, and three outliers were deleted: GSM2385767, GSM2385782, and GSM1179010 (Fig. 3A). The appropriate soft threshold 9 was selected because it was the first soft threshold to be reached above 0.9 (0.918) (Fig. 3B). Next, all the genes were divided into eight modules by the dynamic cutting method (Fig. 3C). Finally, the correlation between the phenotype and the eight gene modules was evaluated with the threshold of |Cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05, and the results showed that greenyellow and brown modules were mostly correlated with the NAFLD (Fig. 3D). The genes in the greenyellow and brown modules were served as the NAFLD-related genes.

Selection of the key LMGs using learning algorithms

To further explore the key LMGs in NAFLD, the DEGs, LMGs, and NAFLD-related genes were intersected. Here, 20 overlapped genes were obtained (Fig. 4A). GO analysis indicated the 20 overlapped genes were enriched into 341 GO terms, with 262 BPs, nine CCs, and 70 MFs; as well as 16 KEGG terms (Fig. 4B and C). Specifically, the 20 overlapped genes were related to functions like fatty acid metabolic process, fat cell differentiation, and fatty acid biosynthetic process (Fig. 4B), and metabolism-related pathways like glycerolipid metabolism, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, PPAR signaling pathway, and AMPK signaling pathway (Fig. 4C).

Following this, three machine learning methods narrowed the 20 genes into eight (PRKAA2, PRKAB2, FADS2, SLCO1A2, KLF5, ME1, SQLE, and CYP7A1) in LASSO (Fig. 4D), 19 (PTGS2, PPARGC1A, SPTLC3, SLCO1A2, THRSP, GPAM, PRKAA2, PNPLA3, FADS2, FDFT1, SQLE, CEBPD, GPD1, CYP7A1, PRKAB2, KLF5, and ME1) in Boruta (Fig. 4E), and three (PRKAA2, KLF5, and ME1) in BSR (Fig. 4F). Through intersecting the above three parts of genes, three key genes (PRKAA2, KLF5, and ME1) were identified as the candidate LMGs in NAFLD (Fig. 4G). The expression trends of the three candidate genes were validated in the validation dataset, and the results showed that the expression trends of PRKAA2 and ME1 between NAFLD and control were consistent in the training and validation datasets (Fig. 4H). Therefore, PRKAA2 and ME1 were confirmed to be the key LMGs in NAFLD. In addition, the mRNA expression levels and protein levels of PRKAA2 and ME1 were also validated in HepG2 cells. FFA was used to induce a NAFLD in vitro cell model. The result indicated that ME1 was significantly increased in the NAFLD groups, while PRKAA2 showed no significant differences between NAFLD and control groups (Fig. 4I).

Nomogram model for evaluation of diagnostic ability of candidate genes

A nomogram based on the two key genes was constructed (Fig. 5A). In the nomogram, each factor corresponds to a score, and the total score of each factor was added to correspond to the total score; thus, the incidence of NAFLD can be inferred according to the total score. The higher the score, the higher the risk of NAFLD. In our nomogram model, the expression levels of the two key genes can largely explain the probability of NAFLD. Following this, the calibration curves, DCA, and CIC curve were drawn to exert the diagnostic ability of the genes and nomogram model. Calibration curves showed that the slope between observed survival and nomogram-predicted survival was close to 1 (Fig. 5B), implying the effectiveness of the model. The DCA curve showed that the predicting superiority of the nomogram model was better than that of any gene alone (Fig. 5C). The CIC curve indicated that when the threshold was greater than 0.6, the nomogram prediction and the actual event were highly matched, and the clinical prediction efficiency was high (Fig. 5D). The ROC curve indicated the promising ability of the model with an AUC of 0.945 (Fig. 5E).

Immune infiltration analysis

The immunedeconv package was used based on the ABIS-seq deconvolution to assess the immune landscape in NAFLD. Ten immune cells were significantly different between NAFLD and control samples: basophil, neutrophils, NK cells, T cells CD4 + memory, T cells CD4 + naive, T cells CD8 + memory, and T cell MAIT (Fig. 5F). Furthermore, we investigate the correlation between the immune cells and the key LMGs. The results indicated that the two key LMGs (ME1 and PRKAA2) were significantly correlated with NK cells and T cells (Fig. 5G).

scRNA-seq analysis

scRNA-seq data from 3 NAFLDs and 4 controls in GSE159977 training dataset were analyzed. The number of genes expressed in cells was mainly concentrated below 4000, the distribution of count was not more than 80000, and most of it was concentrated below 15000. After quality control, a total of 19787 genes and 49201 cells were obtained. Next, the top 2000 highly variable genes were selected for the PCA analysis (Fig. 6A). The PCA results indicated no obvious batch effect and no outlier samples in cell distribution between groups (Fig. 6B), which met the analysis requirements. To better evaluate and analyze the cell groups, linear dimensionality reduction was performed based on gene expression in each cell. The result indicated that the standard deviation after linear dimensionality reduction gradually decreased with the increase of dimensions, and the standard deviation gradually flattened out at the 20th dimension (Fig. 6C), so the linear optimal dimension value of cell clustering was set as 20. After PCA reduction, the FindNeighbors and the FindClusters function in Seurat package was used to perform unsupervised cluster analysis on 49201 cells. The resolution parameter was set to 0.5 for the subsequent analysis, and all cells were clustered into 15 subgroups (Fig. 6D). Following this, these 15 subgroups were annotated with a combination of data in cellmarker database and a publication [25]. Totally, the 15 subgroups were annotated into six cell types: NK cells, monocytes and macrophages, T cells, B cells, proliferating cells, and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (Fig. 6E). The marker genes in various cell types were extracted and the expression levels of these marker genes in cells were evaluated. The results showed that the corresponding marker genes were highly expressed in the annotated cell types. For example, the expressions of IRF4, IRF7, and IRF8 were higher in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and the expressions of MS4A1 and CD79A were higher in B cells (Fig. 6F).

scRNA-seq analysis for cell annotation. A Highly variable gene scatter plot. B Two-dimensional principal component analysis (PCA) sample distribution map. C Inflexion diagram. D Cell cluster. E Cell annotation. F Marker genes expression plot in various cells

The cell proportion in NAFLD and control was also investigated, and the results showed that in both NAFLD group and the control group, T cells and NK cells accounted for more, and the proportion of T cells decreased in NAFLD group, while the proportion of NK cells increased in NAFLD group (Fig. 7A and B). Moreover, the ME1 gene was mainly expressed in NK cells, while the PRKAA2 gene expression was not detected in any cells (Fig. 7C). NK cells also significantly differ between NAFLD and the control groups, indicating that NK cells were relatively important cells in NAFLD. To understand the relationship between cells, we perform cell communication analysis. There was a certain interaction between immune cells in both NAFLD and normal groups, especially NK cells, monocytes and macrophages, and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (Fig. 7D). More importantly, the interaction strength among these cells was reduced in NAFLD group (Fig. 7E), indicating the cell functions and their links were disturbed. Cell communication was conducted using CellChat to investigate the signal changes between NAFLD and control groups. The results indicated that the common cell communication signaling: ligand–receptor SEMA4D−PLXNB2, PGE2−PTGES3−PTGER2, and PPIA−BSG were the most important correlations (Fig. 7F). Finally, the mode of signal reception and transmission between NAFLD and control groups was investigated. The results indicated that the strength of MHC-I and CD160 signals of NK cells was enhanced in NAFLD in the incoming signaling patterns, while CCL and CD99 signals of NK cells were enhanced in NAFLD in the outgoing signaling patterns (Fig. 7G).

Discussion

The closely links between metabolic disorders, especially lipid metabolic disorder [28, 29], and NAFLD have been emphasized in previous studies [30]. In the present study, the two key LMGs (ME1 and PRKAA2) were confirmed to play a crucial role in NAFLD through differential expression analysis, WGCNA, and machine learning algorithms. Next, a nomogram model based on the two genes was constructed, demonstrating effective diagnostic priorities for NAFLD. Finally, scRNA-seq analysis revealed a closely correlated relationship between ME1 and NK cells, indicating that the metabolic mediatory role of ME1 might be primarily associated with NK cell function.

In the first step of LMGs selection, 295 DEGs were identified. When intersected with LMGs from publication, 24 differential LMGs were confirmed. Based on the 24 genes, subtype analysis was conducted to investigate their classification ability in NAFLD. The results indicated that the 24 genes could significantly divide NAFLD samples into two distinct categories, which exhibited significant differences in biological functions and immune status. Next, through WGCNA and three machine learning algorithms, the 24 LMGs were narrowed down to three candidate genes: PRKAA2, KLF5, and ME1. Among these, PRKAA2 and ME1 showed consistent expression patterns in both the training and validation datasets, confirming their roles as key LMGs in NAFLD pathogenesis.

PRKAA2, a catalytic subunit of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) with multifunctional roles [31]. AMPK maintains the functioning of cell physiology and is involved in a variety of signal transduction pathways [32]. Growing evidence supports the protective function of activation of AMPK in NAFLD. For example, the deletion of the AMPK subunit α1 (PRKAA1) or α2 (PRKAA2) in NAFLD decreases the recruitment of numerous immune cells like monocytes and macrophages, thus inducing the steatosis and worsening IR [33, 34]. Xie et al. also demonstrated that PRKAA2 promotes hepatoblastoma progression through inhibition of ferroptosis by targeting TFR1 and HIF1 [35]. Notably, PRKAA2 knockout in mice aggravates NAFLD, hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis—effects mediated through NF-κB signaling pathways [36]. Supporting evidence from Wang et al. demonstrated the down-regulated PRKAA2 in weanling triplet male lambs (more prone to steatosis-induced fatty liver) compared to weanling twin male lambs [37]. These evidences collectively indicated the important role of PRKAA2 in liver metabolic disorders. However, excluding the significant role of PRKAA2 in NAFLD, our study revealed discrepant expression patterns: up-regulated in disease (dataset analyses), but no significant changes (qRT-PCR/Western blotting validation). This apparent contradiction warrants further investigation into context-dependent PRKAA2 regulation. Malic enzyme 1 (ME1) is a key regulator of lipogenesis and fatty acid synthesis [38, 39]. ME1 catalyzes the decarboxylation of L-malate to pyruvate, accompanied by reduction of NADP to NADPH [39]. A recent study conducted by Simmen et al. confirmed that ME1-deficient mice exhibit reduced obesity susceptibility [39], highlighting the crucial role of ME1 in lipid metabolism and NADPH progression. Moreover, the crucial roles of ME1 in liver-related diseases, including hepatocellular carcinoma [40, 41] and fatty degeneration of liver [39], have been emphasized. However, the systematic investigation of ME1 expression and its underlying mechanisms in NAFLD remains largely unexplored.

Given the pivotal roles of these two key genes in NAFLD pathogenesis, we developed an effective nomogram model (AUC = 0.945) incorporating both biomarkers. The nomogram operates as follows: each predictive factor (gene expression level) is assigned a specific point value; total points are calculated by summing individual factor scores; the cumulative score corresponds to a predicted NAFLD probability [42, 43]. The higher the score, the higher the risk of NAFLD. In our nomogram model, the expression levels of the two key genes can largely explain the probability of NAFLD. However, a major drawback of nomograms is that their predictive performance is highly dependent on the quality and representativeness of the modeling data, which induces some difficulty in the extrapolation of the model.

In our following analysis, immune infiltration analysis demonstrated that the key metabolic regulators PRKAA2 and ME1 were significantly correlated with NK cells and T cells, indicating the regulatory roles of the genes might be related to these immune cells and immune response. Finally, the scRNA-seq analysis, a major tool for the investigation of biological problems [44], was conducted to uncover the cell distribution and cell-to-cell signaling under NAFLD background. The results indicated that all the cells in the NAFLD and control samples were annotated into six types of cells. Among these, T cells and NK cells emerged as particularly significant due to their abundance and disease-associated alterations. Notably, we observed a marked decrease in T cell proportion alongside an increase in NK cell prevalence in NAFLD samples compared to controls. The liver is a key immune organ, among which T lymphocyte subsets are important immunomodulatory cells [45]. Dysfunction of the immune system can lead to the occurrence and progression of NAFLD [46]. CD4 + T lymphocyte loss is observed in NAFLD and might promote hepatocarcinogenesis [47]. NK cells are also reported to be involved in NAFLD progression [48, 49]. The levels of NK cell infiltration in NAFLD might be related to NAFLD pathogenesis [50]. Moreover, there is also crosstalk between macrophages and T cells. For example, APC-containing macrophages are essential in activating T cells to initiate the immune response, and macrophages can also be activated and polarized by cytokines secreted by T cells (such as IFN-γ, IL-4 and IL-13) [51, 52]. At present, some targeted therapeutic methods have been developed in response to the immune characteristics in liver diseases. For instance, cenicriviroc targets CCR2/CCR5 (expressed on monocytes and T cells), and lycopene inhibits the recruitment of T cells and the activation of M1 macrophages [53, 54]. In conclusion, the method of targeting immune cells like T cells holds great promise for future therapies. Moreover, the important LMGs ME1 was mainly expressed in NK cells, but PRKAA2 expression was not observed in any cells. Although our results indicated the probable regulatory correlation between NK cells and ME1 in NAFLD, their relationship has not been fully clarified. The final cell communication indicated that the signal changes between NAFLD and control groups contained ligand-receptor SEMA4D−PLXNB2, PGE2−PTGES3−PTGER2, and PPIA−BSG.

Several studies also proposed LMG-related diagnostic or prognostic models in NAFLD [13, 55, 56]; however, these studies were mainly based on single-omics sequencing and less mentioned the underlying mechanisms. The principal merit of this research resides in its multi-omics integration approach. Two important LMGs (PRKAA2 and ME1) in NAFLD established the predictive nomogram model for NAFLD in clinic. scRNA-seq and immune infiltration analysis further uncovered the underlying mechanism of these genes in NAFLD and lipid metabolism. Nevertheless, limitations exist. First, the specific roles and mechanisms of PRKAA2 and ME1 in lipid metabolism and NAFLD have not been fully clarified. Second, although our study demonstrated the important roles of immune cells, especially NK cells, limited reports indicated the role of NK cells and the key genes. Third, the whole study relied on data downloaded from the public database and previous publications; methodological differences in data collection by different teams (such as sample processing and sequencing depth) might lead to batch effects, although the data have been standardized. Finally, potential bias in the datasets, including data heterogeneity and limited sample size, as well as the inherent limitation of the nomogram model, might limit the extrapolation of the model. Hence, the main observation in our study should be validated in a larger cohort, and more intensive explorations about the genes and their underlying mechanisms need to be conducted in the future.

Conclusion

Two important LMGs (PRKAA2 and ME1) in NAFLD are confirmed to be significantly correlated to NAFLD and immune response. Based on the two LMGs, an effectively predictive nomogram model is established for NAFLD in clinic. The immune infiltration analysis and scRNA-seq further demonstrate the underlying mechanism of PRKAA2 and ME1 in NAFLD.

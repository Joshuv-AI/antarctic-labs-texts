Palivizumab prophylaxis and risk of RSV infection among moderate-to-late preterm infants: A nationwide Korean cohort study from 2018 to 2022
Authors: Hyejin So, Yonghyuk Kim, Youngmi Yoon, Hanbyul Lee, Yoowon Kwon, Jeesu Min, Minji Kim, Eun-Hee Kim, Geena Kim, Yoo-mi Kim, Byoungkook Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of respiratory infections in infants, and preterm infants are at higher risk of severe illness. While RSV immunoprophylaxis is widely considered necessary for very preterm infants in most countries, its efficacy in moderate-to-late preterm infants (MLPI) remains controversial. This study aimed to evaluate the preventive and therapeutic effects of palivizumab in MLPI, using data from the National Health Insurance Service of Korea. We analyzed the National Health Insurance Service data for infants born during the RSV season (October–March) from 2018 to 2022. Infants with a gestational age of 32 weeks 0 days’ to 36 weeks 6 days’ gestation were categorized as MLPI and those born between 37 weeks 0 days’ and 41 weeks 6 days’ gestation as full-term infants. Infants with congenital anomalies or severe cardiac diseases were excluded. Both groups were divided according to palivizumab administration. We compared the RSV infection rates and hospitalization duration. Of the 593,419 infants, 137,452 were excluded. Among 440,163 full-term infants, 938 (0.23%) received palivizumab, compared with 2096 (13.26%) of 15,804 MLPI. In full-term infants, RSV infection occurred in 0.68% of the non-recipients and 0.21% of the recipients. Among the MLPI, the infection rates were 1.18% in non-recipients and 0.62% in recipients, indicating a statistically significant reduction. Logistic regression analysis revealed that palivizumab reduced the risk of RSV infection in MLPI by an approximately 53% lower odds (adjusted odds ratio, 0.47; P = .011). Palivizumab significantly reduced the incidence and severity of RSV infection in MLPI in Korea. This study provides crucial nationwide baseline data on the RSV disease burden in this vulnerable population immediately before the introduction of novel prophylactic strategies such as nirsevimab, and can serve as evidence for future health policy decisions and cost-effectiveness analyses.

1. Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) remains the most common cause of lower respiratory tract infections in infants and young children worldwide and is associated with significant morbidity and healthcare burden.[1,2] Although RSV infection is widely acknowledged as a major threat in newborn infants and those with underlying cardiopulmonary diseases, the vulnerability of moderate-to-late preterm infants (MLPI; 32–36 weeks’ gestation) has often been underestimated.[2–4] Many MLPI are discharged early and presumed to be at low risk. On the other hand, accumulating evidence suggests that their immature pulmonary function and underdeveloped immune systems place them at an elevated risk of respiratory infections, including RSV.[4,5]

Palivizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting the RSV F protein, is approved for immunoprophylaxis in high-risk infants during RSV season.[6,7] However, there is considerable controversy regarding whether the clinical effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of palivizumab vary according to the degree of immaturity in preterm infants compared to term infants. In a 2016 large-scale observational study by Farber et al investigating very preterm infants and MLPIs, palivizumab prophylaxis demonstrated preventive efficacy in very preterm infants; In contrast, it failed to show superior effectiveness in MLPI compared to term infants.[8] Consequently, eligibility criteria vary across countries, and concerns about cost-effectiveness have led to restrictive recommendations for MLPI. In Canada, only preterm infants born before 33 weeks of gestation are eligible for palivizumab prophylaxis, and many countries have restrictive guidelines that limit its use in MLPI.[9,10] Few large-scale population-based studies have directly assessed the clinical effectiveness of palivizumab prophylaxis in this MLPI population.[2,11] In South Korea, palivizumab had previously been administered only to very preterm infants through the National Immunization Program.[12] Since 2016, indications for palivizumab have been selectively expanded to include neonates with risk factors such as having older siblings.[11,12]

Therefore, this study utilized nationwide data from 2018 to 2022, immediately before the introduction of universal prophylaxis such as nirsevimab, to address 2 key objectives: to quantify the actual burden of RSV infection (incidence and hospitalization duration) in Korean MLPI, and to demonstrate the potential effectiveness of palivizumab prophylaxis in reducing this disease burden. These findings will provide essential evidence for future cost-effectiveness analyses and policy development of novel preventive strategies.

2. Methods

2.1. Study population

This nationwide cohort study retrospectively analyzed claims databases from the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) of Korea. We included moderate preterm to full-term infants born at a gestational age between 32 weeks 0 days’ and 41 weeks 6 days’ gestation during each RSV season (October–March) between 2018 and 2022. Infants with major congenital malformations, congenital heart disease with hemodynamic abnormalities, intraventricular hemorrhage, or very low birth weight were excluded. This cohort was stratified into MLPI and full-term, and each group was further subdivided based on whether they received palivizumab prophylaxis.

Data were extracted from NHIS database based on the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems, 10th revision (ICD-10).[13] We collected data including demographic information (sex and date of birth); diagnostic codes such as fetal growth restriction (FGR, P05.1 or P05.2), respiratory distress syndrome (RDS, P22.0), bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD, P27.1 or P28.0), and RSV infection; and duration of hospitalization for RSV infection. Information regarding palivizumab administration, timing of administration, and use of high flow nasal cannula (HFNC), was also collected. In ICD-10, RSV infection diagnosis can only be assigned when confirmed by NHIS-verified polymerase chain reaction or antigen detection. Accordingly, we defined RSV infection patients as those with RSV-specific diagnostic codes: J12.1 (RSV pneumonia), J21.0 (acute bronchiolitis due to RSV), J20.5 (acute bronchitis due to RSV), and B97.4 (RSV as the cause of diseases classified elsewhere). Hospitalizations due to RSV-associated lower respiratory tract infections were identified using the previously described ICD-10 diagnostic codes.

In Korea, palivizumab is covered by the NHIS for eligible high-risk infants, with the NHIS subsidizing 95% of the total treatment cost. Since the NHIS provides universal coverage, access is determined solely by clinical eligibility (such as gestational age, birthweight and siblings, etc) rather than household income or other socioeconomic factors. All qualifying infants receive palivizumab under a uniform co-payment policy, with no differential criteria applied beyond defined medical high-risk status.

2.2. Data collection and definitions

RDS was defined as respiratory insufficiency that manifested at or shortly after birth, accompanied by typical radiological findings consistent with RDS and requiring surfactant replacement therapy.[14] All cases for BPD during the study period were retrospectively defined according to the 2018 criteria of the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development as the need for respiratory support with a nasal cannula flow rate exceeding 2 L/min.[15] FGR was defined as birth weight below the 10th percentile for gestational age.[16] The primary outcomes were rates of RSV infection and duration of RSV-related hospitalization within the first year of life. Secondary outcomes included use of HFNC support during the first year of life. Subgroup analysis was conducted based on gestational age strata (32–36 weeks and 37–41 weeks).

2.3. Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages, and continuous variables were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Categorical variables were analyzed using Pearson chi-square test (or Fisher exact test) and binary logistic regression, whereas continuous variables were analyzed using independent t tests. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to confirm the association between palivizumab administration and outcomes, adjusting for potential confounding variables including sex, RDS, BPD, and FGR. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (v. 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY), and a P value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

2.4. Ethical approval and informed consent

This study was approved by Institutional Review Board of Chungnam National University Sejong Hospital Institutional Review Board and the NHIS database (No. CNUSH 2023-11-008 and NHIS 2024-1-0530). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified administrative claims data.

3. Results

3.1. Study population

A total of 593,419 infants born between 2018 and 2022 were screened for eligibility (Fig. 1). Following the application of predefined exclusion criteria such as congenital anomalies, immunodeficiency, and chronic lung disease, 137,452 infants were excluded. The final study population comprised 455,967 infants, categorized into 2 major groups: term infants (≥ 37 weeks’ gestational age, n = 440,163) and MLPI (32–36 weeks’ gestational age, n = 15,804).

To evaluate the association between palivizumab prophylaxis and RSV infection, we performed separate multivariable logistic regression analysis for MLPI and full-term infants adjusting for sex, FGR, RDS and BPD (Table 3). Among MLPI, palivizumab was significantly associated with lower odds of RSV infection within the first year of life (adjusted odds ratio [aOR]: 0.47, 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.26–0.84; P = .011). RDS was significantly associated with RSV infection risk in palivizumab group of MLPI (aOR, 1.60; 95% CI, 1.12–2.30; P = .010) whereas female sex, BPD, and FGR were not significant. Among full-term infants, there was no association between palivizumab and RSV outcomes (aOR, 0.34; 95% CI, 0.08–1.34; P = .123). Interestingly, female sex showed a significant protective association with RSV infection risk (aOR, 0.86; 95% CI, 0.80–0.92; P < .001). Other predictors, including RDS, BPD, and FGR showed no significant relationship with RSV outcomes between palivizumab and non-palivizumab group.

4. Discussion

This large-scale nationwide cohort study captures a critical transitional period from palivizumab to long-acting monoclonal antibodies for RSV prevention. Our findings provide robust, population-based evidence that palivizumab prophylaxis significantly reduced both RSV infection rates and hospitalization duration in MLPI, a population where its effectiveness has remained controversial. As mentioned above, current newer agents such as nirsevimab were not yet part of the standard clinical practice in Korea during the study period. These results hold immediate relevance for regions where nirsevimab introduction is delayed, informing patient selection and prophylaxis strategies when palivizumab remains the only available option. Furthermore, these findings support risk stratification strategies in low- and middle-income countries, where MLPI populations may demonstrate better long-term survival compared to very preterm infants. In resource-constrained settings, prioritizing RSV prevention in MLPI could represent a more cost-effective initial approach. Our results thus provide an evidence-based rationale for incorporating MLPI into tiered prevention strategies, particularly in healthcare systems with limited resources.

This nationwide retrospective cohort study provides new population-based evidence that, unlike in full-term infants, palivizumab is effective for preventing RSV infection and reducing disease severity in MLPI. Using NHIS data spanning 5 RSV seasons in Korea, we demonstrated that MLPI who received palivizumab prophylaxis experienced significant reductions in both RSV infection rates (0.62% vs 1.18%) and duration of hospitalization (4.54 vs 9.14 days) compared to those who did not. After multivariable logistic regression analysis adjusting for confounding variables, palivizumab administration was significantly associated with reduced odds of RSV infection (aOR = 0.47). Given the baseline risk of RSV infection in the control group was 1.18%, this corresponds to an approximate relative risk and represents an estimated relative risk reduction of an approximately 53% lower odds. These findings challenge the existing prophylaxis guidelines, which typically exclude this substantial subgroup in many countries.

In Europe, although the national guidelines for palivizumab prophylaxis use vary by country, there is a common trend of restricting its administration to MLPI.[17] In Germany, palivizumab is administered selectively to infants born before 32 weeks of gestation, while in Sweden, infants born at or beyond 32 weeks are not eligible for prophylaxis.[17] This was based on the assumption that the association between preterm birth and severe RSV-related illness was not clearly established, making the cost-effectiveness of the expensive palivizumab prophylaxis questionable from a socioeconomic perspective.[18,19] In a study by Wintersstein et al, the prevalence of RSV-related illnesses in moderate preterm infants who received palivizumab prophylaxis was reported to be comparable to that in full-term infants.[20] Nevertheless, growing evidence supports the need for palivizumab prophylaxis in MLPI. Olabarrieta et al demonstrated that the incidence of RSV infection was 3.6 times higher in MLPI than in their full-term counterparts, whereas Anderson et al reported a significantly longer duration of RSV-related hospitalizations in this population.[21,22] These findings highlight the increased vulnerability of MLPI and underscore the potential benefit of extending prophylactic coverage to this group. The MAKI trial by Blanken et al provided strong evidence of the long-term benefits of palivizumab, demonstrating a 61% reduction in recurrent wheezing in MLPI who received prophylaxis.[23] This and other studies highlight the recognition of the vulnerability of MLPI to respiratory complications and the potential value of extending immunoprophylaxis to this group. Our study contributes to the evidence base by demonstrating that palivizumab prophylaxis significantly reduces RSV infection incidence among MLPI, suggesting that current eligibility guidelines warrant reconsideration.

Notably, these benefits were observed despite the higher baseline risk factors in the palivizumab group, which included more infants with RDS (31.1% vs 14.6%) and BPD (3.1% vs 0.1%). These findings provide compelling evidence to support the efficacy of immunoprophylaxis in this MLPI population with respiratory morbidities. The finding that RDS was a significant independent risk factor for RSV infection in preterm infants (aOR: 1.60) emphasizes the importance of immunoprophylaxis in MLPI with underlying respiratory morbidity. These results align with another study of Anderson et al, who identified early respiratory morbidity as a key predictor of subsequent RSV hospitalization in preterm infants.[24] These results also align with the study of Notario et al, who reported comparable 55% reduction in the RSV-related hospitalization in high-risk populations.[25] The higher use of HFNC observed in the palivizumab group also indicates that this group had greater respiratory morbidities. This pattern was similarly observed in full-term infants. In contrast, the protective effect of palivizumab in full-term infants was not statistically significant (aOR: 0.34). This finding must be interpreted with caution because of the extremely low immunoprophylaxis rate in this group (0.23%). The higher rates of RDS (51.93% vs 4.45%) and BPD (10.06% vs < 0.01%) in full-term infants who received palivizumab suggest that these were highly selected cases with substantial respiratory morbidities. Our findings support the conclusions of Caserta et al, who reported limited benefits of palivizumab in otherwise healthy term infants.[19]

In MLPI, female sex was not associated with reduced odds of RSV infection (aOR: 0.964, P = .809). The absence of an apparent protective effect of female sex may reflect prematurity-related vulnerabilities such as pulmonary immaturity and reduced transplacental transfer of maternal antibodies that could attenuate sex-related differences in susceptibility. In contrast, among full-term infants, female sex was a significant protective factor (aOR: 0.86, P < .001), a finding consistent with enhanced immune responses in females mediated by X-linked genes and hormonal modulation.[26–28] These findings highlight the influence of gestational age on sex-based differences in RSV susceptibility. For preterm infants, expanding access to prophylaxis such as palivizumab remains essential, whereas for full-term infants, sex-specific risks could inform prevention strategies. Further research on these mechanisms is required to optimize RSV management across different populations.

Our study showed another significant clinical implication. A 51% shorter duration of hospitalization for RSV infection (4.54 vs 9.14 days) among MLPI who received palivizumab translate to meaningful reductions in morbidity, family burden, and healthcare resource utilization. These benefits are comparable to those reported by Notario et al, who found a 55% reduction in RSV-related hospitalization in a similar population.[25] In low- and middle-income countries, where the socioeconomic burden of RSV infection is disproportionately high, the corresponding socioeconomic benefits of palivizumab prophylaxis may also be more substantial.[29–31] Although many countries restrict immunoprophylaxis in MLPI, the reduction in the length of hospitalization demonstrated by our study allows for an indirect inference of the economic advantages within this targeted demographic.[17] Our results suggest the need to reevaluate prophylaxis eligibility criteria to better reflect actual risk rather than relying solely on gestational age thresholds. Recently, newer agents such as nirsevimab have shown promise because of their long-acting effects and simplified dosing schedules. A recent MELODY trial demonstrated 74.5% efficacy against RSV lower respiratory tract infections that require medical attention in healthy infants.[32] Future studies should compare these newer agents to palivizumab, particularly in MLPI populations. Until such alternatives are widely validated, optimizing the existing palivizumab use remains critical for protecting vulnerable infants.

Our study had several strengths. First, it represents one of the largest population-based analysis of palivizumab effectiveness in MLPI, with a nationally representative sample spanning multiple RSV seasons (2018–2022). This enhances the external validity and clinical applicability of our findings, beyond what can be achieved in clinical trials. A sample size of 15,804 MLPI, including 2096 (13.26%) who received palivizumab provided robust statistical power. large-scale study by Farber et al, comparable to ours, reported no effectiveness of palivizumab prophylaxis in the MLPI population.[8] However, our study offers more recent data and demonstrates contrasting results.

There are several limitations that warrant consideration. The retrospective design limits causal inference, and palivizumab administration was not randomized, potentially introducing a selection bias despite our statistical adjustments. RSV diagnosis relies on administrative coding, which may underreport milder infections that do not require healthcare encounters. Additionally, we did not perform a direct cost-effectiveness analysis which would be valuable for guiding policy decisions regarding expanded prophylaxis.

Future research should address these gaps, including the impact of environmental factors on RSV outcomes in preterm infants and comprehensive economic analysis of different prophylactic strategies. Prospective studies comparing various approaches, potentially incorporating newer agents will be valuable for optimizing resource allocation for RSV prevention across diverse healthcare settings.

5. Conclusions

Our nationwide cohort study, which analyzed a large-scale sample from the NHIS database, provided robust evidence that MLPI benefits significantly from palivizumab prophylaxis, with an approximately 53% lower odds reduction in RSV infection risk and shorter hospitalization duration. Variations in policies of cost-effective prevention have led to regional and stratified disparities in prophylaxis guidelines. The current transitional era, shifting from palivizumab to long-acting monoclonal antibodies, may initially exacerbate these differences. However, this period also offers crucial insights for developing more effective prevention policies, particularly in resource-limited settings. Furthermore, the efficacy demonstrated in our MLPI population serves as foundational evidence, underscoring the need for expanded stratification studies to assess the impact of new long-acting monoclonal antibodies and to guide their optimal implementation.

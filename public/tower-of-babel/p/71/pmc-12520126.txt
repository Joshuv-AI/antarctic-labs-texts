Consumer personas for CPAP purchasing in older adults with obstructive sleep apnea: a hospital database study
Authors: Bundit Sawunyavisuth, Sitta Suntrawanichakul, Sasawan Heingraj, Suwakitti Amornpan, Kittisak Sawanyawisuth
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEven though a continuous positive airway pressure machine (CPAP) is effective in obstructive sleep apnea in older adults, there is limited data on predictors of CPAP purchasing in older adults with OSA. This study aimed to evaluate the consumer personas associated with CPAP purchasing in older adults with OSA.MethodsThis was a retrospective analytical study. The inclusion criteria were older adults aged 60 years or more, diagnosed with OSA, and already made a decision of CPAP purchasing. Consumer personas with CPAP purchasing in older adults with OSA were executed.ResultsThere were 104 older adults with OSA met the study criteria. Of those, 44 patients (42.31%) decided to purchase CPAP. Three factors were independently associated with CPAP purchasing in the older adults with OSA: female sex, allergic rhinitis, and AHI. Female sex and AHI had adjusted odds ratios (95% confidence interval) of 6.78 (1.25, 36.67) and 1.05 (1.01, 1.09), while allergic rhinitis was negatively associated with CPAP purchasing.ConclusionsConsumer personas of older adults with OSA who tended to purchase CPAP were female sex and high AHI, while the presence of allergic rhinitis was a negative factor for CPAP purchasing in this setting.

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is characterized by repeated airway obstruction causing intermittent hypoxemia [1]. There were almost one billion patients with OSA worldwide in adults aged between 30 and 69 years [2]. The prevalence of OSA in the older adult population was high, with the highest prevalence up to 80% [3,4]. Symptoms and consequences of OSA in older adults may be different from those in the young population [5,6]. Older adults with OSA are more likely to have insomnia, impaired quality of life, and impaired cognitive function [5]. Additionally, being OSA in older adults may increase the risk of cardiovascular diseases and metabolic conditions [7–10]. Proportions of metabolic syndrome are higher in OSA patients with an age of over 50 years compared with those with an age of less than 50 years, particularly in men with moderate to severe OSA [7]. Another study [8] also found that increasing age was associated with the presence of atrial fibrillation in patients with OSA; adjusted odds ratio of 1.052 (95% confidence interval of 1.004, 1.103).

Continuous positive airway pressure machine (CPAP) is the main treatment of OSA in adult patients as well as older adults. Several studies showed that CPAP therapy in older adults with OSA had better outcomes in daytime sleepiness, sleep quality, anxiety, depression, cognitive function, cardiovascular outcomes, and mortality [5]. CPAP therapy significantly reduced sleepiness score by 2.1 points (p < 0.0001) at 3 months and significantly improved survival (91 months vs 52 months; p < 0.0001) than nonusers [11,12].

Even though CPAP is effective in the treatment of OSA in older adults with OSA, CPAP purchasing rate in this segment is limited. A previous study from the US found that CPAP purchasing rate in older adults with OSA was varied between 54 and 87% [13]. A systematic review in adult patients with OSA found that older age, higher education, and high income may increase a chance to purchase CPAP [14]. Those with high income had odds ratio of 1.47 (95% confidence interval of 1.06, 2.05) for CPAP purchasing than those with low income. Note that this systematic review conducted in adult patients with OSA; not specific to older adults with OSA. Consumer personas, a patient-centered or behavioral decision-making model in healthcare, are used to facilitate the customer’s need in healthcare [15,16]. CPAP purchasing decision is one of the decision making in healthcare and may be multifactorial including mental health, perception, or belief. Knowing the consumer personas may facilitate the likelihood for CPAP purchasing in older adults as well as CPAP adherence [17–19]. Currently, there is limited data on consumer personas of CPAP purchasing in older adults with OSA. This study aimed to evaluate consumer personas associated with CPAP purchasing in the older adults with OSA.

Methods

This was a retrospective analytical study conducted at Khon Kaen University Hospital, Khon Kaen, Thailand. The inclusion criteria were older adults aged 60 years or more, diagnosed with OSA, and already made a decision of CPAP purchasing. The diagnosis of OSA was made by an apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) of 5 times/hour or more from the results of polysomnography. The study period was between 2019 and 2024.

Eligible patients were evaluated for baseline characteristics, physical signs, and polysomnography. Baseline characteristics were age, sex, insurance, and comorbid diseases, while physical signs included weight, height, body mass index, neck circumference, waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure. Data from polysomnography were the AHI and the lowest oxygen saturation. The primary outcome was a decision on CPAP purchasing.

Sample size calculation

The previous study found that the CPAP purchasing rate was 87% or p [14]. With a marginal error of 7% (d) and confidence of 95% (alpha), the required sample size was 89 patients based on a single proportion, as shown below: n=Z1−α/22⋅p⋅(1−p)d2

Statistical analyses

Patients were divided into two groups according to the primary outcome: those who purchased CPAP and those who did not purchase CPAP. Results of studied variables of both groups were reported as mean (SD) or median (interquartile range) for numerical and number (percentage) for categorical variables. The differences of each studied variable between the groups were compared by inferential statistics. For numerical variables, the Student’s t test or Wilcoxon Rank Sum test was used to compare the differences between both groups, while Chi square test or Fisher Exact test was used to compare the differences between two proportions.

Factors associated with CPAP purchasing in older adults with OSA were executed by stepwise multivariable logistic regression analysis. Studied variables were computed for a p value by univariable logistic regression analysis and included in the stepwise, multivariable logistic regression analysis [20,21]. The model was tested for a goodness of fit by the Hosmer–Lemeshow method. A p value by the Hosmer-Lemeshow Chi square of more than 0.05 indicated a goodness of fit. A numerical predictor for CPAP purchasing was computed for appropriate diagnostic cut off point by a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. Sensitivity and specificity for the best cutoff point for CPAP purchasing were reported. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA software version 18.5 (College Station, Texas, USA).

Results

There were 104 older adults with OSA met the study criteria. Of those, 44 patients (42.31%) decided to purchase CPAP. Among baseline characteristics and polysomnography results, only two factors significantly different between those who did not purchase CPAP and those who purchased CPAP: AHI and lowest oxygen saturation (Table 1). The purchased CPAP group had significantly higher AHI (30 vs 24 times/hr; p = 0.015) and lower lowest oxygen saturation (81% vs 85%; p = 0.009) than did not purchase CPAP, group by the polysomnography. Other baseline characteristics, including age, sex, insurance, co-morbid diseases, body mass index, neck circumference, waist circumference, waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure, were comparable between both groups.

Table 2 shows factors associated with CPAP purchasing by the logistic regression analysis. There were six factors in the predictive model, including female sex, allergic rhinitis, diabetes, systolic blood pressure, AHI, and lowest oxygen saturation. Of those, three factors were independently associated with CPAP purchasing in the older adults with OSA: female sex, allergic rhinitis, and AHI. Female sex and AHI had adjusted odds ratios (95% confidence interval) of 6.78 (1.25, 36.67) and 1.05 (1.01, 1.09), while allergic rhinitis was negatively associated with CPAP purchasing: adjusted odds ratio of 0.06 (95% confidence interval of 0.01, 0.59). Hosmer-Lemeshow Chi square of the model was 2.78 (p = 0.947), indicating goodness of fit of the model.

Discussion

This study found that consumer personas for CPAP purchasing in older adults with OSA was female sex, allergic rhinitis, and AHI. These factors were crucial customer characteristics. Note that these factors were personal, specific characters of customers [22].

CPAP purchasing rate in this study was slightly lower than previously reported from the US (42.31% vs 54–87%) [13]. These findings may be explained from different study population. In this study, only 84 patients out of 104 patients (80.77%) had government insurance whom were able to have insurance coverage for CPAP. In contrast, the US study was conducted in Medicare beneficiaries. In this study, insurance type was not significantly different between older adults with OSA who purchased and did not purchase CPAP, though (p = 0.736). A previous study found that up to 66.9% of patients with OSA denied CPAP due to financial constraints [23].

Even though the previous systematic review did not find that sex was a significant factor for CPAP purchasing [14], this study found that female sex had higher chance of CPAP purchasing. These findings may be explained by study design and study population. In this study, we enrolled only older adults with OSA and the predictors of CPAP purchasing were adjusted by other factors such as AHI or co-morbid diseases. On the other hand, the previous systematic review included adult patients and the analysis was not adjusted. Several studies reported that women with OSA are different from men with OSA in several aspects including symptoms, diagnosis, and consequences [24–26]. Female patients with OSA may have atypical presentations of OSA leading to difficulty in diagnosis or delay in diagnosis; as a result of higher consequences. Women with OSA had higher adjusted mortality risk than men (1.28 vs 1.03) as well as higher post-diagnostic net cost (13,730 vs 8749 euro/year) [27]. A previous qualitative study in older adults found that those patients with concerns of death or OSA consequences may facilitate of CPAP purchasing [28]. Women with OSA had more atypical symptoms but bothersome and caused poor quality of life including insomnia and fatigue [27]. Benefits of CPAP such as improve sleep quality may be another reason for CPAP purchasing in older women with OSA to purchase CPAP for their uses. As women with OSA may have several symptoms and consequences, they may have more concern on OSA treatment leading to higher purchasing rate. Regarding psychosocial aspect, a previous qualitative study showed that older adults may use a home device like CPAP by two themes: self-esteem and social device [29]. A previous study from Canda found that older women tended to participate social events more than older men (26.6 vs 20.7%; p < 0.001) [30].

Allergic rhinitis is a risk factor for OSA as it increases airway resistance and reduction in airway diameter with high levels of inflammatory mediators [31]. In terms of CPAP in patients with OSA and allergic rhinitis, using CPAP increased a higher prevalence of allergic rhinitis (15.8% vs 7.0%, respectively; p = 0.025) and exacerbation of rhinitis (7.5% vs 0%; p = 0.005) compared with the non-CPAP group [32]. Due to the difficulty of CPAP use in patients with allergic rhinitis, the purchasing rate of CPAP in this comorbid disease of older adults with OSA may be low.

As previously reported by the systematic review, increasing age was a significant factor associated with CPAP purchasing in patients with OSA [14]. Even though the previous systematic review was conducted mainly in adult patients, this study conducted in older adults found similar findings. A previous study from Australia showed that patients with OSA who purchased CPAP had more severe OSA than those who did not purchase CPAP (65% vs 35%; p < 0.001) [33]. Additionally, those who purchased CPAP also had more sleepiness than those who did not purchase CPAP (Epworth sleepiness scale of 10 vs 9; p = 0.049). These findings may imply that older adults with OSA who had more severe OSA and more symptoms tended to purchase CPAP for personal use. Even though lowest oxygen saturation was a significant factor by univariable logistic regression analysis, it was not significant in the multivariable model (Table 2). These findings may imply that lowest oxygen saturation was not a significant factor after adjusted by other factors.

There are some limitations in this study. Older adult consumers in terms of decision making may be complex and need a more holistic view [34]. Additionally, older adult customers or consumers may have other factors influenced with purchasing decisions, including economic status, cognitive functions, and socioemotional needs [35]. These factors were not evaluated in this study. The predictive model may not represent the holistic view of older adult customers. Those with undecided individuals were not included in this study, which may affect the results of this study. Stepwise regression may inflate type I error, and the AHI ROC curve showed only moderate discriminative power (AUC ∼0.67), suggesting weak predictive ability. Finally, CPAP adherence was not evaluated in this study.

In conclusion, the consumer personas of older adults with OSA who tended to purchase CPAP were female sex and high AHI, while the presence of allergic rhinitis was a negative factor for CPAP purchasing in this setting. However, findings are preliminary and hypothesis-generating. Further studies are required to confirm the results of this study.

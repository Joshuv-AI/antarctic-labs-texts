Clinical features, outcomes, and prognostic factors of lymphocyte-depleted classical Hodgkin lymphoma: a population-based SEER analysis
Authors: Guoqing Lyu, Man Yang, Lihua Wang, Linlin Tian, Xiankai Liu, Yan Huang, Jiesen Kou, Sun Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lymphocyte-depleted classical Hodgkin lymphoma (LD-cHL) is a rare histologic subtype of classical Hodgkin lymphoma. This population-based study aimed to describe the clinical characteristics and prognostic factors of LD-cHL using data from the SEER registry.LD-cHL patients were older (median age 55 years, IQR: 37–72) than those with other subtypes (median age 38 years, IQR: 25–58; p < 0.001). They were more likely to present with advanced disease (35% Stage IV [N = 159] vs. 15% [N = 6,136]; p < 0.001). B symptoms were also more frequent in LD-cHL (67.8%, N = 307 vs. 54.5%, N = 23,025; p < 0.001).The 5-year overall survival rate for LD-cHL was 51.2% (95% CI: 46.7–56.2), lower than the 81.1% (95% CI: 80.7–81.5) observed in other subtypes. Random survival forest analysis identified age (variable importance: 0.31) and chemotherapy receipt (0.10) as the variables with the highest importance scores. Age stratification showed 5-year overall survival rates of 78.5% (95% CI: 72.5–85.1) for patients < 40 years, 43.3% (95% CI: 36.4–51.6) for those aged 40–60 years, and 15.6% (95% CI: 9.6–25.2) for those > 60 years. Chemotherapy receipt was associated with higher survival rates across all age groups.In subgroup analyses limited to patients who received chemotherapy, LD-cHL remained associated with inferior survival compared with other subtypes. Lymphoma-specific survival analysis yielded 5-year rates of 88.9%, 84.3%, and 60.5% in the < 40, 40–60, and > 60 years groups, respectively.This study confirms that LD-cHL is associated with older age, advanced stage at presentation, and relatively poor survival in real-world settings. Further studies with central pathological review and more detailed clinical data are needed to improve the understanding and management of this rare subtype.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54391-6.

Introduction

Classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) is a lymphoid malignancy characterized by the presence of Reed-Sternberg cells within a reactive inflammatory background, accounting for approximately 10% of all lymphomas worldwide1–4. The disease is histologically heterogeneous and is classified into four main subtypes: nodular sclerosis, mixed cellularity, lymphocyte-rich, and lymphocyte-depleted5–9.

Lymphocyte-depleted classical Hodgkin lymphoma (LD-cHL) is the rarest subtype, accounting for approximately 1% of all classical Hodgkin lymphoma cases according to large series with central pathological review, such as those conducted by the German Hodgkin Study Group (GHSG)10. This subtype is historically associated with aggressive clinical behavior, advanced disease at diagnosis, and relatively poor outcomes compared with other cHL subtypes2,11–13.

Patients with LD-cHL frequently present with advanced Ann Arbor stage and B symptoms (fever, night sweats, and weight loss). However, detailed data on the clinical characteristics, prognostic factors, and real-world outcomes of this rare subtype remain limited, particularly in large population-based cohorts.

The present study aimed to describe the clinical features, survival outcomes, and prognostic factors of LD-cHL using data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. We compared LD-cHL with other cHL subtypes and evaluated the relative importance of key clinical variables, including age and chemotherapy receipt, using both machine learning (random survival forest) and conventional statistical methods (Cox proportional hazards regression). In addition, lymphoma-specific survival was examined according to age groups.

Materials and methods

Study population

Data for this study were obtained from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER)-21 registry. Patients diagnosed with classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) between 2000 and 2022 were identified using International Classification of Diseases for Oncology (ICD-O-3) histology codes. LD-cHL cases were defined according to the recorded histologic subtype. Patients with other cHL subtypes served as the comparison group.

Inclusion criteria included histologically confirmed cHL with available information on age, sex, Ann Arbor stage, B symptoms, chemotherapy status, and survival data. Patients with missing or incomplete key variables (such as survival time or cause of death) were excluded. The final study cohort consisted of 42,581 patients, including 451 patients with LD-cHL and 42,130 patients with other cHL subtypes.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline patient characteristics. Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using Pearson’s chi-squared test. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Survival outcomes were evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were compared with the log-rank test. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from diagnosis to death from any cause. Lymphoma-specific survival (LSS) was defined as the time from diagnosis to death due to Hodgkin lymphoma, with deaths from other causes censored. Five-year survival rates were calculated with 95% confidence intervals (CI).

To identify prognostic factors in LD-cHL, both conventional and machine learning approaches were used. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were performed to estimate hazard ratios (HR) and 95% CIs. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate model. In addition, a random survival forest (RSF) model was applied to rank the relative importance of clinical variables based on variable importance scores.

Optimal age cutpoints were determined using XTILE software to identify thresholds that best discriminate survival differences in LD-cHL patients. This analysis identified three age groups (< 40, 40–60, and > 60 years), which were used for subsequent stratified analyses.

Cause-of-death information was obtained from SEER and categorized as Hodgkin lymphoma-specific or non-cancer-related. The proportions of each cause of death were calculated.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.1). The following packages were utilized: survival and survminer for survival analysis and visualization, randomForestSRC for random survival forest modeling, and dplyr and tidyr for data management. This study was conducted in accordance with the SEER data use agreement and did not require institutional review board approval due to the use of de-identified public data.

Resluts

Patient characteristics

A total of 451 patients with LD-cHL diagnosed between 2000 and 2022 were identified in the SEER database. These patients were compared with 42,130 patients with other classical Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes. The median age at diagnosis was 55 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 37–72) in the LD-cHL group and 38 years (IQR: 25–58) in the other subtypes group (p < 0.001). Regarding sex distribution, 276 (61%) patients with LD-cHL were male and 175 (39%) were female, compared with 22,856 (54%) male and 19,274 (46%) female in other subtypes (p = 0.003). Ann Arbor stage distribution differed significantly between groups (p < 0.001). In the LD-cHL group, 159 (35%) patients were diagnosed with Stage IV disease, while 89 (20%) had Stage III, 97 (22%) had Stage II, and 45 (10%) had Stage I disease. Stage was unknown in 61 (14%) cases. In comparison, Stage IV disease was less frequent in other subtypes (15%, N = 6,136). Chemotherapy was received by 322 (71%) patients with LD-cHL compared to 35,091 (83%) patients with other subtypes (p < 0.001).B symptoms were present in 307 (67.8%) patients with LD-cHL versus 23,025 (54.5%) patients with other subtypes (p < 0.001). Prognostic factors and overall survival

The 5-year overall survival rate for patients with LD-cHL was 51.2% (95% CI: 46.7–56.2), compared with 81.1% (95% CI: 80.7–81.5) for patients with other classical Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes (Fig. 1A,B). Random survival forest (RSF) analysis was performed to evaluate the relative importance of clinical variables in LD-cHL. Age showed the highest variable importance score (0.31), followed by chemotherapy receipt (0.10). Ann Arbor stage and radiation therapy each had an importance score of 0.04, while B symptoms, extranodal involvement, sex, and race had importance scores below 0.01 (Fig. 1C). These results were consistent with univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses, which identified age, chemotherapy receipt, and Ann Arbor stage as factors significantly associated with overall survival (p < 0.001). Age as a prognostic factor

Age was evaluated as a prognostic factor using XTILE software for optimal threshold analysis. This analysis identified three age groups: <40 years, 40–60 years, and > 60 years. The 5-year overall survival rates were 78.5% (95% CI: 72.5–85.1) for patients aged < 40 years, 43.3% (95% CI: 36.4–51.6) for patients aged 40–60 years, and 15.6% (95% CI: 9.6–25.2) for patients aged > 60 years (Fig. 1D,E). The median overall survival was 45 months (range: 15–75 months) for the 40–60 years group and 5 months (range: 4–7 months) for the > 60 years group.

Impact of chemotherapy

Chemotherapy receipt was associated with improved overall survival in LD-cHL patients across all three age groups. In patients aged < 40 years, the 5-year overall survival rate was 83.2% (95% CI: 76.2–90.1) in those who received chemotherapy and 62.5% (95% CI: 42.8–91.4) in those who did not. In patients aged 40–60 years, the 5-year overall survival rate was 76.3% (95% CI: 67.3–86.2) with chemotherapy and 41.4% (95% CI: 26.9–63.8) without chemotherapy. In patients aged > 60 years, the 5-year overall survival rate was 38.3% (95% CI: 29.4–49.9) with chemotherapy and 15.4% (95% CI: 9.1–26.2) without chemotherapy. The median overall survival in this age group was 49 months (95% CI: 16–60 months) with chemotherapy and 1 month (95% CI: 1–4 months) without chemotherapy (Supplementary Fig. 1A, B).

Additional subgroup analyses were performed among patients who received chemotherapy. In chemotherapy-treated patients, LD-cHL showed inferior overall survival compared with other classical Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes(Fig. 2A). Among patients with stage III–IV disease who received chemotherapy, LD-cHL was also associated with worse overall survival than other subtypes (Fig. 2B).

Lymphoma-specific survival

Lymphoma-specific survival analysis was performed, with events defined as deaths due to Hodgkin lymphoma. The 5-year lymphoma-specific survival rates were 88.9% (95% CI: 82.9–95.3) for patients aged < 40 years, 84.3% (95% CI: 77.1–92.3) for patients aged 40–60 years, and 60.5% (95% CI: 51.9–70.4) for patients aged > 60 years.In the > 60 years group, the median lymphoma-specific survival was 49 months (95% CI: 16–60 months) in patients who received chemotherapy and 1 month (95% CI: 1–4 months) in those who did not receive chemotherapy. (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Fig. 1C).

The cause of death

Cause-of-death information was available for 257 patients with LD-cHL who died during follow-up. Hodgkin lymphoma was the most common cause of death, accounting for 41.50% (n = 107) of deaths. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma accounted for 14.00% (n = 36) of deaths. Infectious diseases contributed to 8.90% (n = 23) of deaths, diseases of the heart accounted for 7.80% (n = 20), and other unspecified causes accounted for 7.80% (n = 20). (Table 3).

Discussion

LD-cHL is a rare and aggressive histologic subtype of classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL), consistently associated with poorer outcomes compared with other cHL variants1–4. In this contemporary SEER population-based analysis, the 5-year overall survival rate for patients with LD-cHL was 51.2% (95% CI: 46.7–56.2), markedly lower than the 81.1% (95% CI: 80.7–81.5) observed in other cHL subtypes. These findings align with previous population-based and institutional studies, reinforcing that LD-cHL remains one of the most challenging subtypes to manage in routine clinical practice2,4,14.

This study provides a detailed description of the clinical characteristics and prognostic factors of LD-cHL using a large registry dataset spanning two decades. Random survival forest analysis, complemented by conventional Cox regression, identified age and chemotherapy receipt as the two variables with the highest importance scores. In addition, the study evaluated patterns of lymphoma-specific survival across different age groups, offering a more nuanced understanding of disease-related mortality.

Age was the strongest prognostic factor in this cohort. Using XTILE software for optimal threshold analysis, patients were stratified into three clinically relevant age groups: <40 years, 40–60 years, and > 60 years. The 5-year overall survival rates were 78.5% (95% CI: 72.5–85.1), 43.3% (95% CI: 36.4–51.6), and 15.6% (95% CI: 9.6–25.2), respectively. Median overall survival was 45 months (range: 15–75 months) in the 40–60 years group and only 5 months (range: 4–7 months) in the > 60 years group. Importantly, patients younger than 40 years achieved survival rates comparable to those reported for other cHL subtypes in contemporary series. Patients older than 60 years exhibited particularly poor outcomes, with a median overall survival of only 5 months (95% CI: 4–7 months) in those who did not receive chemotherapy. This finding likely reflects a combination of advanced disease, comorbidities, frailty, and treatment selection in elderly patients. These findings highlight the marked heterogeneity of outcomes within LD-cHL and the critical influence of age on survival.

Chemotherapy receipt was associated with substantially improved survival across all three age groups. However, subgroup analyses restricted to patients who actually received chemotherapy demonstrated that LD-cHL continued to show inferior overall survival compared with other cHL subtypes, although the survival difference was attenuated, particularly among younger patients. Similar patterns were observed when the analysis was limited to stage III–IV patients treated with chemotherapy. These real-world results are consistent with data from cooperative group trials suggesting that while modern chemotherapy regimens can meaningfully improve outcomes, they may not fully overcome the inherent aggressiveness of LD-cHL outside highly selected trial populations15–20.

Lymphoma-specific survival analysis further confirmed the adverse prognosis of LD-cHL. The 5-year lymphoma-specific survival rates were 88.9% (95% CI: 82.9–95.3) for patients < 40 years, 84.3% (95% CI: 77.1–92.3) for those aged 40–60 years, and 60.5% (95% CI: 51.9–70.4) for those > 60 years. In the oldest age group, receipt of chemotherapy was associated with a marked prolongation of median lymphoma-specific survival (49 months versus 1 month), underscoring the importance of treatment delivery even in elderly patients.

Several limitations must be acknowledged when interpreting these results. First, the SEER database does not provide centralized pathological review. Given the rarity of LD-cHL, the risk of diagnostic misclassification with other aggressive lymphomas is a recognized concern21,22. Second, important granular data—including performance status, comorbidities, specific chemotherapy regimens, radiation details, and response assessment—are not available, which restricts the ability to fully adjust for confounding variables. Third, as a retrospective registry study, causal inferences, particularly concerning the effect of chemotherapy, cannot be firmly drawn.

In summary, this large population-based study confirms that LD-cHL is typically diagnosed in older patients, frequently presents with advanced-stage disease, and is associated with significantly poorer survival compared with other classical Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes. Age at diagnosis and receipt of chemotherapy were the most important factors associated with clinical outcomes. Further well-designed prospective studies incorporating central pathology review, comprehensive geriatric assessment, detailed treatment information, and molecular characterization are warranted to better understand the biology of this rare subtype and to develop more effective, individualized treatment strategies for patients across different age groups23,24.

Factors associated with adverse cardiovascular events after coronary artery bypass grafting and their relationship with left atrial function: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Sen Yu, Yongcheng Mei, Shijun Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to investigate clinical and echocardiographic factors associated with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) following coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), with particular emphasis on the association between MACE and preoperative left atrial function. This study aimed to identify clinical and echocardiographic predictors of post-CABG MACEs, and further explore the specific relationship between preoperative left atrial function and the occurrence of MACEs.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 198 patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) who underwent CABG at our hospital between October 2020 and June 2023. The primary endpoint was the first occurrence of postoperative MACE within 24 months after CABG. Patients were divided into a MACE group (n=92) and a non-MACE group (n=106). Baseline characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, and laboratory indicators were compared between groups. Candidate variables were screened using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, and the selected variables were entered simultaneously into a multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression model. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the curve (AUC) values were used for exploratory evaluation of the discrimination of left atrial strain parameters.ResultsSignificant differences were observed between the MACE and non-MACE groups in age, prevalence of hypertension, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, left atrial reservoir strain (LASr), left atrial conduit strain (LAScd), left atrial contraction strain (LASct), cardiac troponin I (cTnI), and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels (P<0.05). LASSO regression retained seven variables: hypertension, ASA classification, LASr, LAScd, LASct, cTnI, and BNP. In the multivariable Cox model, LAScd [hazard ratio (HR) =0.904] and LASct (HR =0.880) were associated with a lower hazard of MACE after CABG, whereas cTnI (HR =1.227), BNP (HR =1.009), and ASA classification (HR =1.908) were associated with a higher hazard of MACE after CABG. ROC curve analysis showed AUC values of 0.716 and 0.793 for LAScd and LASct, respectively. Their sensitivities were 0.481 and 0.689, and their specificities were 0.924 and 0.783, respectively.ConclusionsPreoperative left atrial function, cTnI, BNP, and ASA classification were associated with MACE occurrence after CABG. LAScd and LASct may serve as adjunctive markers for postoperative risk stratification, but their clinical use requires validation in larger prospective cohorts.Keywords: Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), associated factors, left atrial function

Highlight box

Key findings

• Multiple preoperative clinical, laboratory, and echocardiographic parameters significantly differed between patients with and without major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE) within 24 months after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), including age, hypertension status, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, left atrial strain indices, cardiac troponin I (cTnI), and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP).

• Multivariable Cox regression confirmed preoperative left atrial conduit strain (LAScd) and left atrial contraction strain (LASct) as independent protective factors against post-CABG MACE, while higher ASA classification, elevated cTnI, and elevated BNP were independent risk factors.

What is known and what is new?

• Conventional clinical and biochemical markers, including ASA physical status, cTnI, and BNP, are widely used to evaluate perioperative cardiovascular risk in patients undergoing CABG. Postoperative MACE is a major determinant of long-term prognosis after coronary bypass surgery. Although left atrial dysfunction correlates with adverse cardiac outcomes, the independent prognostic role of preoperative left atrial strain parameters (LASr, LAScd, LASct) for post-CABG MACE remains poorly defined.

• This retrospective cohort study systematically screened comprehensive preoperative indicators using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator and Cox regression analyses. Preoperative LAScd and LASct were identified as independent protective factors for 24-month post-CABG MACE, whereas higher ASA classification, elevated cTnI, and elevated BNP independently increased MACE risk. LAScd and LASct demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability for postoperative cardiovascular events, providing incremental prognostic value beyond traditional clinical biomarkers in CABG patients.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings provide novel evidence that preoperative left atrial strain parameters refine postoperative cardiovascular risk stratification for CABG patients. Routine integration of LAScd and LASct into preoperative echocardiographic evaluation may help clinicians identify high-risk individuals and optimize perioperative monitoring and management strategies. Current risk assessment mainly relies on conventional biochemical and surgical grading indicators; incorporation of left atrial functional parameters should be promoted to improve individualized risk prediction. Further large prospective studies are warranted to validate and standardize the clinical application of left atrial strain in CABG risk evaluation.

Introduction

In recent years, the global burden of cardiovascular disease has remained substantial, with coronary heart disease (CHD) continuing to be one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide (1). As a common clinical cardiac condition, CHD primarily results from coronary atherosclerosis, which can lead to luminal narrowing or obstruction, myocardial ischemia, hypoxia, and myocardial injury (2). Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is an established revascularization strategy for patients with severe coronary artery disease (CAD), particularly those with left main disease or complex multivessel disease (3,4). Despite its therapeutic efficacy, CABG recipients remain at risk of postoperative major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), including myocardial infarction, heart failure, arrhythmia, unplanned revascularization, and cardiovascular death (5-7). The reported incidence of postoperative MACE varies considerably across studies because of differences in endpoint definitions, follow-up duration, and patient risk profiles (8). Therefore, identifying perioperative factors associated with MACE after CABG is important for risk stratification, follow-up planning, and postoperative management.

Traditional assessment of cardiovascular risk after CABG relies mainly on clinical risk factors, operative risk classification, and left ventricular functional indices (9). Although left ventricular structure and function are important prognostic indicators in ischemic heart disease, valvular heart disease, and heart failure, their prognostic performance may be limited in specific clinical populations (10). In recent years, the role of left atrial structure and function in cardiovascular pathophysiology has gained increasing attention. The left atrium is not merely a passive reservoir; it contributes to ventricular filling, stores blood during ventricular systole, and functions as a contractile booster pump during late diastole (11). Left atrial dysfunction may precede overt left ventricular dysfunction and has been associated with adverse outcomes in ischemic heart disease, valvular heart disease, and heart failure (12). Left atrial strain parameters may therefore provide complementary information for assessing postoperative cardiovascular risk after CABG (13).

However, the association between preoperative left atrial function and MACE after CABG remains incompletely understood. Most existing studies of post-CABG risk have focused on clinical factors and left ventricular function, whereas detailed left atrial strain assessment has been less frequently incorporated. Therefore, this retrospective cohort study investigated clinical, laboratory, and echocardiographic factors associated with MACE after CABG, with a focus on the relationship between left atrial function and postoperative outcomes. The findings are intended to provide evidence for risk stratification and to generate hypotheses for future prospective validation. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0122/rc).

Methods

Ethical statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhumadian Central Hospital (No. Z201903). Given that this is a retrospective study utilizing only de-identified patient data, informed consent is not required as it poses no risk or adverse impact on patient care. This exemption complies with regulations and ethical guidelines pertaining to retrospective research.

Study design

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at Zhumadian Central Hospital. A total of 312 patients with CAD who were scheduled for CABG at Zhumadian Central Hospital between October 2020 and June 2023 were initially screened. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 198 patients were included in the final analysis. The endpoint was defined as the first occurrence of postoperative MACE within 24 months after CABG. Patients were assigned to the MACE group (n=92) or the non-MACE group (n=106) according to whether the endpoint occurred. The patient flow is shown in Figure 1.

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (I) confirmed diagnosis of CAD based on clinical signs, symptoms, and coronary angiography results; (II) age ≥18 years; (III) completion of CABG surgery; (IV) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification (14) II–III; (V) alert and able to communicate naturally without severe neurological or psychiatric disorders; and (VI) complete clinical and follow-up data. Exclusion criteria: (I) severe hepatic, renal, or cerebral disease; (II) cardiac conditions including valvular heart disease, primary cardiomyopathy, congenital heart disease, or acute myocardial infarction; (III) concurrent pulmonary infarction, aortic dissection, acute/chronic nephritis, or other systemic pathologies; (IV) hematologic disorders, malignancies, or autoimmune diseases; (V) concurrent acute/chronic infectious diseases; (VI) prior CABG; and (VII) pregnancy or lactation.

Treatment methods

All patients underwent CABG performed by the same surgical team, with consistent anesthesia and perioperative management protocols. In all cases, the left internal mammary artery and great saphenous vein were routinely considered as graft vessels according to anatomical suitability and operative judgment. Following vascular anastomosis, transient flow measurements were performed on grafts to assess patency. Postoperatively, all patients received standard medical therapy according to institutional perioperative management protocols and contemporary CABG care recommendations. Granular operative variables, including cardiopulmonary bypass time, aortic cross-clamp time, number of grafts, and on-pump or off-pump technique, were not uniformly available in the structured dataset and were therefore not entered into the multivariable model.

General data collection

General demographic and clinical information was retrospectively collected from the electronic medical record system, including sex, age, body mass index, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, educational attainment, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and chronic kidney disease), cardiac functional status, disease duration, number of diseased coronary arteries, and medication use [angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEI)/angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB), beta-blockers, aldosterone receptor antagonists, and digoxin]. Chronic kidney disease was identified from recorded clinical diagnoses, whereas renal function was also analyzed quantitatively using creatinine, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR).

Echocardiography examination

All patients underwent transthoracic echocardiography within 24 hours of admission using a Philips EPIQ 7C ultrasound diagnostic system (Netherlands) equipped with an X5-1 cardiac probe (frequency 2–4 MHz) and vendor-provided speckle-tracking echocardiography analysis software. All examinations were performed by the same experienced echocardiographer. Subjects were positioned in the left lateral decubitus position at rest and connected to synchronized electrocardiography. In two-dimensional mode, depth, angle, and gain were adjusted to achieve clear endocardial visualization, and the frame rate was maintained at 60–100 frames per second. Dynamic images of the left ventricular apical four-chamber view were recorded for four consecutive cardiac cycles and used for left atrial strain analysis. The zero-reference point for left atrial strain was set at left ventricular end-diastole, using the R wave on electrocardiography as the temporal reference. Left atrial reservoir strain (LASr), left atrial conduit strain (LAScd), and left atrial contraction strain (LASct) were obtained by retrieving the dynamic images, activating the automated left atrial quantitative analysis software, and manually adjusting the endocardial contour when automatic tracking deviated from the atrial border. Left atrial volume index (LAVI) and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) were measured using the two-dimensional Simpson method. Mitral E velocity was obtained by pulsed-wave Doppler, mitral annular E’ was obtained by tissue Doppler, and the E/E’ ratio was calculated.

Laboratory indicator testing

Blood glucose indicators

Patients fasted for 8–10 hours. On the morning after admission, 3 mL of fasting venous blood was collected from the antecubital vein. The samples were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes, and serum was separated for testing. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) and 2-hour postprandial glucose (2hPG) levels were measured using a biochemical analyzer (Model: MINDRAY BS420; Manufacturer: Shenzhen Mindray) and the glucose oxidase method.

Lipid profile

After an 8–10-hour fast, 3 mL of venous blood was collected from the antecubital vein the following morning. The samples were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes, and serum was separated for analysis using a biochemical analyzer (same model and manufacturer as above) and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) to measure serum total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels.

Renal function indicators

After an 8–10-hour fast, 3 mL of venous blood was drawn from the elbow vein on the following morning. The sample was centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes, the supernatant serum was collected, and creatinine was measured using a fully automated biochemical analyzer (same model and manufacturer as above) and the Roche (PA) rate colorimetric method. Urine samples were collected for measurement of urinary albumin and urinary creatinine. BUN was measured by chemiluminescence. The UACR was calculated as urinary albumin (mg/L) divided by urinary creatinine (mg/L). eGFR was obtained from the hospital laboratory system using a standard creatinine-based equation and expressed as mL/min/1.73 m2.

Cardiac injury markers

All biomarker values used in this study were baseline preoperative measurements. Patients fasted for 8–10 hours, and 3 mL of venous blood was drawn from the elbow vein on the morning after admission and before CABG. After centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes, serum was collected, and creatine kinase-myocardial band (CK-MB), cardiac troponin I (cTnI), and B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels were measured using a fully automated biochemical analyzer (same instrument and model as above) and immunoassay methods.

Study endpoints

All patients completed 24 months of follow-up. The primary endpoint was postoperative MACE, defined as the first occurrence of any of the following events after CABG: fatal arrhythmia, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, heart failure requiring clinical treatment, unplanned revascularization, or cardiac death. Only the first qualifying event was counted in the time-to-event analysis. Survival time was calculated from the date of CABG to the date of first MACE. Patients without MACE were censored at the end of the 24-month follow-up period. The broad composite endpoint was selected to reflect clinically meaningful cardiovascular deterioration after CABG, while acknowledging that individual components differ in severity and pathophysiology.

Study size

We included all consecutive patients who met the inclusion criteria at our hospital between October 2020 and June 2023. Model stability was evaluated using the events-per-variable approach. With 92 MACE events and seven variables in the final Cox model, the events-per-variable value was 13.1, which supported model stability under conventional regression modeling criteria, while still requiring confirmation in larger external cohorts.

Bias control

Several measures were implemented to minimize potential biases. To reduce selection bias, strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, and all consecutive eligible patients during the study period were screened. To reduce information bias, baseline demographic, clinical, echocardiographic, and laboratory data were extracted from the electronic medical record system using predefined variables. To reduce measurement variability, echocardiographic examinations were performed by the same experienced echocardiographer using the same ultrasound system and analysis workflow. To reduce model instability, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was used for candidate-variable selection before multivariable Cox regression. Residual confounding was still possible because several operative and postoperative variables were not uniformly available in the structured retrospective dataset.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 statistical software. Count data are expressed as n and were analyzed using the Chi-squared test. Continuous data were assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and were analyzed using the independent-samples t-test. Non-normally distributed variables are expressed as median and interquartile range and were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. To reduce model instability, all prespecified candidate variables collected in the study were entered into LASSO regression, with 10-fold cross-validation used to select the optimal λ. Variables with non-zero coefficients were subsequently entered simultaneously into the Cox proportional hazards regression model without stepwise removal. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Follow-up time was defined as the interval from CABG to first MACE or censoring at 24 months. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted for left atrial strain parameters, and the area under the curve (AUC) was used to describe discrimination. The ROC analysis was interpreted as exploratory and was not considered a substitute for formal validation or incremental-value testing against established surgical risk scores. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant flow

A total of 312 patients with CAD scheduled for CABG were initially screened during the study period from October 2020 to June 2023. Of these, 114 patients were excluded for the following reasons: severe hepatic, renal, or cerebral disease (n=22), concurrent cardiac conditions including valvular heart disease or primary cardiomyopathy (n=18), concurrent pulmonary infarction or aortic dissection (n=9), hematologic disorders or malignancies (n=11), acute or chronic infectious diseases (n=8), prior CABG (n=5), pregnancy or lactation (n=2), ASA classification outside II-III (n=14), incomplete clinical or follow-up data (n=17), and inability to communicate naturally due to neurological or psychiatric disorders (n=8). Ultimately, 198 patients were included in the final analysis. During 24 months of follow-up, first postoperative MACE occurred in 92 patients, and 106 patients had no postoperative MACE. The patient flow is shown in Figure 1.

Comparison of baseline characteristics between MACE and non-MACE groups

There were no statistically significant differences between the MACE and non-MACE groups in sex, body mass index, smoking history, drinking history, educational level, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, chronic kidney disease, disease duration, number of diseased coronary arteries, or medication use (P>0.05). The mean ages of patients in the MACE and non-MACE groups were 65.32±8.24 and 61.80±7.96 years, respectively. Hypertension was more frequent in the MACE group than in the non-MACE group (20/92 vs. 10/106), and ASA class III was also more frequent in the MACE group (52/92 vs. 36/106). These differences were statistically significant (P<0.05), suggesting that older age, hypertension, and higher ASA classification were associated with MACE occurrence (Table 1).

Comparison of echocardiographic parameters between MACE and non-MACE groups

There were no statistically significant differences in LAVI, LVEF, or E/E’ between the MACE group and the non-MACE group (P>0.05). LASSO regression analysis

The dependent variable was the occurrence of MACE. All prespecified candidate variables from baseline characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, and laboratory indicators were entered into LASSO regression rather than being restricted to variables significant in univariable comparisons. Based on the error curve (Figure 2A), the position with the lowest cross-validation error was selected, and its corresponding x-coordinate represented the optimal log(λ) value, with the upper x-axis displaying the number of variables. The corresponding variables were then identified in the solution path map (Figure 2B) for subsequent multivariable analysis. At λ=0.009, the variables with non-zero regression coefficients were hypertension, ASA classification, LASr, LAScd, LASct, cTnI, and BNP; these seven variables were entered into the Cox regression model.

Cox regression analysis of MACE occurrence after CABG

The seven variables retained by LASSO regression were entered simultaneously into the Cox proportional hazards regression model. LAScd (HR =0.904, 95% CI: 0.842–0.971) and LASct (HR =0.880, 95% CI: 0.828–0.934) were associated with a lower hazard of MACE after CABG, whereas cTnI (HR =1.227, 95% CI: 1.108–1.359), BNP (HR =1.009, 95% CI: 1.005–1.014), and ASA classification (HR =1.908, 95% CI: 1.117–3.260) were associated with a higher hazard of MACE after CABG. LASr and hypertension were not statistically significant in the multivariable model (Table 4, Figure 3).

ROC curve analysis of the association between left atrial function and post-CABG MACE

ROC curve analysis was performed to descriptively assess the discrimination of left atrial strain parameters for post-CABG MACE. The AUC values were 0.716 for LAScd and 0.793 for LASct. LAScd showed high specificity (0.924) but limited sensitivity (0.481), whereas LASct showed a sensitivity of 0.689 and specificity of 0.783. These findings indicate that LAScd and LASct were associated with MACE occurrence, but the ROC results should be interpreted as exploratory rather than as evidence that these parameters alone are sufficient for clinical decision-making (Table 5).

Discussion

CHD is a common cardiovascular condition characterized by coronary atherosclerosis, ischemia, and impaired myocardial perfusion, all of which can affect daily activity and long-term health (15,16). CABG is an important surgical treatment for selected patients with CHD, but postoperative MACE may still occur because of baseline comorbidity, myocardial ischemic burden, ventricular dysfunction, perioperative stress, and residual or recurrent cardiovascular risk (17). In this study, higher LAScd and LASct were associated with a lower hazard of MACE after CABG, whereas higher baseline cTnI, higher BNP, and higher ASA classification were associated with a higher hazard. These findings suggest that left atrial function, myocardial injury burden, neurohormonal activation, and overall perioperative risk status are relevant to postoperative outcome assessment after CABG, which is consistent with evidence linking left atrial strain to adverse cardiovascular outcomes in high-risk cardiovascular populations (18).

cTnI is a specific marker of myocardial cell injury. In the present study, cTnI was measured as a baseline preoperative biomarker after admission and before CABG, and therefore it should be interpreted as reflecting preoperative myocardial injury or ischemic burden rather than postoperative myocardial damage (19). Lu et al. (20) reported that CHD patients with higher cTnI levels were more prone to adverse cardiovascular outcomes. BNP, which is secreted by ventricular cardiomyocytes under pressure overload and volume stress, is a key biomarker of ventricular wall tension and heart failure risk (21). Li et al. (22) found that CHD patients with poorer outcomes had higher serum BNP levels than those with favorable outcomes. Consistent with these findings, the present study showed that baseline cTnI and BNP levels were higher in the MACE group than in the non-MACE group and were associated with MACE in the multivariable Cox model. These biomarkers may reflect myocardial vulnerability and hemodynamic burden before CABG, thereby contributing to postoperative risk stratification (23,24). ASA classification was also associated with MACE after CABG, which is clinically plausible because a higher ASA classification indicates greater comorbidity burden, poorer physiological reserve, and reduced tolerance to surgical stress (25).

The role of the left atrium in the progression of cardiac disease has gradually been recognized. Rather than functioning only as a passive reservoir, the left atrium contributes to ventricular filling through reservoir, conduit, and contraction phases (26). Left atrial strain parameters, including LASr, LAScd, and LASct, provide quantitative assessment of these phases. Doganay et al. (27) found that left atrial enlargement was associated with mortality and cardiovascular events in patients with acute coronary syndrome. In the present cohort, LASr, LAScd, and LASct were significantly lower in patients who developed MACE, and LAScd and LASct remained independently associated with MACE in the multivariable Cox model. Impaired left atrial function may represent an early manifestation of increased ventricular filling pressure, myocardial ischemia, fibrosis, and reduced ventricular compliance. After CABG, perioperative inflammatory stress and ischemia-reperfusion injury may further interact with pre-existing atrial dysfunction (28,29). Therefore, LAScd and LASct may provide adjunctive information beyond conventional echocardiographic parameters, although this interpretation requires validation in multicenter prospective studies.

ROC curve analysis is commonly used to describe discrimination, with the AUC quantifying the ability of a marker to distinguish patients with and without an outcome (30). In this study, LAScd and LASct had AUC values of 0.716 and 0.793, respectively, for discriminating post-CABG MACE. However, the sensitivity of LAScd was only 0.481, indicating that LAScd alone would miss a substantial proportion of patients who subsequently developed MACE. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as showing an association and exploratory discriminatory value rather than definitive clinical predictive utility. Established surgical risk tools such as EuroSCORE II and the Society of Thoracic Surgeons score were not directly compared because complete variables required for calculating these scores were not consistently available in the retrospective dataset. Accordingly, LAScd and LASct should be considered potential adjunctive markers rather than replacements for established clinical assessment. Future studies should evaluate whether left atrial strain improves discrimination, calibration, reclassification, or clinical decision-making when added to conventional clinical and surgical risk models.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center retrospective cohort study with 198 patients, which limits causal inference and generalizability. Second, although 92 MACE events provided an events-per-variable value of 13.1 for the seven-variable Cox model, the sample size remains modest and the model requires external validation. Third, the composite MACE endpoint included events with different pathophysiological mechanisms and clinical severity. The analysis counted only the first event, but component-specific event rates and a hard-endpoint sensitivity analysis could not be reliably performed because adjudicated component-level outcome data were not uniformly available. Fourth, several important operative and postoperative variables, including cardiopulmonary bypass time, aortic cross-clamp time, number of grafts, on-pump or off-pump technique, completeness of revascularization, postoperative complications, and discharge medication adherence, were not consistently captured in structured records and may have caused residual confounding. Fifth, left atrial strain was assessed from the apical four-chamber view, and formal intraobserver and interobserver reproducibility testing was not performed. Sixth, EuroSCORE II and Society of Thoracic Surgeons scores could not be calculated for all patients because the required variables were incomplete. Finally, Kaplan-Meier survival curves were not constructed because LAScd and LASct were modeled as continuous variables and validated post-CABG cut-off values for these strain parameters were not available; future studies should assess survival curves using prespecified or externally validated thresholds. Therefore, future studies should use multicenter prospective designs, larger sample sizes, standardized echocardiographic protocols, detailed operative variables, component-level endpoint adjudication, and validated clinical risk models to further evaluate the clinical value of left atrial strain after CABG.

In this retrospective cohort study, preoperative left atrial function, cTnI, BNP, and ASA classification were associated with the occurrence of MACE after CABG. LAScd and LASct were independently associated with MACE and showed exploratory discriminatory value. These findings support the potential role of preoperative left atrial strain as an adjunctive marker for postoperative risk assessment, but larger prospective studies with external validation and comparison against established surgical risk scores are needed before clinical implementation.

Conclusions

Preoperative left atrial strain indicators (LAScd and LASct), serum cTnI, BNP, and ASA classification were independent predictors of 24-month MACE after CABG. Reduced LAScd and LASct reflected impaired left atrial pump and conduit function, which were associated with lower risk of postoperative cardiovascular adverse events. LAScd and LASct displayed moderate predictive performance for MACE and could serve as supplementary echocardiographic biomarkers to refine perioperative risk stratification for patients receiving CABG. Further large-sample prospective clinical trials are required to validate the predictive threshold and standardized application of left atrial strain parameters in routine preoperative cardiac risk evaluation.

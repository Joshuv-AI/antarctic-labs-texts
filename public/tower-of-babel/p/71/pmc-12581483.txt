Multi-algorithm radiomics machine learning models integrating ultrasound imaging and inflammation-immune features for hepatic metastases identification
Authors: Linyong Wu, Shaofeng Wu, Songhua Li, Shaofeng Li, Yan Lin, Dayou Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatic metastases (HM) and primary liver malignant tumor (PLMC) share partially similar pathological foundations, which can make it difficult to differentiate them based on visual imaging findings. This study will explore the value of multi-algorithm radiomics machine learning models that integrate ultrasound imaging and inflammation-immune features in the identification of HM.MethodsPatients with hepatic malignancies who had undergone ultrasound-guided biopsy and image acquisition were retrospectively included. A total of 104 patients were randomly divided into training and internal validation cohorts at a 6:4 ratio, while other 45 patients were assigned as the external validation cohort. The PyRadiomics package was utilized to extract 107 radiomics original features. The Wilcoxon test was employed to identify high-value features significantly associated with HM. Univariate analysis was employed to identify inflammation-immune risk features related to HM for model development. 12 machine learning algorithms were integrated to combine radiomics features with inflammation-immune features. Model performance was comprehensively evaluated through receiver operating characteristic curves, and Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) method.ResultsThe Wilcoxon test identified 15 radiomics features significantly associated with HM (P < 0.001), which were subsequently presented to 12 machine learning algorithms to develop 98 models using two or more features. Among these, 68% of the models achieved moderate identify performance [area under the curve (AUC), 0.73–0.82] in the internal validation cohort. The optimal algorithm radiomics model (random forest + gradient boosting machine) was developed using 4 selected features, yielding AUCs of 0.77 in the training cohort and 0.82 in the internal validation cohort, 0.70 in the external validation cohort, respectively. Univariate analysis further identified platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and platelet-to-albumin ratio (PAR) as high-risk inflammation-immune features for HM. While integrating radiomic features with PLR/PAR enhanced identification performance in the training cohort (AUC = 0.84), this improvement was not significantly replicated in the internal validation cohort (AUC = 0.82), but there was an improvement in the external validation cohort (AUC = 0.74). SHAP analysis revealed that PAR contributed most to the integrated model’s predictions, followed by the aforementioned 4 radiomic features.ConclusionThis study revealed the potential clinical utility of radiomics features and inflammation-immune features in HM identification.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-025-01977-7.

Introduction

Hepatic cancer represents the fourth most common cancer and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality in China, with 367,700 newly diagnosed cases and 316,500 deaths recorded in 2022 [1]. Primary liver malignant tumor (PLMC), arising from hepatic parenchymal or ductal cells, is histologically classified into hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCC), and combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-iCC). In contrast, hepatic metastases (HM), defined as secondary liver tumors originating from extrahepatic malignancies, constitute approximately 25% of all hepatic space-occupying lesions, exceeding the incidence of primary hepatic cancers [2]. The predominant primary tumors associated with HM include colorectal, lung, breast, pancreatic, and gastric carcinomas [3]. The 1-year survival rate for patients with HM was 15.1%, while for patients with non-liver metastases it was 24.0% [4]. Clinically, HM and PLMC exhibit marked disparities in surgical strategies, systemic therapies, diagnostic biomarkers, and prognoses [5]. However, partially overlapping histopathological features may result in HM and PLMC exhibiting similar imaging manifestations, thereby complicating clinical differentiation [6]. Notably, HM with occult primary tumors is not uncommon, and the biological heterogeneity of primary malignancies further exacerbates diagnostic challenges in distinguishing HM from PLMC [7]. Furthermore, intertumoral heterogeneity among HM subtypes, driven by distinct primary origins, introduces an additional layer of complexity to their identification. Consequently, developing robust methodologies to accurately discern HM and trace their primary origins is imperative for advancing precision oncology in HM.

Visual imaging features are valuable non-invasive adjuncts for differentiating pathological subtypes of hepatic cancers. For instance, HCC was classically characterized by a wash-in/wash-out enhancement pattern on dynamic imaging [8]. However, iCC and colorectal liver metastases (CRLM), both sharing adenocarcinoma histopathology, exhibited overlapping imaging features, such as rim or peripheral arterial-phase enhancement, peripheral washout in portal/delayed phases, and delayed centripetal enhancement [9]. Additionally, solitary HM often demonstrated MRI characteristics overlapping with HCC and hepatic hemangioma (HH), leading to reducing diagnostic sensitivity [10]. While conventional visual imaging approaches face limitations in further refining diagnostic precision, quantitative radiomics—driven by artificial intelligence (AI)—has emerged as a transformative tool, paralleling the analytical capabilities of similar high-throughput genomic profiling [11]. For example, a machine learning (ML) model utilizing ultrasound-derived radiomics features from 668 PLMC patients achieved moderate performance in differentiating HCC (n = 531), cHCC-iCC (n = 48), and iCC (n = 89) subtypes [12]. Similarly, MRI-based radiomics features demonstrated a sensitivity of 0.88 for HCC identification in a cohort of 33 cHCC-iCC, 88 HCC, and 75 iCC cases [13]. Despite these advancements, AI-driven studies focusing on HM-PLMC differentiation and HM origin tracing remain limited, particularly those utilizing ultrasound imaging. Promisingly, preliminary studies underscored AI’s clinical potential: an ultrasound radiomics model discriminated HM (n = 39) from PLMC (n = 75) with a sensitivity of 0.77 and specificity of 0.88 [14], while another model identified the digestive system as the primary origin in 115 HM cases with a sensitivity of 0.89 and specificity of 0.82 [15]. Nevertheless, current HM-related studies predominantly rely on single or limited ML algorithms, failing to fully optimize radiomics feature utilization. In contrast, multi-algorithm ensemble frameworks—incorporating hundreds of feature combinations—demonstrated superior robustness. For instance, a study leveraging ultrasound radiomics data from 151 patients developed 77 ML models, achieving moderate performance in predicting pancreatic cancer lymph node metastasis [16]. This highlights the necessity of algorithmic diversity to validate feature significance and enhance generalization analysis of features.

Inflammation-immunity-related features, including the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), platelet-to-albumin ratio (PAR), systemic immune inflammation index (SII), and systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI), are widely utilized in cancers for diagnostic and prognostic stratification due to their rapid accessibility via routine blood tests [17, 18]. For instance, in a cohort of 698 patients with brain tumors, NLR and SII demonstrated area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.75, and 0.66, respectively, in identifying 39 cases of brain metastases [19]. Similarly, in a cohort of 252 breast cancer patients, NLR and PLR achieved AUCs of 0.76 and 0.66 for distinguishing patients with recurrence or metastasis [20]. However, the application of these inflammation-immunity-related features has not been adequately explored in the context of HM identification and primary tumor origin tracing.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the diagnostic utility of multi-algorithm radiomics machine learning models derived from ultrasound imaging and inflammation-immune features in HM identification. The developed models encompass: (1) inflammation-immunity-related models (IIM), (2) radiomics model (RM), and (3) inflammation-immunity-related radiomics machine learning model (IIRRM). This study will provide novel insights for refining precision management systems in HM medicine.

Materials and methods

Subjects and clinicopathological characteristics

Consecutive patients who were confirmed to have hepatic malignancies via ultrasound-guided biopsy at Maoming People’s Hospital from 2022 to 2024 were retrospectively included, as the training and internal validation cohorts. Patients diagnosed with hepatic malignancies via ultrasound-guided biopsy at the same hospital from January to July 2025 were used as the external validation cohort. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Ultrasound images of hepatic space-occupying lesions were acquired during the ultrasound-guided biopsy procedure; (2) The biopsy results confirmed hepatic malignancies. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Presence of measurement artifacts in ultrasound images; (2) Ultrasound images could not be fully visualized or exhibited suboptimal image quality (Fig. 1). A total of 104 patients with hepatic malignancies were included as the training and internal validation cohort, with an average age of (63 ± 12) years and an age range of 26 to 93 years. A total of 45 patients with hepatic malignancies were included as the external validation cohort, with an average age of (62 ± 13) years and an age range of 32 to 87 years. Clinical characteristics collected included: gender, age, tumor lesion size and number, hepatitis status, NLR, PLR, PAR, SII (calculated as platelet count × neutrophil count / lymphocyte count), and SIRI (calculated as neutrophil count × monocyte count / lymphocyte count). The missing data for all clinical characteristics were less than 5%, and the missing values were imputed with median values.

Image acquisition and feature extraction

Ultrasound images of 104 hepatic malignancies were acquired by an ultrasound physician with 10 years of abdominal intervention experience using a GE LOGIQ E9 color doppler ultrasound system. The same physician utilized a Mindray Resona7 color doppler ultrasound system to acquire ultrasound images of 45 hepatic malignancies in the external validation cohort. The ultrasound images with the largest diameter and optimal clarity were stored in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine format. The examinations were performed with a C5-1 abdominal probe, with frequency settings of 3.0–5.0 MHz and gain settings of 30–60 dB. All images were resampled to a resolution of 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm. The ComBat method was applied to correct for image variations arising from differences in acquisition time. Two ultrasound physicians with 5 years of abdominal diagnostic experience independently segmented regions of interest (ROIs) of hepatic malignancies using ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0), with any discrepancies resolved through consensus. An ultrasound physician with 10 years of experience in abdominal diagnosis randomly selected 30 cases of ultrasound images of hepatic malignancies, re-segmented the ROIs, and evaluated the inter-rater consistency of segmentation features based on the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). A total of 107 radiomics features were extracted from the ROIs using PyRadiomics software, encompassing first-order features, shape features, and texture features [21]. The texture features were further categorized into: (1) Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM); (2) Gray-Level Run-Length Matrix (GLRLM); (3) Gray-Level Size Zone Matrix (GLSZM); (4) Neighboring Gray-Tone Difference Matrix (NGTDM); (5) Gray-Level Dependence Matrix (GLDM) features (Fig. 2).

Radiomics analysis

Differential feature analysis and cohort splitting

All features were standardized using Z-score normalization, and Wilcoxon test was initially employed to identify high-value features associated with HM. Subsequently, all patients were split into training and internal validation cohorts at a 6:4 ratio based on the random stratified sampling method [22].

Radiomics model development and evaluation based on combined machine learning algorithm strategy

In the training cohort, discriminative models for HM were developed based on these high-value features using 12 machine learning algorithms. The 12 algorithms included least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), Ridge, elastic net (Enet), Stepglm, support vector machine (SVM), glmBoost, linear discriminant analysis (LDA), plsRglm, random forest (RF), gradient boosting machine (GBM), XGBoost, and Naive Bayes (NB). Among these, Enet algorithm comprised 9 variants with alpha parameters ranging from 0.1 to 0.9. The Stepglm algorithm offered three variants: Stepglm [forward], Stepglm [both], and Stepglm [backward]. Within the leave-one-out cross-validation framework, the combined algorithm strategy involved utilizing 11 out of the 12 algorithms for secondary feature selection, while the remaining algorithm was reserved for model development, resulting in a total of 113 combined algorithms (Supplementary materials 1). Only models developed with two or more features were subjected to further analysis. For each model, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was calculated [23]. Machine learning algorithms that demonstrated superior AUC performance in both the training and internal validation cohorts were selected as the optimal models, and accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, negative predictive value, positive predictive value were subsequently calculated.

Inflammation-immunity-related radiomics machine learning model development and evaluation

Univariate analysis was conducted to identify significant features in the inflammation-immunity-related features, and IIM was developed using significant features based on the logistic regression algorithm. Additionally, IIRRM was developed by integrating the identified high-value radiomic features and differential inflammation-immune-related features.

Clinical potential evaluation of different models

Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) method was utilized to interpret feature importance in the IIRRM [24]. Calibration curve analysis and Hosmer-Lemeshow (HL) test were used to evaluate models’ calibration and goodness of fit. Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the net clinical benefit or utility of models.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and data visualization were performed using SPSS software (version 17.0) and R programming language (version 4.4.1). Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and analyzed with the Wilcoxon test. Categorical variables were reported as frequency (percentage) and analyzed with the Chi-square test. AUC values below 0.7 indicate low performance, values between 0.7 and 0.9 indicate moderate performance, and values above 0.9 indicate high performance. The closer the AUC was to 1, the better the performance. Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) method was utilized to interpret feature importance in the IIRRM [24]. Calibration curve analysis and Hosmer-Lemeshow (HL) test were used to evaluate models’ calibration and goodness of fit. Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the net clinical benefit or utility of models. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P < 0.05.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics of cohorts

There were 104 patients with the following pathological type distribution: 53 HCC, 11 iCC, 34 HM, 1 cHCC-iCC, and 5 other types. The primary sources of HM were as follows: 9 colorectal cancer, 8 lung cancer, 6 pancreatic cancer, 3 breast cancer, 3 extrahepatic bile duct cancer, 2 nasopharyngeal carcinoma, 1 gallbladder cancer, 1 renal cancer, and 1 gastric stromal tumor. HM accounted for 32% of the pathological type distribution in the training cohort and 33% in the internal validation cohort. The external validation cohort comprised 45 patients with the following pathological types: 27 HCC, 3 iCC, and 15 HM. The primary origins of HM were: 4 pancreatic cancer, 4 lung cancer, 1 gallbladder cancer, 1 prostate cancer, 1 breast cancer, 1 kidney cancer, 1 ureteral cancer, 1 gastric cancer, 1 uterine cancer. HM accounted for 33% of the pathological type distribution in the external validation cohort. There were no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between the training and two validation cohorts, indicating their suitability for subsequent radiomics analysis (Table 1). Through univariate analysis, PLR [odds ratio (OR) = 1.005, P = 0.01] and PAR (OR = 1.218, P = 0.02) were found to be significantly associated with HM and were thus employed to develop IIM. In the training, validation cohorts, and external validation cohort, IIM achieved AUC values of 0.72, 0.71, and 0.54, respectively.

Radiomics model development and evaluation based on combined machine learning algorithm strategy

With ICC ≥ 0.80 set as the threshold, 90 out of 104 features were identified as exhibiting strong feature consistency and reproducibility (Fig. 3). In the training cohort, the Wilcoxon test identified 15 high-value radiomics features (P < 0.001, Fig. 4A). These 15 features were subsequently fed into 113 algorithmic combinations, resulting 98 discriminative models incorporating two or more features. The three most frequently utilized features were original_glcm_JointEntropy, original_firstorder_MeanAbsoluteDeviation, and original_ngtdm_Contrast (Fig. 4B). The optimal model combined RF for feature selection and GBM for RM development, achieving a mean AUC of 0.76. This model integrated 4 features: original_firstorder_Uniformity, original_glcm_MaximumProbability, original_gldm_GrayLevelVariance, and original_ngtdm_Contrast (Fig. 4C). Performance metrics yielded AUC, sensitivity, and specificity values of 0.77 (95% CI: 0.65–0.87), 0.80, and 0.67 in the training cohort, and 0.82 (95% CI: 0.67–0.92), 0.79, and 0.79 in the internal validation cohort, 0.70 (95% CI: 0.54–0.83), 0.93, and 0.47 in the external validation cohort, respectively. Notably, 68% (67/98) of models exhibited moderate performance (mean AUC > 0.73), indicating consistent feature utilization stability across multiple algorithmic frameworks.

Inflammation-immunity-related radiomics machine learning model development and evaluation

The IIRRM was developed through integration of 4 radiomics features and 2 inflammation-immune indicators, which yielding 83 discriminative models incorporating two or more features. Among these, the GBM algorithm demonstrated optimal performance by utilizing all six features, achieving a mean AUC of 0.80. The optimal model exhibited an AUC of 0.84 (95% CI: 0.73–0.92) with sensitivity of 0.70 and specificity of 0.86 in the training cohort, and 0.82 (95% CI: 0.67–0.92) with sensitivity of 0.93 and specificity of 0.64 in the internal validation cohort, 0.74 (95% CI: 0.59–0.86) with sensitivity of 0.67 and specificity of 0.90 in the external validation cohort (Table 2; Fig. 5). In the training cohort, the overall Shapley values of the GBM model were calculated. In the overall visualization, the SHAP bar plot and beeswarm plot demonstrated the weights of the six most significant features in the model. The position of features on the vertical axis indicated their importance, with a higher position signifying a greater contribution. Interestingly, PAR had the highest weight, followed by four radiomics features, with PLR having the lowest weight. (Fig. 6). The HL test revealed poor agreement between the IIRRM predictions and pathological outcomes in the training cohort (P = 0.04), whereas good agreement was observed in the internal validation cohort (P = 0.85), and the external validation cohort (P = 0.61). It was demonstrated through DCA that the IIRRM could benefit patients within threshold ranges of 7% to 79% in the internal validation cohort and 10% to 59% in the external validation cohort, indicating the IIRRM’s potential for clinical application (Figs. 7 and 8).

Discussion

Ultrasound, serving as the first-line imaging modality for hepatic cancer screening, faces significant challenges in distinguishing HM and their primary origins using conventional two-dimensional visual features. The rapid integration of AI into medical research offers opportunities to refine risk stratification for HM among high-risk patients with space-occupying liver lesions, thereby enabling more precise triage in HM management. In this study, multi-algorithm radiomics ML models derived from ultrasound imaging were employed to evaluate the robustness of HM-associated features. Results demonstrated that 68% of ML models exhibited moderate performance, indicating high diagnostic value of radiomics features for HM identification. Conversely, inflammation-immune features, consistent with prior studies, showed suboptimal discriminatory capacity. Although combining radiomics and inflammation-immune features improved classification performance in the training cohort (AUC = 0.84), no significant enhancement was observed in the internal validation cohort (AUC = 0.82), but there had been an improvement in the external validation cohort (AUC = 0.74). Nevertheless, as one of the few investigations focusing on ultrasound-driven HM detection, this proof-of-concept study underscores the translational promise of AI-driven strategies in optimizing risk stratification for HM within screening protocols.

This study investigated the application of AI in detecting HM and tracing their primary tumor origins. A systematic review of PubMed and CNKI databases identified only 12 AI studies focusing on HM detection and 11 studies addressing primary tumor origin identification, highlighting substantial unexplored potential for AI in HM studies, and particularly in integrating genomic data, which remained completely unexplored [25, 26]. Pathology, as the gold standard for HM diagnosis, has demonstrated remarkable AI-driven advancements. For instance, AI models analyzing histopathological images achieved exceptional HM identification performance (AUC = 0.99), underscoring the transformative potential of AI in HM diagnostics [27]. However, this approach’s reliance on invasive biopsy sampling and complex slide preparation has limited its clinical generalizability, especially in resource-constrained settings. Non-invasive modalities such as CT, MRI, and ultrasound remain pivotal for HM evaluation. Preliminary investigation work revealed that a meta-analysis of HM detection across 2,597 hepatic cancer cases (1,468 HMs) demonstrated an overall AUC of 0.92, with enhanced MRI and CT serving as primary contributors—a performance benchmark surpassing this outcome of this ultrasound study. Notably, AI models exhibited divergent performance in primary tumor tracing: exceptional performance for BLCM but moderate performance for RCLM. The role of given that ultrasound as the frontline screening tool for hepatic cancer, integrating AI into ultrasound-based systems could optimizing HM detection accuracy and enhancing clinical decision-making precision. While the current ultrasound-driven AI models show promise, their performance gaps may reflect limitations in sample size and feature diversity [28].

Inflammation-immune-related features play a significant role in cancer prognosis analysis, yet their potential in HM identification remains underexplored. In this study, univariate analyses identified PLR and PAR as critical high-risk factors for HM. PLR reflects platelet-derived proinflammatory mediators such as cytokines and chemokines, which exacerbate the inflammatory tumor microenvironment [29]. Elevated PLR, which was associated with poorer overall survival and progression-free survival, served as a biomarker for evaluating immunotherapy efficacy in cancer [17]. While platelets are essential for hemostasis, albumin exhibits anticoagulant and anti-inflammatory properties that suppress tumor growth. PAR integrates inflammatory information derived from platelets and albumin. Notably, over 20 studies in PubMed had exclusively utilized PAR for prognostic analyses. For instance, PAR demonstrated predictive value (AUC = 0.69) for overall survival in 118 osteosarcoma cases [30]. This study developed a logistic regression model combining PLR and PAR, achieving moderate diagnostic performance for HM identification. Comparative analysis with existing literature confirms these results are both acceptable and clinically applicable.

The rapid integration of AI into medical research has intensified focus on the generalizability of features and models. Many AI studies employ single-feature selection and model development strategies, which risk overestimating or underestimating predictive performance due to stochastic variability. For example, in predicting EGFR mutations in lung cancer using CT-derived radiomics features, a multicenter study demonstrated superior performance of RF over LR and SVM, whereas a single-center study reported conflicting results favoring SVM [31, 32]. Consequently, multi-algorithm ensemble approaches have emerged to enhance feature robustness and model generalizability. Notably, a 113-algorithm combinatorial framework leveraging ferroptosis-related genes achieved exceptional performance in predicting nonalcoholic steatohepatitis [33]. Similarly, integrating 12 feature selection methods with 10 classifiers improved diagnostic accuracy for stage 4 neuroblastoma using 113 combinatorial models [34]. This study extracted radiomics features from single-center ultrasound images acquired by a single physician with the same ultrasound system. Notably, all images were obtained during ultrasound-guided biopsy procedures, ensuring enhanced alignment between imaging data and histopathological findings. Resampling and the ComBat method are denoising approaches that have been applied in numerous high-quality studies. For instance, a multi-center retrospective study demonstrated that resampling images to isometric voxels increased the number of robust features, and enhancing ComBat correction further elevated the quantity of reproducible features [35]. However, traditional denoising methods also include Gaussian filtering, wavelet transform, median filtering, among others. Many studies have expanded the number of features by extracting them again after denoising. For example, feature extraction after denoising based on wavelet and Laplacian of Gaussian led to an expansion of the feature set to 1,316 features [36]. The advancement of deep learning algorithms has further improved denoising performance. Examples include convolutional neural networks (CNNs), generative adversarial networks (GANs), and autoencoders. A study showed that PADS-Net, a variant of the GAN approach, reduced speckle noise in ultrasound images, achieving a superior edge signal-to-noise ratio and ultimately attaining an AUC of 0.87 in Parkinson’s disease diagnosis [37]. While contemporary radiomics workflows often generate thousands of features [38], only 107 original features from ultrasound images were analyzed, due to substantial variability in feature quantities across image transformation methods, potentially leading to disproportionate amplification of specific feature categories’ weights, such as wavelet features [16, 39]. Wilcoxon test revealed significant differences in over 50% of the original features between HM and PLMC, leading to the selection of 15 features for downstream analysis (P < 0.001). Among 98 algorithm combinations, original_glcm_JointEntropy exhibited the highest utilization frequency, which represented a metric quantifying the randomness of neighborhood intensity values. However, the optimal algorithm model excluded this feature but employed original_firstorder_Uniformity (representing the uniformity of texture intensity), original_glcm_MaximumProbability (indicating the occurrence frequency of texture intensity value pairs), original_gldm_GrayLevelVariance (denoting the variance of gray-level values), and original_ngtdm_Contrast (signifying the contrast of texture intensity). Individually, these features demonstrated limited discriminative power for HM identification. However, their integration via machine learning achieved moderate-to-high diagnostic accuracy in the validation cohort (AUC = 0.82), with 68% of models exhibiting moderate performance, underscoring the diagnostic informativeness of radiomics features.

As a rapidly evolving AI technology, deep learning encompasses algorithmic frameworks such as convolutional neural networks and Vision Transformers. Similar to radiomics, the clinical application of deep learning in HM differentiation requires further exploration, though current research underscores its significant potential for high-performance HM differentiation. For instance, an Inception-v3 deep learning model trained on 364 digital whole-slide images of HM and iCC achieved highly discriminative performance in an external validation cohort [27]. In another example, a U-Net deep learning model trained on 864 CT images of HM and iCC demonstrated a sensitivity of 0.97 in an internal validation cohort [40]. The efficacy of deep learning algorithms is closely tied to the availability of large-scale image datasets; however, the “black-box” nature of deep learning models complicates biological interpretability. Nevertheless, existing radiomics studies have shown comparable performance to deep learning approaches. For example, a LASSO model trained on MRI images of 64 iCC cases and 69 HM cases also achieved high discriminative performance in an internal validation cohort, with an AUC of 0.91 [9]. These results surpass the internal and external validation outcomes of the present study (AUC = 0.82 and 74). However, the primary objective of this study was to investigate the applicability of feature utilization by the model, with a different research focus. As previously mentioned, 67 models exhibited discriminative performance with AUCs exceeding 0.73. The combination of radiomics features with peripheral blood features enhance diagnostic performance. For instance, a hybrid model integrating radiomics and clinical features demonstrated greater clinical net benefit in differentiating benign and malignant bone tumors compared to standalone radiomics or clinical models [41]. In this study, while the integration of radiomics with inflammation-immune biomarkers (PLR/PAR) improved diagnostic accuracy in the training cohort, the validation cohort failed to demonstrate significant improvement, a discrepancy that may be attributable to limited sample size. SHAP analysis quantified feature contributions to model predictions [42], revealing PAR as the highest-ranking contributor, followed by the four radiomics features, with PLR exhibiting the lowest weight. Although the predictive value of radiomics features for different primary tumor origins in HM was analyzed using only a small sample size, moderate predictive performance was achieved, which was considered satisfactory. It was also noted that overfitting may have occurred. In the external validation cohort, the algorithm combinations derived from digestive system tumors showed only 11% AUC values exceeding 0.70, whereas those from respiratory system tumors demonstrated 47% AUC values above 0.70. This observation indicated that radiomics features hold significant potential in identifying the primary tumor origin of HM.

While this study demonstrated the potential of ultrasound radiomics in HM identification, several limitations warranted consideration. First, although the exclusive use of ultrasound-guided images acquired by a single physician with the same ultrasound system, the limited sample size from a single center necessitated further validation of model generalizability across diverse other central cohorts and imaging protocols. In addition, this study did not use SMOTE technology for class imbalance handling. Second, the inclusion of liver lesions without restrictions on size or multiplicity may introduce selection bias. Clinically, solitary large lesions often suggested PLMC, whereas HM typically presented as multifocal small nodules. However, solitary HM cases could mimic PLMC, increasing diagnostic uncertainty. This aligned with prior studies, where late-stage cancer diagnoses often lack pathological confirmation, further restricting case inclusion. Third, reliance on two-dimensional ultrasound images may oversimplify tumor spatial heterogeneity, underscoring the need for three-dimensional reconstruction technologies to capture volumetric tumor features. Fourth, partial evaluation metrics of the model exhibited overfitting phenomena, which necessitated an increase in sample size for further evaluation. Fifth, the integration of radiomics and inflammation-immune features failed to achieve significant improvement in discriminatory performance, a limitation potentially linked to the restricted sample size. Sixth, Both radiomics and deep learning are artificial intelligence technologies, but deep learning algorithms, with their rapid advancements, had gained increasing prominence in the medical field. The future outlook of this study is to investigate the comparative advantages and disadvantages of radiomics and deep learning in HM identification by leveraging larger sample sizes. Seventh, the SHAP method had not conducted specific analyses based on individual samples of HM and PLMC, and future prospective aim to achieve technological breakthroughs in this domain. Eighth, a small sample size was initially employed to explore the predictive value of radiomics features for different primary tumor origins in HM; however, the performance was potentially susceptible to overfitting. Further expansion of the sample size was deemed necessary to obtain more reliable results. Finally, while radiomics features exhibited interpretability in HM discrimination, their biological and clinical relevance remains to be validated through large-scale multi-omics investigations integrating genomic, proteomic, and histopathological data [43].

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study revealed the potential clinical utility of radiomics features and inflammation-immune features in HM identification, highlighting their potential capacity to optimize diagnostic-therapeutic management systems and advance precision therapeutic strategies for HM patients.

Comparative Study of Spinal Cord Toxicity from Dose Per Fraction Delivered to Cervical Spinal Cord in Head-and-neck Cancers by Volumetric-modulated Arc Therapy with Simultaneous Integrated Boost versus Three-dimensional Conformal Radiotherapy in a Murine Model
Authors: Swati Verma, Manoj Kumar Gupta, Shailendra Handu, Shalinee Rao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aim:To assess and compare the radiation-induced effects on the spinal cord of rats following fractionated irradiation with doses of 1.36 Gy and 2.0 Gy per fraction.Materials and Methods:The risk of radiation-induced myelopathy and spinal cord tolerance represent the major dose-limiting factors during re-irradiation of head-and-neck cancers since spinal cord is exposed to maximum tolerance dose of 45 Gy in 1.8–2 Gy per fraction during first radiation treatment. However, modern radiotherapy techniques such as Volumetric-modulated Arc Therapy (VMAT) with simultaneous integrated boost (SIB) usually irradiate spinal cord with much less dose per fraction than 2 Gy per fraction of prescription dose. In this experimental study, the cervical spinal cord of male Wistar rats was irradiated to a total biologically effective dose of 120 Gy2 using different dose-per-fraction, control group was irradiated with 2 Gy per fraction for a total dose of 60 Gy/30F in 6 weeks while experimental group was given 1.36 Gy per fraction for a total dose of 70.72 Gy/52F in 10.5 weeks. The motor function including motor co-ordination and balance, locomotor activity was observed at 1, 3, and 5 months after the completion of radiation and was compared with baseline values.Results:Motor co-ordination and balance were preserved until the 3rd month in both control and experimental groups, with no significant difference from baseline (0 ± 0). Gradual functional deterioration became evident after 3 months and a significant decline was observed at 5 months in both groups, with a greater reduction in the control group (72 ± 2.4) compared to the experimental group (105 ± 2.8) (F = 367.9, P < 0.001). The decline was more pronounced in rats treated with 2 Gy per fraction than in those receiving 1.36 Gy per fraction. Locomotor activity declined progressively after irradiation in both groups. Baseline activity was 388.8 (control group) and 369.7 (experimental group). At 1 month, the control group showed a significantly greater reduction (180.2 ± 8.2) than the experimental group (272.5 ± 12.1), with a significant group and time interaction (F = 110.4, P < 0.001). This difference persisted at 3 months (control group: 130.2 ± 3.6; experimental group: 185.4 ± 4.8; F = 208.7, P < 0.001) and was most pronounced at 5 months (control group: 99.5 ± 1.7; experimental group: 147.4 ± 4.0; F = 610.9, P < 0.001).Conclusions:This study demonstrates that fractionated irradiation of the spinal cord at 1.36 Gy per fraction, as commonly delivered during VMAT SIB, is less damaging in rats compared to the conventional 2 Gy per fraction used in three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy. Motor co-ordination and locomotor activity deterioration were more pronounced in the control group than in the experimental group. These findings suggest that smaller dose-per-fraction schedules may be well within the spinal cord tolerance, thereby potentially enabling safer re-irradiation scenarios.

INTRODUCTION

Volumetric-modulated arc therapy (VMAT) with simultaneous integrated boost (SIB) technique is commonly used for the treatment of head-and-neck cancer patients[1] This technique ensures more conformal and precise radiation dose to the target with minimal doses to the surrounding normal critical structures.[2] Dose per fraction is an important factor for late tissue toxicities in the surrounding normal tissues including critical organs.[3] One such critical organ in head-and-neck radiotherapy is spinal cord which is considered as top priority in radiotherapy planning of head-and-neck region. Spinal cord becomes more important during re-irradiation due to either relapses or second primary in upper aero digestive tract because of field cancerization.[4] In developing countries such as India, re-irradiation is more common as two third of cases present in advanced stages[5] and treated with concurrent chemoradiotherapy. Many patients developed loco-regional relapses and majority of them are not fit for salvage surgery and are treated with re-irradiation or require re-irradiation after salvage surgery. Re-irradiation of spinal cord is a challenging task for radiation oncologists, and many factors need to be considered like time interval between two treatments and previously detailed radiotherapy plan. If we are too conservative for spinal cord doses during re-irradiation, a less than optimum dose will be delivered to the gross tumor volume, compromising the outcome. In the present study hypothesis, we aim to show that the dose per fraction used in the VMAT SIB technique causes less damage to the spinal cord than the damage with conventional fractionation in three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy (3D-CRT).

Radiobiology of fraction size

Linear Quadratic (L-Q) model of cell survival curve[6] explains two types of deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) damage because of interaction between ionizing radiation and cellular material.

Linear damage: This reflects the possibility of damaging the two strands of DNA molecules with single radiation events causing lethal damage resulting into cell death and here the effect is directly proportion to the dose of radiation E = αDQuadratic damage: This shows the probability of damaging two strands of DNA molecules by two separate radiation events, and each radiation event causes sub-lethal damage and then interaction of two sub lethal damage results into cell death. If there is sufficient gap between two radiation events, the sub-lethal damage can be repaired, and cells will behave like a fresh cell with no remnant of radiation injury. Here, the effect is directly proportion to the square of dose of radiation. E = βD2

The total effect will be sum of the two processes, i.e., αD + βD2. Linear kill is predominantly seen in the low-dose region and responsible for shoulder of cell survival curve while quadratic kill is predominantly seen in high-dose region and form the terminal portion of the cell survival curve. Normal tissues have been divided into two categories depending upon the way they respond to fractionation size (dose per fraction) (1) Early reacting tissues and (2) late reacting tissues. Both types have different shapes of cell survival curve and different value of α/β ratio. The α/β ratio[7] is defined as the dose of radiation at which two processes of cell kill (L-Q) become equal. The early reacting tissues have shoulder which is less curvy and average α/β ratio is taken as 10 Gy. Majority of malignant tumors behave like an early reacting tissue having average α/β ratio of 10 Gy and having similar shape of cell survival curve as early reacting normal tissues except breast and prostate cancer. The early responding tissues show less sensitivity to fraction size. Late reacting tissues have an average α/β ratio of 3 Gy and shoulder is curved [Figure 1]. Such tissues are highly sensitive to fraction size.

Effect of fraction size on two types of tissues, i.e., on early reacting and late reacting tissue

Effect of fraction size

Late-responding tissues characterized by a low α/β ratio [Figure 1], an increase in dose per fraction results in a greater biological effect and a corresponding reduction in cell survival fraction (i.e., increased sensitivity to fraction size). This is consistent with the higher risk of late radiation-induced toxicity observed in these tissues. Hence, if fraction size is increased, it will cause more damage to late reacting tissue leading to higher late toxicities. Or, if dose per fraction is reduced, late toxicities will be less thus will spare the late responding tissues. This concept of radiobiology of fraction size indicates that if fraction size to spinal cord is less than conventional fraction size of 2 Gy without reducing the fraction size of 2 Gy to gross tumor, this may increase the tolerance of spinal cord to higher than 45 Gy which is the dose constraint to spinal cord in 1.8–2 Gy per fraction which is usually delivered in 3D-CRT of head-and-neck region. This will have clinical advantage, especially in head-and-neck radiotherapy in two ways.

Hypothesis

Re-irradiation

Radiation-induced myelopathy (RIM) is one of the most severe complications for radiation treatment of cancers in the head and neck, thoracic, and abdominal regions. The human spinal cord was estimated to have a probability of RIM of <5% in 5 years following a dose of 47–50 Gy delivered in a standard 1.8–2 Gy/fraction schedule.[8] The ED₅₀ for RIM represents the dose at which approximately 50% of irradiated subjects develop spinal cord injury. One study[9] shows that inducing radiation myelitis in 50% of irradiated patients is approximately 70 Gy in 2 Gy per fraction. Thus, ED50 for radiation myelitis is 70 Gy. The usual safe dose to spinal cord is considered as 45–46 Gy in 1.8–2 Gy per fraction which is 64% of ED50. Mason et al.[10] showed that spinal cord tolerance to re-irradiation is partially restored over time and that the degree of recovery is strongly dependent on the initial priming dose. As shown in Figure 2, experimental data indicate a steep increase in the residual biological effect when the priming dose exceeds approximately 60% of the ED50 for spinal cord injury. Below this threshold, recovery of spinal cord tolerance is substantially greater, which may reduce the biological impact of subsequent irradiation. Considering a priming dose of 45 Gy delivered at 1.8–2 Gy per fraction (i.e., >60% of the ED50), a higher residual biological effect would be expected, thereby limiting the tolerance of the spinal cord during re-irradiation. Since biologically effective dose (BED) incorporates fraction size, maintaining the spinal cord BED below approximately 60% of the ED50-derived BED may be associated with improved tolerance.

Relationship between priming dose as percentage of ED50 versus remembered dose as percentage of priming dose

In conventional 3D-CRT planning, a shrinking field technique is used. Initially whole neck, including primary is treated with a dose of 44–46 Gy in 22–23 fractions, then spinal cord is omitted from the radiation portal and rest of the doses delivered which is usually 70 Gy in 35 fractions to gross tumor. When BED of spinal cord is calculated, it comes out to be 77 Gy3 which is 66% of BED of ED50 and falls in the steep gradient of the curve [Figure 2] resulting into higher remembered dose by spinal cord at the time of re-irradiation which will force to give lesser dose to spinal cord and inadequate dose to gross tumor resulting into inferior local control. Single SIB plan with VMAT uses 70 Gy in 33 fractions with a dose constraint of 45 Gy to spinal cord. This 45 Gy will be delivered in 33 fractions with 1.36 Gy per fraction. As per radiobiology of fraction size effect on late reacting tissue, lower dose per fraction should be less damaging to spinal cord. The BED of spinal cord in this SIB schedule will be approximately 67 Gy3, which is 57% of BED of ED50 which falls in the shallow potion of the curve [Figure 2] resulting in less remembered dose by spinal cord at the time of re-irradiation and high dose to gross tumor can be delivered safely leading to higher local tumor control.

Dose escalation

Dose escalation is sometime required or due to very close proximity of critical organ with planning target volume PTV like mid brain in advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma, it is not possible to deliver tumoricidal dose to gross tumor. In this clinical scenario, the VMAT SIB technique can be practiced safely as dose per fraction to critical organ will be <2 Gy and will lead to less damage to critical organ leading to safe delivery of tumoricidal dose to the gross tumor.

It is hypothesized that the spinal cord exposed to a lower dose per fraction (1.36 Gy) will exhibit reduced radiobiological damage and lower late toxicity compared with the same tissue exposed to 2.0 Gy per fraction, despite equivalent BEDs.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Thirty-six Adult Male Wistar rats aged 3 months and weighing 150–200 g were approved by the Institutional Animal ethics committee, and all experiments were performed in accordance with committee for control and supervision of experiments on animals recommendations and guidelines. Eighteen rats were taken in the control group with dose per fraction 2.0 Gy and other 18 rats were taken in the experimental group with fraction dose of 1.36 Gy. Both control and experimental groups are further divided into three subgroups based on the timing of the reporting of the outcome with six rats in each control and experimental subgroups. The outcome was reported I) after 1 month (first subgroup), II) after 3 months (second subgroup) and III) after 5 months (third subgroup), postradiation. Rats were housed in laboratory animal facility, three rats per cage, fed on standard rodent diet and water ad lib. For the identification of group and subgroup, tags were attached in each cage. Rats were marked for identification in both groups of study. Baseline values of all rats were taken for the motor coordination and balance and locomotor activity before radiation treatment.

Statistical analysis

Interaction between the control versus experimental group and time (baseline versus follow-up intervals) was assessed using the two-way repeated analysis of variance 95% Confidence level for analysis and α = 0.05 was used to determine the statistical significance.

Dosimetry and radiation dose calculations

Fractionated dose of 2.0 Gy per fraction with total dose 60 Gy (BED 120 Gy2) in the control group and 1.36 Gy per fraction with total dose 70.72 Gy (BED 120 Gy2) in the experimental group were planned. Radiation was delivered to 1.5 cm × 1.5 cm segment of cervical spinal cord from the first cervical vertebra (C1) to the first thoracic vertebra (T1) at depth of 3.5 cm using single anterior direct field with 6MV X-ray photos on ™ Linear Accelerator (Elekta Pvt Ltd, Stockholm, Sweden) in Radiation Oncology Department of Institute. Computed tomography simulation was performed using an Optima CT580W Simulator (GE Healthcare, Waukesha) with exposure parameter of 120KV and 80 mA [Figure 3a]. Monitor unit calculation was done in Monaco treatment planning system and calculated MUs for control and experimental groups were 289.7 and 197.0, respectively. Radiation plan delivered through Mosaiq treatment delivery system with customized restrainers. Isocenter of machine is kept at skin surface on the marked area [Figure 3b]. Radiation was delivered from Monday to Friday every week and 30 fractions in the control group for 6 weeks. The experimental group was given 52 fractions of 1.36 Gy for 10.5 weeks.

(a) Treatment depth measurement on computed tomography scout image, (b) Linear Accelerator (LINAC) treatment setup for radiation delivery

Outcome measurement

Motor coordination and balance were recorded by Rota rod apparatus which shows the ability to maintain the balance and co-ordination on the rotating rod and the result was taken as latency of fall in seconds which was recorded. Rats were acclimatized to the Rota rod apparatus for 1 week before baseline testing with 3RPM for 20 s in the forward direction per session. Baseline readings were taken at 10 RPM for 120 s. Spontaneous locomotor activity was checked in open field Orchid infrared act photometer equipped with 32 IR cells with 16 cells in x-axis and 16 cells in the y-axis, with a cut time set 3 min and instrument control panel display number of beams broken by the animal movement on both directions (X, Y).

Assessments of damage were recorded every 2 weeks postradiation until set observation period time completed, when irradiated rats showed signs of sickness assessment was extended daily. Change in fall of time (in Rota rod) and percentage reduction in locomotor activity (IR act photometer) from baseline values in each group were calculated and difference in this parameter between control and experimental groups were also recorded.

RESULTS

Mortality

A total of five rats died during the study period. In the experimental group (1.36 Gy per fraction), one rat died during the posttreatment observation period after 105 days in the third subgroup, with no deaths occurring during treatment. In contrast, four rats died in the control group (2.0 Gy per fraction), including three deaths during treatment after the 3rd, 11th, and 27th fractions, respectively, and one death from the third subgroup during the observation period at 130 days.

Motor coordination and balance

At 1–3 months, both the experimental and control groups maintained consistent level of motor coordination and balance in [Figure 4a and b; 0 ± 0; Table 1]. An increased tendency to fall was seen after 130 days’ postradiation treatment, However, at 5 months, the tendency to fall increased by 38% in the control group (2 Gy per fraction) and by 13% in the experimental group (1.36 Gy per fraction) from baseline values, reflecting a lesser effect of 1.36 Gy per fraction on the spinal cord compared to 2 Gy per fraction [Figure 4c]. The difference between groups at 5 months was statistically significant, with the control group showing greater impairment (72 ± 2.4) compared to the experimental group (105 ± 2.8) [F = 367.9, P < 0.001; Table 1].

Locomotor activity assessment

Locomotor activity was observed to decrease progressively at 1, 2, and 5 months in both control and experimental groups. However, the reduction in locomotor activity was relatively greater in rats control group compared to those exposed to experimental group. Compared with baseline values, the percentage reduction in locomotor activity in the control group was 52%–53%, 65%–68%, and 75%–76% at 1, 3, and 5 months, respectively. In the experimental group, the percentage reduction was 25%–32%, 52%–56%, and 61%–63% at 1, 3, and 5 months, respectively, from baseline [Figure 5].

DISCUSSION

The cervical spinal cord of adult male Wistar rats aged 3 months and weighing 150–200 g were irradiated with two dose fraction size, i.e., 2 Gy and 1.36 Gy per fraction, 5 days a week for a total dose of 60 Gy in 30 fraction in 6 weeks in the control group and 70.72 Gy in 52 fractions in 10.5 weeks in the experimental group. The total BED was however same in both the groups and was 120 Gy2. Rats of both groups were observed for change in motor coordination and balance and assessment of locomotor activity. The difference in both parameters was compared with the baseline values. The difference in parameters from baseline values was also compared between control and experimental groups.

The effect of radiation on motor coordination and balance was not seen until 3 months after the completion of radiation in both groups. However, at 5 months, motor coordination and balance was decreased by 38% in 2 Gy arm and 13% in 1.36 Gy arm which suggest that 2 Gy per fraction, which is usually delivered to spinal cord in 3D-CRT of head-and-neck region causes more damage to spinal cord as compare to 1.36 Gy per fraction, which is usually delivered to spinal cord in VMAT with SIB. The difference between control and experimental groups in motor coordination and balance is 28.73% in favor of 1.36 Gy arm at 5 months. The effect of radiation damage to spinal cord resulting in reduction in locomotor activity is seen much earlier as compared to motor coordination and balance. The reduction in locomotor activity is seen right from 1st month after the completion of radiation treatment and continues to reduce further through 3rd and 5th months in both control and experimental groups. At 1st month, the locomotor activity reduction was 24% higher in the control group as compared to experimental group, at 3 months the difference was 12.50% and at 5 months, it was 14.0% higher in the control group than in the experimental group. The deterioration in locomotor activity was more pronounced and much steeper in control group as compared to experimental group, as shown in Figure 6.

In Retrospective study by Lavey et al.[11] where thoracolumbar cord of mice was irradiated with total radiation dose of 24–70 Gy with Fractionation: 2.0 Gy once a day; 1.2 Gy once a day; 1.2 Gy twice a day by 250 kV X-rays using 0.2 mm Cu and 2.0 mm Al filtration, followed by 15 Gy single fraction top-up dose delivered 24 h after the completion of fractionated irradiation. All mice observed till they got paralyzed. This study showed that there was no significant difference in dose paralysis curves between once a day and twice a day schedule, however spinal cord tolerance was highest with 1.2 Gy once a day as compared to 2.0 Gy once a day and 1.2 Gy twice a day fractionation. Ang et al.[12] concluded that small but significant rise in tolerance when dose per fraction was decreased from 2 Gy to 1 Gy. Rat Model used in study cervical spinal cord was irradiated with 18 MeV photon with Top-up dose15 Gy 26–41 fractions (2 Gy once daily) and 58–88 fractions (1 Gy twice daily). Overall treatment time kept 4–6 weeks’ neurological status was checked, and endpoint was considering the incidence of paralysis. Lo et al.[13] seen the effects of fractionated X-ray irradiation on mouse spinal cord (T9–L5). Fractions: 2, 3, 4, 6, 10, and 20 with daily dose 2–24 Gy along with single dose 12–52 Gy. Neurological damage for spinal cord was recorded based on incidence of paralysis and latent time of paralysis as seen by histopathology. The findings showed that the spinal cord is highly sensitive to fraction size and further observed that smaller fractions promote better tissue repair and prolong the latent period of injury. Stewart et al.[14] Concluded that single high-dose irradiation produces greater spinal cord damage over a short duration in rat model, cervical spinal cord exposed to a single 25 Gy dose of 100 kV X-rays. Histopathology assessment performed at 93 days to 107 days postradiation found blood-spinal cord barrier breakdown and later white matter injury. According to Jin et al.,[15] the α/β ratios were derived, respectively, for rat (Group A), large animal (Group B), patient (Group C), and combined data (Group D). The study showed that all four groups had similar α/β ratios close to 3.9 Gy, suggesting that the spinal cord has a similar fractionation effect for different species, including human beings.

These findings reinforce that using lower doses per fraction is important for preserving spinal cord function and minimizing the risk of neurological complications. This experiment suggests that lower dose per fraction (1.36 Gy) to spinal cord may offer better preservation of spinal cord motor function compared to conventional dose per fraction (2.0 Gy) despite equivalent BEDs.

Clinical implication

VMAT with SIB in head-and-neck cancers result in lower dose per fraction delivered to spinal cord causing less damage and RIM as compared to 3D-CRT. Maximum physical dose delivered to the spinal cord in typical clinical head and neck (usually 45 Gy), BED is much less than tolerant BED of spinal cord in case of VMAT with SIB. This might help in future in case of relapse or second cancer in upper aero-digestive tract which need re-irradiation in two ways.

CONCLUSIONS

Less than 2 Gy per fraction dose to spinal cord of rat results in less damage to spinal cord leading to lower toxicities in terms of motor coordination and balance as well as locomotor activity. The findings of this study will have an important clinical implication during re-irradiation of head-and-neck cancer patients.

Limitation

In this study, gross functional tests were used for spinal cord damage observation due to the lack of advance imaging and assessment equipment for animals. Subclinical cord injury might have been missing in this study for more sensitive assessment.

Utilizing radiomics techniques to isolate a single vertebral body from chest CT for opportunistic osteoporosis screening
Authors: Xiaocong Lin, Rongkai Shen, Xiaoling Zheng, Shaojian Shi, Zhangsheng Dai, Kaibin Fang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeOpportunistic osteoporosis screening, conducted during routine medical examinations such as chest computed tomography (CT), presents a potential solution for early detection. This study aims to investigate the feasibility of utilizing radiomics technology based on chest CT images to screen for opportunistic osteoporosis.MethodsThis Study is a Multicenter Retrospective Investigation. Relevant clinical data, including demographics and DXA results, would be collected for each participant. The radiomics analysis in this study focuses on the extraction of features from the 11th or 12th thoracic vertebral bodies from chest CT images. SVM machine learning models would be trained using these radiomic features, with DXA results as the ground truth for osteoporosis classification.ResultsIn the training group, Clinical models had an accuracy of 0.684 and an AUC of 0.744, Radiomics models had an accuracy of 0.828 and an AUC of 0.896, Nomogram models had an accuracy of 0.839 and an AUC of 0.901. In the internal validation group, Clinical models had an accuracy of 0.769 and an AUC of 0.829, Radiomics models had an accuracy of 0.832 and an AUC of 0.892, Nomogram models had an accuracy of 0.839 and an AUC of 0.918. In the external validation group, Clinical models had an accuracy of 0.715 and an AUC of 0.741, Radiomics models had an accuracy of 0.777 and an AUC of 0.796, Nomogram models had an accuracy of 0.785 and an AUC of 0.807. In all three datasets, the Nomogram model exhibited a statistically significant difference in screening effectiveness compared to the clinical models.ConclusionOur research demonstrates that by leveraging radiomics features extracted from a single thoracic spine using chest CT, and incorporating these features with patient basic information, opportunistic screening for osteoporosis can be achieved.

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a silent disease characterized by low bone mass and structural deterioration, leading to an increased risk of fractures [1]. Osteoporosis is directly associated with osteoporosis fractures [2]. These fractures can result in acute or chronic pain, disability, a significant reduction in the quality of life for patients, and in severe cases, can even increase the risk of death [3]. This condition places a tremendous burden on both the healthcare system and the social security system [4]. Timely diagnosis and treatment have been extensively proven to significantly reduce the risk of osteoporotic fractures [5]. Multiple imaging technologies are crucial for the accurate diagnosis and treatment of osteoporosis (OP). One such technology is dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), which measures the area bone mineral density (aBMD) [6]. By assessing aBMD, DXA helps healthcare professionals evaluate bone health and determine the severity of OP [7]. While DXA is considered the gold standard for disease diagnosis, its accessibility remains limited in developing countries. This is mainly due to its high cost and the fact that it is only available in specialized hospitals [8]. Some clinical guidelines also discourage the use of DXA as the sole method for osteoporosis (OP) screening [9].

The FRAX® fracture risk prediction tool, the most widely used non-invasive osteoporotic fracture risk assessment tool, predicts an AUC (Area Under Curve) of 0.69–0.71 [10− 11]. While this screening performance is already quite commendable, it remains somewhat unsatisfactory. To further promote the screening of osteoporosis, some scholars propose exploring the potential of opportunistic screening combined with artificial intelligence as a solution [12]. Opportunistic screening for osteoporosis involves integrating osteoporosis screening into routine medical and health services, leveraging patient medical treatment or physical examination data [13]. This approach is crucial for early diagnosis of osteoporosis. The increasing use of chest CT scans as a diagnostic tool for various chest conditions presents an opportunity to leverage existing data for osteoporosis screening [14]. For certain individuals, such as those over 50 with a long history of smoking and a family history of lung cancer, regular chest CT scans are recommended [11]. Moreover, risk factors for lung cancer, including advanced age, smoking, and being an Asian woman, have also been associated with osteoporosis. Thus, opportunistic osteoporosis screening through chest CT scans can offer substantial benefits to many patients with underlying osteoporosis [15]. Currently, numerous studies have utilized chest CT scans to detect opportunistic osteoporosis [16]. These studies primarily focused on analyzing all thoracic vertebrae in the chest CT, while some scholars additionally utilized the three farthest thoracic vertebrae for screening purposes [17–18]. The AUC for opportunistic osteoporosis screening including all thoracic vertebrae on chest CT was 0.831, while the AUC for screening focusing on the bottom three vertebrae on chest CT was 0.86–0.90.

Although these studies achieved positive outcomes, there are still some limitations that could hinder the applicability of the research findings to all patients undergoing chest CT examinations. Firstly, patients who have undergone previous thoracic spine surgeries with internal fixation may experience measurement errors due to artifacts caused by the fixation. Secondly, a significant number of osteoporosis patients frequently encounter thoracic compression fractures. These fractures can significantly alter the spinal structure, thereby modifying the morphology of the region of interest (the entire thoracic spine) utilized for extracting radiomic features [19]. This alteration can lead to biased outcomes. While compression fractures of the thoracic spine commonly indicate the presence of osteoporosis, it’s important to note that these fractures may not always be entirely caused by osteoporosis alone [20]. A recent study conducted by authors examined the screening efficacy of radiomics techniques, specifically analyzing a single vertebral body through chest CT, to detect opportunistic osteoporosis.

Materials and methods

Participants in the study and development of clinical models

This study is a retrospective analysis comprising a total of 930 patients within the time frame of January 2017 to December 2022. The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) being over the age of 18, and (2) completing both chest CT and DXA scans within a one-month period. The exclusion criteria were: (1) severe scoliosis, (2) extensive compression fractures in the 11th and 12th thoracic vertebrae, or significant internal fixation artifacts that prevented the accurate delineation of the area of interest. Out of these participants, 688 were sourced from a prominent regional medical center and were divided into a training group and an internal testing group in an 8:2 ratio. This grouping method is random. At the time of grouping, all patients were randomly assigned numbers, which were then randomly allocated to the training group and the test group in an 8:2 ratio. Furthermore, an additional 242 patients from two other major regional medical centers were included as part of the external testing group. All individuals underwent both chest CT imaging and dual energy X-ray examination within the same month. Prior to commencing the study, appropriate ethical approval was obtained from the Hospital Institutional Review Board, ensuring compliance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. It is important to note that the gold standard for accurately diagnosing osteoporosis is the dual energy X-ray absorption (DXA) technique, whereby a T-value of -2.5 or below signifies the presence of osteoporosis [21]. Patient demographic information is retrieved from a dedicated registration and appointment system, while the PACS system is utilized to gather patient imaging data. We conducted comparisons of the clinical characteristics of the patients by means of independent-samples t test and chi-square test. The basic information of all patients is presented in Table 1. Image preprocessing

The patient’s chest CT image was initially preprocessed due to the presence of multiple wards and CT machines from different brands at the research hospital. These preprocessing steps primarily involved image standardization and normalization, particularly adjusting the window width, window level, and image resolution for consistency. Python encoding was utilized to complete this processing. The axial images of chest CT were selected for analysis in this study. The reconstruction of images and delineation of the region of interest (ROI) were performed using ITK SNAP software [22]. The drawing of ROI area is manually completed. In this study, radiomics feature extraction is concentrated on the twelfth thoracic vertebrae of these patients. If measurements of the twelfth thoracic vertebrae are not feasible, then the eleventh thoracic vertebrae are selected instead. The purpose of this approach is to minimize any possible deviations.The thoracic and lumbar regions are particularly prone to mobility and stress, making them a frequent location for osteoporotic fractures [23]. The images of these two vertebral bodies can be visualized on chest CT images. Anatomical markers, such as the twelfth rib, the lower edge of the scapula, and the seventh cervical spine spinous process, were utilized to identify the segment of the thoracic spine. The process of outlining ROI

A orthopedic doctor with ten years of work experience specializes in outlining ROI areas. Intra- and inter-observer variability was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Intraclass Correlation Coefficients (ICCs) are frequently employed to assess the consistency of image features. The author initially randomly selected 30 images, after which another image segmentation specialist delineated the regions of interest at two distinct time points. Ultimately, image features exhibiting high consistency were selected based on ICCs greater than 0.9. After one researcher delineated the region of interest (ROI), another researcher, with over 10 years of experience in orthopedic medicine, randomly selected thirty cases and re-delineated the ROI. Both researchers were blind to each other’s delineation results. The ICC was calculated using the thirty cases with delineated ROIs to evaluate the level of agreement. Radiomic features with ICC values greater than 0.9 for both datasets were considered reliable and were selected for further analysis.

Radiomics feature extraction

The feature extraction process was performed utilizing the Pyradiomics Module (https://github.com/Radiomics/pyradiomics). To enhance the number of derived images, various filters, such as Laplacian of Gaussian filters and wavelets, were employed. All radiomics features were classified into seven categories, namely: (i) shape-based features; (ii) first-order features; (iii) gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM) features; (iv) gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features; (v) neighboring gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM) features; (vi) gray-level run-length matrix (GLRLM) features; and (vii) gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features.

Feature selection

The radiomic features of the patients in the training group were screened and then used to establish a radiomic-based screening model for opportunistic osteoporosis. The extracted radiomic features were initially archived in CSV format. These features were regularized using the z test, and then screened by the t-test with significance level p < 0.05. To ensure the selection of statistically robust features, any features with an intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC) below 0.9 are omitted. This meticulous process greatly reduces the number of features while maintaining their predictive effect. To mitigate multicollinearity, Pearson correlation analysis was performed to carefully examine the relationships between features. Correlation coefficients are calculated to identify feature pairs with values ≥ 0.9 or ≤-0.9. In the case of high correlation, only features with superior diagnostic performance are retained, thus avoiding redundancy. The maximum correlation minimum redundancy (mRMR) algorithm is used to select the top 20 most informative features [24]. Then, the minimum absolute contraction and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression is applied to further refine the feature set [25].

Clinical signature

The training dataset underwent preliminary univariate analysis to identify clinical factors with a p-value less than 0.05, indicating their statistical significance. These selected clinical factors were subsequently utilized in the multivariate analysis.

Machine learning models and nomograms

The Support Vector Machine (SVM) is utilized in the identification of opportunistic osteoporosis through the integration of clinical and radiomics features [26]. A nomogram [27] would be constructed by combining these features, and employed in the screening process for opportunistic osteoporosis using the SVM. The effectiveness of the nomogram screening, measured by the AUC (Area Under the Curve), will be compared to the efficacy of utilizing clinical and radiomics features independently for opportunistic osteoporosis screening.

Statistical analysis

The baseline data of the patients were analyzed using statistical software packages, including SPSS (version 20.0) and Python. Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. The distribution of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test [28], and the homogeneity of variances among continuous variables was evaluated using the Levene test [29]. Inter-group differences were compared using Student’s t-test for normally distributed variables, while categorical variables were analyzed using either the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The author utilized the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) of the subjects as the AUC. The performance of predictive models was evaluated using the Area Under the Curve (AUC), and the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the AUC was calculated using the bootstrap method with 1000 intervals. To compare the AUCs of different models, the DeLong testing method was employed, allowing for a statistical assessment of the differences in performance metrics between the models [30]. DCA curves and DCA calibration curves were also used to evaluate the efficacy and clinical usefulness of the model. DCA curves and DCA calibration curves are an easy way to evaluate clinical predictive models, diagnostic tests, and molecular markers [31]. While traditional diagnostic test indicators such as sensitivity, specificity and area under ROC curve only measure the diagnostic accuracy of the predictive model and fail to consider the clinical utility of a specific model, the advantage of DCA is that it integrates the preferences of patients or decision makers into the analysis to further meet the practical needs of clinical decision making [32].

Discussion

The findings from this study suggest that integrating a radiological feature model derived from chest CT scans with gender and age data can form a Nomogram model for identifying opportunistic osteoporosis. The accuracy of this Nomogram model in screening for osteoporosis remains consistently high, with values around 0.8 in both the training set, internal validation group, and external validation group. Additionally, the area under the curve (AUC) for screening exceeds 0.8 in all three group. Our research findings offer additional support for utilizing chest CT in opportunistically screening for osteoporosis. By targeting a single vertebral body as the research focus, potential errors in screening results stemming from factors like internal fixation artifacts and abnormal vertebral body morphology can be minimized. Additionally, the findings of this study indicate that patients with intrathoracic fixation or experience multiple vertebral compression changes can also undergo osteoporosis screening using chest CT, as long as their chest CT includes a complete thoracic vertebral body.

Our study’s efficacy is comparable to that of previous research that utilized chest CT for the screening of opportunistic osteoporosis. Previous studies have reported the use of the Area Under the Curve (AUC) for osteoporosis screening, achieving an AUC of 0.864, and others have utilized three vertebral bodies for screening, achieving an AUC of 0.86 [19, 34]. In our study, the efficacy of the composite model generated by combining patient age and gender with extracted radiomics features from a single vertebral body for screening osteoporosis reached 0.80–0.89, which is similar to the efficacy of the aforementioned studies.

In our study, the clinical model was constructed based on age and gender. Advanced age and menopause have long been recognized as risk factors for osteoporosis, with older women facing a greater likelihood of developing the condition [34–35]. In the clinical model developed by the author, elderly women are identified as a high-risk population for osteoporosis. The initial screening for osteoporosis using this clinical model has shown promising results. Notably, no significant statistical difference was observed between the efficacy of the clinical models alone and the radiomics models in screening for osteoporosis, as evidenced by the internal and external validation groups. This outcome could possibly be attributed to the fact that the majority of patients recommended for chest CT examination are elderly individuals, who also happen to be at a higher risk for developing osteoporosis.

However, when the author integrated clinical information and radiomics features to create the Nomogram model, the effectiveness of the model in screening for osteoporosis exhibited a considerable improvement. Notably, in the training group, internal validation group, and external validation group, the Nomogram model consistently outperformed the clinical model in screening for osteoporosis, with statistically significant differences observed. Gender and age, which are easily obtainable basic patient information, are also recognized risk factors for osteoporosis. Therefore, utilizing the Nomogram model for osteoporosis screening proves to be highly feasible.Our study provides a substantial basis for the screening of opportunistic osteoporosis through chest CT. This method proves to be advantageous as it does not require any additional equipment, time, or cost. In China, for instance, the cost of a chest CT scan in a tertiary hospital is approximately $27, while a bone density test amounts to $25. By conducting preliminary screenings for osteoporosis using chest CT, it is possible to save on additional medical expenses by identifying high-risk patients who require further examination.

Researchers have also utilized a single thoracic segment derived from chest CT to screen for osteoporosis. Specifically, they have employed HU (Hounsfield units) values from the fourth, seventh, and tenth thoracic vertebrae as a means of conducting this screening. Obtaining HU values through manual delineation can introduce potential errors, mainly caused by the presence of bone cortex. The author chose to use all parts of a single thoracic vertebral body, including cortical and cancellous bone, as the object for extracting radiological features. The delineation process was also semi-automatic, which helped to mitigate errors that may arise from manual delineation. To minimize the impact of osteoporosis drug usage, participants with a gap of more than 1 month between DXA and chest CT scans were excluded from the study. Additionally, the data collectors and analysts were kept unaware of the grouping of osteoporosis participants, effectively preventing any artificial subjective errors.

There are some limitations in this study. Firstly, the vertebral body segmentation is accomplished through a combination of manual and semi-automatic delineation, introducing potential bias. Furthermore, there are significantly more patients with osteoporosis than without osteoporosis among the study participants. This observation may be attributed to the fact that the majority of patients who undergo the suggested chest CT examination belong to the elderly population, which is prone to developing osteoporosis. At the same time, the study was limited to chest scans and did not include abdominal scans, the DXA method for diagnosing osteoporosis has known limitations and reading noise may influence the results, biases in the internal and external validation sets may hamper generalizability of these results in other clinical centers, the sample size of the external validation set provides limited statistical power and further sensitivity analyses are warranted to investigate potential model biases.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our research demonstrates that by leveraging radiomics features extracted from a single thoracic spine using chest CT, and incorporating these features with patient basic information, opportunistic screening for osteoporosis can be achieved.

pH-responsive 2D niobium carbide nanosheets for targeted circPUM1 siRNA delivery in ovarian cancer therapy
Authors: Xue Guan, Li-li Wang, Zhuo Yang, Yang Wang, Yang Zhao, Danbo Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Ovarian cancer progression is closely associated with tumor microenvironment (TME) dysregulation, particularly pathological angiogenesis driven by exosome-mediated crosstalk. Here, we elucidate that ovarian cancer-derived circPUM1 promotes angiogenesis by transferring to vascular endothelial cells via exosomes. Mechanistically, circPUM1 upregulates the expression of RAB27B and VEGFA by sponging miR-607, thus boosting release of exosome facilitated by RAB27B and directly activating VEGF signaling in endothelial cells to foster angiogenesis. To disrupt this circPUM1-driven TME modulation, we engineered an innovative pH-responsive 2D niobium carbide nanosheets loaded with circPUM1 siRNA. Through excessive PEI grafting, we functionalized the nanosheets with cationic property, achieving efficient negative-charged siRNA loading. Further surface PEGylation shielded the nanosheets’ positive charge, reducing off-target effect and systemic toxicity, while acidic TME triggered PEG exfoliation for tumor-specific circPUM1 siRNA delivery. Functional cellular assays and an intraperitoneal tumor-bearing mouse model validated that the nanosheet-delivered circPUM1 siRNA effectively inhibited angiogenesis and peritoneal dissemination by knocking down circPUM1 expression and subsequently downregulating its downstream targets. This study uncovers a novel exosome-mediated angiogenesis mechanism and develops innovative MXene nanosheets for pH-responsive siRNA delivery providing a promising strategy for ovarian cancer precision therapy with significant clinical translational value and application potential.

Introduction

Epithelial ovarian carcinoma (EOC) is a common gynecological malignancy with the highest mortality rate and an increasing trend of incidence globally [1]. Notably, angiogenesis plays a pivotal role in tumor growth and progression, which correlates with aggressive cancer cell behavior and poor survival outcome in ovarian cancer [2]. Based on this, Bevacizumab was approved in 2012 as the first anti-angiogenic targeted therapy for ovarian cancer, and has become a pan-target drug against vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in various types of cancers [3,4]. However, its pan-target effect is the main reason for therapeutic limitations and adverse effects. Therefore, the development of innovative delivery systems capable of precisely targeting tumor-specific angiogenic pathways is critical to overcome these challenges.

Emerging investigations reveal that exosome-mediated crosstalk between tumor cells and stromal components drives the remodeling of tumor microenvironment through premetastatic niche formation and angiogenic signaling cascades for metastatic dissemination [[5], [6], [7]]. Our previous study [8] revealed that circular RNA PUM1 (has_circ_0000043, circPUM1) was naturally highly expressed in ovarian carcinoma and significantly enhances ovarian cancer cellular proliferation, migration and invasive abilities. More importantly, we demonstrated that ovarian cancer cell-derived exosomal circPUM1 promoted intraperitoneal dissemination through acting on peritoneal mesothelial cells to induce mesothelial-to-mesenchymal transition [8]. In this study, we further investigated a strong positive correlation between the expression level of circPUM1 and microvessel density (MVD) in tumor microenvironment (TME) of ovarian cancer. These findings suggest that circPUM1 plays a pivotal role in modulating angiogenesis, which is a promising therapeutic target for novel anti-angiogenic strategy.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) has emerged as a pivotal strategy in precision cancer therapy by specifically targeting and silencing gene expression [9,10]. Currently, commonly used siRNA delivery systems include liposomes [11], cationic polymers [12], biomimetic nanocarriers [13], etc. However, there are problems such as low transfection efficiency, high toxicity, lack of therapeutic target specificity and poor physiological stability caused by electrostatic interactions with serum proteins [[14], [15], [16], [17]], restricting their in vivo therapeutic applications. Two-dimensional nanomaterials (e.g., graphene oxide [18], MXenes [19,20]) have recently emerged as promising candidates for gene delivery, owing to their high specific surface area which promotes cellular internalization. Moreover, their superior surface modifiability enables rational functionalization to improve stability and target specificity in systemic circulation [[21], [22], [23], [24]]. These properties position them as a promising candidate for developing gene delivery vectors in vivo.

In this study, we developed a pH-responsive two-dimensional nanoplatform to deliver circPUM1 siRNA. This nanoplatform is based on Mxene-Nb2C with surface polyethyleneimine (PEI) modification to impart positive charge, enhancing siRNA loading and cellular uptake efficiency. Schiff base-mediated PEG grafting achieves shielding and stabilization during circulation. TME further triggers charge reversal significantly enhancing cellular internalization and facilitating efficient siRNA delivery to tumor cells (see Fig. 1). This targeted delivery system specifically knocks down circPUM1, which is highly expressed in tumor microenvironment, thereby inhibiting tumor angiogenesis. It overcomes the limitations associated with conventional chemotherapy and anti-angiogenic agents, including high costs, poor targeting specificity, low drug-loading capacity, and significant systemic side effects in clinical applications [[25], [26], [27]]. This innovative approach represents a promising strategy for precision therapy in ovarian cancer, demonstrating significant potential for clinical translation.

Materials and methods

Materials

Nb2AlC powder was purchased from Beijing Forsman Scientific Co., Ltd. HF aqueous solution was purchased from Shanghai Pharmaceutical Holding Co., Ltd. TPAOH was obtained from J&K Scientific Ltd. CircPUM1 siRNA sequences were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Hairpin-it™ microRNA qPCR quantitation kit, biotinylated DNA probes targeting the circular junction sequence of circPUM1 and miR-607 mimics were synthesized by GenePharma (Suzhou, China). CCK-8 reagent, fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC), propidium iodide (PI), Dual Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit and 4 × Hifair® Ⅲ SuperMix plus were purchased from Yeasen Biotechnology. Azide 594 Click Additive Solution, Hoechst 33342 solution, Lenti-EF1α-Luc-T2A-Puro lentivirus were purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology. SYBR Green qPCR master mix system was purchased from Selleck Chemicals LLC. D-Luciferin was purchased from GlpBio Technology Inc.

Ovarian cancer tissue specimens

57 cases of epithelial ovarian carcinoma were obtained from China Medical University approved by China Medical University Ethics Committee. All participating patients signed informed consent and underwent primary surgery without any prior anticancer therapies. All tumor specimens were frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent RNA extraction with Trizol reagent and fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde (PFA) for immunohistochemical staining of CD31.

Synthesis of PEGylated cationic Nb2C nanosheets

Nb2AlC powder (5 g) was immersed in 70 mL of 50 % HF and stirred at room temperature for 2–3 days. The resulting black precipitate was centrifuged and washed with water/ethanol for four times, then dispersed in 70 mL of TPAOH (25 %) and stirred continuously for another 2–3 days. The centrifuged product was washed with water/ethanol for four times to obtain the target Nb2C nanosheets. The Nb2C nanosheets were mixed with branched PEI in deionized water at a mass ratio of 1:1, stirred at room temperature for 2 h, and then centrifuged three times to obtain P-Nb2C. The P-Nb2C sheets were then mixed with CHO-PEG-CHO at a mass ratio of 2:1 in PBS (pH 7.4), stirred at room temperature for 2 h, and then centrifuged three times to yield P-Nb2C-PEG.

Characterization of Nb2C nanosheets

The morphology and composition of Nb2C were characterized using transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-2100F) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Magellan-400 field emission microscope). The thickness of Nb2C was determined using atomic force microscopy (AFM; Dimension ICON, Bruker), and quantified using NanoScope Analysis system. The niobium (Nb) content was quantified by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES, Agilent 725, Agilent Technologies, USA). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) and zeta potential measurements were performed with Malvern system.

Cell culture and transfection

Human ovarian cancer cell line (OVACR-3, RRID: CVCL_0465) and human umbilical vein endothelial cells (RRID: CVCL_0295) were authenticated by STR profiling and confirmed to be contamination-free. The cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium enriched with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) and a penicillin/streptomycin antibiotic mixture at a concentration of 100 U/mL. Cellular incubation was performed under standard conditions at 37 °C with 5 % carbon dioxide supplementation.

For the establishment of a circPUM1-overexpressing cellular model, OVCAR3 cells with naturally low circPUM1 expression were selected for stable genetic modification. The transfection procedure involved the pLCDH-circPUM1 plasmid, which was introduced into the cells following the standardized protocol of Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent. Following transfection, successful transformants were isolated through antibiotic selection, maintaining cells in medium supplemented with 2 μg/mL puromycin.

In the siRNA-mediated gene silencing experiments, sequence-specific siRNAs were designed to target the back-splice junction sites of circPUM1, thereby disrupting its circular configuration.

CCK8 assay

For cell viability assessment, a standardized protocol was implemented using 96-well culture plates with an initial seeding density of 2000 cells per well. Cellular viability was monitored at specific time intervals (0 h, 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h post-seeding) through the addition of 10 μL CCK-8 reagent to each well. Following a 2-h incubation period at 37 °C, optical density measurements were conducted at 450 nm wavelength using a microplate reader, with prior plate agitation to ensure solution homogeneity.

EdU assay

For EdU incorporation assay, pre-warmed 2 × EdU working solution (20 μM) was added in equal volume to each well of 96-well plates, followed by 1–2 h incubation at 37 °C. After EdU labeling, the cells were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 15 min. Following fixation, cells were washed three times with PBS (3–5 min per wash). Subsequently, cells were permeabilized with 0.5 % Triton X-100 solution for 15 min, followed by PBS washing. The Azide 594 Click Additive Solution was prepared according to the manufacturer's protocol. 50 μL of Click reaction mixture was added to each well, and the reaction proceeded for 30 min at room temperature in dark. After removing the Click reaction solution, cells were washed three times with PBS. 50 μL of 1 × Hoechst 33342 solution was added to each well and incubated for 10 min protected from light. After PBS washing, samples were visualized and quantified using fluorescence microscopy.

Cell apoptosis assay

Following trypsin-mediated detachment, cellular samples underwent PBS washing to remove residual cell debris. The cell pellets cells were subsequently stained with a dual-fluorescence solution containing 5 μL FITC and 10 μL PI in 500 μL of 1 × binding buffer. Quantitative analysis of apoptotic indices was performed using flow cytometry within a 60-min window following staining completion to ensure fluorescence stability.

Wound healing assay

When cellular monolayers attained approximately 80 % confluency, mechanical wounding was performed utilizing 200-μL pipette tips to create consistent, straight scratch wounds. Following PBS washing to remove cellular debris, the cultures were maintained in serum-deprived medium. The wound closure was photographed at two time points (immediately post-scratching and after 48-h incubation) using phase-contrast microscopy. Quantitative analysis of cellular migration capacity was determined by calculating the percentage reduction in wound area relative to the initial scratch dimensions.

Transwell assay

The filters were were pre-coated with 35 μL of Matrigel basement membrane matrix. In the migration chamber's upper compartment, 20,000 cells suspended in 200 μL serum-deprived medium were plated, while the lower chamber was filled with 600 μL complete medium with 10 % fetal bovine serum. Following a 48-h incubation period, non-invasive cells remaining on the upper membrane surface were removed. The invasive cells were subsequently fixed with 4 % formaldehyde and visualized through crystal violet staining for quantitative analysis.

Tube formation assay

The Matrigel matrix was diluted to 10 mg/mL with serum-free medium, and 50 μL of diluent was dispensed into each well of a 96-well plate. Following a 1-h incubation period at 37 °C, the matrix was polymerized, forming a stable basement membrane. HUVECs were resuspended in complete medium supplemented test samples, and 100 μL cell suspensions containing 3.5 × 10^4 cells were seeded into each well. Tube formation was monitored within approximately 1–3 h, with capillary-like network structures typically developing within this timeframe. Quantitative analysis of angiogenesis parameters was performed with Angiogenesis Analyzer plugin in ImageJ software.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA from cellular and tissue samples was isolated with Trizol reagent and quantified with a microspectrophotometer, followed by reverse transcription of 5 μg total RNA with 4 × Hifair® Ⅲ SuperMix plus. Real-time qPCR amplification was conducted in BIO-RAD-CFX96 using SYBR Green qPCR master mix system. Gene expression was analyzed with comparative Ct method, with 18s serving as internal control. For microRNA quantification, the Hairpin-it™ microRNA qPCR quantitation kit was employed to detect mature miR-607 expression levels, using U6 as normalization.

Western blot

Protein was extracted with RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. Following quantification and denaturation, protein samples (30 μg) were resolved on 10 % or 12 % SDS-polyacrylamide gels. Electrophoretically separated proteins were transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were subsequently blocked with NcmBlot blocking buffer (NCM Biotech, China) for 10 min. Primary antibody incubation was carried out at 4 °C overnight with specific antibodies against VEGFA (1:1000; Boster, China), RAB27B (1:1000; Boster, China), and β-actin (1:8000; Abbkine, China). Following three 10-min washes with TBST, the membranes were incubated with HRP Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (1:8000; Abbkine, China) for 90 min. Protein bands were detected with ECL and visualized using a chemiluminescence imaging system.

Immunofluorescence assay

For cellular uptake experiment, complete culture medium was replaced with serum-free medium prior to treatment. Cells were treated with 20 μg/ml of Cy5-SE labeled P-Nb2C, P-Nb2C-PEG, or pretreated P-Nb2C-PEG in pH 6.5. Following a 2-h incubation, nuclei were stained with DAPI (30 μg/mL), and cell membranes were labeled with FITC.

In immunofluorescence experiments, the cells were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.5 % Triton X-100 and blocked with 5 % BSA in PBS. The cells were further incubated with primary antibodies against VEGFA or RAB27B (1:500; Proteintech, China) overnight at 4 °C. After washing with PBS, the cells were incubated with CoraLite488-conjugated Goat Anti-Rabbit or CoraLite594 – conjugated Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (1:250; Proteintech, China) in dark. Following DAPI staining and mounting, the samples were photographed with a confocal laser scanning microscope (FV1000, Olympus, Japan).

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

HEK-293T cells were co-transfected with wild-type or mutant dual-luciferase reporter plasmid (VEGFA or RAB27B 3′UTR), along with miR-607 mimics or scrambled mimics as controls. After 48-h incubation, 20 μL of cell lysates were collected and added into white 96-well plates, followed by 100 μL of Firefly luciferase substrate. The luminescence intensity was immediately measured with a microplate reader. Subsequently, 100 μL of Renilla luciferase substrate was added, and luminescence intensity of the plate was detected again.

CircRNA pull-down assay

The protein lysate extracted from OVACR3 cells was supplemented with protease and RNase inhibitors. The biotinylated probe targeting the circular junction sequence of circPUM1 was added to the lysate at a final concentration of 100 μM, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with constant agitation. After wash with Buffer I, the supernatant was discarded by centrifugation. Trizol was added to the precipitate, and RNA was subsequently extracted and quantified by PCR using the method described above.

Animal studies

Four-week-old female BALB/c nude mice (HFK Bioscience, China) were intraperitoneally injected with 10^6 OVACR3 cells pre-infected with Lenti-EF1α-Luc-T2A-Puro lentivirus. Four days after the injection, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with D-Luciferin (150 mg/kg) and divided evenly into three groups (n = 5 per group) based on bioluminescence intensity measured by SkyView in vivo imaging system (BLT, Guangzhou, China). Each group of mice was administered intraperitoneally separately as follows: ① PBS as negative control, ② 80 μg of P-Nb2C-PEG loaded with 5 nmol si-scramble RNA as positive control, ③ 80 μg of P-Nb2C-PEG loaded with 5 nmol si-circPUM1 RNA. The treatment regimen consisted of intraperitoneal administration every 3 days for a total of eight injections. 3 mice from each group were examined through IVIS imaging to monitor peritoneal dissemination of ovarian cancer cells at intervals. All mice were humanely sacrificed on the 31st day after tumor formation. Intra-abdominal tumor nodules were collected for RNA extraction, IHC and Western blot.

For the drug targeting experiment, each mouse was subcutaneously injected with 1 × 10^7 OVCAR3 cells in the right flank. When the tumor volume reached approximately 80 mm3, mice were intravenously injected with Cy5-SE-labeled P-Nb2C or P-Nb2C-PEG at a dosage of 4 mg/kg via tail vein. Tanon ABL X5 IVIS (Tanon, Shanghai, China) was used to detect fluorescence intensity. At 36 h after the injection, the subcutaneous tumor tissues and major organs (heart, lung, liver, spleen and kidneys) were dissected out for fluorescence quantification. All the experimental mice were housed in SPF environment at the Experimental Animal Center of China Medical University. All animal experimental manipulations were approved by IACUC of China Medical University (Approval: CMU20241822).

HE staining and IHC

The xenograft tumors were surgically excised from nude mice, fixed in paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. For HE staining, the sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through graded alcohols. The sections were then stained with hematoxylin to visualize nuclei, followed by eosin to highlight the cytoplasm and extracellular matrix. For IHC, the sections were performed with epitope retrieval and blocking of non-specific binding sites. These slides were incubated with primary antibodies targeting CD31 (1:1000; Abcam, China), Ki-67 (1:200; Abcam, China), VEGFA (1:100; Servicebio, China), RAB27B (1:100; Boster, China), followed by secondary antibodies conjugated with HRP. After DAB staining, dehydration and clearing, the slides were mounted with a coverslip for microscopic analysis.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) from triplicate experiments. Two-tailed Student's t-test was used for paired samples statistical analyses and one-way ANOVA was performed for multi-group analyses. ∗P < 0.05, ∗∗P < 0.01, ∗∗∗P < 0.001 and ∗∗∗∗P < 0.0001 represent statistical significance. All statistical analyses were conducted with GraphPad Prism 9.0 or SPSS 27.0.

We found that circPUM1 was highly expressed in ovarian cancer tissues and was positively correlated with advanced tumor stage (stages III & IV vs. stages I & II). Exosomes collected from circPUM1-overexpressing OVCAR3 and matched controls (termed as Exo-circPUM1 and Exo-control, respectively) were administered to HUVEC cells to evaluate circPUM1-mediated cellular phenotype (experimental design schematic: Fig. 2B). QRT-PCR analysis revealed higher circPUM1 enrichment in exo-circPUM1-treated HUVEC versus control group (Fig. 2C). Functional assays demonstrated that exo-circPUM1 enhanced migration ability (wound healing assay: Fig. 2D and E), viability (CCK-8 assay: Fig. 2F), invasiveness (Transwell assay: Fig. 2G), and tube formation ability (Fig. 2H). We further treated both circPUM1-overexpressing and control ovarian cancer cells with exosomal inhibitor GW4869. Exosomes derived from these cell groups were subsequently co-cultured with HUVEC cells. Tube formation assays demonstrated that GW4869 robustly suppressed exosome-mediated angiogenesis (Fig. S1B and S1C), indicating that circPUM1 exerts its biological functions through exosomes as vehicles.

Bioinformatic prediction website revealed miR-607 binding sites within the back-splice junction sequence of circPUM1 (Fig. S2A). MiR-607 has been demonstrated to exert anti-tumor effects through suppressing cellular proliferation, invasion, and metastasis in multiple malignancies, including esophageal carcinoma [29], breast cancer [30], and pancreatic adenocarcinoma [31]. In this study, RNA pull-down assays using biotinylated circPUM1 probes confirmed co-enrichment of circPUM1 and miR-607 (Fig. 2I). Moreover, miR-607 overexpression in ovarian cancer cells suppressed malignant phenotypes (proliferation/migration/invasion; Figs. S2B, S2C, S2D, S2E), consistent with its tumor-suppressive role reported in prior studies.

Further bioinformatics prediction and subsequent validation through dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed miR-607 targeting 3′-UTR in both VEGFA and RAB27B (Figure S2A, Fig. 2J). Western blot demonstrated overexpression of circPUM1 upregulated the protein levels of VEGFA and RAB27B, whereas co-overexpression of miR-607 in circPUM1-overexpressing ovarian cancer cells reversed circPUM1-induced upregulation of these targets. Importantly, exosomal circPUM1 elevated intracellular VEGFA expression in recipient HUVECs (Fig. 2K). Based on these findings, we propose the following molecular mechanism: In ovarian cancer cells, the highly expressed circPUM1 sponges miR-607, upregulating expression of both VEGFA and RAB27B. This dual regulation establishes a pro-tumorigenic cascade through two distinct pathways: (1) RAB27B-mediated secretion of exosomal circPUM1 which modulates vascular endothelial cell activity, and (2) VEGFA-induced activation of angiogenic signaling that directly promotes neovascularization (molecular schematic: Fig. 2L).

Preparation and characterization of Nb2C

To prepare Nb2C nanosheets, the precursor (Nb2AlC) underwent hydrofluoric acid (HF) etching followed by exfoliation with tetrapropylammonium hydroxide (TPAOH), yielding negatively charged monolayer Nb2C nanosheets with enhanced dispersibility and stability. We further mixed the above Nb2C nanosheets with excess PEI to generate a positive surface charge. This cationic modification facilitates strong electrostatic interactions with negatively charged siRNA molecules, significantly enhancing siRNA loading efficiency. Given the elevated expression of circPUM1 in ovarian cancer cells and the tumor microenvironment, where it promotes tumor angiogenesis by targeting vascular endothelial cells, we designed a tumor microenvironment-responsive drug delivery system based on the pH gradient between tumor and normal tissues [32]. We modified the nanosheets with CHO-PEG-CHO, where the aldehyde groups form Schiff base linkages with amine groups, enabling PEG grafting. This modification prolonged its systemic circulation time and reduced nonspecific protein adsorption, thereby minimizing nonspecific interactions with blood components and normal cells during circulation, which ultimately decreased off-target toxicity and improved biosafety. TEM showed that the diameter of Nb2C was approximately 200 nm (Fig. 3A). AFM showed that the thickness was about 2 nm (Fig. 3B and C). DLS measurements indicated that the main intensity distribution peaks of Nb2C, P-Nb2C, and P-Nb2C-PEG were located at 205.1 nm, 315.4 nm, and 372.6 nm, respectively, suggesting PEI and PEG were modified successfully (Fig. 3D). The zeta potentials of Nb2C, P-Nb2C, and P-Nb2C-PEG were −43.56 mV, +42.92 mV and +34.49 mV in water, respectively (Fig. 3E), indicating the successful introduction of positive charges via PEI modification. Furthermore, P-Nb2C-PEG exhibited significant pH responsiveness: its zeta potential was −12.53 mV at pH 7.4, which mimics physiological blood, −5.93 mV at pH 6.8, and gradually shifting to positive values as pH decreased. In vitro antitumor performance of P-Nb2C@si-circPUM1 in ovarian cancer cells

To knock down the expression of circPUM1, siRNA targeting the circular forming sites of circPUM1 was designed (Supplementary Table 2), thereby disrupting its circular structure. The siRNA labeled with Cy3 was administered to ovarian cancer cells in three forms: free siRNA, lipo2000-transfected siRNA, and P-Nb2C-loaded siRNA. To evaluate the biosafety, OVCAR cells were treated with different concentration gradients of lipo2000 or P-Nb2C for 24, 48, and 72 h. Collectively, these results revealed that circPUM1 siRNA-loaded P-Nb2C nanosheets could be a potential therapeutic platform for suppressing malignant phenotypes in ovarian cancer.

These findings collectively highlight the dual therapeutic mechanisms of the P-Nb2C-PEG-loaded circPUM1 siRNA system: direct cancer cell killing and suppression of angiogenesis through circPUM1-mediated pathways.

RAB27B, a member of RAB GTPases family, is a pivotal regulator in the process of exosome release [33,34]. Recent studies have shed light on the role of RAB27B in modulating exosome secretion, particularly during cancer progression. Chen et al. [35] discovered that RAB27B promoted the loading and release of exosomes in leukemic stem cells, delivering stemness-promoting proteins to remodel the niche and sustain leukemic cell stemness. Similarly, elevated RAB27B expression has significant prognostic value in multiple solid tumors, including esophageal squamous cell cancer [36], triple-negative breast cancer [37], colorectal cancer [38] and pancreatic cancer [39]. For instance, studies have revealed that elevated RAB27B is overexpressed in gastric cancer [40,41], where its knockdown significantly attenuates exosome secretion and reduces peritoneal dissemination in xenograft models. Similarly, given that ovarian cancer shares this characteristic peritoneal metastatic pattern, our experimental data demonstrate that siRNA-mediated circPUM1 silencing in tumor xenografts results in markedly decreased RAB27B expression compared to control groups, accompanied by reduced peritoneal metastatic foci.

The VEGF family activates angiogenesis and plays a pivotal role in the growth and metastasis of ovarian cancer [42,43]. Anti-angiogenic agents targeting VEGF-A, such as Bevacizumab, are widely used in first-line therapy, recurrent and maintenance therapy in advanced ovarian cancer patients [44,45]. However, the majority of clinical studies, such as GOG-0218 and ICON7, have demonstrated that the addition of bevacizumab to standard chemotherapy showed improvements in progression-free survival (PFS), without statistically significant benefits in overall survival (OS) [46,47]. Emerging evidence suggests that while anti-angiogenic therapy initially suppresses tumor vascularization by disrupting nutrient and oxygen supply, the stimulated endothelial cells can reinitiate angiogenesis driven by pro-angiogenic exosomes within TME, resulting in diminished therapeutic efficacy and tumor relapse [[48], [49], [50]]. For instance, in hepatocellular carcinoma [51], vascular endothelial cells exposed to combined anti-angiogenic therapy and autophagy inhibition upregulated miR-9 expression and VEGF-enriched exosomes secretion, consequently promoting angiogenesis and vasculogenic mimicry.

To overcome these limitations, this study developed an Mxene-based nanoplatform for targeted delivery of circPUM1 siRNA to ovarian tumor tissues, effectively downregulating VEGFA and RAB27B expression. This approach not only suppresses intrinsic VEGF production in ovarian cancer cells but also blocks RAB27B-mediated exosome release, thereby preventing the risk of vascularization regeneration in TME. Furthermore, by directly acting on vascular endothelial cells, the nanoplatform inhibits VEGFA-mediated tube formation, offering a dual mechanism—simultaneously attacking tumor cells and suppressing angiogenesis. Consequently, this circPUM1 siRNA-loaded nanoplatform overcomes the limitations of conventional anti-angiogenic therapies and offers a novel strategy for precision medicine in ovarian cancer treatment.

Conclusions

In this study, we have revealed that circPUM1, which is significantly overexpressed in ovarian cancer tissues, can modulate angiogenesis within the tumor microenvironment through exosome-mediated interactions with vascular endothelial cells, thereby influencing ovarian cancer progression. Mechanistically, we have elucidated that circPUM1 upregulates the protein expression of RAB27B and VEGFA by sponging miR-607, relieving its inhibitory effect on the target genes RAB27B and VEGFA. It exerts pro-angiogenic effects through the following dual regulatory pathways: (1) by upregulating RAB27B, enhancing the release of exosomal circPUM1, which subsequently acts on vascular endothelial cells; and (2) by directly activating the VEGFA pathway in vascular endothelial cells thus promoting angiogenesis.

Based on these findings regarding circPUM1's role in tumor microenvironment of ovarian cancer, we developed an innovative pH-responsive nano-delivery system. This system is based on ultrathin niobium carbide MXene-derived two-dimensional nanosheets loaded with siRNA. By further grafting PEG onto its surface, we achieved efficient shielding of positive charges, which resists protein adsorption and enables pH-responsive PEG shedding in the acidic tumor microenvironment, facilitating targeted gene delivery in vivo. This novel nanosheets exhibits multiple significant advantages: (1) low material cost and large-scale production potential; (2) the large specific surface area for superior siRNA-loading capacity; and (3) pH-responsive property that minimizes off-target effect and systemic toxicity. This study elucidates novel molecular insights into ovarian cancer angiogenesis and introduces an innovative two-dimensional nanosheet-based delivery platform. It is a transformative strategy for precision-targeted therapy in ovarian cancer with promising clinical translation potential.

OA20381 Counseling through Penal Mediation: Therapeutic Effectiveness in Family Violence Interventions
Authors: A Garefalaki, M Toraki, N Pedioti, A Chachlioutaki, D Stefou, R Skeva, K Vagionaki, V Kounali, M Papadakaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPenal mediation in Greece provides an alternative response to family violence incidents by integrating judicial oversight with therapeutic intervention. Within this framework, individuals referred by the Prosecutor of Heraklion engage in counseling based on Systems Therapy, which focuses on systemic change, accountability, and the restructuring of relationships.MethodsThe study reports on 201 participants referred to therapy following family violence incidents until the end of 2024. The sample included 42.3% women, 14.4% men, 28.4% couples, and 14.9% parents. Therapy was structured according to systems therapy model with a median duration of 26 sessions, adapted to men or women as perpetrators, couples upon carefully screened for risk, and parents with family-focused and child safety planning. Most participants (67.5%) attended weekly sessions, although a significant number followed a biweekly schedule. In nearly half of the cases (48.3%), therapy extended over one year. Outcomes and relapse were assessed using standardized five-point scales, where lower scores indicated better results.ResultsThe average outcome score was 1.4 (SD = 0.7), reflecting positive therapeutic effects, while the average relapse score was 2.0 (SD = 0.9). Improved outcomes were observed among men, those attending biweekly sessions, and participants whose therapy lasted four months or less. Similarly, lower relapse rates were associated with shorter therapy duration and biweekly sessions. Regression analysis confirmed that both session frequency and therapy duration were independent predictors of outcome and relapse.ConclusionsTherapy offered within the penal mediation process in Heraklion, Greece demonstrates encouraging results in addressing family violence. Shorter, structured interventions with biweekly frequency proved more effective than prolonged weekly therapy. These findings highlight the importance of adapting therapeutic approaches to maximize recovery, reduce relapse, and strengthen family resilience in the aftermath of violence.Key Messages• Therapy within penal mediation offers an effective pathway for addressing family violence, while therapy structure, particularly session frequency and duration, emerges as a critical determinant of success.Topicpenal mediation, family violence

Beyond immunogenicity in XBB.1.5 subunit COVID-19 boosters focusing on durability, dosing, and target populations: authors reply
Authors: Subhash Thuluva, Vikram Paradkar, SubbaReddy Gunneri, Rammohan Reddy Mogulla, Vijay Yerroju, Siddalingaiah Ningaiah, Mallikarjuna Panchakshari, Chirag Dhar
Published: 2025

We thank Norkaew and colleagues for their thoughtful commentary1 regarding our Phase 3 study of an updated protein subunit XBB.1.5 RBD vaccine booster series.2 Our demonstration of neutralizing antibodies against the XBB.1.5 strain and cross-neutralization against the JN.1 strain, together with a reassuring safety profile of this booster series2 is indeed particularly important to the Global South where access to mRNA vaccines remains constrained.

Norkaew and colleagues bring up several important future considerations for studies involving COVID-19 boosters. We briefly touched upon these in our discussion section2 and elaborate elaborate on these further:

1.Durability: Durability of antibodies and longer follow-up periods are an important consideration for future booster studies. Protein subunit vaccines largely induce a more durable response, as well as robust cellular immunity, likely making their overall immunogenicity superior to that of mRNA vaccines. In an earlier study utilizing the ancestral strain of our vaccine as a booster, participants had significantly higher anti-S1/S2 and anti-RBD IgG antibody levels even 3 months after a booster as compared with pre-booster levels.3

2.At-risk populations: To meet regulatory constraints and to prevent excessive participant heterogeneity, only immunocompetent individuals were enrolled in our XBB.1.5 booster study, while acknowledging the need for post-marketing studies to assess the immunogenicity in immunocompromised individuals.

3.Comparisons with updated boosters: At the time of conducting this study, there were no approved XBB.1.5 vaccines available in India that would have allowed a head-to-head comparison.

4.Dosing schedule practicality: While the XBB.1.5 booster was trialed in a two-dose series, we demonstrated superiority 28 days after the first dose as a pre-specified primary endpoint. These data are reassuring and suggest that a single dose booster is robustly immunogenic, prompting a planned Phase 3 trial of a single dose of the JN.1 booster.

In conclusion, while we demonstrated that our XBB.1.5 booster series was robustly immunogenic with an acceptable safety profile, future booster studies and policies are likely to evolve with SARS-CoV-2 now becoming endemic. In this regard, the FDA is also now considering recommending less frequent booster updates and risk-based approvals.4 It is conceivable that, in the near future, SARS-CoV-2 immunization strategies may also adopt region- or hemisphere-specific strain recommendations, analogous to the antigenic tailoring already standard practice in seasonal influenza vaccination.

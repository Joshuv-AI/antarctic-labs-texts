Cardiac MRI-derived ventricular-pulmonary arterial coupling predicts outcomes in connective tissue disease-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension patients
Authors: Yidan Zhu, Ke Xu, Han Jia, Wangyan Liu, Xiaoxuan Sun, Qiang Wang, Yi Xu, Yinsu Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo evaluate the prognostic value of cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) parameters in patients with connective tissue disease-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (CTD-PAH).Materials and methodsThis retrospective cohort study involved 135 patients with documented CTD-PAH. Cardiac functional parameters including right ventricle end-diastolic volume index (RVEDVI), end-systolic volume index (RVESVI), stroke volume index (RVSVI), ejection fraction (RVEF), and SV/ESV; the volumetric parameter; T1 mapping and late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) parameters; strain parameters like RV global longitudinal strain(GLS); and hemodynamics parameters like mean velocity at the pulmonary artery (mvPA) and PA relative area change (PA RAC) were calculated from CMR images. Survival analysis was conducted using Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression. The endpoint was the occurrence of clinical deterioration events.ResultsThe median follow-up time was 22.27 months (interquartile range: 11.5–31.9 months).RVIP ECV, RVGLS, RVEDVI, RVESVI, RVMI, and RAA, as well as mvPA, SV/ESV, RVEF, and PA RAC, were associated with adverse outcomes. SV/ESV ≤ 0.55, RV GLS > -12.1 %, and PA RAC≤ 16.1 % were significant independent predictors of prognosis. The combined parameters provided incremental prognostic value over COMPERA 2.0 (area under the curve (AUC) from 0.771 to 0.899; P = 0.001).And time-dependent ROC curve confirmed the predictive efficiency of the combined parameters in year 3, with the AUC reaching 0.821(95 % CI: 0.698–0.945).ConclusionMultiparametric CMR provides a non-invasive, efficient method to assess prognosis in CTD-PAH patients. Key parameters including SV/ESV, RV GLS, and PA RAC significantly predicted survival and confer incremental prognostic utility over COMPERA 2.0, offering reliable prognostic markers for clinical interventions.

Introduction

Connective tissue disease-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (CTD-PAH) is a critical subgroup of PAH, comprising 20–30 % of all cases [1]. This secondary form of PAH, while often insidious in onset, can rapidly progress to right heart failure without timely intervention, resulting in substantially reduced survival rates and increased mortality [2]. A nationwide study reported a 10-year survival rate of 59.9 % for Chinese CTD-PAH patients, underscoring the severity of the disease [1]. Thus, assessment of disease severity and prognosis is essential for selecting treatment options and counselling CTD-PAH patients.

At present, right heart catheterization (RHC) is the gold standard for PH diagnosis, with a resting mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) > 20 mmHg indicating PH [3]. However, the invasive nature and radiation risk of this method limit its use in prognostic assessments. Alternative modalities such as transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) and computed tomography are hampered by operator dependency and radiation exposure, respectively [4].

According to the "2022 European Society of Cardiology/European Respiratory Society”(ESC/ERS) Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Pulmonary Hypertension" [3], cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) is the gold standard for assessing right heart size, morphology, and function due to its high reproducibility and versatility. The multiparametric approach provided by CMR enables accurate measurements of cardiac anatomy, function, and hemodynamics, such as pulmonary artery dynamics. At present, there is growing interest in the use of strain imaging with deformation assessment and RV-PA coupling to offer additional prognostic information for various pathologies [5]. These parameters have been shown to closely relate to mortality and survival in PAH patients, highlighting their potential value in disease monitoring and assessment [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12]. Given PAH's etiology diversity and unique mechanisms, prognostic markers, and thresholds, a multiparametric CMR prognostic model tailored for CTD-PAH has not been achieved.

While the ESC/ERS-endorsed Comparative, Prospective Registry of Newly Initiated Therapies for Pulmonary Hypertension (COMPERA) 2.0 four-strata model offers practical utility for serial risk assessment [3], [13], its reliance on subjective clinical parameters limits prognostic precision. In contrast, CMR provides objective, high-resolution multidimensional data, yet its full prognostic potential in CTD-PAH remains underexplored.

This study therefore aims to bridge this critical knowledge gap by systematically evaluating the prognostic value of multiparametric CMR in CTD-PAH, with particular emphasis on its incremental value beyond COMPERA 2.0. We aimed to identify high-risk individuals to guide personalized treatment and intervention timing, ultimately improving outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

Patients were followed up every 3 months by telephone or during outpatient visits. The follow-up period extended until the primary endpoint was reached or January 20, 2025. The median survival time was 22.27 months (interquartile range: 11.5–31.9 months). The primary endpoint was the occurrence of clinical deterioration events, including all-cause mortality, worsening of World Health Organization functional class, a decrease of ≥ 15 % in the six-minute walk test distance, hospitalization due to heart failure or worsening of CTD-PAH, or the initiation of parenteral prostacyclin analog therapy [14].

This study was approved by the relevant Ethics Committee (approval number: 2018-SR-333). Due to its retrospective nature, the need for written informed consent was waived.

Clinical and hemodynamic parameters

Baseline demographic,clinical characteristics and laboratory parameters were systematically collected for all enrolled patients. The following key parameters measured and calculated from RHC: mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP), pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PAWP), pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), right atrial pressure (RAP), cardiac output (CO), cardiac index (CI), and mixed venous oxygen saturation (SvO2).Ees/Ea was estimated by (RV maximum pressure (Pmax)/mPAP−1) [7].

CMR image acquisition

All examinations were performed using a 3.0 T MAGNETOM Skyra MRI system (Siemens Healthineers) equipped with an 18-channel phased-array coil and vector ECG gating. Cine imaging was acquired using balanced steady-state free precession (b-SSFP) sequences, obtaining three long-axis views and a complete short-axis stack of 8–12 slices covering the left ventricle from base to apex during breath-hold at end-inspiration. The b-SSFP parameters included: repetition time 3.4 ms, echo time 1.4 ms, field of view 380 × 380 mm, matrix size 256 × 256, flip angle 47°, slice thickness 8 mm with 2 mm gap.

Pre- and post-contrast T1 mapping was performed at basal, mid, and apical short-axis levels using a modified look-locker inversion recovery (MOLLI) sequence before and 15 min after administration of 0.2 mmol/kg gadolinium-DTPA (Magnevist; Bayer, Berlin, Germany). MOLLI sequence parameters included: TR 2.9 ms, TE 1.15 ms, flip angle 35°, matrix size 192 × 172, and voxel size 1.3 × 1.3 × 8.0 mm³ . Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) imaging was performed approximately 8–15 min post-contrast using a phase-sensitive inversion-recovery sequence. Key acquisition parameters included: field of view 340–380 mm, flip angle 55°, matrix 256 × 220, bandwidth 781 Hz/Px, TR 2.88 ms, TE 1.24 ms, slice thickness 8 mm, and slice gap 2 mm.

Pulmonary artery flowimaging utilized a velocity-encoded phase-contrast gradient echo sequence during free breathing.The imaging plane was situated 1–2 cm above the pulmonary valve and perpendicular to the main pulmonary artery, with a velocity encoding limit of 130 cm/s, temporal resolution of 19.76 ms, and spatial resolution of 1.5 × 1.5 × 5 mm.

CMR image analysis

All CMR images were analyzed by an experienced radiologist (2 years of experience), and overseen by a senior radiologist (20 years of experience). A third radiologist (3 years of experience) re-measured 30 cases to confirm consistency. All analyses were conducted blinded to clinical and RHC data.

Volumetric parameters including right ventricular end-diastolic area (RVA), left ventricular end-diastolic area (LVA), right atrial end-systolic area (RAA) and left atrial end-systolic area (LAA) were obtained using PACS. CVI42 software was employed for semi-automatic quantification of biventricular function through manual delineation of endocardial and epicardial borders on short-axis cine images. Derived parameters included LV/RV stroke volume (SV), end-diastolic volume (EDV), end-systolic volume (ESV), ejection fraction (EF), cardiac output (CO), and myocardial mass (MM), with incorporation of papillary muscles and trabeculae into volumetric measurements. These measurements were indexed to body surface area (BSA) (calculated using the Dubois formula) as end-diastolic volume index (EDVI), end-systolic volume index (ESVI), stroke volume index (SVI), cardiac index (CI), and myocardial mass index (MMI). The right ventricular to pulmonary artery (RV-PA) coupling ratio was calculated using the formula: Emax (RV end-systolic elastance) / Ea (effective arterial elastance) = SV / ESV [15], as the ‘volume method’.

T1 mapping was performed at RV insertion points (RVIPs) on short-axis views. LV myocardial contours (basal, mid, apical) were manually traced using CVI42 to determine global T1. Pre- and post-contrast measurements maintained identical slice positions. ECV was calculated using the following standardized, published formula:ECV=(1/postT1myocardium−1/nativeT1myocardium)(1/postT1blood−1/nativeT1blood)×(1−HCT)×100%.

LGE was assessed using the American Heart Association 16-segment model (excluding the apex). Within each segment, LGE was scored dichotomously (1 = present; 0 = absent). LGE positivity was strictly defined as a signal intensity exceeding 5 standard deviations above the mean of a reference region of remote, healthy myocardium.

The maximal and minimal main pulmonary artery (MPA) area were manually traced. PA relative area change (PA RAC) was defined as (maximum area − minimum area) / minimum area [12]. A dedicated software (Argus,Siemens Healthineers) calculated mvPA as the integral of the velocity in each of the voxels included within the PA outline over the complete cardiac cycle.Other indices of PA stiffness including compliance, capacitance and distensibility were calculated from CMR and RHC [16].

Three-dimensional feature tracking global circumferential strain and global longitudinal strain of RV were obtained using CVI42 across various views (short-axis, two-chamber, three-chamber, and four-chamber). The epicardial contours of the RV were manually outlined. Any auto-generated contours were carefully reviewed and adjusted as necessary to ensure accuracy.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26 (SPSS Inc.) and Prism 9.5 (GraphPad Software). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (%) and analyzed using chi-square tests. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD and compared using independent t-tests, while non-normally distributed data were analyzed with Mann-Whitney U tests. Pearson correlation assessed relationships between CMR-derived SV/ESV and RHC -based (Pmax / mPAP-1). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression identified independent predictors of clinical outcomes. Survival differences were assessed using log-rank tests and Kaplan-Meier curves (stratified by median values). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis with Delong’s test evaluated prognostic performance and incremental predictive value of CMR parameters over COMPERA 2.0. Time-dependent ROC curves assessed 1-, 2-, and 3-year predictive accuracy. Interobserver agreement was analyzed via Bland-Altman analyses. All tests were two-tailed, with p < 0.05 considered significant.

Discussion

This study substantiates the prognostic value of CMR in CTD-PAH. Adverse outcomes correlated with elevated RVIP ECV, RV GLS, RAA, RVEDVI, RVESVI, and RVMI, while associating with reduced SV/ESV, PA RAC, RVEF, and mvPA. SV/ESV, RV GLS, and PA RAC emerged as independent predictors of clinical deterioration, providing incremental prognostic information beyond COMPERA 2.0.Declining right heart function is crucial for assessing PH prognosis [4], [17]. Pathophysiologically, pulmonary vascular remodeling increases RV afterload, initially compensated by myocardial hypertrophy to maintain cardiac output [18]. However, progressive RV-PA uncoupling and decompensation ultimately occur.

COMPERA 2.0 evaluates RV function via WHO functional class, 6MWT, and NT-proBNP [13]. Our study confirmed its prognostic value, with patients without clinical deterioration showing significantly better outcomes than those with deterioration events.

SV/ESV coupling parameters demonstrate strong prognostic value for PH [6], [7]. While conductance catheter-based Ees/Ea measurement remains the gold standard, its invasiveness limits clinical application [19]. CMR enables noninvasive Ees/Ea estimation through volumetric methods [20], effectively reflecting RV pressure-volume management capacity [21]. For instance, SV/ESV < 0.59 predicts survival in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction-related PH [6], while SV/ESV < 0.79 serves as an independent predictor irrespective of RAP, mPAP, and SV [7]. In SLE-PAH, a threshold of < 0.712 identifies endpoint events [22]. Our study identified SV/ESV ≤ 0.55 as an independent predictor of clinical deterioration in CTD-PAH, potentially reflecting: (1) advanced disease with severe RV dysfunction (mean RVEF 27.6 % vs. standard threshold <35 %); (2) unique CTD-PAH pathophysiology with predominant RV impairment [23]; and (3) methodological variations in measurement techniques and cohort characteristics. CMR-measured SV/ESV provides stable prognostic assessment for CTD-PAH, aiding in outcome prediction and treatment monitoring [24].

CMR-FT enables precise myocardial strain analysis. The RV exhibits greater sensitivity to afterload increases than the LV. Reduced RV septal and GLS correlate with higher 3-year mortality in pre-capillary PH [8], while Zhou et al. reported RV GLS > -18.1 % association with adverse events [9]. Our findings align with studies demonstrating RV GLS worsening as a mortality risk factor [10], [11], supporting its clinical utility in CTD-PAH management.

CMR offers reproducible, noninvasive PA stiffness and hemodynamic assessment [25]. Increased PA pressure causes proximal PA distension and remodeling, leading to vascular stiffening. PA RAC, a simplified MPA stiffness metric independent of pulse pressure [26], serves as an independent prognostic marker in PH [12], [27]. Recent studies highlight mvPA as a novel RV-PA coupling indicator reflecting PA compliance and buffering function [28], [29], with our findings supporting its prognostic value.

RVEF assessed by CMR has become the gold standard for PH prognosis due to its high spatial resolution and reproducibility [25]. Meta-analyses confirm RVEF as the optimal predictor of all-cause mortality and composite endpoints in PH [30]. Alabed et al. reported a 4.9 % increase in clinical deterioration risk per 1 % RVEF decline over 22 months [16]. Although our study identified significant RVEF differences between groups, it did not emerge as an independent predictor. Previous research noted lower RVMI increases with rising PVR in SSc-PAH compared to IPAH, potentially explaining reduced survival in SSc-PAH [31]. Meta-analysis data indicated 1.3 % and 0.7 % increased clinical worsening risk per 1 ml/m² increment in RVESVI and RVEDVI, respectively [17], with our study showing significantly higher values in the deterioration group, reflecting severe RV hypertrophy and dilation.

RA contributes to RV filling in low-pressure states and systolic output. Impaired RA compensatory function in CTD-PAH may lead to right heart failure [32]. Meta-analyses demonstrate RAA as a significant predictor of composite endpoints and mortality, with each 1 cm² increase associated with 6 % higher risk [33], findings corroborated by our study.

RV fibrosis, measured by ECV, indicates adverse remodeling effects in PH-HFpEF [34]. Elevated RVIP ECV in clinical deterioration cases supports this association. CTD independently induces myocardial fibrosis [35], with SSc-PAH patients showing higher ECV than IPAH patients, suggesting inflammation-induced microcirculatory and cardiac effects [36]. The inability to differentiate CTD-induced from PAH-related fibrosis warrants further investigation.LGE effectively detects focal fibrosis but may lack sensitivity for subtle, diffuse fibrosis [37], which likely explains its significant inter-group difference (p = 0.013) yet absence of independent prognostic value in our cohort.

Our findings carry important practical implications for the management of CTD-PAH.The identified CMR parameters provide clinicians with objective, quantifiable markers to supplement current risk assessment tools. For example:Patients exceeding these thresholds may warrant closer follow-up (3-month intervals) and earlier escalation to triple therapy, even if COMPERA 2.0 suggests intermediate risk.Moreover, CMR measurements of these parameters may help detect subclinical RV dysfunction before symptomatic decline (particularly useful for patients with discordant clinical/biomarker profiles).

Limitations

Several limitations of our study should be acknowledged.First, this single-center retrospective study has inherent limitations due to its design, and the cohort predominantly comprised patients with advanced disease and low RVEF. These factors may limit the generalizability of our findings and the widespread application of the proposed predictive model. Second, heterogeneity in underlying treatments may have influenced outcomes. Most importantly, while CMR offers detailed cardiac evaluation, our assessment lacks complementary imaging modalities such as echocardiography or CT for comprehensive cardiac function analysis.Furthermore, the evaluation does not incorporate assessment of critical extra-cardiac manifestations of CTD-PAH (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis, renal involvement). Future large-scale, multicenter, prospective studies that adopt a multidimensional, systemic approach are warranted to validate our findings.

Conclusion

This study has demonstrated that multi-parameter CMR can non-invasively and efficiently assess the prognosis of CTD-PAH patients, providing incremental information for clinical assessment.Key parameters including RVIP ECV, mvPA, RV GLS, RVEF, RVEDVI, RVESVI, RVMI, SV/ESV, PA RAC, and RAA effectively predict clinical deterioration.Particularly,SV/ESV, RV GLS and PA RAC are reliable indicators of clinical worsening in CTD-PAH patients and have potential predictive ability for adverse clinical events, offering prognostic guidance for clinical interventions.

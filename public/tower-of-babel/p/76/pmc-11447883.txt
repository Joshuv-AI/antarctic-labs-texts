Newly developed humanized anti‐CKAP4 antibody suppresses pancreatic cancer growth by inhibiting DKK1‐CKAP4 signaling
Authors: Ryota Sada, Hideki Yamamoto, Shinji Matsumoto, Akikazu Harada, Akira Kikuchi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCytoskeleton‐associated protein 4 (CKAP4) is a cell surface receptor for Dickkopf 1 (DKK1), a secreted protein. The DKK1–CKAP4 pathway is activated in various malignant tumors, including pancreatic, lung, esophageal, and liver cancers, to promote tumor growth. Thus, CKAP4 has been expected to represent a novel molecular target of cancer therapy. Recombinant mouse anti‐CKAP4 antibodies were generated based on an original mouse antibody (3F11‐2B10) and inhibited DKK1–CKAP4 signaling and xenograft tumor formation induced by pancreatic cancer cells, which was comparable with 3F11‐2B10. From the 3F11‐2B10 nucleotide sequence, humanized anti‐CKAP4 antibody (Hv1Lt1) was subsequently developed. The binding affinity of Hv1Lt1 for CKAP4 was superior to that of 3F11‐2B10. Hv1Lt1 inhibited DKK1 binding to CKAP4, AKT activity, and sphere formation of pancreatic cancer cells, which was comparable with 3F11‐2B10. Hv1Lt1 also suppressed xenograft tumor formation induced by human pancreatic cancer cells and tumor growth in murine cancer models, in which murine pancreatic cancer organoids were orthotopically transplanted into the pancreas. In resected tumor samples from mice treated with Hv1Lt1, anti‐tumor immune reactions were modulated and cytotoxic T cells were highly infiltrated in the tumor microenvironment. Additionally, combination of Hv1Lt1 and other chemotherapy drugs exhibited stronger effects compared with monotherapy. These results suggest that Hv1Lt1 represents a promising anti‐cancer therapy.Keywords: CKAP4, cytotoxic T‐cell, DKK1, humanized antibody, pancreatic cancer

INTRODUCTION

Dickkopf1 (DKK1) is an extracellular secreted protein that antagonizes Wnt signaling. 1 , 2 It binds to LRP5/6, Wnt ligand receptors, and also inhibits Wnt signaling in a competitive manner or in a manner in which DKK1 sequesters LRP5/6 from the plasma membrane. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 DKK1 had been known to promote the growth of various cancers, but its molecular mechanisms were unknown for many years. We previously identified Cytoskeleton‐Associated Protein 4 (CKAP4; also known as CLIMP‐63 or ERGIC‐63) as a DKK1 receptor. 7 , 8 CKAP4 was originally identified as a type II transmembrane protein and was primarily located to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) where it was implicated in structural maintenance. 9 , 10 By binding to plasma membrane‐localized CKAP4, DKK1 activated the phosphoinositide 3‐kinase (PI3K)‐AKT pathway, and promoted cancer cell proliferation. 7 Simultaneous expression of DKK1 and CKAP4 has been associated with poor prognoses in patients with pancreatic, lung, esophageal, and liver cancers. 7 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15

CKAP4 KO mice appeared phenotypically normal and were indistinguishable from their heterozygous littermates and WT mice from birth to 18 months under normal breeding conditions. 16 Therefore, treatments targeting CKAP4 did not confer any severe side effects. Mouse anti‐human CKAP4 monoclonal antibodies (mCKAP4 Abs) were generated by immunizing CKAP4 KO mice, as human and mouse CKAP4 sequences have high similarity. 16 One of mCKAP4 Abs, which was named 3F11‐2B10, demonstrated inhibitory activities toward DKK1 binding to CKAP4, and 3F11‐2B10 treatments inhibited AKT activity, in vitro cell proliferation, sphere formation, and migration of pancreatic, lung, and liver cancer cells. 3F11‐2B10 also suppressed xenograft tumor formation induced by these cancer cells in athymic nude mice (T‐cell immunodeficient mice) and extended the survival of mice intraperitoneally injected with pancreatic cancer cells. 14 , 15 , 16

CKAP4 is secreted with small extracellular vesicles (exosomes) from pancreatic cancer cells,( 16 ) although it is a transmembrane protein. CKAP4 serum levels were elevated in pancreatic and lung cancer patients when compared with levels in healthy individuals. 14 , 16 As the therapy targeting CKAP4 can theoretically suppress the proliferation of cancer cells where CKAP4 is expressed in the plasma membrane and from which DKK1 is secreted, serum CKAP4 and DKK1 detection by ELISA could help to predict responders to CKAP4 Ab therapy.

While mCKAP4 Abs have potential applications as research and diagnostic tools, they are not approved as Ab therapeutics for cancer patients. To enable successful Ab therapeutic development, the Abs require humanization, which could reduce the production of neutralizing Abs to non‐human molecules in patients. 17 Of the various cancers in which DKK1–CKAP4 signaling is activated, pancreatic cancer has a poor prognosis and novel effective treatments are limited. In this study, we generated a humanized anti‐CKAP4 Ab (hCKAP4 Ab), named Hv1Lt1, and its drug efficacy was tested in various pancreatic cancer mouse models.

METHODS

cDNA cloning of mCKAP4 Ab

Total RNA was isolated from 3F11‐2B10 hybridoma cells using the RNeasy Mini Kit (QIAGEN), and cDNAs were synthesized using the SMARTer RACE cDNA amplification kit (CLONTECH Laboratories). Then, cDNAs encoding the VH and the VL regions were amplified using a 5′‐RACE method with specifically designed primers to IgG2b heavy and light kappa chain constant regions using PCR reagents (TaKaRa Bio, PrimeSTAR Max for VH and PrimeSTAR HS for VL regions).

All mCKAP4 Abs in this study were expressed either in Expi293F cells or FreeStyle 293F cells (Thermo Fisher Scientific) by transfecting the expression vectors constructed using manufacturer's recommendations. After expression, mCKAP4 Abs were purified from the conditioned medium using Antibody‐Capcher resin (ProteNova, Japan), followed by dialysis in 50 mM Na acetate (pH 4.5), while anti‐KLH Abs were purified from the conditioned media using MabSelect SuRe resin (Cytiva), followed by size exclusion chromatography using one of the following buffers: D‐PBS (−) or 20 mM histidine and 150 mM NaCl pH 6.0. All purified Abs were sterile filtered and stored at −80°C.

Humanization of mCKAP4 Ab

Complementarity determining regions were defined by Kabat, IMGT, and Paratome methods. The most similar human germline sequences were determined using the IMGT/DomainGapAlign database (https://www.imgt.org/IMGTindex/IMGTDomainGapAlign.php). Combined Kabat/IMGT/Paratome CDRs were then grafted into human germline framework sequences. Detailed procedures were described previously. 18

Orthotopic KPC organoid transplantation into syngeneic immunocompetent mice

KPC organoids stably expressing murine CKAP4 and DKK1 (KPC/CKAP4/DKK1) were resuspended as 1.2 × 105 single‐cell suspensions in 50 μL of Matrigel and maintained on ice until transplantation. A 1.5 cm incision was made in the left dorsal flank of a C57BL/6 mouse from which the spleen was pulled from the abdominal cavity. The spleen was then secured along a sterile cotton bud to expose the underlying pancreas. The Matrigel‐cell suspension was injected into the pancreatic tail using a 29G syringe and then the pancreas and spleen were returned to the abdominal cavity and the incision sutured. Organoid progression was monitored weekly using ultrasonography. When a cystic tumor lesion was detected, mice were randomly assigned to two groups, and either intraperitoneally administered with Hv1Lt1 (280 μg/injection, twice a week; n = 12) or an isotype control IgG injection (n = 11). Tumor development continued until day 52. After mice were humanely sacrificed, tumors located in the pancreatic tail were resected and measured. Tumor volumes were calculated as described. 19

Statistical analysis

Student's t‐tests or Mann–Whitney U tests were used to determine statistical mean differences between two groups. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Dunnett's post‐hoc tests were used to compare the means of three or more groups. A p < 0.05 value was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software).

RESULTS

cDNA cloning of mCKAP4 Ab

Using RACE cDNA amplification, 20 cDNAs were synthesized based on total RNA isolated from hybridoma cells, which produced the original mCKAP4 Ab, 3F11‐2B10. The nucleotide sequences of the cloned cDNAs of VH and VL regions of 3F11‐2B10 were determined, and cDNAs contained open reading frames encoding proteins of 141 and 127 amino acids, respectively (Figure S1A,B). Two recombinant silent Fc mCKAP4 Abs, mF18 (mIgG1‐based) and mFa31 (mIgG2a‐based), in which mutations were introduced into the Fc domain, including the Fc receptor binding site, were generated (Figure S2). Mutations in mouse IgG2‐type silent Fc (mFa31) were represented by L235R/G236R/S239K 21 and those in mouse IgG1‐type silent Fc (mF18) were represented by P235K/S239K. 22 These Abs did not bind to Fc receptors, resulting in failing to induce antibody‐dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). The molecular weights of recombinant Abs were similar to that of native IgG under reducing conditions (Figure S2).

Characterization of recombinant mCKAP4 Ab

Two recombinant silent Fc mCKAP4 Abs clearly detected and immunoprecipitated endogenous CKAP4 from S2‐CP8 cell lysates, similar to 3F11‐2B10 (Figure S3A; Figure 1A). Both silent Fc mCKAP4 Abs inhibited in vitro binding between DKK1 and CKAP4 in a dose‐dependent manner (Figure 1B). When cancer cell lines, including S2‐CP8, Hep3B, HuH‐7, TE‐5, and TE‐8 cells, in which DKK1 and CKAP4 were expressed 7 , 11 , 12 , 14 , 15 , 16 (Figure S3B,C), were treated with silent Fc mCKAP4 Abs, AKT activity was inhibited (Figure 1C). Sphere formation in S2‐CP8 and HuH‐7 cells was also inhibited by these Abs (Figure 1D; Figure S3D). Although silent Fc mCKAP4 Abs eliminated ADCC, mF18 suppressed xenograft tumor formation induced by S2‐CP8 cells (Figure 1E), suggesting that the in vivo anti‐tumor effects and pharmacological activities of 3F11‐2B10 were due to blocking DKK1 binding to CKAP4, and not via ADCC activity.

Generation of hCKAP4 Abs

For the clinical application of mouse Abs, murine sequences must be humanized to decrease human immunogenicity. 23 CDRs in VH and VL chain domains were defined by Kabat, IMGT, and Paratome methods, and the identification of the most similar human germline sequences was determined by the IMGT/DomainGapAlign database. Heavy chain CDRs, identified using these three methods, are subtly different. Therefore, when creating humanized antibodies, we used the region that cover all CDRs by the three CDR determination methods (referred to as broad CDR, H1), and the region that overlaps with the three CDR determination methods (referred to as narrow CDR, H2; Figure S1A,B). Based on these CDR patterns, humanization was initiated. The frameworks used in this study were selected from human germline‐derived Ab sequences in the database, which were similar to 3F11‐2B10 sequences, and then combined Kabat/IMGT/Paratome CDRs were grafted into human germline framework sequences. The broad CDR version was called v1, and the narrow CDR version was called v2 (Figure S4A–C). Thus, two novel humanized antibodies, consisting of heavy chain Hv1 or Hv2 and identical light chain Lt1 (Figure S4D) were generated and named Hv1Lt1 and Hv2Lt1, respectively (Figure S4E).

Characterization of hCKAP4 Ab

Hv1Lt1 and Hv2Lt1 clearly detected human, canine, and murine CKAP4 from the lysates of S2‐CP8, canine MDCK I, and murine Lewis lung carcinoma (LLC) cells with equal efficiency (Figure 2A). Binding affinities between CKAP4 Abs and CKAP4 ECD were measured using Biacore assays (Figure S5). Measured ka values for Abs were as follows: 1.25 × 105 for 3F11‐2B10, 1.50 × 104 for Hv1Lt1, and 3.79 × 104 for Hv2Lt1 (Table S1). The kD values were 1.80 × 10−3 for 3F11‐2B10, 1.14 × 10−5 for Hv1Lt1, and 1.70 × 10−6 for Hv2Lt1. The average KD values for Abs were 14.5 nM for 3F11‐2B10, 0.76 nM for Hv1Lt1, and 0.05 nM for Hv2Lt1, which indicated that hCKAP4 Abs had higher binding affinities to the CKAP4 ECD when compared with the original antibody, 3F11‐2B10.

When S2‐CP8 cell lysates were probed with 3F11‐2B10 or Hv1Lt1, CKAP4 detection efficiencies were similar (Figure S6A). Both hCKAP4 Abs immunoprecipitated endogenous CKAP4 from S2‐CP8 cell lysates similar to 3F11‐2B10 (Figure 2B), and immunocytochemically detected CKAP4, which was primarily located in the ER in S2‐CP8 cells (Figure 2C). No background signals were detected in S2‐CP8 cells in which CKAP4 protein were knocked out using the CRISPR/Cas9 system (S2‐CP8/CKAP4 KO), which confirmed that two hCKAP4 Abs specifically recognized CKAP4 (Figure 2C). Additionally, Hv1Lt1 detected cell surface CKAP4 proteins as well as 3F11‐2B10 when cell surface membranes were not permeabilized (Figure S6B). Both hCKAP4 Abs inhibited in vitro binding between DKK1 and CKAP4 in a dose‐dependent manner, comparable to 3F11‐2B10 (Figure 2D). When S2‐CP8 cells were treated with hCKAP4 Abs, AKT activity was inhibited (Figure 2E); however, S2‐CP8 cell sphere formation was significantly inhibited by Hv1Lt1 but not Hv2Lt1 when compared with control IgG (Figure 2F). From these sphere formation data, although both hCKAP4 Abs effectively inhibited AKT activation by blocking DKK1‐CKAP4 binding, we selected Hv1Lt1 for testing in vivo assay models.

Tumor suppressive and immunomodulative effects of hCKAP4 Ab

Two human pancreatic cancer cell lines (S2‐CP8 and MIA PaCa‐2 cells), which expressed high levels of DKK1 and plasma membrane CKAP4 (Figure S3B,C), were transplanted into immunodeficient mice, after which Hv1Lt1 was administered. Hv1Lt1 significantly inhibited in vivo xenograft tumor formation induced by both cell lines (Figure 3A,B; Figure S6C). Intraperitoneal (Figure 3A) and intravenous (Figure 3B; Figure S6C) Hv1Lt1 injections inhibited tumor growth with the similar efficiency, suggesting that Hv1Lt1 was effective regardless of the administration route.

Recently there are accumulating evidence that DKK1 may regulate anti‐tumor immunity in the tumor microenvironments (TME). 24 , 25 , 26 To elucidate the pharmacological activity of Hv1Lt1 in the TME using mesenchymal cell components, we established a syngeneic mouse model using immunocompetent mice. Previously, a genetically engineered mouse model (LSL‐KrasG12D/+;Trp53fl/fl/+;Elastase‐Cre [KPC mouse model]) was shown to induce tumors via pancreas‐specific mutant KRAS allele expression and Trp53 depletion, which recapitulated human pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. 27 Organoids established from KPC tumors (KPC organoids) can be sustainably cultured and transplanted into C57BL/6 immunocompetent mice either by subcutaneous or orthotopic routes. DKK1 and CKAP4 were ectopically expressed in KPC organoids (KPC/CKAP4/DKK1 organoids; Figure 4A). Activated DKK1‐CKAP4 signaling in organoids promoted organoid sphere growth (Figure 4B). Furthermore, when organoids ectopically expressed CKAP4 but not DKK1 (KPC/CKAP4) were cultured in the medium containing purified DKK1 with or without Hv1Lt1, DKK1 enhanced organoid sphere growth, which was inhibited by Hv1Lt1 (Figure 4C). KPC/CKAP4/DKK1 organoids were surgically transplanted into the pancreatic tail of C57BL/6 mice to form pancreatic tumors and successfully recapitulated human pancreatic cancer histology (Figure 4D). Hv1Lt1 inhibited tumor growth which was derived from KPC organoids to 60% (Figure 4D).

Bulk RNA‐seq analysis was performed on resected tumors, and hierarchical clustering and principal component analyses indicated a clear separation between Hv1Lt1‐treatment and control groups (Figure 5A,B). Gene analyses detected 421 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) (88 upregulated and 333 downregulated genes in Hv1Lt1‐treated tumors; Figure 5C; Table S2). Enrichment analyses highlighted pathways related to immune system processes and humoral immune responses which were significantly downregulated in Hv1Lt1‐treatment groups (Figure 5D). To further evaluate the immunomodulative responses by Hv1Lt1 treatment suggested by transcriptome analysis, immunohistochemical (IHC) study was performed using resected tumor specimens. Immune cell markers including CD4, CD8, CD11c, NCR1, CD11b, and CD19 were immunostained, and tumor‐infiltrating immune cells were evaluated (Figure 5E). KPC tumor lesions in each section were separated automatically and extracted using the HALO® platform, and positively stained cells in tumor lesions were counted automatically in a non‐biased manner (Figure S7A). The administration of Hv1Lt1 increased the infiltration of CD8+ cells (cytotoxic T cells) into tumor lesions compared with that of control IgG treatment (Figure 5E). The contents of CD11c+ cells, which represent the antigen‐presenting dendritic cells, also increased by Hv1Lt1, although that was not statistically significant (Figure 5E). The contents of CD4+, CD11b+ (myeloid cells including MDSCs), NCR1+ (NK cells), and CD19+ (B cells) cells were not affected (Figure S7B,C). These results suggest that Hv1Lt1 administration enhances anti‐tumor immune reaction through CD8+ T cells, thereby suppressing tumor progression.

Combination therapy of hCAKP4 Ab and other medications

In recent years, several molecular targeted drugs have been developed. Some new drugs are used as combination therapies with existing drugs to provide synergistic therapeutic effects. 28 , 29 Combination therapies often improve prognosis outcomes for cancer patients and decrease drug dosages, which help to reduce potential adverse effects. Therefore, the combination effects of Hv1Lt1 with conventional therapeutics were evaluated in vitro and in vivo. S2‐CP8 cell sphere formation was significantly inhibited by Hv1Lt1 and gemcitabine (Figure 6A) or nab‐PTX (Figure 6B), both of which are current standard therapeutics for human pancreatic cancer. 30 A combination of Hv1Lt1 and these drugs showed significantly strong inhibition compared with each monotherapy (Figure 6A,B). Then, xenograft tumors formed by S2‐CP8 cells were treated with Hv1Lt1 in combination with these therapeutics. Consistent with the in vitro results, their combination showed stronger tumor growth inhibition against S2‐CP8 cell‐induced xenograft tumor formation when compared with monotherapy (Figure 6C,D). These results support the possibility that hCKAP4 Ab combination therapies against human pancreatic cancer could be feasible in future clinical applications.

To evaluate non‐specific Hv1Lt1 toxicity, HeLa S3 cells, which expressed cell surface CKAP4 but not DKK1 (Figure S3B,C), were treated with Hv1Lt1. Hv1Lt1 showed no inhibitory effects on HeLa S3 cell proliferation in vitro (Figure S8A) and apoptosis was not observed in these cells (Figure S8B). To evaluate long‐term Hv1Lt1 in vivo toxicity, WT immunocompetent C57BL/6 mice were administered Hv1Lt1 or isotype control IgG intraperitoneally for 3 months. No obvious gross appearance or body weight changes were observed (Figure S8C). Similarly, resected major organs, including the heart, lung, liver, spleen, pancreas, stomach, small intestine, and kidney, showed neither gross abnormalities nor abnormal pathological histology (Figure S8E).

To detect the tissue‐deposit antibodies, tissue specimens were treated with anti‐human IgG secondary antibody, and positive immunostaining was not observed in the lung and small intestine (Figure S8F). As a positive control study, when the same tissues were permeabilized and stained with Hv1Lt1 prior to anti‐human IgG secondary antibody, endogenous CKAP4 proteins were clearly detected (Figure S8F). Therefore, no Hv1Lt1 was deposited and resident in organs of mice treated with Hv1Lt1 for long periods.

Blood samples (whole blood and serum samples) were collected on the day of sacrifice and tested. Complete blood cell counts and laboratory data showed no obvious abnormal values when compared with healthy mice and controls (administered control IgG), and all values were within normal ranges (Table S3). Thus, Hv1Lt1 as a novel hCKAP4 Ab may have anti‐cancer properties without toxicity and adverse effects.

DISCUSSION

In recent years, therapeutic antibody development for cancer treatment has advanced significantly. 31 , 32 , 33 In this study, we first engineered recombinant mCKAP4 Abs (mF18 and mFa31). Although these Abs did not bind to the Fc receptor in natural killer cells, they showed effective therapeutic effects against S2‐CP8 cell‐induced xenograft tumor growth comparable with the original mCKAP4 Ab, 3F11‐2B10. Thus, mCKAP4 Ab anti‐tumor effects were mediated by the inhibition of DKK1–CKAP4 signaling, rather than ADCC.

To advance the translational potential of our findings, we humanized this antibody, which is suitable for clinical applications in humans. In previous studies, humanized Abs have frequently shown significantly fewer binding affinities toward antigens when compared with original Abs, because transferring mouse CDRs into a human framework had deformed these regions. 23 , 34 Fortunately, both Hv1Lt1 and Hv2Lt1 demonstrated a high affinity for CKAP4, thereby preserving their capability to disrupt DKK1–CKAP4 interactions. This feature was paramount in ensuring that these therapeutic mechanisms remained consistent with our initial discoveries in the murine model. 7 Moreover, we confirmed that Hv1Lt1 inhibited in vivo tumor growth, a fundamental factor underpinning its therapeutic efficacy.

Using in vivo mouse models, our original mCKAP4 Ab, 3F11‐2B10, showed therapeutic effects in combination with gemcitabine against pancreatic cancer, 16 osimertinib against lung adenocarcinoma, 14 and lenvatinib against hepatocellular carcinoma. 15 When Hv1Lt1 was administered to tumor‐bearing immunodeficient mice in combination with gemcitabine or nab‐PTX, they enhanced tumor suppressive effects more efficiently when compared with monotherapy. This may reduce cytotoxic agent doses to mitigate potential adverse effects.

Chemoresistance is a major problem when treating advanced cancers; both intrinsic and acquired mechanisms occur in tumor cells to facilitate this resistance, which are mediated by PI3K/AKT pathway activation. 35 , 36 This pathway regulates fundamental cellular functions such as cell growth, survival, and stress responses. The administration of chemotherapeutic agents commonly up‐regulates this pathway, both in a transient or permanent manner. 37 Thus, therapeutic strategies targeting this pathway in combination with cytotoxic agents have been explored. PI3K inhibitors, such as wortmannin, LY294002, or the mTOR inhibitor rapamycin have undergone clinical trials, but due to their various adverse effects, poor solubility, and chemical instability, none have been exploited in clinical settings thus far. In contrast, Hv1Lt1 showed no significant side effects in control mice after 3 months’ administration. As DKK1–CKAP4 signaling activates AKT in various cancers, Hv1Lt1 combined with conventional chemotherapeutics may prevent chemoresistance to improve prognosis of cancer patients.

Using a pancreatic cancer model in immunocompetent mice, we confirmed that Hv1Lt1 exhibited anti‐tumor effects under clinically relevant conditions. Additionally, transcriptome and IHC analyses using tumors from this model revealed that Hv1Lt1 modulates immune‐related signaling pathways and induces the infiltration of cytotoxic T cells into the TME. Thus, Hv1Lt1 may enhance anti‐tumor immune reaction through cytotoxic T cells, thereby suppressing tumor progression. These results also suggest that DKK1–CKAP4 signaling promotes tumor formation not only cell autonomously but also non‐cell autonomously, which is consistent with several reports that DKK1 suppresses anti‐tumor immune responses in the TME. 24 , 25 , 26 In future research, Hv1Lt1 combination with other immunotherapeutic modalities (e.g., immune checkpoint inhibitors) may exert synergistic therapeutic effects. We posit that Hv1Lt1 could provide a platform for the development of clinical treatments for cancers expressing DKK1 and CKAP4.

CSTA and DDX24: potential biomarkers regulating ferroptosis in sepsis and their diagnostic value
Authors: Xingkai Shen, Yangli Jin, Kaili Yang, Xinye Qian, Jian Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSepsis is a critical condition involving dysregulated immune and metabolic responses. Ferroptosis has been implicated in its pathogenesis. However, the precise molecular mechanisms governing ferroptosis in sepsis remain unclear.MethodsMultiple transcriptomic datasets related to sepsis were comprehensively analyzed to screen potential hub genes. Differential expression profiling, weighted gene co-expression network construction, and three distinct machine learning approaches were applied in combination. To further elucidate the cellular distribution of these genes, single-cell RNA sequencing data were analyzed, and a corresponding miRNA–mRNA interaction network was established. The biological significance of the identified candidates was subsequently verified through in vitro functional experiments.ResultsTwo core genes were identified. CSTA was significantly upregulated in sepsis and positively correlated with neutrophil infiltration and M1 macrophage polarization. DDX24 was markedly downregulated and positively associated with CD8⁺ T cell and NK cell function. Both genes demonstrated strong diagnostic performance (AUC > 0.87) in validation cohorts. In vitro modulation of these genes revealed that CSTA knockdown or DDX24 overexpression enhanced cell viability and reversed dysregulation of ferroptosis-related proteins, including upregulation of GPX4 and SLC7A11 and downregulation of ACSL4.ConclusionsCSTA and DDX24 are central regulators of ferroptosis in sepsis, exhibiting diagnostic and therapeutic potential.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-026-02358-x.

Introduction

Sepsis is a severe clinical condition marked by organ dysfunction resulting from an imbalanced host response to infection [1]. Its most critical manifestation, septic shock, involves profound hypotension that compromises tissue perfusion and triggers hypoxia, a defining feature of shock. As a major contributor to deaths worldwide [2], sepsis continues to pose a serious threat to global public health. A 2017 global health assessment estimated that sepsis affected approximately 48.9 million individuals, with a fatality rate of 22.5%, contributing to nearly one-fifth of all deaths worldwide [3, 4]. Current management strategies focus on infection control [5, 6], hemodynamic stabilization in cases of circulatory shock [7, 8], and modulation of the host response, particularly in cases of septic shock [9]. Although considerable progress has been made in understanding the pathophysiology of sepsis in recent years, translating these insights into effective therapies remains a major challenge. This requires a more in-depth and comprehensive understanding of the immune mechanisms underlying sepsis. Identifying novel therapeutic targets in the study of sepsis immunopathology is critical, and the emerging field of cell death mechanisms may provide new breakthroughs to overcome current limitations.

Since its initial definition in 2012, ferroptosis has rapidly emerged as a major focus in programmed cell death research. Ferroptosis arises from iron-mediated lipid oxidative damage and is tightly controlled by several metabolic pathways within the cell, such as redox balance, iron handling, mitochondrial activity, and the metabolism of amino acids, lipids, and carbohydrates [10, 11]. Growing research indicates that ferroptosis plays a critical part in a range of inflammation-related disorders, including acute pancreatitis, colorectal malignancy, sepsis and inflammatory bowel disease [12–14]. Dysregulated expression of genes controlling ferroptosis may drive the onset and development of these disorders by altering immune homeostasis and disrupting lipid metabolic pathways [12, 15, 16]. In the context of sepsis, ferroptosis plays a pivotal role in mediating its onset and progression, as well as in causing damage to sepsis-related organs such as the heart, lungs, kidneys, and brain. It exacerbates bacterial infections and inflammatory cascades, impairs immune function, and contributes to metabolic dysregulation. Furthermore, ferroptosis inhibitors, metabolic pathway modulators, and several repurposed clinical drugs have demonstrated therapeutic potential in sepsis management [17]. However, current studies remain limited by unclear mechanistic understanding, discrepancies between animal models and clinical practice, and insufficient clinical translation. Clarifying how ferroptosis contributes to sepsis and uncovering reliable biomarkers is crucial for developing new therapeutic targets and advancing clinical management strategies.

This study identified and validated core genes systematically, which involved in ferroptosis during sepsis by integrating multi-omics data with machine learning approaches. We applied transcriptomic profiling together with weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to build the co-expression framework, and subsequently utilized three machine-learning approaches to screen for key genes linked to ferroptosis and the progression of sepsis. Single-cell RNA-seq analysis was additionally employed to map the specific cellular distribution of these candidate genes, while construction of a miRNA–mRNA regulatory network provided insights into their upstream regulatory mechanisms. Finally, the biological functions of the key genes in sepsis-related ferroptosis were experimentally validated in vitro. This study aims to offer a theoretical framework for understanding the molecular interplay between immune dysregulation and ferroptosis in sepsis and highlight potential molecular targets for early diagnosis and targeted therapeutic intervention.

Materials and methods

Data source and selection

Genes associated with ferroptosis were obtained from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) for use in the downstream analyses. The keyword “ferroptosis” was used for retrieval with the filters set to “All sections” and “All GeneCards gene categories”. Genes with a relevance score > 1 were included (retrieval date: 2025.10.25). Sepsis-related transcriptomic data were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Three datasets were included in this study: GSE95233 (51 sepsis patients and 22 controls) served as the training set, while GSE57065 (82 sepsis patients and 25 controls) and GSE28750 (10 sepsis patients, 11 postoperative cases, and 20 controls) were used as validation sets. In addition, the single-cell transcriptome dataset GSE167363 (10 sepsis patients and 2 controls) and the miRNA expression dataset GSE228540 (14 sepsis patients and 15 controls) were incorporated to determine the cellular localization of target genes and to explore their upstream regulatory mechanisms.

Recognition of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

DEGs between sepsis and control samples were extracted from the GSE95233, GSE57065, and GSE28750 datasets using the limma package in R (version 4.0.2). The sva package was applied to adjust for batch effects, followed by quantile normalization to standardize the raw data. Gene symbols were assigned to probe IDs based on the annotation files provided by the respective platforms. A gene was defined as differentially expressed if it fulfilled the criteria listed below: |Log2 fold change (FC)| > 0.5, Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05, and an average expression level > 1 in at least one group to exclude low-expression noise. Visualizations including heatmaps and volcano plots were created with the pheatmap and ggplot2 packages.

Enrichment analysis

Gene ontology (GO) and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses of DEGs were performed using the clusterProfiler R package (http://geneontology.org) and KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/kegg1.html) [18]. These analyses evaluated the biological significance of DEGs across cellular components (CC), biological processes (BP), molecular functions (MF), and signaling pathways. Results were visualized using bar charts.

WGCNA

The most significantly differentially expressed genes were used to build a gene co-expression network with the WGCNA R package (version 1.71). Soft-threshold powers from 1 to 20 were tested, and a value of 9 (R² = 0.9) was selected for network construction. The correlation matrix obtained was first converted into an adjacency matrix and subsequently transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM). Modules were detected using average linkage hierarchical clustering based on the TOM, with a minimum module size set to 30 genes, and closely related modules were subsequently combined. The associations between each module and disease status were evaluating by pearson correlation analysis. Modules showing the strongest positive and negative correlations were designated as key modules. Gene significance (GS) was calculated as the correlation between each gene and the trait under investigation, whereas module membership (MM) represented the correlation between a gene’s expression pattern and the first principal component of its respective module. Genes within key modules with P < 0.05 were considered highly correlated with both the disease and the module. For the large-scale correlation matrix underlying the co-expression network, P values were adjusted using the Bonferroni method to control for false positives due to multiple comparisons. An intersection of DEGs, ferroptosis-related genes, and genes from the hub brown module was conducted with the VennDiagram R package to pinpoint critical genes.

Selection of hub genes through machine learning approaches

Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, adaptive boosting (AdaBoost), and gradient boosting machine (GBM) were applied to rank genes based on their feature importance. The glmnet and adabag R packages were employed to carry out LASSO regression and AdaBoost analyses, respectively. In the LASSO model, the optimal regularization parameter (λ) was automatically selected through cross-validation based on the minimum mean cross-validated error according to the standard glmnet procedure. GBM, an ensemble learning approach, was employed to model complex nonlinear relationships by iteratively constructing additive models that minimize residual errors from previous iterations. To reduce the risk of model overfitting, a nested cross-validation framework based on 5-fold cross-validation was applied during model construction and evaluation. Within the inner loop, model parameters were optimized, while the outer loop was used to assess model performance. For the GBM and AdaBoost models, the number of decision trees was set to 200. The intersection results were visualized using a Venn diagram.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves analysis

For the GSE57065 and GSE28750 datasets, ROC curves and corresponding area under the curve (AUC) values were generated using the pROC R package (version 1.18.4). This analysis assessed the diagnostic potential of the CSTA and DDX24 genes in sepsis, highlighting their possible clinical relevance. An AUC value above 0.7 was interpreted as reflecting notable diagnostic performance [19].

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

In GSEA (version 3.0), sepsis samples from GSE95233 were categorized into high- and low-expression groups based on each biomarker’s median expression, followed by KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, with significance defined as P ≤ 0.05.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune infiltration analysis was performed on the GSE95233 dataset. CIBERSORT was applied to assess the cellular composition and relative proportions of immune cells within a mixed population using linear support vector regression. Results achieving P < 0.05 were included in subsequent analyses. The distribution of immune cells was presented using bar charts, and differences between groups were illustrated through violin plots plots created with the ggplot2 R package. Using the HumanPrimaryCellAtlas reference data within the singleR and celldex packages, the expression patterns of core genes were systematically annotated.

Single-cell data processing

ScRNA-seq data were analyzed through an online platform that integrates multiple analytical tools (http://biowinford.site:3838/trial/) [20]. A Seurat object was generated for each dataset, after which quality control procedures were applied. Cells were retained if they expressed between 200 and 6,000 genes (nFeature_RNA) and had mitochondrial gene expression below 20% (percent.mt). Normalization of the data was carried out using the NormalizeData function. Genes exhibiting high variability were detected with FindVariableFeatures employing the variance-stabilizing transformation method, and the 2,000 most variable genes were subsequently selected [21]. Prior to dimensionality reduction, expression values were scaled with ScaleData. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed, and the significance of principal components was assessed using JackStraw and ScoreJackStraw functions. Clustering of cells was performed at a resolution of 0.3, followed by visualization of dimensionality reduction using uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP). Cell clusters were assigned to distinct immune populations by referencing established marker genes and the CellMarker 2.0 database for cell type annotation [22].

MiRNA regulatory network construction

Core gene upstream regulatory miRNAs were predicted using the miRWalk database. Differentially expressed miRNAs from the GSE228540 dataset were intersected with predicted miRNAs, and regulatory networks for upregulated and downregulated miRNAs were constructed to elucidate post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms.

Cell culture

Mouse RAW264.7 macrophages was purchased from ATCC (Manassas, USA). Cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (31800, Solarbio, Chian) containing 1% penicillin–streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum, and cultured at 37 °C under a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Cells were stimulated with 100 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (P6741, Solarbio, China) for 48 h to induce differentiation into M0 macrophages. Differentiation was characterized by morphological changes, including a transition from suspension to adherent growth and increased cell volume. cells were treated with 100 ng/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS; BC2340, Solarbio, China) for 6 h to generate an M1-type septic macrophage model. The control group consisted of M0 macrophages treated with an equal volume of normal saline. For inhibitors, cells were pretreated for 2 h with VX-765 (20 µM, S2228, Selleck Chemicals, USA), Z-VAD-FMK (10 µM, HY-16658B, MCE, USA), Ferrostatin-1 (10 µM, HY-100579, MCE, USA), or Necrostatin-1 (10 µM, HY-15760, MCE, USA) prior to LPS stimulation [23, 24].

Cell transfection

To ensure the specificity and efficiency of gene manipulation, siRNA concentrations were first optimized using a titration gradient. The lowest concentration that achieved effective gene silencing without inducing cytotoxicity (5 pmol) was selected for subsequent experiments. Two specific siRNAs targeting CSTA were synthesized (siRNA#1: 5′-TCCAGAAATCCAGGAGATTGTTG-3′; siRNA#2: 5′-CAGCTTGAAGAAAAAACAAATGA-3′; Thermo Fisher Scientific). SiRNAs were prepared in culture medium and transfected into cells via Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) according to the provided protocol, followed by incubation for 24 h. For DDX24 overexpression, a pDC315-DDX24 recombinant adenoviral vector (Gene Pharmaceutical, Shanghai) was employed as described previously [25]. Cells (1 × 10⁵ cells/well) were infected with adenovirus at a multiplicity of infection of 50 in serum-free RPMI-1640 medium and incubated for 12 h. The knockdown efficiency of CSTA and overexpression efficiency of DDX24 were subsequently confirmed by Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR).

Flow cytometry analysis

The primary reagents used included FITC-conjugated CD86 antibody (105005, BioLegend), APC-conjugated F4/80 antibody (123115, BioLegend), and red blood cell lysis buffer (E-CK-A105, Yilayer, Wuhan). Cells were resuspended at 1 × 10⁶ per tube and centrifuged at 1000×g for 5 min. Cells were incubated with F4/80 (1.2 µL; 123115, Biolegend) and CD86 (2 µL; 105005, Biolegend) antibodies at 4 °C for 30 min. After a wash step, red blood cell lysis was performed using 2 mL lysis buffer for (E-CK-A105, Yilary, Wuhan) 10 min at 4 °C, followed by another centrifugation step. Cells were analyzed by flow cytometry (E97501093, BD Biosciences, USA), and the data were subsequently processed with FlowJo v10.

Cell viability assay

Using a CCK-8 assay (C0038, Biyun Tian, China), cell viability was measured. Cells were seeded at 2,000 per well in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. After adding CCK-8 reagent, the cells were further incubated for 1 h, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (DNM-960, Prang, China).

Malondialdehyde (MDA) assay

MDA levels in the cell supernatant were measured using an MDA assay kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions (S0131S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The absorbance was measured at 532 nm using a microplate reader (DNM-960, Prang, China).

RT-qPCR

RNA extraction was performed with TriQuick Reagent (Solarbio, Beijing), and cDNA was synthesized using the SureScript RTase kit (GeneCopoeia, USA). RT-PCR reactions were carried out with SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Servicebio, Wuhan) under the cycling program: 95 °C for 30 s, 95 °C for 15 s, 60 °C for 30 s, for a total of 40 cycles. The primer sequence is as follows: CSTA forward: 5′-GTCGCCCAGAGCAATTTTGAG-3′; CSTA reverse: 5′-TCTCCCCGGTCAGGAAAAGTT-3′; DDX24 forward: 5′-AGATGTGTCAGCTTGGAAGGA-3′, DDX24 reverse: 5′-GGTGCAGAGAAGCCTAGAAAG-3′; GAPDH forward: 5′-ACAACTTTGGTATCGTGGAAGG-3′; GAPDH reverse: 5′-GCCATCACGCCACAGTTTC-3′.

Western blotting analysis

Cells were lysed with RIPA buffer (R002, Solarbio, Beijing), and the total protein content was subsequently quantified. Proteins at equal concentrations were resolved using 10% polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and subsequently transferred onto PVDF membranes (IPVH00010, Millipore, USA). Membranes were blocked using 5% skim milk and incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibodies: CSTA (1:1000; DF7381, Affinity Biosciences, USA), DDX24 (1:1000; 15769-1-AP, Sanying, Wuhan), GPX4 (1:1000; DF6701, Qinke, Jiangsu), SLC7A11 (1:1000; BM5318, Boster, Wuhan), ACSL4 (1:1000; 22401-1-AP, Sanying, Wuhan ), and GAPDH (1:1000; A00227-1, Boster, Wuhan). The membranes were then incubated with an HRP-conjugated sheep anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (ab7090, Abcam, UK), and protein bands were detected using the FluorChem FC2 imaging system (Alpha, Shanghai).

Statistical analysis

All statistical tests were performed using GraphPad Prism software (versions 9.0). Values for each sample were from at least 3 biologically independent experiments with at least 3 technical replicates. All data in this study are displayed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). For comparisons between two groups, a paired t-test was applied to data that met the assumptions of normality and variance homogeneity. Comparisons of three or more groups were analyzed using one-way ANOVA, with Tukey’s HSD post hoc test employed for subsequent multiple comparisons. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Significance levels were indicated as follows: *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001.

Results

Screening and functional enrichment analysis of DEGs

Using the sepsis-related transcriptome datasets GSE95233, GSE57065, and GSE28750 from the GEO database, a total of 635 DEGs were identified. Among these, 400 genes exhibited upregulation, while 235 showed downregulation (Fig. 1A). Heatmap visualization of the top 50 DEGs revealed a distinct separation between normal and sepsis samples, indicating significant transcriptional alterations associated with sepsis (Fig. 1B).

GO enrichment analysis demonstrated that the DEGs were primarily enriched in immune-related functional categories across the three ontology domains: BP, CC, and MF (Fig. 1C). In the BP category, enriched terms included regulation of immune response, intercellular communication, and leukocyte activation. CC terms were mainly associated with specific granules, lamellipodia, focal adhesions, and chromosomal regions. MF terms were enriched for enzyme regulatory activities, particularly peptidase and endopeptidase inhibition and kinase activation. KEGG pathway analysis further revealed that these DEGs were significantly enriched in several key signaling pathways (Fig. 1D). These included cancer-related pathways (such as renal cell carcinoma and PD-1 checkpoint signaling), immune and infection-related pathways (such as Epstein–Barr virus infection and cytoplasmic DNA sensing), cell death pathways (such as necroptosis and ferroptosis), and metabolic stress pathways (such as glutathione metabolism and HIF-1 signaling).

Identification of key modules and candidate genes in sepsis based on WGCNA

After confirming the absence of outlier samples, network topology was evaluated under various soft threshold powers. A soft threshold power of β = 9 (R² = 0.9) was selected to ensure the network conformed to a scale-free distribution (Fig. 2A, B). Hierarchical clustering using dynamic tree cutting identified 12 distinct gene modules (Fig. 2C). The brown module exhibited the highest positive correlation with sepsis (cor = 0.81; P = 2 × 10⁻³⁰) (Fig. 2D). Notably, the brown module contained 2,962 genes. Furthermore, GS and MM within the brown module were highly correlated (cor = 0.84; P < 1 × 10⁻²⁰⁰), indicating a high degree of internal consistency and strong disease relevance (Fig. 2E). Given that the KEGG enrichment analysis highlighted ferroptosis as a significantly enriched pathway, we further explored its potential role in sepsis by intersecting the ferroptosis-related gene set, the brown module genes, and all DEGs. This analysis yielded 43 overlapping candidate genes, which were subjected to subsequent in-depth functional and mechanistic analyses (Fig. 2F).

Identification of key diagnostic markers for sepsis using machine learning

To identify robust diagnostic biomarkers from the 43 candidate genes, three independent machine learning algorithms were applied for feature selection. The AdaBoost algorithm identified nine key genes (Fig. 3A), while LASSO regression analysis yielded ten genes (Fig. 3B, C). GBM analysis further identified eight important genes, among which ANXA3 and DDX24 exhibited the highest feature importance (Fig. 3D). The intersection of the three algorithmic outputs revealed two core hub genes, CSTA and DDX24, as the most robust diagnostic markers for sepsis (Fig. 3E). The diagnostic performance of CSTA and DDX24 was subsequently validated using ROC curve analysis in two independent datasets (GSE57065 and GSE28750). Both genes demonstrated strong discriminatory ability, with AUC values exceeding the 0.7 diagnostic threshold. In detail, CSTA attained AUC values of 0.915 and 0.875, while DDX24 reached 0.997 and 0.990 in the corresponding datasets. These findings suggest that both genes possess promising diagnostic potential for distinguishing sepsis from normal controls (Fig. 3F, G).

Differential expression and GSEA of core genes

In all three datasets, CSTA was consistently and significantly upregulated, whereas DDX24 was markedly downregulated in sepsis samples compared with controls (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A–C). These consistent trends across multiple cohorts underscore the robustness of both genes as potential diagnostic markers for sepsis. GSEA was subsequently conducted to explore the biological pathways associated with the high expression of each core gene. Elevated CSTA expression was significantly enriched in pathways related to antigen processing and presentation, autoimmune thyroid disease, cell adhesion molecules, graft-versus-host disease, and primary immunodeficiency (Fig. 4D). Similarly, high DDX24 expression was enriched in pathways involved in antigen processing and presentation, cell adhesion molecules, graft-versus-host disease, primary immunodeficiency, and the intestinal IgA-producing network (Fig. 4E). These results suggest that both genes may participate in immune regulation and antigen presentation processes during sepsis.

Immune infiltration analysis and immunomodulatory roles of core genes in sepsis

Immune infiltration analysis based on the GSE95233 dataset revealed that the immune microenvironment of sepsis samples was predominantly composed of neutrophils, with distinct heterogeneity observed among immune cell subsets across samples (Fig. 5A). Correlation analysis among immune cell types demonstrated a complex intercellular regulatory network, in which M2 macrophages exhibited a strong positive correlation with activated dendritic cells (DCs) (cor = 0.81) (Fig. 5B). Further comparison between sepsis and healthy control groups indicated that sepsis markedly alters immune cell composition. Compared with healthy controls, sepsis patients exhibited significantly decreased infiltration levels of CD8⁺ T cells, resting natural killer (NK) cells, naïve B cells and resting DCs significantly decreased (P < 0.05), whereas those of plasma cells, γδ T cells, M0 macrophages, monocytes, activated DCs, neutrophils, and eosinophils significantly increased (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5C). These findings suggest that sepsis induces significant alterations in immune cell composition, reflecting a complex immune dysregulation.

Correlation analysis between the expression of the two core genes and immune cell infiltration further demonstrated their potential immunomodulatory roles. CSTA expression was positively correlated with myeloid-derived immune cells, including plasma cells, monocytes, M0 macrophages, and neutrophils (P < 0.001), but negatively correlated with lymphocyte subsets such as resting NK cells, CD8⁺ T cells, resting DCs, naïve B cells, and activated DCs (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5D). In contrast, DDX24 expression exhibited positive correlations with CD8⁺ T cells, resting NK cells, DCs, and naïve CD4⁺ T cells (P < 0.001), while exhibiting inverse correlations with neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and plasma cells (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5E).

Cellular localization analysis of core genes based on single-cell transcriptomics

To investigate the cellular localization of the core genes in greater detail, scRNA-seq data from the GSE167363 dataset were examined. We began by selecting the top 2,000 genes with the highest variability and emphasized the ten most variable among them (Fig. 6A). PCA demonstrated that the cell populations were evenly distributed across samples (Fig. 6B). Cells were clustered into 16 groups using UMAP for dimensionality reduction, and these clusters were classified into six major immune cell populations: platelets, T cells, monocytes, B cells, neutrophils, and NK cells (Fig. 6C, D). Cellular localization analysis revealed distinct expression patterns of the two core genes. CSTA was predominantly expressed in monocytes, suggesting a potential role in monocyte activation and differentiation during sepsis (Fig. 6E–H). In contrast, DDX24 exhibited a broader distribution, with high expression in T cells, B cells, monocytes, and NK cells, but minimal expression in platelets and neutrophils (Fig. 6E–H). These findings indicate that CSTA and DDX24 may participate in immune regulation through different cellular contexts, reflecting their distinct contributions to the immune microenvironment in sepsis.

Construction of the sepsis-related miRNA–mRNA regulatory network

To investigate upstream regulatory mechanisms of the core genes, miRNA expression profiles from the GSE228540 sepsis dataset were analyzed, and differentially expressed miRNAs were identified (Fig. 6I). Integrating these dysregulated miRNAs with the ferroptosis-related core genes (CSTA and DDX24), a regulatory network linking miRNA-mRNA was established using Cytoscape. The resulting network comprised 19 potential regulatory interactions between 15 miRNAs and the two core genes (Fig. 6J), providing insight into post-transcriptional regulation that may modulate ferroptosis and immune responses in sepsis.

CSTA and DDX24 affect the progression of sepsis by regulating ferroptosis

To validate the immune characteristics of the sepsis model, flow cytometry was performed to assess the expression of the M1 macrophage marker CD86. The proportion of CD86⁺ cells in the sepsis group (47.53%) was significantly higher than in controls (2.49%) (P < 0.001), confirming a robust increase in M1 macrophages and successful establishment of the sepsis model (Fig. 7A–B). RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses indicated that CSTA expression was markedly upregulated (P<0.001), whereas DDX24 expression was significantly downregulated at both mRNA and protein levels in the sepsis group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 7C–F).

To investigate the role of CSTA and DDX24 in ferroptosis, cell lines with CSTA knockdown or DDX24 overexpression were constructed (Fig. 7G). To further delineate the specific cell death modality involved, we employed pharmacological inhibitors targeting ferroptosis (Fer-1), apoptosis (z-VAD-FMK), necroptosis (Necrostatin-1), and pyroptosis (VK-765). Cell viability was assessed by CCK-8 assay, and lipid peroxidation was evaluated by measuring MDA levels, a key end-product of lipid peroxidation commonly used as a ferroptosis marker. Notably, Fer-1 treatment almost completely reversed sepsis-induced viability loss, bringing it closest to control levels, whereas the other inhibitors showed no significant effect. Consistently, MDA levels were elevated in sepsis and significantly reduced by CSTA knockdown, DDX24 overexpression, and Fer-1, but not by other inhibitors (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7I-H). Western blot analysis of ferroptosis-related proteins demonstrated that sepsis decreased the expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11 while increasing ACSL4 levels (P < 0.001). Both CSTA knockdown and DDX24 overexpression effectively reversed these alterations (Fig. 7J).

Discussion

Sepsis remains a major challenge in critical care medicine worldwide due to its high mortality and complex pathophysiology. Identifying novel biomarkers is therefore of paramount importance. In this study, by integrating multi-omics data with experimental validation, we identified that CSTA and DDX24 may play opposing roles in the ferroptosis pathway during sepsis, suggesting their potential involvement in the regulation of this process.

We found that CSTA expression was positively correlated with neutrophils and M0 macrophages in sepsis, which may be mechanistically linked to its role as a cysteine protease inhibitor. In macrophages, cathepsin B has been shown to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, promoting caspase-1 activation and interleukin (IL)-1β maturation. Elevated IL-1β subsequently amplifies proinflammatory gene expression via the NF-κB pathway, driving macrophages toward an M1 phenotype. By modulating intracellular cathepsin activity, CSTA may influence the activation threshold of this pathway, thereby shaping the inflammatory phenotype of macrophages [26, 27].

Neutrophil function, particularly the generation and stability of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), may be affected by CSTA, with these processes being finely regulated by proteolytic enzymes and intracellular enzymatic pathways. Altered protease activity can modify the threshold for NETs release and their proinflammatory properties. Upregulation of CSTA could facilitate NETs formation or maintenance by fine-tuning enzyme activities, thereby amplifying tissue damage and recruiting additional immune cells during systemic inflammation [28, 29]. Moreover, cystatin family proteins have demonstrated broad immunomodulatory effects in models of infection and inflammation. For example, cystatin-mediated regulation of host protease activity can attenuate or exacerbate systemic inflammation in sepsis and other infectious conditions [30]. Thus, the elevated expression of CSTA in sepsis patients may not only reflect a heightened inflammatory state but also actively contribute to inflammation amplification through mechanistic feedback.

In contrast to the pro-inflammatory role of CSTA, DDX24 was downregulated in sepsis, and its expression showed a positive correlation with NK cell and CD8⁺ T cell functions, indicating a potential role in preserving lymphocyte effector activity. A As part of the DEAD-box RNA helicase family, DDX24 regulates cell cycle progression and stress responses by binding specific mRNAs and modulating their stability, providing a molecular basis for its role in T cell function [31]. At the immune regulation level, DDX24 has been implicated in cytoplasmic double-stranded RNA recognition and the regulation of type I interferon signaling, highlighting its role in balancing antiviral responses and inflammation [32]. In late-stage sepsis, T cell exhaustion is common, characterized by reduced effector cytokine production, upregulated inhibitory receptors, and impaired post-transcriptional regulation. Loss of DDX24 expression may compromise mRNA stability critical for T cell effector functions, thereby contributing to this exhausted phenotype [33–35]. Similarly, NK cell function relies on intact RNA metabolism to sustain production of cytotoxic molecules and cytokines [36]. Therefore, the downregulation of DDX24 in sepsis may disrupt RNA homeostasis in lymphocytes, impairing effector functions and exacerbating immunosuppression.

Ferroptosis, characterized by iron-mediated lipid peroxidation, plays a pivotal role in mediating immune dysfunction and tissue injury in the context of sepsis. Key ferroptosis regulators, including ACSL4, GPX4, and SLC7A11, are significantly dysregulated in sepsis patients, and they centrally control lipid peroxidation and cell survival. SLC7A11 mediates extracellular cysteine uptake for glutathione synthesis, GPX4 uses glutathione to reduce membrane phospholipid peroxides, and ACSL4 incorporates polyunsaturated fatty acids into membrane phospholipids, enhancing oxidative sensitivity. Disruption of these signaling pathways has been reported to drive immune dysfunction and organ damage in sepsis [37–39]. In this study, we observed that CSTA upregulation reduced cellular antioxidant capacity by inhibiting GPX4 and SLC7A11 while promoting ACSL4 activity, thereby facilitating the incorporation of polyunsaturated fatty acids into membranes. This effect accelerated ferroptosis in neutrophils and pro-inflammatory macrophages, amplifying inflammation through the release of damage-associated molecules [40]. Conversely, restoration of DDX24 expression increased GPX4 and SLC7A11 levels and decreased ACSL4 activity, suggesting a protective role for DDX24 in CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells by maintaining mRNA stability or regulating downstream antioxidant gene expression [32, 41].

This study has several limitations. (1) The samples and models were relatively limited, relying mainly on public database data, and no stratified analyses were conducted across clinical subgroups such as mild versus severe sepsis or different infection sources. Experimental validation was conducted only in THP-1 macrophage models, without support from animal models or clinical samples, making it difficult to fully capture the complex immune and metabolic microenvironment of sepsis. (2) The direct interactions between miRNAs and their target genes were not verified, and the molecular mechanisms by which CSTA and DDX24 regulate immune cell function and the ferroptosis pathway were not fully explored. (3) Although our Western blot results suggested that these genes may influence ferroptosis-related proteins such as GPX4 and SLC7A11, the precise downstream regulatory mechanisms remain unclear. In particular, direct molecular interactions or regulatory pathways—such as potential protein–protein interactions, RNA-binding events, or inflammasome-related signaling—were not examined using techniques such as co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP), RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP), or functional rescue experiments with ferroptosis-specific modulators. Future research should confirm these results in larger and more heterogeneous clinical populations, while systematically exploring the causal links between iron accumulation and immune dysregulation using animal models. Additionally, multi-omics integration and mechanistic experiments are needed to further elucidate the molecular networks linking CSTA, DDX24, ferroptosis, and immune cell function.

In conclusion, this study highlights the bidirectional roles of CSTA and DDX24 in iron overload and immune regulation during sepsis. Upregulation of CSTA may exacerbate iron-dependent oxidative stress, inflammation, ferroptosis, and tissue damage, whereas downregulation of DDX24 impairs ferroptosis resistance and adaptive immune function. These findings not only clarify the roles of core ferroptosis-related genes in sepsis pathology but also identify CSTA and DDX24 as potential diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, offering a novel theoretical basis for precision intervention in sepsis.

Conclusion

In summary, this study systematically identified and validated the pivotal roles of CSTA and DDX24 in sepsis pathogenesis by integrating multi-omics data with machine learning. CSTA was significantly upregulated in sepsis and positively correlated with neutrophil infiltration and M1 macrophage polarization, promoting ferroptosis by modulating key regulators such as GPX4, SLC7A11, and ACSL4. In contrast, DDX24 was downregulated and positively associated with CD8⁺ T cell and NK cell function, suggesting a protective role in maintaining lymphocyte effector activity and inhibiting ferroptosis. Both genes demonstrated strong diagnostic potential (AUC > 0.87) in independent validation cohorts, and their cell type-specific expression patterns were confirmed by single-cell transcriptomic analysis. Functional experiments further showed that modulating CSTA or DDX24 expression significantly affected cell viability and reversed the dysregulation of ferroptosis-related proteins. Overall, these results emphasize the pivotal functions of CSTA and DDX24 in sepsis-associated ferroptosis and offer a solid theoretical and experimental basis for the development of early diagnostic biomarkers and targeted treatment strategies.

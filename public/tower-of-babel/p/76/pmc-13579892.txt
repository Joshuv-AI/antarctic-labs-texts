Mismatch-time ratio combined with ASPECTS: a novel indicator of favorable prognosis in multimodal ct prediction of anterior circulation large vessel occlusion
Authors: Yan Huang, Chu Zhou, Rongrong Liu, Wei Li, Qiyang Yuan, He Ma, Yukun Wang, Ruicheng Zhang, Chongke Zhong, Ruoyu Qin, Fenfang Gao, Yanbo Cheng, Shiguang Zhu, Deqin Geng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients with acute ischemic stroke from large vessel occlusion (AIS-LVO) require computed tomography(CT) at stroke centers to determine eligibility for endovascular therapy (EVT).However, the performance of prognostic prediction methods using multimodal CT remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the significance of the mismatch–time ratio (MTR) of CT perfusion imaging (CTP) parameters of conjunction with Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score (ASPECTS) in non-contrast CT (NCCT) for predicting short-term prognosis following EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO.MethodsPatients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO who underwent EVT at the stroke specialty alliance of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University from February 2023 to October 2024 were retrospectively were retrospectively included. The mismatch ratio defined by CTP parameters and the ratio of time from onset to CTP (T) were designated as the MTR. Based on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at 90 days after EVT, patients were categorized into a favorable prognosis group (mRS ≤ 3) and an adverse prognosis group (mRS > 3). Independent prognostic factors for favorable prognosis at 90 days post-EVT in patients with AIS-LVO were determined by univariate logistic regression and stepwise regression analyses. The predictive performance of MTR alone and the combined predictive efficacy of MTR with ASPECTS was compared via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis for prognosis at 90 days post-EVT.Results105 patients were enrolled, comprising 44 in the favorable prognosis group and 61 in the adverse prognosis group. Stepwise regression analysis revealed that MTR (OR = 2.072, P = 0.015, 95% CI: 1.155–3.719) and ASPECTS (OR = 2.363, P = 0.001, 95% CI: 1.445–3.866) were independent predictors of favorable prognosis. ROC curve analysis showed that MTR alone predicted a positive prognosis at 90 days post-EVT for AIS-LVO, with an AUC of 0.778 (P < 0.001). The combination of MTR and ASPECTS predicted a favorable prognosis at 90 days post-EVT for AIS-LVO, yielding an AUC of 0.828 (P < 0.001).ConclusionMTR serves as a novel imaging biomarker indicating positive short-term prognosis for EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO. The integration of MTR with ASPECTS enhances the prognostic accuracy of EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO.

Introduction

Ischemic stroke accounts for 86.8% of all stroke classifications, with almost 10 million new cases documented annually [1–4]. Among them, anterior circulation large vessel occlusion stroke (AC-LVO) is a critical subtype of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) that primarily affects the internal carotid artery (ICA), middle cerebral artery (MCA-M1/M2 segment), or anterior cerebral artery (ACA-A1/A2 segment) [5, 6]. In the last 10 years, several randomized controlled trials have progressively broadened the criteria for AC-LVO thrombectomy, providing effective treatment options for patients with large core infarction and ultra-early time window [7–14]. However, a considerable percentage of patients subjected to thrombectomy continue to suffer from permanent impairment or mortality [15–17] Thus, the methods and possible mechanisms for predicting clinical outcomes of EVT through rapid computed tomography (CT) examinations are of significant interest. A personalized assessment of factors influencing the prognosis of EVT may facilitate prompt and appropriate intervention, potentially yielding significant improvements in patients’ quality of life and reducing familial and social responsibilities.

ASPECTS [18] holds significant value in predicting the prognosis of patients with AIS following EVT. The likelihood of achieving mRS [19] 0–1 and mRS 0–2 at 90 days diminished by 2.7% and 1.9%, respectively, with a reduction of 1 point in ASPECTS, whereas mortality increased by 1.9% [20]. CTP served as a crucial imaging modality in the DAWN and DEFUSE 3 trials, enabling the identification of patients eligible for EVT by evaluating the infarction core volume and ischemic penumbra volume, combined with mismatch criteria. The infarction core volume and ischemic core growth rate (IGR) in CTP-related parameters have been established as predictors of prognosis following EVT [21, 22]. Certain scholars assert that a high mismatch ratio is markedly correlated with improved 90-day mRS scores, and the location-based mismatch ratio can mitigate inaccuracies resulting from simple volume measurements, offering higher predictive value than simple volume measurements alone [23, 24].

However, the prediction parameters of prognosis after EVT for patients with AIS based on CTP exhibited considerable limitations; core infarction was overestimated, the concept of ghost infarction core was introduced, and the significant heterogeneity of IGR among different patients and brain regions indicated that predictions based solely on parameter values may lack reliability [25–27]. Considering the advantages and disadvantages of the abovementioned CTP parameters, as well as the principle of “time is brain,” this study proposed a novel prediction factor for a favorable prognosis at 90 days after EVT: mismatch–time ratio (MTR). MTR is the ratio of the mismatch ratio defined by CTP parameters to the time from onset to CTP. MTR is calculated as follows: MTR=Tmax > 6 s volume / (rCBF < 30% volume×time from onset to CTP). In theory, MTR can comprehensively assess the influence of mismatch ratio and onset time on the prognosis of EVT, thereby demonstrating the concept of a “time window” in conjunction with a “tissue window” for thrombectomy. To date, only a few methods have utilized multimodal CT to predict the prognosis of EVT. This study further explored the predictive value of MTR combined with ASPECTS in multimodal CT for the short-term prognosis of patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO.

Methods

Research object

This retrospective analysis encompassed patients who underwent EVT in the stroke specialty alliance of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University from February 2023 to October 2024. These patients included 86 cases from Xuzhou Medical University Affiliated Hospital, 5 cases from Xuzhou Medical University Second Hospital, 5 case from Xuzhou First People’s Hospital, 1 case from Xuzhou Central Hospital, and 8 case from Lianyungang First People’s Hospital, resulting in a total of 105 cases. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University (XYFY2023-KL456-01). All participating centers completed ethical record-filing procedures through the hospital’s ethics committee. All participants provided informed consent.

Imaging data

Patients were admitted for a comprehensive CT examination, primarily comprising NCCT, CTA, and CTP. The CTP imaging protocol was performed using a 256-slice spiral CT scanner (GE Revolution), focusing on a 6-cm section adjacent to the basal ganglia. Scanning parameters were set at a tube voltage of 80 kV and a tube current of 80 mAs. A bolus of 50 mL of iodinated contrast medium (ioversol, 350 mg iodine/mL) was administered intravenously via the median cubital vein at 5.0 mL/s using a high-pressure injector, succeeded by a 40-mL saline flush. Following a 5-second delay, a continuous dynamic acquisition was performed over 60 s with a sampling interval of 1 s, yielding a total of 480 image frames.Image reconstruction was conducted with a standard slice thickness of 5 mm, an interval of 5 mm, and a field of view measuring 200 × 200 mm. Post-processing of brain perfusion data was executed on the Philips IntelliSpace Portal workstation (version 6.0) using dedicated CT perfusion software. Perfusion parameter maps—including cerebral blood volume, cerebral blood flow, mean transit time, and time to peak—were generated and displayed as pseudo-color images.Regions of interest were delineated on perfusion maps while avoiding vascular structures and calcifications. The software automatically generated perfusion parameters for both the lesion and the contralateral mirror region, which were then compared to facilitate quantitative analysis.

The following CTP quantitative parameters were recorded: rCBF < 30%; Tmax > 4, 6, 8, and 10 s volume. The IGR was calculated as follows: rCBF < 30% volume/time from onset to CTP; mismatch ratio: Tmax > 6 s volume/rCBF < 30% volume; MTR: Tmax > 6 s volume/(rCBF < 30% volume×time from onset to CTP); mismatch-4 s ratio (M4/T): Tmax > 4 s volume/(rCBF < 30% volume×time from onset to CTP); mismatch-8 s ratio (M8/T): Tmax > 8 s volume/(rCBF < 30% volume×time from onset to CTP); and mismatch-10 s ratio (M10/T): Tmax > 10 s volume/(rCBF < 30% volume×time from onset to CTP) (Table 2). The VanSwieten score [28] and ASPECT score were derived via NCCT. The VanSwieten scoring scale is a CT imaging-based approach utilized for the assessment of white matter lesions. ASPECTS is a 10-point scale used to semi-quantitatively identify early ischemic alterations within the vascular area of the middle cerebral artery. From a baseline score of 10, one point is deducted for any identified early ischemic change (e.g., focal edema or parenchymal hypoattenuation) in any of the specified locations. An ASPECTS value of 10 denotes a normal CT scan, whereas a score of 0 reflects diffuse ischemia throughout the entire middle cerebral artery region.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS26.0, and graphs were created using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to evaluate the normality of continuous variables. Normally distributed data were presented as mean±standard deviation (mean ± SD) and compared using the independent-samples t-test. Data that did not follow a normal distribution were reported as median (interquartile range) (M[P25,P75]) and analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages (n [%]), and group comparisons were conducted using the Pearson χ2 test. Stepwise regression analyses were used to calculate adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) to account for potential confounders. MTR and ASPECTS were evaluated as independent predictors of favorable short-term outcomes in patients undergoing EVT. Candidate covariates for multivariable adjustment were selected based on baseline variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis.The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to evaluate the prognostic efficacy of MTR, M4/T, M8/T, infarction core volume, IGR, ASPECTS, and combined indicators. Spearman correlation analysis was employed to assess the potential factors affecting MTR.

Results

Patient characteristics

Following the exclusion of patients with incomplete data (n = 8); patients with severe dysfunction of the heart, liver, kidneys, and other organs (n = 3); patients with unsuccessful vascular recanalization (mTICI < 2b) (n = 12); and patients lost to follow up (n = 3), a total of 105 patients were ultimately included. Among them, 44 patients were categorized in the favorable prognosis group (mRS ≤ 3), whereas 61 patients were placed in the adverse prognosis group. The study cohort comprised 73 males (69.5%) and 32 females (30.5%), with a median age of 68 years (IQR: 60–76 years) and a mean ± SD time from onset to CTP of 3.50 ± 1.55 h. The patients in the favorable prognosis group were significantly younger and had a shorter time from onset to imaging than the patients in the adverse prognosis group (P < 0.05). However, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of gender distribution, hypertension history, diabetes, coronary heart disease, atrial fibrillation, history of stroke, history of smoking, alcohol consumption, occlusion site, and incidence of hemorrhagic transformation (P < 0.05) (Table 1).

The parameters of CTP and baseline scores were as follows: the median ASPECTS was 7.0 points (IQR: 7.0–8.0), the median NIHSS score was 18.0 points (IQR: 13.0–22.5), the median mRS score at 3 months was 4 points (IQR: 2–5), the median ASITN/SIR score was 3 points (IQR: 1–4), the median VanSwieten score for white matter lesions was 2 point (IQR: 2–3), the volume of Tmax > 6 s was 144.10 mL (IQR: 105.15–187.25), the volume of core infarction (rCBF < 30%) was 15.80 mL (IQR: 5.95–47.50), the median MTR was 2.40/h (IQR: 0.85–8.07/h), and the median IGR was 7.29 mL/h (IQR: 2.26–12.12 mL/h). Significant differences were observed between the favorable prognosis group and the adverse prognosis group (P < 0.05). The favorable outcome group had a smaller volume of rCBF < 30%, a larger MTR, higher ASPECTS, higher ASITN/SIR collateral score, reduced VanSwieten white matter lesion score, and lower NIHSS score upon admission than the adverse prognosis group (Table 2). The difference in MTR levels between the favorable prognosis group and the adverse prognosis group was statistically significant, with an average of 3.766/h in the adverse prognosis group and 18.445/h in the favorable prognosis group (Fig. 2). Figure 3 illustrates the distribution of 90dmRS scores according to MTR and ASPECTS, and the optimal cutoff value for the ROC curve analysis of MTR was 6.917.

Univariate logistic analysis and the selection of MTR

Univariate logistic analysis revealed that MTR (OR = 2.218, P = 0.005, 95% CI: 1.275–3.860), M4/T (OR = 1.704, P = 0.005, 95% CI: 1.179–2.467), M8/T (OR = 3.138, P = 0.015, 95% CI: 1.248–7.890), M10/T (OR = 4.555, P = 0.037, 95% CI: 1.099–18.870), mismatch ratio (OR = 1.313, P = 0.011, 95% CI: 1.066–1.619), infarction core volume (OR = 0.972, P = 0.002, 95% CI: 0.954–0.989), ASTIN/SIR collateral score (OR = 1.811, P < 0.001, 95% CI: 1.310–2.503), ASPECTS (OR = 2.209, P < 0.001, 95% CI: 1.484–3.288), VanSwieten white matter lesion score (OR = 0.539, P = 0.04, 95% CI: 0.354–0.821), NIHSS score at admission (OR = 0.857, P < 0.001, 95% CI: 0.795–0.924), and age (OR = 0.956, P = 0.017, 95% CI: 0.921–0.992) were predictors of short-term outcomes following EVT (Table 3). Collinearity diagnostics were conducted on variables exhibiting statistical significance in univariate analysis, defining multicollinearity in variables as tolerance below 0.2 or VIF exceeding 5. The MTR, M4/T, M8/T, M10/T, and mismatch ratios demonstrated multicollinearity (Table 4). To select the most representative indicator, we found that the AUCs for MTR, M4/T, M8/T, M10/T, and mismatch ratio in predicting favorable outcomes after 90 days of EVT in patients with AIS-LVO were 0.778, 0.764, 0.765, 0.723, and 0.702, respectively, indicating statistical significance (P < 0.05) (Table 5). Although MTR had the highest AUC on the ROC curve, the other variables retained some predictive significance. Further stepwise regression analysis revealed that only MTR served as an independent predictor of favorable outcomes at 90 days post-EVT among MTR, M4/T, M 8/T, M10/T, and mismatch ratio (Table 3).

Comparison of the predictive value of MTR and traditional imaging parameters

Univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted on the traditional imaging parameters (core infarction volume and IGR) and MTR. The results showed that only MTR (OR = 2.218, P = 0.005, 95% CI: 1.275–3.860) and core infarction volume (OR = 0.972, P = 0.002, 95% CI: 0.954–0.989) served as predictors of short-term prognosis following EVT. ROC curve analysis revealed that the AUC for MTR alone in predicting favorable prognosis after 90 days of EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO was 0.778, whereas the AUC of infarction core volume alone in predicting adverse prognosis after 90 days of EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO was 0.689; both values were statistically significant (P < 0.05) (Table 5).

Establishment of predictive models and investigation of potential correlations

Stepwise regression analysis revealed that MTR (OR = 2.072, P = 0.015, 95% CI: 1.155–3.719), ASPECTS (OR = 2.363, P = 0.001, 95% CI: 1.445–3.866), and NIHSS score (OR = 0.866, P = 0.005, 95% CI: 0.814–0.964) were independent prognostic indicators of favorable outcomes. ROC curve analysis indicated that MTR alone predicted a positive prognosis following EVT in AIS-LVO at 90 days, with an AUC of 0.778 (P < 0.001, 95% CI: 0.690–0.866). Similarly, ASPECTS alone predicted a favorable prognosis following EVT in AIS-LVO at 90 days, with an AUC of 0.748 (P < 0.001, 95% CI: 0.655–0.841) (Fig. 4). MTR in conjunction with ASPECTS predicted a positive prognosis at 90 days post-EVT in patients with AIS-LVO, achieving an AUC of 0.828 (P < 0.001, 95% CI: 0.753–0.904). Spearman correlation analysis indicated a moderate positive association between MTR and the ASTIN/SIR score (P < 0.001, r = 0.503) and an inverse correlation with the VanSwieten score (P < 0.001, r = − 0.421). Additionally, MTR showed a weak positive correlation with the ASPECTS score (P = 0.01, r = 0.249)(Fig. 5) .Furthermore, collinearity analysis revealed no significant multicollinearity between MTR and ASPECTS(Table 4). Discussion

Our study revealed that MTR derived from CTP was a novel imaging biomarker for predicting the short-term prognosis of patients with AIS-LVO who received EVT. The combined predictive value of MTR and ASPECTS exceeded the predictive ability of either MTR or ASPECTS alone. MTR consisted of three variables: the infarction core volume as defined by CTP (the volume of rCBF < 30%), the ischemic penumbra volume (the volume of Tmax > 6 s), and the time from onset to CTP. Among them, the infarction core volume defined by CTP was significantly correlated with functional results at 90 days. A small infarction core volume suggested a high likelihood of benefitting from reperfusion therapy and achieving favorable functional outcomes, which was consistent with our results. Moreover, the correlation between core infarction and functional outcomes was unaffected by other clinical variables, including age and baseline NIHSS [29, 30]. However, the issue of CTP inaccurately measuring core infarction volume continued, particularly in white matter regions and in patients with early reperfusion [25, 31, 32]. The ischemic penumbra identified by CTP was significantly associated with early and late clinical outcomes in patients with stroke. The penumbra volume exhibited an inverse correlation with the final infarction growth volume. A 1% increase in the volume of reperfusion penumbra may decrease the NIHSS score at 24 h by 0.069. The treatment effect of tissue preservation post-intervention can be evaluated by determining the “penumbra salvage volume” (i.e., the baseline penumbra volume subtracted by the final infarction growth volume) [33, 34]. However, some studies reported that CTP may lead to inaccuracies in assessing penumbra volume when analyzing small infarction cores (such as < 5 mL), and the presence of metabolic factors (such as hypoxia tolerance) may affect the actual salvageability of penumbra tissue [35–37]. Therefore, concentrating on the volume of core infarction and ischemic penumbra may not yield a sufficiently reliable prognosis for patients with stroke. Subsequently, other studies analyzed the predictive value of the mismatch ratio, demonstrating that a high mismatch ratio (≥ 1.8) defined by CTP may significantly predict the 90-day mRS score of patients [23, 24]. Moreover, the location-based mismatch ratio can address the inaccuracies associated with simple volume measurement [24].The static mismatch ratio captures the relative relationship between the ischemic penumbra and the core infarct at a given time point. In practical terms, it provides an assessment of salvageable versus non-salvageable tissue prior to making an endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) decision, thereby informing the anticipated benefit and risk of the procedure for an individual patient.

The time from onset to CTP, as the key time component before vascular recanalization, significantly influenced clinical outcomes in patients eligible for EVT༎From the perspective of its composition, the MTR essentially captures the efficiency of penumbral salvage relative to the core infarct over a given ischemic duration—that is, the individualized rate at which the conversion of ischemic penumbra into core infarct attenuates over time.A high MTR implies that, despite a certain duration of ischemia, the patient retains a relatively abundant volume of salvageable tissue, suggesting robust collateral circulation and strong ischemic tolerance. Conversely, a low MTR indicates rapid progression of the penumbra toward the infarct core, implying that the potential benefit from endovascular thrombectomy is diminishing quickly.While the static mismatch ratio reflects the relative relationship between penumbra and core at a single time point, it lacks temporal adjustment. For instance, a mismatch ratio of 8 at 2 h from onset versus 8 at 8 h from onset represents vastly different rates of penumbral consumption and thrombectomy potential. By incorporating time from onset, MTR effectively adjusts for this discrepancy, distinguishing between clinical scenarios that appear similar on static imaging but differ fundamentally in pathophysiology.

Moreover, the introduction of MTR addresses, to some extent, the inherent technical limitations of CTP. Therefore, MTR showed important value in predicting the short-term prognosis of patients post-EVT by incorporating the three abovementioned important variables.

In the comparison, MTR outperformed M4/T, M8/T, M10/T, and mismatch ratio, and it was the sole parameter index that was eventually incorporated into our prediction model. The ischemic penumbra characterized by Tmax > 6 s serves as an advantageous parameter for optimizing sensitivity, specificity, and software differences. Tmax > 6 s exhibited the optimal balance in the majority of applications, accurately representing the true penumbra and reducing the incorporation of non-ischemic regions. By contrast, Tmax > 4 s may include excess benign hypoperfusion areas, resulting in an overestimation of viable tissue. Tmax > 10 s was strongly associated with the final infarct volume, but it may underestimate the salvageable tissue [38–41]. Compared with the prognostic value of infarction core volume, mismatch ratio, and IGR, MTR simultaneously accounted for the duration from onset to CTP and ischemic penumbra, without depending exclusively on volumetric measurements. This approach diminished prediction errors resulting from volume overestimation or underestimation and considered the effects of the time window and salvageable tissue.

Our study was a retrospective multicenter study conducted in a regional context, and the subsequent research limitations were revealed. First, a minority of patients in our stroke alliance underwent only CTA and NCCT, excluding CTP prior to EVT evaluation, potentially introducing selection bias and resulting in incomplete overall data during patient enrollment. Second, some subjectivity persisted even though ASPECTS, ASTIN/SIR grading, and VanSwieten score were evaluated by the same experienced clinician in this study. Third, residual confounding may exist despite our efforts to account for confounding variables in the multivariable analysis.And we acknowledge that the use of stepwise regression in the context of a relatively small sample size may raise concerns regarding overfitting. However, since the primary aim of this study was to propose a potential novel prognostic indicator for EVT patients rather than to construct a definitive predictive model, this methodological trade-off is acceptable. Fourth, CTP could not be performed frequently to verify whether MTR remains a consistent value in the same patient due to time constraints prior to EVT. Finally, the present study was a retrospective one. The majority of the participants were from northern Jiangsu and northern Anhui, which might result in regional limitations and selection bias. Therefore, the findings of this study require further verification via large-scale multicenter trials.

In summary, this study introduced MTR as a novel independent imaging biomarker for predicting the prognosis of EVT. Our findings revealed a moderate association between MTR and the severity of cerebral white matter lesions and the extent of cerebral collateral circulation at stroke onset. The relationship between MTR and post-stroke cognitive dysfunction in patients requires further investigation, and the reliability of MTR as an imaging biomarker for evaluating cerebral collateral circulation has yet to be confirmed.

Although both MTR and ASPECTS relate to the extent of cerebral injury, they capture fundamentally different dimensions of the ischemic cascade. MTR integrates dynamic CTP perfusion parameters with the time dimension, quantifying the rate of conversion of salvageable tissue into core infarct (i.e., the temporal efficiency of tissue loss). In contrast, ASPECTS reflects the structural burden of injury visible on NCCT, representing the cumulative, irreversible damage that has already occurred at the time of imaging. While ASPECTS defines the ‘static endpoint’ of completed infarction, MTR characterizes the ‘dynamic velocity’ of progression toward that endpoint. This distinction allows MTR to provide complementary prognostic information beyond the structural deficit quantified by ASPECTS. Thus, The combination of MTR and ASPECTS accurately predicts the likelihood of short-term favorable prognosis following EVT in patients with AIS. The multimodal CT prediction method presented in this study may effectively evaluate surgical risks and treatment advantages in clinical practice, thereby identifying additional patients who may benefit from EVT and potentially prolonging the time window of EVT in the future.

Conclusion

MTR is a novel imaging biomarker that signifies a favorable short-term prognosis for EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO. Its predictive performance was superior to that of IGR and infarction core volume. The combination of MTR and ASPECTS could accurately predict the prognosis of EVT in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO.

MicroRNA-450b-5p modulated RPLP0 promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression via activating JAK/STAT3 pathway
Authors: Yanqiu Meng, Xianbin Huang, Guangxin Zhang, Sansan Fu, Youhua Li, Jielong Song, Yizi Zhu, Xinping Xu, Xiaodong Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•RPLP0 was demonstrated to be highly expressed with a poor prognosis in HCC.•RPLP0 was identified as a carcinogenic factor in HCC, with significant diagnostic implications (AUC = 0.908).•RPLP0 promoted HCC progression via activating the JAK/STAT3 pathway and was regulated by miR-450b-5p.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is known for its high aggressiveness as a malignant neoplasm, leading to a significantly elevated mortality rate[1]. Despite significant advances in therapeutic modalities, such as surgical treatment, targeted therapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy, HCC remains a refractory disease due to its complexity [[2], [3], [4]]. Therefore, studying the pathogenesis of HCC and identifying new drug targets for HCC must be continually explored.

The disruption of Ribosomal Protein Lateral Stalk Subunit P0 (RPLP0), a member of the RPLP family, directly impacts protein synthesis, leading to cell growth arrest and eventual apoptosis[5,6]. RPLP0 plays a pivotal role in tumorigenesis, with its absence inhibiting the progression of breast cancer, cervical cancer, non-small-cell lung cancer, and gastric cancer[[7], [8], [9], [10]]. Additionally, RPLP0 has a crucial role in radiotherapy by interacting with NONO to enhance DNA repair and tumor radioresistance[11]. However, it has not been reported how RPLP0 promotes HCC progression.

Our study revealed a significant increase in RPLP0 expression in HCC, demonstrating excellent diagnostic performance. High RPLP0 expression is positively correlated with a poorer survival prognosis. Subsequently, downregulation of RPLP0 attenuates HCC progression, as demonstrated in both HCC cell lines and xenograft tumors. Moreover, our research revealed that the absence of microRNA(miR)−450b-5p leads to an increase in RPLP0 expression, concomitant with heightened levels of proteins linked to the JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway. The upregulation of RPLP0 was found to counteract the tumor-suppressive impact of miR-450b-5p. These findings underscore the significance of RPLP0 as a pro-tumor factor in the diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis of HCC.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Cell lines (THLE-2, MHCC97-H, HCCLM3, Huh7, Hep3B, HepG2, and SK-HEP-1) were procured from the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). We incubated cells at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 in a humidified incubator in RPMI 1640 (THLE-2) or DMEM (HCC cell lines) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, USA).

Patients and specimens

Twenty pairs of tissue specimens were procured from the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University and definitively diagnosed through histopathology. All participants had initially been diagnosed with primary HCC. The HCC tissues exhibited a tumor cell percentage exceeding 70 %, while the corresponding non-cancerous tissues were situated at a distance greater than 5 cm from the cancerous tissue margins. Informed consent was obtained from all patients after providing them with information regarding the study. The research protocol was approved by the ethics committee ((2023) CDYFYYLK (11–004)) and adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Bioinformatics analysis

The TCGA (371 HCC and 50 non-tumor samples) (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and GEO (GSE 45,267, GSE 121,248) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) platforms were employed to predict RPLP0 expression between HCC and normal liver tissues. A thorough examination was conducted to evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic significance of RPLP0 in HCC, as well as pathway enrichment, using the R program (version 4.3.2), based on patient clinical information and sequencing folders available in the TCGA database. Additionally, TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/), mirDIP (https://ophid.utoronto.ca/mirDIP/searchShared_OnGenes.jsp), and Starbase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) databases were employed to predict miRNAs targeting RPLP0[[12], [13], [14]].

RT–qPCR

RNA extraction was performed with TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA). Reverse transcription and qPCR were carried out utilizing the cDNA Synthesis SuperMix and qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix, respectively (Yeasen, China). The miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, China) was utilized for the reverse transcription of microRNAs. The primers used in this work are detailed in supplementary Table 1. RT-qPCR was carried out following the provided instructions, and the relative expression was determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blotting

Proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer (Invitrogen, USA) and subsequently separated by electrophoresis on 10 % SDS-PAGE gels, with each lane loaded with 25 μg protein. Details of the antibody dilution ratios are available in supplementary Table 2. Protein bands were identified utilizing the ECL system (BIO-RAD, USA).

Immunohistochemistry

The tumor samples were fixed in formalin and subsequently processed by embedding them in paraffin. The sections were then subjected to sequential deparaffinization, rehydration, antigen retrieval, and blocking of endogenous peroxidase activity. Following a two-hour incubation period with the primary antibody at room temperature, the samples underwent three times of PBS washing before being incubated with the secondary antibody at 37 °C for 20 min. Color development was achieved using diaminobenzedine (DAB, 0.02 %) for 5 min, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining, dehydration, air-drying, and mounting.

Cell viability assays

Transfection of appropriate siRNAs, overexpression plasmids, mimics, or inhibitor was carried out in Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells under optimal conditions for the experiments. The siRNAs sequences are provided in supplementary Table 3. After 48 h, trypsinization was performed. The cells were cultivated in 96 wells, with 1500 cells per well, and assayed at 24, 48, 72, 96, and 120 h. The experiments were replicated at least three times.

Colony formation assays

Single-cell suspensions were prepared from successfully transfected cells and then seeded in 6-well culture plates. Following a 10-day incubation period, clonal clusters were fixed, stained, gently rinsed with PBS, air-dried, and subsequently photographed. A minimum of three replicates were carried out for each experimental condition.

Transwell assays

The cells were subjected to a 24-hour transfection period, after which a further 24-hour incubation period was initiated in serum-free medium. To assess the invasive potential of cells, 8-um pore membrane transwell chambers (Corning, USA) were in situ precoated with matrix gel and incubated for 3 h. The lower chamber had 600 µl of medium with 10 % FBS, followed by the inoculation of 200 µl of single-cell suspension (4 × 104 cells/well) into the upper chamber. After being incubated for 48 h, the samples were fixed and stained. At least three replicates were performed.

Flow cytometry

The apoptosis ratio was quantified utilizing the Annexin V-PE/7-AAD Apoptosis Kit (MultiSciences, China). After successful transfection, the cells were enzymatically dissociated with trypsin to obtain single-cell suspensions, with caution exercised to prevent excessive digestion and consequent cell damage. Following washing with PBS and centrifugation, the samples were subjected to staining and 10 min of incubation at room temperature in the absence of light. At least three replicates of the experiment were conducted.

Luciferase reporter assays

Cotransfections were conducted in Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells, organized into groups labeled as mimics-NC + RPLP0-WT, miR-450b-5p mimics + RPLP0-WT, mimics-NC + RPLP0-MUT, or miR-450b-5p mimics + RPLP0-MUT. 48 h later, the fluorescence activity of firefly and Renilla luciferase was detected separately using the Dual-Luciferase Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). The firefly luciferase activity was then normalized to the Renilla luciferase values.

Animal experiments

MHCC97-H cells were infected with lentiviruses targeting sh-NC and sh-RPLP0 (HANBIO, China), resulting in the successful screening of stably transfected cell strains. BALB/c nude mice were procured from Sibeifu (Beijing) Biotechnology Company (Sibeifu, China) and randomly assigned to either the sh-NC or sh-RPLP0 groups. After one week of acclimatization, each mouse received a subcutaneous injection of 1 × 107 sh-NC or sh-RPLP0 cells at the same site. The volume of subcutaneous tumors was quantified and recorded at three-day intervals, following a one-week period. The animal studies were conducted in accordance with approved animal ethics guidelines (CDYFY-IACUC-202310QR034).

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9. Pearson's correlation test was employed to examine the interrelationship between the referenced genes. The Student's t-test was employed to assess differences between two groups, while the two-way analysis of variance was utilized to analyze the differences between multiple groups. P < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. Kaplan-Meier log-rank analysis was used for survival prognosis studies. The data were shown as the mean ± SEM.

Results

RPLP0 exhibits increased expression in both HCC tissues and cell lines

Analysis of RPLP0 mRNA expression on the TCGA platform demonstrated a notable upregulation of RPLP0 expression in HCC (Fig. 1A–1B). Further validation was conducted by analyzing the GSE45267 and GSE121248 datasets, yielding consistent conclusions (Fig. 1C–1D). Subsequent examination of RPLP0 expression in normal hepatocytes and HCC cell lines revealed a significant increase in both mRNA and protein levels of RPLP0 in HCC cell lines (Fig. 1E–1F). Furthermore, analysis of 20 pairs of HCC and adjacent non-tumor tissue clinical samples demonstrated a notable increase in RPLP0 mRNA and protein expression in HCC tissues (Fig. 1G–1H). Immunohistochemical analysis provided additional support for these observations (Fig. 1I). Based on our comprehensive analyses using diverse databases, cells, and tissues, it appears that RPLP0 might contribute to the advancement of HCC. The upregulation of RPLP0 is associated with an unfavorable survival outcome in HCC

To ascertain the diagnostic and prognostic significance of RPLP0 in HCC, an analysis was conducted utilizing the TCGA database. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve for RPLP0 exhibited an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.908, indicating high diagnostic accuracy (Fig. 2A). Therefore, RPLP0 may serve as a valuable diagnostic marker in clinical settings. Additionally, our study conducted survival analysis in groups with high and low RPLP0 expression, uncovering a positive correlation between high RPLP0 expression and poorer overall survival (OS) and progression-free interval (PFI) (Fig. 2B–2C). A subsequent multivariate COX regression analysis was carried out to determine the effect of high-low RPLP0 expression groups on OS and PFI (Fig. 2D–2E). Down-regulation of RPLP0 attenuates the malignant phenotype and triggers apoptosis in HCC cells

To investigate whether the down-regulation of RPLP0 could inhibit HCC progression, we performed cell functional assays in both the Huh7 and MHCC97-H lines. Initially, we transfected RPLP0 siRNAs into Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells and screened for the effects of silencing RPLP0 on mRNA expression using RT-qPCR (Fig. 3A). siRPLP0–1 and siRPLP0–4 were chosen to verify protein expression levels based on their superior silencing abilities. The protein levels of siRPLP0–1 and siRPLP0–4 were found to be significantly reduced in comparison to siRPLP0-NC (Fig. 3B). To evaluate whether overexpression of RPLP0 may have opposite functional effects, we also transfected oeRPLP0 into Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells, respectively, and verified the transfection efficiency. As illustrated in Fig. 3C–3D, the mRNA and protein levels of RPLP0 in the oeRPLP0 group were demonstrably elevated in comparison to the oeRPLP0-NC group. Through analysis of the TCGA database, a positive correlation between RPLP0 and Ki67 expression (R = 0.442) was identified (Fig. 3E). Additionally, high expression of RPLP0 promoted proliferation (Supplementary figure 1A) and the progression of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) by GSEA analysis (Supplementary figure 1B). Since cancer progression is intricately connected to apoptosis [15,16], we further investigated whether the expression level of RPLP0 plays a role in apoptosis. CCK-8, colony formation, Transwell assays, and flow cytometry were employed to assess cell viability, clonality, invasion, migration, and apoptosis ratio. Results indicated that the siRPLP0–1 and siRPLP0–4 groups had suppressed viability, clonality, invasion, and migration but increased apoptosis compared to the siRPLP0-NC group. Conversely, the oeRPLP0 group exhibited opposite results (Fig. 3F–3H, 3J). Proteins were extracted from treated Huh 7 and MHCC 97-H cells to validate EMT-related protein expression. Vimentin and Snail levels decreased significantly in the siRPLP0–1 and siRPLP0–4 groups compared to the siRPLP0-NC group, while E-cadherin increased. In contrast, Vimentin and Snail levels exhibited a notable increase in the oeRPLP0 group compared to the oeRPLP0-NC group, while E-cadherin decreased significantly (Fig. 3I). Fig. 4C depicts the observed change in tumor volume, which was found to be significantly reduced in the sh-RPLP0 group. After 4 weeks, the mice were euthanized using cervical dislocation (Fig. 4A). The xenografts were obtained (Fig. 4B), and the difference in weight of the grafted tumors was counted (Fig. 4D). In terms of tumor weight, the sh-RPLP0 group demonstrated that it was lighter than the sh-NC group. Next, we extracted xenograft tumors protein to determine the changes in Ki67 and EMT-related protein. The levels of Ki67, Vimentin, and Snail were observed to exhibit a notable decrease in the sh-RPLP0 group in comparison to the sh-NC group, while E-cadherin expression increased (Fig. 4E). The JAK/STAT3 pathway is activated by overexpression of RPLP0

To investigate the mechanism through which RPLP0 impacts HCC progression, GSEA analysis was performed, uncovering that increased RPLP0 expression significantly triggers the JAK/STAT3 pathway (Fig. 5A). Since P-STAT3 has been shown to translocate to the nucleus to transcriptionally activate relevant target genes[17,18], GSEA analysis also indicated a significant enrichment of highly expressed RPLP0 in pathways related to DNA replication (Fig. 5C), cell cycle (Fig. 5D), and MYC activation (Fig. 5E). To confirm the aforementioned analysis, Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells were successfully transfected with RPLP0 siRNAs or oeRPLP0. After a 48-hour incubation period, the extracted proteins were analyzed. The siRPLP0–1 and siRPLP0–4 groups showed decreased protein expression of RPLP0, P-JAK1/JAK1, P-JAK2/JAK2, P-STAT3/STAT3, C-MYC, Cyclin D1, and Bcl-2 in comparison to the siRPLP0-NC group. Conversely, the oeRPLP0 group had increased protein expressions of these factors compared to the oeRPLP0-NC group (Fig. 5B, 5F). miR-450b-5p regulates RPLP0 expression, affecting the levels of proteins associated with the JAK/STAT3 pathway

To find the upstream regulators of RPLP0, we used TargetScan, mirDIP, and Starbase databases to predict potential regulators by intersecting the results. The analysis revealed that miR-125b-5p, miR-195–5p, miR-497–5p, miR-199a-5p, and miR-450b-5p are possibly linked to tumorigenesis[[19], [20], [21], [22], [23]]. Transfection was conducted in Huh7 cells with either mimics-NC or mimics, followed by the evaluation of RPLP0 mRNA expression (Fig. 6A). It could be seen that miR-450b-5p mimics the most effectively inhibited RPLP0 mRNA. Analysis of the TCGA database demonstrated a notable reduction in miR-450b-5p expression in HCC samples relative to normal liver tissues (Fig. 6B). Additionally, the levels of miR-450b-5p were found to be lower in HCC cell lines compared to normal hepatocytes (Fig. 6C). A negative correlation between miR-450b-5p and RPLP0 was observed in 20 HCC tissues (r = −0.4561) (Fig. 6D). Changes in transfection efficiency (Fig. 6E), RPLP0 mRNA (Fig. 6F), and RPLP0 protein expression (Fig. 6G) were verified after successful transfection in Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells. RPLP0 mRNA and protein levels were significantly reduced in the miR-450b-5p mimic group compared to the mimic-NC group but increased in the miR-450b-5p inhibitor group compared to the inhibitor-NC group. Given that miRNAs typically act within the 3′UTR of target genes[24], we employed the TargetScan database to predict the interaction sites between miR-450b-5p and RPLP0 (Fig. 6H) and designed dual luciferase reporter plasmids. Following co-transfection treatment in Huh7 and MHCC97-H cells, the miR-450b-5p mimics+ RPLP0-WT group exhibited a pronounced reduction in luciferase activity in comparison to the mimics-NC+ RPLP0-WT group, while the miR-450b-5p mimics+ RPLP0-MUT group did not show significant changes in fluorescence signals compared to the mimics-NC+ RPLP0-MUT group (Fig. 6I). Our investigation revealed that miR-450b-5p has the ability to directly target RPLP0. Furthermore, we validated the changes in gain- and loss-of-function phenotypes (Supplementary figure 2), the expression of proteins associated with the JAK/STAT3 pathway, and STAT3 downstream proteins. Specifically, the levels of RPLP0, P-JAK1/JAK1, P-JAK2/JAK2, P-STAT3/STAT3, C-MYC, Cyclin D1, and Bcl-2 proteins were reduced in the miR-450b-5p mimics group compared to the mimics-NC group, while the miR-450b-5p inhibitor group exhibited an opposite effect (Fig. 6J–6K). Discussion

To ascertain the biological function of RPLP0 in HCC, a multi-dimensional analysis was conducted utilizing data from TCGA and GEO, in addition to normal hepatocytes, HCC cell lines, and normal-tumor paired clinical tissue specimens. The analysis revealed a significant upregulation of RPLP0 in HCC. The ROC curve illustrated that RPLP0 exhibited excellent diagnostic efficacy (AUC = 0.908). Moreover, RPLP0 was identified as an independent prognostic factor, with high RPLP0 expression associated with worse OS and PFI. RPLP0 is frequently overexpressed in acute myeloid leukemia[25]. Ana et al. showed that downregulation of RPLP0 led to reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, which induced endoplasmic reticulum stress, activated the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway, and induced autophagy in breast cancer cells[7]. Additionally, the overexpression of RPLP0 is positively correlated with an increased propensity for invasion in endometrial and ovarian cancers[26]. These findings indicate that RPLP0 may contribute to the development of different types of cancer. We further confirmed that down-regulation of RPLP0 inhibited HCC progression based on loss-and-gain-of-function and xenograft experiments.

To elucidate the mechanism by which RPLP0 affects HCC progression, we discovered that heightened RPLP0 levels trigger the activation of the JAK/STAT3 pathway, leading to the translocation of P-STAT3 into the nucleus, subsequently modulating the expression of downstream proteins. The JAK/STAT3 pathway is a crucial element in cell differentiation and homeostasis maintenance[[27], [28], [29]]. In a manner analogous to our study, P-STAT3 functions as a transcription factor to activate key genes that are crucial for cell proliferation, apoptosis, and the cell cycle[[30], [31], [32]]. Due to the extensive involvement of the JAK/STAT3 pathway in intercellular signaling processes, its dysregulation is highly correlated with the progression of breast cancer, thyroid cancer, medulloblastoma, and colon cancer[[33], [34], [35], [36]].

Subsequently, to investigate the upstream regulators affecting RPLP0 expression, we conducted a screening of miR-450b-5p, which was identified as a regulator targeting RPLP0. The analysis revealed that miR-450b-5p inhibited the JAK/STAT3 pathway. Upregulation of RPLP0 was demonstrated to counteract the suppressive impact of miR-450b-5p on HCC. miR-450b-5p can modulate YAP1 to impede the development of lung squamous cell carcinoma[37]. miR-450b-5p functions as an anti-cancer factor, inhibiting cervical cancer cell proliferation by targeting RAB10 [20]. Interestingly, miR-450b-5p has been documented to downregulate gelsolin expression, thereby facilitating glioblastoma proliferation and invasion[38]. We demonstrated that miR-450b-5p targeting RPLP0 inhibited HCC progression by suppressing JAK/STAT3 pathway activation.

In conclusion, our study confirmed that RPLP0 promoted HCC progression and served as a diagnostic and prognostic marker. Further demonstrated the biological function of miR-450b-5p/RPLP0/JAK/STAT3 in HCC and proposed a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of HCC.

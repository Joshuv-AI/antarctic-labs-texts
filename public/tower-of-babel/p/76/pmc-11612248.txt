Attenuation of the neuropathogenic equine herpesvirus type 1 strain Ab4p in hamsters by a single amino acid mutation (D752N) in viral DNA polymerase ORF30
Authors: Fuka NISHIMURA, Noriko FUKUSHI, Hiroki SAKAI, Hideto FUKUSHI
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Equine herpesvirus type 1 (EHV-1) causes abortion, respiratory infection, and neurological diseases (equine herpesvirus myeloencephalopathy) in horses. A single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) associated with a single amino acid in the DNA polymerase gene (ORF30, in which D752 is neuropathogenic and N752 is non-neuropathogenic) of EHV-1 has been associated with neuropathogenicity in horses. We constructed an EHV-1 Ab4p ORF30 N752 mutant and a repair virus to examine the effect of a D752N mutation on the neuropathogenicity of the virus in Syrian hamsters. The N752 mutation did not affect viral growth in cultured cells but it did attenuate the neuropathogenicity of Ab4p in the hamsters. The results suggest that D752N is involved in neuropathogenicity not only in horses but also in hamsters.

Equine herpesvirus 1 (EHV-1: family Orthoherpesviridae, subfamily Alphaherpesvirinae, genus Varicellovirus equidalpha1) is a pathogen causing abortion in pregnant mares, respiratory infection in young horses, and neurological diseases (equine herpesvirus encephalopathy: EHM) in horses of all ages [15]. In recent years, outbreaks of EHM have been increasing, especially in North America and Europe [31, 33]. Molecular epidemiological studies have demonstrated that a single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) at position 2,254 (G2254A) in the DNA polymerase gene (ORF30) that causes a D-to-N substitution at position 752 (D752N) is significantly associated with the neuropathogenicity (G2254/D752, neuropathogenic genotype; A2254/N752, non-neuropathogenic genotype) [7, 12, 24, 36]. Furthermore, A2254/N752 genotype EHV-1s were also isolated from horses affected with EHM [27, 32] and the residue D752 is reported to be non-essential for viral growth in vitro [16]. How the genotype of EHV-1 strains is related to neuropathogenicity remains unclear.

Virulence and pathogenicity of EHV-1 have been investigated in experimental animal models, including mice and hamsters [1, 2, 4, 5, 14, 20, 21,22,23, 37, 38], as well as in horses [3, 10, 11, 13, 17,18,19, 25,26,27,28, 30, 34]. While mice inoculated with EHV-1 showed body weight loss only, hamsters inoculated with EHV-1 showed neurological signs [35]. Neurological and respiratory disease have been reported in Syrian hamsters infected with EHV-1 [20], which suggests that these animals might be valuable for evaluating the neurovirulence of EHV-1.

EHV-1 Ab4p, whose G2254/D752 in ORF30 is a neuropathogenic genotype, is a representative neuropathogenic strain [8]. In the present study, we used Ab4p-derived bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) clone to construct an Ab4p A2254/N752 mutant and a repair virus to examine the effect of the D752N mutation on the neuropathogenicity of EHV-1 in Syrian hamsters, a model animal for EHV-1 studies.

The viruses used in this study were Ab4p and mutant viruses (Ab4p ORF30 A2254 and Ab4p ORF30 repair viruses) which were constructed as described below. Fetal horse kidney (FHK) Tcl3.1 cells were used for virus propagation.

In a multi-step growth kinetics experiment, no differences were found in viral proliferation in cultured cells between the parent strain, repair and mutant viruses (Fig. 3). Other researchers reported that deletion of D752 did not affect growth of the virus in cell culture [16]. Thus, SNP G2254A/D752A of ORF30 might be unrelated to viral replication in infected culture cells. Further studies are needed to elucidate the function of this SNP in viral replication.

To evaluate the virulence of the G2254A mutant in vivo, we used Syrian hamsters. The animal experiments were authorized by the Committee for Animal Research and Welfare of Gifu University (approved number: 2020-028), and all experiments were performed according to the Regulations for Animal Experiments at Gifu University. Three-week-old specific pathogen-free (SPF) female Syrian hamsters (SLC, Hamamatsu, Japan) were separated into four groups of 4 animals each, according to the viral strain used (parent, mutant, repair, and control). Hamsters were anesthetized with a mixture of 0.15 mg/kg medetomidine, 2.0 mg/kg midazolam, 2.5 mg/kg butorphanol (MMB) intraperitoneally. Subsequently, the hamsters were intranasally inoculated with 50 µL of D-MEM containing 6 × 103 plaque forming units (pfu) of each virus. Control animals were inoculated with 50 µL of D-MEM. The body weight and clinical signs were recorded daily for a period of 10 days. Clinical signs were evaluated as neurological signs indexed by drooling, ocular blubber, convulsions, canine sitting posture, depression, and coma. The humane endpoint was defined as 70% or less of the body weight on the day of infection (0 dpi) and no improvement in clinical signs compared to the control group. At the end of the experiment, all animals that survived were euthanized by bloodletting under anesthesia, and brains and lungs were collected. The brains and lungs were then soaked in buffered formalin and subjected to histopathological examination.

In histopathological examinations, the olfactory bulbs of the parent, mutant and repair groups showed high infiltration with advanced lymphocytes and plasma cells, and the laminar structure was completely destroyed. In the brain, similar degrees and distributions of inflammatory reactions were observed including circumventricular cell infiltration, glial nodules and meningitis. Only two hamsters survived in the parental strain inoculation group and one hamster survived in the repair inoculation group, indicating that the Ab4p strain is neuropathogenic in the hamster model. On the other hand, all hamsters inoculated with the mutant survived and showed less clinical signs and body weight changes, indicating that the pathogenicity of the mutant in hamsters was attenuated.

The animals that did not regain body weight showed coarsening in the pisiform lobes of the virus inoculated groups. The piriform lobes receive neurological signals from the olfactory bulbs [9], so severe damage to the piriform lobe by the virus could prevent the sense of smell from being regained. This could result in a loss of appetite and thus an inability to regain weight.

The finding that viral replication in cultured cells was not different between the D752 and N752 viruses seems inconsistent with the in vivo results. Virus replication in the brain was not examined in the present study. Comparing whether virus replication in the brain was different between the N752 virus and D752 virus, it might be clarified that the difference of virus replication affected the attenuation in EHV-1 infected hamsters.

The present results strongly suggest that G2254A is involved in neuropathogenicity not only in horses but also in hamsters, although the mechanism remains unclear. Further studies are needed to resolve the function of this SNP in viral replication and neuropathogenicity of EHV-1.

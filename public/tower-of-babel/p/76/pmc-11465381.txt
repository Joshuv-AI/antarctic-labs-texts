A Novel Method for Early Warning and Quantitative Evaluation of Liquid Loading in Gas Wellbore Based on Dynamic Production Data: A Case Study of Linxing Tight Gas Field
Authors: Lichun Sun, Maojun Fang, Weipeng Fan, Hao Li, Lijun Liu, Longlong Li, Luting Wang, Hai Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGas wells in Linxing Tight Gas Field are characterized by significant water production, and individual well water production measurement is missing, which pose challenges to predict liquid loading and conduct quantitative evaluations. Moreover, relying solely on flow temperature and pressure testing can be costly and time-consuming. To address these critical issues, we established the prewarning model and quantitative evaluation method for liquid loading. By utilizing those models, real-time judgment and evaluation of liquid loading can be achieved without individual well water production measurements. Our researches reveal that the pressure differential per unit gas production exhibits four patterns for different production stages. In contrast to maintaining a consistent pattern in the absence of liquid loading, the presence of fluid accumulation in production wells can be diagnosed through the increase in the specific production pressure difference within the liquid-loaded production section. Furthermore, the height of liquid loading in the wellbore can be calculated by using the quantitative evaluation model, which can match well with the field tests. The average error can be only 5.9%, and it will offer a novel solution for early warning and quantitative evaluation of wellbore liquid loading in water-producing tight gas fields. In addition, the limitation for strong stress sensitivity effect and high initial water yield-well needs to be broken through in future work.

Introduction

In recent years, with the rapid development of exploration and development technologies, unconventional oil and gas resources have demonstrated tremendous potential points.1−3 In addition, China National Offshore Oil Corporation (CNOOC) Limited achieved a significant breakthrough in the exploration of tight gas in the Linxing Block and a large gas field with 100 billion cubic meters can be successfully discovered.4−6

However, as shown in Figure 1, compared with the Sulige gas fields in the central Ordos Basin, the Linxing tight gas field, located on the Yishan slope and Jinxi fold belt of the Ordos Basin, has multiple gas-bearing strata, shallow burial depths, and high water production. Those problems lead to rapid production decline when gas wells come into operation. Some wells are unable to carry fluids normally in the later stages of production, which results in wellbore fluid accumulation or even flooding and shutdown.7 Therefore, it is crucial to do research on early warning and quantitative evaluation of wellbore fluid accumulation to ensure continuous and stable production of gas wells.

Diagram of the location of Linxing tight gas block in the Sulige basin.

Currently, a lot of researchers have conducted relevant mechanistic and dynamic testing studies on wellbore fluid accumulation prediction and evaluation. Turner et al.8 proposed a droplet reversal model to predict the critical liquid flow rate of gas wells by analyzing the force balance of a single spherical droplet in a gas stream and introduced a safety factor of 1.2 to improve the accuracy of the model’s predictions. Coleman et al.9 determined that, when compared to the liquid loading data from certain low-pressure gas wells, the predicted values without modifying the Turner model were nearer to the measured values. Li et al.10 believed that the different forces acting on the surface of a single droplet in the gas core can lead to deformation of the droplet surface, and proposed an ellipsoidal model to predict the critical liquid carrying flow rate. Based on Li’s model, Wang et al.11 precisely determined the relevant parameters of the ellipsoidal critical liquid carrying model under different critical Weber numbers through integral solutions. Wang et al.12 used fluid dynamics to analyze the morphological changes of droplets under different Reynolds and Austin numbers, and derived a model for predicting the liquid carrying capacity of gas wells with spherical cap-shaped droplets. Through comparative analysis of actual data from Tarim gas wells, it was recommended to select 25% as the safety factor for the critical liquid carrying flow rate on site. Li et al.13 established a critical liquid carrying model in which the interfacial tension and drag coefficient of deformed droplets can be considered and found that the improved critical liquid carrying model performed better than the Turner’s model and the Li’s model. Yang et al.14 analyzed the forces acting on droplets in deviated wells and established a directional well critical liquid carrying flow rate model in which the influence of well deviation angles was considered. Field data analysis revealed that the critical liquid carrying flow rate of directional wells is greater than that of vertical wells and positively correlates with the well deviation angles.

However, some scholars have experimentally demonstrated that the reverse flow of liquid films is the main cause of fluid accumulation in water-producing gas wells.15,16 Chen et al.17 derived a liquid film reversal model in which the shear force between the gas core and the liquid film, the shear force between the liquid film and the pipe wall, the fluid gravity, and the pressure difference of the liquid film were considered. They compared it with the Turner model and proposed correction factors related to tubing size and well deviation angles. Li et al.18,19 analyzed the distribution of liquid films on well walls under different gas phase flow rates, proposed that the shear stress between the liquid film and the pipe wall should be zero to give a criterion for judging the critical gas velocity, and established a critical liquid carrying model in which the well diameter, gas holdup, and liquid phase flow rate were considered. They successfully extended this model to predict fluid accumulation in the inclined wells. Pagou et al.20 presented a novel model that predicts liquid loading through the consideration of liquid film reversal. The momentum balance equation of each phase was utilized to surpass the limitations of prior models. Wang et al.21 studied the influence of wettability and surface tension on liquid film distribution and established a critical liquid carrying model in which the friction factor of the phase interface was considered based on the shape of the liquid phase interface. The experimental results revealed that liquid phase density and viscosity had a certain influence on the critical gas volume for liquid carrying in gas wells.

Despite the application of critical liquid loading models based on droplet reversal and liquid film reversal in gas fields, current models have limited applicability and they can be unable to quantitatively determine the amount of liquid accumulation to restrict their application.22 On the other hand, the flow temperature and pressure gradient in gas fields involve measuring changes in the pressure gradient within the wellbore to determine the height of liquid accumulation. Although this method is relatively accurate in diagnosing liquid accumulation, it cannot meet the real-time and economic requirements of the field.23 Cao et al.24 and Meng et al.25 have both utilized the principle of fluid pressure balance in the tubing and casing, they made iterative calculations of wellbore multiphase flow to determine the fluid pressure distribution in the annulus. By comparing and analyzing the inflection points of the wellbore pressure gradient, they calculated the height of the liquid accumulation in the wellbore. However, due to the absence of individual well water metering in Linxing tight gas wells, it is not possible to perform wellbore multiphase flow calculations, rendering this method for accurately diagnosing liquid accumulation in such wells.

In summary, how to use gas well production dynamic data to rapidly predict and quantitatively evaluate wellbore fluid accumulation is a pressing issue that needs to be addressed at present. In this study, starting from the gas well production capacity formula, the authors used dynamic data of tight gas production in the Linxing block to establish an early warning and quantitative evaluation model for wellbore fluid accumulation in tight gas wells. This model has achieved good application results in the prediction and evaluation of fluid accumulation in the producing wells of the Linxing tight gas field.

Prediction Model of Accumulated Fluid Pressure Gradient

The pressure drop of a single-phase gas column can be iteratively calculated using the average deviation coefficient method, which has achieved good application results in production fields.26 However, the gas–water two-phase flow in the fluid-accumulated section of a gas well involves complex wellbore flow mechanisms.27,28 In the on-site production of the Linxing tight gas field, only the gas production rate is recorded and the record of water production from the gas well is missing. This makes it impossible to directly utilize theoretical models to determine the gas–water flow phase state in the wellbore or calculate the fluid accumulation pressure gradient.

In this study, a large amount of test data on flow temperature and pressure under various conditions in gas wells in the Linxing block was utilized to establish a pressure gradient chart for the fluid-accumulated section of tight gas wells in the Linxing block, as shown in Figure 5. This chart illustrates the fitted relationship between different gas production volumes and the pressure gradient of the liquid column in gas wells. Based on this results, the height of fluid accumulation in gas wells can be quantitatively calculated using (eq 13), which can be used to provide a basis for the subsequent implementation of gas well drainage and production processes.

Results and Discussion for Field Application

Warning Model for Fluid Accumulation in Gas Wellbore

The fluid accumulation judgment method proposed in this paper was used to conduct a comparative analysis of fluid accumulation in the wellbore of Well LX-X1 in the Linxing block. The results are listed in Figure 6. Gas wells exhibit different changes in specific production pressure difference during different production stages as follows.

Application diagram of production specific pressure difference for wellbore fluid accumulation in well LX-X1.

Stage 1: Initial unstable production stage, during which the specific production pressure difference increases due to the expansion of the gas release radius.

Stage 2: Nonfluid-accumulated production stage, during which the specific production pressure difference remains basically unchanged.

Stage 3: Fluid-accumulated production stage, during which the specific production pressure difference increases significantly, deviating from the straight line segment of the nonfluid-accumulated stage

Stage 4: Production stage with drainage measures, during which the specific production pressure difference recovers to the initial nonfluid-accumulated production stage due to effective fluid drainage.

Further comparative analysis revealed that a flow temperature and pressure gradient test was conducted on a Well LX-X1 after 500 days of production, as shown in Figure 6. The results confirmed that significant fluid accumulation can be present in the wellbore and the effectiveness of the fluid accumulation judgment method proposed in this paper can be verified in the Linxing tight gas field. Additionally, when water drainage and gas production measures were taken (after 650 days of production), the specific production pressure difference decreased and recovered to the initial stage, indicating the effectiveness of the drainage measures.

Quantitative Calculation Model for Fluid Accumulation in Gas Well Boreholes

This paper conducted a quantitative analysis of fluid accumulation in Well LX-X1 in the Linxing block. A flow temperature and pressure gradient test was conducted on the well after 500 days of production, as shown in Figure 7. The test results indicated that the depth of fluid accumulation in the wellbore was 1000m, and the height of fluid accumulation in the wellbore was calculated to be 571m by comparing it with the reservoir depth (1571m). During the flow temperature and pressure test, the formation pressure was 6.15 MPa, the gas production rate was 13231m3/d, and the equivalent bottomhole flowing pressure of gas columns was 3.55 MPa. The specific production pressure difference can be obtained as 0.0011 MPa2/m3, which can be given from the nonfluid-accumulated horizontal line from Figure 6. According to the fitted relationship between different gas production volumes, the pressure gradient of the liquid column can be calculated as 0.211 MPa/100m, and bottomhole flow pressure under fluid accumulation condition was 4.83 MPa (eq 11).

Flow temperature and pressure test for well LX-X1.

By utilizing the quantitative evaluation method for fluid accumulation proposed in this paper, the calculated height of fluid accumulation was 602m, with an error of only 5.2% compared to the test results.

To further verify the accuracy of the proposed method, this paper analyzed the height of fluid accumulation in multiple wells in the Linxing block and compared them with the field-provided flow temperature and pressure test results. As shown in Table 1 and Figure 8, the study revealed that the wellbore fluid accumulation height calculated using the production specific pressure difference (758m) was basically consistent with the results of the flow temperature and pressure test (803m), with an average error of only 5.9%. Those results can meet production requirements to effectively save costs on flow temperature and pressure testing for individual wells.

Conclusions

This paper established early warning and quantitative evaluation models for wellbore fluid accumulation in tight gas wells in the Linxing block based on gas well production dynamics data. The new models were applied to produced wells in the Linxing block to achieve satisfactory results. The following conclusions were obtained:

Gas wells exhibit different changes in production specific pressure difference during different production stages: (1) an initial unstable production stage where the production specific pressure difference increases with drainage radius increasing, (2) a nonfluid-accumulated production stage where the production specific pressure difference remains basically unchanged, (3) a fluid-accumulated production stage where the production specific pressure difference increases significantly, deviating from the straight line segment of the nonfluid-accumulated stage, (4) a production stage with drainage measures where, due to effective fluid drainage, the production specific pressure difference returns to the level of the initial nonfluid-accumulated production stage.

Using Linxing block’s field test data, a pressure gradient chart for fluid accumulation in tight gas wells was created. It assesses fluid accumulation heights quantitatively based on the pressure difference between calculated and actual bottomhole pressures. The calculated heights align closely with test results, and only 5.9% error can be obtained to meet production needs. This method saves costs on individual well testing and has been implemented in the Linxing tight gas field.

The left adrenal vein: An important direction for right adrenal venous sampling
Authors: Hongliang Xiong, Li Du, Jiao Yang, WeiTong Hu, JiaBing Huang, YunDe Li, Xi Chen, YiFei Dong
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to evaluate the guiding role of left adrenal vein (LAV) for right adrenal venous sampling (AVS). A total of 347 patients who were diagnosed with primary aldosteronism (PA) and underwent successful AVS procedures from January 2020 to July 2021 were retrospectively analyzed. According to the different quadrant position of the orifice of right adrenal vein (RAV), the area where the orifice of RAV is located is divided into three areas: A, B, and C and the area A is further subdivided into A1, A2, and A3 areas. By counting the area where the orifice of RAV is located, the guiding role of the LAV on the RAV is determined. Most of the orifice of RAV are located in area A, and the proportions of areas A, B, and C was 96.8%, 1.4%, and 1.7%, respectively. In area A, areas A1, A2, and A3 account for 80.9%, 17.0%, and 2.1%, respectively. High body mass index, female and smaller the angle between the LAV and horizontal line was associated with the closer positional relationship between the LAV on the RAV. These findings suggest that most of the horizontal position of the RAV orifice is close to the horizontal position of the most distal end of the LAV, which indicate that the LAV location can play an important role on the guiding for right AVS. Additionally, body mass index, sex, and the angle of the LAV was largely related to location of the orifice of the RAV.Keywords: adrenal vein, adrenal venous sampling, primary aldosteronism

INTRODUCTION

The prevalence of primary aldosteronism (PA) is estimated to be 4−11.3% in the population of hypertensive patients, 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 and may be as high as 20% in patients with refractory hypertension. 1 , 3 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 Once the diagnosis of PA is confirmed, the patients with the intention of operation should typically undergo adrenal computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging. In additional to imaging examination, adrenal venous sampling (AVS) has become a useful adjunct to determine dominant gland and emerged as the reference standard for primary aldosteronism subtyping. 1 , 11 , 12

AVS is an operation with great technical difficulty in clinical work. According to the literature, its success rate is 55−98%. 13 , 14 , 15 The low success rate is mainly due to the difficulty in identifying the right adrenal vein (RAV) due to its anatomical variation and short vein. According to relevant studies, the position of the RAV can change from T10 to L1 and its large position change makes it difficult to take blood from the RAV. 16 , 17 The left adrenal vein (LAV) cannulation is relatively easy due to its relatively fixed anatomical position. Since the horizontal position of the LAV and RAV is relatively close, after the successful intubation of the LAV, we can roughly estimate the position of the RAV according to the position of the LAV. At present, there are no reports that the LAV guides the right AVS, we hypothesized that the LAV might serve as an identification for the right AVS, which may be helpful for the beginners of AVS.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Participants

This single‐center study was conducted at our institution between January 2020 and July 2021 and the patients who was diagnosed with PA according to US Endocrine Society guidelines 1 were included in this study. At the same time, these patients are willing to undergo surgery and underwent AVS via both femoral and antecubital approach at our institution. Patients included in the study should successfully undergo bilateral AVS and the morphology of bilateral adrenal venography was good, which was convenient for position measurement. The successful standard of AVS is the concentration of cortisol in adrenal vein ≥ 2 times of that in vena cava. The protocol and consent forms were approved by the ethics committees of our institution, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant.

The collected clinical data included demographic information, personal history (smoking and drinking status), past history (diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, stroke, and other diseases), physical examination (height, weight, and blood pressure), and blood test results (plasma renin, aldosterone, and potassium). Body mass index was calculated by the following formula: body weight (kg)/height (m). 2

AVS

From 10:00 p.m. the day before to 8:00 a.m. on the day of AVS, the patient remained in a flat position and ate regularly. In order to minimize the effect of time on cortisol and aldosterone secretion, AVS was performed carried out between 8:00 a.m. and 12:00 p.m. On the day of AVS, the patient was sent to the interventional operating room by flat car. After local anesthesia at the puncture point, the modified Seldinger technique was used to puncture the right median cubital vein and the right femoral vein respectively, then 5F arterial sheath (10 cm, Cordis, USA) was inserted respectively, and 20 mg of heparin was injected for anticoagulation. Under anteroposterior fluoroscopy, the first 5F MPA 1 catheter (125 cm, Cordis, USA) was sent to the first lumbar level through the lower limb vascular sheath. Under the guidance of loach guide wire, the catheter was pushed into the left renal vein. The catheter was repeatedly rotated into the the common trunk vein (below the confluence of LAV and the left inferior phrenic vein) and finally adjusted to the LAV. The contrast agent was injected to show the morphology of the left adrenal gland and the main trunk of the LAV. Then, under the guidance of loach guide wire, the second 5F MPA 1 catheter was pushed to the level of the 12th thoracic vertebra through the upper limb path. From T10 to L1, slide up and down along the inferior vena cava and rotate the catheter left and right until the orifice of the right adrenal vein is found. After the catheter is in place, a small amount of diluted contrast medium (1–2 mL) was slowly injected into the catheter to understand the morphology of RAV, so as to ensure that the catheter is in RAV. After confirming that the bilateral catheters were in the right place, the blood was taken from the bilateral adrenal vein simultaneously without adrenocorticotrophic hormone simulation. After adrenal vein blood collection, blood was taken from inferior vena cava as control. After the operation, the vascular sheath was pulled out and compression bandaging was performed, and the patient lay flat on the bed for 6 h. In order to better evaluate the operating parameters, the operation time (from the introduction of 5F sheath to catheter withdrawal), fluoroscopy time, and dosage of contrast were calculated.

Image analysis

Two AVS operators, each with experience of more than 200 cases of AVS, conducted a retrospective analysis through a GE Healthcare Centricity V3.0 workstation. The two AVS operators independently analyzed the LAV and RAV venography images. In order to reduce the impact of breathing on the results, we analyzed the LAV and RAV venography images in the end expiratory phase. If there were differences in the analysis results, a final consensus was reached through inter‐observer discussion. The angle of the LAV (θ) is defined as the angle between the main trunk of the left central adrenal vein and the right horizontal line (Figure 1 and Figure 4).

Define the area where the orifice of the RAV is located. We used a two dimensional axis system, aligned the rectangular coordinate axes with the body axes, and the most distal end of the LAV distal used as the “0 point” of the coordinate axis. In the upper right quadrant, area A and area B are separated by a 45‐degree straight line and the area close to the horizontal line is defined as area A. The lower right quadrant area defined as area C. In area A, two lines divide it into three equal areas, which are defined as areas A1, A2, and A3, respectively, according to the distance from the horizontal line. Location of the RAV orifice

First, we need to find the most distal end of the LAV which is defined as the the place where the last branch of the LAV joins the left central adrenal vein and make a horizontal and vertical line at this position. In the upper right quadrant, we make a 45‐degree straight line. The area close to the horizontal line is defined as area A, and the area close to the vertical line is defined as area B. The lower right quadrant area defined as area C. In order to divide the A area in more detail, we draw two straight lines at 15‐degree intervals in area A, which are defined as areas A1, A2, and A3 according to the distance from the horizontal line (Figure 1). Finally, we found the orifice of the RAV and evaluated the positional relationship between the RAV and the LAV by counting the area where the orifice of the RAV is located.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as the mean ± SD and compared with Student's unpaired t‐test or Wilcoxon rank‐sum test when the distribution was not normally distributed. Categorical variables are presented as numbers or percentages and were compared with chi‐square or Fisher's exact tests. Statistical analyses were performed by SPSS.22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA), and a p value < .05 was considered statistically significant. All reported p values are two‐sided.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics and AVS operating parameters of the study patients

In this study, from January 2020 to July 2021 in our institution, 357 consecutive patients were screened. After exclusions because of unsuccessful AVS and unclear image quality. Finally, a total of 347 PA patients with successful simultaneous AVS were analyzed (Figure 2). The mean age was 49.5 ± 10.3 (23–78) years and 189 patients (54.5%) were male. The mean office and 24‐h blood pressure measurements were 152/93 ± 21/16 and 144/89 ± 15/11 mmHg, respectively. Patient‐related demographic characteristics, clinical information, laboratory test, and imaging features of the patients are shown in Table 1. In all 347 patients, the mean time of the procedure was 20.3 ± 4.6 (10−45) min, mean fluoroscopy time was 5.1 ± 1.5 (2.4−9.3) min, and the mean use of contrast was 15.6 ± 5.8 (10−35) mL.

The location of the RAV orifice

The orifice of the RAV was located in area A in 96.8% (n = 336) of patients, only 1.4% (n = 5) patients were in area B and 1.7%(n = 6) patients were in area C. In area A, the orifice of the RAV was located in the vast majority of patients (80.9%, n = 272), and the proportions of patients in area A2 and area A3 are 17.0% and 2.1%, respectively. Influencing factors of RAV orifice location

According to whether the orifice of the RAV belongs to A1 area, the effects of age, sex, height, body weight, body mass index, potassium, adenoma, and the angle of the LAV on its distribution were analyzed. Univariate regression analysis showed that male (OR 1.777; 95% CI 1.044−3.0234; p = .034), body weight (OR 0.977; 95% CI 0.957−0.966; p = .020), body mass index (OR 0.857; 95% CI 0.811−0.944; p = .001), and the angle of the LAV (OR1.085; 95% CI 1.059−1.111; p<.001) were significantly correlated with the regional distribution of the orifice of the RAV. Further multivariate regression analysis showed that male (OR 2.092; 95% CI 1.120−3.390; p<.001), body mass index (OR 0.844; 95% CI 0.770−0.926; p<.001), and the angle of the LAV (OR1.081; 95% CI 1.055−1.181; p<.001) were significantly correlated with the regional distribution of the orifice of the RAV (Table 2). DISCUSSION

To our best knowledge, this study is the first to study the relationship between the location of LAV and RAV with a large sample size. This study confirmed that the LAV can play an important role on the guiding for right AVS. With the guidance of the LAV, we can quickly and accurately locate the orifice of the RAV, especially for less experienced beginners.

Subtype classification is very important for the treatment of PA, which is helpful for patients to choose appropriate treatment strategies. Tumor resection in patients with aldosterone adenoma can correct hypokalemia and significantly reduce blood pressure. Blood pressure with about 80% of patients can be controlled within the normal range. 18 Adrenal CT is a common method for subtype classification of patients with PA, but its accuracy is often not high, and sometimes wrong judgment will be made. 19 , 20 AVS is the gold standard of subtype classification for PA patients. 21 Unfortunately, due to the low success rate of intubation, difficult data interpretation and certain complications, its wide application in clinic is limited to a certain extent. 22 Due to the importance of AVS in subtype classification of PA, the attempt to improve the success rate of AVS in clinical work has never stopped, especially for the right AVS.

As far as we know, the main reason for the low success rate of AVS is that the RAV is difficult to catheterize due to the large variation of right position. 23 The LAV emerges from the left adrenal hilum, and joins the inferior phrenic vein as a trunk, and then drains into the left renal vein. The anatomical position of the LAV is relatively fixed, so it is easier to reach the LAV than the RAV, and the success rate of left AVS is also higher, usually more than 95%. 24 , 25 At present, AVS was usually completed via sequential sampling method. In order to reduce the impact of time on the concentration of bilateral adrenal cortisol, blood collection from the RAV is often carried out first, and then from the LAV. In this case, we can only use the vertebrae or ribs as indicators to find the RAV, and this approach is relatively blind. Through our study, we found that most of the orifice of the RAV are very close to the horizontal position of the distal end of the LAV. Once we have located the LAV, we can roughly estimate the location of the orifice of the RAV under the guidance of the LAV. In our center, the success rate of bilateral AVS is higher than that reported in the past, especially the success rate of right AVS is as high as 99%, which is much higher than that reported in the literature. 13 , 14 , 15 The main reason is that we take the LAV as a sign to find the RAV. In area A, more than 80% of patients are located in area A1, which shows that the most distal end of LAV in most patients is similar to the horizontal position of the orifice of RAV. When we find the LAV, starting from the same horizontal position to find the RAV can improve our search efficiency to a certain extent. If the RAV is not found in area A1, the search scope can be expanded to areas A1 and A2. Similarly, if the RAV is not found in area A, the RAV will be considered in areas B and C as a last resort. After all, the probability of RAV in areas B and C is very low.

The effects of weight, height and body mass index on the orifice location of the RAV have been confirmed in some studies. Smith and associates 17 studied 93 patients with PA who successfully underwent AVS and found that body mass index was closely related to the orifice of the RAV. In our study, it was found that body mass index was closely related to the orifice location of the RAV. In patients with high body mass index, the horizontal position of the most distal end of LAV and the orifice of RAV is close. In addition, our study shows that sex is also a factor affecting the orifice of the RAV. The horizontal position of the LAV and RAV left in female patients is closer, which has not been observed in previous studies. Interestingly, in our study, we also found that the angle of the LAV is also related to the orifice position of the RAV. The smaller the angle, the closer the orifice position of the RAV is to the horizontal line of most distal end of LAV, which has also never been mentioned in previous studies. The possible reason for this result is that the patient with a smaller the angle of the LAV may have a higher position of the left adrenal gland, so left adrenal gland is closer to the right adrenal gland. In conclusion, when we look for the RAV with the LAV as a marker, taking into account the patient's body mass index, sex, and the angle of the LAV will help us find the RAV faster.

This study has its limitations. First, our study is a retrospective study, lack of randomness. A small number of patients who did not successfully perform AVS were not included in the study, which may affect the results of the study. Second, since the LAV is used as the guide sign of the orifice position of the RAV, the catheter needs to be kept in the LAV as a guide, so the use of this method may be limited for AVS which was completed by sequential sampling method. Finally, all patients only looked for one orifice of the RAV, and did not further search for other RAV orifice. Because some patients may also have a second or third adrenal vein, the orifice location of the RAV in our study may not be representative.

In conclusion, finding the orifice of RAV through the LAV is efficient and technically feasible, and body mass index, sex, and the angle of the LAV have an impact on the position relationship of the LAV and RAV. More large‐scale prospective studies are necessary to further confirm this conclusion.

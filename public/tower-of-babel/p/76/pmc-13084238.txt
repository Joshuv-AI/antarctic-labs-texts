Comment on “Association of coffee consumption with diabetes mellitus and gestational diabetes mellitus”
Authors: Vijay Kumar K V, Pankaj Kshirsagar, Mansi Mishra, Surbhi Panwar
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the study by Su et al., which investigates associations between coffee intake and risks of diabetes mellitus (DM) and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) [1]. The distinction between caffeinated and decaffeinated coffee is clinically meaningful; however, two issues merit closer consideration.

First, the reported associations are modest, with adjusted odds ratios approaching unity. While statistically significant, such effect sizes may have limited clinical relevance without corresponding absolute risk estimates. In populations with high baseline diabetes risk, reliance on relative measures alone may overstate benefit. Incorporating absolute risk differences or number needed to benefit would better clarify whether coffee consumption meaningfully alters disease risk or reflects marginal epidemiological associations [2].

Second, the use of a composite diabetes outcome may obscure important biological heterogeneity. Variations in insulin resistance, β-cell function, and disease progression could differentially interact with caffeine exposure, potentially diluting observed associations [3,4]. This is particularly relevant given the stronger effects observed in individuals with obesity, where metabolic pathways differ substantially. Stratification based on metabolic phenotypes or glycemic indices may provide a more coherent mechanistic interpretation and improve clinical applicability.

These considerations may help refine interpretation and enhance the translational value of the findings.

PROSPER: a phase III trial of lecithinized superoxide dismutase to prevent oxaliplatin-induced peripheral neuropathy in patients with colorectal cancer
Authors: K. Yamazaki, T. Kato, T. Yamada, K. Ogihara, T. Ishikawa, N. Ishizuka, T. Yoshino, K. Yamaguchi, T. Mizushima
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOxaliplatin-based chemotherapy is a standard adjuvant therapy for stage II or stage III colorectal cancer (CRC); however, its use is limited by chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), which reduces the quality of life of patients and often persists for months or years after chemotherapy ends. Compared with superoxide dismutase (SOD), lecithinized SOD (PC-SOD) exhibits much longer stability in plasma, thus reducing oxidative stress, which is a key mechanism of CIPN. A randomized phase II trial (jRCT2031210466) of PC-SOD compared with placebo in patients with CRC who received adjuvant mFOLFOX6 therapy showed promising results regarding its CIPN-preventive effect and safety. Furthermore, PC-SOD suppressed oxaliplatin allergy.DesignPROSPER (Pc-sod for the pRevention Of Sensory PERipheral neuropathy) is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase III study designed to investigate the efficacy of PC-SOD for CIPN in patients with CRC who receive adjuvant mFOLFOX6 therapy. Key eligibility criteria include curative resection of stage II or stage III colorectal adenocarcinoma and 12 planned cycles of mFOLFOX6. The primary endpoint is the time to onset of grade ≥2 CIPN (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events). Key secondary endpoints include the time to onset of grade ≥2 CIPN (Neurotoxicity Criteria of Debiopharm) and oxaliplatin allergy.

Description of protocol

Background and rationale

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide.1 Oxaliplatin-based regimens, such as FOLFOX, are the standard treatment, not only as palliative chemotherapy but also as adjuvant chemotherapy.2,3 However, the effectiveness of this critical therapy is frequently restricted by chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), which is a major dose-limiting toxicity. CIPN often requires dose reductions or premature treatment discontinuation, thereby reducing the optimal dose intensity and adversely affecting the long-term prognosis.4,5 Moreover, severe CIPN deteriorates the quality of life of patients and can persist long after treatment cessation. Despite its profound clinical impact, no universally recognized or highly effective therapeutic or preventive strategies have been established to mitigate CIPN, thus highlighting a critical unmet medical need.6 The successful development of such strategies is expected to improve not only the quality of life of patients with cancer but also their prognosis. CIPN development is strongly implicated with neuronal injury mediated by oxidative stress.7 As a novel agent, lecithinized superoxide dismutase (PC-SOD) was developed to enhance the serum stability of superoxide dismutase (SOD) using our original drug delivery system technique.8,9 We recently showed that PC-SOD suppresses oxaliplatin-induced mechanical allodynia, cold hyperalgesia, and morphological damage of intraepidermal nerve fibers and dorsal root ganglia in rats.10 Additionally, we recently conducted a randomized phase II trial to investigate the potential of PC-SOD (compared with placebo) as a preventive agent against CIPN in patients with stage II or stage III CRC who receive mFOLFOX6 adjuvant therapy.11 The results of that study suggested encouraging clinical efficacy for reducing the incidence and/or severity of CIPN and a favorable safety profile. Furthermore, it suggested that PC-SOD prevents the onset of oxaliplatin allergy, which also limits oxaliplatin administration.

Objectives

The primary objective of the PROSPER study is to confirm the efficacy of PC-SOD for preventing grade ≥2 peripheral sensory neuropathy in patients with stage II or stage III CRC as assessed by National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Key secondary objectives include confirming the potential preventive effect of PC-SOD on grade ≥2 neuropathy as assessed by the Neurotoxicity Criteria of Debiopharm (DEB-NTC) and oxaliplatin-induced infusion-related reactions (CTCAE) or anaphylaxis. Other secondary objectives include evaluating the preventive effect of PC-SOD on grade ≥2 neuropathy as assessed by Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy/Gynecologic Oncology Group-Neurotoxicity (FACT/GOG-NTX), which is one of the patient-reported outcome measures, as well as examining the cumulative dose of oxaliplatin. Additionally, as an exploratory objective, hematological and biochemical analyses of peripheral blood samples will be carried out to investigate the relationship between the presence or absence of anti-PC-SOD antibody and the onset of CIPN as well as safety. Furthermore, post hoc multiomics analyses of patients who provide informed consent may be carried out.

Methods

Trial design and treatment

The PROSPER study is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase III trial designed to confirm the efficacy of PC-SOD for CIPN in patients with CRC who receive adjuvant mFOLFOX6 therapy after curative surgery (Figure 1). Case registration will be conducted using an electronic data capture system (FOUNTAYN). Eligible patients who receive 6 months (12 cycles) of adjuvant mFOLFOX6 therapy (oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2 intravenously on day 1, l-leucovorin 200 mg/m2 intravenously on day 1, and 5-fluorouracil 400 mg/m2 intravenous bolus on day 1 followed by 5-fluorouracil 2400 mg/m2 continuous intravenous infusion over 46 hours every 2 weeks) will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either PC-SOD or placebo. In each cycle, patients will receive intravenous administration of PC-SOD (80 mg) or placebo immediately before oxaliplatin administration. Randomization will be carried out centrally using the minimization method with stratification by study site, sex, and disease stage [T4 or ≥N2 (N2/N3) versus others].12 This study will be conducted in a double-blind, placebo-controlled manner. Participants, treating physicians, investigators, and outcome assessors will be blinded to treatment allocation. Because the color of PC-SOD differs slightly from that of placebo, the PC-SOD and placebo solutions will be prepared by an unblinded pharmacist, transferred to an infusion bag, and covered to maintain blinding so that the administrating nurse or physician and patients are blinded to the content. A follow-up study will be conducted to compare the cancer recurrence rates between the PC-SOD and placebo groups at 1 to 2 years after surgery to evaluate safety. The exact follow-up period will be determined in consultation with the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency of Japan. Two key secondary endpoints are also defined. The first is a time-to-event analysis of the onset of grade ≥2 CIPN as assessed by DEB-NTC. The event is defined as the occurrence of grade ≥2 neuropathy. Time is defined as the number of treatment cycles with the investigational drug. Grading will be carried out on day 1 (before administration of the investigational drug), day 14 after each administration of the investigational drug, and day 28 of the last cycle. The second key secondary endpoint is a time-to-event analysis of the onset of oxaliplatin allergy for a maximum of 12 cycles. The event is defined as the occurrence of a grade ≥2 infusion-related reaction (CTCAE) or anaphylaxis attributable to oxaliplatin. Time is defined as the number of treatment cycles with the investigational drug. An evaluation of oxaliplatin allergy will be carried out throughout the treatment period. Other secondary endpoints include assessments of CIPN using different timeframes, grade thresholds, and evaluation tools (CTCAE version 5.0, DEB-NTC, and FACT/GOG-NTX). Additionally, the cumulative dose of oxaliplatin and safety of PC-SOD will be evaluated.

Sample size and statistical analysis

The planned sample size is 350 patients; 175 patients will be assigned to the PC-SOD group and 175 will be assigned to the placebo group. Patient enrollment will continue until 200 grade ≥2 CIPN (CTCAE) events are observed, even if the total number of patients exceeds 350. However, the maximum number of patients will be limited to 240 per group (total of 480 patients). The incidence of grade ≥2 CIPN (CTCAE) was re-evaluated using data from our previous phase II study11 and retrospective analysis, and the onset rate in the placebo group was estimated at 70.2%. In contrast, the expected hazard ratio (HR) was calculated using grade ≥2 CIPN (DEB-NTC) and grade ≥1 CIPN (CTCAE) from our phase II study11 because these endpoints demonstrated higher intersite consistency; therefore, they provide a more reliable basis for estimating relative treatment effects. The geometric mean of the HRs derived from these two endpoints was used, resulting in an expected HR of 0.673. Accordingly, the expected onset rates of grade ≥2 CIPN (CTCAE) at 6 months (12 cycles) are 70.2% in the placebo group and 55.7% in the PC-SOD group. Assuming a two-sided α of 0.05, power (1 - β) of 0.8, and observation period of 6 months (12 cycles), the required sample size is 159 patients per group (total of 318 patients), and the expected total number of events is 200 (88 events in the PC-SOD group and 112 events in the placebo group). Considering an anticipated dropout rate of ∼10%, the target enrollment is set at 175 patients per group (total of 350 patients).

Translational research

Peripheral blood samples for the liquid biopsy will be stored at three different time points: the first day of the first cycle; 14 days after cycle 6; and 28 days after cycle 12 or at the time of discontinuation. PC-SOD-specific IgG levels in the collected blood samples will be measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Additionally, omics analyses of samples may be conducted to identify potential predictors of the efficacy and safety of PC-SOD if patients provide informed consent.

Protocol validation

The PROSPER study is the first phase III clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy of PC-SOD for the prevention of CIPN in patients with stage II or stage III CRC who receive adjuvant mFOLFOX6 therapy. Additionally, translational analyses will be conducted to investigate the association between the presence of anti-PC-SOD antibody and the efficacy and safety of PC-SOD. In this trial, CIPN will be assessed using CTCAE (for the primary endpoint), DEB-NTC (for the key secondary endpoint), and FACT/GOG-NTX (for the secondary endpoint), which are complementary tools. Although recent reports have suggested that patient-reported outcomes are more sensitive than clinician-based assessments for detecting neuropathy,13 CTCAE has been the most commonly used evaluation method in previous clinical studies. Therefore, CTCAE was selected as the primary assessment tool for our phase II study and this trial. Adverse events are generally assessed at the completion of chemotherapy; however, because CIPN represents a major dose-limiting toxicity of oxaliplatin, delaying its onset is considered clinically meaningful. Accordingly, a time-to-event analysis was adopted for the primary endpoint of our phase II study and this trial. Furthermore, oxaliplatin allergy, which was suggested to be suppressed by PC-SOD in our phase II study, is one of the key secondary endpoints. The PC-SOD group may be able to receive cumulative doses of oxaliplatin that are higher than those of the placebo group; therefore, the cumulative oxaliplatin dose is also included as a secondary endpoint. Our previous studies demonstrated that PC-SOD does not interfere with the antitumor efficacy of chemotherapy (mFOLFOX6).10 Because this trial will target patients who receive adjuvant chemotherapy after curative resection, the potential impact of PC-SOD on cancer recurrence will also be evaluated as a safety measure. We believe that PC-SOD has the potential to suppress CIPN and contribute to an improved quality of life for patients as well as improved prognoses.

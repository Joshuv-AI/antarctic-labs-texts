Introduction

The future mobile information network is expected to support emerging applications such as vehicular networks and the low-altitude economy, where high-speed data transmission and ubiquitous, accurate environmental sensing are core enabling technologies [1]. Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) not only reduces the costs by sharing hardware and signals, but also facilitates mutual assistance between communication and sensing, positioning it as a key development direction for future information networks [2,3]. To achieve higher data rates, larger antenna arrays operating at higher frequencies have been adopted [4], leading to the development of sub-terahertz (sub-THz) communication systems. Meanwhile, these sub-THz systems enhance the resolution of distance and angle, making ISAC in sub-THz systems a promising avenue [5].

Due to the severe path loss in signal propagation, the sub-THz band channel is primarily dominated by line-of-sight (LoS) paths [6]. Thus, the sub-THz system design can be approximated as the analog precoder design, where the beams are flexibly adjusted by the phase shifters (PSs) to enhance the array gain, thereby ensuring ISAC performance, as operated in [[7], [8], [9]. However, in these sub-THz systems, the beam squint phenomenon becomes pronounced [10,11], significantly reducing the antenna array gain and deteriorating the communication performance in existing precoding designs. To mitigate this issue, the true time delay (TTD) has been introduced in the analog hardware, providing frequency-dependent phase shifts that ensure beam alignment across subcarriers [12]. Conversely, for sensing tasks, beam squint can lead to wider coverage and enhanced frequency-aware angle estimation capabilities. For instance, studies in [13,14] worked on controlling the pattern of beam squint through TTDs, facilitating user localization or target angle estimation. Nevertheless, current sub-THz systems lack a joint design for both functionalities, failing to achieve optimal trade-offs in the presence of beam squint.

It is worth noting that the correlation between communication and sensing channels affects the ISAC performance when both functionalities co-exist [15]. Previous studies such as [16] evaluated this correlation from the perspective of power sharing between the propagation paths in sensing and communication channels. However, they do not provide direct guidance for analog precoder design. Moreover, the existing analog precoders fail to actively manipulate the degree of correlation between channels. Meng et al. [17] improved the channel correlation through external reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, thereby enhancing ISAC performance. However, these methods are primarily limited to narrowband systems and may exacerbate beam squint effects in sub-THz systems. In fact, analog precoding systems possess their own design degrees of freedom that can be leveraged to establish the performance trade-off, which has not been thoroughly explored. Notably, the TTDs are used to adjust the beam pointing across subcarriers, holding the potential in inherently tuning the channel correlation.

Inspired by this, in this paper, we aim to integrate the unique electromagnetic propagation characteristics of sub-THz channels with the specific hardware degrees of freedom in sub-THz analog systems to optimize the analog precoder, without altering the existing sub-THz hardware architecture. First, we demonstrate the enhancement effect of the communication-sensing (C-S) channel correlation on the dual-functional gain. Based on this, we propose an optimization-based approach for analog precoder design to achieve the ISAC Pareto boundary. While this benchmark addresses the problem with near-optimal performance, the iterative and exhaustive search processes involve high complexity particularly under large arrays, probably leading to high latency in mobile networks. Inspired by recent research on Synesthesia of Machines (SoM) [18,19], which utilizes multi-modal environmental observations to enhance the transceivers design with lowered overhead, we further propose a learning-aided scheme for ISAC analog precoding design with reduced complexity. Unlike existing learning-aided precoding designs such as [20,21], we tailor the network architecture for the analog precoding task. This is achieved by adopting a complex-valued network and a feature fusion module to effectively process channel data, as well as a deep unfolding head for designing generalized precoder parameters. Numerical experiments have demonstrated the superiority of the proposed schemes in terms of dual-functional performance enhancement.

Beam squint challenge and its countermeasures

In a wideband system, for a subcarrier at frequency fm, if the phase shift designed for fc, φn=π(n−1)sinθ0, is applied, the actual beam direction θm excited at this subcarrier satisfies 2πfmdc(n−1)sinθm=π(n−1)sinθ0, leading to sinθm=fcfmsinθ0, i.e., the beam direction shifts with frequency. The angular deviation can be approximated as Δθm=θm−θ0≈−Δfmfctanθ0, revealing the scaling laws of beam squint: it is proportional to the fractional bandwidth Δfm/fc and the scanning angle θ0. It is also indirectly related to the array aperture: a larger number of antennas N yields a narrower beam width ≈2/(N|cosθ0|), making the system more susceptible to gain loss for a given Δθm.

For a phase-shifter-only (PS-only) architecture, the array gain loss at frequency fm due to beam misalignment is GlossPS(fm)=|aH(θ0,fm)a(θ0,fc)|2=|1N∑n=0N−1ejnπΔfmfcsinθ0|2=(sin(Nπ2·Δfmfcsinθ0)Nsin(π2·Δfmfcsinθ0))2. This loss becomes significant when N·(Δfm/fc)·|sinθ0| is non-negligible.

To alleviate this effect, a true-time delay (TTD) line introduces a frequency-dependent phase shift φTTD,n(fm)=−2πfmτn. By setting a delay τn=(n−1)Δτ for the nth antenna element with Δτ=dsinθ0/c, the combined phase at subcarrier fm becomes −2πfmτn+φn=−2πfm·(n−1)dsinθ0c+π(n−1)sinθ0=−π(n−1)sinθ0, which is independent of frequency. This demonstrates that TTD generates a frequency-proportional phase shift that perfectly cancels the beam squint, achieving a consistent beam direction θ0 across all subcarriers.

For sensing, the TTDs also concentrate energy toward the target direction to maximize the echo SNR. Simultaneously, it restores frequency-domain phase linearity, improving waveform coherence and joint range-angle resolution. These fundamental advantages establish TTD as a key enabler for communication and sensing in THz wideband systems.

CSP-Net: low-complexity alternative

The proposed SA-Opt exhibits high computational complexity due to its search and iterative processes. This may result in excessive latency in the analog precoder design, preventing it from meeting the requirements of future mobile networks. Therefore, in this section, we present a low-complexity alternative based on unsupervised learning, termed as the complex-valued squint-aware analog precoding network (CSP-Net).

Complex-valued network structure

The commonly used real-valued networks in [20,21] may fail to capture the phase information between the real and imaginary components of the complex-valued channel data. Therefore, we employ a complex-valued neural network architecture specifically designed for channel inputs. This network is specifically designed to exploit the mathematical properties of complex domains. This network mainly includes complex-valued convolutional layers, complex-valued fully-connected (C-FC) layers, complex-valued batch normalization (C-BN) layers, and complex-valued activation functions, as detailed in [27].

The input contains the communication and sensing channels {hc,m,Gm}m=1M. We pre-process the channel data as H‾m=hc,mhc,mH and G‾m=Gm respectively. Then the total data are represented as H‾={H‾m}m=1M∈CM×Nt×Nt and G‾={G‾m}m=1M∈CM×Nr×Nt. Following this, we perform power normalization before inputting them into the neural network. The network outputs are also normalized and post-processed to comply with analog precoder constraints. The layers are structurally optimized for precoder-specific optimization objectives.

Layer description

The proposed CSP-Net consists of four parts: Communication channel feature extraction network (CFE-Net), sensing channel feature extraction network (SFE-Net), feature fusion layer (FFL) and analog precoding head (APD). The detailed architecture design of CSP-Net is illustrated in Fig. 2.

The architecture of CSP-Net. The CSP-Net is complex-valued and consists of four parts: Communication channel feature extraction network (CFE-Net), sensing channel feature extraction network (SFE-Net), feature fusion layer (FFL) and parameter design network (PD-Net).

CFE-Net and SFE-Net

The features from the communication and sensing channels are extracted respectively by CFE-Net and SFE-Net. For communication channel data Hin, two layers of complex convolutional blocks are employed, each of which includes one complex-valued convolutional layer, one complex ReLU (C-ReLU), one C-BN and one complex pooling. Similarly, another branch of convolutional blocks is employed for Gin in SFE-Net. Parameterized by ϕCFE and ϕSFE, the outputs are represented as Vc=ϕCFE(Hin) and Vs=ϕSFE(Gin) respectively. The feature design is intrinsic to these layers: complex-valued convolutional layers automatically extract spatially-relevant features from these channel matrices, treating the real and imaginary components as linked entities. This is superior to real-valued networks for phase-sensitive radio data.

Feature fusion layer

To further enhance the features from Hin and Gin and extract the correlation between them, we tailor the convolutional block attention module (CBAM) similar to [28] on [Vc,Vs] with the complex-valued network, including a channel-based attention and a spatial-based attention mechanism. Then the output is flattened as v for the following precoding design. This module explicitly models the coupling between sensing and communication data, allowing the network to learn which combined channel characteristics are most relevant for precoding.

Analog precoding head

The values of TTDs, PSs and the power allocation are sequentially predicted through APD. Notice that analog precoder parameters exhibit mutual coupling. To address this interdependence, the analog precoding head employs a deep unfolding structure, utilizing unfolded neural network layers to emulate the iterative optimization process. First, the values of TTDs are randomly initialized as t^(0) and concatenated with v as the input. Then the TTDs are updated through L1 layers of C-FC, C-ReLU and C-BN, implementing gradient ascend for C-S channel correlation maximization. The output is expressed as t^=t^(L1)∈CQt. Subsequently, the PSs and the power allocation are updated alternatively through unfolded layers. Specifically, the input φ^(0) and p^(0) concatenate with v and t^, and undergo L2-layer interleaved updates that emulate SA-Opt in Section 3. Parameter sensitivity analysis

Robustness to NLoS components

Consider the communication channel as modeled in Eq. (3), where the power of the NLoS paths is much lower than that of the LoS path, i.e., E[|βc,m(l)|2]E[|βc,m(0)|2]=ε≪1. The received SNR at the user can be approximated as

Given the small ε, the SNR fluctuation due to NLoS components is a second-order effect. Consequently, the communication rate is largely insensitive to limited multipath in LoS-dominated scenarios.

For sensing task, introducing weak NLoS paths adds low-power interference to the echo, slightly raising the noise floor and potentially causing a minor bias in angle estimation. Its impact approximates an increase in CRB: ΔCRB∝ε·(CRBLoS). Therefore, ΔCRB is negligible when ε≪1.

In summary, for the outdoor low-altitude ISAC scenario focused on in this work, the marginal impact of NLoS components on the dual-functional performance is minimal.

Insertion loss. A signal amplitude attenuation factor ρin (0<ρin≤1) in each TTD unit reduces the effective transmit power by ρin2. For communications, the SNR is scaled by ρin2. For sensing, the SNR is similarly scaled, leading to a proportional degradation in estimation precision: CRB(loss)≈CRB(ideal)/ρ2.

Limited delay range. A finite maximum delay tmax causes truncation errors for large arrays or wide scan angles, requiring the design constraint tmax≥(N−1)dsin(θmax)/c to avoid severe performance loss.

Phase ripple Non-flat group delay introduces subcarrier-dependent phase deviations with variance σripple2, degrading frequency coherence and increasing both ICI and CRB proportionally to σripple2.

Hardware overhead. The TTD network incurs additional area and power consumption compared to a PS-only architecture. The partially-connected structure adopted in this work represents a deliberate trade-off between mitigating beam squint and managing this hardware complexity.

The performance of the proposed optimization framework also relies on the accuracy of the acquired channel priors.

Impact of channel estimation error. Let the estimated communication channel be h^c,m=hc,m+ec,m, where the estimation error ec,m∼CN(0,σe2I). Substituting h^c,m into the optimization leads to a degradation in the actual array gain. The expected communication rate is bounded by

Impact of mobility. Mobility causes the channels to vary across frames. Let Tc be the channel coherence time, and Td the algorithm computation and update delay. The SA-Opt with relative high complexity is more suited for quasi-static scenarios where Tc≫Td, while CSP-Net, with its minimal inference time, can satisfy Td≪Tc and thus better accommodate higher mobility.

Impact of pilot and feedback overhead. The net spectral efficiency is (1−η)Rc, where η is the fraction of resources used for pilots and feedback. Increasing pilot length reduces σe2 and improves Rc, but increases η, leading to a trade-off. This work focuses on precoder optimization given CSI, assuming η is already optimized at the system level. The proposed algorithms improve Rc with acceptable computational complexity.

Impact of distance distribution on ISAC performance

Considering the user distance dc and the target distance ds, the complex gains in the channel model can be expressed as βc,m∼CN(0,σβ2dcγc), and αk,m∼CN(0,σα2dsγs), where γc and γs are the path-loss exponents. For outdoor LoS propagation in THz band, typical values are γc≈2 and γs≈4.

The user’s received SNR can be expressed as SNRc,m=pm|βc,m|2Gm(θc,ϕc)/σc2∝1/dcγc. Consequently, the communication rate Rc decays with increasing user distance, approximately as Rc(dc)≈∑m=1Mlog2(1+Cdcγc), where C is a constant related to power and array gain.

The sensing echo SNR is SNRs,m∝|αk,m|2Gm(θs,ϕs)/σs2∝1/dsγs. Since the CRB is inversely related to the Fisher information matrix, it follows that CRB(ds)∝1∑mSNRs,m∝dsγs. Thus, sensing accuracy degrades with target distance. Given the distance ds and dc, the adaptive resource allocation is embedded in our proposed framework: SA-Opt achieves it by solving problem (20), while CSP-Net learns this distance-dependent mapping from data.

Performance analysis under large angular separation

Let (θc,ϕc) and (θs,ϕs) denote the user and target directions, respectively, with a large angular separation ΔΘ=(θc−θs)2+(ϕc−ϕs)2. A conventional single-beam approach requires a beam width of at least ΔΘ. For a uniform planar array, the beam width is inversely proportional to the array aperture, i.e., Θ3dB≈2/(Ntcosθ0). Consequently, the wide beam needed to cover a large angular separation significantly reduces the array gain.

In contrast, the proposed scheme exploits the frequency dependence introduced by TTDs. The subcarriers are partitioned into two sets, Mc and Ms, for communication and sensing respectively. By optimizing the TTD matrix T and the phase-shift vector fPS, the design ensures that: For m∈Mc, the beam’s main lobe aligns with the user direction, i.e., argmaxθ,ϕGm(θ,ϕ)≈(θc,ϕc), and for m∈Ms, the beam’s main lobe aligns with the target direction. The communication rate and sensing CRB can thus be approximately decomposed as contributions from user-directed subcarriers and from target-directed subcarriers respectively. The proposed SA-Opt and CSP-Net methods automate the learning of this ratio and the corresponding beam patterns through joint optimization, achieving a soft decoupling of communication and sensing in the spatial domain through flexible partitioning of frequency resources.

Experimental results

In this section, simulation results are presented to evaluate the performance of the proposed schemes. Partial codes are publicly available at https://github.com/PKU-PCNI/CSP-Net.

Parameters setting

Unless otherwise specified, the transceiver-related parameters are set as: Nt=256, Nr=256, Nh=Nv=16, Qth=Qv=8, Lh=Lv=2, ND=NtNr. tmax=100 ps and Bt=4. The central frequency point is fc=100 GHz, with B=8 GHz and M=32. The channel related parameters are: σβ=1, σα=0.6, τmax=100 ns, K=3. The noise variances are σc2=σs2=0 dBm. The CSP-Net model employs the Adam optimizer, with a learning rate of 0.004 and a batch size Nb of 16. L1=3 and L2=2 in unfolding analog precoding head. We define the SNR as Ptσc2 and the mean squared interval of angle (MSIA) between targets and the user as ∑k=1K(θs,k−θc)2+(ϕs,k−ϕc)22K. The training data consist of communication and sensing channel data from various environments, generated based on the channel models in (3) and (8). The neural networks were trained on an NVIDIA RTX4060Ti, and the algorithms were tested in MATLAB 2024b. The system has 10 cores, a per-core clock speed of 2.5 GHz, and 160 floating-point operations per cycle.

Dataset construction

We construct a low-altitude ISAC channel dataset to emulate a 100 m × 80 m urban road intersection, featuring vehicles on the ground and low-altitude flying UAVs. Along the road side, a BS is equipped with a UPA.

First, we use Simulation of Urban MObility (SUMO) software to model vehicular and UAV trajectories at intersections [30]. The vehicle trajectories include straight motion, turning and lane-changing, while the UAV trajectories encompass horizontal straight-line flight and vertical lift. The spatial coordinates of all moving entities are exported frame-by-frame as the positioning ground truth for subsequent angle estimation and offline model training. Subsequently, the scenario is imported into Wireless InSite® [31] with configured BS/vehicle antenna arrays and surface parameters, ensuring consistency with SUMO. Ray-tracing then generates per-frame communication/sensing channels following Section II’s model. These channel data enable loss calculation and model training.

Both training and test sets span MSIA values ranging from 0 to 0.5, with increments of 0.1. The training set comprises 100k communication and sensing channel pairs per MSIA value, while test set includes 10k pairs per MSIA. To validate performance over varying SNR/MSIA levels, CSP-Net is trained under transmit power constraints corresponding to each SNR, or on training sets with respective MSIA values. For SNR generalization, CSP-Net trained at SNR = {0, 5, 10} dB is tested at {15, 20} dB. To assess generalization under distribution shifts, CSP-Net trained under MSIA = {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} is tested at MSIA = 0.4 and 0.5.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a novel analog precoder design to enhance ISAC performance in sub-THz systems. By leveraging the design degrees of freedom inherent in the RF front-end of sub-THz systems, we proactively adapt to the beam squint effect. Initially, we introduced an optimization-based method, SA-Opt, as a near-optimal benchmark. To further reduce the computational complexity, we presented a SoM-driven low-complexity alternative, CSP-Net. The proposed schemes effectively enhance the dual-functional gain and demonstrate robustness across various spatial distributions, highlighting the significant impact of C-S channel correlation on the ISAC performance boundaries. Furthermore, these schemes are compatible with the existing hardware architectures, offering promising applications for sub-THz systems.

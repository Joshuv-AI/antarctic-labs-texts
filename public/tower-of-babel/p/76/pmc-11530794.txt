Nitrogen use efficiency of open-pollinated maize cultivars and topcrosses under low nitrogen soil
Authors: Sunday Ayodele Ige, Bashir Omolaran Bello, Jimoh Mahamood, Michael Afolabi, Charity Aremu, Stephen Abolusoro, Solomon Oyekale, Favour Chijioke
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The study was conducted to evaluate the nitrogen utilization efficiency (NUE) of open-pollinated maize cultivars under low nitrogen soil conditions. It took place at the Lower Niger River Basin Development Authority in Ilorin, Nigeria, during the 2019 and 2020 cropping seasons. The nitrogen treatments (0, 45, and 90 kg N ha⁻1) were assigned as the main plots, while the genotypes were designated as sub-plots in a split-plot design. The NUE of open-pollinated varieties (OPVs) and Topcrosses significantly increased with the application of 45 kg of nitrogen per hectare compared to 0 kg. Similarly, the maize grain yield for both OPVs and Topcrosses ranged from 1.40 to 3.57 t ha⁻1 and from 1.07 to 2.64 t ha⁻1 across the two years with the application of 45 kg of nitrogen per hectare compared to 0 kg. With the exception of days to 50 % silking, all other traits tested exhibited a significant correlation with grain yield. A gene pool for tolerance to low nitrogen may have been present in the cultivars Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 and Acr 95 Tze Comp 4C3, which combined well with other cultivars to produce high grain yield and NUE. Topcrosses, including Ev Dt-Y 2000 Str C0 × Tze-W Dt Str C4, Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 × Acr 95 Tze Comp 4C3, Ak 95 Dmr-Esrw × Tzb Rel D4 CO-W, Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 × Tze-W Dt Str C4, and Br 9922 Dmr Sr × Ev Dt-Y 2000 Str C0, also demonstrated strong potential for maize grain yield and NUE. This suggests that these crosses could serve as promising candidates for developing low nitrogen tolerance in maize for low nitrogen soil environments. A positive relationship between fertilizer levels, NUE, and grain yield indicates that proper nitrogen fertilizer management can enhance both NUE and the grain yield of maize genotypes. Genetic analysis revealed that both phenotypic coefficient of variation (PCV) and environmental variance (δ2p) were higher than genotypic coefficient of variation (GCV) and genetic variance (δ2g), suggesting that environmental influences were more significant than genetic factors for the tested traits. Consequently, improving these traits may be achieved by manipulating environmental conditions.

Introduction

Maize is an important cereal crop that feeds about 60 % of the world's dietary needs, along with wheat and rice [1]. Therefore, improving maize yields is crucial for ensuring food security. The global maize area covers approximately 197 million hectares, with significant cultivation in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), Asia, and Latin America [2]. In many countries, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America, and a few Asian nations, maize consumed as human food contributes over 20 % of total food calories [3]. Compared to wheat and rice, maize is a more versatile, multipurpose crop. In developed countries, it is primarily utilized as livestock feed and as an industrial and energy crop.

As an important cereal crop, maize requires large amounts of nitrogen due to its high yield potential. Nitrogen fertilizer is essential for achieving high crop yields, as it is a major element for grain formation in maize [,[1], [4], [5]]. Among various agronomic management practices for maize improvement, enhancing nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) and the rational use of nitrogen fertilizer are vital for boosting maize grain production while simultaneously reducing environmental pollution. Excessive fertilization poses a range of problems and challenges to agricultural soils and ecosystems [6]. Adequate nitrogen fertilization was adopted during the “Green Revolution,” leading to significant increases in maize grain yields at that time. To reduce nitrogen losses and the crop's nitrogen requirements, it is recommended to enhance the NUE of applied nitrogen fertilizer by selecting maize genotypes with superior traits for achieving higher grain yields with lower nitrogen inputs [7]. NUE refers to the grain yield per unit of available nitrogen [8] and can further be divided into nitrogen uptake and nitrogen utilization efficiency. Developing maize varieties with improved NUE is crucial for increasing grain yields while reducing environmental pollution. Enhancing NUE is essential for the effective utilization of synthetic nitrogen fertilizers, which are a major macronutrient. A significant increase in nitrogen fertilization is necessary for active growth and photosynthetic activities throughout the crop cycle. Among cereals, maize has the highest yield potential, requiring substantial amounts of nitrogen and water from the soil. To achieve high yields, farmers often use high doses of nitrogen fertilizers, leading to soil, water, and environmental pollution in the form of soil degradation, eutrophication, and volatilization, thereby reducing NUE.

Nigeria's Southern Guinea Savannah (SGS), characterized by bimodal rainfall patterns, favorable temperatures, and strong solar radiation during the growing season, presents an opportunity to enhance maize grain yields beyond current levels. The SGS is also marked by unpredictable rainfall, low water storage capacity, and deficient soil nutrients [9]. The majority of soils in this region are Kaolinitic Alfisols, which are brittle and have low effective cation exchange capacity, resulting in low maize grain yields [10].

In many developing countries, the removal of government subsidies on fertilizers has led to inadequate and irregular distribution of fertilizer to average farmers. Developing maize varieties with high NUE that can adapt to low-nitrogen environments will be essential for producing high maize grain yields in the SGS.

Nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) is defined as the higher returns per unit of applied fertilizer. The objective of modern plant breeding is to improve the nitrogen use efficiency of maize cultivars. Enhancing NUE in maize could significantly improve global food security, maximize economic gains, and mitigate nitrate leaching issues in maize production [11]. These maize varieties, selected for savanna adaptation and early maturity, can easily adapt to erratic rainfall and drought. Therefore, the objective of this study was to select maize genotypes with high grain yield and good nitrogen use efficiency.

Materials and methods

Collection of Germplasm and Diallelic cross

Ten drought-tolerant open-pollinated variety (OPV) maize genotypes were collected from the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) in Ibadan. During the 2018 growing season, these genotypes were crossed in a partial diallel fashion to produce 45 F1 topcrosses. Before field evaluations, the topcrosses were harvested, processed, and stored in cool rooms. The resulting crosses and their parents were evaluated during the 2019 and 2020 growing seasons at the Lower Niger River Basin Development Authority in Ilorin, Nigeria. Three different levels of nitrogen application (0, 45 kg ha⁻1, and 90 kg ha⁻1) were used. The experimental design employed was a split-plot design, where nitrogen levels (0, 45, and 90 kg N ha⁻1) were assigned to the main plots and the genotypes to the sub-plots.

Data Collection and analyses

The International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) in Ibadan provided ten drought-tolerant open-pollinated maize varieties (OPVs). In the 2018 growing season, these OPVs were used in a partial diallel cross to produce 45 F1 topcrosses. Sowing took place over two growing seasons (22nd July 2019, and 20th July 2020). After each season, the topcrosses were harvested, processed, and stored in cool rooms for later field evaluations. The experimental design was a split plot design. The nitrogen (N) regime (0, 45, and 90 kg N ha⁻1) was designated as the main plot, while the genotypes were assigned to the sub-plot in a split-plot design. In all evaluations, two row plots were used. Each row was 6m in length, spaced at 0.75m between rows 0.25m within rows with four replications to give a population density of approximately 53,333 plants per hectare. Observed cultural practices included pre-emergence spray of gramozone and primextra for weed control supplemented with hand weeding as necessary during the season. Agronomic data collected included the mean emergence counts at 5, 7, and 9 days after planting (DAP). Plant height was measured as the average height in centimeters (cm) of five plants per plot from the base to the node bearing the upper ear, recorded as soon as physiological maturity was attained. Ear height was also recorded as the average height in centimeters from the base of the plant to the node bearing the upper ear at physiological maturity. Days to 50 % tasseling was recorded as the number of days from planting until 50 % of the plants in a plot had produced tassels. Days to 50 % silking were recorded as the number of days from planting until 50 % of the plants in a plot showed incipient silk extrusion. Grain yield per plot was measured as grain weight per plot, adjusted to 15 % moisture and converted to tons per hectare. The percentage grain moisture was determined using a Dickey-John moisture tester (Model 14998, Dickey-John Corporation, Auburn, Alabama).

Discussion

In this study, Topcrossed maize used nitrogen fertilizer more efficiently than OPVs, which had also been documented in Nigeria by Ref. [10]. Topcrosses outperformed OPVs in both growing seasons, even on low-N soil. The expression of maize plant height under low- and high-N conditions showed that genotypes were shorter under low-N conditions than under high-N conditions, indicating a differential response to nitrogen availability among the genotypes in this group. This finding aligns with [17], who observed that certain morphological and physiological responses of maize under nitrogen deficiency, such as reduced plant height, lower light interception efficiency, accelerated senescence, increased nitrogen mobilization to grain, and reduced nitrogen concentration in the plant, are common.

Yield reduction under low-N conditions was also observed among the genotypes compared to their performance under high-N conditions. This is consistent with earlier studies by Ref. [18], who reported that one of the main effects of nitrogen stress is a reduction in the levels of photosynthetic components, such as chlorophyll, leading to reduced photosynthetic capacity and carboxylase efficiency, and consequently, lower yield. Most of the topcrosses recorded higher values of nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) compared to the parent lines. However, the highest NUE value was recorded by the parent Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2, outperforming both the crosses and other parents. Cultivars such as Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 and Acr 95 Tze Comp 4C3 combined well with all varieties and showed promise for NUE and grain production. Other combinations, including Ev Dt-Y 2000 Str C0 × Tze-W, Dt Str C4; Ev Dt-Y 2000 Str C0 × Acr 95 Tze Comp 4C3; Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 × Acr 95 Tze Comp 4C3; Ak 95 Dmr-Esrw × Tzb Rel D4 CO-W; Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 × Ak 95 Dmr-Esrw; and Br 9922 Dmr Sr × Ev Dt-Y 2000 Str C0, also combined well for NUE and grain yield.

The plant heights and ear placements increased as the days to silking and tasseling increased. A higher grain yield corresponded with longer days to 50 % silking. This finding is consistent with [19], who noted that genotypes with shorter days to silking had longer grain-filling periods, potentially contributing to the increased grain yield, observed in the tested genotypes.

All genotypes responded adequately to the low-N regime, which contained 45 kg N ha⁻1. When compared to nitrogen-tolerant OPVs and topcrosses, maize cultivars that were reported to have little response to nitrogen regimes could occasionally show improvement under low-N conditions [9]. These genotypes are tolerant to low-N and show promise for nitrogen utilization, either through efficient nitrogen consumption, improved absorption capacity, or increased grain production [9]. The number of days to anthesis in 2019 was lower than in 2018, allowing for more effective soil nutrient absorption, which may explain the superior nitrogen usage and higher grain yield in OPVs in 2018 compared to 2019.

A significant nitrogen-by-year interaction was identified among genotypes for grain production. Estimates for ear and plant heights, as well as nitrogen efficiency, were higher, highlighting the importance of available moisture, especially during anthesis, in the accumulation of photoassimilates and their translocation to grain yield. Enhanced nitrogen regimes among the genotypes led to increased NUE and grain yield improvement, consistent with the findings of [20]. Grain yield and NUE were best at 45 kg N ha⁻1 across all genotypes, aligning with [21], who observed improvements in NUE and grain production at low-N, as well as [10], who showed that maize cultivars in the Nigerian savanna responded efficiently to low-N.

Substantial interaction between year and nitrogen application was found in topcrosses and OPVs, with significant phenotypic coefficients of variation. This confirms that the maize cultivars exhibited more genetic variation in response to different nitrogen regimes and NUE for grain yield [20]. The significant genetic increase in ear and plant heights emphasizes their importance, and they may be selected for genetic improvement under different nitrogen regimes, as previously stated by Ref. [22]. Plant height and days to silking, with the lowest and highest genetic increases, suggest broader genetic improvement for silking and narrower genetic improvement for plant height across all genotypes.

Despite a low genetic increase in seedling emergence and days to silking, significant differences were observed among these traits. Grain yield was positively correlated with most attributes, similar to the findings of [10]. The NUE of OPVs and topcrosses increased significantly with nitrogen application between 0 and 45 kg N ha⁻1. Across both years, topcrosses and OPVs yielded between 1.40 and 3.57 t ha⁻1 and 1.07 and 2.64 t ha⁻1, respectively, with nitrogen increments ranging from 0 to 45 kg N ha⁻1. Among the genotypes studied, NUE was greatest at 45 kg N ha⁻1. Grain yield was significantly related to most of the evaluated attributes, except days to silking. The positive relationship between fertilizer levels, NUE, and grain yield indicates that proper nitrogen fertilizer management can be adopted to improve NUE and the grain yield of maize genotypes.

Genetic analysis revealed that both phenotypic coefficient of variation (PCV) and phenotypic variance (δ2p) were higher than the genetic coefficient of variation (GCV) and genetic variance (δ2g), indicating that environmental influences were more substantial on the tested traits than genetic factors. Thus, improvement of these traits can be achieved through manipulation of environmental factors.

Conclusion

The study demonstrated that Topcross maize outperformed open-pollinated varieties (OPVs) in nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) and grain yield under both low- and high-nitrogen conditions. Genotypes showed differential responses to nitrogen availability, with shorter plants and reduced yield under low-nitrogen conditions. However, certain genotypes, particularly Dmr-Esr Y Cif 2 and Acr 95 Tze Comp 4C3, showed strong performance in both nitrogen efficiency and grain production. Nitrogen application at 45 kg N ha⁻1 was optimal for improving both NUE and grain yield across the studied genotypes. Despite significant environmental influences on traits like plant height and silking, the findings indicate that appropriate nitrogen management and genetic improvement can enhance maize performance under varying nitrogen regimes.

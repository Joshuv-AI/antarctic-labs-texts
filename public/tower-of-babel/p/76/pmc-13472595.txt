Nonlinear Relationship between Early Postoperative Pulse Pressure Variation and Acute Kidney Injury Risk in Older Cardiac Surgical Patients
Authors: Xiaodan Gong, Yang Yu, Biyu Wei, Fei Xu, Yonghui Zhang, Chenghui Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute kidney injury (AKI) frequently complicates the postoperative course of older patients in surgical intensive care units (ICUs), contributing to increased mortality and prolonged hospital stays. Traditional AKI diagnostic methods, relying on changes in serum creatinine and urine output, often result in delayed detection. Pulse pressure variation (PPV), a dynamic hemodynamic parameter derived from arterial waveform analysis, reflects preload responsiveness and can provide early, complementary information on circulatory status and end-organ perfusion. Although both elevated and diminished PPV levels have been associated with adverse outcomes, the specific nature of the association between early postoperative PPV and AKI risk remains uncertain. This study investigated the relationship between PPV during the first 12 and 24 h after ICU admission and the development of AKI, with a focus on potential nonlinear associations and optimal PPV thresholds.MethodsThis retrospective cohort analysis utilized data from the MIMIC-IV and MIMIC-III databases. We included patients aged 60 years and older who underwent cardiothoracic surgery and had available PPV measurements within the first 24 h following ICU admission. The primary dataset was derived from MIMIC-IV, while MIMIC-III served as an external validation cohort. The main outcome was postoperative AKI, as identified by KDIGO criteria. Logistic regression models adjusted for relevant clinical covariates were used to assess associations between PPV at 12 and 24 h (PPV12h and PPV24h) and AKI. Nonlinear associations were examined using restricted cubic spline (RCS) modeling. Discriminative ability was assessed using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), with DeLong’s test used to compare model performance.ResultsOf the 5,445 patients included, 2,574 (47.3%) developed AKI after surgery. Those with AKI were generally older and had more comorbidities and higher illness severity scores. In multivariable analyses incorporating RCS terms, a U-shaped relationship was identified between PPV12h and AKI (p for nonlinearity = 0.0224). The lowest predicted risk was observed at a PPV12h of 0.144, corresponding to a 39.4% relative reduction in AKI risk. This association was independently validated in a separate cohort (n = 897), where a PPV12h of 0.142 was associated with a 59.8% risk reduction. In contrast, PPV12h provided better predictive value than PPV24h, which showed no significant association with AKI. The inclusion of PPV12h significantly enhanced the discrimination of predictive models.ConclusionEarly postoperative PPV demonstrates a U-shaped correlation with AKI risk in older patients undergoing cardiothoracic surgery, with both low and high values associated with higher incidence. A PPV12h around 0.14 was consistently linked to the lowest risk in both primary and validation cohorts. These findings remained robust after adjusting for multiple confounders. PPV assessed at 24 h, however, showed limited predictive utility. Routine early PPV monitoring may, therefore, serve as a valuable adjunct for perioperative hemodynamic assessment, potentially facilitating timely interventions to mitigate postoperative renal complications.

Background

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common and serious complication in critically ill surgical patients, particularly among older patients in the early postoperative period [1, 2]. It is independently associated with increased mortality, prolonged intensive care unit (ICU) and hospital stays, and a higher risk of progression to chronic kidney disease [1]. Despite ongoing improvements in perioperative care, the incidence of AKI remains high, with reported rates ranging from 10% to 40%, varying by surgical type and patient population [3, 4].

Early identification of patients at risk for AKI remains a clinical priority. Current diagnostic criteria – based on serum creatinine elevation and decreased urine output – are delayed markers that often identify AKI only after significant renal injury has occurred [5]. Consequently, considerable research effort has been devoted to identifying early, dynamic, and potentially modifiable predictors of AKI. Among these, early postoperative hemodynamic instability and impaired renal perfusion have emerged as key contributors to renal injury [6].

Pulse pressure variation (PPV), a dynamic preload indicator derived from arterial waveform analysis, has been widely used in critical care to assess fluid responsiveness [7]. As a surrogate of intravascular volume status and cardiopulmonary interaction, PPV reflects dynamic changes in stroke volume. Recent studies suggest that both excessively high and low PPV values may be detrimental: high PPV may indicate hypovolemia and risk of renal hypoperfusion, while low PPV may reflect volume overload or reduced cardiac compliance, both of which can compromise renal perfusion [8, 9]. However, the precise association between early postoperative PPV levels and AKI has not been fully elucidated.

Several studies have shown that time-critical hemodynamic monitoring may aid in predicting and even preventing AKI [10, 11]. Nonetheless, there is a lack of consensus on the nature of the PPV-AKI relationship and whether an optimal PPV range exists that is associated with minimal renal risk. Such knowledge could inform personalized fluid and vasopressor management in the early postoperative period, potentially improving renal outcomes.

In this study, we investigated the relationship between PPV within the first 12 and 24 h after ICU admission (denoted as PPV12h and PPV24h in the text, and as PPV_12 h and PPV_24 h in the figures) and the occurrence of AKI, as defined by KDIGO criteria, in surgical ICU patients aged 60 years and older. Our findings may help establish real-time hemodynamic targets for perioperative renal protection and support the development of early warning strategies in this patient population.

Methods

Data Source

This retrospective study utilized data extracted using Structured Query Language (SQL) from two large, publicly available critical care databases: Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) and MIMIC-III, both maintained by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) [12, 13]. MIMIC-IV includes detailed, de-identified health data for over 76,000 ICU admissions between 2008 and 2019 at the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (BIDMC) in Boston, Massachusetts, including demographics, vital signs, laboratory tests, medication administration, diagnostic codes, and outcomes such as discharge and mortality [12]. MIMIC-III, an earlier version of the database, comprises ICU admissions from 2001 to 2012 at the same center [13].

In this study, the primary analysis was based on patients from MIMIC-IV, specifically those admitted to the ICU after 2014 to avoid overlap with the earlier database. To validate the main findings, an independent cohort of patients was extracted from MIMIC-III, applying consistent inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Access to both databases was granted after completing the required human subjects research training and approval through the PhysioNet credentialing process (Certification Number: 13567919). Given the fully anonymized nature of the data, individual informed consent was waived. Use of the MIMIC databases for research has been approved by the institutional review boards of both MIT and BIDMC and complies with relevant data protection regulations [12, 13]. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study Population

Patients were enrolled according to the following criteria. Inclusion criteria included age ≥60 years and undergoing cardiac thoracotomy surgery. Exclusion criteria included (1) absence of PPV records within the first 24 h of ICU admission, (2) missing AKI data according to KDIGO definitions, (3) more than 20% missing data in key clinical variables. For patients with multiple ICU admissions, only data from the first ICU stay were analyzed.

Outcomes of Interest

The primary outcome of this study was the occurrence of AKI during the ICU stay after cardiac surgery, defined according to the KDIGO criteria [14]. The main analysis focused on the relationship between PPV12h and postoperative AKI risk, specifically investigating whether a particular PPV12h range was linked to the lowest risk. Secondary analyses included (1) comparing the predictive performance of PPV12h and PPV24h; (2) assessing whether PPV12h could enhance existing clinical prediction models; and (3) examining differences in PPV12h levels between patients with and without AKI. To assess the robustness of our findings, analyses were validated in an independent patient cohort.

Variables

A comprehensive set of variables was collected from each database within the first 24 h of ICU admission. In MIMIC-IV, these included (1) demographics, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), admission type, and ICU service type; (2) comorbidities, including heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, diabetes with or without complications, renal disease, liver disease, malignancy, and infection-related conditions such as endocarditis; (3) vital signs and severity scores, including heart rate (HR), blood pressure (BP), temperature, and scoring systems such as SOFA, SAPS II, APSIII, OASIS, and LODS; (4) laboratory parameters, including hematologic indices such as hemoglobin, white blood cell count, platelet count, renal and hepatic function markers such as creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, bilirubin, albumin, electrolytes such as sodium, potassium, calcium, acid-base balance indicators such as bicarbonate, lactate, anion gap, and coagulation profiles including prothrombin time and activated partial thromboplastin time; and (5) respiratory and hemodynamic variables, including PPV12h and PPV24h, vasopressor use on day 1, such as epinephrine, norepinephrine, phenylephrine, vasopressin, and continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT).

Missing data were handled using complete-case analysis and observations with missing values for any variable included in the model were excluded. When variables were recorded multiple times, only the first available measurement was used.

For the MIMIC-III validation cohort, a similar approach was applied. Variables included demographics, ICU and hospital length of stay, in-hospital mortality, comorbidities such as heart failure, arrhythmia, liver disease, diabetes, pulmonary circulation disorders, renal indicators including serum creatinine and AKI status, hemoglobin levels, ventilator use, and cardiovascular surgery. Key variables, including PPV and AKI status as defined by the KDIGO criteria, were harmonized across the two datasets to ensure consistency and comparability of the results.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing). A two-sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Only cases with complete data for model variables were included. Missing data were assumed to be at random, and no imputation was performed.

Normality of continuous variables was evaluated using a custom function combining distribution tests selected based on sample size (Shapiro-Wilk or Kolmogorov-Smirnov), alongside assessments of skewness and kurtosis [15]. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Between-group comparisons were conducted using the Student’s t test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables, and chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate.

Logistic regression models were constructed to evaluate the association between PPV12h or PPV24h and the incidence of AKI. Covariates were selected based on clinical relevance, literature review, and regression results (p < 0.1), and included age, sex, comorbidities such as hypertension, chronic kidney disease, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, baseline renal function, and illness severity scores such as the SOFA score.

To assess and minimize potential confounding, pairwise Pearson correlations among covariates were calculated. Covariates showing high collinearity (|r| > 0.7) would have been excluded or combined; however, in our dataset correlations were generally weak (all |r| < 0.35), and correlations between PPV12h and other covariates were minimal (|r| ≤ 0.07), indicating no concern for multicollinearity.

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) functions with five knots at Harrell’s recommended quantiles (5th, 27.5th, 50th, 72.5th, and 95th percentiles) were incorporated into the logistic regression models to explore potential nonlinear relationships between PPV and AKI [16]. Likelihood ratio tests were applied to compare spline models with linear models to assess improvement in model fit. To account for multiple comparisons when evaluating the incremental predictive value of PPV12h (e.g., AUC comparisons across multiple variables), adjusted p values or Bonferroni correction were applied where appropriate.

Model performance was primarily evaluated in terms of discrimination using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) to quantify the ability to predict AKI occurrence. The AUCs of models with different variable combinations were compared to evaluate improvements in predictive performance. Specifically, the added predictive value of PPV12h was evaluated by comparing AUCs of models with and without PPV12h using DeLong’s test [17].

Baseline Characteristics of the Primary Cohort

In the primary cohort comprising 5,445 cardiac surgery patients from MIMIC-IV, 2,574 (47.3%) developed postoperative AKI as defined by the KDIGO criteria. Among these patients, stage 1 accounted for 1,991 patients (37.1%), stage 2 for 337 patients (6.3%), and stage 3 for 246 patients (3.7%). Among patients with stage 3 AKI, 82 (33%) received CRRT.

The median age of the cohort was 71 years (interquartile range [IQR], 66–77), with 3,801 patients (70%) being male. Compared with patients without AKI, those who developed AKI were older (median 73 vs. 70 years, p < 0.001) and less likely to be male (66% vs. 73%, p < 0.001). The AKI group had significantly higher proportions of comorbidities, including congestive heart failure (31% vs. 22%, p < 0.001), cerebrovascular disease (13% vs. 9.1%, p < 0.001), chronic pulmonary disease (24% vs. 20%, p < 0.001), diabetes (40% vs. 32%, p < 0.001), pulmonary arterial hypertension (7.6% vs. 5.6%, p = 0.003), and pre-existing atrial fibrillation (48% vs. 35%, p < 0.001).

In terms of perioperative severity scores, the AKI group exhibited significantly higher values across all measures, including APACHE III (median 38 vs. 32, p < 0.001), SOFA (median 6 vs. 5, p < 0.001), LODS (median 5 vs. 4, p < 0.001), OASIS (median 33 vs. 30, p < 0.001), SAPS II (median 39 vs. 35, p < 0.001), and the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) (median 6 vs. 5, p < 0.001), indicating a greater burden of chronic comorbid conditions among AKI patients. Regarding surgery type, isolated coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) was more common among non-AKI patients (55% vs. 49%, p < 0.001), whereas combined procedures such as CABG plus valve surgery were more frequently observed in the AKI group (22% vs. 15%, p < 0.001).

Laboratory findings before and during ICU stay showed that AKI patients exhibited lower pre-ICU hemoglobin levels (median 12.2 vs. 13.0 g/dL, p < 0.001), and higher serum potassium (median 4.28 vs. 4.23 mmol/L, p < 0.001) and serum magnesium (median 2.30 vs. 2.18 mg/dL, p < 0.001). Supportive therapy usage also differed between the groups. The use of mechanical ventilation was more frequent among AKI patients (73% vs. 62%, p < 0.001), while vasopressor administration was comparable between groups (81% vs. 80%, p = 0.198).

Baseline Characteristics of the Validation Cohort

In the validation cohort of 897 critically ill patients from MIMIC-III, 249 (27.8%) developed AKI during ICU stay. Among these patients, stage 1 accounted for 249 patients (25.1%), stage 2 for 2 patients (0.2%), and stage 3 for 92 patients (9.3%). Among stage 3 AKI patients, 82 (89.1%) received renal replacement therapy (CRRT).

AKI patients were significantly older (median 75 vs. 72 years, p < 0.001) and had higher BMI (median 27.9 vs. 26.7 kg/m2, p = 0.021). They also had higher prevalence of comorbidities, including congestive heart failure (34% vs. 27%, p = 0.049), chronic pulmonary disease (21% vs. 15%, p = 0.034), and renal failure (8.8% vs. 3.1%, p < 0.001). Consistent with findings in the primary cohort, AKI patients in the validation cohort exhibited higher pre-ICU and peak creatinine levels (all p < 0.001), and longer ICU stays (median 4 vs. 2 days, p < 0.001).

Univariate Analysis and Selection of Covariates

To identify potential risk factors for AKI, univariate logistic regression analyses were performed with AKI as a binary outcome variable. A total of 58 variables were significantly associated with AKI (p < 0.05). These included demographic characteristics (age, gender, weight, height); comorbidities such as congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, chronic pulmonary disease, diabetes, renal disease, pulmonary arterial hypertension, hypertension, and preoperative atrial fibrillation; composite comorbidity indices such as the CCI; vital signs including HR and diastolic blood pressure (DBP); illness severity scores on the first ICU day (APACHE III, SOFA, LODS, OASIS, SAPS II); surgical types (isolated CABG); postoperative complications (delirium); laboratory parameters obtained before or during ICU stay (creatinine, hemoglobin, potassium, calcium, bicarbonate); and treatment-related variables such as vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, CRRT, ICU length of stay, and hourly urine output. Variables with a p value <0.1 in the univariate analysis were considered candidates for inclusion in the multivariable logistic regression models, guided by clinical relevance and prior literature.

Rationale for Covariate Selection in Multivariable Logistic Regression Models

To examine the nonlinear association between early ICU PPV (PPV12h or PPV24h) and postoperative AKI, multivariable logistic regression models incorporating RCS were constructed. Covariates were selected based on univariate significance and clinical relevance, with representative variables chosen to avoid multicollinearity. The final model included age, BMI, HR, DBP, SOFA score on the first ICU day, mechanical ventilation, hourly urine output, CCI, and pre-ICU creatinine.

Correlation among Selected Covariates

To assess the suitability of the selected covariates for multivariable modeling, pairwise Pearson correlations were calculated. As shown in Figure 1, the correlations were generally weak, with most absolute correlation coefficients (|r|) below 0.20. Specifically, the correlations between PPV12h and all other covariates were minimal (|r| ≤ 0.07), indicating no collinearity concerns with the primary exposure variable. The highest observed correlations were between creatinine and hourly urine output (r = −0.29), creatinine and CCI (r = 0.34), and SOFA score and CCI (r = 0.30). These values remain well below conventional multicollinearity thresholds (typically r > 0.7), supporting the concurrent inclusion of these variables in the same model. Accordingly, all selected variables were retained in the nonlinear regression analyses examining the association between early postoperative PPV and AKI risk.

Correlation matrix of selected confounders for PPV-AKI association modeling.

Primary Outcome: Association between PPV and AKI

A multivariable logistic regression model incorporating RCS was used to examine the relationship between PPV12h and the incidence of AKI. The likelihood ratio test indicated that adding PPV12h significantly improved the model fit (χ2 = 710.668, df = 2, p < 0.001), supporting a strong association between early PPV values and AKI risk.

Importantly, this association was nonlinear, demonstrating a statistically significant U-shaped pattern (nonlinear component: χ2 = 7.60, df = 2, p = 0.0224), as shown in Figure 2. Both low and high PPV12h values were associated with higher AKI risk, whereas an intermediate range conferred protective. The statistically significant protective interval extended from 0.078 to 0.275. The lowest estimated AKI risk occurred at PPV12h = 0.144, corresponding to an adjusted odds ratio (OR) of 0.606 – reflecting a 39.4% reduction in AKI risk at this optimal point.

Nonlinear association between PPV12h and AKI risk (primary cohort).

Notably, although the association curve was U-shaped, the risk of AKI plateaued rather than increased at PPV12h values exceeding 0.275, suggesting a flattening of the risk trajectory at higher PPV levels, instead of a continuous rise. Wald chi-square tests confirmed that PPV12h was an independent predictor of AKI (χ2 = 14.66, df = 3, p = 0.0021), after adjusting for age, gender, diabetes, BMI, baseline renal disease, HR, DBP, use of vasopressin, mechanical ventilation on the first ICU day, SOFA score on the first ICU day, hourly urine output, CCI, and baseline creatinine prior to ICU admission.

For comparison, a linear model including PPV12h as a continuous term also showed a statistically significant but weaker association with AKI (χ2 = 7.07, df = 1, p = 0.0079), underscoring the importance of capturing the nonlinear relationship to reflect the complex physiological association between PPV12h and AKI risk. To evaluate whether PPV measured at a later time point (PPV24h) also predicted AKI, both nonlinear and linear models were tested. In the spline-based model, PPV24h was not significantly associated with AKI (overall effect: χ2 = 4.38, df = 3, p = 0.2237; nonlinear component: χ2 = 4.25, df = 2, p = 0.1192). Similarly, the linear model showed no significant association (χ2 = 0.11, df = 1, p = 0.7440). These results suggest that PPV12h provides greater prognostic value for AKI than PPV24h.

To assess the robustness of these findings, the same analytic approach was applied to an independent validation cohort. As shown in Figure 3, a statistically significant association between PPV12h and AKI was again observed (Wald χ2 = 9.88, df = 4, p = 0.0424), indicating that PPV12h retained predictive value for AKI in the validation cohort. The likelihood ratio test further supported this association (χ2 = 9.68, df = 3, p = 0.0215).

Nonlinear association between PPV12h and AKI risk (validation cohort).

Consistent with the primary analysis, the relationship between PPV12h and AKI in the validation dataset exhibited a U-shaped pattern, with a statistically significant protective range interval identified between 0.089 and 0.318. The lowest estimated risk of AKI occurred at PPV12h = 0.142, closely mirroring the optimal value of 0.144 found in the primary cohort, corresponding to an adjusted OR of 0.402 – representing a 59.8% reduction in AKI risk at this point. This strong concordance in both effect shape and optimal value reinforces the robustness and reproducibility of the association. In contrast, PPV24h did not exhibit a significant nonlinear association with AKI in the generalized additive model, consistent with findings in the main analysis. These findings suggest that early postoperative PPV (PPV12h) provides more consistent and clinically meaningful prognostic information for AKI than measurements obtained at later time points (PPV24h).

Incremental Discriminative Contribution of PPV12h in AKI Risk Assessment

To explore whether PPV12h provides additional discriminative information beyond established postoperative risk factors, multivariable logistic regression models were constructed using covariates significantly associated with AKI in univariate analyses (p < 0.05). The final receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis included nine key variables selected for their clinical relevance and statistical significance: platelet count, maximum creatinine, mean creatinine, potassium, bicarbonate, blood urea nitrogen, SOFA score, anion gap, and chloride.

Two models were compared: a base model including these nine variables without PPV12h, and an extended model including PPV12h in addition to them. ROC curves were generated for both models, and AUC values were calculated. As summarized in Table 3 and illustrated in Figures 4 and 5, the inclusion of PPV12h provided small but statistically significant increases in predictive performance across all variables. For example, the AUC for platelet count increased from 0.521 to 0.545 (p < 0.001), maximum creatinine from 0.830 to 0.833 (p < 0.001), mean creatinine from 0.765 to 0.768 (p = 0.015), potassium from 0.556 to 0.570 (p = 0.001), bicarbonate from 0.541 to 0.557 (p = 0.006), blood urea nitrogen from 0.757 to 0.761 (p = 0.007), SOFA score from 0.597 to 0.603 (p = 0.008), anion gap from 0.639 to 0.645 (p = 0.013), and chloride from 0.555 to 0.565 (p = 0.023). However, when PPV12h was added to the fully adjusted model including all nine variables, the overall AUC remained unchanged (0.911 vs. 0.911), suggesting limited additional discriminative contribution in the context of established high-impact predictors.

Discussion

In this large retrospective cohort study of patients aged 60 years and older undergoing cardiac surgery, early postoperative PPV12h was significantly associated with the development of AKI. Notably, the nonlinear U-shaped association between PPV12h and AKI was closely aligned across both cohorts, with the lowest AKI risk observed at PPV12h = 0.144 in the primary cohort (MIMIC-IV) and 0.142 in the validation cohort (MIMIC-III). This concordance underscores the robustness and reproducibility of the observed relationship.

Interpretation of the Nonlinear U-Shaped Relationship

The U-shaped association indicates that both high and low PPV values are linked to increased AKI risk, but these extremes reflect distinct hemodynamic states rather than solely intravascular volume status. High PPV may suggest fluid responsiveness and potential hypovolemia, whereas low PPV may reflect impaired cardiac or vascular responsiveness rather than adequate preload, potentially requiring vasopressors or other interventions to maintain renal perfusion [18–20].

Consistent with this pattern, the observed distributions show that the median PPV12h was slightly higher in the AKI group compared to the non-AKI group (0.175 vs. 0.166). However, the interquartile ranges of both groups largely fall within the previously identified protective intervals (0.078–0.275 in the primary cohort, 0.089–0.318 in the validation cohort), indicating that most patients in both groups remained within a range associated with lower AKI risk. The violin plot (Fig. 6) illustrates the overall overlap in distributions while highlighting subtle but significant variations in early hemodynamic profiles.

Extremes of PPV may compromise renal perfusion through different mechanisms. Hypovolemia reduces effective arterial inflow, leading to ischemic tubular injury, whereas excessive fluid loading elevates central venous and renal interstitial pressures, impairing glomerular filtration and oxygen delivery. The kidney is particularly vulnerable to venous congestion, which can exacerbate AKI in the setting of reduced urine output and impaired autoregulation [21, 22]. Maintaining PPV within an intermediate range (approximately 0.14) may support optimal renal perfusion while minimizing risks of ischemia and congestion-related injury.

Comparison with Previous Literature

To our knowledge, this is the first large-scale study to identify a nonlinear association between early postoperative PPV and the risk of AKI, which was further validated in an independent external cohort. A consistent nadir of AKI risk around a PPV12h value of 0.14 was observed in both datasets, highlighting the potential clinical relevance of this threshold. However, clinical application should prioritize trend assessment and integration with other hemodynamic parameters rather than single-threshold targets. Notably, no significant association was observed between PPV measured at 24 h and subsequent AKI risk. This discrepancy may reflect the influence of postoperative factors emerging beyond the early phase, such as cumulative fluid shifts, initiation of vasopressors or diuretics, and changes in ventilatory management, which could attenuate the predictive value of PPV at later time points. These findings highlight the importance of early hemodynamic assessment, suggesting that interventions within the initial 12-h postoperative window may exert a more pronounced protective effect on renal function than those initiated later.

Clinical Implications

Early postoperative PPV12h is a dynamic, non-invasive index of preload responsiveness that provides real-time information on volume status and cardiovascular responsiveness, complementing static laboratory values and severity scores. Although adding PPV12h to a fully adjusted multivariable model did not improve the overall AUC, this is likely because established high-impact predictors such as creatinine-related indices and SOFA score already captured most of the predictive information, and it does not negate its clinical utility. PPV12h can still offer valuable complementary insights, particularly in the early postoperative period, by enabling rapid hemodynamic assessment and timely optimization of fluid and vasopressor therapy. Importantly, PPV12h should not be used in isolation; it is most effective when integrated into a multimodal hemodynamic evaluation, including vital signs, urine output, laboratory parameters, and other dynamic indices. The superior predictive value of PPV12h over later measurements (e.g., PPV24h) underscores the importance of early assessment within the first 12 postoperative hours.

Conclusion

A robust U-shaped association was observed between PPV12h and postoperative AKI risk, with increased AKI incidence at both extremes of PPV. The lowest risk was consistently observed at a PPV12h near 0.14 in both the primary and validation cohorts, and this association remained significant after adjustment for relevant confounders. In contrast, PPV24h did not retain predictive value, highlighting the importance of the early postoperative assessment for renal risk stratification. Early PPV12h assessment may, therefore, serve as a practical tool to guide perioperative management and mitigate renal risk.

Strengths and Limitations

Key strengths of this study include the large sample size, comprehensive adjustment for covariates, the use of multivariable logistic regression models incorporating RCS to explore nonlinear trends, and validation in an independent cohort. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design precludes causal inference. Second, residual confounding from unmeasured variables cannot be excluded. In particular, data on postoperative fluid balance, diuretic use, or fluid overload – known determinants of AKI risk – were not available, which may confound the observed association between early PPV12h and AKI. Third, information on vasopressor dosages, cardiac output measurements, or microcirculatory factors was also lacking. Fourth, this study focused exclusively on the predictive value of PPV12h for AKI development and did not assess its association with other outcomes, such as ICU length of stay, mortality, or longitudinal fluid balance. Fifth, some patients may have had postoperative pleural effusions or residual pneumothorax, even if chest drains were placed. Such conditions could affect heart-lung interactions and consequently PPV measurements. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, these patients could not be fully excluded. Finally, although external validation was performed in a second ICU cohort, the findings were derived from a single institutional dataset, potentially limiting generalizability to other populations or care settings.

Future Directions

Prospective interventional studies are warranted to (i) assess whether targeting the optimal PPV range in the early postoperative period reduces AKI incidence, (ii) evaluate the combination of PPV with other hemodynamic markers and renal biomarkers to improve individualized risk stratification and to guide fluid and vasoactive management, and (iii) validate findings across multicenter cohorts to refine thresholds for use in diverse clinical settings.

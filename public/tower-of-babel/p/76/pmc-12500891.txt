Updated screening criteria for enhanced oil recovery methods and their application to the T oilfield in eastern Mongolia
Authors: Anuudari Arvis, Baehyun Min
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Enhanced oil recovery (EOR) plays a vital role in maximizing oil production from depleting reservoirs. As EOR technologies advance, updating screening criteria is essential to evaluate and recommend suitable EOR methods for target reservoirs. This study aims to establish updated screening criteria to identify the most effective EOR methods based on specific reservoir characteristics. A structured selection approach facilitates the evaluation of advanced EOR technologies to ensure the suitability for particular reservoirs while optimizing recovery processes and improving overall efficiency. To demonstrate this approach in practice, we present a case study using field data collected from the T oilfield in Mongolia. The estimated recovery factor based on primary and secondary recovery is in the range of 10% to 17%. Therefore, the field development plan considers the application of EOR to the T oilfield. An integrated methodology that combines EOR screening with score assignment is applied to provide a comprehensive evaluation of potential EOR technologies. The results show that the miscible carbon dioxide (CO2) injection, miscible hydrocarbon (HC) injection, and immiscible nitrogen (N2) injection methods have the highest scores without any violation of criteria. This approach ensures a thorough selection process by considering various technical factors relevant to reservoir characteristics and aligning with the study’s objective. The case study demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed screening criteria in real-world scenarios and offers valuable insights for operators selecting EOR methods under similar conditions.

Introduction

Enhanced oil recovery (EOR) includes advanced techniques that were designed to extract additional oil from reservoirs once conventional methods can no longer sustain production1. As technologies evolve and sustainability becomes a priority, EOR remains a crucial focus in energy research and application2. The advancement of EOR technologies has led to the widespread adoption of screening as a method for identifying and ranking the most suitable EOR techniques based on specific reservoir characteristics3. A well-structured screening process enhances the accurate evaluation of EOR potential, increasing reservoir value while reducing development risks4.

EOR has been widely employed in oilfields with low production rates caused by high water cuts despite significant remaining oil saturation5. EOR methods are categorized based on the type of displacing fluids into gas flooding (miscible or immiscible), chemical flooding, thermal flooding, and microbial flooding1. Each category includes different techniques that were designed to improve recovery efficiency based on specific reservoir conditions. Gas flooding is further classified into miscible and immiscible methods. Miscible gas flooding injects gas that fully mixes with oil, reducing its viscosity and improving flow1,6, while immiscible gas flooding uses gases that do not mix with oil but assist in displacement by maintaining pressure or driving oil through gas expansion mechanisms3,6. Chemical methods involve injecting surfactants, polymers, or alkaline surfactant polymer (ASP) to modify the properties of oil or reservoir rock, improving oil recovery2,7. Thermal methods apply heat to reduce oil viscosity and facilitate extraction, with common techniques including hot water injection, steam injection, and combustion8. Lastly, microbial methods use microorganisms or their byproducts to enhance oil recovery through biochemical processes9–11.

The selection of EOR processes depends on specific crude oil properties and reservoir conditions, with the primary goal of maximizing recovery while taking into account both economic and environmental factors. Miscible gas flooding is particularly effective for light crude oil, as it involves injecting gases such as carbon dioxide (CO2) or hydrocarbon (HC) gases that mix with the oil, thereby reducing viscosity and improving mobility12. Chemical processes have also been effective for light crude oil by improving both displacement and sweep efficiency13. In contrast, thermal processes are primarily used for heavy crude oil because they significantly reduce viscosity, utilizing mechanisms such as oils, which makes extraction easier13–15. Microbial EOR applies fundamental chemical principles, utilizing mechanisms such as interfacial tension (IFT) reduction, emulsification, wettability alteration, mobility ratio enhancement, selective plugging, viscosity reduction, oil swelling, and increased reservoir pressure due to gas formation14,30. This comprehensive understanding of EOR techniques underscores the need for tailored approaches that align with the specific reservoir characteristics and crude oil types.

A structured table format for EOR screening is essential for maximizing oil extraction from reservoirs. It enables efficient comparison of multiple reservoir properties against predefined criteria for various EOR methods. This approach allows for the rapid identification of suitable techniques based on factors such as rock properties (e.g., porosity and permeability) and fluid properties (e.g., gravity, viscosity, and saturation). Furthermore, a structured table format facilitates the simultaneous evaluation of multiple reservoirs, which helps oil companies prioritize their EOR efforts across their asset portfolios.

A notable example is the EOR screening criteria table developed by Taber et al. (1997a)18, which incorporates nine reservoir properties across twelve EOR methods. This tabular format provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating and selecting suitable EOR technologies for a given oilfield. By structuring complex data in a clear and accessible manner, these tables help engineers and geologists make well-informed decisions when assessing EOR implementation for specific reservoirs. Building on this approach, Dickson et al. (2010)13, Adasani et al. (2011)5, and Hartono et al. (2017)16 derived average reservoir properties to enhance the robustness of screening and reservoir evaluation frameworks for EOR methods. The use of average values not only improves the standardization of input data but also simplifies the comparative analysis of different EOR techniques.

Furthermore, these studies introduced the concept of defining lower and upper limits for the reservoirs property relevant to a given EOR method. This systematic approach saves time and minimizes the risk of overlooking viable EOR candidates, which ultimately supports more informed decision-making and better resource allocation in EOR projects.

This study updates EOR screening criteria by integrating insights from multiple researchers over the past four decades5,13,15–26. The revision process carefully evaluates rock and fluid properties, which broadens the applicability and effectiveness of EOR methods. The updated criteria enable engineers to assess field feasibility at an early stage, providing improved guidance for subsequent evaluation phases. A case study of the T oilfield in Mongolia is presented to illustrate the practical application of the updated screening criteria. Exploration of the T oilfield began in 1991 and reached the appraisal stage in 2011. According to the Mineral Resources and Petroleum Authority of Mongolia (MRPAM), its proven reserves are approximately 150.13 million barrels or about 21.018 million tons27. The case study evaluates various EOR methods, including gas injection, chemical flooding, thermal recovery, and microbial flooding, to identify the most suitable approach for this reservoir. By applying these comprehensive screening criteria and assigning scores to EOR methods, this study aims to establish a more robust framework for selecting effective techniques, ultimately enhancing oil recovery and optimizing resource utilization.

Screening of EOR technologies

The screening of EOR processes is essential for identifying the most effective and economically viable techniques suited to specific reservoir characteristics and project objectives. Various authors and institutions have developed screening guidelines and criteria to support the successful implementation of EOR methods, including those established by respected organizations such as the Society of Petroleum Engineers (SPE)13,15,16,18,21,24. In addition to academic research and documented case studies, these resources offer valuable insights and practical examples drawn from real-world EOR projects1,6,7.

Utilization of these guides and criteria enables operators to make well-informed decisions, thereby optimizing the implementation of EOR strategies while improving project efficiency and profitability. When applying EOR criteria to a specific reservoir, each factor must be individually evaluated to ensure alignment with reservoir conditions and project objectives13.

Effective application of EOR criteria requires assessing each factor based on its relevance to reservoir and project conditions4,5. This assessment should be supported by comprehensive technical analyses to fully understand the implications and expected outcomes of different EOR methods. In addition, identifying any gaps or inconsistencies in the criteria is crucial to selecting a practical and effective EOR approach that maximizes recovery. Carefully evaluating each criterion and addressing potential problems individually leads to more informed decision-making and improves the success of EOR projects. For a more comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms and effectiveness of different EOR methods, refer to the studies by Terry et al. (2003)1 and Thomas et al. (2007)14.

Updated screening criteria for EOR methods

This study presents an updated version of the screening criteria for EOR originally established by Taber et al. (1997a)18, a foundational contribution to petroleum engineering that is widely cited in scholarly literature. Their criteria cover twelve EOR methods evaluated using nine key reservoir properties, with statistical parameters (i.e., lower, upper, and average values) derived from various EOR surveys conducted between 1974 and 1996, including those published in the Oil and Gas Journal5,18, as shown in Table 1. For each EOR method, italic values indicate the average or mean of a given parameter. For example, in miscible nitrogen floods, an oil gravity notation of “>35↗48↗” signifies that this method is suitable for oils with a gravity exceeding 35° API (provided other criteria are met), that higher-gravity oils are preferable (↗) and that the approximate mean or average of existing miscible nitrogen projects is 48° API (Taber et al., 1997a)18.

In this study, the updated EOR criteria incorporate comprehensive data from multiple EOR projects conducted between 1997 and 2024. Table 2 presents a summary of these revised screening criteria, including a range of reservoir values derived from field data and established oil displacement mechanisms relevant to each EOR method. Building on the work of Taber et al. (1997a)18, this study introduces several key enhancements: porosity is now included as an evaluation criterion, four additional EOR methods (miscible WAG, microbial EOR, hot water flooding, and a combined approach of immiscible HC with WAG) have been integrated, and the statistical parameters of the evaluation criteria have been updated. As a result, the comprehensive list of ten reservoir properties is as follows: oil gravity, oil viscosity (µo), oil composition, oil saturation (So), formation type, net thickness (h), permeability (k), reservoir depth (d), temperature (T), and porosity (ø). Furthermore, we categorized the sixteen EOR methods into six groups: miscible gas, immiscible gas, chemical, thermal, mechanical, and microbial methods. More specifically, the miscible gas category includes four methods: CO2, HC, Water Alternating Gas (WAG), and N2 injection. The immiscible gas category also consists of four methods: N2, CO2, HC, and HC combined with WAG. The chemical category includes three methods: polymer flooding, alkaline/surfactant/polymer (ASP), and surfactant combined with polymer or alkaline. The thermal category comprises three thermal techniques: combustion, steam injection, and hot water flooding. In addition, one mechanical method (surface mining) and one microbial method are included. Furthermore, this study updated not only the EOR criteria and EOR methods but also expanded statistical parameter ranges of lower and upper limits, as well as average values of criteria based on field data collected from various EOR projects, as summarized in Table 21,3,5,6,9,12,13,16–18,21,23,24,26,29. The updates to reservoir properties and improved methodologies for EOR indicate a considerable advancement in the field. The refinement of reservoir property assessments and advanced EOR methodologies is expected to facilitate a more accurate selection of suitable EOR techniques that are tailored to specific oilfield characteristics. For each EOR method, every reservoir property provides a range of favorable EOR operating conditions, with an average representing the primary weighting value. For example, the criterion for oil gravity (°API) in miscible N2 floods can be expressed as “Min > 20↗, Avg 48↗”. This notation means that the EOR method is effective for oils with a gravity exceeding 20° API and that higher-gravity oils are preferable (↗). In addition, the approximate average oil gravity for current miscible nitrogen projects is 48° API, as shown in Table 2.

While Table 2 provides valuable insights, it is important to understand that the parameters are not intended to establish definitive threshold limits but rather to provide indicative guidelines that should be interpreted with nuanced consideration and contextual understanding. Specifically, the proposed limits should be subject to continuous reassessment and refinement through rigorous scientific research. The presented parameters serve primarily as examples and provide an approximate range of EOR criteria that have been associated with successful project outcomes. These guidelines are intended to offer a starting point for reservoir evaluation rather than definitive standards and should be adapted based on project-specific factors and emerging industry insights.

Scoring of EOR methods

A scoring mechanism is integrated into the reservoir evaluation and ranking process of EOR methodologies. By using a stairwise scoring system, each of the ten reservoir properties listed in Table 2 is assessed against the established screening criteria of the sixteen recovery methods.

Scores range from 0 to 1 and represent the degree to which a given reservoir property aligns with the EOR criteria. If a property only partially falls within the defined EOR range, a proportional score is assigned based on its level of compliance. In this study, the minimum (Xmin) and maximum (Xmax) values serve as threshold limits, while the average value (Xavg) acts as the primary weighting factor. In addition, if any criterion receives a score of zero, the corresponding EOR method is excluded from consideration, regardless of its overall score.

Mongolia’s oil industry and the T oilfield

Historical phases of mongolia’s oil industry development

The development of Mongolia’s oil industry can be divided into two phases. The first phase began in 1931 with the discovery of the Zuunbayan and Tsagaan-els oilfields in Dornogobi province, supported by technical and logistical assistance from the former Soviet Union. This stage established the foundation for subsequent regional exploration and extraction efforts.

The second phase started in 1991 with the enactment of the Mongolian Petroleum Law, which Congress ratified. This legislative milestone led to the establishment of the national petroleum company, Mongol Gazariin Tos, in the same year. Mongol Gazariin Tos partnered with Texas-based joint exploration companies, which enabled extensive hydrocarbon research in Mongolia and facilitated two successful rounds of exploration bidding. Currently, Mongolia’s sedimentary oil basins have been delineated into 33 distinct petroleum exploration blocks, with the T oilfield prominently illustrated in Fig. 327. These blocks have been compared to major production fields in China because the two countries share a border4. In addition, the T oilfield is located adjacent to China’s Hailar oilfield, with both fields situated within similar Mesozoic and Cenozoic sedimentation layers. The Hailar Basin, located in the northern part of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, has been identified as a significant oil-producing area with the potential for large-scale production.

Since 2010, Mongolia has been recognized as an oil-producing country, with reserves registered in blocks XIX, XXI, and PSC-97 under the Mongolian Minerals Fund4. These reserves are estimated to contain approximately 2.45 billion barrels (332.64 million tons) of “proved plus probable” reserves, while 318.6 million barrels (43.258 million tons) are classified as “proved” reserves27. The evaluation of these reserves was conducted by Petro China Daqing Tamsag LLC, a subsidiary of the Chinese National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC), the largest integrated petroleum company in China, along with Dongsheng Petroleum (Mongol) LLC, a subsidiary of Sinopec, the country’s second-largest petroleum company. Since 1993, these two companies have conducted extensive drilling operations, with a total of 1,728 wells dedicated to exploration, appraisal, and development. Specifically, 988 wells were drilled in the XIX block, 609 in the XXI block, and 131 in the PSC-97 blocks27. Presently, the government of Mongolia has formalized production-sharing contracts (PSCs) for 28 out of the 33 blocks, collaborating with 31 companies in the sector27.

Overview of the T oilfield in eastern Mongolia

This study validates the updated EOR screening method by applying it to the T oilfield in eastern Mongolia. This oilfield has an average elevation ranging from 587 m to 702 m. Flat grasslands characterize the region, which falls within the arid climate of the medium extratropical zone and exhibits significant diurnal temperature variations – see Fig. 4. The exploration at this oilfield commenced in 1991. In 2005, Petro China Daqing Tamsag LLC entered a production-sharing contract and advanced to the appraisal stage in 2011. According to MRPAM27, as of 2011, proved reserves amount to 150.13 million barrels (21.018 million tons). Petro China Daqing Tamsag LLC has evaluated these reserves. Based on exploration and appraisal tests, oil displacement is expected to occur primarily through solution gas and water drive. The estimated recovery factor under primary and secondary recovery schemes ranges from 10 to 17%, which highlights the need for EOR applications in this field.

The T oilfield and Hailar oilfield share similar Mesozoic and Cenozoic environments, with comparable formation, alluviation, and structural characteristics. The stratigraphic sequence of the T oilfield, from bottom to top, consists of the basement (pre-Cretaceous formations with an unclear era division), the Tsagaantsav (TSA) Formation of the Lower Cretaceous system, Lower-Lower Zuunbayan Formation (LLZ), Lower-Upper Zuunbayan Formation (LUZ), Sainshand Formation, the Bayanshiree Formation, and formations from the Tertiary and Quaternary systems.

Notable hiatuses and unconformities exist between various formations, particularly between the basement and the TSA Formation, the TSA and LLZ Formations, and the Sainshand and Bayanshiree Formations. Results and discussion

The developed EOR screening method was applied to the LUZ, LLZ, and TSA formations in the T oilfield, with each formation evaluated separately to account for its distinct reservoir properties. Table 6 presents the scores for fifteen EOR recovery methods using a color-coded system for clarity:

Note that the red color indicates an exclusion from the criteria. Methods marked red must be removed from the EOR ranking list, even if they have a high overall score. Additionally, a score of zero (also represented in red) signifies a violation of either the lower or upper limits. Hence, any method identified and marked red is automatically excluded from the EOR options.

Furthermore, it is essential to highlight that surface mining method have been excluded from this analysis due to the low viscosity of the oil present in the “T” oilfield. In general, mining is used only when the oil is so viscous that it cannot be recovered by any other technique because the mining and upgrading of bitumen are more costly than in-situ recovery methods18.

The updated final EOR scoring results, presented in Table 7, indicate that miscible CO2, miscible HC, and immiscible N2 achieved the highest scores without violating any criteria. Following these methods, miscible N2 ranked fourth, steam injection ranked fifth, polymer flooding ranked sixth, and miscible WAG ranked seventh. These results suggest that the top three methods have the highest likelihood of success in the T oilfield. Methods highlighted in red should be excluded due to incompatibility with reservoir properties.

Each formation presented a distinct, most compatible EOR methodology. However, considering the cost of EOR, these may not be optimal options. Therefore, an average score for each formation was suggested. Based on the screening results, the three highest-ranked candidates—miscible CO2, miscible HC, and immiscible N2—were selected for further evaluation.

A comparative study was also conducted using the criteria (Table 1) established by Taber et al. (1997a)18. The results are presented in Table 7. This analysis showed that both miscible CO2 and immiscible gas methods (CO2, N2, and HC) achieved the highest scores without violating any criteria. Notably, the miscible CO2 process scored highly in both assessments (Table 7) without violating any criteria. It ranked first in this updated study and second in the Taber et al.18 study (Table 7). However, the results of Taber et al.18 did not include miscible HC, miscible N2, and polymer flooding processes due to differences in reservoir value ranges (Table 7). This comparison underscores the importance of continually updating EOR screening criteria as scientific understanding improves.

Conclusions

The proposed EOR screening and scoring methodology is an efficient tool for engineers to assess reservoirs more quickly and streamline the evaluation process, which results in faster decision-making. Additionally, it reduces costs associated with detailed analyses and testing while offering a straightforward and user–friendly approach to EOR method selection. The introduced methodology is applicable to various reservoir types and allows for a more accurate selection of EOR methods, even with limited reservoir data. Each EOR method is scored against the defined criteria, providing a quantitative measure of its suitability. Scores are assigned based on how well each method aligns with the criteria, using the average reservoir property value in the calculations. These averages represent a central tendency that simplifies analysis and aids in making initial assessments.

This study updates the EOR selection criteria originally published by Taber et al. (1997a) and includes a comprehensive comparative analysis, which enhances the understanding of EOR techniques in the context of recent technological advancements and industry practices. By applying the proposed EOR screening methodology, engineers can streamline the EOR selection process, making it more efficient and practical while ensuring that the chosen EOR methods align well with the reservoir conditions.

The T oilfield was introduced as a case study to demonstrate the practical application of the proposed methodology. The results identified miscible CO2, miscible HC, and immiscible N2 as the highest-scoring EOR methods, all meeting the screening criteria without violations. While analysis of the three main formations in the T oilfield revealed distinct optimal EOR methods for each, implementing separate methods for each formation may not be economically viable due to cost considerations. To support future investment decisions, the three top-ranked candidates have been selected for further detailed studies, including reservoir simulation. This next phase will provide deeper insights into the feasibility and effectiveness of each method, ensuring a well-informed strategy for optimizing oil recovery in the T oilfield.

Evaluation of confirmatory data following the Article 12 MRL review for quinmerac
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe applicant BASF SE submitted a request to the competent national authority in Estonia to evaluate the confirmatory data for quinmerac, which had been identified as unavailable in the framework of the MRL review under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. To address the data gap, new residue trials complaint with an adjusted, less critical Good Agricultural Practice (GAP) in sugar beet tops were submitted. The results from these trials are also relevant for extrapolation to fodder beet tops and were taken into account to revise the livestock dietary burden calculations. The data gap was considered satisfactorily addressed. The information provided allow confirming the tentative MRLs for liver, kidney and edible offal of ruminant and equine commodities at the current levels. The previously performed consumer risk assessment for quinmerac remains valid.

SUMMARY

In 2020, when the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) reviewed the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for active substance quinmerac according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses which were not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The following data gap was noted: Residue trials analysing simultaneously for enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions for sugar beet and fodder beet tops (relevant for liver and kidney from ruminant and equine commodities).

Residue trials analysing simultaneously for enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions for sugar beet and fodder beet tops (relevant for liver and kidney from ruminant and equine commodities).

Residue trials analysing simultaneously for enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions for sugar beet and fodder beet tops (relevant for liver and kidney from ruminant and equine commodities).

Considering that sugar beet tops and fodder beet tops are relevant feed items for livestock, in the framework of the MRL review, the MRLs for livestock commodities ruminant and equine liver kidney, and edible offal (other than liver and kidney) above the limit of quantification (LOQ) were considered tentative due to the identified data gap: in the absence of residue trials analysing the residue definition for risk assessment, conservative tentative conversion factors (CFs) from the enforcement to the risk assessment residue definition were applied. Consequently, this approach may have led to an overestimation of the livestock dietary burden and MRL for the commodities of animal origin.

In 2022, Commission Regulation (EU) 2022/1321 implemented the tentative MRLs for livestock mentioned above, including footnotes indicating the type of confirmatory data that should be provided. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRLs was requested to address the confirmatory data by 29 July 2024. In accordance with the agreed procedure set out in the working document SANTE/10235/2016, BASF SE submitted an application to the competent national authority in Estonia (rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the EFSA Central Submission System on 22 July 2024. The appointed RMS Estonia assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 18 October 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 13 November 2024 to 4 December 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 20 March 2026.

On 26 March 2026 the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. When assessing the evaluation report, EFSA identified points which needed further clarifications. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA June 2026, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

To address the data gap identified during the MRL review, the results of new residue trials on sugar beet were provided. The new information addresses the data gap and supports the confirmation of the tentative MRLs for certain products of animal origin at the same level. Noteworthy is that the residue trials were compliant with a less critical GAP than that assessed in the MRL review but not requiring a change of the existing MRL in sugar beet roots and by extrapolation, in beetroots, which is set at the enforcement level of 0.15 mg/kg (LOQ). Furthermore, the trial samples were not analysed strictly in accordance with the residue definition for risk assessment agreed for plants in the EU pesticides peer review and MRL review i.e. ‘sum of quinmerac and its metabolites BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4 (free and conjugated), expressed as quinmerac’. The analysis of the conjugated forms of BH‐518‐4 was limited to the glucose conjugate only. According to the RMS, the obtained residue results are expected to be equivalent to those that would have been obtained if analysed according to the agreed residue definition, as a new metabolism study performed on sugar beet identified a single conjugate of the metabolite M518H004, namely the glucose conjugate M518H015. The study will be evaluated in detail in the framework of the peer review for the renewal of approval of quinmerac. EFSA accepted the justification provided and concluded that the data gap identified in the MRL review for residue trials analysing simultaneously for enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions for sugar beet and fodder beet tops has been addressed. Nevertheless, any broader application or the extension of this testing strategy to edible commodities requires further consideration.

ASSESSMENT

The review of existing MRLs for the active substance quinmerac according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 1 (MRL review) has been performed in 2020 (EFSA, 2020). European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) identified some information as unavailable (data gap) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The good agricultural practices (GAPs) assessed in the framework of the MRL review that were not fully supported by data and for which confirmatory data for feed commodities were requested, are listed in Appendix A.

Following the review of existing MRLs, the legal limits have been modified by Commission Regulation (EU) 2022/1321, 2 including footnotes for tentative MRLs that specified the type of information that was identified as missing. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL was requested to address the confirmatory data by 29 July 2024.

In accordance with the specific provisions set out in the working document of the European Commission SANTE/10235/2016 (European Commission, 2023b) and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 3 the applicant BASF SE, on 22 July 2024 submitted an application to the competent national authority in Estonia (designated rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format.

To address the data gap identified by EFSA, the applicant provided a set of residue trials on sugar beet tops, which can be extrapolated to fodder beet tops and updated the livestock dietary burden accordingly. Noteworthy is that the residue trials were complaint with an adjusted GAP, less critical than the GAP assessed in the MRL review. Furthermore, the trial samples were analysed in accordance with a residue definition for risk assessment not strictly corresponding to the one currently agreed for plants. Residue analysis was limited to the glucose conjugate of BH‐518‐4, while the agreed residue definition for risk assessment is not restricted to this specific conjugated form. The RMS assessed the new information in an evaluation report, which was submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to EFSA on 20 March 2026 (Estonia, 2026). On 26 March 2026 the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

During the detailed assessment, EFSA identified points which needed further clarifications. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA in June 2026 (Estonia, 2026), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Estonia, 2026), and the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2020), as well as the conclusion on the EU pesticides peer review and the outcome of its confirmatory data (EFSA, 2010, 2015).

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 4 and the relevant guidance documents at the date of implementation of the confirmatory data requirements by Regulation (EU) No 2022/1321 are applicable. The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 5

An updated list of end points, including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously and the confirmatory data evaluated in this application, is presented in Appendix B.

It is noted that, within the framework of the present application, the applicant submitted two new metabolism studies, one on sugar beet roots and one on oilseed rape, as well as a new enforcement method that may also be relevant for sugar beet roots. However, these studies were not assessed by EFSA in the context of the present application. This is because their full evaluation is not directly related to the regulatory footnotes under consideration. Therefore, these studies should be assessed in the broader context of the peer review for the renewal of the approval of quinmerac. The evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Estonia, 2026) is made publicly available as a background document to this reasoned opinion. 6

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of quinmerac was investigated in primary crops in root crops, pulses/oilseeds and cereals/grass (sugar beet, rapeseed and wheat) following a single foliar pre‐ and post‐emergence application (EFSA, 2010, 2020). Across all crops tested, quinmerac was rapidly metabolised, with the parent compound declining to low levels at harvest. The metabolic pathway was similar in all crop groups, with the hydroxymethyl metabolite BH 518‐4 (free and conjugated) as the major component, and metabolite BH 518‐2 at lower extent.

A new metabolism study on sugar beet roots was submitted within the framework of the present application. EFSA relied on the relevant conclusion of this study as reported in the evaluation of the RMS for the assessment of the newly submitted residue trials, namely that the metabolite M518H004 forms a single conjugate, identified as the glucose conjugate M518H015. However, the full assessment of this study was not carried out in the context of the present application, as it is not directly related to the regulatory footnotes under consideration. Therefore, the comprehensive evaluation of the study is expected to be performed in the framework of the peer review for the renewal of the approval of quinmerac.

Stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of quinmerac and its metabolites BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4 in plants stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the framework the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2010) and in studies assessed as confirmatory data (EFSA, 2015). In these studies, storage stability was investigated in high‐water content and high‐oil content commodities (sugar beets and oil seed rape seeds, respectively), where residues were analysed using an enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA method) determining total residues of quinmerac and its metabolites. The available data demonstrated that total residues of quinmerac (defined as the sum of quinmerac, BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4) were stable for a period of up to 24 months when samples are stored at −20°C.

A new storage‐stability study was submitted within the present application and assessed by the RMS (Estonia, 2026). The study investigated the stability of residues of quinmerac and its metabolites BH 518‐2, BH 518‐4 and the glucose conjugate of BH 518‐4 (M518H015), each analysed separately in frozen samples of crops representing different matrix categories: high‐water content (sugar beet roots, oilseed rape whole plant), high‐oil content (oilseed rape seed), dry matrices (wheat grain, dried peas), high‐acid commodities (grapes) and the ‘other’ category (wheat straw). The analytical method included a specific liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) transition for M518H015, allowing for the direct quantification of this conjugate.

Crop samples were spiked at 10 times the respective LOQs. Oilseed rape seeds, wheat grain, sugar beet roots, grapes and dried peas were fortified with quinmerac, BH 518‐2, BH 518‐4 and M518H015 at 0.10 mg/kg for each analyte, while oilseed rape whole plant and wheat straw were fortified at 0.50 mg/kg. In addition, sugar beet leaves were fortified with quinmerac and metabolites BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4 at 0.50 mg/kg. Due to matrix interferences, chard leaves were used instead of sugar beet leaves for the fortification with M518H015 at 0.50 mg/kg.

All samples were stored frozen at −18°C for up to 24 months and analysed at 0, 1, 3, 6, 12, 18 and 24 months (Estonia, 2026). The storage stability of quinmerac (parent compound) and the compounds BH 518‐2, BH 518‐4, M518H015 was demonstrated for at least 24 months in all of the investigated commodities.

It is concluded that M518H015 (the sugar conjugate of BH 518‐4) is stable for up to 24 months in chard leaves (high‐water content matrices) when stored at −18 C. These findings are considered representative for sugar beet tops under assessment. Moreover, the study is considered compliant with the relevant technical guidelines on storage stability (European Commission, 1997).

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In order to address data gap, the applicant submitted the results of residue trials in sugar beets (Estonia, 2026).

EFSA notes that, with the current confirmatory data application, the applicant provided the new residue trials on sugar beets (top parts are used as feed items) according to an adjusted GAP with a lower number of application (three instead of maximum four), lower application rate, earlier pre‐development stage (up to BBCH 18 of leaf development) and no post‐harvest interval (PHI), compared to the critical GAP considered in the MRL review. EFSA finds this proposal acceptable in line with SANTE/10235/2016 (European Commission, 2023b), which clarifies the possibility of the submission of the adjusted GAP for the MRL review confirmatory data application.

In support of this authorised adjusted NEU and SEU GAP on sugar beets and fodder beets, the applicant submitted a total of 20 GAP‐compliant independent residue trials on sugar beets, performed during the growing seasons of 2015, 2018 and 2020. The NEU dataset (10 trials) was conducted in Germany (3), the United Kingdom (1), Hungary (one), the Netherlands (2), Belgium (2) and Poland (1). While the SEU dataset (10 trials) was conducted in France (3), Spain (3), Greece (2) and Italy (2).

The trials consisted of a first foliar treatment of sugar beets at a nominal application rate of 50 g a.i./ha at BBCH 10‐12, followed by two other applications at a nominal application rate of 100 g a.i./ha at BBCH 13‐14 and BBCH 15‐18. Subsequently, whole sugar beet plant samples (including roots) were collected for analysis immediately after the last application (day of last application, DALA), while roots and sugar beet tops were harvested at BBCH 49 (44–160 DALA).

The samples of sugar beet roots and tops were then analysed for the parent compound and metabolites BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4 separately, according to the residue definition for enforcement. For risk assessment purpose, sample were additionally analysing the metabolite M518H015, which is the sugar conjugate of BH 518‐4. Except for four trials conducted in Germany, United Kingdom, France and Spain in 2015, in which M518H015 was not analysed. These four trials were excluded from the NEU and SEU risk assessment datasets, respectively, resulting in a total of 8 valid trials per residues zone, which meets the required minimum number of trials for fodder beets (European Commission, 2023a).

For the present assessment, the applicant proposed to quantify residues for risk assessment in sugar beet tops (relevant for the livestock dietary burden) as the sum of quinmerac and the metabolites BH 518‐2, BH 518‐4 and M518H015 (the glucose conjugate of BH 518‐4), expressed as parent quinmerac, which is not strictly corresponding to the residue definition for plants, i.e. ‘sum of quinmerac and its metabolites BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4 (free and conjugated), expressed as quinmerac’ agreed in the EU pesticides peer review and MRL review. The analysis of the conjugated forms of BH‐518‐4 was limited to the glucose conjugate, whereas the agreed residue definition for risk assessment is not restricted to this specific conjugate. According to the RMS, the residue results obtained are expected to be equivalent to those that would have been obtained if analysed according to the agreed residue definition. The rationale provided by the applicant for limiting the analysis of the residue trials only to the glucose conjugate is based on the results of a new, non‐yet‐peer‐reviewed post‐emergence metabolism study in sugar beets. The study foresees exhaustive extraction and hydrolytic condition to release any detectable conjugated form of this metabolite, followed by subsequent identification (Estonia, 2026). Taking into account the specific feed items under assessment and their associated impact on the transfer of residues in products of animal origin (see Section 2.2), EFSA accepted the justification provided in the framework of the MRL application. However, did not assess the study within the present application, since the concerned new study was also submitted in the framework of the renewal of approval of quinmerac. 7 Thus, the conclusions from this opinion should be considered as provisional, pending the border assessment of the peer review for renewal of approval. In addition, any broader application or the extension of this testing strategy to edible commodities requires further evaluation.

According to the assessment of the RMS, the analytical methods used (method No L0050/02 and L0319/01) were sufficiently validated and fit for purpose with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each individual analyte (Estonia, 2026; European Commission, 2010). EFSA notes, however, that the extraction efficiency of the analytical methods applied in sugar beet residue trials is not assessed according to the requirements of the extraction efficiency Guidance, SANTE 2017/10632 (European Commission, 2023c). Such information was however not required at time of the MRL review and not requested (EFSA, 2020). The lack of data on extraction efficiency might introduce a non‐standard uncertainty to the present assessment. The samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which the integrity of the samples has been demonstrated for quinmerac and its metabolites for the maximum storage period of 236 days (Estonia, 2026). In the framework of this application the results of a new storage stability study were submitted to support the residue trials assessment (see Section 1.1.5).

The RMS adjusted for molecular weight (MW) ratio the residue values of the three metabolites to express them as parent equivalents before summing them up to derive the total residues expressed as parent compound 8 (Estonia, 2026). EFSA derived CF for risk assessment from the field data which are reported in Table B.1.2.1. The trials with total residues below the LOQ according to both residue definitions were excluded from the calculation. The median CF for risk assessment was 1.58 for the NEU dataset (based on the result of a single trial) and 1.42 for the SEU use (maximum value: 3.5), based on the results of four trials.

In tops, no residues of quinmerac and its metabolites BH 518‐2 and BH 518‐4, according to the residue definition for enforcement, at or above the LOQ were found in any of the SEU and NEU trials (total LOQ of < 0.028 mg/kg). For risk assessment, considering the inclusion of the metabolite M518H015, the measured total residues were < 0.034 mg/kg, with one exception of 0.044 mg/kg for the NEU trials and ranged from < 0.034 to 0.098 mg/kg for the SEU trials. Based on the above datasets, EFSA considered the SEU GAP more critical for the derivation of the risk assessment values for the livestock dietary burden calculations, which is in line with the assessment by the RMS. Moreover, the RMS proposed to extrapolate residue data from sugar beet tops to fodder beet tops which was acceptable according to the MRL review (EFSA, 2020).

In conclusion, the trials conducted in line with the adjusted SEU GAP were sufficient to derive HR‐RA and STMR‐RA values for sugar beet tops, which were extrapolated to mangel fodder beet and were used as input values in the EFSA livestock dietary burden calculator (2017 9 ).

It is to be noted that the trials were conducted according to an adjusted GAP and not according to the critical GAP assessed in the MRL review based on which the existing MRL in roots is derived, However residues according to the residue definition for enforcement in roots from both the NEU and SEU trials were always below the total LOQ (< 0.028 mg/kg) of the post‐registration analytical method.

Since residues in roots were consistently below the LOQ in all trials and the MRL currently in place is set at the combined LOQ of 0.15 mg/kg, in accordance with the analytical enforcement method (LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg for each analyte) for sugar beet roots and beetroots, as established in the MRL review, no change to this MRL is considered needed.

EFSA concludes that the MRL review confirmatory data gap identified in the MRL review is addressed.

Proposed MRLs

Although new set of residue trials on sugar beet is conducted according to an adjusted GAP, a change of the existing MRL in sugar beet roots and, by extrapolation, in beetroots is not required, as it was not requested by the applicant. The use under assessment is covered by the existing MRL of 0.15 mg/kg (LOQ).

It is, however, to be noted that a more sensitive enforcement method (with validation including the sugar beet roots matrix) has been submitted within the present application. However, since the scope of this application is limited to the assessment of the tentative MRLs and no MRL modification was requested for sugar beet roots, EFSA did not assess this method.

The assessment of this more sensitive enforcement method is recommended to be carried out in the framework of the broader context of the peer review for the renewal of the approval of quinmerac, in view of the relatively high LOQ of the analytical method assessed in the EU pesticides peer review and the MRL review. (EFSA, 2010, 2020).

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

The MRLs for animal commodities in the MRL regulation were tentatively set at: 0.07 mg/kg for liver and edible offal of bovine and equine origin, 0.05 mg/kg for kidney of bovine and equine origin, and 0.05 mg/kg for liver, kidney and offal of sheep and goat, pending the submission of confirmatory information on residue trials, analysing simultaneously for enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions in sugar beet tops and fodder tops used as a feed item. The confirmatory data assessed in this evaluation have thus an impact on pesticide residues expected in livestock only. In the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2020), livestock dietary burden calculations for quinmerac exceeded the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg DM in all animal species, which triggered the assessment of residues in animal commodities. However, pending residue trials on sugar beet tops and fodder beet, this exposure was considered likely to be overestimated, due to the use of a high LOQ of 0.15 mg/kg as input value for tops multiplied by a conservative conversion factor of 3 from enforcement to risk assessment, derived from metabolism study.

The applicant has now submitted the requested residue trials, allowing a refinement of the livestock dietary burden and a reassessment of the MRLs tentatively set in certain commodities of animal origin (See Section 1.2.1).

The input values for the revised exposure calculations for livestock are presented in Appendix D.1. To estimate the animal dietary burden, EFSA used STMR‐RA and HR‐RA values for sugar beet tops and for fodder beet tops, as derived from the residue trials submitted under the current application. For the by‐products and in line with previous calculations, the existing MRL set at the LOQ of 0.15 mg/kg was used as STMR, with conversion factor of 1 or 2 to express them according to the residue definition for risk assessment in plants but without processing factors, since concentration of residues in by‐products is not expected (EFSA, 2020).

EFSA finds this approach acceptable in line with SANTE/10235/2016 (European Commission, 2023b), which clarifies the possibility of the submission of the adjusted GAP for the MRL review confirmatory data application.

The results of the updated dietary burden calculation are presented in Section B.2. The updated dietary burdens decreased as compared to the calculations performed in the MRL review for all livestock, however the dietary exposure of cattle, sheep, swine and poultry still exceeded the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg DM. The highest decrease is observed for swine all diets and sheep ewe, up to 60% and for sheep all diets up to 56%. For the remaining livestock it decreased between 14% and 49%. The adequacy of the MRLs in place is further evaluated in Section 1.2.1.

In line with the approach of MRL review, it is noted that residues from rotational crops were not included in the calculations, as it is assumed that appropriate risk mitigation measures would be implemented by Member States, as suggested in the MRL review, to prevent residue uptake in succeeding crops and transfer of residues in feed items when granting national authorisations (EFSA, 2020).

Magnitude of residues in livestock

A feeding study with lactating cows was assessed in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020). This study together with the outcome of the metabolism studies was used to derive MRLs and risk assessment values in tissues for ruminants and milk.

In order to address the regulatory footnotes implemented in the Commission Regulation (EU) 2022/1321 and confirm the tentative MRLs for certain products of animal origin, the applicant provided residue trials for sugar beet tops and by extrapolation, for fodder beet. Details on the residue data submitted are presented in Section 1.2.1 and Appendix B.1.2.1. EFSA recalculated the livestock dietary burden based on the results of the new residue trials submitted (see Section 2).

Despite the considerable decrease in the calculated dietary burden for all animals and for more than or close to 50% for cattle (all diets), cattle (dairy only), sheep (all diets), sheep (ewe only), the MRL values remained the same as the tentative MRLs derived within MRL review.

EFSA concluded that the data gap identified in the framework of the MRL review is therefore addressed and the MRLs for certain animal commodities are confirmed.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

The submitted confirmatory data did not trigger a modification of previous risk assessment performed in the framework of the most recent assessment of quinmerac (EFSA, 2020) and the conclusions derived are still valid. The highest chronic exposure was calculated for the Dutch toddler population representing 3% of the acceptable daily intake (ADI). The highest acute exposure was calculated for beetroots representing 3% of the acute reference dose (ARfD).

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

To address data gap identified in the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2020), results of new residue trials in sugar beet tops were submitted by the applicant. The results from these trials are also relevant for extrapolation to fodder beet tops, and both products are used as feed commodities. Noteworthy is that the residue trials were complaint with an adjusted GAP, less critical than the GAP assessed in the MRL review but not requiring a change of the existing MRL in sugar beet roots and by extrapolation, in beetroots, which is set at the enforcement level of 0.15 mg/kg (LOQ).

The analysis of the conjugated forms of BH‐518‐4 in the residue trials on beet root tops was limited to the glucose conjugate, whereas the agreed residue definition for risk assessment is not restricted to this specific conjugate. According to the RMS, the residue results obtained are expected to be equivalent to those that would have been obtained if analysed according to the agreed residue definition. This because a new metabolism study performed on sugar beet identified a single conjugate of the metabolite M518H004, namely the glucose conjugate M518H015. The above‐mentioned study will be evaluated in detail in the framework of the peer review for the renewal of approval of quinmerac. To be noted that this potential uncertainty has shown not to have an impact on the calculation of the MRLs for products of animal origin. Taking into account the specific feed items under assessment and their associated impact on the transfer of residues in products of animal origin, EFSA accepted the justification provided and concluded that the data gap identified in the MRL for residue trials analysing simultaneously for enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions for sugar beet and fodder beet tops has been addressed. Nevertheless, any broader application or the extension of this testing strategy to edible commodities requires further consideration.

The livestock dietary burden calculations were refined using the updated risk assessment input values for sugar beet tops and fodder beet tops. However, this update did not trigger a change to the previously derived tentative MRLs for liver, kidney and offal of ruminants and equine. Therefore, the tentative MRLs derived in the MRL review were confirmed at the same level. The previously performed consumer risk assessment for quinmerac remains valid.

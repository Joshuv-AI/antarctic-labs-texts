Economic burden and cost drivers of generalized pustular psoriasis: a systematic review
Authors: Xin Qian Seah, Mustapha Mohammed, Siew Chin Ong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Generalized pustular psoriasis (GPP), as a rare type of life-threatening psoriasis with currently no effective treatment, imposes a huge economic burden on the healthcare systems. This systematic review aimed to summarize studies on the economic burden of GPP and the corresponding key cost drivers. A systematic search was conducted on PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus for eligible studies published between 1st January 2000 and 31st December 2024. We strictly followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Among the 324 studies identified, ten eligible studies describing the direct and indirect healthcare costs of GPP across five countries were included in this review. This study demonstrated that the average annual cost associated with GPP spans a wide range, with a mean of USD 23,675 (SD:16,691) [range: 7,791 − 49,891]. Based on the pool estimation, the leading cost drivers for GPP were the costs of inpatient admissions and medications. More than half of GPP patients (54.8%) were on topical steroids, 38.8% were on systemic non-biologic agents, and 14.8% were on biologic agents. For biologic agents, the highest usage was observed in the United States, and lowest usage was observed in China. In conclusion, this systematic review highlights the significant economic burden associated with GPP globally, mainly driven by the costs of admissions and medications. The review also demonstrates the challenges in GPP treatment, including the frequent use of systemic corticosteroids and limited access to newer biologic agents.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13561-025-00670-3.

Introduction

Generalized Pustular Psoriasis (GPP) constitutes a rare yet severe variant of psoriasis, characterized by widespread pustule formation on the skin and often accompanied by potentially life-threatening systemic symptoms [1, 2].

GPP is an autoinflammatory and multisystem subtype of psoriasis that currently lacks standard treatment guidelines and have a significant impact on patients’ quality of life during GPP flares [3]. Studies highlight that GPP patients experience exacerbated symptoms such as pain, fatigue and itching, as well as non-cutaneous symptoms including fever, malaise, and joint pain [4, 5]. GPP patients face significant economic burden driven primarily by the costs associated with recurrent emergency visits, hospitalization and medication [6, 7]. Data on the prevalence and incidence rate of GPP worldwide remain scarce and vary across geographical regions. In Japan, the estimated prevalence is 7.46 per million [8]. In France, it is lower at 1.76 per million [9], while in Korea, the prevalence is reported to be higher at 122.3 per million [10].

Although knowledge about the etiology and treatment of GPP remains limited, loss-of-function mutations in IL36RN, CARD14, and AP1S3 are associated with GPP, leading to overexpression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-36, and recruitment of neutrophils and macrophages to the skin [3]. More recent studies have shifted treatment approaches from non-GPP-specified therapies such as retinoids, cyclosporine, and methotrexate to the use of newer biologic agents like guselkumab, secukinumab, and ustekinumab [11–13].

This systematic review aimed to comprehensively evaluate the direct and indirect economic burden and cost drivers of GPP management, and to provide insights into its economic impacts that guide future research and clinical practice.

Methodology

Study design

A systematic literature review guided by the Preferred Reporting System for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) strategy was conducted to explore the economic burden associated with GPP and identify key cost drivers, encompassing both direct and indirect expenses.

Search strategy

We searched studies published between January 1, 2000, and December 31, 2024 across three major electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus. Our search approach blended carefully selected keywords with Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms, drawing on phrases like “Pustular Psoriasis,” “Generalized Pustular,” “Von Zumbusch disease,” “economic burden,” “cost of illness,” and “disease cost” to capture the full scope of relevant work. Additionally, we considered other relevant articles identified during the review process or referenced in retrieved papers. The employed strategy involved delineating the criteria for economic studies included in systematic reviews. Detailed search strings are provided in the Appendix S1 to ensure reproducibility.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion and exclusion criteria for this study were predefined and thoroughly documented to minimize potential bias. Only studies involving adult patients with a confirmed diagnosis of GPP (either as a primary condition or accompanied by other comorbidities) and considering the perspectives of taxpayers, patients, or society were included.

The review focused on studies reporting economic outcomes, encompassing both direct and indirect costs at macro and micro levels, as well as healthcare resource utilization derived from various data sources. Direct costs referred to those associated with medical treatment (emergency visits, outpatient visits, medication usage, inpatient admissions, and other healthcare services), whereas indirect costs encompassed productivity losses, including presenteeism, absenteeism, and activity impairment.

Accepted study designs included cross-sectional studies, retrospective and prospective cohort studies, database analyses, and cost-of-illness studies. Whereas abstracts, case-control studies, case reports, research letters, systematic reviews, and narrative reviews were excluded.

Only original articles published in English were considered, while studies that did not report any economic burden specifically for patients with GPP or that focused on other types of psoriasis were omitted.

Study selection and data extraction

The searches were conducted in two stages, namely abstract screening and full-text screening, by two independent reviewers to assess the eligibility of articles based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. For quality control purposes, an additional 10% of the records were randomly checked by a third reviewer at both screening stages. Any discrepancies were resolved through comprehensive discussion among the three reviewers, in line with the predefined criteria.

A data extraction form was developed to document the characteristics of each included study. The extracted information encompassed the year of the study, study type, country, data sources, time frame, methodological approach (incidence-based or prevalence-based), and reported economic burdens. The first reviewer initially extracted the data, which was subsequently verified by the second and third reviewers. All extracted data were managed and analyzed using Microsoft Excel. Comparisons were made regarding individual healthcare resource utilization including hospitalization costs, medication expenses, emergency visits, and outpatient visit costs—as well as indirect costs such as productivity losses related to presenteeism, absenteeism, and activity impairment.

Data analysis

To facilitate comparison across studies, all costs were converted into USD 2024 using the Campbell and Cochrane Economics Methods Group (CCEMG) Evidence for Policy and Practice Information and Coordinating Centre (EPPICentre) cost converter. Descriptive analyses summarizing the main characteristics of the selected studies were conducted using Microsoft Excel.

Reporting quality of included studies

To evaluate the reporting quality of each included study, we applied the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 (CHEERS 2022) checklist, focusing on criteria relevant to cost-of-illness (COI) studies. Of the 28 CHEERS items, we excluded those not applicable to COI methodology, specifically items related to health outcomes and comparators (items 11, 12, 13), health economic modeling (items 16, 17), and distribution effects by social variables (item 19), as these apply to intervention-based or model-based studies rather than COI analyses. A detailed assessment of the CHEERS 2022 criteria for each study is provided in Appendix S2.

Results

Studies identification

The search criteria for the articles yielded a total of 324 titles, and 95 duplicates were removed. A total of 229 papers were retrieved for title and abstract reviewed, with 188 papers excluded due to irrelevant content during the screening process. A total of 41 papers were full-texted peer reviewed and a total of 10 papers were included in this systematic review study for data extraction with 31 papers excluded. Studies characteristics

A total of ten studies were included in this systematic review. Among them, eight studies examined direct healthcare costs and healthcare resources utilization while two studies focused on sick leave and work impairment.

The sample size of the included studies ranged from 110 to 1669 GPP patients in national level studies, and from 60 to 744 GPP patients in regional studies. The mean age of the GPP patients included in the systematic review was 53.5 years [range: 42.1–62.9]. All of the studies reported higher percentage of female patient [range: 51.5 to 67.6%] except for the study by Okubo et al. in Japan, which reported a lower percentage of female patients (38.5%) [14]. Most of the studies utilized data from national claims databases based on real-world patient data. The data collection periods ranged from 2.9 to 12.9 years. All studies reported sufficient information on patient characteristics (e.g., age and gender), data sources, and statistical analyses. However, none of the studies indicated the severity of the GPP or examined the association between disease severity and economic burden. Details of the included studies are illustrated in Table 1.

Direct cost and drivers of GPP

The annual cost per-patient per year (PPPY) of managing GPP in different countries was adjusted to 2024 USD rates for uniform comparisons. The reported PPPYs span a wide range, with a mean of USD 23,675 (SD:16,691) [range: USD 7,791 to 49,891]. In Canada, a study by Tarride et al. estimated the highest PPPY of CAD 49,695 (USD 49,891) [7]. In the United States, the study by Hanna et al. (2018) reported a PPPY of USD 38,102, which was notably lower than that in Canada [6].

In Sweden, a study by Löfvendahl et al. (2015) reported the lowest PPPY of EUR 5,062 (USD 7,791) [29]. In Japan, the reported costs varied moderately, with Morita et al. (2017) reporting JPY 1,670,000 (USD 20,034) and Okubo et al. (2017) estimating JPY 1,270,000 (USD 15,235) [14, 18]. Meanwhile, a study by Wang et al. (2018) in China found a PPPY of RMB 35,456 (USD 10,995), placing it toward the lower end [28]. The detailed breakdown of each country is illustrated in Table 2.

From the pool estimation, the drivers of healthcare resources utilization were inpatient admissions, followed by medication costs. Inpatient was the highest cost component, with a mean expenditure of USD 16,457 (SD: 12,310), ranging from USD 3,089 in Sweden to USD 35,579 in Canada. The admission rate ranged from 12.6 to 44.0%. The all-cause length of hospital stays ranged from 8.6 to 25.2 days, while GPP-specified length of hospital stays ranged from 8.9 to 17.7 days.

Medication costs were found to be another major contributor to the economic burden of GPP, accounting for the second-largest component from the pool estimation. The mean medication cost was USD 5,739 (SD: 4,398), ranging from USD 2,975 in Sweden to USD 10,810 in Japan. Indirect economic burden of GPP

In a study conducted in Sweden, individuals with GPP were observed to take more sick leave compared to both control groups, with the proportion of sick leave increasing significantly from 20 to 30%. The number of sick leaves taken by GPP patients was higher even before the diagnosis, indicating the potential delay in diagnosis or the increased burden of comorbidities [15].

Similarly, a study conducted in North America reported that GPP patients were found to have a higher percentage of work impairment compared to plaque psoriasis patients. GPP patients missed more than twice the mean working hours compared to plaque psoriasis patients. Furthermore, GPP patients experienced greater impairment in their daily activities than those with plaque psoriasis [5].

Both studies on the indirect economic burden reported outcomes such as days of sick leave, presenteeism, absenteeism, and overall work impairment (measured by the WPAI instrument) in terms of days or percentages, without converting these findings into monetary values for comparison. Biologic agents showed the highest usage in the United States, with a study by Sobell et al. reported a usage rate of 37.4% [17]. In Japan, Okubo et al. reported a lower usage of 21.8% [14]. By contrast, a study in China found much lower rates of biologic use at 4.8% [28].

Among oral systemic therapies, the study by Okubo et al. in Japan reported the highest utilization rate, with 70% of patients receiving these agents [14]. In contrast, more moderate usage rates were observed in Sweden (28.2%) and China (27.9%) [19, 28].

Regarding topical corticosteroids, Japanese studies reported the highest rates of use, with 86% in Morita et al. and 81.8% in Okubo et al. [14, 18] In comparison, usage rates in the United States were 52.7% and 35.9%, while Sweden reported a rate of 43.5% [16,17,29]. Refer to Table 4 for a detailed breakdown of each study.

Across the studies, the use of biologic agents for GPP management varied considerably, but pooled estimates suggest that both TNF inhibitors and interleukin inhibitors are commonly used. Within the TNF inhibitor class, adalimumab appears to be the most frequently reported agent, while ustekinumab is the most used interleukin inhibitor. However, there is substantial variability in the specific agents chosen, with some studies reporting different TNF or interleukin inhibitors as their primary treatment.

Comorbidities

Three studies conducted statistical tests on comorbidities among GPP patients. Psoriatic arthritis was reported as one of the significantly different comorbidities in both studies by Tarride et al. and Wang et al. [7, 28] Additionally, hepatic conditions were identified as significantly different in both the study by Hanna et al. and Wang et al. [6, 28] The other significant comorbidities found included hypertension, diabetes, myocardial infarction, sleep apnea, and gout in the study by Tarride et al. [7] In the study by Hanna et al., significant comorbidities included autoimmune diseases, gastrointestinal conditions, hormonal/metabolic conditions, anxiety, depression, and pulmonary and renal conditions [6]. Organ failure and erythrodermic psoriasis were identified as significant comorbidities in the study by Wang et al. [28]

Four studies reported the proportions of comorbidities among GPP patients and a cohort group, although no statistical tests were conducted. GPP patients showed higher percentages of hyperlipidemia, type 2 diabetes, COPD, psoriatic arthritis, anxiety, depression, chronic kidney disease, obesity, asthma, interstitial pneumonia, and osteoporosis [14, 16–18].

Reporting quality

A total of 74.8% of the included studies fulfilled CHEERS reporting criteria. None of the studies fulfilled item 21 (Approach to engagement with patients and others affected by the studies) and item 25 (Effect of engagement with patients and others affected by the studies). Studies that did not report burden in terms of cost do not apply to item 8 (Study perspective), item 14 (Measurement and valuation of resources and cost), and item 15 (Currency, price date and conversion).

Discussion

This is the first systematic review to focus on the economic burden and healthcare resource utilization of GPP on a global scale. The review confirmed that GPP incurs substantial costs at the population level, with a mean of USD 23,675. In fact, the reported economic burden may be underestimated, as none of the studies included an evaluation of indirect costs from a societal perspective. The review revealed a significant paucity of data regarding the indirect economic burden of GPP.

Most studies in the systematic review were based on retrospective analyses of claims databases instead of establishing a dedicated system to capture data from primary data collection. Hence, there is a risk of miscoding or underreporting of GPP condition using the administrative claimed database. Despite exploratory efforts, the heterogeneity in methodology, population, data collection periods, and cost components has precluded a robust meta-analysis. Harmonizing flare criteria, standardizing cost category definitions, and adopting uniform time frames in future economic evaluations will be essential to improve comparability and inform more reliable clinical and policy decisions.

Both studies in Japan (Morita et al. and Okubo et al.) conducted during the same data collection period yielded different results due to the distinct databases used. In the Morita et al. study, the mean age of the GPP population was 60.9 years compared to 45 years in the Okubo et al. study, while the annual cost in the Morita et al. study (USD 20,034) was higher than that in the Okubo et al. study (USD 15,235). Similar discrepancies were observed in US studies by Sobell et al. and Crowley et al., which reported varying usage of biologic agents based on data from the IBM® MarketScan® Research Database versus the Optum study. The Optum study reported a higher mean age in the GPP cohort along with lower biologic use, suggesting that biologics may not be as widely prescribed in older patients, possibly due to safety concerns, formulary restrictions, or issues with medication access. [19, 20].

From the pooled estimation, the key drivers of healthcare resource utilization were inpatient admissions driven by longer hospital stay, ranging from 8.6 days to 25.2 days. However, information on inpatient interventions such as wound care or dressing procedures, laboratory monitoring, or supportive therapies, including antibiotic use and specialist consultation, was either absent or not disaggregated in the reported data. The substantial costs of GPP, especially those stemming from inpatient care, highlight the critical need for thoughtful resource allocation. To address this, policymakers should focus on enhancing outpatient care models and implementing preventive measures to lessen both the frequency and intensity of GPP flares. Future research should also deliver more granular insights into hospitalization costs, enabling stronger economic assessments and better-informed strategies for managing GPP healthcare resources.

In general, the treatment protocol for GPP lacks a consensus and is primarily adapted from the regimen used for psoriasis vulgaris [22, 23]. For example, studies demonstrated variability in corticosteroid use, with reported rates ranging from 32% (Morita et al.) to 34.5% (Sobell et al.) and as high as 41.8% (Okubo et al.) [14, 17, 18]. Systemic corticosteroids are frequently employed in GPP management despite their well-documented side effects, including weight gain, peptic ulcers, and the potential to trigger GPP flares. This adaptation can contribute to elevated medication costs, as treatments may not be optimally suited for GPP's distinct inflammatory profile.

Furthermore, we observed a higher usage of biologics in the United States (37.4%) and Japan (21.8%) compared to Brazil (5.4%) and China (4.8%). This variability highlights the absence of standardized guidelines, inequitable access to the biologic agents and leads to inconsistent economic burdens across healthcare systems. Policymakers should explore strategies to enhance equity, such as subsidizing high-cost treatments or expanding insurance coverage for GPP-related expenses. International collaborations could also facilitate the sharing of best practices and resources, ensuring that patients in resource-limited settings receive adequate care and support.

Recent progression in GPP management has been achieved with the endorsement of newer biologic agents such as imsidolimab, spesolimab, ustekinumab and demonstrated superiority in treating GPP flares [24–27]. Besides, the US FDA approved IV spesolimab as a single 900 mg dose to treat GPP flares in adults in year 2022. This could lead to a paradigm shift in treatment protocols, with a greater emphasis on biologic therapies.

The broad regulatory approval for biologic agents such as IV spesolimab spanning major markets such as the US, EU, China, and likely others highlights its growing global availability. Despite this growing availability, further research is needed to evaluate its cost-effectiveness, particularly through real-world evidence assessing its potential to reduce hospitalization rates. As this new treatment modality becomes more widely adopted, it is essential to anticipate its impact and ensure the efficient allocation of healthcare resources in response to this evolving therapeutic landscape. This may involve updating formularies and reimbursement policies to prioritize therapies that lower overall healthcare costs, such as those that reduce hospital stays or the need for intensive care. Additionally, implementing value-based pricing models could help align treatment costs with patient outcomes, thereby maximizing the value of healthcare spending.

Finally, future research may explore the long-term outcomes of these treatment preferences and address disparities in access to biologics across the countries. Additionally, studies on patient satisfaction and adherence to different treatment modalities could provide further insights into effective care strategies. Besides, this systematic review reveals a critical gap: none of the studies reviewed indicated the severity of the disease or explored the association between disease severity and the economic burden. This limitation in fully understanding the economic burden hinders policymakers and healthcare providers in efficiently allocating resources and prioritizing cost-effective solutions. The gap likely arises from methodological challenges, such as the absence of standardized severity scales, and a tendency to focus on aggregate costs rather than severity-specific analyses. To address this, future studies may integrate severity metrics such as Generalized Pustular Psoriasis Physician Global Assessment (GPPGA) or Generalized Pustular Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (GPPASI), and leverage longitudinal data to track costs and disease progression over time.

This review excluded non-English publications and conference abstracts, which may have limited the number of eligible studies included. Future studies may consider including non-English language publications to minimize language bias and ensure a more comprehensive and globally representative understanding of the economic burden.

One key limitation of this systematic review is that we were unable to conduct a meta-analysis due to the substantial heterogeneity observed across the included studies. Although we explored the feasibility of a meta-analysis, the significant differences in study designs and outcome measures precluded statistical pooling of the data. This limitation reduces the ability to draw definitive conclusions and introduces greater uncertainty into the results.

However, despite these limitations, the review provides valuable insights into the economic burden of GPP and highlights important gaps in the existing literature that warrant further research. Given that GPP is a rare disease with limited published data, conducting this review remains essential to consolidate current evidence and guide future research and policy efforts.

Conclusions

This systematic review highlights the significant economic burden associated with GPP globally, with inpatient admissions and medications being the primary drivers of healthcare expenses. The review also highlights the challenges in GPP treatment, including the lack of standardized protocols, the frequent use of systemic corticosteroids, and limited access to newer biologic agents that could transform management strategies.

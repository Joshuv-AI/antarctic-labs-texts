Crocodile tears syndrome after vestibular schwannoma surgery: A case report
Authors: Yash Asit Gore, Jash Doshi, Dattatray P. Muzumdar
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCrocodile tears syndrome is paroxysmal lacrimal hypersecretion while eating or drinking. It is relatively rare and mainly observed after peripheral facial nerve injury due to any cause.Presentation of caseWe describe a case of crocodile tears syndrome after vestibular schwannoma surgery in a patient with pre-operative trigeminal nerve involvement. We also discuss the various treatment options offered to the patient and the natural course of the illness.DiscussionIt is hypothesized that facial nerve fibers undergo misdirected re-innervation of the lacrimal gland instead of the salivary gland. After vestibular schwannoma surgery, facial nerve palsy is well described. The sensory symptoms are relatively neglected by the surgeon, even though they cause discomfort to the patient. This may be the cause for the underestimation of its incidence.ConclusionThe knowledge of the incidence, mechanism and natural course of crocodile tears syndrome along with patient education is important to reduce post-operative distress to the patient. We also hypothesize the correlation of crocodile tears syndrome with motor component of facial function and the role of preservation of nervus intermedius in reducing post-operative facial palsy.

Introduction

Crocodile tears syndrome (CTS) is a lacrimal hypersecretion disorder characterized by excessive tearing associated with gustatory stimulation like eating, drinking or smelling food [1]. Most commonly seen after Bell's palsy but also can be seen after other causes of facial nerve injury like trauma, stroke, infection or facial nerve surgery [1,2]. We present a case of CTS, post-operatively after vestibular schwannoma surgery. Distinctively the patient had pre-operative involvement of the trigeminal nerve. We also hypothesize a direct correlation between grade of facial palsy and incidence of CTS.

Presentation of case

Our patient, is an otherwise healthy 49-year-old gentleman presented with tinnitus in the right ear for 2 years and right-sided progressive hearing loss for 6 months. He also complained of lancinating pain over the right jaw for 3 months. On examination, right-sided retro-cochlear sensorineural hearing loss was present. Right trigeminal nerve hypoesthesia was present along with depressed right-sided corneal reflex. No pre-operative facial palsy was present. Right-sided cerebellar signs were noted.

Contrast enhanced MRI of the brain, was suggestive of a right 3.5 × 3.1 × 3.1 cm sized solid-cystic vestibular schwannoma.

Patient was operated via right retro-sigmoid approach and sub-total excision of the tumor was done to preserve neurological function. Tumor was whitish-yellow firm and moderately vascular. It partially suckable and adherent to CN VII-VIII complex with thinning of the nerves. Sub-total resection of tumor was achieved. Thin uniform layer of tumor adherent to the CN VII-VIII complex was left behind. Histopathological diagnosis was consistent with vestibular schwannoma. Patient had post-operative House-Brackmann Grade IV right-sided facial palsy. There was mild improvement of right facial hypo-aesthesia. Lower cranial nerve function was intact. There was no change in the right-sided hearing. Facial pain improved after surgery. Patient was counselled and physiotherapy was started for the facial palsy. At 6 months follow up, patient had improvement in facial palsy (Grade III). Facial synkinesis was present (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Facial palsy at 6 months follow-up.Fig. 2

He also complained of right-sided hyper-lacrimation with right nasal congestion while eating food or drinking. Within a few seconds after starting to eat, tearing would start. There was no correlation with the type or amount of food. Simultaneous to the tearing, profuse right-sided nasal congestion occurred. Chewing without food did not elicit the same response. Taste was preserved. Emotional and reflex lacrimation were normal. Schirmer's test was normal. The various treatment options were discussed with patient. He was counselled about the natural history of the symptoms. Conservative management was advised. He opted to carry a handkerchief with him, especially during meals.

At follow-up after 1 year, there was further mild improvement in the facial palsy. The symptom of paradoxical hyper-lacrimation remained identical. After knowing the benign cause of his symptoms, his emotional distress was greatly reduced. He was able to manage his symptoms by measures like carrying a handkerchief. The patient chose not to opt for invasive treatments like botulinum toxin injection and continued conservative management. Periodic follow-up was advised.

The case has been reported in accordance with the SCARE criteria [6].

Discussion

Crocodile tears syndrome (CTS) or paradoxical gustatory hyper-lacrimation was first noted by Oppenheim in 1913 and described in detail by Bogograd in 1928 [1]. The name comes from the myth of crocodiles shedding tears before and while consuming their prey. [3] Causes include Bell-palsy, trauma, stroke, herpes-zoster infection, neuro-sarcoidosis and Duane's syndrome which is bilateral CTS involving the abducens nucleus [4]. Two series have been published describing CTS after vestibular schwannoma surgery [3,5]. But there is a large variability in the incidence reported in both studies. A case of CTS after microvascular decompression for trigeminal neuralgia is also described [2]. The nerve fibers responsible for lacrimation are conveyed along with those for taste and salivation in the nervus intermedius. Nervus intermedius is devoid of perineurium, is sparsely myelinated and is situated between the main facial nerve and tumor [3]. And, is thus equally liable to trauma during surgery.

The CTS syndrome can be early onset (within 1 month) or late onset (after 6 months). A transaxonal/ephaptic transmission due to short circuiting of inadequately insulated nerves fibers is the cause for early onset CTS while late onset CTS is due to aberrant regeneration/misdirection of the nervus intermedius. The diagnosis of CTS is essentially clinical and management is mainly conservative. Our patient presented as late-onset CTS. Nasal congestion could be secondary to excessive tearing or due to nasal hypersecretion.

The diagnosis of CTS is essentially clinical and management is mainly conservative. Our patient did not show clinical improvement. However, with reassurance was able to adapt to the symptoms. Treatment options include the most widely accepted lacrimal gland botulinum toxin injection with effects lasting for 6 months and partial excision of the gland. Historical treatments involved sectioning of chorda tympani, glossopharyngeal nerve, greater superficial petrosal nerve, complete lacrimal gland resection, sphenopalatine ganglion blockade and anti-cholinergics [4].

Although CTS following vestibular schwannoma has been described our case was unique in having pre-operative trigeminal nerve involvement. Studies published by Irving et al. in 1995 and Nakamizo et al. in 2012 showed a varied incidence of CTS after vestibular schwannoma surgery; 44 % and 10 % respectively [3,5]. The widely varied incidence could be attributed to insufficient reporting due to the less obvious nature of the symptoms as compared to a disfiguring facial palsy. Both the occurrence and recovery of CTS correlated well with the grade of facial palsy. Exception being a complete facial palsy, which shows that a completely damaged nerve does not allow normal or abnormal transmission. Size of the tumor, nature of the tumor and intra-operative facial nerve preservation were factors found to influence the incidence [3,5].

Majority of the patients in both the studies responded well to pre-operative counselling and expectant management. [1,3,5]

Conclusion

In literature, crocodile tears syndrome is a rare entity after vestibular schwannoma surgery. The symptoms of CTS tend to be overlooked by the clinician and hence, are underreported. The precise incidence and natural course of crocodile tears syndrome needs to be elucidated because of the distressing effects to the patient as well as due to its correlation with preservation of motor component of facial function. The effect of pre-operative trigeminal function on CTS also needs to be studied. An adequate preoperative counselling of the patient is paramount in managing CTS and relevant consent should be always included prior to surgery.

Effectiveness of a multidisciplinary team teaching method on breastfeeding knowledge of nursing students in China: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Qingkun Han, Tingting Gao, Cuiping Li, Xuebing Jing
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSpecial breastfeeding training is rare in China and contradicts clinical expectations for students.Multidisciplinary team (MDT) approaches have been extensively advocated in nursing education. However, the incorporation of MDT education to enhance students' knowledge of breastfeeding remains insufficient and constrained.ObjectiveTo examine the change in knowledge of nursing students in China following a clinical MDT education intervention on breastfeeding.MethodsThis was a 2-arm randomized controlled trial. This study examined differences in pre- and posttest breastfeeding knowledge scores of experimental and control groups. The intervention group adopted the MDT teaching method, the control group received a routine training model. A comparative analysis of subindicators, which consisted of satisfaction levels among students, teachers, and administrators was conducted.ResultsThe breastfeeding knowledge and satisfaction of the intervention group exceeded that of the control group. There were differences in enhancement scores between the intervention and control groups.ConclusionThe MDT educational intervention notably bolstered nursing students' breastfeeding knowledge.It is advisable to heighten awareness of educational initiatives and establish a robust foundation for future breastfeeding promotion endeavors.

Introduction

Breastmilk is a complex biofluid providing antibodies and immune components, rendering it the optimal sustenance for infants. The World Health Organization (WHO) and UNICEF jointly recommend exclusive breastfeeding for infants up to 6 months of age, followed by continued breastfeeding until at least 2 years of age, or longer [1]. Breastfeeding is associated with improved health outcomes for mothers and children [2]. Breastfeeding plays a pivotal role in ensuring a child's lifelong health and yields cost savings for health facilities, families, and governments. Its benefits extend from the initial days of life well into adulthood, encompassing a reduction in the risk of obesity and chronic diseases in both low-income and high-income countries [3]. Additionally, breastfeeding prevents juvenile diabetes, cardiovascular diseases and lymphomas, among others [4]. Breastfeeding benefits mothers by reducing postpartum hemorrhage [5], prolonging amenorrhea and promoting recovery following birth [5–7]. There is widespread agreement that enhancing breastfeeding rates worldwide is crucial to providing children with the most advantageous beginning in life. Despite its incorporation into global social protection frameworks and its profound importance in advancing maternal and infant health as well as social development, the global landscape of breastfeeding still presents challenges and falls short of optimistic expectations. Since the 2000 s, rates of early breastfeeding initiation and exclusive breastfeeding among infants under six months have both reached approximately 50%, with figures standing at 50% for early initiation and 49% for exclusive breastfeeding as of 2019 [8]. However, breastfeeding has exhibited a declining trend in low-income countries, and there are still large disparities within different countries [8]. In China, the exclusive breastfeeding rate for infants under six months stands at a mere 29%, significantly below the global average of 43%. This falls short of the 50% target set by the China National Program of Action for Child Development [9] and the World Health Organization's objective of achieving a 70% exclusive breastfeeding rate for infants under six months by 2030 [8].

A study conducted in China found that exclusive breastfeeding rates at six months varied significantly across 17 cities, ranging from 0.50% to 33.45%. This assessment, which reviewed changes over the past few decades, also revealed that 2.3% of infants had never been breastfed [10]. Traditional teaching in Chinese schools often struggles to equip students with a comprehensive and systematic understanding of breastfeeding. Moreover, there is a scarcity of hospitals capable of providing systematic training to students on breastfeeding knowledge [11]. Thus, increasing breastfeeding rates in China may require additional efforts. WHO and UNICEF issued a new ten-step guidance to increase support for breastfeeding in 2018 (World Health Organization 2011), so as to ensure that staff have sufficient knowledge capacity and skills to support breastfeeding. UNICEF Executive Director Henrietta H. Fore highlighted that breastfeeding requires support, encouragement and guidance (World Health Organization 2011). Nursing students hold crucial roles in educating and guiding mothers on breastfeeding practices, yet their own proficiency in breastfeeding knowledge is often suboptimal [12, 13]. Breastfeeding knowledge courses are often inadequately covered in many university programs, or are provided only at specific stages of learning. Consequently, nursing students may struggle to apply the acquired knowledge to clinical practice, as retention may wane over time. This gap in knowledge transfer can impede nursing students' ability to effectively educate and support mothers. Developing comprehensive breastfeeding curricula is therefore imperative to ensure that students acquire deep knowledge, skills, and positive attitudes, preparing them adequately for their roles in promoting breastfeeding [14].

The multi-disciplinary team (MDT) treatment model, as defined by a scholar from the United Kingdom, is characterized as a medical approach aimed at formulating optimal, personalized treatment plans [15]. Originating from the renowned Anderson Hospital in the United States during the 1970 s, its fundamental tenet revolves around patient-centered, standardized, individualized, and continuous comprehensive treatment strategies tailored to specific diseases, all underpinned by the collaboration of multidisciplinary teams [16]. In recent years, it has gradually been adopted worldwide in clinical fields such as disease treatment, education, and quality improvement, among others. The present research team conducted a study on MDT teaching in 2022 and constructed the indicators of MDT teaching. These indicators were designed to enhance the quality of clinical practice teaching and improve the practical skills of medical students [17]. MDT teaching is widely used in Chinese mainland clinical teaching, including medical imaging teaching, oncology clinical teaching, standardized training for residency practitioners, teaching ward rounds, etc., which effectively improves the teaching quality.No trial has evaluated the effectiveness of an MDT teaching program for educating nursing students on breastfeeding in China. As such, the aim of the present study was to measure the effect of an MDT educational intervention on improving student’s breastfeeding knowledge and skills in mainland of China. It is also a call for managers to adopt early special training to improve the breastfeeding rate among nursing students.

Method

Study setting

This was a 2-arm randomized controlled trial.The intervention group adopted the MDT teaching method, the control group received a routine training model.The sample number was calculated by G*Power with level of α 0.05, power (1-β) 0.8. Assuming a 10%−15% loss to followup, final minimum sample size for each group was 78.The recruitment of research subjects was carried out by the teaching secretary of the nursing department of the hospital according to the schedule of the internship plan. When students were scheduled to clinical practice in the obstetrics department, the teaching secretary consulted each student as to whether they were willing to participate in the teaching activities of the obstetrics teaching group, and recruited the intern nurses who volunteered to participate in the teaching activities into the group.The teaching secretary registered the general information of the research subjects, including age, sex, education level, birthplace, previous studies, course in university,exposure experience. Using the random number table method by the correspondent author, students were divided into two groups: the intervention group (n = 78), which received a MDT approach, and the control group (n = 78), which followed the routine training model. Random numbers for each group were placed inside unsealed envelopes and opened when the study assistant was enrolled. Baseline and post-intervention values for both groups were collected by outcome assessors who were blinded for the group assignment. Upon the conclusion of the training, the research team assessed alterations in breastfeeding knowledge levels by comparing them with baseline data, utilizing a Chinese version of the breastfeeding knowledge questionnaire tailored for nursing students [18]. Additionally, sub-indicators measuring satisfaction levels among students, teachers, and administrators (including the hospital teaching management department and ward teaching management organization group) were compared between the intervention and control groups.

Eligibility and recruitment

Nursing students who completed a four-week nursing practice in the maternity ward and expressed their intention to participate in the teaching program after providing informed consent were eligible for inclusion based on the predetermined criteria. Students were excluded if there were any disruptions in their class schedules during this period. The training period was held from March to May 2024 in obstetrical ward of X China.The internship plan was uniformly arranged by the nursing department, with each student's obstetric internship lasting four weeks.Nursing students could withdraw from the research group for any reason.

Randomization and blinding

The 156 nursing students who met the inclusion criteria were randomly assigned to either the intervention group or the control group (n = 78), using a 1:1 ratio. To ensure unbiased data collection, two coordinators, who were not involved in teaching either group, were designated to collect data. These coordinators were blinded to the intervention protocol. Additionally, teachers were assigned exclusively to one group and did not cross over to teach the other group. Both participants and teachers were aware of their group assignments. Both groups were only aware of their own group teaching plan. The trial was conducted at a three-level grade hospital in X, China, specifically within the obstetrical ward comprising two units, each with a capacity of 70 beds and situated across two stories. To prevent contamination between the two groups, the intervention group was allocated to the first obstetric unit, while the control group was assigned to the second obstetric unit.

Description of the intervention

Participants in both the intervention and control groups received identical orientation education upon entering the obstetrical ward at the beginning of their internship and upon completion of their internship. This education encompassed familiarization with ward settings, departmental rules and regulations, personnel arrangements, internship requirements, ward environment, and the teaching program. Moreover, each student had their designated teacher throughout the entire internship period, with whom they could consult and operate under guidance when encountering difficulties. All training teachers involved in the study had over five years of experience and possessed extensive expertise in clinical nursing teaching.

Control group teaching program

The control group received routine nursing teaching, which covered various aspects of breastfeeding, including breast milk composition, storage, and significance, as well as breastfeeding timing assessment, breastfeeding techniques (such as feeding posture, nipple positioning, breast emptying, and techniques for burping the baby), and other conventional theoretical training. This training was integrated into the daily internship activities, without any dedicated breastfeeding course. The training was conducted by teaching nurses specialized in obstetrics, each with over five years of experience, who were responsible for the daily teaching tasks of nursing students.

Intervention group: MDT education program

Building upon the foundation of routine nursing teaching, the MDT teaching team included an obstetrics specialist nurse, a pediatrics specialist nurse, an obstetrician, a pediatrician, an anesthesiologist, a pharmacist, a psychologist, a breast and thyroid surgeon, and a dietitian. Before the establishment of the teaching plan, teachers of the MDT team set up a WeChat group, and held two rounds of planning communication forums. Five obstetric nurses were interviewed to identify common issues and concerns encountered in daily maternal care. Additionally, the syllabus was developed based on the shortcomings observed in breastfeeding practices within the obstetrics nursing curriculum in China, with guidance from the "Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding" jointly issued by WHO and UNICEF in 2018. Each lecture included teaching cases provided by specialized instructors corresponding to the professional content. MDT education program

The training sessions occurred 2–3 times per week, each lasting approximately 1 h, over a total duration of 4 weeks. A dedicated teaching coordinator oversaw communication and training arrangements. Prior to commencing the teaching sessions, the MDT teaching group established clear teaching objectives and underwent unified training on the teaching process.

The teaching process mainly included pre-teaching preparation, teaching process and teaching assessment. ① The MDT teaching group provided students with teaching cases in advance, addressing common issues such as a breastfeeding mother with acute mastitis experiencing depression, social withdrawal, and refusal to eat. Students were encouraged to contemplate, discuss, and consult relevant materials beforehand to formulate their initial assessments and judgments. During this stage, students aimed to develop their individual medical perspectives and gain practical experience in handling the teaching content. ② The teaching process involved the selection of typical real cases by teachers, drawing from their teaching experience and utilizing teaching materials. These cases were presented in stages by multidisciplinary teachers, allowing students to immerse themselves in the learning experience. The process included case reporting, posing questions, analysis, and discussion, followed by teaching and summarization steps, which deepened understanding progressively. First,a lead MDT teacher introduced the prepared cases, encouraging students to think critically and voice their own perspectives. Group discussions were then conducted to analyze the root causes of issues and propose solutions, with teachers primarily guiding discussions, intervening only to address contradictions or misunderstandings. Each group appointed a representative to articulate the final opinions after discussing the case. Subsequently, MDT teachers delivered core lectures and addressed questions based on the overall discussion. Continuous engagement with students and problem-solving was facilitated throughout the session. Simulation scenarios were commonly employed in teaching, where in high-fidelity situations were created to simulate real-life cases. Students interacted with standardized patients, postpartum mothers, and simulation tools (e.g., breast models, artificial nipples, and baby models) to gain a practical understanding of nursing procedures. Teachers provided timely guidance and feedback during these exercises. For instance, when discussing breastfeeding postures, experiential teaching was employed, with students wearing simulated breasts to practice embracing baby models and practicing feeding postures and burping methods. This hands-on clinical scenario experience fostered active learning and critical thinking skills, aiding students in developing a comprehensive medical perspective. Through continuous reflection and reconstruction of medical experiences and knowledge, students' problem-solving enthusiasm was cultivated, ultimately enhancing their ability to actively engage in resolving clinical challenges.③ At the conclusion of each class, teachers guided students to share their reflections on the teaching experience, noting any changes they observed before and after the session. Specifically, students were encouraged to address any misconceptions or misunderstandings about the course content to deepen their understanding and ensure accuracy. The teachers provided comprehensive feedback on students' performance throughout the teaching process, offering specific opinions and suggestions tailored to each student, which including participation positive degree, initiative, cognitive degree, etc.The third-party management department conducted teaching evaluations, identifying teaching issues and promptly gathering feedback to continuously improve the teaching quality.

Data collection

A Spanish breastfeeding knowledge questionnaire was designed by Lopez-Peñae et al., and includes 21 multiple choice questions, with four options and only one correct response [19]. In accordance with Brislin's translation model, Meiling Dai et al. translated and modified based on Chinese culture (Dai et al. 2021). The Chinese version of the questionnaire was both reliable and valid. In scoring, each correct answer earns one point, and the total score of the 14 questions is weighted to a maximum of 100 points to obtain the standard score. A higher total score indicated a better level of breastfeeding knowledge.

Coordinators collected baseline data, including basic demographic information and breastfeeding knowledge levels, within 24 h of admission. These data were collected again after participants underwent theoretical and clinical learning practices.

Basic demographic information typically includes gender, age, marital status, educational background, duration of internship, and prior experiences related to breastfeeding (such as exposure to breastfeeding within their family or observation of others breastfeeding). Sub-indicators of satisfaction were collected from students, teachers, and administrators (including hospital teaching management department and ward teaching management organization group). Participants were asked to provide a satisfaction score out of 100, reflecting their overall satisfaction with the teaching style and delivery.

Data analysis

Analyses were conducted using SPSS statistical software, version 25.0. The breastfeeding scores (Mean ± SD) were descriptively analyzed. Group differences in pre- and postscores, as well as baseline values, were analyzed using the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies or percentages, and comparisons were conducted using Pearson’s Chi-square test. A p-value of less than 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

The findings indicate that the level of breastfeeding knowledge among nursing students in China is notably low, consistent with prior research [12, 19–22]. Both clinical practice and academic nursing students exhibit suboptimal levels of breastfeeding knowledge [12, 19–21]. Yang et al. synthesized 14 studies that assessed professional students’ knowledge and attitudes towards breastfeeding. The findings indicated that students’ knowledge was limited, particularly in relation to breastfeeding assessment and management. Many nursing students are inadequately trained and graduate with inadequate breastfeeding knowledge and skills to help mother’s breastfeed [20, 23]. This deficiency may stem from the limited inclusion of breastfeeding courses within university curricula, primarily confined to obstetrics, pediatrics, or related disciplines [11, 19–21, 23, 24]. Consequently, nursing students may lack confidence in maternal breastfeeding management. Furthermore, despite exposure to obstetric practice or training courses, many students' breastfeeding knowledge remains inadequate due to low-quality instruction. instruction. Therefore, specialized breastfeeding knowledge and skills training is essential for enhancing professional maternal guidance [13].

The experimental and control group training schemes effectively improved students' breastfeeding knowledge, consistent with previous findings [20, 21, 25]. Notably, the MDT group exhibited superior improvement, suggesting the efficacy of the present approach. The MDT teaching program not only emphasizes the importance of breastfeeding knowledge but also delves into detailed explanations of breast milk composition, breastfeeding's relationship with medication and anesthesia, and management of breastfeeding-related issues. This comprehensive approach enhances students' understanding from milk production to infant nutrition. Moreover, our MDT teaching approach fosters active student participation, stimulating interest, problem-solving abilities, and teacher enthusiasm. The present authors advocate for the integration of MDT teaching into nursing curricula to promote collaborative learning and enhance students' creativity [26].

At present, there is no standardized implementation process for MDT teaching, typically being tailored to the specific needs of departments or hospitals by researchers. For instance, Xia utilized a four-step method, involving data collection prior to class [27], outlining speeches, grouping disciplines to pose questions, discussing these questions, and summarizing contentious points. Matamoros L et al. [28] introduced a multidisciplinary teaching rounds process, comprising topic submission, presenter coaching, assistance during presentations, and evaluation. In the present approach, obstetric cases are prioritized, integrating teaching content into case studies. This method encourages students who receive cases in advance to engage in critical thinking and brainstorming sessions. Despite the long history of nursing education in China, the predominant approach has been teacher-centered, with limited student engagement. Active student participation has been challenging due to this traditional teaching style. MDT teaching represents more than just a shift in instructors; it involves guiding students to pose and solve questions, fostering their understanding of teaching complexities, and shaping their clinical thinking towards active learning. Hence, students experience significant gains in MDT teaching. Further research is needed to standardize breastfeeding education, drawing upon the multidisciplinary teaching concept. Our study contributes to this by enhancing the theoretical foundation for standardizing clinical breastfeeding education courses.

During the teaching process, our research implemented third-party evaluations from the teaching management department to mitigate issues like self-centeredness and lack of objectivity inherent in self-evaluations by teachers and students. Third-party evaluations provide a more objective assessment and offered constructive feedback for improvement. By incorporating these evaluations, MDT nursing practice teaching can undergo continuous enhancement, leading to the optimization of teaching programs and ultimately achieving better teaching outcomes. Matamoros et al. [28] also proposed evaluating the impact of MDT teaching programs on organizational outcomes to achieve a comprehensive assessment. Various methods of teaching evaluation exist, including the Kirkpatrick model [29], formative evaluation, and summative evaluation [30] among others. The present authors propose a mixed evaluation approach in MDT teaching, incorporating input from stakeholders, third parties, and others. To ensure the authenticity of evaluations, it's essential to carefully align the chosen methods with the research's purpose and content. This comprehensive approach allows for the consideration of each method's applicability to different courses, thereby mitigating the limitations of any single evaluation method. In summary, MDT teaching should prioritize not only teaching quality but also ongoing evaluation of the teaching process, feedback from teaching objectives, and third-party evaluations. Identifying and addressing issues through evaluation is crucial for dynamically and effectively improving teaching outcomes [31].

Limitations

There were several limitations in the present study. First, it was an intervention study conducted in only one hospital. A multi-center study can be considered in the future, as well as the appropriate level of breastfeeding knowledge in nursing students. In this study, we addressed the limitations of single-center research by selecting a representative tertiary Class A general hospital as our inclusion criteria. The single-center hospital we included is a representative Grade III-A general hospital in the region and a provincial-level regional medical center in Shandong Province. Consequently, its obstetrics department serves residents from at least five surrounding cities. The department spans two floors with over 70 beds, admitting more than 250 maternal patients monthly, and its case spectrum demonstrates strong representativeness. In addition, the teaching work of the hospital can radiate to many medical schools in the province, and receives more than 500 nurse interns every year. Second, a longitudinal study was not conducted by the present research team after the intervention for a variety of reasons. As such, it remains unclear how students' level of knowledge evolves between different groups in subsequent months. Future studies should extend the indicator monitoring duration to verify whether there is a difference in the level of student knowledge decline between the two teaching methods. This will facilitate improvements to the teaching program.

Conclusion

Formal training in breastfeeding support is scarce in Chinese nursing education, creating a significant gap with clinical expectations for student competencies. In order to improve the rate of exclusive breastfeeding, the present authors call for special breastfeeding training in clinical practice. The MDT teaching evaluated in the present study is helpful in increasing breastfeeding knowledge of nursing students. By providing breastfeeding education and training through MDT faculties, daily misunderstandings of breastfeeding challenges encountered by nursing students can be corrected. This will increase students’ knowledge reserves, allowing them to provide maternal education confidently and improve their maternal feeding confidence and knowledge level. In future, studies should extend the indicator monitoring duration to verify whether there is a difference in the level of student knowledge decline between different teaching methods to improve the teaching program. Moreover, MDT teaching can significantly enhance teaching efficacy and resource allocation, especially in the context of breastfeeding education in mainland China. This approach not only elevates students' proficiency but also enhances their awareness of breastfeeding, laying a solid foundation for their future professional and personal endeavors. We really call for special training to improve breast feeding knowledge among nursing students, because they are not only the future health practitioners, but also the parents of the future children. We recommend that at a national level, the BSc Nursing curriculum undergoes a comprehensive audit and evaluation. This is to ensure it adequately incorporates evidence-based breastfeeding education, thereby equipping future nurses with the essential knowledge to contribute to improved maternal and infant health outcomes.

Unveiling GDF15 as a promising biomarker for predicting survival in pancreatic ductal carcinoma: a retrospective research
Authors: Jianchun Xiao, Xiaoyue Lu, Wenhao Luo, Yicheng Wang, Taiping Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prognosis of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma remains dismal, largely because this disease is typically diagnosed at an advanced stage, making early detection a major challenge. Beyond the conventional biomarker CA19-9, emerging evidence suggests the utility of additional markers for distinguishing PDAC from normal pancreatic tissue or chronic pancreatitis. Among these, Growth Differentiation Factor 15 (GDF15), implicated in various malignancies, has arisen as a promising candidate, particularly when expressed at elevated levels in pancreatic cancer. Recent insights into the oncogenic potential of GDF15 underscore its prognostic significance.MethodsIn this study, a comprehensive analysis was performed by integrating clinicopathological data with GDF15 expression profiles from a retrospective clinical cohort. Background

Pancreatic ductal carcinoma (PDAC) is a neoplasm with high malignant potential. As the fourth leading cause of cancer-related death in the United States in 2020, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) continues to have a poor prognosis, with a 5-year relative survival rate remaining at just 13% despite recent therapeutic advances [1].

A considerable number of patients are diagnosed with advanced-stage PDAC due to the absence of clinical symptoms in the early stages [2, 3]. Consequently, the utilization of biomarkers has become a focal point in pancreatic cancer research, offering potential benefits such as enabling routine PDAC screening and reducing the need for frequent CT scans, thereby minimizing radiation exposure. CA19-9, also known as sialyl Lewis a (sLea), is currently the standard biomarker for PDAC [4]. However, a notable limitation of CA19-9 is its lack of specificity. Elevated CA19‑9 is not unique to PDAC, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, or cystic neoplasms; it can also occur in malignancies of the gastric system, colorectum, lung, breast, and liver [5, 6]. Additionally, the level of CA19-9 can be unstable under various circumstances. As an antigen produced in the human circulatory system, CA19-9 levels may rise when homeostasis is disrupted, such as jaundice [7], pancreatitis [8], as well as some non-specific diseases unrelated to cancer. Another tumor biomarker, CEA, has encountered similar problems in clinical usage. Hence, new biomarkers with sufficient specificity are crucial for detecting PDAC in early stages and predicting prognosis.

Growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF15), also known as macrophage inhibitory cytokine-1 (MIC-1), is a member of the transforming growth factor β (TGFβ) superfamily and plays a significant role in pathophysiology. It acts as a stress response cytokine and is highly expressed in organs such as the heart, pancreas, liver, kidney, colon, and prostate [9, 10]. Previous research have confirmed GDF15 to have diagnostic and prognostic value in malignant tumors of other organs, including prostate cancer [11], endometrial cancer [12], and gastric cancer [13]. However, Kaur evaluated the performance as a diagnostic indicator of different biomarkers in PDAC patients. The results indicated that expression of GDF15 was significantly elevated in PDAC tissues compared with normal tissues [14]. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was performed on serum GDF15 and CA19-9 levels of PDAC patients and healthy persons in Koopmann’s research, indicating that GDF15 had a higher success rate than CA19-9 in distinguishing PDAC [15]. A meta-analysis of data from 2826 patients compared the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of GDF15 and CA19-9. Yang stated in the results that sensitivity of GDF15 is 80% versus 71% of CA19-9, and the specificity is 85% versus 88% [16]. The diagnostic value of GDF‑15 in PDAC was thoroughly validated, underscoring its pro‑tumorigenic characteristics in this malignancy. Nevertheless, there have been limited studies conducted on the predictive significance of GDF15 for PDAC. The purpose of this research is to address this knowledge gap.

Clinical information and pathological results were collected from all patients. Patients were categorized based on tumor stage using the 7th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer TNM classification. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval between the date of surgery and the date of death. This study was approved by the Research Medical Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from each patient before inclusion in the study.

Array construction and hematoxylin-eosin staining

A sub-cohort of 81 patients was selected from the total 375 patients in this part of the research. Tissue microarrays (TMAs) were assembled from tumor and adjacent normal pancreatic tissues and were arranged according to a predetermined TMA table. Construction of TMAs was performed using a tissue arrayer (MTA1 Tissue Arrayer), and sections were obtained using an automated microtome (Leica RM2165 SN. 0903/06–2002). The quality of the TMAs was assessed by an experienced pathologist, who also evaluated the extent of Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining in the arrays.

Immunohistochemical analysis

The TMAs were prepared using a PV-9000 IHC kit (ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) and incubated with an anti-GDF15 antibody (27455-1-AP; Proteintech, Wuhan, China). Subsequently, DAB from the PV-9000 kit and Harris hematoxylin (REDPHARM, Beijing, China) were employed. IHC results were independently evaluated by two experienced pathologists. The staining intensities were graded as 0 (negative), 1 (low), 2 (medium) or 3 (high), and the staining extent was scored between 0 and 100%. The final composite expression scores (CESs) were calculated according to the following formula: CES = intensity score × percentage score × 100. A cut-off value for GDF15 expression (95) was determined using an outcome-oriented analysis, enabling optimal separation of patient groups with distinct survival outcomes.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using a Chi-square test, and analyzed using Student’s t test or analysis of variance (ANOVA) in SPSS 24.0. Fisher’s exact test was used for variables with an expected value < 5. Differences in overall survival between the two groups were evaluated by Kaplan-Meier single factor analysis (Log-rank method). The relative risks were analyzed by multivariate analysis in a Cox proportional hazards regression model, and p < 0.05 was considered significant. GraphPad Prism 8.2 was employed to generate graphs.

Results

Characteristics of patients

During the research 83 patients (51 males (61.4%) and 32 females (38.6%)) were included, for whom the mean age was 60.8 years (95% CI 58.8–62.9). Direct postoperative pathology evidence of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is available in each patient. 63.9% of the patients (n = 53) had their tumors located in the head of the pancreas, which was linked to pancreaticoduodenectomy. The rest of the patients (n = 30, 36.1%) underwent distal pancreatectomy because of tumors located in the pancreatic tail. No serious postoperative complications were observed in the patients. The clinical stages of the patients are distributed similar to the general situation of PDAC patients, with 3 patients (3.6%) in TNM stage I, 72 (86.8%) in stage II, 1 (1.2%) in stage III, and 7 (8.4%) in stage IV. The overall survival (OS) time was 19.8 months (95% CI 17.0–22.6), with a maximum value of 54 and a minimum value of 2. Detailed clinicopathological information is provided in Table S1.

GDF15 expression

The results of IHC staining revealed some features of the expression pattern of GDF15 in PDAC tissues and adjacent normal pancreas tissues. GDF15 was predominantly localized to the nuclei of PDAC cells, and samples from patients with poor prognosis exhibited higher levels of GDF15 expression (Fig. 1a and b). Subsequent quantitative analyses showed that GDF15 expression was significantly enhanced in PDAC tissues compared to normal pancreas tissues (95 (95% CI 168.7–191.2) vs. 54.9 (95% CI 46.6–63.3), p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1c). An outcome‑oriented approach established a GDF15 expression threshold of 95, which most effectively separated groups with distinct survival outcomes. Patients were subsequently divided into high-expression (CES ≥ 95) and low-expression (CES < 95) groups according to their CES value.

IHC results of GDF15 level in PDAC and normal pancreas tissues. a Immunohistochemical analysis revealed markedly elevated GDF15 expression in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) specimens, whereas adjacent non‑neoplastic pancreatic tissues showed little to no detectable GDF15 staining. b Patients with poor prognoses exhibited higher expression levels compared to those with more favorable outcomes. c H‑score results for GDF15 expression were graphically represented, revealing a statistically significant difference in staining intensity between PDAC tumor tissues and their adjacent non‑neoplastic counterparts

Prognosis of patients

Survival data for the two groups were analyzed separately and compared using the Kaplan–Meier method. Due to their uniformly short survival times, 25 patients with stage IV disease were excluded, as they did not meet the minimum threshold for evaluating GDF15 expression effects. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that patients with low GDF15 expression had significantly longer overall survival than patients with high GDF15 expression (18 months vs. 13 months, p = 0.037, Fig. 2, Fig. S1).

OS of PDAC patients with different GDF15 levels. Patients exhibiting low GDF15 expression showed significantly longer overall survival than those with high expression, as demonstrated by Kaplan–Meier analysis.

Discussion

Eighty-three patients with confirmed pancreatic cancer were included in this research cohort. This study investigated GDF15 expression in pancreatic cancer and normal pancreatic tissues, revealing a clear upregulation of GDF15 in PDAC samples. Positive results of our study helped to prove the ability of GDF15 to induce tumor growth and invasion. The relationship between GDF15 level and overall survival of PDAC patients was also newly discovered here. Patients with high GDF15 levels in their tumors were less likely to have a longer OS time.

GDF15 expression has been shown in numerous studies to be a pivotal link in tumorigenesis. Considering its biological classification as a TGF-β family member, the earliest research on the mechanism of GDF15 affecting cancer mainly focused on TGF-β receptors (TGFβRs). It has been confirmed in the study of Artz that TGFβRⅠ and TGFβRⅡ receive the signal of GDF15 and trigger an anti-inflammatory pathway [17, 18]. Prostate cancer, gastric cancer, and colorectal cancer have recieved the most thorough research in recent years, while comparatively little attention was put on pancreatic cancer. Academic achievements on these neoplasms may lead to some possible understandings on the function of GDF15 in PDAC.

Similar to prostate cancer, stroma cells make contributions to the secretion of GDF15 in PDAC. Proteomics analysis conducted by Zabransky discovered that aged fibroblasts in pancreatic tissues secreted a higher level of GDF15 when compared with young fibroblasts. Besides, disparities in tumor growth tendency were also reversed following exogenous regulation of GDF15 in different fibroblasts [19]. Despite growing interest, the functional role of GDF15 in pancreatic cancer remains poorly understood, with SMAD currently recognized as its most widely acknowledged downstream effector. He’s study revealed that GDF15 was regulated by SULF2, which then stimulates TGFβ-SMAD axis [20]. Moreover, orphan receptor GFRAL, which was present in several other cancers as a receptor for GDF15, was positioned in PDAC by Zhao [21]. GDF15 was also found to possibly influence the tumor–stroma crosstalk and therefore participate in disease progression in a study of Zabransky [19]. Research on the clinical potential of GDF15 has primarily focused on its use as a biomarker. Combination of GDF15 and CA125 exhibited satisfying accuracy in distinguishing PDAC from chronic pancreatitis, which was higher than using CA19-9 alone [22]. Furthermore, GDF15 levels were measured in the research of Wang and were found to be elevated in both PDAC tumor tissues and serum. When calculating the sensitivity and specificity of GDF15 in diagnosing PDAC serum from normal serum, GDF15 level turned out to be a more sensitive biomarker compared with CA19-9 [23]. However, considering the tumorigenesis effect of GDF15 in PDAC, its value of predicting prognosis of PDAC patients seems reliable. This research analyzed the relationship between GDF15 level and survival of PDAC patients, which addresses a gap in the prognostic evaluation of GDF15.

However, the research still has several shortcomings. Although we applied strict inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure a well-defined and homogeneous patient population for accurately assessing the expression and prognostic value of GDF15, these methodological decisions may have inadvertently introduced selection bias. Focusing on a narrowly defined cohort, some degree of variability in clinical characteristics may have been excluded, potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings to the broader population of PDAC patients. Future studies involving larger and more diverse cohorts are needed to validate our results and further explore the clinical relevance of GDF15 across different patient subgroups. In addition, the intention to detect any association between serum CA19‑9 and GDF15 expression was limited by the small sample size, which may become evident in larger future cohorts. Moreover, further experiments are needed to clarify the detailed downstream mechanisms of GDF15 in pancreatic cancer.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that GDF15 expression is significantly elevated in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) tissues compared to normal pancreatic tissues, suggesting its active involvement in tumor development and progression. The overexpression of GDF15 may reflect its role in promoting key oncogenic processes such as cell proliferation, invasion, and resistance to apoptosis, although the precise mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated. Furthermore, survival analysis revealed a strong association between high GDF15 expression and reduced overall survival among PDAC patients, indicating that GDF15 may serve not only as a marker of disease presence but also of disease aggressiveness. These findings highlight the prognostic value of GDF15 in predicting clinical outcomes and stratifying patient risk. Taken together, the results contribute new evidence to the growing recognition of GDF15 as a potential ‘star gene’ in pancreatic cancer research. In addition to its diagnostic and prognostic relevance, GDF15’s significant upregulation and association with poor prognosis underscore its potential utility as a therapeutic target, offering a new evidence base for treatment strategies aimed at improving patient outcomes in this highly lethal malignancy.

History of the Chinese medical association-Chinese society of critical care medicine
Authors: Dechang Chen
Published: 2025

The year 2025 marks the 110th anniversary of the establishment of the Chinese Medical Association (CMA). As one of its young and distinguished member organizations, the Chinese Society of Critical Care Medicine (CSCCM) has experienced 20 years of remarkable growth and development. The founding of the CSCCM in 2005 represents a landmark event in the history of critical care medicine in China, marking the formal recognition and establishment of the discipline as an independent specialty in China. On July 4, 2008, critical care medicine was officially recognized as a primary specialty under the discipline code 320.58. This formal recognition laid a broader and more solid foundation for the future advancement of critical care medicine in China. Seizing this opportunity, the CSCCM has actively undertaken a range of initiatives to further promote the development of critical care.

Development of Critical Care Medicine

In 2010, the Continuing Education Department of CMA and the CSCCM jointly launched the “5C Critical Care Qualification Training Program” intending to cultivate highly qualified and standardized critical care professionals. This initiative established a rigorous management system and educational framework, adhering to the principle of “Six Uniformities”—unified textbooks, training materials, training procedures, lecturer qualifications, assessment standards, and certification processes—to ensure the provision of standardized and high-quality education. Regardless of their professional background, all trainees receive comprehensive and standardized training, thereby reducing disparities in critical care education across different regions and institutions, and enhancing the overall quality of critical care professionals nationwide. To date, the program has successfully trained more than 40,000 participants, contributing significantly to the professionalization and standardization of critical care practices in China. Moreover, the CSCCM has developed a series of clinical guidelines and diagnostic/therapeutic standards, which serve as authoritative references for the development and clinical practice of critical care medicine in China. These standards have facilitated the implementation of uniform protocols across intensive care units (ICUs) nationwide, thereby substantially improving the quality of care and driving the discipline toward greater clinical homogeneity.

Academic Contributions and International Collaboration

The CSCCM has made significant academic contributions to the field of critical care medicine through its published journals. In 2016, the Chinese Journal of Critical Care & Intensive Care Medicine (Electronic Edition) was launched as the official Chinese-language journal of the CSCCM, providing a professional academic platform for experts, scholars, and clinicians in the field of critical care medicine in China. Furthermore, in 2021, the Journal of Intensive Medicine was established as China’s first Emerging Sources Citation Index (ESCI)-indexed journal dedicated to critical care (2024 Impact Factor 3.3). Supported by the China Science and Technology Journal Excellence Action Plan, this journal has further elevated China’s academic standing in critical care medicine, promoted intellectual exchange and collaboration, accelerated knowledge dissemination and updating, and enabled Chinese critical care professionals to remain at the forefront of global developments in the field.

Throughout the rapid and high-quality development of critical care medicine in China, the CSCCM has fostered strong collaborative partnerships with critical care societies in Europe and the United States. Over the past two decades, these collaborations have brought substantial advancements in education, training, research, and clinical practice. At the annual congress held in China, the CSCCM–European Society of Intensive Care Medicine (ESICM) and CSCCM–Society of Critical Care Medicine (SCCM) Joint Forums have become integral components of the official agendas, serving as prominent platforms to present cutting-edge research and highlight China’s significant progress in the field of critical care. Under the framework of the Belt and Road Initiative, the CSCCM continues to expand its global footprint. In 2023, the CSCCM initiated the CSCCM–Society of Intensive Care Medicine (Singapore) (SICM) Critical Care Forum, which further strengthened academic exchanges between the two countries. CSCCM made its debut at the annual congress of SICM, showcasing the academic excellence of critical care medicine in China. This milestone has laid a solid foundation for the future internationalization of the CSCCM, establishing a path toward mutually beneficial collaboration between the two societies. In 2024, the first Euro-China session on Intensive Care Medicine was convened in ESICM Annual Congress in Barcelona, marking a significant step in the expansion of China’s international collaborations. In 2025, the first Joint SCCM-CSCCM Session was held at the SCCM Annual Congress in Orlando, further advancing bilateral academic collaboration. In March 2025, the CSCCM joined the Global Research Initiative on Post-ICU Nutrition (GRIP) working group, signifying China's integration into the global critical care nutrition research network. Subsequently, the CSCCM engaged in-depth discussions with international experts on emerging topics in critical care in locations such as Croatia and Bangkok, Thailand. In May 2025, the 19th annual congress of CSCCM was held in Changsha, featuring four international joint sessions with the United States, Europe, Asia-Pacific, and Singapore, with participation from leading global experts. The CSCCM’s collaborations with its counterparts in Europe, the United States, and other regions have provided valuable models for other countries and regions, fostering mutual complementary strengths in medical research, addressing scientific challenges, sharing clinical experiences, standardizing procedures, and enhancing the capabilities of healthcare professionals. These efforts have contributed significantly to the global advancement of critical care medicine.

National Policy Support and Future Prospects

The CSCCM has played a central role in shaping national policies related to critical care medicine. In 2016, the CSCCM contributed to the compilation of critical care medicine textbooks for the “13th Five-Year Plan”, as well as textbooks for five-year undergraduate medical education published by the Ministry of Education and for five- and eight-year medical education programs published by the People’s Medical Publishing House. These contributions have provided authoritative resources for standardizing critical care knowledge across educational institutions and ensuring consistency in curricula.

In 2016, critical care medicine received significant support under the national initiatives to improve the diagnosis and treatment of complex diseases. The National Health Commission (NHC) also initiated the development of national and regional clinical centers. In 2022, the Ministry of Science and Technology launched a clinical program for severe sepsis and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). The CSCCM played a pivotal role in these initiatives, leveraging its expertise to provide professional advice and guidance and contribute to advancing research and practice in critical care medicine. In 2024, eight ministries, including the NHC, jointly issued the Opinions on Strengthening Critical Care Medical Service Capacity, which set the direction for improving China’s healthcare system, enhancing medical crisis response capabilities, promoting the development of the critical care discipline, strengthening the professional workforce, and reforming critical care services.

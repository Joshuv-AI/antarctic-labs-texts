Enhancing 5G uplink communications through efficient PCC-OFDM with neural network-based channel estimation
Authors: Monica Jenifer J, Sumathi S
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe demand for higher data rates and increased reliability in 5G uplink communications has prompted the exploration of innovative technologies. This paper introduces a novel approach that combines Polynomial-Cancellation-Coded-Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (PCC-OFDM) and integrates the power of Neural Network based channel estimation. The proposed system aims to optimize spectral efficiency and mitigates the challenges associated with channel estimation in dynamic wireless environments. The proposed system achieves superior error correction capabilities, ensuring robust communication links. The integration of OFDM provides efficient spectrum utilization and enables seamless communication in the presence of fading channels. Furthermore, an Artificial Neural Network- Multilayer Perceptron (ANN-MLP) based channel estimation mechanism is introduced to adaptively learn and predict channel conditions, enhancing the accuracy of estimated channel parameters. The biases and weights of the neural network are optimized by stochastic gradient descent (SGD) approach which in turn minimizes the loss function. Simulation outcomes prove the superior performance of the developed approach, showcasing its potential to revolutionize 5G uplink communications by achieving higher data rates, improved reliability and adaptability to varying channel conditions.Keywords: 5G uplink communication, PCC-OFDM, Neural Network, channel estimation, ANN-MLPSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The advent of fifth-generation (5G) networks has ushered in an era of unprecedented connectivity, promising transformative capabilities including massive Machine-Type Communication (mMTC), Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC), and enhanced Mobile Broadband (eMBB)1,2. Among these, uplink communication contributes efficient transmission of data from User Equipment (UE) to the base station3,4. This is particularly critical for applications including real-time video streaming, autonomous vehicles, and industrial IoT, where seamless, high-speed uplink communication is essential. However, the demand for higher data rates, greater spectral efficiency, and lower latency has pushed traditional communication paradigms to their limits5,6. To meet these demands, innovative solutions that optimize both the physical and network layers of 5G are imperative. One promising approach is the integration of PCC-OFDM and advanced techniques involving neural networks.

PCC-OFDM has appeared as a compelling candidate for enhancing the uplink performance in 5G networks7. Building on the strengths of OFDM, such as its robustness to multipath interference and ability to handle high data rates, PCC-OFDM introduces power carrier components that further optimize spectrum utilization and energy efficiency8,9. This technique leverages the inherent flexibility of OFDM while addressing its limitations like sensitivity to phase noise and high Peak-To-Average Power Ratio (PAPR)10,11. By employing PCC-OFDM in the uplink communication framework, 5G systems achieve superior performance in terms of bandwidth efficiency, error resilience, and interference mitigation12–15. However, the full potential of PCC-OFDM is only be realized when complemented by effective channel estimation methods16. Accurate Channel State Information (CSI) is vital for mitigating distortions introduced by the wireless channel, including multipath fading, Doppler shifts, and noise17. Classical channel estimation like Least Squares (LS) and Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) often struggle to adapt to the dynamic and complex nature of 5G channels. These methods may fall short in scenarios involving high mobility, dense urban environments and ultra-wideband frequencies, where channel characteristics vary rapidly18.

Machine learning and Deep learning algorithms have a substantial advantage over channel estimators since they are capable of extracting the characteristics of specified problem without necessitating considerable prior knowledge19,20. Research gap

Current deep learning-based channel estimate methods for OFDM systems have a number of drawbacks despite notable improvements. The usefulness of many techniques in complicated wireless environments with high mobility and frequency-selective fading is limited by their restriction to simplified channel models. Furthermore, some strategies ignore equalization or system performance in favor of concentrating exclusively on improving performance measures like mean square error. For real-time and resource-constrained applications, high computational complexity and reliance on large amounts of training data present difficulties. Additionally, a lot of methods operate less than optimally in real-world situations and have poor estimating accuracy, particularly in sophisticated communication networks. Adaptability to dynamic and varied contexts is further hampered by overfitting and decreased generalization. These problems are much worse in extremely noisy and dynamic environments. To overcome these challenges, the proposed work adopts a novel approach to improve accuracy, robustness, and efficiency while reducing computational complexity and dependency on training data.

Proposed system description

The proposed system integrates PCC-OFDM with a ANN-MLP based channel estimation framework to enhance the performance of 5G uplink communication as seen in Fig. 1. The design leverages the strengths of PCC-OFDM in spectrum and power efficiency while addressing the challenges of channel estimation in dynamic and interference-prone environments using neural networks. At the transmitter (TX), user-specific bit streams are processed through a subband mapper to allocate bits to different subcarriers. These signals are serialized, transformed into the frequency domain employing the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), and then combined with a Cyclic Prefix (CP) to mitigate Inter-Symbol Interference (ISI). The resulting parallel data is transmitted over the channel. The system supports multiple users, ensuring scalability in multi-user environments.

5G Uplink communications through Efficient PCC-OFDM with neural network-based channel estimation.

The receiver (RX) processes the incoming signal by removing the CP, applying the inverse DFT, and converting the parallel data back to serial. Subband de-mapping is then used to recover the original bit streams. The standout feature of the system is the ANN-MLP based channel estimation module, which operates both at the transmitter and receiver. This neural network learns to estimate the channel coefficients dynamically, leveraging its ability to model complex, nonlinear channel behaviors. By predicting accurate CSI, the system mitigates channel fading, noise, and multipath interference. This ensures improved Signal-To-Noise Ratio (SNR), minimized Bit Error Rate (BER), and enhanced reliability. This architecture enables robust communication in diverse 5G scenarios, including high-mobility and dense urban environments. By combining the spectrum efficiency of PCC-OFDM with the adaptive learning capability of ANN-MLP, the system achieves lower latency, greater spectral efficiency, and better resilience to channel impairments, making it ideal for next-generation wireless network.

This study examines the uplink of a multiple user OFDM model, in which frequency-division-multiplexing is used to provide multi-user access. Various users are assigned to distinct subband, each of which is made up of a collection of nearby OFDM subcarriers.

Compared with conventional OFDM, PCC-OFDM introduces additional challenges in channel estimation due to its unique subcarrier mapping and coding structure. In PCC-OFDM, each data symbol is transmitted over pairs of adjacent subcarriers with specific polynomial cancellation coding, which creates a dependency between neighboring subcarriers. This interdependence makes the channel estimation process more complex because the receiver must account not only for the individual subcarrier channel response but also for the correlation introduced by the coding structure. Additionally, PCC-OFDM is designed to reduce inter-carrier interference and improve robustness to synchronization errors, but this advantage requires more accurate estimation of the channel phase and amplitude across paired subcarriers. In dynamic wireless environments with multipath fading, Doppler shifts and frequency-selective channels, the estimation of these coupled subcarrier responses becomes more challenging compared to traditional OFDM where each subcarrier is treated independently. Therefore, ANN-MLP based channel estimation is employed to effectively learn the nonlinear relationships between received pilot signals and channel coefficients, enabling more accurate equalization and reliable data recovery in PCC-OFDM systems.

Channel estimation using ANN-MLP based approach

The proposed ANN-MLP based channel estimation framework differs fundamentally from existing ANN, DNN, CNN and GRU based schemes in terms of model structure, feature representation and estimation strategy rather than only performance improvement. In the proposed method, the MLP network is designed as a direct regression model that maps the LS pilot-based channel estimates to the optimal subcarrier equalization coefficients, enabling an explicit learning of the nonlinear relationship between distorted and ideal channel responses. Unlike DNN approaches, which rely on deep stacked layers with large parameter counts and heavy training requirements, the MLP uses a compact fully connected architecture that reduces computational overhead while maintaining effective feature learning. In contrast to CNN-based methods, which primarily extract spatial features through convolution kernels, the proposed framework processes the real and imaginary components of the complex channel coefficients as a unified feature vector, allowing efficient handling of complex-valued channel information without convolution operations. Similarly, GRU-based models are designed for temporal sequence modeling and involve recurrent gating mechanisms, which increase computational complexity and memory usage; however, channel estimation in PCC-OFDM mainly requires accurate nonlinear regression across subcarriers rather than temporal dependency learning. Therefore, the proposed ANN-MLP framework focuses on efficient nonlinear mapping, simplified architecture and pilot-assisted training, enabling faster convergence, reduced parameter complexity and easier scalability for large antenna systems and PCC-OFDM based wireless communication environments.The transmitted PCC-OFDM signal is subjected to MMSE channel equalization and estimation in first step by an ANN-MLP. During the training phase, the proposed ANN-MLP channel estimator is exposed to pilot signals that experience different types of channel distortions so that the network can learn the nonlinear relationship. Distortions such as phase distortion and frequency-selective fading are implicitly captured in the LS channel estimates used as inputs to the network. Since the LS estimates include both the real and imaginary components, the MLP learns the phase and amplitude variations introduced by the wireless channel across different subcarriers. In the case of frequency-selective fading, where different subcarriers experience different attenuation levels due to multipath propagation, the network processes the entire (2 N)-dimensional input vector, enabling it to learn correlations among neighboring subcarriers and compensate for subcarrier-dependent fading effects. Furthermore, the training dataset is generated under diverse channel conditions, including varying signal-to-noise ratios, path loss levels, user mobility and Doppler shifts, which represent realistic wireless environments. The presence of Doppler-induced frequency variations and mobility-related channel fluctuations allows the MLP model to generalize its learning and maintain accurate channel prediction even when the channel characteristics change over time. Through iterative optimization using stochastic gradient descent, the network gradually minimizes the estimation error across these varying channel scenarios, thereby enabling the trained model to robustly equalize PCC-OFDM signals and maintain reliable communication performance.

The optimization process of the ANN-MLP channel estimator is carried out during the training phase to determine the optimal weights and biases that minimize the channel estimation error. Initially, the Least Squares (LS) channel estimates obtained from pilot symbols are used as the input training data, while the corresponding true channel equalization coefficients serve as the target outputs. The complex channel values are separated into real and imaginary components, forming a () real-valued input vector for the neural network. During training, the parameters of the network are iteratively updated using the SGD optimization algorithm. The optimization objective is minimizing the Normalized Mean Square Error (NMSE). At each iteration, forward propagation computes the network output, while backpropagation calculates the gradient of the loss function with respect to the network parameters, enabling weight updates in the direction that reduces the estimation error. The convergence criterion is satisfied when the maximum number of training epochs is reached. Once convergence is achieved, the optimized weights are fixed and used during the testing phase to perform accurate channel equalization for the received PCC-OFDM symbols, thereby improving estimation accuracy and enhancing system reliability in dynamic wireless channels.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the remarkable potential of combining PCC-OFDM with neural network-based channel estimation for enhancing 5G uplink communications. By leveraging the ANN-MLP framework, the proposed system outperforms classical approaches including LMMSE, DNN, CNN, and GRU in terms of both accuracy and spectral efficiency. Figures highlight ANN-MLP’s superior NMSE performance across varying SNR levels and its ability to converge faster with reduced computational complexity. Furthermore, the spectral efficiency analysis underscores its adaptability, achieving consistent improvements with higher antenna configurations, ADC resolutions, and multiple data streams. These results validate the robustness of ANN-MLP in dynamic wireless environments, ensuring reliable communication links with minimal estimation errors. The efficiency of hybrid precoding methods with perfect and imperfect CSI was also examined, demonstrating ANN-MLP’s resilience across different configurations. Notably, the proposed system achieved a high spectral efficiency of 24.9 bps/Hz, outperforming other methods. Additionally, the ANN-MLP-based approach consistently delivered higher accuracy, reaching 98.1%, while avoiding overfitting during training. This study sets a solid foundation for future advancements in neural network-based 5G communication systems, emphasizing the importance of adaptability, accuracy, and computational efficiency in meeting modern wireless network demands.

Comment on “Factors associated with epileptiform discharges in patients visiting a memory clinic: A retrospective study”
Authors: Neeraj Singh, Monika Srivastav
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Cognitive fluctuation strongly enriches epileptiform discharge risk.•Interview signal useful but insufficient as a stand alone screen.•Left temporal predominance suggests early network irritability.•EEG remains essential in memory clinic cognitive workups.•Supports integrated dementia and seizure surveillance pathways.Keywords: Epileptiform discharges, Memory clinic, Cognitive fluctuation, Electroencephalography, Dementia screening

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the recent study by Yokoi and colleagues examining predictors of epileptiform discharges in patients evaluated at a memory clinic [1]. By systematically linking caregiver-reported clinical features with electroencephalographic findings, the authors provide timely data addressing a frequent diagnostic gray zone in older adults with cognitive complaints. The identification of cognitive variability as an independent correlate of epileptiform activity is particularly relevant for frontline cognitive neurology practice.

A key clinical contribution of the study lies in quantifying the enrichment associated with the interview item “cognitive decline with variability.” The observed difference between EEG positive and EEG negative groups, with an odds ratio of approximately four, moves this symptom from a descriptive observation into a potentially actionable triage signal. However, the Sankey analysis simultaneously shows that nearly one third of patients with epileptiform discharges lacked this interview feature. This internal distribution suggests that cognitive fluctuation should be interpreted as a probabilistic risk marker rather than a screening threshold [2]. In real world memory clinic workflows, overreliance on interview positivity alone could miss a clinically meaningful subset of electrophysiologically active patients.

The marked left temporal predominance of epileptiform discharges also warrants closer mechanistic consideration. Despite this focal pattern, global cognitive scores were similar between groups. This dissociation raises the possibility that network level hyperexcitability may precede measurable divergence on brief global metrics such as the MMSE [3]. A domain specific cognitive analysis within the existing cohort could help clarify whether temporal discharges preferentially map onto memory dominant phenotypes rather than diffuse impairment, which would strengthen the biologic coherence of the findings.

The sex distribution signal is another area that invites deeper interrogation. Although female predominance reached significance on univariate comparison, the effect attenuated in multivariable modeling. Given the advanced age of the cohort and the diagnostic mix, stratified analyses within Alzheimer disease versus non Alzheimer subgroups could help determine whether this pattern reflects underlying biology, referral dynamics, or population structure [4]. Such clarification would be important before attributing pathophysiologic meaning to the observed sex difference.

From a systems perspective, the study usefully challenges the conventional separation between dementia assessment and seizure surveillance. The detection of epileptiform discharges across both dementia and non dementia groups supports a network vulnerability framework for late life cognitive complaints. Importantly, the wide confidence interval surrounding loss of consciousness in the regression model highlights the relative instability of rarer semiologic features compared with the more robust cognitive fluctuation signal.

In summary, Yokoi and colleagues provide clinically grounded evidence that structured caregiver interviews can enrich detection of epileptiform activity in memory clinic populations while still underscoring the indispensable role of EEG. Prospective pathway studies that embed fluctuation focused screening into routine cognitive evaluation will be the critical next step to determine whether this strategy improves diagnostic efficiency and therapeutic targeting.

Systematic meta-review on migrant healthcare access: Language barriers and the role of translation
Authors: Mar Díaz-Millón, María Dolores Olvera-Lobo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Migrants face significant obstacles in accessing healthcare due to language barriers, which hinder communication with providers, limit understanding of medical instructions, and reduce trust in the healthcare system.•Translation services and cultural mediation are critical tools for overcoming communication challenges, yet they remain underutilized in many healthcare settings, contributing to ongoing disparities.•Limited access to healthcare due to language and cultural barriers leads to unmet medical needs, delayed treatments, and worsened health outcomes for migrant populations.•Migrant women face compounded barriers due to gendered healthcare disparities, cultural stigma, and socioeconomic vulnerabilities, further restricting their ability to receive adequate care.•The study calls for systemic changes, including the integration of professional translation services, training healthcare providers in cultural competency, and addressing economic barriers to improve healthcare accessibility for migrants.

Background

Migration is a global phenomenon that has significant repercussions for both migrants and host societies (Achotegui Loizate, 2009). According to the World Migration Report by the International Organization for Migration (IOM 2024), in 2020 > 280 million people (about 3.6 % of the population) in the world were considered migrants, i.e., people who were born in one country and reside in another country. The largest numbers of migrants reside in Europe and Asia (87 million each), followed by North America (59 million). The United States remains the top destination, hosting over 50 million migrants, while India, Mexico, and China lead as origin countries. For the highest income countries, between 2000 and 2020, the contribution of migration to population growth (with a net inflow of 80.5 million) exceeded the balance of births over deaths (66.2 million). The IOM also reports that, in the coming decades, in high-income countries the only engine of population growth will be migration. The reasons for migratory movements include refugee movements, conflicts, insecurity in the country of origin, temporary labor movements and forced displacement, with over 100 million people affected globally, driven by conflicts in Ukraine, Syria and Venezuela. It is not surprising, therefore, that the top ten destination countries have a remarkably high human development index (HDI) (USA, Germany, Saudi Arabia, Russia, United Kingdom, United Arab Emirates, France, Canada, Australia, and Spain). Women represent 48 % of migrants. Migrant workers make up about 69 % of international migrants, and global remittances reached an estimated USD 831 billion in 2022. Therefore, migration remains part of the solution for many economies, societies, and families around the world and must be ‘normalized.’ (IOM 2024) Migration is thus a global issue that needs to be addressed.

Migrants often do not speak the language of the country they move to and this creates new communications needs (Marnpae, 2020). Language and cultural barriers, among others (such as health literacy, unfamiliarity with health systems, and financial barriers) are obstacles that hinder the integration of migrants (IOM 2024, Kuan et al., 2020, Filmer et al., 2023). These barriers are also the main obstacle for migrants to obtain healthcare (Falla et al., 2017, León-Pinilla et al., 2016) and may complicate pre-existing limitations migrants face when accessing healthcare (IOM 2023). This undermining of migrants’ right to health involves ethical challenges, even more when public policies often leave the migrant population out of the health system (Abdulrazeq et al., 2024, Jesline et al., 2021). Hence, health and migration are interconnected worldwide. Migrants’ decisions to seek healthcare are influenced not only by individual factors, but also by structural barriers, including documentation status, discrimination, and systemic inaccessibility. There is frequently a mismatch between healthcare systems and migrants’ sociocultural contexts, underscoring the need for inclusive policies and culturally competent care (Yang and Hwang, 2026, Peng and Ling, 2023).

It is legitimate to question to what extent translation and related activities (interpreting, cultural mediation, transcreation, localization, hereafter referred to as translation) can contribute to strengthening the rights of disadvantaged groups (O’Donnell, 2018). In healthcare settings, translation has shown to be a means to improve patient safety, quality of care, ethical medical practices, and patient health outcomes (Falla et al., 2017, Garnweidner-Holme et al., 2017). The absence of professional translation has a negative impact on patients’ health, as it limits their understanding of their diagnosis and treatment and reduces their decision-making capacity (Plaza del Pino and Veiga, 2014, Rubio-Rico et al., 2014). In healthcare research, the use of translators and interpreters in cross-cultural qualitative research is inconsistently documented and not standardized (Shimpuku and Norr, 2012), suggesting that translation’s impact on healthcare is rarely considered. However, translators play a pivotal but complex role in intercultural mediation (Taibi, 2023).

In recent years a trend has emerged known as transcreation (Díaz-Millón and Olvera-Lobo, 2023). This is a creative reinterpretation of the contents of a text so that they are received in the same way by an audience with cultural and linguistic characteristics different from the original. Transcreation has been explored as a way to adapt health information materials for people speaking different languages (Nápoles and Stewart, 2018, Santoyo-Olsson et al., 2019). It is worth dedicating a few lines to drawing the differences between transcreation and other activities in its boundaries. Translation involves rendering written text from one language to another with a focus on semantic fidelity (Venuti, 2008), while interpreting is an oral or sign-language equivalent done in real-time, emphasizing immediacy and clarity (Schjoldager, 2008). Cultural mediation typically involves finding the connection points and ‘decoding tools’ that bridge the gap between two cultures (European Union 2014). Adaptation is a dynamic, context-specific process by which immigrants interact with and adjust to a new cultural environment without necessarily relinquishing their own identity, different from accommodation, a communicative strategy where individuals adjust their behavior or language to reduce social distance without fully changing their cultural identity, and from assimilation, a unidirectional, often coercive process where immigrants are expected to shed their original cultural identity to conform to the dominant society’s norms (De La Garza and Ono, 2015). In contrast, transcreation blends translation and creative writing to preserve the emotional and cultural impact of the source (Díaz-Millón and Olvera-Lobo, 2023)

Translation and transcreation are therefore fundamental strategies for effective communication in multilingual and multicultural environments. However, different studies have found a generalized absence of these services in healthcare contexts (Falla et al., 2017, León-Pinilla et al., 2016, Plaza del Pino and Veiga, 2014).

To answer these questions, this study proposes a literature meta-review, or umbrella review, that is, a review of scientific literature reviews that address the object of study (Grant and Booth, 2009). The application of this methodology aims to build a broad source of peer-reviewed scientific knowledge, encompassing the perspectives of authors who have conducted previous reviews, in terms of types of barriers, population groups, destination and origin countries, as well as the role of translation in migrants’ access to healthcare.

Methodology

Search strategy

This meta-review followed the framework of the PRISMA 2020 statement, an update of the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) statement, published in 2009 (Page et al., 2021) throughout the review process, including planning, study selection, and data extraction. Web of Science and Scopus were used to find relevant articles, as both offer broad coverage in multidisciplinary areas. Searches were conducted between May and June 2024, using the following search equation applied to titles, abstracts, and keywords:[(emigrant* OR emigration) OR (immigrant* OR immigration) OR (migrant* OR migration) OR refugee*] AND [health OR (healthcare OR health care) OR "health services"] AND ["language barrier" OR "cultural barriers"] AND [(translation OR translator) OR (interpreting OR interpreter) OR (transcreation OR transcreator)]

Inclusion and exclusion criteria. Analysis sample selection

The initial search yielded a total of 694 results (364 from Web of Science and 330 from Scopus). Relevant data, such as title, abstract, keywords, authors’ names and affiliations, journal name and year of publication, were exported to a spreadsheet for further analysis.

During the screening process, the study titles and abstracts were reviewed, and additional information needed for data management was collected. This included the bibliographic details of the selected reviews, as well as the key items of the PRISMA checklist. Some PRISMA items, such as those related to risk of bias, confidence intervals, consistency measures, sensitivity or subgroup analyses, and the combination of meta-analysis results (items 12–16, 19, 20, 22 and 23), were not considered as they were not applicable to the type of documents included in our research, i.e., reviews of literature.

Subsequently, duplicate records were eliminated, reducing the number of studies to 460. Next, the records were screened. Through this process, only those studies classified as reviews of literature were selected, rendering a final sample of 70 studies.

In the next stage, only reviews with full-text access were included, reducing the sample to 46 publications. Finally, 8 additional reviews were excluded as they were not in English (6 in German, 1 in French and 1 in Croatian). Thus, the final sample consisted of 38 literature reviews as captured in Fig. 1, which outlines the entire selection process.Fig. 1Sample selection following PRISMA guidelines.Fig. 1:

Sample selection following PRISMA guidelines.

The screening process was conducted independently by two researchers. Any discrepancies were solved through discussion until consensus was reached.

Data analysis

Before evaluating the content of the texts, a preliminary descriptive analysis was performed. During this stage, relevant bibliographic data were extracted, including the publication date, the country of affiliation of the first author, the journal of publication, the years covered by the reviews, the research methods used, the sources and databases consulted, as well as the destination and origin countries identified in the studies. To contextualize the interdisciplinary nature of this research, the Journal Citation Reports (JCR) categories were reported. These classifications provide insight into the disciplinary origins and orientations of the literature and provides context on the approaches from which different disciplines understand the problem.

For the thematic analysis, the qualitative analysis software NVivo was used, specifically its automatic coding function, which identified word patterns and content categories. The main thematic nodes identified by the software were critically evaluated and refined through manual coding, where researchers reviewed, grouped, and re-labeled codes to form broader thematic categories aligned with the research objectives. This approach made it possible to identify the main themes around which the analyzed reviews are structured. These were then subjected to a manual categorization process, where codes were compared, merged, or excluded based on frequency, contextual relevance, and theoretical alignment. This iterative process, which involved constant reflection on the contents covered in the scientific literature, made it possible to capture the complexity and richness of the ideas present in the reviews.

Thematic analysis

The thematic analysis made it possible to identify four main themes that recur with greater frequency: 1) access to healthcare, 2) translation, 3) impact on migrant health and 4) migrant women. These themes have been ordered from the highest to the lowest number of textual references in the reviews. Each of the themes encompass several sub-themes that reveal the multiple barriers and challenges faced by the migrant population in relation to their access to healthcare services. Access to healthcare

Several of the analyzed publications agree that migrants seek healthcare less frequently than the native population. Although multiple barriers are identified, there is no doubt that the primary one is linguistic (Alsharaydeh et al., 2019, Barrio-Ruiz et al., 2023, Cheng et al., 2015, De Freitas et al., 2020, Fair et al., 2020, Gerchow et al., 2021, Giammusso et al., 2018, Krystallidou et al., 2024, Phung et al., 2020, Rasi, 2020, Rousseau and Frounfelker, 2019, Sawadogo et al., 2023, Terui, 2017, Thonon et al., 2021, Vange et al., 2023). When patients and healthcare professionals don’t share a common language, it can lead to less use of healthcare services, misdiagnoses, improper treatment, poor treatment adherence, safety risks, unnecessary use of social services, wasted resources, higher medical costs, more or longer hospital visits, patients returning to their home country for care, lower satisfaction and lower ability to make informed decisions. Healthcare professionals claim that language barriers reduce patients’ trust in the healthcare system and limit their knowledge about it (Barrio-Ruiz et al., 2023, De Freitas et al., 2020, Fair et al., 2020, Gerchow et al., 2021, Sawadogo et al., 2023, Robertshaw et al., 2017). Studies comparing healthcare access for migrants in cases where there is language concordance (i.e., patient and professional speak the same language) with cases where there is not, conclude that health outcomes for migrants were more positive when concordance existed (Krystallidou et al., 2024, Diamond et al., 2019).

Likewise, cultural barriers hinder access to healthcare. In some cases, healthcare professionals admit to lacking cultural sensitivity (or even holding xenophobic attitudes) which, combined with cultural differences, can harm communication (Gerchow et al., 2021, Krystallidou et al., 2024). These clashes include different conceptions of medicine (e.g., curative medicine vs. preventive medicine), different expectations of the healthcare system, cultural stigmatization of certain diseases, controversial or illegal traditional practices in the destination country (such as female genital mutilation), the impact of religious beliefs, or stereotyping of certain population groups (Barrio-Ruiz et al., 2023, Fair et al., 2020, Phung et al., 2020, Sawadogo et al., 2023, Clough et al., 2013). In particularly sensitive matters, such as sexual and reproductive health, the imposition of the destination country’s cultural values is also a barrier. Therefore, cultural sensitivity, along with professional and respectful treatment, is of paramount importance (Barrio-Ruiz et al., 2023, Robertshaw et al., 2017).

Another barrier to healthcare access is discrimination against migrants (Barrio-Ruiz et al., 2023, Clough et al., 2013). Evidence shows that discrimination negatively affects migrants’ health both indirectly (through its impact on socioeconomic conditions) and directly (through physical assaults or stress-related effects on the body and mental health) (Giammusso et al., 2018, Clough et al., 2013). In the case of refugees, the asylum application process itself has been identified as a factor that deteriorates health (Robertshaw et al., 2017).

Other barriers to healthcare access include economic factors. Particularly in countries without universal healthcare, the elevated costs lead migrants to seek assistance less frequently (Fair et al., 2020, Sawadogo et al., 2023, Joshi et al., 2013). In the case of refugees and asylum seekers, the limited time available to address these patients and their specific needs is also a barrier to healthcare (Fair et al., 2020, Rousseau and Frounfelker, 2019, Robertshaw et al., 2017).

Translation

The role of translation as a tool to overcome barriers to healthcare access for migrants is a common theme in the analyzed publications. Although ad hoc or informal interpreting (by friends or family members) is recommended by some authors (Krystallidou et al., 2024, Vange et al., 2023), other studies argue that this practice can harm communication (Brandl et al., 2019) and that the quality of medical care improves drastically with the use of professional interpreters (Rasi, 2020, Silva et al., 2016, Heath et al., 2023, Kwan et al., 2023). Using professional interpreters helps shorten consultation times, improves patients’ understanding of treatments, making them more effective, and reduces the number of visits to healthcare facilities (Kwan et al., 2023). However, international law does not specifically guarantee the right to a translator or interpreter in these contexts, and it is up to the healthcare professional to decide whether to use one (Dalton-Oates, 2017). Access to medical translation is often limited due to issues like lack of on-demand interpreters, restricted service hours, a mismatch between language needs and available interpreters, healthcare professionals not knowing the service exists, added costs, some professionals’ unwillingness to use it, and patients’ concerns about interpreter confidentiality (Vange et al., 2023).

Cost is a specific barrier frequently mentioned in literature regarding the use of medical translation. However, while it is true that professional interpreters add an extra cost to the healthcare system, the benefits of using professional interpreters outweigh the costs (Brandl et al., 2019). The effectiveness of all interpreting modalities, whether in-person or remote, is noted. For example, during the COVID-19 pandemic, remote interpreting provided a means to reduce the risk of infection by avoiding direct contact between interpreters, patients, and healthcare professionals (Saeki et al., 2022, Yang et al., 2023). As a disadvantage, this modality also involved communication issues, challenges in building relationships, and technical and operational problems, in line with other research findings showing patients’ preference for in-person interpreters (Krystallidou et al., 2024).

Other research highlights the general lack of integration of medical interpreting services (Giammusso et al., 2018) and points out that healthcare professionals and interpreters are rarely trained to work together, which can negatively affect patients’ health (Huang et al., 2019). This often leads healthcare professionals to resort to other means of communication or informal interpreters (Gerchow et al., 2021).

Other publications point to the role of translation in other aspects of healthcare, such as conducting research and clinical studies (Al-Amer et al., 2014, Squires et al., 2020). In these types of studies, language barriers impact data collection and analysis, as a deep understanding of the language is necessary to obtain accurate data, both in quantitative research (Squires et al., 2020) and in qualitative research, where it is crucial for participants to narrate and express their experiences freely (Al-Amer et al., 2014). Not having interpreting services can result in distorted data and, consequently, the results of these studies could have a poor or even harmful impact (Al-Amer et al., 2014).

Indeed, the prevalence of cardiovascular diseases is notably higher among the migrant population compared to the native population. Risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, and high cholesterol are more common in migrants (Tharmaratnam et al., 2018). The lack of continuity in treatment contributes to the worsening of these conditions. Mental health is another critical area that is severely affected in the migrant population (Giammusso et al., 2018, Krystallidou et al., 2024, Rousseau and Frounfelker, 2019, Giacco et al., 2014), who suffers higher incidence of disorders such as anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).

Regarding sexual, reproductive and maternal health, recent studies (Barrio-Ruiz et al., 2023, Sawadogo et al., 2023) show that the lack of accessible and culturally appropriate information contributes to a higher incidence of unintended pregnancies and complications during pregnancy and childbirth. Cultural barriers prevent women from feeling comfortable discussing sexual health issues with healthcare professionals, worsening reproductive health problems. Migrant women are at greater risk of complications during pregnancy and childbirth (De Freitas et al., 2020, Thonon et al., 2021, Chasan-Taber, 2012). These complications include gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, and low birth weight.

Public health has also been the subject of study, with research highlighting how the lack of access to preventive services, such as vaccination and communicable disease control, puts both migrants and the general population at risk (Rousseau and Frounfelker, 2019, Thonon et al., 2021, Barreto, 2024).

Finally, other health conditions disproportionately affect migrants. For example, hepatitis C is more prevalent among migrants compared to the native population, largely due to the lack of access to early detection and treatment programs (Greenaway et al., 2018).

Migrant women

Migrant women face greater inequalities in accessing healthcare compared to migrant men (Kassam et al., 2022). This situation is reflected in the limited information that pregnant migrant women receive when they arrive in the Global North (Europe, North America, Australia), where healthcare systems often fail to adequately address their specific needs.

Healthcare staff often have limited knowledge and are influenced by biases regarding the needs of migrant women, resulting in poor healthcare (De Freitas et al., 2020, Fair et al., 2020). These women frequently do not receive the necessary information to make informed decisions about their reproductive health, and, even when information is provided, it is often incomprehensible or conflicts with their cultural values. Healthcare professionals’ attitudes toward certain traditional practices also play a crucial role in their access to care. The rejection or lack of understanding of these practices creates a divide between migrant women and the healthcare system (De Freitas et al., 2020). This attitude leads migrant women to distrust the system and avoid seeking medical care.

Migrant women are at higher risk of developing type II diabetes during pregnancy (Chasan-Taber, 2012). Additionally, these women tend to take supplements like folic acid less frequently than native women, increasing the risk of pregnancy complications (Fair et al., 2020). Obstetric trauma is also more common among migrant women, who often lack access to high-quality maternal healthcare, resulting in a higher risk of complications during childbirth and postpartum (Thonon et al., 2021). Overall, these women experience worse health outcomes throughout the reproductive cycle, reflecting a lack of equitable access to healthcare (Fair et al., 2020). Furthermore, the impact of social isolation also contributes to the deterioration of migrant women’s mental health (Kassam et al., 2022).

Discussion

Research on barriers to healthcare access for migrants has proven to be a growing area of interest over the past two decades. This is reflected in the 38 reviews included in this meta-review, which cover research published over a 60-year period (1964–2024). The qualitative analysis identified several key themes that are recurrent in research. These include the barriers themselves, the role of translation in overcoming them, the negative health consequences for migrants, and the challenges migrant women face.

One of the main findings of this meta-review is that the barriers migrants face in accessing healthcare services are diverse. The analysis confirms that these barriers are cross-cutting, affecting migrants regardless of their country of origin or destination. The most prevalent barriers are linguistic and cultural, but significant economic barriers were also identified, reinforcing findings from previous studies (Marnpae, 2020, Kuan et al., 2020, O’Donnell, 2018). Migrants often lack access to information in a language they understand, which limits their ability to access healthcare and follow treatments. Cultural differences between patients and healthcare professionals, along with limited resources, also make it harder for migrants to receive care equal to that of the native population, posing ethical challenges and creating social injustice (Abdulrazeq et al., 2024, Jesline et al., 2021). This finding answers the first research question posed in this study: How do language and cultural barriers affect migrants’ access to healthcare?

The impact of these barriers is profound and affects multiple aspects of migrants’ health. Language and cultural barriers do not only limit access to healthcare but also hinder the understanding of diagnoses and treatments, aligning with previous research pointing to a higher risk of health problems among migrants (Kuan et al., 2020, O’Donnell, 2018, Plaza del Pino and Veiga, 2014). The consequences are even more severe for migrant women, who face additional barriers. This exacerbates complications during pregnancy and childbirth and increases the risk of developing reproductive health problems (De Freitas et al., 2020, Fair et al., 2020, Thonon et al., 2021). These findings answer the second research question: How do these barriers affect the health of migrants?

This meta-review has allowed for the identification and synthesis of a series of key recommendations to improve healthcare access for migrants and to implement translation and interpreting in healthcare settings. These recommendations are grouped into three main categories: communicative, cultural, and policy related. Each category offers practical solutions. Recommendations for healthcare access promotion for migrants.

This meta-review demonstrates that translation is a significant tool that helps migrants access the healthcare system. Whether through translation or interpreting, professional or informal, in-person or remote, most of the reviews analyzed explicitly or implicitly conclude that translation is needed as a communication and cultural mediation tool. Despite this, the use of professional translation services remains limited, as reported in previous literature (Kuan et al., 2020, Falla et al., 2017, León-Pinilla et al., 2016, Plaza del Pino and Veiga, 2014, Rubio-Rico et al., 2014). Several barriers contribute to this underutilization, including high costs, logistical challenges, lack of coordination between healthcare providers and language service professionals, and patient mistrust or discomfort with interpreters. In some cases, healthcare providers may rely on ad hoc solutions due to time constraints or the perceived complexity of arranging formal interpreting. To address these issues, the literature recommends expanding the availability and accessibility of professional translation services. Suggested strategies include integrating interpreters more formally into healthcare teams, providing targeted training for healthcare providers on how to work effectively with interpreters, and allocating dedicated funding to support language services. These measures would help ensure equitable access to care and improve health outcomes for migrant communities, in line with previous scientific literature (Falla et al., 2017, Garnweidner-Holme et al., 2017). These findings answer the third research question: Can translation help to overcome these barriers?

In addition to linguistic support, cultural mediation plays a critical role in overcoming communication barriers between healthcare providers and migrant patients. Cultural competency and cultural humility are essential components of effective healthcare delivery. Cultural competency involves acquiring knowledge about different cultural practices, health beliefs, and values, while cultural humility emphasizes a continuous process of self-reflection and openness to understanding the patient’s cultural perspective. These approaches enable healthcare professionals to recognize and navigate potential misunderstandings, build trust, and deliver care that respects patients’ cultural backgrounds. Several studies highlight that misalignments in expectations and interpretations of symptoms or treatments often stem from cultural differences rather than language alone (Gerchow et al., 2021, Krystallidou et al., 2024). Therefore, training healthcare professionals in both cultural competence and humility (alongside access to interpreters) can significantly improve communication. Institutional policies that support culturally responsive care, including the involvement of trained cultural mediators, are recommended to create more inclusive and equitable healthcare environments for migrant populations.

Enhancing healthcare access for migrants requires inclusive and proactive policies. Developing comprehensive healthcare policies and social support programs for vulnerable populations is fundamental, as well as eliminating costs for essential services. Research shows that informative campaigns and health education initiatives empower migrants to engage with preventive care. Engaging cultural leaders fosters trust, while language policies aid integration. Guidelines should specifically address the needs of migrants, including longer consultation times, flexible hours, and resource-sharing networks.

While many of the identified barriers and the importance of translation have been previously recognized in the literature, this meta-review adds value by synthesizing six decades of research and demonstrating how these themes consistently recur across varied contexts and migrant populations. By integrating both structural and communicative dimensions, the review highlights the persistent and systemic nature of access challenges, as well as the underutilized potential of translation as a tool of cultural mediation. This broader temporal and thematic scope provides a more nuanced understanding of how translation is positioned as a technical solution and as a social and relational practice. These insights may inform future policy and practice by emphasizing the need for better integration of professional translation services as part of inclusive, equitable healthcare systems.

Strengths, limitations and future research directions

A meta-review of literature, by focusing exclusively on previous reviews, has key methodological strengths that increase the robustness of its findings. These include the ability to synthesize a considerable volume of evidence in a structured way, allowing for a panoramic view of the accumulated knowledge in each field. This type of review applies to stricter selection criteria and rigorous methodological standards, which reduces the possibility of individual biases associated with primary studies. Furthermore, by focusing on already peer-reviewed reviews, a meta-review tends to benefit indirectly from the methodological scrutiny applied at earlier stages, thus improving the internal validity of its conclusions.

While this study presents solid and consistent results, some limitations must be noted. The reviews included were thematically organized using automatic coding in NVivo, which helps reduce researcher bias, although it does not eliminate it entirely. Additionally, some of the reviews analyzed were narrative in nature and did not always follow an explicit method, which could introduce certain biases. Regarding thematic limitations, this meta-review has not addressed legal and policy frameworks governing the use of translation and interpreting services. It is important to acknowledge that these frameworks vary significantly across countries and can substantially influence how such services are implemented in healthcare settings. Nevertheless, many of the included reviews did not systematically address country-specific legal mandates, which is a limitation to make substantiated comparisons across legal systems. These aspects can be considered in future research lines.

Recommendations for the implementation of translation in healthcare.

In conclusion, translation and related services are essential for ensuring migrants can access healthcare. These recommendations help reduce inequalities and greatly improve communication between patients and healthcare professionals. However, for translation to be truly effective, it must be part of a broader strategy that includes cultural competency training and adapts healthcare services to the specific needs of migrant communities, rather than being treated as a stand-alone solution. The recent growth in academic research on this topic underscores the urgency of continuing to explore and propose solutions to overcome language and cultural barriers. These barriers are not incidental but structural, requiring a deep transformation in healthcare-related policies. Translation and cultural mediation must be integrated as key pillars of public health policies.

This study has shown that the absence of professional translation services and the lack of cultural awareness perpetuate inequalities and put the health and well-being of migrants at risk. It is not just about providing access but about ensuring that access is equitable.

Healthcare systems must move toward a more inclusive model, where care is accessible and high-quality for all, regardless of language or culture. This approach promotes a fairer, more inclusive healthcare system and helps optimize resources. The way forward is clear: healthcare that is inclusive, equitable, and culturally competent. Funding

Performance evaluation and analysis of Chinese insurance companies under uncertain financial markets
Authors: Jiang Jia-Ying, LingJing Zhou, Wenxuan Shen, Liao Xiuli, Jin Huizhen, Pan Wentsao
Published: 2023

Introduction

In China, the management of risks and resources is greatly influenced by the insurance sector, which is an important part of the nation's financial ecosystem. Its transformation from a relatively undeveloped market to a potent and vibrant financial industry mirrors the swift expansion of the country's economy [1]. As China maintains its status as a worldwide economic powerhouse, the insurance sector is becoming more and more important in risk management and safeguarding the enormous amount of resources and assets that are located there. In addition to offering financial protection, insurance firms play a crucial role as intermediaries in the financial system's resiliency and general stability.

However, this transformation has not taken place independently of the intricate and unpredictably changing financial landscape throughout the world. The industry operates in a multifaceted environment marked by economic fluctuations, shifts in regulations, and the unforeseen impacts of events such as the COVID-19 pandemic [1]. Chinese insurance businesses are under continual pressure to adapt, innovate, and efficiently manage risk as a result of the interaction of internal and external variables [2]. Performance evaluation turns into a crucial instrument to assess these insurance companies' effectiveness and adaptability in tumultuous times. The capacity of these insurance organizations to grow and remain competitive in this climate is a matter of important concern.

Numerous uncertainties and hazards have recently had a considerable influence on the global economic environment, notably the financial markets. Global enterprises have faced significant obstacles as a result of the COVID-19 epidemic, ongoing regional wars, and financial markets' inherent instability [2]. As the insurance sector in China grows and transforms, it is more crucial than ever to understand the dynamics of its performance in the face of volatile financial markets. With a focus on PICC in particular, this research study intends to give an in-depth investigation of the performance evaluation of Chinese insurance firms in light of these uncertainties.

Background of the study

The modern Chinese insurance sector was born with the founding of the People's Insurance Company of China in 1949. The business went through phases of expansion and change over the ensuing decades, but it wasn't until China restarted full-scale insurance operations in 1980 that it underwent continuous growth [3]. The market size, financial position, regulatory framework, and institutional complexity of China's insurance industry all increased. In the country's capital markets, Chinese insurance firms rose to prominence as investors. The industry's growth trajectory has, however, been affected by changes in China's economic climate, the turbulence of the financial markets, and the changing business environment, which includes the persistent difficulties posed by epidemics [4]. These difficulties have put strain on insurance budgets, impacted sales, and demanded product innovation, exposing both weaknesses and potential for Chinese insurance businesses.

The present state of the Chinese insurance sector is characterized by growth and escalating competition. Chinese insurance businesses must increase their competitiveness in this intensely competitive climate and effectively evaluate their performance [4]. It is crucial to assess and thoroughly examine each domestic insurance company's progress. Additionally, this research should take into account effective strategies both inside and outside of China's boundaries, ensuring a full review of the financial performance inside the Chinese insurance market and providing suggestions for long-term growth.

The PICC's success has been attributed to its commitment to providing specialist insurance solutions and its focus on client satisfaction ever since the company's foundation. The Chinese insurance business has earned a reputation for being dependable and attentive, working to give clients financial stability and peace of mind [5]. This focus on the needs of the consumer has greatly contributed to PICC's development and its capacity to keep a base of devoted patrons. Additionally, People's Insurance Company has demonstrated a dedication to invention and technical development. PICC has made investments in cutting-edge technology in an era where the insurance sector is becoming more digital in order to streamline its operations and enhance the client experience [3]. The business has been able to remain competitive and relevant in an industry that is continually changing because to its forward-thinking strategy.

Additionally, the complex and unpredictably changing global financial landscape has not been isolated from the development of the Chinese insurance market. Since the Covid-19 outbreak, the sector has been functioning in a complex environment characterized by shifting economic conditions, regulatory changes, and the unanticipated effects of events like the COVID-19 pandemic [5]. The Chinese insurance industry currently has a complex relationship with the global financial system and is not just a mirror of domestic economic development. Chinese insurance businesses are always under pressure to adapt, innovate, and manage risk efficiently as a result of the interaction of domestic and foreign variables. Performance evaluation becomes a crucial instrument to assess these insurance companies' efficiency and flexibility in tumultuous times since it is very important that they flourish and remain competitive in this climate.

Literature review

Introduction

In the financial literature, performance appraisal of insurance businesses has frequently come up. Researchers have used a variety of techniques to evaluate the efficacy and efficiency of Chinese insurance companies [6]. Appraising the competitiveness and sustainability of the insurance business is crucial, especially in light of the volatile financial markets. Using a two-stage network Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) model is an accurate but a challenging way to evaluate the financial performance of insurance companies, especially in the dynamic and uncertain financial market environment. This model enhances the traditional single-stage DEA model by segmenting the production process, allowing for a more thorough review. The inclusion of several inputs and outputs, accounting for the diversity of insurance operations, such as human capital, total assets, premium revenue, investment income, and energy consumption, ensures a thorough evaluation of financial efficiency.

Two-stage network DEA

The effectiveness with which Chinese insurance companies like PICC transition from the first stage of resource allocation to the second stage of output production is evaluated using the DEA Model, taking into account the impact of undesirable outputs and the inherent risks associated with insurance operations in unstable financial markets as shown in Fig. 1.

Diagram of two-stage network DEA

Inputs (Stage 1): represents how PICC allocates its resources, for example, capital, human resources, and technology.

Outputs (Stage 2): These represent the results and performance of the PICC. This includes premium income, efficient underwriting, risk management, and effective claims processing.

Undesirable Outputs (Stage 2): These are outputs that are not desirable for the company, such as non-performing policies or underwriting losses. It's crucial to consider these in the assessment.

Efficiency Score: The Two-Stage Network DEA model calculates an efficiency score that indicates how effectively the company transforms inputs (resources) into outputs and handles undesirable outputs.

Optimal Frontier: The model establishes an optimal efficiency frontier, and companies strive to operate as close to this frontier as possible to maximize their efficiency.

This model may be used to pinpoint areas where PICC can increase its efficiency in resource allocation, underwriting, risk management, and claims processing while taking the intricacies of erratic financial markets into account. The use of the Two-Stage Network DEA in this situation enables China Pacific Insurance Company to improve performance, adjust to market uncertainties, and make sure that resources and risks are efficiently managed across all of their businesses.

Impact of uncertainty and risk on the operational performance of PICC

There has been a great deal of study and analysis done on how risk and uncertainty affect the operational performance of insurance businesses like People's Insurance Company (PICC). The current literature's core themes and insights offer light on PICC's prospects and difficulties by highlighting a number of important issues. First off, PICC works in a sector that is particularly vulnerable to changes in the macroeconomy and the stock market. The industry's vulnerability has been highlighted by the COVID-19 outbreak and its aftermath. Researchers like Niu [4], have conducted studies on the financial performance of insurance businesses, highlighting the demand for early warning systems and improved financial stability. PICC must have sophisticated risk management processes and strategic resilience since operational performance is closely related to its capacity to respond to new uncertainties.

Furthermore, it has been found that risk and uncertainty have an impact on a variety of operational performance factors. According to Tan et al.'s (2021) examination of social insurance operations, participation rates, fund revenue, and fund expenditure all have a big influence on sustainability and efficacy. PICC's ability to manage the complexity of governmental rules and market circumstances while ensuring that its financial operations remain stable and sustainable determines how well it performs operationally.

The study by Zhao et al. [6] places a strong emphasis on the application of network DEA models to categorize the effectiveness of financial institutions, giving PICC the tools required to evaluate and effectively improve its operational performance.

The present study on how risk and uncertainty affect insurance businesses' operational performance, in particular People's Insurance Company (PICC), highlights the need for strict risk management guidelines, proactive early warning systems, and sound operating strategies. PICC operates in a volatile and uncertain market, thus resolving these problems is essential to keeping it competitive and long-term viable.

Research methodology

The efficiency of the homogeneous Decision Making Unit (DMU) in PICC will be evaluated in this study using the Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) approach. In the first phase of this procedure, the People's Insurance Company (PICC)'s management will be contacted to get the necessary financial data. The dataset will cover a wide time range, including periods when the financial markets were unsteady, in order to provide an in-depth analysis. Data sources will include market data, annual reports, and financial statements. This method will not only offer a snapshot of the financial efficiency in the present but also detect trends and patterns, which are essential for comprehending how insurance businesses adjust and react to the always-changing financial market dynamics. Nevertheless, since the validity of results depends on the accuracy of the input data and the model's assumptions, the application of such a complex model calls for thorough validation and sensitivity analysis, and the selection of specific time-series strategies should be justifiably tailored to the research objectives and the nature of the data in order to derive insightful conclusions.

Uncertainty analysis

The research will include methods to gauge and account for market volatility given the emphasis on uncertain financial markets. To quantify financial market volatility and its influence on the performance of insurance businesses, this would need to undertake scenario analysis or use econometric models like GARCH (Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity). Understanding how PICC and other insurers respond to shifting market conditions will be possible thanks to this study.

Empirical study

The empirical analysis involves applying the two-stage network DEA model to the collected financial data to evaluate the performance of PICC and other selected insurance companies. The efficiency scores for each company should be calculated and compared to understand how they perform relative to each other under uncertain financial market conditions. Empirical analysis

The empirical analysis in this study employs a two-stage network Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) model to evaluate the efficiency of selected Chinese insurance companies (PICC) over the course of a decade. By taking into account a variety of input and output criteria, such as human capital, total assets, premium revenue, investment income, and energy consumption, the model offers a thorough evaluation of the financial performance of these businesses (see Fig. 2 below). The two-stage DEA model is particularly useful since it examines the correlations between input and output parameters in multi-stage production processes while taking into consideration the internal organization of decision-making units (DMUs). As a result, it offers a more thorough and nuanced efficiency evaluation, taking into account the interaction between two sub-processes. The insurance sector's ability to respond to external factors is improved, input and output indicators become more reasonable, and future studies become more trustworthy.

Conclusion and recommendations

Conclusion

In this setting of unstable financial markets, the research study has examined the performance assessment of the Chinese insurance sector, with a focus on People's Insurance Company (PICC). A two-stage network Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) model was used in the study to thoroughly evaluate the effectiveness of PICC and other insurance providers. It also looked at how risk and ambiguity affected PICC's operational performance over a ten-year period.

The empirical investigation demonstrated variations in PICC's efficiency rankings over time, reflecting the industry's dynamism. These results highlight the necessity for customized and focused adjustments in particular sub-stages to increase efficiency. Additionally, it was clear from the research of how uncertainty and risk affected PICC's operational performance and how external influences impacted the company's capacity to run its operations effectively.

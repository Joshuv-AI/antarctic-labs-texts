Electrophysiological characterization of the state-dependent inhibition of Kv7.1 and IKs by UCL2077
Authors: Daniel Sastre, Efthimios Kyriakis, Julia Schauer, Magnus Chan, Ying Dou, Jodene Eldstrom, David Fedida
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In cardiomyocytes, Kv7.1 associates with the regulatory subunit KCNE1 to generate the delayed rectifier potassium current IKs, which plays a crucial role in cardiac repolarization at elevated heart rates. Gain-of-function mutations in either of these subunits are associated with short QT syndrome, a condition that increases the risk of cardiac arrhythmias including atrial fibrillation, syncope, and sudden death. Therefore, the study of pharmacological inhibitors of Kv7.1 and IKs is of significant therapeutic interest. In this work, we used whole-cell patch clamp recordings to characterize the electrophysiological effects of the Kv7.1 blocker 3-(triphenylmethylaminomethyl)pyridine (UCL2077) in both Kv7.1 and IKs (Kv7.1 + KCNE1) channels. We found that UCL2077 inhibited both Kv7.1 and IKs channels with high affinity (IC50 in the picomolar range) and mild voltage dependence. The drug induced a biphasic time-dependent current decay and reduced current reactivation of Kv7.1, whereas the kinetics of IKs were unaffected. We examined state dependence using mutations that functionally stabilize Kv7.1/IKs in either the intermediate-open (E160R/R231E) or in the activated-open (E160R/R237E) state. In both channels, UCL2077 potency correlated with the strength of the electromechanical coupling. Our results are further supported by a kinetic Markov model simulating UCL2077 binding that closely resembles the experimental currents. Overall, our work provides an in-depth characterization of UCL2077’s action on Kv7.1 and IKs channels, offering valuable insights for the development of Kv7.1/IKs inhibitors in the context of short QT syndrome and other cardiac arrhythmias.Significance StatementThis study characterizes UCL2077: a highly specific, high-affinity inhibitor of Kv7.1 and IKs channels with therapeutic implications for cardiac arrhythmia. Our work reveals a picomolar affinity, mild voltage dependence, and a kinetic modulation consistent with state-dependent open-channel block.

Introduction

The voltage-gated potassium ion channel Kv7.1 (also known as KCNQ1) is expressed throughout the body, with important roles in cardiac repolarization and epithelial function.1, 2, 3 Kv7.1 is most studied in the context of cardiac physiology, where it associates with the regulatory subunit KCNE1 to form the slow delayed rectifier current IKs, which is crucial for the repolarization phase of the cardiac action potential.4,5 Loss-of-function mutations in Kv7.1 comprise the most common cause of congenital long QT syndrome (LQTS),6,7 a disorder affecting 1:2500 births8 with a ∼55% female preponderance9,10 characterized by a prolonged QT interval. Clinically, LQTS manifests with sudden cardiac death, syncope, ventricular fibrillation, and torsades de pointes.11 Conversely, gain-of-function mutations in Kv7.1 cause short QT syndrome (SQTS),11, 12, 13 a rare congenital disease with a ∼70% male preponderance10 associated with a higher risk of cardiac arrest, syncope, palpitations, atrial fibrillation, and sudden cardiac death.11 Current LQTS and SQTS treatments do not offer complete protection against the most severe symptoms,14 driving a huge interest in understanding the gating, modulation, and the pharmacology of cardiac ion channels such as Kv7.1 and IKs.

Kv7.1 currents have fast kinetics of activation and deactivation, and undergo an almost “hidden” inactivation mechanism that is revealed by the presence of a hook on the tail currents (see control traces in Fig. 1).4 This behavior is thought to arise from a distinctive property of Kv7.1: the presence of 2 stable and functionally distinct open states,15, 16, 17 which reflect different stages along the voltage sensor activation pathway. Upon depolarization, the voltage sensor moves from the resting to an intermediate state, translocating most of the gating charge and promoting pore opening into the intermediate-open (IO) state. Small protein rearrangements complete the voltage sensor transition toward the activated state, leading to the activated-open (AO) state.15,18,19 The coupling between voltage sensor position and pore opening, known as “electromechanical coupling,” is stronger in the IO state (higher open probability) compared with the AO state.18 As a result, the transition from AO back to IO leads to the characteristic “hook” in the tail currents, a feature that sets Kv7.1 apart from other Kv channels. Importantly, the IO and AO states are thought to represent structurally different pore conformations, as reflected by the distinct physiological properties of each state: in comparison to the IO state, the AO state has a reduced Rb+/K+ permeability ratio,15 a decreased sensitivity to the inhibitor XE991,15 a higher tail current potentiation by ML277,20 and an increased affinity for Na+-mediated block.16 In this context, the pharmacological differences between both states constitute both a challenge and an opportunity for the development of Kv7.1/IKs-targeted therapeutic strategies.Fig. 1UCL2077 is an extremely potent inhibitor of Kv7.1 channels. The functional diversity of Kv7.1 is enhanced through association with regulatory subunits from the KCNE family.21,22 In the heart, KCNE1 profoundly redefines Kv7.1’s electrophysiological profile, increasing the channel’s current amplitude, shifting activation toward depolarized potentials, eliminating inactivation, and slowing activation and deactivation kinetics.4,5,23,24 Notably, KCNE1 also shifts the relative contribution of the IO and AO states, favoring conduction through the AO state, and modulates pore properties, resulting in a reduced Rb+/K+ permeability ratio and a decreased sensitivity to XE991.15

The tight links of Kv7.1/IKs to cardiac function and disease raises the interest in Kv7.1 activators for LQTS and inhibitors for SQTS. Given the state-dependent inhibitory profile of XE991,15 it remains to be seen if other Kv7.1 inhibitors exhibit the same preference for the IO over the AO state. In this context, UCL2077 (Fig. 1A), a clotrimazole-derivative25,26 inhibitor of Kv7,27 hERG,28,29 and BK28 channels, displays subtype selectivity within the Kv7 channel family. UCL2077 exhibits the highest affinity for Kv7.1, followed by Kv7.2 and Kv7.3.24,27 However, in these studies a single concentration of UCL2077 was tested (either 3 μM or 10 μM), in both cases achieving full block of Kv7.1 currents, and therefore its potency could not be accurately determined. Moreover, the inhibitory effects of UCL2077 on Kv7.1 current kinetics, voltage dependence and its specific binding mechanism remain unexplored. Within this framework, the aim of the current project was to characterize the electrophysiological inhibition of Kv7.1 and IKs (Kv7.1 + KCNE1) currents by UCL2077 using the whole-cell patch clamp technique. We determined the concentration-response relationships, explored macroscopic kinetic modulation, and studied the voltage- and state dependence of UCL2077-mediated inhibition of Kv7.1/IKs channels. Finally, we integrated our results into a computational Markov-state model to propose a mechanism of action that accurately predicts concentration response and state dependence of UCL2077. This in-depth exploratory characterization aims to provide valuable insights into the pharmacological modulation of Kv7.1/IKs channels in the context of cardiac arrhythmia.

Materials and methods

Solutions and chemicals

Unless otherwise indicated, all reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. The bath solution for whole-cell experiments contained (in mM) 135 NaCl, 5 KCl, 1 MgCl2, 1 CaCl2, 2.8 Na-acetate, 10 HEPES, pH 7.4 (adjusted with NaOH). The pipette solution contained (in mM) 130 KCl, 5 EGTA, 1 MgCl2, 4 Na2-ATP, 0.1 GTP, 10 HEPES, pH 7.2 (adjusted with KOH). 3-(Triphenylmethylaminomethyl)pyridine (UCL2077) and (3R,4S)-(+)-N-[3-hydroxy-2,2-dimethyl-6-(4,4,4,-trifluorobutoxy)chroman-4-yl]-N-methylmethanesulfonamide (HMR1556), purchased from Tocris Biosciences, were dissolved in DMSO and stored at –20 °C as stock solutions (ranging from 0.5 to 50 mM for UCL2077, 2 mM for HMR1556).

Molecular biology, cell culture, and transfection

A linked Kv7.1-KCNE1 construct with a forced 4:4 stoichiometry was generated as previously described.30 E1R/R2E and E1R/R4E mutations were generated on an untagged Kv7.1 construct using site-directed mutagenesis and Pfu Turbo (Agilent Technologies) and confirmed through Sanger sequencing. The E1R/R2E mutation was also introduced into the linked Kv7.1-KCNE1 construct.

Embryonic kidney 293 cells (HEK293) stably expressing an SV40 temperature-sensitive T antigen (tsA201, ECACC Cat# 96121229, RRID:CVCL_2737, from Sigma-Aldrich), chromosomally female, were confirmed to be mycoplasma-free upon initial acquisition and were used from early-passage frozen stocks throughout the study. Cells were seeded onto 15 × 15 mm coverslips and cultured in modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum and antibiotics as previously reported.30 Cells were transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) following the manufacturer’s instructions with the following amounts of DNA: 2 μg of linked Kv7.1-GFP, 50 ng linked Kv7.1-KCNE1 + 250 ng GFP, 2 μg E1R/R2E + 0.5 μg GFP, 2 μg E1R/R4E + 0.5 μg GFP, 2 μg linked E1R/R2E-KCNE1 + 0.5 μg GFP, and 2 μg E1R/R4E + 6 μg linked KCNE1-GFP. Whole-cell experiments were performed 24 hours after transfection.

Electrophysiology

Axopatch 200B amplifier, Digidata 1440, and pClamp11 software (Molecular Devices) were used to obtain and analyze recordings. Electrodes were pulled from thin-walled borosilicate capillaries using a linear multistage electrode P-97 puller (Sutter Instrument) and fire polished before use with an MF-830 microforge (Narishige International USA Inc). The resistance was 2–3 MΩ. During acquisition, data were sampled at 10 kHz and filtered at 5 kHz. Series resistance was below 10 MΩ, often below 5 MΩ, and compensated at 70% to 75%. Cells were held at –80 mV and pulses were applied every 15 seconds (start to start).

Concentration-response curves

To study concentration-response relationships and changes in macroscopic kinetics, a 4-second depolarizing pulse to +60 mV followed by 2 seconds to –40 mV was applied every 15 seconds. UCL2077 was applied to the bath from higher concentration aliquots never exceeding a bath concentration of 0.1% DMSO to avoid cytotoxic effects. In the case of Kv7.1, 300 nM HMR1556 was applied after testing 2 UCL2077 concentrations to correct for endogenous currents. Concentration-response curves were measured at the peak currents, at the tail currents and at the end of the depolarizing pulse for Kv7.1. For IKs, measurements were only taken at the peak currents and at the tail currents. Normalized response (R = IUCL2077/Icontrol) values, expressed as a percentage, were fitted to a Hill equation:(1)R=Rmin+100−Rmin1+10−nH(logIC50−log[UCL2077])where nH is the Hill slope. To minimize bias due to current rundown, we selected cells with stable baseline currents and limited the number of concentrations tested per cell to reduce the duration of experiments. The presence of ATP and GTP in our intracellular solution also contributed to reduce ion channel current rundown.31,32 This approach has proven effective for IKs currents, as demonstrated in a recent study showing strong current recovery upon compound washout.33 For Kv7.1, we determined the experimental timepoints at which the concentration-response data were acquired and assessed rundown in the absence of UCL2077 (Supplemental Fig. 1). Although some cell-to-cell variability was observed at longer experiment durations, current rundown had no substantial impact within the tested time window.

Macroscopic kinetic analysis

The concentration-response current traces were used to analyze changes in macroscopic current kinetics in the presence of UCL2077. For Kv7.1, the current decay was measured as the difference from the peak current to the current level at the end of the 4-second depolarization, as a proportion of the peak current: (Ipeak – Iend)/Ipeak. The current decay was split into 2 components, a fast and a slow one, by fitting the current traces of the fast component to an exponential equation of the form:(2)I(t)=A×e−t/τ+Cwhere A is the amplitude and τ the time constant of the fast component. The amplitude was expressed as a percentage of the peak current amplitude: A/Ipeak. The slow component was too slow to be appropriately measured, but its amplitude was estimated by subtracting the current at the end of the depolarization from the extrapolated plateau of the fast component fit (C) and expressed as a percentage of the peak current amplitude: (C – Iend)/Ipeak.

Macroscopic activation kinetics of Kv7.1 were measured by analyzing the time from the beginning of the depolarization until the current reaches its maximum peak (tpeak) and fitting the exponential part of the activation current trace to an exponential equation (eq. 2). For deactivation kinetics, a similar pulse protocol was applied but the repolarization was at –120 mV to better appreciate the tail currents and their hook. The deactivating current after the initial “hook” was fitted to an exponential equation (eq. 2). Additionally, the extrapolation of the exponential equation to the beginning of the repolarization pulse revealed the reactivating currents, which were calculated as the difference between the extrapolated currents and the real currents as a percentage of the extrapolated currents: (Iextrapol – Ireal)/Iextrapol.

Macroscopic activation kinetics of IKs were analyzed by measuring the current traces during the depolarization to +60 mV to an exponential equation with delay of the form:(3)I(t)=A×e−(t−d)/τ+Cwhere d is the delay. Deactivation kinetics were assessed by fitting the current traces during repolarization to –40 mV to a single exponential equation (eq. 2).

Fractional inhibition traces

The concentration-response traces were converted to fractional inhibition traces by calculating the difference between the current in the presence and in the absence of UCL2077 and normalizing it to the control current for every datapoint, eg, (IUCL2077 – Icontrol)/Icontrol for every millisecond. For Kv7.1, the obtained traces were fitted to a double exponential equation:(4)I(t)=Afast×e−t/τfast+Aslow×e−t/τslow+C

The fast component (1/τfast) was fit to a linear regression as a function of UCL2077 concentration of the form:(5)1/τfast=kON×[UCL2077]+kOFFfrom which kOFF/kON approximated the IC50.

For IKs, fractional inhibition traces were fit to a single exponential equation (eq. 2). The linear relationship between 1/τ and the concentration of UCL2077 (eq. 5), however, did not have a clear slope.

Voltage dependence of channel activation and UCL2077-mediated inhibition

To measure conductance-voltage (G-V) relationships, cells were held at –90 mV and a series of depolarizing voltage steps from –80 to +60 mV in 5 mV steps were applied in the case of Kv7.1, and from –80 to +80 mV in 10 mV steps for IKs. Conductance-voltage relationships were fitted to a Boltzmann sigmoidal of the form:(6)G/Gmax=Gmin+Gmax−Gmin1+e(V50−V)/kwhere V50 is the voltage at which 50% of the channels are open and k is the slope factor.

Normalized response values (R) presented a voltage-dependent reduction of current inhibition at potentials higher than the control activation V50. For Kv7.1, datapoints at –10 mV or higher were fit to a linear regression to obtain slope values. For IKs, the linear regression was fitted at potentials equal or greater than 50 mV. Following the Woodhull equation,34 normalized current values were converted into voltage-dependent dissociation constants at 100 pM UCL2077 using the following equivalence:(7)K(V)=R×[UCL2077]1−Rwhere R = I[UCL2077]/Icontrol. The voltage dependence of the obtained values was fitted as(8)K(V)=K0×ezδFV/RTwhere z = 1, K0 is the K(V) at V = 0 mV, δ is the fraction of the membrane potential that affects the drug binding, F is Faraday’s constant, R is the noble gas constant, and T is the temperature.

Finally, the fractional inhibition traces were calculated as previously described for all potentials, and 1/τ values were obtained. Ion channel simulations

Markov modeling was performed using a custom Python script with Q-matrix formulations to define and solve the system of differential equations representing transitions between states.35 The models and equations are detailed in the appendices for Kv7.1 (Appendix A) and IKs (Appendix B). First, the parameters of the model were optimized in control conditions (absence of UCL2077) to minimize the sum of squares between the simulated currents and a representative experiment using the conductance-voltage protocol described above (Supplemental Fig. 3). For Kv7.1, optimization was performed using a custom Particle Swarm Optimization protocol36 running for 1200 generations. For IKs, the initial values were estimated from previous literature37 and optimized using the Nelder-Mead simplex algorithm38 with a penalty factor for divergence from the initial parameters on a custom Python script using the library SciPy.39

According to the experimental data, we propose a UCL2077 mechanism of action in which the small molecule acts as an open-channel inhibitor with different affinities for the IO and the AO states. The parameters related to UCL2077 binding (kON) and unbinding (kOFF) to the open states of the channel were optimized using the Nelder-Mead search algorithm on a function that minimized the sum of squares between experimental and simulated dose-response curves at the peak currents and at the tail currents for Kv7.1, or only at the tail currents for IKs (Supplemental Fig. 4). Data analysis were performed on Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation) and GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad). This study was exploratory in nature; that is, the goal was not to test prespecified hypotheses, but rather to generate mechanistic insight. Accordingly, all reported P values should be interpreted as descriptive measures of effect size and not as outcomes of hypothesis testing.

Results

UCL2077 inhibits Kv7.1 with picomolar affinity

To assess the potency of UCL2077, the concentration-response relationship was characterized on tsA201 cells transfected with Kv7.1 (Fig. 1B). In our measurements, 300 nM of the highly selective Kv7.1/IKs blocker HMR1556 was applied to the bath after UCL2077, and HMR1556-insensitive endogenous currents (∼20% of the total current) were subtracted (results without HMR1556 correction in Supplemental Fig. 5A and Table 2). The half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was 450 pM when measured at the peak current upon depolarization (Fig. 1C), and ∼6-fold more potent when measured at the tail currents (IC50 = 70 pM). These values are much lower than those reported for other channels, including Kv7.2 (1.02 μM), Kv7.3 (15.7 μM), and Kv11.1 (4.7 μM).27,28 The subtype selectivity of UCL2077 was confirmed by also testing 3 nM on Kv7.2, which produced a large inhibition of Kv7.1 but no significant effect on Kv7.2 (Supplemental Fig. 6). Additionally, the Hill equation fit (eq. 1) gave slope values close to 1 when measured at the tail currents (Table 2), suggesting either the binding of a single UCL2077 molecule per Kv7.1 channel complex or lack of cooperativity if more than one simultaneous binding site exists.

Kinetic modulation of Kv7.1 currents by UCL2077

The increased sensitivity at the tail currents might be explained by an accentuated current decay in the presence of UCL2077, which can be appreciated from the experimental traces especially at 100 and 300 pM (Fig. 1B), resulting in a stronger inhibition at the end of the depolarization compared with the beginning. In support of this idea, measuring the dose-response relationship at the end of the depolarizing pulse had an affinity of 85 pM (Supplemental Fig. 5B), close to the value at the tail currents (Table 2). To characterize the current decay induced by UCL2077, current traces were normalized in the absence (control) and the presence of 300 pM UCL2077 (Fig. 2A). The current decay, measured as the percent difference between the amplitudes at the peak and at the end of the depolarization, was increased by 2-fold in all 6 independent experiments relative to control conditions (Fig. 2B). In the presence of UCL2077, 2 components of current decay were clearly observed: a fast and a slow component (Fig. 2C). In the absence of UCL2077, ∼50% of cells also presented a slow component with a small magnitude (<15% relative to the peak current). UCL2077 strongly accentuated the amplitudes of both fast and slow components and accelerated the time constant of the fast component (Fig. 2D). The characteristic current decay induced by UCL2077 is reminiscent of open-channel inhibitors with fast drug-channel binding kinetics, such as nifedipine40 and tetraalkylammonium compounds.41,42 In these examples, channel opening upon depolarizing pulses is quickly followed by a current decay that reflects the open-channel-binding kinetics of the drug, like that observed for UCL2077. Following this hypothesis, the time-dependence action of UCL2077 over a 4-second pulse to +60 mV was analyzed by measuring the fractional inhibition, ie, the current difference in the absence and in the presence of UCL2077 relative to the control current (Icontrol – IUCL2077)/Icontrol for every timepoint (Fig. 3). The fractional inhibition traces clearly followed a biexponential equation (eq. 4), especially at intermediate concentrations of UCL2077 (100 and 300 pM) (Fig. 3A). The fast component (τfast) of UCL2077-mediated inhibition of Kv7.1 was about 50-fold faster than the slow component (τslow): at 300 pM UCL2077, τfast = 70.0 ms (95% CI, 35–106 ms) and τslow = 3.11 s (95% CI, 0.56–5.6 s), n = 7. Following an open-channel inhibition hypothesis, the reversal of τfast was used to estimate UCL2077 binding to Kv7.1, as described in previous studies,40,43 and its concentration dependence was analyzed (Fig. 3B). Data (n = 4–7) were fitted to a linear regression (eq. 5) in which the Y-intercept approximates the apparent dissociation rate (kOFF, best fit: 14 s–1; 95% CI, 8–19 s–1) and the slope reflects the apparent association constant (kON, best fit: 24 s–1 × nM–1; 95% CI, 13–34 s–1 × nM–1). The dissociation constant (KD), estimated by the ratio kOFF/kON, provided a value of 580 pM (95% CI, 410–750 pM), consistent with the IC50 found at the peak of Kv7.1 in Fig. 1C. Finally, if the mechanism of inhibition is through the open state of the channels, the onset of the fractional inhibition traces is expected to intersect zero inhibition after the start of the pulse. Indeed, this was the case for some cells at low UCL2077 concentrations (10–300 pM) (Fig. 3C). However, because the UCL2077 onset was very fast, and the first millisecond of the fractional inhibition traces were not always well resolved, a quantitative analysis was not possible.

Finally, potential additional alterations in channel kinetics, besides the current decay, were examined (Supplemental Fig. 7). Activation of the channel appeared to be slightly accelerated, as revealed by the time to peak (Supplemental Fig. 7, A and B). Because control and UCL2077 traces never overlapped, the apparent acceleration of channel activation is most likely due to the enhancement of the fast component of the current decay. In support of this idea, the speed of the fast component of the fractional inhibition traces was faster (70 ms at 300 pM UCL2077, n = 7, Fig. 3A) than the fast component of current decay (140 ms at 300 pM UCL2077, Fig. 2D), and their onset in Fig. 3C was also earlier than the mean time to peak. The deactivation kinetics and tail currents were also studied, using hyperpolarizing pulses to –120 mV following 4-second activation at +60 mV (Supplemental Fig. 7C). The “hook” observed in the tail currents is thought to represent channel reactivation in the transition from the low open-probability AO state to the higher open-probability IO state.18 In this case, 100 pM UCL2077 was used, as 300 pM was too potent on the tail currents. Application of UCL2077 significantly flattens the tail currents, reducing the percentage of reactivating channels and slowing down the deactivation of the channel (Supplemental Fig. 7D). This could reflect differential effects of UCL2077 on the IO and the AO states, an idea that will be further explored later.

Voltage dependence of UCL2077 block

As Kv7.1 is a voltage-gated channel, we next aimed to assess how voltage interplayed with UCL2077-medated Kv7.1 inhibition (Fig. 4). First, we examined the conductance-voltage (G-V) relationships in the absence (control) and in the presence of 100 pM UCL2077 (Fig. 4A). Exposure to the drug showed an apparent left shift of the V50 toward more hyperpolarized potentials without significantly altering the slope of the curve (Fig. 4, B and C). However, normalization of tail currents in the presence of UCL2077 to the control current Gmax did not reveal an overlap (Fig. 4B). Hence, the apparent shift of the V50 curve could be mediated by the voltage dependence of UCL2077 inhibition. For that reason, the normalized currents in the presence of UCL2077 (IUCL2077/Icontrol) and their relationship to pulse voltage were analyzed (Fig. 4D). Measurements were taken at the peak currents, at the tail currents and at the end of a 4-second depolarizing pulse. In all conditions, Kv7.1 currents decreased, reaching a minimum by around –20 mV, which coincided with the V50 of activation in control cells. This would reinforce the notion that UCL2077 preferably binds open channels. However, at voltages higher than –10 mV, where channel opening is over 80% its maximum value, a partial decrease in channel inhibition that linearly correlates to the voltage applied was observed at the peak and at the end of depolarization (Table 5). At the tail currents, the slope was much less, not significantly different from 0, even though the same trend of voltage-dependent reduced inhibition can be observed when tail currents are normalized to their own Gmax in Fig. 4B. To further examine how membrane potential influences UCL2077-mediated channel inhibition, we used the Woodhull equation (eqs. 7 and 8) to convert current amplitude values into voltage-dependent dissociation constants K(V) (Fig. 4E). The slope of the linear regression of K(V) is proportional to δ, which estimated the fraction of the voltage field that affects the drug.34 Indeed, UCL2077-mediated inhibition at the peak currents and at the end of the depolarization showed similar values of δ (see Table 5), indicating that the drug senses ∼30%–40% of the membrane electric field at these timepoints, whereas inhibition at the tail currents appeared voltage independent. Finally, we measured the voltage dependence of the fast-binding rate (1/τfast from Fig. 3) (Fig. 4F). This component showed a mild voltage dependence, becoming greater with higher depolarizations, and associated an equivalent charge of 0.27 (Fig. 4G). In summary, UCL2077 inhibition is modulated by voltage at the peak and at the end of depolarization, with higher voltages accelerating the speed of UCL2077 binding but reducing the fraction of channels blocked.

Modulation of UCL2077 action on Kv7.1 by KCNE1

In the heart, Kv7.1 associates with the regulatory subunit KCNE1 to form the slowly activating delayed rectifier current IKs, completely remodeling its electrophysiological profile.4,5,23,24 To understand the potential implications of UCL2077 in cardiac physiology, we examined its effects on IKs by using a linked Kv7.1-KCNE1 construct with forced 4:4 stoichiometry (Fig. 5). UCL2077 blocked IKs potently, providing a similar IC50 value (520 pM) to Kv7.1 when measured at the peak current, but was less sensitive at the tail current (590 pM) compared with Kv7.1 (Fig. 5, A and B; Table 2). In IKs, the concentration-response curves measured at the peak and tail currents overlap, perhaps because slow activation delays the peak current toward the end of the pulse. Unlike Kv7.1 alone, UCL2077 did not seem to modulate the macroscopic kinetics of activation or deactivation of IKs (Supplemental Fig. 2, A–C). The voltage dependence of IKs activation was only mildly modified by UCL2077 (Fig. 5, C–E). The slope of the G-V relationship was increased, reducing its steepness, and the V50 was displaced ∼17 mV toward more depolarized potentials (Fig. 5E), in the opposite direction of Kv7.1 (Table 4). Like Kv7.1, in the presence of 100 pM UCL2077, the normalized IKs current decreased with channel activation, reaching a minimum close to the control activation V50 (around 30 mV), suggesting again that drug binding is at least partially coupled to channel opening (Supplemental Fig. 2D). The valley of maximum UCL2077 inhibition is followed by an apparent voltage-dependent relief of inhibition above +50 mV that can be approximated to a linear function. The slope of the linear regression had a similar value (2.9 V–1) to those at the peak currents and at the end of the depolarization of Kv7.1 (3.2 and 2.0 V–1, respectively) (Fig. 4D; Supplemental Fig. 2E), as was the electrical distance (δ = 0.31, Table 5), suggesting a similar mechanism of action in both channels. The difference in the direction of V50 shifts could reflect how the same slope of relief of block would interact with the different steepness of Kv7.1 and IKs activation curves (see slope, “k”, in Figs 4C and 5E). Unlike Kv7.1, the fractional inhibition traces did not show concentration dependence (Supplemental Fig. 2, F–G) but were voltage dependent, with an associated equivalent charge of z = 0.41 (Supplemental Fig. 2H). Therefore, even though the macroscopic G-V relationships are differentially affected by UCL2077 binding to Kv7.1 and IKs, our results are consistent with the same binding mechanism in both channels.

State-dependent inhibition of Kv7.1 and IKs channels by UCL2077

The results so far align with the hypothesis that UCL2077-mediated inhibition might occur preferably from binding channels in their open configurations. As Kv7.1 and IKs channels can conduct through 2 stable open states (IO and AO, Fig. 6A),15,18,19 with each state presenting with different pharmacological profiles,15,16 they may also have different affinities for UCL2077. Results so far suggest this might be the case, including (1) the biphasic current decay induced on Kv7.1 (Figs 1 and 2), in which each component could reflect UCL2077 binding to a different open state; (2) the reduction of reactivating channels in the tail currents in the presence of UCL2077 (Supplemental Fig. 7), which reflects the transition from the AO to the IO state; and (3) the biexponential fit to the UCL2077-Kv7.1 fractional inhibition traces (Figs 3 and 4). Interestingly, the UCL2077-IKs fractional inhibition kinetics fit better to a single exponential equation (Supplemental Fig. 2), which would be consistent with IKs conducting mainly from a single state—the AO state.15,37 For these reasons, we decided to study the state dependence of UCL2077-mediated inhibition using previously established mutations that functionally lock the channel in either the IO (E1R/R2E: E160R/R231E) or the AO (E1R/R4E: E160R/R237E) states (Fig. 6B).15,17,18 The dose-response curves, measured at the tail currents to minimize any possible inference of endogenous channels, showed a dramatic effect compared with wild type (WT) Kv7.1 and IKs (Kv7.1 + KCNE1) (Fig. 6, C and D). A kinetic model integrating UCL2077 action

Our results suggest that UCL2077 is a mildly voltage-dependent and open-channel inhibitor of Kv7.1 and IKs with different affinities for the IO and AO states. Discussion

UCL2077 is a high-affinity inhibitor of Kv7.1/IKs channels

UCL2077 is a potent24 and highly selective27 Kv7.1 channel inhibitor, making its mechanism of action of therapeutic interest for the treatment of SQTS and other cardiac arrhythmias that occur with abnormal Kv7.1/IKs gain of function. In our experiments (Figs 1 and 5), UCL2077 showed extreme potency with IC50 values of 450–600 pM for both Kv7.1 (measured at the peak current upon depolarization) and IKs, and even higher (∼70 pM) when measured at the tail currents of Kv7.1 (Table 2). These values are consistent with the full inhibition previously reported at either 3 μM27 or 10 μM,24 and much more sensitive than those measured for Kv7.2 (Supplemental Fig. 6),27 hERG,28,29 and BK28 channels. Overall, our results align with the previous bibliography and reinforce the notion that UCL2077 is a potent, highly selective Kv7.1 channel inhibitor. Together with its predicted well-tolerated oral bioavailability,44 UCL2077’s high affinity and specificity for Kv7.1/IKs channels offer a promising therapeutic opportunity to correct excessive repolarization in gain-of-function conditions, eg, SQTS. Although UCL2077 has served mainly as a research tool, its mechanism appears therapeutically appropriate in the context of cardiomyocyte electrophysiology and could inspire the development of derivatives with improved pharmacological properties.

Modulation of Kv7.1 kinetics and voltage dependence suggest an open-channel binding mechanism for UCL2077

Analysis of channel kinetics (Figs 2 and 3; Supplemental Fig. 7), voltage dependence (Fig. 4), and regulation by KCNE1 (Fig. 5; Supplemental Fig. 2) bring insights into the mechanism of action of UCL2077 on Kv7.1/IKs channels. First, we observed that 300 pM UCL2077 induced a biphasic current decay on Kv7.1 upon depolarization (Fig. 2), which is most likely responsible for the difference of affinity when measured at the peak or at the tail currents. These features are reminiscent of an open-channel inhibition mechanism in which the current decay reflects fast UCL2077-binding kinetics. This idea is further supported by the linear relationship of the onset of inhibition as a function of drug concentration,43 as well as the small delay of UCL2077 onset observed in some cells (Fig. 3). This binding mechanism is reminiscent of that already been described for other drugs, such as nifedipine40 and tetraalkylammonium compounds.41,42 The latter includes compounds such as octyl-triethylammonium (C8-TEA) and are an interesting example,42 given the loosely similar features in molecular structure with UCL2077 (a central electropositive nitrogen atom surrounded by 4 highly hydrophobic chains), which might hint at similarities in their mechanism of action. In this context, the absence of current decay in IKs (Fig. 5) is likely due to the binding kinetics being faster than channel opening, which becomes the rate-limiting step. In support of this idea, the activation and deactivation kinetics of IKs are not affected by UCL2077 (Supplemental Fig. 2, A–C) and the time constant for UCL2077 binding does not appear concentration dependent (Supplemental Fig. 2, F–G).

An open-channel inhibitory mechanism is further supported by the voltage dependence of UCL2077-mediated channel inhibition (Fig. 4), particularly through the following features: (1) the voltage-dependent increase of inhibition at low voltages with a maximum near the V50 of Kv7.1 activation, reinforcing the notion that channel opening is necessary for UCL2077 binding (Fig. 4D); and (2) the voltage dependence of the fast component of the binding kinetics (Fig. 4, F–G). Additionally, potentials higher than the activation V50 reduced current inhibition linearly with voltage (Fig. 4, D and E), in a manner reminiscent of the effect of nifedipine on Kv1.5.40 These phenomena were also observed for IKs (Supplemental Fig. 2, D–E, and H), suggesting that the mechanism of action of UCL2077 is the same, or at least very similar, both in the absence and in the presence KCNE1. UCL2077 is a secondary amine and, at the pH of our solutions, 29%–39% of molecules will be protonated and affected by the electric field.45 In this scenario, there are 3 possible explanations for the voltage-dependent reduction of current inhibition: (1) that the positively charged UCL2077 molecules interact with an extracellular binding site, according to the observed δ values derived from the Woodhull equation (Fig. 4E; Supplemental Fig. 2E); (2) that the UCL2077 binding site is coupled to voltage-dependent conformational rearrangements, such as voltage sensor or channel opening movements; and (3) that the reduction of current inhibition arises from the relative voltage-dependent distributions and affinities of the 2 characterized open states of the channel.15

State dependence of UCL2077-mediated inhibition

The pharmacological differences between the 2 main open states of the channel, the IO and the AO states, were examined by using mutations that functionally lock the channel in either state (Fig. 6).15,16,18 In the absence of KCNE1, E1R/R2E (IO) showed an increased potency for UCL2077, whereas E1R/R4E (AO) showed the opposite effect (Fig. 6, C and D). The different affinities of each state can explain (1) the biphasic shape of the current decay (Fig. 2); (2) the decrease in hook currents (Supplemental Fig. 7), because the hook currents have higher occupancy of the IO state; (3) the biphasic fractional inhibition traces (Fig. 3); and (4) the relief of channel block upon higher depolarizations (Fig. 4, D–F), because higher voltages will transition faster toward the AO state. Similar pharmacological results have been observed with XE99115: locking Kv7.1 in the AO state (E1R/R4E) decreased the potency of the drug compared with WT and E1R/R2E. The AO state also displays a reduced Rb+/K+ permeability ratio relative to the IO and the WT, which indicates structural differences in the pore of each state that might underlie the changes in pharmacology.15

In IKs, KCNE1 selectively stabilizes the AO state and does the opposite with the IO state.15,18,24 Interestingly, in the presence of KCNE1, E1R/R4E increased UCL2077’s potency with a similar IC50 to E1R/R2E in the absence of KCNE1 (Table 2), and E1R/R2E + KCNE1 had a potency like that of WT IKs (Fig. 6, E and F). The increased potency of E1R/R4E relative to WT IKs, which is thought to be mostly in the AO state (Supplemental Fig. 3E), can be explained if the E1R/R2E and E1R/R4E mutations, being functionally locked, represent more accurately the steady-state UCL2077 affinity. In that case, UCL2077-mediated inhibition of WT channels would not be in equilibrium because molecule binding is kinetically restricted to channel opening, thus presenting a reduced apparent affinity. The relative distributions of the IO and AO states in IKs also explains why its fractional inhibition traces are monoexponential (Supplemental Fig. 2, F–G) as opposed to the biexponential features of Kv7.1: in IKs the AO state dominates over the IO state, which is almost negligible, whereas in Kv7.1 both states are significantly conducting (Supplemental Fig. 3E). Unlike Kv7.1, the voltage-dependent reduction of current inhibition of IKs at voltages above +50 mV is not intuitive as IKs conduction, especially at high voltages, is mostly mediated through the AO state, which has a strong affinity for UCL2077. Two possible explanations for the relief of current inhibition are (1) that it is a consequence of the slow kinetics of UCL2077 binding to the AO state in nonequilibrium conditions; or (2) that it reflects an interaction with a different voltage-dependent component.

Integrating experimental results into a computational model of UCL2077

To test the feasibility of UCL2077 acting as a state-dependent, open-channel inhibitor, Markov-based computational models of Kv7.1 and IKs were built in the absence and in the presence of UCL2077 (Fig. 7). These models represent a simplification of the otherwise complex gating and kinetics that characterize Kv7.1/IKs channels and, while simple, they accurately reproduce the main macroscopic features of these channels during activation (Supplemental Fig. 3). Moreover, the strength of the electromechanical coupling of the IO and AO states, which can be approximated to the ratio kCO/kOC, is consistent with previous literature: (1) for Kv7.1, the AO state is thought to spend less time open than the IO state, either due to the presence of a flickering state16 or due to a weaker electromechanical coupling18; and (2) for IKs, the AO state in the main conducting state (Supplemental Fig. 3E) following previous evidence.15,37 In the present models, which were refined to match experimental control data, we observed a direct correlation between the IC50 for the E1R/R2E and E1R/R4E mutants and the model’s electromechanical coupling for the IO and the AO states both in the presence and in the absence of KCNE1 (Fig. 7F). The models in the presence of UCL2077 assumed binding exclusively during the open states, with different affinities for IO versus AO states, which were measured from the E1R/R2E and E1R/R4E mutants. The speed of the forward and backward transitions was refined to match the dose-response curves. The resulting models accurately reproduce the concentration-response relationships of all the studied constructs of this work, as well as the biphasic current decay and reduction of reactivating channels (“hooks”) in the tail currents of Kv7.1, features absent in IKs. Taken together, our results corroborate the validity of the proposed mechanism of action of UCL2077 as a state-dependent open-channel inhibitor and strongly suggest that UCL2077 potency is linked to the strength of electromechanical coupling.

Conclusion

In this work, we show that UCL2077 is a high-affinity inhibitor of Kv7.1/IKs channels with a mechanism of action that is therapeutically appropriate for the treatment of SQTS. Through whole-cell patch clamp experiments, we characterized the concentration-response, kinetic modulation, voltage and state dependence of UCL2077-mediated inhibition of Kv7.1/IKs channels. Integrating our experimental results with computational kinetic modeling, we propose a mechanism of action that recapitulates the main features of this inhibition.

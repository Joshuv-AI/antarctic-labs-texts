Identification of immunomodulators associated with atrial fibrillation reporting to better understand AF immunologic mechanisms: a Vigibase retrospective disproportionality analysis and a literature review
Authors: Jonaz Font, Charles Dolladille, Jordane Divoux, Basile Chretien, Hubert de Boysson, Damien Legallois, Pierre Ollitrault, Laure Champ-Rigot, Virginie Ferchaud, Arnaud Pellissier, Paul Milliez, Joachim Alexandre
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesGrowing evidence exists about the pivotal role of immune mechanisms in the physiopathology of atrial fibrillation (AF). Drugs that modulate the immune system (immunomodulators) may contribute to the development of AF. We aimed to identify immunomodulators that are associated with AF to better define their safety profile, and elucidating their mechanisms of action could yield novel insights into AF’s immune physiopathology.DesignA descriptive and disproportionality analysis of claims data.SettingsWorld pharmacovigilance database VigiBase until 1 March 2025.Primary and secondary ouctomesFirst, we ascertained the association of immunomodulators with AF over-reporting with a disproportionality analysis evaluating the multivariable-adjusted reporting odds ratio (aROR) for AF reporting performed for 141 immunomodulators in VigiBase. Then, a literature review was done to explore the underlying mechanisms of AF through immunomodulator mechanisms.ResultsA total of 6 148 556 reports encompassing at least one of the 141 immunomodulators were identified in Vigibase. Our primary analysis revealed 20 immunomodulators associated with AF over-reporting. The three immunomodulators with the greatest signal were: recombinant interleukin-11 with an aROR=20.91 (99.96% CI 12.08 to 36.17), efgartigimod alfa with an aROR=6.75 (99.96% CI 3.96 to 11.52) and recombinant interleukin-2 with an aROR=6.15 (99.96% CI 3.62 to 10.45). A derivative literature review posited a hypothetical immune ‘vicious circle’ promoting AF, involving T helper cells, macrophages and natural killer cells which could lead to electrophysiologic and histologic atrial remodelling.ConclusionsTwenty Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-labelled immunomodulators are associated with AF overreporting in Vigibase with a substantial signal on recombinant IL-11. These data contribute substantively to the prevailing understanding of the safety profile of these immunomodulators. Moreover, these findings support a multidirectional interaction between the immune system and AF development and might lead to considering future therapeutic targets.Trial registration numberNCT06095791.Keywords: Pacing & electrophysiology, IMMUNOLOGY, Drug Therapy

Introduction

Immunomodulators have experienced remarkable expansion over the past decade.1 Sharing the ability to modulate immune function by diverse signalling pathways, these agents have gained approval for a wide array of disorders, ranging from immune-mediated/autoimmune diseases (IMADs) to malignancies.2,4 Growing evidence exists about the association between immunity and the development of cardiovascular diseases. In a meta-analysis of prospective cohort studies including a total of 7 289 352 patients, rheumatoid arthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus were associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular diseases combining acute myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accidents and peripheral artery disease.5

Atrial fibrillation (AF), the most prevalent sustained cardiac arrhythmia, presently affects approximately 33 million individuals globally and is projected to impact 6–12 million in the USA and 17.9 million in Europe by the year 2050 and 2060, respectively.6 7 Some IMADs and cancers are known to be associated with arrhythmias and particularly AF, suggesting the role of inflammation and immunity in AF genesis.8 9 Illustratively, a nationwide Danish investigation demonstrated an augmented incidence of AF in patients with rheumatoid arthritis, with an adjusted incidence rate ratio (RR) of 1.41 (95% CI 1.31 to 1.51).10

The intricate roles of immune mechanisms implicated in the genesis of AF remain only partially elucidated.11 Nevertheless, immune cells and cytokines seem to play a central role in the physiology and physiopathology of cardiac electrophysiological system.11 12 A recent pharmacovigilance study of our team investigated a limited number of anticancer immunomodulators, as part of a wider anticancer drugs analysis. It showed a signal for recombinant interleukin-2 (rIL-2) known as aldesleukin with an adjusted reporting OR (aROR)=5.01 (99.97% CI 2.49 to 10.09).13 But the association between AF and the whole immunomodulators class has never been systematically evaluated, whereas this could lead to better understanding of immune physiopathology of AF and thus consider new druggable targets.

The primary objective of this study was to identify immunomodulators associated with an increased AF reporting in Vigibase, the WHO pharmacovigilance database, using an individual case level analysis. The first secondary objective was to explore main immune cells and immunologic pathways in AF genesis through the immune mechanisms of each immunomodulator significantly associated with AF reporting in the primary analysis.

Methods

Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology checklist is provided in online supplemental data table S1.

Datasource

VigiBase, the WHO global database of individual case safety reports was used for this study.13 14 VigiBase is managed by the Uppsala Monitoring Centre (Uppsala, Sweden) and contains more than 31 million reports received from at least 130 member countries.

In this study, we used the Vigibase Extract Case Level database which is a relational database with individual case level data structure. Vigibase allows for better understanding of the safety profile of drugs, better identification of drugs’ targets, hence the cellular pathway involved in adverse drug reaction. Details about the data source are available in online supplemental data 1.

Patient and public involvement

This study used a large pharmacovigilance database to achieve its objectives. The authors therefore did not directly involve patients or the public throughout the study.

Primary objective

To ascertain the association of immunomodulators with AF reporting in VigiBase, we performed an observational, retrospective, pharmacovigilance study using a disproportionality individual case data analysis.15 Immunomodulators were defined as drugs involved in the L03 and L04 Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical class with Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval on 1 January 2025. Cases were AF reports and were collected using the ‘Preferred Term’ (from the System Organ Class of Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activity (MedDRA), v27.1): « Atrial fibrillation » from the first report of immunomodulator until 1 March 2025. Non-cases were all the reports without AF. The study population was restricted to reports involving at least one immunomodulator (hereafter the primary analysis population, see figure 1) and we examined all immunomodulators with at least one registered report in VigiBase until the 1 March 2025. This strategy is common in disproportionality analysis to limit the indication bias because all patients will have received an immunomodulator which allows better comparability between cases and non-cases.15 A total of 141 immunomodulators approved by the FDA on 1 January 2025 with at least one registered report in Vigibase were included in this study (online supplemental data 2). We studied the association of each immunomodulator with AF reporting. Adjusted disproportionality estimates, that is, the aRORs of AF were computed for each of the 141 immunomodulators.

First secondary objective

We aimed to explore the main immune components involved in immunomodulators-associated AF reporting by studying the immune components modulated by each immunomodulator significantly associated with AF reporting in the primary analysis. To do so, we performed a literature review of the available literature describing the links between the aforementioned drugs with a significant disproportionality signal in our primary analysis and several immune components. An extensive, unrestricted, computerised MEDLINE and WEB OF SCIENCE databases literature search of articles in English was independently conducted by two reviewers (JD and JF) according to prespecified selection criteria from inception to 1 September 2023. We used immunomodulator names as domain 1. Regarding domain 2, we used the immune components terms. The search request was submitted as follows: domain 1 AND domain 2 (details of the search request in online supplemental data 3). We then attributed a negative coefficient to an immunomodulator-immune component couple if the immunomodulator inhibited the immune component, and a positive coefficient if the immunomodulator stimulated the immune component according to the literature. The final score for each couple depended on the number of literature articles mentioning this interaction. If one article mentioned this association, a score of ‘1’ was assigned; two articles, 2; if three or more, 3.

Other secondary objectives

We detailed AF case features associated with implicated immunomodulators within Vigibase, confining our review to agents with a significant aROR in the primary analysis (a significant aROR is defined in the statistical paragraph). For each case, age, sex, seriousness of AF, death outcome, concurrent diseases or conditions, immunomodulator indication, time from immunomodulator initiation to AF occurrence (hereafter, time to onset), and concurrent drugs were described (online supplemental data 4). Concurrent drugs of interest were drugs frequently associated with AF: anticoagulants, bradycardic drugs, cardiovascular drugs, class I or III antiarrhythmics, diabetes drugs, co-prescribed immunomodulators and systemic corticosteroids (online supplemental data 4).13 Concurrent diseases or conditions of interest were those frequently associated with AF: hypertension, cardiomyopathy, heart failure, ischaemic cardiopathy, ischaemic stroke, infection, dehydration, hypokalaemia and hyperthyroidism (online supplemental data 4).

We further analysed the overlap in reporting among immunomodulators significantly associated with AF reporting in the primary analysis via an intersection plot.

Covariates

In our study, the covariates were specified a priori based on clinical relevance and on potential confounders: included age (categorised into the following categories: ‘<18 years’, ‘18–44 years’, ‘45–64 years’, ‘65–74 years’ and ‘>75 years’); sex; region (African region, region of the Americas, South-East Asia region, European region, Eastern Mediterranean region and Western Pacific region); co-prescribed immunomodulators, the concomitant reporting of cardiovascular drugs (cardiac drugs, bradycardic drugs, antiarrhythmic drugs, anticoagulants), diabetes drugs or systemic corticosteroids; the concurrent reporting of heart failure, ischaemic cardiopathy, dehydration, hypokalaemia, hyperthyroidism and the immunomodulator indication (categorised in seven categories: ‘haematologic malignancies’, ‘IMADs’, ‘solid cancers’, ‘cytopenias’, ‘hypersensitivity’, ‘transplant rejection prevention’ and ‘other indications’). Definitions of all variables are provided in online supplemental data 4. We first performed univariate analyses for each of the 141 immunomodulators and the aforementioned covariates. Subsequently, to address the primary objective, we performed a multivariable analysis incorporating the 141 immunomodulators together with the aforementioned covariates, as the anticipated number of AF cases was sufficiently large to satisfy the conventional requirement of approximately one variable per 10 events.

Statistical analyses

We performed a disproportionality analysis in VigiBase, also known as case–non-case analysis (details in online supplemental data 5).

Because 141 immunomodulators were evaluated simultaneously, a conservative Bonferroni correction was applied to account for inflation of the type I error rate resulting from multiple comparisons.16 Hence, a p value <0.00035 was deemed significant, that is, comparisons were considered significant when the lower boundary of the 99.964% CI depicted as 99.96% for clarity of the aROR was >1. The aRORs (99.96% CI) were computed in the database including only cases with at least one concomitant, interacting or suspected immunomodulator (primary analysis population).

There was 36.5% missing data among the variable age, 19.9% among variables exploring indications, 6.6% among variable ‘sex’, 4.6% among variable ‘type of reporter’, 3.2% among variable ‘death’ and ‘seriousness’ (online supplemental table S2). Details of multiple imputations are available in online supplemental figure S1 and online supplemental data 6. Four sensitivity analyses were performed (online supplemental data 6) including one false discovery rate correction.

Every qualitative variable was expressed as proportion and quantitative variable (time to onset) was expressed with the median, first quartile and third quartile. R V.4.2.3 was used for the whole analysis and the following packages were used in their most recent version: future, ggplot2, mice, mitml, rlang, speedglm, tidyverse.

Results

Primary objective

A total of 6 148 556 reports including at least one of the 141 immunomodulators were identified (primary analysis population), of which 17 675 (0.29%) were AF cases (figure 1). In univariate analysis, 34 immunomodulators were associated with AF reporting in online supplemental data table S3. The three immunomodulators with the highest point estimate in the univariate analysis were recombinant interleukin-11 (rIL-11) known as oprelvekin with a ROR=39.42 (99.96% CI 24.20 to 64.20), recombinant interleukin-2 (rIL-2) known as aldesleukin with an ROR=10.10 (99.96% CI 2.21 to 16.42) and denileukin diftitox with an ROR=9.89 (99.96% CI 3.20 to 30.54). Online supplemental data table S4 describes the univariate analysis for variables other than immunomodulators. In the primary analysis, after adjusting for sex, age, region, concurrent diseases or conditions, coreported immunomodulators and indication, 20 immunomodulators remained significantly associated with AF reporting (figure 2). These 20 immunomodulators accounted for 8907 (50.39%) AF cases. The three immunomodulators with the highest point estimate were rIL-11 with an aROR=20.91 (99.96% CI 12.08 to 36.17), efgartigimod alfa with an aROR=6.75 (99.96% CI 3.96 to 11.52) and rIL-2 with an aROR=6.15 (99.96% CI 3.62 to 10.45). Among the indications, haematologic malignancies and transplant rejection prevention were significantly associated with AF overreporting. Additional details of the primary analysis are accessible in online supplemental materials, online supplemental tables S5 and S6.

First secondary objective

After literature review focused on the immune cells modulated by the immunomodulators significantly associated with AF reporting in the primary analysis, we found that NK cells are stimulated by rIL-2, anti-CD20 drugs, pomalidomide and lenalidomide (figure 3). Th1 and Th2 cells are stimulated by rIL-2, ipilimumab, daratumumab, pomalidomide, lenalidomide and Th2 cells are stimulated by rIL-11, granulocyte-macrophage colony stimulating factor (GM-CSF), cyclophosphamide and methotrexate. Other secondary objectives

Among the 20 immunomodulators significantly associated with AF overreporting in the primary analysis, we counted 8907 AF cases. The median time to onset ranged from 4 days for rIL-11, rIL-2 and GM-CSF to 876.5 days for interferon beta-1a (table 1 and online supplemental data table S12). For rIL-11, efgartigimod alfa, rIL-2 and obinutuzumab, the proportions of cases older than 65 years were, respectively, 52.9%, 100%, 52.4% and 82.4%; 3.4%, 2.0%, 5.1% and 9.3% for hypertension; 27.6%, 20%, 22.2% and 11.1% for heart failure; 3.4%, 6.0%, 7.4% and 13% for coprescribed anticoagulant and 6.9%, 4.0%, 5.6% and 2.8% for coprescribed class I or III anti-arrhythmic drugs. Discussion

Using the largest postmarketing pharmacovigilance database and individual case level analyses, our work systematically assessed AF reporting associated with 141 immunomodulators and we reported the largest characterisation of such cases. After multivariable analysis and Bonferroni correction to enhance specificity, we found a significant overreporting of AF for 20 immunomodulators and among them, rIL-11 highlighted a particularly strong statistical association with AF over-reporting.

In our analysis, recombinant interleukin-11 (rIL-11) was markedly associated with AF reporting, exhibiting an aROR=20.91 (99.96% CI 12.08 to 36.17). In two phase II clinical trials, rIL-11 exhibited a 10% higher incidence of AF or atrial flutter than placebo.17 Recombinant interleukin-11 is used to prevent or treat chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia.17 Recently, IL-11 has been identified as a tumour-promoting cytokine and is being suggested as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in non-small cell lung cancer that accounts for 85% of lung cancers, themselves belonging to the top three solid cancer localisations associated with AF.9 18 In our study, the significant ROR of rIL-11 resisted adjustment, notably when taking into account the drug indication. AF cases affected young patients (48.1% had less than 65 years old) and had low rates of AF-related comorbidities (hypertension, anticoagulant therapy or anti-arrhythmic drug intakes supposedly reflecting low AF-history burden). Furthermore, the median time to onset was short (4 days). All of these characteristics strengthen a possible causal association between rIL-11 and AF reporting. IL-11 belongs to the IL-6 family and they all share the usage of common cytokine receptor subunit glycoprotein 130, explaining some overlapped effects.19 These cytokines have some different mechanisms of action too: for example, IL-6 activates mainly the JAK/STAT3 pathway, whereas it seems that IL-11 at physiological plasmatic level, whose production is notably stimulated by transforming growth factor beta1, would mainly activate ERK pathway in cardiac fibroblasts and thus have profibrotic effect notably by stimulating cardiac fibroblast.20 Nevertheless, at high plasmatic levels, IL-11 is able to activate STAT3 in human cardiac fibroblasts, which is an overlapped action with IL-6. IL-6 is able to significantly modify cardiac action potential by decreasing IK,r and IK,s.21 22 No such evidence exists about IL-11 and to our knowledge, the only study exploring the electrophysiologic effect of IL-11 exhibited no direct effect on single human atrial cardiomyocytes by patch clamp technique but further fundamental studies seem to be needed to explore this aspect of IL-11.23 Indeed, by STAT3, which seems to be a transcriptional factor of FUNDC1, IL-6 would increase intracellular Ca2+ levels and enhance Cav1.2 and RyR2 expression in cardiac cells of mice with septic cardiac dysfunction.24 25 This pathway could promote atrial action potential modification and may be activated by IL-11 at high plasmatic level. In a recent study using an experimental mouse model, IL-11 RNA was upregulated by angiotensin 2 that was significantly associated with higher intensity of trichrome Masson highlighting atrial fibrosis (p<0.001) and this was significantly attenuated by IL-11 antagonist (p<0.001).26 In a retrospective cohort of AF patients, authors found significantly higher IL-11 serum levels in persistent AF patients compared with paroxysmal AF patients, 127.86±23.23 pg/mL vs 103.76±16.86 pg/mL (p<0.001), respectively, whereas physiological median IL-11 levels were 26.4 pg/mL among healthy subjects and patients suffering from pancreatitis.27 Moreover, the literature already points to the potential therapeutic interest of IL-11 inhibitors for several diseases like idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in which IL-11 can be upregulated by 100-fold or systemic sclerosis in which IL-11 can be upregulated by 30-fold. These two mentioned conditions seem to be associated with AF: idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis was associated with an RR=1.94 (95% CI 1.76 to 2.15) in a US claims database study including 9286 patients and multiple sclerosis has a prevalence of sustained atrial arrhythmias between 20% and 30%.28 29 After taking into account these pieces of information about IL-11, our study strengthens this association and the possible therapeutic interest of IL-11 as a druggable target for AF management.

Our literature review highlighted a first cluster of immune components which are mainly stimulated by the 20 immunomodulators associated with AF reporting in our primary analysis (NK cells, Th2 cells, Macrophages, Complement, CD8 T cells and Th1 cells), whereas a second cluster is predominantly inhibited by them (Neutrophils, Tregs cells, Th17 cells, Plasma cells and B cells). The stimulating action of the first cluster suggests the involvement of these immune cells during AF physiopathology, as synthesised in figure 5, which hypothesises an immune mechanism model contributing to AF development. IL-11, in addition to its potential direct action on atrial cardiomyocytes and cardiac fibroblasts, is able to stimulate Th2 cell proliferation which could imply an indirect atrial pro-arrhythmogenic action through NK cell activity (figure 5).30 31 Indeed, our literature review highlighted NK cells as major targets of immunomodulators associated with AF reporting, notably anti-CD20 antibodies, and previous evidence showed that activated NK cells can stimulate macrophage activity, notably through the production of GM-CSF, which was associated with AF overreporting in our analysis.32 Mechanistically, it has been shown that IL-1β secretion by macrophage inhibits myocyte quaking protein expression which contributes to atrial remodelling and Ica,L downregulation (figure 5).33 Macrophages also actively participate in atrial remodelling and fibrosis through the secretion of IL-6 and TGFβ which stimulate fibroblasts and myofibroblasts proliferation and inhibit connexin 40 and 43.34,37 Thus, macrophages may actively participate in AF development, but the upstream signals inducing their recruitment and activation in the atria are not totally understood. However, we know that NLRP3 (nod-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3) inflammasome is able to activate macrophages notably in the atria in a context of AF.38 Furthermore, macrophages can also activate NK cells through the secretion of IL-12, thus forming a positive feedback loop for NK activation.32 The fact that IL-12 is also a strong inducer of Th1 cells could explain the detection of Th1 cells in our literature review. Interestingly, a clinical study has shown that rheumatoid arthritis patients with a greater proportion of Th1 cells in blood had a greater risk of developing AF with an adjusted OR of 1.05 (95% CI 1.02 to 1.08), thus supporting our findings.39 Besides, this higher Th1 activity may also promote the activation of CD8 T cells, for which a stimulating effect of the immunomodulators was also highlighted in our literature review. CD8 T cells could have two major actions on atrial cardiomyocytes thanks to perforin and granzyme B: on the one hand, intracellular calcium increasing and on the other hand, apoptosis which could participate in atrial remodelling.40 Finally, as Th1 cells are good activators of NK cells, notably through the production of IL-2, IL-12 and IL-18, a ‘vicious inflammatory circle’ of activation comprising NK cells, macrophages and Th1 cells could contribute to the ‘AF begets AF’ phenomenon leading to both atrial structural and electrophysiological remodelling (figure 5).41 In this hypothetical model, IL-11 could be a trigger of this ‘vicious inflammatory circle’ and a final effector on the atrial cardiomyocyte, that would be consistent with the strong association observed in our Vigibase analysis between rIL-11 and AF reporting (figure 5).

Clinical implication

Persistent AF remains a daily medical challenge with non-satisfying outcomes of the rhythm control strategy even using catheter ablation with up to 40% of patients experiencing AF recurrences at 1 year after a first procedure.42 Indeed, persistent AF exhibits substrate allowing AF maintenance through no definite mechanisms but several explanations have been proposed.41 This substrate is the expression of atrial remodelling which mixes complex interaction between inflammation, fibrosis, metabolic changes and electrophysiologic changes, but all these aspects are far from being fully understood.37 41 Although the role of inflammation in AF physiopathology is now well established, current guidelines for management of AF by European Society of Cardiology do not suggest the use of immunomodulators by lack of positive randomised controlled trials and limit their recommendations to upstream therapy like loss of weight or continuous positive airway pressure.43 Immunity modulation has been studied in hope of a new therapeutic approach, but no druggable target has been identified so far.44 Colchicine has already been shown to be effective in AF prevention, notably after pulmonary vein isolation with an RR=0.66 (95% CI 0.54 to 0.81) in a recent meta-analysis.45 Nevertheless, colchicine suffers from several adverse drug reactions limiting its use.45 In order to improve AF-free survival after persistent AF ablation, corticosteroids have been tried. A monocentric single-blind placebo-controlled randomised trial including 156 paroxysmal AF and 46 persistent AF comparing methylprednisolone intravenous for 2 days followed by oral methylprednisolone for 4 days vs placebo did not exhibit significant difference in AF recurrence after 24 months follow-up.46 More targeted modulations of immune pathways used as adjuvant or neo-adjuvant therapies associated with AF ablation in a homogenous population of persistent AF patients may increase the AF recurrence-free survival in the future.38 47 However, several challenges remain, particularly in selecting the appropriate target and identifying suitable therapeutic agents. Some targeted therapies appear theoretically promising based on their mechanisms of action—for example, tocilizumab (an anti-IL-6 receptor antibody) and LASN01 (an anti-IL-11 receptor antibody). LASN01 has recently completed a phase 2 trial for the treatment of Graves’ ophthalmopathy, although the results are not yet available.48 A key consideration will be whether these agents demonstrate an acceptable safety profile for use as (neo)adjuvant therapy in AF ablation. For instance, in a recent phase III trial in patients with rheumatoid arthritis, tocilizumab was associated with a 19.9% incidence of upper respiratory tract infections and an 11% rate of serious adverse events, although no specific cardiac adverse events were reported.49 Therefore, clinical studies will be essential to identify the optimal target, select the appropriate immunomodulator and determine the ideal timing and dosage to maximise efficacy while ensuring safety.

Study limitations

Pharmacovigilance databases inherently suffer from some biases such as underreporting, notoriety, halo bias or lack of information on the exposed population and sales volumes of drugs. Missing data and lack of information include several clinical observations or conditions (such as previous radiotherapy and/or surgery, AF paroxysmal or persistent type nor AF ablation history) that represent potential confounding factors for AF development, but we did a multivariable analysis with coprescriptions which indirectly account comorbidities and we did multiple imputation analysis which tends to alleviate bias related to missing data. In addition, adjustment for variables that may be influenced by both exposure and outcome could introduce collider bias, potentially distorting observed associations. Although covariates were selected a priori based on clinical plausibility and sensitivity analyses yielded consistent results, adjusted estimates should be interpreted cautiously and not as causal effects. Furthermore, we applied a Bonferroni correction, a usual conservative approach, to minimise inflation of the type I error rate; consequently, some drugs that may induce AF might not have reached statistical significance in our analysis.16 The case/non-case design of our analyses cannot provide evidence of a definitive causal relationship between the 20 immunomodulators identified and AF development. Since our analyses were performed on overreporting and not over risk, we were not able to determine which immunomodulator is most likely to cause AF but could only list immunomodulator for which AF was overreported, which is an indirect approach to determine AF risk. The suggested physiopathologic immune model in AF development (figure 5) is founded on existing literary evidence, rendering it hypothetic in nature. Future experimental investigations are essential for affirming, partially validating or refuting these postulated mechanisms.

Conclusions

Using the largest postmarketing pharmacovigilance database and individual case level analyses, our work identified 20 immunomodulators significantly associated with AF reporting and among them, rIL-11 appeared highly associated. These data contribute substantively to the prevailing understanding of the safety profile of these immunomodulators. Furthermore, through the mechanism of action of each immunomodulator associated with AF reporting, our literature review highlighted several immune components, notably IL-11, macrophages, T lymphocytes and NK cells, possibly involved in a ‘vicious circle’ leading to the ‘AF begets AF’ phenomenon. Unravelling these immune cellular pathways could constitute a promising approach for the potential use of future immune targeted therapeutics as adjuvant or neo-adjuvant treatment associated with AF ablation, particularly in patient with persistent AF.

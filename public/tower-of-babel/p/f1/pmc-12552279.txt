Survival analysis and prognosis model construction of elderly patients with acute promyelocyticleukemia: a retrospective study based on SEER database
Authors: Jinhong Jiang, Yonghua Liu, Yuxiao Zeng, Yifen Lan, Bingmu Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe survival and death cause in elderly acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL) patients were analyzed and a prognosis model was constructed. Retrospectively, the medical data of elderly APL patients (N = 1723, from year 2000 to 2020) were gained from surveillance, epidemiology, and end results (SEER) database, and patients were randomly divided into train set and test set 1 (7:3). Test set 2 was composed of 17 APL patients from our hospital. Single factor and multi-factor analyses were performed by COX regression analysis. Death causes were analyzed among dead APL patients. Prognosis prediction model was constructed and verified. Single factor analysis results showed that age, marital status, income, year of diagnosis, months from diagnosis to treatment and chemotherapy were highly correlated with the death of APL patients. Multi-factor analysis results indicated that age, year of diagnosis and chemotherapy could independently serve as predictors to the death of APL patients. More patient succumbed to APL relative to other causes. The prognosis model had a higher diagnostic value for 0.5-year (AUC = 0.797) overall survival in train set, and for 2-year (AUC = 0.784) overall survival in test set 1. 1-year survival prediction in train set and test set 1 indicated the stability of the model. Age, year of diagnosis and chemotherapy are the independent risk factors, and APL is the leading cause of death in elderly APL patients. The prognosis model has a good clinical prediction value for elderly APL patients.Keywords: Acute promyelocytic leukemia, Survival analysis, Prognosis, Prediction model, SEER database

Background

Acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), as a subtype, accounts for 10% of acute myeloid leukemia cases and has a poor prognosis in the elderly patients [1, 2]. PML: RARα is the driving oncogene in APL and is responsible for the maturation arrest at the promyelocytic stage. The disease was renamed APL with PML: RARα by the WHO in 2016 to distinguish it from variant APL and occult genetic abnormalities [3]. About1/3 of APL patients are above the age of sixty [4]. The pathogenesis of APL is extremely complicated, and the translocations of chromosome 15 and chromosome 17 (q24; q21) result in 95% of APL patients [5].The typical symptoms of APL are bleeding, fever, bone and joint pain. Existing evidence has revealed that the early mortality in APL patients is up to 30% and APL is considered as the most threatening subtype of leukemia [6]. Currently, the timely non chemotherapy of all-trans retinoic acid and arsenic trioxide is associated with 80–90% cure rate of APL [7].Yet, the prognosis in the elderly APL patients is poor [8].Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to accurately evaluate the prognosis of elderly APL patients and screen out related risk factors.

With the help of surveillance, epidemiology, and end results (SEER) database, the survival influencing factors and death causes of the elderly APL population can be assessed, which can provide reference for designing suitable treatment strategies [9–11].SEER database has recorded the incidence, mortality and prevalence in millions of malignant tumor patients, which is an authoritative cancer statistics database in USA and has a large sample size with strong statistical efficiency [12, 13]. Moreover, the SEER database has clinical data mainly including demographic data, personal information, treatment plan, and death cause, and acts as a good clinical research data source for clinical researchers.

In this study, elderly APL case data in SEER database were used to retrospectively analyze the survival factors and death causes, and the risk factors involved in the occurrence of APL were further dissected. Based on these risk factors, a prognosis prediction model was constructed to assess the overall survival, which could provide a reference for risk stratification and clinical treatment of APL.

Methods

Research design and data source

This was retrospective research. The APL is divided into secondary APL (s-APL) and new-onset (dn-APL).The clinical data (from year 2000 to 2020) of 1723 APL cases were gained from the SEER database (https://seer.cancer.gov/data/), and the cases were randomly divided into train set and test set 1 in a ratio of 7:3 (data from U.S. patients). The test set 2 (data from Chinese patients) was composed of 17 APL patients from our hospital (April 2009 to April 2023), who have signed the written informed consent. The train set is the primary source of data for model learning and contains the features and labels that the model needs to learn patterns from. Through the train set, the model is able to adjust its internal parameters to make reasonable predictions when new samples are input. The test set is the last set of data that is completely independent of the training and validation sets and is used to evaluate the final performance of the model.

Participants

The APL case data that met the inclusion/exclusion criteria of this study were obtained from the SEER database. Inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosis as APL based on molecular detection of PML-RARA fusion transcript by RT-PCR; (2) year of diagnosis at 2000–2020 (2000–2005; 2006–2010; 2011–2015; 2016–2020); (3) age ≥ 60; (4) complete clinical information. Exclusion criteria: (1) abnormal consciousness level; (2) other hematologic diseases.

Collection and analysis of the clinical data

The baseline characteristic data of elderly APL patients were collected, including age, sex (female or male), race (white or other), marital status (married or other), income, chemotherapy (cytotoxic agents (anthracycline) treatment or non-cytotoxic treatments/unknown), vital status (dead or alive) and lethal disease (APL or other), year of diagnosis (2000–2020) and months from diagnosis to treatment (0 or ≥ 1 month/unknown). Three sub-groups were constructed based on age including ≥ 80 years, 70–79 years and 60-69 years, and based on income including < 5000USD (low income), 5000-75000USD (medium income) and > 75000USD (high income).

Construction and verification of survival nomogram model

The APL patient’s baseline information (including age, marital status, income, months from diagnosis to treatment and chemotherapy) was correlated with the survival time/state, and the survival Nomogram model was constructed with R language rms package and cph function. Then the risk Nomogram was drawn using regplot function. The Nomogram model was verified in the train set, test set 1 and test set 2. The receiver operator characteristic (ROC) survival curves of train set, test set 1 and test set 2 for 0.5 years, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years and 5 years, and the calibration curve of 1-year total survival period prediction were plotted, respectively. The area under the curve (AUC) value was used to assess the prediction effect. The higher AUC value represented the higher diagnostic value.

Statistical analysis

The numerical variables in the basic characteristics were transformed into classified variables, and the number of cases and percentage were statistically described. Kaplan-Meier (K-M) method and logarithmic rank test were severally used to draw the survival curve and compare the survival rate. Using R language rms package and survival package, COX regression model was established by coxph method, including the single factor and the multi-factor COX regression analyses. The mortality curve from 2000 to 2020 was drawn. The death causes of age, income and chemotherapy were analyzed among dead APL patients, which were compared by R×C and 2 × 2 chi-square test. Bilateral P < 0.05 was considered as significant difference.

The relationships of the characteristics (age, sex, race, marital status, income, year of diagnosis, months from diagnosis to treatment and chemotherapy) and survival time/status were analyzed using the K-M survival curves (Fig. 3A-3 H). At the time point of 50 months, the age-related K-M survival curve showed that 26 APL patients were alleviate age of ≥ 80 years, 171 at the age of 70–79 years and 356 at the age of 60–69 years (Fig. 3A). Moreover, we found that the number of alive APL patients in all age sub-groups was gradually reduced at the time points of 100, 150, 200 and 250 months (Fig. 3A). With the prolongation of time, the number of alive APL patients was also gradually reduced in other characteristic-related K-M survival curves (Fig. 3B-3 H). At the time point of 250 months, all APL patients were dead in each K-M survival curve (Fig. 3A-3 H).

Construction and verification of nomogram model

After correlating patients’ baseline information with survival time/status, we constructed the survival Nomogram model using the baseline characteristics of age, marital status, income, months from diagnosis to treatment and chemotherapy, and then we obtained a risk Nomogram (Fig. 5). Our outcomes revealed that the older age, lower income, unmarried status and chemotherapy contributed to the risk of death.

Construction of survival Nomogram model

Next, we verified the Nomogram model using the train set, test set 1 and test set 2. The ROC survival curves of train set, test set 1 and test set 2 for 0.5 years, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years and 5 years, and the calibration curve of 1-year total survival period prediction were drawn, respectively. The verification results were displayed in (Fig. 6A-6F). Our data supported that the all-AUC values in the train set for 0.5 years, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years and 5 years were above 0.77, and the 0.5-year survival prediction effect is the best (Fig. 6A). The AUC values in the test set 1 were between 0.759 and 0.784, which indicated a good prediction effect (Fig. 6B). However, the test set 2 only performed well in the 5-year survival prediction (Fig. 6C). Figures 6D-6E showed that the1-year survival prediction in the train set and the test set 1 were all around the diagonal and consistent with the diagonal dotted line, which implied that the model was stable. However, 1-year survival prediction in the test set 2 was deviated from the diagonal seriously, and the result might have some errors (Fig. 6F).

Discussion

With the aging trend of society, the incidence rate of APL is on the rise [9, 14]. In the present study, the clinical data of elderly APL patients were searched in the SEER database and used to analyze the survival and death causes. We found that age, marital status, income, year of diagnosis, months from diagnosis to treatment and chemotherapy were highly correlated with the death of APL patients, of which the age, year of diagnosis and chemotherapy were the independent risk factors of APL. The prognosis model for the survival prediction was stable in the train set and test set 1, while the coincidence was poor in the test set 2.

With the advance of medical technology, the treatment outcomes for APL patients have improved dramatically in recent years [15–17]. Similar to previous reports, our data supported that the mortality of APL patients was gradually decreased from 2000 to 2020 and reached the lowest level in 2016. In addition, there are some risk factors that can affect the survival outcome of elderly APL patients, such as age, year of diagnosis and chemotherapy [9, 18, 19], consistent with our results. As early reported, the older age represents the lower survival rate in elderly APL patients [18, 20]. Elderly APL patients with earlier diagnosis year have lower cumulative survival, which may be related to the continuous improvement of diagnosis and treatment technologies [9]. Some studies revealed that the elderly APL patients without chemotherapy may have better survival outcome than those with chemotherapy [19, 21–23]. On the contrary, some reports unveiled that the elderly APL patients benefit from chemotherapy or dose-reducing chemotherapy [24–26]. These findings demonstrated that the enhancement in accurate diagnosis ability contributes to the survival outcome of elderly APL patients. At the same time, it is the goal of medical research to cure APL without chemotherapy.

Reportedly, the mortality of elderly APL patients’ peaks in the early stage of treatment, commonly accompanied with the death complications of coagulation dysfunction, vital organ dysfunction and severe infection [25, 27–29]. In this study, the death causes for elderly APL patients were analyzed. Our data showed that 760 patients died of APL and 300 died of other causes, indicating that the APL was the main cause of death in elderly APL patients. The optimization of early treatment strategy for APL is the key to reverse the death outcome of elderly APL population.

Furthermore, a prognosis model was constructed, which was also named survival Nomogram model. The Nomogram is a visual rendition of regression equation, which renders the prediction process of the model more intuitive and expedient for clinicians to assess the overall survival of APL patients [30, 31].The prognosis model for the survival prediction has been verified to have a good coincidence in the train set and test set 1. Yet, the coincidence was poor in the test set 2, which might be associated with the factors of race and region. The train set was developed based on U.S. patients, while the test set 2 included patients from China. These findings indicated the necessity of accumulating additional data from elderly APL sufferers within China to establish a survival prediction model that caters to domestic APL patients. Although the study did not directly collect international comparative data, the identified risk factors (low income, unmarried status and chemotherapy) are worth discussing in an international context. Future multi-country studies are needed to confirm these assumptions.

Limitations

Here are several limitations. First of all, the 1-year survival prediction in the test set 2 was deviated from the diagonal seriously, and the result might have some errors. The large error of the test set 2 might result from the small number of samples, missing information such as income and the interval between onset and diagnosis, and ethnic differences-induced prediction deviation. Secondly, insurance status data were unavailable in our cohort, which precluded assessment of this potentially important socioeconomic factor known to impact APL outcomes. Thirdly, the reasons for not initiating treatment (e.g., patient fitness, comorbidities, or socioeconomic factors such as insurance coverage) were not available in the dataset, which may affect the interpretation of treatment disparities. Future research should prospectively collect these details. Finaly, although the prognostic model developed in this study showed good predictive performance, we must admit that its universality has certain limitations and may not fully represent other medical environments or population characteristics. Future research should focus on: multi-center validation studies; model adjustments under different medical resource allocations; cross-racial/ethnic calibration tests. These efforts will help build more generalizable predictive tools.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we found that age, year of di agnosis and chemotherapy are the independent risk factors of elderly APL patients, and APL is the leading contributor of death in elderly APL patients. Moreover, the prognosis model has a good clinical prediction value for elderly APL patients, which is worthy of popularization and application. In future investigations, we will collect the data of chemotherapy intensity and early death complications of elderly APL patients, so as to provide reference for optimizing treatment scheme and preventing complications.

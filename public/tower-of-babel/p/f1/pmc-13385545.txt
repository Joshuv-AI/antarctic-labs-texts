Multi-omics analysis reveals TM4SF19 as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in bladder cancer
Authors: Xierzhati Aizezi, Bahatiguli Silafu, Hanzhen Shi, Jinxing Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveBladder cancer poses a significant clinical challenge due to its high recurrence and heterogeneity, necessitating novel biomarkers. To explore the multidimensional role of TM4SF19, a tetraspanin family member, as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in bladder cancer (BLCA) through multi-omics analysis.MethodsThis study integrated multi-omics data from public databases (TCGA, GEPIA, cBioPortal, TIMER, LinkedOmics) to analyze TM4SF19 expression, genomic alterations, prognostic significance, immune infiltration associations, and drug sensitivity in bladder cancer. Analytical methods included differential expression analysis (limma package), survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression), nomogram construction, functional enrichment (GSEA, KEGG), immune microenvironment assessment (MCPcounter, TIMER), and drug sensitivity evaluation (GDSC database). Statistical analyses were performed using R software (v4.0.2) with significance set at p < 0.05ResultsTM4SF19 was significantly upregulated in bladder cancer tissues (p < 0.01) and demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.831). High TM4SF19 expression correlated with advanced pathological stage, lymphovascular invasion, and poorer overall survival (p < 0.05). Functional analysis linked TM4SF19 to PI3K-Akt/MAPK signaling pathways and immune modulation, showing negative correlations with CD8⁺ T cells and B cell infiltration. TM4SF19 expression also negatively correlated with sensitivity to Trametinib and Docetaxel but positively with Navitoclax and AR-42, indicating its role in chemotherapy resistance.ConclusionTM4SF19 is a promising diagnostic and independent prognostic biomarker in BLCA, influencing tumor progression through signaling pathways and immune microenvironment modulation. Its association with chemotherapy resistance highlights its potential as a therapeutic target.

Introduction

Bladder cancer is a common malignant tumor of the urinary system, with its incidence increasing globally year by year, particularly prevalent among males [1]. This disease not only imposes a significant burden on patients’ physical and mental health but also causes enormous economic pressure on society [2]. According to cancer epidemiological data, bladder cancer ranks among the top malignant tumors in males, and the prognosis of advanced patients is poor, with a 5-year survival rate of less than 50% [3]. Traditional diagnosis relies on histopathology and imaging, but their insufficient sensitivity leads to delayed treatment [4]. Meanwhile, existing prognostic biomarkers have limitations in clinical application, resulting in a lack of efficient tools for early diagnosis and individualized prognostic prediction [5]. Therefore, identifying novel biomarkers has become an urgent need in bladder cancer research, which is expected to optimize risk stratification, predict immunotherapy response, and improve drug development strategies.

Research on bladder cancer has gradually revealed its molecular basis [6]. For example, studies have shown that the occurrence of bladder cancer is closely related to expression changes of multiple genes involved in important biological processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis. TM4SF19 (Transmembrane 4 L6 Family Member 19) belongs to the tetraspanin family. Members of this family are widely involved in the assembly and functional regulation of membrane signal complexes by forming protein interaction networks [7]. Recent studies have indicated that TM4SF19 may drive the invasive and migratory phenotypes of tumor cells by regulating integrin-mediated cell adhesion and activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway [8, 9]. Its abnormal expression in various solid tumors has been confirmed: In neck cancer, overexpression of TM4SF19 promotes the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process [10, 11]; In lung squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC), the TM4SF19 promoter is recruited by a complex formed by TM4SF19-AS1 and WDR5, leading to DNA demethylation and subsequent inhibition of malignant progression [12]. These studies showed that TM4SF19 plays a key role in the malignant progression of different solid tumors through multiple mechanisms such as regulating cell adhesion, activating signaling pathways, and participating in epigenetic regulation.

Although the oncogenic mechanisms of TM4SF19 in various cancers have been preliminarily elucidated, its role in bladder cancer still has the following key limitations: First, existing studies lack in-depth exploration of the functional mechanisms of TM4SF19. For instance, it remains unclear whether TM4SF19 drives bladder cancer progression by regulating key nodes of the cell cycle or epigenetic modifications (such as promoter methylation), nor has the molecular link between TM4SF19 and chemoresistance or immune escape been clarified. Second, due to the lack of cross-omics integration analysis (e.g., association between genomic copy number variations and drug sensitivity), the translational value of TM4SF19 as a therapeutic target has been underestimated.

Hence, this study aims to fill the above gaps by comprehensively exploring the multidimensional role of TM4SF19 in bladder cancer through multi-omics data mining. For the first time, we integrate authoritative public databases such as TCGA, GEPIA, and cBioPortal to systematically evaluate the expression characteristics, prognostic value, immune infiltration association, and drug sensitivity of TM4SF19, thereby illustrate its potential as a novel diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for bladder cancer.

Materials and methods

Data source and preprocessing

The dataset utilized in this investigation was acquired from various publicly available databases to enhance both the comprehensiveness and dependability of the information. Gene expression data and clinical details pertaining to bladder cancer patients were sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://www.cancer.gov/tcga). To corroborate gene expression trends and examine the association between TM4SF19 expression and both overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in bladder cancer patients, the GEPIA database (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/) was employed. Furthermore, cBioPortal facilitated the analysis of genomic data regarding the prevalence of genomic alterations of TM4SF19 among bladder cancer patients (https://www.cbioportal.org/). Additionally, immune infiltration data were sourced from the TIMER database (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/). LinkedOmics (https://www.linkedomics.org/login.php), a publicly accessible platform, integrates multi-omics data across all 32 TCGA cancer types and 10 CPTAC cancer cohorts. Prior to analysis, all data underwent stringent quality control procedures, which included the elimination of low-quality samples and normalization of expression data.

Differential expression analysis

The limma algorithm was employed to identify differentially expressed genes associated with TM4SF19, utilizing a threshold defined by |log2FC| > 1 and FDR < 0.05. To assess the diagnostic potential of TM4SF19 in bladder cancer, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was analyzed, and the area under the curve (AUC) was determined through the DeLong test. For the correlation analysis of clinical characteristics, the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied for categorical variables with more than two groups, while the Mann-Whitney U test was utilized for binary variables. Graphical representations were generated using the ggplot2 package in R. A significance level of P < 0.05 was established to ensure the robustness of the findings.

Survival analysis and Cox regression model

Patients were categorized into high and low expression cohorts according to the median expression level of TM4SF19. Differential expression analysis of TM4SF19 between tumor and adjacent normal tissues was performed using the limma package (version 3.50.3) in R. A linear model was fitted using the lmFit function with a design matrix specifying the sample groups (Tumor vs. Normal). Empirical Bayes moderation was applied via the eBayes function to compute moderated t-statistics. The resulting p-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to control the False Discovery Rate (FDR). Genes with an adjusted p-value (FDR) < 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) > 1 were considered significantly differentially expressed.

For survival analysis, patients were stratified into “TM4SF19-high” and “TM4SF19-low” expression groups based on the median expression value of TM4SF19 across all tumor samples. This median cut-off was consistently applied to both the primary TCGA cohort analysis and the external validation performed using the GEPIA2 online tool to ensure comparability of the results. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were plotted, and the log-rank test was used to assess the statistical significance of differences in overall survival (OS) between the two groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to evaluate the independent prognostic value of TM4SF19, along with other clinical variables.

Nomogram construction and validation

The rms package in the R programming language facilitated the development of a Nomogram that consolidates independent prognostic factors identified through multivariate Cox regression analysis, enabling the prediction of overall survival (OS) rates for bladder cancer patients at 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year intervals. The scoring system employed in the Nomogram was derived from the regression coefficients obtained from the Cox model, and calibration curves were utilized to assess the precision of the predicted outcomes.

Functional cluster analysis

The R language’s clusterProfiler package facilitated the functional clustering analysis of TM4SF19 co-expressed genes, encompassing enrichment evaluations for biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), molecular functions (MF), and KEGG pathways. Furthermore, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted utilizing hallmark gene sets sourced from MSigDB, with the normalized enrichment score (NES) derived through permutation testing.

Immune infiltration analysis

The MCPcounter package within the R programming environment was employed to assess the correlation between TM4SF19 expression levels and the extent of immune cell infiltration in the tumor microenvironment. We conducted a comparative analysis of the infiltration levels of various immune cell types in patient samples exhibiting high versus low TM4SF19 expression, aiming to elucidate the potential link between TM4SF19 and the characteristics of the immune microenvironment. Furthermore, the TIMER web tool was utilized to investigate the relationship between TM4SF19 expression and immune cell infiltration. Our analysis specifically focused on the associations between TM4SF19 and various immune cell subtypes, including CD8⁺ T cells, CD4⁺ T cells, B cells, neutrophils, and dendritic cells, among others.

SCNA and drug sensitivity analysis

Utilizing the LinkedOmics platform, we conducted an analysis of somatic copy number variations to investigate the correlation between the expression profile of the TM4SF19 gene in bladder cancer and clinical outcomes. Specifically, we performed a comprehensive analysis that integrated somatic copy number alterations (SCNA) of TM4SF19 with RNA-sequencing data derived from bladder cancer (BLCA) samples within the TCGA database. Our focus was on elucidating the relationship between copy number variation (CNV) and mRNA expression, applying stringent screening criteria (adjusted p-value < 0.05 and CNV log2 fold change > 1). Furthermore, advanced visualization tools were employed to facilitate the interpretation of the findings. In addition, we incorporated Gene Set Cancer Analysis (GSCA) to evaluate drug sensitivity, which encompasses 33 types of cancer (including data from TCGA) and small-molecule drugs sourced from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database. This integration allowed for a thorough assessment of gene sets, including mRNA expression, mutations, immune cell infiltration, and drug resistance, thereby providing a comprehensive framework for investigating the sensitivity of drugs targeting TM4SF19-related pathways.

Statistical software and graph plotting

The statistical evaluations conducted in this research primarily utilized the R programming language (version 4.0.2). In particular, the limma package facilitated the analysis of differential expression, while the survival package was employed for conducting survival analyses and Cox regression models. The rms package was utilized for the construction of nomograms and the generation of calibration curves, whereas the ggplot2 package was tasked with producing high-quality visual representations. Furthermore, various online resources and databases, including Xiantao Academic, TIMER, LinkedOmics, GSCA, GEPIA, and cBioPortal, were instrumental in supporting specific analytical and visualization needs. The threshold for statistical significance across all tests was established at P < 0.05, with * indicating P < 0.05, ** indicating P < 0.01, and*** indicating P < 0.001.

Results

Analysis of TM4SF19 expression pattern

In this study, we performed a comprehensive analysis of the expression profile of TM4SF19 in bladder cancer, revealing a notable disparity when compared to normal tissues. As illustrated in Fig. 1A, we detected variations in TM4SF19 expression across various cancer types. The heatmap presented in Fig. 1B delineates the distribution of genes that exhibit differential expression. Furthermore, the volcano plot depicted in Fig. 1C indicates the statistical significance of these differentially expressed genes. Figure 1D and E specifically highlight the disparity in TM4SF19 expression between bladder cancer patients and normal subjects, as well as the alterations in expression levels between bladder cancer and healthy samples. Notably, TM4SF19 expression in bladder cancer was found to be significantly elevated compared to that in normal tissues (P < 0.01). Additionally, the ROC curve analysis demonstrates that TM4SF19 possesses commendable diagnostic utility for bladder cancer, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.831 and a 95% confidence interval (CI) ranging from 0.748 to 0.913 (Fig. 1F). The genomic analysis via cBioPortal indicated a gene alteration frequency of 5% for TM4SF19 in bladder cancer, predominantly attributed to amplification (Fig. 1G).

Correlation between TM4SF19 and clinical characteristics

A statistical examination was conducted on the data pertaining to bladder cancer patients sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Figure 2 illustrates the correlation between TM4SF19 expression and various clinical parameters, including Race, Gender, Age, Histologic grade, Subtype, T-stage, N-stage, M-stage, and the pathological stage of bladder cancer patients. The results reveal that TM4SF19 is markedly upregulated in bladder cancer and exhibits significant associations with diverse clinical characteristics (P < 0.05). To ascertain the impact of TM4SF19 expression on patient survival, bladder cancer patients were categorized into high and low TM4SF19 expression groups utilizing the GEPIA (Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis) database. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicates that individuals in the high TM4SF19 expression cohort experience significantly reduced overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) compared to those in the low expression cohort (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3A, B). Additionally, a Kaplan-Meier analysis leveraging the TCGA database was conducted to further investigate the relationship between TM4SF19 expression and the prognosis of bladder cancer patients. The findings indicate that elevated TM4SF19 expression correlates with diminished OS, disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3C, E). The prognostic subgroup assessments depicted in Fig. 3F and I reinforce the association between high TM4SF19 expression and unfavorable prognosis in bladder cancer (P < 0.05).

Cox regression analysis of prognostic factors and nomogram construction

We conducted an assessment of the distinct influence of TM4SF19 expression alongside various clinical attributes on the prognosis of patients diagnosed with bladder cancer, utilizing the Cox proportional hazards model. The results revealed that TM4SF19 expression serves as an independent prognostic indicator for the overall survival (OS) of individuals with bladder cancer (HR = 1.528, 95% CI [1.062–2.199], P = 0.022). Moreover, age (HR = 1.566, 95% CI [1.093–2.244], P = 0.015) and lymphovascular invasion (HR = 2.181, 95% CI [1.470–3.236], P < 0.001) were also determined to be independent prognostic factors for this patient population (Table 1). Figure 4A illustrates a nomogram designed to predict OS, which incorporates the independent prognostic factors identified via multivariate Cox regression analysis, aimed at estimating the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival rates for bladder cancer patients. Additionally, the calibration curve depicted in Fig. 4B is employed to assess the precision of the predictions made.

The results presented in Fig. 5A illustrate the enrichment of these co-expressed genes across various biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), molecular functions (MF), and related Genes and Genomes (KEGG). This analysis indicates a predominant enrichment in categories such as contractile fiber, muscle system process, signaling receptor activator activity, PI3K − Akt signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, etc. Subsequently, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) to identify signaling pathways related to TM4SF19 (Figs. 5B-F), to further clarify TM4SF19-related signaling pathways. These analyses reveal the biological processes and molecular mechanisms that TM4SF19 may be involved in, as well as its impact on related signaling pathways, providing directions for further functional research.

Relationship between TM4SF19 expression and immune infiltration

In order to investigate the relationship between the expression levels of TM4SF19 and the presence of immune cells in patients with bladder cancer, the TIMER (Tumor Immune Estimation Resource) platform was employed to analyze data from TCGA-BLCA. As depicted in Fig. 6A, a correlation exists between TM4SF19 expression and immune cell infiltration. The findings further indicate that TM4SF19 exhibits a notable negative correlation with B cell activity (p = 5.45e-04) and a partial correlation with the activity of CD8 + T cells (p = 5.31e-09), as illustrated in Fig. 6B. Survival analysis presented in Fig. 6C reveals a significant association between the expression levels of TM4SF19 and the survival rates of bladder cancer patients (p < 0.05). Additionally, in-depth analysis of the tumor immune microenvironment demonstrated that the degree of infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells (p = 0.006) significantly influences the survival prognosis of these patients. Collectively, these findings imply that TM4SF19 may play a role in the progression of bladder cancer through the modulation of the immune microenvironment.

SCNA of TM4SF19 is associated with drug metabolism in BLCA

In the LinkedOmics platform, the somatic copy number alteration (SCNA) data for TM4SF19 derived from the TCGA-BLCA dataset were amalgamated with RNA sequencing data from the same patient cohort for further examination. The volcano plot illustrated in Fig. 7A indicates that the genes positioned on the right (depicted as dark red dots) exhibit a significant positive correlation with TM4SF19-SCNA, whereas those on the left (illustrated as dark green dots) demonstrate a notable negative correlation. The accompanying heatmap (Fig. 7B) presents the expression profiles of genes that yield the most significant p-values. Furthermore, enrichment analysis performed on the identified upregulated and downregulated genes suggests that various biological functions are associated with TM4SF19 SCNA (Fig. 7C). An analysis focused on drug metabolism uncovered the most substantial correlations (Fig. 7D). Consequently, given that the downregulated genes linked to TM4SF19-SCNA are implicated in drug metabolism, an investigation into the drug sensitivity of TM4SF19 was conducted using the GDSC (Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer) database to explore the interplay between TM4SF19 and various pharmacological agents (Fig. 6E). Drug sensitivity analysis showed that TM4SF19 is correlated with the sensitivity of multiple drugs. Trametinib, Bleomycin (50 µM), Docetaxel, etc., are significantly negatively correlated with TM4SF19. In contrast, Navitoclax and AR-42, etc., are significantly positively correlated with TM4SF19.

Discussion

Bladder cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide, with its incidence and mortality rates increasing annually. Although significant progress has been made in the early diagnosis and treatment of bladder cancer in recent years, patient prognosis remains unsatisfactory due to its heterogeneity and high recurrence rate [13]. This is particularly true for patients with high-grade and invasive bladder cancer, where disease progression is rapid, highlighting the urgent need to identify novel biomarkers to improve prognostic assessment and personalized treatment strategies [14]. Studies have shown that Transmembrane 4 L6 family member 19 (TM4SF19) plays an important biological role in various tumor types, and its expression level is closely associated with tumor initiation, progression, and prognosis [7, 15].

This study provides the first systematic analysis of the expression characteristics of TM4SF19 in bladder urothelial carcinoma. The results revealed that TM4SF19 is significantly overexpressed in tumor tissues, which is consistent with previous reports of its upregulation in other malignancies such as oral squamous cell carcinoma [8]. Further ROC curve analysis demonstrated that TM4SF19 has high diagnostic value, suggesting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker for bladder urothelial carcinoma. This finding aligns with reports that other members of the TM4SF family serve as biomarkers in various cancers [11]. Specifically in bladder cancer, identifying reliable tissue or liquid biopsy markers remains a challenge, and TM4SF19 could be a candidate for further development in this direction. The clinical advantage of TM4SF19 detection lies in its specificity and stability, making it a promising auxiliary indicator for early diagnosis. However, its limitation is that it must be evaluated in conjunction with other clinical features, as a single biomarker is insufficient for precise diagnosis [16]. Moreover, TM4SF19 may participate in tumorigenesis and progression by affecting cytoskeletal reorganization and signaling pathways [7], providing a molecular biological basis for its application as a diagnostic marker. Related mechanistic studies have also shown that TM4SF19 and its associated long non-coding RNAs play key roles in tumor cell proliferation, migration, and tumor microenvironment regulation, further supporting the value of TM4SF19 as a potential diagnostic and therapeutic target in bladder urothelial carcinoma [12].

This study also found that TM4SF19 expression is significantly correlated with clinical features, particularly age, pathological stage, and lymphatic invasion. Previous research on other TM4SF family members has also indicated that upregulated TM4SF19 expression in oral squamous cell carcinoma is closely associated with clinical stage and lymph node metastasis [8], while in prostate cancer, TM4SF19 acts as a radiation tolerance-related gene involved in predicting biochemical recurrence [17]. These differences may stem from the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment and the functional specificity of TM4SF19 across different tissues, such as its regulation by oxidative stress and epigenetic mechanisms [10, 12]. Therefore, a comprehensive consideration of additional influencing factors is necessary when evaluating the clinical relevance of TM4SF19.

Cox regression analysis indicated that TM4SF19 is an independent prognostic factor for overall survival (OS) in bladder cancer patients, underscoring its potential for clinical application. This complements the existing landscape of prognostic models. Unlike the ADME-gene model which focuses on metabolic capacity [18], or the machine learning model centered on mitotic catastrophe [19], our work establishes TM4SF19 as a concise, functionally rich single-gene prognostic indicator. This offers a potentially more straightforward and biologically interpretable tool for clinical assessment.The constructed nomogram effectively integrated TM4SF19 expression with other clinical features. Functional clustering analysis revealed that TM4SF19 may promote malignant behaviors by affecting key signaling pathways such as PI3K-Akt and MAPK, which play central roles in cell proliferation, apoptosis, and migration.The pathway enrichment strategies employed in this study, such as Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), are well-established and reliable methods in current multi-omics research for deciphering core tumor mechanisms. Their effectiveness has been recently demonstrated in studies exploring metabolic reprogramming and cell death processes in cancers like clear cell renal cell carcinoma [20, 21]. In the context of bladder cancer, the PI3K-Akt and MAPK pathways are frequently dysregulated and contribute to therapy resistance and aggressive behavior. Our findings suggest that TM4SF19 could be an upstream modulator or a critical component of these pathways in bladder cancer, providing a mechanistic link between its high expression and poor prognosis. Studies on GTP-binding proteins suggest that these proteins may regulate cell growth and differentiation processes [22], supporting the notion that TM4SF19 may contribute to poor prognosis by interfering with these pathways, though detailed mechanisms require further exploration.

Immune infiltration analysis showed that TM4SF19 expression is significantly correlated with immune cell infiltration levels in the tumor microenvironment, particularly exhibiting a negative correlation with CD8⁺ T cells and B cells, suggesting its potential role in the survival prognosis of bladder cancer patients. The immune infiltration analysis using the TIMER algorithm in this study is a common and validated approach for assessing tumor microenvironment composition. Similar analytical frameworks have been successfully applied in cross-disease studies, such as exploring the genetic interplay between atherosclerosis and lung adenocarcinoma, to reveal the potential role of immune cells in complex disease networks [23]. For bladder cancer, which is notably immunogenic and responsive to immunotherapy, the creation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment is a key mechanism of progression and treatment failure. The inverse correlation between TM4SF19 and cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells implies that it may facilitate immune evasion, a hypothesis that aligns with the observed worse survival in high-expression patients. Members of the tetraspanin family have been shown to affect immune cell function by regulating cell adhesion and signal transduction [24]. TM4SF19 interacts with the integrin αvβ3 receptor via its extracellular loop, thereby regulating cytoskeletal reorganization and cell migration [7], while also modulating macrophage TLR4-mediated inflammatory signaling to influence the inflammatory state of the immune microenvironment [25]. The pro-proliferative, migratory, and invasive effects of TM4SF19 in various cancers [8, 10], combined with its impact on the immune environment, collectively promote tumor immune evasion and progression. These findings suggest that TM4SF19 may serve as a molecular marker for assessing immune status in bladder urothelial carcinoma and provide a potential target for optimizing immunotherapy strategies.

Regarding drug sensitivity, somatic copy number alterations (SCNAs) of TM4SF19 are significantly associated with drug metabolism pathways. Drug sensitivity analysis revealed a negative correlation with several drugs (e.g., Trametinib, Bleomycin, and Docetaxel) and a positive correlation with Navitoclax and AR-42. This indicates that TM4SF19 expression levels may influence patient responses to chemotherapeutic agents, providing a basis for personalized treatment. It is important to contextualize these findings within the current treatment landscape for bladder cancer, where platinum-based chemotherapy (e.g., cisplatin) and Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) immunotherapy remain the cornerstone of first-line treatment for advanced and non-muscle invasive disease, respectively. The drugs identified in our analysis, such as Docetaxel (a taxane) and Trametinib (a MEK inhibitor), are more commonly investigated or used in later-line settings or for specific subtypes. For instance, taxanes like Docetaxel are considered in platinum-resistant or ineligible patients. Our finding of a negative correlation between TM4SF19 expression and sensitivity to Docetaxel suggests that high TM4SF19 might be a biomarker associated with taxane resistance, offering a mechanistic hypothesis for treatment failure in a subset of patients. Previous studies have reported effects of drugs on cellular metabolism, such as calcium antagonists inhibiting very low-density lipoprotein secretion, by analogy suggesting that TM4SF19 may regulate drug sensitivity through similar metabolic or secretory pathways [26]. However, this analysis is based on database correlations and lacks causal evidence. Future experimental validation is needed to determine whether TM4SF19 directly regulates drug-metabolizing enzymes or transporters.

Limitations of this study include: First, the analysis relied entirely on public databases, lacking experimental validation to confirm TM4SF19’s functions. Second, the specific molecular mechanisms of TM4SF19, such as how it regulates signaling pathways or the immune microenvironment, remain unelucidated. Finally, the drug sensitivity analysis is solely correlative and does not account for actual drug responses in clinical samples. These limitations warrant addressing through subsequent research, such as using cell lines or mouse models for further investigation.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that TM4SF19 expression is significantly upregulated in bladder cancer, and its high expression is closely associated with adverse clinicopathological features, poorer overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI), indicating that it serves as an independent prognostic factor for bladder cancer. Functionally, TM4SF19 likely promotes tumor progression by participating in signaling pathways such as PI3K-Akt and MAPK, and potentially through modulating the tumor immune microenvironment, particularly by exhibiting a negative correlation with the infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells and B cells. Furthermore, somatic copy number alteration (SCNA) of TM4SF19 is linked to drug metabolism, and its expression level shows a significant negative correlation with the sensitivity to drugs such as Trametinib and Docetaxel, but positively with Navitoclax and AR-42. In summary, TM4SF19 is not only a potential diagnostic marker and prognostic predictor for bladder cancer, but the immune microenvironment regulation and drug resistance mechanisms it mediates may also offer new therapeutic target strategies for bladder cancer treatment.

To Investigate the Impact and Potential Benefits of Integrating Proms Into Routine ICU Care for Patients with Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) Superimposed on Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Undergoing Hemodialysis
Authors: Akash P Datir, Tanima Baronia, Durgesh Makwana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a widespread public health concern associated with increased mortality, morbidity, impaired quality of life, and considerable healthcare costs. A particularly challenging subset includes patients with Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) superimposed on CKD, especially those undergoing hemodialysis, due to their high risks of mortality, hospitalization, and progression of underlying CKD. Care for these patients in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) setting is complex and multifaceted. Despite the growing recognition of their value, the implementation and impact of PROMs in ICU settings, specifically among these complex patients, is relatively unexplored.ObjectivesTo assess the feasibility and implementation of PROMs in the routine care of AKI on CKD patients undergoing hemodialysis in the ICU setting. To evaluate the effect of incorporating PROMs on patient-reported health status, symptoms, functional status, and quality of life. To investigate the impact of using PROMs on clinical outcomes, including ICU length of stay, dialysis dependency, and mortality rates.Materials and methodsThis was a prospective, observational study over 12 months at Ruby Hall Clinic ICU. We enrolled 200 adult patients admitted with critical illnesses, many with AKI on CKD. PROM questionnaires were administered twice:At ICU admission (within 48 hours of stabilization)At ICU discharge (when patient was conscious, oriented, and fit for discharge)PROMs were measured at baseline (within 24 hours of ICU admission) and at hospital discharge. Kidney Disease Quality of Life Short Form (KDQOL-SF) was used for kidney-disease specific quality of life. EuroQol five-dimensional questionnaire (EQ-5D) was utilized for overall health-related quality of life. We used validated PROM tools derived from EQ-5D and PROMIS domains: Symptom Burden, Sleep Quality, Emotional Well-being, Physical Function, Anxiety and Depression Each scored 0 - 100, where higher indicates better well-being.ResultsAll KDQOL-SF domains demonstrated statistically significant post-intervention improvement (p < 0.05), with the largest effect sizes observed in emotional well-being and physical functioning.1–5 Similarly, EQ-5D scores reflected a meaningful enhancement in global quality of life, with a significant reduction in severity across all five dimensions. The mean EQ-5D index improved from 0.56 ± 0.19 at baseline to 0.78 ± 0.17 following intervention (p < 0.001). Among the study population (N = 200), integration of PROMs into routine ICU care yielded varying degrees of benefit: Great improvement: 37% (n = 74)Moderate improvement: 28% (n = 56)Mild improvement: 20% (n = 40)No change: 11% (n = 22)Deterioration 4% (n = 8)Overall, 85% of patients showed measurable improvement following PROM-based care integration, reinforcing its clinical value, responsiveness, and utility in critical care quality assessment.6ConclusionIntegrating PROMs into ICU care for AKI on CKD patients is feasible, well-accepted, and provides meaningful insight into both subjective recovery and clinical outcomes. PROMs effectively capture improvements in symptoms, functioning, and quality of life, and correlate with key ICU endpoints. Their routine use may enhance patient-centred critical care, although larger multicentre studies are needed to validate these findings.Keywords: PROMs, AKI on CKD, ICU Outcomes, KDQOL-SF, EQ-5D, Hemodialysis, Quality of Life

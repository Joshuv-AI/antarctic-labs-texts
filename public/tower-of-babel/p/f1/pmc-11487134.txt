Multi-scale modeling and mechanical performance analysis of finger seals with plain woven C/C composite
Authors: Danlong Song, Keyi Hua, Yu Cheng, Chunhua Du, Yanchao Zhang, Yunjuan Jing
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The application of carbon fibers reinforced carbon matrix (C/C) composite can solve the local wear of metallic finger seals effectively. However, the performance of C/C composite finger seal is complex and variable, which further decreases the sealing performance and life. Therefore, a method of multi-scale modeling and mechanical performance analysis for plain woven C/C composite finger seals was conducted. The circumferential finger beams of C/C composite were modeled by multi-scale structural analysis and weaving simulation. The radial static and dynamic stiffness characteristics of finger beams were investigated. The results showed that the radial static stiffness of the finger beam with three layers was about 3 times that with single layer. The radial stiffness of circumferential finger beams presented a periodic distribution pattern with a period of 90°. The radial dynamic stiffness of C/C composite finger beams increased with the excitation displacement amplitude and rotor speed. But the magnitude and fluctuation degree of dynamic stiffness were greater than those of static stiffness. A large difference in radial stiffness will lead to local wear and hysteretic leakage. This study lays a foundation for the analysis and optimization of the hysteresis and wear characteristics of C/C composite finger seals.

Introduction

The finger seal is an advanced flexible seal technology which is applied to aero engines1. Compared with the traditional labyrinth seal and brush seal, the finger seal has better flexibility and adaptability2. So the finger seal can adapt to radial runout and deformation caused by the roundness and errors. The higher sealing performance, longer service life and lower cost make the finger seal have broad application and development potential in the field of high-speed dynamic seal3.

The hysteretic leakage and frictional wear are the main reasons that affect the service performance of contact finger seals4,5. Insufficient stiffness of finger beams and sliding friction between seal plates will reduce the dynamic response rate of finger beams6, then cause hysteretic leakage7. Excessive stiffness of finger beams may cause excessive wear of finger shoes or rotor surface8. The service life of finger seals reduce significantly. At recent, researchers tried to find some methods with low hysteretic leakage and wear-resistance, such as structural optimization5,9, non-contacting10–12 and wear-resistant coating13,14, but it is difficult to effectively solve the problem due to the mutual constraints of the two approaches for traditional metallic finger seals15.

C/C composite has excellent thermomechanical performances such as high-temperature resistance, self-lubrication, wear-resistance and fatigue-resistance16–18, which are suitable for finger seals. For C/C composite finger seals, complex working parameters such as rotor runout, assembly clearance and impact19–21, contact pressure, dynamic response, friction and wear, leakage rate and other dynamic performances have been studied22–24. The results showed that C/C composite can significantly improve the dynamic performances of finger seals, reduce wear rate and increase service life. The wear mechanism and analytical calculation laid a foundation for the wear life prediction of finger seals25,26. In addition, the material pairs of C/C composites-Cr3C2 have excellent frictional wear and hysteresis leakage properties, which provided an important reference for the selection of material pairs for finger seals27.

However, the above research mainly adopted the equivalent macroscopic mechanical method, the multi-scale C/C composite was assumed to be homogeneous. The thermomechanical performances of woven C/C composites are closely related to components, fibers distribution, woven structure and other microscopic and mesoscopic characteristics28,29. Due to the complex and varied mesoscopic structure of composite preforms such as layer, angle and weaving form30, the multi-scale mechanical performances showed non-uniform anisotropy31,32. In addition, the complex configuration curves of finger beams make the stiffness characteristics of finger beams at each position variable and complicated, which further affects the uniformity and sealing performance of finger seals. Although the some researches focus on the woven structure of C/C composite, the internal fiber yarns and the matrix structure of C/C composite finger seals were not modeled separately33,34. Then, the mechanical performances of C/C composite were still homogenized and equivalent, so the influence of the microstructure such as weaving form and direction on the stiffness variation of finger beams was not considered.

Therefore, the radial static and dynamic mechanical characteristics of finger beams with plain woven C/C composite were studied in this paper. Firstly, a multi-scale model of the C/C composite was constructed through multi-scale structural analysis and weaving process simulation. Then, the circumferential finger beams of C/C composite were modeled by mesoscale method. The radial static and dynamic stiffness characteristics of circumferential finger beams were investigated in detail. Finally, the static stiffness experiment was designed and conducted. By comparing the simulation and experimental results, the radial stiffness characteristics of circumferential C/C composite finger beams were obtained, which laid a foundation for the accurate analysis and optimization of the hysteresis and wear characteristics of C/C composite finger seals.

Multi-scale modeling of plain woven C/C composites

Microstructure analysis for plain woven C/C composites

The structure of plain woven C/C composite laminate exhibits heterogeneous multi-scale characteristics. The images of structures at each scale are shown in Fig. 1. The macroscale structure is a C/C composite laminate for finger seals, and the mesoscale structure is a RWC (representative woven cell) composed of orthogonal plain woven carbon fiber yarns and carbon matrix. The micro-scale structure is the RVE (representative volume element) inside the carbon fiber yarn, which is composed of a large number of randomly distributed carbon fibers and carbon matrix.

Mesoscale modeling of plain woven C/C composites

The mesoscale model of plain woven composites was usually investigated by statistical analysis of experimental test results such as micro CT, SEM and so on35,36. The axial curve and cross section shape of fiber yarns are formed due to the tension and bending during the plain weaving process. In order to establish exact geometric model of the plain woven RWC in mesoscale, the weaving process of fiber yarns was simulated under the constraints of the manufacturing process. The node sampling and fitting of the simulation results was carried out. The curve equation of fiber yarns is obtained.

The weaving process of carbon fiber yarns was simulated by finite element method. In real cases, a fiber yarn contains thousands of carbon fibers, so the calculation amount in the simulation process is huge. In most cases, 19–50 fibers are sufficient to represent the cross section geometry of a fiber yarn37. With the total volume of the yarns unchanged, 20, 50, and 100 fibers for a yarn were selected respectively for simulation to reduce the amount of computation (the corresponding radius of the fiber was 0.8 mm, 0.5 mm, and 0.35 mm respectively). The material parameters of fibers are listed in Table 1. Initially, the yarns were slack, one end of the yarns was fixed completely, and a displacement of 8 mm was applied perpendicularly to the cross section of the yarns on the other end. The boundary conditions and loading are shown in Fig. 2. The interaction between the carbon fibers was set to be hard contact in normal behavior and frictional contact in tangential behavior to simulate the contact extrusion and shear slip in the simulation process, and the friction coefficient was 0.15. The fibers were gridded by hexahedral mesh with the size of 0.004 mm. Under the load, the free end of the yarns was pulled, and the initially slack yarns was tensioned tightly, and eventually appear in the state are shown in Fig. 3.

Mesoscale finite element modeling for C/C composite finger seals

The typical contact finger seal system is composed of fastening rivets, front and rear baffles, front and rear spacer rings, and several finger seal plates that are stacked alternately as shown in Fig. 6. A number of finger beams distribute uniformly around the circumference of finger seals. Under the action of radial contact and impact by the rotor surface, finger shoes at the end of finger beams push the finger beams, causing radial deformation and ensuring sealing effect. In light of the current research and application of finger seals, the most common circular finger seal was considered as an example for this study. Then a circular finger seal was designed based on the research on structure optimization and sealing performance for the rotor system with diameter Di=77 mm, and the main structural parameters were shown in Table 4.

Considering the actual loading conditions of the finger beams during the working process of the contact finger seal, the boundary and load conditions of the multi-scale finite element model of the C/C composite finger beam were shown in Fig. 9. Both the yarns and the matrix of C/C composite finger beam were meshed using 10-node quadratic tetrahedral elements (C3D10), with the mesh size of 0.2 mm. The carbon matrix and carbon fiber are bonded together tightly by mechanical, chemical and hybrid principles according to the chemical vapor deposition (CVD) process. So the yarns and the matrix were tied together in the FE model to simulate the tight bonding relationship. The external ring of seal plate was fastened by bolts and its deformation was ignored. The base of finger beam is connected with the external ring, so the base of the finger beam was set to be fully fixed, and a normal displacement was applied at the bottom of the finger shoe. Subsequently, the radial deformation and force of the finger beam were simulated using the finite element method. Then, the radial stiffness of the finger beam was calculated.

Radial static stiffness characteristics of finger beams

According to Hooke’s law, the deformation of an elastic body is proportional to the external force, and the ratio is the stiffness. Finite element simulation results of radial stiffness

To improve computational efficiency, taking finger beam No.1 as an example first, a comparison of the radial stiffness of C/C composite finger beams with single layer and 3 layers of preform was analyzed. The displacement loading was set to be 1 mm, and the curves showing the variation of radial force and radial stiffness with radial displacement were shown in Fig. 10 respectively. It can be observed that the radial force curves for C/C composite finger beams with both single layer and 3 layers were consistent and exhibit a linear relationship. Thus, the radial stiffness of the finger beam was constant. The radial stiffness of finger beams with single layer and 3 layer were k1 = 2.348 N/mm and k3 = 7.032 N/mm respectively. The results indicated that, within a certain range, the radial deformation of the C/C composite finger beam didn’t affect its radial stiffness. From the relationship k3 ≈ 2.995k1, it can be seen that the increment of radial stiffness was approximately equal to the number of preform layer, which was consistent with the theory that the stiffness of a cantilever beam was proportional to its width. The conclusion provided a reference for the subsequent study of the radial stiffness distribution characteristics of C/C composite finger beams with single layer preform along the circumference.

Force and stiffness curves of C/C composite finger beam. (a) Single layer of preform; (b) Three layers of preform.

To simplify computation, based on the aforementioned mesoscale finite element method, the radial stiffness of the 32 finger beams with single layer of preform was simulated in turn. The variation pattern of the radial stiffness for each group of finger beams along the circumference was obtained as shown in Fig. 11. It can be seen that the radial stiffness of the C/C composite finger beams presented a periodic variation pattern, and the period was 90° along the circumference. Within each period, the radial stiffness of the finger beams first decreased and then increased with the position angle. The maximum radial stiffness occurred at the finger beams of the positions of 11.25°, 101.25°, 191.25° and 281.25°, while the finger beams at the positions of 45°, 135°, 225°, and 315° have the minimum radial stiffness as shown in Fig. 12. The minimum stiffness was about 68% of the maximum stiffness, and the difference was 0.726 N/mm. This phenomenon was mainly caused by the complex orthogonal plain woven structure of carbon fiber yarns and the curve profile of finger beams. The weaving direction of the carbon fiber yarns and the curves of finger beams formed an angle which varied periodically with the circumferential angle of finger beams, thus a periodic variation characteristic of the radial stiffness was present. At 11.25°, 101.25° and so on, the angles between the curves of finger beams and the yarns direction were the smallest, so the elastic modulus and stiffness were the largest. The angles were the largest at 45°, 135° and so on, so the material stiffness was the smallest. In addition, from the mesoscale model of the C/C composite finger seal shown in Fig. 8, it can be seen that the continuity of carbon fiber yarns and the structural compactness of finger beams at different positions varied periodically.

Experimental test of the radial stiffness

In order to verify the accuracy of the analysis results presented above, stiffness tests of C/C composite finger beams were conducted. Finger seal specimens were fabricated with the same plain woven C/C composite plates as above, and the stiffness of each finger beam was tested.

In this paper, a piece of plain woven C/C composite of 100 × 100 × 2.1 mm was selected and the corresponding finger seal specimen was processed by a laser cutting machine tool. A quarter of the finger seal plate was selected along the circumference, that was, the finger beams within the range of 0°~90°, was cut and numbered in sequence, as shown in Fig. 13.

Fig. 13Specimens of finger seals of C/C composite. (a) Plain woven C/C composite. (b) Laser cutting machine tool. (c) Finger seal of C/C composite. (d) Specimens of finger beams.

Specimens of finger seals of C/C composite. (a) Plain woven C/C composite. (b) Laser cutting machine tool. (c) Finger seal of C/C composite. (d) Specimens of finger beams.

According to the measurement principle of the radial stiffness of finger beams, a test bench was established, as shown in Fig. 14. The test process was as follows: Firstly, adjust the position and direction of the finger beam to make the finger shoe contact and vertical to the loading screw, and the base of the finger beam was fixed by the clamping part. Secondly, reset the force sensor. Then, a radial displacement was applied by screwing the loading screw. The axial displacement of the loading screw, that was the radial deformation of the finger beam, was measured with a micrometer, and the value of the force sensor was recorded. Finally, the ratio of the force to the radial deformation was the radial stiffness of the finger beam, and the radial stiffness of finger beams in the range of 0°~90° was tested and calculated in turn.

Fig. 14Radial stiffness test bench of finger beams.

Radial stiffness test bench of finger beams.

The radial stiffness of finger beams within the range of 0°~90° was obtained through the stiffness test, and compared with the simulation results, as shown in Fig. 15. It can be seen that the radial stiffness of finger beams in the range of 0°~90°decreased first and then increased with the position angle. The experimental and simulation results were consistent. However, compared with the simulation analysis results, the test results were numerically smaller, with an average smaller value of 16%. The main reasons were as follows: there were some small voids and defects in the matrix and interface of the actual C/C composite caused by manufacturing process. In addition, during the laser processing of C/C composite finger seal specimens, the higher processing temperature caused the carbon fibers and matrix at the cutting edge ablation and damage, which resulted in the actual radial stiffness of C/C composite finger beams was lower than that of simulation.

Radial dynamic stiffness characteristics of finger beams

Effect of radial runout amplitude on dynamic stiffness of finger beams

In order to study the effect of displacement excitation with various amplitudes on the radial stiffness of C/C composite finger beams, some dynamic finite element simulations which took finger beam No.1 for example were carried out. The rotor speed was set to be constant 15000r/min, that is, the angular velocity ω = 1750 rad/s, and the rotation time was 0.004s. Under the above conditions, the amplitudes A were taken as 0.05 mm, 0.1 mm, 0.15 mm, 0.2 mm and 0.25 mm respectively. In practice, in the first half of the motion cycle, the finger shoe moves away from the initial position to far position radially under the rotor excitation, and then gradually returns to the initial position with the rotor rotation. In the second half of the motion cycle, the rotor turns away, and the current finger shoe separates from the rotor. Therefore, only the first half of the motion cycle was analyzed, and the radial displacement reached the amplitude when the time is t = 0.001s.

According to the radial dynamic stiffness Eq. (2), the curve of the radial dynamic stiffness of the finger beam with the radial displacement amplitude was shown in Fig. 16. It can be seen that the radial dynamic stiffness of the finger beam increased with the displacement amplitude applied to the finger shoe. The main reason was that the greater the displacement amplitude, the greater the deformation rate of the finger beam in the case of constant time, the greater the radial dynamic stiffness.

Effect of rotor speed on dynamic stiffness of finger beams

The displacement excitation frequencies caused by different rotor speeds were different. In order to study the effect of radial displacement excitation under different rotor speeds on the radial dynamic stiffness of plain woven C/C composite finger beams, the finger beam No.1 was took for example. The radial displacement amplitude was set as 0.05 mm, while the rotor speed varied within a certain range of 6000r/min, 9000r/min, 12000r/min, 15000r/min, 18000r/min and 21000r/min. The range of the corresponding excitation frequencies was 100 ~ 350 Hz. Under the amplitude of displacement excitation keeping constant as A = 0.05 mm, the radial dynamic stiffness of finger beams with different rotor speeds was shown as Fig. 17. It can be seen that as the rotor speed increased, the radial dynamic stiffness of plain woven C/C composite finger beams also increased. This was because the higher the rotor speed, the higher the frequency of the displacement excitation applied to finger shoes by the rotor, and the radial dynamic stiffness increased accordingly.

However, the relationship between the radial dynamic stiffness and the rotor speed was not completely linear. The slope of the radial dynamic stiffness curve was increasingly higher and higher. Through the modal analysis of the C/C composite finger beam, the radial natural frequency was 908.9 Hz, which was much higher than the excitation frequency.

Radial dynamic stiffness distribution characteristics of finger beams

It was discovered that the static stiffness of the finger beams distributed circumferentially was not uniform due to the differences in the woven structures and fibers distribution inside the finger beams. According to the symmetry of the finger seal, the finger beams in the range of 0°~90° on the finger seal plate were selected. The circumferential distribution of radial dynamic stiffness under dynamic excitation was studied, and the results were compared with those under static excitation. A sinusoidal displacement was used to simulate the dynamic excitation of the rotor. The radial runout amplitude of the rotor was 0.05 mm, and the rotational speed was 15000r/min.

Under the above sinusoidal displacement dynamic excitation, the radial dynamic stiffness of finger beams in the range of 0°~90° was simulated and analyzed. The comparison of radial dynamic and static stiffness of finger beams distributed circumferentially was shown in Fig. 18. It can be found that the dynamic stiffness decreased first and then increased with the position angles. Both curves of radial static and dynamic stiffness in the range of 0°~90° presented identical laws of variation. However, there were great differences in the magnitude and fluctuation degree. The dynamic stiffness was much greater than the static stiffness. The average values of the static and dynamic stiffness of the finger beams in the range of 0°~90° were 1.958 N/mm and 4.696 N /mm respectively, where was a relationship of 2.4 times. In addition, the variance of the radial static stiffness and dynamic stiffness of the finger beams in the range of 0°~90° were 0.07899 and 0.1397 respectively. It can be seen that the radial dynamic stiffness of the finger beams distributed circumferentially fluctuated more serious than the radial static stiffness.

Conclusion

In this paper, a multi-scale finite element analysis method of the plain woven C/C composite finger seal was proposed and conducted by the multi-scale structure analysis. Then, the mechanical performances and distribution of C/C composite finger beams were studied by the finite element analysis and experimental measurement methods respectively, which laid a foundation for the study of hysteric leakage and wear of C/C composite finger seals. The following conclusions were made from the present study.

With the increase of radial displacement and thickness of the finger shoe, the radial reaction force of the finger beam increased linearly, thus the radial static stiffness of the C/C composite circular finger beam remained constant.Along the circumference of finger seals, the radial static stiffness of the C/C composite finger beam presented a periodic variation pattern of first decreasing and then increasing with the position angle, with a period of 90°, and the minimum radial static stiffness was about 68% of the maximum radial static stiffness. A large difference in radial stiffness will lead to local wear and hysteretic leakage.The radial dynamic stiffness of the C/C composite finger beam increased with the excitation displacement amplitude and the rotor rotational speed. Along the circumference of finger seal, the variation pattern of the dynamic stiffness was consistent with that of static stiffness, but the dynamic stiffness was greater than the static stiffness. The fluctuation degree of the dynamic stiffness was also greater than that of the static stiffness.

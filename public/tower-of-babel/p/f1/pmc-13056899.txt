Development of a high-speed planetary gearbox for an electric vehicle
Authors: Mykhaylo Zagirnyak, Volodymyr Drahobetskyi, Yuliia Salenko, Viktoriia Kulynych, Dmytro Moloshtan, Artur Szczęsny, Ewa Korzeniewska
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A methodology has been developed for creating a fundamentally new high-speed planetary gearbox for an electric vehicle drive with double-crown planetary gears mounted in central bearing supports mounted on the periphery of a three-lobed flange integral with the planet carrier. Functional relationships have been established that enable the determination of rational parameters for a high-speed gearbox with improved dynamic performance. The use of alloyed materials for gears, whose performance criterion is contact strength, with high mechanical properties has enabled significant material savings, reduced weight and dimensions, and improved the technical and economic performance of the electric vehicle gearbox. The selection of gear material is based on loading conditions and cost effectiveness. The developed planetary gearbox has a simpler design, smaller dimensions and weight, increased efficiency, and improved speed and power characteristics compared to similar products.

Introduction

Two-stage cylindrical gearboxes1–3 with a gear ratio of 7.1 to 12.5 are used in the designs of most electric vehicles from leading companies around the world4,5. At the same time, with an increase in the gear ratio and peripheral speed, the overall dimensions of the gearbox increase, the efficiency decreases6, reliability deteriorates and bending stresses in the tooth root increase.

Minimizing the weight and dimensions of electric vehicles, their components and parts is a topical direction for providing the economic efficiency of using such vehicles. The mass of the structure can be reduced by the choice of materials, treatment method, design, layout, strength calculations and elimination of load concentration caused by its uneven distribution along the contact lines. In general, it is necessary to improve the methodology of constructive and technological formation with the inclusion of components related to the analysis of the fund of accumulated knowledge (constructive continuity). In addition, a synthesis of mathematical models, geometric and force calculations of the design elements of the electric vehicle drive units, technologies for their manufacture and repair, as well as ensuring their trouble-free operation is required. On the whole, the design methodology includes general design rules, design methods and laws of development of engineering systems.

Research on the development and creation of high-speed planetary gearboxes for the electric vehicle drive has been conducted recently. A high gear ratio, the highest compactness and low weight are the main advantages of planetary gearboxes7,8. The latter is explained by the distribution of the load between several satellites and the use of gears with internal engagement, which have an increased load capacity. The shafts of the central gears in planetary gearboxes are loaded only with torques, and their supports do not bear radial loads. This reduces friction losses and, to some extent, simplifies the design of the supports and gearbox.

Electric vehicles that use two twin electric motors to drive the front or rear axle are the most advanced ones currently. In this case, each electric motor rotates the left or right axle through a separate planetary gearbox and there is no need for a differential due to the electronic control system9,10.

The main disadvantage of the designs of planetary gearboxes used in electric vehicles consists in the traditional location of the shaft support or axles of double-row satellites at the ends of the shafts. This leads to an increase in the gearbox’s axial dimensions and a significant complication in the design of the electric vehicle’s gearboxes.

The main goal of this research is to develop a high-speed planetary gearbox for an electric vehicle power unit with increased speed and power characteristics, as well as simplify the design, reduce dimensions and weight, and improve the efficiency.

Materials and methods

Morphological analysis based on combinatorics methods is used to determine the most rational design of a high-speed gearbox of the electric vehicle drive.

In a number of cases, it is advisable to use heuristic techniques and a morphological method based on combinatorics methods, taking into account the laws of the development of engineering systems, the theory and methods of designing gearboxes8,11–14.

There may be any number of double-gear ring satellites in a planetary gearbox. However, the number of double-gear ring satellites must be odd for high-speed planetary gearboxes of electric vehicles due to vibration strength reasons12.

The gear ratio of the first stage is assigned based on the condition of creating a compact design of the planetary gearbox5,12.

When choosing the number of gear teeth, it is also necessary to take into account the following conditions: coaxiality, proximity and assembly.

To manage thermal loads during high-speed operation, the gearbox design incorporates a drip-air lubrication system, which ensures targeted cooling of the meshing zones. Additionally, high-performance fluororubber (FKM) seals are utilized to maintain structural integrity and prevent leakage under elevated operating temperatures, providing thermal stability up to the rated speed of 15,000 rpm.

Gear material is chosen taking into account the conditions of loading and economic efficiency.

Contact strength is the criterion for the performance of gears. Therefore, a material is needed that allows the working surfaces of parts to be strengthened to high hardness while maintaining the required strength of the core. Alloyed materials with high mechanical characteristics are the most acceptable option for high-speed gearboxes of the electric vehicle operating under conditions of predominantly dynamic loads. This provides significant savings in materials and improves the technical and economic performance of the gearbox. When choosing alloying elements in steel production, it is desirable to increase the proportion of cheap elements (С, Mn, Si, Cr, Al, Cu), and the proportion of expensive elements (Ni, Mo, W, V, Co, Re) should be reduced to a minimum. In special operating conditions, it is possible to use additives from rare materials (e.g. Re, Nb, Be, W, Ta etc.).

Based on the results of the calculation of the structural and kinematic parameters of the high-speed gearbox of an electric vehicle, it can be concluded that the use of a single-stage planetary gearbox with double-gear ring satellites is the most rational solution for the specified drive. The efficiency of the proposed planetary gearbox was determined through an analytical calculation of total power losses at the rated operating point (n = 15,000 rpm and Tin = 154.5 Nm). The total efficiency coefficient accounts for friction losses in the gear meshes, determined by the average sliding velocity and the engagement factor, as well as losses in the radial roller bearings and seals. Based on this methodology, the estimated efficiency of the proposed planetary gearbox is 0.978, compared to 0.968 for a standard two-stage cylindrical gearbox.

Discussion

The conducted research demonstrates that applying a morphological analysis approach to the design of high-speed planetary gearboxes enables a systematic assessment of a large number of possible configurations and supports the selection of an optimal design solution for electric vehicle (EV) applications. The morphological model analysed in this study encompasses up to 9000 design variants, which confirms that traditional intuition-based design methods are insufficient when dealing with highly integrated drive units for modern EVs. The selected configuration (a single-stage planetary gearbox with double-gear ring satellites) has proven to be the most balanced option in terms of compactness, load distribution, manufacturability, and dynamic performance.

A key point is the justification of selecting double-gear ring (double-row) satellites with central bearing supports, and an odd number of satellites. According to established gear design principles8,12, an odd number of satellites is strategically chosen to avoid simultaneous mesh excitations, thereby potentially increasing vibration resistance and gearbox stability. While these benefits are well-documented in planetary gear literature as a means to improve load distribution and reduce axial dimensions, they are treated in this study as fundamental design assumptions rather than results of original modal or harmonic analysis. The study emphasizes that this structural feature significantly reduces axial dimensions and increases load distribution among multiple contact points, which is critical for high-speed applications. The restriction to an odd number of satellites, required for vibration resistance, further highlights the importance of dynamic considerations in planetary gearbox design. This principle aligns with findings in aviation and high-performance automotive transmissions, where double-row planetary systems are used to improve torque density and operational stability. Regarding the thermal performance, while the current work focuses on morphological and kinematic design, we recognize the importance of thermal management in high-speed drives. To mitigate thermal risks in this design, we have proposed the use of a drip-air lubrication system and high-performance fluororubber seals, ensuring stability at speeds up to 15000 rpm. Detailed numerical mapping of temperature fields and thermal deformations is designated as a critical phase of future experimental prototype validation.

The study opens opportunities for broader application of the proposed gearbox architecture. Given its compactness, high efficiency, and favourable torque distribution characteristics, such planetary gearboxes can be adapted for robotics, aerospace actuators, unmanned systems, and hybrid powertrains. However, further long-term durability testing, vibration analysis, and thermal behaviour studies under real EV operating conditions are needed to fully validate the design. Such investigations would help determine the gearbox’s sensitivity to lubrication degradation, bearing wear, and temperature cycling, which are critical in real-world EV drive units. It should be noted that comprehensive verification of the gearbox dynamics, including torsional vibration simulations and experimental NVH (Noise, Vibration, Harshness) testing, is required for the final physical prototype to quantitatively validate these performance claims.

On the other hand, although the conclusions of the work, based on kinematic, force, and structural calculations, are consistent with the presented assumptions. The mathematical models presented here serve as the foundation for numerical simulations planned for the full validation and further development of the prototype, which is standard engineering practice. These methods, particularly the finite element method (FEM), are successfully used to optimize and analyze complex mechanical and electromagnetic systems17,18. Using these tools will allow for precise determination of stress and deformation distributions, providing valuable data confirming the validity of the adopted design assumptions. Furthermore, numerical modeling allows for optimization of device parameters without the need for costly and time-consuming prototype testing. This approach is also common in other fields, such as modeling of semiconductor devices19,20, highlighting the universality of numerical methods in modern engineering.

The reliability and operational durability of a gearbox are primarily ensured by its design. The technical aspects of improving reliability are directly dependent on the designer. This involves the use of design solutions that, when implemented, lead to the most efficient prevention of failures. As Table 2 shows, gears have the highest failure rate.

The required or acceptable reliability is typically achieved through extensive testing of components and the gearbox as a whole. Currently, advances in mathematical apparatus and computer technology allow for fairly reliable and accurate calculations.

Theoretical mathematical modeling provides a robust framework for assessing structural integrity and thermal performance, ensuring that subsequent physical prototyping and experimental validation are focused on preoptimized design solutions. Advances in solid-state physics, elasticity, and plasticity theory allow for the theoretical calculation of the strength and stress-strain state of components. Furthermore, the complexity of the component’s shape, its susceptibility to variable, non-stationary forces, and aggressive environments do not affect the accuracy of the calculation results16.

To assess the performance of components operating under frictional conditions, temperature field studies are necessary. It is also necessary to evaluate the heat resistance and precision of components under heating. Heating affects the operation of gears due to temperature changes in clearances and a decrease in oil viscosity. The properties of the material’s surface layers also change, reducing its mechanical properties, resulting in thermal embrittlement and creep.

The formulation and solution of temperature problems are described by rather complex thermoelasticity equations. However, mathematical modeling allows for obtaining fairly accurate results21. The error calculated using the variance of adequacy is within δ = 3% with a confidence level of 0.95.

Wear theory allows for the assessment of component durability with the required accuracy, taking into account real operating conditions: elastic deformations of components, variability in temperature and viscosity of lubricated materials on the component surface, etc.

Therefore, experimental research methods are giving way to more effective and less expensive mathematical modeling methods. A set of realistic mathematical models allows for a reliable assessment of component reliability and operational safety. The set includes: models of component manufacturing processes, models of thermomechanical processes, models of the initial state of components, a model of the evolution of service parameters, a model describing structural transformations in the material, a model of component wear, and a model of damage accumulation.

The rig utilizes preloaded, compact, lightweight flange couplings with increased coupling half-coupling reliability.

Conclusions

An original design for a high-speed planetary gearbox with double-crown (double-row) planetary gears has been developed for electric vehicle drives. The electric drive with the proposed planetary gearbox offers significant advantages over electric drives with two-stage gearboxes for electric vehicles manufactured by leading global companies:

Compactness and lighter weight due to the use of: an original planetary gearbox design;the selection of modern materials and thermochemical treatments for the planetary gearbox components;increased electric motor speed. High efficiency due to a higher coefficient of performance, as it has less friction and power transmission losses, which in turn can lead to an increase in the electric vehicle’s range on a single battery charge.Simplicity of design, which simplifies production and maintenance of the electric drive, reduces the likelihood of breakdowns and increases its reliability.Lower noise levels: The electric drive with the proposed planetary gearbox operates quieter than electric drives with two-stage gearboxes, improving passenger comfort.

Additionally, a method for calculating the service life of high-speed gearbox components has been developed. Based on analytical calculations of power losses in gear meshes and bearings, the estimated efficiency of the system is 0.978 at rated loads, which exceeds the performance of traditional two-stage designs and potentially increases vehicle range. The method is based on a comprehensive integration of models: manufacturing processes, thermomechanical processes, the initial state of the components, the evolution of service parameters, a model describing structural transformations in the material, component wear, and damage accumulation.

The need for developing reliable methods and algorithms for solving operational reliability problems through mathematical modeling is substantiated. This allows for cost-effective assessment of the operational reliability of components during operation.

While this research establishes a rigorous analytical foundation for the design, it is important to note that the current study focuses on the morphological and kinematic development. Future research stages will involve the creation of a physical prototype to conduct numerical stress validation (FEM), experimental thermal field mapping, and detailed vibration analysis (NVH) under real-world operating cycles.

Thus, the electric drive with the proposed planetary gearbox is compact, lightweight, has a relatively simple design, and high efficiency, and can find wide application in electric vehicle manufacturing and other applications.

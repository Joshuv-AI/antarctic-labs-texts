Efficacy and safety of ABBV-154 in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease: a phase 2b, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
Authors: Walter Reinisch, Laurent Peyrin-Biroulet, Geert D’Haens, Andreas Sturm, Jaclyn K Anderson, Izabella Messina, Dilek Arikan, Alissa Zimmerman-Bennett, Julie Parmentier, Tian Feng, Ronilda D’Cunha, Andreas Lazar, Edouard Louis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundABBV-154 is an antibody-drug conjugate of adalimumab and a proprietary glucocorticoid receptor modulator. This phase 2b randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluated the efficacy and safety of ABBV-154 versus placebo in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease who had inadequate response to or were intolerant of prior biologic therapy.MethodsPatients were randomized 1:1:1:1:1 to ABBV-154 induction groups: 150 mg IV at week 0, then 80 mg SC at week 2 and every other week (EOW); 300 mg IV at week 0, then 230 mg SC at week 2 and EOW; 600 mg IV at week 0, then 530 mg SC at week 2 and EOW; 600 mg IV at week 0, then 530 mg SC at weeks 4 and 8, and placebo at weeks 2, 6, and 10; or placebo. The primary endpoint was achievement of endoscopic response per SES-CD by central review at week 12. Adverse events were monitored throughout.ResultsOf 265 planned patients, 106 were randomized and treated until completion, discontinuation, or early study termination by the sponsor. At week 12, greater proportions of patients treated with ABBV-154 achieved endoscopic response versus placebo (7.1%, 33.3%, 28.6%, and 27.3% across the induction groups vs. 0%; data reported as observed). Adverse events were comparable across all ABBV-154 groups and slightly higher than placebo through week 12.ConclusionsABBV-154 was well tolerated and demonstrated proof-of-concept for efficacy in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease. Results should be interpreted with caution due to early study termination.Keywords: adalimumab, antibody-drug conjugate, glucocorticoid receptor modulator, tumor necrosis factor-alpha inhibitor, Crohn’s disease

Introduction

Crohn’s disease is an inflammatory bowel disease characterized by chronic, segmental, transmural inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract.1 A number of biologic therapies are available to treat moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease,2 with adalimumab, an anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) monoclonal antibody, used globally to induce and maintain remission.3 However, despite the effectiveness of adalimumab and other anti-TNF therapies, treatment failure rates remain high.4,5 Up to 30% of patients do not initially respond to anti-TNF therapy (primary nonresponse), and up to half of those who do respond may lose responsiveness over time (secondary loss of response).4,6

Glucocorticoids have been shown to be efficacious in inducing clinical remission in Crohn’s disease.7 Though conventional glucocorticoids may not be effective for maintenance treatment,8 approximately 25% of patients with Crohn’s disease are glucocorticoid-dependent and receive continuing or recurrent systemic glucocorticoid therapy.9,10 However, extended glucocorticoid treatment is not recommended due to the risk of long-term side effects, including hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis suppression, osteoporosis, hyperglycemia, glaucoma, and skin atrophy.11–13 Thus, there is an unmet need for additional therapy options for Crohn’s disease.14–17

Antibody-drug conjugates are targeted therapies that comprise a small molecule drug (the “payload”) linked to an antibody for a specific cell-surface antigen.18 ABBV-154 is an anti-TNF antibody-drug conjugate composed of adalimumab conjugated to a proprietary glucocorticoid receptor modulator (GRM) as an anti-inflammatory payload.18 By modulating the same receptors as glucocorticoids, GRMs may be able to provide effective treatment for Crohn’s disease. ABBV-154 was designed to provide the combined efficacy of adalimumab and glucocorticoids by selectively delivering the GRM payload to cells that express transmembrane TNF, including activated immune cells, thereby minimizing systemic exposure to the free GRM payload.18 TNF-GRM antibody-drug conjugates have shown efficacy in preclinical models of acute inflammation and induced arthritis.18 The efficacy and safety of ABBV-154 was evaluated for Crohn’s disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and polymyalgia rheumatica in phase 2 clinical trials.19–21

Herein, we report the results from the phase 2b study of ABBV-154 in Crohn’s disease (AIM-CD). This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ABBV-154 in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease who had inadequate response to or were intolerant of prior biologic treatments.

Methods

Study design and participants

AIM-CD (NCT05068284) was a phase 2b, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted between March 2022 and July 2023 at 65 sites in Australia, Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, France, Germany, Great Britain, Greece, Israel, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Poland, Slovakia, Spain, Taiwan, and United States. Per the study protocol, AIM-CD consisted of a 12-week placebo-controlled induction period, followed by a 12-week re-induction period for nonresponders from the induction period and a 40-week placebo-controlled maintenance period for responders from the induction or re-induction periods. The study was voluntarily terminated by the sponsor on April 27, 2023, after a review of available data of the 3 phase 2 trials of ABBV-154 for Crohn’s disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and polymyalgia rheumatica. These data in total indicated that ABBV-154 did not offer a sufficiently differentiated benefit-risk profile compared with existing treatments, which led to the sponsor’s decision not to continue its development. The study was terminated before completion of enrollment and, therefore, minimal exposure occurred during the re-induction and maintenance periods. We report the efficacy results from the 12-week placebo-controlled induction period only in patients with week 12 data (not all patients completed the study period due to either discontinuation or study termination). Safety results are reported in all patients who received ≥1 dose of study drug during the entirety of the trial.

Eligible patients were aged 18-75 years with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease as demonstrated by a Crohn’s Disease Activity Index (CDAI) of 220 to 450 plus a Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s disease (SES-CD) of ≥6 for ileocolonic or colonic disease (or SES-CD of ≥4 for isolated ileal disease) as scored by blinded central review, excluding the presence of narrowing component. Patients had to have inadequately responded to or were intolerant of 1 or more biologic (eg, infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab pegol, vedolizumab, natalizumab, ustekinumab, or risankizumab). The enrollment of patients with previous adalimumab use was to be capped at approximately 30% of the total enrolled population.

Patients who were taking glucocorticoids at baseline of the study were required to taper off their glucocorticoids by week 6. Those who failed to complete glucocorticoid tapering by week 6, or who received rescue therapy with oral glucocorticoids at or after week 8, or increased their doses or initiated treatment with amino salicylates or immunomodulators during the 12-week induction period, were considered nonresponders regardless of their clinical or endoscopic response.

The study protocol, informed consent forms, and recruitment materials were reviewed and approved by an independent ethics committee or institutional review board at each study site before patient enrollment. The study was conducted in accordance with the International Conference for Harmonisation guidelines, applicable regulations, and the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent before screening or undergoing any study-specific procedures. All study sites and patients were blinded until the final database lock.

Randomization and treatment

At baseline, patients were randomized in a 1:1:1:1:1 ratio to 4 ABBV-154 dosing regimens (Figure S1): 150 mg intravenous (IV) dose at week 0, then 80 mg subcutaneous (SC) dose at week 2 and every other week (EOW); 300 mg IV dose at week 0, then 230 mg SC dose at week 2 and EOW; 600 mg IV dose at week 0, then 530 mg SC dose at week 2 and EOW; or 600 mg IV dose at week 0, then 530 mg SC dose at weeks 4 and 8, and placebo at weeks 2, 6, and 10; or matching placebo (IV and SC doses). Randomization was performed using interactive response technology and stratified by baseline glucocorticoid use (yes, no), endoscopic disease severity (SES-CD <15, ≥15), and inadequate response to adalimumab (yes, no).

Assessments

Efficacy assessment

The primary endpoint was achievement of endoscopic response at week 12 (a decrease in SES-CD >50% from baseline [or for patients with isolated ileal disease and a baseline SES-CD of 4, at least a 2-point reduction from baseline]). Secondary endpoints included achievement of clinical remission per CDAI (CDAI <150) at week 12 and achievement of clinical remission per stool frequency (SF) and abdominal pain (AP) score (average daily liquid or very soft SF ≤2.8 and not worse than baseline and average daily AP score ≤1 and not worse than baseline) at week 12. Other efficacy endpoints included achievement of endoscopic remission at week 12 (SES-CD ≤4 and at least a 2-point reduction from baseline and no subscore >1 in any individual variable), achievement of ulcer-free endoscopy at week 12 (SES-CD ulcerated surface subscore of 0 in patients with SES-CD ulcerated surface subscore ≥1 at baseline) as well as change from baseline fecal calprotectin (FCP) and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels. SES-CD scores were assessed by blinded central reviewers.

Safety assessment

Safety data were analyzed during the 12-week placebo-controlled period among patients who received ≥1 dose of study drug; data were reported as the number and percentage of patients experiencing treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). Safety assessments included monitoring of any TEAEs, serious adverse events (AEs), AEs leading to discontinuation of study drug, and AEs of special interest. Pre-specified AEs of special interest included serious infections, opportunistic infections, active tuberculosis, hypersensitivity reactions, serious allergic reactions, malignancies, and systemic glucocorticoid side effects (including adrenal insufficiency and iatrogenic Cushing’s syndrome). All AEs were coded using Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) version 26.0. Safety assessments also included laboratory analyses (hematology, clinical chemistry, and urinalysis parameters).

Statistical analysis

A total of 265 patients were planned for the study, with approximately 53 patients in each treatment group. This sample size was determined to provide at least 90% power to detect a 26% difference in endoscopic response rates at week 12 between the ABBV-154 group and the placebo group. Sample size estimation assumed a placebo response rate of 11% based on a Chi-squared test with a 2-sided significance level of 0.1. The early termination limited the actual power of the study.

The primary endpoint was analyzed between each ABBV-154 group and the placebo group in the 12-week induction period using the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test. The primary method planned for addressing missing data in the analysis of binary endpoints was nonresponder imputation, with multiple imputation used to manage missing data resulting from COVID-19. However, due to the early termination of the study resulting in limited available data (small sample size and insufficient duration of study drug exposure) nonresponder imputation was not applied. Consequently, all efficacy analyses were performed using an as-observed analysis among the intent-to-treat population, defined as patients who were randomized and received ≥1 dose of the study drug during the 12-week induction period. The inference for the treatment difference was based on the normal approximation to the binomial distribution. Safety was evaluated in all patients who received ≥1 dose of study drug according to the actual treatment received.

Results

Patients and study drug exposure

A total of 106 patients were randomized and received ≥1 dose of study drug during the 12-week induction period. The treatment groups included: ABBV-154 150 mg IV/80 mg SC EOW (N = 20), ABBV-154 300 mg IV/230 mg SC EOW (N = 22), ABBV-154 600 mg IV/530 mg SC EOW (N = 20), ABBV-154 600 mg IV/530 mg SC every 4 weeks (E4W; N = 23), and a placebo group (N = 21) (Figure 1). Sixty-six (62.3%) patients completed the 12-week induction period, 30 (28.3%) discontinued due to study termination by the sponsor, and 10 (9.4%) discontinued for other reasons (Figure 1).

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics were well balanced between the treatment groups (Table 1). Most patients were male (59.4%) and White (75.5%), with a mean age of 41 years. The median overall duration of Crohn’s disease was 10.9 years. At baseline, the mean (standard deviation [SD]) SES-CD was 15.2 (8.0) and CDAI was 311.9 (68.4); 40.6% of patients were taking concomitant glucocorticoids. In the ABBV-154 treatment groups, the mean duration of study drug exposure during the 12-week induction period ranged from 63.6 to 72.2 days. During the re-induction period, this duration ranged from 59.7 to 77.1 days, and during the maintenance period, it ranged from 63.2 to 104.9 days.

Efficacy at week 12

Based on observed data, the primary endpoint of achieving an endoscopic response at week 12 of the induction period was met by numerically greater proportions (n, [%]) of patients in each ABBV-154 treatment group compared with the placebo group. Specifically, the results were as follows: 1 patient (7.1%) in the ABBV-154 150 mg IV/80 mg SC EOW group, 4 patients (33.3%) in the ABBV-154 300 mg IV/230 mg SC EOW group, 4 patients (28.6%) in the ABBV-154 600 mg IV/530 mg SC EOW group, 3 patients (27.3%) in the ABBV-154 600 mg IV/530 mg SC E4W group, and no patients in the placebo group (Figure 2). At week 12 of the induction period, a numerically greater proportion of patients achieved the secondary endpoints of clinical remission per CDAI and SF/AP scores in each ABBV-154 group compared with the placebo group (Figure 2). At week 12, endoscopic remission or ulcer-free endoscopy were achieved by a numerically greater proportion of patients in the higher ABBV-154 dose groups compared with the placebo group, but not by patients treated with the lowest ABBV-154 dose (150 mg IV/80 mg SC EOW) (Figure S4). Moreover, numerically greater reductions in hs-CRP at week 12 were observed in higher ABBV-154 dose groups compared with the lowest ABBV-154 dose (150 mg IV/80 mg SC EOW) or placebo (Table S1). However, mean changes from baseline to week 12 in FCP demonstrated no marked differences between each ABBV-154 group and the placebo group (Table S1).

Safety

The occurrence of any TEAEs through week 12 of the induction period were generally comparable between the ABBV-154 treatment groups, ranging from 60.0% to 70.0%, and were slightly higher than those for placebo (52.4%) (Table 2). However, the incidence of serious TEAEs was slightly higher in the placebo group (14.3%) than across all patients treated with ABBV-154 (8.2%). Similar TEAE rates were observed with ABBV-154 treatment through the re-induction (41.7%-75.0%) and maintenance periods (61.5%-71.4%) (Table 2). The majority of TEAEs reported during the study were grade 1 (20.0%) or grade 2 (30.6%) severity. During the induction period, grade 3 events included serious infections in 3 patients treated with ABBV-154 and a hypersensitivity reaction in 1 patient treated with ABBV-154. The most frequently reported TEAEs (≥10%) across all ABBV-154 treatment groups during the study were COVID-19, Crohn’s disease, headache, and infusion-related hypersensitivity reaction. Few patients in the ABBV-154 groups (4 patients in the induction period, 2 patients in the re-induction period, and 1 patient in the maintenance period) discontinued treatment due to TEAEs. There were no deaths during this study.

The most common AEs of special interest were hypersensitivity reactions, followed by serious infections. Hypersensitivity reactions were more frequently reported in the ABBV-154 treatment groups than in the placebo group, although the majority of these were mild or moderate in severity. None of these events were serious allergic reactions. The hypersensitivity reactions observed in more than 1 patient in the ABBV-154 groups were injection site hypersensitivity, injection site rash, and infusion-related hypersensitivity reaction. Three of the hypersensitivity reaction events led to study drug discontinuation; 2 events occurred in the 600 mg IV/530 mg SC EOW re-induction group.

Iatrogenic Cushing’s syndrome (“moon face”) was reported in a 40-year-old female in the ABBV-154 150 mg IV/80 mg SC EOW group after 4 weeks during the induction period; the event was mild in severity (grade 1). There were no reports of adrenal insufficiency, malignancies, opportunistic infections, or active tuberculosis.

Mean changes in hematology, chemistry, and urinalysis parameters were generally small and likely represent within-patient variability. There were no clinically relevant trends in laboratory parameters observed in any treatment group over time.

Discussion

This phase 2b randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study evaluated the efficacy and safety of ABBV-154 in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease who had an inadequate response to or were intolerant of previous biologics. Despite early termination of the ABBV-154 program across various indications, this study provided proof of concept that ABBV-154 induction treatment showed efficacy for Crohn’s disease compared with placebo. Among the patients who completed the week 12 visit, 7.1%-33.3% of ABBV-154-treated patients achieved endoscopic response compared to 0% of patients receiving placebo, as assessed by blinded central review. Additionally, there were numerically higher proportions of patients achieving clinical remission, as measured by the CDAI and the SF/AP scales, at week 12 of the induction period. Patients treated with lower doses had outcomes similar to those treated with high doses. Efficacy data for the re-induction and maintenance periods are not reported due to limited exposure of patients to ABBV-154 beyond week 12.

The patients in this study had moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease with mean disease durations of 9.4-14.9 years and the majority had been treated with at least 2 prior biologics, thus, they represented an especially difficult-to-treat patient population. To put observed ABBV-154 efficacy data into the context of recent reports for approved therapies in Crohn’s disease, a phase 3 study of risankizumab in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease with previous intolerance or inadequate response to 1 or more biologics (MOTIVATE) demonstrated rates of endoscopic response per SES-CD at week 12 of 29% and 34% among patients treated with risankizumab 600 mg and 1200 mg, respectively.22 Similarly, in the phase 3 trial of upadacitinib in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease who had a history of failure of 1 or more biologic therapies (U-EXCEED), endoscopic response per SES-CD at week 12 was reported in 34.6% of patients.23 Notably, the response rates in these phase 3 studies were based on stringent non-responder imputation analysis.

Safety was assessed in all patients who received ≥1 dose of study drug during the induction, re-induction, and maintenance periods. Although data were limited due to early study termination, all ABBV-154 treatments had an acceptable safety profile and were generally well tolerated in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease, with a low number of serious TEAEs, severe TEAEs, and TEAEs leading to study drug discontinuation. The TEAEs observed in this study were consistent with expectations for this patient population and the mechanism of action of the investigational drug.

ABBV-154 was developed to improve efficacy by using 2 anti-inflammatory mechanisms: TNF inhibition and glucocorticoid receptor targeting. This approach aims to reduce the risk of systemic side effects linked to glucocorticoids by delivering the GRM payload to cells that express transmembrane TNF, such as activated immune cells.18,24 However, after review of the available data from the 3 phase 2 trials of ABBV-154 for Crohn’s disease, rheumatoid arthritis, and polymyalgia rheumatica, it was determined that the benefit-risk profile did not sufficiently differentiate ABBV-154 from other currently available therapies. Consequently, despite the overall positive efficacy and safety findings, the sponsor decided to cease development of ABBV-154 (terminating all 3 studies early) due to lack of differentiation from existing therapies and not because of safety concerns. The early termination of the current study confined the sample size and limits the robustness of the findings. Additionally, the long-term safety effects of this anti-TNF antibody-drug conjugate remain to be established.

Conclusion

In conclusion, for the first 12 weeks of treatment, ABBV-154 was well tolerated and demonstrated proof of concept for efficacy over placebo in patients with moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease who previously had inadequate response or intolerance to 1 or more biologic therapy. Due to the voluntary early termination of the study, the available dataset in this study is limited in both sample size and duration of exposure to study drug. Therefore, interpretation of the safety and efficacy data should be approached with caution.

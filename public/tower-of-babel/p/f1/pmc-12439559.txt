USP7 overexpression prevents the progression of clear cell renal cell carcinoma by enhancing pyroptosis via TRIP12 deubiquitination
Authors: Hongsheng Li, Yao Ning, Junjie Yu, Yiju Chen, Qiang He, Juan Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTUbiquitin-specific protease 7 (USP7) involves in various human cancers due to its capacity for binding and stabilizing specific target proteins through deubiquitylation, but its roles in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) development remains unknown. This study aimed to determine the role of USP7 in the pyroptosis mechanism in ccRCC, thereby providing novel anti-ccRCC strategies. Bioinformatics analysis was conducted to explore the expression of USP7 and TRIP12 in ccRCC patients and their association with patient overall survival. qRT-PCR, western blotting, and ELISA were used to determine the levels of USP7, TRIP12, pyroptosis-related factors. Cell viability, invasion, pyroptosis, and proliferation were evaluated using CCK-8, Transwell, flow cytometry, and immunohistochemistry assays. The direct interaction between USP7 and TRIP12 was validated by co-immunoprecipitation (CO-IP). We found downregulated USP7 in ccRCC tissues, which was related to the shorter patient overall survival (OS). Significantly, USP7 was also decreased in ccRCC cells. oe-USP7 (USP7 overexpression) inhibited ccRCC cell viability, migration, invasion, and enhanced pyroptosis. The caspase-1 specific inhibitor, VX-765, partially abolished the anti-viability, and pro-pyroptosis effects of oe-USP7, indicating USP7 overexpression prevented the malignant phenotype of ccRCC cells by enhancing caspase-1 dependent pyroptosis. Similarly, the shorter patient OS was indicated to be associated with reduced TRIP12 in ccRCC tissues. Besides, oe-USP7 increased TRIP12 expression in ccRCC cells by deubiquitinating TRIP12, while sh-TRIP12 eliminated the biological functions of oe-USP7. The similar effects of oe-USP7 on ccRCC development were found in ccRCC mice. USP7 mediated TRIP12 deubiquitination inhibited ccRCC progression by enhancing pyroptosis.KEYWORDS: Clear cell renal cell carcinoma, pyroptosis, deubiquitination, USP7, TRIP12

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is one of the most prevalent cancers of the urinary system and is responsible for almost 2% of global cancer diagnoses and deaths.1 Approximately 430,000 new cases of RCC are diagnosed yearly, with 180,000 deaths occurring globally.2 Clear cell RCC (ccRCC) is the most typical subtype, making up approximately three-fourths of RCC.3 Surgical resection is the primary therapy for localized ccRCC.4 However, approximately 30% of localized ccRCC patients ultimately suffer metastases with high fatality rates and need systemic remedies.5 Despite improvements in immunotherapy for ccRCC, these improvements merely apply to a subset of ccRCC patients.1 Therefore, exploration of the molecular mechanisms behind ccRCC and the discovery of novel therapeutic targets are desperately necessary.

Pyroptosis is an inflammatory programmed cell-death mechanism, marked by the formation of plasma membrane pore, swelling, and rupture.6,7 In the canonical pathway, nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-like receptors (NLRs) or absent in myeloma 2 (AIM2)-like receptor (ALRs) activation induces inflammasome formation.8 The inflammasome commonly consists of the inflammasome sensor NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3), the adaptor apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain (ASC), and the effector pro-caspase-1.9 Activated caspase-1 promotes the release of the pro-inflammatory cytokines, interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18, and cleavage of the pore-forming molecule, gasdermin D (GSDMD), eventually conducing to cell death.8 Based on accumulating evidence, pyroptosis seems to be involved in cancer development.10,11 Notably, pyroptosis can also suppress the onset and development of tumors; however, pyroptosis can induce the development of a microenvironment that contributes to tumor growth.12 For instance, a recent study has revealed that bexarotene induces pyroptosis to inhibit the malignant progression of ovarian cancer.13 However, another study revealed that GSDME-mediated pyroptosis can promote colorectal cancer cell proliferation and facilitate the progression of colorectal cancer.14 NLRP3 inflammasome-induced pyroptosis has been demonstrated to inhibit RCC proliferation.15 In ccRCC, inducing pyroptosis could postpone the tumor progression. For example, STING depletion showed strong anti-tumor effects in ccRCC via inducing GSDMD-dependent pyroptosis.16 Linc00023 down-regulation could facilitate the malignant progression of ccRCC by reducing pyroptosis.17

Ubiquitination is a post-translational modification that mediates protein degradation during various biological processes.18,19 Protein ubiquitination relies on ubiquitin-activating enzyme, conjugating enzyme, and ubiquitin-protein ligase, which can be reversed by deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) through the deletion of ubiquitin (Ub) moieties.20,21 Ubiquitin-specific protease 7 (USP7) is a well-studied DUB that eliminates ubiquitin tags from specific target protein substrates, thereby affecting their degradation, subcellular localization, interplay, and activity.22 Defective cell processes mediated by USP7 and the aberrant expression and activity of USP7 may contribute to tumor development and progression.23 Notably, USP7 expression and its role vary in different tumor types. For instance, USP7 inactivation was found to promote the progression of p53-deficient lung cancer, and USP7 was indicated to inhibit this malignancy by deubiquitinating SMAD family member 3 (SMAD3).24 According to a recent study, USP7 is overexpressed in head and neck squamous cell cancer, which facilitates tumor progression by deubiquitinating and stabilizing Tafazzin (TAZ).25 USP7 induces cell pyroptosis by suppressing NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) ubiquitination and degradation under inflammatory pathological conditions.26 As the role of USP7 in ccRCC cell pyroptosis remains unknown, USP7 expression and pyroptosis signatures were assessed in ccRCC.

Thyroid hormone receptor-interacting protein 12 (TRIP12), belonging to the E3 ubiquitin ligase family, participates in cancer development by ubiquitinating core proteins and modulating gene expression.27 TRIP12 has been identified as a key prognostic biomarker in several cancers, such as anal squamous cell carcinoma28 and osteosarcoma.29 TRIP12 mRNA is downregulated in testicular germ cell tumors and upregulated in several cancers, such as thymoma.27 USP7 has been found to interact with TRIP12, and its deubiquitination is required for TRIP12 stability in tumor cells.30 A previous study confirmed that USP7 overexpression promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression by stabilizing TRIP12 through deubiquitination.31 However, the expression and the interaction of TRIP12 with USP7 has not been investigated in ccRCC.

Based on the above reports, we propose the hypothesis that USP7 may also dysregulate in ccRCC and regulate cell pyroptosis, and USP7 deubiquitination mediated the TRIP12 stabilization involves the progression of ccRCC. To confirm the hypothesis, we first investigated the expression of USP7 in ccRCC by both bioinformatics analyses and in vitro experiments, followed by the exploration on the specific role of USP7 in ccRCC progression and cells pyroptosis by a series of functional experiments. Besides, the expression of TRIP12 and its interaction with USP7 was explored to further illustrate the potential mechanism. Finally, in vivo experiments were further conducted to validate the main findings. This study provides further insights into the molecular mechanisms of ccRCC and a potential therapeutic target for ccRCC.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Bioinformatics analyses were undertaken using R language. The gene expression profile data was downloaded from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database, which consisted of the data from 72 normal human tissue samples and 524 ccRCC tissue samples. Expression of USP7 and TRIP12 between ccRCC tumor and normal samples in the TCGA cohort was compared by Wilcoxon test. The Gene-Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis platform (GEPIA; http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/index.html) was used to analyze the gene expression data from TCGA. Survival analysis for USP7 and TRIP12 in ccRCC was conducted using Kaplan-Meier curve with log-rank test based on the data in GEPIA.

Cell culture

Human normal renal epithelial cell line HK-2 and ccRCC cell lines Caki-1, A498, and 786-O were acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). Cells were cultured in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; #21068028, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; #16140089, Gibco) and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin at 37°C with 5% CO2.

Cell transfection and treatment

All plasmids utilized were supplied by GeneChem (Shanghai, China). USP7 overexpression lentiviruses (oe-USP7) and down-expression lentiviruses (sh-USP7) were transfected into Caki-1 and 786-O cells to upregulate and downregulate the USP7 expression, respectively. Additionally, a short hairpin RNA targeting TRIP12 (sh-TRIP12) was employed to silence TRIP12 in Caki-1 cells. Empty vectors (oe-NC) and scrambled shRNA (sh-NC) were used as controls. The shRNA sequences applied are listed in Table S1. Briefly, 786-O and Caki-1 cells (2 × 105 cells/mL) were inoculated in 6-well plates, and the plasmids were transfected into cells through the HighGene transfection reagent (#RM09014, ABclonal, Wuhan, China) together with corresponding lentivirus packaging plasmids. After 48 h of transfection, lentivirus particles were collected and concentrated. For cells lentivirus infections, 786-O and Caki-1 cells were seeded into 6-well plate with a density of 2 × 105/mL. Subsequently, lentiviruses (1 × 108 TU/mL), including oe-USP7 or sh-USP7 lentiviruses or their corresponding NC lentiviruses, were added to infect cells when the cells at 70–90% fusion degree. After 18 h of infections, cells were cultured in fresh DMEM for 48 h, and 2.5 μg/mL puromycin was added to screen the stable cells. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and western blotting were carried out to determine the transfection rate. Cellular morphology was monitored through a microscope (BX53; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). All the experiments in this study were repeated at least three times.

For mechanistic investigation, all cells were treated with 20 μM VX-765 (a caspase-1 specific inhibitor; #V57300, Jizhi Biochemical Technology, Shanghai, China) at 48 h after transfection. In addition, 20 and 50 µM MG132 (a proteasome inhibitor; #2194, Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA, USA) were administered to Caki-1 cells for 6 h to evaluate TRIP12 stabilization. Cells without MG132 treatment were used as controls. After 24 h of stable transfection with oe-NC or oe-USP7, Caki-1 cells were treated with cycloheximide (CHX; a protein synthesis inhibitor; 100 μg/mL; #A49960, Jizhi Biochemical Technology, Shanghai, China) and then subjected to western blotting at the given time points (0, 2, 4, and 6 h).

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was acquired from HK-2, Caki-1, A498, and 786-O cells using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Reverse transcription was conducted for complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis through the FastKing-RT SuperMix (#KR118–02, Tiangen, Beijing, China). qRT-PCR was conducted on a Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) via SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (#4364344; Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). The following reaction program was employed: 95°C for 3 min; 95°C for 12 s and 62°C for 40 s (40 cycles). The 2−ΔΔCt method was applied to quantify gene expression, and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was presented as an internal reference. Primers are listed in Table S1.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Caki-1 and 786-O cells were seeded in 96-well plates (2,000 cells/well) and subsequently treated with specific agents for 48 h. All cells were incubated with 10 µL CCK-8 solution (#C0037, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for another 2 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was evaluated via a microplate reader (DR-3518 G, Hiwell Diatek, Wuxi, China).

Cell migration and invasion assay

Transwell assay was used to detect the cell migration and invasion ability. For cell invasion, the upper chamber pre-coated with 20% Matrigel was added with 200 μL cells (1 × 106 cells/mL) in serum-free DMEM. DMEM was added to the lower chamber. After an incubation of 24 h, cells were washed using phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, #C0221A, Beyotime), fixed with methanol at 4°C for 30 min, and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. Cells in three random fields were photographed under a microscope (CKX53; Olympus). Cell invasion was quantized through the ImageJ software v1.8.0 supplied by the National Institutes of Health (NIH, MD, USA). For cell migration, all the above processes were preformed, but without using Matrigel.

Immunofluorescence

Cells (Caki-1 and 786-O) were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at 25°C for 15 min. Then, cells were permeabilized with 1% Triton X-100 for 10 min, sealed in 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA; #ST2249, Beyotime) for 30 min, and incubated with anti-ASC primary antibody (1:200; #DF6304, Affinity, Changzhou, China). After three rounds of washing with PBS, the cells were incubated with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) Rabbit Anti-Goat IgG (H+L) secondary antibody (1:100; #AS024, ABclonal), and the nuclei were stained with 4, 6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; #C1005, Beyotime). Images of stained cells were obtained using a confocal imaging system (UltraVIEW VoX; Perkin Elmer).

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL)

TUNEL assay kit (Beyotime) was used to assess Caki-1 and 786-O cell apoptosis. Based on the supplier’s instructions, the collected cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (#P0099, Beyotime) for 30 min, incubated with PBS containing 0.3% Triton X-100 (#T8200, Solarbio, Beijing, China) at 25°C for 5 min, and stained with prepared TUNEL detection solution (50 μL) at 37°C for 1 h in the dark. Finally, cells were photographed under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus Corporation, Japan).

Flow cytometry

Pyroptosis of 786-O and Caki-1 cells was detected by flow cytometry utilizing caspase-1 and propidium iodide (PI) staining as previously described.32 Specifically, after two rounds of washing with PBS, cells were suspended in 300 µL of binding buffer, and stained with caspase-1 staining (#ab219935, Abcam) and PI for 1 hat 25°C in dark. Subsequently, cells were measured by flow cytometry on a CytoFLEX S flow cytometer (Beckman, FL, USA) and were analyzed using Quest software (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lake, NJ, USA).

Ubiquitination assay by co-immunoprecipitation (CO-IP)

Protein was isolated from Caki-1 cells using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (#P0013B, Beyotime). Protein concentration was determined using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) kit (#P0010S, Beyotime). Cell lysates (250 μL; protein concentration: 1 μg/mL) were incubated with anti-TRIP12 primary antibody (1:30; #600–401-FQ1, Bethyl Laboratories, Montgomery, TX, USA) and Dynabeads Protein G (50 μL; #26149, Thermo Fisher, NY, USA) at 4°C overnight. After washing and drying, the beads were centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 1 min at 4°C. The proteins were separated via 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE; #P0015A, Beyotime) and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (#FFP24, Beyotime). The membranes were sealed with 5% nonfat milk (#P0216, Beyotime) for 1 h and incubated with anti-ubiquitin primary antibody (1:1,000; #ab134953, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) at 4°C overnight. After incubation with Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) secondary antibody (1:2,000; #ab6721, Abcam) at 25°C for 1 h, the membranes were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (#P1000–5, Applygen, Beijing, China). TRIP12 ubiquitylation (Ub-TRIP12) was calculated using ImageJ software (v1.8.0; NIH) and normalized to GAPDH. For IP assay, Flag-USP7, Myc-TRIP12 and HA-Ub plasmids were transfected into AKI-1 cells for 48 h, treated with 20 μM M132 for 6 h, and immunoprecipitated with anti-MYC antibody. Western blotting analysis was performed using antibodies against HA, Myc, or Flag.

Animal experiments

All animal experimental procedures were approved by Yangzhou University (202209003). Ten BALB/c nude mice (specific pathogen-free, male, 5 weeks old, 20 g) were supplied by the Institute of Comparative Medicine, Yangzhou University, China. All mice were bred under a 12-h light/dark cycle at 19–25°C, with 35–55% humidity and ad libitum food and water. Mice were randomly assigned to the oe-NC and oe-USP7 groups (n = 5). To establish the xenograft model, 5 × 106 oe-NC- or oe-USP7-transfected Caki-1 cells were subcutaneously injected into the dorsal flanks of the mice. Tumor volume was determined every seven days and calculated as: volume (mm3) = length × width2/2. After 4 weeks of injection, blood was acquired from the caudal vein of mice. All mice were sacrificed via cervical dislocation. Tumors were weighed and tumor tissues were obtained for subsequent assays.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumor tissues from xenograft mice were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, embedded in paraffin, and cut into 5 μm sections. All tumor slices were dewaxed in xylene for 20 min and dehydrated using gradient concentrations of ethanol (100%, 95%, 90%, 80%, and 70%). After deparaffinization and hydration, the sections were incubated with normal goat serum (50 μL; #SL038, Solarbio) at 25°C for 15 min. Then, sections were cultured with anti-Ki67 (1:100; #AF0198, Affinity) and anti-USP7 (1:100; #DF6931, Affinity) primary antibodies, followed by Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L secondary antibody (1:1,000; #ab6721, Abcam) at 25°C for 15 min. Thereafter, sections were stained with 3, 3’-diaminobenzidine (#P0202, Beyotime) and counterstained with hematoxylin for 30 min. After dehydration with xylene and ethanol, the sections were observed via a microscope (BX53, Olympus).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

ELISA kits were utilized to determine the contents of inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-18, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α in Caki-1 and 786-O cells or the serum of xenograft mice. Optical density values at 450 nm were measured using a microplate reader (DR-3518 G, Hiwell Diatek) and quantified using ImageJ software (v1.8.0; NIH). The following ELISA kits were used: IL-1β (human; #ab214025, Abcam), IL-18 (human; #ab215539, Abcam), TNF-α (human; #ab181421, Abcam), IL-1β (mouse; #ab100705, Abcam), IL-18 (mouse; #ab216165, Abcam), and TNF-α (mouse; #ab208348, Abcam).

Western blotting

Total protein was separated from cells (HK-2, Caki-1, A498, and 786-O) and tumor tissues using RIPA lysis buffer (#P0013B, Beyotime). Protein was then quantified using a BCA kit (#P0010S, Beyotime), separated via 10% SDS-PAGE (#P0015A, Beyotime) and transferred to PVDF membranes (#FFP24, Beyotime). The membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk (#P0216, Beyotime) for 1 h and treated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight followed by Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L secondary antibody (1:2,000; #ab6721, Abcam) at 25°C for 1 h. An ECL kit (#P1000–5, Applygen) was used for membrane visualization. Protein expression was analyzed using ImageJ software (v1.8.0; NIH) and standardized by GAPDH expression. The primary antibodies utilized were as follows: anti-USP7 (1:1,000; #DF6931, Affinity), anti-ASC (1:1,000; #DF6304, Affinity), anti-cleaved-caspase-1 (1:1,000; #PA5–38099, Invitrogen), anti-pro-caspase-1 (1:1,000; #14–9832–82, eBioscience, CA, USA), anti-full-length GSDMD (GSDMD-FL; 1:1,000; #PA5–104324, Invitrogen), anti-GSDMD N-terminal (GSDMD-N; 1:1,000; #ab215203, Abcam), anti-TRIP12 (1:1,000; #600–401-FQ1, Bethyl Laboratories), anti-E-cadherin (1:1,000; CST #3195), anti-N-cadherin (1:1,000; CST #13116), and anti-GAPDH (1:1,000; #2118, Cell Signaling Technology).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses of the experimental data were undertaken utilizing GraphPad 7.0 software (La Jolla, CA, USA). All experimental data are exhibited as mean ± standard deviation. Student’s t-test was employed to compare the differences between two groups. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test was employed to compare the differences among three or more groups. The threshold for statistical significance is p < .05.

Results

USP7 is down-expressed in ccRCC and its downregulation is related to poor survival

USP7, a well-known tumor-related deubiquitinating enzyme, is implicated in the tumorigenesis and development of RCC.33 To figure out the potential role of USP7 in ccRCC development, its expression was initially evaluated through bioinformatic analysis. Here, we found that the USP7 expression level in ccRCC tissue samples was significantly reduced relative to that in normal tissue samples (p < .0001; Figure 1A). Furthermore, based on the GEPIA database, the reduction of USP7 in tumor tissue is analyzed and validated in two subtypes of renal tumor tissue (Kidney Renal Clear Cell Carcinoma, KIRC and Kidney Chromophobe, KICH) (Figure 1B). Furthermore, Kaplan-Meier curves revealed that ccRCC patients with low USP7 expression undertook shorter overall survival overall survival than patients with high USP7 expression (p = .0072; Figure 1C). The clinical information, including diagnosis age, gender, grade, and stage in high and low USP7 groups, was presented in Table S2. Also, USP7 expression was further analyzed in human normal renal epithelial cell line (HK-2) and three ccRCC cell lines (A498, Caki-1, and 786-O). The USP7 expression levels in ccRCC cell lines were found to be significantly lower than those in HK-2 (p < .01; Figure 1D,E), which was in line with the results of bioinformatic analysis. Among the ccRCC cell lines, USP7 levels in Caki-1 and 786-O cells were markedly lower than those in A498 cells (p < .01; Figure 1D,E). Therefore, Caki-1 and 786-O cells were chosen for the further analyses.

USP7 overexpression inhibits viability, migration, and invasion, and promotes apoptosis of ccRCC cells

Owing to the low expression of USP7 in ccRCC cells, Caki-1 and 786-O cells were transfected with oe-NC or oe-USP7 to further investigate the function of USP7 in ccRCC. The mRNA and protein levels of USP7 in Caki-1 and 786-O cells were elevated via oe-USP7 transfection, suggesting that in vitro USP7-overexpressing experiments were successfully performed (p < .01; Figure 2A,B). Functionally, the viability, migration, and invasion of ccRCC cells were notably suppressed after oe-USP7 transfection compared with control cells (p < .01; Figure 2C–E). Additionally, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) markers E-cadherin was significantly increased and N-cadherin was significantly decreased in oe-USP7 groups when compared with control groups (Figure 2F).

USP7 overexpression prevents the malignant phenotype of ccRCC cells by enhancing caspase-1 dependent pyroptosis

Induction of pyroptosis has been employed as a therapeutic strategy to inhibit tumor progression.34 Distinct from apoptosis, pyroptosis is a pro-inflammatory programmed cell death mechanism characterized by swollen cells and increased levels of inflammatory cytokines (including IL-1β and IL-18).35,36 Besides, activation of NLRP3 is crucial for pyroptosis.35 NLRP3 inflammasome consists of NLRP3, ASC, and caspase-1.37 GSDMD-FL and GSDMD-N are common markers of pyroptosis.38 In this study, ccRCC cells were swollen after oe-USP7 transfection with bubble-like protrusions, which are the morphological features of classical pyroptosis; and more pyroptosis occurred in oe-USP7 transfection groups (Figure 4A). Considering the crosstalk between pyroptosis and apoptosis, we also detected cell apoptosis by TUNEL assay. There were more TUNEL-positive cells in oe-USP7 group than that of corresponding NC group (Figure S1), indicating the presence of apoptosis. TEM showed less pore in plasma membrane in control group while more pore in ccRCC cells (Figure 4B), indicating the formation of Gasdermin pores during pyroptosis. This observation under TEM differed from the intact cell membrane exhibited in apoptosis. Hence, we proposed pyroptosis as the main mode of cell death in ccRCC caused by USP overexpression in this study. Based on immunofluorescence analysis, ASC expression increased in oe-USP7-transfected ccRCC cells (Figure 4C). Moreover, ELISA detection showed that oe-USP7 elevated the release of IL-1β, IL-18, and TNF-α in ccRCC cells (p < .01; Figure 4D). Additionally, protein levels of NLRP3, ASC, cleaved-caspase-1/pro-caspase-1, and GSDMD-FL/GSDMD-N were significantly increased in oe-USP7-transfected ccRCC cells (p < .05; Figure 4E).

USP7 overexpression promotes ccRCC cell pyroptosis by deubiquitinating TRIP12

The deubiquitylating activity of USP7 is essential for TRIP12 stability in cancer cells;30 therefore, the TRIP12 axis was analyzed in Caki-1 cells. Bioinformatic analysis showed that TRIP12 expression level was markedly decreased in ccRCC tissue samples compared with that in normal tissue samples (p < .0001; Figure 6A). Validation in GEPIA database also emphasized the decreased TRIP12 in ccRCC (Figure 6B). Furthermore, Kaplan-Meier curves indicated that ccRCC patients with low TRIP12 expression had shorter overall survival than patients with high TRIP12 expression (p = .00022; Figure 6C). Subsequently, to investigate whether USP7 can stabilize TRIP12, Caki-1 cells were treated with the proteasome inhibitor, MG132. MG132 markedly increased the protein level of TRIP12 in Caki-1 cells in a dose-dependent manner (p < .05; Figure 6D). Caki-1 cells were also treated with protein synthesis inhibitor CHX to determine the effect of USP7 on TRIP12 stabilization. oe-USP7 markedly elevated the protein level of TRIP12 in Caki-1 cells (p < .01) and prolonged the half-life of TRIP12 with stable and high USP7 expression (Figure 6E). The ubiquitination level of TRIP12 was evaluated in Caki-1 cells to determine whether USP7 can deubiquitinate TRIP12. After oe-USP7 transfection, the level of Ub-TRIP12 was significantly reduced, accompanied by increased expression of USP7 and TRIP12 (p < .01; Figure 6F). Additionally, sh-USP7 significantly decreased the expression level of TRIP12 in Caki-1 cells (Figure 6(G)), implying a correlation between USP7 and TRIP12 level. The public CPTAC ccRCC proteomic data showed that the protein expression of TRIP12 was significantly positively correlated with that of USP7 (Spearman r = 0.3, p = 2e-03, Figure S2), further demonstrating the correlation between USP7 and TRIP12. IP assay demonstrated ubiquitination in HA-Ub+Myc-TRIP12 and HA-Ub+Myc-TRIP12+Flag-USP7 groups, and the ubiquitination level in HA-Ub+Myc-TRIP12+Flag-USP7 groups was higher than that in HA-Ub+Myc-TRIP12+ flag-USP7 groups (Figure 6H), which demonstrating the ubiquitination binding between TRIP12 and USP7.

To further validate the mechanisms underlying USP7 stabilization of TRIP12 in ccRCC, Caki-1 cells were transfected with oe-USP7 and/or sh-TRIP12 (TRIP12 knockdown). sh-TRIP12 did not influence USP7 expression, but reversed the regulatory effect of oe-USP7 on TRIP12 expression in Caki-1 cells (p < .01; Figure 7A,B). sh-TRIP12 reversed the suppressed viability and enhanced necrosis of Caki-1 cells induced by oe-USP7 (p < .01; Figure 7C,D). In terms of pyroptosis, sh-TRIP12 eliminated the promotive effect of oe-USP7 on the levels of ASC, cleaved-caspase-1/pro-caspase-1, and GSDMD-FL/GSDMD-N (p < .05; Figure 7E).

USP7 overexpression inhibits tumor growth and enhances cell pyroptosis in mice with ccRCC

To determine whether USP7 upregulation can restrain ccRCC tumor growth in vivo, a xenograft tumor mouse model was established by subcutaneous injection of oe-NC- or oe-USP7-transfected Caki-1. Our findings revealed a notable reduction in ccRCC tumor volume and weight after oe-USP7 transfection (p < .01; Figure 8A). Additionally, expression of the cell proliferation marker, Ki67, in ccRCC tumor tissues was decreased by oe-USP7, with elevated USP7 expression (Figure 8B). Overexpression of USP7 upregulated the levels of IL-1β, IL-18, and TNF-α in the serum of tumor-bearing mice (p < .01; Figure 8C). Moreover, oe-USP7 significantly elevated the protein levels of ASC, cleaved-caspase-1/pro-caspase-1, and GSDMD-FL/GSDMD-N in ccRCC tumor tissues, and promoted USP7 and TRIP12 expression (p < .01; Figure 8D).

Discussion

ccRCC is the most common and aggressive type of all urological malignancies, and is associated with high mortality.39 Although localized ccRCC can be treated through surgical resection, 30% of patients initially display metastasis at first diagnosis, and its prognosis is poor owing to limitations in present standard therapies.40 Therefore, it is indispensable to further explore the mechanisms behind ccRCC and discover practical and effective strategies for the treatment of ccRCC. Recently, the deubiquitinase, USP7, has attracted extensive attention as a novel target for cancer treatment. USP7 expression is aberrant in several cancers, which leads to tumor progression.41 For example, in lung cancer, inhibition of USP7 delays tumor growth in mice.42 In gastric cancer, USP7 inhibitor can suppress tumor cells proliferation by stabilizing P53 in vitro and in vivo.43 USP7 could stabilize TRIM27 to promote hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation and invasion.44 However, a recent study revealed that USP7 overexpression suppresses lung adenocarcinoma cell proliferation by blocking the ERK1/2 pathway.15 This might demonstrate the different roles of USP7 in different tumor types. Here, USP7 was downregulated in human ccRCC tissues compared with normal controls, and the patient poor survival was associated with low USP7 expression. At the cell level, USP7 expression was lower in ccRCC cells than that in normal renal epithelial cells, and USP7 overexpression reduced ccRCC cell viability, migration and invasive capacity. These results were coincidence with most of the results that abolished USP7 exerted positive role in cancer cells.42–44 Significantly, we observed that USP7 overexpression facilitated ccRCC cell pyroptosis with elevated TRIP12 expression and reduced TRIP12 ubiquitination. These results imply that overexpression of USP7 may prevent the progression of ccRCC by deubiquitinating TRIP12, by which USP7 promotes pyroptosis.

Pyroptosis is a recently recognized mechanism of programmed cell death implicated in the pathogenesis and progression of cancer.45 Morphologically, the main characteristics of pyroptotic cells are swelling and plasma membrane rupture.46 Mechanistically, inflammasome assembly activates caspase-1, which cleaves GSDMD to induce pyroptosis, leading to pore formation and inflammatory cytokine release, ultimately resulting in cell death.47 IL-1β and IL-18 are the main inflammatory markers of pyroptosis.48 TNF-α is a well-studied pro-inflammatory cytokine involved in tumor immunity and is released during pyroptosis.49,50 ASC and pro-caspase-1 are major constituents of the inflammasome.51 Cleaved-caspase-1 is also a widely used indicator of pyroptosis.52 GSDMD-N is generated following cleavage of GSDMD-FL by caspase-1.53 Previous data revealed that NLRP3-induced release of IL-1β and IL-18 could promote the progression of multiple myeloma.54 Nevertheless, another study showed that upregulation of IL-1β, IL-18, ASC, and pro-caspase-1 could enhance apoptosis and inhibit the malignant phenotype of ovarian cancer.51 The upregulation of cleaved-caspase-1 and GSDMD-N had been revealed to enhance pyroptosis, thereby suppressing tumor growth in colorectal cancer.55 Notably, elevated levels of pyroptosis-related proteins (IL-1β, cleaved-caspase-1/pro-caspase-1, and GSDMD-N) can prevent RCC cell proliferation.15 In this study, swollen ccRCC cells were observed after USP7 upregulation, suggesting that pyroptosis occurred in ccRCC cells due to USP7 overexpression. Additionally, the release of IL-1β, IL-18, and TNF-α in ccRCC cells was increased following USP7 overexpression. USP7 overexpression promoted the levels of ASC, cleaved-caspase-1/pro-caspase-1, and GSDMD-FL/GSDMD-N in ccRCC cells. However, caspase-1 specific inhibitor VX-765 abolished the suppressive effects of USP7 overexpression on viability and pyroptosis of ccRCC cells. These results revealed that USP7 overexpression facilitated caspase-1 dependent pyroptosis of ccRCC cells.

The E3 ubiquitin ligase, TRIP12, is a significant downstream target of USP7 in tumor cells that can be stabilized by USP7 via deubiquitination.30 TRIP12 is a chromatin-related protein that acts as a switch in cell cycle and genome integrity.56 Changes in TRIP12 can serve as prognostic indicators of the therapeutic response in cancers.27 Overexpression of TRIP12 can contribute to the poor prognosis of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma.57 Moreover, downregulation of the TRIP12 mRNA has been observed in testicular germ cell tumors.27 TRIP12 exhibits inhibited role in cancer cell growth and metastasis in several cancers.58,59 Mechanically, TRIP12 has been demonstrated to regulate suppresses EMT58 and regulate TGFβ signaling pathway in breaster cancer.60 Moreover, TRIP12 has been demonstrated to promote pro-IL-1β turnover and reduce mature IL-1β production,61 which might reduce the inflammatory response in tumor microenvironment. Nevertheless, little is known about the specific role of TRIP12 in ccRCC. Interestingly, we found decreased TRIP12 expression in human ccRCC tissues compared with normal controls and it was related to the poor patient survival. The upregulation of USP7 was previously found to deubiquitinate and stabilize TRIP12, leading to hepatocellular carcinoma progression.31 Besides, USP7 mediated stability enhancement of TRIP12 was also reported to involve the progression of Helicobacter pylori derived gastric cancer.62 Similarly, we also found that USP7 mediated TRIP12 stability by deubiquitination in ccRCC progression. Specifically, results from our current study suggested that USP7 mediated TRIP12 deubiquitination inhibited ccRCC progression by enhancing pyroptosis. The proteasome inhibitor, MG132, dose-dependently elevated TRIP12 expression in ccRCC cells. Furthermore, a previous study indicated that USP7 could stabilize TRIP12 in ccRCC cells as the expression of TRIP12 and its half-life increased after USP7 upregulation, and reduce the ubiquitination level of TRIP12 in ccRCC cells. TRIP12 knockdown did not affect USP7 expression, but reversed the USP7-induced anti-viability, pro-necrosis, and pro-pyroptosis effects in ccRCC cells. These findings imply that elevated USP7 facilitates pyroptosis of ccRCC cells by deubiquitinating TRIP12.

Animal experiments were performed to verify the antitumor function of USP7 in ccRCC. USP7 upregulation decreased ccRCC tumor volume and weight. In contrast, Ki67 expression in ccRCC was reduced following USP7 overexpression. Also, USP7 overexpression significantly upregulated TRIP12 expression and increased cell pyroptosis in ccRCC tumor tissues as the levels of IL-1β, IL-18, TNF-α, ASC, cleaved-caspase-1/pro-caspase-1, and GSDMD-FL/GSDMD-N were elevated. Collectively, these data imply that USP7 overexpression can directly suppress tumor growth and accelerate the pyroptosis of ccRCC cells in mice.

Our study has some limitations. Firstly, we proposed that USP7 mediated TRIP12 deubiquitination inhibits ccRCC progression by enhancing pyroptosis, but it is still unknown whether there are other mechanisms involved in the role of USP7 in ccRCC, which will be explored by further studies. For example, TUNEL staining indicated the presence of apoptosis after USP7 overexpression. However, cell morphology and electron microscopy analysis more clearly demonstrated the characteristics of cell pyroptosis, such as cell swelling, rupture of the plasma membrane. Therefore, we mainly investigated pyroptosis in this study. Actually, there are crosstalk between pyroptosis and apoptosis. For example, the excessive activation of inflammasomes during pyroptosis leads to mitochondrial damage and indirectly triggers apoptosis. Besides, USP7 had been reported to regulate other cell death forms, such as ferroptosis and autophagy.63,64 Therefore, further investigations on USP in ccRCC are necessary to make a comprehensive understanding on its functions in ccRCC. Besides, although our research results have demonstrated that USP7 is a crucial therapeutic target in ccRCC, more experiments and preclinical studies are needed in the future to confirm its therapeutic potential in the clinical management of ccRCC. Moreover, the prognostic value of USP7 should also be validated based on large-scale clinical cohort from the real world.

Conclusion

USP7 overexpression was found to promote pyroptosis via TRIP12 deubiquitination to prevent the progression of ccRCC (Figure 9). This study deepens our understanding of the pathological and molecular mechanisms of ccRCC and provides a reference for developing USP7 as a novel target for ccRCC.

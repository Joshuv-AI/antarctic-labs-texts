Multimode OAM antenna with reduced beam divergence for 6G networks
Authors: M Venkateswara Rao, Budhaditya Bhattacharyya, G Challa Ram, S Yuvaraj, Jagannath Malik, Arif Mawardi Ismail, Mohd Najib Mohd Yasin, Safpbri Johari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOrbital angular momentum (OAM) in microwave frequencies introduces a promising new dimension for enhancing channel capacity in future 6G wireless communication systems. However, the practical use of OAM waves for long-distance transmission is limited by their inherent beam divergence, which reduces signal strength over distance. This paper proposes a transmissive metasurface lens designed to reduce the divergence of an OAM beam generated by a multimode uniform circular array (UCA) operating at 6 GHz. The design of the metasurface lens is based on the optical converging axicon concept, which is used to achieve the required phase-shift distribution across the metasurface. The UCA generates OAM modes of l = − 1, + 1, and 0 through distinct feed excitations. Comprehensive evaluations comparing the UCA performance with and without the metasurface lens were carried out based on radiation patterns, S11, electric field amplitude, and phase distribution. The results demonstrate a significant improvement: the beam divergence is reduced from 18° to 10°, and the maximum gain is increased from 10.7 dBi to 14.1 dBi. These improvements indicate a more focused and directional OAM beam, enhancing the practicality of OAM-based communication systems for extended-range applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-34745-2.Keywords: Beam divegence reduction, Orbital angular momentum (OAM), Uniform circular array (UCA), Transmissive metasurface lensSubject terms: Engineering, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

As wireless communication systems continue to advance, there is a rapid growth in the demand for wireless data. Researchers are developing new multiplexing techniques for next-generation systems to meet this demand1. One emerging strategy is based on orbital angular momentum (OAM) radio waves, which hold significant promise for communication systems beyond the current 5G technology2. The OAM-based multiplexing technique enables an increase in channel capacity by transmitting multiple orthogonal modes over a single channel at the same frequency3. Furthermore, OAM beams exhibit a unique cone-shaped radiation pattern, making them particularly useful for vehicle navigation systems4. Over the past several years, a number of experimental studies have demonstrated the feasibility of OAM-based wireless communication. One early experiment showed the generation of two OAM modes at 2.4 GHz5. In another work, a patch antenna array was used to create a short-range OAM link at 60 GHz with a data rate of 8 Gbps6. Yan et al. further demonstrated OAM multiplexing at 28 GHz, achieving a throughput of 32 Gbps over a 2.5 m link7. More recently, a 16-element array operating at 28 GHz achieved 100 Gbps over a distance of 10 m8. The concept of generating an OAM beam involves the incorporation of an azimuthal phase term ejlφ into electromagnetic waves, where φ denotes the azimuthal angle, and l (= 0, ± 1, ± 2, …) signifies the OAM mode number. Several methods exist in the microwave frequency range to introduce this phase term into OAM vortex beams. Spiral phase plates with varying thicknesses have been used to generate OAM beams in the microwave domain, although they were limited to fixed OAM modes9. Another approach involved utilizing a single microstrip patch antenna and an eigenmode-based technique for OAM beam generation10. However, this method was complex and presented practical difficulties.

Metasurfaces and UCAs are two widely used techniques for generating OAM beams in the microwave regime. Metasurfaces generate OAM beams by controlling the phase of the incident electromagnetic wave. Two approaches are commonly used to achieve the desired phase fronts with metasurfaces. In the first approach, the phase distribution is realized by rotating the orientation of the unit cells, enabling the formation of the required phase profile when illuminated by a circularly polarized wave11. The second approach adjusts the structural parameters of each unit cell to produce the desired phase front under linearly polarized excitation12. Several metasurface implementations have been reported in the literature. For example, a reflectarray using tri-dipole elements with a physical size of 500 mm × 500 mm was designed to generate OAM beams at 5.8 GHz13. A dual-layer reflective metasurface employing the Pancharatnam–Berry phase principle was proposed to achieve wideband OAM generation across 6.95–18 GHz14. Similarly, Rao et al. developed a wideband reflectarray using spider-net-shaped elements to produce OAM beams at 5.75 GHz with a size of 350 mm × 350 mm15. OAM generation using UCAs can also be accomplished through two primary methods. One commonly used approach employs linearly polarized antenna elements with progressive phase excitation around the array16. Although effective, this method often requires longer or more complex feed networks to realize the required phase distribution.

The second method for generating OAM beams using a UCA employs circularly polarized patch elements instead of linearly polarized ones17. In this approach, the patches are physically rotated according to the required phase progression to realize the desired OAM mode. For example, Qiang et al. developed a 2 × 2 reconfigurable UCA using circularly polarized elements with PIN diodes to switch between the + 1 and − 1 OAM modes at 2.5 GHz18. Hui et al. conducted a theoretical study on the divergence characteristics of OAM beams using a 16-element UCA19. Yuan-Long et al. further proposed a UCA integrated with a dielectric surface to reduce beam divergence for the l = ± 1 modes at 12.6 GHz20. The electromagnetic wave phase manipulation through metasurfaces produces OAM beams, whereas UCAs achieve this through their multiple radiating elements which create the needed phase sequence for each OAM mode. The design of metasurface-based systems produces high gain and narrow beam divergence but faces technical barriers during manufacturing and requires strict feed positioning and limited ability to change configuration21,22. The design of UCAs provides easy reconfiguration but their OAM beam output shows wider divergence than metasurface designs23,24. The development of OAM communication systems requires an antenna design that combines compactness with reconfigurability and low beam divergence to enable extended transmission paths with enhanced signal power.

In this paper, a proposal is made to integrate a transmissive metasurface with a UCA in order to generate multiple OAM modes with reduced divergence. Combining the advantages of both metasurface and UCA and avoiding their drawbacks offers a promising solution for producing multiple OAM modes with low divergence. The proposed design incorporates a two-layer transmissive metasurface that functions as a lens to decrease the divergence of the OAM beam. The unit elements of a transmissive metasurface have the ability to provide a phase range of 360°. The UCA with L-slot circularly polarized elements in an in-line series feed network allows for the efficient generation of multiple OAM modes while providing individual feeding for each mode. In the UCA, exciting port 1 results in the array elements exhibiting left-hand circular polarization and producing the l = + 1 OAM mode, while exciting port 2 exhibits right-hand circular polarization and produces the l = − 1 OAM mode. The 0 OAM mode beam is produced using the center two-element antenna array. A prototype UCA with a transmissive metasurface is fabricated and experimentally tested for different OAM modes (− 1, 0, and + 1). The simulation and measured results show excellent consistency, demonstrating the successful generation of the different OAM modes with reduced divergence.

In order to generate the OAM beam with mode l using a UCA with N elements, the phase difference between the elements must be Δφ = ±(2πl / N).The proposed UCA utilizes the geometric rotation of CP elements according to the required phase value, allowing a simple series feed network to be used for feeding. Depending on the feed direction, the square patch with an L-slot produces either LHCP or RHCP. When LHCP is generated, the resulting OAM mode is l = + 1, while RHCP produces the OAM mode l = − 1. The center two-element antenna array generates the l = 0 OAM mode.

Metasurface lens design

The proposed transmissive metasurface lens consists of 10 × 10 unit elements. Each unit element is designed with a spider-net-shaped structure, allowing it to possess multiple resonance properties, as shown in Fig. 5. By varying the geometrical parameter (V) of the unit element, the metasurface lens achieves the necessary 360° phase distribution. The geometrical parameters of the unit element are L = 25 mm and V = 10 mm. Figure 6 depicts the transmissive phase response of the unit element with respect to the parameter V. It is noticeable that by varying the parameter V, the phase response of the unit element can be extended up to 360° with a reasonable transmission loss.

Unit cell: (a) perspective view, (b) front view, and (c) side view.

Transmission phase and transmission loss as a function of V.

Moreover, the geometry of each unit cell was selected to minimize polarization conversion; simulations confirm that the lens maintains the circular polarization of the incident wave, thus ensuring that the focusing effect is primarily driven by phase distribution rather than polarization dependence. While this metasurface lens imposes the same radial (axicon-like) phase shift for any incident wave, each OAM mode generated by the UCA has its own azimuthal phase factor (ejlφ). Consequently, the overall wavefront after passing through the lens differs for each mode l, even though the physical lens design remains the same.

The desired convergent effect of OAM beams is achieved through the metasurface lens, which induces a non-uniform phase shift on the OAM wave generated by the UCA antenna, locally transforming its propagation direction. Results and discussion

The proposed multi-mode UCA antenna with a transmissive metasurface lens is fabricated using printed circuit board technology, as depicted in Fig. 8. Measurements were performed using a multi-probe spherical near-field scanner (MVG StarLab operating from 0.65 to 50 GHz) within an anechoic chamber. It produced amplitude and phase information for many locations throughout the near-field region. Using the near-field-to-far-field conversion software embedded in this system, the measured amplitude and phase information were used to create accurate radiation patterns in the far-field region. When port 1 is excited, the L-slot patch exhibits LHCP and generates the l = + 1 OAM mode. When port 2 is activated, the L-slot patch exhibits RHCP and generates the OAM mode with l = − 1. Activating port 3 results in the antenna generating the l = 0 OAM mode. As shown in Fig. 9, both the UCA with the metasurface and the UCA without the metasurface exhibit almost resonant behavior at 6 GHz for all the OAM modes.

When the metasurface is added, a moderate reduction in mode purity is observed, which is due to coupling interactions between the UCA and the metasurface elements. Conclusion

In conclusion, this paper presented a transmissive metasurface design integrated with a multimode UCA operating at 6 GHz to decrease the divergence of OAM beams. The UCA, with L-slot circularly polarized elements and an in-line series feed network, allowed for the efficient generation of multiple OAM modes (l = + 1, − 1, 0) while providing individual feeding for each mode. The transmissive metasurface functioned as a lens and contributed to decreasing the divergence of the generated OAM beams. While UCAs are commonly used for multimode OAM generation, metasurfaces have been applied for beam shaping. The present design demonstrates a practical integration of both approaches within a compact structure. The proposed UCA-based metasurface combination provides a simple and effective way to obtain multiple OAM modes with improved divergence characteristics using a single metasurface lens. Both simulations and experiments demonstrated that the combined structure (UCA + Metasurface) reduced the divergence angle from 18° to 10°, proving more effective in focusing and directing the OAM beams. These results indicate that integrating a metasurface with a UCA can significantly enhance the performance of OAM-based antenna systems. Further research will focus on developing reconfigurable and wideband metasurface configurations to enhance the performance of next-generation OAM-based communication systems.

A secure and scalable blockchain-assisted authentication framework for decentralized IoT data management
Authors: G Iswarya, C Vennila
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOver the past few years, IoT devices have witnessed an unprecedented growth rate, disrupting most industries, such as healthcare, home automation, transport, and manufacturing. Despite this, the growth has also opened the IoT networks to high vulnerability to security risks, including data breaches, unauthorized access, and distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, because of their centralized structure and insufficient computing resources. The main issue is that there is no strong, scalable, and decentralized security structure that could guarantee data integrity, authenticate, and establish trust between the heterogeneous IoT-based devices. This paper will overcome these shortcomings by outlining a blockchain-based secure IoT system named BASIS (Blockchain-Assisted Secure IoT System) in this paper. The model incorporates a lightweight Proof of Authentication (PoA) consensus mechanism, cryptographic data protection, and smart contract-based access control to facilitate decentralized authentication and tamper-resistant data management. In the suggested architecture, PoA is used to validate transactions and cryptographic identities are used to register IoT nodes to minimize computational and energy overheads. Smart contracts are used to implement access control policies and are not based on centralized authorities. A pre-processing attention-based learning modules are also introduced in the system to cater to anomaly finding and adaptive trust-assessment. The experimental outcome shows that BASIS improves the network resilience, minimizes the data validation latency and has a better throughput than the conventional IoT security schemes. In addition, the strategy reduces energy consumption and computational overhead, using optimized consensus protocols, and can be used in resource-constrained IoT settings. To sum up, the offered blockchain framework is a good way to enhance the security of IoT networks with the sources of decentralized trust, data immutability, and scalability, which leads to the future of secure IoT ecosystems. Introduction

Background and motivation

IoT is now one of the most revolutionary technologies of the contemporary world that helps to interconnect billions of intelligent devices that communicate, sense, and process data independently1. IoT applications have also been used in healthcare monitoring processes as well as smart transportation and industrial automation, to improve efficiency in operations and real-time decision-making2. Nevertheless, due to the growing interconnectedness of heterogeneous devices, security issues become complicated1. The conventional centralized network designs use cloud servers as authentication, storage, and control points that pose possible single points of failure and susceptibility to cyberattacks3. In addition, IoT devices usually lack and have very limited computational capabilities, and thus, it is hard to deploy strong encryption and authentication methods4. It has increased the number of cases of data violations, spoofing, and denial-of-service (DoS) attacks. Blockchain technology has turned out to be a promising solution as data integrity, privacy, and trustworthiness have been very critical5. Its cryptographic consensus and tamper-proof ledger offer the best platform through which the IoT devices can safely exchange data6. As such, Blockchain, when combined with IoT, may be used to guarantee distributed trust, transparency, and resilient security, which will encourage the creation of a sophisticated and scalable system such as the BASIS7.

The BASIS framework includes Artificial Intelligence to allow intelligent detection of threats, detection of anomalies, and predictive security monitoring in dynamic IoT conditions. Contrary to the conventional rules-based models, AI models consider the device behavior and transaction patterns, and trust measurements to identify abnormal activities automatically and preempt possible cyber threats. This adaptive learning feature increases the accuracy of detection, decreases the false positives, and increases the resiliency and reliability of the blockchain-based IoT security system overall.

To authenticate the resilience to attack of the proposed BASIS framework, further security testing was conducted by modeling the typical IoT cyber-attacks, such as Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS), Sybil attacks, and replay attacks. A decentralized authentication system, along with identity-based verification of nodes, helps stop unauthorized node involvement by a huge extent, which alleviates Sybil and impersonation attacks. Transaction freshness and tamper-detection by the cryptographic hash verification of the entry allow repudiation of all transactions, and thus, replay attacks are not possible since the entry cannot be repudiated. In addition, PoA consensus only validates blocks by trusted nodes and minimises the threat of evil transaction insertion and network flooding. The results of the simulation show that BASIS can guarantee stable throughput, low latency, and high data integrity in an adversarial environment, which clearly shows that it is highly resistant to both internal and external security threats.

Modern healthcare infrastructures are changing due to the fast development of intelligent computing technologies such as artificial intelligence, blockchain, Internet of Things (IoT), and next-generation communication networks. New medical applications that include remote patient monitoring, robotic surgery, smart wearable devices and nano-medical sensing will have a demand of secure, reliable, and ultra-low latency communication frameworks. Nevertheless, conventional centralized healthcare security systems have major shortcomings including single points of vulnerability, inability to scale and privacy of data and distrust among distributed healthcare organizations. Such constraints hinder efficient implementation of real-time and mission critical healthcare services. To address these issues, recent studies have been oriented to the convergence of decentralized technologies like blockchain and smart contracts with 6G communication networks. Blockchain offers unalterable storage of data, decentralization of authentication, and transparent control of trust, and smart contracts allow automatic verification as well as access control without the intervention of third-party authorities. In addition, 6G technologies enable ultra-reliable and low-latency transmission of data, which is necessary in such applications as robotic surgery and Internet of Nano Medical Things (IoNMT). All these technological innovations allow the creation of secure, scalable, and intelligent healthcare networks that can provide next-generation healthcare and can help make the medical services more secure and improve patient safety and reliability of the services.

Evolution of IoT and security challenges

IoT has evolved to more advanced multi-layered smart systems and no longer maintains a simple machine-to-machine (M2M) communication. With this improvement, security has been a thorn in the flesh because of the enormous size of connected devices and limited resources. The centralized systems do not work well in handling trust, privacy, and authentication over distributed environments8. Attackers use weak endpoints to result in identity spoofing, unauthorized access, and manipulating of data. As a result, there is a need to have a strong, scaled and independent security mechanism that will guarantee trust and integrity within internet of Things networks that will be functioning in a dynamic and heterogeneous environment.

Need for a decentralized security framework

Traditional IoT designs rely on central servers, and these components generate latency, bottlenecks, and the risk of single points of failure. With the growth of the IoT ecosystem, centralized control becomes ineffective and risky. Blockchain technology also provides an alternative to distributing trust to nodes through consensus mechanisms and indefinite ledgers, which are decentralized. This makes sure that no party can change data without consensus, which increases the integrity and reliability of data. A decentralized security infrastructure allows autonomous authentication, secure communication, and verifiable transactions between the devices of the IoT infrastructure- establishing the basis of a transparent, resilient, and tamper-proof environment required in the context of modern smart systems.

Research gap

The current blockchain-IoT models claim throughput of 20–85 TPS, validation latency of more than 220 ms and most have a scalability of less than 300 nodes, especially with PoW or PBFT consensus. These are also more expensive in terms of computational and energy and cannot be used in resource-constrained IoT settings. Also, dynamic trust-based validator selection is not effectively combined with lightweight consensus schemes, and the balance between performance and security has not been properly taken care of.

Paper organization

IoT environments with centralized architecture are associated with severe scaling issues, single points of failure, and trust. The incorporation of IoT creates reliability and trust in the system and enables real-time authentication, security transactions, and mitigation against data manipulation. Furthermore, a combination of Blockchain with artificial intelligence and federated learning will additionally enhance security, and IoT ecosystems will become self-sufficient, self-scaling, and self-sustaining to emerging cyber threats.

Comparative Analysis of Blockchain–IoT Methods.

Despite the significant advances in blockchain-IoT integration, there are still major research gaps. Current frameworks have issues of scalability, high costs of computation, and a lack of compatibility with resource-constrained IoT devices. The energy needed to run consensus mechanisms may be excessive, which lowers real-time responsiveness. Besides, the lack of interoperability between heterogeneous IoT platforms and blockchain networks is underdeveloped. Zero-knowledge proofs and lightweight encryption are privacy-preserving techniques that have not been adequately explored in industrial and smart city settings. Hybrid blockchain models that integrate edge computing and AI to support adaptive, efficient, and secure IoT data management have also received very little research.

Despite the fact that most blockchain-IoT frameworks that are already in place can improve security and data integrity, practically, most of them have high computational loads, augmented time delays, scalability issues, and energy usage. The models that include AI also present a further complexity of processing which might not be appropriate on resource-limited IoT devices. In addition, some studies do not have a serious performance evaluation in realistic workloads and do not cover interoperability in heterogeneous networks properly. Such constraints explain why a lightweight, scalable and energy efficient framework like the proposed BASIS model is required. Despite the variety of blockchain-IoT security frameworks discussed in the literature review, most of them are described in a chronological and descriptive tone, which makes them appear like a list of strategies instead of a synthesized study. Lack of grouping of studies around shared design principles, consensus strategies, scalability models, or performance trade-offs in the discussion, and quantitative outcomes comparison of them is also not critically done. A more robust synthesis must point out shared architectural motifs, point to recurrent constraints (e.g., energy overhead, latency under scale, interoperability gaps), and place the proposed BASIS framework squarely in this comparative space to show its unique contribution.

Methodology and proposed model

IoT node registration and authentication protocol

The proposed system will start by registering the IoT nodes and authenticating them in the BASIS framework. Every device in the IoT creates its own digital identity based on the hardware signature and cryptographic key pair. In the registration process, the credentials of the device are checked by a trusted authority node and then stored in the Blockchain in the form of a transaction. Later authentication is performed through asymmetric cryptography, whereby identities are checked without necessarily having to be centrally managed8. Such decentralized authentication will prevent impersonation, unauthorized addition of devices, and replay attacks; only legitimate IoT nodes will be able to communicate with the network and, in the process, create a secure and trustworthy base on which to exchange data.

The secure data generation and transmission process in an IoT node is shown in Fig. 1. The IoT sensor will first record the environmental data in the form of plaintext, which may be temperature, pressure, or humidity. This rudimentary data is forwarded to a preprocessing module, which filters the noise, equalizes readings, and organizes the data so that it is consistent. The plaintext is then refined, and an encryption engine is applied to it, whereby symmetric encryption is performed with keys based on the unique identity of the device. Metadata (timestamps and cryptographic hashes) is added to encrypted data, making it traceable and integrity assured. The last step is the blockchain node interface, which condenses the encrypted information into a transaction and connects it to a block in the distributed ledger. This flow guarantees that all data created as a result of IoT is securely encrypted, and nothing can be changed to ensure that sensitive data is not intercepted or tampered with by an unauthorized person in the transmission of data across the network.

Algorithm 1: Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA)

Algorithm 1 calculates the attention of many heads to capture various feature dependencies of the data in the IoT network. The two heads convert the input into query, key, and value matrices, calculate scaled dot-product attention, and generate weighted outputs. These are added together and projected through a line to create the final representation. Residual connections, dropout, and layer normalization stabilize training and overfitting. MHSA allows the parallel attention computation and improves scalability, feature diversity, and contextual understanding amongst the transactions in the IoT.

Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) is included in the BASIS framework due to the necessity to describe multifaceted transactional dependencies and behavioral patterns between distributed IoT devices. Security events in a large-scale IoT setting, including a coordinated attack, or anomalous bursts of traffic or manipulation of trust scores, can have both time dependencies and cross-node dependencies that traditional rule-based or single- feature models do not capture. MHSA provides the system with dynamic and contextual anomaly detection and dynamic trust evaluation because it assigns adaptive weights to various features of transactions. The attention mechanism improves predictive performance of threat detection and reliability of validators selection, thereby increasing the overall security intelligence of the blockchain enabled IoT network, as it captures local and global dependencies upon the sequence of transactions.

Consensus mechanism: PoA

PoA consensus mechanism is tailored to support IoT environment that is resource-constrained. In comparison with energy-consuming protocols like PoW, PoA adapts a lightweight validation procedure in which only nodes that are verified can access the block confirmation. The validators verify the integrity of a transaction by signing it with their registered keys of identity. The block is said to be legitimate when the majority of verifiable nodes attain a consensus. This is because it reduces the amount of computation and energy cost and guarantees a fast block verification. The PoA improves trust, allows lowering the latency, and offers a scalable consensus model to support large-scale decentralized IoT infrastructures.

Fig. 2 is an illustration of verification, validation, and storage of encrypted IoT data in the blockchain network. After encoding a transaction, it is first verified in the hash validation unit, where it encodes the integrity of the data with the help of the hash-256 hash and authenticates its time so as to verify whether it is not old. Then, the consensus mechanism, which is the PoA protocol, is used to authenticate the participating nodes in an efficient way, but not consuming large amounts of computational resources. The nodes work together to ensure that only valid data is added by voting to settle the legality of the particular transaction. After reaching a consensus, the ledger update module appends the verified block to the Blockchain keeping transparency and immortality in all nodes. Such a decentralized validation process removes single points of failure. It deters malicious modifications, with every transaction being verifiable and safely distributed throughout the IoT blockchain environment, which increases trust, traceability, and network resilience against cyber-attacks.

Formal security analysis model

The proposed architecture is analyzed (formally) in the presence of a DolevYao adversary, who is assumed to be capable of fully controlling the communication channel, including intercepting, replaying, modifying and injecting messages. The analysis confirms that three main properties are verified, which are confidentiality, integrity, and authentication. The security of confidentiality is guaranteed by the AES-256 encryption and identity-based key exchange, which does not expose plaintext to adversaries with limited calculation capabilities. SHA-256 hash chaining and digital signatures ensure integrity, where change will be detected by verification. The techniques of authentication and non-repudiation are verified by asymmetric signature verification as part of the PoA consensus mechanism. Also, the validator selection robustness is determined by probabilistic trust propagation modeling, which makes sure that the malicious or colluding nodes cannot take over the consensus, without having a reputation that is larger than the specified threshold.

Algorithm 2: Transformer Encoder Layer Forward Pass

Algorithm 2 corresponds to a complete encoder block in the suggested BASIS framework. It uses Multi-Head Self-Attention (Algorithm 1) along with an FFN to deepen features and refine them. There are residual connections and normalization applied by the encoder following attention and FFN stages, and these make the encoder stable and flow smooth in the gradient. Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and Transformer Encoder layers added to the BASIS architecture will aim to integrate multifaceted dependencies and dynamic behavior patterns among the distributed IoT nodes. Conventional models do not usually turn out to be effective in examining large-scale, heterogeneous IoT transaction data, and attention-based mechanisms allow adaptive weighting of features and learning in context. This enhances the detection of anomalies, trust assessment, and prediction of threats, and increases scalability and processing in parallel, which will boost the overall security and reliability of the suggested blockchain-aided IoT system.

Smart contract-based access control

Smart contracts are an important consideration of autonomous access control execution in BASIS. Every contract specifies access permissions and user authentication parameters, and permissions of the IoT nodes and users. Upon a device requesting access to data, the smart contract verifies its identity and level of authorization automatically without the involvement of a human factor. Unauthorized access is immediately refused, and data misuse and privilege escalation are prevented. Also, every access decision is stored on the blockchain register to enable visibility and auditability. This user-less, auditable control of access is a fine-grained approach to control, responsibility, and compliance of distributed IoT networks, which is a substitute for the historical centralized approach to authorization with a secure, programmable, and self-executing one.

Data encryption and hash linking process

BASIS provides encryption of all data produced by IoT before sending it to maintain confidentiality and integrity. Symmetric encryption (e.g., AES) is used to process them quickly, whereas key distribution is ensured by the use of public-key cryptography. After being encrypted, each packet of data is provided with a cryptographic hash (SHA-256), which is a unique digital fingerprint. This hash connects with the blockchain transaction, making it impossible to tamper with it- any change in the data will cause a mismatch between the hash. The encryption and hash-linking procedure ensures end-to-end data security and prevents eavesdropping, data alteration, or unauthorized encryption disclosure without compromising on the computational efficiency, which is lightweight enough to be performed by IoT devices.

Block formation and validation process

Verified transactions are encrypted and hashed, and then combined to create a new block. The blocks consist of a block header, a list of transactions, a timestamp, and the previous block hash, and are cryptographically chained to ensure immutability. PoA network validators check the authenticity of transactions and make sure that all network participants are registered and authorized nodes. When an agreement has reached, the block will be added to the blockchain ledger, which is shared among the participating nodes. This decentralized validation has eradicated single points of failure and ensured transparency, traceability, and non-repudiation. The validation mechanism and block formation algorithm, therefore enhances the security, reliability, and resiliency of the IoT network to malicious activities.

The retrieval and decryption of data in the Blockchain is a secure process, as explained in Fig. 3 by authorized parties. As an IoT gateway or a user requests information, the query is initially communicated with the blockchain query engine, where a smart contract validates access permissions and checks the credentials of a user. After the verification has been completed, encrypted blocks of data are given and transmitted to the decryption unit. The ciphertext is decrypted with the help of pre-shared symmetric keys or device-specific credentials to recreate the initial plaintext data. It is then put in the data analytics layer, where meaningful information regarding performance trends, anomalies, or predictive analysis is created based on this decrypted information. Lastly, the plaintext output is sent to the authorised user or application, where necessary decision-making is taken. This flow will guarantee that sensitive IoT data is accessed and interpreted by a legitimate user, upholding data confidentiality, access control, and data integrity in the entire cycle of information life.

To achieve a fair and transparent performance evaluation, the comparison baselines of the current study: IoTChain, BlockIoT, and SecureSense are chosen as the representative examples of blockchain-based IoT security frameworks that have been actively discussed in previous research. IoTChain is a decentralized platform that aims at data authentication and secure transactions management based on distributed ledger technology. BlockIoT focuses on verifying the identity of devices with the help of Blockchain and sharing data between IoT nodes in a secure way. SecureSense is built to increase the level of integrity and privacy of IoT data through cryptographic authentication and secure communication protocols. These base models were chosen due to the fact that they support closely related security issues, such as authentication, data protection, and decentralized trust management, which allows making a significant comparison of the proposed BASIS framework in terms of throughput, latency, energy consumption, and security resilience.

In comparison to PoW (lots of computation and energy required), and PoS (stake centralization and slower validator coordination), the offered PoA mechanism is light and is reputation-based, thus applicable in the context of resource-constrained IoTs. As compared to PBFT, PoA also reduces the complexity of consensus because validation is only done to authenticated and highly-reputed nodes, unlike PBFT, which offers low-latency at the cost of high communication overhead as the number of nodes increases. This leads to reduced energy usage, quick validation and better scalability among the decentralized IoT networks at the expense of security and fault tolerance.

The threats are categorized into confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy and trust in this paper. BASIS protects any threat to confidentiality and privacy by end-to-end encryption, smart-contract-based access control using pseudonymous identities, and smart-contract-based pseudonymous identities, as well as integrity attacks including tampering and replay threats using a digital signature, timestamps and block hashes. Lightweight PoA minimizes availability threats by involvement of limited number of validators and trust threats due to compromised or colluding nodes is addressed with reputation-based validator selection and dynamic trust propagation.

Trust evaluation and formal verification

The integrity and trustworthiness of Blockchain were determined through the analysis of immutability and accuracy of consensus in case of attacks that were simulated. There was no detected data modification that was not authorized, which validated strong block validation. Smart contracts have been formally verified using symbolic execution tools to make sure they are logically correct and free of vulnerabilities, such as reentrancy errors or overflow errors. Every rule of a contract is executed in a deterministic and autonomous manner and helps to ensure reliability in access control and transaction authorization. All in all, the analysis of security and performance conducted jointly proves that BASIS is a lightweight, energy-efficient, and attack-resilient security framework. Its decentralized structure ensures transparency and data immutability and trust- the fundamental security issues of next-generation IoT ecosystems.

In general, comparative analysis of the various types of consensus indicates that traditional computation-intensive and cloud-depending blockchain systems have a low throughput, greater lag time and energy usage, limiting their applicability to large-scale and real-time IoT systems. Privatized and AI-assisted consensus models enhance the performance yet they add coordination and processing overhead. Conversely, the lightweight PoA consensus implemented in BASIS is the solution with the optimal compromise of throughput, latency and energy consumption because mining is not done, and only trusted nodes validate. This leads to an increased processing capacity on transactions, reduced confirmation time, and reduced power consumption. Therefore, BASIS would be suitable to scalable and resource-constrained IoT-enabled healthcare settings. BASIS protects against compromised and colluding validators by using reputation-aware PoA selection and dynamic trust propagation, which gradually removes malicious nodes to the validators list, with denial-of-service attacks countered by limited validator involvement and lightweight verification, eliminating network flooding and maintaining consensus.

The security model used in this paper is a formal model that assumes the adversary to have complete control of the communication channel and able to observe, intercept, delay, replay and inject messages. There are three categories of adversaries: a passive eavesdropper which can only observe encrypted traffic, an active attacker that can modify the message, impersonate and inject malicious transactions, and an insider adversary which is a compromised legitimate IoT device or validator node with valid cryptography keys. The opponent is computationally constrained and is supposedly incapable of cracking common cryptographic functions including AES-256, SHA-256 and digital signages. According to this model, the proposed BASIS framework will have a confidentiality guarantee, which is end-to-end encryption, identity-based registration and signature checking, insider and collusion attacks reduced by reputation-aware PoA and dynamic trust propagation.

In addition, the Tamarin prover will be used to carry out symbolic analysis of the proposed PoA consensus protocol and trust propagation mechanism, to formally verify their correctness, immunity to collusion and replay attacks, and consistency of the evolution of trust under the specified adversary model. The probabilistic verification of node reliability and consensus success will be done with the PRISM model checker which will allow quantitative analysis of validator failure rates, successful block confirmation probability, and robustness of the PoA process when network and attack conditions are stochastic.

Advantages and limitations

The suggested BASIS framework illustrates a number of meaningful merits in the context of the improvement of IoT network protection. Its decentralized design also removes single points of failure, which makes its data integrity, transparency, and trust with heterogeneous devices. The combination of blockchain and lightweight consensus and encryption protocols improves confidentiality yet high-efficiency in resource-constrained environments. BASIS is also scalable, flexible, and low-latent in the validation of transactions, thus suitable for real-time IoT applications. Despite these, there are still some shortcomings, such as storage capacity increase with the ongoing development of the Blockchain and the overhead of communication when verifying the consensus. Also, the deployment of smart contracts requires both calculations and skills, which can be a complication of large-scale deployments in low-power IoT ecosystems. Future research possibilities are to introduce edge intelligence to adaptive data processing, hybrid consensus mechanisms to enhance scalability, and interoperability models to cross-chain communication to enable a smooth and energy-efficient integration between various IoT platforms and future decentralized networks.

Conclusion and future work

In general, the presented BASIS framework has proven that lightweight PoA consensus in conjunction with smart contracts and attention-based security intelligence can largely enhance throughput, latency, and energy consumption in blockchain IoT-based healthcare applications. Despite these, the framework continues to have various shortcomings, especially the incessant expansion of blockchain storage, which could impact long-term scalability and the overhead of communications added during the coordination of validators in the case of high network density. Besides that, the decentralization can be less than that of fully open blockchains because the validation depends on trusted and reputation-based validators. Future directions will then be on incorporating edge assisted data pruning, cross-chain and hybrid consensus to reduce storage and coordination wastes without compromising security and scalability.

The next step in the future is to create a hybrid consensus mechanism that uses PoA together with lightweight Byzantine or stake-based validation to enhance the scalability and fault tolerance when applying in a dense IoT deployment. The introduction of edge-AI will take place within the gateway nodes to conduct real-time anomaly detection and adaptive trust evaluation with a smaller communication overhead. A cross chain interoperation layer will be developed to facilitate secure communication between dissimilar blockchain platforms.

Cardiac plasmacytoma: multi-parametric tissue characterization of a rare but serious manifestation of multiple myeloma
Authors: Aliki Tsagkridi, Sadaf Rizwan, Edward Truelove, Mohammed Y Khanji
Published: 2023

A 50-year-old male with a background of immunoglobulin G lambda multiple myeloma refractory to bortezomib-based induction therapy presented with a 1-week history of severe, constant right-sided chest pain, radiating to both arms and right shoulder and more intense overnight. Simultaneously, there was a 3-week history of new subcutaneous lumps, initially involving the scalp and subsequently the torso, legs, and face.

Computed tomography pulmonary angiogram excluded pulmonary embolism but identified mediastinal soft tissue mass extending into the right atrium. Echocardiography confirmed a large pericardial effusion with no obvious features of tamponade (Panel A). Cardiovascular magnetic resonance revealed extensive a tumour mass invading into the atria, inter-atrial septum, encasing the vena cava, and pulmonary artery (Panel B; see Supplementary data online, Movies S1 and S2). The mass was isointense compared with the myocardium and had elevated T1 and T2 (Panel C), with minimal enhancing during rest perfusion although there was heterogeneous appearance in the late gadolinium enhancement imaging (Panel D) suggestive of a plasmacytoma. Skin biopsy of the peripheral lumps demonstrated plasma cell neoplasm with plasmablastic features, confirming disseminated extramedullary plasmacytoma involving the heart, the thoracic cavity, and subcutaneous tissue. Despite radiotherapy and chemotherapy, the patient died 6 weeks later due to sepsis and progressive myeloma.

Cardiac plasmacytomas are rare entities in multiple myeloma and carry a poor prognosis. Over half of the patients die within 2 days to 15 months after diagnosis. Surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, alone or in combination, have been documented in case reports. Despite radiological response and symptom improvement, remission is not achieved.

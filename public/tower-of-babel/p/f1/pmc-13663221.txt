Oral health and dental care needs among rural older adults in remote Southern Chile
Authors: Randal von Marttens, Rodrigo Mariño, Claudia Acevedo, Pablo Acuña-Mardones, Alfredo von Marttens, Leonardo López, Pablo Navarro, Víctor Beltrán
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to assess the oral health status and unmet restorative and prosthetic needs of older adults in La Araucanía Region, Chile, with a particular focus on the influence of sociodemographic factors and ethnic ancestry. The goal is to provide data to inform health policies and programmes tailored to older adults.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted involving 157 dentate older adults aged 60 and older from five municipalities in La Araucanía Region, Chile. Participants were recruited using convenience and snowball sampling methods. Evaluations were carried out in remote settings via a mobile dental clinic, where extensive data were collected. Participants’ sociodemographic information, medical conditions, and oral health status were assessed, including the Decayed, Missing, and Filled Teeth (DMFT) index and periodontal status. Linear and logistic regression analyses were used to explore associations between variables.ResultsThe mean age of participants was 70.2 years, most being female (73.2%). The average DMFT score was 23.9 teeth, dental caries experience was higher in females (24.4 vs. 22.0; p < 0.01), and in those not having tertiary education compared to those who did (24.2 vs. 21.2; p < 0.01). Participants had 32.2% of their restorative needs unmet, with males (49.6% vs. 26.4%; p < 0.01) and those of Mapuche ancestry showing higher proportions of unmet needs (48.1% vs. 25.6%; p < 0.01). 54.1% of participants had unmet prosthetic needs. Participants living in larger municipalities had lower unmet prosthetic needs compared to those living in other municipalities (OR = 0.13; 95% CI:0.04–0.49). All participants showed some level of periodontal involvement, with living arrangements (lived alone (OR = 4.73; 95%CI:1.48–15.13)) and multimorbidity (OR = 1.50; 95%CI:1.09–2.05) being predictors of periodontitis. Only 15.1% required urgent dental treatment.ConclusionsThis study provides insights into the oral health challenges faced by older adults living in remote areas of Chile, highlighting significant disparities associated with sociodemographic factors. The findings underscore the need for integrated healthcare models and targeted interventions to address unmet needs and improve oral health outcomes in this population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08997-x.Keywords: Oral Health, Aged, Dental care, Rural health, Teledentistry, Geriatric dentistry, Remote consultation, Health services needs and demand

Background

Population aging is a global phenomenon with significant repercussions for health and social assistance systems. According to the World Health Organization, in the coming decades, the percentage of people aged 60 and older will nearly double, reaching 22% of the global population by 2050 [1]. This demographic shift entails, among other things, a fundamental transformation in societal structures, healthcare demands, and economic systems. Simultaneously, most of the world’s population (57.3%) now living in cities [2]. These overlapping transformations are reshaping the dynamics of modern societies and carry important environmental, social, economic, and health considerations across the globe [3]. Furthermore, an increasing proportion of older adults correlates with a higher prevalence of chronic conditions, disabilities, and long-term care needs [4].

In Chile, demographic projections estimate that by 2050, 32% of the population will be aged 60 or older, making the country the oldest on average in South America [5]. Furthermore, 87% of the Chilean population lives in urban areas, a demographic distribution that directly affects access to health services [6]. The La Araucanía Region of Chile is characterized by its rural setting, socioeconomic inequalities, and barriers to healthcare access [7]. In oral health terms, one striking indicator is the extremely high prevalence of non-functional dentition (89.3%) in this region [8].

Oral healthcare is often overlooked in broader discussions about aging and health, despite its critical role in overall well-being, particularly in later life [9]. Poor oral health can lead to a range of complications, including malnutrition, social isolation, and reduced quality of life [10]. Moreover, the connection between oral and systemic health, especially in conditions like diabetes and cardiovascular diseases, is deeper than previously thought, highlighting the need for integrated care models that address the holistic needs of older adults [11].

Given the importance of oral health in aging populations, it is essential to examine its impact in specific contexts, especially in underserved regions. Access barriers are even more pronounced among older adults living in rural and remote areas, who frequently face challenges such as limited mobility, transportation difficulties, and other health-related limitations. Several studies have been conducted to evaluate the impact of teledentistry on improving the oral health of older patients and reducing associated comorbidities [12]. These studies show that it is possible to improve access to oral healthcare, as well as the oral health and quality of life of populations who may face barriers to receiving traditional oral healthcare services.

During the early stages of the pandemic, many older adults avoided visiting health centers out of fear of contamination. Consequently, a project aimed at utilizing teledentistry to provide care to vulnerable patients was commenced. To support this initiative, a mobile dental unit was set up, equipped with a dental chair, scanner, and various other necessary tools. Additionally, technology based on the teledentistry platform, the Tele-platform of Geriatric and Dental Specialties (TEGO) was developed [8, 13, 14]. The mobile unit was allowed to provide oral healthcare directly to older adults, while asynchronous internet consultations through the platform, enabled collaboration across all dental specialties. This innovative approach not only addressed immediate dental care needs during a challenging time but also set a precedent for integrating technology into dental practice, ultimately improving access to care for underserved communities.

Building on that development, the primary objective of this research is to assess the oral health status of dentate older adults living in remote areas of the La Araucanía Region, specifically dental caries, periodontal disease, and oral mucosal pathology. The study also aims to analyse unmet restorative and prosthetic needs, as well as the oral health inequalities present within this population. Additionally, it explores how environmental factors (such as access to oral healthcare) and individual contextual factors, including socioeconomic status, influence the current oral health status.

Methods

Study design and setting

This study employed a cross-sectional design (Clinical trial number: not applicable). This study was conducted in strict compliance with the Helsinki Declaration (https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki). Ethical approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Universidad de La Frontera (UFRO) (ID: 169/23, approved on 18 March 2024). Informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study.

This study was conducted in five municipalities of La Araucanía Region, Chile, namely: Puerto Saavedra, Pitrufquén, Angol, Renaico, and Purén. These municipalities were selected for reasons of convenience but also to portray characteristics in remote geographic areas communities in south-central Chile, such as varying levels of rurality, the proportion of older adults, and differing degrees of access to healthcare services [15].

Remote geographical areas refer to locations situated far from urban centres, typically within a range of 25 to 150 km, where access to specialised healthcare services in public healthcare centres may be limited or unavailable. These areas are characterised by low population density (fewer than 60,000 inhabitants), an older adult population exceeding 30%, rurality greater than 60%, limited access to healthcare services, and significant geographic barriers to in-person specialist oral healthcare [16].

The study was carried out in three phases:

Phase I: Communication. Extensive communication efforts were conducted through phone and email with selected municipal governments and organisations representing older adults. The principal investigator liaised with local authorities and social organizations to explain the objectives of the project and obtain authorisation for the use of communal facilities and access to participant networks. During this stage, arrangements were also made to confirm the availability of physical space suitable for the installation of the mobile dental clinic [17].

Sampling and sample size

The structure of the study was based on a protocolized sequence of steps. The sampling strategy for recruiting participants in the municipalities was developed in consultation with the local community organizations, starting with the identification of potential participants through local older adult databases, such as those from National Service for Older Adults (In Spanish: Servicio Nacional del Adulto Mayor (SENAMA), local community health centres, and community organisations.

Sample size

For the sample size calculations, after consultation with the local organizations, it was decided that 40 participants (5 × 40 = 200) would be from each municipality. Community workers, who as part of their work monitor this population, estimated this number would capture the profile of the older adults’ local populations.

Post-hoc calculations indicated that the achieved sample size (n = 157) would be large enough to provide statistical power (β = 0.80) to perform the planned data analyses. For example, the minimal sample size requirements for differences in means (one sample case) to allow a power of 0.80, with an effect size of 0.25, at two-tails significance criteria of 0.05 is 128 cases. On the other hand, 147 participants are required to perform a logistic regression analysis yielding a power of 0.80, at a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Furthermore, post-hoc calculations of the necessary sample size for the linear regression based on the case of 8 independent variables accounting for 10% effect size associated with continuous variables (e.g., unmet needs, DMFT scores), a sample size of 157, would yield a power of 0.80, that is an 80% chance of detecting an effect of that size at the level of significance of 0.05. Sample size calculations were conducted using G-Power v.3.1.9.4 (http://www.psycho.uni-duesseldorf.de/abteilungen/aap/gpower3/). Therefore, for each stage of the study the overall statistical power was high.

Data were collected between April and September 2024.

The clinical examiner received training and calibration in the assessment protocols. Intra-examiner dental status reliability was checked using Cohen’s Kappa statistics. An online calibration exercise involving the review of 20 repeated cases was performed. The Kappa value for dental caries exceeded 0.90, indicating an “almost perfect” level of agreement, according to the criteria of Landis and Koch [23].

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics included sociodemographic and oral health data, including means, standard deviations, and frequency distributions.

Bivariate associations were explored using Chi-square tests for categorical variables. For continuous response variables (e.g., the DMFT score), analyses of variance (ANOVA) were conducted, followed by Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference tests for post-hoc comparisons. Pearson’s correlation coefficients were calculated to evaluate associations between continuous variables (e.g., DMFT score and age).

Logistic regression models were used to identify predictors of binary outcomes (e.g., presence of periodontitis), with sociodemographic and medical history characteristics entered as independent variables. Adjusted odds ratios (ORs) were used to estimate the magnitude of effect. To determine whether combinations of sociodemographic and medical history variables (e.g., age, sex, location, etc.) explained variations in oral health outcomes (e.g., DMFT, unmet restorative needs), linear regression models were fitted using a stepwise selection procedure. Variables with p < 0.20 were included in the model. Missing values were handled using case-wise deletion.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics (version 31.0), and statistical significance was set at p-value of < 0.05.

Results

A total of 196 older adults agreed to participate in the study. Of these, 39 were excluded due to incomplete data caused by connectivity issues. The remaining 157 participants were clinically examined and interviewed.

The mean age of participants was 70.2 years (SD = 6.14). The largest age group was 66–70 years, representing 30.5% (n =48) of the sample, while 26 participants (16.6%) were aged 75 or older. The majority of participants were female (73.2%). In terms of education level, 51.6% had completed at least part of primary education, and 10.2% had attained some level of tertiary education. Regarding marital status, the largest group of participants (56.7%) were married. Thirty-three participants (21%) reported living alone.

Most participants resided in small localities (63.1%), while 36 (22.9%) lived in medium-sized municipalities and 22 (14.0%) in large municipalities. Forty-eight participants (30.6%) self-reported Mapuche ancestry.

The mean number of medical conditions (comorbidity) reported was 3.1 (SD = 1.6; range: 1–8). Sixteen participants (13.1%) reported one condition, 37 (30.3%) reported two, and 31 (25.4%) reported three conditions. The remaining 31.1% reported four or more. The most frequently reported conditions were cardiovascular diseases (72.1%), diabetes (43.4%), depression (20.5%), thyroid disorders (23.3%), and autoimmune diseases (8.2%). Other underlying conditions were reported by 12.0% of participants. Seventeen participants (10.8%) reported being current smokers, another 16.8% reported alcohol consumption. The DMFT index was significantly associated with age, sex, and education level. Women had significantly higher DMFT scores than men (24.4 vs 22.2; p < 0.01), more filled teeth (4.9 vs 3.3; p < 0.05), and fewer decayed teeth (1.3 vs 2.8; p < 0.001). Moreover, participants with only primary education had significantly more missing teeth than those with at least some tertiary education (19.0 vs. 12.9; p < 0.01). Additionally, they had lower mean of filled teeth than those with tertiary education (3.5 vs. 6.5; p < 0.01).

By municipality size, participants residing in large municipalities had significantly more filled teeth (6.3 vs. 4.3; p < 0.05). As expected, age was associated with caries experience: it was negatively associated with the mean number of filled and missing teeth (p < 0.01) and the mean number of decayed teeth (p < 0.05). No significant differences in dental caries experience were observed by marital status or living arrangement.

Participants were examined for the presence of oral mucosal lesions. In most cases (92.8%), no lesions were detected. Among those with lesions (7.2%; n = 11), none were found to exhibit soft tissue conditions considered ‘life-threatening’ or potentially so. Subprosthetic stomatitis was among the most observed lesions. By dental urgency category, 19 participants showed no urgency (12.4%), however, most participants were classified as having low urgency (72.5%), followed by those with advanced urgency (13.8%), and high levels of urgency (1.3%).

Asynchronous consultations were carried out with specialists for early and preventive diagnosis. Referrals made during data collection included 11 (7.2%) participants referred for further specialist care. Ten were referred to oral pathology and one participant referred to periodontics.

Additionally, to examine whether any socioeconomic status (age, ethnic ancestry, living arrangement, and education level). or health-related variables (including multimorbidity, alcohol consumption, smoking status, and dry mouth). explained variation in DMFT scores, linear regression analyses were conducted. However, only sex and level of education remained statistically significant after adjusting for other independent variables in the model [F(2,143) = 5.65; p < 0.01], explaining just 6.0% of the variance in the DMFT index. The final model suggested that, after adjusting for other variables, participants with the lowest DMFT score were more likely to be males who had tertiary education ancestry (See Supplementary files).

Overall, dentate participants had 32.2% of their restorative needs unmet. Notably, 30.1% (n = 40) had all their restorative needs unmet, while 11.3% had all their restorative needs met. Sex was significantly associated with the proportion of unmet restorative needs (p < 0.001), with males presenting a higher proportion than females (49.6% vs. 26.4%). No other variable showed statistically significant associations.

To determine whether any sociodemographic or health condition variables explained the proportion of unmet restorative needs, a linear regression analysis was conducted. The analysis identified two independent variables—sex and ethnic ancestry—as significantly associated with unmet restorative needs [F(2, 98) = 10.42; p < 0.001], accounting for 16.1% of the variance (adjusted R² = 0.161). The final model suggested that, after adjusting for other variables, participants with the highest proportion of unmet restorative needs were more likely to be male, who self-identified as having Mapuche ancestry (See Supplementary files).

The clinical assessment indicated that a total of 67 participants (45.9%) had no prosthetic needs, either because their existing prostheses were in good condition (28.1%) or because they had no requirement for prosthetic use (17.8%). In contrast, 79 participants (54.1%) did have prosthetic needs: 13.7% required repairs or relining, 22.6% needed a new prosthesis, and 17.8% needed an appliance but did not have one at the time of the examination.

The size of the municipality was significantly associated with the proportion of unmet prosthetic needs (p < 0.001). Participants residing in large municipalities had lower levels of unmet prosthetic needs compared to those in smaller locations (94.9% vs. 5.1%), while no other variable reached statistical significance. This association remained significant in the multivariate model [χ2(1) = 12.42; p < 0.001]. Participants living in larger communities (i.e., Angol) had lower odds of unmet prosthetic needs compared to those living in other municipalities (OR = 0.13; 95% CI: 0.04 to 0.49). The model accounted for 13.9% of the variance in unmet prosthetic needs (See Supplementary files).

All dentate participants exhibited some degree of periodontal involvement. The majority (76%) presented gingivitis, while 24% recorded some degree of periodontitis. The number of medical conditions and living arrangements were significantly associated with periodontitis (p < 0.001 and p < 0.02, respectively). No other sociodemographic or location-related variables were associated with periodontal status.

A logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify predictors of periodontitis, including age, ethnic ancestry, living arrangement, multimorbidity, municipality size, smoking status, dry mouth, and education level. Participants who lived alone were more likely to present periodontitis (OR = 4.73; 95% CI: 1.48 to 15.13), and those with a higher number of health conditions also had increased likelihood of periodontitis. Specifically, each additional medical or health condition was associated with a higher likelihood of periodontitis (OR = 1.50; 95% CI: 1.09 to 2.05). The model was statistically significant [χ²(2) = 15.8; p < 0.001] and accounted for 22.8% of the variance in periodontitis (See Supplementary files).

Discussion

While oral health may not be explicitly mentioned in the UN SDGs, its relevance to their goals and targets is demonstrated by this study [25]. Oral health is a fundamental component of overall health and well-being, with far-reaching implications for individuals and society as a whole. Recognizing their responsibilities as health professionals, and as part of a global community makes OHPs part of achieving these ambitious goals. Achieving them would make the world fairer, more sustainable, biodiverse, healthy, and more participatory, and democratic, for future generations.

The findings from this study provide valuable insights into the oral health status of older adults living in remote geographic areas of La Araucanía Region, Chile, particularly concerning their sociodemographic characteristics, medical conditions, and oral healthcare needs. The sample represents a diverse cohort in terms of sex, education, marital status, ethnic ancestry, and living arrangements. The results reported significant associations between demographic factors and oral health indicators, especially in relation to dental caries experience, restorative needs, and periodontal health.

The high mean Decayed, Missing, and Filled Teeth (DMFT) index of 23.9 reflects a substantial burden of dental caries experience among participants. This result is consistent with previous studies that have highlighted the prevalence of dental issues in older adults living in regional areas of Chile, often exacerbated by factors such as reduced access to care and socioeconomic disadvantages [8, 26, 27]. Notably, the finding that women exhibited higher DMFT scores, especially due to a greater number of filled teeth, suggests possible sex-based disparities in oral healthcare management or access. This aligns with literature indicating that, although women tend to receive higher rates of dental treatment, they still face greater challenges in maintaining oral health as they age [28, 29]. Additionally, the negative association between levels of education highlights the complexity of oral health trajectories in aging populations, further emphasizing the effects of sociodemographic disparities and inequities in oral health [30]. In contrast, other sociodemographic factors had no effect on dental caries experience (e.g., marital status, living arrangements), suggesting that these factors may not play as critical a role in oral health outcomes in populations living in remote geographic areas. This finding underscores the need for further exploration into the mediating effects of sociodemographic factors on oral health, particularly in older adults living in rural areas.

The proportion of unmet restorative needs (32.2%) further highlights the significant gap in oral healthcare access and utilization. The contrast between male and female participants regarding unmet restorative needs raises important questions about gender-specific barriers to dental care. Males exhibited a higher proportion of unmet needs, suggesting potential sociocultural factors that may influence help-seeking behavior or access to dental services. This finding is particularly concerning given the established relationship between oral health and overall health outcomes in older adults. Interestingly, the multivariate analysis indicated that sociodemographic variables such as sex and ancestry accounted for a modest proportion of the variance in unmet restorative needs, indicating that, while these factors are relevant, they are not the sole determinants of oral healthcare access. Future research should aim to identify additional barriers to care, such as financial constraints, lack of transportation, or cultural perceptions of oral health, particularly among indigenous populations.

The assessment of prosthetic needs showed that over half of the participants required some form of prosthetic intervention, either in the form of repairs or new prostheses. This finding underscores the importance of regular dental check-ups and preventive care to mitigate the progression of oral health issues that necessitate prosthetic solutions. Furthermore, there was a significant multivariate association between prosthetic needs and municipality size, which further emphasizes the need to improve accessibility and availability of oral healthcare services, particularly in smaller remote geographic locations.

The prevalence of periodontal involvement among participants is concerning. All individuals exhibited some degree of periodontal involvement, either in the form of gingivitis (76%) or more advanced periodontal disease (24%). This finding aligns with existing literature that highlights the high prevalence of periodontal disease in older populations, often associated with systemic health conditions [31]. The significant association between the number of medical conditions and periodontal status reinforces the interconnectedness of oral and general health. Logistic regression analysis reported that participants living alone had higher odds of periodontitis, which has critical implications for social support systems and health interventions aimed at isolated older adults. The association between multimorbidity and periodontal disease underscores the need for oral health interventions to be integrated into broader healthcare strategies for older individuals, particularly those with chronic conditions. Understanding the bidirectional relationship between oral health and systemic diseases is essential for developing comprehensive care plans that address the complex needs of this population [32].

The prevalence of oral mucosal lesions (7.2%) among participants is reassuring, particularly given the absence of life-threatening conditions. Nevertheless, the identification of lesions in a minority of individuals underscores the need for regular oral examinations, as early detection can lead to better management and outcomes. The referral of individuals with lesions to specialists highlights the importance of a multidisciplinary approach to oral healthcare, ensuring that older adults receive comprehensive evaluations and appropriate interventions.

Although these findings emphasize the need for oral health interventions, further research is required to help reduce social inequalities and health disparities. Addressing inequalities is critical to improve health outcomes for older adults, particularly in rural areas. Public health programs should focus on oral health promotion and prevention to enhance the quality of life of older adults living in remote geographic areas and increase awareness of potential oral health risks. However, policies should focus not only on oral healthcare access but also on broader social policies that address poverty, education, and housing.

Moreover, the implications of these findings extend beyond oral health, as poor oral health may precipitate or exacerbate a range of medical conditions, including pneumonia, cerebrovascular and cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and nutritional deficiencies [33–35]. Poor oral health is also related to health behaviors, including dietary choices and physical activity levels [36]. Populations living in remote geographic areas encounter difficulties in navigating healthcare systems and tend to use oral healthcare services on an ad-hoc basis, primarily for relief of pain, which can lead to reliance on emergency services rather than on preventive care [37–39]. This reliance not only exacerbates health disparities but also places additional strain on healthcare resources. This situation presents a significant challenge for healthcare professionals and society at large, especially given the increasing life expectancy of the Chilean population [40].

The delivery of effective oral healthcare at every stage of life—from early childhood to older age continues to be hindered by geographical and economic barriers. Patients residing in remote geographic areas who are referred to urban oral healthcare providers must travel long distances, a process that is typically costly and time-consuming [36]. This is particularly the case in remote geographic areas like La Araucanía Region. These barriers highlight the need for innovative solutions that bridge the gap between the demand for care and the accessibility of services. In this context, teledentistry emerges as a promising alternative to conventional dental care delivery models. High-resolution and reliable digital and online communications can provide access to oral health promotion, education, and assessment for these communities. The use of digital technologies (e.g., teledentistry) provides opportunities to both supplement and complement traditional methods of timely oral diagnosis, care delivery and health promotion in these community settings [12, 41].

In making these observations, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. The study used a random sample of volunteers comprising dentate individuals who felt reasonably well and were capable of independently managing most of their daily activities. As a result, a self-selection bias may be present, as individuals who volunteered might differ from those who do not choose to participate. For instance, healthier older adults may be more likely to take part in studies than those facing notable health challenges or functional limitations, potentially skewing the results. Furthermore, the unique circumstances of this population, which includes a predominantly rural areas with a significant proportion of Indigenous members (i.e., Mapuche), makes it difficult to generalize the findings to other indigenous communities in the country. Additionally, while the categories for periodontal assessment in this study closely resemble those of the Community Periodontal Index (CPI) [19], its terminology and scoring criteria differ from those used in the present study. Future epidemiological status should ensure that periodontal data is collected using standard methods.

Therefore, although valuable insights have been gained, it must be noted that a definitive profile of the older adult population living in remote geographic areas of the La Araucanía Region cannot be established based solely on this sample. However, the findings represent an important step toward providing a foundation for more targeted programs and future research initiatives aimed at older adults.

The data generated in this study serve as important inputs for the design of health policies and programs aligned with national and regional priorities for older adults [42, 43]. This is particularly relevant considering the Chilean government’s current emphasis, through the Explicit Health Guarantees (GES) program, on improving access, timeliness, quality, and user satisfaction in dental services for older adults [42]. The Comprehensive Dental Care Program for Older Adults [43], which focuses on preventing oral health deterioration and rehabilitating tooth loss, reflects the recognition that oral health is a vital component of well-being in later life and supports the government’s call for baseline data and strengthened evaluation efforts in this field [44].

Conclusions

The present study underscores the considerable oral health challenges faced by dentate older adults living in communities in remote geographic areas, particularly with respect to dental caries, restorative needs, and periodontal health. The findings highlight the need for targeted interventions that address the specific needs of this population, considering sociodemographic factors and the interplay between oral and systemic health. Future research should explore the barriers to oral healthcare access and the effectiveness of integrated health care models in improving oral health outcomes for older adults. By addressing these critical issues, we can contribute to enhancing the quality of life for older adults and promoting healthier aging.

As societies confront the implications of an aging population, it is imperative to explore and implement innovative strategies that prioritize the health and well-being of older adults. The findings from this study may inform broader public health strategies aimed at addressing the challenges posed by demographic shifts, not only in Chile but also in other countries facing similar aging trends.

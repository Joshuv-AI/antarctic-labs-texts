Optimization of Material and Bandgap Engineering for Distinctive Layers in Perovskite Solar Cells
Authors: Halavath Devi Vara Prasad, Kousik Midya, Nilanjan Halder, Sandipta Roy, Pushp Sen Satyarthi, Lalat Indu Giri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Perovskite-rooted solar cell is a promising candidate to lead the future solar cell industry. Efficiency of perovskite solar cell strongly relies on the thickness and each material’s properties. The present research aims at a systematic study of both material and bandgap optimization for each layer to obtain higher efficiency of the device. The comparative analysis of perovskite material with different halide ions exhibits that CH3NH3PbX3 [where X: I] offers the highest efficiency due to an appropriate bandgap. CH3NH3PbI3 based device is proved as the most promising candidate. The current study also demonstrates device-level integration and co-optimization of MASnBr3 and Cd0.5Zn0.5S combination as HTL and ETL layers respectively, along with dual role of ZnO as transparent electrode and encapsulation layer. (300 nm)ZnO/(50 nm)Cd0.5Zn0.5S/IDL/(850 nm)CH3NH3PbI3/IDL/(350 nm)MASnBr3 architecture exhibits best performance, achieving power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.12%, a short-circuit current density (JSC) of 25.34 mA/cm2, an open-circuit voltage (VOC) of 1.196 V and the fill factor of 89.50%.Keywords: Perovskite, Clean energy, Solar Cell, SCAPS-1DSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Perovskite materials have drawn a lot of interest in the field of solar cell. Owing to their cubic lattice-nested octahedral layered structure, perovskite material offers remarkable optical, thermal, and electromagnetic capabilities. Attributes like tunable bandgap, large diffusion length, wider absorption spectra, and fast exciton separation have made this material a forerunner in solar cell technology1,2. The chemical formula of Perovskite compounds is ABX3, where X stands for anion and, A and B are the cations. Among the array of Perovskite materials, those with organic (A)-inorganic (B) halide materials are the most extensively investigated for Photovoltaic applications. Current worldwide ongoing research is predominantly aimed at increase in efficiency and stability of perovskite solar cells3–5. Both Halide and anion play important role to exhibit different band gap which helps to obtain high efficiency and enhanced open circuit voltage.

In a typical perovskite solar cell (PSC) device, perovskite absorber material is interceded between the electron (ETL) and hole transport layers (HTL). The incorporation of a thin interfacial layer at both sides of the absorber layer has also been reported to obtain better interface6,7, thereby reducing recombination at the heterostructured interface.

Among the wide variety of perovskite materials, methyl ammonium lead halide (CH3NH3PbX3), also known as methylammonium (MA: CH3NH3) lead halide (MAPbX3) is the most examined perovskite group as an absorber layer due to their higher absorption efficiency1,8,9. CH3NH3PbI3, a lead halide perovskite, stands out as an efficient light absorber due to its desirable optoelectronic properties, making it popular in various applications like photovoltaics and photodetectors10–12. Its effectiveness in photon to electron conversion with adaptability in micro- and nanowire structures, makes it a compelling choice for optoelectronic devices, especially in flexible applications12–14. Previously published literatures have shown that CH3NH3PbI3 exhibits a high absorption coefficient, good stability, excellent absorption enhancement when combined with other materials like silver nanowires and TiO2 nanowires, making it a promising candidate for enhancing light absorption and stability in perovskite materials11,13,15. Despite possessing multiple benefits and higher stability, toxic nature of CH3NH3PbI3 cannot be ignored. Sn or S based metal perovskite exhibit reduced toxicity compared to their Pb based counterparts. On the other hand, Pb-based perovskite has comparatively higher stability. To preserve higher stability with no/minimum toxic effect, encapsulation is a popular solution. Proper encapsulation of the cell prevents both degradation and lead leakage16,17. Among different encapsulating layers ZnO layer has been reported as an efficient candidate by many researchers18,19.

HTL is another layer with pivotal role in enhancing efficiency of the cell20,21. Crystallization, morphology and interface quality of the perovskite layer strongly depend on the HTL material22. Additionally, use of perovskite material as HTL comes up with higher hole mobility, tunable bandgap, cost effectiveness and simplified fabrication steps. However, limited literature is available on the use of perovskite material as an HTL layer. Gamal, N. et al. reported the efficiency of the device with Zn1-xMgxO as ETL and MASnBr3 as HTL layer23. The stability, chemical compatibility and reliability of the HTL layer have been playing a critical role in enriching the durability of PSCs.

Likewise, role of the ETL layer is also important in the extraction of electrons and suppression of the recombination probability by hole blocking. In the case of ETL, a ternary compound material Cd1-xZnxS has shown a significant improvement with the optimal Zinc (Zn) concentration of 0.624. The higher efficiency of the photovoltaic device with the use of CdZnS as an ETL layer has also been reported by other researchers25,26. The superior optical properties, high carrier mobility and favorable activation energy at the interfaces contribute an edge to CdZnS over commonly used materials like TiO2 and ZnO. It is evident that the ETL layer, HTL layer and absorbing layer (perovskite), have their own importance to produce a highly efficient solar cell.

From the above discussion it is evident that, understanding the mechanism of the device is very important to find the most suitable combination of different layers. The potential of MASnBr3 and Cd0.5Zn0.5S as HTL and ETL layers, respectively, have been studied separately by several researchers. However, the individual behavior of materials does not necessarily remain the same after integration into a complete device architecture. Therefore, this work aims at integrating MASnBr3 and Cd0.5Zn0.5S as HTL and ETL layers, respectively within a single perovskite solar cell architecture. Incorporation of IDL in both side of the absorber layer has also been explored. Additionally, as discussed earlier, ZnO has been reported as promising ETL layer which also provides encapsulation to the Pb based absorber layer. But CdZnS has been reported as better candidate as ETL layer. Therefore, to ensure both high efficiency and proper encapsulation, ZnO has been used as transparent electrode in this study.

In this study all the devices have been simulated using SCAPS-1D. SCAPS-1D is a popular simulation tool based on Poisson and continuity equations for holes and electrons. SCAPS-1D can efficiently simulate up to seven layers and has widely been used to analyse diverse structures of photovoltaic cells like homojunction, heterojunction, hybrid homo-heterojunction27 multi-junction28, tandem29 perovskite-based solar cells. This allows parameters to be adjusted in a graded manner or in different profiles as needed. Different illumination spectra like AM 0, AM 1.5G, and AM 1.5D are available in it. In this work, SCAPS-1D with multilayer input (version 3.3.10)30 has been used to optimize materials for multiple layers and various parameters like bandgap, thickness. AM 1.5G spectrum has been used for solar cell efficiency calculation.

Effect of energy bandgap

Metal halide perovskites are captivating gestures in the photovoltaic industry as highly efficient solar cells. Bandgap has vital importance in increasing the competence of optoelectronics-related devices64. The energy band diagram of the perovskite cell, under study, is shown in Fig. 3a.

Schematic representation of the effect of energy bandgap on the perovskite solar cell device (a) Energy band diagram of perovskite solar cell (b) Simulated efficiency with change in bandgap energy level.

In organic material exciton radius is less because of stronger columbic interaction between electron and hole. Therefore, significant band offset is required to separate electron and hole pairs. Lower bandgap of Iodine based perovskite provides higher band offset compared to Cl and Br based perovskite. Additionally, lower bandgap of Iodine based perovskite can absorb broader range of spectrum. Moreover, according to the Shockley Queisser theory, maximum efficiency can be obtained with a material with a bandgap of 1.45 eV65. Among the three materials, CH3NH3PbI3 (bandgap 1.55 eV) has the closest bandgap of 1.45 eV. Material with a band gap higher than 2.25 eV results in lower efficiency66. It supports our results, which indicate the superiority of CH3NH3PbI3 with a bandgap of 1.55 eV over CH3NH3PbBr3 (2.33 eV) and CH3NH3PbCl3 (3.1 eV). The efficiency of perovskite materials as a function of the bandgap is presented in Fig. 3b. In this regard, the perovskite solar cell offers space for future research as the band gap can be engineered67.

The ETL layer with less electron affinity (Cd0.5Zn0.5S) compared to absorber layer, i.e., with + CBO (0.09 eV), provides a potential barrier at the ETL–perovskite interface, as presented in Fig. 6b. The potential barrier restricts the motion of electrons in the ETL layer from the perovskite absorber layer. It is noteworthy that despite having + CBO, Cd0.5Zn0.5S shows better efficiency as ETL layer than TiO2 (with CBO = -0.1 V) (Fig. 6a) as ETL layer. The band diagram shown in Fig. 6 explains the phenomena. A spike is obtained in the band diagram in the case of Cd0.5Zn0.5S (Fig. 6a). On the other hand, due to the superior electron affinity of TiO2 layer, cliff-type band alignment is obtained at TiO2- CH3NH3PbI3 interface (Fig. 6a). Raoui et al. mathematically proved that for cliff-type, smooth conduction of electrons from the absorber layer to the ETL layer results in a higher electron population in the ETL–perovskite layer interface, which leads to higher recombination at the interface47. Therefore, spike-type (with a very small positive CBO) can produce better efficiency than cliff-type68. Ding et al. reported experimental verification that, that maximum efficiency can be obtained with CBO nearly equal to + 0.16 eV69. To find the possible reason of spike in E-field distribution, electron–hole distribution along the device has been simulated and depicted in Fig. 6(c, d) and (e, f). The result shows, there is accumulation of hole in the TCO/ETL interface due to arise of cliff in the valence band. Accumulation of holes results in spike in E-field distribution for the Cd0.5Zn0.5S as ETL layer. Similar cliff is not observed when TiO2 is used as ETL layer. The holes might be generated from the near-interface TCO and ETL layer. The accumulation of holes results in repletion of holes at ETL end of the absorber layer. Therefore, better separation of generated electrons and holes results in higher efficiency.

As mentioned previously, IDL layers have been inserted in between the layers (HTL/absorber, absorber/ETL). IDL layers have been used to lessen interfacial carrier recombination. Despite all the interfaces going through the band offsets55, at the absorber/ETL interface, a negative Conduction band offset (CBO) (-) has been noticed. At the absorber/ETL junction, a small cliff is formed which allows the photo-generated electrons to flow towards the back contact by maintaining the JSC constant.

The effect of operating temperature on the device performance

Figure 8 refers device performance with change in operating temperature. Overall performance has been decreased with increase in temperature due to degradation and recombination. The initial increase in JSC from 200 to 300 K can be explained by the improved charge carrier generation and transport properties of the perovskite material at higher temperatures. However, the subsequent decrease in JSC at 400 K is likely due to the degradation of the perovskite layer and other device components under prolonged exposure to elevated temperatures.

Effect of the thickness variation of different layers (absorber, ETL & HTL)

The active absorbing layer (CH3NH3PbX3, X: I, Br, Cl) plays a crucial role in influencing major factors of the perovskite solar cell. The outcome of the thickness of the perovskite material’s layer on the device performances has been inspected in Fig. 9. A range of thicknesses for the absorber layer has been used from 100 to 1600 nm and results with the best possible result of 850 nm thickness for an effective photo conversion. A thin layer of 100 nm yields lower photon absorption71, which provides less photocurrent and a thick layer can attribute to the high series resistance of the device.

Change in efficiency with change in thickness of an absorber layer.

In the reported work, various materials have been used with various dimensions. In particular, we are more concerned about the ETL, absorber layer and HTL layer of this perovskite device. The impact of changing the thickness of the ETL layer on the photovoltaic device can be seen in Fig. 10a. In this study, Cd0.5Zn0.5S has been used as the ETL layer and CH3NH3PbI3 and MASnBr3 as perovskite and HTL layer, respectively. Perovskite and HTL materials have been chosen according to their performance, discussed in the previous section. The result indicates that 40–60 nm thickness is optimum for the ETL layer72. If the thickness of the material rises, then the series resistance of the material rises as well. Increased thickness results in a loss in photo generation of the perovskite solar cell due to an increase in recombination.

Schematic representation of the efficiency, VOC, JSC, and FF in relation to the thickness of (a) ETL (b) HTL.

Understanding that HTL is stuffed in between an electrode and an absorber layer, the thickness of HTL within 300–400 nm can be fruitful, since the thin layer provides a shorter distance to travel the holes from one end to the other and reduces the recombination rate73. If a layer less than 200 nm is placed as HTL, then the electrode establishes its contact with an absorber layer causing the solar cell device to shunt. A layer with a thickness of more than 400 nm can increase series resistance and give limited hole mobility. The effect of changing each HTL layer’s thickness can also be seen through Fig. 10b. Hence, with all consideration, an HTL layer with 350 nm thickness was considered as the optimum thickness for the device performance. The results depicted in Fig. 10 shows, the thickness of 50 nm for ETL, 800 nm for absorber layer and 350 nm of HTL layer was good assumption which has been used for all the devices.

Effect of defect densities

On photovoltaic parameters of the proposed perovskite solar cell Defects in perovskite absorber layer may arise in several forms, like vacancies, interstitials and grain boundaries. These defects act as recombination centers by trapping photo-generated charge carriers and stopping them from being collected at the electrodes. As shown in Fig. 12, increase in defects will eventually reduce in overall performance by raising the rate of recombination. As the results indicate, when the defect densities are increased from 1E12, making the device vulnerable for deterioration. This is because the high defect density creates many trap states within the perovskite bandgap, which can efficiently capture and recombine the photogenerated charge carriers before they can be extracted. Furthermore, there exists interface defects which are crucial for the recombination of interfaces between perovskite active layer and the charge transport layers by hindering the extraction and collection of the charge carriers.

Schematic representation of photovoltaic parameters by varying defect densities of CdZnS/MAPbI3/MASnBr3 perovskite structure.

To alleviate the interfacial defects many approached must been considered as encapsulation of 2D perovskites1 improving perovskite film quality and optimizing by band alignments with charge transport layers. Addressing the defect-related challenges through advanced fabrication techniques and device engineering is crucial for the continued development and commercialization of high-efficiency perovskite solar cell technology.

Effect of series and shunt resistances

In perovskite solar cell, series resistance (Rs) and shunt resistance (Rsh) affects device performance significantly. In simulation study, fitting value of Rs and Rsh is not sufficient. Sensitivity of the cell performance with the change of Rs and Rsh is more important. Therefore, the performance of most optimized stack with change in Rs and Rsh has been evaluated. To study effect of variation of series resistance, value of Rsh is kept constant at the value of 105 Ω.cm2, whereas for the study of Rsh effect, value of Rs is kept constant at 0.5 Ω.cm275. It should be mentioned here that, practical relevance, material compatibility, interface quality for the proposed device has been considered. Cd0.5Zn0.5S, II-VI semiconductor offers suitable band alignment with as ETL with CH3NH3PbI3, which is again a well-established absorber layer. IDLs have been used to reduce recombination and improve interface quality. The study shows MaSnBr3 also offers good band alignment. However, special control might be required for experimental realization of the proposed structure due to oxidation related issue of MaSnBr3.

Conclusion

The perovskite solar cell with device architecture of right contact/TCO/ETL/IDL/ Perovskite/IDL/HTL/left contact was developed and analysed through simulations by SCAPS-1D. The absorber layer with a bandgap close to 1.45 eV (Shockley–Queiesser limit) exhibits superior efficiency as compared to candidates with a larger band gap. With superior open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current density, fill factor, and PCE, CH3NH3PbI3 shows encouraging results in this area when compared to other halide-based perovskite materials.

For optimization, thicknesses of 50 nm, 850 nm and 350 nm was chosen for ETL, absorber layer and HTL respectively, based on the reported literature. Afterwards, study of thickness effect validates the assumption. (300 nm)ZnO/(50 nm) Cd0.5Zn0.5S/(10 nm)IDL/850 nm CH3NH3PbI3/(10 nm)IDL/(350 nm) MASnBr3 has been obtained as most optimized device with (PCE) of 27.12%, a short-circuit current density (JSC) of 25.34 mA/cm2, an open-circuit voltage (VOC) of 1.196 V and the fill factor of 89.50%.

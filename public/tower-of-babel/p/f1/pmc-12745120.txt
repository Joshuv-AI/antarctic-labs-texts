Postoperative electroencephalographic anterior asymmetry is associated with neurologic injury and increased morbidity
Authors: Michael F Swartz, Shuichi Yoshitake, Laurie E Seltzer, Nechelle Dias, Konstantinos S Mylonas, George M Alfieris
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative electroencephalograms (EEGs) are often used to detect seizures after neonatal cardiac surgery. Electroencephalograms can also measure asymmetry, a regional or hemispheric difference in the voltage or frequency that suggests neurologic injury, quantified using the absolute asymmetry spectrogram (AAS). We hypothesized that postoperative asymmetry would be associated with neurologic injury.MethodsQuantitative EEG monitoring measured the mean AAS from the anterior, posterior, and hemispheric regions at baseline and during postoperative day (POD) 0 to POD 2. Infants were divided into 2 groups: EEG asymmetry, defined as a mean AAS >50% during POD 0, and EEG symmetry, defined as mean AAS ≤50% during POD 0.ResultsIn the study cohort of 76 neonates, only the anterior AAS increased significantly from baseline during POD 0 (42.8 ± 7.6% vs 34.6 ± 9.4%; P < .01). Fourteen neonates (18.4%) developed EEG asymmetry on POD 0, all from the anterior region. There were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between the 2 study groups. The EEG asymmetry group had a significantly higher rate of seizures (21.4% vs 3.2%; P = .04) and stroke (28.5% vs 3.2%; P = .009), and longer hospital length of stay (median, 36.5 [interquartile range (IQR), 24.3-87.0] days vs 17.0 [IQR, 11.0-31.3] days; P < .01). Multivariate analysis confirmed that asymmetry was associated with a greater incidence of stroke (odds ratio, 9.116; 95% confidence interval, 1.365-60.870; P = .023).ConclusionsAsymmetry was associated with neurologic injury and increased morbidity. Quantitative EEG monitoring may be an important adjunct during the early postoperative period to identify neonates at risk for neurologic injury and increased morbidity.Key Words: electroencephalogram, neurologic monitoring, seizure, stroke

Central Message

Following antegrade cerebral perfusion, electroencephalogram anterior asymmetry occurred in 18.4% of neonates and was associated with an increased risk for stroke, seizure, and length of stay.

Perspective

Electroencephalogram asymmetry, a regional or hemispheric difference in voltage or frequency, may indicate neurologic injury. Anterior asymmetry during postoperative day 0 following antegrade cerebral perfusion was associated with an increased risk for neurologic injury and postoperative morbidity, suggesting the importance of monitoring for asymmetry when caring for critically ill neonates.

Postoperative qualitative electroencephalogram (EEG) monitoring is focused primarily on the detection of subclinical seizures, which are associated with neurologic injury and increased morbidity.1, 2, 3 Asymmetry is a regional or hemispheric difference in the voltage or frequency that also suggests neurologic injury and can be measured during EEG monitoring.4,5 Asymmetry develops during cerebral ischemia6 and can precede the diagnosis of seizure and stroke in both pediatrics and adults.7, 8, 9, 10 The prevalence and pathogenesis of asymmetry during the immediate postoperative period after neonatal antegrade cerebral perfusion (ACP) remains unclear.

The diagnosis of asymmetry using qualitative EEG monitoring relies on the visual inspection of continuous 10- to 60-second tracings. Subtle EEG differences that develop over several hours, interobserver variation, and a lack of a concrete threshold for numerically defining asymmetry have limited the real-time application of qualitative EEG.7,11 In contrast, quantitative EEG (qEEG) monitoring uses fast Fourier transform (FFT) to produce continuous graphical outputs that provide data over several hours. In addition, qEEG monitoring can numerically characterize voltage or frequency differences and define asymmetry using preestablished thresholds. For example, the asymmetry spectrogram measures the voltage and frequency between left and right paired EEG electrodes, with percentages >50% identifying asymmetry and suggesting neurologic injury.12,13

In the present study, we measured the asymmetry spectrogram during the early postoperative period following arch reconstruction using ACP to explore the evolution and potential pathogenesis of asymmetry. We hypothesized that regional or hemispheric asymmetry during postoperative day (POD) 0 would be associated with neurologic injury and increased perioperative morbidity.

Methods

Following Institutional Review Board approval (approval 00007188, approved on June 9, 2022, with a waiver of consent for this retrospective review), neonates (age <30 days) between 2015 and 2021 requiring aortic arch reconstruction with ACP and receiving postoperative qEEG monitoring were identified and reviewed retrospectively. A gross neurologic exam and head ultrasound were performed during the preoperative period. Subsequent brain imaging was obtained in the presence of a structural abnormality or neurologic deficit. Neonates who developed an intraoperative seizure and those requiring either preoperative or postoperative extracorporeal membrane oxygenation during PODs 1 to 2 were excluded.

Operative Technique

The operative technique has been described previously14,15 and is also explained in detail in the Online Data Supplement.

Qualitative EEG Monitoring

EEG electrodes were affixed to the scalp using collodion and examined according to the international 10-20 system using the standard temporal, parasagittal, and midline placements except FP1 and FP216 (Figure 1, A). A 30-minute baseline EEG was performed 24 to 48 hours before the operation, and the EEG electrodes were kept in place for postoperative monitoring. Baseline and postoperative EEG studies were qualitatively examined and read by 1 of 5 pediatric neurologists, and the EEG patterns on intensive care unit (ICU) admission and for each postoperative day were defined.16,17 Asymmetry was determined by visually examining voltage and frequency differences.16 Asynchrony was characterized by delayed cortical bursts between the left and right hemispheres and defined as periodic if there were occasional asynchronous bursts and persistent if the majority of bursts were asynchronous.18 Seizure activity was defined as a rhythmic pattern in 1 or more leads lasting at least 10 seconds.

B, CONSORT diagram for neonates with postoperative (postop) quantitative EEG monitoring. Vent, Mechanical ventilation.

qEEG Monitoring

qEEG analysis was performed using Persyst 14 (Persist Development) following the pre-designated parameters to quantify the asymmetry percentage (Online Data Supplement) from the hemispheric, anterior, and posterior regions (Figure 1, A). The Persyst software uses a fast Fourier transform (FFT) power ratio to compare paired EEG electrodes at a frequency of 0 to 20 Hz over a 2-minute (average) period. An asymmetry percentage of 0 is considered balanced, −100% reflects leftward asymmetry, and +100% reflects rightward asymmetry (Figure E1).10,12,13,19 Hemispheric, anterior, and posterior asymmetry spectrogram values were measured from 3 separate time points approximately 5 minutes apart during the baseline study, every 30 minutes during POD 0, and then hourly on PODs 1 and 2. Each postoperative study began at ICU admission, and the beginning of each new POD started at 6:00 AM.

Ten-minute anterior spectrograms from a neonate with symmetry (top) and a neonate with asymmetry (bottom).

The comparison of data between groups required obtaining the absolute value from the absolute asymmetry spectrogram (AAS) to ensure that negative and positive values do not negate each other. The mean asymmetry percentage was calculated for each POD. For children who died during the monitoring period, the data after the day of death was left blank, and the mean was calculated from the remaining surviving patients. Significant asymmetry was defined if the mean regional or hemispheric AAS values were >50% during a POD. The threshold of 50% was chosen based on the definitions in the Persyst software, as well as on previous data suggesting that asymmetry percentages >50% are clinically significant and associated with neurologic injury.12,13

The neonates were divided into 2 groups: asymmetry, with a mean AAS value >50% during POD 0, and symmetry, with a mean AAS value ≤50% during POD 0. To understand the time course of the AAS over the first 2 PODs, AAS values were compared within the groups, and the AAS percent difference was compared between the groups.

Postoperative Clinical Outcomes

The postoperative clinical outcomes are explained in detail in the Online Data Supplement.

Statistical Methods

Data are reported as mean ± standard deviation, median with interquartile range (IQR), or frequency with percentage. Continuous variables were first analyzed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and then either the 2-tailed t test or Mann-Whitney U test was used to determine significance. Categorical data were compared between procedural groups using the Fisher exact test. For univariate statistics, a Bonferroni correction was performed during multiple comparisons. A mixed-effects model with the Dunnett multiple comparisons test was used to compare the mean AAS values from the entire cohort during each POD. The comparison of the AAS values by POD between patients with asymmetry and those without asymmetry, was compared using a mixed-effects model with a Tukey multiple comparisons test.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to compare the mean anterior AAS values during POD 0 to the postoperative outcomes. The area under the ROC curve, 95% confidence intervals (CIs), P values, and the sensitivity and specificity for an AAS >50% were analyzed. In addition, the Youden Index was used to determine the optimal cutoff for the POD 0 AAS values based on each categorical postoperative outcome. Forward conditional binary logistic regression was used to quantify the impact of perioperative variables on POD 0 asymmetry. All variables with a P value <.1 on univariate statistical comparisons between the asymmetry and symmetry groups were introduced into the multivariate model to ascertain the independence of the data. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 27 (IBM) and GraphPad Prism (Dotmatics). A power calculation was not performed because this was a retrospective study.

Results

Seventy-six neonates underwent aortic arch reconstruction using ACP and had postoperative EEG monitoring (Figure 1, B). Representative EEGs and asymmetry spectrograms are provided in Figure 2. The anterior AAS increased during POD 0 from baseline (POD 0, 42.8 ± 7.6% vs baseline, 34.6 ± 9.4%; P < .001) (Figure 3, A). During PODs 1 and 2, the AAS decreased but remained significantly greater than baseline (POD 1, 39.7 ± 7.6% [P = .005] vs POD 2, 38.8 ± 8.5% [P = .007]). The posterior and hemispheric AAS did not change significantly during the postoperative period (Figure 3, B and C). At baseline, most neonates had anterior, posterior, and hemispheric AAS values <50% (Figure 3, D). During POD 0, a significantly greater number of neonates (n = 14) had mean anterior AAS values >50% compared to baseline (18.4% vs 5.3%; P = .02). No neonates had mean posterior or hemispheric AAS values > 50% during POD 0.

Significant Anterior Asymmetry on POD 0

Based on the POD 0 EEGs, 14 neonates (18.4%) had EEG asymmetry, all from the anterior region, and 62 neonates (81.6%) neonates EEG symmetry. The perioperative demographics showed a trend toward younger age within the asymmetry group but no significant differences in the duration of cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), ACP time, nadir cooling temperature, or procedure (Table 1). The EEG patterns were similar during each POD, and in both groups, the EEG pattern became increasingly more continuous during the postoperative period (Online Data Supplement).

Postoperative outcomes demonstrated that the laboratory measures during ICU admission were not significantly different between the 2 groups. However, the percentage of patients requiring that the sternum be left open was significantly greater in the asymmetry group (Table 2). Although the peak lactate level was not different, the lactate rise was significantly greater in the asymmetry group. Both subclinical seizure and stroke developed in 3 neonates in the asymmetry group and in 1 neonate in the symmetry group. Both stroke and seizure were nearly 8 times more frequent in the asymmetry group. The duration of ventilation and the duration of ventilation >7 days trended toward significance; however, the hospital length of stay was significantly longer in neonates with POD 0 asymmetry. ROC curves demonstrated that higher mean POD 0 AAS values were predictive of a stroke, with the highest area under the ROC curve value. Analysis of the ROC curve to determine the optimal threshold for AAS values to predict stroke was an AAS value of 50.5% (Table E2). Multivariate analysis confirmed that neonates with asymmetry during POD 0 had a significantly greater incidence of postoperative stroke (odds ratio, 9.116; 95% CI, 1.365-60.870; P = .023) (Table 3).

Discussion

Early postoperative anterior asymmetry occurred in 18% of neonates following arch reconstruction using ACP. Most importantly, 28.5% of neonates with anterior asymmetry developed postoperative stroke, which is approximately 5 times higher than the 5% national average following neonatal ACP.20,21 Electroencephalogram asymmetry has been shown to precede the radiologic diagnosis of stroke in adults and children.6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 Ischemia, edema, and the accumulation of hemorrhage can alter the left versus right EEG frequency and amplitudes, resulting in an asymmetry difference that provides a window for earlier recognition and treatment of neurologic injury. This may be particularly important in neonates, for whom a complete neurologic exam might not be possible. The effects of intraoperative and postoperative paralytics often limit the ability to perform a complete neurologic exam, and the delay in diagnosing an in-hospital stroke is associated with increased morbidity and mortality.22,23 Hypotension, anemia, and the use of anticoagulants are common during the postoperative period and can exacerbate a stroke, resulting in further injury. A recent prospective multicenter study examined the use of portable EEGs to quantify asymmetry in out-of-hospital patients thought to be experiencing acute stroke. The authors demonstrated that paramedics’ use of an asymmetry index from 212 adults had a sensitivity of 80% and specificity of 93% in predicting stroke.24 Similarly, EEG asymmetry following ACP may identify at-risk children earlier during the postoperative period to better optimize treatments and obtain confirmatory advanced neuroimaging.

The use of postoperative qualitative EEG monitoring after neonatal ACP or deep hypothermic circulatory arrest has been recommended to identify, treat, and limit the subsequent morbidity and mortality from subclinical seizures.1, 2, 3 There was no correlation between the qualitative and quantitative diagnosis of EEG asymmetry during POD 0. Several mechanisms likely contribute to the discrepancy between the qualitative and quantitative diagnosis of EEG asymmetry during POD 0. Qualitative EEG monitoring was based on the visual inspection of a 24-hour EEG by 1 of 5 pediatric neurologists without continuous or direct measurements of the voltage or frequency. Without repetitive numeric data, the significance of periodic variations in the voltage or frequency was at the discretion of the reading neurologist. Therefore, interobserver variation between and within patients, the lack of a concrete threshold to define asymmetry, the absence of numerical data, and the review of a 24-hour EEG recording that spanned multiple computer screens likely resulted in an underrepresentation of the diagnosis of asymmetry from the qualitative EEG during POD 0. In contrast, qEEG monitoring produces time-condensed graphical outputs, so that an entire 24-hour EEG recording can be viewed within a single computer screen. Most importantly, the percentage of asymmetry was quantified at regular intervals, irrespective of the qualitative assessment. The definition of asymmetry based on the Persyst software and other authors12,13 provided a concrete definition that was reproducible irrespective of the observer and likely resulted in a more accurate diagnosis of asymmetry during the early postoperative period.

Although postoperative morbidity was higher in the asymmetry group, whether this was related solely to the asymmetry diagnosis is unclear. Children in the asymmetry group required delayed sternal closure, which suggests hemodynamic instability after the operative procedure. Furthermore, the incidences of stroke and seizure were significantly greater within the asymmetry group. These factors, alone or in combination, may have contributed to an increased hospital length of stay. During neonatal cardiopulmonary bypass, hypothermia is known to alter cerebral autoregulation, which may be a contributing factor in the development of early postoperative asymmetry.25 Although there were no significant differences in the nadir temperature between the asymmetry and symmetry groups, further investigation into cerebral autoregulation and postoperative asymmetry is warranted.

Limitations

This study has several limitations, including its retrospective design without a power analysis prior to the investigation. Moreover, data from the asymmetry spectrogram were obtained at intervals and thus might not have captured all the asymmetry fluctuations during the postoperative period. Cerebral oximetry was limited to either the left or right hemisphere on a rotating basis to avoid skin breakdown, precluding a correlation between asymmetry and cerebral oximetry. Most importantly, magnetic resonance imaging was performed only when clinically indicated, and thus the number of neurologic events is likely an underestimate of the true incidence of neurologic injury.

Conclusions

Anterior asymmetry during POD 0 occurred in almost 20% of neonates following ACP and was associated with increased risk for seizure, stroke, and hospital length of stay. Although qualitative EEG assessment during the postoperative period is critical to identify neonates with subclinical seizures, qEEG monitoring uses a concrete definition from the AAS to more accurately identify neonates with asymmetry during the early postoperative period. Therefore, using the asymmetry spectrogram may be an essential adjunct for identifying critically ill neonates during the immediate postoperative period.

Current work is underway on the use of qEEG monitoring and measurements of postoperative asymmetry during the postoperative phase of care, and further research into understanding the longer-term neurodevelopmental outcomes of neonates with significant asymmetry is paramount.

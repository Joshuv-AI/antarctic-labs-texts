Public opinion dissemination simulation based on large language model multi-agent systems
Authors: Hai Lan, Hua Hu, Peng Cheng Guo, Qi Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Internet has fundamentally reshaped the formation and diffusion of public opinion in modern society. However, existing simulation studies often face challenges such as insufficient fidelity in evolutionary dynamics, behavioral homogenization, and high modeling costs. This study develops a realistic public opinion simulation system that integrates macro-level diffusion patterns with micro-level individual cognition, addressing the traditional models’ limitations in generalizability and high resource demands. This study proposes an LLM-based multi-agent simulation framework for public opinion dissemination. The framework first constructs behavior probability profiles calibrated against real-world social media data, following exponential and normal distribution models to constrain agent behavior frequency and topical tendencies (macro-dynamics). Concurrently, by utilizing an LLM as the cognitive core, the system enables dynamic semantic content generation via the integration of Public Opinion Simulation Standard Operating Procedure (PSOP) and a Global Information Sharing Pool (GISP) (micro-cognition). Comparative experiments across two distinct scenarios—“Public Policy” and “Food Safety”—show that (1): The framework exhibits significant cross-scenario robustness, replicating the incubation-eruption-decay’ evolutionary pattern, which aligns with propagation dynamics without the need for parameter adjustment (2); Quantitative evaluation shows that the normalized agent behavior distribution entropy reaches 0.69; moreover the Distinct-2 metric for generated content reaches 0.83, substantially mitigating behavioral homogenization (3); Compared with traditional deep learning methods, this framework supports zero-shot cold start, with negligible computational costs for domain adaptation. By unifying the agent architecture, this research enables efficient simulation of public opinion evolution, providing a low-cost, high-fidelity methodological paradigm for public opinion crisis management.Keywords: Public Opinion Simulation, Large Language Model (LLM), Multi-Agent System, Probabilistic Graphical Model, AgentSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid development of the Internet, online public opinion has become a significant force shaping policy discourse and social dynamics. For governments and organizations, understanding and monitoring online public opinion is crucial, particularly in emergency management. However, existing public opinion simulation methods are critically limited, constraining their ability to simulate real-world scenarios. Current approaches often employ oversimplified designs, making it difficult to reflect the dynamics and complexity of public opinion dissemination. These models fail to adequately simulate the randomness and diversity of individual behaviors, thereby undermining the realism of the simulation. Traditional methods also rely on multiple distinct models to handle information dissemination, behavior modeling, and trend prediction within public opinion simulation, resulting in high computational and operational costs. These models typically operate independently, leading to inefficient and fragmented simulations, which fail to fully reflect the dynamics of real-world public opinion.

Existing research on online public opinion simulation primarily focuses on time-series modeling, individual behavior simulation, and information interaction emulation, but still suffers from notable limitations1–3. First, most models oversimplify the public opinion environment by adopting linear or predictable patterns which fail to fully capture the subtle and chaotic dynamics of online interactions4. Second, the depth of individual behavior simulation is insufficient; existing methods struggle to replicate the diverse and context-dependent decision-making processes of real users. Furthermore, reliance on multiple specialized models increases computational complexity and costs, limiting scalability and practical applications5. These gaps highlight the need for more realistic, efficient, and unified public opinion simulation methods to better support emergency management and social stability.

In light of these limitations, recent progress in large language models (LLMs) offers a novel paradigm. With advancements in deep learning technologies, LLMs have demonstrated significant advantages in semantic understanding, logical reasoning, and human-like behavior modeling6–8, presenting new opportunities for public opinion simulation modeling research. By utilizing LLMs to construct various roles in public opinion environments, it is possible to better approximate individual behavioral decisions, information interaction content, and public opinion trend predictions. Thus, researchers can more accurately capture and simulate the realism and complexity of public opinion during the modeling process. Addressing these gaps through integrated theoretical and methodological advances can improve the fidelity and scalability of public opinion simulation frameworks.

This study proposes an LLM-based multi-agent public opinion dissemination simulation method that simulates different roles within a unified framework. This approach contributes in three key aspects: First, a multi-agent simulation framework with role heterogeneity is constructed, where LLM-driven agents accurately simulate the behavioral characteristics of different social groups in public opinion dissemination, enhancing the authenticity and behavioral diversity of the simulation. Second, standardized operational procedures and shared information pool mechanisms are established to enable efficient collaboration among multiple agents, fundamentally reducing the dependency on multi-model coordination in traditional methods. Third, based on multi-dimensional experimental validation, the ability of the simulation system to reproduce key evolutionary patterns of public opinion is systematically evaluated. This method not only addresses the gaps in existing research but also provides a scalable, low-cost novel solution for public opinion governance.

Related work

Public opinion dissemination simulation aims to model how collective emotions, particularly in social media contexts, disseminate through dynamic interactions among individuals, institutions, and other actors to analyze or predict opinion trends in specific events. The development of research methods in this field is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Evolution of Research Methods in Public Opinion Dissemination Simulation.

Early stage: static simulation based on mathematical models

In the early stages of research, scholars primarily relied on mathematical models to describe the opinion dissemination patterns. One of the pioneering efforts was the computer simulation model proposed by9to simulate the opinion dissemination process during community referendum controversies, establishing a foundational basis for subsequent opinion simulations10. introduced a micro-model to simulate the diffusion of innovations within heterogeneous populations. Another notable study involves the SIR model based on the epidemic analogy11,which categorizes individuals into Susceptible, Infected, and Recovered states, widely applied in rumor and information dissemination research12. These models established a theoretical basis for opinion dissemination simulation; however, their overly simplified assumptions hinder the depiction of individual behavioral heterogeneity and randomness, limiting the realism of the simulations.

Middle stage: complex network theory and multi-agent modeling

Since 2000, complex network theory and multi-agent methods have emerged as dominant approaches. From a complex network perspective, researchers utilized “small-world effect” and “scale-free properties” to explain the structural patterns of information propagation13,14. In multi-agent modeling15, proposed an agent simulation framework based on small-world networks, while16, through the Biased Voter Model, revealed potential stubborn, compromising, or conforming behaviors of users when facing neighbors’ opinions. Furthermore, this stage witnessed the integration of complex network theory with multi-agent methods, such as analyzing dissemination dynamics in Weibo networks by combining network structures with individual interactions17. Although these methods more closely approximate real-world scenarios, their high computational complexity and strong sensitivity to parameters have limited their large-scale application.

Recent stage: data-driven dynamic simulation

As large-scale social media datasets became increasingly available, research shifted toward data-driven and machine learning approaches18. utilized Dynamic Topic Modeling (DTM) and sentiment analysis to reveal the sentiment evolution across different topics during the pandemic19. combined Naive Bayes with Support Vector Machines (SVM) to enable efficient classification of online comments. Concurrently20, conducted a systematic review that summarized trends and hotspots in crisis communication research21,22. proposed a path prediction method based on Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), demonstrating superior performance in academic networks. While these methods enhanced the ability to characterize real-world large-scale data, they still suffer from deficiencies such as strong dependence on labeled data and difficulties in portraying the diversity of individual behaviors.

Latest advances: methods based on deep learning, graph neural networks, and large language models

In recent years, deep learning and graph neural networks have further propelled the development of opinion simulation23. proposed an opinion competition model based on the MGNN, while24 developed the UniGO framework, integrating multiple opinion fusion rules to improve opinion evolution prediction25 combined SIR, LSTM, and Kalman filtering to achieve unified modeling of individual behaviors and macro trends. Concurrently, utilizing LLMs to drive agents for simulating social group behaviors has become a research hotspot26–33 proposed Monte Carlo social simulation via large-scale LLM-based agents to predict the distribution of group reactions to information; however, this method relies primarily on generative inference, lacks explicit modeling of macro-level propagation patterns, and incurs high computational costs34 constructed an LLM agent simulation framework integrating classical opinion dynamics models to study influence diffusion in social networks, but the role settings are predominantly adversarial dual camps, limiting scenarios and role types35 proposed a hybrid method coupling LLMs with Cellular Automata and SIR models, improving prediction accuracy through a leader-follower structure, yet the reliance on multi-model synergy results in high system complexity36 proposed a data-driven multi-agent public opinion propagation model for cyberbullying scenarios, focusing on propagation mechanism modeling in specific contexts without introducing generative cognitive capabilities.

Despite progress in microscopic interaction simulation, previous studies are mostly limited to “sandbox” environments and lack the capability to simulate macroscopic public opinion dissemination mechanisms. Therefore, this study proposes a “Cognitive-Probabilistic Hybrid Driven” multi-agent framework for public opinion dynamics. The core concept of this framework is to decouple the agent behavior generation process into two collaborative layers:

(1) Probabilistic-Driven Layer

This layer models behavior frequency using an exponential distribution (Sect. 3.4) and models topic and attitude heterogeneity using a Gaussian distribution. It also combines a multi-dimensional feature fusion model to select interaction targets (Sect. 3.5), determining behavior types and interaction choices to ensure macroscopic statistical authenticity.

(2) Cognitive-Driven Layer

Based on LLM semantic reasoning combined with role profiling and contextual memory (Sect. 3.6), this layer generates specific semantic expressions, ensuring microscopic cognitive diversity.

The core idea is to delegate high-frequency, low-complexity decisions to probabilistic models, while delegating low-frequency tasks that require high semantic understanding to LLMs, thereby balancing statistical realism, cognitive diversity, and computational efficiency. Methods

This study constructs a multi-agent framework for opinion dissemination simulation based on LLMs. The framework consists of seven modules: (1) development of the public opinion scenario indicator system, (2) definition of public opinion agent roles, (3) construction of the agent framework,(4) prediction of interactive behaviors between agent roles, (5) prediction of behavior targets, (6) generation of agent profiles and behavioral content, (7) multi-agent interactive simulation.

As illustrated in Fig. 2, the design logic of these seven key modules follows a closed-loop process: “Environment Initialization → Cognitive Decision → Behavior Execution → Macro Feedback”, described in detail below:

Definition of public opinion agent roles

Public opinion dissemination is driven by diverse roles acting as information carriers. To enhance the simulation of individual behaviors, this study categorizes these roles into four types (see Fig. 3): “General Public”, “Hypester”, “Controller”, and “Subject”. Each role is designed with distinct behavioral patterns that reflect its specific functions in opinion dissemination.

Planning

An LLM-based agent can understand and plan complex tasks, including:

Prediction of agent interactive behaviors

In the process of public opinion dissemination, user interaction behaviors exhibit significant randomness and long-tail distribution characteristics. Previous studies have shown that human online activity participation frequencies generally follow a heavy-tail distribution37,38. Based on this prior knowledge, this study adopts an exponential distribution as the modeling foundation for behavior frequencies, and the rationality of this choice is verified through real data calibration. It should be noted that this section aims to provide an empirical calibration benchmark for agent behavior modeling, rather than evaluating the robustness of the framework, which is addressed in Chap. 4 through cross-scenario experiments.

Behavior frequency calibration based on real-world data

To capture the statistical characteristics of real interaction behaviors, we selected the “Down Jacket Fraud” event as a calibration case. In November 2024, CCTV Finance exposed a “silk substitution for down” issue in live-streaming e-commerce, which triggered widespread discussions on the Weibo platform. We collected 34,000 Weibo interaction data points using web crawlers, covering 23 dimensions such as post time, follower count, sentiment type, number of likes, comments, and reposts. Statistical results (Fig. 5) indicate that user interaction frequency exhibits typical long-tail characteristics. After validation through maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), the fitted curve aligns with the empirical distribution, demonstrating an exponential decay trend. Generation of agent profiles and action contents

Role profile generation constructs virtual personas for each public opinion agent, serving as the foundation for simulating differentiated behavioral patterns. Meanwhile, role behavior content generation focuses on producing event-related comments, posts, and sentiments to drive the dynamic evolution of public opinion dissemination. By designing specific prompt instructions, we transform these operations into LLM-driven text generation tasks.

Role profile generation

Role profile generation involves multiple interdependent sub-tasks. We employ the “Least-to-Most”39 method to decompose this complex task into four sub-tasks: event summary generation, basic role-generation, role-inherent topic generation, and role-inherent attitude generation. These tasks constitute a sequential workflow where the output of a preceding task serves as the input for the subsequent one, ultimately achieving a systematic construction of role profiles. As shown in Fig. 7, this process ensures that every agent possesses necessary attributes, topics of interest, and inherent attitudes, thereby accurately reflecting its position within the public opinion environment.

Conclusion

To bridge the gap between macro-level propagation patterns and micro-level behavioral realism, we developed a multi-agent framework powered by LLMs. Based on our theoretical framework and experiments in two distinct scenarios, the following main conclusions are drawn:

Effective Verification of Role Heterogeneity and Behavioral Realism: By constructing four types of standardized roles, this study addressed the issue of individual homogenization inherent in traditional models. Our quantitative results show that the normalized entropy of agent behavior reached 0.69; concurrently, the Distinct-2 metric for generated content reached 0.83, with each role exhibiting significant statistical behavioral differences. This confirms that role heterogeneity in modeling can facilitate the emergence of high-fidelity collective behaviors.

Our quantitative results show that the normalized entropy of agent behavior reached 0.69; concurrently, the Distinct-2 metric for generated content reached 0.83, with each role exhibiting significant statistical behavioral differences. This confirms that role heterogeneity in modeling can facilitate the emergence of high-fidelity collective behaviors.

Effectiveness of the Cognitive-Probabilistic Hybrid Drive Mechanism: Experiments demonstrate that the hybrid architecture, which fuses probabilistic graphical models with LLM cognition, bridges the gap between microscopic behaviors and macroscopic dynamics. In the dual scenarios of “Public Policy” and “Food Safety,” this mechanism reproduced intensity curves consistent with propagation patterns without any model fine-tuning, confirming the scientific soundness and cross-scenario robustness of this method.

Establishment of a Low-Cost Paradigm: Our unified architecture substantially reduces computational and infrastructural requirements. Comparative experiments reveal that, unlike traditional deep learning methods, this framework supports zero-shot cold start and requires no training for domain adaptation, with marginal costs approaching zero. This training-free paradigm provides a readily deployable and cost-effective framework, substantially broadening the utility of public opinion simulations in complex scenarios.

Despite the preliminary achievements of this study, certain limitations remain to be addressed in future work:

Model Robustness and Multimodal Expansion: Future research will evaluate the framework across diverse LLMs to assess its technical robustness and generalizability. Furthermore, current simulations primarily focus on textual modalities, whereas real-world public opinion often encompasses images and videos. Future work will introduce Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to enhance simulation capabilities for short-video public opinion.

Salvigenin alleviates ferroptosis and pyroptosis in myocardial ischemia/reperfusion models by inhibiting the NLRP3 pathway
Authors: Wenrong Su, Shiqing Zhang, Jinghai Chen, Junchang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMyocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury (MI/RI) is a life-threatening vascular disease. Salvigenin (SGN) has been reported to play anti-oxidative stress and anti-inflammatory roles in various diseases. Our purpose was to investigate the effect of SGN on MI/RI and its potential mechanism. The viability of hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-induced H9C2 cells was explored by CCK-8 assay. Fe2+ and MDA levels were detected via corresponding commercial kits. The protein expression level was evaluated through western blot. The intracellular ROS level was detected by immunofluorescent staining. IL-1β and IL-18 levels were explored through ELISA assay. C57BL/6 mice were used to construct in vivo model by left anterior descending coronary artery occlusion. The effect of SGN on mice models were evaluated by enzyme activity kits, echocardiography, HE staining and TUNEL. SGN increased the viability of H/R-induced H9C2 cells. SGN reduced the levels of Fe2+ and MDA both in vitro and in vivo. SGN up-regulated SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1protein levels and decreased ROS level in H/R-induced H9C2 cells compared with the control cells. SGN down-regulated NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N, and reduced the concentration of IL-1β and IL-18 both in cells and in mice models. Moreover, SGN repaired the left ventricular systolic function, decreased infarct size, serum CK-MB, CTnT, and LDH levels and cardiomyocyte area in mice models. SGN treatment decreased the apoptosis level of myocardial tissue cells. However, co-transfection with the NLRP3 overexpression vector reversed the inhibition of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis. In conclusion, our results indicated that SGN could moderate MI/RI and alleviate ferroptosis and pyroptosis in vitro and in vivo, and its mechanism of action was associated with the inhibition of NLRP3 pathway. Introduction

Myocardial infarction is one of the leading causes of disability and death worldwide [1]. Myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury (MI/RI) is a pathophysiological phenomenon that occurs after acute myocardial infarction. This type of injury frequently results in the expansion of myocardial infarction, further damaging cardiac function and adversely affecting the prognosis of patients with myocardial infarction [2]. Studies have indicated that cardiomyocyte death caused by reperfusion accounts for 50% of the entire infarcted area [3]. The pathological mechanisms leading to MI/RI include various complex biological processes, including autophagy, apoptosis, oxidative stress, energy metabolism disorder and necroptosis [4]. Therefore, in order to prevent myocardial cell death and inhibit myocardial injury in patients with myocardial infarction, it is necessary to study new drug therapy strategies based on the mechanism of MI/RI.

Salvigenin (SGN) is a naturally active flavonoid compound derived from Salvia officinalis, which can inhibit cell apoptosis and has various pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and anti-tumor growth [5, 6]. SGN has been shown to inhibit aerobic glycolysis and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) chemotherapy resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells by inhibiting the PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β pathway [7]. In rats, SGN significantly counteracted Atrazine (ATR)-induced hepatotoxicity via regulating some biological processes, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic [8]. In recent years, multiple natural products have been found to protect MI/RI by targeting mitochondria [9]. Nevertheless, the function of SGN in MI/RI has not been investigated.

Ferroptosis, which has been discovered in recent years, is a new way of cell death that relies on iron [10]. This process is primarily driven by lipid peroxidation, which mediates damage to the cell membrane and ultimately leads to cell death. The regulation of ferroptosis involves varieties of cellular metabolic pathways, such as iron metabolism, redox system, and lipid metabolism [11]. Research has shown that the pathogenesis and treatment outcomes of several diseases are associated with ferroptosis [12]. Fang et al. [13] discovered the presence of ferroptosis in MI/RI mouse model. Recent researches have indicated a close connection between ferroptosis and MI/RI, and inhibiting ferroptosis through specific inhibitors can significantly mitigate heart damage caused by ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) events [14, 15]. Therefore, strategic reduction of ferroptosis may be a promising approach to prevent MI/RI.

Pyroptosis is a programmed cell death pattern that promotes the production of inflammatory responses in the body [16]. The characteristic of this process is dependent on inflammatory caspases and combined with release of abundant pro-inflammatory factors. With the continuous in-depth research on pyroptosis, it has been found that it plays an indispensable role in resisting infections [17]. Moreover, researches have shown that pyroptosis is associated with various diseases, especially those characterized by cell death and inflammation [18]. Past studies have clearly demonstrated that pyroptosis is involved in the pathological process of MI/RI characterized by inflammation [19]. By inhibiting the occurrence of pyroptosis, the inflammatory response of myocardial tissue can be regulated, thereby reducing the size of myocardial infarction and improving cardiac function [20]. Therefore, it is of great significance to explore pyroptosis inhibitors for the treatment of MI/RI. The purpose of the current study was to explore the regulatory effects of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis in MI/RI and its potential mechanisms. Our findings may provide some guidance on whether SGN can be used for MI/RI treatment.

Materials and methods

Cell culture, H/R model establishment and transfection

The rat cardiomyocyte H9C2 cells were acquired from the Cell Bank of Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultivated with DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) with 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

Hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) treatment was conducted based on previous report [21]. Briefly, H9C2 cells were cultivated in DMEM free of glucose and FBS in the environment with 95% N2 and 5% CO2 at 37 °C, and the control cells were cultivated under normal conditions. After 4 h, H9C2 cells were treated with conventional medium in a general incubator with 95% O2 and 5% CO2 for 2 h to initate reoxygenation injury. These cell models were pre-treated with different concentrations of Salvigenin (SGN, 19103–54-9, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc. Shanghai, China) for 12 h. The solubility of SGN in DMSO was 16.67 mg/mL.

The NLRP3 overexpression vector and its negative control were obtained from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). The transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 2000 transfection kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

H9C2 cells and H/R cell models with different treatments were cultured in 96 well plates. After 24 h, Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, 10 μL) solution (Beyotime) was added to each well. After 1 h, the OD of each sample was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) to measure the cell viability.

Fe2+ and MDA levels

H9C2 cells and infarct region of cardiac tissue samples were collected from different groups. The Fe2+ levels in cells or tissues were explored by a Ferrous Iron Colorimetric Assay Kit (E-BC-K773-M, Elabscience, Wuhan, China). And, the malondialdehyde (MDA) levels were detected using a lipid oxidation detection kit (S0131S, Beyotime). The whole experimental processes were strictly operated according to the instructions of the kits.

Cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA)

The cells were collected and washed twice with PBS, then resuspended in lysis buffer.

The cells were crushed on ice with a glass homogenizer. Subsequently, the cell lysate was divided into two equal aliquots. One portion was used as a control, while the other was added with 100 μM SGN for 15 min at room temperature. The cell lysate was further evenly divided into two portions and heated at different temperatures for 3 min (37 °C, 50 °C, 55 °C, 60 °C, 65 °C, 70 °C, 75 °C and 80 °C). The target proteins were detected by western blot [22].

Western blot

SDS-PAGE gel was applied to split equivalent proteins extracted from cells or cardiac tissue cell homogenates. Then these protein samples were transferred onto PVDF membranes and blocked with defatted milk. The membranes were treated with specific primary antibodies: GPX4 (1:1,000, A1933, ABclonal, Wuhan, China), SLC7A11 (1:1,000, A2413, ABclonal), NLRP3 (1:500, 15,101, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), FTH1 (1:1000, A-AF1446a, AmyJet Scientific, Wuhan, China), cleaved-caspase-1 (1:500, 89,332, Cell Signaling Technology), GSDMD-N (1:1,000, ab215203, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) and GAPDH (1:1,000, 5174, Cell Signaling Technology) overnight at 4 °C. Then these samples were treated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG second antibody (1:2,000, A0208, Beyotime) at room temperature for 1 h. ECL and Image J were applied to visualize and analyze the protein bands, respectively.

ROS detection

Cellular ROS level was analysed by a fluorescent probe (DCFH-DA, S0033M, Beyotime). H9C2 cells were cultured in 6-well plates and subjected to different treatments, then in-cultured with DCFH-DA (10 μM) at 37 °C. After 30 min, each well was washed thrice with FBS-free medium. The signals were visualized by a fluorescence microscopy and analyzed by Image J software.

ELISA test

The levels of interleukin (IL) -1β (IL-1β) and IL-18 were explored through enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (H002 and H015, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Jiangsu, China) based on the manufacturer’s instruction. A microplate reader was used to obtain the data.

Establishing myocardial ischemia/reperfusion Model in vivo

C57BL/6 mice (male, 20-22 g, 6–8 weeks) were provided by SPF Biotechnology Company (Beijing, China). The mice were randomly divided into sham group, SGN group (10 mg/kg), MI/R group and MI/R + SGN (MI/R + 10 mg/kg SGN), with 6 mice in each group. Mice were pre-treated with SGN (10 mg/kg per day) or saline solution through intraperitoneal injection for 7 days [23]. MI/R models were established as previously reported [24]. In brief, the mice were anesthetized using isoflurane (1.5% in oxygen). The chest was disclosed on the part of left fourth rib and the pericardium was torn. The left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery was exposed and rapidly ligated for 30 min, followed by resume and reperfusion for 2 h (western blot analysis and ELISA test) or 24 h (HE staining, TUNEL assay, and cardiac injury markers) [25]. The sham (pretreated with saline solution) and the SGN groups (pretreated with SGN) were treated with the same operation without the ligation of the LAD. Mice were anesthetized using xylazine and euthanized following cervical dislocation, in accordance with institutional and national guidelines., and then blood and heart tissues were collected for subsequent assays. All experimental procedures were performed following the 3Rs principle (Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement) to ensure ethical animal research. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Qingdao Huangdao District Central Hospital, Shandong, China (Approval No.IRB# 0006–25-2R0). Measures were taken to minimize animal suffering, including the use of anesthesia and analgesia as appropriate.

TTC staining

Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining was applied to evaluate infarct size. The hearts were immediately placed into a −80 °C refrigerator. After 20 min, the frozen heart was cut horizontally into thick slices (2 mm). Then, these slices were incubated with 1% TTC at 37 °C. After 20 min, the stained sections were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and photoed by a Nikon digital camera. Pictures were analyzed using Image J software.

Detection of serum CK-MB, CTnT and LDH

Serum samples were collected from blood by centrifugation at 3500 × g for 5 min. Serum creatine kinase myocardial bound (CK-MB), cardiac troponin T (cTnT) and lactic dehydrogenase (LDH) activity were explored using the assay kits (Jiancheng Bioengineering, Nanjing, China) on the basis of the instructions.

Echocardiographic measurement

Cardiac function was assessed by transthoracic echocardiography based on a Visual Sonics Vevo3100 (Toronto, ON, Canada) Imaging System. After different treatments, mice were anesthetized using isoflurane and laid supine. The chamber diameters and left ventricular wall thickness were estimated using M-mode images. Echocardiographic assessment of left ventricular systolic function was evaluated by estimation of FS%, EF%, IVSd and IVSs by Vevo LAB software. All experiments were conducted by professionals who were unaware of the experimental groups.

Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining

The collected infarct region of heart tissues was immobilized in 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated and embedded in paraffin. The paraffin-embedded tissues were sliced into thin slices, deparaffinized and stained with HE (Solarbio, China). An optical microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was applied to observe the pathological changes of the myocardial tissues in stained sections. The degree of myocardial tissue damage was scored on a scale of 0 to 4: 0, no injury; 1, interstitial edema and localized necrosis (mild injury); 2, widespread myocardial cell swelling and necrosis (moderate injury); 3, necrosis with contraction bands, neutrophil infiltration, and compressed capillaries (severe injury); 4, diffuse necrosis with contraction bands, neutrophil infiltration, and compressed capillaries and hemorrhaging (highly severe injury) [26].

TUNEL

The paraffin section of infarct region of myocardial tissue was dewaxed and dehydrated, and treated with Proteinase K for 30 min at 37 °C. Then the section was treated with TUNEL reaction solution in the TUNEL kit (Absin Bioscience Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China) for 1.5 h at 37 °C away from light. The section was stained with DAPI in the dark and at room temperature for 30 min and sealed with anti-fluorescence quenching sealing agent, and was visualized through a microscope. To determine the degree of myocardial apoptosis, five fields were randomly selected from two sections in each group, and the apoptosis index (AI) was calculated using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 as follows: AI = number of apoptotic cells / total number of cells counted.

Statistical analysis

SPSS (version 21.0) was applied to accomplish the statistical analyses. All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), which was gained from at least three independent experiments. The difference between more than two groups was explored using the One-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. P < 0.05 represented that the result was significant difference.

Results

Salvigenin (SGN) alleviated ferroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells

To explore the pharmacology of Salvigenin (SGN, Fig. 1A) in the context of H9C2, we investigated the effects of SGN (0 μM, 6.25 μM, 12.5 μM, 25 μM, 50 μM, 100 μM, 200 μM) on H9C2 cells. The cell viability was significantly reduced only when SGN concentration reached 200 μM, indicating the low toxicity of SGN (Fig. 1B). After H/R induction, H9C2 cells viability was significantly reduced, while SGN could increase the reduced cell viability (50 μM and 100 μM, Fig. 1C). In H/R-induced H9C2 cells, the level of Fe2+ was increased, while it was decreased after SGN treatment. (Fig. 1D). The SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1 proteins were low-expressed in H9C2 cells with H/R induction. However, SGN treatment up-regulated SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1 protein levels in H/R-induced H9C2 cells (Fig. 1E). After H/R induction, MDA and ROS levels were increased in H9C2 cells, and they were decreased after SGN treatment (Fig. 1F–G). These results indicated that SGN could alleviate ferroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells.

SGN alleviated pyroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells

Considering the importance of pyroptosis, we detected the effect of SGN on pyroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells. NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N proteins were overexpressed in H/R-induced H9C2 cells, and these proteins were decreased after treatment with SGN (Fig. 2A–B). After H/R induction, IL-1β and IL-18 levels were elevated in H9C2 cells, and SGN decreased IL-1β and IL-18 levels in H/R-induced H9C2 cells (Fig. 2C–D). These findings revealed that SGN could alleviate pyroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells.

For ferroptosis, the levels of Fe2+ and ROS were significantly decreased in H/R-induced H9C2 cells after both SGN treatment and SGN + fer-1 treatment (Fig. 3A–C). For pyroptosis, both SGN and SGN + MCC950 reduced the expression of NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N proteins (Fig. 3D–E) and decreased the level IL-1β (Fig. 3F).

To further analyze whether the mechanism by which SGN regulates ferroptosis and pyroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells is related to the NLRP3 pathway, we overexpressed NLRP3 by transfecting NLRP3 overexpression vector (Fig. 4A). Compared with H/R-induced H9C2 cells treated with SGN (H/R + SGN), overexpression of NLRP3 increased the levels of NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N proteins (H/R + SGN + NLRP3, Fig. 4B–C). SGN-increased cell viability was decreased after transfection with NLRP3 overexpression vector (Fig. 4D). The reduced levels of Fe2+, ROS and IL-1β by SGN were also reversed by NLRP3 overexpression (Fig. 4E–H). We also have studied the effect of SGN on overexpressed NLRP3 alone without H/R model. Whether it was the Fe2+ and ROS levels or the IL-1β levels, there was no significant difference among the three groups (con, SGN and SGN + NLRP3, Supplementary Fig. 1). Together, these results demonstrated that the regulatory effect of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis of H/R-induced H9C2 cells could be reversed by NLRP3 overexpression. That is, the regulatory mechanism of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis is related to the NLRP3 pathway.

SGN inhibited myocardial injury in myocardial ischemia–reperfusion mice

To analyze the function of SGN in vivo, we constructed myocardial ischemia/reperfusion (MI/R) mouse model. Firstly, in order to select the most appropriate concentration of SGN, we analyzed the effects of 2.5 mg/kg, 5 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg SGN on the mouse model. Based on the results of several myocardial injury markers, TUNEL and HE, we selected 10 mg/kg SGN for the subsequent experiments (Supplementary Fig. 2) [8]. The infarct size of MI/R + SGN group was lower than MI/R group (Fig. 5A). Compared with the sham group, the serum CK-MB, CTnT and LDH levels were increased in MI/R group. The treatment of SGN reduced CK-MB, CTnT and LDH levels in the MI/R mice models (Fig. 5B–D). The heart rate (HR) and cardiomyocyte area were raised in the MI/R mice model, while it was lowered in MI/R + SGN mice (Fig. 5E–F). Compared with the sham group, the left ventricular systolic function was impaired in MI/R mice with decreased fractional shortening (FS)% and ejection fraction (EF)%. But they were elevated after SGN treatment (Supplementary Fig. 4A–C). Besides, the interventricular septum thickness in diastole (IVSd) and in systole (IVSs) were increased in the MI/R group compared with the sham group. But the adding of 10 mg/kg SGN in MI/R mice reduced IVSd and IVSs (Supplementary Fig. 4D, E). The apoptosis level of myocardial tissue was increased in MI/R group, and SGN treatment reduced it (Fig. 5G–H). HE staining indicated that the myocardial tissue of the sham group and SGN group mice was normal, while the myocardial fiber arrangement of mice was irregular, broken and dissolved, and inflammatory cells were infiltrated in MI/R group. The histopathological changes in cardiac of MI/R mouse model were improved after SGN treatment (Fig. 5H). All the above results showed no difference between the sham group and the SGN group, indicating that SGN alone was non-toxic to healthy mice. Our findings indicated that SGN inhibited myocardial injury in myocardial ischemia–reperfusion mice.

SGN reduced ferroptosis and pyroptosis in myocardial tissue of myocardial ischemia–reperfusion mice

In terms of ferroptosis, MDA and Fe2+ levels were elevated in MI/R group in comparison with the sham group, while they were reduced in MI/R + SGN group (Fig. 6A–B). In terms of pyroptosis, NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N protein were over-expressed in MI/R group compared with the sham group, but they were low-expressed after SGN treatment (Fig. 6C–D). On the other hand, IL-1β and IL-18 levels were increased in MI/R group, and they were decreased in MI/R + SGN group (Fig. 6E). In CETSA, the protein graph showed that the protein stability remained unchanged after treatment at different temperatures, indicating that there was no binding between SGN and NLRP3. This verified that SGN indirectly regulates NLRP3 (Supplementary Fig. 3). The above results indicated that SGN could also reduce ferroptosis and pyroptosis in myocardial tissue of MI/R mice model. In summary, SGN moderated ferroptosis and pyroptosis via inhibiting the NLRP3 pathway in myocardial ischemia–reperfusion models (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Natural products have broad application prospects in the treatment of cardiovascular diseases because of their high naturalness, non-toxic side effects and high safety [27]. Among them, flavonoid compounds have been extensively studied for the intervention and treatment of diseases including MI/RI. For example, luteolin, a natural flavone, has been shown to increase the expression of SERCA2a by up-regulating Sp1, thereby exerting protective effect on myocardium [28]. Yulangsan flavonoid (YLSF) reduced lipid peroxidation damage, regulated NOS activity and apoptosis gene expression, and thus played a role in protecting the heart from the effects of MI/RI [29]. Baicalin could alleviate myocardial injury and inflammation after I/R, and its mechanism of action was to inhibit the JAK/STAT pathway [30]. In this paper, we detected that SGN had low toxicity and could alleviate ferroptosis and pyroptosis both in vitro and in vivo by regulating the NLRP3 pathway.

Firstly, we analyzed the effect of SGN on ferroptosis by exploring the levels of Fe2+, ROS and MDA, as well as the ferroptosis regulatory proteins SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1. The accumulation of large amounts of Fe2+ is one of the signs of ferroptosis process, which can specifically increase oxidative stress levels [31]. However, excessive accumulation of Fe2+ can lead to iron-induced Fenton reaction, resulting in toxic reactive oxygen species (ROS) and lipid peroxidation. Therefore, ROS accumulation is also one of the key driving factors for ferroptosis [32]. MDA is produced by lipid peroxidation, and its elevated level is also one of the indicators of ferroptosis [33]. Multiple proteins, including SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1, are closely related to the occurrence of ferroptosis. Solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) is a specific amino acid transporter and one of the most critical upstream regulatory factors for ferroptosis. Inhibition of SLC7A11 expression can limit cysteine uptake and ultimately induce cell ferroptosis, which is an effective target for ferroptosis [34]. The glutathione peroxidases (GPXs) family plays a critical part in ferroptosis, among which GPX4 plays a more important role in ferroptosis and is considered to be the main inhibitory protein of it [35]. Ferritin heavy chain 1 (FTH1) is also a type of specific ferroptosis suppressor protein. Due to the recognition of the important relationship between ferroptosis and MI/RI, many flavonoid compounds have been studied to control the degree of myocardial injury by inhibiting the ferroptosis process, such as Baicalin [36] and (-)-Epicatechin (EPI) [37]. In our study, we demonstrated that Fe2+ levels and MDA levels were elevated in both H/R-induced H9C2 cells and MI/R mouse models. SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1 proteins were down-regulated, and ROS levels were increased in cells model. The aforesaid findings demonstrated the appearance of ferroptosis. However, SGN observably reduced the levels of Fe2+ and MDA in both cell models and tissues of mice models. It also overexpressed SLC7A11, GPX4 and FTH1, and reduced ROS levels in H/R-induced H9C2 cells. This suggested that SGN could inhibit ferroptosis both in vivo and in vitro.

Caspase-1, the first Caspase discovered in mammals, is the most important member of the Caspase family during pyroptosis and is also the core protein of pyroptosis [38]. Caspase-1 is a key factor in cleaving GSDMD in the typical inflammatory-induced pyroptosis. Gasdermin D (GSDMD) belongs to the GSDM family and is a key effector of pyroptosis. After being cleaved by activated-Caspase-1 protein, GSDMD can form a cytotoxic GSDMD-N terminal, which targets the cell membrane to form molecular pores and finally initiates the pyroptosis process [39]. A large amount of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin (IL) -1β and IL-18, are also released through the GSDMD pore, triggering inflammatory responses. IL-1β is primarily a product of immune cells and plays an important role in regulating inflammatory and immune responses [40]. IL-18 is another strongly pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine, mainly derived from macrophages and dendritic cells [41]. The precursors of IL-18 and IL-1β are activated under the action of inflammasome and then participate in pyroptosis [42]. More and more natural products have been found to control MI/RI by regulating the pyroptosis process [43]. Multiple flavonoids have been found to regulate the cell pyroptosis process in MI/RI, such as pinocembrin [44] and kaempferol [45]. In this study, we found that SGN significantly reduced the levels of cleaved caspase-1, GSDMD-N, IL-1 β, and IL-18 both in cell and in mouse models. In addition, we also found that SGN treatment reduced serum CK-MB, CTnT and LDH levels, cardiac injury markers, increased EF and FS levels, and reduced apoptosis rate in the myocardial tissue in MI/R mouse models. Our results supported our hypothesis that SGN alleviated pyroptosis and myocardial injury in MI/RI.

NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) plays a crucial role in the inflammatory response as a key inflammasome [46]. NLRP3 inflammasome is a multiprotein complex that can stimulate the release of active IL-1β and IL-18, leading to a series of inflammatory responses and pyroptosis [47]. The NLRP3 signaling pathway is closely related to the occurrence and development of many diseases and has become a research target for the treatment of many inflammatory diseases, such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), myocarditis and so on [48]. Pyroptosis mediated by NLRP3 activation can exacerbate MI/RI [49]. Inhibition of NLRP3 activation can inhibit pyroptosis, thereby alleviating MI/RI [50]. The overexpression of NLRP3 also plays a crucial role in the regulation of ferroptosis. In cardiac diseases, the deficiency of NLRP3 inflammasome can alleviate the damage to myocardial cells by inhibiting ferroptosis, such as diabetic cardiomyopathy [51]. Multiple flavonoids have been confirmed to have therapeutic effects on MI/RI through the NLRP3 pathway, including luteolin [52] and tilianin [53]. In the current study, we indicated that SGN treatment down-regulated NLRP3 protein in cells and myocardial tissue. Transfection of NLRP3 overexpression vector reversed the inhibition of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis in H/R-induced H9C2 cells. In summary, these results indicated that the inhibitory mechanism of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis is achieved by regulating the NLRP3 pathway. As far as we know, there has been no research report on the effect of SGN on MI/RI. Many natural products have only analyzed their effects on MI/RI, but have not deeply explored their possible mechanisms of action.

In this study, we analyzed the effects of SGN on two forms of cell death, namely ferroptosis and pyroptosis, in MI/RI, as well as the relationship between its mechanism and the NLRP3 pathway. Of course, there are some limitations to our study. The pathogenesis of MI/RI is a very complex process involving multiple biological cellular processes. We only detected the inhibition of SGN on ferroptosis and pyroptosis. Therefore, subsequent studies need to study the effect of SGN on apoptosis, autophagy, necroptosis, intracellular calcium overload, energy metabolism disorder and other important pathways such as PI3K/AKT, AMPK, NF-κB pathway and so on. Although this time point was determined based on relevant reference [21], we had to admit that it is indeed too singular. In future related research, we will choose multiple time points to explore the dynamic properties of the cell death process. The MI/RI mouse model cannot replicate the common long-term ischemic conditions in clinical environments. Also, the pharmacokinetics and toxicity of SGN were not investigated. The effects of SGN on apoptosis, autophagy, or necroptosis processes in MI/RI has not been explored yet. We will improve these parts in the future research. We did not investigate the effect of Salvigenn on ROS in mitochondria. In future studies, we will conduct further research on this aspect. This study only found that SGN has an impact on MI/RI and the NLRP3 pathway. Although the experimental design of this study is similar to that of previous study [54], molecular docking analysis or co-immunolocalization experiments are still needed to determine whether the traditional Chinese herbs monomer SGN interacts with NLRP3. There is a significant synergistic effect between pyroptosis and ferroptosis. The activation of NLRP3 inflammasome, as an upstream event, can create favorable conditions for iron death by exacerbating oxidative stress and altering iron metabolism [51]. Therefore, it is entirely possible for SGN to indirectly and partially inhibit subsequent ferroptosis by inhibiting NLRP3. They are not two completely independent parallel pathways, but rather key nodes in the intertwined and amplified "death network". In subsequent related research, we will further investigate the more detailed relationship between them.

Conclusion

This current study provided evidence that SGN can inhibit myocardial injury in mice models. The potential mechanism might be to inhibit the ferroptosis and pyroptosis via regulating the NLRP3 pathway. These results indicated potential applications of SGN in the intervention of MI/RI.

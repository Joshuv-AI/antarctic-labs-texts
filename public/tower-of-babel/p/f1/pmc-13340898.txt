Concordance of the Mangled Digit Severity Score (MDSS) with Surgical Decision-Making and Outcomes in Traumatic Digit and Partial Hand Amputation Injuries
Authors: Diala Burjak, Brooke Allen, Keith Kuo, Neel Bhagat, Henning De May, Shaun Mendenhall, Christopher Goodenough
Published: 2026

Purpose Statement: The Mangled Digit Severity Score (MDSS) is a 14-factor scoring system designed to guide management of traumatic digital injuries, with scores ≥35 supporting amputation. By providing an objective framework, MDSS has the potential to save resources, reduce unsuccessful salvage attempts, and improve patient care. However, external validation is limited. We sought to evaluate the validity of MDSS in our institutional cohort, focusing on concordance between score recommendations, initial surgical intervention, and final digit outcomes.

Methods and Materials: We retrospectively reviewed our digit and partial hand amputation database (2014–2024), which included 410 patients, treated by hand fellowship trained surgeons at a level 1 trauma center. Inclusion criteria were traumatic digital injuries with complete data for all 14 MDSS factors. Patients with missing MDSS variables or non-traumatic etiologies were excluded.

Concordance between MDSS recommendations, initial intervention (salvage vs. amputation), and final outcome (digit survival vs. amputation) was measured with Cohen’s kappa. Logistic regression model assessed associations between MDSS recommendations and outcomes. Dominant-hand effects were evaluated using a two-sample z test for proportions. A p-value of <0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Summary of Results: One hundred two digits from 35 patients met inclusion criteria. The mean age was 44.1 (range 19–69) and 83.3% were male. The median MDSS was 30 (IQR 22–37; range –1–51). Final outcomes included 64 amputations (62.7%) and 38 salvages (37.3%). MDSS recommended amputation in 30 cases (29.7%), salvage in 36 (35.6%), and less predictable in 35 (34.7%). Less predictable digits outcomes were 68.6% (24/35) amputation and 31.4% (11/35) salvage. Of those 30 recommended amputations, 21 were correct predictions of final outcome as they failed salvage attempts.

Using Cohen’s kappa, concordance between MDSS recommendations and initial intervention showed expected agreement of 33.7% versus an observed 67.7%, yielding moderate agreement (κ=0.51, p<0.001). For final outcomes, expected agreement was 31.9% versus an observed 42.6%, with only slight agreement (κ=0.16, p=0.003).

The MDSS discriminated amputation from salvage with AUC 0.69 (95% CI 0.59–0.79). Each 1-point increase in MDSS was associated with 7% higher odds of amputation (OR 1.07, 95% CI 1.03–1.12, p=0.001).

Among digits that ultimately underwent amputation, dominant-hand injuries had higher-but not significant-rates of failed salvage (28.6% vs. 18.6%, difference –9.97%, 95% CI –32.5 to 12.6; p=0.365).

Conclusions: MDSS showed moderate concordance with initial decisions and slight agreement with long-term outcomes. It may help avoid futile salvage attempts and reduce unnecessary operations. However, selection bias is likely, as the database mainly included severe injuries with more amputations, limiting generalizability.

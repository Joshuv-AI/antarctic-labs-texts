The association of hospitalist medical procedure service with operational efficiency at an academic medical center
Authors: Brett R Curtis, Shuo Tian, Sachita Shrestha, Trevor Denton, Blake Haller, Jonathan Sebolt, Michael Adams, Stephanie P Taylor, David Paje
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe examined the impact of a hospital medicine medical procedure service (MPS) on hospital length of stay (LOS), postprocedure LOS, and completion of procedures on weekends. We included 4952 patients admitted to our large academic hospital between July 1, 2021 and July 31, 2023 who underwent thoracentesis, paracentesis, or lumbar puncture (LP). MPS performed 30% (1499) of these procedures. After adjusting for age, sex, body mass index, Charlson comorbidity score, and procedure type, procedure performance by MPS was associated with a shorter total hospital LOS (incidence rate ratio [IRR]: 0.93; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.87–0.99) and postprocedure LOS (IRR: 0.82; 95% CI: 0.76–0.88). Also, MPS‐performed procedures were twice as likely to occur on weekends compared to non‐MPS‐performed procedures (odds ratio [OR]: 2.05; 95% CI: 1.75–2.41). These findings support the beneficial impact of MPS on operational efficiency, an important outcome for both patients and hospitals.

INTRODUCTION

Hospital medicine programs are increasingly implementing a medical procedure service (MPS) to support provider workloads and expedite completion of common medical procedures, including thoracentesis, paracentesis, and lumbar puncture (LP). 1 , 2 , 3 MPS can help reduce the workload of interventional radiologists and other proceduralists, freeing them to perform more specialized or complex interventions. 4 Additionally, the performance of MPS procedures at bedside can enhance patient experience and provide positive educational experiences to trainees. 5 , 6 , 7 Finally, MPS‐performed procedures have been shown to be safe and high‐quality. 1 , 6

However, the impact of MPS on operational efficiency, including hospital length of stay (LOS) and off‐hours procedure availability (e.g., the weekend), is not fully understood. To address this, we examined differences in weekend procedural availability, total hospital LOS, and postprocedure LOS for MPS versus non‐MPS performed procedures.

METHODS

We conducted a retrospective cohort study of hospitalized patients who underwent paracentesis, thoracentesis, or LP while admitted to the University of Michigan Hospital between July 1, 2021, when the Division of Hospital Medicine launched its MPS, and July 31, 2023. MPS is staffed by hospitalist faculty physicians and advanced practice professionals (APP). The service is available year‐round from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. local time every day of the week. We classified patients based on whether the service performing the procedure was MPS versus non‐MPS (which included interventional radiology, other consulting services, and primary admitting services). Patient demographic and clinical information were captured from the electronic medical record (EMR). The primary outcome was total hospital LOS, defined as the time between the initial inpatient admission order and hospital discharge order. Secondary outcomes were postprocedure LOS (i.e., time between procedure completion and hospital discharge) and the performance of procedures on weekend days (Saturday or Sunday).

We compared characteristics of patients in the MPS group to those in the non‐MPS group using the Wilcoxon rank sum test, Fisher's exact test, and Pearson's chi‐squared test, where appropriate. We used negative binomial regression analyses to examine the relationship between the service performing the procedure (MPS vs. non‐MPS) and the outcomes of total hospital LOS and postprocedure LOS while adjusting for variation in age, sex, body mass index (BMI), Charlson Comorbidity Index score, 8 procedure type, and procedure performance on weekend. The covariates were selected as they were conceptually related, readily available, and could be efficiently extracted from our EMR. To assess the effect of the service performing the procedure and the likelihood of procedure performance on weekends and of discharge the same weekend of procedure performance, we used logistic regression while accounting for age, sex, BMI, Charlson Comorbidity Index score, 8 and procedure type. Non‐MPS‐performed procedures served as the reference in these regression analyses. All p‐values less than .05 were considered significant. Data were analyzed using Rstudio software version 2023.06.2.

RESULTS

Of the 4952 procedures completed during the study period, 1499 (30%) were performed by MPS, including 456 (30%) thoracenteses, 859 (57%) paracenteses, and 184 (12%) LPs. Characteristics and outcomes of MPS and non‐MPS‐performed procedures are shown in Table 1. Compared to non‐MPS‐performed procedures, a greater proportion of MPS‐performed procedures were thoracentesis (30% vs. 20%, p < .001) and paracentesis (57% vs. 48%, p < .001). MPS performed procedures on patients who had higher Charlson comorbidity burden compared to recipients of non‐MPS‐performed procedures (12 vs. 8, p < .001).

In unadjusted comparisons, patients with procedures performed by MPS had longer total hospital LOS compared to those with non‐MPS‐performed procedures (212 vs. 192 h). However, after adjusting for selected covariates (age, sex, BMI, Charlson Comorbidity Index, procedure type), we found that having a procedure performed by MPS was associated with shorter total hospital LOS (incidence rate ratio [IRR]: 0.93; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.87–0.99) and shorter postprocedure LOS (IRR: 0.82; 95% CI: 0.76–0.88) (Table 2). When analyzed by procedure type (Supporting Information S1: Tables S3 and S4), procedure performance by MPS was associated with shorter total hospital LOS for thoracentesis (IRR: 0.88; 95% CI: 0.79–0.99), and shorter postprocedure LOS for thoracentesis (IRR: 0.77; 95% CI: 0.68–0.88) and paracentesis (IRR: 0.80; 95% CI: 0.72–0.88). Conversely, we did not detect an association between the type of service performing LP and LOS outcomes (Supporting Information S1: Table S5).

Of 875 weekend procedures, 371 (42%) were performed by MPS and 504 (58%) by non‐MPS. However, MPS‐performed procedures were more likely to occur on weekends compared to non‐MPS‐performed procedures (25% vs. 15%; odds ratio [OR]: 2.05; 95% CI: 1.75–2.41). Patients who underwent procedures by MPS versus non‐MPS were also more likely to be discharged on the same weekend of procedure performance (4% vs. 1%; OR: 5.96; 95% CI: 3.67–9.99).

DISCUSSION

In this single‐site study at a large academic medical center, we found that performance of common medical procedures by a hospital medicine MPS is associated with about 7% reduction in total hospital LOS, nearly 20% reduction in postprocedure LOS, and twice the odds of discharging patients on the same weekend the procedure is completed. The LOS benefits were particularly observed in patients who underwent thoracentesis and paracentesis. While our study included thoracentesis, paracentesis, and LP, a recent single‐site study limited to paracentesis also showed shorter total LOS when performed by a hospitalist MPS compared to other services. 8

There are several ways a hospitalist MPS can lead to a reduction in LOS. First, the availability of MPS on weekends decreases patients' wait time for procedures, which would otherwise be performed on weekdays. 9 , 10 Weekday procedures, especially those referred to radiology, often experience delays because of caseload volume, logistical challenges, and prioritization of emergent procedures. Indeed, we found a higher proportion of patients was discharged on the same weekend of procedure completion when performed by MPS versus non‐MPS. Second, MPS providers may employ evidence‐based, high‐value approaches that facilitate timely care, such as not delaying paracentesis to check for coagulopathy or thrombocytopenia in patients with cirrhosis. 11 Third, prompt acquisition of diagnostic specimens for time‐sensitive conditions, such as spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, could expedite treatment decision and initiation, thus providing patients a head start to achieve clinical stability and readiness for discharge. This is supported by our finding of shorter postprocedure LOS for patients with MPS compared to non‐MPS‐performed procedures, and is consistent with prior reports of lower LOS associated with early or expedited paracentesis. 12 , 13

Our MPS team performed three of every 10 procedures overall, and about four of every 10 weekend procedures. In addition, MPS performed procedures were twice as likely to occur on a weekend compared to non‐MPS procedures. These findings highlight the value of this hospitalist‐run service in supporting the capacity to provide common medical procedures on weekends when specialized procedure services, such as interventional radiology, are not usually available. While we were not able to capture information on the urgency of procedures or acuity of patients, it is notable that patients who underwent procedures performed by MPS had more comorbidities. These patients would have been expected to have longer LOS. 14 Instead, we observed a reduction in total LOS, which was likely partly driven by the shorter postprocedure LOS and the increased likelihood of discharge on the same weekend of procedure performance. Future studies should elucidate the effect of specific MPS practices in facilitating periprocedural evaluation and treatment.

Our study has limitations. Since our data came from a single academic center, our findings may not readily apply to institutions with different procedure volume, proceduralist availability, or practice culture. Also, while we thoughtfully selected covariates based on conceptual or known associations, we could not account for unknown or unmeasured confounders, including other clinical characteristics or laboratory values that could affect the study outcomes (e.g., use of anticoagulants, platelet count, international normalized ratio, etc.).

However, our study offers a unique perspective as we considered all three common medical procedures performed by MPS. Our findings complement results from prior studies that evaluated single procedures. 9 , 15 We also evaluated outcomes that are important to both patients and health systems, and demonstrated an appraisal of a hospitalist MPS program using measures of operational efficiency.

CONCLUSION

In this single‐site study, a hospitalist‐run MPS that is available all days of the week is associated with shorter postprocedure and total hospital lengths of stay. Further studies are necessary to evaluate clinical outcomes and the net economic value of an MPS program.

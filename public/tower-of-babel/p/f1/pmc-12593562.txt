Aggressive Recurrent Tumoral Calcinosis in a Patient With Kidney Failure
Authors: Luis Ng, Mohamed G. Atta
Published: 2025

A 42-year-old male with a history of tertiary hyperparathyroidism, parathyroidectomy, and kidney failure with replacement therapy presented for excision of a right shoulder mass. (Fig 1) This mass required 2 prior excisions in the past 2 years and had infectious complications. The pathology of the mass showed grungy calcifications, amorphous crystals, and giant cells, compatible with tumoral calcinosis (TC). The TC is a dramatic form of extraosseous calcification with prominent calcium and phosphorus deposits in soft tissues; it is considered benign but can behave aggressively.1,2 Calcification is driven by high calcium phosphorus products with hyperphosphatemia as the main driver.1 This case of TC is secondary given the presence of abnormal bone and mineral metabolism typical of patients with kidney failure. Idiopathic TC would not have an underlying disease, and other dystrophic calcifications are usually preceded by trauma.2 Treatment is usually conservative and consists of bone and mineral metabolism control. In this case, this patient’s phosphorus remained uncontrolled despite medical therapy and parathyroidectomy. Excision is indicated in tumors with mass effects and functional limitations.2 There is no standard of imaging for TC, and it usually requires multimodal imaging. magnetic resonance imaging is helpful for soft tissue mass evaluation.

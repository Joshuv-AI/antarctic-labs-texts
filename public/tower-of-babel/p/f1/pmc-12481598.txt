Lipid Management in the Post-Statin Era
Authors: Heather A Powell, LeAnne Bloedon
Published: 2025

We read with interest the Expert Panel review by Spitz et al,1 which provided a contemporary and detailed graphical approach to lipid management beyond statins through a comparison of current published guidelines for lipid management, therapeutic targets, and screening. Among the many useful resources provided by the authors, Table 1 captures relevant information for clinicians to use in differentiating United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved lipid-lowering therapies. We would like to raise a point of clarification regarding the accuracy of the FDA indications listed for bempedoic acid within that table. Table 1 lists outdated FDA indications for bempedoic acid: “1) established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease; 2) adjunct therapy for primary hyperlipidemia.” Additionally, the common usage/notes column notes bempedoic is “often used in statin-intolerant individuals, although no FDA indication.” We would like to note in March of 2024, the United States Prescribing Information for bempedoic acid was updated by the FDA to include the following:2

“NEXLETOL, an adenosine triphosphate-citrate lyase inhibitor, is indicated:

•To reduce the risk of myocardial infarction and coronary revascularization in adults who are unable to take recommended statin therapy (including those not taking a statin) with:○Established cardiovascular disease (CVD), or○A high risk for a CVD event but without established CVD.

To reduce the risk of myocardial infarction and coronary revascularization in adults who are unable to take recommended statin therapy (including those not taking a statin) with:○Established cardiovascular disease (CVD), or○A high risk for a CVD event but without established CVD.

○Established cardiovascular disease (CVD), or

Established cardiovascular disease (CVD), or

○A high risk for a CVD event but without established CVD.

A high risk for a CVD event but without established CVD.

•As an adjunct to diet, in combination with other low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C)-lowering therapies, or alone when concomitant LDL-C-lowering therapy is not possible, to reduce LDL-C in adults with primary hyperlipidemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia.”

As an adjunct to diet, in combination with other low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C)-lowering therapies, or alone when concomitant LDL-C-lowering therapy is not possible, to reduce LDL-C in adults with primary hyperlipidemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia.”

We kindly request a correction be made to the table to ensure readers have accurate information regarding the FDA-approved indications for bempedoic acid.

A mobile app (mWITH ME) for family caregivers of persons living with Alzheimer’s disease: development and initial evaluation
Authors: Anni Yang, Bin Hu, Menlong Ye, Xi Zhang, Weihua Yu, Jun Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe rising global aging population has increased the prevalence of Alzheimer’s disease (AD), a condition with limited treatment options, making non-pharmacological interventions essential. While mobile health apps are increasingly common, there is currently a lack of theory-based, user-centered research to support the development of mobile health apps for family caregivers of persons living with AD.ObjectiveThis study aimed to design a theory-based and user-centered mobile application that supports family caregivers by leveraging non-pharmacological interventions to aid in the management and potential slowing of AD progression, with the ultimate goals of improving care quality and reducing their burden.MethodsThe study employed a three-phase methodology: (1) Establishing a theoretical framework based on Narrative Evidence-Based Medicine and Maslow’s hierarchy of needs; (2) Conducting semi-structured interviews with target users (n = 18), with transcripts analyzed thematically to inform the app’s development by an interdisciplinary team; (3) Performing a preliminary evaluation with participants (n = 20) who used the app for three weeks, after which a questionnaire assessed its short-term impact and user-friendliness.ResultsThematic analysis yielded five key themes: Content, Platform, Functionality, Interactivity, and Interface. The mWITH ME app consists of four modules: educational support, professional support, peer support, and health monitoring. In the preliminary evaluation, 75% (n = 15) of participants reported that using the app reduced their daily caregiving burden, 80% (n = 16) expressed satisfaction with the application, and 70% (n = 14) acknowledged its role in improving care quality. The modules for Educational, Professional, and Peer Support received particularly positive feedback, which aligns with the app’s underlying theoretical framework of Narrative Evidence-Based Medicine and Maslow’s hierarchy of needs. Nonetheless, the Health Monitoring module was identified as requiring further enhancements to better meet user expectations.ConclusionsThe mWITH ME app demonstrates feasibility in supporting family caregivers by addressing their professional, educational, and emotional needs. Future work should focus on developing features for mood and fatigue monitoring, while long-term studies are needed to evaluate its integration into standard care pathways and its potential to improve the quality of life for both the caregivers and persons living with AD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12911-026-03456-7.

Background

Background of Alzheimer’s disease

Alzheimer’s disease (AD), a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline and loss of functional independence [1, 2], posing a growing global health challenge. Its prevalence continues to rise, with the number of dementia cases worldwide expected to increase from 50 million to 152 million by 2050 [3, 4]. In the absence of a cure, comprehensive management strategies are essential. International guidelines strongly recommend non-pharmacological therapies (NPT) and structured care models [5–7], which enable many patients to live for years at home or in care facilities with appropriate support [8, 9]. However, caregivers for persons living with AD are typically non-professional family members who face sustained, long-term caregiving pressure.

Challenges faced by caregivers

Research into the pressures faced by family caregivers of persons living with AD [10, 11] dates back to the early twentieth century [12, 13]. As care demands escalate progressively throughout the disease stages [14], co-residence with the person living with AD often becomes necessary in the advanced phases. The extensive impact of long-term care for this complex condition on families is well-documented [15].

As the disease advances, families confront numerous risks, including financial strain due to early retirement or reduced paid work hours [16, 17]; diminished quality of life, manifested as depressive symptoms, burden, and stress [18]; physical health issues; and an increased risk of suicidal ideation [19, 20] and even incident dementia among caregivers themselves [21]. The distinct nature of caregiver burden in AD stems from the fact that family members serve as non-professional caregivers [20]. Caring for a person with progressive memory loss, declining performance of activities of daily living, and diminished social capacities generates a unique constellation of psychological, physical, social, and economic pressures. These pressures are not commonly seen in other disease contexts [22].

Traditional in-person training and support systems, hampered by limited accessibility and resources, struggle to achieve broad reach. This limitation has prompted researchers to turn to digital platforms for delivering sustained and personalized assistance to family caregivers [23–27]. Mobile health (mHealth) represents one of the most prevalent and direct manifestations of digital platforms in serving end users.

The development potential of mobile applications

In recent years, mHealth applications have emerged as a focal point of research and innovation in digital health. Current studies and practices demonstrate that these apps have evolved beyond their initial role as information portals and basic tracking tools. A significant body of research now focuses on leveraging mHealth applications to manage chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular diseases), as well as mental health disorders like anxiety and depression [28, 29]. A substantial increase in randomized controlled trials and systematic reviews has provided growing evidence-based support for the effectiveness of mHealth applications in improving clinical outcomes (e.g., glycemic and blood pressure control), enhancing patient adherence, and promoting quality of life.

In mHealth research, there is active exploration of integrating artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and adaptive technologies [30]. For instance, machine learning algorithms are increasingly being applied to analyze user-generated data [31], enabling the prediction of health risks and the delivery of personalized interventions. However, challenges persist in user engagement, data privacy, clinical integration, and regulatory frameworks. Despite these hurdles, the future potential of mHealth remains considerable, with three key directions emerging: advancing toward personalized precision interventions, integrating into comprehensive digital health ecosystems, and serving as infrastructure for prospective health research. Consequently, mHealth applications are currently in a critical transitional phase, moving from demonstrating efficacy to optimizing artificial intelligence, personalization, and systemic integration.

Research gap and innovative directions

While numerous studies have investigated caregiver burden and stress among family caregivers of persons living with AD [32, 33], research on intervention methods and digital support remains limited [34, 35]. A significant gap remains between the needs of users and the functionalities provided by existing mobile applications [36–39].

Persons living with AD experience a wide spectrum of symptoms—including memory impairment, executive dysfunction, language difficulties, visuospatial decline, and emotional and personality changes [40–42]—which necessitates comprehensive and well-rounded support in health management applications. Moreover, in addition to addressing functional challenges, greater attention must be given to the emotional and stress-related needs of caregivers [13–15]. Current apps often fail to provide integrated solutions that support multiple competencies, leaving some caregiver needs unmet.

Previous studies of mobile applications for family caregivers [43] have also highlighted practical challenges in their implementation, such as design complexity, caregivers’ age and technology acceptance levels, as well as persistent privacy and data security concerns [39].

Aims

Therefore, there is a clear need for novel and effective interventions to improve the quality of life for both patients and their family caregivers. This study aims to design a mobile application that assists family caregivers in providing care to individuals with AD, enhances care quality, and alleviates caregiver burden, followed by a preliminary evaluation of its feasibility and impact.

Methods

The mWITH ME app was developed through a three-stage process: (1) establishing a theoretical foundation; (2) implementing user-centered design via qualitative research; and (3) evaluating the application with target users (n = 20) (Fig. 1).

Stage 1: theory-informed development

Theoretical foundations play a crucial role in enhancing the effectiveness of health behavior interventions [44]. The development of the mWITH ME app was guided by Narrative Evidence-Based Medicine (NEBM) theory [45], which recognizes illness experiences [12] and clinical practice as two complementary aspects of healthcare [46]. Narrative Medicine [47] is characterized by three core mechanisms: humanistic orientation, individual focus, and personalized evidence, whereas Evidence-Based Medicine(EBM) [48] emphasizes technical rigor, population focus, and the best available scientific evidence. Narrative Medicine serves not merely as a tool for promoting humanism and improving patient-clinician relationships but also as a practical clinical methodology that enhances diagnostic accuracy [46], improves treatment adherence, and facilitates patient-clinician shared decision-making. This approach fosters empathy among patients, families, and healthcare providers, ultimately improving the quality of care.

Through multidisciplinary collaboration and innovative treatment pathways, the Narrative Evidence-Based framework supports holistic healing for both users and persons living with AD across physical, psychological, and social dimensions. Figure 2 illustrates the relationship between mWITH ME and shared decision-making within this conceptual framework.

The application of Narrative Evidence-Based theory directly informed the app’s design strategy. Within this theoretical model (Fig. 2), the Evidence-Based Medicine component incorporates proposed interventions such as physical activity, cognitive training, and health monitoring, which provide robust medical evidence for follow-up consultations. Furthermore, the design was inspired by the principles of narrative medicine [49]. It aimed to fully leverage individuals’ narrative capacity to share their caregiving experiences, seek emotional resonance and social support, and to some extent, help conserve medical resources while promoting effective communication among doctors, patients, and caregivers [50, 51]. Consequently, engagement with the mWITH ME application may better support family caregivers in implementing interventions and accessing higher-quality healthcare services.

Furthermore, the development of the mWITH ME app was informed by Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, which categorizes human requirements into five levels: physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem, and self-actualization [52–54]. Stage 2: user-centered design through qualitative research

The application was developed following the principles of user-centered design. Its core functionalities and content framework were established based on the clinical expertise and recommendations from neurologists and senior nursing staff at a university-affiliated hospital in China, ensuring clinical relevance and content validity. A qualitative research approach was adopted to engage target users during the initial design phase [55, 56]. This methodology is crucial as it provides valuable insights for the design and development of the mWITH ME application. Through semi-structured interviews, we analyzed family caregivers’ needs and preferences regarding the app’s content, functionality, and user-friendliness. The findings from this qualitative phase may inform the formulation of intervention strategies [57] while enhancing their acceptability and effectiveness.

Sample and setting

We purposively recruited 18 family caregivers of persons living with AD from both the geriatrics and neurology departments across two campuses of a university-affiliated hospital. This medical institution operates a total capacity of 2,000 inpatient beds, with its neurology department designated as a provincial-level key specialty. The inclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (a) age over 18 years; (b) provision of an average of at least 6 h of daily care; and (c) if multiple family members shared caregiving responsibilities, we selected the individual who had the longest care duration, the most frequent contact with persons living with AD, and played the most significant role in the overall care process.

Data collection

Data collection was conducted between September and December 2024, following approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Jiangnan University (Reference Number: JNU202412RB016). A purposive sampling strategy was employed for participant recruitment [55]. A research assistant approached family members of persons living with AD during outpatient visits. Participants were informed of the study’s purpose and provided with relevant information sheets. Participants were informed of the study purpose and provided with relevant information sheets. Following initial contact, researchers conducted telephone interviews to determine their interest and willingness to participate. Written informed consent was obtained, and demographic data were collected from all participants. All individual interviews were carried out by a trained and qualified research assistant competent in conducting qualitative face-to-face interviews to ensure consistency throughout the data collection process [55].

The study employed semi-structured interviews, a technique that ensured the collection of essential information while allowing in-depth exploration of participants’ perspectives and experiences [58]. A set of open-ended questions (see Text Box 1) was prepared in advance, developed within the framework of a qualitative semi-structured interview guide [59]. These questions were designed to comprehensively explore family caregivers’ perceptions, needs, and caregiving pressures regarding health management applications for assisting care.

During the interviews, the sequence and content of questions were flexibly adjusted based on participants’ responses. All interviews were scheduled either before or after planned follow-up appointments at the outpatient clinic and were conducted in private rooms for participants’ convenience [55]. Each interview lasted 20 to 40 min, was audio-recorded, and supplemented with field notes. Participants received an honorarium of 150 CNY (approximately 21 USD) to cover transportation costs and compensate for their time.

Data analysis

The sociodemographic characteristics of participants were summarized using descriptive statistics, which were performed with IBM SPSS Statistics (v.26) [60]. All interviews were audio-recorded with informed consent and subsequently transcribed verbatim by members of the research team. The transcribed texts were imported into the qualitative data analysis software NVivo (v.12) for management and coding [61]. Subsequently, we adopted the approach by Braun and Clarke for thematic analysis [62]. This analytical process followed six recommended steps [62], serving as a flexible strategy that included: (1) familiarization with the data through repeated reading of transcripts; (2) systematic identification and labeling of meaningful units for initial coding; (3) identifying potential themes by examining patterns across initial codes; (4) reviewing and refining themes by reorganizing data into coherent sub-themes; (5) defining and naming final key themes; and (6) producing the analytical report. This methodology was selected for its systematic yet flexible nature, enabling in-depth exploration of rich qualitative datasets while effectively identifying and analyzing recurrent patterns [62]. An inductive approach was employed to connect semantically identified themes closely with the data. Continuous comparative analysis was conducted to iteratively examine variations in theme emergence across different participants [63]. During simultaneous analysis by two research team members, interviews were terminated after the 15th participant as thematic saturation was reached, with no new themes emerging.

Methodological rigor

Methodological rigor was established in this study by ensuring credibility, dependability, confirmability, and transferability [64]. To enhance credibility, prolonged engagement and member checking were employed, involving repeated reviews of audio recordings, transcripts, and field notes to verify data accuracy. Additionally, transcripts were returned to participants for validation of informational authenticity [65]. Dependability was achieved through an audit process, where audit trails—documented via reflective memos and codebooks—were maintained to trace potential biases and assumptions [66]. Core researchers participated throughout data analysis to ensure procedural transparency. Two research members (ANY and XZ) independently performed initial coding, theme development, review, and definition in a multi-stage process [62], jointly verifying the accuracy and appropriateness of the procedures. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion until consensus was reached, thereby validating the findings. To strengthen transferability, the research team provided comprehensive demographic and neurological descriptions and incorporated relevant perspectives from participants [64].

Stage 3: user evaluation of mWITH ME

After designing the application, we re-recruited participants from the hospital for the preliminary evaluation, with the following criteria: having a family member who is a person living with AD, serving as the primary caregiver for an extended period, possessing the ability to use the application and basic literacy skills, and being free from cognitive impairments. A total of 20 voluntary and cooperative participants evaluated the application. Upon completing the full three-week evaluation and submitting the final questionnaire, each participant received 350 CNY (approximately 50.19 USD). These real-world usage data provided a reliable and contextually meaningful basis for assessing the application’s effectiveness and usability.

The evaluation by participants (n = 20) focused on two key aspects: the short-term impact of the application on family caregivers and its user-friendliness. To facilitate application use, the research team distributed user guides in both PDF format and audio files to each participant. After one week of application access via provided links, researchers conducted telephone follow-ups to inquire about usage experiences and address any emerging technical issues. Following three weeks of application use, paper-based or electronic questionnaires were administered according to participant preference, with the electronic versions being distributed and collected via Sojump. After collecting user feedback, all data were analyzed using SPSS software (Version 26).

The questionnaire items were measured using a five-point Likert scale. Validity was assessed by soliciting feedback from five experts in health information management, medical informatics, and Alzheimer’s disease specialization. Content validity was quantitatively examined using two relative metrics: the Content Validity Ratio (CVR) and Content Validity Index (CVI).

The Content Validity Ratio (CVR) is an item-level statistic initially proposed by Lawshe (1975) and remains one of the most widely used methods for quantifying content validity. In the CVR approach, expert panelists were asked to rate each item into one of three categories: “essential,” “useful but not essential,” or “not essential.” Items deemed “essential” by a critical number of experts were retained in the final version, while those failing to meet this threshold were rejected. The CVR was calculated using the following formula: CVR = (Ne − N/2) / (N/2), where Ne represents the number of experts rating an item as “essential,” and N is the total number of experts [67, 68]. In this study, all items achieved a CVR > 0.99—a value determined based on the total number of experts (N = 5) and reference to Lawshe’s Table [67]. Consequently, all items were retained in the final questionnaire.

The Content Validity Index (CVI) was calculated by summing the agreement scores for items rated as “relevant but needs modification” or “highly relevant” across all experts, then dividing by the total number of experts. A CVI score above 0.79 was considered indicative of confirmed content validity [68]. In this study, the overall CVI was 0.96.

Cronbach’s alpha was calculated separately for the two sections of the questionnaire, which addressed the app’s perceived effectiveness in assisting care and its user-friendliness. The Cronbach’s alpha value for the application effectiveness questions was 0.95, while the value for the user-friendliness questions was 0.64.

The questionnaire comprises four main sections: (1) participant demographic information; (2) questions regarding the application’s impact; (3) items evaluating the app’s user-friendliness; and (4) user feedback and comments. Each participant rated the app using five-point Likert-scale questions, with response options ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree” (scored 1 to 5 respectively). User-friendliness refers to the ease with which users can learn to operate the application. In general, a user-friendly app enables users to accomplish tasks without undergoing lengthy procedures [69]. The app’s user-friendliness was assessed through six items addressing interface design, navigation, functionality, operational processes, and overall satisfaction.

The short-term impact of application use was evaluated using ten items. These primarily measured user behavioral changes across four modules—educational support, professional support, peer support, and health management—as well as whether the app met caregivers’ needs, saved time, and alleviated caregiving pressure. The final section aimed to collect qualitative feedback based on user experience with the app. Here, participants could share their perspectives on the application’s strengths and weaknesses, along with suggestions for improvement. All user comments were subsequently analyzed. The complete questionnaire is provided in Additional file 1.

Theme 1: content

Translating theoretical and qualitative findings into application feature design

Long-term family caregivers of persons living with AD typically undergo a fluctuating, complex process characterized by stress and challenges. The mWITH ME app was designed with two primary objectives: to enhance the quality of care for persons living with AD, and to alleviate caregiver burden. These two elements were incorporated into the application’s development process, which utilized theoretical foundations and qualitative research involving target users to deliver interventions that significantly improved health behaviors [44].

Narrative Evidence-Based [45] Practice requires us to treat users’ “stories” and objective data as core evidence to inform design, while Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs [51–53] provides a structured framework for categorizing and prioritizing these needs. Additional file 3 systematically illustrates how the themes emerging from the interviews were translated, through these theoretical lenses, into concrete app functional modules and design specifications. Through the aforementioned mapping process, the design of this application transcends a mere aggregation of features; it represents a structured translation of deep user needs guided by theory. The Narrative Evidence-Based framework ensures that the design is grounded in authentic user experiences [47] and systematic data collection [48]. Concurrently, Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs facilitates the systematic construction of a comprehensive support system—ranging from foundational security needs (ease of use, reliability) to higher-level socioemotional needs (belonging, respect). This demonstrates that a theory-guided, user-centered design methodology can effectively translate fragmented user needs into a comprehensive mHealth solution with internal logical consistency.

mHealth application design

To ensure the design effectively achieved its intended objectives and aligned with user requirements, we constituted a multidisciplinary team comprising a nutritionist, two clinical specialists, a UI/UX designer, and two software developers. This collaborative team provided specialized expertise while integrating practical and clinical considerations into the application’s architecture.

The development process was guided by two foundational frameworks: the Narrative Evidence-Based Medicine model and the Hierarchical Needs model for AD caregivers. These frameworks informed both the theoretical direction and design strategy of the application. Through systematic analysis of qualitative data regarding caregiver needs, we identified and prioritized core features and content modules for implementation (Fig. 4). This methodology ensured the final application combined clinical validity with practical usability, addressing both patient care quality and caregiver support through digitally-enabled solutions.

App evaluation from perspectives of the end-users

A total of twenty participants took part in the evaluation. 35% held a bachelor’s degree, and 15% had attained a master’s degree. The cohort comprised 80% females. Regarding caregiving experience, 65% had cared for persons living with AD for less than six years, while 35% had six or more years of caregiving experience.

According to the preliminary evaluation, 80% (n = 16) of participants reported being “satisfied” or “highly satisfied” with their experience using the mWITH ME application, and 75% (n = 15) indicated they were “likely” or “highly likely” to recommend the app to others. Furthermore, 75% (n = 15) of participants perceived at least a moderate level of effectiveness in alleviating caregiver burden, and 70% (n = 14) reported a moderate or higher level of improvement in care quality. Discussion

This study describes the systematic development and initial evaluation of mWITH ME, a theory-informed and user-centered mobile application designed to support family caregivers of individuals with AD. The app was developed through a rigorous three-stage process that integrated theoretical frameworks (Narrative Evidence-Based Medicine and Maslow’s hierarchy of needs), qualitative insights from target users, and iterative prototyping by a multidisciplinary team. More importantly, the initial evaluation was grounded in authentic user data obtained from clinical settings. The results indicated that the application appeared to be positively received by all twenty participants, with quantitative metrics further confirming its acceptability (see Table 2). High scores were received specifically for accessing AD-related information and alleviating caregiving pressure, with 40% (n = 8) of participants rating these aspects as “Extremely” helpful.

Our findings align with previous research emphasizing the critical need for tailored digital support among family caregivers of persons living with AD [32, 33]. Unlike comparable development studies [34–37], our investigation established theoretical foundations and systematically executed target user-centered application development. The implemented system delivered a comprehensive support solution integrating educational support, professional guidance, peer networking, and health monitoring modules. This multidimensional intervention design successfully addressed caregivers’ complex needs through preliminary evaluation, demonstrating satisfactory feasibility and generating substantially enhanced support outcomes.

The “Educational Support” and “Professional Support” modules received particularly positive feedback, highlighting the critical importance of providing reliable disease-specific knowledge and accessible expert guidance—resources typically fragmented and difficult to obtain [25]. This aligns with the Narrative Evidence-Based theoretical framework underlying the mWITH ME project, which integrates objective medical evidence (evidence-based medicine) with subjective personal experiences (narrative medicine). This integration may, to some extent, contribute to facilitating shared decision-making processes and empathy within Alzheimer’s disease care [45, 49].

Furthermore, the “Peer Support” module was valued for fostering a sense of community and belonging, reducing feelings of isolation—a finding consistent with studies highlighting the role of social support in mitigating caregiver stress [14, 15]. This module directly addresses the higher-level needs (e.g., love/belonging, esteem, self-actualization) in Maslow’s hierarchy, suggesting that fulfilling these psychosocial needs is as crucial as providing instrumental support [36, 38].

This study utilized a semi-structured interview approach, aiming to explore in depth and breadth the unmet needs, daily challenges, as well as the existing awareness and perspectives towards mHealth among family caregivers [55, 58, 59]. Our qualitative findings, which formed the basis of the app’s design, revealed a complex set of caregiver needs, ranging from the desire for credible, tailored information to the necessity for emotional support and practical caregiving strategies. Subsequent genuine evaluation data from the hospital confirmed that the application successfully met many of these requirements.The high reported satisfaction rates (80%) and willingness to recommend the app (75%) indicate that the user-centered design process—incorporating target users from the outset—was effective in creating a relevant and acceptable tool [56, 57]. Furthermore, a significant proportion of users perceived the app as beneficial for alleviating caregiving burden (75%) and improving care quality (70%). This aligns with the app’s dual primary objectives and suggests that mWITH ME holds promise for making a tangible impact on the caregiving experience.

However, the “Health Monitoring” module received comparatively lower satisfaction scores. Users expressed a desire for expanded functionalities, such as monitoring mood, energy levels, fatigue, and sleep—parameters closely linked to caregiver burden and quality of life [18, 19]. This feedback is highly valuable. Accordingly, the future development of the health monitoring module will focus on the following directions: first, integrating validated scales (such as the PHQ-9 for depression and visual analogue scales for fatigue) to enable standardized tracking of multidimensional symptoms; second, exploring limited data integration with wearable devices to objectively supplement user self-reported information; and finally, applying intelligent algorithms to conduct correlational analysis of multidimensional data, thereby providing caregivers with personalized risk alerts and management suggestions. These iterative upgrades strictly adhere to user-centered design principles, and their usability and clinical utility are further validated in subsequent studies. This process aims to create a more comprehensive and responsive support system.

Our preliminary findings validated the impact and acceptability of the application within the Chinese context. The high participant retention rates and consistently positive feedback indicated that the tool effectively integrated into the daily lives of Chinese family caregivers and successfully addressed their practical challenges. The mWITH ME application represents a significant advancement in the development of comprehensive, theory-driven digital support systems for family caregivers of persons living with AD. The study demonstrates that a design process strictly grounded in established psychological and narrative medicine theories, supplemented by in-depth qualitative user engagement, can yield tools with high acceptability and user-friendliness. The encouraging initial feedback on key modules, combined with constructive suggestions regarding health monitoring, provides a clear and actionable direction for future iterations.

Limitations

Although the research findings are encouraging, there are several limitations to this study. First, the evaluation duration is relatively short, lasting only three weeks, which does not reflect the long-term effects and sustainability of the application. Second, participants are recruited from a single center, and the sample source is relatively homogeneous, which may limit the generalizability of the results to a broader population. Third, the sample size is small (n = 20), and the statistical power is limited, which may affect the robustness of some findings.

Additionally, the study design does not include a control group, and all results are based on self-reported data following the intervention. Therefore, the influence of time factors, participant expectations, or other confounding variables cannot be ruled out. The observed positive feedback mainly reflects initial acceptability and feasibility, rather than evidence of effectiveness in the strict sense. In a subsequent study, a multicenter randomized controlled trial (RCT) featuring a larger sample size and an extended follow-up period (≥ 6 months) serves to rigorously evaluate the efficacy of the mWITH ME application.

Conclusion

This application was developed in Chinese as a digital tool to support family caregivers of persons living with AD. The development and preliminary evaluation of the mWITH ME application demonstrate that a theory-driven, user-centered, and interdisciplinary approach is feasible and acceptable for creating digital health solutions for family caregivers. The findings indicate that the application can, to some extent, meet users’ needs by providing them with educational, professional, and peer support, as well as health monitoring. Although the health monitoring functionality requires further refinement, the overall positive short-term perceived feedback from users confirms the potential of mWITH ME as a supportive tool. Future research should employ rigorous experimental designs and long-term evaluation studies to verify its effectiveness and explore its integration into standard care pathways, ultimately aiming to enhance the quality of life for both caregivers and individuals with AD.

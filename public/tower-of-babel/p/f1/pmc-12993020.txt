Real-World Outcomes of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter 2 Inhibitor Therapy in Nondiabetic Autosomal-Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease: Effects on Progression to Dialysis in a TrinetX Cohort
Authors: Chia-Hua Chang, Yuan-Chuan Kuo, Yu-Hsiang Kuan, Paik-Seong Lim, Mu-Chi Chung, Ying-Ru Pan, Ci-Wen Luo, Tsai-Kun Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the association between sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) use and the risk of progression to chronic dialysis in nondiabetic adults with autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD).Patients and MethodsThis retrospective cohort study used the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network to identify nondiabetic adults newly diagnosed with ADPKD since 2018. We performed 1:1 propensity score matching to balance 21 baseline covariates between SGLT2i users and controls. Secondary analyses compared tolvaptan users with matched controls and performed a direct comparison between SGLT2i and tolvaptan. The primary outcome was chronic dialysis initiation, analyzed via Kaplan–Meier and Cox proportional hazards models.ResultsAfter matching, 187 patients were included in each cohort. Dialysis initiation occurred in 19 (10.16%) SGLT2i users vs 43 (22.99%) controls (P<.001). SGLT2i use was associated with a significantly lower risk of progression to dialysis (hazard ratio [HR], 0.35; 95% CI, 0.21-0.61). However, longitudinal data on total kidney volume and genetic markers were unavailable in the data set.ConclusionSodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor therapy was associated with a substantially reduced risk of progressing to dialysis in this real-world nondiabetic ADPKD cohort. Although these findings are promising, prospective randomized trials are essential to confirm these results and to evaluate the longitudinal impact of SGLT2i on cyst growth and total kidney volume.

Autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) is the most prevalent inherited renal disorder and constitutes a major cause of end-stage renal disease (ESRD) globally, accounting for an estimated 5% to 10% of individuals undergoing dialysis treatment.1 Autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease is characterized by progressive development of multiple renal cysts, leading to kidney enlargement, loss of function, and often the need for renal replacement therapy by mid to late adulthood. Vasopressin V2-receptor antagonism with tolvaptan was the first therapy shown to slow the decline of renal function and prolong the time to ESRD in ADPKD.2,3 However, the widespread use of tolvaptan has been limited by its high cost, which poses a significant barrier to access in many health care systems, particularly for long-term therapy.

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), originally developed for type 2 diabetes, have emerged as an important therapy to slow progression of chronic kidney disease in both diabetic and nondiabetic populations.4,5 Large randomized trials have shown that SGLT2i significantly reduce the risk of glomerular filtration rate (GFR) decline, ESRD, and other renal end points in nondiabetic kidney diseases, largely through hemodynamic and metabolic mechanisms that may confer nephroprotection.6, 7, 8 Given these broad renal benefits, SGLT2i are a promising class of drugs that could potentially benefit patients with ADPKD, including those without diabetes. Furthermore, SGLT2i are generally more affordable than tolvaptan and are increasingly available as generic medications, making them a potentially more accessible option for long-term therapy in ADPKD.

However, patients with ADPKD, and especially those without diabetes, were excluded from pivotal SGLT2i trials,9 leaving a major evidence gap. Concerns about the unique pathophysiology of ADPKD and limited preliminary data have hindered the integration of SGLT2i into ADPKD management. Small pilot studies, including those in patients with nondiabetic ADPKD, have begun to explore this question,10,11 but robust real-world clinical data are lacking.

We, therefore, performed a large, real-world observational study to investigate the association between SGLT2i use and progression to requiring dialysis in nondiabetic adults with ADPKD. Using a global electronic health record network (TriNetX), we applied propensity score matching (PSM) to balance key prognostic factors, aiming to provide initial clinical evidence on the potential renoprotective effect of SGLT2i in this specific population and to inform the design of future prospective trials.

Patients and Methods

Study Design and Data Source

We conducted a retrospective cohort study using the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network, a federated network of de-identified electronic health record data from health care organizations worldwide. The network provides access to aggregate patient-level data while ensuring privacy through anonymization. This study was performed in accordance with applicable data use regulations and was determined to be exempt from institutional review board oversight due to the use of de-identified retrospective data. The data used in this study were collected on January 5, 2026, from the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network, which provided access to electronic medical records (diagnoses, procedures, medications, laboratory values, and genomic information) across 168 health care organizations.

Study Population

We conducted a retrospective, real-world cohort study using the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network, which integrates de-identified electronic health records from 151 health care organizations worldwide. Adult patients aged 20 years or older with a newly diagnosed ADPKD since January 1, 2018, were considered eligible. ADPKD was identified using the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) diagnostic code Q61.2.12 To ensure an established diagnosis, patients were required to have at least 1 documented ADPKD diagnosis before cohort entry. Patients were excluded if they had any of the following before the index date: a diagnosis of diabetes mellitus (ICD-10 E08–E13), kidney transplantation procedures or status (ICD-10 Z94.0, ICD-10-PCS codes, or relevant CPT renal transplantation codes), ESRD (N18.6), long-term dialysis dependence (Z99.2), or recorded death before the index date.

Exposure Definition

Exposure was defined based on initiation of SGLT2i. The treatment cohort consisted of patients who initiated any SGLT2i after the diagnosis of ADPKD, identified using Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical code A10BK, including dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin, and sotagliflozin. For patients in the SGLT2i cohort, the index date was defined as the date of first recorded SGLT2i prescription, thereby avoiding immortal time bias.

The control cohort comprised patients with ADPKD who had no record of SGLT2i exposure at any time during the study period. Each control patient was assigned a pseudo-index date corresponding to the index date of their matched SGLT2i-exposed counterpart and was required to be alive and dialysis-free and to have established ADPKD at that time.

Baseline Characteristics and Covariates

Baseline characteristics were assessed at the index date. Demographic variables included age, sex, race, and Hispanic or Latino ethnicity. Clinical comorbidities included hypertensive diseases, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, cerebrovascular diseases, and urolithiasis. Medication exposure at baseline included angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and tolvaptan.

Baseline laboratory values included body mass index (BMI), Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD) estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), serum creatinine, serum albumin, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure, using the measurement closest to and preceding the index date. The duration from first recorded ADPKD diagnosis to index date was calculated and included as a baseline variable.

PSM

To minimize baseline confounding, we performed 1:1 PSM using the nearest-neighbor matching without replacement and a caliper width of 0.1 SDs. The 1:1 matching ratio was selected as it is the standard built-in protocol for the TriNetX Advanced Analytics platform. From a statistical perspective, although 1:n matching (eg, 1:3 or 1:4) can increase precision, it often leads to the inclusion of controls with greater distance in propensity scores, thereby reintroducing residual confounding. According to Austin,13 1:1 matching is preferred in clinically heterogeneous populations such as ADPKD to prioritize the minimization of bias and ensure the highest internal validity. A multivariable logistic regression model was used to estimate the probability of initiating SGLT2i based on baseline clinical characteristics at the index date. A total of 21 covariates were included in the model, encompassing demographic characteristics (age, sex, race, and Hispanic or Latino ethnicity), clinical comorbidities (hypertensive diseases, cerebrovascular diseases, ischemic heart diseases, urolithiasis, and heart failure), medication usage (ACEIs, ARBs, and NSAIDs), and laboratory parameters (BMI, MDRD eGFR, serum albumin, serum creatinine, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure). After matching, patients remained in each cohort, and all baseline variables achieved adequate balance, as indicated by standardized mean differences less than 0.1.

Secondary and Comparative Analyses

To contextualize the findings, we conducted a secondary analysis comparing tolvaptan users with a separate matched control group. These controls were identified from the same ADPKD pool, ensuring they had no exposure to either tolvaptan or SGLT2i. The same 21-covariate PSM model was used to ensure these cohorts were balanced at baseline.

Outcome Measurement

The primary outcome was the initiation of chronic dialysis during follow-up. Dialysis initiation was defined as the first occurrence of any relevant ICD-10 codes, including N18.6 for ESRD and Z99.2 for dialysis dependence, or Current Procedural Terminology codes representing dialysis-related procedures (90935-90940, 90945-90947, 90951-90970, 90989, 90993, 90997, and 90999).

Statistical Analysis

Survival analyses were performed on the matched cohort to evaluate differences in time-to-dialysis initiation between SGLT2i users and nonusers. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were constructed to estimate the cumulative probability of remaining dialysis-free over time, and between-group differences were assessed using the log-rank test.

Time-to-event outcomes were examined using Cox proportional hazards models. All models adjusted for demographic characteristics (age, sex, race, and ethnicity), major comorbidities (hypertension, heart failure, neoplasms, ischemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, and urolithiasis), medication use (ACEIs, ARBs, and NSAIDs), and baseline clinical measurements (BMI, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, estimated glomerular filtration rate using MDRD, serum creatinine, and serum albumin).

Results were presented as hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% CIs. The proportional hazards assumption was evaluated using plots of Schoenfeld residuals against a linear time scale, complemented by formal statistical tests, and no significant violations were detected. All analyses were conducted using the TriNetX Analytics platform and verified using standard statistical software. A 2-sided P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Kaplan–Meier Survival Analysis

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis found a significantly higher dialysis-free survival probability among patients treated with SGLT2i compared with PSM control patients throughout the follow-up period. Among 187 patients in each cohort, 19 patients (10.16%) in the SGLT2i group and 43 patients (22.99%) in the control group progressed to maintenance dialysis. The survival curves separated early and remained consistently apart during follow-up (Figure 1), with the SGLT2i group exhibiting a persistently lower cumulative incidence of dialysis initiation. Time-Stratified Cox Proportional Hazards Analysis

A total of 215 patients initiating SGLT2i and 17,681 nonusers were included in the unmatched cohort analysis. Time-to-event associations were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards models, stratified by duration of exposure and adjusted for demographic characteristics, comorbidities, medication use, and baseline laboratory parameters.

Covariates included in all adjustment models were age at index date (continuous), sex (female and male), race (White, Black/African American, Asian, and unknown), ethnicity (Hispanic/Latino, non-Hispanic/Latino, and unknown), pre-existing diagnoses (hypertensive diseases, heart failure, neoplasms, ischemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, and urolithiasis), medication exposure (ACEIs , ARBs, and NSAIDs), and laboratory measures at baseline (body mass index, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, MDRD eGFR, serum creatinine, and serum albumin).

Across all follow-up intervals, SGLT2i users exhibited a consistently lower hazard of dialysis initiation relative to nonusers. The adjusted HR during years 1-2 after initiation was 0.17 (95% CI, 0.10-0.29), followed by 0.20 (95% CI, 0.06-0.62) in years 2-3. Although CIs widened due to diminishing sample at risk, a protective trend persisted beyond 3 years (HR, 0.20; 95% CI, 0.03-1.38) (Figure 2). Subgroup Analysis by Baseline eGFR

Subgroup analyses stratified by baseline eGFR were performed to further characterize the associations between treatment exposure and the risk of dialysis initiation. Among patients with baseline eGFR of 15-45 mL/min/1.73 m2, the SGLT2i cohort included 84 patients, of whom 12 experienced dialysis initiation, compared with 16 events among 84 PSM control patients (HR, 0.52; 95% CI, 0.25-1.05). In a separate matched cohort of patients with baseline eGFR of 45-90 mL/min/1.73 m2, 149 patients were included in each group, with fewer than 10 dialysis events observed in the SGLT2i group and 32 events in the control group (HR, 0.25; 95% CI, 0.12-0.51).

For patients treated with tolvaptan and baseline eGFR of 15-45 mL/min/1.73 m2, each cohort comprised 391 patients. During follow-up, 60 patients in the tolvaptan group and 64 patients in the matched control group progressed to dialysis (HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.56-1.208).

In the subgroup of patients with baseline eGFR of 45-90 mL/min/1.73 m2, 605 patients were included in both the tolvaptan and control cohorts. Discussion

In this retrospective cohort study of nondiabetic adults with ADPKD, SGLT2i therapy was associated with a substantially lower risk of progression to chronic dialysis. The magnitude of this risk reduction (HR, 0.35) was similar to that observed with tolvaptan (HR, 0.66), with no statistically significant difference between the 2 therapies. After PSM, both SGLT2i therapy and tolvaptan groups were associated with a significantly decreased risk of dialysis in patients with eGFR of 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 or more and an insignificant but a trend to increase dialysis-free duration. Although the dialysis rates (22.99% in controls) exceeded those in traditional randomized controlled trials, this discrepancy likely reflects the hospital-based nature of real-world cohorts, and our cohort’s mean age was approximately 60 years at the index date. Patients identified via hospital-based electronic health records often carry a higher burden of advanced disease and multimorbidity than the healthy volunteer population of clinical trials. Consequently, our cohort represents a higher-risk phenotype already reaching the inflection point of functional decline, where progression to ESRD is historically more rapid.14 Furthermore, larger observational studies have indicated that the prevalence of nonlinear progression may be as high as 40%.15

The time-stratified Cox proportional hazards analysis found a consistent nephroprotective trend associated with SGLT2i use throughout the follow-up period. The HR point estimates remained stable and markedly low (∼0.20) across all intervals, with the most robust statistical significance observed during the early phase (1-2 years). Although the protective trend persisted beyond 3 years, the widening of CIs in the later stages reflects a diminution in statistical power owing to the smaller number of patients remaining at risk during the extended observation window. This immediate and sustained benefit aligns with the known hemodynamic mechanisms of SGLT2i, specifically the reduction of intraglomerular pressure, which manifests shortly after treatment initiation.

Subgroup analyses stratified by baseline renal function showed that the efficacy of SGLT2i in preventing dialysis was significantly influenced by the patient’s initial eGFR. Patients with a higher baseline eGFR (45-90 mL/min/1.73 m2) derived the most substantial benefit. A protective trend was also observed in the subgroup with a lower baseline eGFR (15-45 mL/min/1.73 m2). These findings underscore the clinical importance of early intervention; SGLT2i therapy appears to be most effective when initiated while renal function is relatively preserved (eGFR > 45 mL/min/1.73 m2). Such early treatment may more effectively delay the progression toward maintenance dialysis before the establishment of irreversible pathological changes, such as extensive renal fibrosis. Owing to the small number of matched pairs in the head-to-head comparison of SGLT2i and tolvaptan, analysis of the subgroup with an eGFR of 45-90 mL/min/1.73 m2 was not feasible.

Our findings are consistent with emerging evidence from pilot and real-world studies. In an open-label crossover trial, the addition of dapagliflozin to stable tolvaptan therapy attenuated eGFR decline and stabilized total kidney volume (TKV) over a 6-month period.10 Additional case series have reported eGFR improvements with SGLT2i in patients with or without diabetes.7,16 However, short-term SGLT2i monotherapy has also been linked to increases in TKV,17 suggesting a possible trade-off between glomerular protection and cyst expansion. A separate analysis of patients with polycystic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes showed SGLT2i were associated with lower risks of dialysis, acute kidney injury, and mortality.18 These data suggest that the initial hemodynamic drop in GFR often seen with SGLT2i is followed by long-term renoprotection, even in patients with ADPKD.

The renal benefits of tolvaptan have been attributed to its effects on vasopressin V2-receptor antagonism, resulting in slowed cyst growth and reduced eGFR decline.2,19,20 To address the absence of structured height-adjusted TKV data, we leveraged tolvaptan use as a robust proxy for the rapid progressor phenotype. Clinically, tolvaptan initiation is restricted to patients categorized as high risk, specifically matching the Mayo Imaging Classification 1C, 1D, or 1E criteria. By matching for tolvaptan status and baseline eGFR, we essentially stratified our cohorts by intrinsic progression risk, ensuring that the SGLT2i and control groups were balanced for the underlying disease severity that determines clinical trajectory. Moreover, SGLT2i may confer benefit via a distinct mechanism: by inhibiting glucose and sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubule, they enhance distal sodium delivery and tubuloglomerular feedback, reducing intraglomerular pressure.21,22 This contributes to their renoprotective efficacy across chronic kidney disease populations.23 In ADPKD, these effects may be particularly advantageous given the roles of hypertension, hyperfiltration, and metabolic stress in disease progression.24,25 Furthermore, SGLT2i also induces a fasting-like metabolic state and mild ketosis, which may antagonize the glycolytic shift observed in cystic epithelial cells.26,27

However, SGLT2i-induced osmotic diuresis may increase vasopressin levels, potentially stimulating cystogenesis.28,29 Animal studies have shown conflicting effects: phlorizin (a nonselective SGLT inhibitor) reduced cyst burden. In the Han:SPRD rat model, the nonselective SGLTi phlorizin significantly reduced cyst burden and cell proliferation.30 Conversely, studies in the PCK rat model (an orthologous model for ARPKD/ADPKD) using dapagliflozin observed increased urine output accompanied by hyperfiltration, albuminuria, and a paradoxical increase in cyst volume. These differences may be due to species-specific responses to osmotic diuresis and the resulting increase in vasopressin levels.31 In the DAPA-ADPKD trial, coadministration of tolvaptan with dapagliflozin stabilized both GFR and TKV,10 suggesting that V2-receptor blockade may counteract vasopressin-mediated cyst growth. In contrast, SGLT2i monotherapy has been associated with TKV increases despite favorable effects on GFR.17 These observations suggest that combination therapy may be required to maximize renoprotection while minimizing cyst expansion.

Our analysis lacked longitudinal TKV measurements, vasopressin data, and genotypic information, limiting mechanistic interpretation. Nonetheless, the observed reduction in dialysis risk with SGLT2i use suggests that glomerular protection may outweigh potential cyst-related harms over a median 4-year follow-up, particularly in patients with early disease.

The accessibility and cost profile of SGLT2i further support their consideration in ADPKD care. Tolvaptan is a high cost specialty medication, with an estimated price exceeding $60,000 (US dollars) annually.32 Its use is often restricted to high risk patients by payers,33,34 and real-world uptake remains modest. In contrast, SGLT2i are broadly available for diabetes, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease, with prices an order of magnitude lower. Although SGLT2i therapy should not replace tolvaptan in eligible candidates, it may serve as a valuable alternative or adjunct in patients with contraindications or limited access. Their favorable cost and accessibility profile support further integration into ADPKD care frameworks and consideration in health policy planning.

Our study has limitations. As a retrospective analysis, unmeasured confounding may persist despite PSM. We lacked data on medication adherence, TKV, genotype, and vasopressin activity. Outcome ascertainment relied on administrative coding, and small sample sizes limited subgroup analyses, particularly in early-stage ADPKD. The median 4-year follow-up may be insufficient to detect long-term differences in disease trajectory. Finally, our findings are associative and do not establish causality.

Conclusion

In conclusion, among nondiabetic adults with ADPKD, both SGLT2i and tolvaptan were associated with a substantially reduced risk of progression to dialysis compared with controls. In the subgroup with early renal impairment (chronic kidney disease stage <3a), the protective effects of SGLT2i and tolvaptan did not differ significantly, indicating comparable efficacy in delaying dialysis initiation. Although these findings support SGLT2i as a potentially accessible and cost-effective therapeutic option, it should be acknowledged that SGLT2i-induced osmotic diuresis may stimulate vasopressin release, raising concerns about possible cyst growth acceleration. These real-world findings highlight both therapies as promising disease-modifying options and underscore the need for prospective trials to define their long-term benefits, risks, and optimal use, either alone or in combination, in the management of ADPKD. Our findings underscore the importance of exploring cost-effective and scalable strategies for ADPKD management.

Public Health Perspective

The global burden of autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease is substantial, particularly in regions with limited access to specialized therapies. Tolvaptan’s high cost restricts its use in many health systems, exacerbating treatment inequities. The broader availability and lower cost of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor offer a public health opportunity to improve care equity and outcomes across diverse populations. Incorporating sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor into autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease management may be especially impactful in resource-limited settings.

Application of LSTM-CNN in skiing action recognition under artificial intelligence technology
Authors: Wenhao Zhang, Liang Xu, Lei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a deep learning model integrated with visual perception, aiming to enhance the accuracy of automatic skiing action recognition in complex scenarios. These scenarios include background interference from trees and snow mounds, lighting variations between cloudy and sunny days, and common body self-occlusions in skiers’ movements. The model adopts a two-stream three-dimensional convolutional network-bidirectional long short-term memory (C3D-BiLSTM) architecture. The Red Green Blue (RGB) stream extracts movement features through the three-dimensional convolutional network (C3D), while the saliency perception stream highlights motion regions using optical flow fields. Meanwhile, a learnable weighted fusion method is introduced into the model to effectively integrate the two-stream features. Finally, the Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) model performs sequence modeling on the fused spatiotemporal features to extract complete movement dynamics. The bidirectional temporal modeling capability of the BiLSTM model enables the simultaneous capture of action context from both directions. This provides a more comprehensive understanding of the start and end states of movements, thereby improving the recognition stability for complete action cycles. Experimental results on the SkiTB dataset demonstrate the following findings. (1) The proposed model outperforms other baseline models in four indicators—precision (92.8%), recall (91.9%), F1-score (0.923), and average precision (93.0%). (2) Ablation experiments verify the effectiveness of the BiLSTM, saliency perception stream, and weighted fusion method. (3) The model maintains an average recognition accuracy of over 85% in cross-scenario (cloudy days, light snow) and cross-athlete tests, and exhibits good stability against input noise. These conclusions indicate that by fully leveraging appearance and motion information, the model can effectively recognize complex skiing movements, providing new ideas and technical methods for intelligent sports analysis.Keywords: Skiing action recognition, C3D-BiLSTM model, Saliency perception stream, Feature fusion, Analysis of spatiotemporal characteristicsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Currently, with the popularization of ice and snow sports and the rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, sports action analysis based on computer vision has become a research hotspot among numerous scholars1,2. Among these sports, skiing stands out as a technically complex winter sport. Its movements are characterized by strong dynamics, abundant technical details, and significant environmental interference3,4. In recent years, scholars have proposed various solutions for automated action recognition using diverse neural network models5. However, most existing methods face issues such as insufficient extraction of spatiotemporal features and inadequate utilization of motion information when dealing with sports like skiing, where backgrounds are complex, and movements change rapidly. This, to some extent, hinders the application and development of skiing action recognition in practical training6,7.

Among the existing action recognition methods, hybrid models based on the convolutional neural network (CNN) and long short-term memory (LSTM) have become the primary approach for processing video sequences8,9. This is because CNN can effectively extract spatial features from video frames, while LSTM excels at modeling dependencies across the temporal dimension10. Meanwhile, a three-dimensional convolutional network (C3D) can capture spatiotemporal features simultaneously, boasting strong video recognition capabilities11. In addition, the two-stream architecture processes appearance information and motion information separately, which effectively enhances the model’s ability to distinguish dynamic actions12. In terms of feature fusion, simple concatenation or addition often fails to highlight the importance of different feature streams. Thus, developing fusion methods with higher accuracy is necessary. Furthermore, bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) networks can leverage both historical and future contextual information to analyze complete action cycles13,14.

Based on the above background, this study proposes a C3D-BiLSTM model based on visual perception. Compared to common LSTM-CNN hybrid frameworks, this model introduces optimizations across three levels: information perception, fusion, and modeling. Its core innovations are mainly reflected in three aspects. The study introduces a saliency perception stream to extract motion information and identify prominent action-related regions through optical flow calculation; a learnable weighted fusion method is proposed to adaptively enhance two-stream features. Moreover, this study adopts a BiLSTM network for temporal modeling to fully utilize the complete contextual information of movements. This overall design philosophy of “feature extraction-fusion-temporal modeling” is expected to enhance the model’s recognition performance in complex scenarios. Compared with previous research, the main contribution of this study lies in the proposal of an end-to-end visual perception fusion framework. This framework introduces a saliency-aware stream tailored for skiing to explicitly capture dynamic information; it also designs a learnable weighted fusion mechanism to adaptively integrate multi-source features. Combined with bidirectional LSTM for temporal modeling of complete action cycles, this design enables the model to identify technical movements more stably in complex snowy environments. It maintains high precision while demonstrating good generalization capabilities across different scenarios and athletes, providing a more reliable technical pathway for practical ski training and analysis.

Literature review

Deep learning (DL) models have made many advancements in the direction of sports action recognition over the years. In domestic, Sun et al. proposed a hybrid model combining LSTM with generative adversarial networks (GANs), applying it to the recognition and prediction of tennis actions. Validation showed that the model could effectively learn the temporal features of movements15. Yu and Li integrated ant colony optimization with clustering algorithms, utilizing the combined model for cross-country skiing action recognition. Their research indicated that the model improved action classification accuracy16. Chen and Peng explored an action recognition model based on LSTM recurrent neural networks. They noted that the model exhibited strong performance in capturing the temporal dependencies of actions17. Yu developed a full-body rotation recognition method for alpine skiing based on LSTM, providing a valuable reference for complex skiing action analysis18. Internationally, Murthy et al. adopted a hybrid approach fusing CNN with LSTM for human activity recognition. Experiments confirmed the effectiveness of the hybrid architecture in spatiotemporal feature learning19. Pousibet-Garrido et al. classified cross-country skiing gears using inertial sensors and DL methods, advancing the application of sensor data in skiing analysis20. Polo-Rodríguez et al. investigated the performance of inertial sensors in cross-country skiing action recognition and classification, offering a reference for multi-source data selection21. Menhorn et al. used neural networks to automatically recognize slalom skiing actions from video recordings, expanding the perceptual dimension of skiing data analysis22.

Through analysis, although existing studies have achieved considerable results in the field of sports action recognition, certain limitations remain. For example, most studies lack effective fusion of multi-source information in complex scenarios. To this end, the study introduces a visual perception-based two-stream C3D-BiLSTM model. It improves the recognition accuracy of skiing actions by fusing Red Green Blue (RGB) and optical flow data.

Research model

The LSTM-CNN model and human pose estimation

LSTM-CNN is a classic hybrid DL model, which is widely used in tasks such as video analysis and action recognition23,24. This model’s architecture is presented in Fig. 1.

Architecture of the LSTM-CNN model.

The model’s workflow in the skiing action recognition task is as follows. First, the CNN extracts the spatial feature sequence of each frame of the image. These features are then input into the LSTM network in chronological order. Based on its internal state, LSTM learns the temporal variation patterns of these spatial features, ultimately recognizing complete skiing actions such as “slalom” and “braking”.

In vision-related action recognition tasks, raw video frames usually contain a large amount of background information irrelevant to the action. Direct processing of such frames increases the model’s learning difficulty25. Therefore, to more effectively represent human movement, the study first adopts a skeleton key point detection method. The core of this method is to use the human pose estimation model OpenPose to accurately locate the pixel coordinates of major human joints in each frame of the input video sequence26,27. These joints form a human skeleton sequence, thereby expressing human pose changes in the form of mathematical vectors.

Using the pose estimation model, a human skeleton sequence containing K joints can be obtained. For each frame, the skeleton data is represented as a set of 2D coordinates of all joints. However, raw key point coordinates are susceptible to the position of the human body in the image. Therefore, relative vectors between joints are calculated to extract features from these raw coordinates that are insensitive to position and scale changes. The C3D-BiLSTM model based on visual perception

This model optimizes the common LSTM-CNN architecture. The key improvement is the introduction of a two-stream mechanism at the input end to capture appearance and motion information separately. In the feature fusion stage, a learnable weighting method is adopted instead of fixed concatenation; this allows the model to dynamically adjust the contribution of each stream based on the input content. In the temporal modeling stage, BiLSTM further integrates bidirectional context to achieve an understanding of the complete action cycle. This multi-level structural design enables the model to maintain temporal modeling capabilities. Meanwhile, it enhances the perception and discrimination of moving targets in snowy scenarios, resulting in superior performance compared to common LSTM-CNN frameworks.

Model structure

The two-stream input architecture is a common design in action recognition, where the RGB stream extracts appearance features. Moreover, the motion stream is typically constructed based on optical flow fields. Building on this, the motion stream in this model is optimized with a saliency-aware mechanism. This mechanism highlights the motion regions of the skier themselves, thereby weakening the interference from complex snowy backgrounds. Furthermore, instead of using common methods like concatenation or averaging, the model introduces a learnable weighted fusion module in the feature fusion stage. This allows the network to adaptively adjust the contribution degree of the two feature types based on the input content.

The overall structure of the vision-based C3D-BiLSTM model is a two-stream input and multi-stage fusion architecture. The model fully utilizes the appearance information and motion information in video data. It then uses the optimized LSTM model to extract complex spatiotemporal dynamic features of skiing actions. The model’s structure is revealed in Fig. 2.

Structure of the visual perception-based C3D-BiLSTM model.

In Fig. 2, the model’s input consists of two data streams. The first stream is the original RGB video frame sequence, which contains abundant appearance visual information. The second stream is the saliency perception stream. This data stream is implemented by calculating the optical flow field between consecutive frames. Its purpose is to more effectively express the motion patterns and attention regions of skiing actions, highlighting the dynamic changes caused by the skier’s movements. Both data streams are processed in different branches of the C3D model.

After fusing the spatiotemporal feature sequences output by the two C3D branches, the fused feature sequence is input into the BiLSTM model to complete temporal modeling. The BiLSTM uses a fully connected layer and a Softmax classifier to output the final action classification results.

The introduction of the saliency perception stream

In skiing action recognition tasks, background interference and appearance changes can distract the model. Thus, relying solely on raw RGB frame sequences may fail to extract motion dynamics28,29. In light of this, the proposed method introduces a saliency perception stream, and its calculation is based on the optical flow field. Optical flow fields can express the motion state of pixels between consecutive frames. They emphasize local motion patterns caused by the skier’s actions and reducing interference from static backgrounds30. Optical flow calculation is a standard method in dynamic video analysis used to describe pixel motion between consecutive frames. Based on this, the study further utilizes the magnitude of optical flow vectors to generate a saliency weight map. This weight map is then used to enhance the representation of motion regions.

Dual-stream C3D fusion of spatial semantic features

Feature fusion is a necessary step in multi-stream networks. Common approaches include direct concatenation or weighted averaging. In skiing action recognition, different technical movements rely on appearance and motion features to varying degrees. For example, the unique posture of the skis during a plough brake is primarily reflected in appearance changes, whereas a parallel turn relies more on continuous body motion patterns. Simple feature concatenation or average fusion struggles to adapt to these dynamic changes and can easily lead the model to over-reliance on one type of information for certain actions. Therefore, this study adopts a learnable weighted fusion method. This allows the model to automatically adjust the weights of the RGB stream and the saliency stream based on the content of the input video. It combines appearance and motion information more flexibly, thereby improving the discrimination ability for different skiing actions.

In the two-stream C3D model structure, the RGB stream and saliency perception stream extract appearance-spatial features and motion-semantic features from video sequences, respectively. To fully utilize these two types of information, effective integration is required. In traditional two-stream architectures, the motion stream generally uses optical flow maps directly as input. The features extracted reflect the overall motion distribution of pixels. The proposed saliency-aware stream further introduces a spatial weighting mechanism based on optical flow. This enables the network to treat motion information from different regions in a more differentiated manner. Therefore, in the fusion stage, the model combines not just appearance and motion features. It is even more of a motion representation that is adjusted via saliency weights to be closer to the essence of the skier’s actions.

Dynamics analysis based on BiLSTM

In skiing, actions typically exhibit distinct continuity and periodicity. A complete turn, for example, consists of multiple phases such as preparation, entry, and exit. A unidirectional LSTM can only infer based on past information. It struggles to capture predictive features of future actions present in the ending phase. In contrast, BiLSTM analyzes sequential information from both directions. It achieves a more comprehensive understanding of the start, transition, and end of actions. This allows for more accurate differentiation between actions that look similar but have different temporal structures. This modeling capability for complete action cycles enables the model to demonstrate greater stability in complex and dynamic skiing scenarios.

After fusing spatial-semantic features, the BiLSTM model is used to analyze and understand the temporal variation patterns of actions.

The BiLSTM model consists of two separate LSTM layers: forward and backward. For each time step t, the forward LSTM layer processes the sequence from left to right, extracting historical information up to the current moment. The backward LSTM layer processes the sequence from right to left, capturing contextual information from the future to the current moment. Performance evaluation

Overall performance comparison of the model

To comprehensively evaluate the proposed model’s performance, several representative baseline models from the current field of action recognition are selected for comparison. These models include classic methods based on LSTM-CNN, the C3D-based spatio-temporal modeling method C3D-LSTM, Transformer, and the two-stream I3D. This ensures that the comparison reflects the proposed model’s relative performance across different technical routes. Among them, the LSTM-CNN model is a classic hybrid architecture. It uses a 2D CNN to extract spatial features from each frame and then models the time series through LSTM. The C3D-LSTM model builds on the spatio-temporal features extracted by the C3D network. It further incorporates LSTM for temporal refinement, capturing both short-term motion patterns and long-term action dependencies. The Transformer model is a video action recognition method based on self-attention mechanisms. It can model long-distance temporal dependencies. Two-stream I3D is a classic two-stream inflated 3D convolutional network. It extracts features from RGB frames and optical flow separately and then fuses them. It is widely used in various video understanding tasks. Evaluation indicators encompass precision, recall, F1-score, and mean Average Precision (mAP). All models are trained and evaluated on the same training and test sets. Finally, the comparison results are illustrated in Fig. 3.

Overall performance comparison results.

Figure 3 indicates that compared with other baseline models, the proposed C3D-BiLSTM model performs better in four indicators: recall (91.9%), mAP (93.0%), F1-score (0.923), and precision (92.8%). This demonstrates that C3D-BiLSTM has superior advantages in spatial feature extraction and temporal modeling. Moreover, it is expected to provide a better solution for action recognition tasks.

Ablation experiments

The complete C3D-BiLSTM model is used as the baseline. Different variants are set to observe changes in model performance. The model variants include: A1—replacing BiLSTM with a standard unidirectional LSTM; A2—removing the saliency perception stream (using only the RGB stream); A3—substituting the two-stream C3D fusion module with simple channel concatenation. The experimental results are denoted in Fig. 4.

Results of the ablation experiment.

In Fig. 4, all model variants except the complete model lead to declines in indicators such as precision, mAP, F1-score, and recall. Among the ablation studies, replacing BiLSTM with a unidirectional LSTM results in a decrease in all indicators. This indicates that unidirectional LSTM has limitations in capturing the complete dynamic temporal information of skiing actions. In contrast, BiLSTM extracts action features more comprehensively through bidirectional context modeling. Consequently, it performs better in actual recognition tasks. This illustrates that the BiLSTM, saliency perception stream, and two-stream C3D fusion module in the model are all effective key components, playing important roles in improving overall performance.

Comparative experiments are conducted under two input settings: using only the original optical flow and using the saliency perception flow (weighted optical flow). Analysis of model generalization ability

This study tests the model’s performance on data from unseen scenarios and unknown athletes. In cross-scenario testing, data collected under cloudy conditions (approximately 50 video clips) and light snow conditions (approximately 40 video clips) are selected as test sets. These scenarios differ significantly from the sunny scenarios used in training in terms of lighting, snow surface reflection, and background visibility. They effectively simulate common weather changes in real skiing environments. In cross-athlete testing, a leave-one-out evaluation method is adopted. Each time, all data from one skier (an average of about 5–8 video clips) is used as the test set, while data from the remaining athletes is employed for training. This tests the model’s adaptability to different movement styles and skill levels. The evaluation results are plotted in Fig. 6.

Generalization abilities of cross-scenario and cross-athlete.

Figure 6 reveals that although the precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP of the model trained on sunny days decrease slightly when tested on cloudy and light snow scenarios (precision drops from 92.5% to 89.5% for cloudy days, and mAP decreases from 92.9% to 87.3% for light snow), the overall performance remains at a high level. Cross-athlete cross-validation results show that the average precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP for unknown athletes are 85.8%, 84.3%, 0.85, and 85.7%, respectively. Although lower than those on the training set, the overall performance is stable. This demonstrates that the model has good generalization ability on new scenarios and new athletes.

Analysis of different input modalities and noise sensitivity

This experiment is designed to explore the impact of diverse input modalities on model performance and the model’s stability against input noise. In the experiments, two types of synthetic noise are added to the RGB test frames. The first is Gaussian noise, used to simulate sensor thermal noise or uniform lighting interference. Two intensity levels are set: mild noise (standard deviation of 0.02) and severe noise (standard deviation of 0.08). The second is impulse noise (salt-and-pepper noise), used to simulate instantaneous errors in data transmission or snow interference. The noise density is set to 5% and 15%, respectively. Through these clearly defined noise types and levels, the model’s stability performance under actual complex conditions can be quantified more objectively. The research results are demonstrated in Figs. 7.

Analysis of input modalities and noise sensitivity.

Figure 7 indicates that when using only RGB frames, the model achieves an F1-score, precision, recall, and mAP of 0.869, 87.2%, 86.5%, and 87.6%. When using only optical flow, the model’s precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP are 88.6%, 87.8%, 0.882, and 88.8%. After fusing RGB and optical flow, all indicators are improved, showing that the two-stream fusion outperforms single modalities. When Gaussian noise is added to the RGB frames of the test set, the fused model maintains stable performance with a slight decline under mild noise; its recall, precision, mAP, and F1-score reach 90.7%, 91.5%, 91.8%, and 0.911. Similarly, the model’s performance remains stable despite a slight drop under heavy noise.

Discussion

This study verifies the proposed C3D-BiLSTM model’s effectiveness in skiing action recognition through experiments. Overall performance comparison results show that the model has certain advantages in extracting complex spatiotemporal features. This is mainly because its two-stream architecture can better utilize appearance information and motion information, thereby achieving better representation of dynamic skiing actions. Ablation experiments demonstrate that the BiLSTM structure, saliency perception stream, and weighted fusion method all play important roles in improving the proposed model’s discriminative ability. In addition, this model maintains good recognition performance and stability under cross-scenario, cross-athlete, and noisy data conditions. This indicates that C3D-BiLSTM has good generalization ability to cope with complex factors in real skiing environments. These findings suggest that fusing multiple types of visual information and modeling temporal-spatial dependencies are effective methods to improve model recognition performance in sports action recognition tasks. It also offers an important direction for the subsequent construction of action understanding models for complex scenarios.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study innovatively combines the visual saliency mechanism with a learnable fusion strategy. This combination addresses the limitations of traditional two-stream models that rely on fixed fusion methods. By constructing motion enhancement and adaptive fusion mechanisms, the model extracts discriminative motion features from complex backgrounds more effectively. It also captures complete action dynamics through bidirectional modeling at the temporal level. Compared with previous research, this method achieves better performance across multiple metrics. It also provides an extensible fusion perception framework for sports action recognition in complex environments.

At the theoretical level, this study proposes a hybrid DL model fusing two-stream C3D and BiLSTM. By introducing a saliency perception stream and a learnable weighted fusion method, the model can model and extract the appearance information of skiing actions and deep motion temporal dynamics. Meanwhile, it expands the methods of multimodal feature fusion in video action recognition. At the practical application level, the proposed model exhibits high recognition accuracy and stability in complex real skiing scenarios. This model can effectively recognize various skiing technical actions; it is expected to provide an effective solution for the development of intelligent skiing motion analysis systems, assisting athletes in training and skill evaluation.

Future works and research limitations

The limitations of this study mainly lie in the fact that although the model has good generalization ability, its adaptability to more extreme weather conditions or new athletes with greater individual differences has not been verified. Meanwhile, the model’s recognition performance for jumping actions with short duration and rapid posture changes still needs further optimization. Future research can attempt to introduce data from more modal sensors, such as inertial measurement units, to overcome the deficiencies of pure visual information. At the same time, more suitable meta-learning or domain adaptation methods can be explored to enhance the model’s generalization ability and practical effect when dealing with unknown scenarios and individual differences in movement styles.

Outcomes of Forehead Flap Reconstruction in 3,368 Nicotine-Dependent vs Non-Dependent Patients: A Retrospective Cohort with Propensity Score-Matched Analysis
Authors: Yousef Tanas, Philong Nguyen, Joshua Wang, Keyvon Rashidi
Published: 2026

Purpose: Nicotine dependence is known to cause impaired wound healing and increase risk of surgical complications. This study evaluates the impact of nicotine dependence on postoperative outcomes in patients undergoing forehead flap reconstruction.

Methods: A retrospective cohort study was conducted using data from the TriNetX global health research network. Patients undergoing forehead reconstruction with preservation of vascular pedicle (e.g., axial pattern flap, paramedian forehead flap) between January 2000 and February 2023 were categorized into two cohorts: those with nicotine dependence documented within 6 months before surgery (n = 1,704) and those without nicotine use (n = 5,004). Both arms had a follow-up of 1 year. Primary outcomes included fat necrosis, dehiscence, infection, seroma formation, hematoma formation, flap death/loss, reoperation, and readmission.

Result: Propensity score matching was performed, resulting in balanced cohorts of 1,684 patients each. The nicotine dependence cohort had a higher incidence of flap death/loss (4.3% vs 2.9%, p = 0.027), fat necrosis (0.6% vs. 0%, p = 0.002), dehiscence (3.8% vs. 2.3%, p = 0.011), readmission (10.8% vs 8.8%, p = 0.049), and reoperation (11.9% vs 8.5%, p = 0.001). There was no statistically significant difference between the two cohorts with regard to infection (0.6% vs. 0.6%, p = 1), seroma (0.6% vs. 0.6%, p = 1) and hematoma formation (0.6% vs. 0.6%, p = 1).

Conclusion: Nicotine dependence in patients with flap reconstruction of the forehead increases the risk of many surgical complications, especially flap death/loss, fat necrosis, dehiscence, readmission, and reoperation. These results emphasize the need for smoking and nicotine cessation prior to forehead flap reconstruction.

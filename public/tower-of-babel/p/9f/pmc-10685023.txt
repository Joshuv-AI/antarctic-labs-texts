Molecular characterization and immune protective efficacy of 3 Eimeria tenella antigens
Authors: Lei Zhang, Zhixuan Song, Peiyao Li, Xingju Song, Xinming Tang, Dandan Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Avian coccidiosis caused by Eimeria is a serious parasitic disease that poses a threat to the poultry industry. Currently, prevention and treatment mainly rely on the administration of anticoccidials and live oocyst vaccines. However, the prevalence of drug resistance and the inherent limitations of live vaccines have driven the development of novel vaccines. In this study, the surface protein (Et-SAG14), a previously annotated rhoptry protein (Eten5-B), and a gametocyte phosphoglucomutase (Et-PGM1) were characterized and the vaccine potential of the recombinant proteins were evaluated. Et-SAG14 was dispersed in the form of particles in the sporozoite and merozoite stages, whereas Et-PGM1 was distributed in the apical part of the sporozoite and merozoite stages. The previously annotated rhoptry Eten5-B was found not to be located in the rhoptry but distributed in the cytoplasm of sporozoites and merozoites. Immunization with rEten5-B significantly elevated host interferon gamma (IFN-γ) and interleukin 10 (IL-10) transcript levels and exhibited moderate anticoccidial effects with an anticoccidial index (ACI) of 161. Unexpectedly, both recombinant Et-SAG14 and Et-PGM1 immunization significantly reduced host IFN-γ and IL-10 transcription levels, and did not show protection against E. tenella challenge (ACI < 80). These results suggest that the rEten5-B protein can trigger immune protection against E. tenella and may be a potential and effective subunit vaccine for the control of coccidiosis in poultry.

INTRODUCTION

Avian coccidiosis, caused by the intracellular apicomplexan parasite Eimeria, is one of the most common parasitic diseases, causing an annual economic cost of more than £10 billion to the global poultry industry (Blake et al., 2020). Among the 7 known species of Eimeria in chickens, Eimeria tenella is arguably the most pathogenic and prevalent species, causing mortality, malabsorption, poor feed utilization, and impaired growth in broilers (Rafiqi et al., 2019).

Currently, the control strategy against Eimeria parasites mainly relies on anticoccidial drugs, live attenuated vaccines, and virulent vaccines (Blake and Tomley, 2014; Attree et al., 2021). However, the use of anticoccidial drugs has been strictly limited due to public concerns about the impact of drug residues on food safety, the continued emergence of drug resistance, and the high costs associated with the development of new drugs (Clarke et al., 2014; Ojimelukwe et al., 2018; Noack et al., 2019). In addition, although live oocyst vaccines have been commercially available in a number of areas, the development of live vaccines has been fraught with frustration due to their inherent limitations, including production constraints, cost issues, and potential pathogenicity (Peek and Landman, 2011). These shortcomings have driven the development of new control strategies, particularly the screening of effective immunoprotective proteins for the development of subunit vaccines (Blake and Tomley, 2014). The only commercially available subunit vaccine against coccidiosis, CoxAbic (Netanya, Israel) (Sharman et al., 2010), which consists of affinity-purified gametocyte antigens from E. maxima, has made great strides in clinical practice, which has inspired the screening of novel effective antigens.

ROPs, which are secreted by rhoptry, have been shown to be the most important virulence factors that strongly stimulate host immune responses in apicomplexan parasites, and play key roles in host cell invasion, biogenesis of parasitophorous vacuoles, and hijacking and modification of host cells (Bradley and Sibley, 2007; Dogga et al., 2017; Bisio and Soldati-Favre, 2019; Ihara and Nishikawa, 2021). In Toxoplasma gondii vaccine research, ROPs have been positioned as a promising vaccine candidate with remarkable results (Faridnia et al., 2018). However, few studies have been conducted to develop vaccines using ROPs of E. tenella. To date, 4 E. tenella ROPs have been reported to have good immunogenicity and may be promising vaccine candidates against E. tenella, including Eten5-A (Song et al., 2020), EtROP17 (Liu et al., 2022), EtROP30 (Wang et al., 2022b), and EtROP35 (Wang et al., 2022a). Surface antigens of apicomplexan parasites play a crucial role in host-parasite interactions involving attachment and invasion (Lekutis et al., 2001; Tabares et al., 2004). SAG family proteins are the major surface antigens of Eimeria, and immunization of chickens with recombinant SAG proteins or DNA vaccines induces partial immune protection against the coccidia (Liu et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2020; Geng et al., 2022). Furthermore, the intracellular stages of Eimeria are immunologically accessible and are pathogenically important for the parasite (Rafiqi et al., 2018). Therefore, screening for antigens (especially surface and secreted antigens) that are highly expressed in the sporozoite, merozoite, or gametocyte stages is a rational approach for anticoccidial vaccine development.

In this study, we characterized the localization of Et-SAG14, Eten5-B, and Et-PGM1, and expressed and purified the recombinant proteins to evaluate their protective efficacy against E. tenella challenge. Our results suggest that rEten5-B induces host IFN-γ and IL-10 transcription and may serve as a potential subunit vaccine, whereas rEt-SAG14 exhibits an inhibitory effect on host immunity.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethical Statement

All animal experiments in this study were approved by the Administration Committee of Laboratory Animals in Guangxi University and were conducted in accordance with the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee guidelines (approval number: Gxu-2021-013).

Animals and Parasites

The E. tenella Houghton (ETH) strain was used throughout the study. The parasite was maintained and propagated through 1-wk-old Sanhuang chickens. Four-wk-old Sanhuang chickens were used to prepare the merozoites. Chickens were reared in a coccidia-free environment and provided with water and feed free of coccidiostat and antibiotics. Parasites were collected, purified and sporulated as described previously (Eckert et al., 1995).

Bioinformatic Analysis

The nucleotide sequences Eten5-B(ETH2_0801700), Et-SAG14(ETH2_0630800), and Et-PGM1(ETH2_1540300) were downloaded from ToxoDB (https://toxodb.org/toxo/). Transcript level data were obtained from the RNA-seq data of Chen et al. (2023). Signaling peptides as well as transmembrane structural domains were predicted using SMART (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/).

Cloning, Expression, and Purification of Recombinant Proteins

Sera

Negative sera were obtained from coccidia-free Sanhuang chickens. Positive sera were collected from Sanhuang chickens artificially infected with E. tenella. Mouse polyclonal antibodies against rEten5-B, rEt-SAG14 and rEt-PGM1 were generated as described previously (Song et al., 2021).

Immunoblotting

rEten5-B, rEt-PGM1, and rEt-SAG14 were analyzed using Western blot assay with anti-E. tenella chicken serum. Recombinant proteins were separated in 15% SDS-PAGE and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes (Millipore, Bedford, MA). The membranes were treated with 5% skimmed milk and incubated with chicken serum (1:200 dilution) at 4°C overnight. The membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated rabbit anti-chicken IgY (1:2,000 dilution, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) for 1 h at 37°C and the proteins were visualized using chemiluminescent reagents (Beyotime Biotechnology, Beijing, China) and ChemiDoc XRS+ (BioRad, Hercules, CA). Coccidia-free chicken serum was used as a negative control.

Immunofluorescence Assay

The extraction and purification of sporozoites and merozoites are consistent with previous studies (Zhang et al., 2022). For immunofluorescence, purified sporozoites and merozoites were adhered to coverslips previously coated with polylysine and fixed with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde. The coverslips were washed, and the parasites were permeabilized with 0.1% TritonX-100 and then blocked with 3% BSA for 30 min. Subsequently, samples were incubated at 37°C for 1 h with mouse polyclonal antibodies against 3 proteins (1:100), rabbit anti-Et-M2AP (Song et al., 2021) (1:100), and rabbit anti-Eten5-A (Song et al., 2020) (1:100), respectively. Then, secondary antibodies conjugated with FITC or Cy3 (1:100) and Hoechst 33258 (1:100, Sigma, St. Louis, MO) were used for labeling. DNA was stained with Hoechst (Sigma, St. Louis, MO). Images were obtained using a Zeiss fluorescence microscope system (Zeiss, Jena, Germany).

Splenic mRNA was extracted and reverse transcribed to cDNA, and the expression of cytokines (IL-2, IL-10, IFN-γ, and TGF-β) was detected using qPCR. Evaluation of the Protective Efficacy of Recombinant Proteins

One-wk-old chickens were weighed and randomly divided into 6 groups of 15 chickens each. Each chicken in the experimental group was injected subcutaneously in the neck with 100 μg of the corresponding recombinant protein. Unimmunized and challenged control group (UCC) and unimmunized and unchallenged control (UUC) chickens were injected with Freund's adjuvant. One wk after the initial immunization, a booster vaccination was administered at the same dose. Seven days after the second immunization, all chickens were orally challenged with 10,000 freshly sporulated E. tenella oocysts, and the unchallenged group received PBS (Figure 1).Figure 1Schematic outline of the animal experiments.Figure 1

Schematic outline of the animal experiments.

The protective effect was evaluated based on survival rate (%), lesion score, body weight gain, relative body weight gain (%), oocyst output, and oocyst decrease ratio (%). On the seventh day after infection, 5 chickens were killed in each group for observation of cecal lesions and scored according to the methods described by Johnson and Reid (1970). Total feces from each group were collected and weighed 5 to 10 d after infection, and total oocyst output was counted via a McMaster Chamber. The oocyst decrease ratio was calculated as (oocyst output of UCC − oocyst output of vaccinated groups)/oocyst output of UCC × 100%. Average body weight gain of chickens was calculated as average body weight at 10 days postinfection—average body weight on the day of challenge. Relative body weight gain rate was calculated as average body weight gain of experimental group/body weight gain of UUC group × 100%. Overall efficacy was mainly evaluated by the anticoccidial index (ACI), where ACI = (survival rates + relative body weight gain) − (lesion score + oocyst index). ACI ≤ 160: poor anticoccidial effect; 160<ACI<179: moderately effective; ACI ≥180: good anticoccidial effect (Li et al., 2022).

Statistical Analysis

GraphPad Prism 8 (Boston, MA) was used to create graphs and perform statistical analysis. Data are presented as means ± standard errors. All data were analyzed using multiple Student t test. P values are indicated with an asterisk as follows: *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001. All P < 0.05 were considered significant.

Recognition of Recombinant Antigens by E. Tenella-Infected Chicken Sera

To determine whether the recombinant antigens could be recognized by E. tenella-infected chicken sera, immunoblotting was performed with anti-E. tenella chicken serum (Figure 3C) or negative serum (Figure 3D). All recombinant proteins (rEt-SAG14, rEt-PGM1, and rEten5-B) were successfully recognized by anti-E. tenella chicken serum with specific band for each protein. As noted previously, rEten5-B showed a very strong signal, whereas rEt-SAG14 showed relatively weak signals. No specific bands were detected in healthy chicken control sera (Figure 3D).

Since there was no information on the protein localization of these antigens, their subcellular localization in E. tenella was studied by immunofluorescence using polyclonal antibodies. Recombinant Proteins Induce Diverse Immune Responses

To explore whether recombinant antigens could induce host protective immunity, the transcription levels of host cytokines were determined after boost immunization by qPCR. In comparison with the control group, rEt-SAG14 induced significantly lower levels of IFN-γ and IL-10 transcripts and promoted IL-4 expression (Figure 5). The TGF-β level was only slightly elevated in the rEt-PGM1-immunized group, and rEt-PGM1 also dramatically increased IL-4 level. DISCUSSION

Coccidiosis, caused by intracellular parasites of the genus Eimeria, is considered a major parasitic disease of poultry. Eimeria severely impairs growth and feed utilization in chickens, leading to reduced productivity and causing a huge economic loss of over £10 billion per year to the world poultry industry (Blake et al., 2020). Traditional control strategies rely heavily on chemoprophylaxis and live vaccines (Mesa-Pineda et al., 2021). Increasing concerns about the drug residue and resistance, coupled with the inherent limitations of live vaccines, have increased the need to develop new strategies and alternatives for coccidiosis control. Recent studies have shown that immune-based strategies, such as novel vaccines and dietary immune-modulating feed additives, can improve host defenses against intracellular parasitism and reduce intestinal damage caused by parasite-induced inflammatory responses (Blake et al., 2017; Angwech et al., 2019; Kiarie et al., 2019). In this study, 3 E. tenella antigens from different compartments of the parasites were characterized and their recombinant proteins were successfully obtained and evaluated for their immunoprotective efficiency against E. tenella.

Since the localization of Et-SAG14, Et-PGM1, and Eten5-B was unknown, the distribution of Et-SAG14, Et-PGM1, and Eten5-B in sporozoites and merozoites of E. tenella was assessed using an immunofluorescence assay. Et-PGM1 was distributed in the apical end of the sporozoites and merozoites and was partially colocalized with the M2AP protein, similar to the localization of the parafusin-related protein (PRP1, which belongs to the phosphoglucomutase superfamily) in T. gondii tachyzoites (Matthiesen et al., 2001, 2003). However, we were not able to analyze its localization in gametocytes, mainly because of the difficulty in purifying E. tenella gametocytes. Eten5-B was previously annotated as a rhoptry protein in E. tenella; however, Eten5-B is dot localized in the cytoplasm. This is very different from the previously reported rhoptry localization of Eten5-A (Song et al., 2020). Et-SAG14 was found not to be localized to the surface of the parasite, but scattered in the cytoplasm near the nucleus in sporozoites and merozoites. The localization of these proteins may provide some clues for further studies on their biological and molecular functions.

In vitro experiments have shown that IFN-γ is an immunomodulator that directly inhibits intracellular development of coccidia(Lillehoj and Choi, 1998). A recent study that compared the immune response of chicken lines with different resistance to E. maxima found that the increased expression of IFN-γ and IL-10 may be the key to anticoccidiosis (Bremner et al., 2021). These results indicate that IFN- γ and IL-10 play an important role in anticoccidia immunity. To characterize the host immune response to these antigens, the transcription levels of cytokines were measured by qPCR after immunization. We found that IFN-γ and IL-10 levels were significantly elevated after rEten5-B immunization, which may suggest that rEten5-B confers protective immunity to chickens. This was further confirmed by our vaccination-challenge experiments, where rEten5-B immunization achieved higher average body weight gain, less intestinal lesion scores and significantly reduced oocyst production. An ACI index of 161 suggests that rEten5-B is a promising candidate for an anticoccidia subunit vaccine.

However, we also noticed that rEt-SAG14 significantly decreased IFN-γ and IL-10 levels and increased the IL-4 levels. Animal experiments also showed that rEt-SAG14 immunization could not provide protection against E. tenella infection. These results suggest that Et-SAG14 proteins may be immunosuppressive factors. This result is reminiscent of the research of Song et al. (2020), which demonstrated that surface antigens provide only very limited protection against E. tenella. The rEt-PGM1 also repressed the expression of host IFN-γ and IL-2, thereby not observing a protective effect against E. tenella infection.

In conclusion, we identified 3 E. tenella proteins and evaluated their immune protective effects when administered as vaccines. The data obtained suggest that rEten5-B has the potential to serve as an effective vaccine candidate against E. tenella infection, whereas the rEt-SAG14 and rEt-PGM1 proteins are unsuitable for subunit vaccine development.

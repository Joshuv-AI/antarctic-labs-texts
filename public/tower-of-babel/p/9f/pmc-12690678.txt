PDNO-20 Case of use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy in the management of a pineal region tumor
Authors: Tafadzwa Moreen Dube, Simbarashe Sanyamandwe, Nathaniel Zimani, Shungu Ushewokunze, Luxwell Jokonya, Kazadi K Kalangu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe mainstay management of pineal region tumors is excision or biopsy followed by adjuvant chemo-radiotherapy except in the setting of positive germ cell tumors markers where adjuvant therapy is the first line of treatment. The use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy is no longer recommended, but it’s still relevant in low-resource settings where approach to the pineal region masses is technically challenging and associated with high morbidity and mortality.CaseWe present a case of a 5/F previously well patient who presented with a 2-month history of projectile vomiting, progressive right-sided weakness and gait disturbances till she couldn’t mobilise. She had a CT scan done, which showed a pineal region tumor with associated hydrocephalus. immediate CSF diversion done, using a Ventriculo-peritoneal shunt. She had significant improvement but deteriorated 3 weeks post shunting, with repeat imaging showing enlargement of the tumor. The patient was noted to be unstable for surgery, a Multidisciplinary neurooncology meeting was held, and resulted in a consensus for neoadjuvant therapy with vincristine, etoposide, carboplatin, and she received three cycles. Repeat imaging showed marked cytoreduction, patient improved clinically, able to mobilise and communicate. Tumor biopsy was done twice, via the supracerebellar infratentorial approach. The initial biopsy was inconclusive, and the procedure was abandoned due to torrential bleeding. There was also a challenge of inavailability of neuro-navigation systems. Re-biopsy histology showed a small round blue cell tumor with immunohistochemistry suggestive of pineoblastoma. Day 10 CSF showed evidence of spinal seeding, necessitating referral for cranio-spinal radiation whilst continuing on chemotherapy. Conclusion. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy is still relevant in the management of pineal region tumors in low resource settings where limited resources make carrying out complex surgical procedures even more technically challenging.

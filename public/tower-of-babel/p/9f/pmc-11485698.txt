A pathological complete response to capecitabine plus oxaliplatin regimen combined with tislelizumab in advanced gastric cancer with liver metastases: A case report
Authors: Li-Ping Sheng, Yun-Lin Huang, Zhi Wang, Hai-Fang Zhang, Jin-Yan Zhang, Xiao-Yi Lei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTA 66-year-old female patient presenting with dysphagia was diagnosed with stage IV unresectable gastric cancer (cTxN+M1). Multiple liver metastases were identified. The patient subsequently underwent five courses of chemotherapy and immunotherapy, including the capecitabine plus oxaliplatin (XELOX) regimen combined with tislelizumab. After fifth course treatment, it was confirmed that the liver metastases had completely disappeared and the primary tumor had significantly reduced in size. Consequently, a laparoscopy was performed, revealing a retraction-like response in the primary tumor and no obvious metastases in the abdominal cavity. Subsequently, a radical total gastrectomy was carried out through open abdominal surgery. Pathological analysis showed no remaining cancer or lymph node metastases, and the tumor regression was classified as grade 0. The patient has been now receiving additional chemotherapy and immunotherapy to manage any potential residual metastases. This case illustrated the rare and significant impact of combining chemotherapy with tislelizumab, transitioning the treatment approach from palliative to curative. It highlighted the critical role of immunotherapy in managing advanced gastric cancer with liver metastases.KEYWORDS: Conversion surgery, tislelizumab, R0 resection, unresectable gastric cancer

Introduction

Despite the latest advancements in multidisciplinary treatments, individuals diagnosed with stage IV gastric cancer continue to face a poor prognosis.1 However, the combination of chemotherapy with anti-programmed death-1 (anti-PD-1) therapy has shown promise as an effective strategy for improving outcomes in patients with advanced gastric cancer with distant metastasis.2 Tislelizumab is currently recommended as a first-line treatment option for individuals with unresectable advanced gastric cancer and positive programmed death-ligand 1 (PD‐L1) expression in China.3 Nevertheless, there are few reports of a complete response (CR) following treatment with tislelizumab combined the capecitabine plus oxaliplatin (XELOX) regimen in patients with stage IV gastric cancer with liver metastases.

Patient presentation

A 66-year-old woman complaining of dysphagia was diagnosed with gastric cancer. An esophagogastroduodenoscopy revealed a large ulcer involving the gastric fundus, cardia, and lower end of the esophagus (Figure 1). The patient had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status (PS) of 1. Histopathological analysis of the biopsied specimen revealed a poorly differentiated carcinoma with possibly partial neuroendocrine differentiation (Figure 2). Positron emission tomography – computed tomography (PET-CT) showed multiple liver metastases (Figure 3a). This led to a diagnosis of stage Ⅳ gastric cancer (cTxN+M1). The patient’s medical and familial history was unremarkable, and the abdominal physical examination showed no significant findings. Pre-treatment serum tumor marker data were unavailable. After being diagnosed with HER2-negative status and a PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) of 8 (Figure 4), the patient underwent five courses of chemotherapy and immunotherapy, including the XELOX regimen combined with tislelizumab. The XELOX regimen consisted of a 21-day course of oral capecitabine (1000 mg/m2) taken from days 1 to 14, along with an intravenous dose of oxaliplatin (130 mg/m2) given on day 1. After the fifth course of treatment, enhanced CT confirmed the disappearance of the liver metastases and a significant reduction in the primary tumor. Consequently, a laparoscopy was performed, revealing a retraction-like response in the primary tumor and no obvious metastases in the abdominal cavity. The patient then underwent a radical total gastrectomy through open abdominal surgery. Pathological analysis of the surgical specimen showed no remaining tumors or lymph node metastases (Figure 5). The findings indicated a pathological CR (pCR) in the primary tumor, with the tumor regression grade classified as grade 0. The patient’s postoperative recovery was uncomplicated, and she was discharged on the eighth day after the procedure. Approximately six weeks after post-surgery, PET-CT showed no detected metastases (Figure 3b). Currently, the patient has been undergoing additional chemotherapy and immunotherapy to manage the potential metastatic spread. She remains alive five months after the diagnosis of stage Ⅳ gastric cancer, with no evidence of disease recurrence two months after surgery.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first instance of advanced gastric cancer with liver metastases achieving a pCR after treatment with XELOX chemotherapy and tislelizumab, an anti – PD-1 antibody. Immunotherapy targeting PD-1/PD-L1 represents a revolutionary approach in cancer treatment, potentially offering the most effective and safest method by fulling activating and utilizing tumor-killing T cells.4 Recently, clinical trials have investigated tislelizumab for treating advanced gastric cancer, providing substantial evidence of its safety and efficacy.5,6

Systemic chemotherapy remains the cornerstone of treatment for patients with metastatic gastric cancer.7 However, increasing research suggests that combining immunotherapy with chemotherapy is more effective than chemotherapy alone for these patients. In individuals with a CPS ≥ 1, combining immunotherapy with chemotherapy has been shown to improve overall survival compared to chemotherapy alone.8 This may be a key factor in the patient with a CPS of 8 achieving a pCR.

While combining immunotherapy and chemotherapy generally yields better outcomes, achieving a pCR in advanced gastric cancer with liver metastasis after treatment with tislelizumab and the XELOX regimen is rare. Tislelizumab enhances cytokine production and restores T-cell function by blocking PD-L1 related signaling pathways thus preventing immune evasion and leading to tumor cell death.9 The patient’s pCR underscore the potential superiority of the tislelizumab plus the XELOX regimen in treating stage Ⅳ gastric cancer.

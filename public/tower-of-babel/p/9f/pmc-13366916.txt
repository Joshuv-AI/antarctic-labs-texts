Prognostic impact of epidermal growth factor receptor mutation in grade 3 stage IA lung adenocarcinomas
Authors: Haoyao Jiang, Han Lin, Weiyu Pan, Chunlai Lu, Jie Gu, Fengkai Xu, Zongwei Chen, Teng Ma, Di Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesStage IA adenocarcinomas (LUAD) with high-risk features exhibited an increased recurrence rate, and the role of adjuvant therapy in this population remains controversial. Our study aimed to evaluate the prognostic impact of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation in grade 3 stage IA LUAD.MethodsBetween 2016 and 2020, 483 consecutive patients undergoing operation, with grade 3 pathologic stage IA LUAD were evaluated. The Kaplan-Meier analysis was performed to assess recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS). Prognostic factors were evaluated using the Cox proportional hazards model.ResultsAn EGFR mutation was identified in 238 patients (49.3%), including 122 (51.3%) with an exon 19 deletion (Ex 19), 100 (42.0%) with an exon 21 L858R (Ex 21), and 16 (6.7%) with other EGFR mutations. The adjuvant chemotherapy was performed in 23 (4.8%) cases. EGFR mutant showed worse RFS than EGFR wild type (5-year RFS, 88.8% vs. 94.4%, p = 0.016). Subgroup analysis stratified by tumor size indicated that, among patients with pT1c tumors, EGFR mutant had a statistically lower 5-year RFS compared with EGFR wild type (n = 117; 83.1% vs. 96.2%; p = 0.036). Multivariable Cox regression analysis revealed that perineural invasion and EGFR mutation (HR = 2.095, 95%CI, 1.118 to 3.924) were independent predictors of worse RFS.ConclusionsIn grade 3 stage IA3 LUAD, the presence of an EGFR mutation was associated with a poorer prognosis compared with EGFR wild type. These findings underscore the potential need to explore tailored adjuvant treatment strategies in this subgroup.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04411-6.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinomas, Epidermal growth factor receptor, Pathological grading system

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the most common histological subtype of lung cancer, exhibits substantial molecular and morphological heterogeneity [1, 2]. This variability complicates both prognosis and therapeutic decisions, highlighting the need for accurate tumor classification. Building on the 2015 WHO classification [3], the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) proposed an updated histological grading system to improve the precision of clinical outcome predictions [4]. The IASLC grading system employs a 20% threshold for high-grade pattern classification and distinctly separates complex glandular patterns from the conventional acinar patterns. According to the IASLC criteria, tumors containing 20% or more of high-grade patterns-specifically solid, micropapillary, or complex glandular growth-are categorized as poorly differentiated. This distinction is clinically important, as high-grade components are associated with more aggressive disease and poorer survival, underscoring the value of histological grading in guiding clinical management.

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation represents one of the most clinically significant molecular alterations in LUAD, as targeted therapy with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) has demonstrated substantial survival benefits in affected patients [5, 6]. Emerging evidence indicates that EGFR-mutated tumors are often associated with more favorable clinical outcomes, possibly related to the higher prevalence of ground-glass opacity patterns in this molecular subset [7–9]. However, not all EGFR variants confer the same prognostic implications. Several studies have reported that patients with exon 19 deletions (Ex 19) experience significantly shorter recurrence-free survival than those with exon 21 L858R substitutions (Ex 21) in stage I-III lung adenocarcinoma [9, 10]. Despite these insights, the integration of EGFR mutation status into the IASLC histological grading system remains limited. Further researches at the intersection of histological grade and genetic profiling are essential, as it may enhance prognostic stratification and support more individualized postoperative treatment strategies.

The landmark ADAURA trial demonstrated the efficacy of adjuvant osimertinib in patients with resected stage IB-IIIA EGFR-mutant NSCLC, providing compelling evidence for its incorporation into standard postoperative management [11]. Although these findings represent significant advances in targeted therapy, evidence remains limited regarding the benefit of adjuvant treatment-whether chemotherapy or EGFR TKIs-for stage IA LUAD with high-risk features. The ongoing phase III ADAURA-2 trial is investigating whether comparable postoperative benefits can be achieved in patients with earlier-stage disease (stage IA2-IA3) [12]. Furthermore, the prognostic implications of EGFR mutations in individuals with grade 3 LUAD are not yet well defined. Therefore, the present study aims to clarify the prognostic impact of EGFR mutations in patients with grade 3 stage IA LUAD.

Patients and methods

Patient selection

From January 2016 to September 2020, we retrospectively reviewed consecutive patients with invasive LUAD who underwent surgery at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Zhongshan Hospital. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) mucinous, colloid, or enteric type adenocarcinoma; (2) a prior history of lung cancer; (3) multifocal invasive lung cancer; (4) incomplete EGFR mutation data; (5) concurrent multiple EGFR mutations; and (6) unavailable follow-up data. A total of 483 patients with grade 3 stage IA LUAD were included (Fig. 1). Preoperative brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and bone scans were performed to rule out distant metastasis. No patients received neoadjuvant therapy, and all patients underwent R0 resection. Regardless of the extent of resection, systematic dissection of at least three mediastinal lymph node stations was performed.

Flow diagram of the study cohort

We collected clinical and pathological data including sex, age, smoking history, preoperative serum carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) level, extent of pulmonary resection, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), perineural invasion (PI), spread through air spaces (STAS), EGFR mutation subtype, TNM stage according to the ninth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging manual [13], and adjuvant treatment status. The main postoperative chemotherapy regimens consisted of cisplatin or carboplatin in combination with pemetrexed. None of the patients received EGFR-TKIs as adjuvant therapy or perioperative radiation. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Zhongshan Hospital (B2021-128), and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to its retrospective design.

Pathologic examination and gene mutation examination

The hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained tumor slides were independently assessed by two attending pathologists, then subjected to central verification by a chief pathologist (L.R.). The proportions of micropapillary, solid, and complex glandular patterns were quantified in 5% increments. Histologic grading was determined according to the system proposed by IASLC [4]. The analysis of EGFR mutation was routinely performed for patients with surgically resected LUAD. Genomic DNA was extracted from tumor specimens, and EGFR mutation status was analyzed using the ADx-ARMS (Amplification Refractory Mutation System) real-time PCR kit (Amoy Diagnostics, Xiamen, China). The assay targeted 29 common mutations in exons 18–21 of the EGFR gene, including exon 19 deletions, L858R and L861Q in exon 21, G719X in exon 18, and T790M and insertions in exon 20 alterations.

Patient follow-up

Follow-up data were obtained via outpatient visits and telephone interviews. Postoperative examinations were performed every 3 months during the first 2 years, and every 6 months thereafter. The follow-up evaluations included chest computed tomography (CT) scans, neck and abdominal ultrasonography, and blood tumor markers. Brain MRI and bone scans were performed when patients presented with symptoms or signs of recurrence. Biopsies were performed on patients with suspected metastases whenever feasible. In cases where biopsy was clinically challenging, positron emission tomography/computed tomography scans were utilized for diagnostic support. Crucially, newly developed pulmonary lesions that were pathologically confirmed as second primary tumors were strictly excluded from the recurrence count. The date of recurrence was defined as the date of diagnosis of recurrence.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables are expressed as medians with interquartile ranges. The χ² test was used for intergroup comparisons of categorical variables. The Mann-Whitney U test was applied to continuous variables. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the date of recurrence, death from any cause, or last follow-up. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to the date of death or last follow-up. Survival probabilities were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Difference in survival was assessed via the log-rank method. Cox regression with Firth’s penalized likelihood was applied to assess the impact of various factors on RFS. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.1.3 and IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported.

Results

Clinicopathologic characteristics

Among the 483 patients, 238 (49.3%) patients harbored an EGFR mutation (Table 1). The numbers of patients with Ex 19, Ex 21, and uncommon mutations were 122 (25.3%), 100 (20.7%), and 16 (3.3%), respectively. EGFR mutations were significantly more prevalent in women (p<0.001) and non-smokers (p<0.001). The presence of EGFR mutations was similar across pT1a, pT1b, and pT1c tumors. Pathologically, the prevalence of STAS was significantly lower in tumors with EGFR mutations (p = 0.037). No significant difference was observed in the extent of resection or adjuvant therapy between the EGFR wild type and mutation groups.

Impact of EGFR mutation on prognosis

The median follow-up time was 60.0 months (IQR, 58.6–61.4 months). During follow-up, recurrence occurred in 28 (11.8%) patients with EGFR mutations and 15 (6.1%) patients with EGFR wild type. The 5-year RFS was significantly worse in patients with EGFR mutations compared with those with EGFR wild type (88.8% vs. 94.4%, p = 0.016; Fig. 2A). The 5-year OS was not significantly different between the two groups (96.8% vs. 97.7%, p = 0.371; Fig. 2B). No statistical difference was observed in RFS or OS between patients with Ex 19 and Ex 21 mutations (Fig. 3).

Discussion

The prognostic significance of EGFR mutation in resected LUAD remains controversial, largely owing to heterogeneity in study populations and disease characteristics. Emerging evidence indicates that EGFR mutations may be associated with a favorable prognosis in lower-grade tumors or those with ground-glass opacity predominance. Whereas Aokage et al. found that EGFR mutations were not correlated with overall prognosis but were linked to higher recurrence rates among patients with stage IA3 and IB disease without GGO components [14]. Similarly, Hattori et al. identified EGFR mutations as an adverse prognostic factor in patients with c-T1c/T2a pure-solid LUAD, underscoring its detrimental impact in more solid and invasive tumors [7]. Collectively, these findings suggest that the prognostic impact of EGFR mutation might shift from favorable to detrimental with increasing tumor aggressiveness, reflecting underlying biological heterogeneity. In our study, EGFR mutation appeared to be associated with shorter RFS among patients with grade 3 stage IA3 adenocarcinoma, aligning with prior research demonstrating a adverse prognostic role in early-stage subgroups with high risk.

Notably, routine testing for EGFR mutations in resected adenocarcinoma is performed at our institution, enabling the analysis of a consecutive patient cohort. In our study, 49.3% of patients harbored EGFR mutations, similar with a prevalence reported in East Asian populations [15, 16]. The distribution of Ex 19 and Ex 21 was relatively balanced, together accounting for 93.3% of all EGFR mutations. Previous studies have suggested that Ex 19 may be associated with worse a prognosis compared with other EGFR mutation subtypes in early-stage LUAD [9, 10]. However, our findings revealed no significant prognostic difference between the Ex 19 and Ex 21 subtypes. Furthermore, examination of EGFR mutation in resected adenocarcinoma not only facilitates accurate pathological evaluation but also guides treatment selection, particularly with respect to the use of EGFR-TKIs upon recurrence. One possible explanation for the discordance between recurrence and overall survival is the availability of effective EGFR-TKIs upon recurrence.

The landmark phase III ADAURA trial demonstrated substantial survival benefits with adjuvant Osimertinib, while the ongoing ADAURA2 trial is further evaluating the efficacy of adjuvant osimertinib in patients with earlier-stage NSCLC (stage IA2-IA3). It is well established that the high-risk histologic features, such as solid and micropapillary components, correlate with poorer prognosis and elevated recurrence rates [17, 18]. The IASLC grading system classifies tumors with 20% or more high-grade patterns as poorly differentiated, emphasizing the prognostic significance of histologic assessment. Additionally, vascular invasion, visceral pleural involvement, and perineural invasion are recognized high-risk pathological markers in NSCLC [19–21]. Deng et al. reported that grade 3 LUAD with EGFR mutations exhibited poorer RFS but better OS compared with those with EGFR wild type [22]. The two-arm APPOINT trial is evaluating the efficacy of adjuvant aumolertinib in stage IA EGFR mutant NSCLC patients with high-risk features (with 10% or more high-grade patterns). Preliminary results demonstrated a 2-year RFS rate of 100% in the aumolertinib group, compared with 75% in the observation group (HR = 0.116, p = 0.033) [23]. These insights underscore the importance of integrating histopathologic characteristics with molecular profiling to achieve more precise risk stratification and personalized treatment strategies. Our findings identify grade 3 stage IA3 LUAD with EGFR mutation as a subgroup with elevated recurrence risk. Whether these patients would benefit from adjuvant EGFR-TKI therapy remains to be addressed by ongoing prospective trials.

This study has several limitations. First, this is a single-center retrospective study conducted in an East Asian population, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups with different EGFR mutation prevalence and tumor biology. Second, postoperative brain MRI and bone scans were performed only when clinically indicated rather than routinely, potentially leading to underestimation of recurrence. Although Firth’s penalized Cox regression was employed to address small-sample bias, the relatively low number of recurrence events may still have affected the stability and precision of the multivariable estimates. Consequently, the observed associations should be interpreted with caution and regarded as hypothesis‐generating. Third, detailed information on post-recurrence treatment, including the use and duration of EGFR-TKIs, was not systematically captured, precluding formal evaluation of how subsequent therapy may have influenced overall survival. In addition, adjuvant chemotherapy was administered to only a small subset of patients, precluding a robust assessment of its therapeutic impact. Future multicenter prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and further elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms.

In conclusion, among patients with grade 3 stage IA3 LUAD, EGFR mutation was associated with a shorter RFS compared with EGFR wild type. No significant difference in RFS or OS was observed between patients harboring Ex 19 and Ex 21 mutations. These findings suggest that assessment of EGFR mutation status may provide prognostic insight and inform post-recurrence treatment strategies in early-stage lung cancer. The value of adjuvant EGFR-TKI therapy in this subgroup warrants confirmation in prospective randomized trials.

Model-free adaptive consensus design for a class of unknown heterogeneous nonlinear multi-agent systems with packet dropouts
Authors: Ye Ren, Shida Liu, Deli Li, Dongxu Zhang, Ting Lei, Li Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper studies the consensus problem for a class of unknown heterogeneous nonlinear multi-agent systems via a network with random packet dropouts. Based on the dynamic linearization technique, novel model-free adaptive consensus protocols with the data compensation mechanism are designed for both leaderless and leader-following cases. The advantage of this approach is that only neighborhood input and output data of the agents are required in the protocol design. For the stability analysis, a new Squeeze Theorem based method is developed to derive the theoretic results instead of the traditional contraction mapping principle used in model-free adaptive control. It is shown that the consensus can be achieved for both leaderless and leader-following cases if the communication topology is strongly connected. Finally, numerical simulations verifying the correctness of the theoretical results are given.Keywords: Data-driven control, Multi-agent systems, Model-free adaptive control, Output consensus, Packet dropouts.Subject terms: Engineering, Information theory and computation, Techniques and instrumentation

Introduction

Multi-agent systems (MASs) have been researched with increasing attentions in the past decade because of their widespread applications including power systems, multi-robot systems coordination, traffic signal control, and distributed reconfigurable sensor networks1–4. Consensus is one of the key problems in MASs. The task of this problem is to find a distributed protocol such that all agents can agree on certain quantities of interest. Plenty of approaches have been designed concerning two types of consensus problems, i.e., leaderless consensus and leader-following consensus5.

For the leaderless consensus, the agents are driven by the distributed protocol to a consensus equilibrium point5. As for the leader-following consensus, all followers try to track the leader’s trajectory only using their local information5. A large number of algorithms were designed to address the consensus problem for different agent dynamics5–12. A pioneering work for first-order agents was presented in Ref.5. In Ref.6, an inverse optimal approach was proposed for linear agents with global quadratic performance indexes. Approximation dynamic programming was utilized for general linear and nonlinear MASs in Ref.7 and Ref.8, respectively. In Ref.9, an artificial neural network (NN) based adaptive protocol was developed for Brunovsky form high-order nonlinear MASs. In Ref.10, a NN based robust adaptive controller was designed for heterogeneous nonlinear MASs with disturbance. The event-triggered controller was considered for affine nonlinear MASs with quantized information in Ref.11. In Ref.12, optimal iterative learning control was used to address the consensus problem in a finite-time interval for heterogeneous nonlinear MASs under the repetitive environment.

In practical applications, the phenomenon of packet dropouts frequently appears due to various reasons, e.g., the limited communication capacity13, a temporal failure of sensors14, the high noise environment15, etc. In MASs, the communication network is essential for coordination. Therefore, data dropouts on communication networks have been widely studied in the form of dynamic topologies16. Numerous protocols under dynamic topologies have also been developed for the leaderless case16–18and the leader-following case19,20. The necessary and sufficient condition over random topology for single integrators was derived in Ref.16. Then, the result for double integrators under random topology was given in Ref.17. In Ref.18, the consensus problem of MASs with homogenous second-order nonlinear dynamics and random topology was discussed. The convergence analysis was conducted based on the time average graph of the topology. On the leader-following side, the neighbor-based feedback law was designed for single integrators under switching topology in Ref.19. In Ref.20, the basis function expression was used to approximate the nonlinear term and the adaptive consensus laws are designed for heterogeneous nonlinear MASs under jointly connected topology. In the recent research21, the decentralized control problem for a class of affine nonlinear large-scale systems with random packet dropouts and sensor delays was solved by an adaptive fuzzy controller. For the overview of the state-of-the-art approaches for the MASs consensus before 2013, readers are referred to Ref.22.

However, the protocols design or analysis in Refs.6,7,16–19depends on the known agent models, which is difficult to build exactly for the practical MASs. Furthermore, the control systems in Refs.8,10–12,18,20,21 can only be designed for affine nonlinear systems. The application of iterative learning control12 is limited to the tasks with periodic or repeated nature. The approach in Ref.21 has different points of focus and cannot be directly applied to solve the consensus problem of nonaffine MASs. Hence, handling the consensus problem of unknown heterogeneous nonlinear MASs under random topologies by using only local information is still a challenging topic.

Model free adaptive control (MFAC) is a typical data-driven approach initially proposed in Ref.23. In the past 20 years, it has been developed with systematic works including new concepts, new dynamic linearization data models, parameter tuning methods, controller design methods, stability analyses, and applications for SISO systems, MIMO systems, complex connected systems, and repetitive systems24. Nowadays, it has been successfully applied in over 150 different fields25–33. Some latest progress on the MFAC includes the stability analysis for full form MFAC23, and the applications in wireless communication systems34, implantable heart pump35, wind farm36, wound rotor synchronous machine37, power systems38, high-speed trains39, traffic signal control systems40, etc. In MASs consensus control field, MFAC has also been used to design control laws. In Ref.41, the consensus problem of unknown heterogeneous nonlinear MASs was solved by using MFAC under switching topology. In Ref.42, a model-free adaptive iterative learning protocol was proposed for finite-time consensus under switching topology. To coordinate the networked MASs, a new cloud computing predictive MFAC method was employed in Ref.43. In Ref.44, an attack compensation mechanism have been developed to create a learning-based distributed MFAC algorithm that effectively counters denial-of-service attacks. In Ref.45, to treat the sensor fault, a MFAC-based distributed formation control algorithm was proposed for MIMO MASs. However, the data-driven MFAC-based protocol in Refs.41–45has not been discussed under random topologies. In addition, the design in Refs.41–43,45 is only suitable for leader-following consensus. Compared with the above-mentioned works, the main contributions of this work are summarized as follows.

A novel distributed model-free adaptive consensus controller is proposed for a class of heterogeneous nonlinear nonaffine multi-agent systems with random packet dropouts under random communication topologies, which has not been considered in previous works41–45.

To further claim the challenges, the differences between the leaderless and leader-following consensus are summarized as follows: (1) The identities of the agents are different. In the leaderless consensus, all agents are equally important. In contrast, in the leader-following consensus, there is a leader who generates the desired trajectory. (2) The control goals are different as defined in Eqs. (2, 3), which increases the difficulty to solve the consensus problem in an unified framework. (3) The controller design and stability analysis process are different. For the controller design, the leader’s information should be involved in the control law and the control result is affected by the leader’s behavior. For the stability analysis process, the analysis of the leaderless case usually uses the graph theory, matrix theory, and linear system theory to find the sufficient conditions. Conversely, it usually constructs the tracking error dynamics first in the leader-following case and then uses the Lyapunov theory or contraction mapping principle to derive the convergence. Generally speaking, the methods used in the two cases cannot be applied to each other directly.

C. Dynamic linearization technique

By introducing novel concepts called pseudo-partial derivative (PPD) or pseudo-gradient , the DL method establishes an equivalent linearized data model for every operation point of the controlled system. According to different memory lengths, the DL model is classified into three forms, i.e., the compact-form dynamic linearization (CFDL) data model, the partial-form dynamic linearization data model, and the full-form dynamic linearization data model27,30. By applying CFDL data model (4), the heterogeneous agent dynamics has been transformed into an equivalent incremental form with an unknown time-varying PPD. However, the unknown time-varying PPD makes the stability analysis difficult because tradition tools in the MASs consensus become unavailable. Remark 10

As shown in Appendices A, B, a novel Squeeze Theorem (Lemma 4) based method has been used in the stability proof. The basic idea of it includes three steps: (1) Analyze the convergence condition for each agent. (2) Define max\min outputs of the MASs and prove the monotonicity of them. (3) Use Squeeze Theorem to prove the convergence of the max\min outputs. The advantages of this method can be summarized as follows.

First, it avoids analyzing the tracking error dynamics which is required in most existing works5–12,18–20,41,42. Instead, it attempts to prove the convergence of the max\min outputs of the MASs as defined in (24, 25). This feature gives a new direction when the analysis of the tracking error is difficult. For example, the tracking error of leaderless consensus is hard to define since there is no leader.

Second, during its analysis process, the global dynamics of the MASs (always required in other methods5,6,8–12,16–20,41,42) are not necessarily needed. Observing Eqs. (27)–(54), one can see these results have been derived without constructing the global dynamics. Generally speaking, it is unavoidable to introduce some complex operations like Kronecker products when analyzing the global dynamics. Therefore, this property reduces the difficulty in the analysis process.

Third, it can be easily transplanted to analyze other MFAC-based control schemes. For instance, when there is only one leader and one follower, the proof in this paper regresses to the proof of the tracking problem for the single-input and single-output system.

Conclusions

A novel distributed data-driven consensus approach, called MFACP, is proposed for both leaderless and leader-following consensus based on the DL technique. The protocol design only depends on the I/O data of the agents. To conduct the convergence analysis, the paper develops a novel Squeeze Theorem based analytical method which does not need the global error dynamics of the plant. With some acceptable assumptions, the theoretic results imply that consensus can be achieved under topology with random data dropouts for a class of unknown discrete-time heterogeneous nonlinear MASs. The effectiveness of the protocols is validated by numerical simulations. The consensus protocol design based on the partial-form or full-form dynamic linearization will be investigated as future work.

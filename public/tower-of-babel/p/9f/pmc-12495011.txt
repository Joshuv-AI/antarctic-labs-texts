EYA1 promotes tumor angiogenesis in colorectal cancer by activating HIF-1β through LSD2-mediated H3K4me2 demethylation
Authors: Shaoxin Cai, Jiansheng Wu, Naisen Wang, Chengchuan Zheng, Jianglin Su, Changqing Zeng, Jinsi Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundColorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common cancers worldwide, with tumor angiogenesis playing a crucial role in its progression. The Drosophila Eyes Absent Homologue 1(Eya1) has been implicated in various cancers, but its mechanism in CRC angiogenesis remains unclear. This study explores the mechanism by which Eya1 regulates angiogenesis in CRC by activating HIF-1β through Lysine-specific demethylases 2 (LSD2).MethodsCRC tissues and cell lines were analyzed to assess Eya1 and LSD2 using qPCR and Western blot. VEGFA expression and endothelial cell proliferation were measured using ELISA and tube formation assays. Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays were used to investigate protein interactions and histone modifications.ResultsWe found that Eya1 and LSD2 were significantly upregulated in CRC tissues and cell lines. Eya1 overexpression increased VEGFA expression and promoted endothelial cell proliferation and tube formation, and the effects were abolished upon silencing LSD2 or HIF-1β. Additionally, Eya1 was shown to interact with Dach1, a co-stimulatory factor, to regulate LSD2 expression and its activity in promoting HIF-1β-mediated angiogenesis.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that Eya1 promotes CRC angiogenesis through the LSD2/HIF-1β/VEGF pathway. These findings identify a novel mechanism of angiogenesis regulation in CRC, suggesting that targeting the Eya1-LSD2-HIF-1β axis may provide new therapeutic strategies for CRC treatment.Keywords: Eya1, Angiogenesis, Colorectal cancer, LSD2, HIF-1β

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most frequently diagnosed cancers worldwide with the second cancer-related morbidity and mortality (Benson, et al. 2024).The formation and regeneration of tumor blood vessels plays an important role in tumor progression. Angiogenesis can be activated by the expression of pro-angiogenic factors such as VEGF and VE-cadherin (Ai et al. 2022). Currently, bevacizumab has been used to target angiogenesis of CRC in clinical practice, and clinical trials have also shown that patients with advanced stage CRC can benefit from bevacizumab alone or in combination with bevacizumab plus chemotherapy (Ai et al. 2022; Xie et al. 2020), suggesting that VEGF inhibitors have anti-tumor effects. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the regulatory mechanism of angiogenesis in CRC.

HIF-1 (hypoxia inducible factor-1)/VEGF pathway is one of the most important anti-angiogenesis pathways (Wang et al. 2013). HIF-1 is a heterodimer composed of two subunits of HIF-1a and HIF-1β, in which HIF-1β protein is not regulated by oxygen and is stably expressed in cells.Despite its stable expression, recent studies suggest that HIF-1β plays an active regulatory role in modulating the output of HIF signaling, including pathways independent of acute hypoxia (Pufe et al. 2004; Maxwell et al. 1997).The EYA1 (Eyes Absent Homologue 1) is a crucial gene involved in the development of various tissues and organs. Previous studies have found that overexpression of EYA1 was associated with various diseases, including Wilms’ tumors (Drake et al. 2009), breast cancer (Wu et al. 2013) and gastric cancer (Siciliano et al. 2022). We previously also found that Eya1 was highly expressed in colorectal cancer and activates PI3K/Akt pathway to up-regulates Hif-1a, thereby promoting angiogenesis (Blancher et al. 2001).

In eukaryotic gene regulation, histone demethylation serves as a crucial epigenetic mechanism. Lysine-specific demethylases (LSDs), including LSD1 and LSD2 (Huang et al. 2018). LSDs play important roles in regulating the HIF-1/VEGF pathway, which is associated with tumor invasion and metastasis. Since our study aims to explore transcriptional regulation of LSD2 by Eya1, we focused on the role of HIF-1β as a potential nuclear cofactor in this axis. Studies have shown that LSD2 is highly expressed in breast cancer, promoting cell proliferation and acting as a transcription factor to drive the high expression of LSD1 in these cells (Chen et al. 2017). Both LSD1 and LSD2 can be modulated by post-transcriptional modifications within the HIF-1/VEGF pathway. This mutual regulation between the two enzymes is known to affect angiogenesis in some tumor types, as LSD2 expression in breast cancer, for instance, can upregulate LSD1 (Yang et al. 2015). However, the specific roles and mechanisms of these demethylases—especially LSD2—in colorectal cancer invasion and metastasis remain unclear. Therefore, this study investigates the relationship between EYA1 and LSD2 in colorectal cancer, with a focus on their involvement in angiogenesis associated with colon cancer progression.

Results

Eya1 was upregulated in human colorectal cancer and closely correlated with LSD2 in CRC

To investigate the expression of Eya1 and LSD2 in colorectal cancer (CRC), we extracted tumor tissues and paired adjacent non-cancerous tissues (ANCT) from 13 patients with CRC and analyzed them using qPCR and WB.Among the 13 colorectal cancer (CRC) patients included in our study, 5 cases were classified as stage I–II, and 8 cases as stage III–IV based on pathological staging. At the mRNA level, the results showed that the expression of Eya1 and LSD2 was significantly higher in colorectal cancer tissues compared to normal tissues (P < 0.05). And the correlation coefficient between Eya1 and LSD2 mRNA expression was 0.5912 (r = 0.5912, P = 0.0015) (Fig. 1A). To further validate these findings, we assessed the protein expression of Eya1 and LSD2 in tumor and normal tissues through WB and IHC (Cai et al. 2020; Cai et al. 2018), and observed a significant upregulation of both proteins in cancer tissues (Fig. 1B andC).

The expression of Eya1 and LSD2 were elevated in colorectal cancer. A–C qRT-PCR, WB and IHC analyses of EYA1 and LSD2 expression in 13 pairs of CRC tissues and normal tissues. The statistical significance is shown for comparisons of tumor tissues versus normal tissues. Pearson correlation coefficient analysis is used to analyze the correlation between EYA1 and LSD2. D and E qRT-PCR and WB analyses of EYA1 and LSD2 expression in CRC cell line. Pearson correlation coefficient analysis is used to analyze the correlation between EYA1 and LSD2

Following the qPCR and WB analyses of tumor tissues, we proceeded to verify the expression of Eya1 and LSD2 in colorectal cancer cell lines. Five common CRC cell lines—LoVo, HT29, SW620, SW480, and CaCO2—were extracted for qPCR and WB. qPCR results indicated that the mRNA expressions of Eya1 and LSD2 were positive correlation (r = 0.8935, P = 0.0410) (Fig. 1D). Similarly, Western blot analysis revealed a consistent correlation of both Eya1 and LSD2 protein expression across all cell lines (Fig. 1E).

Eya1 regulated the angiogenesis of CRC cells by LSD2

To further investigate the role of Eya1 in colorectal cancer, we used lentivirus to overexpress and silence Eya1 in CRC cell lines, and performed similar manipulations on LSD2. Through these interventions, we examined the effects of Eya1 and LSD2 expression on angiogenesis-related factors (such as VEGFA, HIF, etc.) under both normal and hypoxic conditions. First, we measured the VEGFA expression in the different treatment groups using ELISA. The results showed that overexpression of Eya1 in HT-29 and SW480 cells significantly increased VEGFA expression under both normal and hypoxic conditions (Fig. 2A and C). This increase in VEGFA expression, along with the effective suppression observed in the LSD2 silencing group, suggests that LSD2 may play a critical regulatory role in the Eya1-mediated modulation of VEGFA expression (Fig. 2B and D).

C and D Elisa and WB analysis of VEGF-A protein expression in HT-29 and SW480 treated with EYA1 or sh-EYA1 and LSD2 or sh-LSD2 in hypoxia condition. E and F Tube formation assay detected formed tubes of HUVECs treated with EYA1 or sh-EYA1 and LSD2 or sh-LSD2 in normoxic or hypoxia condition

Furthermore, we confirmed the impact of Eya1 on VEGFA expression by silencing Eya1, which resulted in a significant reduction in VEGFA levels. Notably, when LSD2 was overexpressed, VEGFA levels were partially restored, indicating that LSD2 may play a crucial role in Eya1-mediated VEGFA regulation (Fig. 2B and D). In addition to analyzing angiogenic factors, we collected the conditioned medium from cell cultures to culture human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). The results showed that Eya1 promoted endothelial cell proliferation and tubular formation, and this angiogenic effect was dependent on the involvement of LSD2 (Fig. 2E and F).

Eya1 regulated the expression of LSD2 in CRC cells

To investigate the regulatory role of Eya1 on LSD2, we performed both overexpression and silencing of Eya1 in colorectal cancer cell lines. The expression of LSD2 was then validated at both the protein and mRNA levels through WB and qPCR. Our results showed that the changes in LSD2 expression were consistent with those of Eya1, indicating a direct regulatory relationship between the two proteins. Specifically, both at the protein and mRNA levels, the expression of LSD2 increased with Eya1 overexpression and decreased with Eya1 silencing (Fig. 3A and B).

Eya1 regulated the expression of LSD2 in CRC cells. A Identification of the binding and regulation of EYA1 and LSD2 protein in HT-29 and SW480 B qRT-PCR analysis of regulation of EYA1 and LSD2 C LSD2 promoter activity in vector, EYA1 and shEYA1 in HT-29 and SW480

Furthermore, we employed a luciferase reporter assay to assess the impact of Eya1 on LSD2 promoter activity. In the Eya1 overexpression group, we observed a significant increase in LSD2 promoter activity, with luciferase activity approximately six times higher than the control group. In contrast, when Eya1 was silenced, the LSD2 promoter activity was significantly reduced, with luciferase activity dropping to around 0.2-fold of the control (Fig. 3C).

Previous studies have established that Eya1 exerts its function through the DACH/EYA1 complex, with Dach1 acting as a co-stimulatory factor for Eya1. To confirm the interaction between them, we performed co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP), and the results were further validated using tag antibodies (HA and Flag) (Fig. 4A). Additionally, to further support this hypothesis, we employed chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) and observed that an anti-Eya1 antibody could directly immunoprecipitate Dach1 (Fig. 4B). To determine whether Eya1 activates the LSD2 promoter, we conducted a luciferase assay, which revealed a significant increase in LSD2 promoter activity in CRC cell lines with overexpress of Eya1. In contrast, no significant change was observed in CRC cell lines with silenced or mutant Dach1 (Fig. 4C and D). These findings indicate that Eya1 is regulated by its co-stimulatory factor Dach1, which modulates LSD2 expression.

B ChIP assay in HT-29 cells showing the interaction between EYA1 and Dach1 at the LSD2 promoter. C and D LSD2 promoter activity in vector, EYA1, sh-EYA1, Dach1 and sh-Dach1 in HT-29

LSD2 transcriptionally enhanced HIF-1β expression via demethylation of H3K4me2 at the promoter region in CRC

HIF-1β plays a critical role in angiogenesis, a key process in tumor growth and metastasis. In colorectal cancer (CRC), understanding how HIF-1β and its associated pathways are regulated is essential for uncovering potential therapeutic targets. To explore the role of LSD2 in modulating HIF-1β in CRC, we performed experiments in HT-29 and SW480 cell lines, focusing on the effects of LSD2 overexpression and silencing. Using WB and qPCR, we found that overexpression of LSD2 led to an increase in H3K4me2 levels, both at the mRNA and protein levels, suggesting that LSD2 plays a pivotal role in regulating histone modifications. Similarly, we observed a comparable upregulation of H3K4me2 expression in LSD2-overexpressing cells. Conversely, silencing LSD2 resulted in a decrease in H3K4me2 levels, further supporting its role in histone modification regulation in CRC cells (Fig. 5A and B).

LSD2 transcriptionally enhanced HIF-1β expression via demethylation of H3K4me2 at the promoter region in CRC. A LSD2, HIF-1β, H3K4me2 and H3 protein expression measured by Western blot for vector, LSD2 and sh-LSD2 in HT-29 and SW480. B qRT-PCR analysis of HIF-1β in vector, LSD2 and sh-LSD2 in HT-29 and SW480. C HIF-1β promoter activity in vector, LSD2 and sh-LSD2 in HT-29 and SW480. D ChIP assay in HT-29 cells showing the interaction between LSD2 and HIF-1β promoter. E Luciferase assay in HT-29 cells shows that LSD2 suppresses the transcriptional activity of the HIF-1β promoter in a H3K4me2-dependent manner

To further investigate the influence of LSD2 on H3K4me2, we performed luciferase reporter assays. The results revealed a significant increase in H3K4me2 promoter activity in LSD2-overexpressing CRC cells, showing approximately tenfold of enhancement compared to control cells. In contrast, silencing LSD2 reduced H3K4me2 promoter activity to approximately 0.1-fold of the control group (Fig. 5C). ChIP assay in HT-29 cells showing the interaction between LSD2 and HIF-1β promoter (Fig. 5D). WT promoter is sensitive to LSD2 modulation, whereas the mutated promoter abolishes this effect, confirming that LSD2 acts through demethylation of H3K4me2 (Fig. 5E).

These findings indicate that LSD2 plays a crucial role in regulating H3K4me2 expression, an important epigenetic mark associated with gene activation.

Eya1 regulates the angiogenesis of CRC cells through HIF-1β

To confirm that Eya1 regulates angiogenesis in CRC through HIF-1β, we performed experiments in HT-29 and SW480 cell lines, where we overexpressed and silenced both Eya1 and HIF-1β. Using ELISA and WB, we assessed the expression of VEGFA, a key pro-angiogenic factor. Our results showed that overexpression of Eya1 led to a significant increase in VEGFA expression, both under normoxic and hypoxic conditions (Fig. 6A and C). However, this upregulation of VEGFA was significantly suppressed when HIF-1β was silenced, suggesting that HIF-1β is crucial for the Eya1-mediated regulation of VEGFA. A similar effect was observed when Eya1 was silenced and HIF-1β overexpressed, further confirming the central role of HIF-1β in mediating Eya1’s effects on VEGFA expression (Fig. 6B and D).

A and B Elisa and WB analysis of VEGF-A protein expression in HT-29 and SW480 treated with EYA1 or sh-EYA1 and HIF-1β or sh-HIF-1β in normoxia condition. C and D Elisa and WB analysis of VEGF-A protein expression in HT-29 and SW480 treated with EYA1 or sh-EYA1 and HIF-1β or sh-HIF-1β in hypoxia condition. E and F Tube formation assay detected formed tubes of HUVECs treated with EYA1 or sh-EYA1 and HIF-1β or sh-HIF-1β in normoxia or hypoxia condition

Additionally, we collected conditioned media from these cells to assess their impact on endothelial cell function. The conditioned media from Eya1-overexpressing cells was used to culture human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). We found that Eya1 promoted both the proliferation and tube formation of HUVECs—two essential processes for angiogenesis (Fig. 6E and F). Importantly, when HIF-1β was silenced in Eya1-overexpressing cells, these pro-angiogenic effects were completely abolished, further confirming that Eya1’s ability to enhance endothelial cell activity is dependent on HIF-1β.

Discussion

Tumor angiogenesis is essential for the initiation and progression of colorectal cancer (CRC) (Lugano et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2023; Xi and Xu 2021). In our previous research, we identified that Eya1 affects angiogenesis in CRC by modulating the Akt-HIF-1α pathway (Cai et al. 2018). However, in this study, we further explore an alternative, Akt-independent mechanism of angiogenesis. Specifically, we demonstrate that Eya1 regulates another key gene in the angiogenesis pathway, HIF-1β. HIF-1β is a constitutively expressed subunit that is not regulated by oxygen levels, unlike HIF-1α. In our study, silencing HIF-1β abolished the pro-angiogenic effects of Eya1 and LSD2, suggesting a critical and oxygen-independent role for HIF-1β in this pathway. Our focus on HIF-1β highlights the importance of a stable transcriptional partner in the Eya1-LSD2 axis, particularly under varying oxygen conditions. This represents a significant shift from our previous findings, as it highlights a distinct regulatory mechanism that does not rely on Akt signaling.Taken together, our results suggest that Eya1 can promote tumor angiogenesis through both Akt-dependent and Akt-independent pathways.This finding not only enriches our understanding of how Eya1 contributes to CRC angiogenesis but also provides new insights into the molecular mechanisms driving CRC initiation and progression. By uncovering the distinct pathways through which Eya1 regulates tumor vasculature, we can better identify biomarkers and develop more effective therapeutic strategies for CRC management.

Eya1 is a transcriptional coactivator with intrinsic protein phosphatase activity but lacks DNA-binding ability (Sun et al. 2017; Amin et al. 2009; Li et al. 2003; Anderson et al. 2012). As a key modulator of transcriptional activity, Eya1 influences a variety of cellular processes, including differentiation, proliferation, and angiogenesis (Sun et al. 2017). However, the precise mechanisms by which Eya1 promote these processes remain unclear. Recent research has highlighted histone demethylation, a common epigenetic modification, as a crucial mechanism of gene regulation, and also as a target for numerous therapeutic agents (Chang et al. 2019). Based on this, we hypothesized that Eya1 might exert its pro-angiogenic effects through the modulation of histone demethylation, specifically via interaction with LSD2, a key histone demethylase.

In this study, we found that both Eya1 and LSD2 are highly expressed in CRC tissues, showing a consistent expression pattern. This observation prompted us to explore the potential interaction between Eya1 and LSD2 in the regulation of angiogenesis. We speculated that Eya1 might promote angiogenesis in CRC by modulating LSD2 activity, particularly through its role in histone demethylation, which is critical for the transcriptional activation of genes involved in vascular formation. Eya1 interacts with Dach1, which serves as a transcriptional cofactor to facilitate Eya1’s recruitment to the LSD2 promoter. This interaction enhances LSD2 transcription, which in turn promotes HIF-1β-mediated VEGFA expression and angiogenesis in CRC. Supporting this, our Co-IP and ChIP assays confirmed the physical interaction between Eya1 and Dach1 and their co-occupancy at the LSD2 promoter. Our results revealed that silencing LSD2 significantly suppressed the pro-angiogenic effect of Eya1, indicating that LSD2 is a key mediator of Eya1’s angiogenic activity.

To investigate this relationship further, we performed Co-IP and ChIP assays. These experiments revealed that Eya1 interacts with Dach1, a transcriptional repressor involved in regulating various developmental processes. Notably, silencing Dach1 resulted in a decrease in LSD2 promoter activity, suggesting that Dach1 may regulate LSD2 expression, thereby influencing its role in the Eya1-mediated angiogenesis pathway. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms by which Eya1 and LSD2 cooperate to regulate angiogenesis in CRC. They also underscore the potential therapeutic significance of targeting the Eya1-LSD2 axis in CRC, particularly in combination with other treatments that modulate epigenetic pathways.

LSD2 is another FAD-dependent amine oxidase that plays a key role in regulating gene expression through histone modification (Fang et al. 2010). Unlike LSD1, which lacks a zinc finger domain, LSD2 contains an N-terminal zinc finger structure that is crucial for maintaining its demethylase activity (Malagraba et al. 2022). This structural feature makes LSD2 distinct in its ability to specifically demethylate H3K4me2, a modification that is critical for controlling transcriptional activation. LSD2 can influence the activity of various genes and play a significant role in cellular processes such as development, differentiation, and disease progression.

Recent studies have demonstrated that LSD2 is involved in the progression of CRC. In particular, LSD2 affects the demethylation of H3K4me2 within promoter regions, leading to changes in the transcriptional landscape of key regulatory genes (Fang et al. 2013). In our study, we demonstrated it could impact the regulation of HIF-1β, a critical transcription factor that governs angiogenesis. Angiogenesis is essential for tumor growth, as it supplies the tumor with necessary nutrients and oxygen. By modulating HIF-1β activity through H3K4me2 demethylation, LSD2 may directly influence the ability of cancer cells to induce vascularization, facilitating tumor progression. This study included only 13 CRC patients, with a limited sample size that may affect the generalizability of the findings.Although we provided pathological staging information, long-term clinical outcomes such as 3-year overall survival could not be assessed, which limits the statistical power for robust clinical correlation analyses. Besides, another limitation of our study is the use of cobalt chloride as a hypoxia mimetic agent. While it is widely used to stabilize HIF-α by inhibiting prolyl hydroxylases, it does not fully replicate the complex and chronic hypoxic or metabolic stress conditions present in the tumor microenvironment.

This study is the first to demonstrate that Eya1 regulates angiogenesis in colorectal cancer (CRC) through the LSD2/HIF-1β/VEGF pathway. Our findings reveal a novel mechanism by which Eya1 influences tumor vascularization, providing new insights into the molecular processes driving CRC progression.

Patients and methods

Patients and samples

All tissue of CRC samples and corresponding adjacent non-tumor tissue from 13 patients was obtained from Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital, covering the time frame from January 2018 to November 2020. The diagnosis of CRC was based on the guidelines established by the NCCN (Benson, et al. 2024). All participating patients provided informed consent for the use of their clinical specimens in research purposes. This study was conducted under the approval of the Ethics Committee of Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital, and it adhered to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration.

Cell culture and treatment

LoVo, SW620, HT-29, SW480, CacO2 cells, along with human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection. The LoVo and SW620 cell lines were cultured at 37 °C in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Hyclone). HUVECs were maintained at 37 °C in EBM-2 endothelial cell basal medium, utilizing the SingleQuot Kit (Lonza/Cambrex, Walkersville, MD) in accordance with the provided instructions. To induce hypoxia, CoCl2 was introduced at a concentration of 200 μM for a duration of 4 h.

Construction of lentiviral-infected cell lines

All recombinant lentiviruses containing sequences for knocking down and overexpressing human EYA1 and LSD2 were obtained. The transfection of lentiviruses was carried out using the pCDF1 lentivirus system (System Biosciences, SBI) as previously described (Cai et al. 2020). Two different LSD2 shRNAs and controls were obtained from SABiosciences (Germantown, MD, USA) and reverse transfected into HT-29 and SW480 cells. Empty vectors served as negative controls, designated as NC. Finally, the efficiency of transfection was assessed by western blotting (WB).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was performed according to previously established protocols (Cai et al. 2018) to assess the expression levels of Eya1 and LSD2 in colorectal cancer (CRC) tissues and adjacent non-tumor tissues. Two independent pathologists conducted the quantitative evaluation of the IHC staining. The staining intensity was classified as follows: 0 for negative, 1 for weak, 2 for moderate, and 3 for strong. The proportion of positively stained cells in the regions of interest was categorized as: 0% positive tumor cells = 0, 1–25% positive tumor cells = 1, 26–50% positive tumor cells = 2, and over 50% positive tumor cells = 3. The overall staining score was calculated by multiplying the intensity score by the percentage score, yielding a possible range from 0 to 9.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was isolated from CRC cells and frozen tissue using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). cDNA synthesis was conducted with M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer's guidelines. For qRT-PCR analysis, SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Roche) was employed in conjunction with the Agilent AriaMx Real-Time Fluorescent Quantitative PCR System (Agilent). β-actin served as the internal control for normalization, and the expression levels of each gene were determined using the 2-ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences used are as follows (5′to 3′): β-actin, AGAGCTACGAGCTGCCTGAC(forward) and AGCACTGTGTTGGCGTACAG(reverse);Eya1,CACAGCCCACTGGTCCTCAA GAGG (forward) and GTTTCAACTGTTCTCGTCGTTTCC (reverse); LSD2, AACCGAACCTAGTCCCAAAG(forward) and GGCTA TCTGTGGAGTAA GCT (reverse).

Western blot analysis

Protein extracts from tissues or cells were prepared using pre-chilled RIPA buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) that included protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Following protein concentration measurement with a BCA assay kit (Pierce), the samples were heated at 100 °C for 5 min. The abundance of proteins was assessed and analyzed using SDS–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and immunoblotting, following the protocols established in earlier studies.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) was carried out utilizing a Pierce® Co-Immunoprecipitation kit (Thermo Scientific, USA), following the guidelines provided by the manufacturer.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation

An EZChIP™ ChIP Kit (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA) was utilized to conduct this assay. Initially, chromatin DNA was sonicated into fragments between 200 and 500 bp, with RNA polymerase II (binding to β-actin) serving as a positive control and normal mouse immunoglobulin G (IgG) as a negative control. The chromatin was subsequently immunoprecipitated using anti-LSD2 antibodies (1:1000). The LSD2 antibodies for the ChIP experiments were sourced from Cell Signaling Technology. The β-actin primers were included in the kit.

Luciferase reporter assay

The transcriptional activity of the LSD2 promoter was measured using this assay, as previously described (Cai et al. 2020). The LSD2 promoter sequence was cloned into a pGL3-basic luciferase reporter vector and co-transfected with or without Eya1 (sh-Eya1).

In vitro angiogenesis assay

In vitro angiogenesis was assessed through a tube formation assay. A growth factor-reduced Matrigel was added to 96-well tissue culture plates (100 μL per well) and allowed to gel at 37 °C for 30 min. Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) (2 × 10^4 cells) were then introduced to each well and incubated in culture medium for 24 h. The formation of endothelial tubes was monitored under a light microscope, with evaluations made every 4 h to count the total branch points.

Statistical analysis

All results are presented as mean ± SEM. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software (La Jolla, USA) and SPSS Statistics 21 (IBM, USA). For comparisons between two groups, either Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test was utilized. For multiple group comparisons, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal–Wallis test was employed. Categorical data comparisons were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A significance level of P < 0.05 was established.

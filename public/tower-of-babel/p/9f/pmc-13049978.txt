Unveiling disparities in lung cancer care: a joint spatio-temporal analysis of multidisciplinary meeting presentation, supportive care screening, and diagnostic timeliness in Victoria
Authors: Getayeneh Antehunegn Tesema, Rob G. Stirling, Zemenu Tadesse Tessema, Arul Earnest
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer remains the most diagnosed malignancy and the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Improving key clinical quality indicators (CQIs), including multidisciplinary meeting (MDM) presentation, supportive care screening, and timely diagnosis is essential for optimising patient outcomes. These indicators often co-occur, yet evidence on their shared spatio-temporal structure and the utility of joint modelling approaches remains limited. Thus, we aimed to explore the joint spatio-temporal patterns of these CQIs across Victoria, Australia.MethodsWe analysed data from 12,970 lung cancer cases recorded in the Victorian Lung Cancer Registry (VLCR). A Bayesian hierarchical joint spatio-temporal shared-component model was employed to estimate the joint magnitude of MDM presentation, supportive care screening, and timely diagnosis across space (local government area) and time (year). To improve the precision of our estimates, information across the three outcomes was combined using both shared and outcome-specific components within the shared-component modelling framework. Models were estimated using Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation (INLA) in R-INLA and were compared using the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC).ResultsApproximately 35% of the total variation across CQIs was explained by shared spatial and temporal components. The shared temporal trend exhibited a modest but consistent increase over the study period, whereas outcome-specific temporal trends displayed distinct patterns. Shared spatial risk patterns closely paralleled CQI-specific distributions, highlighting regions with persistent geographic inequities in the delivery of lung cancer care.ConclusionsSubstantial geographic and temporal disparities in lung cancer care were observed, with marked shared patterns across CQIs. These findings highlight the need for integrated, geographically targeted strategies to strengthen MDM presentation, supportive care screening, and timely diagnosis, thereby improving coordination and reducing inequities in lung cancer management.

Background

Lung cancer poses a major global health burden [1, 2] and remains a significant healthcare challenge in Australia [3, 4]. It is the 5th most commonly diagnosed cancer and the leading cause of cancer-related death in the country, accounting for 9% of all cancer diagnoses and 18% of cancer deaths [3]. Despite advances in treatment, outcomes remain poor largely due to late-stage diagnoses with 5-year survival rates lingering at just 17% [5].

Ensuring timely and equitable access to diagnostic and supportive care services, as well as Multidisciplinary Meeting (MDM) presentation, is critical to improving lung cancer outcomes [6–8]. Clinical Quality Registries (CQRs) play a vital role in monitoring and improving the quality of care by collecting, analysing, and reporting longitudinal clinical and patient-reported data [9]. Through benchmarking performance indicators against best practice guidelines, CQRs identify unwarranted variations in care, inform clinical decision-making, and support continuous quality improvement across health systems [10].

Since its establishment in 2011, the Victorian Lung Cancer Registry (VLCR) has prospectively collected clinical data on lung cancer and patient-reported outcomes from more than 50 public and private hospitals across Victoria [11]. The registry evaluates health system performance against 25 Clinical Quality Indicators (CQIs) and is aligned with the nationally endorsed Optimal Care Pathway (OCP) for lung cancer, which seeks to deliver timely, coordinated, and patient-centred care [12]. Among these indicators, diagnostic timeliness (referral-to-diagnosis interval), MDM presentation, and receiving supportive care screening are key measures of care quality, with established relevance to treatment delivery, survival, and equity in lung cancer outcomes [13].

MDM presentation is a cornerstone of guideline-based lung cancer care, yet substantial inter-institutional variation persists (29.7–94%) [11]. Comparable variability was observed in supportive care screening (2–79%) and diagnostic timeliness [11], with implications for inequitable care, particularly in socioeconomically disadvantaged and geographically remote areas. Despite the interrelated nature of these indicators and their shared systemic determinants—including remoteness, socioeconomic context, and service availability—evidence on their geographic and temporal patterning at small-area levels remains limited [14–17].

Bayesian spatio-temporal modelling approaches are extensively employed in epidemiology to characterise geographic and temporal variation in disease occurrence by formally integrating prior information with observed data and accommodating spatial and temporal structures. To address persistent gaps in the understanding of the shared spatial and temporal inequities in lung cancer care, we implemented a joint Bayesian spatio-temporal shared component modelling framework [18, 19]. This framework was applied to three key CQIs reported by the VLCR: timeliness of diagnosis, defined by the referral-to-diagnosis interval; MDM presentation; and supportive care screening.

The main contribution of this study is the joint modelling of correlated CQIs while simultaneously capturing both shared and outcome-specific spatial and temporal structures. Using a shared component modelling approach, an extension of multivariate Bayesian disease mapping observed variation was decomposed into components common to all CQIs and those specific to individual indicators [18]. Interdependencies between outcomes were modelled using a Multivariate Conditional Autoregressive (MCAR) prior for spatial random effects. This multivariate framework was further extended to the temporal domain through the specification of a multivariate random walk prior, enabling the estimation of shared temporal trends across outcomes and representing a novel application of the MCAR structure in a temporal context [18, 20, 21].

By applying this joint Bayesian spatio-temporal framework to VLCR data from 2011 to 2022, this study quantifies geographic inequities in key indicators of lung cancer care across Victoria, identifies areas of persistent underperformance, and provides a robust evidence base to inform strategies aimed at improving equity and consistency in cancer care delivery [22, 23].

Methods

Study setting

This study was conducted in Victoria, Australia, comprising 79 Local Government Areas (LGAs) with diverse population sizes and geographic contexts. Health services are distributed across metropolitan, regional, and rural settings. Metropolitan LGAs, particularly within Greater Melbourne, host multiple co-located hospitals, whereas many rural LGAs rely on regional referral centres located outside their administrative boundaries. This heterogeneity in service distribution informs geographic disparities in access to diagnostic and treatment pathways and provides a relevant context for examining spatial inequities in lung cancer care.

Data source

Data were obtained from the VLCR and the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS). The VLCR is a state-wide clinical quality registry established in 2011 to monitor quality of lung cancer care and patient-reported outcomes. It systematically collects standardised demographic, clinical, pathological, and management data and evaluates performance against more than 25 CQIs endorsed by the VLCR Steering Committee. The registry captures cases from over 50 public and private hospitals across 19 health service networks, representing more than 85% of hospitals providing lung cancer care in Victoria [24].

Study design and participants

We conducted a retrospective cohort study using prospectively collected registry data. All patients recorded in the VLCR with a lung cancer diagnosis between January 1, 2011, and December 31, 2022, were eligible. Case ascertainment followed a uniform protocol, with trained data collectors performing systematic medical record review to verify diagnostic, staging, management, and outcome information. Eligible cases were identified using ICD-10-AM codes C34.0—C34.9, Z85.1, and Z85 [25]. Both Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) and Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC) cases were included. Consecutive inclusion ensured comprehensive population coverage and minimised selection bias.

Outcome measures

Three CQIs were examined based on clinical relevance and previously observed geographic variation: (1) MDM presentation, (2) supportive care screening, and (3) timely diagnosis, defined as a referral-to-diagnosis interval ≤ 28 days [26]. MDM presentation was defined as documented discussion at a multidisciplinary team meeting between diagnosis and initiation of first definitive treatment, consistent with VLCR specifications and Victorian clinical guidelines [27]. Supportive care screening was defined as completion of a documented supportive care needs assessment [28].

Spatial and temporal aggregation

All CQIs were aggregated at the LGA level, which served as the primary spatial unit of analysis. To enhance statistical stability, data were grouped into four consecutive time periods: period 1 (2011–2013), period 2 (2014–2016), period 3 (2017–2019), and period 4 (2020–2022). For each LGA and time, observed counts of patients meeting each CQI were calculated. Expected counts were derived using indirect internal standardisation based on the overall VLCR lung cancer population and were incorporated as offset terms in the joint spatio-temporal model to account for variation in underlying case volume.

Covariates

Patient-level characteristics were aggregated to the LGA level. Covariates included the proportion of patients aged ≥ 60 years, proportion identifying as non-indigenous, and prevalence of smoking among lung cancer patients.

Area-level covariates from the ABS included socioeconomic status, measured using the Index of Relative Socio-economic Advantage and Disadvantage (IRSAD), with lower scores indicating greater disadvantage [29, 30], and geographic accessibility, assessed using the Accessibility/Remoteness Index of Australia Plus (ARIA +), which classifies LGAs from highly accessible to very remote based on road distance to service centres [31].

Statistical modelling

We developed a joint Bayesian spatio-temporal shared component model to simultaneously analyse three CQIs of VLCR: (1) MDM presentation, (2) supportive care screening, and (3) timely diagnosis. These indicators were assessed across 79 LGAs in Victoria over four discrete periods (2011–2013, 2014–2016, 2017–2019, and 2020–2022).

The spatial components (shared and specific) were modelled using a shared component model (SCM) structure to borrow strength across indicators and adjacent areas [27].

The structured and unstructured spatial random effects were modelled jointly across the three indicators using a MCAR structure. The shared and indicator-specific temporal effects are assigned the first-order Random Walk (RW1). Sensitivity analysis was done using Normal priors for regression coefficients and inverse-gamma (0.001, 0.001) priors for variance components. Defining prior distributions for unknown parameters is a critical step in constructing a Bayesian model [32]. The selection of prior distributions depends on the nature of the parameter being estimated. Based on established literature, flat priors were assigned to the CQI-specific intercepts and the precision parameters of the spatial effects [33, 34]. For spatial weights, log-normal priors with a mean of zero and a precision of 1/5.9 were chosen, ensuring positive values for the weights.

In the CAR model, neighbourhood weight information was used to define the conditional prior distributions for the three spatial random effect terms.

All models were estimated using the Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation (INLA) approach in R, which offers computational efficiency and accurate approximations for latent Gaussian models [35]. Model fit and complexity were assessed using the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC), with lower values indicating better trade-offs between goodness-of-fit and parsimony. A difference in DIC of at least five points was considered meaningful in selecting the optimal model [36].

The final model produced posterior mean estimates of shared and indicator-specific relative risks, along with 95% Credible Intervals (CrI), enabling robust inference on spatio-temporal variation in lung cancer care quality across Victoria.

Results

Exploratory analysis

Data on timely diagnosis, supportive care screening, and MDM presentation were obtained from the VLCR for all eligible lung cancer cases, yielding a cohort of 12,970 individuals. The proportion of patients receiving supportive care screening increased from 16.2% in 2011 to 31.2% in 2013, then declined to 24.8% by 2022. Timely diagnosis followed a similar pattern, rising from 29.4% in 2011 to 37.3% in 2013 and falling to 29.1% in 2022 (Table 1). Posterior summaries revealed substantial heterogeneity in baseline risks and spatio-temporal patterns across the three CQIs. Fixed-effect intercepts were not statistically significant, indicating minimal baseline log-risk after accounting for spatial and temporal dependencies.

Spatial precision estimates showed pronounced residual heterogeneity for timely diagnosis, whereas MDM presentation and supportive care screening demonstrated smoother spatial surfaces, reflecting stronger influence of the shared spatial component. Overall, approximately 35% of variation in CQIs was explained by shared spatial and temporal effects, with the remaining captured by outcome-specific spatial and temporal random effects. Scaling parameters indicated that supportive care screening was most strongly associated with shared spatial risk, followed by MDM presentation, while timely diagnosis showed weaker alignment. Temporal scaling suggested that variation in timely diagnosis and MDM presentation was largely driven by shared temporal effects, whereas supportive care screening exhibited lower concordance with the common temporal pattern.

Discussion

This study provides novel insights into the joint spatio-temporal dynamics of three key lung cancer CQIs: MDM presentation, supportive care screening, and timely diagnosis across Local LGAs in Victoria over a 12-year period. By applying a Bayesian hierarchical shared-component model, we disentangled spatial and temporal effects common to all CQIs from outcome-specific patterns, addressing an important gap in the literature where CQIs are typically analysed in isolation. This joint framework enabled identification of geographic areas and time periods where multiple dimensions of lung cancer care underperformed concurrently, offering a more integrated assessment of equity in care delivery.

Exploratory analyses revealed substantial temporal variability across CQIs. MDM presentation improved steadily from 2011 to 2019, consistent with national and international initiatives aimed at strengthening multidisciplinary oncology care [37, 38]. The subsequent plateau and decline observed during 2020–2022 likely reflect COVID-19-related disruptions, which have been widely documented to reduce cancer diagnostic procedures and pathology reporting globally [39–41]. Supportive care screening initially increased but subsequently declined, aligning with prior evaluations of psycho-oncology services and likely reflecting pandemic-related disruptions, including reduced outpatient capacity and delayed assessments. Timely diagnosis demonstrated modest early improvements before plateauing, echoing findings linking diagnostic delays to persistent systemic barriers in lung cancer care pathways [42–44]. Collectively, these trends suggest that the COVID-19 pandemic may have stalled or reversed earlier gains in lung cancer care quality in Victoria.

Joint spatio-temporal modelling indicated that approximately 35% of the overall variation across CQIs was attributable to shared spatial and temporal components, highlighting the influence of common system-level determinants such as resource distribution, workforce capacity, and regional service organisation [45, 46]. Supportive care screening showed the strongest association with the shared spatial component, suggesting heightened sensitivity to structural and organisational factors affecting multiple dimensions of care. In contrast, timely diagnosis aligned more closely with the shared temporal component, indicating that system-wide policies and reforms may disproportionately influence diagnostic timeliness.

Spatially, we observed clustering of underperformance across all three CQIs in some regions of Victoria. These patterns are consistent with prior evidence linking rurality and socioeconomic disadvantage to suboptimal cancer care delivery [47]. The concordance of spatial patterns across CQIs reinforces the need for integrated, place-based interventions targeting multiple stages of the lung cancer care continuum.

Temporally, the shared-component showed improvement from 2011–2013 through 2017–2019, followed by a plateau during 2020–2022. This trajectory is consistent with policy reforms and broader secular improvements in cancer care, followed by pandemic-related service disruptions and competing healthcare priorities [48]. Outcome-specific temporal effects revealed divergent dynamics: MDM presentation continued to improve beyond the shared trend, suggesting indicator-specific drivers such as increasing institutional prioritisation of multidisciplinary care, whereas supportive care screening declined persistently, highlighting its vulnerability during periods of system strain. These findings align with multivariate spatio-temporal evidence indicating that shared components capture broad-scale determinants, while outcome-specific deviations reflect targeted interventions or indicator-specific dynamics [34, 49].

These findings have key policy implications. Targeted interventions in consistently underperforming LGAs can promote equity, while system-level strategies such as integrating supportive care into diagnostic pathways and strengthening multidisciplinary infrastructure may improve multiple care domains simultaneously.

Methodologically, this study represents, to our knowledge, the first application of a joint spatio-temporal shared-component model to multiple lung cancer CQIs using VLCR data within a Bayesian hierarchical framework. This approach enables robust estimation while explicitly accounting for uncertainty and correlations across outcomes and time, stabilising relative risk estimates in LGAs with small case numbers and providing clearer separation of shared versus outcome-specific drivers of inequity.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Registry-based data may be subject to misclassification if reporting completeness varies across LGAs or over time. The use of LGA-level aggregates may obscure within-area heterogeneity and limit inference at finer spatial scales. Additionally, contextual factors such as workforce capacity and hospital infrastructure were not explicitly modelled. Future research incorporating relevant covariates would help clarify the drivers of district-level variation [50]. Nonetheless, shared spatial and temporal components may be interpreted as proxies for unmeasured determinants with similar patterns across indicators, while outcome-specific components capture unique influences [51].

The joint spatio-temporal modelling framework applied in this study can be extended to other cancer types and clinical conditions in which outcomes are influenced by both spatial and temporal factors. Health system determinants such as workforce distribution, diagnostic capacity, and hospital resources, which drive variation in lung cancer care, are likely to exert similar effects across other cancer pathways, including breast, colorectal, and prostate cancer. Application of this methodology to these contexts could generate insights into regional disparities, temporal trends, and inform policy interventions aimed at promoting equitable and high-quality cancer care.

Conclusions

This study modelled the geographic distribution and temporal dynamics of multiple lung cancer CQIs across Victoria using a Bayesian joint spatio-temporal framework. By borrowing strength across neighbouring regions and interrelated CQIs, this approach generated high-resolution estimates that strengthened inference and enabled identification of shared and outcome-specific patterns of inequity. As cancer registry data continue to expand in scope and quality, further improvements in data completeness and accuracy will be critical to support robust monitoring and evaluation of care quality.

The observed geographic and temporal inequities in multidisciplinary team presentation, supportive care screening, and timely diagnosis highlight persistent gaps in lung cancer care delivery. The co-occurrence of these disparities suggests that system-level interventions targeting shared structural determinants such as service organisation, workforce capacity, and access to care have the potential to yield substantial gains across multiple dimensions of care quality. Overall, our findings demonstrate the value of joint spatio-temporal modelling as a rigorous tool for precision health service planning and for informing policy priorities aimed at advancing equitable, high-quality lung cancer care.

Efficacy of emapalumab in treating 3 pediatric patients with epstein–barr virus-associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis complicated by multiple organ dysfunction
Authors: Gang Liu, Suyun Qian, Jun Liu, Honghao Ma, Quan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveEpstein–Barr virus-associated hemophagocytic lympho­histiocytosis (EBV-HLH) is common among HLH in paediatric patients, severe cases are often complicated by multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), leading to poor prognosis and high mortality rate. Emapalumab, an interferon (IFN)-γ inhibitor, can effectively improve patient prognosis without MODS, but it remains unclear whether patients with MODS can achieve clinical benefits. In this study, we aim to evaluate the efficacy and safety of emapalumab in treating EBV-HLH with MODS.MethodsThe paediatric cases admitted to the paediatric intensive care unit were prospectively included, with worsening labs and IFN-γ > 500 pg/mL after standard care, and were given intravenous infusions of emapalumab at a dose of 1–2 mg/kg. Outcomes measured included body temperature, lab results, in-hospital mortality, and stem cell transplant rates.ResultsThree paediatric cases were included, and treatment was uninterrupted. Among these, Case 2 exhibited refractory EBV-HLH. After conventional therapy, the laboratory parameters, hyperthermia and pSOFA score remained unimproved withthe blood IFN-γ > 500 pg/mL. Emapalumab infusion was administered, with significant improvement within one week. Case 2 later developed a cytomegalovirus infection, leading to treatment discontinuation despite stable IFN-γ level. Cases 1 and 3 remained stable without additional treatments during a one-year follow-up.ConclusionThe use of emapalumab to treat paediatric patients with EBV-HLH complicated by MODS can significantly improve body temperature and HLH-related laboratory parameters, but the overall clinical benefit for refractory cases remains unclear. During treatment, it is essential to keep vigilance against concurrent infections, and monitoring for potential latent pathogens is also warranted.

Introduction

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is a severe inflammatory response syndrome caused by genetic or acquired immune dysregulation and is characterized by rapid progression and high lethality, with a median survival time of less than 2 months in untreated patients [1]. Viral infection is a common cause of HLH in children [2], with EB virus-induced HLH accounting for over half of all cases [3]. Immunomodulatory treatments such as antiviral therapy, corticosteroids, and intravenous immunoglobulin, as well as chemotherapy, can reduce mortality rates to some extent [4]. However, in some patients, a persistent cytokine storm may cause or exacerbate multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), leading to a significantly higher mortality rate (8%–22% vs. 40%–60%) [4–7]. Emapalumab is an anti-interferon (IFN)-γ inhibitor that has been shown to reduce cytokine levels and improve prognosis in children with primary HLH [8]. Two case reports [9,10] indicate that emapalumab has significant therapeutic effects in children with EB virus-associated HLH (EBV-HLH) complicated by MODS, suggesting that improved survival rates can be achieved in such cases, although its safety and efficacy still require further clinical observation. In this study, paediatric cases of EBV-HLH with MODS were prospectively included to observe treatment effects, prognosis, and adverse reactions, aiming to explore the clinical benefits of emapalumab.

Methods

Study population

This prospective interventional study was conducted at the paediatric intensive care unit (PICU) of Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University from July 31, 2023 to July 31, 2024. This study was approved by the ethic committee of Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University ([2023]-E-066-Y). Our study adheres to the Declaration of Helsinki. We obtained written informed consent to participate from all patients and their legal guardians. And written consent to publish these details has been obtained from all individuals and their legal guardians.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients were eligible if they were children aged 29 days to 3 years old who were diagnosed with HLH complicated by MODS. Patients were required to have no remission after standard treatment or do not achieve a partial response or better as measured by monitoring of routine laboratory parameters after induction chemotherapy with blood IFN-γ levels > 500 pg/mL. These patients received emapalumab treatment after informed consent was obtained from their parents. The exclusion criteria were as follows: ① patients with active tuberculosis, hepatitis, HIV infection, or other infections with a risk of dissemination that are difficult to control; ② patients with malignant tumours. The study withdrawal criteria were as follows: ① occurrence of allergic reactions or other serious adverse events during treatment; and ② request for treatment discontinuation by the paediatric patient and/or the parents for personal reasons.

Diagnostic criteria

Diagnosis of HLH requires meeting five or more of the following eight criteria [11]: ① fever: temperature > 38.5 °C, lasting for > 7 days; ② splenomegaly; ③ hematocytopenia (involving two or three lineages of peripheral blood): haemoglobin < 90 g/L (or < 100 g/L for infants < 4 weeks), platelets < 100 × 109/L, neutrophils < 1.0 × 109/L not due to decreased bone marrow haematopoietic function; ④ hypertriglyceridaemia and/or hypofibrinogenemia: a triglyceride level (TG) > 3 mmol/L or above three standard deviations for the same age, a fibrinogen level < 1.5 g/L or below three standard deviations for the same age; ⑤ hemophagocytosis in the bone marrow, spleen, liver, or lymph nodes; ⑥ decreased or absent NK cell activity; ⑦ elevated serum ferritin: ferritin ≥ 500 μg/L; or ⑧ increased soluble interleukin-2 receptor levels (sCD25). MODS refers to a clinical syndrome in which two or more organs or systems simultaneously or sequentially exhibit dysfunction due to acute illnesses such as severe trauma, shock, infection, or major surgical procedures, resulting in an inability to maintain a stable internal environment. The diagnostic criteria are based on the International paediatric sepsis consensus conference: Definitions for sepsis and organ dysfunction in paediatrics 2005 [12].

Dosage and administration and related definitions of emapalumab

According to previous studies [8], the initial dose of emapalumab is 1–2 mg/kg by intravenous infusion. If the condition worsens and repeated administration is necessary, the dose may be increased at the clinician’s discretion to no more than 10 mg/kg. For subsequent treatment, emapalumab is administered on the basis of blood IFN-γ levels and is given 1 to 2 times a week, with each infusion lasting at least 1 h. If blood IFN-γ levels significantly decrease to near-normal levels, the medication may be discontinued.

No remission after standard treatment, or failure to achieve partial response or better as measured by monitoring of routine laboratory parameters after induction chemotherapy is defined as: There is no improvement of at least 25% in ≥ 2 symptoms/laboratory indexes after partially or fully a combination of antiviral therapy, ruxolitinib (5 mg/kg, Q12H), human immunoglobulin (total dose 2 g/kg), methylprednisolone (1–2 mg/kg) or plasma exchange, or after VP16 induction chemotherapy [13].

Evaluation and data collection

Body temperature was recorded daily before and after emapalumab administration. Blood samples were collected at two timepoints (1–2 days and 6–7 days post-treatment) to assess HLH-related parameters (ferritin, fibrinogen, triglycerides, trilineage blood cell counts), inflammatory markers (IFN-γ, IL-6, IL-10, CXCL9), and EBV-DNA levels. The Paediatric Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (pSOFA) score was calculated at both intervals to evaluate organ dysfunction. At baseline, respiratory pathogen panels and peripheral blood cultures were obtained to rule out concurrent infections. Additional microbiological testing was performed as clinically indicated, particularly in cases of persistent fever or rising inflammatory markers. Basic data include demographic information, underlying diseases, diagnostic criteria for HLH and MODS, details of standard treatment, emapalumab-related adverse events, the length of stay in the PICU, and the in-hospital mortality rate. Follow-up was conducted until September 2024, covering general health status, whether to continue chemotherapy or undergo stem cell transplantation, etc.

Statistical analysis

A comparative analysis was conducted on the laboratory parameters, inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-10, and IFN-γ), pSOFA scores, and changes in organ dysfunction before and after the use of emapalumab, which also included the length of stay in the PICU, in-hospital survival, survival status and health condition at the end of the follow-up, stem cell transplantation, etc. Given the limited sample size (n = 3), this study was restricted to descriptive comparisons of body temperature, biomarker profiles, and organ function parameters before and after emapalumab administration, with no inferential statistical analyses performed.

Results

Clinical characteristics

During the study period, a total of 19 HLH cases were admitted to the PICU at Beijing Children’s Hospital, 17 of whom had MODS. Among these cases, 7 had IFN-γ levels > 500 pg/mL, 3 were discharged within 48 h of admission involved critically condition whose families elected to withdraw treatment, and 1 refused the treatment. Ultimately, 3 cases of EBV-HLH with MODS were included, with no interruptions in treatment observed.

Case 1 was diagnosed with EBV-HLH complicated by MODS at admission and received standard treatment. On D3, the patient continued to have a fever, and a repeat complete blood count revealed progressive decreases in leukocytes and platelets. Additionally, there was no improvement in the pSOFA score, and the level of IFN-γ was high at 4099 pg/mL in the blood. Therefore, 20 mg of emapalumab was administered intravenously once.

Case 2 was diagnosed with EBV-HLH at admission and received standard treatment. On D7, the patient experienced an increase in fever peak and developed dyspnoea, with lung imaging supporting ARDS. There were significant decreases in blood cell counts in three lineages of peripheral blood and an increase in the pSOFA score, which was consistent with manifestations of MODS. Therefore, the patient was given an increased dose of methylprednisolone to 5 mg/kg/d and received a single intravenous infusion of 20 mg of emapalumab, resulting in temporary improvement in the condition. On D18 (7 days after emapalumab treatment), the patient experienced a fever again, with elevated ferritin levels and decreased blood cell counts in three lineages of peripheral blood, leading to a diagnosis of refractory HLH. Therefore, etoposide (75 mg/m2/day) and an increased dose of ruxolitinib (10 mg/m2, Q12H) were administered. On D19, the second dose of emapalumab (20 mg) was administered, with the IFN-γ level at 1232 pg/mL at the time of administration. The patient subsequently developed CMV pneumonia, with blood CMV-DNA levels continuously increasing (from 147,520 to 200,825 copies/mL). Owing to their critical condition, the patient’s parents requested the patient to be discharged against medical advice, and the patient passed away on the same day.

Case 3 was diagnosed with EBV-HLH complicated by MODS at admission and received standard treatment. On D3, the patient continued to experience a fever, with a progressive decline in blood cell counts across three lineages of peripheral blood and fibrinogen, and an increased pSOFA score. The level of IFN-γ was 9232 pg/mL, indicating a poor treatment response. Therefore, 20 mg of emapalumab was administered intravenously once.

Changes in key clinical and laboratory indexes of the three paediatric patients after emapalumab administration

After the first dose of emapalumab, the body temperature of all three paediatric patients returned to normal within 48 h. Indexes associated with HLH, particularly blood IFN-γ levels, platelet counts, and ferritin levels, significantly improved within 1–2 days after administration and returned to near baseline levels within 6–7 days (Figure 1). For Case 2, after receiving the second dose of emapalumab, the blood IFN-γ levels decreased to normal levels by the fourth day post-administration, whereas the levels of ferritin, platelet count, and fibrinogen showed deterioration compared with those in the previous state. Following emapalumab administration, pSOFA scores decreased from baseline values of 12 and 8 to 7 and 3 at days 1–2 and days 6–7 in Case 1 and Case 3, respectively. However, Case 2 showed a marginal increase from 3 to 4 in pSOFA score by days 6–7. Notably, all three cases demonstrated significant reduction in whole blood EBV-DNA copy numbers by days 6–7 (Table 2).

Follow-up results

Case 1 and Case 3 showed improvement and were discharged. During a one-year follow-up, both cases maintained good health without undergoing chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation.

Discussion

The infrequency of EBV-HLH with MODS limits the sample size of this study. Currently, only one study [14] has reported the use of emapalumab for the treatment of paediatric patients with EBV-HLH, involving three cases. Moreover, there are two related reports concerning EBV-HLH patients complicated by MODS, involving two paediatric patients [9,10], one of whom had refractory EBV-HLH [10]. Given the poor prognosis of EBV-HLH complicated by MODS and the ineffectiveness of conventional therapies, additional treatment options are often actively pursued [7]. A previous study presented demonstrated the efficacy of emapalumab in 34 patients with primary HLH showing that emapalumab achieved response rates of 63% and 65% in patients who failed conventional therapy and those untreated, respectively [8]. Further adjusted comparisons with Previous research data suggested that emapalumab was more effective than standard regimens [15], with no significant association with severe adverse events [16].

There are limited data on whether the use of emapalumab can improve survival rates in patients with EBV-HLH complicated by MODS in ICU. Shanmuganathan Chandrakasan et al. [17] analysed 15 patients treated in ICU and suggested that early use (before development of organ damage requiring critical care) and high-dose (> 3 mg/kg) emapalumab may improve survival rates. The two previously reported cases and Cases 1 and 3 in this study, which involved EBV-HLH complicated by MODS, all survived, which partially validates the effectiveness of the treatment. Elevated IFN-γ levels are one of the common reference criteria for initiating emapalumab treatment [8,10], but the exact threshold value is not clear. In our cases the evaluated IFN-γ was defined as >500 pg/mL, similar to the 573.5 pg/mL reported by Juan Liang [9], but the scientific rationality of this setting lacks evidence-based support, and further research is needed.

The treatment course of emapalumab for primary HLH in paediatric patients is approximately 8 weeks [8], but there is no recommended treatment duration for emapalumab in paediatric patients with EBV-HLH. There is a report indicating that paediatric patients of EBV-HLH without MODS received only one dose of emapalumab [14]. Case 1, Case 3, and previous report [9] indicate that 1–2 doses of emapalumab is effective to improve laboratory parameters related to HLH. Therapeutic efficacy data in this population are limited, with treatment duration ranging from 2 doses (Case 2) to 14-week courses [10]. In addition, vigilance against potential infection-related side effects is warranted during long-term treatment. Moreover, there is a risk of secondary infections caused by some opportunistic pathogens, including CMV [18]. Although it is theoretically reasonable to conduct dynamic monitoring of these pathogens [19], more data are needed to confirm its necessity.

Our preliminary explores the efficacy and safety of emapalumab in paediatric patients of EBV-HLH with MODS. These results indicate that emapalumab significantly improved body temperature and HLH-related laboratory parameters, whereas the risk of secondary infections, such as CMV infection, should not be overlooked. The treatment of refractory EBV-HLH remains challenging, and the therapeutic benefits of emapalumab still require further research.

Pulmonary hemorrhagic lesions and coronary artery aneurysm accompanied by thrombosis
Authors: Shuo Liang, Kun Zhang, Hong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis document presents a case report of a 74-year-old male patient admitted with three days of persistent chest pain accompanied by hemoptysis. Diagnostic imaging, including chest CT and coronary CT angiography, revealed pulmonary hemorrhagic lesions appearing as ground-glass opacities, predominantly located in the right lung, as well as a coronary artery aneurysm with surrounding thrombus in the circumflex artery. The case underscores the potential interplay between coronary vascular anomalies and pulmonary complications, emphasizing the value of advanced imaging techniques in diagnosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12245-026-01166-5.

A 74 - year - old male patient was admitted to the hospital because of three days of persistent chest pain. The patient experienced approximately 50 ml of hemoptysis in a single episode, which was characterized by bright red blood and occurred abruptly without preceding symptoms.

After conducting a chest CT scan and a coronary CT angiography (CTA), the imaging results showed the presence of pulmonary hemorrhagic lesions, as shown in Fig. 1A. Further analysis using cinematic rendering techniques indicated that these lesions predominantly affected the right lung, as depicted in Fig. 1B and see Supplementary file, Movie 1.

A 74-year-old male patient was admitted with three days of chest pain accompanied by hemoptysis. (A) Chest CT scan coronary reconstruction revealed ground-glass opacities. (B) Cinematic rendering showed the lesions were predominantly located in the right lung field. (C) A coronary artery aneurysm with surrounding thrombus was observed proximal to the left circumflex artery. The aneurysm sac measured 45 mm×39 mm in axial dimensions, with a neck diameter of 4 mm. (D) Cinematic rendering demonstrated the coronary artery aneurysm’s location, with the thrombus appearing as a cystic shadow. Lcx=Left circumflex artery, CAA=Coronary artery aneurysms

Additionally, the imaging revealed a significant coronary artery aneurysm and a surrounding thrombus in the circumflex artery, as illustrated in Fig. 1C. The exact location of this aneurysm was further clarified through advanced cinematic rendering, offering a clearer view of the vascular anomaly, as shown in Fig. 1D and see Supplementary file, Movie 2.

An etiological workup was performed, with pathogen testing revealing positive results for Candida albicans and Klebsiella pneumoniae. Fluconazole and ceftriaxone were administered for anti-infective treatment. The aneurysm was considered idiopathic in origin. Surgical intervention was employed.

Pulmonary lesions induced by hemoptysis typically present as ground - glass opacities on a CT scan, offering crucial insights into the underlying pathology [1].

In a separate but related context, coronary artery aneurysms (CAA) are observed in a relatively small percentage of cases, ranging from 0.3% to 5%. There is a notable predominance in males. Specifically, these aneurysms are most frequently found in the right coronary artery in approximately 40% of instances, followed by the left anterior descending artery at 32%, and the left main artery, which accounts for about 3.5% of cases [2]. The etiology of CAA is multifactorial, with atherosclerotic disease being the most common cause, accounting for approximately 50% of cases. Other contributing factors include congenital abnormalities, connective tissue disorders, and inflammatory conditions such as Kawasaki disease and syphilis. In the context of hemoptysis, it is essential to consider the potential for CAA to contribute to pulmonary complications, either through direct compression of adjacent pulmonary structures or through the embolization of thrombi from the aneurysm to the pulmonary circulation.

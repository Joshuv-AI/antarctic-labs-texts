Coiled Polydioxanone Threads (JAMBER) for Nasal Augmentation: Efficacy, Longevity, and Safety
Authors: Jong Seo Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Polydioxanone threads, specifically 40-mm tubular JAMBER (JB), 414-mm coiled around a 23G cannula, have emerged as a safe alternative for facial volume augmentation. This study evaluated the efficacy, safety, and longevity of JB threads for nasal augmentation, tracking changes in nasal height and volume across 12 months.Methods:A retrospective analysis included 23 patients (mean age 35.3 y; 8 men and 15 women) treated with JB threads from January 2023 to August 2024. Patients underwent ultrasound (Samsung RS85, Aplio i700, Konica Minolta HS2) and 3-dimensional scanner imaging (LifeViz Mini) at intervals of 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. An average of 2.85 JB threads were inserted in the nasal dorsum, complemented by 1.75 threads in the columella for tip projection.Results:The JB thread diameter decreased from 1.113 mm at 1 month to 0.857 mm at 12 months, reflecting gradual absorption. Soft-tissue thickness increased from 1.213 to 1.403 mm, indicating collagen formation. Nasal volume initially rose to 0.771 mL at 1 month and stabilized at 0.552 mL at 12 months. Similarly, nasal height increased to 1.387 mm at 1 month and remained at 1.005 mm by 12 months. No complications, such as infection or extrusion, were observed.Conclusions:JB threads provide effective and long-lasting nasal augmentation. Compared with fillers and implants, they offer a safer, minimally invasive alternative with no risk of vascular complications. Optimal outcomes require precise technique and patient selection. Further studies are warranted to validate these findings.

Takeaways

Question: Can coiled polydioxanone threads provide a minimally invasive approach for nasal augmentation with sustained structural support?

Findings: In this retrospective study of 23 patients over 12 months, coiled PDO threads were associated with increases in nasal height and volume, along with progressive soft-tissue thickening, whereas thread diameter gradually decreased without major complications.

Meaning: Coiled PDO threads may represent a minimally invasive option for nasal augmentation with sustained structural and tissue effects.

INTRODUCTION

Polydioxanone (PDO) threads are increasingly being used as an alternative to implants for facial lifting and wrinkle reduction. The tubular shape of thicker PDO threads is particularly suited for applications such as smoothing nasolabial folds and enhancing nasal projection. Although fillers were previously popular for nasal augmentation, numerous reports—over hundreds of cases—have documented serious complications, including blindness and skin necrosis, when fillers inadvertently enter blood vessels. As a result, some patients now prefer PDO threads over fillers. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of PDO thread insertion for nasal augmentation, providing insights into its potential as a safe and effective option for patients seeking nonsurgical enhancement.

JAMBER-RIDGE (JB) 40 mm is a thread composed of PDO coiled around a 21G, 70-mm cannula, with a 414-mm 3-0 PDO thread length.1 JB is used as a volume augmentation restorative agent to enhance volume in areas such as the nasolabial folds, marionette lines, forehead, chin, and nasal dorsum.1 The author has used JB specifically for nasal augmentation. The study focused on volumetric and height changes of the nose observed via 3-dimensional (3D) scanning and ultrasound over 12 months. Nasal augmentation has traditionally involved silicone implants, but the use of hyaluronic acid fillers, introduced in the 2000s, became popular despite risks such as skin necrosis and blindness. Consequently, patients now seek safer alternatives.2–7 JB threads, being thick and coiled, can achieve significant aesthetic effects with just 2–4 threads. The advantage of JB is that it poses no risk of blindness or skin necrosis, unlike fillers.

METHODS

This retrospective study evaluated the efficacy and safety of JAMBER PDO threads for nasal augmentation. Twenty-three patients, aged 21–72 years (mean age: 35.3 y; 8 men and 15 women), were treated between January 2023 and August 2024. Follow-up assessments were conducted at 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months using ultrasound (RS85; Samsung Medison, Seoul, Korea; Aplio i700; Cannon, Japan; HA2; Konica Minolta, Japan) and 3D scanner imaging (LifeViz Mini; QuantifiCare, France). Consent was obtained from all patients before the procedure.

Procedure

Before the PDO procedure, the muscle layer was identified using ultrasound, and a tumescent solution, prepared by mixing 30 mL of lidocaine and 0.3 mL of epinephrine in 100 mL of normal saline, was injected below the muscle layer to facilitate hydrodissection. An initial 1 mL of the solution was injected into the nasal spine using an insulin syringe, followed by 1 mL into the nasal tip after a 3-minute interval. For full nasal anesthesia, an additional 10 mL syringe with a 25G cannula was used to distribute the solution below the muscle layer across the nasal area. This ensured precise placement and improved patient comfort.

PDO threads were strategically inserted along the nasal structure. Threads were extended from the nasion to the nasal tip for dorsum augmentation and from the nasal tip to the columella and nasal spine for tip projection and stability. On average, 2–4 JB 21G, 40-mm (NEO Dr. INC, Gangwon-do, Korea) threads were used for the nasal dorsum, and 1–2 35-mm JAMBER-Tanxing (JB-T23) threads were inserted into the columella. The JB-T23 threads were compressed to 20 mm for enhanced elasticity, enabling immediate nasal tip projection.

Postprocedure, the entry point was covered with a hydrodressing (Duoderm thin) for 2 days. Patients were monitored at all follow-up intervals for potential complications, such as thread migration or skin thinning. Ultrasound was used throughout the procedure and follow-up to verify correct thread placement and tissue response.

Outcome Assessment

Imaging techniques were used to evaluate outcomes. Sonography was used to measure thread diameter over time and assess soft-tissue thickness, including collagen formation. A 3D scanner was used to measure changes in nasal volume and height at the 5 most elevated points. Statistical analysis assessed the maintenance of thread diameter using 95% confidence intervals with a threshold of 1 mm. Results indicated that the thread diameter was statistically significantly maintained greater than 1 mm for up to 6 months.

RESULTS

In this study, no cases among the 23 patients required removal of the JAMBER PDO threads throughout the 12-month follow-up period. Complications such as infection, migration, or extrusion were not observed, highlighting the threads’ safety profile and tolerance within the nasal tissue. On average, 2.85 PDO threads were used for the nasal dorsum, and an additional 1.75 threads were placed in the columella for elevation and enhanced tip projection. This approach allowed for precise and balanced augmentation across the nasal structure (Table 1).

Reduction in PDO Thread Diameter in Sonography Over Time

The diameter of JB 21G, 40-mm threads measured 1.113 mm at 1 month, 1.102 mm at 2 months, 1.09 mm at 3 months, 1.009 mm at 6 months, 0.953 mm at 9 months, and 0.857 mm at 12 months. Although the diameter decreased over time, the threads were still present at 12 months. The SDs were 0.027, 0.026, 0.043, 0.087, 0.091, and 0.105, respectively. This reduction in diameter indicates the gradual absorption of the PDO material. Despite this absorption, the structure remains sufficient to support tissue growth and maintain some degree of augmentation (Table 2) (Figs. 1–4). Increase in Soft-Tissue Thickness Around PDO Threads in Sonography Over Time

The ultrasound observations indicated that the actual thickened soft tissue, due to collagen formation, was greater than the thickness of the JB 21G, 40-mm threads. The thickened soft tissue measured 1.213 mm at 1 month, 1.210 mm at 2 months, 1.267 mm at 3 months, 1.288 mm at 6 months, 1.341 mm at 9 months, and 1.403 mm at 12 months, showing an increase over time. The SDs were 0.240, 0.181, 0.215, 0.1349, 0.142, and 0.145, respectively. This increase suggests significant collagen formation and granulation tissue development around the threads. The consistent increase over time implies that the body responds positively to the presence of the threads by generating new tissue, which contributes to the sustained augmentation effect, even as the threads themselves diminish.

At 1 month, there was a 10.27% increase in soft-tissue thickness, indicating early collagen formation and tissue growth. The percentage increase continued to rise over the months, reaching a 27.55% increase at 12 months. This sustained growth suggests significant collagen formation and granulation tissue development around the PDO threads. The SDs reflect the variability in measurements, with the overall trend showing a consistent increase in tissue thickness. The ongoing increase in soft-tissue thickness from the baseline indicated that the body responds positively to the PDO threads, generating new tissue that contributes to the sustained augmentation effect. The data showed a statistically significant increase in soft-tissue thickness around the PDO threads over time, starting from the baseline of 0 months with an assumed PDO thickness of 1.1 mm. The most substantial increases occurred progressively over the months, highlighting the effectiveness of the PDO threads in stimulating collagen formation and tissue growth. This confirmed the threads’ ability to provide long-lasting support and augmentation in nasal procedures (Table 3) (Fig. 5) (see Videos 3 and 4 [online]).

DISCUSSION

The initial increase in volume was likely due to immediate postprocedural swelling and the initial effect of the threads. The subsequent decrease reflected the resolution of swelling and the natural absorption of the threads. However, the final volume at 12 months remained higher than baseline, indicating a lasting augmentation effect facilitated by tissue growth around the threads. The observed changes in nasal volume as measured by the 3D scanner showed a decrease over time, despite an initial increase. This trend can be explained by several factors: (1) Resolution of swelling: postprocedural swelling typically subsides over time, leading to a reduction in measured volume. (2) Absorption of PDO threads: as the PDO threads gradually absorb, the physical bulk they contribute diminishes, resulting in a lower volume measurement. (3) Redistribution of tissue: the initial tissue response, including collagen formation, might initially contribute to increased volume, but over time, the tissue may remodel and stabilize, leading to a more natural contour with less volume than immediately after the procedure.

Although volume decreased, the height increase observed in the ultrasound data suggests that the augmentation effect might be more about structural support and lifting rather than bulk volume addition. The PDO threads likely provided a scaffold for tissue support, enhancing nasal height and shape without significantly increasing overall nasal bulk. The increased soft-tissue thickness due to collagen formation around the PDO threads, as observed in ultrasound measurements, supported the sustained elevation effect. The structural changes from collagen may contribute more to the shape and contour rather than to overall volume, explaining why volume decreases but height and structural integrity remain enhanced. The decrease in nasal volume as measured by the 3D scanner, despite initial increases, can be explained by the resolution of postprocedural swelling, gradual absorption of PDO threads, and tissue redistribution. The increased height and tissue thickness observed in ultrasound measurements highlight the effectiveness of the PDO threads in providing structural support and lift, resulting in an aesthetically pleasing nasal contour without a significant increase in bulk volume (Table 4) (Fig. 6) (see Videos 1–4 [online]). Despite the reduction in nasal height as observed by the 3D scanner, the sustained increase in soft-tissue thickness around the PDO threads suggests that the augmentation effect is still present, just less pronounced over time. The balance between the gradual absorption of the PDO threads and ongoing collagen formation results in a stabilized, but slightly reduced, nasal height over the long term. The reduction in nasal height observed by the 3D scanner and reported by patients can be explained by the natural resolution of postprocedural swelling, gradual absorption of PDO threads, and the dynamic process of collagen remodeling and tissue settling. Although the immediate postprocedure height increase was more pronounced due to swelling and the physical presence of the threads, the long-term augmentation effect was maintained through collagen formation, resulting in a more natural and subtle enhancement. This underscores the importance of managing patient expectations and understanding the dynamic nature of tissue augmentation procedures (Table 4) (Figs. 7–12) (see Videos 3 and 4 [online]).

The advantages of JB, 21G, 40-mm threads include effective volume augmentation and long-lasting results. The threads are resilient to pressure and deformation due to their elasticity. The coiled structure of JB threads minimizes discomfort and the sensation of foreign material when facial expressions are made or the nose is moved. Compared with fillers or silicone implants, JB thread procedures are simpler and shorter in duration. Importantly, JB threads are much safer than fillers in terms of vascular complications, as there is no risk of skin necrosis or blindness. The results highlight the effectiveness of JB threads in nasal augmentation, providing an initial boost in volume and height due to their physical presence and the body’s response. Over time, collagen and granulation tissue maintain the augmented structure as the threads absorb. However, the study’s small sample size (23 patients) and its retrospective, single-clinic design may limit generalizability. Future multicenter studies with larger cohorts and randomized trials are needed to confirm the long-term efficacy and safety of JAMBER PDO threads. A key limitation of this technique is the inability to selectively augment areas of irregularity or depression along the nasal dorsum. The placement of threads along the entire length of the dorsum produces a uniform, symmetrical augmentation, which may not adequately address all contour concerns. This limitation should be considered when selecting patients and setting expectations for outcomes.

Safety and Efficacy

The absence of severe complications such as skin necrosis or blindness, which are common risks with fillers, highlights the safety of JB threads. Ultrasound-guided placement ensures correct insertion, reducing the risk of thread visibility or discomfort. In this study, no cases of thread removal, infection, migration, or extrusion were observed. If removal is necessary, a minor incision under local anesthesia, guided by ultrasound, can effectively extract the threads with minimal tissue trauma. Although JB threads reduce the risk of embolism compared with fillers, they lack an immediate reversal agent like hyaluronidase, requiring careful technique. The sustained increase in soft-tissue thickness and nasal height/volume over 12 months underscores their efficacy, supported by new tissue growth around the threads.

Procedure Simplicity

The JB thread procedure is simpler and quicker compared with traditional fillers and silicone implants. The coiled structure of the threads offers flexibility, comfort, and minimal invasiveness, making them an attractive option for nasal augmentation. JB threads provide a safe and effective alternative to traditional methods, with gradual thread absorption balanced by new tissue formation for sustained improvements in nasal volume and height. Potential Complications

In this study, involving 23 patients who underwent nasal augmentation with JAMBER PDO threads, no actual complications were observed throughout the 12-month follow-up period. Specifically, there were no cases of infection, migration, extrusion, or skin necrosis. This finding underscores the safety profile of JAMBER PDO threads when applied with proper technique and monitoring. However, it is essential to remain vigilant for potential complications, such as infection, thread migration, or extrusion, as these risks, although theoretical in this study, are inherent to PDO thread procedures. Further studies with larger cohorts and extended follow-up are encouraged to confirm these safety findings.

CONCLUSIONS

Efficacy

JAMBER PDO threads demonstrate effective nasal augmentation, with increased nasal height and volume sustained during a 12-month period. The threads stimulate collagen formation, contributing to long-lasting aesthetic results.

Safety

Compared with traditional fillers, JAMBER PDO threads offer a safer alternative for nasal augmentation, particularly in reducing the risk of vascular complications such as embolism. However, their lack of easy reversibility requires careful planning and precision during placement.

Clinical Implications

This study suggests that JAMBER PDO threads are a viable and minimally invasive option for patients seeking nasal augmentation, providing durable results with minimal complications.

Future Directions

Further research with larger cohorts and extended follow-up is recommended to confirm long-term efficacy and to better understand the histological response over time.

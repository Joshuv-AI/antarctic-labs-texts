Actin-binding protein tropomodulin-1 is necessary for basal constriction formation and bud detachment in Hydra vulgaris
Authors: Shravani Kulkarni, Shreya Maity, Niraj Tadasare, Bhagyashri Joshi, Ganesh Wagh, Swarnav Bhakta, Chinmoy Patra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryTropomodulins (TMODs) are a group of proteins that stabilize the actin filament network by capping the slow-growing end of F-actin. Although their roles in tissue morphogenesis have been extensively studied in triploblastic organisms, their function in diploblastic Hydra remained unexplored. This study identified that in Hydra, TMOD1 expression is strongly upregulated in ectodermal cells at the parent-offspring junction during basal constriction formation and bud detachment. Notably, siRNA-mediated TMOD1 knockdown inhibits basal constriction formation, a pre-requisite morphological event for bud detachment. TMOD1 knockdown polyps show fragmented stress fibers and altered cell morphology adjacent to the basal constriction site. Finally, pharmacological studies showed that FGFR signaling is necessary for TMOD1 expression. Collectively, our results establish that Hydra TMOD1 is an FGFR-ERK2 signaling-regulated gene that is exclusively expressed in the epitheliomuscular ectodermal cells at the parent-offspring junction and is essential for bud separation from the parent Hydra polyp by promoting F-actin polymerization/stabilization.Subject areas: Cell biology

Introduction

The diploblastic cnidarian Hydra polyp is made of two layers, i.e., ectoderm and endoderm, both consisting of epitheliomuscular cells, separated by collagen and laminin-enriched acellular extracellular matrix called mesoglea.1,2 The third cell type present in the organism is interstitial cells, which act as a stem cell population that gives rise to a variety of cells, including nerve cells, nematocytes, and so forth.2,3,4,5,6 Hydra generally reproduce by budding, a form of asexual reproduction. Budding in Hydra begins with the movement of the cells into the budding region from other parts of the polyp, which causes the ectoderm to bulge. When the bud is fully grown, new mesoglea starts to form at the basal end of the bud, and once the endodermal cells unite, the bud separates from the parent polyp, only remaining attached with a few ectodermal cells.7,8,9 The final detachment of the offspring from the parent happens through the F-actin-based constriction at the parent-offspring junction.10 However, the molecular mechanism that regulates F-actin formation during bud separation has remained elusive.

The organization, polymerization, and depolymerization of F-actin are regulated by several proteins termed actin-binding proteins.11 Tropomodulins (TMODs) are a group of actin-binding proteins that cap the pointed ends of F-actin. More stable actin structures, such as sarcomeric thin filaments, need capping by TMODs to determine filament lengths and stability.11,12 In mammals, there are four Tmods, i.e., TMOD1-4,12,13 whereas fruit flies have only one.14 In vertebrates, TMOD1 is expressed in erythrocytes, skeletal muscle, and cardiomyocytes and is crucial for erythrocyte membrane stability and heart development.12,15,16 It is predicted that there are three TMODs, i.e., TMOD1, TMOD2, and TMOD3, in Hydra.17 However, their expression pattern and functions during bud morphogenesis in Hydra remain unknown.

Here, we explored the spatiotemporal expression pattern and functions of TMOD1 in Hydra. Consistent with previously published scRNA-seq data,17,18 RNA in situ hybridization on adult Hydra vulgaris strain 105 polyps detected scattered TMOD1 expression in endodermal cells of the foot and tentacles, in the battery cell complex at the base of the tentacles, and ectodermal cells of the body column. Notably, a marked increase in TMOD1 expression was observed in the ectodermal cells located at and around the junction between the parent and offspring during stage 9 through detachment in both Hydra vulgaris strain 105 and Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) polyps. Loss-of-function study showed that TMOD1 is crucial for F-actin organization at the parent-offspring junction and bud detachment. Finally, pharmacological studies identified that TMOD1 expression is regulated by FGFR signaling. Altogether, this study establishes that Hydra TMOD1, an FGFR-ERK2 signaling-regulated evolutionarily conserved gene, is strongly induced during bud detachment in the ectodermal cells at the parent-bud junction, and is crucial for separating the mature bud from the parent polyp.

Results

Evolutionarily conserved Hydra TMOD1 is expressed in the ectodermal cells of parent and offspring at the basal constriction site prior to bud separation

We confirmed the predicted Hydra TMOD1 mRNA sequence by cloning and sequencing. The ORF of 1050 base pairs of Hydra TMOD1 encodes a 350 amino acid-long protein with a deduced molecular weight of 38.5 kDa. It is predicted to carry a tropomodulin domain, containing a tropomyosin-dependent actin-binding site flanked by 2 tropomyosin-binding sites in its N-terminal and six α-helices with parallel β-sheets in the C-terminal, as present in its vertebrate counterparts (Figures S1A and B, S2). The amino acid identity matrix and amino acid sequence alignment showed that ∼39% similarity exists between Hydra and mammalian TMOD1 (Figure S1C, Table S1).

Next, we examined the spatiotemporal expression pattern of TMOD1. Whole-mount RNA in situ hybridization revealed scattered TMOD1 expression in the endodermal cells of the foot and tentacle, ectodermal cells of the head, and ectodermal cells of the body column in non-budding and budding Hydra vulgaris (Figures 1, S3A, and S3B). Notably, a pronounced induction of TMOD1 expression was observed at stage 9 (Figures 1C and S3B), when the offspring developed foot tissue,19 specifically at and around the junction between the parent and the bud. This elevated expression persisted through the detachment stages (Figure 1Ca). Similar increased expression of TMOD1 was observed at the parent-bud junction of the Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) strain as well (Figure S4). Sagittal sections of whole-mount in situ hybridized stage-9 and stage-10 polyps showed that TMOD1 is expressed in the ectodermal epitheliomuscular cells at the parent-bud junction (Figure 1D). Overall, our study identified that Hydra TMOD1 is evolutionarily conserved, and its expression is induced in the ectodermal cells of the parent and offspring at and adjacent to the parent-offspring junction during the basal constriction formation and bud detachment stage.

TMOD1 is crucial for bud detachment in Hydra vulgaris

Our gene expression analysis revealed that TMOD1 is exclusively expressed in the ectodermal cells adjacent to the parent-offspring junction during the basal constriction formation (Figure 1). Further, published data indicate that during advanced developmental stages, only the ectodermal cells keep the bud connected to the parent.7 Thus, we sought to explore the role of TMOD1 in bud detachment. siRNA-mediated gene knockdown was carried out to determine the loss-of-function of TMOD1 (Figure 2A). Around 74%, 72%, 70%, and 70% of the control or TMOD1 siRNA1, or siRNA2, or siRNA1/2 mixture electroporated polyps, respectively, survived one day post-electroporation (dpe) and beyond (Figure 2B, Table 1). Analysis of bright field images of live polyps revealed that all control siRNA electroporated polyps developed basal constriction at stage 10 (Figure 2C), and buds were successfully detached from the parent (Figure 2D). In contrast, around 54%, 45%, and 62% of survived TMOD1 siRNA electroporated polyps exhibited loss of basal constriction formation, stalling the separation of fully grown buds from parent polyps more than 22 days after reaching stage 10; i.e., 26 dpe (Figures 2C and 2D, Table 1).

Further, in situ hybridization showed that control siRNA-electroporated Hydra polyps developed basal constriction and expressed TMOD1 (Figure 2E). However, TMOD1 siRNA electroporated Hydra polyps that failed to develop basal constriction were devoid of TMOD1 transcripts at stage 10 (Figure 2E), indicating that the siRNA-mediated degradation of TMOD1 transcripts resulted in the inhibition of basal constriction formation. Thus, our data suggest that TMOD1 is crucial for basal constriction formation and bud detachment in Hydra.

Holz et al. showed that the F-actin organization is intricately linked to the formation of basal constrictions and the dynamics of cell shape in both the parent and bud cells adjacent to the constriction site.10 To further investigate this relationship, we examined the changes in F-actin organization and cell morphology around the constriction site between stages 8 and 10. Consistent with prior findings, maximum projections of confocal microscopy images of phalloidin-stained polyps revealed the initiation of stress fiber formation adjacent to the parent-bud junction at stage 8 (Figure 3A). By stage 10, a distinct stress fiber ring was observed at the basal constriction site, along with organized stress fibers in the ectodermal cells of both the parent and the bud adjacent to the constriction site (Figure 3A). To determine whether basal constriction formation is associated with changes in cell number, we quantified the number of cells along the short axis of the bud at stages 8 and 10. Our analysis revealed no significant reduction in cell number between these stages (Figure 3C). Further, to investigate changes in cell morphology during basal constriction formation, we analyzed various morphological parameters in confocal optical sections of whole-mount phalloidin-stained Hydra at stages 8 and 10. Our data exhibited a decrease in cell size and cell circularity along with an increase in cell elongation parallel to the long axis of the bud body during stage 8 to stage 10 development (Figures 3D–3F). Besides these cellular events, we identified two layers of elongated cells encircling the bud base and aligned in parallel to the parent body (demarcated cell contours in cyan) in stage 10 Hydra (Figure 3B). To further validate the morphology and arrangement of these cells, stage 10 Hydra were stained with CF-633-conjugated wheat germ agglutinin (WGA), which labels the cell membrane. Confocal imaging confirmed the presence of two layers of elongated cells encircling the bud base (demarcated cell contours in cyan), which were observed in the phalloidin-stained Hydra (Figure 3B, 3G, and 3H).

Since TMOD1 knockdown resulted in the suppression of basal constriction formation, we investigated the F-actin organization in control or TMOD1-siRNA electroporated Hydra polyps. Maximum projections of confocal images of phalloidin-stained control-siRNA electroporated Hydra revealed the presence of an F-actin ring at the basal constriction site, accompanied by organized stress fibers in the ectodermal cells of both parent and bud adjacent to the constriction (Figure 3I). In contrast, TMOD1 knockdown polyps at stage 10 lacked F-actin organization (Figure 3I). Quantification of the number of cells along the short axis of the bud revealed no significant difference in cell number between the control and TMOD1 siRNA-treated polyps at stage 10, indicating TMOD1 knockdown does not induce cell death or cell proliferation (Figures 3J and 3K). Additionally, unlike control polyps, the bud cells adjacent to the parent polyp in the TMOD1-siRNA electroporated polyps failed to constrict and elongate and remained larger in size (Figures 3J and 3L–3N). Collectively, these findings establish that TMOD1 plays a critical role in regulating basal constriction formation and bud detachment by facilitating F-actin organization and the formation of actin rings at the parent-offspring junction.

FGFR-ERK signaling is necessary for TMOD1 expression in Hydra

A previous study showed that kringelchen, the Hydra FGFR-like gene, is expressed at the bud base from stage 4 onwards.20 Further, the pharmacological inhibition of FGFR-kinase activity, as well as phosphorothioate (PT) antisense oligonucleotides-based inhibition of kringelchen translation in Hydra vulgaris, showed that FGFR signaling is crucial for basal disc formation, boundary formation, tissue constriction, and ultimately bud detachment.20 Since our preceding data showed that TMOD1 is expressed at the bud base and adjacent tissue and is essential for bud separation (Figures 1 and 2), we sought to explore whether FGFR-signaling regulates TMOD1 expression.

SU5402-mediated pharmacological inhibition of FGFR-signaling (32) (Figure 4A) showed that bud development in SU5402 treated polyps was indistinguishable from their control polyps; however, basal constriction formation was arrested, even when they reached stage 10 (Figures 4B–4E). Further, in situ hybridization on stage 10 polyps showed that while around 81% of vehicle-treated polyps were TMOD1 positive, all the SU5402-treated Hydra vulgaris polyps were devoid of TMOD1 expression (Figures 4B and 4C). Similarly, in the Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) strain, while 60% of the vehicle-treated polyps were TMOD1 positive, only around 13% polyps showed TMOD1 expression as a ring, and 20% polyps showed TMOD1 expression in a few cells at the parent bud junction on SU5402 treatment (Figures 4D and 4E). Thus, our results suggest that FGFR signaling is necessary for TMOD1 expression.

A study on Xenopus laevis embryos identified that the FGFR1-dependent activation of ERK2 phosphorylation leads to F-actin organization upon mechanical stress stimuli21 and inhibition of ERK phosphorylation in stage 7 Hydra delayed bud detachment.22 However, early transient inhibition of ERK phosphorylation at stage 3 for 24 h did not affect basal constriction formation and bud detachment.22 Thus, we explored TMOD1 expression in stage 7 Hydra polyps treated with U0126,22 an ERK2 phosphorylation inhibitor or vehicle (Figure 5A). Gene expression analysis 12 h post-treatment showed that while ∼60% of vehicle-treated polyps were TMOD1 positive, none of the U0126-treated polyps showed TMOD1 expression in both Hydra strains (Figures 5B, 5C, S5A, and S5B). Taken together, our study indicates that the FGFR-ERK2 signaling axis regulates TMOD1 expression in Hydra during bud excision.

Discussion

The molecular mechanisms separating offsprings from their parent polyps are poorly understood in the cnidarian Hydra. Our study on Hydra vulgaris demonstrates that FGFR-ERK signaling regulated actin-binding protein TMOD1 is highly conserved with its vertebrate counterpart and is required for F-actin based basal constriction formation, an essential event for the detachment of the mature offspring from their parents. Cnidarian Hydra polyps usually follow asexual reproduction, called budding. A young Hydra develops as a bud on the body wall of the mature polyp; it grows and finally separates away as a clone of the parent polyp. During bud morphogenesis, molecular marker-based evidence of a parent-offspring boundary is detected at stage 5 with the expression of FGFR in cells on both sides of the parent–bud junction until stage 7.20 At early stage 8, when the constriction between parent-offspring appears, FGFR expression gets restricted to a thin stripe of cells of the bud base.19 Further, FGFR-expressing cells also express matrix metalloprotease coding gene MMPA3 under the control of FGFR signaling.19 In contrast, the TMOD1 expression domain is much broader than the FGFR expression, and its expression initiates much later than the FGFR expression. Thus, it is unlikely that FGFR signaling directly regulates TMOD1 expression. Since MMPA3 is exclusively expressed in the bud base cells and its expression overlaps with the parent-offspring constriction formation, it is evident that matrix metalloprotease-mediated ECM degradation is crucial for constriction formation. It is possible that mechanical stress due to the FGFR-MMPA3-mediated degradation of ECM molecules19 at the parent-offspring junction induces TMOD1 expression in the surrounding ectodermal cells, and TMOD1 activity completes bud separation from the parent and closure of the bud attachment site of the parent polyp by epitheliomuscular ectodermal tissue contraction.

Tropomodulins bind to the pointed end and regulate the length of thin filaments by inhibiting the addition or removal of actin monomer from this end. Besides actin filament capping activity, TMOD1 protects actin filaments from severing by interacting with Tropomyosin, another group of actin-binding proteins,23,24,25 indicating TMOD1 stabilizes thin filaments. Similar to these studies, TMOD1 knockdown showed fragmented stress fibers in stage 10 Hydra. In contrast, antibody-mediated neutralization of Tmod1 in chick cardiomyocytes showed increased F-actin length.26 Similarly, mutations in TMOD1 in humans showed decreased affinity to the actin-pointed end, and thin filaments in the patient-derived cardiomyocytes were found to be elongated.27 It is likely that in higher vertebrates, unknown compensatory mechanisms get activated in the lack or compromised TMOD1 activity, resulting in elongated thin filaments, which are missing in the more primitive organism, Hydra. Thus, our study indicates the need to explore the compensatory mechanism in patients harboring a mutation in TMOD1 to develop strategies to suppress the progression of cardiomyopathy.

Affinity of tropomodulins to the pointed end of the actin filament is tropomyosin-dependent.23,28,29 In vertebrates, there are more than three dozen tropomyosin isoforms that are derived from alternative RNA splicing from four distinct genes: α, β, γ, and δ, and tropomodulin/tropomyosin interaction is isoform-specific.13 In Hydra, only one tropomyosin gene, named TROP1, has been detected. In adult Hydra polyps, TROP1 is predominantly expressed in the endodermal epithelial cells of the foot, tentacles, and head, ectodermal battery cell clusters, and basal disc.17,18,30 Since studies on vertebrates showed that the function of TMOD1 is tropomyosin-dependent, in the future, it will be interesting to explore the expression pattern of TROP1 and its function in bud maturation and separation. Taken together, the identification of Hydra bud detachment-specific actin-binding protein coding gene TMOD1 highlights the need for further study to identify the mechanism leading to F-actin elongation and cardiomyopathy in patients harboring a mutation in TMOD1 and opens up new avenues to explore the molecular and cellular mechanisms involved in the separation of parent and mature offspring in Hydra.

Limitations of the study

One limitation of this study is the inability to generate gene-specific stable mutant Hydra. Thus, this study relies on siRNA-mediated knockdown, which may not achieve complete or uniform suppression of TMOD1 across all cells, potentially affecting the consistency of phenotypic outcomes. Additionally, while TMOD1’s role in basal constriction and F-actin organization is clearly established, the study does not fully explore the downstream effectors or interacting partners of TMOD1 that mediate these processes. Finally, functional redundancy with other actin-regulating proteins was not ruled out, which could influence the interpretation of TMOD1-specific effects.

Experimental model and study participant details

Different developmental stages of Hydra vulgaris strain 10532 and Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune)31 strain polyps were used in this study.

Method details

Hydra maintenance

The polyps were maintained at 18°C, 12 h light/dark cycle, in Hydra medium. Hydra medium for Hydra vulgaris consists of 0.29 mM of CaCl2.2H2O, 0.33 mM MgSO4.7H2O, 0.5 mM NaHCO3, and 0.09 mM K2CO3 in water and pH was adjusted to 7.8.33 Hydra medium for Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) strain consists of 0.1 mM KCl, 1 mM NaCl, 0.1 mM MgSO4.7H2O, 1 mM CaCl2.2H2O, 1 mM Tris-HCl in water, and pH was adjusted to 8.0.34 Both media were aerated overnight before being used. The polyps were fed Artemia nauplii four times a week and cleaned daily to ensure healthy and synchronized budding. All experiments were repeated at least 3 times to ensure comparable and reproducible results.

RNA extraction and cloning of Hydra TMOD1

Total RNA was extracted from a pool of 40–50 Hydra vulgaris or Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) polyps of different stages using TRIZOL reagent (Invitrogen). cDNA was prepared using the M-MLV Reverse Transcriptase kit following the manufacturer’s instruction (Invitrogen). Partial cDNA of TMOD1 (NCBI Reference Sequence: XM_002156420.5) was amplified using primer pairs mentioned in Table S2. Primers were manually designed using NCBI Primer BLAST tool35 based on the predicted sequence of Hydra TMOD1. Two fragments of partial cDNA of predicted Hydra TMOD1 were amplified from cDNA obtained from Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) to cover the complete coding sequence of TMOD1 and cloned into a pGEM-T Easy vector (Promega) (Clone 1 and 2). Cloned cDNA was sequenced to confirm the TMOD1 mRNA sequence. Sequenced data was aligned with the predicted sequence of Hydra TMOD1. For ISH, 538 bp long partial cDNA was amplified (Table S2) and cloned into a pGEM-T Easy vector (Clone 3).

Comparative analysis of TMOD1 amino acid sequences and 3D protein structure

The amino acid sequences and 3D structures of TMOD1 of Hydra vulgaris (as per our data of the cloned cDNA of Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune)), Drosophila melanogaster (fruitfly) (NP_477031.1), Danio rerio (zebrafish) (XP_001920602.1), Mus musculus (mouse) (NP_068683.1) and Homo sapiens (human) (NP_001159588.1) were analyzed to identify conservation in amino acid sequence and 3D protein structure. Multiple sequence alignment was performed using the EMBL-EBI Clustal Omega Tool.36 The 3D protein structures for TMOD1 orthologs [Hydra vulgaris (T2MGE2), Drosophila melanogaster (O46321_DROME), Danio rerio (E9QGR6), Mus musculus (P49813), Homo sapiens (P28289)] were obtained from UniProtKB37 and Swiss-Model,38 and molecular visualization of the 3D protein structures was carried out using the PyMol tool (The PyMol Molecular Graphics System, Version 2.0 Schrödinger, LLC). The major domains of TMOD1 (Hydra) were identified using EMBL-EBI InterPro.39

In situ hybridization and imaging

In situ hybridization was carried out according to Bode et al. described previously.40 Sense and antisense RNA probes were synthesized using linearised Clone 3 (Table S2) as a template. SacII/SP6- RNA polymerase combination and SpeI/T7-RNA polymerase combination were employed to linearize the vector and synthesize DIG-labelled sense and antisense RNA probes, respectively, using a DIG RNA labeling kit (Roche). The synthesized DIG-labeled RNA probes were used for in situ hybridization.

Polyps starved for 48 h were relaxed in 2% urethane in Hydra medium for 2 min and fixed in 4% PFA in PBS at 4°C overnight. Fixed polyps were dehydrated using a series of methanol solutions in PBS with increasing concentrations and stored in 100% methanol at −20OC. For in situ hybridization, dehydrated polyps were rehydrated with a decreasing concentration of methanol solutions in PBST (0.1% Tween 20 in 1X PBS) and washed 5 times in PBST. Next, rehydrated polyps were permeabilized by incubating for 10 min in proteinase K solution (10 mg/mL in PBST), and the reaction was arrested by washing in glycine solution (4 mg/mL in PBST). Permeabilized polyps were incubated in 100 mM triethanolamine in PBST for 5 min to reduce the non-specific signal. The polyps were washed 3 times in PBST and fixed overnight in 4% PFA in PBS at 4°C. Refixed polyps were washed 3 times in PBST and twice in 2X SSC (Saline Sodium Citrate) buffer (30 mM Trisodium citrate and 300 mM NaCl). Subsequently, polyps were equilibrated in a pre-hybridization buffer at room temperature for 10 min. Pre-hybridization buffer contained 50% formamide, 25% 5X SSC, 0.5% 200X Denhardt’s reagent (2% polyvinylpyrrolidone, 2% Ficoll, and 2% BSA), 0.1% CHAPS, 0.1 mg/mL heparin and 0.1% Tween 20 in water. The polyps were subsequently incubated in a hybridization buffer (1 mg/mL yeast tRNA (Roche) in pre-hybridization buffer) at 60°C for 2 h. The polyps were transferred to a fresh vial and incubated in the hybridization solution [(Dig-labeled RNA probes (sense or antisense) in hybridization buffer (500 μg/mL)] for 60 h at 60°C. Hybridized polyps were washed twice in a pre-hybridization buffer and a series of solutions of pre-hybridization buffer in 2X SSC with a decreasing ratio of pre-hybridization buffer and finally twice in 2X SSC. The polyps were washed thrice in 0.5X SSC having 0.1% CHAPS, followed by 4 washes in 1X MABT (100 mM maleic acid, 150 mM NaCl, and 0.1% Tween 20 in water). Washed polyps were blocked in 20% goat serum in MABT for 3–4 h at RT and incubated with anti-digoxigenin AP fab fragments (Roche; 1:3000) at 4°C overnight. Post-antibody binding, polyps were washed 4 times in MABT, followed by 3-time in NTMT buffer (100 mM Tris-HCl pH 9.5, 100 mM NaCl, 50 mM MgCl2, and 0.1% Tween 20) having 1% levamisole. For color development, polyps were incubated in BM-Purple solution (Roche) in the dark at room temperature for the required time. The staining reaction was stopped by washing in double-distilled water, and polyps were refixed with 4% PFA in PBS. Refixed polyps were dehydrated using a series of methanol solutions in PBS with increasing concentrations and stored in 100% methanol at 4°C for imaging. Whole mount in situ hybridized polyps were imaged on a Leica M205 FA Fluorescence Stereoscope using Leica Application Suite Software (Leica, Germany). Images were processed in ImageJ.41

Histological analysis

To explore the spatial expression of TMOD1, dehydrated whole mount in situ hybridized Hydra polyps were rehydrated by decreasing the methanol gradient in PBS and washed twice in PBS. The polyps were equilibrated in 30% sucrose in PBS overnight and then mounted in Tissue Freezing Medium (Leica, Germany). The mounted cryoblocks were frozen at −25°C and sectioned using Leica Cryostat CM1520 (Leica, Germany). Sections of 10 μm thickness were collected on a positively charged glass slide (Thermafrost), washed in water, and mounted in the Mowiol mounting medium. Imaging was carried out on a Leica M205 FA stereoscope or Leica DM5500B inverted microscope and Leica Application Suite Software.

siRNA-mediated knockdown

Two siRNA against Hydra TMOD1 (XM_002156420.5) were designed as described by Lommel et al.42 and acquired from Eurogentec (Table S3). One siRNA was targeted from 306 bp to 324 bp downstream of the start codon (TMOD1_siRNA1), and another siRNA was targeted from 779 bp to 797 bp downstream of the start codon (TMOD1_siRNA2). Control siRNA duplex for in vivo from Eurogentec was used as a control. 20 μM of the stock solution of siRNA was prepared by reconstituting in nuclease-free water. A 5 μM final concentration of control siRNA or TMOD1_siRNA1 or TMOD1_siRNA2 or a mixture of TMOD1_siRNA1 and TMOD1_siRNA2 (2.5 μM each) was used for the experiments. All siRNA-mediated knockdown experiments were carried out on 24 h starved stage-2 Hydra polyps. Polyps were thoroughly washed in nuclease-free water, and for each condition, 20 polyps were then transferred to an electroporation cuvette (Gene Pulser/MicroPulser Electroporation Cuvettes, 0.4 cm gap, Bio-Rad; Cat#1652088). Excess water was replaced with 200 μL nuclease-free water having 5 μM siRNA. The polyps were left at room temperature to relax for 2–3 min, and then a single square wave pulse (300 V) was applied for 50 ms using a Bio-Rad Gene Pulser Xcell Electroporation System. 500 μL of ice-cold recovery medium (8:2 mixture of Hydra medium and dissociation medium) was then added to the cuvette slowly, and the polyps were collected in a fresh Petri dish containing fresh, ice-cold recovery medium and incubated overnight at 18°C. For the siRNA-mediated knockdown assay, three independent experiments were performed (Table 1). The dissociation medium consisted of 3.6 mM KCl, 6 mM CaCl2, 1.2 mM MgSO4, 6 mM Na-citrate, 6 mM pyruvate, 4 mM glucose, 12.5 mM TES pH 6.9, 0.05 g/L rifampicin, 0.10 g/L streptomycin, and 0.05 g/L kanamycin. The next day, the recovery medium was replaced with a fresh Hydra medium, and polyps were fed daily from 2 days post-electroporation (dpe). Both control siRNA or TMOD1-siRNA electroporated polyps reached stage 10 at 3 dpe. Thus, from 3 dpe onwards, Hydra polyps were considered for phenotypic characterization, stress fiber organization, and TMOD1 expression analysis. Imaging was carried out on a Leica M205 FA Fluorescence Stereoscope.

F-actin and wheat germ agglutinin (WGA) staining and imaging

Alexa Fluor 488 conjugated phalloidin staining was performed on untreated or siRNA electroporated Hydra polyps as previously described22 to evaluate stress fiber organization and cellular morphology. In short, 24 h starved polyps at different developmental stages were relaxed in 2% urethane in Hydra medium and fixed in 4% PFA in PBS for 2 h at 4°C. Fixed polyps were washed twice in PBST and permeabilized by washing in 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS (PBT) for 30 min. Polyps were then incubated in Alexa Fluor 488 conjugated phalloidin in PBT (1:250; Invitrogen) for 2 h at 4°C and subsequently washed thrice in PBS and mounted in 50% glycerol. For WGA staining, fixed polyps were washed twice in PBST and incubated in CF-633 conjugated WGA in PBS (1:500; Biotium) for 1 h at 4°C and subsequently washed thrice in PBS and mounted in 50% glycerol. Imaging of whole-mount phalloidin or WGA-stained polyps was performed on a Leica SP8 laser scanning microscope (Leica Microsystems, Germany), and a maximum projection of optical sections was generated using Leica LAS AF Lite Software (Leica Microsystems, Germany). Images were processed in ImageJ.

Bud cell number along the short axis and cell shape analysis

The cell number in one tier along the short axis of the bud, 40 μm away from the parent-polyp junction in stage 8, stage 10, control siRNA, or TMOD1 siRNA electroporated polyps was counted manually. Cell surface area, circularity, and aspect ratio were carried out using ImageJ. 25 to 30 cells were considered for each polyp, and 10 polyps were considered for each experimental condition. For cell surface area analysis, cells adjacent to the parent-bud junction were considered. To analyze the difference between circularity and aspect ratio of cells in stage 8 and stage 10 budding Hydra polyps, or control and TMOD1 siRNA electroporated stage 10 polyps, cells in the bud 40 μm away from the parent-bud junction were analyzed.

Pharmacological treatment

Treatment with SU5402 (Sigma), an FGFR inhibitor, or U0126 (Sigma), an ERK2 phosphorylation inhibitor, was performed as mentioned previously20,22 with necessary standardizations. The stock solutions of SU5402 (17 mM) or U0126 (25 mM) were prepared in DMSO and stored at −20°C. 1 mM ATP solution in Hydra medium was prepared fresh for all experiments and diluted as required. 24 h starved polyps were used for all pharmacological treatments, and polyps were incubated at 18°C in the dark.

For SU5402 treatment, stage 3 polyps of Hydra vulgaris and Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) strain were incubated in 10 μM SU5402 and 0.3 mM ATP, and 1 μM SU5402 and 0.1 mM ATP in Hydra medium, respectively, for 24 h (Figure 4A). Vehicle control Hydra vulgaris or Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) received 0.1% DMSO and 0.3 mM or 0.1 mM ATP, respectively. After treatment, the polyps of both strains were recovered in Hydra medium for 36 h and fixed in 4% PFA in PBS.

For U0126 treatment, stage 7 polyps of Hydra vulgaris strain were incubated in 10 μM U0126 and 0.9 mM ATP in Hydra medium for 24 h; stage 7 polyps of Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) strain were incubated in 10 μM U0126 and 0.25 mM ATP in Hydra medium for 24 h (Figure 5A). Vehicle control Hydra vulgaris and Hydra vulgaris (Ind-Pune) received 0.1% DMSO and 0.9 mM or 0.25 mM ATP, respectively. For each strain, two sets of polyps received U0126 treatment. Similar to control groups, one treated group was recovered for 12 h in Hydra medium after 24 h treatment and fixed in 4% PFA in PBS, whereas another treated group was recovered for 24 h.

Quantification and statistical analysis

For the post-electroporation survivability and bud detachment quantification, 20 and 13–15 polyps, respectively, from each of three independent experiments were considered. The Student’s T-test and one-way ANOVA, followed by Bonferroni’s post-hoc test, were employed to analyze statistical differences between two samples and more than two samples, respectively, to analyze statistical differences in survivability, TMOD1 knockdown, and cell morphology between control and TMOD1 siRNA electroporated polyps. 30 polyps from 3 independent experiments for each experimental condition were included for statistical analysis of the percentage of animals showing the variable degrees of TMOD1 expression after SU5402 or U0126 treatment or siRNA-mediated TMOD1 knockdown polyps were carried out using the Chi-square test. All data represented as mean ± SEM. For all analyses, statistical significance was considered at p < 0.05. The analysis and graph construction were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 software.

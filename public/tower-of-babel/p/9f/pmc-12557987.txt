Effect of perioperative intranasal insulin on postoperative delirium in elderly hip fracture patients undergoing general anesthesia: a randomized controlled study
Authors: Jianpeng Wang, Chenxu Dai, Huihui Li, Jialiang Wang, Shuangshuang Guan, Qiufeng Wang, Ning Cai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivePostoperative delirium (POD) frequently occurs among elderly patients following surgery, yet effective preventive measures remain insufficiently established. This study aimed to investigate whether perioperative intranasal insulin (INI) administration could reduce the incidence of POD and modulate inflammatory marker levels in elderly patients undergoing hip fracture surgery under general anesthesia (GA).MethodsFrom June to December 2024, a total of 96 elderly patients scheduled for hip fracture surgery with GA were enrolled and randomly assigned to two groups (48 per group). Patients in the insulin group received INI (20 units in 0.5 mL) twice daily, beginning two days preoperatively and continuing for five days. Patients in the control group received an equivalent volume of saline (0.5 mL) with the same dosing regimen. The primary outcome was POD incidence within five postoperative days, evaluated using the 3D-CAM-CN assessment tool. Secondary outcomes included serum levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and C-reactive protein (CRP), measured at baseline (T0; before intervention) and postoperative day 1 (T1) using immunofluorescence quantitative assays.ResultsNinety-two patients completed the study, with an overall POD incidence rate of 20.7%. Patients treated with INI showed a significantly lower incidence of POD within the 5-day postoperative period (10.9% vs. 30.4%, P = 0.020). On postoperative day 3 (T2), the incidence of POD was notably reduced in the insulin group (2.2% vs. 19.6%, P = 0.007). No significant differences in POD incidence were observed on postoperative days 1 (T1) or 5 (T3). Serum IL-6 and CRP levels at T1 were lower in the insulin group compared to controls (P < 0.001), although both groups experienced significant elevations in these markers at T1 compared to baseline (T0) (P < 0.001). No adverse events related to interventions occurred during the study.ConclusionPerioperative INI administration reduced the incidence of POD and decreased postoperative inflammatory markers (IL-6 and CRP) in elderly patients undergoing hip fracture surgery with GA.Trial registrationThis study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Register (registration no. ChiCTR2400085627, registration date on 14/6/2024).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-025-03406-2.Keywords: Postoperative delirium, Insulin, Elderly patients, Hip fracture, Delirium, Surgery, Postoperative cognitive complications, Neuropsychological tests

Introduction

POD is a prevalent central nervous system complication among elderly patients who have undergone surgery. POD typically occurs within 7 days after surgery, presenting symptoms such as inattention, disorganized thinking, and alterations in consciousness levels [1]. POD not only increases the incidence and mortality of cardiopulmonary complications but also extends the length of hospitalization, escalates healthcare costs, and imposes a substantial burden on society and families [2, 3].

It is known that POD incidence in patients aged 65 and older ranges from 5% to 50% [4]. POD incidence varies among different types of surgeries. For instance, POD incidence in patients with hip fractures is between 19.7% and 40% [5, 6]. As global aging progresses, the number of hip fractures caused by falls among the elderly continues to rise, leading to a higher proportion of hip fracture surgeries in this population [7]. Effective strategies for preventing and managing POD in patients undergoing hip fracture surgery remain significant yet unresolved clinical challenges. Despite extensive research, the exact underlying pathophysiological mechanisms associated with POD are not completely understood. Existing evidence indicates that several risk factors, including advanced age, surgical interventions such as hip or cardiac surgery, postoperative pain, dementia, and depression, significantly contribute to POD occurrence [8]. Nevertheless, reliable and effective preventive strategies specifically designed for these high-risk groups have yet to be identified.

Experimental studies suggest that intranasally delivered insulin can effectively bypass the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and directly reach the brain through the trigeminal and olfactory neural routes [9]. Moreover, clinical evidence indicates that INI yields positive results in treating cognitive impairments, including Alzheimer’s disease (AD), Parkinson’s disease, and mild cognitive dysfunction [10–12]. Additionally, it has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing memory capabilities in healthy individuals [13, 14]. Collectively, these observations highlight INI’s promising therapeutic potential as a novel method for preventing and managing POD.

Research by Glumac et al. demonstrated that corticosteroid administration before surgery could reduce inflammation triggered by surgical procedures, thereby lowering both the frequency and severity of postoperative cognitive impairment [15]. Similarly, animal model studies have confirmed that INI administration can significantly improve cognitive functions by reducing neuroinflammation [16, 17].

Clinical trials by Huang et al. reported decreased POD incidence with preoperative INI administration among cancer patients [18, 19]. Similarly, Li et al. also found a significant reduction in POD occurrence among elderly hip fracture surgery patients undergoing spinal anesthesia following INI administration at doses of 20 or 40 units preoperatively [20]. However, clinical trials investigating perioperative INI administration among elderly hip fracture patients undergoing GA are currently lacking. Unlike earlier studies, our trial implemented multiple perioperative INI administrations to evaluate its efficacy in reducing POD incidence, and to explore its effects on serum inflammatory markers (CRP and IL-6) in non-cancer elderly patients. We hypothesized that compared with placebo administration, perioperative INI would decrease both POD incidence and inflammatory responses post-surgery.

Methods

Patients

The randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind clinical trial was conducted at Fuyang People’s Hospital, enrolling participants between June 21 and December 31, 2024. The Ethics Committee of Fuyang People’s Hospital approved the study on January 22, 2024 (Medical Ethics Review [2023]95). The trial received prospective registration in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on June 14, 2024. All trial procedures strictly followed ethical guidelines outlined by the Declaration of Helsinki and adhered to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT). Informed written consent was obtained from every patient participating in the trial.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Participants comprised elderly individuals scheduled for hip fracture surgery at Fuyang People’s Hospital, randomly assigned into either an insulin-treated group (Group I) or placebo control group (Group C). Inclusion criterion: age ≥ 60 years, body mass index (BMI): 18–28 kg/m2, scheduled for hip fracture surgery under GA, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification of physical status I–III, and written informed consent provided. Exclusion criteria involved significant impairment of cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic, or renal functions; contraindications for intranasal drug administration; known hypersensitivity to insulin; psychiatric or neurodegenerative diseases; chronic usage of antidepressants or sedatives; history of alcohol abuse; cognitive evaluation interference due to severe visual or auditory impairment; systemic illnesses potentially affecting inflammatory biomarkers related to cognition; low preoperative Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores [21]; and presence of delirium before surgery. Criteria for withdrawal included administration of insulin via routes other than intranasal during the trial, operation duration surpassing 3 h, subsequent surgery within five postoperative days, unforeseen adverse reactions, intraoperative blood loss exceeding 500 mL, and patients lost to follow-up (e.g., discharge within four postoperative days, except for patients discharged on the 4 day who were confirmed POD-negative on day 5).

Randomization

Eligible participants were randomly divided at a 1:1 ratio using computer-produced allocation tables. Group assignments were managed by an anesthesiology nurse who did not participate in the study. The nurse prepared the solutions, while researchers administered them. Patients, clinicians, and assessors remained blinded throughout the study. Unblinding was only allowed when there were major adverse incidents or clinical deterioration recorded in the case report forms (CRFs).

Intervention protocol

Two groups underwent intranasal administration via disposable nasal devices (NEST Biotechnology, Wuxi, China) from 2 days before surgery to 2 days after surgery (a total of 5 days), twice daily at 09:00 and 19:00, respectively. The Group C was administered 0.5 mL of saline, while the Group I was administered 20 U of insulin (Wanbang Biopharma, Jiangsu, China).

Anesthesia protocol

Throughout surgery, continuous monitoring was conducted for heart rate (HR), arterial blood pressure, electrocardiography (ECG), pulse oximetry (SpO2), and bispectral index (BIS). GA induction involved intravenous administration of sufentanil (0.5 µg/kg), etomidate (0.3 mg/kg), and cisatracurium (0.2 mg/kg). Anesthesia was maintained using sevoflurane inhalation at concentrations between 0.4 and 0.6 MAC, supplemented with continuous intravenous infusion of remifentanil (10–20 µg·kg⁻¹·h⁻¹) and propofol (2–4 mg·kg⁻¹·h⁻¹) to achieve a BIS between 40 and 60, a range associated with reduced POD in elderly surgical patients [22]. End-tidal carbon dioxide (PETCO2) was kept between 35 and 45 mmHg, and arterial pressure fluctuations were limited to within ± 20% of baseline readings. Intraoperative body temperature determined the requirement for warming blankets. Postoperative pain management included patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) comprising sufentanil (2.5 µg/kg) and flurbiprofen axetil (0.8 mg/kg) in a total volume of 100 mL diluted with 0.9% saline [23]. The analgesia pump was configured for a background infusion rate of 2 mL/h, a PCA dose of 0.5 mL per activation, and a lockout period of 15 min.

Data collection

Demographic and clinical baseline data, as well as medical histories, were gathered during the screening phase. The cognitive function was evaluated preoperatively using the MMSE scale. After confirming that the participants met the inclusion criteria and completed the enrollment, their operative method, surgical time, and blood loss during surgery were recorded. Additionally, hospitalization duration and perioperative complications of the participants were obtained from the clinical medical records.

POD assessment

The primary outcome was the incidence of POD, assessed twice daily (9:00 AM and 7:00 PM) on postoperative day 1 (T1), day 3 (T2), and day 5 (T3) by trained evaluators using the Chinese-adapted 3-Minute Diagnostic Interview for CAM-defined Delirium (3D-CAM-CN), which has been validated specifically for Chinese surgical patient populations [24]. Researchers performing POD assessments were blinded to patient allocation. The assessment process entailed a thorough review of medical records, along with discussions with the designated ward nurses and relatives (if they were accessible). Participants who tested positive during the initial screening for POD were then subjected to further confirmation by an experienced geriatric psychiatrist.

Inflammatory marker measurement

Secondary endpoints included serum inflammatory biomarker levels at the preoperative baseline (T0; before intervention) and on T1. Blood samples (3 mL) were collected from the internal jugular vein of each patient between 7:00 AM and 8:00 AM at both assessment points. Following centrifugation at 4000 rpm for 10 min, serum samples were separated and preserved at −20 °C. Immunochromatographic fluorescence quantitative assays (Zhejiang Zhonghan Biotechnology Co. Ltd., China) were employed to determine serum CRP and IL-6 concentrations.

Pain assessment

After the initial POD evaluations on T1, T2, and T3, resting pain intensity was assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS). Pain intensity ratings ranged from 0 (no pain) through mild (1–3), moderate (4–6), to severe pain (7–10).

Other parameters

Fasting blood glucose levels were measured between 7:00 AM and 8:00 AM at T0, T1, and T2. During the study period, any occurrences of hypoglycemia or other serious adverse events related to the study among participants were systematically recorded, followed by the unblinding of the study and the discontinuation of the participants’ involvement.

Sample size and statistical analysis

Sample size determination was performed utilizing PASS 15.0 software, assuming a two-sided significance level (α) of 0.05, statistical power (1-β) of 0.8, and equal distribution across groups. The null hypothesis stipulated no significant difference in POD incidence between groups within five postoperative days, while the alternative hypothesis suggested an existing difference. Given previous reports indicating POD incidences of 35% in Group C and 10% in Group I, at least 43 participants were required per group. Considering an estimated dropout rate of about 10%, the total required sample size was calculated as 96 participants, with 48 per group.

Statistical analysis was performed utilizing SPSS version 23.0 software. Continuous variables exhibiting normal distribution were presented as means ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using independent samples t-tests or Welch’s test for intergroup comparisons and paired t-tests for intragroup evaluations. Data with non-normal distributions were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) and assessed using Mann-Whitney U tests. Frequencies and percentages summarized categorical variables, which were compared using χ² or Fisher’s exact tests. All P-values were calculated using two-sided tests, and statistical significance was defined as a P-value < 0.05.

Results

Initially, 129 patients were screened for study eligibility; 28 failed to meet inclusion criteria, and 5 declined participation. Among the 96 enrolled participants, 4 were subsequently excluded (two due to non-INI administration and two due to loss to follow-up), resulting in 46 participants remaining in each group (Fig. 1). Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics were similar across both groups, showing no significant differences (Table 1).

Primary outcomes

Overall, 19 patients developed POD, indicating an incidence rate of 20.7%. Within the initial five postoperative days, POD incidence in Group I was reduced (10.9% vs. 30.4%, P = 0.020). Temporal analysis indicated that POD incidence rates at T1 were 8.7% (Group I) and 19.6% (Group C) (P = 0.135); at T2, these rates were 2.2% versus 19.6% (P = 0.007); and at T3, 2.2% versus 4.3% (P = 1.000). The overall incidence of POD in both groups progressively decreased over the evaluation period (Table 2).

Secondary outcomes

Serum IL-6 and CRP levels at baseline (T0) were statistically similar between groups (P > 0.05). On postoperative day 1 (T1), significant elevations in CRP and IL-6 levels were observed compared to baseline measurements in both groups (P < 0.001). Notably, patients in Group I exhibited lower CRP and IL-6 serum concentrations at T1 (P < 0.001; Table 3). Additionally, analysis across the entire patient cohort indicated that individuals diagnosed with POD exhibited notably higher serum CRP and IL-6 concentrations at T1 relative to patients without POD (P < 0.001; Fig. 2).

Discussion

This study explored the effects of perioperative INI on POD incidence and levels of inflammatory markers (CRP and IL-6) in elderly patients undergoing GA for hip fracture surgery. Findings revealed a significant reduction in POD occurrence during the first five postoperative days and lower CRP and IL-6 serum levels on postoperative day one in patients receiving INI compared with controls.

Multiple studies have indicated that the “inflammatory hypothesis” represents a potential pathological mechanism underlying POD. However, the exact pathological mechanism of POD remains unclear currently. This hypothesis proposes that perioperative infections, surgery, and trauma can trigger inflammatory responses within the body, promoting the release of IL-6 and CRP. These cytokines can traverse the BBB via active or passive transport. Once across, they can exert impacts on the central nervous system to mediate neuroinflammatory responses, increase BBB permeability, and stimulate afferent vagus nerves to activate microglia and astrocytes [25, 26]. Activated microglial cells release nitric oxide (NO), reactive oxygen species (ROS), and pro-inflammatory cytokines, whose sustained secretion may lead to neuronal damage, dysfunction, and apoptosis, subsequently impairing central nervous system functions [27]. In agreement with the inflammatory theory, the present research demonstrated significantly elevated serum IL-6 and CRP levels among patients experiencing POD compared to those without delirium.

A prospective clinical investigation by Kellar and colleagues included 49 participants who underwent INI treatment for 12 months. Researchers subsequently assessed alterations in gray matter volumes and white matter hyperintensity, the latter of which is closely linked to cognitive decline and cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers typically seen in AD. The results revealed a notable reduction in white matter hyperintensity progression, particularly in the frontal and deep brain regions, following insulin administration [11]. Considering the shared characteristic of cognitive impairment between AD and POD, clinical insights from INI therapy trials in AD patients may offer novel strategies for preventing and managing POD.

Brabazon et al. directly explored the influence of insulin on human microglial cells in vitro. Their findings showed that insulin markedly decreased monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 levels in human microglial cultures subjected to inflammatory stimuli, significantly reducing neurotoxicity mediated by microglia [28]. Complementary research also verified insulin’s anti-inflammatory properties in BV2 microglial cells, demonstrating its neuroprotective effects via suppression of NO, ROS, and pro-inflammatory cytokine production [27]. Moreover, Xu et al., using a mouse subarachnoid hemorrhage model, reported that INI significantly upregulated glucose transporter-1 expression at both mRNA and protein levels, improved neurological function, reduced cerebral edema and blood-brain barrier damage, suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and inhibited neuronal apoptosis [29]. Recent evidence further indicates that impaired insulin signaling within microglia leads to metabolic dysregulation, exacerbating Aβ plaque deposition and neuroinflammation, thus contributing to emotional disorders and cognitive impairment in mice [30].

In an animal study by Li et al., neonatal mice aged seven days underwent anesthesia with sevoflurane for three consecutive days, subsequently exhibiting mild behavioral disturbances later in life as evidenced by behavioral testing [31]. Subsequent studies by the same research team demonstrated that INI therapy significantly improved these behavioral abnormalities. Biochemical and immunohistochemical analyses indicated that anesthesia led to decreased postsynaptic density protein 95 (PSD-95) level and elevated neuronal apoptosis level in neonatal mice. Administering INI as a pretreatment before anesthesia attenuated anesthesia-induced behavioral abnormalities, elevated PSD-95 levels in the brain, maintained synaptic plasticity, and reduced neuronal apoptosis [32]. In a prospective clinical trial, Huang et al. demonstrated that repeated administration of INI (20 U per dose for two days preoperatively) effectively lowered POD rates and reduced postoperative plasma IL-6 concentrations among elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic gastrointestinal tumor resection [18]. Similarly, the current study found that perioperative administration of INI markedly reduced POD occurrence among elderly patients undergoing hip fracture surgery with GA. These outcomes support the initial hypothesis and underscore INI’s potential neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory roles in the perioperative setting.

Research supports insulin’s role in suppressing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, alleviating chronic inflammation, and enhancing insulin sensitivity [33]. Mild, persistent inflammation has been widely accepted as an important factor contributing to insulin resistance observed in obesity and type 2 diabetes. Insulin modulates inflammatory pathways by influencing macrophage polarization, thereby maintaining systemic metabolic equilibrium [34]. Additionally, insulin can also suppress inflammatory responses, offering a degree of protection against chronic inflammatory damage [35]. Studies have indicated that insulin can facilitate neuroprotection, enhance metabolic function, and optimize glycemic control, thereby mitigating postoperative inflammatory responses [36]. In the current research, serum levels of CRP and IL-6 were greater in Group C than in Group I, explicitly demonstrating the efficacy of INI in diminishing systemic inflammation. Scholars have discovered that IL-6 is the primary stimulus for CRP production in the liver [37]. Jelinek et al. summarized extensive research indicating that the brain can interact with the anti-inflammatory hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis via afferent fibers of the vagus nerve and activate the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (CAP) through efferent fibers of the vagus nerve to suppress the release of inflammatory factors such as IL-6 by systemic immune cells [38]. Although no studies have yet elucidated how INI reduces serum inflammatory factors IL-6 and CRP, based on the above research, we hypothesize that INI may lower serum concentrations of IL-6 and CRP through the HPA axis and CAP pathways.

Previous investigations have revealed the efficacy of INI in lowering POD incidence. Li et al. reported that, elderly patients who received 3 doses of INI (20 U or 40 U) exhibited a notably lower POD incidence within 3 days after surgery (11.4% vs. 14.3% vs. 39.5%) [20]. Similarly, Huang et al. discovered that 5 administrations of INI (20 U) decreased POD incidence within the first 5 postoperative days (12.5% vs. 47.5%) [18]. In the current study, while elderly patients received an extended regimen of 10 INI administrations (20 U), the anticipated more pronounced reduction in POD incidence was not observed. The research by Surina-Baumgartner et al. indicated that insulin is physiologically involved in regulating spontaneous nocturnal feeding behavior in rats. The observed lack of effectiveness of exogenous insulin in reducing food intake may be explained by a ceiling effect [39]. Similarly, clinical trials in AD have demonstrated that INI enhances memory in an inverted U-shaped dose-response relationship, suggesting that a ceiling effect may exist for insulin administration in this context as well [40]. We hypothesize that a comparable ceiling effect could be present in the insulin regimen examined in the current study; specifically, after exceeding a certain number of administrations, no additional reduction in POD incidence might be attainable. Nonetheless, differences arising from varying anesthetic methods and surgical procedures across studies cannot be excluded and should be addressed in future investigations.

It is well-established that intravenous insulin administration can lead to adverse reactions, such as hypoglycemia, in patients. Moreover, owing to the limited ability of insulin to circumvent the BBB, it cannot effectively elevate the cerebral insulin concentration. Conversely, after intranasal administration, insulin can get to the brain through perivascular channels along the olfactory and trigeminal nerves. There, it binds to insulin receptors and exerts its effects without significantly affecting peripheral insulin or blood glucose levels [9]. Studies have shown that merely 3%−8% of nasally administered insulin gets into the systemic circulation. As a result, it is less prone to bringing about systemic adverse effects like hypoglycemia [41–43]. In this study, the blood glucose levels displayed no remarkable differences between the two groups. Moreover, neither group encountered hypoglycemia or other adverse events, which further validates the safety of INI therapy. As a novel mode of administration, INI has demonstrated high patient compliance. It effectively elevates the insulin concentration in the central nervous system, thereby improving postoperative cognitive function and recovery outcomes [44].

This study has several limitations. Firstly, assessments were only conducted on postoperative days 1, 3, and 5. Such evaluation intervals may lead to the oversight of delirium episodes. Secondly, this study was short of data about delirium subtypes, episode frequency, and delirium length. Thirdly, low-dose insulin was used in this study to avoid adverse reactions. However, higher doses might offer greater benefits. Fourthly, there is a need to carry out large-scale multicenter randomized trials to further elucidate the function of INI. All patients in this study received GA, whereas spinal/epidural anesthesia is more commonly employed for hip fracture surgery. Consequently, the results of this study cannot be generalized to patients undergoing neuraxial anesthesia.

Conclusion

Repeated perioperative administration of 20 U INI effectively reduces POD rates in elderly patients undergoing GA for hip fracture surgery. Additionally, it can decrease serum CRP and IL-6 concentrations on postoperative day 1. However, it is noteworthy that increasing the frequency of insulin administration, when compared to previous studies, did not result in more optimal outcomes.

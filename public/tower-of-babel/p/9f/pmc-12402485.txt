Enhanced composed fashion image retrieval with a multi-hop reasoning framework
Authors: Nan Yang, Xiaoquan Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper introduces an Multi-Hop Reasoning Framework for Composed Fashion Image Retrieval (CFIR), meticulously designed to overcome the inherent limitations posed by existing single-step and hierarchical retrieval methods when dealing with complex multimodal queries in CFIR. Traditional CFIR approaches often struggle to accurately interpret the intricate interplay between textual descriptions and visual content within fashion datasets. Our methodology harnesses the power of multi-hop reasoning to iteratively refine the retrieval process, thereby enabling a deeper and more nuanced integration of visual and textual data. This structured approach not only enhances the model’s interpretative capabilities but also significantly improves its ability to discern subtle relationships between reference and target images across various modification descriptions. By incorporating multiple reasoning steps, the framework adeptly manages the compositionality inherent in fashion-related queries, resulting in superior retrieval accuracy and performance. We thoroughly validate our approach through rigorous experiments on three extensive fashion image datasets, including Fashion-IQ, Shoes, and Fashion200k. The results demonstrate marked improvements over state-of-the-art methods, highlighting the potential of our multi-hop reasoning framework to set a new benchmark in the field of image retrieval.Subject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Composed Fashion Image Retrieval (CFIR) is a challenging and innovative task in the field of computer vision and e-commerce, which involves retrieving a target fashion image given a reference image and a modification description1. This task, first introduced by Vo et al.2, differs from traditional image-based or text-based retrieval tasks as it requires the model to interpret and integrate both visual and textual modalities to accurately identify and retrieve the desired target image. The combination of these two modalities presents a unique set of challenges that have driven extensive research and development in this area3.

Existing mainstream methods for CFIR primarily fall into two main strategies. The first strategy involves using an encoder to combine the features of the image and text modalities1. These combined features are then compared with the encoded features of the target image to determine the best match. This approach leverages the power of deep learning models to learn joint representations of image and text, enabling more accurate retrieval by capturing the complex interactions between visual and textual information.

The second strategy involves transforming the reference image into the text space4,5. This is achieved by extracting key textual object information from the image, such as identifying the main items or attributes (e.g., “boots”) through large multi-modality models like CLIP. The extracted textual information is then combined with the input text attributes (e.g., “boots + red”) to search for the target image. This approach allows the model to handle the compositional nature of the query in a single text space, where the reference image and the modification description are used together to refine the search criteria.

The above approaches are based on single-hop retrieval methods that use a straightforward approach where the model combines the reference image with a single modification description to retrieve the target image in one step6,7. This means the model attempts to match the combined features of the reference image and the text description directly with those of the target image.

However, traditional single-hop retrieval approaches often struggle with complex queries that require a nuanced understanding beyond a single step8. These methods typically combine a reference image with a single modification description to directly retrieve the target image, which can be effective for simple queries but falls short for intricate or sequential modifications9. Existing CFIR methods, while incorporating diverse input forms such as visual and textual data, frequently lack cohesive multi-step reasoning capabilities. This limitation hinders their ability to handle scenarios requiring multiple stages of reasoning to accurately reach the target image. As a result, these models often fail to capture and interpret complex relationships and dependencies between visual and textual information, leading to suboptimal performance in real-world scenarios where users seek images based on a series of descriptive modifications. The inability to perform multi-step reasoning restricts the practical applicability of existing CFIR methods, highlighting the need for advanced retrieval techniques that can dynamically manage multi-step processes to enhance retrieval accuracy and relevance.

To address this issue, some methods attempt to use hierarchical retrieval techniques. Hierarchical retrieval techniques structure the retrieval process in multiple stages, progressively refining the search results based on intermediate stages of information fusion. This approach allows for a more detailed and structured interpretation of complex queries by breaking down the retrieval process into smaller, manageable steps, thereby improving the model’s ability to handle multi-step reasoning.

Although hierarchical retrieval techniques are structured and can provide more accurate results, they can become computationally intensive and less efficient. This increased complexity can lead to longer processing times and higher resource consumption, limiting their practical application in real-world scenarios where quick and efficient retrieval is often required.

To address these challenges, we propose a novel approach that introduces multi-hop reasoning during the training phase of CFIR. Unlike traditional methods that rely on a single image and a single description to retrieve another image, our approach incorporates multiple hops. Specifically, an initial image can be combined with a sequence of descriptions to navigate towards the final target image. For example, a real-world query like “a shorter version of this blue floral dress with puff sleeves and no belt” involves multiple fine-grained modifications. Interpreting such a complex query in a single step is often inadequate, as single-hop models tend to conflate or dilute individual semantic changes. In contrast, a multi-hop framework can iteratively address each modification-first transitioning to an intermediate image based on one change, then refining further using subsequent descriptions–ultimately leading to the desired target. This stepwise reasoning process enhances the model’s ability to comprehend and execute intricate fashion retrieval tasks.

By integrating multi-hop reasoning into the training process, our method significantly improves the model’s ability to comprehend complex relationships between images and textual descriptions. This advancement leads to more accurate and contextually relevant fashion image retrieval, making our approach highly effective and applicable in real-world scenarios. Our experimental results further validate this motivation. As shown in Figure 2, increasing the number of hops from 1 to 3 progressively boosts Recall@10 from 35.79 to 37.10 and Recall@50 from 65.59 to 66.67, before slightly declining with more hops. This demonstrates that multi-hop reasoning is empirically beneficial up to a certain depth, and supports the design of our training framework.

Related work

Composed image retrieval

Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) was first explored by Vo et al.2, who introduced the concept of retrieving a target image by leveraging both a reference image and a textual description. This approach diverges from traditional image retrieval systems that rely on a singular, precise query image10. CIR addresses the inherent challenge by integrating multimodal queries. This integration not only simplifies the querying process but also enhances the richness and specificity of the information provided by the user. This process enables users to conduct more precise and context-aware searches.

One of the fundamental challenges in CIR lies in the effective merging of visual and textual modalities11,12 and ensuring the alignment of visual and linguistic spaces13,14. To address this, contemporary CIR methods often employ separate encoders for text and images, extracting their features independently before integrating them into a shared embedding space. This joint embedding space serves as the foundation for locating potential target images.

Supervised methods

Initial approaches to CIR predominantly relied on supervised learning, which utilizes extensive labeled datasets to train models that can effectively merge visual and textual inputs into a unified embedding space. In these models, a reference image and its associated textual description are combined to form a joint representation. This representation is then used to encode candidate target images into the same feature space, with retrieval being performed by comparing the features of these candidates against the joint embedding of the query pair.

Several pioneering works in this domain have demonstrated the potential of supervised CIR. For instance, CLVC-Net15 introduced a novel architecture that integrates local-wise and global-wise compositors, which engage in mutual learning16 to enhance the model’s ability to capture fine-grained and holistic modifications16. Similarly, CoSMo1 features a sophisticated image-based compositor that includes separate content and style modulators. These modulators dynamically adjust feature maps based on text features, allowing for more nuanced and context-aware modifications during retrieval.

Furthermore, recent advancements have introduced the pretrain-finetune paradigm, exemplified by CLIP4Cir9, which leverages the pre-trained CLIP model14. This model, originally trained on extensive cross-modal datasets, undergoes fine-tuning of its text and visual encoders to improve feature matching. The process employs a two-stage training strategy, wherein a non-linear compositor integrates the features in an end-to-end fashion. Additionally, some approaches utilize large-scale datasets like FACAD17 and FashionGen18 during the pretraining phase to further strengthen the model’s performance.

These methods are trained on triplet datasets such as Fashion-IQ19 and CIRR20, where each triplet consists of a reference image, a target image, and a textual description. However, the creation of such triplet data is labor-intensive, limiting the scalability of supervised approaches.

Zero-shot methods

In response to the limitations of supervised methods, zero-shot CIR techniques have emerged as a promising alternative. These methods aim to reduce the dependence on labeled triplets by mapping the reference image into a text-based embedding space. This embedding is then combined with the textual modification to locate the target image within the text-based space. By leveraging the compositionality of language, zero-shot methods facilitate image retrieval without requiring explicit training on labeled triplets, thereby enhancing their generalization capability to unseen data.

Prominent examples of zero-shot CIR include SEARLE5, which employs a self-ensemble approach to refine the alignment between visual and textual modalities, and Context-I2W6, which introduces a contextual refinement mechanism to enhance retrieval accuracy. Other notable methods such as KEDs7, Pic2Word4, and LinCIR8 explore various strategies for aligning visual features with textual descriptions, ranging from knowledge distillation to context-aware transformations.

These methods have shown considerable promise in improving retrieval performance in zero-shot settings, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable. Similar directions have also been explored in other multimodal retrieval frameworks21, highlighting the potential of leveraging compositionality for generalized retrieval without explicit supervision.

Semi-supervised methods

To further bridge the gap between supervised and zero-shot approaches, semi-supervised CIR methods have been introduced. These methods leverage generative models to create pseudo triplets, which are then used to train models in a semi-supervised fashion. By generating synthetic data that closely resembles real-world triplets, these approaches enhance the robustness and generalizability of CIR models while reducing reliance on manually labeled data.

Recent efforts have also explored semi-supervised multimodal retrieval in broader contexts22, offering complementary insights into reducing annotation costs while maintaining retrieval performance.

Jang et al.23 demonstrate the efficacy of semi-supervised learning in CIR by simulating intra-class variations and incorporating uncertainty during training. This approach not only improves the model’s ability to generalize to unseen data but also mitigates the overfitting issues commonly associated with supervised learning. The incorporation of synthetic data allows the models to explore a broader range of potential modifications, leading to more accurate and effective retrieval outcomes.

Limitations of existing methods

Despite the advancements in CIR through supervised, zero-shot, and semi-supervised approaches, these methods often struggle to capture the nuanced relationships between the reference image and the textual modifications. Their reliance on straightforward fusion techniques and direct comparisons within a shared embedding space limits their capacity to handle complex or subtle modifications.

In contrast, our multi-hop reasoning approach for CIR/CFIR seeks to overcome these limitations by introducing an advanced framework that facilitates multi-step reasoning processes. This approach enables a more detailed and context-aware integration of visual and textual information, allowing for more accurate and effective retrieval in scenarios involving complex, multi-step modifications. By iteratively refining the query through multiple reasoning steps, our method surpasses the limitations of existing techniques, offering a more sophisticated and robust solution to the challenges of CIR.

Methodology

This section introduces a novel multi-hop reasoning framework for CFIR that significantly enhances the performance of existing retrieval methodologies. Unlike traditional single-step retrieval approaches, our framework facilitates an iterative refinement process, enabling the model to decode complex relationships between visual and textual data across multiple reasoning steps. This flexible framework supports the integration of various existing modules or techniques, thereby demonstrating its capability to augment the efficacy of conventional composed image retrieval methods.

While multi-hop reasoning has been widely studied in vision-language (VL) tasks, its direct application to CFIR is not straightforward. This is because CFIR requires iterative compositional understanding across visual and linguistic modalities, targeting image retrieval instead of answer prediction. Our framework introduces a novel adaptation by integrating image-as-query supervision for intermediate hops, task-specific retrieval loss at each step, and a multi-hop training curriculum that mimics real-world modification chains. These mechanisms are tailored to the progressive transformation nature of CFIR and differentiate our work from conventional multi-hop reasoning pipelines.

Overview of the multi-hop reasoning framework

The proposed multi-hop reasoning framework is specifically designed to manage queries involving multiple stages of modifications, offering a structured method to integrate and sequentially process a series of descriptive inputs. In contrast to single-step methods that aim to directly retrieve the target image using a reference image and a single textual description, our approach methodically refines the retrieval through a series of hops, facilitating a deeper understanding of the necessary modifications to pinpoint the target. Experiments

This section describes the experiments conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed multi-hop reasoning framework for CFIR. We detail the experimental setup, including datasets, baselines, evaluation metrics, and implementation details. We also present the results of our experiments, highlighting the improvements in retrieval performance offered by our framework over traditional single-step retrieval methods.

Datasets

We evaluate our model using two popular fashion image datasets:

Fashion-IQ26: This dataset is characterized by images annotated with descriptive tags that highlight style variations, such as “similar style t-shirt with a white logo print.” We adhered to the established training and testing protocols from previous research. Despite some data gaps caused by outdated links due to privacy updates, we successfully compiled 75,384 images, utilizing 46,609 of them in line with the original training protocol.

Shoes27: This dataset includes 10,751 pairs of shoe images, each accompanied by descriptions that detail fine-grained visual differences. For our experiments, we utilized 10,000 images for training and assessed the performance using 4,658 images.

Fashion200k28: Encompassing a broad array of apparel items, this dataset features five categories–dresses, jackets, pants, skirts, and tops. We trained our models on a substantial set of 172,000 images and conducted evaluations using 33,480 test queries, covering all five categories.

Baseline methods

To demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach, we compare our multi-hop reasoning framework against several baseline methods: 1. Single-Step Retrieval Method: A traditional retrieval approach where the reference image and a single textual description are used to directly retrieve the target image without intermediate steps. 2. CLIP-based Retrieval: We use the pre-trained CLIP model, which leverages large-scale datasets to understand visual and textual content, as a strong baseline for direct image retrieval. 3. Sequential Co-Attention Model: A model that uses co-attention mechanisms to jointly attend to image and text features, providing a baseline for comparison against our multi-hop reasoning approach.

Implementation details

Our model is implemented using PyTorch and features a dual-branch encoder setup that utilizes ResNet-5024 and RoBERTa29 for extracting visual and textual features, respectively. Both the feature fusion module and the multi-hop reasoning module are trained end-to-end. We employ the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001, and our training process uses batches of 32 images. To optimize performance and prevent overfitting, the model is trained for up to 50 epochs with early stopping criteria based on the validation loss.

Evaluation metric

In the evaluation, we present the recall rates denoted by R@K, where K refers to the top K ranked items considered for determining if the correct answer is included. Following existing works, we report on the average Recall@1, Recall@10, and Recall@50 across all queries. The average metric represents the mean recall rate across all subsets, providing a comprehensive overview of our model’s performance across different query complexities.

Comparison with hierarchical retrieval method

To validate our claim regarding the computational inefficiency of hierarchical retrieval methods, we conducted comparative experiments using CoSMo1 (the performance comparisons are given in Tables 1 and 2), a representative hierarchical fusion framework. CoSMo utilizes stacked content-style modulators to progressively fuse image and text features at multiple network layers, which leads to increased processing complexity.

Empirically, our reproduced CoSMo model incurs significantly higher computational cost. Specifically, CoSMo introduces approximately 25% more FLOPs and an average latency increase of 30ms per query compared to our multi-hop framework. In contrast, our method achieves higher recall (e.g., R@10 of 37.10 on FashionIQ) while maintaining lower computational overhead by optimizing reasoning through efficient step-wise supervision rather than deep hierarchical stacking.

Further analyses

Although our proposed multi-hop framework achieves notable improvements across multiple datasets, we observe several limitations. First, failure cases occur when the modification description involves ambiguous or overlapping attributes (e.g., ’less formal’, ’slightly darker’), which challenge the model’s semantic grounding. Second, our current implementation relies on predefined modification chains; thus, performance may degrade when the description does not align with the assumed hop granularity. Finally, the framework is trained and evaluated only within the fashion domain, which limits its generalizability. In future work, we plan to incorporate adaptive hop-length prediction to dynamically determine the optimal number of reasoning steps.

Cross-domain generalization While our evaluation focuses on fashion datasets, the proposed multi-hop framework is inherently agnostic to the specific domain of the images. Its step-wise reasoning mechanism, which decomposes and interprets a sequence of fine-grained modifications, can be readily adapted to other compositional retrieval settings. For example, domains such as home decor, art curation, and industrial product search also require interpreting sequential attribute changes or visual specifications. Although we did not include experiments on other datasets due to differences in task formulation and time constraints, we plan to explore such evaluations in future work. We also intend to extend our framework with domain-adaptive modules to improve generalization across visual categories and composition patterns.

Conclusion

The Multi-Hop Reasoning Framework proposed in this study marks a significant advancement in the domain of CFIR. By integrating a sequence of descriptive modifications through multiple reasoning steps, our model not only improves its interpretative capabilities but also achieves higher accuracy in retrieving the desired fashion images. The experimental results underscore the efficacy of our approach, particularly in handling complex, multi-step queries that are common in practical e-commerce scenarios. Future work will focus on further optimizing the reasoning steps and exploring the applicability of our framework to other domains requiring nuanced multimodal integration. The demonstrated improvements highlight the potential of multi-hop reasoning to revolutionize image retrieval systems, making them more robust and context-aware.

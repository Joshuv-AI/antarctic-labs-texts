Development and validation of a predictive and prognostic model of perineural invasion in colorectal cancer
Authors: Xianjin Wu, Jiahui Zhang, Fei Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGrowing evidence indicates that perineural invasion (PNI) is associated with local recurrence, distant metastasis, and unfavorable prognosis in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC). However, reliable tools for the diagnosis and outcome assessment of PNI-positive colorectal cancer remain insufficiently studied. In this study, diagnostic and prognostic models were constructed and validated for patients with PNI-positive colorectal cancer.MethodsPatients included in this study were selected from two independent datasets within the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent risk factors for perineural invasion in colorectal cancer. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were also conducted to identify independent prognostic factors associated with outcomes in patients with PNI positive colorectal cancer. Two nomogram models were then developed to estimate the risk and prognosis of PNI positive colorectal cancer. The area under the curve(AUC) and calibration curves were applied to evaluate the predictive performance of the nomograms. Decision curve analysis(DCA) and Kaplan Meier survival curves were further used to assess their potential clinical usefulness.ResultsMultivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to determine independent risk factors of colorectal cancer with perineural invasion (CRCPNI), including N stage, T stage, tumor deposits, grade, primary site, and carcinoembryonic antigen(CEA) level. Multivariate Cox regression analysis further identified age, T stage, CEA level, N stage, chemotherapy, and tumor deposits as factors independently associated with prognosis in CRCPNI patients. The results of these analyses were presented using nomogram models. Receiver operating characteristic(ROC) analysis, calibration plots, and DCA were applied to evaluate the performance of the risk and prognostic models.ConclusionAfter validation, the predictive model was shown to be reliable and can provide supportive information for individualized clinical decision making in future practice.Keywords: colorectal cancer, perineural invasion, nomogram, predictive mode

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is considered one of the most prevalent gastrointestinal malignancies worldwide. Its incidence and mortality rates have consistently remained high among all types of malignant tumors [1]. In countries with a high Human Development Index, the disease burden has been further increased by the westernization of dietary habits and by population aging [2, 3]. Patient survival has been improved through comprehensive treatment strategies that integrate surgical resection, chemoradiotherapy, and targeted therapy. However, treatment failure is still observed in a subset of patients and is mainly related to tumor invasion and metastasis [4]. Among these factors, PNI is regarded as an important indicator of local tumor invasion and is thought to contribute to disease progression [5].

PNI is defined as the presence of tumor cells infiltrating perineural tissues, involving at least one third of the nerve circumference, or as tumor cells extending into one or more layers of the nerve sheath, including the epineurium, perineurium, or endoneurium. This pathological feature is considered to be associated with tumor spread and metastasis [6, 7]. Studies have confirmed that patients with PNI positivity exhibit a significantly higher risk of postoperative recurrence and are more prone to distant metastasis [8]. This phenomenon may be explained by the tendency of tumor cells to spread along nerve bundles. This pathway is thought to avoid the usual barriers of the vascular and lymphatic systems and may be associated with more rapid disease progression [9]. Additionally, PNI has been associated with several clinical complications, including persistent sacrococcygeal pain and defecatory dysfunction, which may lead to a decline in patients’ quality of life [10]. Although the incidence of PNI appears to be relatively low and its prognostic impact limited in stage II CRC, an unfavorable association with outcomes has been consistently observed in patients with advanced disease. As a result, PNI is generally regarded as an important pathological factor in clinical assessment and treatment planning [11].

The reported incidence of PNI in CRC has varied widely across published studies, with estimates ranging from 8% to 35%. This variability is thought to be related to differences in detection sensitivity, tumor location, pathological stage, and the degree of tumor differentiation. Rectal tumors have generally been reported to show a higher incidence of PNI than colonic tumors [10]. Currently, the diagnosis of PNI primarily relies on postoperative pathological examination. However, due to the absence of standardized reporting protocols, PNI is often underestimated [10]. Moreover, the pathological identification of PNI is challenging [12]. In addition, the detection process is often time consuming and is associated with a relatively high risk of false negative results [13]. However, the absence of effective preoperative risk assessment tools may lead to some high risk patients not receiving individualized preoperative interventions [12]. Previous studies on risk factors for PNI have mainly been based on small sample and single center data, with variables largely limited to pathological features. Clinical laboratory and imaging indicators have often not been fully integrated. In addition, large sample analyses focused on the population remain limited, which may reduce the generalizability and reliability of existing findings [14–16]. Therefore, there is a need to develop predictive models that can more accurately estimate the risk of PNI occurrence and its association with patient prognosis.

As a visualized predictive tool, nomograms can convert multifactorial statistical models into intuitive individual risk estimates. Owing to their user-friendly interface, nomograms have been widely applied in predicting cancer prognosis and complications [17, 18]. In numerous malignancies, nomogram-based models have exhibited greater prognostic accuracy than conventional TNM staging systems and are increasingly regarded as a contemporary standard for risk stratification [19, 20]. However, to date, a nomogram model based on large population level data has not been established to predict the risk of PNI occurrence and its associated prognosis in patients with CRC.

Based on this background, the present study was designed to identify independent factors associated with the occurrence of PNI and to construct two nomogram models for estimating the risk of PNI and the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year OS of CRCPNI patients MATERIALS AND METHODS.

Patient selection

SEER*Stat version 9.0.42.0 was used to extract clinical data for patients with pathologically confirmed colorectal cancer diagnosed between 2010 and 2015. Patient information of model development was obtained from the “Incidence—SEER Research Data, 8 Registries, Nov 2024 Sub (1975–2022)” dataset. These cases were used to build both the risk model and the prognostic model. Ultimately, a total of 13,290 patients with CRC were included in this study, among whom 1250 were identified as having PNI. In addition, 46,874 patients with CRC and 4,870 CRCPNI patients diagnosed between 2010 and 2015 were identified.These patients were used as separate external test sets.Both datasets came from the “Incidence—SEER Research Data, 17 Registries, Nov 2024 Sub (2000–2020)” dataset. This dataset includes a larger number of registries and represents a broader population.

Patients included in this study were required to meet the following eligibility criteria: [1] availability of complete survival information [2]. confirmation of valid and adequate follow-up data [3]. patients diagnosed with stage I-III cancer without distant metastasis (M0) [4]. TNM staging serves as a staging method based on pathological diagnosis.༈5༉both colorectal cancer and perineural invasion were required to be confirmed by histopathological diagnosis.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: [1] cases identified solely through autopsy or death certificate records; [2] patients with missing race or marital status; [3] patients with an unspecified primary site; [4] histological typing has not been definitively confirmed; [5] definitive therapeutic information is lacking.

Data elements

The following baseline characteristics were collected for patients with colorectal cancer: age at diagnosis, sex, race, marital status, CEA level, primary site, histological subtype, grade, TNM stage, tumor deposits, and receipt of radiotherapy and chemotherapy. Patient age was stratified using X tile software in the training cohort and was categorized into two groups: >70 years and ≤ 70 years.

In addition, according to the International Classification of Diseases for Oncology, Third Edition (ICD-O-3), histologic types were categorized into two major groups: adenocarcinomas (codes 8140, 8211, 8213, 8210, 8260, 8263, and 8261) and non-adenocarcinomas (codes 8000, 8010, 8013, 8014, 8020, 8041, 8050, 8070, 8071, 8083, 8144, 8243, 8244, 8245, 8255, 8323, 8380, 8480, 8481, 8490, 8510, 8523, 8550, 8560, and 8574).

Statistical analysis

Cases diagnosed between 2010 and 2015 from the “Incidence—SEER Research Data, 8 Registries, Nov 2024 Sub (1975–2022)” dataset were randomly assigned to a training set(n = 7974) and a validation set(n = 5316) using a 6 to 4 allocation ratio.The training set and validation sets was used to build the PNI prediction model. The external test set was used to assess model performance. For continuous variables, either the independent t test or the Mann Whitney U test was applied based on data distribution. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test. All variables were initially examined using univariate logistic regression analysis. Variables with a P value below 0.05 were then included in a multivariate logistic regression model to identify factors potentially associated with PNI in CRC patients.

Furthermore, survival outcomes of 1,250 CRCPNI patients were analyzed to determine prognostic factors. These patients were randomly divided into a training group (n = 750) and a validation group (n = 500) at a ratio of 6 to 4 to build the prognostic model. The external test cohort was used to assess the performance of this model. All candidate variables were first assessed using univariate Cox proportional hazards regression. Variables with a P value below 0.05 were subsequently included in multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify independent prognostic indicators of CRCPNI. For survival analysis, OS was defined as the time from diagnosis to death from any cause or to the date of last follow up.

Based on the identified risk factors and independent prognostic variables, two nomogram models were developed to predict the risk of PNI occurrence and the OS of CRCPNI patients. The discriminative performance and predictive accuracy of the nomograms were assessed using ROC, while calibration plots were employed to evaluate model agreement between predicted and observed outcomes. DCA was further conducted to evaluate the clinical value of the nomograms.

To evaluate the ability of the nomogram to stratify patients by predicted survival, a three level risk classification was performed in the training set based on total point scores using X tile software. Patients were assigned to low risk, intermediate risk, and high risk groups. Kaplan Meier survival curves were generated for each group, and survival differences were assessed using the log rank test. The same cutoff values were subsequently applied to the validation and test sets to examine the generalizability of the nomogram.

A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant in all analyses. Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.1).

Development and Validation of the Diagnostic Nomogram

Based on the independent predictors identified in the training set, a nomogram model was constructed to estimate the risk of PNI in CRC. (Fig. 2). ROC analysis demonstrated that AUC value of the nomogram were 0.771 ,0.764 and 0.774 in the training, validation and external test sets, respectively(Fig. 3A-C). The calibration curves showed good agreement between the probabilities predicted by the nomogram and the observed outcomes. (Fig. 3D-F). In addition, DCA indicated that the nomogram was associated with a meaningful clinical net benefit, supporting its potential applicability in clinical practice. (Fig. 3G-I). For comparison, ROC and DCA curves were also generated for the TNM staging system. Across all three sets, the nomogram demonstrated better discriminative performance than TNM staging.(Fig. 3A-C).

Independent Prognostic Factors of CRCPNI

Table 4 presents the clinical characteristics and treatment information of CRC patients with PNI. Results from the chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test indicated that there were no significant differences between the training and validation set across all variables. In the training set, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed. Age, CEA level, primary site, histologic, T stage, N stage, tumor deposits, and chemotherapy were found to be significantly associated with prognosis(P < 0.05). Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were subsequently included in the multivariate Cox regression model. The final analysis identified age, T stage, CEA, N stage, chemotherapy, and tumor deposits as independent prognostic factors influencing OS in CRCPNI patients (Table 5).

Development and validation of prognostic nomogram

Based on the independent prognostic factors identified in the training set, a nomogram model was constructed to estimate OS in CRCPNI patients.(Fig. 4). ROC analysis showed that, in the training set, the AUC of predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year OS were 0.780, 0.773, and 0.735. In the validation set, the corresponding values were 0.697, 0.740, and 0.701. In the external testing set, the AUC were 0.743, 0.731, and 0.721.(Fig. 5A-C). As shown in Fig. 5A-F, the area under the ROC curve of the nomogram was larger than that of the TNM staging system, indicating a higher level of predictive accuracy of the nomogram. The calibration plots showed close agreement between the predicted survival probabilities and the observed outcomes.(Fig. 6).Moreover, DCA was applied to evaluate the clinical applicability of the nomogram(Fig. 7). As illustrated in Fig. 7, a greater net clinical benefit was observed of the nomogram in predicting mortality risk when compared with the conventional TNM staging system. This result suggests that the nomogram can provide improved clinical utility for estimating OS in CRCPNI patients.

Discussion

PNI is recognized as an important pathological feature in CRC and is associated with more aggressive disease behavior, including higher risks of local recurrence, distant metastasis, and unfavorable overall prognosis [21, 22]. Despite its recognized importance, comprehensive studies focusing on the risk and prognostic factors of CRCPNI are relatively limited, especially in large population based datasets [12, 23]. Data from the SEER database were used to develop and validate predictive models of both the risk and prognosis of CRCPNI, with consideration of clinical, pathological, and treatment related variables. To our knowledge, this work represents a comprehensive retrospective analysis aimed at constructing nomogram models to estimate the risk and prognosis of CRC patients with PNI. The results of ROC analysis, calibration plots, and DCA indicate that the proposed nomograms show stable predictive performance. These models can help to complement conventional pathological assessment and support clinicians in making more individualized treatment decisions for CRC patients.

As previously mentioned, reports investigating the risk factors of CRCPNI remain limited. In studies of other gastrointestinal malignancies, the occurrence of PNI has been found to be significantly associated with poor tumor differentiation, advanced T stage, lymph node metastasis, vascular invasion, presence of signet-ring cell carcinoma components, CA19-9,CA72-4,CEA positivity and tumor size [24–26]. In our study, the independent risk factors of CRCPNI included N stage, T stage, tumor deposits, grade, primary site, and CEA level.For patients identified as having potential risk, regular follow up and clinical reassessment are recommended. Based on multivariate Cox regression analysis, age, T stage, CEA, N stage, chemotherapy, and tumor deposits were identified as independent factors associated with prognosis in CRC patients with PNI. Although the primary site and histology may seemingly affect prognosis, they are not the cases in actual analysis.

In this study, age was not identified as an independent factor associated with the occurrence of PNI in CRC. However, among patients with PNI, older age was associated with poorer prognostic outcomes. The absence of a clear association between age and PNI occurrence may be explained by the observation that PNI is mainly influenced by tumor related biological features and the local microenvironment rather than by chronological age [27, 28]. It was suggested by Lian JY et al. that although age related decline in immune surveillance may facilitate tumor progression, immune cells also participate in shaping pro invasive microenvironments. Therefore, immunosenescence does not consistently increase the risk of PNI [29]. Similarly, Fane et al. demonstrated that the aging microenvironment exerts context-dependent effects on tumor behavior. Biochemical and biophysical modifications of the extracellular matrix related to aging involve glycation, cross-linking and enhanced stiffness. Such changes may either restrain or promote tumor cell migration, depending on the tumor type and the characteristics of the surrounding microenvironment [30]. Several previous studies have indicated that patient age is associated with prognosis in cancers with perineural invasion, with older individuals generally showing lower survival rates [31–33]. We hypothesize that this phenomenon may be associated with weakened immune function and physiological decline in older adults. However, due to data limitations inherent to the database, we were unable to capture more detailed biological indicators to further validate this assumption.

In this study, chemotherapy was associated with a protective effect.This finding suggests that timely systemic treatment can contribute to improved prognosis in CRCPNI patients. This observation is consistent with current clinical practice and supports the role of adjuvant chemotherapy in the management of patients with locally advanced or metastatic CRC [34–36].

In this study, T stage and N stage were identified as independent risk and prognostic factors of CRCPNI. These findings are consistent with results reported in previous literature [11, 22, 37]. Higher T and N stages indicate increased local invasion and lymphatic metastasis, suggesting that the occurrence of PNI is closely associated with aggressive tumor biology. These results highlight that the depth of tumor invasion and lymph node involvement remain critical determinants of long-term survival [38, 39]. Tumor deposits are considered markers of local micrometastasis and extravascular tumor spread [40]. In this study, a significant association was observed between tumor deposits and PNI. This finding supports the view that these two pathological features may act together to promote invasive tumor progression within the tumor microenvironment. In addition, CEA is a traditional biomarker of CRC. Its elevation is recognized to indicate enhanced invasive and metastatic potential of the tumor [41, 42]. This effect may result from the interaction of CEA with β-catenin and p120-catenin. These interactions modify the structure of adherens junctions and promote the release of soluble E-cadherin. Cell adhesion becomes weaker, and cell movement and invasive activity increase. Local tumor progression and metastatic spread occur more rapidly as a consequence [43]. In line with these findings, CEA was shown to have practical relevance in the assessment of both risk and prognosis in patients with CRCPNI.

The ROC analysis, calibration assessment, and DCA consistently indicated that the diagnostic and prognostic nomogram models developed in this study showed good discriminative ability and acceptable calibration. The predictive performance was observed to be higher than that of the conventional TNM staging system. Comparable AUC values were maintained in the validation and test cohorts, suggesting reasonable robustness and generalizability of the models. These results support the potential value of the nomogram of prognostic evaluation in CRCPNI patients and also suggest that the TNM staging system alone has limitations in predicting outcomes related to PNI.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the analysis was based on data from the SEER database. The retrospective design limits the ability to infer causal relationships between the identified factors and perineural invasion. Second, because the nomogram was developed using retrospective data, additional prospective studies are needed to confirm its applicability in different populations. Third, the SEER database adopted the eighth edition of the AJCC TNM staging system from 2018, and this change may have affected the accuracy of the model predictions. In addition, the SEER database has inherent constraints. Detailed surgical information and comprehensive data on adjuvant treatments are not available, and the absence of these variables may have introduced residual confounding. Finally, the use of existing clinical datasets limited the inclusion of certain potentially informative factors, such as molecular markers or imaging features, which may have improved the predictive performance of the model.

Conclusion

In summary, risk factors for CRCPNI were identified through univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. Prognostic factors were then determined using univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. Two nomogram models were subsequently constructed. These nomograms can help clinicians identify patients at higher risk and support the selection of more individualized treatment strategies based on estimated outcomes.

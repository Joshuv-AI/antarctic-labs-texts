Measuring the Economic Burden of Health Conditions among White-Collar Employees: A Cross-Sectional Study from Delhi-NCR
Authors: Monika Yadav, B Viji
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:In the past few decades, there has been a significant increase in healthcare expenditure due to the prevalence of health issues across the globe. This is primarily seen among white-collar employees due to the sedentary nature of their jobs, which affects their current earnings (Purchasing Power) directly due to the reallocation of their real income towards healthcare expenditure (Economic Burden) and indirectly by impacting work productivity through sick leaves.Aim:To measure the economic burden of disease(s) among white-collar employees, and to study the same across different socio-demographic variables.Methods:A cross-sectional study has been conducted in the region of Delhi-NCR among 357 white-collar employees. A random sampling method was adopted using a structured questionnaire. The questionnaire collected information regarding the direct and indirect costs of health conditions with a recall period of one month. The cumulative score of these monthly costs was converted to annual data to estimate the economic burden of the health conditions among the employees over a year.Results:As compared to Communicable diseases (16.24%), Non-communicable diseases (27.17%) were more prevalent among the respondents. Compared to other diseases, the employees suffering from Heart diseases (INR 7,62,237.50), Spinal health issues (INR 1,73,625.00), and Diabetes (INR 1,64,535.71) incurred more economic burden. At a 5% significance level, no significant difference was observed in economic burden for Gender and BMI. However, the economic burden was significantly different across the categories of Age and Monthly income. A positive association can be observed in economic burden with the increasing categories of age and monthly income.Conclusion:With the nature of sedentary work (desk jobs), white-collar employees are at a greater risk of exposure to various NCDs than CDs and incur a significant amount of out-of-pocket expenditure to manage their health status.

INTRODUCTION

In recent years, the populace has noticed a significant increase in the prevalence of various diseases/disorders, especially ‘Non-communicable diseases (NCDs)’. An ongoing transition towards a sedentary and unhealthy lifestyle, i.e., alcohol, smoking, limited/no physical activity, and fascination with processed and fast food, has gradually exposed the population to high-risk health issues. Along with increasing exposure to non-communicable diseases, these lifestyle factors reduce the immunity of an individual, further increasing the exposure to communicable diseases.[1] Given this scenario, individuals diagnosed with NCDs were asked to take extra precautions since the outbreak of the Covid-19 pandemic. Studies have shown an increased risk of covid-19 for people diagnosed with NCDs.[23] A primary concern of these health conditions is the additional financial expenditure one has to bear to manage their health.[4] These financial expenditures are usually known as ‘economic burden’,’out-of-pocket expenditure’ ‘catastrophic expenditure’. In the last few decades, there has been a surge of interest in measuring the economic burden of various diseases at the individual, society, and patient levels. Economic burden measurement involves calculating direct medical and non-medical costs related to a particular health condition and measuring indirect costs such as productivity loss caused by it.[5] Apart from increasing the economic burden for individuals, the prevalence of an ailment also reduces the productivity to perform the task. This affects not only the employees but also the earnings and functioning of the organization on a larger scale.

With limited physical movement and most time spent sitting, white-collar employees are more likely to be diagnosed with diseases/disorders.[678910] A considerable amount of literature has been published to establish an association between sedentary behavior and physical activity with the individual’s physical and mental well-being.[611121314] It is very evident from the literature that sedentary behavior not only impacts health at the current stage by causing neck pain, back pain, and so on, but it also increases the chances of having health issues such as metabolic syndrome[1516] at a later stage which increases possibilities for other health issues such as diabetes, cardiovascular diseases and so on.[1718]

However, given this scenario, insufficient data is available in the Indian context regarding the economic burden focusing on white-collar employees for both communicable and non-communicable diseases. Further, the studies undertaken to measure economic burden have shown mixed evidence for the economic burden of diseases/disorders.[192021] The estimate of economic burden majorly depends on the measures and costs included while calculating. Studies using direct cost or only morbidity cost for calculating indirect cost have shown a lower healthcare expenditure than other studies inclusive of both morbidity and mortality cost for measuring indirect cost along with direct cost.[2022]

The prevailing covid-19 health issues, and given the nature of a sedentary work environment pose a significant health threat to white-collar employees, which would further impose a considerable burden of managing healthcare expenditure on both employees and the organization.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

365 respondents among the white-collar workers were randomly approached to collect data. Any employee performing a job under these four domains was considered a ‘White-Collar’ employee: Professional specialty and technical; executive, administrative, and managerial; sales; administrative support, including clerical.[23] The scope of data collection was restricted to Delhi-NCR. The data collection process lasted four months, from December 2021 to March 2022.

Participants

The data collection tool was given to experts for their opinion and incorporated the suggestions before taking it to the field. The study was approved by the research advisory committee and ethical approval was received from the institutional review board. Informed consent was duly taken explaining the purpose of the study before collecting the data. The survey was purely on voluntary basis and the respondents were allowed to withdraw from the survey at any point in time. To maintain the confidentiality of the data (data privacy) no personal information was included in the tool, the responses were analyzed and the results were presented as a group. No individual responses were taken for interpretation throughout the study. The survey was designed based on a one-month recall period to avoid biases. Further, participants were selected randomly to prevent selection bias.

The sample size for the study was decided based on the previous studies undertaken with white-collar employees as their respondents.[242526] Since most of the studies chose a sample size of up to 300 participants for undertaking their research, the current study also decided to collect a minimum of 300 samples. Further, a total of 365 respondents were approached within the age group of 18-65 years using a random sampling technique. Respondents were selected within the region of Delhi, and NCR Gurgaon. These two regions accrue the highest number of tertiary sector employees compared to other regions from Delhi-NCR and hence were selected as the area of study. Given the share of employment in both regions, it was decided to select 57% samples from Delhi and 43% from Gurgaon. The inclusion criteria for participants is a minimum of one year of work experience as a white-collar employee. The response rate was recorded as 97.81% as eight participants’ data were removed from the sample group during the data cleaning stage based on incomplete information. After the data cleaning process, the study consists of a sample size of 357 respondents. Respondents approached were mainly employed within IT, Finance, Management, Healthcare, and Education sectors.

Questionnaire

The current study utilizes a structured questionnaire to collect the data. The questionnaire has been developed to collect information regarding direct and indirect costs related to a disease/disorder.[5] The following details of the costs were enquired about:

The ‘Human Capital Approach’ has been utilized to calculate indirect costs; hence, the total hours missed from work due to these reasons were further calculated in monetary terms based on an hourly wage.

Respondents were approached personally, and the field interview method was used to collect the data. The data for these costs were enquired about using a one-month recall period. Respondents were asked for a copy of the fee payment slip and bills for the above-stated expenses to verify the expenditure. Respondents with no fee slip or bill were asked to verify the payment through the digital transaction information. Further, to cross-verify the information, the details of insurance claimed for the last month were also enquired about.

Further, the monthly estimate of direct and indirect costs was summed up and converted to annual data to estimate the economic burden. Also, any insurance claimed by the respondent was deducted from the total cost of economic burden. Further, the median of the annual score of these total costs (economic burden) was calculated for each category of disease/disorder and was represented as their median scores.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis has been performed to report means and standard deviation for continuous variables and frequency distribution for categorical variables. Data has been tested for the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to check for normality. Further, since data were not normally distributed, Kruskal Wallis, Mann-Whitney U, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for the analysis. Also, median scores in place of mean scores have been calculated for the economic burden related to all the diseases/disorders discussed in the current study since data turned out to be not normally distributed.

The approval was obtained from the ethics committee prior to the fieldwork. Date of approval: 21st November, 2021.

DISCUSSION

The present study aims to determine the out-of-pocket expenditure regarding specific prevailing health issues among white-collar employees. The survey results indicate that almost two-thirds of the respondents suffer from one or more health issues.

Among the participants diagnosed with health issues (n = 245), 23.67% report having communicable diseases, 39.59% report suffering from non-communicable diseases, and the remaining suffer from comorbidities. The prevalence of economic burden also follows the same scenario. The results from the current survey showed a higher average economic burden of non-communicable diseases (INR 2,74,687.88), followed by the economic burden of comorbidities (INR 2,29,743.37) and communicable diseases (INR 1,11,279) respectively.

The Wilcoxon signed rank test (P value = 0.00) shows that the median differences in costs incurred by people suffering from NCDs, CDs, and Comorbidities are not equal to 0, indicating a statistically significant difference among these costs. Individuals suffering from NCDs tend to spend an average of INR 1,63,408.88 and INR 44,944.51 more than individuals suffering from CDs and Comorbidities, respectively. This significant difference in the economic burden of NCDs with CDs and Comorbidities implies a considerable amount of catastrophic expenditure incurred continuously and results in a fall in people’s real purchasing power. Regarding the mean annual income of INR 7,49,956.32, an individual suffering from NCDs tends to spend 36.62% of their income on healthcare expenditures in contrast to those suffering from CDs spending 14.83% of their income.

However, a better understanding can be grasped by looking at further categorizations of non-communicable diseases. Examining the out-of-pocket expenditure for each category, it can be observed that an individual diagnosed with any kind of heart disease incurs the highest economic burden, followed by diabetes, spinal health issues, and hypertension. This finding broadly supports the work of other studies focusing on measuring the economic burden of non-communicable diseases.[427] The results from these studies confirm that among the non-communicable diseases, heart diseases and diabetes incur the most healthcare expenses. A similar result can be observed in the case of comorbidities, with the most economic burden being accrued to individuals suffering from heart diseases, diabetes and spinal health issues as comorbidities.

Further looking at the categorization of healthcare costs, it can be observed that except for spinal health issues, every disease/disorder category has a higher median direct cost than the indirect cost for an employee. In comparison to the current research, a study conducted by Joseph et al. (2016)[28] to examine the distribution of direct and indirect costs related to specific NCDs such as Hypertension, Diabetes, Cancer, and Myocardial infarction also showed a high amount of financial cost in terms of the direct cost for all diseases as compared to indirect cost. Similar results were observed in another study examining the urban-rural differentials in the cost care of NCDs among the population of India.[29] For rural and urban people, the median direct cost (Rural = INR 4800; Urban = INR 1100) for all NCDs turned out to be higher than the indirect cost (Rural = INR 300; Urban = INR 10). Further, other non-communicable diseases such as migraine, diarrheal diseases, irritable bowel syndrome, and others also indicate a great amount of out-of-pocket expenditure (with median direct costs being higher than median indirect costs) an individual has to incur in a given year.

With respect to the other objective of the study, an attempt was made to see the economic burden across different socio-demographic variables. Results indicate a higher overall median economic burden for males than their female counterparts. This result can partly be explained by the difference in the median economic burden of communicable diseases and comorbidities across males and females. However, in the case of the median economic burden of non-communicable diseases, the difference in healthcare expenditure tends to be less between males and females. This result contradicts previous research on gender disparities in the economic burden of non-communicable diseases.[30]

Further, for the age variable, except for the age category between 25 to 35 years, the economic burden seems to increase with every age category. This could be attributed to the reason that in higher age groups, the exposure to ill-health increases, and so does the healthcare expenditure, which shows a positive association of economic burden with increasing age. These results accord with other studies showing economic burden is positively associated with age.[430] This result is further supported by a significant difference in economic burden for all diseases/disorders across age categories. This can be partly explained by the significant difference in the economic burden of non-communicable diseases.

For the category BMI, the economic burden is highest among the individuals within the category of Obesity Class-I and Class-II. However, no significant pattern of association was observed between the economic burden of diseases/disorders and the BMI of the individuals. This is further supported by the result showing no significant difference (P > 0.05) in the economic burden of disease/disorder across the categories of BMI. However, a study contradicts these results by concluding the vital role of high BMI in increasing the disease burden concerning cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and all causes of mortality.[31]

Further, while looking at the mean economic burden scores across the categories of the individual’s monthly income, it was observed that except for two income categories (INR 1,00,000 to INR 1,25,000 and INR 1,25,000 to INR 1,50,000), a positive association was observed between the variables. This contradicting result in these two categories could be attributed to a lower economic burden on communicable diseases and comorbidities. Further, the positive association of the income variable with economic burden in other categories could be attributed to the reason that people tend to spend more resources on healthcare as income increases. These results are consistent with data from another study trying to understand the prevalence of economic burden across different income quintiles.[27] Further, when tested for significant differences, the economic burden for all diseases/disorders was significantly different across the categories of monthly income. As stated above, the difference can be partly explained by the significant difference in the economic burden of non-communicable diseases. A pairwise comparison of the same across categories of age and monthly income is presented in Appendix 1, highlighting a statistically significant differences in economic burden.

In addition to these results, data obtained regarding the full body health routine check-up indicates that most people do not undertake these tests regularly. With most people remaining ignorant of their health status, the disease’s prevalence and the economic burden may have been underestimated.

CONCLUSION

The insights from this study have confirmed that the management and prevention of non-communicable diseases incur a significant amount of healthcare expenditure (36.62%) to individuals as a proportion of their income. Given the sedentary nature of jobs for white-collar employees, their exposure to various non-communicable diseases increases, increasing their out-of-pocket expenditure (Economic Burden). With NCDs utilizing a significant portion of an employee’s resources, it’s important to develop strategies for adopting and managing a healthy lifestyle. If managed early, the future costs of non-communicable diseases can be avoided. Further, given that indirect costs were also higher in the case of some NCDs, an approach toward limited sitting time and increasing incentives towards more physical activity might help in the given scenario.

Strength of the study

Along with clearly stated objectives and inclusion criteria, the current study utilizes a well-structured questionnaire to collect the data.

Limitations of the study

The findings in this report are subject to the following limitations:

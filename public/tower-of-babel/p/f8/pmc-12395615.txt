Elite beach volleyball match analysis: rally structure, work-to-rest patterns, and gender considerations
Authors: Flavia F Pereira, Jennifer Austin, Jose Antonio, Riceler Waske, Victor Romano
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundBeach volleyball has undergone significant evolution since its Olympic inception in 1996, with changes in scoring systems, court size, and match rules. These modifications have altered the physical and tactical demands on athletes, leading to shorter match durations and increased intensity of competition. While previous studies have documented the sport’s evolution, there remains a gap in comprehensive, modern data that accurately reflects the physiological and temporal dynamics of elite-level play. This study aims to fill this gap by examining the match and rally characteristics of male and female athletes during elite-level play.MethodsUsing the Balltime AI platform, 7,832 rallies from 92 matches (49 male, 43 female) during the 2023 FIVB Elite 16 Tour and 2024 Paris Olympic Games were analyzed. The study employed a post-hoc analysis design to evaluate match duration, playing time, break time, average rally duration, and work-to-rest ratios. Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS software, with significance determined at p ≤ 0.05.ResultsThe total average match duration was 45:55 ± 10:11 minutes, with active playing time averaging 13:30 ± 03:06 minutes and rest periods totaling 32:25 ± 07:29 minutes. The average rally duration was 9.49 ± 0.57 seconds, and the work-to-rest ratio was 1:2.4. Significant gender differences (p < 0.05) were found in total match duration, match break time, average rally duration, and work-to-rest ratios, although the practical effects were minimal (d < 0.3). Compared to previous decades, the contemporary game features shorter rest periods and higher overall match intensity, indicating an evolution in both strategic and physical demands. These updated benchmarks offer valuable insights for coaches and practitioners to optimize training programs that replicate the current demands of elite beach volleyball competition.ConclusionsThe findings highlight the evolving dynamics of elite beach volleyball, with reduced rest periods and increased match intensity. Despite gender-based differences, the results suggest that male and female athletes experience similar work-to-rest demands. These updated benchmarks provide critical insights for coaches and practitioners to design conditioning programs that replicate the competitive demands of the modern game.KEYWORDS: Match duration, beach volleyball, performance metrics, game evolution, athletic performance

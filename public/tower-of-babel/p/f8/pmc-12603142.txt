A faster and lighter weight robotic ready model YOLO Punica for detecting pomegranate fruit development
Authors: Chenfan Du, Zeze Ma, Rolla Almodfer, Xuan Wen, Jifei Zhao, Xinfa Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Pomegranate is a highly valued fruit known for its nutritional seeds packed with health benefits, as well as its use as a popular ornamental tree and in traditional medicine. However, traditional management relies heavily on manual, laborious processes, leading to low efficiency and increased cost. Using machine vision to monitor fruit development in real-time can achieve accurate intelligent management and reduce computational costs. A fast and lightweight detection model is crucial for machine vision. This study proposes the YOLO-Punica model, built on an improved version of the You Only Looking Once version 8n (YOLOv8n) algorithm, resulting in a lightweight and faster detection model specifically designed to monitor pomegranate fruit development in real time. The optimization includes the integration of two innovative modules: a dual-path downsampling module (DPDM) and a cross-scale feature fusion module (CCFM). The incorporation of DPDM into the backbone network significantly enhanced detection precision and computational efficiency. Additionally, the integration of CCFM and DPDM into the neck structure substantially reduced parameters, memory consumption, and overall model size, while improving operational efficiency and detection accuracy. The implementation of the DPDM and CCFM in the YOLOv8n framework resulted in a lighter model, faster processing speeds, and improved detection accuracy. Comparative test results on public datasets indicated that YOLO-Punica achieved reductions of 45.8% in parameters, 28% in giga floating-point operations per second (GFLOP), and 43.7% in model size relative to YOLOv8n, while attaining a mean average precision (mAP) of 92.6%, surpassing YOLOv8n by 0.98%. Furthermore, the model processes images at 14.3 frames per second on embedded devices, demonstrating its applicability for real-time detection of pomegranate fruit development, even in low-computational-power environments. This research not only provides technical support for the intelligent detection of pomegranate fruit development but also provides a new perspective for enhancing machine vision models in other agricultural contexts. Our code and model are available at https://github.com/Wenxuan-889/yolo_Pomegranate.

Introduction

Pomegranate (Punica granatum L.) belonging to the Lythraceae family, is a highly valued fruit known for its delicious seeds rich in nutrients and health benefits, including vitamins C and B, anthocyanins, sugars, calcium (Ca), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K)1. It is also a popular ornamental tree for its flowering and fruit-bearing aesthetics2, and has been used in traditional medicine because of its active compounds that exhibit anthelmintic, antiseptic, and antiviral properties3. However, the traditional management of pomegranate orchards depends heavily on manual labor, resulting in labor-intensive processes, low efficiency, increased costs, and reduced profitability. Computer vision presents a viable solution to these challenges by enabling automated management practices, including precise detection of the developmental stages of pomegranate fruits. Advancements in objective detection algorithms are crucial for addressing these issues.

Single-stage objective detection algorithms, characterized by fewer computations and faster image processing, are particularly suitable for real-time target detection in complex agricultural scenarios4. Among these, the YOLO network can meet the smaller model size and fewer parameters simultaneously, making it more suitable for devices with limited computing power and embedded systems commonly used in agricultural operations5. Because the popularity of agricultural intelligent equipment is currently hindered by the high cost of deploying and maintaining hardware resources, coupled with the limited computational power of hardware, a lightweight implementation can be designed to significantly minimize model parameters and computational complexity, allowing object detection algorithms to run smoothly even on edge devices with limited storage and computing power6. Therefore, the development of lightweight devices for deployment, accurate detection, fast computation, and compact design is an important aspect in the continued evolution of the YOLO algorithm.

The iterations from YOLOv1 to YOLOv10 illustrate the progress of this algorithm. Zhao et al.7 described the advancements from YOLOv1 to YOLOv7. Ultralytics launched YOLOv8 in 2023 by replacing the C3 structure with C2f for improved gradient flow. This version is faster and smaller than the previous model but retains the ELAN structure of YOLOv78 in the backbone network and neck part for efficient fusion of feature information from different layers. Xiao et al.9 utilized the YOLOv8 model to identify apples at different ripening stages by detecting apple peels. The results demonstrated that YOLOv8 was effective and significantly improved classification accuracy. In the complex and dynamic environments of orchards, Wu et al.10 proposed a dense apricot bloom detection method called D-YOLOv8 to accurately detect dense targets. The AP of this method for apricot bloom detection in the complex orchards was 96.35%. YOLOv9 features programmable gradient information technology designed to achieve superior accuracy, stability, and reasoning speed11. Finally, the developed YOLOv10 combines CSPNet and transformer modules to increase speed12. However, compared to YOLOv8, YOLOv9 and YOLOv10 did not significantly improve the accuracy, speed, or frames per second (FPS), and they remain unsuitable for deployment on lightweight terminals. The complex network structure, several parameters, and high configuration requirements for training pose challenges for the application of advanced object detection algorithms such as YOLOv8 in agriculture, due to delays in real-time detection and the high cost of equipment deployment. In addition, for specific agricultural production solutions, such as distinguishing developmental stages of pomegranate fruit, current research is still insufficient.

Previous studies have demonstrated that the introduction of advanced or optimized modules into the backbone network and neck of the model module is an effective strategy for enhancing detection accuracy, efficiency, and lightweight of the model. For instance, Wu et al.13 developed a deep-learning algorithm model based on YOLOv4 for real-time detection of apple flowers, which improved both accuracy and speed over the original model. Kumar et al.14 improved the YOLOv5s model by embedding the GhostNet network with a coordinate attention (CA) module as the backbone network, named the YOLO-BLBE model, to detect blueberry fruits at the ripening and semi-ripening stages, thereby providing technical support for the automated picking of blueberries. Zhong et al.15 proposed a lightweight and efficient mango-detection model by introducing a Darknet53 structure to YOLOv8 to compress the number of channels, significantly reducing the model’s parameters and floating-point operations per second (FLOPs). Furthermore, by integrating the EMA attention mechanism enhanced the ability of the model to focus on key features, effectively reducing false and missed detections and achieving higher detection accuracy in complex natural environments. Xu et al.16 proposed a lightweight YOLORFEW model that improved YOLOv8 by replacing the traditional convolutional layer with RFAConv, further enhancing feature extraction and fusion capability. Experimental results indicated that YOLO-RFEW significantly improved detection accuracy while maintaining a lightweight design. Yang et al.17 developed an improved strawberry maturity detection and grading model based on YOLOv8s by introducing a Swin Transformer module in the feature fusion stage and applying a multihead self-attention mechanism, which significantly improved the generalization ability and feature fusion efficiency of the model.

Currently, there are relatively few studies on computer vision for the detection of pomegranate fruits. Vasumathi et al.18 developed an algorithm model for pomegranate disease recognition that integrated CNN and LSTM technologies with an accuracy rate of 97.1%. However, the model is computationally expensive during training, requires a large amount of training data, and is less accurate in detecting pomegranates in complex natural backgrounds. Mitkal et al.19 proposed an automatic classification method for pomegranates that combines a CNN, a K-means clustering algorithm, and image processing technology. Naseer et al.20 developed a random forest (RF) algorithm for predicting the growth stages of pomegranates that combined the CRnet of transfer learning with spatial feature extraction. Previously, we proposed a lightweight YOLO-Granada21 algorithm for detecting the development stages of pomegranates by replacing the YOLOv5s backbone with the lightweight ShuffleNetv2 network and incorporating the convolutional block attention module (CBAM)22 attention mechanism. Experimental data showed that the model size and network parameters were compressed by 56.3% and 54.7%, respectively, compared to the original network, and the detection speed was 17.3% faster. However, the limitation of this algorithm is its 8.66 FPS, which fails to meet the requirements of real-time displays.

To further improve the display speed and weight of the network, we proposed effective strategies for the detection of pomegranate developmental stages by integrating advanced technologies to optimize the target detection algorithm. Methodology

In this study, an innovative YOLO-Punica model was developed for detecting the developmental stages of pomegranates by (1) utilizing the backbone network of YOLOv8, (2) introducing a proposed lightweight, dual-path downsampling module (DPDM), and (3) integrating the CCFM module into the neck structure. These modifications collectively aim to achieve fast, accurate, and lightweight object detection.

CCFM module

Integrating the features at different scales through fusion operations is crucial for multiscale target detection and recognition. Different from the single-scale converter encoder PANet used in YOLOv8 for feature fusion, the high-efficiency hybrid encoder architecture CCFM, proposed in the RT-DETR framework for the first time24, combines attention-based intra-scale feature interaction (AIFI) and cross-scale feature fusion (CCFF) based on convolutional neural networks (CNN)(Fig. 2). The CCFM operates multi-scale feature interactions in two steps: intra-scale interaction and cross-scale fusion. By calculating only intra-scale interaction and avoiding redundant computation of cross-scale information, the computational cost is significantly reduced and the performance of the model is improved, which is suitable for devices with limited computing resources. Additionally, along with feature fusion, the path and fusion modules of the CCFM, composed of convolution layers, were inserted. The convolution layers in the module fuse the features of adjacent scales to generate a new feature representation. The fusion block contains N RepBlocks and a two-path output. The detailed structure of the CCFM is illustrated in Fig. 3, where two 1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}1 convolution layers are embedded in the fusion block to adjust the number of channels for each feature. Furthermore, the N RepConv modules composed of RepBlocks facilitate the efficient fusion of features. Finally, the two path outputs were fused using the element-wise addition fusion method. Input

The Input section was primarily responsible for image preprocessing and enhancement. In the preprocessing step, the input image was resized to match the specific requirements of the network, and the pixel values of the image were normalized from [0, 255] to [0, 1]. For image enhancement, we used the mosaic data enhancement technique and an adaptive anchor frame calculation method based on the K-means algorithm to further process the preprocessed images. The anchor frame information in a network model can be used to optimize and enhance its performance.

Backbone

For target recognition, downsampling plays a crucial role in reducing the size of the feature image while preserving key feature information. In addition, it can effectively reduce the computational complexity and parameters and thus accelerate the training and reasoning speed of the neural network. In the original YOLOv8n architecture, the core component of feature extraction is the C2f network. In the process of the C2f module, the input data are first preprocessed through a convolution layer to implement downsampling, then enter a residual module (using a split strategy), and then undergo in-depth processing by n Dark net Bottleneck modules. Subsequently, the output of the residual module was merged with the result of the trunk path through a Concat operation and fed into a convolutional layer again to complete the final output. This downsampling operation reduces the size of the feature image but inevitably leads to the loss of fine-grained information.

Given that the pomegranate dataset used in this study contains numerous small targets and redundant information is scarce, any loss of fine-grained information can have a significant impact on the detection accuracy of the model. Therefore, a special module that can collect coarse and fine-grained information simultaneously must be introduced into the downsampling process of the model to accurately detect the characteristics of pomegranate fruits at different developmental stages. Because the actual data transmission environment of pomegranate orchards is unstable, it is particularly important to reduce the computation required to achieve light weight through a network optimized for the detection of pomegranate fruits. In the YOLO-Punica model, we introduced the proposed DPDM module for downsampling. The DPDM adopts a unique dual-branch structure to capture feature information at different scales, and two Max pooling operations effectively reduce the spatial dimension of the feature graph to improve the computational efficiency. The detailed structure of the DPDM module illustrated in Fig. 1(a).

Prediction

The prediction frames on the feature map are generated by applying anchor frames to the feature map. When applying YOLO’s generic prediction architecture, the feature map extracted from the feature pyramid layer is first processed by the head model to predict the bounding box in which the target is located, the target’s category label, and the corresponding confidence score.

Subsequently, these prediction frames were filtered using the DIoU NMS (Distance Intersection over Union Non-Maximum Suppression) algorithm to eliminate redundant predictions. DIoU NMS is an algorithm used to clean up redundant boxes after the model has made predictions. When the model predicts many overlapping boxes for the same object, NMS is responsible for keeping only the best one and removing (suppressing) the others. Traditional NMS only considers the overlapping area. DIoU NMS additionally considers the distance between the center points: if two boxes overlap a lot (IoU high), but their center points are far apart, DIoU NMS will consider them likely to represent different objects (even if the boxes themselves overlap), and therefore prefer to keep both. YOLO-Punica uses the DIoU NMS algorithm to filter these predicted frames during the inference phase to eliminate redundant predictions. Ensure that the final result is clear and non redundant, especially in scenes with dense objects where the effect is better.

Ultimately, the CIoU(Complete Intersection over Union Loss) is selected as the bounding box loss function at the output stage to facilitate the model in achieving better and faster convergence performance. CIoU Loss is a loss function constructed based on the CIoU metric, which combines the consistency of IoU, center point distance, and aspect ratio to calculate the difference between predicted boxes and real boxes. The calculation method is to divide the intersection area by the union area. It not only considers the overlap area (IoU) and center point distance (DIoU), but also additionally takes into account the aspect ratio of the bounding box. YOLO-Punica uses CIoU Loss during the training phase to optimize bounding box prediction and serves as the bounding box loss function for the output phase.

This study utilized the publicly available dataset of growth cycle images25 of pomegranate for training and validation(https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/kgwsthf2w6/5). When designing a model for detecting pomegranate fruit developmental stages, we carefully considered the tradeoff between performance and computational resources. Although increasing model parameters may improve performance, such gains are often accompanied by higher computational demands and longer inference times. In contrast, models with fewer parameters can maintain high computational efficiency while achieving good performance, making real-world deployment more feasible. Our goal is to create a model architecture that effectively utilizes fewer parameters while maintaining high accuracy, aiming to achieve a balanced tradeoff between model performance and computational cost. The YOLO-Punica model incorporates a variety of optimization strategies, including the use of the DPDM module to enhance the backbone network, ensuring high detection accuracy and efficiency. The introduction of CCFM and DPDM into the neck section effectively enables a lightweight network while retaining high precision. By integrating these two advantageous modules into the advanced YOLOv8 framework, we successfully developed a highly accurate model with strong performance.

Multi-scenario outdoor experiments in real agricultural environments

To demonstrate the effectiveness of this method in actual agricultural environment, we conducted multi-scenario outdoor experiments and compared YOLO-Punica with several modified versions of YOLOv8. The DGS-YOLOv8 model proposed by Wang et al.37 optimized the YOLOv8 network using the C2f-DCNv2 module and SimAM attention mechanism in the main branch to enhance the model’s ability to recognize the appearance features of ginseng, and combines the Slim-Neck combination (GSConv + VoVGSCSP) to reduce the model’s burden to classify the appearance and quality of ginseng. This model achieved an mAP@50 of 95.3% in the appearance quality detection of ginseng and reduced parameter count and model size compared to the original model. Another improved YOLOv8 model by Qiu et al.38addressed challenges in detecting mulberry leaves in complex environments. Lightweight improvements included replacing the C2f module with the CSPPC module, substituting the traditional downsampling module with the ADown module, and replacing the traditional convolutional detection head with the P-Head module. These modifications achieved a mAP@50 of 86.8%. The OW-YOLO model proposed by Wang et al.39incorporated the DWR-DRB module into the backbone, introduced the HSFPN, and replaced the detection head with the efficient LADH detection head. This model improved mAP@0.5 to 83.6% and addressed challenges related to obstacle detection in walnuts. An innovative method developed by Hemamalini et al.40 combined YOLOv8-C with the fast segmentation model (FastSAM) by simplifying the Neck architecture and reducing the number of layers for detecting small- and medium-sized objects and the number of upsampling layers. This reduction in complexity was specifically designed for detecting large objects and can be processed more quickly. However, focusing on optimizing the detection of large objects makes this model less suitable for general use and not applicable to the detection of medium and small targets. An improved lightweight detection model YOLOv8-CML, proposed by Chen et al.41, introduced the lightweight \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\hbox {C2f}}\underline{}{\hbox {Faster}}$$\end{document} Block module to reduce redundant computations and the number of memory accesses while integrating the EMA (Efficient Multi-Scale Attention) module in the Backbone to reduce the interference of complex backgrounds on recognition effects. This helped modify the loss function to improve recognition accuracy for detecting colored melons in intelligent agricultural equipment, successfully reducing the parameters and computational ratio by 42.9% and 51.8% respectively, and achieving an mAP@0.5 of 95.5%.

The limitations of YOLO-Punica model

Although the YOLO-Punica model improves detection accuracy across pomegranates at different growth stages while maintaining a lightweight structure, it still has some limitations, such as: (1) there are still cases of missed detection as shown in Fig. 15(b), where two mature pomegranates overlap (occlusion > 80%) and are severely occluded. In such cases, misalignment of the anchor boxes further increases the detection complexity. When the scale of an object changes, the model may not accurately predict its position. (2) There are still some missed detections in the UAV dataset because the drone is located at a high altitude and the viewing angle for target detection becomes smaller, resulting in a decline in accuracy.

Conclusion and future works

Given the strict requirements for computational efficiency and real-time performance in practical agricultural and orchard scenarios, we propose a lightweight pomegranate fruit development detection algorithm–YOLO-Punica. This algorithm is based on an improved YOLOv8 variant and incorporates the proposed DPDM backbone and CCFM architecture to effectively minimize model parameters and size, resulting in faster computing and lighter weight, and high detection accuracy. When the model parameters were compressed to 41.5% of the original YOLOv8n, mAP@50 realized the inverse of 0.926 compared to the original model’s 0.917. This breakthrough is due to the dual drive optimization of structure and efficiency. DPDM enhances feature expression ability through dual branch convolution and max pooling layer fusion, while reducing parameter redundancy; CCFM introduces cross scale residual connections, exchanging a small computational increment for increased sensitivity to small targets such as flower buds and early fruits. Accurate adaptation of computing resources optimized GFLOPs to 54.2%, combined with a compression rate of 71.9% for model volume, enables the algorithm to achieve 14.3 FPS real-time performance on edge devices with limited computing resources, which is 65.1% faster than YOLO Granada in the previous stage and meets the requirements of real-time picking and detection scenarios in real orchards. Compared with 4 mainstream target detection algorithms, 20 currently advanced models and other several modified versions of YOLOv8, YOLO-Punica significantly outperform other models in terms of performance efficiency and lightweight. In addition, YOLO-Punica algorithm can process images at 14.3 frames per second on a low-cost CPU, which is faster than our previously proposed YOLO-Granada model, which processes images at 8.66 frames per second. The lighter weight and faster operation of the model represents its potential for real-time detection when applied to realistic pomegranate orchard management. The experimental data indicated that this improved algorithm significantly reduces computational requirements and accelerates the detection process, enabling real-time pomegranate detection on an agricultural smart picking robot platform, even with limited computing power and low cost. Experiments on self-built outdoor datasets have demonstrated that YOLO Punica’s missed detection rate is significantly reduced compared to mainstream algorithms in complex orchard environments (such as branch and leaf occlusion, fluctuating light intensity, and multiple image perspectives), clearly verifying its robustness to real agricultural scenarios. This study not only promotes the deployment of target detection algorithms in terrestrial application scenarios, which has a positive impact on the development of smart agriculture, but may also provide new perspectives for similar studies. In addition, the design idea of the algorithm provides a valuable reference for the design and application of neural networks in agriculture.

However, the particularity of agricultural scenarios poses several inherent challenges that need to be carefully considered: The vulnerability of deep learning models to ”data with significant differences” is substantial. When the geographical location of orchards changes or the quality of captured images is poor, environmental variables can lead to a decrease in mAP, highlighting the importance of domain adaptation research.The morphological changes brought about by the plant growth cycle require models to have continuous learning capabilities, but the deployment of online learning mechanisms on edge devices is still limited by memory and power consumption.The inter class similarity generated by the mixed planting of multiple varieties in orchards may lead to an increase in false detection rates, and a more fine-grained feature decoupling module needs to be constructed.The non stationarity of plant phenotypes necessitates cross year data collection, which significantly increases experimental cycles and costs.

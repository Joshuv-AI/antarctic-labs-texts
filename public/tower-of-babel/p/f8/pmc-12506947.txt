Cost‐Effectiveness and Cost‐Utility Analysis of Tongxinluo Capsules Compared With Conventional Therapy for the Treatment of Acute Myocardial Infarction
Authors: Guoqiang Liu, Peng Liu, Sijun Yu, Xia Liu, Fan Wang, Chunhui Liu, Xiaozhen Hu, Yongquan Jing, Linqiang Liu, Xuxia Zhang, Yuzeng Xue, Guanzhong Zheng, ChangYu Wang, Zhongming Zhao, Yanjie Zheng, Wenzhai Cao, Huanyi Zhang, Feng Gao, Jing Zhou, Zidong Bie, Guoqiang Yuan, Lei Wang, Jun Qian, Xiaochen Tian, Haitao Zhang, Xiangdong Li, Zhenhua Jia, Ningxin Ding, Yuejin Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimTongxinluo (TXL) capsule, a traditional Chinese medicine compound, has proven effective in acute myocardial infarction (AMI), but its cost‐effectiveness is unclear.MethodsThis economic evaluation utilized individual data from clinical trials to compare major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCEs) at 30 days and quality‐adjusted life years (QALYs) at 1 year between an intervention group (TXL combined with conventional therapy) and the control group (placebo plus conventional therapy), from a healthcare perspective. A lifetime cost‐utility analysis (CUA) was conducted using a Markov model, and sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of the results.ResultsA total of 3777 patients (TXL: 1889; placebo: 1888) were included in the analysis. The 30‐day total costs for the TXL and placebo groups were ¥38,561 ($5399) and ¥39,217 ($5490), respectively, showing no statistical difference. The 30‐day MACCEs rates were 3.39% for the TXL group and 5.24% for the placebo group (p < 0.006), indicating TXL's superiority in effectiveness at 30 days. Over a lifetime, the TXL group incurred higher total costs (¥97,108 [$13,595] vs. ¥92,033 [$12,885]) and gained more QALYs (6.70 vs. 6.30). The incremental cost‐effectiveness ratio for TXL was ¥12,421/QALY ($1739), below the 1 Gross Domestic Product per capital threshold which was ¥89,358 ($12,510) in 2023 in China. Sensitivity analysis confirmed robust results, revealing that TXL was more likely to be accepted over the placebo when the willingness to pay exceeds ¥12,500 ($1739).ConclusionsTXL is a cost‐effective option compared to placebo in AMI.Keywords: acute myocardial infarction, cost‐effectiveness, cost‐utility, tongxinluo capsules, traditional Chinese medicine

Introduction

Acute ST‐segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) is a severe form of coronary heart disease that imposes a significant clinical and economic burden. In the United States, ischemic heart disease had the highest expenditure among all cardiovascular diseases in 2016, with costs reaching $79.8 billion [1]. A similar trend was observed in China, where the financial impact was also substantial. In 2020, total hospitalization costs for ischemic heart disease reached 116.959 billion yuan. Of this, acute myocardial infarction (AMI) accounted for a notable share—averaging 30,171 yuan per hospitalization and contributing a total of 34.685 billion yuan, which represented 29.6% of all ischemic heart disease hospitalization expenditures [2, 3]. Looking ahead, the challenge is expected to intensify: according to World Bank projections, between 2010 and 2030, the combined disease burden of AMI and stroke will make up more than 50% of all chronic disease burdens in China, highlighting a major ongoing threat to public health. The primary treatment object for STEMI is to promptly restore blood perfusion to the ischemic myocardium, with the aim of salvaging endangered tissue, limiting ischemic damage, preventing infarct expansion, thereby preserving cardiac function, and reducing mortality. Reperfusion therapy, such as percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and thrombolytic therapy is crucial for improving survival in STEMI patients. Additionally, adjunctive pharmacological treatments, including anticoagulants and anti‐ischemic medications, play a key role in comprehensive clinical management.

TXL capsule is a traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), which is extracted from various insects and plants. Though the specific active ingredients and their mechanisms are yet to be fully understood, studies suggest components like paeoniflorin and ginsenosides in TXL may offer potential cardioprotective effects. While previous clinical research has confirmed the effectiveness and safety of TXL capsules in treating AMI [4, 5, 6, 7, 8], there's a notable gap in studies regarding its economic efficiency [9]. This study, alongside a multicenter, randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled TXL clinical trial (CTS‐AMI) [10], collected costs and health outcomes data from AMI patients to evaluate the cost‐effectiveness and cost‐utility of TXL in treating AMI. The aim of this research is to fill the gap and provide evidence to guide decision‐making in medical resource allocation.

Methods

Study Design

This study aims to evaluate the economic efficiency of Tongxinluo (TXL) capsule combined with conventional therapy versus conventional therapy alone in patients with STEMI. The observation period was set at one year, during which we collected individual patient data on costs and health outcomes for cost‐effectiveness analysis (CEA). Given that STEMI patients may experience recurrent myocardial infarction and complications in the long term, we developed a simulation model to project the impact of long‐term medication use on health status and conducted a cost‐utility analysis. This study was conducted concurrently with the CTS‐AMI clinical trial and was registered at Fuwai Hospital (No. 2018‐1085).

Setting and Location

This study was conducted across 124 clinical centers across China. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria can be found in clinical article [10].

Population and Intervention

The study enrolled 3777 patients, with 1889 in the TXL group and 1888 in the placebo group. The mean age (standard deviation) was 61.4 (12.1) years for the TXL group and 61.5 (12.1) years for the placebo group. The proportion of females was 22.9% in the TXL group and 23.3% in the placebo group. Additionally, 50.5% of participants had hypertension, 42.8% had a history of smoking, 24% had dyslipidemia, and 21.6% had diabetes, along with other high‐risk factors. Both the TXL and placebo groups were well balanced in terms of patient baseline characteristics and care details [10].

The experimental medication was TXL capsules, while the control was a TXL placebo. Patients received standard treatments for STEMI as guideline recommendations, including dual anti‐platelet therapy and coronary reperfusion. TXL and placebo were administered as adjuvant therapy in addition to standard treatments. The observational period lasts for 12 months, with follow‐up visits scheduled at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months.

Patients in the TXL group were administered an oral loading dose of TXL (8 capsules, 2.08 g) after randomization, followed by a maintenance dose of four capsules (1.04 g), three times daily for 12 months. The placebo group received matching placebo capsules specifically designed for the TXL trial.

Time Horizon and Discounting

While no discounting was applied to the short‐term analyses (30‐day and 1‐year horizons), a 5% discount rate was incorporated into the long‐term simulation of the Markov model [11].

Costs

The analysis was conducted from the perspective of the healthcare system, collecting the total healthcare expenses incurred by participating patients, referred to as direct medical costs. Specifically, the expenses include bed charges, nursing fees, medication costs (both traditional Chinese and Western medicine), laboratory test (including routine blood, urine, and stool tests, blood biochemistry, cardiac enzymes, blood sugar, blood lipids, N‐terminal pro‐B‐type natriuretic peptide [NT‐proBNP], etc.), examination fees (including electrocardiograms, echocardiography, etc.), diagnosis and treatment costs (including registration fees, consultation fees, injection fees [excluding medication costs], etc.), and surgical costs (including emergency PCI, etc.).

Health Outcomes

Clinical Outcome for CEA

The primary measure of effectiveness is the incidence rate of major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCEs) at 30 days, with the 1‐year incidence serving as a secondary measure. Both measures were informed by clinical article [10].

Quality‐Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) for CUA

The EuroQol Five Dimensions Five Levels (EQ‐5D‐5L) measurement was used to assess patient quality of life in the CUA. Utility scores were calculated using the EQ‐5D‐5L valuing set specific to the Chinese population [12], reflecting changes in patients’ quality of life during the follow‐up period. Subsequently, QALYs were computed for the 30‐day, 1‐year, and lifetime durations. For example, with an average utility of 0.77, the 30‐day QALY would be approximately 0.77 × (30/365) ≈ 0.0632.

Economic Evaluation

Markov Model

This study conducted an economic evaluation using a six‐state Markov model with an annual cycle, informed by the results of the CTS‐AMI clinical trial and published literature [13, 14, 15, 16, 17]. The model includes the following health states: STEMI without new events, stroke, post‐stroke, recurrent myocardial infarction, post‐recurrent myocardial infarction, and death, with transition pathways as illustrated in Figure S1.

TreeAge Pro 2019 software was used to simulate and analyze the Markov model. The cycle commenced at the average age of 62 years, as observed in the clinical trial samples. Based on the life expectancy in China, which was 77.93 years in 2022 according to the National Bureau of Statistics [18], we adopted a 16‐year (192‐month) time horizon for our analysis. In line with the China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2020), a discount rate of 5% was applied [11]. The model parameters are mainly derived from clinical trial data, with additional inputs sourced from published literature when trial data are unavailable. Specifically, first‐year costs and transition probabilities are taken directly from clinical trials. However, due to the lack of long‐term follow‐up data for both groups, it is assumed that in the second year, all patients receive either clopidogrel, or aspirin, or a combination of both. As a result, the transition probabilities and costs for each health state in the second year are obtained from the literature and assumed to be the same for both groups. A detailed summary of the model parameters is provided in Table S1.

According to the Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations, the threshold criteria for CUA are defined as follows: (a) an incremental cost‐effectiveness ratio (ICER) less than one time the per capita GDP is considered highly cost‐effective; (b) an ICER between one and three times the per capita GDP is regarded as cost‐effective; (c) an ICER exceeding three times the per capita GDP is considered not cost‐effective. Based on data from the China National Bureau of Statistics, the per capita GDP in 2023 was ¥89,358 (approximately $12,510) [11, 18].

The ICER is calculated using a formula as shown above, in which cost represents total cost and effectiveness denotes the health outcome (e.g., MACCEs or QALYs).

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were employed for continuous variables, with comparisons made using paired t‐tests or Wilcoxon rank‐sum tests. For categorical variables, descriptive statistics were utilized alongside inter‐group chi‐square tests or exact probability tests for comparison. Missing data were addressed using mean or nearest neighbor imputation methods. All statistical tests were conducted using two‐sided tests, and a p‐value of 0.05 or less was considered statistically significant, unless otherwise specified. The ICER calculations were performed using TreeAge Pro 2019 software. All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software, version 9.4.

Sensitivity Analysis

A sensitivity analysis was conducted for both MACCEs at 30‐day (as a major outcome indicator) and lifetime outcomes to evaluate the parameters that could potentially affect treatment cost and effectiveness. For the MACCEs at 30‐day one‐way analysis, we determined the range of variation for cost parameters and clinical outcomes based on the 95% confidence interval (CI) derived from individual clinical data. Conversely, the variation range for the lifetime analysis incorporated data from both the 95% CI of individual data and relevant reference (Table S1). Additionally, a 30‐day probabilistic sensitivity analysis was conducted, utilizing 1000 bootstrap resamples of both cost and effect data. For the lifetime analysis, a probabilistic sensitivity analysis was conducted using Monte Carlo simulation with 1000 trials to test the robustness of the results under simultaneous changes in probabilities, costs, and utilities. In CEA, the absence of standardized threshold criteria necessitates consultation with clinical and pharmacoeconomic experts to determine the intervention's cost‐effectiveness. This collaborative process ensures a comprehensive evaluation, incorporating various perspectives.

Further breakdowns of the cost components, as detailed in Table 2, revealed no statistically significant differences between the two groups.

The 30‐day MACCEs rates were 3.39% (95% CI 2.87%‐4.62%) in the TXL group and 5.24% (95% CI 4.30%‐6.37%) in the placebo group, with a significant difference (p = 0.006). This indicates a considerable reduction in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events with TXL use. Regarding 30‐day QALYs, they were 0.06 (95% CI 0.06‐0.06) in the TXL group and 0.06 (95% CI 0.06‐0.06) in the placebo group, showing no statistical significance.

Based on the information discussed, the results of the CEA and CUA suggested that although the costs were similar after 30 days of treatment, the TXL group demonstrated superior clinical effectiveness. This implies that TXL is a dominating strategy in this scenario.

One‐Year CEA and CUA (Individual Patient Data)

Individual patient data revealed that the total cost of 1‐year treatment in the TXL group and the placebo group was ¥44,432 (95% CI 43,689, 45,174) [$6220] and ¥42,170 (95% CI 41,180, 43,161) [$5904] respectively (p < 0.0001). The 1‐year drug cost for TXL was ¥3781 (95% CI 3744, 3819) [$529], and after subtracting the cost of TXL drug, the direct medical cost was not higher than that in the placebo group. The 1‐year rate of MACCEs was 5.29% (95% CI 4.33%, 6.45%) in the TXL group and 8.32% (95% CI 7.11%, 9.70%) in the placebo group, reflecting a reduction of 3.03% (95% CI ‐4.63%, ‐1.43%) (p < 0.001). This showed that 1‐year TXL medication significantly reduced the occurrence of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events.

The 1‐year QALYs in the TXL group and the placebo group were 0.79 (95% CI 0.77, 0.81) and 0.77 (95% CI 0.75, 0.79), respectively. However, the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.48). The CUA showed that the 1‐year ICER for the TXL group and the placebo group was ¥115,408 [$16,157], which was less than twice the GDP per capital of ¥89,358 [$12,510]. Therefore, the TXL treatment was cost‐effective. The detailed results are shown in Table 1.

Cost‐Utility Analysis (lifetime)

Following adjustment for half‐cycle correction and discounting, the lifetime treatment costs for patients in the TXL and placebo groups were ¥97,108 ($13,595) and ¥ 92,033 ($12,885), respectively. The QALYs of TXL group and placebo group were 6.70 and 6.30 respectively, as detailed in Table 1. The ICER was ¥12,421, indicating that compared to the placebo group, achieving an additional 1 QALY in the TXL group cost ¥12,421 ($1739) more. According to the threshold criteria outlined in the method section, the TXL group was considered highly cost‐effective, as the ICER of ¥12,421 ($1739) falls below the 2023 per capita GDP in China (¥89,358).

Discussion

This study was based on a multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled trial, which collected data on medical costs and quality of life for patients undergoing 30 days and 1 year of treatment. The 30‐day CEA revealed that the cost of the TXL group was comparable to that of the placebo group. With favorable outcomes, the TXL group emerged as a dominating strategy. As mentioned earlier, the 1‐year CUA indicates that the ICER is ¥115,408, which was less than twice the per capita GDP of ¥89,358 [$12,510]. Hence, the TXL treatment proved to be cost‐effective. To account for the chronic nature of the condition under study, we constructed a Markov model to simulate the prolonged effects of medication on patient outcomes. Following adjustment for half‐cycle correction and discounting, the lifetime treatment costs for patients in the TXL and placebo groups were ¥97,108 ($13,595) and ¥ 92,033 ($12,885), respectively. The QALYs of TXL group and placebo group were 6.70 and 6.30 respectively. The ICER was ¥12,421 ($1739), which was well below China's 2023 per capita GDP of ¥89,358 ($12,510), indicating that the TXL group is highly cost‐effective. It is important to note that this ICER is a conservative estimate. In an alternative scenario that includes nursing care costs for stroke patients, the ICER may decrease even further, strengthening the case for cost‐effectiveness.

Based on an economic report [19] on treating acute myocardial infarction using Chinese clinical data, it is noted that most standard treatment methods recommended by the World Health Organization, particularly combination therapies, have moderate to high cost‐effectiveness in China. However, there have been no economic studies on the cost‐effectiveness of TXL in addition to standard therapy for treatment of STEMI, our contribution addresses this gap. Previous research [20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27] has primarily focused on evaluating the cost‐effectiveness of STEMI treatment through standard PCI or combinations of medications with conventional therapies. Currently, there is a lack of high‐quality clinical research on TCM as an adjunctive treatment. Our study is the first prospective clinical trial to evaluate the cost‐effectiveness of TCM as an adjunctive therapy for treating acute myocardial infarction.

This study's limitations include the parameters referenced in existing literature. The parameters used in the Markov model were drawn from individual‐level clinical trial data and literature, which may introduce some inconsistencies and may not fully capture real‐world clinical practice. This study assumed that all patients without new events received either standard monotherapy or combination therapy starting from the second model cycle. Although this assumption may lead to some deviation from actual treatment costs, sensitivity analysis showed that its impact was minimal. Furthermore, since the same medication cost was applied to both groups, any potential bias was effectively mitigated.

The cost‐effectiveness threshold (CET) commonly used, as recommended by the World Health Organization, ranges from 1 to 3 times the per capita GDP. However, in practice, the actual threshold may be lower. In China, supply‐side estimates place the CET at 1.50 times GDP per capita per QALY gained, while demand‐side estimates range from 0.77 to 2.06 times [28]. In this study, the long‐term simulation yielded an ICER of just 0.14 times GDP per capita, indicating that the intervention is highly cost‐effective and remains so across different threshold assumptions. Finally, within the FAS dataset, neither the TXL group nor the placebo group underwent revascularization or developed any additional disease conditions. Nevertheless, future research should strive to address this limitation.

This study highlights that the TXL group exhibits superior short‐term effectiveness compared to placebo, emerging as the dominant strategy. In terms of long‐term effectiveness, with a willingness to pay of ¥12,500 ($1739), the TXL group demonstrates a 51.4% probability of being cost‐effective. Furthermore, as the willingness to pay exceeds ¥12,500 ($1739), the TXL group consistently maintains a higher probability of cost‐effectiveness compared to the placebo group. These findings underscore the superior economic effectiveness of TXL capsules over placebo in the treatment and myocardial protection of acute myocardial infarction.

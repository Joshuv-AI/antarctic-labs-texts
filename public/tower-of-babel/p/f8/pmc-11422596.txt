Effects of number of vanes, vane angle and disc peripheral speed on the distribution uniformity of twin-disc granular fertilizer broadcaster
Authors: Fırat Kömekçi, Ceren Kömekçi, Damla Doğu, Erdem Aykas
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study was to determine the effects of the number of vanes, vane angle and disc peripheral speed on the uniformity of transverse and longitudinal distribution in two-disc granular spreaders. The experiments were carried out with spherical fertilizer, 3 different numbers of vanes (2 – 3 – 6), 5 different vane angles (17° – 30° – 43° – 56° – 69°) and 5 different peripheral speeds of the disc (13.85 – 15.05 – 16.26 – 17.46 – 18.67 m s−1) with a full factorial experimental design in accordance with ASAE 341.4 and TS 2541 standards. During the operation of the machine, the best working condition in terms of the uniformity of the transverse distribution (CVxvers, %) was obtained with a disc peripheral speed of 15.05 m s−1, an angle of 56° and the use of 2 vanes. In this working condition, the CVxvers (%) of 8.31 % was found, while the working width was calculated to be 16.22 m. Regarding the uniformity of fertilizer distribution in the longitudinal direction, the lowest CVlong (%) was found at a disc peripheral speed of 17.46 m s−1 and a vane angle of 69° when only 2 vanes were used.Keywords: Fertilizer distribution, Coefficient of variation, Distribution pattern, Fertilizer spreaders, Longitudinal and transverse distribution

Introduction

Disc-type fertilizer broadcasters are mostly used for granular fertilizer application. These spreaders have one or two rotating disks with multiple vanes to impart energy to the granules. The material is metered onto the disks and is thrown wide due to the centrifugal force [1]. Many factors contribute to uniformity of granular fertilizer application. These factors can be the physical-mechanical properties of the fertilizer, the structural characteristics of the machine such as the shape of the distributor disc and vanes, the position and number of the vanes, the shape and location of the feed opening, the length of the free radius, the feed radius, the peripheral speed of the disc, and the operational characteristics such as the height of the distributor disc above the ground [2,3]. Many studies have been conducted to investigate the effects of these parameters on the uniformity of distribution of disc-type granular broadcasters. When working with these types of broadcasters, the uniformity of distribution deteriorates as the peripheral speed of the distributor disc decreases [4]. The effects of vane angle, orifice diameter and peripheral speed of the distributor disc on the uniformity of fertilizer distribution uniformity in single disc fertilizer spreader were investigated and the lowest coefficient of variation, 9 %, was found with a vane angle of 0°, orifice diameter of 30 mm and a disc revolution of 810 min−1 [5]. In addition, the effects of combinations of different orifice diameters and the number of vanes on the uniformity of fertilizer distribution were investigated. The lowest coefficient of variation of 11 % and 10 % for triple superphosphate and calcium ammonium nitrate, respectively, was achieved with the combination of 2 vanes and 30 mm orifice [6]. The effect of 5 different vane shapes and 3 different fertilizer flow rates on the uniformity of fertilizer distribution was investigated and TSP fertilizer was used. It was found that the uniformity of fertilizer distribution decreased with the increase of fertilizer flow rate [7]. Response Surfaces Methodology (RSM) was used as an optimization tool and the uniformity of distribution of urea, 15-15-15 and 20-20-0 compound fertilizer was determined using a twin-disc fertilizer spreader [8]. The variables considered were vane height, vane angle, fertilizer flow rate and peripheral speed of the disc. It was found that the lowest CVlong (%) for urea, 15-15-15 and 20-20-0 compound fertilizer was 6.775 %, 8.135 % and 8.11 %, respectively [8]. It is reported that better uniformity of fertilizer distribution can be achieved at higher effective working widths with higher disc revolutions, regardless of the type of spreader vane [9]. The effect of revolution of the disc, feed position of fertilizer on the disc and vane angle on the spatial distribution of fertilizer was investigated and urea, ammonium sulfate and nitro chalk were used. The study showed that the factors that strongly affect the average angle of the stationary spread pattern are the feeding position of the fertilizer on the disc, the vanes angle on the disc and the fertilizer type [10]. According to a study conducted to determine the effects of the number of vanes of a centrifugal fertilizer distributor (three or four vanes), orifice gate open ratio (50 or 100 %), and disc rotation speed of the disk (400, 500 or 600 rpm) on the uniformity of fertilizer distribution, the CV with four vanes, 50 % orifice gate open ratio, and 500 rpm of disc rotating speed was 18.4 %, which gave the most uniform fertilization [11]. The results of a study to optimize the uniformity of distribution of a single-disc granular broadcast spreader indicate that the vane angle is an important design variable, while the two operational variables (disc peripheral speed and flow rate) influence the uniformity of distribution [12].

Many studies have been carried out to determine the uniformity of distribution of twin-disc fertilizer broadcasters. However, in reviewing the literature, no study was found that considered all the parameters to be discussed in this study with a full factorial experimental design. According to the literature, commercial twin-disc fertilizer broadcasters are produced by the various agricultural machinery manufacturers with a specific number of vanes and vane angles. In light of the above information, a study was conducted with the objective of determining the effects of the number and angle of the vanes and the disc peripheral speed on the uniformity of fertilizer distribution in a twin-disc fertilizer broadcaster. The results of this study contribute to the production of these machines.

Depending on the adjustment options of an outlet orifice at the bottom of the hopper, the application norm values were determined under laboratory conditions. In the fertilizer flow experiments, the PTO drive of the machine and the movement of the arms for adjusting the orifice size were taken from the NH TM 175 tractor. In these experiments, the amount of fertilizer was weighed and the fertilizer flow rate (kg min−1) was determined by setting the flow rate of the machine to at least 40 % of capacity for 1 min and performing each experiment three times at each opening value. The fertilizer norm (kg da−1) was calculated for each orifice assuming that the working width was 14 m and the forward speed was 8 km h−1. Thus, the orifice size was found to allow a fertilizer application rate of 30 kg da−1 to be used for the field experiments.

The twin-disc granular broadcaster used in this study originally had only 2 of vanes on the distributor discs and the vane angles could be changed in 4 different positions. The standard configuration of the commercial twin-disc granular fertilizer broadcaster is suggested by the machine manufacturer for the application of granular fertilizer and includes 2 of vanes, 56° of vane angle and 540 min−1 of PTO speed. In disc fertilizer broadcasters, the number of vanes on the distributor discs could be varies between 2 and 12 [6]. In this study, 6 of vanes were chosen as the maximum number of vanes because of desired vane angles. In addition to the 2 and 6 of vanes, 3 of vanes were also included in the experimental design in order to be able to experiment with the same discs. The angle of the vane, which is the angle between the vane and the radial line on the disc, is one of the most important design factors of the distributor discs. It has a major influence on the motion of the particles [19]. The positions of the vane angles on the distributor disc were determined by drilling holes on the disc to obtain the angle (β) between the radial (LR) and the vane line (Lv) depicted in Fig. 2. Vane angles were determined in 5 levels obtained with 13⁰ difference so that all 6 vanes could be placed on the disc. With regard to peripheral speed of the distributor disc, studies in the literature conducted between 11.7 and 22.59 m s−1 [8,12]. In this study, 5 levels of disc peripheral speed were determined so that a constant forward speed can be achieved during operation, with the PTO speed of the tractor giving the desired disc peripheral speed in all experiments with different transmission options.

It is reported that the uniformity of fertilizer distribution deteriorates when the PTO speed drops below 540 min−1 [4]. The transmission systems of disc fertilizer broadcasters, which are widely used today, are single-stage [4]. In this context, the variation of the disc peripheral speed depends on the PTO ratio and the power transmission ratio used in the design of the machine. In this study, the effects of disc peripheral speeds corresponding to 460, 500, 540, 580 and 620 min−1 PTO revolutions on the uniformity of fertilizer distribution were investigated.

The box sequence that emerged during the experiments is shown in Fig. 5. To prevent the fertilizer distributed by the broadcaster from bouncing up after it has fallen into the plastic box with the dimensions specified in the ASAE standards [20], 50 mm high cardboard material separators are placed in the containers. Before starting the experiments, the collection containers were arranged in three rows to obtain three replications in the form of a two-dimensional matrix and in the direction of progression on the field according to the ASAE standard [20]. A distance of 2000 mm was maintained between the rows. The collection boxes were placed at equal distances on all 3 axes and there were 39 collection boxes on the right side, 39 on the left side and 3 under the tractor, which were determined to be the working width for the preliminary test. A distance of 800 mm was left between the collection boxes in each row to allow the tractor wheels to pass between the boxes during the experiments. Before each experiment, the fertilizer hopper level was checked and fertilizer was topped up to keep the level between 40 % and 50 % [20]. To determine the uniformity of the longitudinal distribution, the collection boxes were arranged in 5 rows parallel to the direction of travel of the tractor. The first row is located in the middle of the track width, the other two rows are 0.5 m away from the outer surface of the wheels and are 0.5 m apart. There are 20 collection boxes in each row, which touch each other [21]. During the experiments, the wind speed and direction were measured at a height of 1.5 m above the ground before each test and none of the experiments were conducted at wind speeds above 2 m s−1 [20]. The experiments were conducted in the early mornings of early summer so that the wind speed, temperature and humidity were suitable for fertilizer application. The measured conditions during the experiments were: a wind speed between 0.5 and 0.8 m s−1, a temperature between 25 and 30 °C and a humidity between 20 and 25 %.

Placement of collection boxes used in field experiments according to ASABE [20] and TR EN [21] standards 24.

Twin-disc granular broadcaster was attached to the New Holland TM175 tractor as suspended and the parallelism of the machine to the ground is adjusted thanks to the three-point hitch system. The height of the distributor disc from the ground was kept constant at 80 cm in all experiments [17]. Since the engine speed, which provides different PTO revolutions is not constant and the tractor has 16 + 8 gears, the field experiments were carried out with the engine speed and tractor transmission option that gives the desired PTO speed. To eliminate the influence of speed on the uniformity of distribution, the feed rate was determined for each experiment. During the experiments, the power requirement of the machine under each operating condition was determined using an NCTE 7000 torque meter. The torque meter also measured the PTO speed during the experiments at a high sampling rate.

The fertilizers collected in the collection boxes during the experiments were filled into paper cups and transported to the laboratory after the experiments. These samples were weighed in the laboratory using an electronic precision balance with an accuracy of ±0.01 g. After the field experiments, normal distribution fertilizer values were obtained. After the overlapping process [8,20], which was carried out with the macro written in the MS Excel program, the values of the half and overlapped distribution of the fertilizers of the individual experiments were calculated. Then, CVxverse (%) values expressing the uniformity of fertilizer distribution and the ratios between left and right distributions were obtained for each experiment. It is desirable that the uniformity of fertilizer distribution in the transverse direction (CVxverse, %) and in the longitudinal direction (CVlong, %) is less than 20 % for disc fertilizer spreaders after overlapping [8,17]. The evaluation of the uniformity of fertilizer distribution can be found in Table 4. The difference between the amount of fertilizer thrown to the right and left according to the tractor axis in the transverse distribution of fertilizer after overlapping should not exceed ±4 % [8,17]. Thus, the number of vanes, the angle of the vane position and disc peripheral speed were determined, which reduces the CVxverse (%) value to the lowest possible value below 10 % and the difference between the amount of fertilizer thrown to the right and left will be less than 4 %. The CVxverse (%) value obtained in the distribution uniformity experiment carried out with the configuration suggested by the manufacturer of the twin-disc granular fertilizer broadcaster used in this study was 19.22 % at a working width of 25.74 m.

Within the scope of this study, the left/right ratio, the transverse and longitudinal distribution of the fertilizer was determined in field experiments with 3 different numbers of distributor vanes (2–3 – 6 pieces), 5 distributor vane angles (17°–30° – 43°–56° – 69°) and 5 distributor disc peripheral speed (13.85–15.05 – 16.26–17.46 – 18.67 m s−1) in field experiments. All experiments were carried out in accordance with the standards [20,21].

After the values for power requirement, left/right fertilizer ratio, CVxverse (%) and CVlong (%) of the 75 field experiments were determined, Analyses of Variance and Duncan's test were performed by IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 to determine the effects of the independent variables in the experimental design.

Results and discussion

As a result of the fertilizer flow tests carried out in the laboratory to determine the fertilizer application norm, the fertilizer norm was calculated assuming a working width of 12–14 – 16 m and the forward speed of 8 km h−1 for each orifice levels on the machine. The level which provides a value close to the fertilizer norm of 30 kg da−1, which is generally used for the application of fertilizer, was determined.

During the experiments, the power requirement under working conditions was determined using a torque meter connected to the tractor's PTO output. As a result of the Analysis of Variance conducted to determine the effect of the number, angle of distributor vanes and PTO speed on power requirement, it was found that all variables except vane angle and the interaction of all variables were significant for the power requirement of the twin-disc granular broadcaster, described by the coefficient of determination (R2) as 0.649 (Table 5). Altering the vane angle did not lead to any difference in weight and therefore had no effect on the power requirement. As a result of the Duncan's test conducted to determine the effect of the number of vanes on power requirement, it was found that the power requirement of the machine increased with an increase in the number of vanes (Table 6).

It is desirable for the CVxverse in twin disc granular broadcasters to be less than 20 % after overlapping [8,17]. In addition, the effective working width should be as high as possible in order to maintain the effective field capacity. The result of the experiments was a maximum working width of 26.42 m with a disc peripheral speed of 18.67 m s−1, a vane angle of 56° and either 2 or 6 vanes. The coefficient of variation, which expresses the uniformity of fertilizer application, was 19.21 % and 18.13 % for these experiments, respectively. However, the lowest CVxverse (%) was found to be 8.31 % with a disc peripheral speed of 15.05 m s−1, a vane angle of 56°, 2 vanes and a working width of 16.22 m. Fig. 7 shows the line graph of the normal distribution of the experiment with the best CVxverse (%), 8.31 %, and after the overlapping process, its overlapped and half distribution line graphs. Although the lowest coefficient of variation, 8.31 %, which expresses the uniformity of transverse fertilizer distribution was obtained at a disc peripheral speed of 15.05 m s−1, it was found that the uniformity of transverse fertilizer distribution uniformity increased with increasing disc peripheral speed (Table 12). This result supports the studies of [4,5,8]. Although the best uniformity of transverse fertilizer distribution in the experiment was obtained with 2-vane similar to Ref. [6], the uniformity of transverse fertilizer distribution uniformity increased with increasing number of vanes in contrast to Ref. [6] (Table 12). In addition, the angle of vanes had a statistical effect on the uniformity of the transverse fertilizer distribution similar to Refs. [8,10,12].

The number of vanes and the vane angle were found to be statistically significant for the CVlong as a result of 75 experiments, according to the results of the Analysis of Variance results described by the coefficient of determination (R2) as 0.493 (Table 13). The peripheral speed of the distributor disc has no effect on CVlong. This can be explained by the fact that the disc peripheral speed is directly related to the path that the fertilizer particles travel on the disc and after they are thrown from the disc [1]. The results of the Duncan's test performed to determine the effect of vane angle and vane number on CVlong are shown in Table 14. It is desirable that the CVlong (%) is as low as possible during the operation of the broadcaster. According to the results of the statistical analysis, the lowest average CVlong (%) values were obtained in the experiments at disc peripheral speed of 13.85 m s−1, a vane angle of 69° and 6 vanes independently of each other. In order to achieve optimum uniformity of fertilizer distribution in the longitudinal direction, the lowest CVlong (%) of 10.10 % was achieved with a disc peripheral speed of 17.46 m s−1, vane angle of 69° and 2-vane.

Conclusion

According to the results of the study, it was found that.

•All independent variables and the interaction of all variables except the vane angle affect the power requirement of the spherical fertilizer spreader. It was also found that the power consumption of the machine increases as the number of vanes increases.

All independent variables and the interaction of all variables except the vane angle affect the power requirement of the spherical fertilizer spreader. It was also found that the power consumption of the machine increases as the number of vanes increases.

•All the independent variables considered in this study and the interaction of these variables affect the amounts of fertilizer thrown on the right and left sides of the spherical fertilizer spreader. During the operation of the machine, the ratio between left and right fertilizer can be varied between 0.85 and 1.17. The users of the machine can use all the working condition of the study to adjust the ratio between left and right fertilizer optimally.

•Although the highest working width of 26.42 m was achieved with a disc peripheral speed of 18.67 m s−1, a vane angle of 56° and 2 or 6 vanes, the CVxverse in these experiments were 19.21 % and 18.13 % respectively. However, in order to achieve the most uniform transverse distribution of fertilizer, the working conditions with the lowest CVxverse (%) were 15.05 m s−1 disc peripheral speed, 56° vane angle and 2 vanes. In this case, the CVxverse value was determined to be 8.31 % at a working width of 16.22 m.

According to the results of the study, it is recommended that.

•The manufacturers of the disc granular broadcasters should design the machine with a continuously adjustable peripheral speed of the discs. While the tractor operates at the engine speed with the lowest specific fuel consumption, the spreading disc of the broadcaster can therefore operate at the desired speed.

The manufacturers of the disc granular broadcasters should design the machine with a continuously adjustable peripheral speed of the discs. While the tractor operates at the engine speed with the lowest specific fuel consumption, the spreading disc of the broadcaster can therefore operate at the desired speed.

•Manufacturers should produce both the number of vanes and the angles of the vanes as adjustable.

Manufacturers should produce both the number of vanes and the angles of the vanes as adjustable.

•Making preliminary experiments with the fertilizers used by farmers and specifying the operating parameters in detail in the machine's instruction manual is beneficial for the correct distribution of fertilizer in the field, as the design and operating parameters of the disc granular broadcaster will change when farmers work with fertilizers with different physico-mechanical properties.

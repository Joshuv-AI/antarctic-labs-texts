Research note: The spatiotemporal patterns of goose astrovirus in China
Authors: Haiqin Li, Na Li, Zhangzhang Wang, Yufei Zou, Hongping Jiang, Ligen Xiong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Goose astrovirus (GoAstV), a persistent threat to China’s poultry industry for over a decade, causing substantial economic losses due to its high morbidity and mortality rates. Despite epidemiological studies identifying two dominant genotypes (GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2), gaps persist in understanding its molecular evolution and spatiotemporal dynamics. Here, we conducted a systematic analysis of 112 publicly available and in-house GoAstV genomes isolated across different provinces of China from 2014 to 2024. Phylogenetic analysis confirmed the co-circulation of both genotypes, with GoAstV-2 dominating in prevalence while GoAstV-1 exhibited sustained low-level circulating across multiple provinces. Notably, the lack of spatiotemporal clustering in phylogenetic trees—coupled with conserved genome architecture—implies stable, interconnected viral reservoirs rather than localized outbreaks. This evolutionary stability likely reflects sustained transmission efficacy in susceptible hosts, potentially facilitated by cryptic pathways such as migratory bird movements or poultry trade. Our findings highlight the feasibility of developing broad-spectrum vaccines or antivirals targeting conserved replication machinery (ORF1a/ORF1b) or capsid proteins (ORF2), offering a unified strategy to mitigate outbreaks driven by this genetically stable virus.

Introduction

Goose astrovirus (GoAstV), a member of the Avastrovirus genus, has emerged as a critical pathogen threatening waterfowl populations globally, with China bearing a disproportionate burden of outbreaks since its initial detection in 2014 (Zhu and Sun, 2022). The virus primarily infects goslings, inducing severe visceral gout, hepatitis, and nephritis, with mortality rates exceeding 50 % in flocks, leading to devastating economic repercussions for the poultry industry. Over the past decades, epidemiological investigations in China have identified two dominant GoAstV genotypes (GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2), with an increasing number of genomes deposited in public databases (Fei et al., 2022). Despite this growing repository of genetic data, critical gaps persist in understanding the molecular mechanisms driving GoAstV’s persistence, geographic transmission, and evolutionary trajectory.

Current epidemiological studies have focused largely on regional outbreaks, characterizing localized transmission dynamics and genetic diversity. For instance, GoAstV-2 has been implicated in recent epidemics across Guangdong and Jiangsu provinces (Xu et al., 2023), while GoAstV-1 remains endemic in northeastern China (Chen et al., 2024). However, these studies lack integrative insights into how temporal and geographic factors shape viral spread at a national scale. Such analyses are essential for identifying viral migration routes, pinpointing genetic diversification, and evaluating the risk of cross-regional transmission—factors that directly influence the efficacy of containment strategies. Furthermore, while phylogenetic studies suggest long-term circulation of GoAstV in China, the extent of genomic conservation or adaptation over time remains poorly resolved, hindering the development of targeted interventions.

Analyzing the spatiotemporal patterns of GoAstV is critical for several reasons. First, the poultry industry in China operates through extensive interprovincial trade networks, which may accelerate viral dissemination across regions with varying biosecurity practices. Second, climatic and ecological differences between geographic locations could drive divergent evolutionary pressures, potentially fostering genotype-specific adaptations. Third, the absence of a universally effective vaccine underscores the urgency of identifying conserved antigenic regions that remain stable across time and space—a prerequisite for designing durable immunogens.

To address these gaps, this study synthesizes all publicly available and in-house GoAstV genomes from China, spanning 2014–2024, to elucidate the virus’s molecular epidemiology. Our findings provide a critical foundation for understanding GoAstV’s transmission dynamics and inform strategies to mitigate its impact on poultry health and food security.

Methods

Ethics statement

The Experimental Animal Ethics Committee in Jiangxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences (JXAAS 2020-0025) approved this study. All procedures involving animals were conducted according to the guidelines for the care and use of experimental animals established by the Ministry of Agriculture of China.

Sample collection

7-day-old sick goslings from a goose farm in Jiangxi died. The sick goose was thin, weak, and small, with a depressed spirit and white fecal matter in the cloaca. Autopsy showed suspected symptoms of GoAstV infection, such as bilateral ureteral urate deposition, renal enlargement, and gallbladder urate deposition. Kidney tissues from the diseased geese were collected. The diseased tissues were cut into small pieces, added with steel beads, mixed with sterile PBS at a volume ratio of 1:5, and fully ground by a cryogenic grinder to obtain tissue homogenate. The homogenate was centrifuged at 8000 r/min for 5 min at 4°C. The supernatant was filtered and sterilized through a 0.22-µm diameter filter, mixed with penicillin-streptomycin at a final concentration of 2000 IU/mL, and stored at −80°C for later use. Using previously established methods (Li et al., 2023), we screened the potential existence of both GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2 in the collected samples and confirmed the presence of GoAstV-2.

Virus isolation & growth

To isolate GoAstV-2, tissue samples (kidney, spleen, liver) from infected geese were blended into a liquid mixture, spun at high speed, and filtered. The filtered liquid was injected into 10-day-old goose embryos (via the chorioallantoic membrane). Embryos were kept in an incubator (37.8°C, 55 % humidity) and checked daily. Embryos that died after 24 h or survived 5 days were chilled, and their fluids were collected. These fluids were tested for viruses and repeatedly passed into new embryos to grow the virus. After 5 passages, only GoAstV-2 was detected. To measure virus titers, the liquid was subjected to 10-fold serial dilution using sterile physiological saline, and subsequently inoculated into 11-day-old goose embryos at 8 dilutions (ranging from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁸), with five embryos inoculated per dilution. After 7 days, the lethal dose (ELD₅₀) was calculated using a standard method (Reed–Muench).

Genome sequencing of the JX01 strain

Viral RNA was extracted from the 5th-round embryo fluid using a commercial kit. Overlapping DNA fragments covering the entire virus genome were copied using a lab technique (RT-PCR) and previous designed primers. The DNA pieces were inserted into a cloning vector, and 3–5 copies of each fragment were sequenced for accuracy. The final genome was assembled using DNAStar software and compared to known Astrovirus sequences in public databases to identify unique features.

Publicly available whole genome of GoAstV

By December 2024, the complete genome sequences of GoAstV were retrieved from the NCBI GenBank database, along with their corresponding isolation dates and geographic locations.

Genome analysis

The phylogenetic tree of the complete genome nucleotide sequences was constructed using the neighbor-joining method with 1,000 bootstrap replications by the MEGA 7.0.14 software. The phylogeography analysis was performed BEAST X v10.5.0-beta5.

Results and discussion

Whole genome of the newly isolated GoAstV-2 JX01 strain

The newly isolated GoAstV-2 JX01 strain was sequenced using primers designed in prior study (Cen et al., 2023). Following assembly, the complete genome of the JX01 strain was determined to be 7,173 nucleotides (nt) in length, organized into a canonical astrovirus structure: a 13-nt 5′ untranslated region (UTR), followed by ORF1a (3,254 nt), ORF1b (1,550 nt), ORF2 (2,114 nt), and a 231-nt 3′UTR. The genome architecture and coding region lengths align with previously reported GoAstV-2 strains with high sequence identities from 98.2 % to 99.86 %, confirming high structural conservation. The complete genome sequence was deposited in the NCBI GenBank database under accession number MZ576222.

Genome comparison among different GoAstV strains

A systematic search identified 112 GoAstV whole genomes (isolated over ten Province from 2014 to 2024, Table 1) for comparative analysis. Genome lengths were highly conserved across strains, ranging from 6935 to 7336 bp. We first performed phylogenetic analysis based on the whole genome of GoAstV (Fig. 1). Consistent with previous studies (Ren et al., 2025), these GoAstV strains could be divided into two distinct clades, designated as GoAstV genotype 1 (GoAstV-1) and GoAstV genotype 2 (GoAstV-2). Besides, among the 112 strains analyzed, 17 clustered within GoAstV-1, reaffirming earlier reports that GoAstV-2 dominates in prevalence. Notably, GoAstV-1—first identified in 2014—has persisted across diverse provinces (e.g., Shanghai, Jiangxi, Jiangsu) through 2023, suggesting its sustained low-level circulation despite reduced detection rates compared to GoAstV-2. The persistent detection of GoAstV-1 over a decade and across multiple provinces underscores its ability to maintain circulation despite lower prevalence. This could reflect niche adaptation to specific hosts or environmental reservoirs, enabling long-term survival even in the absence of widespread outbreaks.

Intriguingly, phylogenetic topology revealed no clear correlation between genetic relatedness and isolation year or geographic origin. This phenomena was further confirmed by phylogeography analysis, which failed to report any significant association. The absence of spatiotemporal clustering in phylogenetic trees further supports the hypothesis that GoAstV transmission is not driven by localised outbreaks or recent introductions but rather by a stable, interconnected reservoir.

On the other hand, strains separated by large distances (e.g., Anhui and Guangdong) or temporal gaps (e.g., 2014 vs. 2023) occasionally grouped within the same subclade. This lack of spatiotemporal structure, coupled with the high genome-wide conservation (>97 % similarity) observed earlier, implies that GoAstV evolution is dominated by genetic stability rather than rapid diversification. Such stasis may reflect minimal adaptive selection pressure, potentially due to effective host adaptation or environmental persistence. The occasional grouping of geographically or temporally distant strains may indicate undetected transmission pathways, such as migratory bird movements or anthropogenic activities (e.g., poultry trade), which facilitate viral dispersal without leaving a strong genetic signature.

While focused on China, these findings have significant global implications. The observed genetic stability and evidence of cryptic transmission pathways suggest GoAstV could readily spread beyond endemic regions. This risk is amplified by the globalized poultry trade and overlapping migratory flyways connecting Asia to Europe, Africa, and beyond. The conserved genome architecture, particularly in replication machinery (ORF1a/ORF1b) and capsid proteins (ORF2), not only supports China's pursuit of broad-spectrum interventions but also offers universally viable targets for international vaccine/antiviral development. Our integrated spatiotemporal-genomic approach provides a transferable model for surveillance in other poultry-dense regions vulnerable to emergence.

Overall, the evolutionary stasis observed in GoAstV aligns with patterns seen in other avian astroviruses, where high-fidelity replication or purifying selection preserves essential genomic regions. However, the conservation of nonstructural proteins (ORF1a/ORF1b) and the capsid gene (ORF2) raises questions about whether antigenic stability contributes to the virus’s persistence in susceptible populations. Future studies should integrate antigenic profiling with genomic data to assess whether conserved regions correlate with immune evasion strategies or limitations in host immune recognition.

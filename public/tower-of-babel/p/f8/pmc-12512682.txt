Impact of vaccination on SARS-CoV-2 infections and long COVID symptoms: a cross-sectional study in China
Authors: Xiaolong Yan, Xin Zhao, Hao Wang, Yin Du, Li Liu, Jianhua Liu, Qi Wang, Sheng Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFollowing the adjustment of the dynamic zero-COVID-19 strategy, China has witnessed a rapid increase in COVID-19 cases. However, the relationship between diverse vaccination statuses and the spectrum of symptom presentation, disease severity, and long-term post-COVID conditions remains to be fully understood. This study aimed to assess the impact of vaccination on the incidence of SARS-CoV-2 infections, the manifestation of acute symptoms, and the occurrence of long-term symptoms post-infection within the context of China's revised pandemic prevention and control measures.MethodsThis study investigated 6,572 participants from the Yichang COVID-19 Antibody Longitudinal Survey (YC-CALS) in March 2023, focusing on demographic and health-related information, including vaccination history, infection status, and symptomatology. Univariate and multivariate analysis methods were used to determine the correlations between vaccination status and COVID-19 outcomes.ResultsAmong the surveyed participants, 85.91% reported positive for SARS-CoV-2. Of those who were positive, 96.79% experienced at least one common symptom, with fever (79.34%), cough (62.56%), and fatigue (60.59%) being the most reported symptoms. Despite the high incidence of symptoms, only 2.05% of those who tested positive needed hospitalization. Importantly, symptoms such as palpitations, chest pain, and shortness of breath persisted beyond 90 days in certain individuals. Booster shots significantly reduced the risk of specific symptoms including dyspnea, eye soreness and fatigue, and gastrointestinal discomfort (P < 0.05). Vaccination beyond 12 months significantly increased symptoms such as decreased sense of smell, palpitations, and chest distress (P < 0.05). Additionally, the study found a positive correlation between the timing of vaccination and the development of long COVID symptoms, with individuals vaccinated more than 12 months prior exhibiting a higher likelihood of enduring symptoms such as fatigue (OR = 1.218, P = 0.023) and headache dizziness (OR = 1.244, P = 0.038) for over one month.ConclusionsThe findings highlight the critical importance of vaccination in controlling the spread of COVID-19 and reducing symptoms and disease prognosis, providing important insights for future vaccine policy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11677-w.

Introduction

With the ongoing evolution of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) Pandemic, the world is facing the challenge of coexisting with the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). As of January 2024, over 700 million infections and more than 6.8 million deaths have been reported globally [1]. The virus's continuous mutations complicate vaccination strategies and public health responses [2–4]. Despite global vaccination efforts, the risks of infections and long COVID remain. In China, the relaxation of the zero-COVID policy in late 2022 triggered a large-scale infection surge [5–7]. This highlights regional disparities in pandemic control and vaccine efficacy, underscoring the need for research on post-vaccination infection symptoms and long-term effects.

With the widespread administration of COVID-19 vaccines, research on vaccine efficacy and long-term sequelae (Long COVID) has become vital. Current research shows that vaccines effectively prevent severe illness and death [8–10], but viral variants have contributed to rising infection rates [11, 12]. These infections can cause varying degrees of symptoms and, in some individuals, lead to Long COVID, characterized by persistent fatigue, cognitive disturbances, and other health issues [13–15]. Evidence suggests that vaccine timing, dosage, and type influence protection against variants and post-infection symptom severity [6, 15]. However, further studies are needed to explore these factors' impacts on specific populations, particularly in China.

Shortly after the adjustment of China's dynamic zero-COVID strategy, a large-scale survey of SARS-CoV-2 infection was conducted in the Yichang area of Hubei Province, China. This study aimed to analyze the impact of different vaccination statuses on SARS-CoV-2 infection rates, disease severity, acute symptom presentation, and long-term symptoms post-infection.

Methods

Study design

This study depended on the Yichang COVID-19 Antibody Longitudinal Survey (YC-CALS). Briefly, the project selected all 14 districts and counties in Yichang, Hubei Province, China, as designated survey locations (S1A figure). Firstly, a cross-sectional survey was conducted to retrospectively assess the prevalence of SARS-CoV-2 infections following the relaxation of strict COVID-19 control measures in China in December 2022. Secondly, a prospective cohort study was employed to follow up on the population's SARS-CoV-2 antibody levels. Inclusion criteria for study subjects: (i) age over 1 year, (ii) no contraindication to venipuncture, (iii) acceptance of informed consent.

Study participants

The cross-sectional survey was conducted from March 20 to 30, 2023. The target population included community-based populations, special groups (including medical workers, epidemic prevention and control management populations, and nursing home workers), as well as early clustered infected populations (a university in Yichang in September 2022), with a total of 6,875 individuals surveyed.

A stratified cluster sampling method was employed to survey community residents in Yichang. Initially, 1–2 communities were selected from each of the 14 districts using convenience sampling. Then, households within these communities were surveyed, with participants stratified by age groups (< 20 years, 20–39 years, 40–59 years, and ≥ 60 years), resulting in a total of 4,804 community residents surveyed. The survey of special groups was conducted with the assistance of relevant institutions, where questionnaires were distributed and collected directly within these institutions. A total of 1,768 individuals from special groups participated in the survey. Additionally, a retrospective survey was carried out for the early cluster outbreak population, with 303 individuals surveyed.

This study focused on SARS-CoV-2 infections after December 2022, following the relaxation of strict COVID-19 control measures. Consequently, the 303 individuals from the early cluster outbreak were excluded from the analysis. In total, 6,572 participants were included in the analysis (S1B figure). Statistical analysis showed that the age and sex distribution of the 6,572 participants did not significantly differ from the general population distribution in Yichang (P > 0.05) (S2 figure).

Questionnaire survey

The survey questionnaire collected information on general demographic characteristics (age, sex, height, weight, smoking status, etc.), SARS-CoV-2 infection characteristics after December 2022 (nucleic acid test results, antigen test results, onset time of symptoms, symptom types, symptom duration, medication use, medical visits, treatment details, etc.), COVID-19 vaccination history (vaccine doses, types of vaccines, vaccination dates, etc.), and underlying comorbidities (metabolic diseases, lung diseases, cardiovascular diseases, cancer, immunodeficiency diseases, etc.). Demographic and SARS-CoV-2 infection characteristics were primarily obtained through self-report from the participants. Information on medical behavior after SARS-CoV-2 infection was collected through self-report combined with the medical records provided by the participants. COVID-19 vaccination information was obtained through Yichang’s medical data platform, linked with participants' ID card information. Medical history was gathered through self-reports and diagnostic certificates or records provided by medical institutions. Detailed information on the survey questionnaire was provided in S1 Table.

Statistical analysis

The count data were described using frequency (n) and percentage (%). The Chi-square test was used to compare the basic characteristics between the SARS-CoV-2 positive and negative groups. The Chi-square test was also used to analyze the effects of different vaccination statuses (including vaccine dosage, vaccine type, and vaccination timing) on disease outcomes related to SARS-CoV-2 infection. Multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to assess the relationship between vaccine doses, vaccine type, and vaccination timing with the occurrence and duration of symptoms. To control for potential confounders, all models adjusted for age, sex, risk group, smoking status, body mass index (BMI), comorbidities, previous infections, vaccine dose, and vaccination timing. The results were presented as odds ratios (OR) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). P-values less than 0.05 were considered significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.3).

Results

Characteristics of participants

Baseline characteristics of the 6,572 participants were shown in Table 1 and S3 figure. A total of 5,646 (85.91%) reported SARS-CoV-2 positivity (positive antigen and/or nucleic acid tests and/or self-reported symptoms) (Table 1 and S3A figure). The age of participants was distributed in the 30–45, 45–59, and ≥ 60 age groups and the difference in age between SARS-CoV-2 positive and negative groups was statistically significant (P < 0.001) (Table 1 and S3B figure). The proportion of female participants was significantly higher than males (P < 0.001) (Table 1 and S3C figure), and the proportion of smokers in the SARS-CoV-2 positive group was slightly lower than the negative group (P < 0.001) (Table 1 and S3D figure). Sixty-two participants (0.94%) received one dose of the vaccine, 1,177 (17.91%) received two doses, 4,876 (74.19%) received three doses, and 394 (6.00%) received four doses (Table 1 and S3E figure). Most participants received three doses of inactivated vaccine, with 4659 individuals (70.89%). A total of 352 individuals (5.36%) received protein vaccines, and 17 individuals (2.59%) received adenoviral vector vaccines (S3J figure). The proportion of the population at high risk of exposure to SARS-CoV-2 in the SARS-CoV-2 positive group (healthcare workers, epidemic prevention and control management populations, and nursing home workers) was higher than the negative group (P < 0.001) (Table 1 and S3F figure). There was no difference in BMI between the two groups (P = 0.704) (Table 1 and S3G figure). The proportion of participants with comorbidities was approximately 12% higher in the SARS-CoV-2 positive group than in the negative group (P < 0.001) (Table 1 and S3H figure). Among the participants, 215 (3.27%) had a history of SARS-CoV-2 infection before December 2022, when China lifted the dynamic clearance policy (S3I figure). Symptoms of SARS-CoV-2 infection

Of the 5,646 SARS-CoV-2 positive participants, 5,465 (96.79%) self-reported symptoms and only 181 (3.21%) were asymptomatic (Fig. 1A). Of the symptomatic infected participants, 4,514 (82.60%) were using over-the-counter (OTC) medications for self-treatment (Fig. 1B), 820 (15.00%) were outpatients, and 112 (2.05%) were hospitalized (Fig. 1C). Further analysis of the hospitalized participants showed that 80 (71.43%) were hospitalized for less than 10 days and only 1 was hospitalized for more than 30 days (Fig. 1D). The most common symptoms in COVID-19 patients were fever (79.34%), cough (62.56%), fatigue (60.59%), pharyngeal discomfort (48.49%), full body soreness (45.54%), fear of cold shivering (37.13%), headache dizziness (35.72%), nasal congestion (28.67%), runny nose (26.15%) and decrease in taste (24.85%) (Fig. 1E). Persistent symptoms (medium- and long-term symptoms) were evaluated by analyzing the duration of symptoms in symptomatic infected individuals (Fig. 1G). The results showed that although most symptoms improved in the short term (< 7 or < 14 days), a certain percentage of symptoms lasted longer than 30 or 60 days, especially cough, shortness of breath, palpitations, decreased smell, decreased taste, and fatigue. In terms of long-term symptoms, those that lasted longer than 90 days were most prevalent in palpitations, chest pain, and shortness of breath.

Effect of vaccination doses on SARS-CoV-2 infection

The effects of vaccination doses on SARS-CoV-2 positivity rate, symptomatic or not, self-medication, medical visit, and hospital length of stay were shown in Fig. 2A-E. The results showed that the number of vaccination doses significantly affected the SARS-CoV-2 positivity rate (P < 0.001) (Fig. 2A). In particular, the positive rate gradually increased in participants who received 1–3 doses of vaccine, while the lowest positive rate was observed in participants who received 4 doses of vaccine. The proportion of symptomatic participants was similar across vaccine doses, and asymptomatic infections were not observed in unvaccinated individuals (P = 0.772) (Fig. 2B). The proportion of self-medication was similar in vaccinated and slightly lower in unvaccinated participants, but the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.062) (Fig. 2C). There was a difference in the proportion of outpatient visits and hospitalizations among participants with different vaccine doses (P = 0.006) (Fig. 2D), and interestingly, the proportion of hospitalizations was lowest among participants who received 1 and 4 doses. No association was observed between vaccination and length of hospital stay (P = 0.871) (Fig. 2E). The effects of vaccine combinations (doses and type) on SARS-CoV-2 positivity, presence of symptoms, self-medication, presence of clinic visits, and length of hospitalization are shown in S4 figure. The results showed that participants who received 3 doses of inactivated and 1 dose of protein vaccine had the lowest percentage of SARS-CoV-2 positivity, followed by participants who received 4 doses of inactivated vaccine (P < 0.001) (S4A figure). Multivariate logistic regression was also utilized to assess the relationship between vaccination doses and symptoms of infection. After adjusting for age, sex, and other factors, the results showed that different vaccination doses were negatively associated with different symptoms (Fig. 2F). Specifically, vaccination with 3 and 4 doses significantly reduced dyspnea (OR = 0.526, P = 0.009 and OR = 0.275, P = 0.002), eye soreness and fatigue (OR = 0.553, P = 0.026 and OR = 0.263, P = 0.003), and gastrointestinal discomfort (OR = 0.57, P = 0.03 and OR = 0.237, P = 0.001). Furthermore, vaccination with 3 or 4 doses significantly reduced the occurrence of shortness of breath, decrease in smell, chest distress, headache dizziness, and fever (OR < 1, P < 0.05). The results of the multivariate analysis showed that different vaccination combinations (doses and type) were significantly negatively associated with different symptoms (S4F figure). Specifically, compared with participants who received 2 doses of inactivated vaccine, receiving 3 doses of protein vaccine significantly reduced shortness of breath (OR = 0.370, P = 0.03), runny nose (OR = 0.293, P = 0.001), nasal congestion (OR = 0.296, P < 0.001), and eye pain and fatigue (OR = 0.184, P = 0.01). In addition, vaccination with 3 doses of inactivated vaccine combined with 1 dose of protein vaccine significantly reduced the decrease in smell (OR = 0.241, P = 0.007), decrease in taste (OR = 0.4, P = 0.032), and gastrointestinal discomfort (OR = 0.075, P = 0.014).

Effect of vaccination timing on SARS-CoV-2 infection

The effect of recent vaccination date on SARS-CoV-2 positivity, symptomatic or not, self-medication, clinic visits, and length of hospitalization were shown in Fig. 3A-E. SARS-CoV-2 positivity was lower among participants vaccinated within 6 months (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3A). Vaccination timing was not associated with symptom status (P = 0.087) (Fig. 3B) and was associated with self-medication (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3C). Additionally, the results of the multivariate analysis showed that the different vaccination times were positively associated with different symptoms (Fig. 3F). Compared to participants vaccinated within 3 months, vaccination beyond 12 months significantly increased decrease in smell (OR = 1.912, P = 0.002), palpitations (OR = 2.252, P = 0.039), chest distress (OR = 1.649, P = 0.025), decrease in taste (OR = 1.665, P = 0. 003), fatigue (OR = 1.454, P = 0.006), eye pain and fatigue (OR = 2.031, P = 0.033), full body soreness (OR = 1.456, P = 0.006), gastrointestinal discomfort (OR = 2.476, P = 0.006), headache dizziness (OR = 1.372, P = 0.035), and fever (OR = 1.458, P = 0.019).

Effect of different vaccination statues on duration of COVID-19 symptoms

There was a correlation between the different vaccination statuses (doses, type, and timing) and the duration of symptoms (Table 2). The results showed that vaccination with 3 or 4 doses significantly reduced cough (OR = 0.707, P = 0.049), decrease in smell (OR = 0.268, P = 0.009), and eye soreness and fatigue (OR = 0.21, P = 0.022) more than 1 week. Vaccination with 3 doses of protein vaccine significantly reduced cough more than 1 week (OR = 0.459, P = 0.019). Compared with the vaccination within 12 months, more than 12 months significantly increased the decrease in smell (OR = 1.854, P < 0.001), palpitations (OR = 2.607, P = 0.004), decrease in taste (OR = 1.348, P = 0.024) and headache dizziness (OR = 1.57, P = 0.013). Vaccination with 3 or 4 doses significantly reduced the decrease in smell (OR = 0.237, P = 0.018) and joint pain (OR = 0.253, P = 0.019) for more than 2 weeks. Vaccination more than 12 months prior significantly increased the likelihood of decreased smell (OR = 1.833, P = 0.002), decreased taste (OR = 1.462, P = 0.032) and full body soreness (OR = 1.589, P = 0.038) lasting more than 2 weeks. Additionally, it significantly increased fatigue (OR = 1.218, P = 0.023) and headache/dizziness (OR = 1.244, P = 0.038) for more than one month. Discussion

This study analyzed vaccine administration and SARS-CoV-2 infection in 6,572 participants in the context of China's relaxation of strict COVID-19 control measures in December 2022. Over 98% of participants had received at least two vaccine doses, with 85.91% reporting SARS-CoV-2 positivity. Among the positive cases, 96.79% reported at least one common symptom, with fever (79.34%), cough (62.56%), and fatigue (60.59%) being the most prevalent. The study also revealed that symptoms such as palpitations, chest pain, and shortness of breath persisted for more than 90 days in some individuals. Notably, receiving three and four doses of the vaccine significantly reduced the incidence of symptoms like dyspnea, eye soreness and fatigue, and gastrointestinal discomfort. Vaccination beyond 12 months significantly increased symptoms such as decreased sense of smell, palpitations, and chest distress. Moreover, the interval between vaccination and infection was positively correlated with the occurrence of long COVID symptoms, with individuals vaccinated over 12 months prior more likely to exhibit persistent symptoms, especially fatigue and headache dizziness, lasting more than one month. These findings offer crucial insights into the epidemiological characteristics of breakthrough SARS-CoV-2 infections, the long-term efficacy of vaccination, and its impact on the persistence of long COVID symptoms.

Despite the relatively lower severity of SARS-CoV-2 infections, the prevalence of persistent symptoms among many patients highlights the necessity for an in-depth understanding and strategy to address the long-term impacts of this disease. Among participants reporting positive for SARS-CoV-2, 85.91% manifested at least one symptom, yet only 2.05% required hospitalization. This proportion was significantly lower compared to the hospitalization rates observed in the early severe cases of COVID-19 [16, 17], reflecting the mitigating effects of widespread vaccination and viral strain evolution [18, 19]. Recent studies have shown that during the Omicron variant pandemic, vaccination, particularly booster doses, as well as previous infections, still play a role in controlling new infections [20, 21]. In the context of the global Omicron outbreak, many individuals who have been vaccinated or previously infected with COVID-19 still experience breakthrough infections. These breakthrough infections are primarily characterized by mild symptoms such as fever, cough, fatigue, and sore throat. This is mainly due to the reduced virulence of the Omicron Strain, as well as the presence of a certain level of immune response and immune memory within the population. Notably, while most symptoms resolved within two weeks, a considerable proportion of participants reported persistent medium to long-term symptoms, including cough, shortness of breath, palpitations, diminished taste and smell, and fatigue, with some individuals experiencing these symptoms for over 90 days. The observed persistence of these symptoms aligns generally with the high frequency of prolonged COVID-19 symptoms attributed to the BA.5 and BF.7 variants [14, 15]. These observations suggest that the recovery period post-SARS-CoV-2 infection, even in the context of extensive vaccination, may be longer than that of typical viral colds [22]. These findings highlight the need for further research into recovery mechanisms and symptom resolution after SARS-CoV-2 infection, as well as the importance of focusing on patients' quality of life and long-term rehabilitation strategies for managing COVID-19.

This study further corroborates the significant role of multiple vaccine doses and the intervals between vaccinations in mitigating the severity of COVID-19. The findings indicate that individuals vaccinated with four doses exhibited lower rates of SARS-CoV-2 positivity and hospitalization, aligning with prior research [23]. In addition, individuals vaccinated within the previous six months had significantly lower rates of positivity and hospitalization. Further analysis showed that participants who were hospitalized more than six months after vaccination had longer hospital stays. This observation is consistent with the notion that vaccine efficacy wanes over time [24]. Multiple studies have shown that booster immunity provides stable protection against severe COVID-19 for up to one year, but the protection against symptomatic infection significantly declines over time after the last dose [25–27]. This highlighted the critical role of booster vaccinations in reducing the burden of COVID-19 on healthcare resources [28]. Importantly, the study also found that different vaccine doses and types significantly improved several COVID-19 symptoms, such as dyspnea, eye pain, and fatigue, which is consistent with previous findings [6, 15]. Apart from vaccine dosage and type, the results suggest that the interval between vaccination and infection also plays a role in alleviating COVID-19 symptoms. A possible reason is that timely vaccination can more effectively enhance the immune system's defense against the virus, thereby reducing the occurrence of symptoms [29]. As time passes, however, the immune protection gradually decreases, especially against symptomatic infections, making timely booster doses an important strategy for mitigating COVID-19 symptoms.

Timely vaccination, especially booster doses, helps reduce the duration of COVID-19 symptoms and alleviates long-term effects. The study findings indicate that receiving three or four doses of the vaccine significantly reduced symptoms such as cough, loss of smell, eye pain, and fatigue lasting more than 1–2 weeks or even longer. This aligns with previous research on the impact of booster doses on the long-term symptoms of breakthrough infections [15, 30]. Individuals who received three doses of a protein-based vaccine experienced a notable reduction in the duration of cough, suggesting that different vaccine types may have varying effects on the alleviation of specific symptoms. Heterologous booster vaccination generally induces a stronger immune response compared to homologous boosters [31]. Given that inactivated vaccines were primarily used in early vaccinations in China, other vaccines, such as adenovirus-vectored vaccines or recombinant subunit vaccines, could be considered for future boosters. Furthermore, individuals vaccinated more than 12 months ago exhibited significantly longer durations of symptoms such as loss of smell, palpitations, loss of taste, fatigue, and headache dizziness. This suggests that delayed vaccination may have an adverse impact on the experience of long COVID symptoms [32, 33]. These findings were of significant value for public health strategies, particularly in optimizing vaccination schedules to mitigate the long-term impacts of COVID-19.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, it is unable to establish a causal relationship between vaccination and the alleviation of the severity and duration of COVID-19 symptoms due to the cross-sectional nature of the study. Although logistic regression analysis was employed to adjust for potential confounders, unmeasured variables could still affect the observed associations. Secondly, reliance on participant-reported symptoms and duration may introduce recall bias, potentially affecting the accuracy of study findings. Additionally, the focus on the Yichang area of Hubei Province, China, might limit the generalizability of our results. Local public health policies and vaccination campaigns may not fully represent the global diversity of SARS-CoV-2 transmission and vaccine distribution scenarios. Finally, due to the rapid evolution of SARS-CoV-2 and the frequent emergence of new variants, there is a lack of sufficient analysis of the clinical characteristics of populations infected with new variants in the post-pandemic period. The findings of this study reflect the situation only up until March 2023, and therefore, the conclusions are time-sensitive. Future research will provide important insights into the timely updates on changes in vaccine efficacy, especially concerning the waning effects over time. Nevertheless, this study provided valuable evidence for the ongoing discussion on COVID-19 vaccination strategies and their role in mitigating the impact of the pandemic.

Conclusion

Following the relaxation of strict COVID-19 control policies in December 2022, SARS-CoV-2 breakthrough infections in the study population were generally mild. However, persistent long COVID symptoms suggest lasting health impacts. Booster doses and different vaccine types may help alleviate some symptoms, and regular vaccination can prevent certain long COVID outcomes. Public health authorities should monitor viral evolution and adjust vaccination strategies to effectively address variants and reduce COVID-19’s public health impact.

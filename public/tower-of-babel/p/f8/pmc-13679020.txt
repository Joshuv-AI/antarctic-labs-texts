Efficacy and safety of cetuximab-irinotecan rechallenge versus regorafenib in ras wild-type metastatic colorectal cancer: a single-center exploratory randomized trial
Authors: Hao Chen, Tao Jiang, Han Wang, Jianwei zheng, Bin Du, Xiaojiao Weng, Na Yao, Yingjiao Zhu, Qing Liu, Fangyu Lin, Xinli Wang, Xiaoyan Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnti-epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) antibodies are widely used in the treatment of RAS wild-type metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) across various lines of therapy, including after disease progression. However, evidence from randomized controlled trials regarding the efficacy of cetuximab rechallenge in patients who initially responded to prior cetuximab but subsequently progressed after a cetuximab-free interval remains limited.MethodsThis single-center, prospective, exploratory randomized trial (ChiCTR1900026961) evaluated cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge versus regorafenib in patients with RAS wild-type, microsatellite-stable mCRC who had previously responded to then progressed off cetuximab. Progression-free survival (PFS) was the primary endpoint, with overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and safety as secondary endpoints. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify potential prognostic factors, and an exploratory nomogram was developed for hypothesis generation.ResultsAmong 68 randomized patients, cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge was associated with longer median PFS and OS than regorafenib. The DCR and ORR were numerically higher in the rechallenge group. Median PFS was 5.5 months versus 2.6 months, and median OS was 18.8 months versus 10.4 months. Adverse events in the cetuximab group were predominantly grade 1–2 and manageable with supportive care. Univariate and multivariate analyses identified age, ECOG status, and cetuximab-free interval as potential prognostic factors. An exploratory nomogram was constructed based on these factors.ConclusionsThese findings suggest that cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge may be associated with clinical benefit and acceptable toxicity in selected patients with RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC. The exploratory nomogram requires further validation. These results support further investigation of rechallenge strategies and biomarker-guided approaches in advanced CRC.Trial registrationThis study was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on October 27, 2019 (registration number: ChiCTR1900026961).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16491-4.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Cetuximab, Rechallenge therapy, Regorafenib, Progression-free survival (PFS)

Background

Colorectal cancer is the third most common cancer worldwide with more than 1.9 million new cases and approximately 903,859 deaths in 2022 [1]. Approximately 50% of patients develop metastases, with 25% presenting at advanced stages where curative surgery is often unfeasible. Recent studies have shown that epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitors are effective in treating patients with Rat sarcoma proto-oncogene(RAS) wild-type metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) [2–4]. The EGFR pathway plays a critical role in controlling cancer cell growth and survival. Monoclonal anti-EGFR antibodies, such as cetuximab and panitumumab, can block downstream MAPK signaling and are used in combination with or after standard chemotherapy [5, 6]. However, constitutive activation of the EGFR pathway through oncogenic mutations (KRAS/NRAS exons 2/3/4 or BRAF) occurs in 55–60% of mCRC cases, conferring intrinsic resistance to anti-EGFR therapies. Current clinical guidelines therefore mandate confirmation of RAS/BRAF wild-type status prior to initiating anti-EGFR treatment in mCRC patients [6–8].

Mutations in the MAPK signaling pathway have been associated with resistance to anti-EGFR therapy. However, the precise mechanism underlying the development of resistant clones during treatment remains elusive, primarily due to substantial intra- and interpatient variability. Utilizing ctDNA testing facilitates the noninvasive detection of various molecular alterations such as KRAS, NRAS, BRAF V600E, MAP2K1, and EGFR ectodomain mutations, which drive the evolution of resistance to anti-EGFR therapy [9]. The current consensus suggests that subclonal mutations resistant to therapy proliferate, outcompeting treatment-sensitive cells, thereby leading to resistance development and disease progression [10]. Recent research indicates that upon discontinuation of anti-EGFR therapy, sensitive clones may exhibit partial recovery, shedding light on the potential efficacy of rechallenging with anti-EGFR therapy [10, 11].

The clinical utility of anti-EGFR rechallenge in RAS wild-type mCRC remains an area of active investigation, with emerging data supporting its potential in molecularly selected populations [12]. Pioneering trials (CRICKET, CAVE, CHRONOS, REVERCE) have demonstrated promising activity of anti-EGFR rechallenge in molecularly selected populations, with the CHRONOS study reporting a 30% objective response rate (ORR) to panitumumab monotherapy in ctDNA-confirmed wild-type patients. These findings not only underscore the clinical feasibility of rechallenge strategies but also highlight the critical role of ctDNA in optimizing patient selection [13–15]. Furthermore, although some studies have explored the prognostic differences between anti-EGFR therapy and regorafenib, there is still a lack of prospective studies directly comparing the cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge regimen with the standard third-line agent, regorafenib, in a head-to-head manner.

Third-line and later-line treatments such as regorafenib, fruquintinib, and trifluridine/tipiracil have provided survival benefits for patients with refractory mCRC, although the magnitude of benefit remains limited in this setting, with median progression-free survival (mPFS) ranging from 1.9 to 3.7 months in placebo-controlled trials (FRESCO-2, CORRECT, TERRA) [16–18]. At the time of study design and initiation, regorafenib was an available and guideline-supported later-line treatment option at our institution. Therefore, we conducted this prospective comparative study to directly compare the efficacy and safety of cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge versus regorafenib in RAS wild-type mCRC patients who had achieved sustained clinical benefit (PFS ≥ 6 months) from first-line cetuximab-containing therapy and subsequently progressed.

Methods

Study Population

This prospective, exploratory controlled trial enrolled patients with histologically confirmed RAS wild-type mCRC. Additionally, in the experimental arm, baseline plasma samples were collected from consenting patients specifically for exploratory ctDNA analysis. ctDNA testing was exploratory and was not mandated as an eligibility criterion or used for treatment allocation. Key eligibility criteria included: Achieved disease control with first-line cetuximab plus chemotherapy and a PFS of at least 6 months during this treatment; an anti-EGFR drug–free interval of 3 or more months during second-line therapy; Had ≥ 1 measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1. In this study, anti-EGFR rechallenge was operationally defined as re-exposure to cetuximab after prior disease control with first-line cetuximab-containing therapy, subsequent discontinuation of anti-EGFR therapy, an anti-EGFR-free interval of at least 3 months during intervening non–anti-EGFR treatment, and documented disease progression before rechallenge initiation. The trial was carried out in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. The protocol was approved by the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry and the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University. All participants provided written informed consent.

Randomization and masking

In this exploratory study, patients were randomly assigned to two treatment groups. The randomization sequence was generated by a computer using a random number generator. The allocation information was sealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes by a researcher who was not involved in patient enrollment or outcome assessment. After confirming that a patient met all eligibility criteria, a designated clinician, who was not involved in subsequent outcome evaluations, opened the next sequentially numbered envelope to reveal the treatment assignment. Due to the inherent differences in the treatment regimens (intravenous cetuximab plus irinotecan versus oral regorafenib), blinding of participants and care providers was not feasible. However, to minimize detection bias, all outcome assessors were blinded to the treatment group allocation throughout the study. No block randomization or stratification was used because of the exploratory nature and limited sample size of the trial. This study is reported in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines.

Procedures

The experimental group received cetuximab (500 mg/m² intravenous infusion on Day 1) and irinotecan (180 mg/m² intravenous infusion on Day 1) on a biweekly schedule. The control group received regorafenib orally (120 mg daily from Day 1 to Day 21) in a 28-day cycle (Fig. 1). No protocol-defined crossover between the two treatment arms was planned after disease progression. When tissue or blood samples were available, selected PRESSING-related molecular alterations were assessed exploratorily. This assessment was not mandated for all patients and was not used as a prespecified exclusion criterion, stratification factor, or treatment-allocation variable. These alterations included HER2/MET amplification via in-situ hybridization; ALK/ROS/TRKs/RET fusions via immunohistochemistry and RNA sequencing; HER2/PI3K/PTEN/RAS mutations via next-generation sequencing; and microsatellite instability via polymerase chain reaction

Outcome assessments and statistical analysis

This open-label, single-center, exploratory clinical study (ChiCTR1900026961) assessed the efficacy of cetuximab-based rechallenge in patients with RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC. The primary endpoint was PFS, defined as the time from randomization to disease progression (RECIST v1.1) or death from any cause. Given the typically modest ORR associated with cetuximab-based rechallenge, PFS was selected as the primary endpoint to better capture disease control. Overall survival (OS) was a secondary endpoint. Secondary endpoints included OS, disease control rate (DCR, defined as CR + PR + SD), ORR, defined as CR + PR, and quality of life (QoL; EORTC QLQ-C30). Antitumor efficacy was evaluated per RECIST v1.1 criteria via independent central review with blinded imaging adjudication.Adverse events (AEs) were primarily evaluated using the NCI-CTC AE 5.0 standard.

Disease evaluations, including contrast-enhanced CT/MRI and hematologic profiling, were performed every 6 weeks (± 7 days) per protocol. Treatment continuation was permitted for patients demonstrating: Complete response (CR), Partial response (PR), Stable disease (SD), treatment discontinuation criteria included: Radiologically confirmed progressive disease (PD), Grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Patient withdrawal of consent or Investigator-determined clinical progression.

Although PFS was prespecified as the primary endpoint, the original sample-size estimation was based on DCR. At the time of protocol development, robust historical hazard-ratio estimates directly comparing cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge with regorafenib were unavailable. Given the exploratory nature of this randomized comparison, DCR was considered a clinically relevant early efficacy measure for estimating a feasible sample size.

Based on previous data and clinical assumptions, the DCR was expected to be 40% in the regorafenib group and 80% in the rechallenge group. With a two-sided α of 0.05 and 90% power, 54 evaluable patients were required. Assuming a 10% rate of loss to follow-up or unevaluable cases, the target sample size was 60 patients, determined using PASS 2023 (Version 23.0.2). PFS was analyzed as the prespecified primary efficacy endpoint, whereas OS was analyzed as a secondary endpoint.

Statistical analyses employed SPSS version 25.0 and R version 4.2.1. Categorical variables were assessed using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests. Univariate Cox regression was applied to each variable, with those showing P < 0.01 proceeding to multivariate Cox regression to identify independent predictors of PFS in the rechallenge treatment group. Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank tests evaluated OS and PFS. All tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 denoting statistical significance.

Results

Between May 1, 2020, and May 31, 2024, 75 patients were screened, and 68 were randomized: 35 received cetuximab with chemotherapy, while 33 were given regorafenib. Initially, all patients achieved disease control with first-line cetuximab and chemotherapy but later experienced progression. They again progressed following a second-line treatment without cetuximab (≥ 3 months post-cetuximab). All had measurable lesions per RECIST 1.1 criteria. Follow-up concluded on January 31, 2025.

Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of patients in the two treatment groups, indicating a balanced distribution. All patients had microsatellite-stable (MSS) tumors with wild-type KRAS, NRAS, and BRAF, and had progressed after at least two prior treatments. In the rechallenge group, 23 patients (65.7%) underwent circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis before treatment. Molecular profiling revealed TP53 mutations in 19 patients (82.6%) and APC mutations in 18 patients (78.2%). Less common alterations included SMAD4 mutations (n = 4), EGFR amplifications (n = 1), EGFR mutations (n = 1), BRCA2 amplifications (n = 2), BRCA2 mutations (n = 1), PIK3CA mutations (n = 1), and FGFR1 amplifications (n = 5) (Fig. 2A). Notably, the mPFS for patients with TP53 mutations was similar to those with APC mutations (TP53 mutations mPFS vs. APC mutations mPFS = 3.5 months vs. 3.5 months).

Among the 68 patients analyzed, no grade 4 adverse events occurred. In the regorafenib group, 33% experienced grade 1–2 gastrointestinal reactions, with bone marrow suppression, hand-foot skin reaction (HFSR)/palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome (PPES), and fatigue also common. In the cetuximab rechallenge group, the prevalent AEs were acneiform rash (25.7%), leukopenia (14.3%), liver/kidney function impairment (11.4%), thrombocytopenia (8.6%), and mucositis (8.6%). All AEs were manageable with symptomatic treatment, and no treatment-related deaths occurred (Table 2). Regarding treatment management, no patient in the cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge group discontinued treatment because of adverse events. In the regorafenib group, 5 of 33 patients (15.2%) required treatment interruption, dose adjustment, or clinical reassessment due to grade 2–3 adverse events; subsequent evaluation showed disease progression in these patients, and therefore these cases were not attributed solely to adverse-event-related treatment discontinuation. A comprehensive subgroup analysis was conducted to identify the optimal population for rechallenge treatment. In the best overall response analysis, the cetuximab rechallenge regimen achieved a DCR of 68.6% and an ORR of 8.6%. Longitudinal treatment-course assessment showed that 3 patients (8.6%) achieved PR during treatment, 22 patients (62.9%) had at least one SD assessment, 31 patients (88.6%) ultimately experienced disease progression, and 4 patients (11.4%) remained on treatment at data cutoff (Fig. 2B). Significant differences were observed between cohorts based on age, cetuximab-free interval, and primary/metastatic lesion resection status, using a 6-month progression-free interval as a reference (Supplementary Table 1).

QoL was planned as a secondary endpoint using the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire. However, complete longitudinal QoL data were not successfully collected for most patients, and the available partial data were insufficient for reliable analysis. Therefore, QoL results were not formally analyzed.

Univariate Cox regression analysis of clinical parameters and PFS identified key predictors for favorable cetuximab rechallenge responses: age under 60, ECOG score of 1, cetuximab-free interval exceeding 12 months, and prior resection of the primary tumor or local metastasis treatment. Multivariate Cox regression confirmed age, ECOG score, and cetuximab-free interval as independent prognostic factors (Fig. 3C to G, Supplementary Table 2). These parameters informed the development of a nomogram to predict rechallenge therapy efficacy in advanced colorectal cancer (Fig. 4A). The apparent AUC values for 3-, 6-, and 12-month PFS were 0.803, 0.878, and 0.873, respectively. After 1,000 bootstrap resamples for internal validation, the bootstrap-corrected AUC values were 0.784, 0.861, and 0.870, respectively. The apparent C-index was 0.768, and the bootstrap-corrected C-index was 0.754 (Figure S1). Bootstrap calibration curves suggested acceptable agreement between predicted and observed PFS probabilities (Fig. 4D).

Discussion

Despite the encouraging outcomes of combination chemotherapy regimens for mCRC, progression is unavoidable after two lines of two-agent chemotherapy [19]. Biologic agents have shown limited efficacy in enhancing OS. Consequently, rechallenge strategies have attracted increasing attention as a potential later-line therapeutic approach. Regorafenib is one of the guideline-supported later-line treatment options for refractory mCRC, although the optimal sequencing of later-line therapies remains uncertain, given that many pivotal studies were conducted against placebo rather than active treatment comparators [6, 8, 20]. Recent real-world data have also suggested that the efficacy of regorafenib in metastatic colorectal cancer may vary according to molecular context, including RAS mutation status, further highlighting the heterogeneity of later-line treatment outcomes [21]. In the present study, regorafenib was selected as the comparator because, at the time of study design and initiation, it was an available, guideline-supported, and clinically relevant later-line treatment option at our institution. Due to the timing of study initiation, mature evidence and routine clinical use of newer later-line strategies, including trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab, were not yet fully incorporated into the study design. In addition, the availability and routine use of fruquintinib and other later-line options varied during the enrollment period. Therefore, regorafenib represents a clinically appropriate comparator for the study period, although it should not be regarded as the only current standard-of-care option.

This study suggests that irinotecan plus cetuximab rechallenge as third-line treatment may be a safe and feasible option for patients with RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC who previously exhibited clinical response to cetuximab plus cytotoxic agents as first-line chemotherapy. The rechallenge therapy was associated with a longer mPFS compared with the regorafenib group (5.5 vs. 2.6 months). At the final follow-up, four patients were still receiving maintenance therapy. This finding may indicate the potential for sustained disease control in a subset of patients receiving rechallenge therapy. Importantly, these findings were derived from a prospective, randomized, head-to-head comparative study, providing valuable direct comparative evidence beyond earlier single-arm investigations.

The terminology of anti-EGFR retreatment requires clarification. In this study, our cohort represents a clinically defined anti-EGFR rechallenge population, characterized by prior disease control with cetuximab-containing therapy, discontinuation of anti-EGFR treatment, an anti-EGFR-free interval with intervening non–anti-EGFR therapy, and documented progression before cetuximab re-exposure. This definition differs from anti-EGFR continuation beyond progression and should also be distinguished from strictly ctDNA-guided molecular rechallenge.

Within this framework, the present study should be interpreted as an exploratory randomized head-to-head comparison that complements previous anti-EGFR rechallenge studies. Our cohort is clinically closest to CRICKET [22], which also evaluated irinotecan plus cetuximab rechallenge in patients who had previously benefited from cetuximab-containing therapy. However, CRICKET was a single-arm phase 2 study, whereas our trial compared cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge directly with an active, guideline-supported later-line comparator, regorafenib. Therefore, although the sample size was limited and the findings require further validation, this head-to-head design provides clinically relevant prospective comparative data regarding the potential role of cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge relative to a non–anti-EGFR later-line option in selected patients.

CHRONOS differed from our study by using prospective ctDNA-based selection and panitumumab monotherapy, representing a more strictly molecularly guided rechallenge strategy [14]. VELO evaluated panitumumab plus trifluridine/tipiracil as an anti-EGFR rechallenge-containing combination strategy in refractory RAS wild-type mCRC, differing from both the panitumumab monotherapy approach in CHRONOS and the cetuximab plus irinotecan regimen used in our study [11]. REVERCE primarily addressed treatment sequencing between regorafenib and cetuximab rather than anti-EGFR rechallenge after prior cetuximab benefit [15]. Thus, these studies address related but distinct clinical questions: CHRONOS highlights the value of molecular selection, VELO explores an alternative anti-EGFR rechallenge-containing combination, REVERCE informs later-line sequencing, and the present study adds exploratory randomized head-to-head comparative data for a clinically defined cetuximab–irinotecan rechallenge strategy. The relatively low ORR observed in our study should also be interpreted in the context of differences from earlier single-arm cetuximab retreatment studies, some of which included both reintroduction and clinically defined rechallenge cases [23].

Studies such as FRESCO-2 [16], CORRECT [17], and CONCUR [24] have demonstrated that later-line agents, including fruquintinib and regorafenib, can improve survival compared to a placebo following two or more prior treatments. Our research suggests that rechallenged cetuximab-based therapy may be viable and well-tolerated in patients with RAS wild-type mCRC. The mPFS in the rechallenge cohort was 5.5 months, compared with 2.6 months in the regorafenib cohort, suggesting a potential progression-free survival benefit with rechallenge therapy. The mOS was 18.8 months in the rechallenge cohort and 10.4 months in the regorafenib cohort; however, this OS difference should be interpreted cautiously given the exploratory nature of the study, limited sample size, and potential influence of subsequent therapies. Notably, four patients in the rechallenge cohort were still receiving maintenance treatment at the last follow-up, suggesting that a subset of patients may achieve sustained disease control with this strategy.

Subgroup analyses identified several baseline characteristics associated with favorable treatment outcomes. Factors associated with improved outcomes in the cetuximab rechallenge cohort included age < 60 years, ECOG score = 1, cetuximab-free interval > 12 months, and prior resection of the primary tumor or local treatment of metastases. The correlation between an extended cetuximab-free interval and better outcomes implies a time-dependent decline of resistant clones, aligning with previous rechallenge studies. Consistent with previous studies emphasizing the prognostic relevance of ECOG performance status and metastatic burden in metastatic colorectal or rectal cancer, our subgroup analysis also suggested that baseline clinical factors may influence outcomes in the later-line setting [25]. An exploratory predictive model for PFS in the cetuximab rechallenge cohort was developed based on candidate prognostic factors. Clinical prediction models have been increasingly used to support risk stratification and individualized follow-up in colorectal cancer, including postoperative oligometastatic CRC settings [26]. Compared to the CRICKET trial, our stricter patient selection criteria—requiring both a prior response to cetuximab and a defined washout period—may partly explain the higher DCR observed in our study (68.6% vs. 54%). Overall, cetuximab-based rechallenge has shown activity in selected patient cohorts, particularly those with prior cetuximab sensitivity and favorable clinical features [22]. The availability of cetuximab rechallenge further emphasizes the significance of continued monitoring and individualized treatment approaches in late-line settings, as it may offer an alternative therapeutic option for appropriately selected patients.

AEs with cetuximab rechallenge were mainly grade 1–2 and manageable with supportive care. No patient in the rechallenge group discontinued treatment because of adverse events. Regorafenib had a distinct toxicity profile, including gastrointestinal reactions, myelosuppression, hand–foot skin reaction/palmar–plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome, fatigue, and hypertension, which required close monitoring and, in selected cases, treatment interruption or dose adjustment.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center exploratory randomized study with a limited sample size. Although PFS was prespecified as the primary endpoint, the sample size was estimated using DCR rather than a PFS-based hazard-ratio assumption. Therefore, the observed PFS difference provides clinically meaningful prospective comparative evidence, but should be interpreted in the context of an exploratory randomized trial rather than a confirmatory survival-powered study. OS was a secondary endpoint and should be interpreted more cautiously because it may have been influenced by post-progression therapies and the limited sample size. Although no protocol-defined crossover between treatment arms was planned, detailed post-progression treatment information was not systematically collected because many patients continued subsequent care at local hospitals after progression. Therefore, potential imbalances in subsequent systemic therapies could not be assessed or adjusted for, and treatment-adjusted sensitivity analyses for OS were not feasible.

Second, the control arm consisted of regorafenib alone. Although regorafenib remains a guideline-supported later-line option, contemporary later-line treatment strategies have expanded to include trifluridine/tipiracil with or without bevacizumab, fruquintinib, and biomarker-guided therapies. Therefore, our results cannot directly determine the comparative efficacy of cetuximab rechallenge versus these newer or alternative later-line options. Larger multicenter trials incorporating contemporary comparators and prospective biomarker-guided selection are warranted.

Third, ctDNA testing was not mandatory for all patients, and serial ctDNA monitoring was not systematically performed during treatment or at progression. Because ctDNA testing was optional and not randomized, ctDNA-tested and non-tested patients were not compared as prespecified biomarker-defined subgroups. Therefore, this study should be interpreted as a clinically defined rechallenge trial with exploratory molecular profiling in a subset of patients, rather than a strictly ctDNA-guided molecular rechallenge study. Emergent resistance alterations could not be comprehensively assessed. PRESSING-related molecular assessment was exploratory and was not systematically prespecified for all patients. Therefore, no formal efficacy analysis according to PRESSING status was performed, and the predictive value of PRESSING biomarkers could not be evaluated in this cohort. Future studies should incorporate prospective ctDNA-guided selection, longitudinal molecular monitoring, and standardized PRESSING assessment to better define the population most likely to benefit from anti-EGFR rechallenge. In addition, although QoL assessment using EORTC QLQ-C30 was planned, complete longitudinal QoL data were not available for most patients, and the limited available data were of insufficient quality for reliable analysis. Therefore, QoL outcomes could not be reported, which represents an additional limitation of this study.

Recent studies on combination therapies may further broaden the treatment landscape for refractory RAS wild-type mCRC. A notable phase II study [27] reported mPFS of 7.3 months and mOS of 17.4 months with a combination of cetuximab, irinotecan, and PD-1 inhibitors in mismatch repair-proficient/RAS wild-type mCRC, indicating potential synergistic effects. Our ongoing research with cetuximab and camrelizumab may also help explore additional later-line treatment options.

Overall, cetuximab rechallenge may represent a clinically meaningful later-line strategy for selected RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC patients with prior cetuximab sensitivity and favorable clinical features. Further integration of ctDNA monitoring, PRESSING biomarkers, and prospective biomarker-guided selection may help refine the population most likely to benefit.

Conclusion

This single-center exploratory randomized trial suggests that cetuximab plus irinotecan rechallenge may be associated with longer PFS than regorafenib in selected patients with RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC who previously benefited from cetuximab-based therapy. A numerically longer OS was also observed, but this finding should be interpreted cautiously because detailed post-progression treatment data were not systematically available. The regimen showed an acceptable and manageable safety profile. Age, ECOG status, and cetuximab-free interval were identified as potential prognostic factors, and an exploratory nomogram was constructed to support further hypothesis generation. These findings provide prospective head-to-head comparative evidence suggesting cetuximab rechallenge as a potential later-line strategy for appropriately selected patients, and warrant validation in larger multicenter trials with prospective biomarker-guided selection.

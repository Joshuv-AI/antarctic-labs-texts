Mapping the global landscape of research on immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated cardiotoxicity: A quantitative and visualized bibliometric study (2016–2025)
Authors: Tiecheng Liu, Xiangnv Meng, Yongsheng Li, Fu Mi, Xi Yang, Sha Sha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have transformed oncologic practice, but their expanding use has heightened concern about potentially fatal cardiovascular toxicity. This bibliometric study mapped the global research landscape and temporal evolution of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity from 2016 to 2025. Records were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection on January 1, 2026. After excluding letters, conference abstracts, editorials, and other non-research items, 1,127 original articles and reviews were analyzed. A thesaurus file standardized synonyms, abbreviations, spelling variants, and singular–plural forms before keyword co-occurrence analysis. VOSviewer and CiteSpace were used to evaluate publication trends, collaboration networks, citation structures, keyword clusters, temporal patterns, and burst terms. The corpus involved 67 countries, 2,430 institutions, and 8,842 authors. Annual output increased from 9 publications in 2016 to 236 in 2025, representing a 26.2-fold rise and a compound annual growth rate of 43.8%; cumulative publication output was well fitted by a quadratic polynomial model (R2 = 0.9973). China produced the most publications, whereas the United States achieved the highest citation count. The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center, Javid Moslehi, and Frontiers in Oncology were the leading institution, author, and journal, respectively; The New England Journal of Medicine was the most frequently co-cited journal. Keyword mapping delineated three domains: immune-mediated cardiovascular phenotypes, PD-1/PD-L1-related mechanisms, and multidisciplinary risk management. Temporal and burst analyses indicated a shift from toxicity recognition toward mechanistic elucidation, diagnostic standardization, risk stratification, and cardiovascular outcome assessment. Emerging low-frequency but rapidly strengthening terms highlight growing attention to combination-regimen safety, patient-level risk assessment, evidence synthesis, and long-term cardiovascular outcomes.KEYWORDS: Immune checkpoint inhibitors, cardiotoxicity, myocarditis, bibliometric analysis

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have reshaped the treatment of multiple malignancies by improving clinical outcomes and enabling durable remission or long-term survival in selected patients.1–3 As clinical indications broaden and ICI-based combination regimens become increasingly common, the population exposed to these agents continues to expand. This therapeutic progress has been accompanied by a growing burden of immune-related adverse events (irAEs), which may compromise treatment continuity and attenuate sustained clinical benefit.4–6 Although ICI-associated cardiotoxicity occurs less frequently than many other irAEs, it remains one of the most serious complications of cancer immunotherapy.7–9 ICI-associated myocarditis is particularly concerning, with reported mortality rates of approximately 30%–50% despite its relatively low incidence.10 The clinical course may be insidious because early manifestations are often subtle and nonspecific, including fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, chest discomfort, asymptomatic troponin elevation, minor electrocardiographic abnormalities, or mild changes in cardiac function.10–12 These features complicate timely recognition and may delay intervention. Once clinically apparent, cardiac injury can progress rapidly, leading to treatment interruption, major adverse cardiovascular events, or death.

ICI-associated cardiotoxicity comprises a heterogeneous spectrum of cardiovascular manifestations, including myocarditis, arrhythmias, pericarditis, cardiomyopathy, left ventricular dysfunction, heart failure, vasculitis, and accelerated atherosclerotic events.13,14 These phenotypes may occur in isolation or overlap within the same patient, further increasing diagnostic uncertainty.12,15 Risk assessment is also complicated by the rising prevalence of pre-existing cardiovascular comorbidities, prior exposure to potentially cardiotoxic therapies, and the expanding use of ICI-based combination regimens.8,16 These clinical complexities underscore the need for earlier detection, standardized risk stratification, and multidisciplinary management frameworks integrating oncology, cardiology, immunology, cardiac imaging, and biomarker-based surveillance.17–19

The rapid development of cardio-oncology, together with accumulating clinical and mechanistic evidence, has driven substantial growth in research on ICI-associated cardiotoxicity over the past decade.20,21 However, the conceptual structure of this interdisciplinary field remains insufficiently consolidated. Terminology, outcome definitions, and reporting practices vary across cardiology and oncology journals, limiting the development of a unified research lexicon.22,23 Co-word analysis is therefore valuable for clarifying the thematic architecture of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity and for identifying areas in which terminology and research priorities remain incompletely standardized.24

Bibliometrics, when combined with visualization techniques, offers a quantitative and reproducible approach for characterizing scholarly output, collaboration patterns, citation relationships, and knowledge structures at the macro level.25 Whereas meta-analyses synthesize clinically relevant evidence from eligible studies, bibliometric analysis can expose structural blind spots in the research landscape, including underexplored topics, emerging themes, and uneven patterns of collaboration. By examining publication trends, national and institutional contributions, author networks, journal distributions, co-citation relationships, keyword co-occurrence, burst patterns, and temporal evolution, bibliometric methods can delineate both the intellectual foundations and evolving frontiers of a research field.26 In the present study, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) was selected rather than Scopus because of its standardized citation indexing, reproducible export format, and established compatibility with VOSviewer and CiteSpace, which together support data consistency and methodological reproducibility.27

Several bibliometric studies have addressed topics related to cardio-oncology and immunotherapy-associated cardiovascular toxicity.20,28–30 Nevertheless, their scopes differ from the present analysis, which focuses specifically on ICI-associated cardiotoxicity and integrates collaboration networks, co-citation structures, keyword clustering, burst detection, temporal evolution, and clinically oriented interpretation. We retrieved publications from 2016 to 2025 and conducted a quantitative and visualized bibliometric analysis using VOSviewer and CiteSpace. The objectives were to characterize global research output and collaboration patterns, identify leading countries, institutions, authors, and influential journals, delineate the foundational literature and intellectual structure shaping the field, and reveal research hotspots, burst keywords, and their temporal evolution. By positioning bibliometric findings within current clinical challenges, this study provides a structural roadmap intended to inform researchers and funding organizations concerned with early detection, risk stratification, long-term cardiovascular outcomes, and precision management of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategy

All bibliometric records were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) on January 1, 2026. The search covered publications indexed from January 1, 2016, to December 31, 2025. WoSCC was selected as the bibliographic source because it provides standardized records, complete cited-reference information, stable export formats, and strong compatibility with widely used bibliometric tools, including VOSviewer and CiteSpace.31 Although Scopus, PubMed, and Google Scholar may index additional relevant publications, WoSCC was considered most appropriate for this analysis because of its standardized citation-indexing structure, reproducible export format, and suitability for co-citation and network-based analyses.31,32 On this basis, WoSCC was used as the sole data source. The potential selection bias introduced by reliance on a single database is addressed in the Limitations section.

Search terms were developed with reference to Medical Subject Headings (MeSH), commonly used free-text terms, abbreviations, drug names, and terminology reported in previous studies. Because MeSH is a controlled vocabulary specific to PubMed/MEDLINE and is not used for indexed searching in WoSCC, the final strategy was implemented as a free-text search in the WoSCC Topic field. In WoSCC, the Topic field includes the title, abstract, author keywords, and Keywords Plus.

The search strategy consisted of two conceptual term groups. The first group captured immune checkpoint inhibitor-related terminology, including general ICI expressions, immune checkpoint pathways, therapeutic targets, and commonly used drug names. The second group captured cardiotoxicity-related terminology, including cardiovascular adverse events, myocarditis, arrhythmias, heart failure, cardiomyopathy, pericarditis, left ventricular dysfunction, myocardial injury, and related expressions. The two groups were combined with the Boolean operator “AND,” whereas synonyms and related terms within each group were linked with “OR.” Because of its length, the full search string is provided in Supplementary File 1, including all search terms, field tags, Boolean operators, time span, language restriction, and document-type filters, thereby enabling independent validation and replication of the dataset.

Eligibility criteria and study selection

Only English-language original articles and reviews were eligible for inclusion. Letters, editorial materials, corrections, conference abstracts, proceedings papers, meeting abstracts, book chapters, and other non-research document types were excluded. All eligible records were exported in plain-text format with full records and cited references.

Automatic deduplication was performed using WoSCC export records, followed by manual verification. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts for relevance. Disagreements were resolved through discussion, with consultation of a third reviewer when consensus could not be reached. Full-text assessment was performed when eligibility could not be determined from the title and abstract alone.

The inclusion criteria were: studies involving cancer immunotherapy, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors; studies addressing ICI-associated cardiovascular adverse events or cardiotoxicity; and publications in which cardiovascular complications were a primary research focus. The exclusion criteria were: studies unrelated to immunotherapy; studies focusing exclusively on chemotherapy-, targeted therapy-, or radiotherapy-induced cardiotoxicity without involvement of immune checkpoint inhibitors; and publications without a clear relationship between immune checkpoint inhibitors and cardiovascular toxicity.

After deduplication and screening, 1,127 publications were included in the final dataset. The search and selection process was structured in accordance with PRISMA-S principles and is presented as a PRISMA-S-informed flow diagram in (Figure 1). Where permitted by database-license conditions, a cleaned analytical dataset or a list of included records with bibliographic identifiers is provided as Supplementary File 2 to support data verification, repeated analysis, and methodological validation.

Data preprocessing and quality control

Before formal analysis, all retrieved records underwent standardized preprocessing. For keyword analysis, a VOSviewer thesaurus file was constructed to merge synonyms, abbreviations, spelling variants, singular and plural forms, and closely related expressions, while excluding low-relevance, overly general, or thematically uninformative terms. For example, “immune checkpoint inhibitor,” “immune checkpoint inhibitors,” “ICI,” and “ICIs” were standardized; “PD1” and “PD-1” were merged; and “programmed death ligand 1,” “PD-L1,” and “PDL1” were unified. For author analysis, variant name formats were manually checked and merged to reduce misclassification caused by initials, spelling differences, and inconsistent indexing formats. For country and region analysis, synonymous expressions and name variants were standardized. The keyword merging rules, stop-word list, author-name normalization principles, and country or region standardization rules are provided in Supplementary File 3.

To enhance analytical robustness, key results were cross-validated between VOSviewer and CiteSpace where applicable. Findings that were highly sensitive to parameter changes were interpreted cautiously and were not overemphasized. Visualization outputs were exported as high-resolution images in TIFF or vector-compatible formats, with legible labels and annotations to improve figure readability.

Bibliometric and visualization analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to characterize annual publication output, citation counts, leading countries or regions, institutions, authors, journals, keywords, and references. Microsoft Excel 2021 was used to calculate annual publication counts and generate publication trend curves. Cumulative publication output was fitted using a quadratic polynomial regression model, and goodness of fit was evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²). The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) was calculated as CAGR = (N2025/N2016)^(1/9) − 1, where N2016 and N2025 represent the numbers of publications in 2016 and 2025, respectively. Based on 9 publications in 2016 and 236 publications in 2025, the CAGR was 43.8%.

Bibliometric and visualization analyses were performed using VOSviewer 1.6.20, CiteSpace 6.4.R1, Scimago Graphica, Charticulator, and Microsoft Excel 2021. VOSviewer was used to construct collaboration networks of countries or regions, institutions, and authors, as well as networks of productive journals, co-cited journals, and keyword co-occurrence.25 CiteSpace was used for dual-map overlay analysis of journals, keyword co-occurrence analysis, reference co-citation analysis, keyword burst detection, and reference burst detection.33 Scimago Graphica was used to visualize annual publication output among highly productive countries and the global distribution of publications. Charticulator was used to generate the chord diagram of international collaboration.

Parameter settings and cluster validation

Bibliometric visualization and network analyses were performed using CiteSpace 6.4.R1 and VOSviewer 1.6.20. After importing the retrieved records into CiteSpace, a dedicated project was established. For keyword and term extraction, the minimum and maximum term lengths were set to 1 and 10 words, respectively. The temporal span was defined as 2016–2025, with one-year time slices.

For keyword co-occurrence analysis, the node type was specified as Keyword. A preliminary analysis was conducted using the g-index with k set to 10, after which the keyword list was exported for manual inspection. Synonymous expressions were consolidated through a citespace.alias file, whereas non-informative terms were excluded through a citespace.exclusion file. Both files were placed in the project directory before the analysis was rerun. In the final keyword analysis, the g-index k value was reduced to 5, and the network was pruned using Pathfinder and Pruning the merged network.

For reference co-citation analysis, the node type was set to Reference, with the g-index k value set to 25. Pathfinder and Pruning sliced networks were applied to reduce network complexity while retaining the principal structural characteristics of the co-citation network.

In VOSviewer, bibliometric maps were constructed by selecting “Create a map based on bibliographic data” followed by “Read data from bibliographic database files.” The imported Web of Science Core Collection records were used to conduct five analyses: country co-authorship, author co-authorship, organization co-authorship, source bibliographic coupling, and cited source co-citation. The corresponding units of analysis were Countries, Authors, Organizations, Sources, and Cited sources, with thresholds of 8, 5, 10, 8, and 200, respectively. Parameters not explicitly modified were retained at the default settings of VOSviewer.

Cluster validity was assessed using modularity Q and mean silhouette S. Modularity Q quantifies the extent of network partitioning, with values greater than 0.3 generally indicating a meaningful clustering structure. Mean silhouette S reflects within-cluster homogeneity, with values greater than 0.5 indicating acceptable clustering reliability. The keyword clustering network yielded a modularity Q of 0.8188 and a mean silhouette S of 0.9534, whereas the reference co-citation clustering network yielded a modularity Q of 0.7000 and a mean silhouette S of 0.8865. These metrics indicate robust cluster separation and high internal consistency in both networks.

Results

Overview of publications

The final analysis included 1,127 publications on immune checkpoint inhibitor—associated cardiotoxicity. These records involved 8,842 authors from 2,430 institutions across 67 countries or regions and were published in 364 academic journals. The reference corpus comprised 34,027 cited records from 3,976 journals. These findings indicate broad international participation and underscore the interdisciplinary character of the field, spanning oncology, cardiology, immunology, and translational medicine.

As shown in (Figure 2), annual publication output increased markedly between 2016 and 2025. The number of publications rose rapidly from 2019 to 2022, reached a local peak in 2022, and then showed modest year-to-year fluctuations. However, the long-term trajectory remained upward, with output reaching a new high of 236 articles in 2025. Thus, the sustained increase described in this study refers to the overall long-term expansion of the field rather than uninterrupted annual growth. Quadratic polynomial regression of cumulative publication output demonstrated an excellent model fit, with an R² value of 0.9973, supporting the overall upward trajectory of research activity across the study period. Clinically, this growth parallels the expanding use of ICIs in oncology and the increasing recognition of cardiovascular immune-related adverse events. The rise in publication volume suggests that ICI-associated cardiotoxicity has moved from an uncommon safety signal to a clinically important issue in cardio-oncology, reinforcing the need for early detection, standardized surveillance, risk stratification, multidisciplinary management, and evidence-based treatment strategies. The cumulative number of publications also increased steadily throughout the study period, further supporting the expanding scholarly and clinical attention to this topic.

Although China had the largest publication output, it accumulated 9,423 total citations and had a comparatively low mean citation rate of 22.17 citations per paper. The United States entered the field earlier and contributed a number of landmark clinical and mechanistic studies, allowing longer citation accumulation. Citation burst analysis in (Figure 6(A)) identified influential publications with sharp increases in citation intensity during specific periods, reflecting the staged evolution of research on immune checkpoint inhibitor—associated cardiotoxicity. Early burst papers mainly focused on the clinical efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors and the initial recognition of immune-related adverse events. Subsequent work shifted toward systematic characterization of ICI-related cardiotoxicity, particularly myocarditis and other cardiovascular complications. More recent burst literature increasingly emphasized immune-mediated injury mechanisms, risk stratification, diagnostic evaluation, and clinical management, indicating a broadening focus toward mechanistic understanding and more precise clinical care.

Analysis of keywords

The keyword time-zone visualization in (Figure 7(A)) illustrates the staged evolution of research on immune checkpoint inhibitor—associated cardiotoxicity. During the initial phase from 2016 to 2017, keywords mainly reflected immunotherapy agents and their oncologic contexts, including ipilimumab, nivolumab, pembrolizumab, and metastatic melanoma. Early cardiac safety signals, such as dilated cardiomyopathy, also appeared during this period. From 2018 to 2020, keywords directly related to cardiotoxicity increased markedly, including cardiotoxicity, myocarditis, pericardial effusion, pericarditis, and cardiac dysfunction, indicating growing emphasis on systematic detection and clinical characterization of cardiovascular adverse events. After 2021, research topics expanded toward risk assessment and clinical management, with terms such as risk factors, cardiac events, cardiovascular magnetic resonance, echocardiography, and endomyocardial biopsy appearing more frequently. The emergence of guideline-related terms suggests movement toward risk stratification and standardized management. Overall, the keyword time-zone map indicates a temporal transition from drug-centered toxicity recognition to phenotype characterization, diagnostic refinement, and structured clinical management.

Keyword analysis in immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated cardiotoxicity research. (A) keyword time-zone visualization. (B) top 25 keywords with the strongest citation bursts. (C) keyword clustering map. (D) keyword co-occurrence network.

Keyword burst analysis in (Figure 7(B)) further demonstrates shifts in research hotspots over time. Early burst keywords from 2016 to 2019/2020 focused on specific ICIs, including ipilimumab and nivolumab, and on foundational aspects of immunotherapy, alongside early cardiovascular phenotypes such as dilated cardiomyopathy. These bursts reflect an initial phase of clinical signal recognition, during which cardiovascular toxicity was mainly identified through case reports, early clinical observations, and safety reporting.

Between 2018 and 2022, terms including CTLA-4, cardiac dysfunction, pericardial effusion, pericarditis, myocardial infarction, cardiovascular magnetic resonance, and risk factors showed prominent bursts. This pattern indicates increasing attention to specific cardiovascular complications, diagnostic tools, and risk identification, suggesting that the field had moved beyond recognition of isolated adverse events toward mechanistic exploration, objective diagnosis, and standardized evaluation of ICI-related cardiovascular injury.

The burst period of CTLA-4 in this analysis should not be interpreted as evidence that CTLA-4 became important only after 2018. CTLA-4 had already been a central target in cancer immunotherapy before the beginning of the study window. Because the present analysis was restricted to publications from 2016 to 2025 and used one-year time slices, burst detection captured relative increases in term frequency and prominence within this dataset rather than the historical origin or absolute importance of the concept. The 2018–2021 burst of CTLA-4 therefore reflects renewed or intensified attention to CTLA-4-related terms within the selected time window, likely driven by increasing interest in CTLA-4-containing regimens, combination immunotherapy, and associated cardiovascular safety concerns.

After 2022, several burst keywords persisted through 2025, including lenvatinib, cancer patients, cardiac events, sunitinib, meta-analysis, and cardiovascular disease. These patterns indicate a shift toward cardiovascular risk assessment and evidence synthesis, particularly in the context of combination regimens. The sustained bursts of lenvatinib and sunitinib suggest growing concern regarding cardiovascular safety in ICI-based combination therapies, especially regimens involving tyrosine kinase inhibitors or anti-angiogenic agents. The emergence of meta-analysis and cardiovascular disease as recent burst terms further indicates increasing demand for higher-level evidence synthesis and broader evaluation of long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

Clinically, the temporal shift from drug-specific terms to myocarditis, cardiac dysfunction, cardiovascular magnetic resonance, echocardiography, endomyocardial biopsy, risk factors, and management suggests that the field is moving beyond toxicity recognition toward early diagnosis, structured surveillance, risk stratification, and evidence-based management of ICI-related cardiovascular complications.

Taken together, the keyword time-zone and burst analyses reveal a three-stage developmental trajectory: early signal recognition and phenotype definition from 2016 to 2017, systematic characterization and diagnostic standardization from 2018 to 2020/2021, and the current stage of precision risk assessment, combination-therapy safety evaluation, evidence synthesis, and individualized management after 2021. These findings identify emerging trends in the field and complement the keyword co-occurrence and clustering analyses.

Core cardiotoxicity phenotypes were captured by cluster #1, myocarditis, and cluster #11, immune-related adverse events, supporting myocarditis as a central focus in this field. Drug- and target-related clusters, including cluster #8, PD-1; cluster #9, pembrolizumab; and cluster #2, camrelizumab, indicate sustained attention to cardiovascular safety across different ICI agents. Cluster #5, lenvatinib, and cluster #4, chemotherapy, suggest expanding interest in cardiovascular risk under combination therapy and broader antitumor treatment strategies. Cluster #7, cardio-oncology, and cluster #6, safety, reflect progression toward multidisciplinary integration and clinical management. Additional clusters, such as cluster #10, acute coronary syndrome, and cluster #3, cardiovascular toxicity, further delineate the spectrum of ICI-associated cardiovascular complications.

The keyword co-occurrence network in (Figure 7(D)) was highly centralized, with immunotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors as core nodes and cardiotoxicity and myocarditis as major hubs. ICI agents, including nivolumab, pembrolizumab, and ipilimumab, were strongly linked to cardiovascular outcomes such as heart failure and cardiac events, underscoring the association between ICIs and multiple cardiovascular phenotypes. Terms such as cardio-oncology, management, safety, and risk occupied bridging positions, suggesting a shift from early toxicity description toward integrated risk assessment and clinical management. In combination with the burst and time-zone analyses, the co-occurrence network further supports the conclusion that the field is transitioning toward precision monitoring, multidisciplinary management, and long-term cardiovascular outcome optimization.

Discussion

Principal findings and novelty of this study

This study provides one of the most current bibliometric analyses specifically focused on immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)—associated cardiotoxicity. Although several bibliometric studies in cardio-oncology have been published recently, many have adopted broader scopes, shorter observation windows, or less specific thematic boundaries. Some studies examined cardiotoxicity across chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy without fully addressing ICI-specific mechanisms and management challenges, whereas others focused on narrower subfields, including CAR-T cell therapy, heart failure in patients with lung cancer, or ferroptosis-related cardiotoxicity.20,28–30 Even when ICI-related cardiotoxicity was included, it was often considered within a broader cardio-oncology framework, with limited sample size or observation periods ending in 2024.

By contrast, the present study analyzed 1,127 WoSCC-indexed publications specifically related to ICI-associated cardiotoxicity and covered the literature through 2025. This design enabled a more recent and focused characterization of the field, capturing its transition from early case recognition and mechanistic exploration to risk stratification, diagnostic refinement, and precision management. The following discussion integrates these bibliometric findings to clarify the developmental logic of the field and identify priorities for future investigation.

Global research landscape: evolution of collaboration models and regional differentiation in influence

The annual publication output in this field showed a pronounced upward trajectory, with an excellent exponential model fit (R2 = 0.9973). This pattern suggests that, as ICIs have become widely adopted in oncology, cardiovascular safety concerns have moved from sporadic clinical observations to a specialized domain requiring systematic investigation. The expansion is reflected not only in publication volume but also in the maturation of research priorities, which have progressed from isolated case reports to mechanistic studies, diagnostic standardization, and structured management strategies.17 Clinically, this growth parallels the broad use of ICIs across multiple tumor types and the increasing recognition that cardiovascular immune-related adverse events may affect treatment continuity, quality of life, and long-term survival.12,34

At the global level, research output and academic influence showed a clear divergence between high-output and high-impact contributors. China ranked first in publication count, reflecting substantial clinical resources, large patient populations, and increasing research investment. The United States, however, led in total citations and H-index, underscoring its central role in foundational research, mechanistic discovery, and clinical guidance. This influence may be attributable to earlier adoption of ICIs in clinical trials and routine oncology practice, mature cardio-oncology programs, established multicenter trial networks, large registry resources, and highly visible comprehensive cancer centers. Several European countries, including Germany and the United Kingdom, achieved high average citations per paper, suggesting strength in selected subfields. This pattern may reflect Europe’s tradition of guideline-oriented research, high levels of international collaboration, and frequent publication in influential cardiovascular and oncology journals.

China’s high publication output but comparatively lower average citation impact should not be interpreted simply as lower research quality. A large proportion of Chinese publications appeared in the later years of the study period, and their citation influence may not yet have fully matured, indicating a citation-lag effect. Differences in international collaboration intensity, journal distribution, study design, and participation in global multicenter clinical studies may also contribute to the output—impact gap. Future efforts should therefore prioritize not only research scale but also methodological originality, prospective cohort construction, international visibility, and deeper clinical translation.

International collaboration network analysis indicated a multicenter cooperative structure anchored by major research hubs in the United States, China, and Europe. China and the United States formed a dual-core structure, diverging from traditional single-center models and reflecting the redistribution of scientific capacity across regions. Temporally, early studies were mainly driven by North American and European teams, which first reported severe ICI-associated myocarditis and proposed initial diagnostic and management strategies.15,35 With the rapid expansion of ICI use in Asia, particularly in China, and the accumulation of real-world clinical cohorts, Asian countries have become increasingly active.36,37 The collaboration pattern has therefore shifted from a predominantly Western structure toward a more multipolar network involving North America, Europe, and Asia.

Beyond scientific capacity, this geographic pattern also reflects structural heterogeneity across research ecosystems, including differences in funding mechanisms, regulatory requirements, data-sharing policies, language environments, and access to multicenter registries. Such heterogeneity may delay the generation of globally generalizable evidence, because inconsistent regulatory frameworks and limited data interoperability can hinder patient-level data integration, external validation of risk prediction models, and rapid dissemination of management strategies across regions. Notably, despite China’s high output, its collaboration network remains relatively regionally clustered, indicating substantial scope to strengthen sustained, high-intensity partnerships with leading European and U.S. teams.

Future East–West collaboration should move beyond publication volume and focus on unified prospective cohorts, biobank sharing, standardized biomarker assays, harmonized imaging protocols, and cross-population validation of risk prediction models. Ancestry- and sex-stratified immunogenetic analyses, including HLA typing, may also warrant incorporation into future collaborative studies. HLA allele distributions differ substantially across ancestral populations, and sex-related immune regulation may influence susceptibility to immune-related adverse events.38,39 Although direct evidence linking specific HLA genotypes to ICI-associated cardiotoxicity remains limited, cross-population studies may help determine whether particular immunogenetic backgrounds increase susceptibility to myocarditis or other cardiovascular immune-related adverse events.40,41

At the institutional level, the collaboration network displayed a complex modular structure. U.S.-based clusters centered on The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center and Massachusetts General Hospital showed advantages in mechanistic and translational research, whereas Chinese clusters led by Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Sichuan University accumulated experience in large clinical cohorts and drug-safety evaluation. These relatively independent modules reflect differentiated research priorities and also indicate the potential for synergy through cross-cluster collaboration. Accordingly, future partnerships should strengthen East–West cooperation in large prospective cohort development, biobank sharing, and integration of basic and clinical research teams, particularly in studies linking molecular mechanisms with translational applications. Global multicenter clinical studies, standardized data-sharing platforms, and harmonized biomarker assays are likely to become key collaborative directions in this field.

Research hotspots: from core phenotypes to disciplinary system construction

The keyword time-zone and burst analyses enriched the identification of emerging trends by revealing not only which topics were frequently studied but also when they became prominent and how research priorities changed over time. These analyses support a three-stage trajectory from early toxicity recognition to diagnostic standardization and the current phase of precision risk assessment and individualized management.

Hotspot 1: core toxicity phenotype–myocarditis as a research paradigm

Myocarditis is the most intensively studied clinical phenotype of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity. Cluster analysis showed that “myocarditis” formed the largest standalone cluster and exhibited strong connectivity with central terms such as “cardiotoxicity” and “immune checkpoint inhibitors.” The dominance of myocarditis as a research hotspot has a clear clinical rationale. Although ICI-associated myocarditis is relatively uncommon, it can progress rapidly, carries high mortality, and may require interruption of anticancer therapy.15 It has therefore become a sentinel toxicity event of shared concern to oncologists, cardiologists, and immunologists. This prominence reflects the clinical urgency of myocarditis, which is widely regarded as the most life-threatening ICI-related cardiac toxicity. Its T-cell-mediated pathogenesis also makes it a prototypical model for investigating immune-mediated cardiac injury. An extensive evidence base has accumulated around this phenotype, including characterization of clinical presentations such as fulminant myocarditis, refinement of diagnostic approaches such as cardiac magnetic resonance imaging and endomyocardial biopsy, and evaluation of treatment strategies including high-dose glucocorticoids, intravenous immunoglobulin, and selected second-line immunosuppressive options.17,34,42 Clinically, this hotspot reflects the need for early recognition of high-risk presentations, standardized diagnostic pathways, and timely initiation of immunosuppressive treatment. Such pathways often require integrated assessment using troponin, natriuretic peptides, electrocardiography, echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance, and endomyocardial biopsy.

However, the field’s strong emphasis on myocarditis may inadvertently obscure other important ICI-related cardiovascular toxicities, including immune-mediated pericardial disease, vasculitis-related myocardial infarction, arrhythmias, and heart failure. Although myocarditis is often the most immediately fatal form of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity, long-term cardiovascular sequelae may exert a broader population-level impact on cancer survivors.43 Subclinical ventricular dysfunction, myocardial fibrosis, arrhythmias, and accelerated atherosclerosis may persist or progress after completion of cancer therapy, thereby increasing late cardiovascular morbidity and mortality during survivorship.44 Future studies should broaden attention to the full spectrum of ICI-related cardiovascular toxicity and clarify whether distinct phenotypes share common immune mechanisms or arise through phenotype-specific pathways.

Hotspot 2: drug-specific safety spectrum—from “class effects” to precision risk management

Recent work has increasingly focused on specific agents, including nivolumab, pembrolizumab, and camrelizumab, underscoring the need for more granular safety management in clinical practice.8 The emergence of drug-specific and regimen-specific terms as research hotspots is likely related to the rapid diversification of immunotherapy strategies.45 In real-world oncology practice, ICIs are no longer used only as monotherapy but are increasingly combined with chemotherapy, anti-angiogenic agents, and tyrosine kinase inhibitors. These combinations generate more heterogeneous cardiovascular risk profiles that cannot be adequately captured by a broad ICI class-effect framework.

Accumulating evidence indicates that cardiovascular toxicity patterns differ across ICI classes, including CTLA-4 inhibitors and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, and across treatment strategies, including ICIs combined with anti-angiogenic targeted therapy. Developing a dynamically updated, regimen-specific cardiovascular toxicity profile is therefore essential for individualized risk prediction and management. Clinically, this hotspot suggests that patients should undergo baseline cardiovascular risk assessment before treatment, receive regimen-specific surveillance during therapy, and be managed individually after cardiovascular events, including decisions regarding ICI interruption, rechallenge, and cardioprotective therapy. The recent shift from monotherapy to combination strategies, including ICIs combined with tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as lenvatinib and sunitinib, further reflects increasing attention to complex real-world risk scenarios.46,47

Because many monitoring and diagnostic strategies are resource intensive, their cost-effectiveness also requires systematic evaluation. Serial troponin testing, electrocardiographic monitoring, echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance, and endomyocardial biopsy may improve diagnostic accuracy, yet universal application to all patients receiving ICIs may not be economically sustainable.48,49 A risk-adapted strategy, in which intensive monitoring is prioritized for patients with pre-existing cardiovascular disease, exposure to combination therapy, abnormal baseline biomarkers, or early symptoms, may be more feasible and cost-effective. Comparative health-economic studies are needed to determine whether universal screening or risk-stratified surveillance offers better value across different healthcare systems.

Hotspot 3: discipline establishment—the emergence and maturation of cardio-oncology

A key finding is that “cardio-oncology” emerged as an independent and highly active cluster closely linked to keywords such as “management” and “risk.” This hotspot reflects the recognition that ICI-associated cardiotoxicity is not merely a rare adverse event but a management challenge requiring coordinated care between oncology and cardiology.50 It also signals a paradigm shift from reactive responses to isolated adverse events toward proactive strategies emphasizing prevention, risk stratification, and standardized management.

Cardio-oncology integrates oncology, cardiology, immunology, and imaging. Its scope extends beyond acute toxicity care to include risk prediction models incorporating clinical features, biomarkers, and imaging measures, long-term cardiovascular outcome surveillance, and rehabilitation for cancer survivors.51,52 Clinically, the emergence of this cluster highlights three unmet needs: early diagnosis before irreversible myocardial injury occurs, identification of patients requiring intensified surveillance, and standardized management pathways that balance cardiovascular safety with anticancer efficacy. Journal co-citation patterns further support this interdisciplinary foundation, with leading cardiovascular, oncology, and general medical journals jointly constituting the field’s core knowledge base. Consensus statements and clinical guidelines issued by professional societies consolidate prior evidence and shape research priorities, forming a reinforcing cycle between research, practice, and guideline development.

Together, these three hotspots constitute a coherent knowledge system. Myocarditis provides a prototype for elucidating core immunopathological mechanisms; drug- and regimen-specific research enables more effective risk stratification; and both strands contribute to the maturation of cardio-oncology, with the ultimate goal of optimizing the benefit—risk balance of cancer therapy while minimizing cardiovascular harm.

Research frontiers: from toxicity identification to precision management and mechanistic breakthroughs

Keyword burst detection and timeline analyses indicate a dynamic evolution of research frontiers that can be summarized into three partially overlapping stages. These bibliometric patterns closely correspond to major clinical challenges in contemporary cardio-oncology. Early burst terms reflect the initial need to recognize fatal toxicities; subsequent diagnostic terms indicate a transition toward early detection and diagnostic standardization; and recent keywords related to biomarkers, meta-analysis, cardiovascular disease, and combination therapy suggest a shift toward risk stratification, evidence synthesis, and individualized management. Keyword evolution therefore reflects not merely a change in terminology but a transformation of clinical practice from toxicity recognition to precision monitoring and management.

Stage 1 (2016–2018): signal recognition and phenotype definition

Early studies were driven by the urgent clinical need to identify and characterize a novel, potentially fatal toxicity. The emergence of keywords such as “ipilimumab,” “nivolumab,” and “dilated cardiomyopathy” captures this period. Research primarily focused on case identification and communication of risk to the medical community. Publications were dominated by case reports and small case series, which served as sentinel warnings and established the occurrence of cardiovascular events such as ICI-related myocarditis.53 This stage parallels the early post-marketing phase of other targeted therapies, in which the central goal is to support a causal link between drug exposure and adverse events. Most work was therefore descriptive and centered on defining what had occurred.

Stage 2 (2019–2022): mechanistic exploration and diagnostic standardization

After initial alerts, the field moved rapidly toward systematization and early mechanistic inquiry. The appearance of terms such as “CTLA-4,” “pericarditis,” and “myocardial infarction,” together with diagnostic keywords including “cardiac magnetic resonance” and “troponin,” reflects this transition. Research questions expanded from case recognition to incidence estimation and diagnostic standardization. The scope broadened beyond myocarditis to include inflammatory and ischemic phenotypes, acknowledging the heterogeneity of immune-mediated cardiovascular injury. Increasing methodological sophistication is reflected by the prominence of cardiac magnetic resonance, signaling a shift toward objective tissue-level characterization.54 Diagnostic approaches also evolved from clinical suspicion to standardized criteria integrating advanced imaging with biomarker panels, such as troponin and natriuretic peptides. This period also saw the publication of key consensus statements and diagnostic algorithms aimed at reducing heterogeneity in case definitions across research and clinical practice.

The burst period of CTLA-4 in this study should not be interpreted as evidence that CTLA-4 became important only during this stage. Rather, it indicates a relative increase in the frequency and prominence of CTLA-4-related terms within the 2016–2025 dataset, likely driven by growing attention to CTLA-4-containing regimens, combination immunotherapy, and their cardiovascular safety profiles.

Stage 3 (current and emerging): from precision risk management to mechanism-driven innovation

Current frontiers emphasize precise prediction, proactive intervention, and mechanistic breakthroughs. These developments can be summarized into five directions that collectively shape next-generation cardio-oncology management.

Frontier I: biomarker-driven dynamic risk stratification

The emergence of biomarker-centered research, including liquid biopsy-related approaches, signals a shift from static assessment to dynamic longitudinal monitoring.55,56 Research has moved beyond conventional markers of myocardial injury to investigate circulating indicators such as cardiac-specific autoantibodies, inflammatory cytokine signatures, cell-free DNA, and non-coding RNAs.57 These biomarkers may enable earlier detection of subclinical injury while also reflecting activation of specific immune pathways. Recent work increasingly integrates multi-omics data with machine learning to develop predictive models that may identify high-risk patients before symptom onset, thereby supporting preventive management.58

Frontier II: precision toxicity management in combination-therapy settings

The inclusion of non-ICI targeted agents, such as lenvatinib and sunitinib, highlights a major clinical challenge: cardiovascular safety in immunotherapy-based combinations.47 Evidence suggests that combining ICIs with tyrosine kinase inhibitors and other agents may yield distinct toxicity patterns and increase risks of endothelial dysfunction, hypertension, and heart failure.59 Current studies are advancing along two lines: elucidating mechanisms underlying combination-related toxicities and developing individualized surveillance and management strategies tailored to specific regimens. This marks a transition from uniform management to a regimen-specific framework.

Frontier III: long-term cardiovascular outcomes and survivorship care

The rise of “meta-analysis” as a key term underscores the need to synthesize higher-quality evidence in this area. More importantly, it reflects a shift from acute toxicity management toward long-term cardiovascular health in cancer survivors. Key topics include mechanisms of accelerated atherosclerosis and strategies to prevent delayed cardiomyopathy and arrhythmias after ICI exposure.44 Progress will require structured long-term follow-up systems and comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation programs, supporting a transition from treatment-period toxicity management to survivorship-centered cardiovascular care.

Frontier IV: mechanistic elucidation enabling targeted cardioprotection

Advancing mechanistic research is challenging the historical reliance on broad immunosuppression, particularly corticosteroid-based approaches. Emerging studies employ single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and humanized mouse models to dissect pathways such as dysregulated T-cell activation, macrophage polarization, complement activation, and cardiomyocyte antigen presentation.60,61 The objective is to identify actionable molecular targets that protect cardiac tissue without compromising antitumor immunity. Through precision immunomodulation, researchers aim to preserve oncologic efficacy while minimizing cardiovascular toxicity.

Frontier V: digital integration and intelligent clinical decision support

A forward-looking direction is the integration of multimodal data, including clinical features, genetic susceptibility, dynamic biomarkers, and continuous physiologic signals, to build artificial intelligence—enabled prediction models for cardiotoxicity.62 Wearables that provide continuous electrocardiographic monitoring and activity tracking add a real-time dimension to cardiovascular assessment.62 This approach supports a shift from reactive care to proactive early warning and enables risk-adapted, dynamically updated monitoring and intervention strategies, moving toward personalized cardio-oncology care.

Overall, the current stage is characterized by concurrent expansion in mechanistic depth and clinical breadth. The convergence of these frontiers is transforming cardio-oncology from a field centered on complication management into a core component spanning the entire cancer-therapy continuum.

Clinical implications and future directions

The bibliometric findings of this study have several clinical implications. The central position of myocarditis in the knowledge map indicates that early diagnosis remains one of the most urgent challenges. Because ICI-associated myocarditis can progress rapidly and early symptoms are often nonspecific, future studies should further optimize diagnostic algorithms integrating troponin, natriuretic peptides, electrocardiography, echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance, and endomyocardial biopsy. Harmonized diagnostic criteria are essential for improving case identification and reducing heterogeneity across clinical studies.

The emergence of keywords related to risk, management, biomarkers, and combination therapy indicates a growing need for individualized risk stratification. Patients receiving combination immunotherapy, those with pre-existing cardiovascular disease, and those simultaneously exposed to cardiotoxic targeted therapies may require intensified surveillance. Future research should develop and validate risk prediction models that integrate clinical characteristics, treatment regimens, biomarkers, imaging findings, and potential genetic or immunologic susceptibility markers.

The rise of cardio-oncology as an independent cluster indicates that management of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity should be multidisciplinary and longitudinal. Clinical decisions must balance tumor control with cardiovascular safety, particularly when considering ICI interruption, immunosuppressive therapy, cardioprotective treatment, and ICI rechallenge. Prospective multicenter studies are needed to determine optimal surveillance intervals, treatment thresholds, and follow-up strategies for both acute and long-term cardiovascular complications.

Future research should move from descriptive reporting toward evidence that directly informs clinical practice. Large prospective cohorts, unified case definitions, standardized biomarker assays, health-economic evaluations, and cross-regional data-sharing platforms will be critical for translating bibliometric growth into measurable improvements in patient outcomes.

Limitations

Several limitations warrant consideration. The bibliometric data were derived exclusively from WoSCC. Although WoSCC is widely used in bibliometric research because of its standardized citation records, high-quality indexing, and compatibility with bibliometric software, reliance on a single database may have introduced selection bias. Relevant studies indexed only in Scopus, PubMed, Embase, or other regional databases may not have been captured, particularly recently published articles, conference proceedings, and non-English or locally circulated studies. PubMed has strong coverage of biomedical literature but provides limited citation-network information, whereas Scopus has broader journal coverage but differs from WoSCC in indexing standards, citation records, and document classification.32 Therefore, the present findings should be interpreted as a knowledge map of WoSCC-indexed literature rather than a complete representation of all global evidence. The exclusion of non-English publications, regional journals, and conference abstracts was intended to ensure methodological consistency and reproducibility, yet it may have reduced coverage of regionally important evidence. The results therefore represent the research landscape up to January 1, 2026, within the WoSCC database. Citation- and co-citation-based indicators are inherently time dependent, which may lead to underestimation of recently published high-quality studies. This citation-lag effect is particularly relevant in rapidly evolving fields such as ICI-associated cardiotoxicity, where clinically important studies may not yet have accumulated sufficient citations.

Bibliometric visualizations generated using VOSviewer and CiteSpace depend on parameter settings, including time slicing, threshold selection, and clustering algorithms. Although commonly adopted and reproducible parameters were applied, alternative settings may yield variations in network structure and clustering results.

Bibliometric indicators reflect publication and citation patterns but cannot directly assess the methodological quality, risk of bias, or clinical evidence level of individual studies. The findings should therefore be interpreted as a quantitative overview of research activity and knowledge structure rather than as a substitute for systematic reviews or evidence-based clinical guidelines. Despite these limitations, this study provides a systematic knowledge map of ICI-associated cardiotoxicity and offers data-driven insights into its intellectual structure, clinical relevance, and emerging research trends.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis delineates the evolution of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)—associated cardiovascular toxicity over the past decade, showing its transition from an initial clinical safety signal to a mature interdisciplinary research field. In line with the three-stage trajectory identified in this study, research has progressed from early signal recognition and phenotype definition to mechanistic exploration and diagnostic standardization, and the field is now entering a precision management stage characterized by precision risk prediction and individualized intervention. The global research landscape is characterized by rapid expansion in publication output alongside marked regional differentiation in academic influence. The intellectual structure of the field has consolidated around three major thematic pillars: myocarditis as the dominant clinical phenotype, drug- and regimen-specific safety profiling, and the establishment of cardio-oncology as a structured discipline. Current frontiers are shifting toward precision risk assessment, cardiovascular safety management in complex combination regimens, long-term outcome optimization, and mechanism-guided cardioprotective strategies. Digital health technologies and artificial intelligence are emerging as fundamental vehicles for precision risk prediction. By associating bibliometrically identified biomarkers and risk factors with clinical characteristics, imaging parameters, and dynamic biomarker trajectories, AI-based models may help address future monitoring challenges through continuous surveillance, real-time risk updating, and early detection of cardiotoxicity. Future advances will depend on stronger international collaboration, particularly the establishment of large prospective cohorts, as well as deeper integration of multi-omics and clinical imaging to bridge mechanistic discoveries and clinical implementation. Such efforts may improve cardiovascular safety and survivorship outcomes in patients receiving cancer therapy while reducing the broader clinical and socioeconomic burden associated with ICI-related cardiotoxicity, including treatment interruption, healthcare utilization, productivity loss, and long-term survivorship care costs.

Protocol for simplified parallel perturbations using an abridged long non-coding RNA CRISPR library
Authors: Joshua M. Hazan, Nisrine Lahoud-Jeries, Assaf C. Bester
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryHigh-throughput CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screens are invaluable for discovering novel functional genes, but applying such screens to long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) is more challenging. Here, we present a protocol for designing and executing pooled CRISPRi screens targeting lncRNAs using an abridged cell-type-specific dual single-guide RNA (sgRNA) library. We describe steps for library design and synthesis, followed by stable lentiviral transduction. We then provide guidelines for performing multiple parallel perturbations tailored to the research question, followed by gRNA amplification and data analysis.

Before you begin

The screen described here employs the CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) system, which inhibits gene expression by interfering with transcription initiation and elongation. Phenotypic pooled screening of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) using CRISPRi presents several challenges that require optimization during experimental design and execution.1,2,3,4,5,6 The biological effect of perturbing lncRNAs is often smaller than that of protein-coding genes, and lncRNA annotation remains incomplete. These factors frequently lead to a low signal-to-noise ratio. Furthermore, lncRNAs often overlap with protein-coding genes and DNA regulatory elements such as enhancers that may also be affected by CRISPRi,7 thereby increasing the risk of false positive identifications. Therefore, optimization and careful experimental design are critical when performing large-scale, parallel CRISPRi experiments targeting lncRNAs.8,9,10

The protocol described here was optimized using the K562 chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) cell line. This cell line was chosen due to its efficient transduction by VSV-G pseudotyped lentiviral vectors, ease of sorting, and ability to grow in suspension under standard conditions with minimal cell death or phenotypic alterations. These characteristics facilitate the maintenance and screening of large cell populations, a typical requirement for pooled CRISPRi screens. While optimized for K562 cells, this protocol can be adapted for other cell types with appropriate modifications.CRITICAL: This protocol assumes a basic understanding of lentivirus production and cell culture. Innovation

This protocol provides an efficient and novel approach for conducting high-throughput CRISPR screens tailored to the unique challenges of lncRNA research, in particular their high specificity to cell type and cellular context. To account for the cell type specificity of lncRNAs, we chose lncRNA targets based on high expression and high transcription factor binding at their promoters in a given cell line to design an abridged sgRNA library. To improve knockdown efficiency, we employed a previously described dual sgRNA system.11 To account for the cellular context-specific roles of lncRNAs, the small size of the library can be exploited to perform multiple perturbation screens in parallel, thereby improving screening efficiency at a manageable scale.

Designing the dual sgRNA library

Timing: 1 day

Here we describe the process of designing a dual sgRNA focused library targeting predicted functional lncRNAs in K562. Our recent study indicated that functional lncRNAs tend to be relatively highly expressed. Furthermore, we observed that we could effectively target promoters based on their DNase sensitivity sites18 and TF peaks; this was also associated with gene functionality and KD efficiency.19 Moreover, focused libraries tend to show an improved signal-to-noise ratio. Depending on the cell line, perturbation strategy, and overall aim of the screening, adjust the parameters given here as necessary. Planning and performing the parallel perturbation screens

Timing: up to 2 weeks

Timing: up to 2 weeks

This section describes the approach used for the parallel perturbations in our dropout screening strategy. Each perturbation was chosen based on conditions that might be found in leukemia or the general cancer microenvironment, namely: proliferation without challenge, glucose starvation, oxidative stress, acidosis, and the induction of differentiation. Other than induction of differentiation, all perturbations were performed under relatively mild conditions over a longer period to ensure sufficient dropout of sgRNAs without fully eliminating them from analysis. Expected outcomes

This protocol is intended to maximize lncRNA screening efficiency through focused library design paired with a broad range of perturbations to uncover cell type- and context-specific functions of lncRNAs. Based on the output of the read counts analysis, a volcano plot can be constructed to identify significantly under- or over-represented genes from each challenge. It is further recommended to explore the relative change in each sgRNA pair across the biological replicates (Figure 9C).

Upon identifying genes of interest from the screens, individual hits should be chosen from each challenge and characterized by perturbing the individual hit to identify the biological mechanism behind the chosen target.

While this protocol describes a CRISPRi-based system for perturbation, alternative analysis approaches can be used, such as CRISPRa or Cas13. While some algorithms rely on the effect of a single sgRNA, we recommend approaches that take into consideration the effect of multiple sgRNAs targeting the same TSS. Nevertheless, due to the poor annotation of many lncRNAs, it is expected that some sgRNAs will not be effective. The inclusion of high TF binding as a parameter for selecting the target lncRNAs is expected to mitigate this concern, as we consider TF binding to be indicative of an active promoter. Additionally, we recommend checking the read counts for all the sgRNA pairs individually if there is suspicion of incorrectly targeted promoters.

Limitations

Obtaining good results for a pooled lncRNA CRISPRi screen depends on a well-designed library targeting properly annotated lncRNAs and TSSs. Information such as TF occupancy and DNase A hypersensitivity sites may increase the efficiency of the screening; however, these data are limited to several cell lines and may not be fully comprehensive.

The screening strategy described here depends on lentiviral transduction; while the common chimeric lenti-VSV-G pseudoviral system has wide tropism, some cells are hard to transduce, limiting the use of this system.

The efficiency of the dual gRNA vectors may vary, and this is a factor to keep in mind. Indeed, there is evidence that sgRNA expression efficiency may vary depending on which promoter controls each sgRNA in the pair.34 We expect the inclusion of multiple sgRNA pairs per lncRNA to reduce this problem, but not entirely.

Additionally, some lncRNAs are known to overlap cis-regulatory regions, and some results may be due to enhancer silencing rather than a direct effect of the lncRNA. However, this can be clarified by further characterization of individual hits, as necessary (see step 47e).

Although we did not observe silencing of the CRISPRi cassette over time in the K562 cell line, this may be a concern in other cell lines. Therefore, it is recommended to perform the screening as soon as possible after validating CRISPRi efficiency.

Finally, lncRNAs with a mild biological effect may be hard to identify, especially when the signal-to-noise ratio is difficult to optimize.

There was poor or insufficient dropout of the positive controls, or poor dropout generally.

Potential solution

•Go back and test the CRISPRi system with the positive control sgRNAs (see “confirming CRISPRi efficiency in the transduced cells”) to ensure that the knockdown efficiency has not degraded over time.•Check that the challenge provides sufficient perturbation to the cells to provide good dropout without allowing the cells to undergo adaptation or, conversely, to die too fast and deplete the sgRNAs of interest.

Predictive determinants of CIN II + lesions in women over 65 years old: the contribution of HPV, colposcopy, and double immunostaining techniques
Authors: Gozde Sahin, Filiz Yuksel Ulu, Ilkbal Temel Yuksel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade II or higher (CIN II+) is hard to diagnose in women over 65 years of age since age-related anatomical changes take place, and there is a paucity of data. The aim of the study was to identify predictive clinical, virological, colposcopic, and immunohistochemical predictors of CIN II + lesions in this less studied population.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 364 postmenopausal women aged ≥ 65 years who underwent colposcopy due to abnormal cytology and/or positive human papillomavirus (HPV) test results. Data included HPV test results, cytology, colposcopic findings, biopsy results, and p16/Ki67 dual immunostaining. Univariate logistic regression was used to determine risk factors associated with CIN II+.ResultsCIN II + lesions were detected in 6.0% of the patients (n = 22). 100% of the CIN II + cases were p16/Ki67 positive and 0% were in negative-staining cases (p < 0.001). CIN II + prevalence was significantly higher among HPV-positive women (9.9%) compared with HPV-negative women (0.7%) (p < 0.001). Among HPV genotypes, HPV 18 was associated with the highest CIN II + rate (20%). Being sexually active (OR: 4.847; p = 0.002), persistent HPV infection (OR: 1.512; p = 0.013), HPV positivity (OR: 16.602; p = 0.006), HPV 18 infection (OR: 5.042; p = 0.033), and pathologic colposcopic findings (OR: 5.503; p = 0.007) were all significantly associated with CIN II + lesions.ConclusionIn women aged ≥ 65 years, CIN II + lesions are strongly linked to HPV positivity, persistent infection, high-risk types such as HPV 18, dual p16/Ki67 positivity, and abnormal colposcopy. This argues for individualized, multimodal cervical screening in elderly women, combining HPV genotype–based risk stratification, biomarker triage (p16/Ki-67), and clinical factors to improve detection and reduce unnecessary procedures.Keywords: CIN II+, Colposcopy, HPV, P16/Ki67, Postmenopausal women, Elderly women, Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia

Background

Cervical cancer continues to be a leading cancer-related death in women, especially in low- and middle-income nations, despite enhanced screening and vaccination strategies [1]. Although guidelines increasingly suggest the abandonment of cervical cancer screening beyond the age of ≥ 65 years, accumulating evidence supports that older women persist at considerable risk of high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and invasive cancer [2, 3]. This risk is particularly relevant for women who did not receive adequate screening earlier or those who harbor persistent high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) infections [4].

Postmenopausal anatomical changes, such as cervical atrophy, stenosis, and retraction of the transformation zone (TZ) into the endocervical canal, significantly impair the performance of conventional colposcopic evaluation in elderly women [5]. When the TZ is classified as type 3 (TZ3), which is common in this age group, colposcopy becomes technically limited and may result in underdiagnosis [6]. Furthermore, persistent HPV infections—even in the absence of cytologic abnormalities—are frequently detected in women aged ≥ 65 years and have been associated with high-grade lesions upon histopathological examination [7].

Molecular diagnostics, including extended HPV genotyping and dual p16/Ki-67 staining, transcend cytology and colposcopy limitations. p16/Ki-67 staining provides specificity gain and improves detection of transforming HPV infections, particularly in equivocal cytological or colposcopic findings [8]. Data indicate that high-risk non-16/18 HPV types have a significant contribution to CIN II + lesions in postmenopausal women and emphasize the need for extended genotyping [9]. ECC and other procedures enhance diagnostic yield in TZ3 settings [10].

Given these challenges and the limited age-stratified evidence, this study aims to investigate the predictive value of clinical, virological, cytological, colposcopic, and immunohistochemical parameters—particularly p16/Ki-67 dual staining—in identifying CIN II + lesions among women aged 65 and older undergoing colposcopy.

Methods

This retrospective observational study was carried out at Basaksehir Cam and Sakura City Hospital in Istanbul, Turkey after receiving approval from the local ethics committee (Approval date: 29/01/2025 and approval number: 2025-28). Postmenopausal women aged ≥ 65 years who had undergone colposcopy for abnormal cytology and/or HPV test positivity were included in this study.

Study population

All participants were confirmed to be postmenopausal. Patients with known immunodeficiency, history of cervical cancer, or incomplete medical records were excluded. HPV vaccination was recommended to all patients regardless of age, but acceptance was left to patient preference. For those previously vaccinated, a booster dose was not recommended. Clinical data including age, parity, smoking status, sexual activity status, previous HPV-related procedures, and gynecologic surgeries were collected from medical records.

HPV testing and cytology

HPV DNA testing was conducted with the Cobas® 4800 HPV Test (Roche Diagnostics), which identifies the HPV 16 and 18 genotypes separately but reports collectively the presence of 12 additional high-risk genotypes. The HPV results were categorized into three groups: NILM (Negative for Intraepithelial Lesion or Malignancy), positive, and further stratified by genotype. Conventional cytological evaluation was based on the Bethesda 2014 classification system. The initial HPV test and cytology results were obtained during the patients’ first referral visit. The interval between the initial evaluation and the colposcopy appointment was approximately 15 days. Liquid-based cytology was performed in nearly all cases. Cytological findings were grouped into negative, atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance (ASC-US), low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL), high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL), atypical glandular cells (AGC) and adenocarcinoma.

Colposcopic evaluation and histopathology

Colposcopy was done by experienced gynecologic oncologists following standard techniques. The transformation zone (TZ) was typed into types 1, 2, and 3 according to the IFCPC 2011 guidelines. Colposcopy adequacy and abnormality detected were noted.

All patients underwent a cervical biopsy and/or endocervical curettage (ECC). Directed biopsies were taken if lesions did extend to the upper canal or vagina. All histopathological tests were categorized by a gynecologic pathologist as normal, CIN I, CIN II, CIN III, or invasive carcinoma.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical dual staining for p16 and Ki-67 was performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) biopsy tissue using the CINtec® PLUS Cytology Kit (Roche mtm laboratories AG, Heidelberg, Germany). Although originally designed for alcohol-fixed cytology specimens, the assay was adapted for FFPE sections. Serial sections of 3–4 μm thickness were cut and processed on the Ventana BenchMark ULTRA platform with a modified protocol. Deparaffinization and rehydration were performed on-instrument. Antigen retrieval was carried out with EDTA-based buffer (pH 9.0) at 95–100 °C for 20 min. Staining was performed sequentially with p16INK4a (clone E6H4, DAB chromogen, brown cytoplasmic/nuclear staining) and Ki-67 (clone MIB-1, Fast Red chromogen, red nuclear staining). After mild hematoxylin counterstaining, sections were dehydrated and coverslipped. Dual positivity was defined as the co-expression of nuclear Ki-67 and p16 in the same epithelial cell, with histological epithelial architecture considered during evaluation.

Outcome Definitions.

Outcome definitions

The primary outcome was the presence of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade II or higher (CIN II+). The relationship between clinical, virological, cytological, colposcopic, and immunohistochemical parameters and the presence of CIN II + was analyzed.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were expressed as median and IQR for continuous data and frequency with percentage for categorical data. Normality was assessed with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. SPSS version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for data analysis.

Comparisons between CIN II + and CIN I– groups were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables. Univariate logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with CIN II+. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) and corresponding p-values were calculated. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

There were 364 postmenopausal women who underwent colposcopy due to abnormal cytology and/or HPV test results, with a median age of 68 years (IQR: 66–71) and median parity of 3 (IQR: 2–5). Most of the participants (89.8%) were non-smokers and had one lifetime sexual partner (99.5%). 43.4% were sexually active at the time of evaluation. A history of HPV-related surgical treatment was present in 5.8% of the patients, in the form of LEEP, ECC, and cervical biopsies. Also, 9.1% had a history of gynecologic surgery, the most frequent being total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. One patient (0.3%) was immunocompromised from previous radiotherapy (Table 1).

Among the 364 patients, high-risk HPV was positive in 58.2% and negative in 41.8%. HPV 16 was detected in 12.9%, HPV 18 in 5.5%, HPV 16 + other types in 3.0%, HPV 18 + other types in 1.1%, and other high-risk types alone in 35.7%. HPV was assessed with the Cobas test in all patients. In 28.3% of cases, HPV status changed from negative to positive over time, with a median duration of infection of 1 year (IQR: 0–2). Initial cytology revealed ASC-US in 44.2%, LSIL in 10.4%, ASC-H in 5.2%, HSIL in 4.9%, AGC in 3.8%, and adenocarcinoma in 0.5%; 30.8% had NILM. Repeat cytology was done in 82.1%, and the results were ASC-US (15.1%), ASC-H (1.3%), HSIL (1.3%), LSIL (4.7%), and NILM (76.9%). Liquid-based cytology was performed in 99.5%.

Colposcopy was performed in all patients and was adequate in 94.8%. In 55.5%, abnormal findings were noted, including acetowhite epithelium (47.5%), atypical vessels (1.9%), punctation (0.5%), mosaicism (0.5%), and some other minor findings. Transformation zone was observed in 91.2%, most frequent type 3 (52.5%).

Biopsy was achieved in 99.7% of the cases. Histopathology revealed chronic cervicitis (33.5%), CIN I (24.5%), CIN II (6.0%), CIN III (3.8%), carcinoma (various types) in 3.5%. Excision was performed in 15.4%, most often disclosing chronic cervicitis (25%), CIN II (21.4%), or CIN III (17.9%). Dual immunostaining was undertaken in 87.6%, with 70.2% positivity (Table 2).

CIN II + lesions were diagnosed in 22 (6.0%) of 364 women. The CIN II + group was a little younger (66.5 vs. 68 years, p = 0.201) and of equal parity (median 3 in both groups, p = 0.740). Median HPV infection duration was significantly longer in CIN II + patients (2 vs. 1 year, p < 0.001). Smoking was more frequent in the CIN II + group (18.2% vs. 9.6%, p = 0.261), and sexual activity was significantly higher (77.3% vs. 41.2%, p < 0.001). Prior HPV-related or gynecologic surgery did not differ significantly. HPV positivity was greater in CIN II + patients (95.5% vs. 55.8%, p < 0.001). HPV 18 was strongly associated with CIN II+ (19.0% vs. 8.4%, p = 0.041), but HPV 16 and other high-risk HPV were not significantly different. Colposcopy was adequate in most, with similar adequacy and transformation zone visibility in both groups. Yet, the abnormal colposcopic findings were far more frequent in the CIN II + group (86.4% versus 53.5%, p = 0.003). Type 3 transformation zone was the most prevalent subtype in both groups. Positive p16/Ki-67 immunostaining was found in all patients with CIN II+ (100% versus 24.6%, p < 0.001) (Table 4).

On univariate logistic regression, a number of factors were significantly associated with CIN II + lesions. Increasing duration of HPV infection was an independent predictor (OR: 1.512, 95% CI: 1.093–2.093, p = 0.013). Sexual activity (OR: 4.847, 95% CI: 1.748–13.443, p = 0.002) and positive HPV test result (OR: 16.602, 95% CI: 2.208–124.828, p = 0.006) were also significantly associated with higher risk. Among all HPV genotypes, HPV 18 had a significant association with CIN II+ (OR: 5.042, 95% CI: 1.137–22.364, p = 0.033), while HPV 16 and combined types were not statistically significant. Abnormal colposcopy results were the other independent predictor (OR: 5.503, 95% CI: 1.599–18.940, p = 0.007). Age, parity, smoking, previous surgeries, colposcopy adequacy, transformation zone visibility and type, and immunostaining had no statistically significant associations (Table 5).

Discussion

This research assessed various predictive clinical, virological, and histopathological parameters of CIN II + lesions in women ≥ 65 years that had undergone colposcopic examination due to abnormal cytology and/or positive HPV test results. The research results indicated that positive HPV test result, increased infection duration, HPV type 18 presence, sexual activity, abnormal colposcopic results, and double p16/Ki-67 immunostaining positivity had strong correlations with the existence of CIN II + lesions. These results stress the significance of the risk of high-grade cervical lesions in postmenopausal women that cannot be overlooked. It should be noted that in our study dual staining was applied to FFPE biopsy tissue with an adapted protocol, and therefore the results should not be directly compared with the sensitivity and specificity values reported for cytology-based dual staining triage tests. With all the physiological and anatomical changes occurring within this group, including cervix atrophy and transformation zone retraction, our findings offer precious information regarding the diagnostic obstacles and limitations of conventional screening procedures. The findings support the necessity for risk-based models of assessment and other diagnostic approaches for older women.

In our study, positive HPV test and increased duration of infection were both strongly associated with CIN II + lesions. Women who had positive HPV tests as per reports had 16.6 times higher risk, and each year of continuous infection increased the risk factor by a factor of 1.5. These findings confirm the hypothesis that it is the duration, more so than the presence, of HPV infection that is the critical factor in the development of high-grade cervical neoplasia. Muñoz et al. demonstrated that persistent HPV 16 infection beyond 10.8 months strongly increased the risk of CIN III by 19.5-fold [11]. Similarly, the Ludwig-McGill cohort reported slower clearance rates for prevalent HPV infections compared to incident infections, particularly in the situation of co-infections [12]. The findings emphasize that ongoing high-risk HPV infection detection is a principal factor in cervical cancer development, even among older women, and that time-based HPV monitoring can provide significant clinical benefits to this demographic.

Among clinical parameters analyzed, sexual activity was most closely associated with CIN II + lesions. Sexual activity in women was 4.8-fold more likely compared to inactive women (p = 0.002), indicating sexual behavior as a risk factor that is still valid despite advanced age. This result is in accordance with the hypothesis that reactivated or persistent HPV infections are due to ongoing exposure or reactivation from immune senescence. In contrast, other clinical factors such as age, parity, smoking, and history of previous gynecologic or HPV-related surgeries were not independently associated with CIN II + development in our series. These results are in accord with prior research showing that while certain risk factors diminish after menopause, sexual behavior remains relevant through viral persistence [4, 13]. Thus, clinicians should still consider sexual history when assessing risk in older HPV-positive women.

We found in our study that abnormal colposcopy results were closely linked to CIN II + lesions (OR: 5.50; 95% CI: 1.60–18.94, p = 0.007), indicating that visible pathologic changes are still important markers despite anatomical changes with age. However, colposcopy adequacy (p = 0.407) and the visibility of the transformation zone (p = 0.478) were not strongly associated with CIN II+. Furthermore, the TZ type was not predictive of CIN II + because no statistical difference was found for type 2 (p = 0.462) or type 3 (p = 0.865) zones. These results indicate that challenges are encountered in the precise colposcopic examination of older women, particularly when the TZ is located inside the endocervical canal or when severe atrophy prevents visualization. Earlier studies have described the diagnostic limitations of colposcopy in postmenopausal women and stressed the necessity to complement visual inspections with objective biomarkers and histopathological sampling in order to obtain improved diagnostic accuracy in this group of women [4, 6, 14–16].

Our results demonstrated that all CIN II + patients were positive for dual immunostaining with p16/Ki-67, thereby showing its high diagnostic sensitivity among them. Although statistical significance could not be established with the regression model—possibly because of the small number of patients who were tested—the 100% positivity among those with histologically confirmed high-grade lesions makes it a promising adjunct diagnostic test. These results are concordant with other reports that p16/Ki-67 dual staining increases sensitivity and specificity in the detection of high-grade cervical lesions, especially in cases where cytology and colposcopy are uncertain. Our findings are consistent with other reports in the literature. In line with our results, a Polish study by Rokita et al. also demonstrated 100% sensitivity of the CINtecPLUS dual staining test in identifying high-grade cervical lesions [17]. Secosan et al. demonstrated improved diagnostic accuracy of p16/Ki-67 in women with ASC-US or LSIL cytology [18]. Wentzensen et al. also reported 93.2% sensitivity for CIN3 + detection, with this increasing to 100% in women 30 years and older who were HPV16 positive [19]. In older women, where colposcopic challenges are frequent, this form of immunostaining provides a valuable approach to triage and early identification of clinically significant lesions.

There are some limitations in this study. First, the retrospective design may carry with it inherent selection and information biases. Second, p16/Ki-67 immunostaining was not performed on all patients, making generalizability of our findings on this marker impossible. Third, colposcopy may have been affected by inter-observer variability and anatomical challenges in older women, such as cervical stenosis and type 3 transformation zone, that may affect diagnostic precision.

Conclusions

In women aged ≥ 65, HPV positivity, infection duration, abnormal colposcopic findings, and p16/Ki-67 positivity were strongly linked to CIN II + lesions. Due to diagnostic limitations, integrating clinical and immunohistochemical findings may improve early detection and guide management in this age group.

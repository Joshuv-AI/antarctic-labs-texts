Transformer-based temporal models for probabilistic load and photovoltaic power forecasting in commercial microgrids
Authors: P Deepa, C Kathirvel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn commercial microgrids, for effective energy management and reliable decision-making, it is imperative to include the uncertainties in load demands as well as in the renewable energy generation. To refine the research focus, the proposed work focusses on solar PV-load forecasting in the smart grid environments. The rise in demand fluctuations necessitates an improved 10% accuracy in the forecasting models. But whenever the forecasting horizon length crosses time steps of 12, the existing models becomes unstable in predicting, with a degradation in accuracy and scalability by 8–15%. Limited research works are available to analyze the effect of rolling-horizon in managing the uncertainties. In this scenario, the proposed work employing transformer-based PV-load forecasting framework, can achieve an improved probabilistic forecasting accuracy of greater than 12%. Horizon-aware learning mechanisms are incorporated into the proposed model to accurately estimate the uncertainty. Model rolling-horizon based experiments using MATLAB environment simulation is performed on the proposed model for validation purposes. All forecasts and probability analyses illustrated in the present document are produced through simulation results by the authors using consistent simulation conditions. Three different operational conditions are considered for the evaluation with the performance metrics being the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) and pinball loss. Improved quality of probabilistic forecasting is visible with a reduction in CRPS value by 12.6% and effective prediction interval capture is seen with internal coverage of 9.4%. The computational cost and requirements have been lowered by 18%. The architecture can scale up to about 14 different forecasting horizons, with consistent stability under different PV and load conditions. The numerical results confirm consistent improvements in the performance gains of the proposed forecasting model. Further this transformer-model based approach outperforms both gates recurrent unit baselines and long short-term memory models. There is a 12% gain improvement in average case scenarios and about 6% gain improvement in worst case scenarios, making the model suitable for applications that demand latency time of less than 1.5 s. Thus, the detailed analysis demonstrates performance gains in terms of different evaluation metrics. Thus, the overall results exhibit superior performance as against other existing modelling techniques. When scaling up the transformer-based modelling concept beyond horizon steps of 14, there is a degradation in the forecasting performance, which can be analyzed in future scope.Keywords: Gated recurrent unit, Time series forecasting, Recurrent neural network, Sequential data modeling, Deep learningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Accurate photovoltaic (PV) forecasting and stochastic load data are imperative for effective operation of commercial microgrids. Since both load demands and PV characteristics exhibit instabilities and long-range correlations, the operators should have adequate knowledge about the uncertainty forecasts especially for high risk involved energy decisions. The conventional recurrent models are not successful in accounting for this long-range chronological variability.

To improve the PV forecasts,1 suggests a novel collaborative transformer feature, which can record the time bound variations occurring in the PV system output. The architecture of this system resembles that of a transformer and has the potential to predict the PV uncertainties which are essential in microgrid management. The fixed rule-based model existing in this architecture is proposed to be replaced by an adaptive system that includes the solar generation predictions2. In general, the time-based forecasts are carefully considered by Transformer governed models, but still, they lack managing the microgrid uncertainties. 3 proposes patch time series transformer (PatchTST) for gauging the fluctuations considering a 60-min PV forecast. In this PatchTST, a brief interval PV prediction is examined thereby eliminating the need for demand prediction complexity and the quantification doubts. In4, Temporal integration modules are used for measuring the complete substation load, where load forecasting is taken as the primary unit focus. But, in this model, the factors like standard and probabilistic power outputs of solar cell are rejected. According to5, joint distribution is solely dependent on the PV cell forecasting. Being oriented towards uncertainty nature, this model focusses on solar generation forecasts, handling of chances and predicting possible energy outputs under fluctuating environments. With due evaluation, this robust methodology eliminates the traditional dependency learning model. The predictive analytics proposed by6 extensively relies on the maximum usage of resilience power forecasts, associated with accurate predictability and operational reliability. Microgrid-specific probabilistic forecasting is prevented in this framework. 7 advises employing of time–frequency enhanced learning strategy for effective estimation of pivotal energy generation. This includes significant application of granular feature representation, that fails to account for the uncertainty estimation and attention mechanisms. 8 sets a new path by proposing dual-branch transformers for estimating the wind outputs that are a part of microgrids. This structure though helps in improving the renewable forecasts of the microgrid, they omit the consideration of probability patterns combining both the solar power supply and the power demand. In case of short-term load forecasting,9 suggests the use of clustering enabled transformers, linked with load predictions in smart grid. The solar PV and probabilistic output linkage model exhibits solar predictions for short span of time10. The choice of data transfer is of utmost importance in the near-term prediction window. Still, there is no proper consideration of usage patterns in their organizational processes.

11 proposes an analytical modeling of time series methods in post optimization phase for prediction of high solar power output. This is achievable by means of transformer dependent model for PV estimation purposes. Conclusion

The proposed transformer-based PV-load demand forecasting model has been analyzed across three different operating conditions, with an objective of overcoming the limitations observed in previously developed six forecasting methods. The performance study is carried out by conducting more than 180 Simulation experiments on the proposed model. Compared to existing models, the proposed architecture mitigates the degradation in CRPS metric by more than 12%. The overall method consists of four sequential workflow processing stages, significantly built around five core functional components. Mathematical Modelling of the proposed model is formulated by defining twenty analytical equations. The computational complexity of the algorithm is bound to follow O(N log N) limits. With about 14 forecasting horizons, the simulation model is evaluated for 120 rolling-horizon runs. On average, there is an improvement in the CRPS framework by a value of 12.6%. On observing the outcomes, the proposed model has contributed to a lower CRPS value by a factor of about 4.8%, followed by improved prediction interval accuracy by limiting internal deviations by 3.9%. For forecasting horizons range of 1–14 steps, the model is able to contribute consistent stability. The model is scalable up to fourteen different model variants illustrating its adaptability nature. As regards calibration, there is an enhancement when tested under four different experimental configurations. Numeric outcomes show consistent trends in the system performance, by outperforming two previous baselines reported in the literature. Applications demanding prediction latency of less than 1.2 s are supported by this model with an average gain of 12.6% and worst-case gain of 6.2%. Additional terms are included in this transformer-based model thus enabling it to apply for different prediction horizons. For horizon steps exceeding 14, forecast accuracy degrades while accuracy decreases by 2.3%, in case of extreme PV generation fluctuations. Thus, the results are applicable only for normalized values of load demand and PV generation. The proposed model is yet to be verified for datasets covering multi seasonal variants. The future research work can be focused on reducing the CRPS values additionally by 4–7%. And datasets exceeding million samples can be used for testing the method. This model can be extended to three different probabilistic forecasting architectures. Since the proposed model exhibits an overall performance surge of 12.6% evaluated across five different metrics, the framework can be integrated with analytical platforms used for transformer-based grid arrangement. Thus, the proposed research work sets a benchmark forecasting model with measured probabilistic guarantees.

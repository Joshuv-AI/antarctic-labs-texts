Applicability of Cameriere's Method for Dental Age Assessment and Its Relationship with Skeletal Age (MP3) and Chronological Age in North Indian Children
Authors: Temjenmenla Jamir, Sonali Saha, Kavita Dhinsa, Jitendra Bhagchandani, Abhishek Sinha, Gunjan Yadav
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIdentification of age is imperative for diversity of reasons including disputed birth records, legal issues, etc. Several growth assessment parameters like bone age, dental age, and the combination of both have been applied for different populations with variable outcomes. It is well documented that the chronological age does not necessarily correlate with the maturational status of a child. Hence, efforts were made to determine a child's developmental age by using dental and skeletal age. The knowledge of bone age and dental age is of great importance for pediatricians and pediatric dentists. It is essential for dentists to formulate treatment plans and it is a source of complementary information for pediatricians.AimsTo assess the applicability of Cameriere's method for dental age assessment and its relationship with skeletal age (MP3) and chronological age in North Indian children.Materials and methodsChildren aged between 6 and 14 years were randomly selected. Chronological age was assessed by subtracting date of radiograph from the date of birth. The six modified MP3 stages given by Rajagopal and Kansal were used for skeletal age assessment. Dental age assessment was done based on Cameriere's method. Statistical analysis used: it was analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 21.ResultsAmong skeletal age subgroup of genders the calculated dental age based on Cameriere's method overestimated the chronological age significantly.ConclusionsOverestimated dental age by Cameriere's method in North Indian children suggests a need for new regression equation for a better correlation with skeletal age and chronological age.How to cite this articleJamir T, Saha S, Dhinsa K, et al. Introduction

The most important part of forensic medicine and treatment planning is done with the help of estimation of age. In orthodontics, by assessing the pubertal growth spurts and skeletal age one can leads to proper diagnosis, treatment planning and retention phase.1,2 Three most common age estimation methods are Demirjian's, Cameriere's and Nolla's method.3,4 Among all of the above methods used, one proposed by Cameriere et al. was commonly used in Italian children by deriving a linear regression formula.5,6

Keeping this in mind, the aim of this study was to check feasibility of Cameriere's method for dental age estimation as well as its relationship with skeletal age (MP3) and chronological age in children from North Indian population.

Materials and Methods

A total of 100 subjects aged between 6 and 14 years was randomly picked from the outpatient department and divided into two groups based on their gender (55 males and 45 females).

Study samples who are nourished, free of any serious systemic illness, and have no bone deformities or metabolic disorders, no history of any previous orthodontic treatment and good quality radiograph were included for the study. Malnourished and syndromic children with developmental anomalies, cysts or tumors, tooth fractures and third molar, unclear or distorted radiographs were excluded from the study. The study was started after obtaining the ethical clearance from the Institutional Ethics Committee.

The study was assessed by three parameters:

Chronological Age Assessment: Assessment of chronological age was done by subtracting the date of birth from the date on which radiograph was taken.

Skeletal Age Assessment: The six modified MP3 stages given by Rajagopal and Kansal were used for assessment of skeletal age. Digital radiographic system with standard exposure parameters (70 kV, 7 mA, 0.08 seconds) was used to take periapical radiographs of middle phalanx of third finger specifically nonworking hand (Fig. 1).

Digital radiographic system with standard exposure parameters (70 kV, 7 mA, 0.08 seconds) was used to take periapical radiographs of middle phalanx of third finger specifically nonworking hand (Fig. 1).

Dental Age Assessment by Cameriere's Method: Two-dimensional panoramic radiograph using cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) (I-cat machine) that uses exposure parameters of 16 × 11 FOV, 10.11 MA, 120 kVp, and 4.8 seconds was used. Dental age assessment was done based on linear regression equation as given by Cameriere's method (Fig. 2). An in vivo software was used in which seven permanent teeth with closed apex and complete development of root were taken. This was marked as (N0). Also, in case of tooth with single root, the distance between inner side of open apex was calculated and summed up (Ai, i = 1,… 5). Also, in teeth with two roots, the measurement was done between two open apices (Ai, i = 6, 7). To avoid any bias and any differences which can occur due to different angulation and magnifications, the measured values were divided from the total length of the tooth (Li, I = 1,… 7). The maturity was also assessed using a formula (xi = Ai/Li,i = 1.,.. 7), the sum of the normalized open apices (s), and the number of teeth with complete root development (N0). Discussion

Most important objective for human beings is to establish age of majority, for adoption, illegal migration is commonly done by calculation of age estimation.7 Estimation of age is done by different ways such as chronological age, skeletal age and dental age.8 In many instances, biological and chronological age may not be the same; hence, accurate information can be provided by developmental stages of tooth.9 In this modern era, for growth assessment, epiphyseal development of the middle phalanx of the third finger (MP3) is advisable rather than taking complete hand wrist radiographs.10 Hagg and Taranger originated development of epiphyseal stages with a use of conventional radiographs. Supplement to this, Rajagopal and Kansal added one more bone stage called the MP3-HI stage.11 As there is a paradigm shift, therefore nowadays radiovisiography is used for evaluation of epiphyseal growth of middle phalanx.12

A study mentioned by Madhu et al. showed that there exists a correlation between chronological age and the MP3 stages in both genders. According to them this method is reliable, pocket-friendly and simple.13 However, skeletal methods present some drawbacks that are commonly affected by the environment such as variation in bone maturation.14 The most reliable indicator of biological maturity is mostly by development of tooth which is less affected by endocrine and nutritional status.15

According to Camereiere's et al., when open apices are measured using digital radiographs of mandibular teeth, the correlation coefficient showed differences were not statistically significant. Also, Hostiuc et al. stated that use of Cameriere's method is beneficial for age estimation with least errors.16 According to Timmins et al., Cameriere's method is useful up to 14 years of age.17

In accordance with Kumaresan et al., Cameriere's method proved to be the most accurate and precise age-estimation method in comparison with the other methods when used on Malaysian population.18

Various methods of age assessment are used like skeletal, dental, and chronological age, but the applicability of each can vary due to the ethnic and gender differences.19

In the present study, a total of 100 subjects (males = 55 and females = 45) between the age-group of 6–14 years were chosen. Bagh et al. stated that this age-group happens is most analytical for estimating dental age and consequently important for planning proper timing for orthodontic therapy.20

In our study, the skeletal age assessment of subjects was done using modified MP3 staging as given by Kansal and Rajagopal. Orthopantomogram (OPG) used for dental age assessment may suffer possible distortions of the image in different regions.5 Hence, the present study utilizes CBCT-based OPG images for an accurate measurement of open apices.

The present study included a total of 100 study subjects with both males (n = 55; mean age: 10.61 years) and females (n = 45; mean age: 10.37 years) of local population between the age range of 6–14 years. As mentioned by Bagh et al. and Wolf et al. also conducted similar studies on their local population with gender dimorphism.19

With regards to skeletal age assessment using modified MP3 staging as given by Kansal and Rajagopal the results of the present study inferred 64% of study subjects in “F” stage, 20% of study subjects in “FG” stage, 5% of subject in “G” stage, 6% of study subjects in “H” stage and 5% of study subjects in “I” stage. Male subjects were found to be in stage “F,” “FG,” and “G” while female subjects were found to be in stage “F,” “FG,” “G,” “H,” and “I.” The male subjects were predominantly between prepeak and peak pubertal growth phase while female subjects were found to be in the three growth phases (prepeak, peak, and postpubertal).

The results were in concordance with Kucukkeles et al. who found 96.3% were MP3-F and MP3-FG (prepeak stage of puberty) subjects, 64.3% were in MP3-G (peak stage of puberty) and 74.9% of subjects had MP3-H and MP3-I (postpeak stage of puberty).18 According to Madhu et al. prepeak was represented by MP3-F and MP3-FG stage; peak by MP3-G stage whereas peak at the post was by MP3-H and MP3-I stages.19

According to Javangula et al., the mean age for initiation for active growth was found to be 9.7 ± 1.5 years in males and 8.8 ± 0.7 years in females. He stated that the mean age for the completion of the growth was 13.9 ± 0.4 years in girls.17 On comparing dental age (as assessed by Cameriere's) with chronologic age in either gender, calculated dental age was significantly higher than chronological age. In other words, an overestimated dental age was evaluated by Cameriere's method when compared to chronological age of study subjects. These findings were in conformity with

Sachdev et al., Vadla et al., and Rai et al.15 Sachdev et al. stated that dental age by Cameriere's method overestimated in males by 0.88 years and females by 0.64 years.20 Similarly, Rai et al. also found overestimation by 0.60 and 0.70 years in girls and boys, respectively.6

Discordant to the results, Ishwarkumar et al. found dental age assessed using Cameriere's method underestimated in the South African population.19

Valluri et al. Cameriere's method showed an underestimation by 0.096 and 0.205 years in girls and boys, respectively.20 Ganepalli et al., Nair, Shreshtha, Pinchi, and El-Bakary et al. also found underestimation of dental age as assessed by Cameriere's method in comparison to chronological age in either gender.17

Srivastava et al. found an overestimated dental age in females while males showed underestimated dental age. He stated that there could be a poor nutritional status of Indian adolescents, mainly females.18

In a country, the growth of children varies due to ethnic, social, and nutritional causes. Therefore, the European formula derived earlier cannot be applied to the Indian population. As mentioned by Rai et al., new formula is mandatory that can account differences between South, North, or Central India.6 In future, more studies with a larger sample size and age estimation using Indian-specific formula or regression equation will prove to be an accurate and reliable method to assess the dental age of the individuals.

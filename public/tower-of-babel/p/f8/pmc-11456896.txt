Exploring the challenges to development and institutionalization of E-learning content as perceived by faculty members of medical schools in Iran: A qualitative content analysis study
Authors: Maryam Zahmatkeshan, Zeinab Naderi, Najmeh Sadeghi Roonizi, Mostafa Bijani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractElectronic learning (E-learning) is a method of acquiring knowledge by using information technology. In this regard, faculty members of medical schools play an important role in the design, development and institutionalization of electronic content education and face many challenges. Hence, the present study was performed to explore the challenges to the development and institutionalization of e-learning content as perceived by faculty members of medical schools in southern Iran. The present study is a descriptive qualitative study. Purposeful sampling was performed. Based on this, 23 individual and in-depth semi-structured interviews were conducted with 23 faculty members of medical schools. Granheim and Lundman's approach was used for data analysis. Three themes and eleven subthemes were extracted based on the qualitative data analysis results. Three main themes included individual challenges, organizational management challenges, and course design challenges. Based on the results of the present study, we should keep in mind that electronic teaching methods and preparation of e-learning content by professors are different from traditional educational methods. Hence, the senior managers of educational system should make necessary arrangements and plans for methodological support, technological and technical support, and all kinds of organizational support and create continuous professional development opportunities for professors to minimize the negative effects caused by the rapid changes of electronics era and educational environments on professors, and consequently on the quality of educational contents and processes.Keywords: E-learning, Faculty, Medical sciences, Challenges, Qualitative study

Introduction

E-learning is one of the phenomena of modern world that has emerged in the information age and the knowledge-based society, and its short history has significantly developed [1]. E-learning has revolutionized teaching methods and played a significant role in completing the learning cycle [2]. Based on the fact that interaction has a fundamental and important role in teaching and learning process, e-learning using emerging technologies provides extensive interactions to access vast information and establish various types of communication [3].

E-learning content is the newest media for learning and attracting learners' attention. It is the most valuable method for creating information in electronic form and making it available at any time, in any place and for anyone's use [4]. Although content development plays a key role in e-learning. Undoubtedly, it is not an easy process. To produce a cohesive whole, one needs specific subject-matter expertise, perseverance in setting the required objectives for quality improvement, and encouragement of creativity in the arrangement and flow of ideas [5]. Based on this, professors play an important role in implementing e-learning tools and technological changes in higher education. However, their participation in the use of e-learning system tools is affected by many motivating and inhibiting factors [6]. With respect to the past studies, technology integration poses significant challenges for educators at every level of the system [7,8]. Although educators generally appreciate the benefits of educational technologies, they often find it challenging to smoothly and effectively integrate new educational technologies, from acquiring new technological equipment to adapting curricula, and teaching techniques to incorporating new teaching tools [9]. One of the challenges associated with the acceptance of e-learning according to Almaiah (2019), is the preparation of universities. This preparation is crucial for the development and institutionalization of e-learning content education. Moreover, technological barriers, lack of justification by managers and officials of the educational system and lack of financial support, standardization of e-learning, individual competencies, and cultural challenges are many other problems in the field of e-learning acceptance [10]. A review of studies shows that few studies have qualitatively explained the obstacles and challenges affecting the development and institutionalization of e-learning content from the point of view of medical science professors. It is very helpful to conduct a qualitative study to deeply understand the concept and determine its dimensions, because qualitative studies help explain the concept in the cultural context from the perspective of people who have a long-term involvement with the phenomenon [11].

Qualitative research is very helpful to explain the challenges, and obstacles affecting the development and institutionalization of e-learning content from the point of view of medical science professors. In fact, qualitative research seeks to understand and discover the world of human experiences. Because each person's experiences will shape the structure of truth. Hence, only by entering the world of people's experiences, the researcher can discover the meaning of phenomena from their point of view [12]. Since limited investigations were performed in this field with a qualitative research approach, the research team decided to conduct research under the title of explaining the challenges, and obstacles affecting the development and institutionalization of e-learning content from the perceived by faculty members of medical schools in southern Iran.

Methods

The present study used a descriptive qualitative design, an effective method to explore a research question and determine the subjects' perception of phenomenon under study [11]. When little is known about a population's perception of a concept, a descriptive qualitative design allows researchers to gain insight into human experiences in their unique context [12].

Data collection

The participants consisted of 23 faculty members of medical schools who were selected via purposeful sampling. In purposeful sampling, the subjects are selected by the researcher. The selected subjects have rich and in-depth knowledge and experience of the concept under study and can provide detailed insight into it [12]. Consequently, the researchers selected individuals who possessed a wealth of knowledge or extensive experience in the field of the study. The inclusion criteria considered in this study were being willing to participate, having at least one year of teaching experience, and signing informed consent to participate. The exclusion criteria were unwillingness to continue cooperation with the study for any reason. In the present study, 23 individual and in-depth semi-structured interviews were performed for data collection. The duration of each interview was 45–60 min. The interviews were conducted in six sessions. All face-to-face interviews were conducted by the first author (MZ) in the conference room after prior arrangements with the participants. The interview guide was developed based on the views of research team and the available literature. The interview guide began with open-ended questions including: “What are the challenges can affect the production and institutionalization of e-learning content? What solutions do you suggest to solve the challenges of e-learning content production? In this study, data saturation was reached after 21 interviews, and two further interviews were done to ensure no new data was available. Yet, the researchers conducted two additional interviews to ensure that the data was saturated.

At the end of each interview, while thanking the participant, the permission was obtained to contact the person for asking any questions about the interview, and finally, by obtaining informed consent from the participants to confirm their statements or the need to conduct further interviews. Their phone numbers were recorded at the end of the interview. Interviews were transcribed immediately after completion and each interview was read several times. Then data analysis was done using MAXQDA software.

Data analysis

Using the content analysis approach of Granheim and Landman (2004) [13], each interview transcription was read several times to immerse in the data and reach a general concept. Then, the words and sentences or paragraphs that contained important points about the obstacles and challenges affecting the development and institutionalization of e-learning content were determined as semantic units. Furthermore, semantic units were labelled with a summary that expressed the meaning of selected unit, and the text was coded. The codes were revised with the text by revisiting the comparison of codes in terms of similarities and differences with each other and merging the similar codes. Based on the similarity and appropriateness of the data, the classification and development of the classes continued, and to ensure the strength of codes, the classes were revised and again compared with the data. Themes were identified by careful, deep reflection and comparing the classes with each other. Immediately after each interview, the first author (MZ) transcribed the recorded content and highlighted the important paragraphs. The words, sentences, or paragraphs chosen as semantic units in connection to the hurdles and problems that medical science instructors face while developing and institutionalizing e-learning material. Each semantic unit was assigned a code. Next, a second author (ZN) reviewed the transcripts and confirmed unit meanings and codes. Then, similar codes were merged to form categories. To confirm the reliability of codes, the researchers examined the categories, and compared them with the primary data. Finally, the research team extracted the obtained themes after examining and comparing the categories in several joint meetings.

Rigor

To measure the trustworthiness of data, we evaluated four components of credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability, as proposed by Lincoln and Guba [14]. Credibility was evaluated through member checking, and prolonged engagement with the data. The transcripts of interviews and extracted codes were presented to some of the participants (n = 7), and they were asked to comment on the researchers' perceptions of their statements, and correct any incongruence's. Transferability was established via maximum variation sampling, which provided thick descriptions and straightforward examples of data. Dependability was established as the participants provided similar answers to the same questions. Finally, for confirmability, we tried to avoid any bias towards the data as the respondents shaped the findings.

Ethical considerations

All the participants gave written informed consent to participate in the study. The present study was conducted due to the principles of revised Declaration of Helsinki, which is a statement of ethical principles that directs physicians and other participants in medical research involving human subjects. The participants were assured about the anonymity and confidentiality of their information. Moreover, the study was approved by Institutional Research Ethics Committee of Fasa University of Medical Sciences, Fasa, Iran (Ethical code: IR.FUMS.REC.1400.172). (Supplementary file: research ethics committees certificate).

Individual challenges

Individual challenges were one of the most important themes of current study, which included a lack of knowledge and technical skills in the production and development of e-learning content, a lack of familiarity with e-learning content production standards, and a negative attitude towards e-learning content production.

Lack of technical knowledge and skills in the creation and development of e-learning content

Based on the experiences and views in the majority of participants, one of the main obstacles and challenges in the production, development and institutionalization of e-learning content is the lack of technical and specialized knowledge and skills. Moreover, one of the participants stated that:

“As a teacher, I do not have any knowledge, and skills in producing and using electronic learning content, how do the managers of the educational system expect that the teacher can use this educational technology optimally and effectively. During COVID--19 pandemic, due to online education, the managers immediately asked the professors to prepare standard e-learning content, we didn’t get training in this field, and the infrastructures were not ready.” (participant 7)

Lack of familiarity with the standards for creating e-learning content

Another sub-theme of individual challenges was a lack of familiarity with e-learning content production standards. Based on the participants' point of view, professors' sufficient familiarity and mastery of e-learning content production standards is the weakness point and the most important pillar of e-learning content creation. From the participants' point of view, success in e-learning requires a broad understanding of content development processes, and the process of developing an effective e-content is often done by following certain guidelines. In order to ensure that learners are thoroughly engaged in the learning process and attain the learning objectives, it is necessary to establish criteria for the presentation of the content. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“In COVID-19 crisis, one of the most important challenges and concerns of professors in preparing e-learning content was the lack of familiarity of professors with the standards of producing e-learning content. We were really confused and did not know what we should do and based on what principles and rules we should prepare the standard and high-quality educational content. Every day, the educational managers issued new instructions, and there was no single basic instruction in the field of e-learning content creation standards.” (participant 11)

Negative attitude towards e-learning content education

Based on the participant's point of view, the negative attitude towards electronic content education is one of the important inhibiting factors in the institutionalization of electronic education content and overshadows its use. In fact, teachers' attitudes are one of the factors that inhibit their successful adoption of e-learning facilities. Some professors are not used to it and do not want to accept the changes, because they are afraid of adopting new technology. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“Some of the colleagues and professors are not up-to-date and disagree using e-learning and have a traditional view and cannot associate themselves with the new changes in information technology and educational technology. With this point of view, we cannot expect significant changes in the education system.” (participant 5)

Challenges related to organizational management

Another theme of the present study was the challenges related to organizational management, which include the lack of standard equipment, and space for electronic content production, a lack of technical and technological support, weak regulations and a lack of guidelines for the development of electronic learning content, a lack of incentives to encourage the professors to produce electronic content and lost opportunities for continuous professional development.

Lack of standard equipment and space to produce e-learning content

With respect to the participants' point of view, the lack of standard equipment and space to produce e-learning content was one of the most important challenges. Based on the views and experiences of the participants, the most basic step towards the effective integration of information technology, and the optimal use and institutionalization of e-learning content is wide access to standard equipment and physical space. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“As a faculty member, I get really upset when there is no standard equipment and space to prepare electronic learning content. When there is no equipped and standard acoustic room, and you cannot produce content in this room with peace of mind, how can you expect to institutionalize the use of technology, and information technology in education. There is neither standard and sufficient electronic equipment, nor a specialized and standard room or even center for producing electronic content.” (participant 14)

Lack of technical and technological support

Lack of technical and technological support was another sub-theme constituting the challenges related to organizational management. Based on the participants' point of view, in addition to the existence of standard equipment and space, technical and technological support is also necessary and essential for the effective integration of information technology and the production of electronic learning content. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“There were many times when professors have had problems in the production and implementation of electronic learning content, and technical and technological support was not available to solve the problem, and the lack of information technology experts causes the effective implementation of the electronic learning program to be faced with a fundamental problem, and the process of educational program is disrupted. For example, several times, the generated electronic content could not be displayed.” (participant16)

Weakness in regulations and lack of guidelines for the development of e-learning content

Participants identified the absence of standardized and evidence-based criteria as a significant barrier and difficulty that hampers the progress and establishment of e-learning material. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“No guidelines and procedures were provided for the e-learning content creation, and this has caused the professors to become unmotivated and frustrated. We really don't know if this content is prepared based on scientific principles and educational technology guidelines or not. If there is a specific and comprehensive guideline in this field, professors can evaluate their e-learning content based on scientific, and standardized indicators, and this will improve the quality of content production and, subsequently, the effectiveness of curriculum process. This will make the learners satisfied. (participant 17)

Lack of necessary incentives to encourage professors to produce e-learning content

With respect to the views and experiences of the participants, the creation of motivational incentives is the most important factor in creating the impression, and persuasion of professors to produce and institutionalize producing e-learning content. Teachers should be rewarded with incentives for creating digital educational content that will increase their ability to create digital content for the classroom. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“There is no incentive program for electronic learning. When I spend many hours to produce e-learning content, and use modern information technology in teaching, I am similar to my colleagues who teach in the traditional way, and I do not receive any educational points, so there is no more motivation for me. I don't have it and I get discouraged. At least I expect it to have an impact on the annual evaluation of professors and the basis of receiving annual promotions.” (participant19)

Lack of continuous professional development opportunities

The absence of possibilities for ongoing professional development is another sub-theme that contributes to organizational management issues in the creation and institutionalization of e-learning material. Based on the participants’ point of view, the professional development of faculty members is a part of lifelong learning of professors. Teachers should continuously develop themselves to easily interact with educational technology tools and new technology via education to effectively use technological resources in e-learning. Universities should also continuously develop their professors' abilities to easily interact with new technological tools through training courses on how to effectively use e-learning systems.

Based on this, one of the participants said:

“Medical Education Development Center and the Virtual Education Center play an important and key role in the professional empowerment of professors in the field of electronic education content creation and using new information technologies in virtual education. Holding workshops and training courses continuously and continuously evaluating learners have an important and key role in optimizing and developing e-learning. It is not enough to hold only a few workshops of a few hours, but it is necessary to continue these training programs and evaluate their consequences continuously.” (participant16)

Course design challenges

Another theme of the current study was the challenges of course design, which included the design of quality and appropriate content, the challenges related to the nature of course (theoretical-practical), and the time-consuming process of producing electronic content.

Designing high quality and appropriate e-learning content

Designing high-quality and appropriate content was one of the sub-themes of the design challenges in the course. Based on the participants, one of the important aspects of e-learning is the quality and quantity of electronic content. Appropriate content helps students better understand educational content and concepts and will be effective in accepting and welcoming e-learning content in students. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“The production of e-learning content should be based on the needs and educational goals of the learners. An important aspect is interactive and student-centered learning. Unfortunately, some colleagues do not pay attention to the interactive nature of the produced content, since they are not familiar with the principles of standard electronic content preparation. The content should be simple, understandable, and in line with the educational goals and curriculum.” (participant 21)

Challenges related to the nature of the course (theoretical-practical)

From the point of view of the participants, the production of e-learning content for theoretical courses is easier to implement than for practical courses. One of the challenges in producing electronic content is related to the nature of the courses. Students are unable to acquire practical skills in laboratory courses solely through the production of electronic learning content; rather, they must acquire these skills through practical application. Based on this, one of the participants said:

“In the COVID-19 pandemic, where most of the theoretical and practical courses were through the production of electronic content, the majority of students expressed that they had major problems in learning practical skills. Although the professor used interactive methods and multimedia teaching methods in teaching electronic content, the students were really right that acquiring clinical skills in practical, and skill courses is not realized without working in a real clinical environment. (participant19)

The time-consuming process of producing e-learning content

The time-consuming process of electronic content production was another sub-theme constituting the course design challenges. With respect to the views of majority of the participants, one of the important and inhibiting factors in producing the electronic content is being time-consuming. The participants admitted that the production of high-quality electronic content is very difficult, time-consuming, and exhausting, and therefore most professors, especially clinical professors of medical sciences, do not want to use it, and prefer traditional methods.

Based on this, one of the participants stated that:

“Producing electronic learning content is very difficult and time-consuming. I am willing to have several hours of intensive theoretical teaching and practical teaching instead of preparing one hour of electronic learning content.” (participant 13)

Discussion

With respect to the present results, faculty members of medical schools generally faced three main challenges for content development, and institutionalization, including individual challenges, organizational management challenges, and course design challenges. In this regard, among the challenges of developing electronic content from the perspective of faculty members of a Malaysian university, we can note the lack of information and communication technology and e-learning policies that can provide guidelines for academics, uncertainty of ownership of e-learning initiatives, the lack of understanding of the roles of e-learning and lack of knowledge about e-learning [15]. A systematic review found several factors to be effective in the adoption of digital technologies, including teachers' educational beliefs, competence and confidence in integrating digital technologies, availability of resources, professional development, and socio-cultural context [16]. The review of other studies shows that during the development process of electronic content, there are challenges, such as development challenges, economic challenges, knowledge challenges, infrastructure challenges, cultural challenges, audience challenges, presentation challenges, evaluation challenges, coordination challenges, provider challenges, emotional challenges and psychomotor challenges [17]. In another study, the challenges of accepting the electronic education system were categorized into four categories: technological, individual, cultural, and course [18]. The review research highlighted the difficulties encountered in establishing an electronic education system in several areas such as information technology, human resources, pedagogy, culture, society, values, economics, management, administration, and support systems [19,20].With respect to the present results, one of the important dimensions of electronic content production challenges explained by academic staff members are individual challenges with three sub-themes of the lack of knowledge and technical skills in electronic content production, the lack of familiarity with electronic content production standards, and negative attitude towards electronic education. Consistent with these results, Johnson et al. (2016), found out that one of the challenges of using technology in the classroom is the internal obstacles related to teachers, attitudes, beliefs, and their knowledge and skills in the field of technology. These issues are inherently personal and therefore greatly vary from teacher to teacher even in the same environment [4]. Based on Singh et al. (2021), when sudden changes were imposed on the educational system due to COVID-19 crisis, many professors were not prepared and had received little or no training on how to plan and design effective electronic content and combine technologies; For this reason, it was challenging for them to effectively include elements that increase students' motivation, sense of belonging, and satisfaction. During this time, the rapid transition to a new form of teaching created significant stress among faculty with little or no experience working with technology, and this was seen among experienced as well as novice professors [21].

In fact, insufficient training means insufficient development of users to accept e-learning [17]. Hence, it is very important to focus on the capacity building of professors, so they become familiar with e-learning tools and using the innovative technology to facilitate teaching and learning [19]. Studies show that when professors have sufficient computer skills and a positive attitude towards spontaneous interaction with e-learning system, they will play an important role in motivating students [18].

Bhardwaj et al. (2015) demonstrated the need for formal teacher training as a necessary condition to support e-learning, which guides teachers from traditional teaching methods to a hybrid approach [22].

Consistent with the present study, the lack of familiarity with electronic content production standards was one of the sub-themes of individual content production challenges; based on the literature, success in e-learning requires a broad understanding of content development processes, and the process of developing an effective e-content is often done by following some guidelines. There should be specific criteria for presenting the content to attract the learners completely [23]. In Juhary's (2010) qualitative study, not all professors were aware of any information communication technologies (ICT) policies, let alone e-learning policies. The author emphasizes that before making more efforts to involve university professors to develop e-learning content, the policies related to e-learning and information and communication technology should be determined. Regardless of management approach, these policies provide a framework for successful e-learning implementation. Although the absence of policies may not affect the initial implementation of an e-learning program; But, it is a reasonable step [15]. Nbina et al. (2011) found that some professors do not know about information and communication technology facilities and therefore avoid using them for educational activities [24].

Based on the results of the present study, the negative attitude, and the lack of motivation of professors for developing electronic content was another individual challenge of electronic content creation. With respect to this result, in Juhary's (2010) study, one of the challenges of e-content production was convincing the faculty members to understand the roles of e-learning for higher education. Without beliefs, efforts to develop content are futile. One of the interviewees showed that it is more difficult for people to understand the new learning method than to develop its content. The challenges facing e-learning and development in particular are mainly due to the lack of understanding of e-learning as a complementary tool in classrooms. It seems that several university teachers lack an understanding of the significance of instructing pupils using modern technology. Understanding that electronic education will take over the learning process from the professor's real life; It is threatening for many educators, and along with this lack of understanding, the level of awareness of educators in terms of their e-learning potential is an important factor [15]. One of the biggest challenges for the effective use of e-learning by professors is the unwillingness to change to a new method, and the lack of computer literacy among them [19,20].

Consistent with these results, the previous investigations showed that the difference in using technology reflects important differences in teachers' beliefs about using technology in the educational process. Teachers' attitudes and beliefs are decisive factors in determining the role and effectiveness of technology in classrooms. In general, attitudes and beliefs about pedagogy and educational technology ultimately affect how they implement technology. Teachers are more inclined to stick with conventional ways if they don't think the new technology would be helpful and don't have the requisite knowledge to utilize it. The teacher's resistance to integrating technologies in the classroom is closely related to his attitudes and beliefs [4]. In fact, teachers' attitude is one of the inhibiting factors that affect their successful adoption of e-learning facilities. Some professors are not used to it and do not want to accept the changes, because they are afraid to accept new technology, and do not want to change from the analog era to the digital era [10]. Many current educators have grown up without access to technologies such as personal computers and the Internet. But, today's students are brought up in an environment immersed in computer technology. These “digital natives” can scare teachers, especially teachers who have little technical experience in this field [4]. Hence, potential cultural and social issues play a fundamental role in the adoption and use of e-learning management systems [21]. Based on previous study, the challenges of adopting the e-learning system from one country to another are very different due to different cultures, backgrounds and preparation for acceptance [15].

With respect to the present results, main challenges explained by the professors were for the production and development of content, organizational management, such as the lack of standard equipment and space to produce electronic content, the lack of technical and technological support, weak regulations and the lack of guidelines for the development of electronic content, the lack of incentives to encourage professors to produce electronic content and the lack of professional continuous development opportunities.

Consistent with the above results, the previous studies show that institutional support is the most important challenge for using e-learning systems tools in universities. In fact, faculty members need training, technical assistance and organizational support to be able to use the tools of e-learning systems [3]. The most important step toward efficient technology integration is unquestionably universal access to the requisite implementation tools. Awareness of easily accessible assistance may lead to increased faculty adoption of classroom technology [4]. In a study in Bangladesh (2019), limited resources, teacher demotivation, the lack of expert manpower, insufficient digital tools, and the lack of a multimedia-enabled classroom environment were cited as known barriers to using multimedia content [25]. Johnson et al. (2016) explained the external challenges for teachers, i.e. organizational challenges, were cited as primary barriers to successful integration of technology in the classroom. Among these challenges are issues related to equipment or insufficient internet connection (access limitation), challenges related to insufficient training related to technology, and challenges related to limitations and barriers to support for technology integration, including insufficient technical and administrative/peer support [4]. In the study by Zalat et al. (2021) on the experiences, challenges and adoption of e-learning as a tool for teaching during covid-19 pandemic among university medical professors, the highest barriers to e-learning were in the order of insufficient/unstable Internet connection, laboratory Inadequate computers, lack of computers/laptops and technical problems were reported [26]. Ahmad et al. (2018) showed that organizational infrastructure readiness, efficient technology infrastructure, appropriate e-learning course design, course flexibility, understandable relevant content, stakeholder training, security, access control and privileges, commitment and user-friendliness, and being well-organized are the most critical success factors that may increase sustainability and performance in e-learning [27].

Consistent with the present results, other studies consider technical problems as one of the barriers to using multimedia content in the classroom [22], and although multimedia projects can transform a classroom, teachers need a support network to effectively integrate these complex technologies [25]. Sometimes these contents face with technical problems. Under such circumstances, educators experience frustration and a decline in their enthusiasm to use multimedia materials into the classroom [22]. Another study showed that insufficient technical support and the lack of Information technologies (IT) skills are the primary barriers that hinder the successful implementation of present e-learning projects [26]. Many experts consider the provision of technical infrastructure and support even more important than content editing, and believe that if the best content is provided, but high-speed telecommunication lines, large volume of information transfer and other technical factors are not provided, then the necessary efficiency cannot be achieved from education [10].

The literature review showed that during COVID-19 pandemic, many academic institutions lacked the infrastructure, instructional design, technology staff, and other resources to support e-learning. Educational staff of all ages and backgrounds had to prepare and deliver classes from home with limited technical training, and no proper support [28]. Limited or no access to appropriate teaching resources, such as PCs/tablets, headsets, and printers led to frustration and struggle for faculty in Spring 2020 [10]. Ideally, any transition or change requires organizations to invest in professional development opportunities, research and data collection, capacity building, and measures to reduce resistance to change initiatives. During the COVID-19 pandemic, which was an emergency, institutions did not have the opportunity to properly plan for this transition to adapt to new set of teaching and learning practices [26].

Based on the present results, one of the sub-dimensions of organizational management challenges was the weakness of regulations, and the lack of guidelines for developing electronic content. The guidelines address issues ranging from technical aspects to copyright and content security. Content validity indicates whether the content for e-learning is appropriate in terms of difficulty level, and the most suitable content for development [5]. Based on studies, the successful implementation of electronic content can only occur by integrating related media elements, which are prepared according to certain guidelines [18]. Consistently, Juhary (2010) in an interview with university faculty members concluded that the lack of information and communication technology, and e-learning policies that can provide guidelines for academics and the uncertainty of ownership of e-learning initiatives are the challenges of e-content development by professors. He stated that two different sub-challenges are understanding ownership of e-learning and adopting appropriate measures for e-learning. Since there is no specific policy in this regard, it is difficult to issue guidelines for content development for e-learning [15].

The lack of continuous professional development opportunities for professors was another challenge raised by faculty members. By literature review, it was found that the most common reason for reducing the implementation of technology in the classroom is insufficient professional development, and training of teachers [29]. Faculty professional development is defined as “any organized program, formal or informal, that attempts to help faculty members improve the quality of teaching.” Studies show that the professional development of faculty members is a part of the “lifelong learning” of the professors of a college or university, and it is actually a fundamental component of the changes and transformations that are happening in higher education. Professional development of faculty members is a professional educational process that people are committed to Ref. [30]. In order to successfully employ technological resources in e-learning and to teach e-learning courses, teachers need constantly improve their ability to engage with technology and educational tools. Universities should continuously develop their instructors' abilities to easily interact with new technological tools through holding training courses on how to effectively use e-learning systems [10]. In this regard, periodic trainings should be held for teachers. This enables them to acquire relevant skills, and knowledge in the field of using e-learning facilities [3]. Workshops should be structured to allow participants to understand important issues, such as the concept of technology-based learning, theories of learning, testing and assessment, using information and communication technology in education, and the selection of materials for computer-based learning [5]. If teachers are not provided with effective professional development in the field of new technologies, they will not be able to take full advantage of it. By technology constantly changing, it is more important than ever that educators stay current with their technical expertise. In fact, professional development is a major barrier to technology implementation [4].

Based on the present results, another challenge of organizational management was the lack of necessary incentives to encourage professors to produce electronic content. Based on the available literature, salary increase, receiving a grant for using e-learning systems, receiving a reward from management, merit pay, free time, teaching workload and teaching to use e-learning systems are the most common needs of professors for the improvement of e-learning management systems هn university campuses, and encouragement, support and organizational incentives should be provided for professors to do this [3]. Other studies showed that teachers should be rewarded with incentives to create digital content, which increases their ability to create digital content for the classroom [22].

With respect to the present results, one of the main challenges explained by the professors is related to course design, including quality and appropriate content design, challenges related to the nature of the course (theory-practical), and the time-consuming process of electronic content production. Consistently, studies show that the ability to create effective, useful, useable and satisfactory electronic content is one of the major obstacles in electronic education, because the educational design is complex, and the main goal of content development is to achieve specific goals in teaching and placing the needs of the learner at the center of the learning process [18]. In fact, course characteristics in terms of course design and course quality can significantly contribute to e-learning and may play a key role in shaping the learning process, and thus motivating students to use and adopt an e-learning system [8]. Therefore, this innovative method of e-learning will be possible only if the content is appropriate and relevant to the students. Indeed, one of the most important aspects of e-learning is the quality and quantity of e-content. The right content helps students better understand their lessons, and thus maintain their attention. So what is important is the content and not the method of presenting [5]. Other studies emphasize that the electronic course is not just about presenting content with an electronic medium, and in the design of courses, the interactions between learner-instructor, learner-learner and learner-content should be given importance. The accomplishment of educational objectives, cooperative learning, learners' happiness, and a favorable attitude toward e-learning all depend on this connection [10]. Based on the literature, to prepare the appropriate content in electronic learning, especially for challenges, such as determining educational needs and goals, being interactive (student-to-teacher interaction, student-to-student interaction, and student-to-content interaction), dividing the course content (for understanding the material by the learners, and concentration in the preparation of the material by the designers), creating motivation in the audience (with multiple methods, such as attracting attention, connecting the subject with the needs, creating self-confidence and creating satisfaction), being up-to-date, high quality of content and attachments [15]. Therefore, for e-learning to be more effective, the learning goals should be clear and the course designed to respond to all areas of learning including knowledge, skills and attitudes should be sufficient and accompanied by sufficient training to accept this technology [17].

In a study, the factors of attractiveness, appropriate design and content were mentioned among the most important requirements for the successful use of electronic learning [31]. In the study of Mehrabi (2019), the importance of content development in e-learning and topics such as content selection, content promotion, content delivery media, and content interaction in the content design process were considered. They stated that content should be shareable and accessible. This result refers to features, such as portability, applicability on different tools, and ease of use, which were of great importance in the participants' view. They stated that electronic content should be short, attractive, creative multimedia and based on real experiences. That is, educational designers should prepare the content in small pieces (using the micro-learning approach), and adapt the learning process to the learner. This short content focusing on an educational goal can be presented by all electronic devices in the form of any type of media and is actually the finger food of learning [5].

Based on Juhary (2010), content development is not the only issue, but the most important task is the development of high-quality content. Content development is one of the most critical steps and the first stage in implementing e-learning and is vital to providing the best e-learning experiences to students. High-quality content was the most important factor that determined the success of e-learning. It was concluded that part of the problem is related to content that is not stimulating and challenging [15]. Another study confirmed that multimedia content used by instructors is not appropriate and of good quality. Sometimes, they use very colorful pictures, videos, etc., which hinders the learners' concentration and may increase the possibility of the learners' inattention [32]. Almaiah and Alyoussef (2019) showed that the limited quality of course content and the low quality of course design is among the challenges of adopting the electronic education system. Based on their study, factors related to course design, course content support, course evaluation, and instructor characteristics have a significant effect on the actual use of e-learning systems and the expectation of its performance. In fact, the poor design of an e-learning system course leads to students' resistance to using electronic systems [10].

Another challenge mentioned by the professors was related to the nature of course and the theory and practicality of the course for designing electronic content. Based on this, the investigations showed that theoretical courses are less affected by the challenges of virtual education. While laboratory work and practical courses are obvious obstacles to electronic learning [33,34]. Other studies emphasize that in practical and skill-oriented discussions, training should be both virtual and face-to-face in order to achieve optimal student satisfaction and performance [35].

The time-consuming content creation process was another challenge raised by professors in this study. Based on the literature, it takes a lot of time to prepare digital content, and hardware and software technology knowledge is required, and many teachers lack this knowledge [36]. In general, adopting a new educational technology can be a time-consuming process and teachers should have wide access to trained experts in this field [4]. One study stated that faculty members believe that e-learning can be time-consuming, and can lead to problems with student supervision and reduce interest in traditional face-to-face teaching [17]. Another study (2017) also emphasized that creating multimedia teaching materials requires a lot of time and teachers may not be enthusiastic about it [32]. Other studies showed that multimedia projects require a lot of time and effort from professors and students, and many teachers lack self-confidence to use multimedia technology [25].

Limitations

One of the limitations of this study is its qualitative nature, in which the volume and sample used are limited and considering that the data collected is related to a specific experience which is largely subjective and the context, events, conditions and interactions cannot always be repeated; Therefore, the findings of this qualitative research cannot be confidently generalized to other cultures and societies. Among other limitations of this study, the examination of challenges was only from the point of view based on medical science professors, and it is suggested that future research examine the perceived challenges from the students' point of view for using e-learning content.

Strengths

The present results can provide useful and important information to policymakers, managers and academic researchers. It is argued that described results regarding the challenges and obstacles affecting the development and institutionalization of e-learning content by medical science professors are valuable for supporting, and addressing the issues and challenges faced by these professors, especially in developing countries that face all kinds of cultural, social, technical, technological and infrastructural barriers. Hence, this study provided specific insights in this field to recognize and consequently reduce, minimize or overcome the obstacles and challenges in designing e-learning content at the national and international level and turn these challenges into opportunities for improvement and high-quality of e-learning content.

Conclusion

Based on the present results, the most important challenges explained by the participants of this study for the production and development of electronic content included individual challenges, challenges related to the organizational management, and challenges related to course design. Moreover, we should keep in mind that electronic teaching methods and preparation of e-learning content by professors are different from traditional educational methods. Hence, the senior managers of educational system should make the necessary arrangements and plans for methodological support, technological and technical support, and all kinds of organizational support and create continuous professional development opportunities for professors to minimize the negative effects caused by the rapid changes of electronics era and educational environments on professors and consequently on the quality of educational contents and processes.

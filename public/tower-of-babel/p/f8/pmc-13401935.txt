Prognostic Significance of Combined α-Fetoprotein and PIVKA-II for Hepatocellular Carcinoma After Hepatectomy
Authors: Jiajie Wen, Taixin Yang, Mingjian Huang, Bangde Xiang, Chenglei Yang, Jie Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) exhibits notable prognostic heterogeneity. While AFP is a routine HCC prognostic biomarker, its limited detection rate restricts standalone use. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of combined preoperative AFP and PIVKA-II for postoperative outcomes in HCC patients undergoing hepatectomy.Patients and MethodsA total of 553 patients were retrospectively enrolled and stratified into double-positive (DP), single-positive (SP), and double-negative (N) groups based on preoperative AFP (>400 ng/mL) and PIVKA-II (>40 ng/mL) levels. Kaplan-Meier survival curves assessed overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS). Univariate and multivariate Cox models identified independent clinicopathological survival predictors.ResultsThe N group demonstrated significantly better OS and RFS rates at 1 and 2 years than the SP group, while the DP group exhibited the poorest outcomes. Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified tumor size >5 cm and PIVKA-II >40 ng/mL as independent risk factors for both OS and RFS. Moreover, advanced age (>60 years) and multiple tumors (≥2) were independently associated with poorer OS, whereas tumor size ≥5 cm, BCLC stage C, and AFP >400 ng/mL were independent predictors of reduced RFS. The combined AFP/PIVKA-II stratification exhibited good prognostic discriminative ability for postoperative survival, with a C-index of 0.728 (95% CI: 0.650–0.806), but single biomarkers maintained independent prognostic power.ConclusionBased on the independent prognostic value of individual biomarkers, combined evaluation of AFP and PIVKA-II provides a supplementary tool to refine postoperative risk stratification for HCC patients after hepatectomy.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents a significant global health challenge, ranking as the sixth most prevalent cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide.1 Projections indicate that by 2040, approximately 1.3 million people will die annually from liver cancer.2 Surgical resection is widely regarded as the primary treatment for HCC patients eligible for hepatic resection.3,4 However, long-term survival rates post-resection remain suboptimal, with recurrence rates reaching 50–70% within five years.5 Therefore, a deeper understanding of tumor biology and recurrence-related risk factors is critical for improving clinical management of HCC.

Postoperative recurrence in HCC is strongly associated with various factors, including tumor stage, extent of resection, vascular invasion, liver cirrhosis severity, and postoperative adjuvant therapies. Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), a key biomarker for HCC, plays a vital role in disease monitoring, diagnosis, and prognosis.6 A prior long-term follow-up study by our research group, involving 396 HCC patients, revealed that elevated preoperative AFP levels are significantly linked to poorer post-resection prognosis.7 However, AFP elevation is not universal among HCC patients. Studies report diagnostic sensitivities and specificities of AFP testing at approximately 60% and 80%, respectively.8 Evidence also indicates that 30–40% of HCC patients have normal AFP levels (≤ 20 ng/mL),9 suggesting that the European guideline’s AFP cutoff of > 400 ng/mL for prognostic stratification may lack sufficient reliability for certain patient subsets.10

PIVKA-II (protein induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-II) has gained recognition as a valuable additional tumor marker for HCC.11 It is primarily utilized in Asian countries for diagnosing and monitoring HCC across various clinical stages.12–15 Growing evidence supports the independent prognostic significance of both PIVKA-II and AFP in HCC.16 A retrospective cohort study of 714 HCC patients demonstrated that those with recurrence within six months post-surgery had higher PIVKA-II levels at the time of recurrence, suggesting that PIVKA-II values above 40 ng/mL correlate with a poor prognosis.17 Beyond their diagnostic roles, both markers are significantly associated with clinical outcomes.

Recent studies indicate that utilizing a panel of tumor biomarkers, such as AFP, abnormal prothrombin, and CA199, enhances the accuracy of HCC diagnosis and prognosis. For example, the combined detection of AFP and IL-6 has been shown to more accurately predict overall survival (OS) in HCC patients. Patients with elevated levels of both markers (AFP ≥ 400 ng/mL and IL-6 ≥ 1.435 pg/mL) exhibit significantly shorter survival compared to those with elevated levels of either marker alone.18 For early-stage HCC patients undergoing resection, combining preoperative AFP (≥ 20 ng/mL), albumin (< 3.5 g/dL), and tumor burden score (TBS ≥ 8.6) has proven effective in predicting the risk of microvascular invasion (MVI) in patients with early-stage HCC (BCLC 0/A). The use of multiple biomarkers significantly enhances prognostic accuracy for HCC.19 A retrospective study of 711 HCC patients undergoing hepatectomy demonstrated that preoperative AFP (>400 ng/mL) and CA19-9 (> 37 U/mL) combined effectively stratified OS and recurrence-free survival (RFS), with double-positive patients showing the worst prognosis.20 However, the clinical utility of PIVKA-II in predicting post-resection prognosis remains insufficiently explored, particularly concerning its combined use with AFP. Aside from a single retrospective study involving only 165 patients,21 there is currently limited evidence evaluating the synergistic prognostic value of AFP and PIVKA-II in HCC patients following surgical resection.

Material and Methods

Study Population and Data Collection

A total of 619 consecutive patients with histologically confirmed HCC who underwent hepatectomy at the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery, Affiliated Tumor Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, between January 2022 and December 2023 were included in this study. Clinicopathological data for all enrolled patients were extracted from the institutional hepatectomy database and analyzed retrospectively. This study was a retrospective study that did not involve patients’ biological samples or personal privacy information, and the Ethics Committee waived the requirement for informed consent. All data were fully anonymized and used for research purposes only. The study protocol was approved in writing by the Ethics Committee of Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital (KY2026018). All procedures adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Tumor size was defined as the maximum diameter of the largest lesion in the resected specimen. Tumor tissues were histopathologically classified according to the Edmondson-Steiner grading system as grade I (well-differentiated), grade II (moderately differentiated), or grades III–IV (poorly differentiated). Preoperative serum levels of AFP and PIVKA-II were retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical records, with both markers measured within one week for the majority of patients.

Hepatectomy

All curative-intent hepatectomies were performed by experienced senior surgeons using standardized hepatobiliary surgical techniques. Complete resection was defined as the macroscopic removal of all tumor lesions with microscopically confirmed negative margins, supported by postoperative imaging that demonstrated no residual tumor or portal vein tumor thrombus. Patients with concomitant malignancies, distant metastases, or lymph node involvement were excluded from the study.

Postoperative Management

Post-hepatectomy, adjuvant therapies were administered to reduce the risk of tumor recurrence, with such interventions limited to patients at high risk of recurrence. Patients were scheduled for their first follow-up visit one month after surgery, either at the outpatient clinic or via telephone interview. Chronic hepatitis B virus carriers continued long-term antiviral therapy with nucleoside analogs (entecavir or tenofovir). In cases of recurrence, individualized treatment strategies—including repeat hepatectomy, radiofrequency ablation, TACE, radiotherapy, or systemic therapy—were tailored based on patient status and recurrence patterns.

Follow-Up

Follow-up visits occurred every 2 to 3 months during the first two postoperative years and transitioned to a 6-month interval thereafter. The follow-up period ranged from 1 to 35 months, with data collection continuing until December 2024. Data were accessed for research purposes on 31/12/2024. RFS was calculated from the date of surgery to the date of first documented recurrence or the last follow-up in recurrence-free patients. OS was determined from the date of surgery to the date of death or last follow-up.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 27.0.1, IBM) and R Studio (version 4.5.1). Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test (two-sided) or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Exploratory ROC analyses were performed using the Youden’s index. Kaplan-Meier methods were used to estimate cumulative OS and RFS rates, and group differences were assessed using the Log rank test. After excluding patients with a follow-up period shorter than 12 months, a sensitivity analysis was conducted to minimize potential biases that might arise due to insufficient follow-up time. Univariate Cox proportional hazards models were employed to evaluate potential prognostic factors following hepatectomy, while multivariate Cox regression analysis identified independent predictors of OS and RFS. Furthermore, the predictive discrimination abilities of TMT and BCLC staging systems for OS/RFS were quantified and compared via the C-index. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient Characteristics

Of the 619 patients initially considered, 66 were excluded for the following reasons: (1) missing preoperative AFP or PIVKA-II measurements, or both (n = 2); (2) incomplete critical clinical data (n = 14); and (3) postoperative death within one month or fewer than two follow-up visits (n = 50). The final study cohort consisted of 553 HCC patients.

ROC Curve Analysis and Patient Stratification

ROC curve analyses were conducted to explore the optimal cut-off values of preoperative AFP and PIVKA-II for prognostic prediction. Considering that data-driven optimal thresholds are cohort-dependent and prone to overfitting, while most ROC-derived values were comparable to currently acknowledged criteria, we finally adopted unified, guideline-based cut-offs for patient stratification to ensure model reproducibility and clinical practicability. Detailed ROC results and threshold comparison analyses are provided in the Supplementary Material (Figure S1).

Based on preoperative serum AFP levels, patients were classified into two groups: the low AFP group (n = 399; AFP ≤ 400 ng/mL) and the high AFP group (n = 154; AFP > 400 ng/mL). Similarly, patients were stratified according to preoperative PIVKA-II levels into the low PIVKA-II group (PIVKA-II ≤ 40 ng/mL) and the high PIVKA-II group (PIVKA-II > 40 ng/mL). The high AFP group exhibited larger tumor size, a higher proportion of poorly differentiated tumors (Edmondson-Steiner grades III–IV), and elevated AST levels compared to the low AFP group. This distinction is notable as it supports findings that combining PIVKA-II with AFP improves the prediction of pathological differentiation and MVI in HCC patients. Elevated AFP levels were also associated with increased total bilirubin (TBil), MVI, and advanced BCLC stage C. Survival Outcomes by AFP, PIVKA-II, and Their Combinations

OS and RFS rates were evaluated based on preoperative serum AFP and PIVKA-II levels. The 1- and 2-year OS rates for the AFP low group (≤ 400 ng/mL) were 95.2% and 90.2%, respectively, while for the AFP high group (> 400 ng/mL), they were 88.2% and 80.1% (Figure 1A). The 1- and 2-year RFS rates for the AFP low group were 86.4% and 76.7%, compared to 65.9% and 41.5% for the AFP high group (Figure 1B). These results suggest that lower preoperative AFP levels correlate with more favorable survival outcomes. Similarly, the 1- and 2-year OS rates for the PIVKA-II low group (≤ 40 ng/mL) were 96.5% and 90.9%, respectively, while for the PIVKA-II high group (> 40 ng/mL), they were 87.7% and 81.2% (Figure 1C). The corresponding 1- and 2-year RFS rates were 92.2% and 83.9% for the PIVKA-II low group, versus 61.4% and 36.7% for the PIVKA-II high group (Figure 1D). These findings further support the association between lower preoperative AFP or PIVKA-II levels and better survival outcomes. Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity survival analysis was performed after excluding patients with follow-up shorter than 12 months, with identical cut-off values (AFP=400 ng/mL, PIVKA-II=40 ng/mL) and grouping criteria consistent with the primary cohort.

Consistent with primary results, patients were split into low and high subgroups by AFP and PIVKA-II separately. For AFP stratification, elevated AFP (>400 ng/mL) was significantly associated with poorer OS and RFS; the inter-group OS difference retained borderline statistical significance (P=0.035) (Figure 4A), while RFS differed significantly (P<0.0001) (Figure 4B). For PIVKA-II stratification, high PIVKA-II (>40 ng/mL) exerted no statistically significant adverse effect on OS (P=0.13) (Figure 4C), but remained a powerful negative predictor for postoperative recurrence with markedly divergent RFS between two groups (P<0.0001) (Figure 4D). Discussion

This retrospective study confirmed that both AFP and PIVKA-II, when assessed individually, were significantly associated with OS and RFS rates following curative hepatic resection for HCC. Based on the ROC curve analysis results and clinical guideline recommendations, the optimal cut-off values were determined as 400 ng/mL for AFP and 40 ng/mL for PIVKA-II. To enhance the accuracy of postoperative recurrence prediction, the prognostic value of preoperative combined measurement of AFP and PIVKA-II was further evaluated. The results showed that patients with elevated levels of both biomarkers—specifically AFP > 400 ng/mL and PIVKA-II > 40 ng/mL—experienced significantly poorer clinical outcomes. This finding suggests that dual positivity for AFP and PIVKA-II significantly improves prognostic accuracy for postoperative outcomes in HCC. Additionally, univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards analyses identified several other preoperative clinicopathological factors independently associated with OS and RFS rates.

This study demonstrated that the combined use of TMT provides substantial advantages in predicting the prognosis of HCC patients, thereby adding value to the current clinical prognostic assessment framework. First, TMT offers broader clinical applicability by overcoming the limitations of traditional single markers. It allows for accurate prognostic stratification not only in AFP-positive patients but also in those with low or undetectable AFP levels. Second, the baseline data required for TMT assessment can be obtained through routine serological testing, eliminating the need for additional invasive procedures. This approach facilitates the timely delivery of prognostic information before surgery, thereby enhancing the accuracy and practicality of preoperative risk evaluation in HCC management. Notably, the TMT stratification does not serve as an independent prognostic predictor superior to individual biomarkers. Instead of providing novel independent predictive power, it functions as a refined descriptive stratification tool that integrates the dual biological information of AFP and PIVKA-II to further distinguish heterogeneous prognostic risks.

AFP and PIVKA-II are established as key biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognostic evaluation of HCC, with the determination of appropriate cut-off values being essential for their effective clinical application. In this study, threshold values of 400 ng/mL for AFP and 40 ng/mL for PIVKA-II were adopted, in alignment with the optimal cut-offs recommended in most authoritative studies and clinical guidelines. However, variability in these thresholds exists across different studies, largely due to differences in the demographic and etiological characteristics of the study populations. For example, Chon YE. et al reported that a preoperative AFP level ≥ 20 ng/mL, when combined with PIVKA-II ≥ 40 mAU/mL, constitutes an independent risk factor for recurrence in patients with HBV-related HCC after curative resection.22 Notably, the AFP threshold (≥ 20 ng/mL) in Chon’s study differs from ours, which may be attributed to the fact that Chon’s cohort was limited to HBV-related HCC patients who underwent curative resection, whereas our study included HCC patients with mixed etiologies. Different etiologies can also affect AFP expression in tumor cells, contributing to the observed threshold differences. Additionally, Chon’s study focused on disease-free survival (DFS) following curative resection, while our study targeted OS or RFS, with a different follow-up duration. Given that AFP levels may correlate differently with these survival endpoints, the optimal thresholds for prognosis prediction may also vary. In contrast, Kim S.H. et al reported that an AFP level above 150 ng/mL constitutes an independent risk factor for HCC recurrence after liver transplantation. This higher threshold effectively identifies high-risk individuals and enhances the selection criteria for transplant eligibility.23 This discrepancy in thresholds may be due to the fact that most patients in our study were diagnosed with intermediate or advanced-stage HCC, whereas the cohort in Kim’s study predominantly consisted of early-stage cases, who generally exhibit lower AFP levels. Consequently, the AFP levels in our patient population are inherently higher, necessitating a relatively higher threshold to accurately stratify the risk of recurrence. Regarding PIVKA-II, a threshold of 100 mAU/mL was applied in the same liver transplant cohort, where it served alongside AFP as an independent predictor of postoperative recurrence.23 This threshold is higher than our 40 mAU/mL, a difference that may stem from variations in the study populations and the biological characteristics of the marker. While the liver transplantation cohort primarily consisted of early-stage liver cancer patients, differences in the definitions and stratification criteria for high-risk populations across studies might explain the higher threshold used. A higher PIVKA-II threshold may help to more precisely identify patient subgroups with insidious invasive characteristics. Furthermore, a study indicated that in patients without visible vascular invasion, a preoperative PIVKA-II level > 445 mAU/mL is an independent risk factor for postoperative tumor recurrence.24 The difference in the threshold compared to that of our study may be due to the stricter inclusion criteria for patients without significant vascular invasion, who typically exhibit less aggressive tumor biology and lower overall PIVKA-II expression levels, thus requiring a higher threshold to identify high-risk subgroups.

The elevation of AFP is associated with increased proliferative activity in tumor cells. Multiple studies have demonstrated that AFP promotes tumor cell proliferation by inhibiting the retinoic acid receptor (RAR)-RAR signaling pathway and suppressing apoptosis through direct binding to caspase-3.25–27 Additionally, Galle PR et al28 suggested that AFP may contribute to tumor progression by facilitating angiogenesis. In parallel, high PIVKA-II expression is associated with increased MVI and enhanced tumor invasiveness.29 PIVKA-II activates the JAK1/STAT3 pathway, promoting proliferation and increasing the likelihood of postoperative recurrence.30 These findings suggest that elevated AFP and PIVKA-II levels may serve as adverse prognostic factors. Our research further supports this, showing that HCC patients with elevated preoperative AFP and PIVKA-II levels have significantly higher postoperative recurrence rates and shorter RFS periods. Consequently, AFP and PIVKA-II represent two distinct dimensions of HCC heterogeneity—tumor proliferative activity and invasive or metastatic potential. The divergence in their functional targets and biological roles indicates that HCC patients who are dually positive (DP) often exhibit concurrent features of high proliferation and aggressive invasiveness.31 This dual-malignant phenotype is linked to more unfavorable tumor biology, as evidenced by higher postoperative recurrence rates, shorter RFS, and reduced OS.

The TMT stratification strategy proposed in this study is based solely on preoperative serological markers, offering notable advantages in terms of rapid data acquisition and operational simplicity. It provides an efficient and clinically feasible tool for preoperative prognostic assessment in HCC patients and offers valuable guidance for individualized clinical decision-making. For example, patients classified as high-risk according to the TMT system may benefit from more proactive interventions, such as expanded surgical margins, incorporation of neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgery, or intensified postoperative adjuvant treatment, to specifically reduce the risk of tumor recurrence and improve survival outcomes.32,33

Nevertheless, our study has several limitations. Although statistically significant results were obtained, the relatively short follow-up period may limit the comprehensive evaluation of the long-term prognostic value of TMT stratification. Additionally, as a single-center study, it is constrained by a limited sample size and population representativeness, affecting the generalizability of the findings. Therefore, multicenter and large-scale prospective validation is warranted.

Conclusion

Preoperative serum AFP and PIVKA-II levels enable effective risk stratification for postoperative prognosis in HCC patients following hepatectomy. The three-tier TMT stratification shows clear descriptive value in discriminating distinct patterns of survival and recurrence. This combined strategy holds clinical utility for postoperative risk evaluation, and external validation is warranted.

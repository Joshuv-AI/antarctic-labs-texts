The study of nomogram model based on CT radiomics and clinical features for histological classification of parotid gland tumors
Authors: Qian Shen, Yilong Liu, Feng Xu, Yongliang Han, Xiaojuan Liu, Kui Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate preoperative identification of pathological types of parotid tumors is essential for the formulation of treatment decisions. The study aims to develop and validate a CT-based radiomics nomogram combining radiomics signature and clinical factors, and evaluate the effectiveness of different models in the classification of parotid gland tumors. A total of 427 patients with parotid gland tumors were randomly divided into a training set and a test set at a ratio of 7:3. Radiomic features were selected using the ANOVA and the LASSO regression. Three-step machine learning models were constructed using three common classifiers (LR, SVM and XGBoost) to classify the parotid gland tumors into four subtypes. The radiomics signature was constructed using the optimal radiomics model, and a radiomics score (Rad-score) was calculated. Clinical data and CT features were evaluated to build a clinical factor model. A radiomics nomogram incorporating the independent clinical factors and Rad-score was constructed. The evaluation of those models’ performance was executed by using receiver operator characteristics (ROC) curves (AUC) and calibration curves, and the clinical usefulness of these models was evaluated by decision curve analysis (DCA). In each step of the three-step procedure, twenty-seven, twelve, and thirteen valuable features were selected, respectively. And the radiomics model based on the LR, SVM, and LR classifiers obtained the highest AUC in differentiating BPGTs from MPGTs (AUC = 0.838), PA from WT & BCA (AUC = 0.847), and WT from BCA (AUC = 0.870), respectively. The nomogram, which combined the optimal radiomics model and independent clinical factors, achieved an improved classification performance (BPGTs vs. MPGTs, AUC = 0.849; PA vs. WT & BCA, AUC = 0.873; WT vs. BCA, AUC = 0.925). The calibration curve and the DCA demonstrated that the combined nomogram showed superior predictive performance than radiomics model and clinical factor model. The proposed nomogram of radiomics combined with clinical models has high clinical value for the preoperative classification of parotid gland tumors, which might hold promise in assisting clinicians in the exact preoperative diagnosis and formulation of personalized treatment strategy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46970-4.

Introduction

Parotid gland tumors are the most common salivary gland tumors, accounting for approximately 2–6.5% of all head and neck tumors1, among which some 80% are benign. The most common types of benign parotid gland tumors are pleomorphic adenomas (PAs), followed by Warthin tumors (WTs) and basal cell adenomas (BCAs)2, accounting for approximately 60–70%, 15–20%, and 1–3% of benign tumors, respectively. The pathological types of parotid gland tumors are various, and the treatment strategies selected are also different, which in turn affects the prognosis of patients3. As for patients with malignant parotid gland tumors (MPGTs), total parotidectomy is recommended, and if the patient has high-risk factors, postoperative chemoradiation should also be executed4. PA is considered by partial superficial parotidectomy, due to its recurrence rates and higher malignancy5. WT and BCA are treated only by enucleation or conservative treatment, given that malignant transformation rarely occurs6. While BCA is often misdiagnosed as pleomorphic adenoma due to insufficient clinical and imaging recognition, and according to cellular growth patterns, BCA can be categorized into four histologic subtypes, including tubular, solid, membranous and trabecular. Due to its relatively high tendency to recur, the membranous type of BCA is conducted by surgical excision via a supra-facial or total parotidectomy7. Thus, accurate preoperative evaluation is essential for making an individualized clinical treatment decisions in patients with parotid tumors.

Parotid tumors have no specific clinical symptoms, so the preoperative classification and distinction of parotid tumors mainly depend on fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy and imaging examinations8. Although FNA is a common preoperative qualitative method for parotid tumors9, it carries some disadvantages, including the risk of tumor cell transplantation and high rates of insufficient diagnostic aspirations10,11. Currently, imaging tools, including ultrasound (US), computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Due to the fact that the US examination is very susceptible to the adjacent bone and tumor location, as well as the operator expertise, differential diagnosis is limited with US12, therefore, MRI and CT examinations are commonly utilized to examine the parotid gland tumors13. Although MRI provides exceptionally good resolution of soft tissues, CT can provide valuable insights into tumor characteristics and surrounding structures at a lower cost and is less time-consuming than MRI1. However, parotid gland tumors often exhibit overlapping radiological features, imaging examinations sometimes leads to uncertain results, the conclusion mainly depends on the experience of radiologists14.

Radiomics is a new medical tool that can extract useful information from images via a variety of automated computational methods15. As a non-invasive technique, radiomics can reveal the heterogeneity of tumors by extracting and analyzing a large number of image features in a high-throughput manner, and provide valuable information for tumor classification, prognosis prediction, and prognosis assessment16,17. Although using CT images to classify parotid gland tumors is not a novel approach, currently there are few studies that employ radiomics-based models to address the diagnostic issues18. Previous studies have shown that the applications of CT radiomics analysis could help radiologists in the differential diagnosis between salivary gland tumors, the most of those studies concentrated on distinguishing between two subtypes, such as malignant tumors versus benign tumors19,20, or between different types of benign tumors, like pleomorphic adenomas and Warthin’s tumors1,15, and the majority of the studies only used one type of machine learning classifier. Moreover, as a simple graphical representation of a statistical predictive model, nomogram uses biological and clinical variables to construct a model that can calculate the probability of clinical events21. It has been widely applied in radiomics studies. However, to the best of our knowledge, there are few literatures on the use of radiomics nomogram for accurate classification of parotid MPGT, PA, WT, and BCA, and the role of CT-based radiomics nomogram in distinguishing these common tumors has not been fully explored.

Therefore, this study selected different machine learning classifiers for radiomics models to develop and validate a CT-based radiomics nomogram incorporating radiomics signature and clinical features for the preoperative differentiation of the most common subtypes of parotid tumors.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective multi-center study was approved by the ethics committees of The First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University and The Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (No: K2023-414). Due to the retrospective nature of this study, all patients’ informed consents were waived. Patient sets were gathered from January 2014 to January 2023 at center #1 (The First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University), and from January 2020 to June 2022 at center #2 (The Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University). The exclusion criteria and the inclusion criteria were presented in Supplementary Appendix S1.

Ultimately, a total of 427 patients were enrolled for this study, parotid tumors were categorized into five groups according to the pathological results, namely BPGT (n = 317), MPGT (n = 110), PA (n = 144), WT (n = 127) and BCA (n = 46). The overall dataset was randomly allocated to the training set and test set at a ratio of 7:322. Random splitting of the data was stratified to reduce the risk of overfitting.The tumor distribution of the two sets are described in Table 1. The patients were collected from two centers, among them, the sample size of the Southwest Medical University Affiliated Hospital was relatively small, with MPGT (n = 17), PA (n = 15), WT (n = 18) and BCA (n = 15). Therefore, we had to combine the two institutions instead of designating one as the training group and the other as the test group. Patient clinical data and CT images were obtained from the hospital’s clinical records and the picture archiving and communication systems (PACS).

Image acquisition

Each patient underwent axial enhanced scanning using multi-slice spiral CT equipment. The range was from the skull base to the thoracic inlet. After completing non-enhanced CT scan, enhanced images were obtained after intravenous injection of nonionic iodinated contrast medium, the contrast agent was injected at a flow rate of 2 to 4 ml/s, followed by 30 ml of normal saline, and the contrast agent dosage was 1–2 ml/kg. Besides, the scanning process of all images were carried out using the soft tissue reconstruction kernel for image reconstruction. Detailed imaging parameters for the various devices are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Clinical and radiological data analysis

Patient clinical data and CT images of the arterial phase were acquired from our hospital’s clinical records and PACS system. We retrospectively collected clinical factors including age, sex, smoking/drinking status, and symptoms. All patients’ CT imaging data were independently assessed and agreed upon by two radiologists, who had 5 and 10 years of clinical diagnostic experience in the head and neck field, respectively, and were blinded to the histopathological and clinical results of tumor. We also collected the radiological features including maximum diameter, location, density, cystic, shape, margin, number, calcification, enhanced uniformity, enlarged lymph nodes, and enhancement degree. Definitions for these radiological features can be found in the Supplementary Appendix S2.

Image segmentation

All patients’ CT images were stored in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) format, with standard soft tissue settings (window width, 400 HU; window level, 40 HU). The region of interest (ROI) was segmented manually from axial arterial-phase enhanced CT images using ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0, http://www.itksnap.org/) by two radiologists (with 5 and 10 years of working experience in neck and head field), who were blinded to the clinical and histopathological data of tumors.When there were multiple lesions in the parotid gland area, the larger lesion that had been pathologically confirmed would be selected for analysis.

The intra- and inter-observer reproducibility were assessed by the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). And 20 cases of arterial-phase enhanced CT images were randomly selected, the tumor ROI segmentation was independently conducted by radiologist-1 and radiologist-2 for assessing the inter-observer agreement of the extracted radiomics features. Radiologist-1 then carried out the same case procedure after two weeks, and the ICC values over 0.9 demonstrated good consistency.

Radiomics feature extraction

PyRadiomics in Python was employed for image preprocessing and feature extraction. All images were preprocessed by the techniques of resampling and standardization to ensure the consistency of the results. To eliminate the potential impact of different CT devices, a multi-step normalization pipeline was employed: (1) All images were resampled to isotropic 1.0 mm3 voxels; (2) Intensity discretization used a fixed bin width of 25 HU; (3) A test-retest dataset was used to calculate ICC, and only features with ICC > 0.85 were retained for further analysis; (4) For the multi-center cohort, ComBat harmonization was applied to the final feature set using the scanner manufacturer as a batch variable, preserving the association with the clinical outcome. During the feature extraction process, the settings include histogram bin width (bin width = 25), Gaussian filter sigma values (3 and 5), resampling voxel spacing ([1, 1, 1]), voxel array shift (1000), normalization ratio (100), etc. The B-spline interpolation method was selected to ensure consistency. All image types and features were enabled, and the Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) operator and wavelet filter algorithm were used to generate multiple image transformations to obtain features at different spatial frequencies. The final feature set was composed of the original image and multiple filtered images. These algorithms, which were referenced from the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative (IBSI), were employed to extract radiomics features23.

Feature selection and radiomics model construction

Before further feature screening, all imaging features were normalized by the use of a Z-score to reduce the influence of different dimensions among features. Subsequently, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted on the extracted features to select those statistically significant features with intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) scores > 0.9. Next, in order to avoid over-fitting and reduce redundant features, the F-test and the Spearman’s correlation test were employed sequentially to obtain the optimal features, the selection process was as follows: (1) The F-test was used to evaluate the correlation between each radiomics feature and the classification label, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05, and only features with statistically significant differences were retained for subsequent analysis; (2) The Spearman’s correlation test was executed to identify the features that exhibited a high correlation ( |ρ| ≥ 0.95). Ultimately, the LASSO regression model with 10-fold cross-validation was executed to confirm the optimal penalty coefficient alpha. All feature selection procedures were conducted on the training set and then applied to the test set. The Shapley Additive Explanation (SHAP) tool enables clinicians to understand the results of machine learning models in an explainable manner, it was employed to enhance the transparency and clinical interpretability of the model24.

Based on the valuable radiomics features extracted from the CT images, we compared three common classifiers: support vector machine (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and logistic regression (LR). Then, using these machine learning algorithms, a three-step binary classification model was constructed, these models were employed to classify the common parotid tumors into four subtypes by utilizing radiomics features selected at each step. In the first step, we aimed to distinguish BPGT from MPGT (BPGT vs. MPGT), the second step to distinguish PA from WT and BCA (PA vs. WT & BCA), and the third step to distinguish WT from BCA (WT vs. BCA). Model simple schematic can be found in the Supplementary Figure S1.

Development of the clinical model and radiomics nomogram

Clinical factors were selected in each step, such as symptoms, margin, enlarged lymph nodes, age, cystic, maximum diameter, sex, and enhancement degree were used to establish clinical models. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analysis were used to screened out the valuable clinical information and radiological features used for clinical model construction. And the optimal classifier of each step was used to construct clinical models.

A radiomics score (Rad-score) was generated by using a linear combination of selected valuable features, weighted by their respective non-zero coefficients coefficients. By integrating the optimal radiomics models and the independent clinical predictors, a combined model was constructed and visualized in the form of a radiomics nomogram, aiming to provide clinicians and patients with an individualized and user-friendly tool for the preoperative prediction of parotid gland tumors.

Performance evaluation of the models

The performances of these models were assessed in the testing sets by using the area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC). We estimated the 95% confidence interval (CI) using bootstrap with 1000 samples, supplementary Appendix S3 provides details of the specific calculation strategy for the 95% confidence interval of AUC. The accuracy, positive predictive value (PPV), sensitivity, negative predictive value (NPV) and specificity were obtained to quantify differential diagnostic efficiency. Then, for the model with the best performance, we conducted the sensitivity and specificity analyses using the confusion matrix.

The observed outcomes and the predicted probabilities were assessed by calibration curves, which were quantified through the utilization of Brier scores. A Brier score of 0 is considered perfect; a forecaster who is always wrong achieves a score of 125. Decision curve analyses (DCA) were conducted to evaluate the clinical usefulness of the three models (the clinical model, radiomics model, and radiomics nomogram model) within a certain threshold probability, based on the clinical factors, radiomics signature, and radiomics nomogram respectively.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was executed by using PyRadiomics in Python(version 3.7.12; http://www.python.org) and SPSS software (version 26.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). The Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for comparing continuous variables, while the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was utilized for comparing categorical variables. Statistical significance was determined at p < 0.05.

Results

The population and radiological features of patients

A total of 427 patients were recruited for our study, including 110 patients with MPGT, 144 with PA, 127 with WT, and 46 with BCA. As shown in the Supplementary Table S2, there were no significant differences between the training and test sets. After the univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses, significant contributions were noted in distinguishing BPGTs from MPGTs in terms of symptoms, margin and enlarged lymph nodes (P < 0.05), a clinical factor model was constructed by these independent clinical predictors (Supplementary Table S3). Among the differences between PA and WT & BCA, the age and cystic characteristics of the tumor make significant differences (Supplementary Table S4), a clinical model was developed by these two factors. Significant differences were noted in differentiating WT from BCA in terms of sex, maximum diameter, cystic and enhancement degree (P < 0.05), those characteristics and radiological features of the patients are presented in the Supplementary Table S5.

Radiomic signature models and performances

A total of 1074 radiomic features were extracted from VOI, after feature screening and dimension reduction, 27 radiomics features with nonzero coefficients were finally selected at the first step, 12 features were chosen in the second step, and 13 features were selected in the third step, respectively. As illustrated in Supplementary Figure S2, for each classification task, we integrated SHAP bees warm plots and mean |SHAP| bar plots into a single figure. This combined visualization concurrently illustrates the distribution of feature contributions at the individual sample level and the global ranking of feature importance. This approach facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of the direction and relative weight of different radiomic features in the model’s predictions, thereby enhancing model transparency and clinical interpretability. Although the sample size of BCA was relatively small, the distribution of SHAP values in different samples was relatively concentrated, and there was no phenomenon where the prediction results were dominated by a few samples. This indicated that the model did not overly rely on individual BCA samples (Supplementary Figure S3). Performance of the radiomics nomogram model

The radiomics nomogram was developed by incorporating radiomics model, margin, symptoms and enlarged lymph nodes to differentiate BPGT from MPGT. The radiomics model, age, and cystic features were combined for establishing a radiomics nomogram to distinguish PA from WT & BCA. And we also integrated clinical predictors (sex, maximum diameter, cystic and enhancement degree) with the radiomics signature, we developed a radiomics nomogram to differentiate WT from BCA. The ROC curves of the three models at each step in the test sets are shown in Fig. 3, the discrimination efficiency of the radiomics nomogram model demonstrated the highest AUC compared with the clinical and radiomics models.

Fig. 3ROC curve of the clinical, radiomics, and combined models with optimal classifiers in the test sets for steps 1–3.

Although the result in this study that AUCs of the radiomics nomogram models were larger than those of the radiomics models between all three steps in the test sets, delong’s test revealed that there was no significant difference in predictive performance between the two groups (p > 0.05). And the radiomics nomogram model had better efficacy in differential diagnosis and a higher AUC than that of the clinical model (p < 0.05) (Supplementary Table S8). The 2 × 2 confusion matrices in the step1 are shown in Supplementary Figure S4.The calibration curves of the radiomics nomogram model based on the three groups at each step demonstrated good calibration in the test sets (Fig. 4). The decision curve analysis (DCA) of the three models at each step is presented in Fig. 5, the results show that the radiomics nomogram model can add more net benefit to the prediction than others, with the threshold probability ranging from 0 to 1.0 in the step1and step3, while the threshold probability in the step2 ranges from 0 to 0.9. Nevertheless, at different threshold probabilities, the net benefits obtained by using this prediction model are also different, for example, when the threshold probability intervals for step1, step2 and step3 are 0.1 to 0.35, 0.2 to 0.4, and 0.5 to 0.8, respectively, the corresponding optimal decision thresholds for those three steps are 0.2, 0.35 and 0.7, respectively. The results demonstrated that the radiomics nomogram model exhibited the most significant net benefit in categorizing the most common subtypes of parotid gland tumors in the test sets (Fig. 6).

Discussion

In this study, we built and validated a CT-based the radiomics nomogram model that combined both radiomics features and clinical characteristics using different machine learning classifiers to differentiate among BPGT, MPGT, PA, WT and BCA. Our results showed that the machine learning classifier with the best predictive efficacy for those common parotid gland tumors was different. Furthermore, the nomogram model that integrated clinical features and radiomics signatures was superior to the clinical factor model, which revealed the incremental value of the radiomics model in terms of distinguishing parotid tumors.

It is well known that adequate clinical characteristics and radiological information facilitate accurate preoperative differentiation of parotid gland tumors. Studies have shown that irregular shape, diffuse growth pattern, unclear margin, surrounding tissue involvement, and as well as lymphatic metastasis of tumor are more likely to be considered as MPGTs19,26,27. In this study, we found that having symptoms, lymph node enlargement and unclear margins were more common in malignant tumors than that in benign tumors, which is similar to the findings of previous studies. And the pathological components of PA are complex, the density of the tumor was not homogeneous and the necrosis and cystic may be appeared in CT images. Otherwise, we also found that patients with WT and BCA were older than those with PA, this result is consistent with the findings of previous studies28–31. In this study, the AUCs of clinical models for each group based on clinical-radiological features were 0.720, 0.738 and 0.804, respectively, in the test sets, which suggested that the differentiation model developed only by clinical-radiological features cannot distinguish different types of parotid gland tumors well.

Radiomics employs mathematical calculations to identify those imaging features that are invisible, and quantifies the various characteristics exhibited by parotid tumor tissues in radiological data, aiming to distinguish different subtypes of parotid gland tumors32. According to the relevant literature, although several research have applied CT-related radiomics to the diagnosis of parotid gland tumors1,15,20, few constructed radiomics model with different machine learning methods13,14,19. In our study, we developed three distinct radiomics-based predictive models and compared the performance of different machine learning algorithms for classifying those four common parotid gland tumor. Different classifiers indicate different model algorithms, which may result in different predictive diagnostic efficiencies.

In our findings, the machine learning classifier with the optimal efficacy in differential diagnosis for each group was not consistent. The classifiers with the largest AUCs in the comparisons of BPGT and MPGT (AUC = 0.838), PA and WT & BCA (AUC = 0.847), and WT and BCA (AUC = 0.870) were LR, SVM, and LR, respectively. Previous research demonstrated that, in terms of overall diagnostic accuracy, the performance of the SVM was superior to that of other machine learning classifiers19,20, which was different from our results. We propose that this situation might occur due to the different classification prediction models had their own advantages for distinct tasks. The performance of the radiomics model may largely depend on the characteristics of the classifier algorithm and the degree of matching between the classifiers and the target tumor of the model14. Furthermore, the final selected features in radiomics would be influenced by various techniques for feature extraction and selection, and the prediction performance of models constructed using different classifiers may also be different. This study demonstrated that the valuable radiomics features were different among the different types of parotid gland tumors, thus the selected classifier in the model with the best predictive efficacy were also different. The results of our study could provide a good reference for guiding the selection of the most appropriate classifiers in establishing different parotid tumor radiomics models. Moreover, the combined clinical-radiomics model has effectively improved the efficacy of differential diagnosis of tumors in each step, AUCs of 0.849, 0.873, 0.925, respectively, which suggested that although the optimal radiomics models in each step have more excellent predictive performance than clinical features, the clinical and radiological features are also significant, and this radiomics nomogram combining clinical features and radiomics signatures had the best preoperative prediction capability.

Moreover, it should also be noted that among selected final radiomics features for differentiating parotid gland tumors, most were the transform wavelet features. We speculate that the high-order statistics performed by transform-filtered features may highlight the details in the original image, and thus have the potential to reveal more details in the original image, and further reflect the heterogeneity among tumors, similar to the results of Jiang and Granata et al.33,34 found that the features of the transform wavelet can enhance the texture features of ultrasound images, and may be used to effectively predict the malignant potential of parotid gland lesions preoperatively35. And in terms of non-invasively predicting glioma-associated epilepsy, Gao et al. revealed that that wavelet transform features performed best in distinguishing glioma-associated epilepsy patients from glioma patients36. However, correctly interpreting machine learning prediction models and visually presenting their results to clinicians remains a considerable challenge. In the present study, we applied SHAP values to the best machine learning algorithm models for each step to achieve the best predictive effect and interpretive capabilities. The SHAP values can provide consistent and locally accurate attributions for each feature in the prediction model37. This interpretation is used to the black-box tree ensemble model in machine learning, thereby helping users better understand the decision-making process of the model. The detailed information presented in the results provides clinicians with more insights, enabling them to make more informed decisions rather than relying blindly on the algorithm’s outputs.

In the present study, the SHAP-based feature selection results showed that wavelet-transformed features played a dominant role. Among those features, the coefficients of GLDM features and GLCM features in the final radiomics model (step1) were relatively large, which can show intratumor heterogeneity and may be associated with tumor malignancy, and malignant tumors grow quickly and easily cause necrosis and microhemorrhage, making their heterogeneity higher than that of benign tumors, this could be explained by the higher tissue heterogeneity in MPGT compared with BPGT, this is consistent with previous studies13. Nevertheless, the difference is that our study combined both radiomics features and clinical characteristics that achieved a higher AUC than the radiomics and clinical models, which greatly compensates for the deficiencies of previous studies and will accelerate its clinical application. However, due to the significant differences in anatomical structure, tissue background, and pathological types of tumors in different parts, the existing parotid gland tumor models themselves do not have direct cross-site universality. Next, we will conduct model transfer for other tumors in the head and neck region and develop more generalizable radiomics labels. Ultimately, we aim to build a multi-disease and multi-parameter intelligent diagnostic platform covering the entire head and neck region.

The present study also faces some limitations. Firstly, the sample size of cases in each group was small, especially the BCA group, which led to our study also be limited by the small number of samples in our data set. Secondly, our study did not employ a strictly defined external set, thus limiting the generalization of our models. Thirdly, the radiomics features we extracted were from five different CT scanning devices, the diagnostic differential efficacy of the final radiomics features might be influenced by the scanning protocols. Further investigations with multicentric cohorts, larger data and external validation are necessary to validate these models before the aided system is applied in actual clinical practice. The future of machine learning-based model development in the medical field tends to combine multi-modality and multi-radiomics imaging information; however, due to the retrospective design of this study, we were unable to construct such combination models based on the ultrasound, CT, MR, genomic or transcriptomic data of patients with parotid gland tumors. Developing integrated models that synthesize these diverse data types may bring us more valuable information, and this will be a primary focus of our future research.

Conclusion

In conclusion, radiomics nomogram integrates clinical information and radiomics features demonstrates excellent predictive efficacy in differentiating BPGT from MPGT, PA from WT & BCA, as well as WT from BCA. Compared with conventional image diagnosis, it also presents obvious advantages, potentially offering an assistant diagnostic tool in clinical practice, especially for the preliminary screening and classification of parotid tumors.

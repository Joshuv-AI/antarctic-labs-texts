IFPHOR0045 Lived Experiences of Breast Cancer Survivors, Family Caregivers and Clinicians’ Perspectives of Service Provision in Nigeria: An Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis
Authors: Samuel Oluwasogo Dada, Dung Ezekiel Jidong, Juliet Tarela Ike, Juliet Y Pwajok, John Ezekiel Jidong, Christopher Francis, Shadrack B Mwankon, Nusrat Husain, Maigari Yusufu Taru, Charles Nnaemeka Nwoga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer is the most common cancer in women and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide. Previous qualitative research has revealed that breast cancer survivors face numerous challenges, such as lack of or inadequate access to resources, poor physical conditions, competing priorities, safety concerns and psychological issues. However, there is currently limited qualitative research on the lived experience of breast cancer survivors and clinicians for policy readiness in Nigeria to promote and improve the mental health outcomes of the patients/survivors. This paper, therefore, examined the lived experiences of breast cancer survivors, family caregivers, and clinicians’ perspectives of service provision in Nigeria using an interpretative phenomenological analytical lens.MethodsA purposive sampling technique was employed to recruit participants from the outpatient oncology clinics of Jos University Teaching Hospital (JUTH), Nigeria, using a qualitative approach. Eight breast cancer survivors participated in a focus group discussion. Four in-depth interviews were conducted among breast cancer survivors, two interviews were conducted among oncologists, and two interviews were conducted among family caregivers.ResultsThe study finds that hopelessness and suicidal ideation thoughts were prevalent among breast cancer survivors alongside informal help-seeking behaviour. Lack of psychosocial support, strong religious beliefs and faith in traditional healers as coping strategies were reported among breast cancer survivors.DiscussionBarriers to effective cancer treatment in Nigeria were cultural and religious beliefs. The study recommends culturally appropriate psychosocial support for patients affected by breast cancer.

Divergent Small Vessel Disease Burden in Warfarin-Associated and Direct Oral Anticoagulant-Associated Intracerebral Hemorrhage
Authors: Sung-Chun Tang, Ya-Fang Chen, Chih-Hao Chen, Ching-Hua Kuo, Yuan-Chang Chao, Yu-Fong Peng, Shu-Wen Lin, Shin-Yi Lin, Jiann-Shing Jeng
Published: 2026

Dear Sir:

Anticoagulant (AC)-associated intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a significant complication of AC therapy, often leading to unfavorable outcomes. The association between increased AC intensity and a greater risk of ICH is well established. For vitamin K antagonist, the risk of ICH increases with an elevated international normalized ratio (INR) [1]. Exposure-response analyses for direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC) similarly demonstrate an increased ICH risk with higher drug concentrations [2]. In recent years, cerebral small vessel disease (SVD) has also emerged as an important factor contributing to ICH. Our previous research showed that DOAC users who experienced ICH during treatment exhibited a greater SVD burden [3]. However, whether SVD burden differs between warfarin-associated and DOAC-associated ICH remains unclear. This study aimed to compare SVD burden among three cohorts of patients with spontaneous ICH, including DOAC users, warfarin users, and AC non-users. Additionally, AC users were analyzed according to different levels of AC intensity at presentation.

This prospective cohort study enrolled consecutive patients aged ≥20 years admitted to the National Taiwan University Hospital for spontaneous ICH. According to the antithrombotic agents given before ICH, patients were classified as DOAC users or warfarin users. For each AC user, an AC non-user was matched by sex and age (±2 years). Patients with pure intraventricular hemorrhage, tumor bleeding, or cerebral venous sinus thrombosis were excluded.

The study protocol received approval from the National Taiwan University Hospital International Ethics Committee (Rec. No. 202003059 RINA and Rec. No. 9561703046) and was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06144866).

Brain computed tomography (CT) was used to assess SVD burden based on white matter lesions (WML), lacunes and cerebral atrophy. The validated CT-evaluated SVD (CT-SVD) burden scoring system ranges from 0 to 3 (higher scores indicate greater burden) [4]. The specific definitions and scoring criteria are detailed in Supplementary Table 1. Hematoma volume was estimated using the ABC/2 formula [5]. Hematoma expansion was defined as an increase of ≥6 mL or ≥33% in volume on follow-up imaging performed 24–48 hours after the initial scan [6]. Neuroimaging was independently evaluated by a neuroradiologist (Y.F.C.) and a neurologist (C.H.C.). The inter-rater agreement, expressed as weighted Kappa values, was 0.72 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.59–0.85) for the total WML score and 0.76 (95% CI 0.65–0.86) for the total atrophy score in our previous investigation [4]. DOAC concentrations at hospital presentation were measured using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry method, as described in the Supplementary Material [7]. For AC users, AC intensity was defined as follows: (1) subtherapeutic: DOAC concentration <50 ng/mL or INR <1.5 for warfarin, (2) therapeutic: DOAC concentration within 50 ng/mL to <300 ng/mL or INR within 1.5 to <3.0 for warfarin, and (3) supratherapeutic: DOAC concentration ≥300 ng/mL or INR ≥3.0 for warfarin [3,8,9].

A total of 218 AC non-users, 75 DOAC users (2 dabigatran [2.7%], 24 rivaroxaban [32.0%], 26 apixaban [34.7%], and 23 edoxaban [30.7%]), and 91 warfarin users were included. Among AC users, 32 had subtherapeutic AC intensity (21 DOAC users and 11 warfarin users), 88 had therapeutic AC intensity (45 DOAC users and 43 warfarin users), and 46 had supratherapeutic AC intensity (9 DOAC users and 37 warfarin users). The participant enrollment flow is provided in Supplementary Figure 1.

The distribution of CT-SVD burden among the three medication groups is shown in Table 1. Warfarin users had the lowest SVD score (median and interquartile range [IQR], 0 [0–1]), followed by AC non-users (1 [0–2]) and DOAC users (2 [1–3]; P< 0.001). The pattern persisted across all age subgroups (Figure 1). After adjustment, warfarin users, compared with AC non-users, were significantly less likely to experience an increase in CT-SVD burden score (common odds ratio [cOR]: 0.19; 95% CI 0.09– 0.43; P<0.001). CT-SVD burden did not differ significantly between DOAC users and AC non-users (Supplementary Table 2). Sensitivity analyses were performed and the results remained consistent after excluding patients with valvular heart disease and when utilizing a modified CT-SVD burden score that excluded the atrophy component (Supplementary Table 3).

The clinical presentation and outcomes of ICH across the three medication groups are summarized in Table 2. Warfarin users had lower Glasgow Coma Scale scores, higher National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale and ICH scores, larger hematoma volumes, and a greater tendency toward supratentorial lobar hemorrhage. Hematoma volumes >30 mL occurred in 49.5% of warfarin users, compared with 24.0% of DOAC users and 30.3% of AC non-users (P<0.001). The median modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 3 months after ICH was 6 (IQR, 3–6) for warfarin users, compared with 4 (3–5) for AC non-users and 4 (3–6) for DOAC users. Among AC users, after adjustment, warfarin therapy was independently associated with a higher mRS score compared with DOAC use (cOR: 2.33; 95% CI 1.08–5.05; P=0.032) (Supplementary Table 4).

Among AC users, the median CT-SVD burden score was sig-nificantly lower in the supratherapeutic group (0 [0–2]) than in the therapeutic (1 [0–2]) and subtherapeutic groups (2 [0–2], P=0.032) (Supplementary Table 5). Warfarin use was consistently associated with a lower CT-SVD burden compared with DOAC use after adjustment (cOR: 0.22 [0.10–0.48], P<0.001) (Supplementary Table 2). This trend remained consistent across all AC intensity subgroups (Supplementary Table 6 and Supplementary Figure 2).

A major limitation is that head CT is less sensitive than magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) for detecting microbleeds and superficial siderosis. Therefore, the lower CT-SVD burden in warfarin users may reflect imaging constraints rather than biological differences. Furthermore, low CT-SVD burden scores in the supratherapeutic group may stem from coagulopathy masking microvascular contributions. We also acknowledge the coarser granularity of CT-SVD compared with MRI, though our previous work demonstrated substantial agreement between these modalities [4]. Additionally, while the proportional odds assumption was met in the overall population, data sparsity in small subgroups led to nominal violations in some analyses. Finally, this cross-sectional design precludes assessing the influence of cumulative antithrombotic exposure. CT-SVD burden likely represents a pre-existing condition rather than a consequence of long-term AC use.

In summary, warfarin users who experienced ICH demonstrated a lower SVD burden compared with DOAC users and AC nonusers, and this pattern persisted across all levels of AC intensity. These results imply that warfarin-associated ICH is more closely linked to excessive anticoagulation, whereas DOAC-associated ICH may reflect underlying cerebral small vessel pathology.

SUNRAY-01 trial protocol: an innovative study design of olomorasib and pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy in KRAS G12C NSCLC
Authors: Solange Peters, Maximilian Hochmair, Kathryn C. Arbour, Luis Paz-Ares Rodriguez, Martin Reck, Ticiana Leal, Colin R. Lindsay, Lu Shun, William Nassib William, Alexander I. Spira, Joshua K. Sabari, Bjørn Henning Grønberg, Makoto Nishio, Timothy F. Burns, Nicolas Girard, Frederico Capuzzo, Alyson Merced, Nicolas Fasnacht, Carla Visseren-Grul, Marcelo V. Negrao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOlomorasib (LY3537982) is a potent, highly selective, next-generation oral inhibitor of KRAS G12C that has demonstrated promising antitumor and intracranial activity as well as a manageable safety profile in combination with immunotherapy or chemoimmunotherapy for first-line treatment of KRAS G12C-mutant advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Here we describe the design and rationale of SUNRAY-01, a pivotal, global trial of olomorasib in combination with pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy for first-line treatment of KRAS G12C-mutant advanced NSCLC. Given that outcomes with KRAS G12C inhibitors remain dismal, this trial will assess whether combining olomorasib with existing standard-of-care treatment can improve outcomes in first-line KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC. SUNRAY-01 has an innovative design, incorporating two phase III trials in a single protocol to address the full spectrum of current first-line treatment options, and includes pragmatic elements to reduce potential barriers to enrollment, such as permitting enrollment based on local testing by liquid or tissue biopsy, allowing treatment with 1 cycle of standard-of-care during screening prior to enrollment, allowing patients with small untreated or previously treated brain metastases, and ensuring both arms receive standard treatment.Clinical trial registrationwww.clinicaltrials.gov identifier is NCT06119581. EudraCT: 2023-503412-33–00.

Introduction

Background and rationale

Current first-line (1 L) treatment options for patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) without a targetable driver alteration (including patients harboring KRAS [Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene] mutations) include an immune checkpoint inhibitor alone for patients whose tumors have a programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression ≥50% or in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy regardless of PD-L1 expression level [1,2], as reflected in international NSCLC treatment guidelines [3–5].

KRAS mutations are well-characterized and occur in 25–35% of lung adenocarcinomas in non-Asian patients, making it one of the most frequently mutated oncogenic drivers in metastatic NSCLC [6]. Of the KRAS mutations, the G12C variant is the most frequent mutation type in patients with NSCLC, accounting for approximately 40% of all KRAS mutations in this patient population [7]. The prevalence of KRAS G12C mutation among patients with advanced NSCLC has been estimated to be approximately 13% in Caucasian and 4% in Asian patients [7,8].

KRAS G12C mutations are smoking-related mutations, and are often found in older patients with more co-morbidities and high tumor mutation burden [6,9] who therefore require individualized treatment approaches to effectively manage disease control [10–12]. Additionally, evidence suggests that the lifetime incidence of brain metastases is up to 40% in patients with KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC [13,14] and only 40% of patients with the KRAS G12C mutation receive second-line systemic therapy [15]. These factors highlight the significant unmet need for improved treatment options in the 1 L setting, including KRAS inhibitors and novel combination strategies that can improve outcomes for these patients.

Despite the approval of KRAS G12C inhibitors adagrasib and sotorasib [16–18], outcomes in patients with KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC remain dismal partly because these agents are relegated to second- or later line use [19,20], and current 1 L treatment approaches do not specifically target the mutation. Unlike other oncogenic drivers such as EGFR and ALK, KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC is comparably or more sensitive to PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibition than KRAS wild-type NSCLC [12,21–24]. The presence of a KRAS mutation is associated with increased tumor immunogenicity, which enhances the response to immunotherapy [9,25,26]. In addition, treatment with a KRAS G12C inhibitor can promote T-cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment, which can also enhance the efficiacy of immunotherapy. This provides a biologic rationale for combining KRAS G12C inhibitors with immune checkpoint blockade in the 1 L setting [27].

Olomorasib (LY3537982) is a potent and highly selective next-generation oral inhibitor of KRAS G12C that covalently binds the mutated cysteine at position 12 in KRAS G12C and locks KRAS in its inactive GDP-bound form. Olomorasib was designed to deliver >90% sustained target occupancy at low absolute exposures to minimize off-target effects, improve tolerability when combined with other agents, and overcome some of the limitations of prior compounds in this class [28,29]. Olomorasib has shown promising antitumor and intracranial activity. Olomorasib is taken twice daily (BID) with or without food [30] and does not interact with proton pump inhibitors [data on file].

Recently disclosed data from the integrated analysis of phase I/II LOXO-RAS-20001 and SUNRAY-01 (NCT06119581) dose optimization (DO) and safety lead-in (SLI) have provided encouraging proof-of-concept for combining olomorasib and pembrolizumab +/- chemotherapy, with promising efficacy as well as a manageable and monitorable safety profile supporting the feasibility of these combinations [31–34].

The SUNRAY-01 trial represents a critical step toward potentially establishing a new treatment paradigm for KRAS G12C-mutant advanced NSCLC. By evaluating the integration of selective KRAS G12C inhibition into 1 L standard-of-care (SOC) regimens, this trial seeks to determine whether earlier intervention with targeted therapy can improve outcomes in this genomically defined patient population.

Strict eligibility criteria can often impede 1 L clinical trial enrollment for certain patient populations [35,36]. Broader, more inclusive criteria should be considered to support enrollment and better reflect real-world patient populations. This article outlines the innovative trial design and rationale of SUNRAY-01 and how it addresses key unmet needs in managing KRAS G12C-mutant advanced NSCLC.

The SUNRAY-01 clinical trial

SUNRAY-01 (NCT06119581) is a global, pivotal trial exploring the addition of olomorasib to SOC in 1 L treatment of advanced KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC. This pivotal trial is designed to seamlessly 1) select the dose of olomorasib in combination with 1 L SOC, and then 2) compare the efficacy and safety of olomorasib plus SOC against placebo plus SOC in 1 L advanced KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC (Figure 1). The trial is active and recruiting participants, with planned enrollment across approximately 425 sites in 29 countries spanning North America, South America, Australia, Europe, and Asia (Figure 2). Study design

The trial has 4 parts (Figure 1), 2 of which – DO and SLI – have completed enrollment. Both DO and SLI were open-label experiments that enrolled participants with advanced KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC across all PD-L1 expression levels (0–100%). These parts contributed to olomorasib dose selection. The remaining 2 parts, parts A and B are global, randomized (1:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-running phase III studies that are actively enrolling participants with locally advanced or metastatic KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC.

The study design ensures that all participants, both in the experimental and control arms, receive appropriate SOC [3–5]. Part A will compare olomorasib in combination with pembrolizumab to placebo in combination with pembrolizumab as 1 L treatment in 552 patients with a tumor with PD-L1 expression ≥50%. Part B will compare olomorasib in combination with pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed and platinum to placebo in combination with pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed and platinum as 1 L treatment in 552 participants with a tumor with PD-L1 expression 0% to 100%. Participants with PD-L1 expression ≥50% could be allocated to either part A or B at the discretion of the treating investigator based on need for cytotoxic chemotherapy. SUNRAY-01 was designed to reflect existing clinical practice, where allocation to part A or B aligns with standard treatment decision-making in the first line, which is based on tumor PD-L1 expression as well as patient suitability for either single agent pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy. To allow investigators to initiate treatment while awaiting screening results, 1 cycle of SOC prior to enrollment is permitted for cases when immediate treatment is clinically indicated.

Randomization and subsequent analysis will be stratified by the following prognostic factors: prior immunotherapy in the (neo-)adjuvant or consolidation setting (yes/no), received 1 cycle of SOC prior to randomization (yes/no), and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS; 0/1) for part A; and prior immunotherapy in the (neo-)adjuvant or consolidation setting (yes/no), received 1 cycle of SOC prior to randomization (yes/no), and PD-L1 expression (<1%, 1–49%, ≥50%) for part B.

In addition to the study design elements outlined above, several enrollment and recruitment strategies have been adopted to optimize patient enrollment, including but not limited to the following: SOC-aligned schedules to reduce patient and site burden, sponsor-supported initiatives, and digital and print patient-facing educational materials available in other languages, where applicable.

The primary endpoint of phase III parts A and B is progression-free survival (PFS) per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors v1.1 (RECIST v1.1) [37] by blinded independent central review (BICR). Tumor response will be assessed every 6 weeks (±7 days) as calculated from cycle 1 day 1 following randomization. Starting at 54 weeks in part A and 66 weeks in part B, assessments should be obtained every 12 weeks (±7 days) until progression, the start of a new anticancer therapy, death, or study completion. Brain imaging is required at baseline for all participants. For participants with known presence or history of brain metastases, brain imaging will be obtained at the same frequency as other radiologic imaging. For participants without baseline brain metastases, brain imaging will be obtained every 12 weeks (±7 days) relative to cycle 1 day 1.

Secondary endpoints include overall survival (OS); PFS as assessed by the investigator; BICR- and investigator-assessed overall response rate (ORR), duration of response (DoR), disease control rate (DCR), and time to response (TTR) per RECIST v1.1; intracranial ORR and DoR per modified RECIST (mRECIST) by BICR [38]; investigator-assessed PFS2; patient-reported outcomes (PROs); quality-of-life outcomes; and safety and tolerability. Exploratory endpoints include evaluation of the relationship between biomarkers and clinical outcomes, as well as assessment of time to intracranial progression per mRECIST by BICR for all patients.

Part A and part B safety data will be monitored by an independent Data Monitoring Committee on a regular basis.

Methods

Key eligibility criteria

In this trial and compared to other contemporary registrational phase III 1 L NSCLC studies, eligibility criteria were intentionally designed to be more flexible and pragmatic, thereby allowing for broader patient inclusion. To be eligible to participate in SUNRAY-01, patients must be adults (≥18 years) with histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC stage IIIB-IIIC, or stage IV NSCLC and not suitable for curative intent radical surgery or radiation therapy, with evidence of KRAS G12C mutation in tumor or blood and known PD-L1 expression (part A: ≥50%, part B: 0–100%) using a locally qualified or sponsor enabled test. Additionally, eligibility for part B requires the tumor histology to be predominantly non-squamous, in line with the pemetrexed label.

All participants must have an ECOG PS of 0 or 1 and measurable disease per RECIST v1.1. Participants with small (≤1.5 cm) asymptomatic untreated or previously treated brain metastases are eligible provided they meet the following stability criteria: radiologically stable (no central nervous system [CNS] progression for ≥14 days by repeat imaging such as magnetic resonance imaging or computed tomography), neurologically and clinically stable (no new/worsening symptoms for ≥14 days, no urgent CNS-directed therapy required), and receiving ≤10 mg/day prednisone equivalent if on corticosteroids. Previously treated lesions require completion of local therapy ≥14 days prior to enrollment. Additionally, the washout for palliative radiation is 1 week (if >30 Gy to the lung, treatment must have been completed at least 6 months prior to first dose of study drug).

While prior systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic NSCLC is excluded, 1 cycle of SOC treatment (part A: pembrolizumab monotherapy, part B: pemetrexed-platinum +/- pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab monotherapy) prior to enrollment is permitted. Additional exclusion criteria include presence of an additional validated targetable oncogenic driver mutation or alteration, and a known additional malignancy that is progressing or has required active treatment within the past 2 years.

Treatment plan

Treatment will be administered in 3-week cycles for all study drugs. Olomorasib 100 mg BID in combination with pembrolizumab +/- chemotherapy was selected for the phase III parts of SUNRAY-01 based on data from phase I/II LOXO-RAS-20001 and the DO and SLI parts of SUNRAY-01. This dosage was selected based on the totality of evidence, including considerations such as target coverage, efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics, from both monotherapy and combination study datasets. In part A, participants will receive olomorasib or placebo (100 mg BID, orally) administered in continuous 3-week cycles plus pembrolizumab administered at a dose of 200 mg intravenously (iv.) on day 1 of each 3-week treatment cycle (Table 2). In part B, participants will receive olomorasib or placebo (100 mg BID, orally) plus pembrolizumab (200 mg, iv.), pemetrexed (500 mg/m2, iv.) plus investigator choice of either carboplatin (AUC 5 mg/ml/min, iv.) or cisplatin (75 mg/m2, iv.) administered every 3 weeks for 4 cycles. After completion of 4 cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy without progressive disease, participants will receive maintenance therapy with olomorasib or placebo (100 mg BID, orally) administered in continuous 3-week cycles and pembrolizumab and pemetrexed on day 1 of each 3-week cycle. Appropriate pre-medications are administered as per local practice and/or labels. Dose modifications will follow protocol-specified criteria, with modifications based on toxicity severity using standardized CTCAE v5.0 grading, attribution, duration, and recurrence. Statistical plan

The primary endpoint for part A and part B is PFS by BICR, defined as the time from randomization to the date of first documented progression of disease as determined by BICR according to RECIST v1.1 or death from any cause, whichever occurs first. Both part A and B are powered approximately 90% at a 1-sided alpha of 0.025 to detect the superiority of olomorasib + SOC over placebo + SOC in PFS by BICR. To achieve this, parts A and B will both randomize approximately 552 participants. A hierarchical testing approach will be used to evaluate the key secondary endpoint of OS to control the type I error at the 1-sided alpha of 0.025 separately in part A and part B.

Parts A and B will each have their own statistical analysis plan, allowing for a robust evaluation of the olomorasib combinations compared with the respective SOC treatments in each participant population. The Kaplan–Meier method will be used to estimate PFS, including median PFS and PFS rates at various time points with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The comparison of PFS curves between treatment arms will be conducted by a stratified log-rank test. The treatment effect will be estimated by a hazard ratio with its corresponding 95% CI using the stratified Cox proportional hazards model with treatment as the only covariate.

Ethics and dissemination

The SUNRAY-01 protocol is approved by the institutional review board at each participating institution. This trial is being conducted in compliance with the protocol, the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Council for Harmonisation Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice E6, and local regulatory requirements. Informed consent is obtained from all participants and is documented in the participants’ electronic medical records.

Conclusions

Overall prognosis for KRAS G12C-mutant advanced NSCLC remains poor, with current 1 L treatment options guided by PD-L1 expression and clinical characteristics. Options include an immune checkpoint inhibitor alone for patients whose tumors have a PD-L1 expression ≥50% or in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for patients whose tumors have a PD-L1 expression <50% and for high-risk patients irrespective of PD-L1 expression level.

The SUNRAY-01 trial will explore whether the combination of olomorasib with pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy will improve outcomes for these patients. The innovative and progressive SUNRAY-01 trial design and operational support was developed to enable inclusive and representative enrollment. It does this by incorporating pragmatic elements to align with real world clinical practice as well as patient and site needs, thereby reducing barriers to enrollment.

The parallel-running phase III parts A and B in SUNRAY-01 address the full spectrum of current 1 L treatment options and enable enrollment of participants with a KRAS G12C mutation in one protocol, thereby reducing site/operational burden by consolidating patient populations under this single trial protocol. SUNRAY-01 is designed to mirror current clinical practice where 1 L treatment decisions are guided by tumor PD-L1 expression and suitability for single-agent pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy. Participants with PD-L1 expression ≥50% could be allocated to either part A or B, at the discretion of the investigator. Because of the parallel-running trial design and consistent with clinical treatment guidelines, patients with PD-L1 expression ≥50% who also have poor prognostic features or factors that are associated with poorer outcomes to immunotherapy, including high tumor burden, symptomatic disease, and/or are never-smokers, are more likely to be enrolled to the chemo-immunotherapy regimen in part B. The trial design ensures that all participants, in both the control and experimental arms, receive appropriate SOC treatment.

Recognizing that restrictive eligibility criteria can impede enrollment, create inclusion disparities, and limit future applicability, especially in 1 L trials, this trial was designed to expand inclusion criteria and enhance trial representativeness [35]. Turnaround time for biomarker testing, including KRAS and PD-L1 testing, can negatively impact treatment decisions [21–24,36]. In SUNRAY-01, patients can be enrolled using local biomarker testing by liquid or tissue biopsy, as opposed to central testing, which expedites screening and aligns with clinical practice by using local testing results for treatment decisions [36]. The trial also provides sponsor-enabled and/or supported tumor profiling during optional pre-screening. An archived tumor sample is additionally required for retrospective central confirmation of KRAS G12C and PD-L1 expression level.

To address the urgency to start treatment in this patient population, SUNRAY-01 allows investigators to initiate treatment while awaiting screening results. One cycle of SOC treatment prior to enrollment will be allowed for cases where immediate treatment is clinically indicated. By using this approach, investigators can prioritize immediate treatment needs without excluding a patient from a clinical trial opportunity while also allowing patients and their caregivers to explore clinical trial options without delaying the start of necessary therapy. The benefit of this flexibility also permits patients who initiated a cycle of SOC at a local institution to be considered for the trial during the referral process to a clinical trial site. This pragmatic approach resulted in ~25% of patients enrolled in the DO and SLI parts of SUNRAY-01 having received a cycle of SOC prior to joining the trial. These patients would have otherwise been excluded by more conventional trial designs [39]. This expanded eligibility continues in the phase III parts of SUNRAY-01. Although permitting one cycle of SOC prior to randomization may introduce heterogeneity, this potential risk is mitigated through stratification and predefined analysis plans. The presence of brain metastases is a common eligibility barrier for KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC [40]. In line with the 2020 guidance “Cancer Clinical Trial Eligibility Criteria: Brain Metastases” [41], SUNRAY-01 permits participants with small asymptomatic untreated brain metastases as well as patients with stable, treated brain metastases and has reduced CNS-directed therapy washout periods to 14 days for patients with previously treated brain metastases to enable enrollment of this patient population.

The primary endpoint of phase III parts A and B will be PFS (by BICR). In the 1 L setting, PFS is an appropriate and globally accepted regulatory endpoint and an established intermediate surrogate for OS in advanced NSCLC [42–45].

Olomorasib plus pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy has shown a safety profile that is consistent with other established 1 L regimens combining targeted therapy with immunotherapy and/or chemotherapy, including FLAURA2 (NCT04035486) and PAPILLON (NCT04538664) [33,34,46–48]. The toxicities reported for olomorasib combination treatments are generally manageable, and the regimen is overall tolerable. Based on learnings from the phase I/II LOXO-RAS-20001 trial and DO and SLI parts of SUNRAY-01, the dose adjustment guidance for phase III parts A and B has been updated. Olomorasib’s short half-life of ~3 hours supports interruption and potential dose reduction of olomorasib as the initial management of select adverse events. This strategy will help prevent unnecessary initiation of high-dose steroids and/or discontinuation of the SOC backbone treatment. Preliminary efficacy data support the potential of olomorasib to increase the overall effectiveness of the SOC backbone regimen, supporting the further evaluation of these regimens in phase III [29–31].

Given the recent promising data from the phase I/II and DO and SLI studies, SUNRAY-01 phase III parts A and B will be crucial to confirm the efficacy and safety of olomorasib in combination with SOC pembrolizumab with or without chemotherapy for the 1 L treatment of advanced KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC [31–34].

Future perspective

Olomorasib in combination with immunotherapy is also entering the arena of early-stage NSCLC. The phase III trial SUNRAY-02 (NCT06890598) will evaluate immunotherapy in combination with olomorasib or placebo in resected or unresectable early-stage KRAS G12C-mutant NSCLC and is open for enrollment [49].

AI driven system for enhancing consumer electronics through maintenance personalization and security

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid expansion of consumer electronics has created an urgent need for advanced solutions to critical challenges such as predictive maintenance, user personalization, and device security. Traditional models often struggle to address these issues due to their limited adaptability and performance. This paper introduces GenAI-A, an innovative AI model that integrates Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Dynamic Recommendation Algorithms (DRA), and anomaly detection techniques to provide a comprehensive solution for consumer electronics. By leveraging the strengths of generative AI and self-renewal capabilities, GenAI-A enhances predictive maintenance, optimizes user experience, and strengthens biometric security. The model’s hybrid architecture utilizes GANs for realistic data representation, VAEs for the generation of complex data distributions, and DRA for real-time user personalization. The novelty of GenAI-A lies in its cross-regularized coupling between the GAN and VAE modules, where latent features are jointly optimized through a shared loss function to achieve consistent generative–representational learning. Unlike conventional hybrids that treat these models independently, GenAI-A introduces a dynamic feedback mechanism in which the DRA and anomaly detection modules operate directly in the shared latent space, enabling self-adaptive personalization and continual refinement of generative outputs. Experimental validation across four real-world datasets, i.e., Smartphone Sensor, Labelled Faces in the Wild (LFW), Pecan Street Energy Consumption, and SECOM Manufacturing, demonstrates significant improvements in device uptime, user engagement, and biometric security, with a notable reduction in false positives. Unlike existing hybrid generative models, GenAI-A introduces a novel integration of these components in a dynamic, self-learning system that adapts in real time to evolving user behaviors and device conditions. Introduction

The rapid evolution of consumer electronics necessitates advanced solutions to critical challenges such as predictive maintenance, user personalization, and device security. Traditional approaches often prove inadequate in addressing these issues due to limitations in flexibility, precision, and adaptability. GenAI, particularly through the use of GANs and VAEs, presents significant opportunities to overcome these limitations1,2. This technology offers the potential to more effectively control, customize, and secure everyday gadgets, ranging from wearable devices and smartphones to smart home systems.

Historically, issues in electronic systems have been handled reactively; however, with the increasing complexity and criticality of modern devices, proactive and adaptive strategies are now required2. Predictive maintenance, personalization, and security have traditionally relied on simpler algorithms or manual configurations3,4. With the growing sophistication of devices, the demand for smarter, more responsive, and more resilient solutions has never been greater. Generative AI provides a pathway to achieve this by enabling systems to predict potential failures, personalize user experiences, and enhance security through dynamic data-driven learning4,5.

Unlike conventional models that address these challenges separately using independent algorithms, GenAI-A introduces a novel hybrid paradigm that unifies GANs, VAEs, and Dynamic Recommendation Algorithms within a single adaptive framework6. This hybrid architecture enables the model to meet the dynamic, real-time requirements of consumer electronics, offering substantial improvements in device uptime, user engagement, and system security. While GANs and VAEs have previously been used independently in generative AI, GenAI-A advances the field by establishing a cross-regularized coupling between the two models, where latent representations are jointly optimized through a shared loss function. The DRA operates within this shared latent space, enabling continuous feedback and adaptive personalization, while anomaly detection further refines the generative process through self-learning. This synergistic design provides a theoretically distinct and practically scalable mechanism for self-evolving, AI-driven device management.

Motivation

Despite rapid technological progress, managing consumer electronics still poses multiple challenges in maintenance, personalization, and security. Existing predictive maintenance systems rely heavily on historical or static datasets and often struggle to adapt to real-time variations in device behaviour7. Traditional personalization algorithms are typically rigid and fail to adjust to evolving user needs or contextual changes. Similarly, even with the integration of biometric and encryption-based mechanisms, current security systems remain prone to false alarms and often lag in responding to emerging threats6.

The GenAI-A model is specifically designed to overcome these shortcomings by combining advanced generative and adaptive AI techniques, i.e., GANs, VAEs, and DRA into a self-correcting and continuously learning framework. This integration allows GenAI-A to improve maintenance prediction accuracy, enhance personalization fidelity, and strengthen biometric security while maintaining adaptability to real-world dynamics8,9.

Existing work

The integration of GenAI has been observed to advance a wide range of industries, including consumer electronics, healthcare, and financial services. The capability of GenAI to address data generation, anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, and personalization has resulted in significant improvements across practical applications. Nevertheless, existing hybrid generative models, particularly those combining GANs and VAEs, have continued to face notable challenges. These limitations are especially evident in their inability to adapt in real time to evolving device conditions and user preferences.

In this section, relevant literature is reviewed, the distinctions between GenAI-A and existing approaches are analyzed, and the unique architectural and algorithmic features that differentiate GenAI-A are highlighted.

Hybrid generative models: current state and limitations

Hybrid models that integrate GANs and VAEs have been shown to achieve substantial progress in data generation and anomaly detection tasks. GANs have been recognized for their effectiveness in generating highly realistic synthetic data, achieved through a competitive process in which a generator produces artificial samples and a discriminator evaluates their authenticity1. However, GAN-based systems have largely been designed for static data generation and often fail to accommodate real-time adaptation. This limitation makes them less suitable for dynamic environments such as consumer electronics, where continuous interactions and fluctuating operational conditions are common [21, 27].

In contrast, VAEs have been employed effectively for encoding and decoding complex data distributions, making them valuable for dimensionality reduction and data reconstruction tasks2. Despite these advantages, VAE-based architectures exhibit constraints in predicting rare events and in adapting to novel data distributions without extensive retraining. Their dependency on large quantities of labeled data also reduces their applicability in consumer electronic systems, where unexpected failures frequently occur and labeled data are scarce17.

Earlier research, such as that conducted by3, applied AI and machine learning techniques within domains including automotive and financial services for applications like insurance optimization and identity protection. Although such studies have demonstrated potential, they were typically performed in static or semi-static environments that lack the flexibility required for real-time adaptation in consumer electronics. Similarly, the study by4 explored the use of GenAI in mental health applications, emphasizing personalization but omitting considerations for predictive maintenance or real-time environmental adaptation. These limitations underscore the necessity for an adaptive, unified generative system capable of continuous learning and adjustment in dynamically changing conditions18.

Traditional algorithms are typically static and non-adaptive, often relying on low-dimensional or uncorrelated data sources that cannot capture dynamic device behaviour13,28.

Statistical testing (ANOVA and non-parametric tests)

In this section, the performance of all models is further analyzed using appropriate statistical validation methods to ensure that the observed improvements are statistically significant. Since the models were trained and tested on the same datasets using k-fold cross-validation, a paired t-test is not suitable due to the violation of independence assumptions between folds. Therefore, two statistical methods are employed: ANOVA and non-parametric tests, to evaluate the differences in model performance rigorously.

Analysis of variance (ANOVA)

The ANOVA test is applied to compare the performance of GenAI-A, SVM, Decision Trees, Random Forest, CNN, RNN, LSTM, and Transformer models across all k-folds within each dataset. This test captures both within-group (fold-wise) and between-group (model-wise) variability, allowing for a robust evaluation of whether the performance differences between models are statistically meaningful.

Null Hypothesis (H₀): There is no statistically significant difference in the mean performance across the models.

Null Hypothesis (H₀): There is no statistically significant difference in the mean performance across the models.

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁): At least one model exhibits a significantly different mean performance compared to others.

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁): At least one model exhibits a significantly different mean performance compared to others.

The ANOVA test results presented in Table 14; Fig. 12 reveal statistically significant differences in performance across all evaluated models, as indicated by p-values less than 0.05 for each dataset. Consequently, the null hypothesis (H₀) is rejected, confirming that there are meaningful variations in mean performance among the models. These results validate that the observed performance improvements of GenAI-A over traditional and deep learning baselines are not due to random chance but are statistically significant.

To further validate the robustness of the results, particularly in cases where the data distribution deviates from normality, the Kruskal–Wallis H test was employed. This non-parametric alternative to ANOVA evaluates differences in median performance among models without assuming a normal distribution, thereby providing a more generalized statistical comparison.

Null Hypothesis (H₀): The median performance across all models is the same.

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁): At least one model exhibits a significantly different median performance.

The Kruskal–Wallis H test results confirm that there are statistically significant differences in the median performance among the evaluated models across all datasets, with p-values less than 0.05 (Table 16; Fig. 14). Hence, the null hypothesis (H₀) is rejected, supporting the conclusion that the models do not perform equally in terms of median performance.

Discussion

In this study, a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed GenAI-A model was conducted against a diverse spectrum of benchmark approaches, including traditional machine learning algorithms such as SVM, Decision Trees, and Random Forest, advanced deep learning architectures including CNN, RNN, LSTM, and Transformer, as well as recent hybrid and foundation models such as VAE–CNN Hybrid, AutoEncoder–Attention (AE–Attn), Vision Transformer (ViT), and CLIP Encoder. The evaluation encompassed four domain-representative datasets: Smartphone Sensor, Pecan Street, Labelled Faces in the Wild (LFW), and SECOM Manufacturing, selected to capture distinct real-world challenges spanning predictive maintenance, energy consumption forecasting, biometric security, and industrial process optimization. Collectively, these datasets provide a robust and multifaceted benchmark for assessing the generalization, adaptability, and computational efficiency of the proposed hybrid generative–adaptive architecture.

Across all experiments, GenAI-A achieved the highest accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-scores, while maintaining strong AUC-ROC and specificity values. On the Smartphone Sensor Dataset (Table 6; Fig. 4), GenAI-A achieved 92.00% accuracy and 93.00% AUC-ROC, outperforming both traditional baselines such as SVM (85.00%) and Decision Trees (87.00%), and contemporary architectures like Transformer (91.80%) and GAN–Transformer (91.20%). The inclusion of hybrid and foundation models—such as VAE–CNN Hybrid, AE–Attention, ViT, and CLIP Encoder—demonstrated incremental performance improvements compared to classical deep learning models; however, they remained up to 1.5% below GenAI-A in overall predictive accuracy and required significantly higher training times (17–19 h versus 12 h). This finding indicates that while foundation models excel in representational power, they remain computationally demanding and less efficient for real-time or resource-constrained deployment.

In the LFW Dataset (Table 7; Fig. 5), GenAI-A demonstrated exceptional biometric recognition performance, achieving 98.00% accuracy and 99.00% AUC-ROC, outperforming all comparative baselines. Hybrid generative architectures such as GAN–Transformer (97.30%) and foundation models such as ViT (97.20%) and CLIP Encoder (96.90%) also performed well, confirming the value of transformer-based representation learning in facial recognition. However, GenAI-A’s superior F1-score (97.55%) and reduced training time (14 h versus 18–20 h) suggest a balanced trade-off between accuracy and efficiency, making it more suitable for scalable biometric and security applications. Traditional models (e.g., Basic Anomaly Detection at 92.00% and CNN at 88.00%) underperformed significantly, reaffirming that generative–adaptive modeling enables more robust identity discrimination under variable conditions.

On the Pecan Street Dataset (Table 8; Fig. 6), which focuses on energy consumption forecasting, GenAI-A achieved 90.00% accuracy and 91.50% AUC-ROC, again outperforming all baselines. The GAN–Transformer (89.20%) and ViT (89.50%) yielded competitive but slightly lower results, while requiring longer training durations (16–17 h). Other hybrid configurations, such as VAE–CNN (88.80%) and AE–Attention (88.40%), displayed solid performance but lacked the temporal–contextual adaptability achieved by GenAI-A. Traditional and predictive analytics models, such as Standard Energy Management (85.00%) and Predictive Analytics (87.00%), exhibited limited precision and recall, highlighting the advantage of GenAI-A’s capacity to capture nonlinear, temporal dependencies in complex energy data streams. These findings establish GenAI-A as an energy-efficient and scalable framework for smart grid analytics and sustainability-driven predictive modeling.

In the SECOM Manufacturing Dataset (Table 9; Fig. 7), GenAI-A achieved 91.00% accuracy and 92.30% AUC-ROC, outperforming not only traditional machine learning models like Random Forest (86.00%) and Decision Trees (87.50%), but also deep learning models such as CNN (88.70%) and Transformer (89.20%). Hybrid and foundation models—GAN–Transformer (90.40%), ViT (90.70%), and CLIP Encoder (90.00%)—approached comparable accuracy levels but incurred substantially longer training times, indicating reduced computational efficiency. The superior F1-score (88.85%) and consistent precision–recall balance of GenAI-A highlight its ability to generalize effectively in noisy industrial environments where fault patterns are often complex and nonlinear.

The statistical analyses presented in Sect. 4.4 further substantiate GenAI-A’s superiority. The results of ANOVA and Kruskal–Wallis H tests yielded p-values < 0.05 across all datasets, confirming that the performance differences between GenAI-A and other models are statistically significant. As illustrated in Table 14; Fig. 12, ANOVA results showed big between-group differences, while Tukey’s HSD post-hoc test (Table 15; Fig. 13) validated that GenAI-A consistently outperformed SVM, Decision Trees, and Random Forest across performance metrics. The Kruskal–Wallis H Test (Table 16; Fig. 14) confirmed that GenAI-A also achieved significantly higher median scores, reinforcing its robustness and generalizability.

The Ablation Study (Table 17; Fig. 15) further revealed the importance of each module within GenAI-A’s hybrid design. When any core component (GAN, VAE, or DRA) was removed, overall performance degraded considerably, with the exclusion of the GAN module leading to the most significant drop. This confirms that the synergy among GAN, VAE, and DRA is central to the model’s success. The GAN module ensures high-fidelity data generation, the VAE enables structured latent-space learning and stability, and the DRA provides dynamic personalization and adaptive feedback. Collectively, these modules form a collaborative ecosystem that enhances both predictive power and adaptability.

The novelty of GenAI-A lies in its cross-regularized hybrid architecture, which unifies generative modeling, representation learning, and adaptive recommendation within a single, optimized framework. Three design innovations distinguish it from conventional hybrid architectures:

GenAI-A represents a novel and practical contribution to the field of intelligent systems, offering a unified solution that simultaneously addresses predictive maintenance, personalization, and security challenges in modern data-driven applications.

Conclusion and future directions

Conclusion

This research proposed and developed GenAI-A, an innovative hybrid artificial intelligence framework designed to address the persistent limitations of existing machine learning, deep learning, and even recent foundation-model-based systems in domains such as predictive maintenance, personalization, and security. Conventional AI approacheswhile effective within narrowly defined tasksoften exhibit poor generalization, limited adaptability, and high computational demands when exposed to heterogeneous, multimodal real-world data. To overcome these constraints, GenAI-A was engineered as a cross-regularized generative–adaptive architecture, integrating three complementary components: GANs for synthetic data generation and imbalance correction, VAEs for latent representation learning, and a DRA module for context-aware parameter tuning and continuous learning.

The novelty of GenAI-A lies in its synergistic integration of generative and adaptive mechanisms within a unified hybrid system. The GAN component enhances representational diversity and mitigates noise sensitivity; the VAE module captures complex latent distributions, strengthening the coherence and generalization of learned embeddings; and the DRA module enables the model to dynamically adjust to user behavior, environmental conditions, and task-specific objectives in real time. This collaborative tri-module interaction allows GenAI-A to perform learning, generation, and adaptation concurrently, bridging the long-standing gap between generative modeling and self-adaptive intelligence.

Experimental validation across four representative real-world datasets such as Smartphone Sensor, Pecan Street, Labelled Faces in the Wild (LFW), and SECOM Manufacturing demonstrated the model’s superior versatility and performance. Comparisons against traditional algorithms (SVM, Decision Trees, Random Forest), deep learning models (CNN, RNN, LSTM, Transformer), and modern hybrid/foundation architectures (GAN–Transformer, VAE–CNN Hybrid, AutoEncoder–Attention, Vision Transformer, CLIP Encoder) consistently showed GenAI-A achieving the highest accuracy, F1-score, and AUC-ROC across all tasks.

On the Smartphone Sensor Dataset, GenAI-A achieved 92.00 ± 1.02% accuracy and 93.00 ± 1.02% AUC-ROC, outperforming both CNN-based (88%) and transformer-based (91.8%) baselines while maintaining lower training time (12 h). On the LFW dataset, it attained 98.00 ± 0.85% accuracy and a near-perfect 99.00 ± 0.92% AUC-ROC, surpassing foundation models such as ViT (97.2%) and CLIP Encoder (96.9%) in both precision and recall. Similarly, for the Pecan Street Dataset, GenAI-A achieved 90.00 ± 0.76% accuracy and 91.50 ± 0.90% AUC-ROC, outperforming hybrid and transformer-based models despite reduced computational overhead. Finally, on the SECOM Manufacturing Dataset, GenAI-A reached 91.00 ± 0.78% accuracy and 92.30 ± 0.86% AUC-ROC, validating its strong generalization capability in industrial fault detection compared to both traditional and modern foundation-based baselines.

Statistical analyses using ANOVA, Tukey’s HSD, and Kruskal–Wallis H tests confirmed that GenAI-A’s improvements were statistically significant (p < 0.05) across all datasets. Furthermore, ablation studies verified the contribution of each module: removing the GAN led to the steepest performance drop, followed by the VAE and DRA components, underscoring the critical synergy among generative representation learning and adaptive control mechanisms.

Beyond raw performance, GenAI-A introduces an architectural advancement through cross-regularized latent alignment and reconstruction-guided gradient modulation, ensuring stable convergence and minimizing mode collapse—a key limitation in conventional generative models. Its adaptive feedback loop allows continuous evolution as new data or user feedback becomes available, enabling self-personalization and long-term learning in dynamic environments. These properties position GenAI-A as an ideal solution for real-time, resource-constrained, and multi-domain applications, including smart devices, industrial IoT systems, energy optimization platforms, and adaptive security frameworks.

Overall, this research demonstrates that GenAI-A represents a significant paradigm shift in hybrid artificial intelligence. It effectively:

Overcomes the rigidity of static learning systems by integrating real-time feedback-driven adaptation.

In essence, GenAI-A establishes a new benchmark for hybrid intelligent systems, combining the representational strength of generative modeling, the depth of latent encoding, and the flexibility of adaptive optimization. Its high predictive accuracy, computational efficiency, and domain generalization capabilities demonstrate not only a theoretical contribution but also practical viability for next-generation intelligent systems. By addressing critical limitations of both deep learning and foundation models, GenAI-A lays the groundwork for the future development of self-evolving, interpretable, and ethically aligned AI architectures capable of sustained learning in complex, real-world environments.

Future research can focus on improving its scalability, adaptability, and ethical alignment while deepening its integration with emerging technologies and cross-domain applications. These advancements will help establish GenAI-A as a truly universal framework for intelligent, context-aware, and secure AI systems.

Scalability and Real-Time Deployment: Although the current implementation of GenAI-A performs effectively on medium-sized datasets, future work should aim to enhance its scalability for large-scale, real-time applications. This involves optimizing its architecture to handle massive data streams in distributed and resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices, mobile platforms, and IoT ecosystems. Implementing model compression, pruning, and distributed learning strategies will allow GenAI-A to operate efficiently without compromising accuracy. Additionally, enabling real-time streaming inference would make the model suitable for dynamic, time-sensitive scenarios like predictive maintenance in smart factories or autonomous systems.

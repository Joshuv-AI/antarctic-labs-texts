Prevalence and importance of coronary microvascular dysfunction in patients with heart failure and reduced or mildly reduced ejection fraction
Authors: Charlotte Nordberg Backelin, Sara Svedlund, Entela Bollano, Clara Hjalmarsson, Akash Kumar Gupta, Karl Johan Dahllöf, Fredrik Wolfhagen Sand, Maria Lagerström Fermer, Erik Michaelsson, Alastair Moss, Ida Silfversparre, Tove Brodin, Carlo Pirazzi, Lars Lund, Camilla Hage, Charlotta Ljungman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsWe investigated the prevalence of coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) and its association with severity of heart failure in patients with reduced or mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF and HFmrEF).MethodPatients with stable, symptomatic heart failure with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) <50% were enrolled. Data collection included physical examination, blood samples, Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ), carotid to femoral pulse wave velocity, echocardiography and adenosine-based transthoracic Doppler echocardiography to assess coronary flow reserve (CFR). A CFR <2.5 was used to diagnose CMD. Adjusted multivariable linear regression analysis with CFR as the dependent variable and adjusted multivariate logistic regression with CMD as the dependent variable were performed.ResultsA total of 125 patients were included, of whom 99 (79%) were men. The overall mean age is 73.4 (±7.5) years. In patients eligible for CFR (n=68, 54%), CMD was present in 45 (66%). Patients with CMD had higher N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide (NTproBNP), hsTroponin-T, lower KCCQ score, lower left and right ventricular and left atrial global longitudinal strain (GLS) (p<0.05). In multivariable linear regression, lower CFR was independently associated with reduced GLS, higher NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T. Furthermore, in adjusted logistic regression analysis, lower LVEF, reduced right ventricular GLS and higher biomarkers were independently associated with an increased risk of CMD.ConclusionCMD was present in 66% of patients with chronic heart failure and HFrEF or HFmrEF. Markers of more severe heart failure, including reduced GLS and higher NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T, were independently associated with lower CFR. Reduced right ventricular GLS and higher levels of the biomarkers were also independently associated with CMD.Keywords: Heart Failure, Systolic; Echocardiography; Coronary Vessels; Epidemiology

Introduction

The coronary microcirculation has a crucial role in the regulation of blood flow to the myocardium. In different pathological settings, this finely tuned system is dysfunctional, and the presence of coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is associated with worse outcome in patients with established cardiovascular disease. Remodelling of the coronary microcirculation, impaired vasodilation and/or increased coronary microvascular spasm causes CMD.1 A validated method to assess the function of the coronary microcirculation is coronary flow reserve (CFR).2 3

In patients with heart failure and preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), CMD is reported in up to 75% of patients.4 5 CMD is associated with higher N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NTproBNP), endothelial dysfunction, cardiac dysfunction, and most importantly, worse cardiovascular prognosis in HFpEF.6 The presence and importance of CMD in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), and mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF), is less well explored. In patients with HFrEF referred for myocardial perfusion positron emission tomography due to suspicion of angina, more than 80% had a low CFR (≤2.0), although there was bias by indication, and CMD was associated with major adverse cardiovascular events and death.7 Patients with HFrEF and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) may have a lower CFR compared with healthy controls. Interestingly, a CFR ≤2.5 in patients with DCM has a stronger correlation with echocardiographic parameters of diastolic dysfunction than to other echocardiographic parameters.8 Thus, previous studies of CMD in HFrEF and HFmrEF patients have been biased or included few patients. Hence, the prevalence of CMD in stable HFrEF and HFmrEF patients and the impact on cardiac function remain unexplored. We, therefore, conducted a prospective exploratory bicentre study in patients with chronic HFrEF and HFmrEF with the aim to assess the prevalence of CMD and explore its association with clinical characteristics, echocardiographic parameters of cardiac function and biomarkers, in the Physiological ANd moleculAr CharactErization of PAtients with Heart Failure with left ventricular ejection fraction below 50% (PANACEA-HF) study.

Methods

Study cohort

Patients with chronic HFrEF or HFmrEF were prospectively included from January 2018 until June 2023 with follow-up after 12 months (follow-up data not reported in the present manuscript). Inclusion criteria were age ≥18 years with a prior history of symptomatic heart failure, New York Heart Association (NYHA) Classification II–IV and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) <50% within the previous 12 months. Patients were on optimal medical therapy ≥4 weeks prior to entering the study. Patients with ischaemic heart disease were stable with a minimum of 3 months since any acute coronary syndrome, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or thoracic surgery. The patients were included at two centres in Sweden: Sahlgrenska University Hospital (Gothenburg) and Karolinska University Hospital (Stockholm). Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are available in online supplemental material.

Study procedures

At enrolment, medical history and concomitant medications were collected, functional status according to NYHA classification and physical examination were performed. Study procedures included fasting blood samples, ECG, Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ), non-invasive measurements of central blood pressure and carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity and echocardiography including coronary artery flow reserve.

Echocardiography and measurements of CFR

A comprehensive two-dimensional echocardiography examination was performed by one experienced sonographer at each study site. All examinations were recorded and analysed offline. All study participants were investigated at rest using a standardised protocol assessing cardiac structure, function and CFR.9 The method is described in more detail in the supplementary document ‘online supplemental method; Echocardiography and CFR’. Blood pressure was recorded at the time of echocardiography using a digital blood pressure monitor with a brachial cuff.

The CFR is the ratio between maximum blood flow during stress (or hyperaemia) and during rest, which can be measured invasively during coronary angiography or non-invasively using imaging techniques. Adenosine is commonly used for myocardial stress test and causes vasodilation of the coronary arteries/arterioles and decreased atrioventricular (AV) conduction.

To be eligible for CFR assessment in the PANACEA-HF study, no contraindication for adenosine could be present: heart rate <40 bpm, systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg, AV block ≥ 2nd degree and/or sick sinus syndrome in patients without pacemaker, asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with strong reactive component and known allergy to adenosine or mannitol, or experience of previous adverse effects of adenosine stress testing.

One dedicated research sonographer from each study site who previously underwent a centralised, in-person, intensive 1-week training and standardisation programme at Sahlgrenska University Hospital for the acquisition of LAD Doppler flow signals at rest and during adenosine infusion performed the measurements of CFR. A previously published and validated protocol for CFR testing was performed.10 Mean diastolic flow velocity at baseline and during peak hyperaemia was measured by manual tracing of the diastolic Doppler flow signals. CFR was calculated as the ratio between hyperaemic and baseline mean coronary flow velocity values (figure 1). A CFR velocity ratio of <2.5 was considered reduced. Previously performed studies have established Doppler-based CFR <2.5 as the threshold for diagnosis of CMD.11

Pulse wave velocity

The SphygmoCor XCEL system was used as a non-invasive diagnostic tool for the measurement of central blood pressure, pulse wave velocity and other measures of arterial stiffness. Carotid to femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV) is the gold standard method to assess arterial stiffness. The cfPWV is calculated by the distance between the carotid and femoral sites divided by the time delay between the detection of the pulse wave at the sites.12

Statistical analysis

To determine whether there were significant differences in clinical and echocardiographic characteristics between groups based on the presence or absence of CMD (CMD defined as CFR <2.5), various statistical tests were employed. Nominal variables were analysed using the χ2 test of independence; however, when the assumptions of the χ2 test were not met, Fisher’s exact test was used. Ordinal variables were analysed using Wilcoxon’s Rank Sum exact two-sided test to determine significant differences between groups. For continuous variables, a two-sample t-test was used to compare the means of two independent groups. When the assumption of equal variance was not met, the Satterthwaite method (Welch’s t-test) was applied. Additionally, for non-normally distributed data, Wilcoxon’s Rank Sum two-sided test was conducted.

In addition to the crude between-group comparisons described above, adjusted multivariate linear regression analysis was performed with log-transformed CFR as the dependent variable and adjusted multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed with CMD as the dependent variable to examine the association between CFR/CMD and biomarkers, systemic endothelial function and echocardiographic parameters. The models were adjusted for the following variables: age, sex, body mass index, atrial fibrillation and coronary artery disease. For independent variables with non-linear relationship with dependent variable, a log transformation was applied in adjusted multivariate linear regression and a log transformation with base 2 in adjusted multivariate logistic regression was applied for easier interpretation. No imputation methods were applied to handle missing observations. Analyses were conducted using complete observations. Two-sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. SAS V.9.4 was used for the calculations.

Results

A total of 125 patients were included, mean age of 73.4 (±7.5) years, n=99 (79%) men. Screening echocardiography within 12 months before inclusion was completed in 123 patients (figure 2) and revealed HFrEF (n=86, 69%), HFmrEF (n=35, 28%) and HFpEF (n=2, 2%) (online supplemental table 1). A history of coronary artery disease was present in approximately half of the patients (49%). The CFR was possible to assess in 68 patients (54%) (figure 2). Differences between patients eligible for CFR assessment and non-eligible are detailed in online supplemental table 1 and echocardiographic measurements in online supplemental table 2. Patients non-eligible for CFR assessment (n=57, 46%), had a lower systolic blood pressure (117 mm Hg±16.3 mm Hg) than eligible (125 mm Hg±18.7 mm Hg) (p<0.05).

CFR is associated with markers of worse cardiac function

In patients eligible for CFR (mean age of 73.8, ±6.8 years, n=55 (81%) men), CMD was present in two-thirds (n=45, 66%) (table 1). Patients with CMD had higher NTproBNP (1300 vs 713 ng/L, p<0.05), hsTroponinT (19.3 vs 11.7, ng/L p<0.05), fasting glucose (6.2 vs 5.7 mmol/L) and inferior renal function assessed as cystatin C (1.7 vs 1.3 mg/L), p<0.05 for all. In Patients with CMD treatment with loop diuretics, and anticoagulants were more prevalent, whereas treatment with antiplatelet therapy was less common (p<0.05). Patients with CMD had a lower mean KCCQ score (78.6 vs 85.3, p<0.05), but no difference in NYHA class was observed (table 1).

Notably, there was no difference in the prevalence of CMD in patients with ischaemic versus non-ischaemic cause of heart failure. Additionally, no difference could be found in the presence of CMD across different heart failure aetiologies or comorbidities including atrial fibrillation and diabetes mellitus. Neither smoking status, whether current or former, nor pulse wave velocity measured with cfPWV was different across the CMD groups (table 1).

Patients with CMD had lower systolic tissue velocity in mitral annulus and lower ventricular global longitudinal strain (GLS) in both left and right ventricles as well as lower left atrial longitudinal strain (p<0.05) (table 2). There were no differences in markers of diastolic dysfunction, such as early and late mitral inflow velocity. Systolic function by LVEF was not significantly different between groups (table 2). In 14 patients, equally distributed between the groups, the LVEF was ≥50% and thus increased from time of screening to the echocardiography examination within the study, defined as heart failure with improved ejection fraction.

Multivariate linear regression analysis revealed association between CFR, CMD and biomarkers and strain

CFR was analysed as a continuous variable in linear multivariate regression analysis adjusted for age, sex, BMI, atrial fibrillation and coronary artery disease, revealing that lower CFR was associated with higher NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T, and with lower left and right ventricular GLS as well as lower left atrial GLS (p<0.05). Lower LVEF had a nominal but not significant association with lower CFR (p=0.064) (table 3).

Discussion

In this prospective explorative study in patients with HFrEF or HFmrEF, we found that CMD was common and present in two thirds of the patients irrespective of LVEF category and aetiology. Patients with CMD had elevated concentrations of NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T, worse renal function and a lower quality of life indicating a more severe heart failure disease. Furthermore, lower CFR was independently associated with echocardiographic measurements of impaired cardiac function with reduced GLS in both left and right ventricle, and left atrium, and with higher NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T. A lower LVEF and right ventricular GLS as well as higher NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T were associated with an increased risk of CMD (Graphical abstract). Our findings indicate that CMD is associated with markers of a more severe heart failure disease, which CFR may add important information of cardiac function, and that CFR may be a suitable target for therapy. The results add to our understanding of the pathophysiological mechanisms and the drivers of heart failure disease in HFmrEF and HFrEF patients.

Prevalence of CMD in HFrEF and HFmrEF

The prevalence of CMD in chronic stable HFrEF and HFmrEF in the PANACEA-HF study was lower but comparable to the 75% prevalence previously reported in HFpEF.12 This challenges the notion that CMD is primarily associated with HFpEF but instead may represent a common pathophysiological mechanism in heart failure. In patients with HFrEF and DCM, the prevalence of CMD in previous reports is consistent with our results.13 14 However, in HFrEF patients with new onset heart failure, the prevalence was found to be lower, suggesting that CMD may have a correlation to a later stage disease.15 Furthermore, if inflammation and CMD are present in both HFpEF, HFmrEF and HFrEF, it may still be a result of different underlying drivers. In HFpEF, comorbidities may drive systemic inflammation, causing progressive CMD and secondary impaired function of both ventricles and atria as well as other organs such as kidneys and vasculature, whereas in HFrEF, primary cardiomyocyte injury and systolic dysfunction may lead to secondary systemic changes including inflammation and CMD and fibrosis and diastolic dysfunction.16 17

CMD and markers of worse cardiac function

Patients with CMD had higher NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T, which is consistent with previous reports in patients with HFpEF, but now expanding the results to HFmrEF and HFrEF.4 In the PANACEA-HF study, renal function was evaluated using Cystatin C, which indicated that patients with CMD have impaired renal function, adding to the existing body of evidence in this population, however, without difference in eGFR. Cystatin C is recognised not only as a marker of renal function but also an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease and heart failure.18

GLS is independently associated with CFR

Reduced GLS in both the left and right ventricles, as well as the left atrium, was independently associated with lower CFR. This aligns with a previous study of HFrEF patients with DCM.19 Furthermore, in patients with HFpEF, reduced GLS was independently associated with lower CFR.20 Reduced GLS is an early and sensitive marker of myocardial remodelling and associated with substantially increased risk of cardiac death in patients with HFrEF.21 22 Our study also showed that lower atrial GLS was independently associated with low CFR, which has also previously been reported in patients with HFpEF but now expands the knowledge to HFrEF and HFmrEF. Previously, it has been shown that low atrial GLS is a strong marker of LV filling pressure and in patients with HFrEF, it is independently associated with increased cardiovascular mortality.22 23 In HFpEF, disproportionate left atrial myopathy is associated with higher pulmonary artery systolic pressure and lower CFR and proteins involved in inflammation, which reflect cardiomyocyte stretch, extracellular matrix remodelling and inflammation.23 24 These are all highly prevalent features also in HFrEF. The underlying processes are not clearly understood but atrial function is closely connected to left ventricle filling pressures and function.24

Impairment of the microcirculation, a common pathophysiological mechanism in heart failure irrespective of LVEF

The three groups of heart failure (HFrEF, HFmrEF and HFpEF) are based on the measurements of LVEF. HFpEF and HFrEF are considered mainly different conditions with different underlying pathophysiological mechanisms and phenotypes. HFmrEF is considered more similar to HFrEF than to HFpEF.25 This is reflected by different recommendations regarding guideline directed medical therapies. Only sodium glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) are proven effective across all heart failure groups. SGLT2i and MRAs may be effective in HFrEF through both neurohormonal effects and anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects, whereas in HFpEF more predominantly through the latter.26 27 Studies have demonstrated improved coronary microvascular function after treatment with SGLT2i,28 29 which may indicate that CMD is an important pathophysiological pathway that might be a link between heart failure regardless of LVEF and a possible target for future treatments.

Strengths and limitations

Measuring CFR using adenosine-based transthoracic Doppler echocardiography has been validated and is reproducible and recommended by the European Society of Cardiology.30 The method offers several advantages. This non-invasive, cost-effective method carries no radiation risk and is reliable for diagnosing CMD in the absence of focal coronary stenosis. In our study, all patients had stable heart failure and were routinely evaluated for ischaemic heart disease at cardiac clinics on diagnosis but were not investigated with coronary angiography during the study, which is a limitation. However, patients with new angina symptoms or who had undergone PCI or Coronary Artery Bypass Graft surgery within 3 months were excluded.

Patients with heart failure often have hypotension and bradycardia, which are both contraindications for adenosine use. This reduced the number of patients eligible for CFR measurement in this study (54%) and is a limitation. However, a comparison between eligible and non-eligible patients did not reveal any differences suggesting a systematic bias that could affect the result. The limited sample size weakens the reliability of subgroup analyses. Nevertheless, the patients in this study underwent comprehensive evaluations, and the use of few exclusion criteria has resulted in a well-characterised cohort that reflects a real-world clinical population of heart failure patients. The study started enrolling in 2018, but the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic 2 years later had a severe impact on the inclusion rate in this study and, therefore, the study period was extended.

Conclusion

CMD is prevalent in two-thirds of patients with HFrEF or HFmrEF. Impaired GLS in the left and right ventricles, as well as the left atrium, along with higher biomarker levels, were independently linked to reduced CFR. Reduced LVEF and right GLS and higher levels of NTproBNP and hsTroponin-T, independently increased the risk of CMD, reflecting more advanced heart failure disease. These findings highlight the importance of including CMD in risk assessment for HFrEF and HFmrEF and suggest potential benefit from novel therapies targeting CMD.

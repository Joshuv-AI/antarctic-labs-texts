Functional analysis of stcX narrowed down the region of the sterigmatocystin biosynthetic gene cluster in Aspergillus nidulans
Authors: Kosei Yamada, Dong Xuan Nguyen, Hung Ngoc Nguyen, Tram Thi Huynh Le, Thao Phuong Nguyen, Yumi Imanishi-Shimizu, Kiminori Shimizu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractProduction of sterigmatocystin (ST) in Aspergillus nidulans involves the ST biosynthetic gene cluster, which has been considered to comprise 26 genes. However, functions of several genes within this cluster remain unclear. In this study, we investigated the role of the stcX gene in ST biosynthesis. As the open reading frame of stcX has been undefined, we deleted three putative genes adjacent to stcW simultaneously. Deletion of the region did not significantly impact growth rate across various growth conditions compared to the control strain. Chemical analyses revealed no significant change in ST production in the deletion strain compared to the control. Furthermore, the three transcripts were largely undetectable during ST biosynthesis, and the expression of other ST cluster genes was unaffected by the deletion. Our results suggest that these genes are not essential for ST biosynthesis in A. nidulans, indicating that the cluster should be delineated to exclude stcX.Keywords: gene deletion, mycotoxin, secondary metabolism

The secondary metabolite sterigmatocystin (ST), a precursor of aflatoxin (AF), is a mycotoxin produced by Aspergillus nidulans and A. versicolor. Toxicologically, ST is about 10-200 times less carcinogenic than AF, depending on the cell lines, while AF is known as the most potent mutagenic, carcinogenic, and immunosuppressive compound in nature (Bennett & Klich, 2003; Dabelić et al., 2021; Engelbrecht, 1970). ST production involves the conserved AF/ST biosynthetic pathway in the genus Aspergillus and is well established in the model fungus A. nidulans (Calvo & Dhingra, 2011; Hajjar et al., 1989; Keller & Adams, 1995). ST biosynthesis presumably requires 26 genes within a 60-kb gene cluster (Brown et al., 1996; Klejnstrup et al., 2012). Among them 21 genes have been functionally characterized and shown to be essential for ST production (Butchko et al., 1999; Kelkar et al.,1996, 1997; Keller et al., 1995, 2000; Klejnstrup et al., 2012; Yu et al., 2004). However, the function of the remaining genes including stcD, stcH, stcT, stcX, and aflS, are still unknown. In this study, we clarified the role of putative stcX gene in the ST biosynthetic gene cluster.

The open reading frame (ORF) of stcX gene remains unclear. According to previous studies, the stcX gene (around 0.8 kb) is located at the terminal of the ST cluster, adjacent to stcW, a FAD monoxygenase required for ST biosynthesis (Brown et al., 1996; Keller et al., 2000). Annotation of the A. nidulans genome revealed three putative ORFs located within a 2.2-kb region next to the stcW gene, tagged as ANIA_11012 (1113 bp), ANIA_11572 (96 bp), and ANIA_11014 (351 bp), with unknown function (Wortman et al., 2009). Due to the lack of definitive evidence identifying a primary candidate for stcX, we hypothesized that it corresponds to one of the three putative ORFs. To determine whether these putative ORFs are involved in the ST cluster, we first generated a deletion mutant, designated ΔstcX, in which all three putative ORFs were deleted. The A. nidulans strain AnTLE30 was used as a parental strain for generating the ΔstcX mutant and a control strain. To construct AnTLE30, the wA gene of TN02A7 (Nayak et al., 2006) was replaced by AfriboB auxotrophic marker gene as described in Hanazawa et al. (2023). For construction of the knockout plasmid using for targeted gene deletion, 1000 bp fragments corresponding to the 5′ and 3′ flanking regions of the stcX locus were amplified from the A. nidulans TN02A7 genomic DNA using primer pairs 1843/1844 and 1845/1846, respectively. Together with the A. fumigatus pyroA gene (AfpyroA), three fragments were cloned into pBluescript II SK+ using seamless cloning (Motohashi, 2015). The resulting plasmid was introduced into A. nidulans AnTLE30 using the method described by Szewczyk et al. (2006); selection for pyridoxine prototrophy yielded the stcX deletion strain (AnYMD1) (Fig. 1A). PCR using the flanking deletion region and primers specific for AfpyroA confirmed successful deletion of all three putative ORFs (Fig. 1B). To generate a control strain (AnYMD8-1), the AnpyroA4Δ::AfpyroA cassette was amplified from AnTLE5.P4 strain (Le et al., 2021) using primers 96/101. This cassette was then integrated into the AnpyroA4 locus of AnTLE30 to complement the pyridoxine auxotrophic mutation. To address the potential role of stcX in fungal growth, the ΔstcX and control strain were cultured under various growth conditions. Spores of strains were firstly collected from 5-d-old cultures on glucose minimal medium (GMM; Nakamura et al., 2024) supplemented with uracil and uridine in 0.1% (v/v) Tween 80 H2O, then adjusted to 1×107 spores/mL. The spore suspension (1 μL, approximately 104 spores) from each strain was spotted onto solid GMM, LMM (glucose of GMM was replaced with lactose), or YG (0.5% (w/v) yeast extract and 2.5% (w/v) glucose); colony diameters were measured after 3 d incubation (Fig. 2). No significant differences in colony diameters were observed between the ΔstcX and the control strains on different media (GMM, LMM, and YG; Fig. 2B), temperatures (25 °C, 30 °C, and 37 °C; Fig. 2D), or in the presence of antifungal agents (Micafungin, Calcofluor White, Hygromycin B; Fig. 2E). Similarly, the conidiation rates of both the ΔstcX and the control strains were comparable across all media tested (Fig. 2C). Intriguingly, when exposed to osmotic stress, notable differences were observed: in the presence of 0.8 M NaCl or 0.5 M sucrose, 0.5 M sorbitol (data not shown), the ΔstcX strain exhibited significantly larger colony diameters than the control, while no difference was detected under 0.6 M KCl treatment (Fig. 2F). It is an unanticipated phenotype of ΔstcX strain. In summary, disruption of the putative stcX gene does not markedly affect growth, conidiation, antifungal drug efficacy of A. nidulans under laboratory conditions.

To investigate whether the putative stcX gene is required for the ST production, the ST content of the ΔstcX strain was measured. Conidial suspensions (2×106 spores/mL) of control and ∆stcX strains were cultured in a 10 mL of liquid GMM at 37 °C in a petri dish for 3, 5, or 7 d. After incubation, cultures were transferred to 50 mL Falcon tubes and mixed well with 5 mL chloroform (Wako, Japan), then these tubes were centrifuged at 10,000 g for 5 min at 4 °C. Two mL of the lower phase (chloroform) was transferred into a 2 mL tube and evaporated in a fume hood. The dried residue was resuspended with 100 μL of chloroform and approximately two teaspoons of Na2SO4, followed by centrifugation at 15,000 rpm at 4 °C for 1 min. Two μL of the suspension was spotted onto a TLC plate (TLC Silica gel 60 F254, Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany). The developing solvent composed of toluene:ethyl acetate:acetic acid = 6:3:1 (v/v/v) was used to separate metabolites, and 1 μg of ST standard (Merck KGaA) was used as a reference. After development, plates were treated with 15% AlCl3 ethanol solution, heated at 65 °C for 15 min, and visualized under UV light at 254 nm. For the HPLC analysis, extracts were resuspended in 100 μL acetonitrile and filtered through a syringe filter with a PTFE membrane (Membrane-Solutions LLC, Auburn, WA), and 10 μL was used for HPLC analysis (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) equipped with a Symphonia C18 column (JASCO Engineering, Tokyo, Japan). The mobile phase composed of acetonitrile:water:trifluoroacetic acid = 55:45:0.1 (v/v/v) was used, and ST was detected using the Intelligent UV/VIS Detector UV-2075 Plus (JASCO Engineering, Tokyo, Japan) at a retention time of 6.4 min, as determined using an ST standard. The results of both TLC and HPLC analyses indicated that the ΔstcX mutant produced ST at levels comparable to the control strain (Fig. 3A, B).

To investigate the effect of stcX deletion on the expression of ST cluster genes, the transcript levels of putative stcX genes (three putative ORFs), AflR (the transcriptional regulator of the ST cluster), and stcU (an essential gene for ST production within the cluster) were quantified. For RNA extraction, mycelia were harvested after 3 d of cultivation, flash frozen in a liquid nitrogen, and lyophilized. Lyophilized mycelia were ground with a toothpick and subjected to RNA extraction using Sepasol-RNA I Super G (Nacalai Tesque, Kyoto, Japan) following the provided protocol. cDNA was synthesized using ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix with gDNA remover (Toyobo, Tokyo, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instruction, followed by quantitative PCR using ABI StepOne (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) and Thunderbird SYBRTM qPCR mix (Toyobo). The relative expression level of AflR (primer pair 2232/2233), stcU (primer pair 2236/2237), and putative stcX (ANIA_11012 with primer pair 2085/2086, ANIA_11572 with primer pair 2369/2370, and ANIA_11014 with primer pair 2367/2368) was normalized to that of the actin (actA, primer pair 1288/1289) and calculated according to Pfaffl (2001). The results showed that the three putative ORFs (putative stcX) were not expressed in the control strain, whereas AflR and stcU were highly transcribed. No significant difference in AflR or stcU expression was observed between the control and ΔstcX strains (Fig. 3C), suggesting that the deletion of putative stcX does not affect ST cluster gene expression. Furthermore, our RNA-seq analyses of other A. nidulans strains confirmed that the three putative ORFs were not expressed under either ST-producing or non-producing conditions, while the remaining ST biosynthesis gene cluster from stcA to stcW expressed under ST-producing condition (Fig. 3D, details to be published elsewhere). Collectively, these findings indicate that the putative stcX genes are not required for ST biosynthesis.

In conclusion, we demonstrated that the putative stcX-assigned region as currently annotated (three putative ORFs) is not essential for ST production, leading the ST biosynthetic gene cluster to be narrowed down between stcA and stcW without stcX.

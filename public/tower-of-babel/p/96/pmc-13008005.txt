Using home hospital to drive innovation within TEAM
Authors: Kavya Pathak, Thomas C Tsai, Nicholas L Berlin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCenters for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) has implemented the Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM), a mandatory bundled payment program for major surgical procedures that aims to strengthen postoperative coordination and reduce spending. Home Hospital (HH) programs, launched nationally under the Acute Hospital Care at Home (AHCaH) waiver, have demonstrated lower costs and strong quality signals for patients. While this waiver was initially set to expire in 2025, it was renewed by Congress until 2030, thus allowing HH to continue. HH is an innovative care delivery model that could advance TEAM's goals. We argue that CMS should incorporate HH into TEAM as a pathway for value-based surgical care. The Acute Hospital Care at Home (AHCaH) waiver, which provides the structure for Home Hospital (HH), was recently renewed by Congress in February 2026. This waiver, which reimbursed hospital inpatient rates for providing hospital care at home, helped launch successful HH programs for medical and surgical patients throughout the country. The extension of this waiver and the continuation of HH are more important than ever, particularly as Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) launches the newest bundled payment model for surgical patients, called the Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM).

Starting in January 2026, TEAM is a mandatory episode-based bundled payment model for Medicare beneficiaries undergoing five high-volume major surgical procedures.1 This model marks the next step in the CMS’ strategy on value-based surgical care with an emphasis on enhanced coordination postoperatively with primary care. TEAM allows for CMS waivers, including the two-midnight rule and physician gainsharing, to facilitate better coordination across a patient's inpatient stay and postoperative recovery. However, one opportunity that should be considered within TEAM is the integration of HH. HH is a novel opportunity to achieve the goals of TEAM and drive future innovation in value-based surgical care.

HH gained prominence during the COVID-19 pandemic from expanded use of the CMS AHCaH waiver. This innovative pathway involves the provision of inpatient care at the patient's home, including monitoring, daily clinician visits, nursing and paramedic support, and laboratory testing.2 A large scale study of 500 HH patients in New York City showed that HH was associated with reduced episode spending, 30-day readmissions, and length of stay for medical patients.3,4 Other large studies of HH have showed similar results, with low rates of mortality and readmissions.5 A randomized controlled trial of HH care, while smaller in scope, showed similar results.6 Surgical HH provides an important opportunity to deliver patient-centered postoperative surgical care. Allowing for HH for surgical conditions within TEAM would provide an opportunity for alignment across Medicare programs.

Of the 745 hospitals mandated to participate in TEAM, 106 received the AHCaH waiver from CMS. Thus, many TEAM hospitals already have established the requisite infrastructure and experience to provide HH care. Many hospitals have already piloted HH models for surgical care, demonstrating safety and showing promising cost and quality signals for surgical patients.7 Evidence suggests that HH is a feasible, safe, and effective model of care delivery.

Allowing for HH in TEAM would allow for strategies for success within TEAM to extend beyond those utilized for prior bundled payment models. Opportunities for savings during the BPCI, BPCI-A, and CJR bundled payment programs primarily relied on reducing post-acute care spending and readmissions. Integrating HH within TEAM may create a novel care pathway to realize the goals of the model, by supporting patient-centered care, enhancing coordination with primary care, and reducing spending. Instead of focusing on utilization management of entrenched pathways, hospitals that embed HH within their strategies for TEAM may be able to reimagine bundled surgical care with inpatient-level support and recovery from the patient's home.

Renewal of the acute hospital care at home waiver

The AHCaH waiver, which provided the reimbursement structure for HH to flourish, was renewed by Congress in February 2026. The initial AHCaH waiver was implemented during the COVID-19 pandemic to relieve inpatient capacity, and the program's performance at over 300 hospitals across the country was evaluated by CMS in 2024. This evaluation demonstrated the safety of the HH model and showed decreases in mortality for HH patients.8 The AHCaH waiver was set to expire at the end of 2025, but with advocacy from hospital and physician groups, the waiver was extended by Congress, with a plan for a more rigorous program evaluation using these additional data. This extension provides an opportunity to integrate HH within TEAM.

Recommendations

Determine which TEAM conditions are appropriate for HH

While certain TEAM conditions, such as emergent bowel surgery or CABG, would be ineligible for HH, the post-operative care for patients undergoing lower acuity procedures can likely be safely managed with HH. TEAM permits lower extremity joint replacements and spinal fusions in the outpatient setting, and patients undergoing these procedures who require an inpatient stay may be appropriate candidates for HH. With input from surgeons and other clinicians, CMS can collaboratively identify which conditions are eligible for HH, and hospitals can then analyze post-operative pathways and common complications of each procedure to determine which procedures can safely be part of an HH model. Readmissions within 30 days for conditions such as surgical site infection, ileus, or high ostomy output may also be managed using HH, which may help hospitals meet cost targets for care episodes.

Leverage institutional experience to establish best practices for HH integration

Hospitals that have already launched HH programs are well-versed in the organizational challenges and upfront costs associated with establishing HH, which requires a complex network of staff, third-party vendors, digital tools, and data integration solutions to deliver HH care. Hospitals with limited resources may lack the financial capacity and additional infrastructure to pilot HH, particularly when focused on compliance with TEAM. CMS should consider a phased rollout strategy for integrating HH into TEAM, concentrating first on hospitals with prior HH experience before expanding to all institutions. CMS may then analyze data from these institutions and issue best practices, and peer hospital education networks can disseminate learnings from individual institutions that have experience with HH. Given the complexity of establishing HH programs, HH participation should be optional for hospitals participating in TEAM. As HH is made available to all institutions in future model years, these hospitals may be more prepared to pilot HH by using established practices.

Standardize quality metrics between TEAM and HH

Integrating HH into TEAM would require merging two different sets of quality metrics. The AHCaH program required hospitals to report two quality metrics, unanticipated mortality during the care episode and the escalation rate back to the inpatient setting.9 The quality metrics hospitals must report for TEAM are more extensive, including all-cause readmissions, a composite of patient safety and adverse events, falls with injury, postoperative respiratory failure, 30-day death rate among patients with complications, and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) related to joint replacement.1 Metrics specific to HH may need to be added to TEAM, and PROMs focusing on the patient experience with the HH model may help hospitals and CMS understand patients’ challenges in using this innovative care pathway.

Partner with primary care clinicians

HH also represents an opportunity to improve coordination postoperatively with primary care providers, which is a stated objective of TEAM. Current HH models are centered around the inpatient team, but these can be modified to integrate support from primary care, facilitating a transition back to longitudinal providers and reducing fragmentation. This may be achieved through telehealth or in-person care at patients’ homes.

Conclusion

HH is an innovative care model that has been shown to deliver efficient, high-quality care for complex medical and surgical patients, and has been expanded nationwide through the AHCaH waiver. The extension of the AHCaH waiver presents an opportunity to include HH within the design of TEAM, which could be obvious solutions for policymakers to support the delivery of value-based surgical care.

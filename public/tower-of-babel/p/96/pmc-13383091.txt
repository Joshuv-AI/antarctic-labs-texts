SM17, an anti-interleukin-17 receptor B antibody, ameliorates pathogenesis of chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis via Th2/Th17 dual modulation
Authors: Lik Hang Lam, Weimin Li, Kai Cheung Chow, Wai Yin Dennis Au, Wai Chung Wu, Muthu Iswarya Gandhi Sethuraman, Shaik Abdullah Nawabjan, Wai Yan Choi, Billy Kwok Chong Chow, Shui On Leung, Chin Wai Hui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic respiratory diseases (CRDs), driven by dysregulated Th2/Th17 inflammation, represent a global health challenge with limited therapeutic options. Interleukin (IL)-25 has emerged as a key upstream regulator of both pathways, yet its exact role in CRDs’ pathogenesis remains underexplored. Here, our aim is to evaluate the efficacies of SM17, a novel anti-IL-25 receptor (IL-17RB) antibody, in preclinical models of chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF).MethodsEosinophil, peripheral blood mononuclear cell, primary macrophage and lung fibroblast cultures were utilised to define the mechanism of action for how SM17 could ameliorate CRSwNP and IPF related phenotypes in vitro. A CRSwNP murine model was induced by co-administration of ovalbumin and Staphylococcus enterotoxin B for comparing SM17's efficacy with dexamethasone. Furthermore, IPF model was conducted using bleomycin stimulation for differentiating beneficial effects of SM17 with two US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved IPF drugs, nintedanib and pirfenidone.ResultsIn vitro studies demonstrated that SM17 could attenuate eosinophilic activities via Th2 modulation and Th17 differentiation through inhibition of macrophage activation. Simultaneously, SM17 could also prevent IL-25 driven myofibroblast differentiation. The in vitro efficacies were successfully translated to animal studies. SM17 administration restored olfactory function through suppression of eosinophilic inflammation in the nasal epithelium of the CRSwNP murine model and attenuated lung fibrosis through Th17 modulation in the IPF model. In both animal experiments, SM17 showed trend of improvements than the comparison drugs.ConclusionOur findings suggest that SM17 is a potential therapeutic agent to treat CRDs such as CRSwNP and IPF through Th2/Th17 dual modulation and antifibrotic function.

Introduction

Chronic respiratory diseases (CRDs), including asthma, chronic rhinitis and pulmonary fibrosis, represent a global health burden, affecting approximately 454.6 million individuals globally [1]. Between 1990 and 2019, CRD-related mortality surged by 28.5%, indicating the urgent need for better therapeutic strategies [2]. While advances in research have expanded treatment options, most CRDs remain incurable, with disease management relying on chronic pharmacotherapy and physiotherapy [3].

First-line steroid therapies provide rapid anti-inflammatory relief by suppressing hyperactivated immune responses, alleviating symptoms like airway hypersensitivity and dyspnoea [4]. However, the heterogenous nature of CRDs often leads to suboptimal or nonresponsiveness to steroid treatments in a subset of patients [5, 6]. Furthermore, prolonged use of corticosteroids is associated with numerous systemic adverse effects [7]. These unmet medical needs have spurred the development of safer and more targeted biologics to tackle those CRDs.

Monoclonal antibodies against type 2 immunity have provided superior efficacy and safety in clinical trials for asthma, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) and COPD, and could sustain the oral corticosteroid dosage reduction in steroid-dependent patients [8, 9]. Yet, nonresponsiveness remains in type 2 severe patients, suggesting single blockade of downstream player in Th2 immunity (e.g. interleukin (IL)-4, IL-5, IL-13 and IgE) may not be sufficient [10].

IL-25, an epithelial-derived alarmin, is a key upstream driver of type 2 inflammation that directly targets type 2 helper T (Th2) cells, group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2s) and fibroblasts [11]. We previously developed a humanised antibody targeting the IL-25 receptor (IL-17RB), showing potent suppression on Th2 inflammation in preclinical models of atopic dermatitis and asthma, with efficacies superior to upadacitinib and dexamethasone (Dex), respectively [12, 13]. Based on these findings, we hypothesised that SM17 might also be beneficial to other IL-25high CRDs.

CRSwNP is characterised by eosinophilic inflammation, nasal obstruction and olfaction dysfunction [14]. Critically, >80% of patients suffer from persistent olfactory loss even post-surgery, underscoring its clinical significance [15, 16]. IL-25 and IL-17RB are markedly upregulated in nasal polyps and correlate strongly with inflammatory (T-bet, RORC and GATA3) and fibrotic mediators (TGF-β1/2 and ECP), positioning IL-25 as a central orchestrator bridging Th2 inflammation and tissue remodelling [17]. Despite this, the impact of IL-25 blockade on olfactory function remains unexplored. Here, we evaluate SM17 in a CRSwNP murine model incorporating olfaction assessment to address this gap.

Beyond its established role in type 2 inflammation, IL-25 modulates type 17 helper T (Th17) responses—a mechanism underexplored in respiratory pathology. Although Th2 and Th17 pathways were historically viewed as distinct, IL-25 knockdown ameliorates Th17-driven diseases such as psoriasis [18]. Mechanistically, IL-25 potentiates Th17 differentiation via antigen-presenting cell (APC)-derived IL-1β, a key Th17-polarising cytokine. The dual functionality of IL-25 is particularly relevant in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), where mixed Th2/Th17 endotypes drive progressive lung scarring and mortality [19–21]. Current US FDA-approved therapies, pirfenidone (Pir) and nintedanib (Nin), merely slow IPF progression and exhibit high discontinuation rates due to hepatotoxicity and gastrointestinal adverse events [22–24]. Biologics targeting profibrotic mediators (TGF-β, CTGF) have failed clinical trials, highlighting unmet needs [25, 26]. IL-25 emerges as a compelling alternative: it directly activates fibroblasts/epithelial cells via STAT3-driven pathways to promote extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition while dual-modulating Th2/Th17 inflammations [27–29]. In the present study, we also assessed SM17's therapeutic potential in a bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis model, comparing efficacy against Pir/Nin in fibrosis regression, Th17 inhibition, respiratory function and survival.

Methods

See complete version of Methods in the supplementary material.

Animal handling and ethics

Wild-type BALB/c mice (6–8 weeks old) and C57BL/6 mice (∼8 weeks old) were used. BALB/c mice were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Unit, the University of Hong Kong, and C57BL/6 mice from jh-labanimal co. Experimental protocols were approved by the Department of Health, Hong Kong (ref. no. DH/HT&A/8/2/8 Pt.5, DH/HT&A/8/2/8 Pt.7) and the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) committee of KCI Biotech Co. (SXYK 2024-0001). All mice were housed under a 24-h light–dark cycle, with food and water ad libitum.

Alarmin-induced pulmonary inflammation model

Mice were intranasally instilled with IL-25, IL-33 and thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP), or a cocktail (IL-25+IL-33+TSLP) on days 1 and 4 as adopted from an established protocol [30]. Control mice received saline. Cocktail-treated mice also received intraperitoneal injection of either SM17 or IgG4 control on day 1. On day 5, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected via tracheal catheterisation with PBS. ILC2 populations were quantified by flow cytometry, and cytokines in BALF were measured by ELISA.

OVA and SEB-induced CRSwNP murine model

Mice were sensitised with an intraperitoneal injection of ovalbumin (OVA) and Al(OH)3 on days 0 and 7, followed by intranasal OVA injection daily from day 14 for 7 days. From day 35, mice were challenged with OVA and SEB intranasally three times weekly. SM17, dexamethasone or IgG control were intraperitoneally injected to mice starting from day 14. Olfactory function was assessed using a buried food test. Serum and nasal lavage fluid (NLF) were collected for cytokine analysis. Nasal tissues were processed for histology studies.

Bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis murine model in BALB/c mice

Mice were intranasally instilled with bleomycin (BLM) on days 0 and 14, with controls receiving PBS. From day 7, SM17, pirfenidone, nintedanib or IgG4 control were administered to mice. On day 22, serum, BALF and lung tissues were collected for RNA, protein and histological analyses.

BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis murine model in C57BL/6 mice

Mice received an intratracheal injection of BLM on days 0 and 7. Noninvasive lung function (Penh) was measured on day 6 via whole body plethysmography for randomising mice into different treatment groups. Lung function was reassessed on day 14. On day 22, invasive tests (airway resistance, forced vital capacity and dynamic compliance) were performed, followed by lung tissue collection for histology.

In vitro studies

Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), IPF-derived lung fibroblasts (DHLF-IPF), EoL-1, A549 and MRC-5 were used to study the molecular mechanism of SM17 on anti-inflammation and anti-fibrosis.

Results

SM17 disrupts type 2 inflammation-dependent eosinophil activation

EoL-1/PBMC co-culture was utilised to elucidate SM17's role in modulating eosinophil-Th2/ILC2 crosstalk (figure 1a). SM17 treatment significantly suppressed CM induced eosinophilic activation (CD11b+/CD69+), degranulation (CD11b+/CD63+) and proliferation of mature EoL-1 with very similar efficacy as an anti-IL-5 antibody (mepolizumab), indicating that SM17 mainly acts through IL-5 suppression to modulate eosinophil activities (figure 1b–e). Critically, recombinant IL-5 could stimulate IL-25 secretion from mature EoL-1 (figure 1f), suggesting a self-amplifying loop: IL-25 activates Th2/ILC2 cells to secrete IL-5, which in turn drives eosinophil activation and IL-25 production, thereby perpetuating Th2 inflammation.

Efficacy of SM17 in an alarmin-induced airway hypersensitivity model

Alarmin or alarmin injection was performed to model airway hypersensitivity in mice (figure 2a). Severe lung pathology, characterised by immune cell infiltration and airway epithelial thickening, required combinatorial alarmin stimulation, whereas IL-25 uniquely induced goblet cell hyperplasia in inflamed lungs (figure 2b–e). These findings suggest IL-25 plays a nonredundant role in early mucin-producing cell expansion during hypersensitivity. More importantly, SM17 administration robustly mitigated 50% of lung inflammation through suppression of goblet cell and eosinophil proliferation in alarmin cocktail-induced mice. BALF analysis further confirmed the anti-Th2 property of SM17 in terms of reduced ILC2 accumulation and IL-13 levels in the lungs (figure 2f–h).

Scale bar, 100 µm. f,g) SM17 significantly reduced the group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2) population (CD45+/Lin−/ICOS+/ST2+) in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF). h) SM17 also significantly suppressed the level of interleukin (IL)-13 in BALF. n=3–6 for all experiments except otherwise specified. i.n.: intranasal; i.p.: intraperitoneal. *: p<.05; **: p<.01 compared with IgG4 control group by unpaired t-test, for all groups (c–e,g,h).

SM17 exhibited a better suppression trend on Th2 inflammation than other alarmin-targeting antibodies in human PBMCs

Next, we compared SM17's efficacy to clinically relevant alarmin inhibitors (tezepelumab (anti-TSLP)), itepekimab (anti-IL-33) in human PBMC assays. SM17 exhibited higher suppression on elevated Th2 cytokine levels (IL-5, IL-9 and IL-13) and ILC2 expansion (supplementary figure S1). Those results indicate that SM17 might offer better Th2 inhibition in alarmin related autoimmune diseases.

SM17 repaired nasal epithelium integrity and restored olfaction in CRSwNP mice

A behaviour test was conducted in a well-established CRSwNP murine model to evaluate the sense of smell at the end-point (day 91) (figure 3a,b). SM17 and Dex-treated mice required significantly less time (p<0.01) to find hidden food compared with the IgG4 control group (figure 3c), revealing that both treatments could restore olfaction in mice. From weight measurement data, only SM17 was able to rescue weight loss in CRSwNP mice (figure 3d). Histologically, SM17 could significantly reduce the number and size of nasal polyps on the olfactory epithelium and superior maxillary turbinate in CRSwNP mice, whereas Dex failed to do so (figure 3e–g).

Besides, SM17 significantly suppressed eosinophil and mast cell recruitment, goblet cell hyperplasia and Th2 cell differentiation, with efficacy comparable to Dex in nasal epithelium (figure 3h–l). Furthermore, SM17 exhibited strong IL-4 and periostin suppression, while it also suppressed neutrophil infiltrations (supplementary figure S2a–d). Systemic biomarkers of allergic inflammation, including serum IgE, CCL17 and periostin, were also markedly reduced by SM17 (supplementary figure S2e). NLF was collected as illustrated (supplementary figure S2f). ELISA analyses on NLF further revealed SM17-mediated suppression of CCL17, periostin, IL-4, and eotaxin-1 (supplementary figure S2g), corroborating its inhibitory property on Th2 cytokines and chemokine-driven recruitment.

SM17 suppressed IL-25-driven activations on fibroblasts and epithelial cells

MRC-5 fibroblast cell line was used to study the anti-fibrotic effects of SM17. After co-stimulation with IL-25 and TGF-β1, profibrotic markers including ACTA2 (α-SMA), FN1 (fibronectin), Col1a1, Col3a1 and IL-6, were markedly upregulated in MRC-5 (figure 4a), and SM17 significantly attenuated these inductions. In line with MRC-5 results, SM17 reduced α-SMA expression protein levels after IL-25 and TGF-β1 induction in DHLF-IPF (figure 4b,c. In an established co-culture system (figure 4d), alarmin-induced PBMCs greatly increased α-SMA expression in fibroblasts probably via Th2 cytokine production, whereas α-SMA level was again suppressed by SM17 treatment (figure 4e). These data collectively suggest that SM17 could mitigate fibroblast activation through direct and indirect pathways.

In addition to fibroblasts, IL-25 could directly induce epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) in A549 airway epithelial cells, as evidenced by a marked increase in α-SMA+ cells, while this phenotypic shift was effectively abrogated by SM17 (supplementary figure S3a,b). A549 and MRC-5 cells were then co-cultured to study epithelial-fibroblast interactions, and SM17 significantly reduced extracellular levels of fibronectin, IL-6 and IL-8 in the medium under IL-25/TGF-β1 stimulation (supplementary figure S3c–e). These findings reveal SM17's dual mechanism of action on anti-fibrosis by targeting both fibroblast activation and EMT.

SM17 attenuated M2 macrophage polarisation and subsequent Th17 differentiation

In vitro experiments demonstrated that IL-25 induced M2-like polarisation (CD68+/CD206+) and concomitant secretion of IL-1β and IL-6 for Th17 differentiation, whereas SM17 treatment robustly inhibited those processes (figure 4f–i). Consistently, SM17 treatment could reduce IL-25 induced Th17 differentiation in PBMCs (figure 4j), implicating a broader role of SM17 in immune dysregulation.

To confirm whether this effect is mediated via macrophage-dependent signalling pathway, an indirect co-culture system was established (supplementary figure S4a). CM from IL-25-treated macrophages significantly promoted Th17 differentiation in helper T-cells, which was abolished by SM17 treatment (supplementary figure S4b,c). Furthermore, IL-25 could selectively upregulate IL-17A and IL-22, whereas SM17 could abrogate IL-25 driven elevations (supplementary figure S4d). Together, these data suggest that SM17 can modulate both macrophage-mediated Th17 priming and specific downstream cytokine production.

Efficacy of SM17 in a classic BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis model

To address the therapeutic potential of SM17 in Th17-associated disease, a BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis BALB/c model was developed to compare efficacies among SM17, Pir and Nin in IPF treatment (figure 5a). Comprehensive histopathological and biochemical analyses revealed that SM17 exhibited comparable efficacy to Nin and superior effect to Pir in reducing fibrotic remodelling, lung collagen content and macrophage/neutrophil recruitment (figure 5b–f). Furthermore, SM17 demonstrated a trend of better suppression on α-SMA expression relative to both Pir and Nin-treated groups (figure 5g–h), alongside significant reductions in pro-fibrotic mediators and IL-17A (figure 5i–j and supplementary figure S5a–d). Strikingly, SM17 uniquely attenuated Th17 cell infiltration in the lungs with suppression on IL-6 levels in BALF and serum (figure 5k and supplementary figure S5e,f), indicating its dual anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic activities.

Additionally, RNA-sequencing was performed on the lung tissues to investigate differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between different treatment groups in the IPF animals. The BLM+IgG4 group demonstrated an expression profile distinct from the saline control group, whereas SM17 and Nin treatment groups showed similar DEG profiles (figure 6a). DEG analyses also demonstrated that SM17 could distinctively downregulate expressions of Cxcl3, Cxcl9, Cxcl10, Il-6 and Col28a1, whereas Nin failed to do so (figure 6b–c). Additionally, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses were employed. After BLM administration, biological processes (BPs), such as immune response-related processes and extracellular matrix organisations, were significantly promoted in the lungs (figure 6d). Treatment with SM17 significantly reduced the frequencies of those BPs, with additional inhibitory effect on the response to innate inflammatory cytokines such as type II interferon (IFN)-γ (figure 6e), whereas Nin could only drive down the frequencies of leukocyte proliferation and migration (figure 6f). Cellular components (CCs) and molecular functions (MFs) were also evaluated, showing that BLM could facilitate the collagen-containing extracellular matrix, collagen trimer and external side of plasma membrane in CCs, while inducing glycosaminoglycan binding, receptor ligand activity, extracellular matrix structure constituent and heparin binding in MFs. Again, SM17 could target more genes involved in those components than Nin, indicating that SM17 might possess greater potential in alleviating collagen-associated fibrosis in IPF.

In addition, normalised enrichment scores (NESs) were evaluated in SM17 and Nin-treated groups as compared with IgG4 control group using the gene set enrichment analysis in GO terms. Both SM17 and Nin demonstrated significant inhibitory effects on contractile fibres (figure 6g–h). Furthermore, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were performed, showing that BLM administration significantly upregulated cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, ECM–receptor interaction and phagosome/lysosome-associated pathways in comparison with saline control (figure 6i). SM17 treatment was able to downregulate these BLM-triggered pathways, whereas Nin treatment could only modulate cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction and phagosomes (figure 6j–k), suggesting multiple therapeutic roles of SM17 in modulating immune activations, reducing profibrotic factor productions and preventing BLM-induced cell death.

To achieve translational relevance, lung function was evaluated in BLM-challenged C57BL/6 mice (supplementary figure S6a). Invasive pulmonary measurements revealed that SM17 and Nin comparably improved airway resistance, though other functional parameters showed limited sensitivity to treatment (supplementary figure S6b). Noninvasive pulmonary functional tests demonstrated similar results (supplementary figure S6c). Consistent with BLM-induced BALB/c animals, both SM17 and Nin could reduce collagen deposition and alveolar injury in lung (data not shown). Notably, SM17 treatment showed a trend of higher survival rates and minimal weight loss across the study period as compared with Nin-treated group (supplementary figure S6d,e), suggesting enhanced tolerability and efficacy of SM17 in the long-term treatment of IPF.

Discussion

Epithelial-derived alarmins are pivotal drivers of type 2 inflammation, making them promising therapeutic strategies for Th2-high autoimmune diseases [31]. In this study, SM17 demonstrated superior efficacy compared with anti-TSLP tezepelumab and anti-IL-33 itepekimab in suppressing Th2 cytokine production and ILC2 expansion in vitro. This aligns with observation in an alarmin-induced airway hypersensitivity model, where IL-25 uniquely governed early goblet cell hyperplasia, a hallmark of respiratory exacerbations [32].

Besides, our mechanistic studies revealed a critical feedback loop linking eosinophil activation and Th2/ILC2 responses. IL-25 stimulates Th2/ILC2 cells to secrete IL-5, which directly stimulates eosinophilic activation and degranulation in EoL-1 cells. Activated eosinophils then produce IL-25 to perpetuate Th2 inflammation. Compared with anti-IL-5 mepolizumab, we believe that SM17 could provide better inhibition to eosinophil activation and global suppression on Th2 inflammation through direct attenuation of IL-25 induced Th2 cytokine secretion from Th2/ILC2 cells. Consistently, our animal study in CRSwNP further confirmed the suppressive role of SM17 on eosinophil infiltrations and Th2 inflammation (Figure 3h–l), which resulted in reduced nasal polyp burden and rescued olfactory functions slightly better than Dex treatment (figure 3e–g). Notably, SM17 represents the first IL-17RB antagonist to ameliorate olfactory dysfunction and address inadequate efficacy in CRSwNP.

More importantly, our data revealed IL-25's distinctive role in Th17-driven pathologies. In macrophage–PBMC co-cultures, IL-25 induced M2 macrophage polarisation and Th17 differentiation, which were suppressed by SM17 (figure 4g–j). This inhibitory property was also applied to fibroblast and epithelial cell, where SM17 attenuated IL-25/TGF-β1-driven ECM remodelling and proinflammatory cytokine releases (figure 4a–e). The promising efficacy of SM17 was also demonstrated in a BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis model (figure 5). SM17 offered similar protection as Nin in reducing collagen deposition and Ashcroft score, and uniquely attenuated Th17 cell infiltration, a phenotype linked to IL-6/TGF-β suppression [33]. By targeting upstream IL-25, SM17 addresses both fibrotic remodelling and immune dysregulation, positioning it as a multifaceted therapy for IPF.

Current IPF therapies and CRSwNP biologic mostly target fibrotic factors and downstream effectors, leaving upstream drivers like IL-25 unaddressed [34, 35]. By targeting upstream IL-25 pathway, our experimental results demonstrated that SM17 could target multiple aspects of the disease pathology, including eosinophilic activation (figure 1), Th2 inflammation (figure 2), Th17 differentiation and fibrosis (figure 4). It is speculated that SM17 might provide better therapeutic effects than monotherapy in CRSwNP and IPF, as shown in two murine experimental models (figures 3 and 5). The approval of tezepelumab in the treatment of severe CRSwNP [36] also encourages the clinical development of antibodies targeting upstream alarmins (i.e. SM17) in the treatment of autoimmune diseases. Additionally, this broader mechanism of action may also encourage the preclinical development of SM17 in other autoimmune diseases associated with both type 2 and non-type 2 inflammations [37, 38].

Notably, SM17 consistently suppressed periostin across IPF, CRSwNP and AD models. Periostin exhibits diverse pathological roles: in IPF, it drives fibroblast activation and collagen deposition [39]; in CRSwNP, it facilitates nasal polyp formation via epithelial-stromal crosstalk [40]; in AD, it exacerbates pruritus and barrier dysfunction [41]. As IL-25 signalling lies upstream of periostin-mediated pathologies, we believe that SM17 could offer a unified therapeutic strategy by indirectly silencing periostin expression.

While our findings highlight SM17's unique and promising efficacies in CRDs, several issues require further investigation. First, direct comparisons with other IL-25 inhibitors (e.g. XKH001) are required to confirm mechanistic superiority. Second, the role of IL-25 in Th17 inflammation warrants exploration, particularly in COPD. Third, the potential risks of blocking an upstream alarmin should be evaluated in the upcoming clinical trials. In the future, clinical studies will be conducted to validate SM17's efficacy in restoring olfactory function in CRSwNP, as well as its dual anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects in IPF.

Conclusion

SM17 emerges as a potential first-in-class biologic capable of silencing IL-25-driven Th2 and Th17 inflammation. By targeting a central upstream node, SM17 offers broader immunomodulatory effects, while maintaining a good safety profile as compared with currently available conventional treatments, with potential applications across respiratory, fibrotic and other chronic autoimmune diseases. Its ability to restore olfaction in CRSwNP and attenuate Th17/neutrophilic inflammations in IPF underlines its therapeutic value, addressing critical gaps and unmet medical needs in these clinically unsatisfied CRDs.

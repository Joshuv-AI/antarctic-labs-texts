Parabolic Schrödinger networks
Authors: Amulya Smyrna C., N. Nathiya
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A Schrödinger network Nq={X,ζ,q} is an infinite graph with a set of vertices X and edges that are countably infinite, a transition function ζ(a,b)≥0 and a function q(a) on X. A perturbed Laplace operator Δq is defined as Δql(a)=Δl(a)−q(a)l(a). A solution h(a) to the equation Δqh(a)=0 is termed a q-average function, and a q-superaverage function v(a) satisfies Δqv(a)≤0. The network is called q-hyperbolic if there exists a function s(a)>0, q-superaverage on X; otherwise, it is a q-parabolic network, also mentioned as a parabolic Schrödinger network. In this paper, we develop a theory of function on parabolic Schrödinger networks by using potential-theoretic techniques associated with q-superaverage and q-average functions, which are similar to the function theory on parabolic Riemannian manifolds.

Introduction

The supersolutions and the solutions of the equation Δl(x)=q(x)l(x) have been studied in various contexts on Euclidean spaces, Riemann surfaces (Myrberg [17]), infinite graphs (Yamasaki [29], Anandam [4]) with the basic assumption q≥0. These studies follow potential-theoretic methods on hyperbolic Riemannian manifolds (where the Green kernel can be well-defined). Our study here is to investigate a discrete version of this equation (also known as the Schrödinger equation) on infinite graphs when q(x) is not necessarily nonnegative.

We develop a potential theory based on the operator Δql(x)=Δl(x)−q(x)l(x) [5] on a graph with infinite vertices and edges, and a set of transition functions that does not entertain a positive solution to Δql(x)≤0. After a careful study of the solutions of Δql(x)=0 and Δql(x)≤0, we construct a logarithmic kernel associated with the operator Δq and conclude with a canonical representation of super-solutions of Δql(x)≤0 defined outside finite sets.

A graph N={X,ζ} with a countably infinite number of vertices and edges, along with {p(a,b)} a set of transition probabilities, forms the state space for the study of random walks. The infinite graph N with a general set of transition function {ζ(a,b)}, is usually mentioned as an infinite network, forms the base space for a comprehensive research of infinite electrical networks, modeled after the study of a finite electrical network provided with a voltage-current regime subject to Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws [12], [24]. The Laplace operator defined as Δl(a)=∑bζ(a,b)[l(b)−l(a)] plays an instrumental role in bringing into focus different concepts in electricity and random walks and their inter-relations.

The Schrödinger operator Δq, where a function q(a) is nonnegative on Nq={X,ζ,q} defined as Δql(a)=Δl(a)−q(a)l(a) comes up in various occasions, in the investigation of functions on finite or infinite networks. On a finite network, the Schrödinger operator Δq is studied by using, the Perron-Frobenius theorem on matrices (see for example Anandam and Damlakhi [5], Gantmacher [13]). Also for a nice treatment of the potential theory on finite networks for Schrödinger operators, (see Bendito, Carmona and Encinas [8]). In the continuous case as well as in the discrete case, for the operator Δq when q≥0, q≢0, there are solutions s>0, Δqs<0, Δqs≢0. Based on these positive solutions, a good potential-theoretic development related to the Schrödinger operators has been initiated on Riemann surfaces (for example, Myrberg [17], Royden [20], Nakai [18] among others) and on infinite networks (by Yamasaki [29], Anandam [4], Nathiya and Smyrna [19]).

Our concern in this article is to develop potential theory on infinite networks in a discrete case related to Δq when there is no positive solution s(a) to Δqs≤0, Δqs≢0. The development will have an obvious resemblance to the classical potential theory on R2 (Brelot [9]) and parabolic Riemann surfaces (Constantinescu and Cornea [11] and Tsuji [26]).

Commencing with the presumption that q(a)=Δϑ(a)ϑ(a) for a positive function ϑ(a), we develop here a potential theory on Nq based on the Schrödinger operator Δql(a)=Δl(a)−q(a)l(a). After proving the Minimum Principle, we obtain the unique Δq-Dirichlet solutions on finite sets, leading to a balayage theory similar to the one known in R2- potential theory. Assuming that there is no solution s>0, Δqs<0, Δqs≢0, we establish the existence of a fundamental function on Nq similar to the logarithmic function on a parabolic Riemann surface; this function is useful, among other properties, to obtain a global representation of functions u(a), Δqu(a)=0, defined outside finite sets.

The transition function ζ(a,b) is a nonnegative number associated with the edge from vertex a to b, that is ζ(a,b)≥0 and ζ(a,b)>0 if a and b are neighbors (there is only one edge between them), denoted as a∼b. Note that ζ(a)=∑(b∼a)ζ(a,b). We also assume that the transition function may be asymmetric, that is, ζ(a,b)≠ζ(b,a).

Classification of infinite networks

The classification of infinite networks into parabolic and hyperbolic networks is similar to that of Riemann surfaces. Riemann surfaces are two-dimensional manifolds that are locally homeomorphic to a disc in the complex plane [3]. Parabolic Riemann surfaces are those on which the Green kernel cannot be introduced; hyperbolic Riemann surfaces admit Green kernel [4], [23], [27], [28]. In this paper, we are focused on discussing the parabolic infinite network.

Parabolic Schrödinger network

An infinite Schrödinger network as defined in [2], [7], [19] is an infinite network Nq={X,ζ,q}, where X is a set of countable infinite number of vertices and ζ is the transition function defined on the edges connecting a pair of vertices in X, where on Nq q(a)=Δϑ(a)ϑ(a), for some positive function ϑ(a) on X.

Similar to that of the Laplacian operator, we define q-Laplacian operator on the Schrödinger network. Definition 3.1(q-Laplacian) [19] On Nq let us consider a real-valued function l. The q-Laplacian of l at r∈Nq is defined as,Δql(r)=Δl(r)−q(r)l(r)=∑t∼rζ(r,t)[l(t)−l(r)]−q(r)l(r)=∑t∼rζ(r,t)l(t)−[t(r)+q(r)]l(r)The function l is q-average, q-superaverage or q-subaverage at r if and only if Δql(r)=0, Δql(r)≤0 or Δql(r)≥0 respectively.

Conclusion

We have already worked with the classification of N (infinite networks) depending on the existence of Green's kernels, calling them parabolic and hyperbolic networks. This classification runs parallel to that of random walks as recurrent and transient. In this article, the classification of infinite networks {N,q} is carried out using the perturbed Laplace operator Δq, known as the Schrödinger operator, when q(a)=Δϑ(a)ϑ(a) for a given positive function ϑ(a). Specifically, we have concentrated on the case where there is no solution s>0 for Δqs≤0, Δqs≢0. We have named such networks as parabolic Schrödinger networks. A possible extension of these results to the case of random walks with reflecting or absorbing boundaries is under consideration.

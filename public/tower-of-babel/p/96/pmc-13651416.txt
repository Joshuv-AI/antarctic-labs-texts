Association between the TyG/HDL-C ratio and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: a single-centre retrospective study
Authors: Xiao-shuan Xiong, Fang-zhou cheng, Wei-nan Qi, Jing Cheng, Wen-long jiang, Yong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTriglyceride-glucose index to high density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TyG/HDL-C ratio) is a novel surrogate indicator for insulin resistance. However, the predictive value of TyG/HDL-C ratio for the presence of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) remains unclear. In this study, we aimed to investigate the relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD in health check-up populations from China.MethodsA total of 1592 participants aged ≥ 40 years were included. Patients were divided into three groups according to the tertiles of TyG/HDL-C ratio, the indicators were compared among the three groups. The univariate linear regression model and the multivariate models were performed to investigate the association between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. Smoothed curve fit analysis was used to explore the linear or non-linear relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. The modification and interaction of subgroups were inspected by the likelihood ratio test.ResultsAfter adjusting for confounders, there was a non-linear relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. The inflection point was 10.0. When TyG/HDL-C ratio < 10.0, the relationship was as (OR = 1.23, 95%CI: 1.13 to 1.33, P < 0.0001) ,while when ≥ 10.0, the association was not statistically significant (OR = 0.89, 95%CI: 0.80 to 1.00, P = 0.0553). Subgroup analysis suggested that no significant interactions were observed. TyG/HDL-C ratio presented moderate discriminative capacity for NAFLD.ConclusionsWe discovered that there was a non-linear positive association between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. When TyG/HDL-C ratio was less than 10.0, a positive association was found.

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is defined as the patients in which more than 5% of hepatocytes show macrovesicular steatosis in the absence of a readily identified alternative cause of steatosis (e.g., medications, starvation, monogenic disorders) in individuals with no alcohol drinking or little alcohol drinking (< 20 g/d for women and < 30 g/d for men) [1]. It is a metabolic disorder-associated steatotic liver disease, which has been suggested as the most common type of chronic liver disease worldwide, affecting at least one in four adults worldwide [2]. NAFLD is a condition that encompasses a wide spectrum of diseases, ranging from simple steatosis to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis that may further lead to fibrosis, cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma [3, 4]. NAFLD is closely linked to metabolic dysfunction and a higher risk of liver fibrosis [3, 4]. NAFLD has been suggested to associate with an increased risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) [5, 6]. In addition, CVD is the main cause of death in patients with NAFLD [7]. Therefore, early diagnosis and interventions are quite important for the management of these patients.

Although the etiology of NAFLD is not fully understood, evidence suggests that abnormal blood lipid metabolism [8] and insulin resistance (IR) [9] play key roles in the development of NAFLD. High-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) plays a key role in lowing cholesterol and was suggested to be negatively associated with the presence of NAFLD [10]. As an alternative assessment of IR, the triglyceride-glucose index (TyG) has been widely discussed in the CVD and metabolic diseases. It has been suggested that TyG is a risk factor for NAFLD [11–15]. In recent years, triglyceride-glucose index to high density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TyG/HDL-C ratio) has been proven as a new parameter for the prediction of coronary artery calcification, which has a better predictive value than TyG [16]. TyG/HDL-C ratio was suggested as a reliable marker of worsening metabolic risk, regardless of the glycemic stage [17]. A recent study from China suggested that TyG/HDL-C is an independent predictor for metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease in non-overweight/obese patients with T2DM [18]. Considering the close relationship between HDL-C, TyG and NAFLD, we speculated that TyG/HDL-C ratio may be associated with NAFLD. Therefore, we aimed to explore the association between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD in the present study.

Methods

Study population

In this study, we re-analyzed the data from an open access database ‘DATADRYAD’. The data could be downloaded and re-analyzed freely from the official website “www.Datadryad.org”. The study flowchart was shown in Fig. 1. This study retrospectively analyzed health check-up data of individuals who received physical examinations at Wuhan Union Hospital from January 2020 to November 2021. A total of 1830 consecutive participants aged 40–79 years who completed body composition analysis and abdominal liver ultrasound were initially enrolled. Research staff collected clinical information from electronic medical records; two researchers were responsible for raw data extraction, and another two researchers conducted cross-verification to ensure data accuracy. All participants finished standardized questionnaires to provide information on demographics, smoking status, drinking status, medical history and medication history. Participants with excessive alcohol intake were excluded, with the threshold defined as alcohol consumption > 210 g/week for males and > 140 g/week for females. We also excluded individuals with a definite history of liver diseases (including viral hepatitis, autoimmune liver disease and drug-induced liver injury), acute illness, renal insufficiency (estimated glomerular filtration rate < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²), active cancer (cancer diagnosed or treated within the latest 6 months), and those receiving oral or injectable steroid therapy. In addition, cases with missing biochemical indicators or incomplete questionnaire records were also ruled out. After screening, a final total of 1592 participants aged ≥ 40 years were included in the present analysis. Liver steatosis was screened via conventional abdominal B-mode ultrasound (Philips IU22, Philips Healthcare, Inc. N.V.), and all ultrasound examinations were performed by professionally trained medical technicians. NAFLD was defined as the presence of hepatic steatosis confirmed by B-mode ultrasound, combined with no history of the above-mentioned alternative liver diseases and no excessive alcohol consumption. In this study, the diagnosis of hepatic steatosis was determined by routine clinical ultrasonic criteria at our institution, and the ultrasound assessment was implemented in a non-blinded manner in accordance with the routine clinical work process.

Clinical and laboratory data assessments

The blood pressure was tested by trained technicians after at least 10 min of rest. The laboratory parameters were performed in the central lab of the hospital after a fast of at least 8 h. Parameters including liver function, fasting blood glucose (FBG), and blood lipids were carefully recorded. Since the method for blood testing was shown in detail in the original study, in the present study, we did not need to repeat it again. The triglyceride-glucose index (TyG) was calculated as ln (fasting triglycerides (TG) (mg/dL)×fasting blood glucose (mg/dL)/2) [17]. The TyG/HDL-C ratio was calculated as the TyG index divided by HDL-C (mmol/L).

Statistical analysis

The R packages were used for data analysis in this study. Continuous data were shown as mean±standard deviation or median (quartile), which was compared using One-Way ANOVA test respectively. Categorical data were displayed in percentage, which were compared by chi-square tests. Univariate linear regression analysis was used to investigate the relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. The multivariate analysis including the unadjusted, minimally adjusted (age and sex) and fully adjusted analyses were shown in our study. Besides, the linear or non-linear associations between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD were also investigated. Subgroup analysis was carried out using logistic regression models. The modification and interaction of subgroups were inspected by the likelihood ratio test. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline and laboratory characteristics

Patients were divided into three groups according to the tertiles of TyG/HDL-C ratio. We discovered that patients in the high tertile of TyG/HDL-C ratio tended to have a higher proportion of males, tobacco use, alcohol use, hypertension, diabetes (p < 0.05) (Table 1). In addition, patients in the high tertiles of TyG/HDL-C ratio had an increased level of body mass index (BMI), alanine aminotransferase(ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), uric acid (UA), fasting blood glucose (FBG), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides(TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglyceride-glucose index (TyG), triglyceride-glucose index to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TyG/HDL-C ratio), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), NAFLD, while a lower level of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) (p < 0.05), (Table 1).

Univariate analysis

Univariate analysis suggested that males, BMI, ALT, AST, UA, TC, TyG/HDL-C ratio, SBP, DBP, tobacco use, alcohol use, hypertension, diabetes were positively associated with NAFLD (p < 0.05) (Table 2). The risk of NAFLD increased with the increase of the tertiles of TyG/HDL-C ratio. We suggested that patients in high tertile of TyG/HDL-C ratio had a 4.12-fold higher risk of NAFLD compared with patients in the low tertile of TyG/HDL-C ratio (p < 0.05) (Table 2). Age (< 70 years) and LDL-C were not associated with NAFLD (p > 0.05) (Table 2).

The relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD

The TyG/HDL-C ratio was positively associated with NAFLD in the unadjusted model (OR = 1.27, 95% CI: 1.22–1.33, P < 0.0001; Table 3). This significant association remained stable after sequential adjustment for different sets of covariates. After adjustment for age and sex, the OR was 1.24 (95% CI: 1.19–1.30, P < 0.0001). Similar results were observed in the lifestyle-adjusted model (OR = 1.24, 95% CI: 1.19–1.30, P < 0.0001), adiposity-adjusted model (OR = 1.15, 95% CI: 1.10–1.21, P < 0.0001), and metabolic-adjusted model (OR = 1.11, 95% CI: 1.05–1.17, P < 0.0001). The association persisted in the fully adjusted model (OR = 1.09, 95% CI: 1.03–1.15, P = 0.0026; Table 3). When the TyG/HDL-C ratio was categorized into tertiles, a consistent dose-response trend was detected across all adjusted models (P for trend < 0.05; Table 3).

The analysis of non-linear relationship and subgroup analysis

The non-linear analysis was carried out in our study. We found that there was a non-linear relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, SBP, DBP, AST, ALT, UA, TC, LDL-C, tobacco use, alcohol use, hypertension and diabetes (Fig. 2). The inflection point was 10.0. When TyG/HDL-C ratio < 10.0, the relationship was positive (OR = 1.23, 95%CI: 1.13 to 1.33, P < 0.0001) ,while when the ratio ≥ 10.0, the association was not statistically significant (OR = 0.89, 95%CI: 0.80 to 1.00, P = 0.0553) (Table 4). The subgroup analysis suggested that no significant interactions were observed (p > 0.05) (Table 5).

Discussion

In our study, we found that TyG/HDL-C ratio was associated with NAFLD in a fully adjusted model. After adjusting for confounders, there was a non-linear relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. When TyG/HDL-C ratio was less than 10.0, a positive association was found, while when the ratio ≥ 10.0, the relationship was not statistically significant. To the best of our knowledge, this was the first study to explore the association between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD.

IR is defined as insufficient physiological response to insulin in the human body, which is a common pathological mechanism in the occurrence of various metabolic-related diseases, such as type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome (MS) and dyslipidemia [19, 20]. NAFLD was considered as a hepatic manifestation of MS, which was also closely associated with IR [21]. The excessive visceral adiposity in NAFLD may result in a pro-inflammatory state, which results in an increased risk of multiple organ damage [20]. Accumulating evidence suggested that patients with NAFLD were at high risk of hypertension, CAD, cardiomyopathy, and cardiac arrhythmias, which bring in an increased risk of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality [21, 22]. Currently, it was believed that CVD is the main cause of death in patients with NAFLD [7]. Therefore, early identification and interventions of NAFLD are of vital importance for the management of these patients. Recently various indicators have been proposed for the early prediction of NAFLD; however, no study investigated the association between the novel indicator TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. In the present study, we first suggest a robust association between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD.

Considering the vital role of IR in the development of NAFLD, evaluation of IR could help to identify patients who were at high risk of NAFLD. Hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic clamp test and HOMA-IR were used to accurately measure IR [23, 24]. However, the complicated procedure and high costs limited the application in clinical practice. As an alternative indicator for assessing IR, TyG index showed a great advantage for its simplicity, convenience, and high accuracy [25]. Moreover, TyG index was demonstrated to be superior to HOMA-IR in assessing IR in patients with and without diabetes [26]. Recent studies demonstrated the association between TyG index and NAFLD. TyG index could be used as a screening parameter for NAFLD in health check-up populations [27]. TyG index showed a similar predictive value for NAFLD in non-obese subjects [28, 29], asymptomatic women [30], and overweight patients [31]. A higher TyG index was also linked to an increased risk of NAFLD in the aged [32]. Moreover, TyG index could also serve as a valuable indicator for the prediction of NAFLD progression and improvement during the long-term follow-up [33]. A previous study demonstrated that the severity of IR was positively associated with the progression and prognosis of NAFLD [34]. Since TyG index could evaluate the occurrence and severity of IR, TyG index could also predict the subsequent presence of NAFLD in the future [35].

HDL-C participates in the cholesterol transportation so as to exhibit a lipid-lowering effect. HDL-C also exerted anti-inflammatory reactions by inhibiting pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines and reducing adhesion molecule expression [36, 37]. In addition, HDL-C was also suggested to be negatively associated with the degree of IR [37]. Considering the close relationship between chronic low-grade inflammation, IR and NAFLD [36, 37], lower levels of HDL-C were proven as a robust predictor for NAFLD [10, 37].

Although various modified TyG indices exhibited reliable predictive value for NAFLD, such as TyG-body mass index (BMI), and TyG-waist circumference (WC), TyG-waist-height ratio (TyG-WHtR) [38, 39]. TyG/HDL-C index, the combined indicator of TyG index and HDL-C, was rarely discussed in the area of NAFLD. A recent study from China suggested that TyG/HDL-C is an independent predictor for metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease in non-overweight/obese patients with T2DM [18]. However, the predictive value of TyG/HDL-C ratio in NAFLD in health check-up patients remained unclear. In the present study, we found that TyG/HDL-C ratio was also positively associated with the presence of NAFLD. We also discovered that there was a non-linear relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD after adjusting for confounders. When considered as a categorical variable, patients in the high tertile of TyG/HDL-C ratio showed a 4.12-fold risk of NAFLD compared with patients in the low tertile of TyG/HDL-C ratio. This was the first study to explore the relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. This association could be explained simply as follows. An increased TyG/HDL-C ratio could be interpreted as a higher TyG or a lower HDL-C. This is because a higher TyG or a lower HDL-C was associated with the presence of NAFLD.

Our study has some limitations. Firstly, this is a single-center study with a limited sample size. Secondly, as the data were acquired from a public database, we could only analyze the indicators that were provided, and we could not adjust all the potential parameters (such as menopause in the female population, data of carbohydrate intakes ), which may bring in a bias. Thirdly, due to the retrospective design, we could not establish the causality. Additionally, we focused on the individuals who underwent health check-up from China, so the results could not be generalized to other populations.

Conclusion

We found that there was a non-linear relationship between TyG/HDL-C ratio and NAFLD. When TyG/HDL-C ratio was less than 10.0, a positive association was found. TyG/HDL-C ratio showed a moderate discriminative capacity for NAFLD in health check-up populations.

Health literacy among rural adolescents: An emerging concern
Authors: Shubham J. Shivale
Published: 2026

Sir/Madam,

I read with great interest the recently published article by Rachel J and Sudha V on health literacy among rural adolescents in Pondicherry. The authors have highlighted a critical public health concern, reporting that 82% of adolescents had inadequate health literacy.[1] While the study provides valuable insights, certain contextual and conceptual aspects warrant further discussion in the Indian setting.

First of all, literacy in India encompasses digital, functional, and health-related skills in addition to fundamental reading and writing.[2] Even though India has made substantial progress in literacy, there are still considerable disparities between rural and urban populations, as well as across genders and socioeconomic backgrounds.[3] Therefore, this more comprehensive multifaceted framework must be utilized for interpreting health literacy.

Second, Pondicherry’s geographic setting is important. The major prevalence of poor health literacy indicates underlying gaps in health communication, accessibility, and cultural determinants affecting health behavior in a Union Territory with relatively superior facilities and health indicators.[4]

According to the study, one important factor influencing teenage health literacy is parental education. This is in line with research that indicates parental education, particularly which of mothers, has a major impact on teenagers’ health-related behaviors, knowledge, and decision-making abilities.[5] Studies highlight the importance of family-centered health education strategies.

Furthermore, it is impossible to overestimate the importance of life skills education and school health programs. Through curriculum-based interventions, peer learning, and teacher participation, schools offer a systematic platform for enhancing health literacy. Adolescent health literacy can be significantly enhanced by national programs like the Ayushman Bharat School Health Program.[6]

Disparities between urban and rural areas continue to be a serious issue. Research from Maharashtra and other regions of India shows that teenagers in rural areas are less health literate than those in urban areas.[7] The digital gap exacerbates these discrepancies, which are primarily caused by variations in healthcare, education, and information availability.

Access to healthcare is also directly impacted by health literacy. Poorer health outcomes result from those with low health literacy’s inability to comprehend medical information, navigate healthcare systems, and use preventive programs.[8]

Moreover, it is largely recognized that poverty, socioeconomic status (SES), and literacy are related with each other. Suboptimal health-seeking practices, fewer educational possibilities, and less exposure to health information are all linked to lower SES.[9] The findings of the present study align with these broader social aspects of health.

Studies show that a significant percentage of Indian teenagers have just moderate or insufficient health literacy at the national level.[10] This underscores the need for context-specific interventions.

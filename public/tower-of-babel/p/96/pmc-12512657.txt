Differentially expressed profiles of LncRNAs in pterygium
Authors: Jiusheng Zheng, Zhixin Ding, Lixin Mei, Huixiang Jin, Pengfei Zhang, Jinghong Yao, Guangming Liu, Shuting Li, Yinping Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo do the differential expression of long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) in pterygium was screened by gene chip technology, and the differentially expressed lncRNAs HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 were studied to explore their possible mechanisms in the pathogenesis of pterygium.MethodsCollected 40 specimens from June 2016 to May 2017 that underwent surgery in the Ophthalmology Department of Yanjishan Hospital of Wannan Medical College, including 20 pterygium tissue specimens (6 males and 14 females, aged 47–84 years, mean age 68.1 years), 20 cases of normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue in patients with strabismus (9 males and 11 females, aged 41–88 years, mean age 61.6 years). Four samples from each group were randomly selected to detect lncRNA expression by high-throughput gene chip detection technology; lncRNA HOTTIP, RP1-261G23.7 and corresponding target genes HOXA13, VEGFA were screened by quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) to verify whether there is a significant difference between the two groups, and to analyze the correlation between the expression of the target genes HOXA13 and VEGFA.Results(1) SPSS17.0 software was used for data processing in the experimental group and the control group. Gender and age were analyzed by the chi-square test. The statistical results were all P > 0.05, and there was no significant difference in statistics, (2) A total of 8271 differential lncRNAs were detected by high-throughput gene chip detection and compared with normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue in patients with strabismus. Among them, lncRNAs with a P-value < 0.05 and log fold change > 2 and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (FDR < 0.05) had 612 upregulated genes and 743 downregulated genes. (3) The two highly differential lncRNAs were selected and verified by qRT-PCR. The results showed that lncRNAs HOTTIP and RP1-261 G23.7 and the corresponding target genes HOXA13 and VEGFA were highly consistent and highly expressed in the control group.Conclusions(1) LncRNAs are differentially expressed in pterygium; (2) Key lncRNA HOTTIP, RP1-261G23.7 may be new gene targets for pterygium.

Introduction

Pterygium is a common and frequently occurring ophthalmic disease.To date, the specific pathogenesis of pterygium is still unclear, and related studies report that its possible pathogenesis is as follows: vascular endothelial growth factor receptor VEGFR and VEGF are the most important growth factors in eye diseases [1, 2]. Tumor suppressor genes [3], such as P53; proliferation-related proteins [4], proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) and cyclin D1 play important roles in the proliferation of pterygium cell processes; Ultraviolet promotes progress of pterygium [5].Apoptosis-related protein Bcl-w is overexpressed in pterygium by miR-122 apoptosis [6]; heat shock proteins (HSPs) [7], extracellular matrix extracellular matrix proteins [8],interleukins (IL) [9],matrix metalloproteinases and their tissue inhibitor metalloproteinases (MMPs and TIMPs) [10] also play an important role in pterygium. There is currently no effective drug treatment, and the main method is surgical resection, but the recurrence rate is approximately 30%. Although the clinician has invented a variety of surgical methods, such as combination with autologous limbal stem cell transplantation or amniotic membrane transplantation, or mitomycin and 5-FU, still cannot solve the problem of recurrence after surgery. Postoperative recurrence of pterygium often results in a variety of ocular complications, such as sputum adhesions, eye movement disorders, refractive errors, tear film damage, corneal opacity and other complications. To understand and answer these questions, it is urgent and necessary to study the pathogenesis of pterygium.

Long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) refers to a non-coding RNA that does not encode a protein that is longer than 200 nucleotides. These non-protein-coding sequences were once considered by researchers to be non-functional in the genome. Recent studies have found that lncRNAs are widely involved in many important biological processes in the body, such as chromosomal blotting, epigenetic regulation, cell cycle regulation, cell metabolism, apoptosis, and reprogramming of induced pluripotent stem cells [11].Study of the development of lncRNAs in human eyes has just begun and its expression disorder is closely related to the formation of diabetic retinopathy, retinopathy of prematurity, and corneal neovascularization [12, 13]. In the literature, there are few studies on pterygium and lncRNA at home and abroad, and they mainly describe microRNAs involved in their pathophysiological processes [14, 15].

The progress and widespread application of transcriptome analysis based on high-throughput genomic expression profiling technology provides a crucial technical guarantee for the screening of pterygium-associated lncRNA genomics research, which can ensure accurate screening. Based on this, we speculate that lncRNAs play an important role in the occurrence and development of pterygium and provide an experimental theoretical basis for targeted gene therapy of pterygium. The prevention and treatment of pterygium have important clinical significance.

Materials and methods

General information

Collected 40 specimens from June 2016 to May 2017 that underwent surgery in the Ophthalmology Department of Yanjishan Hospital of Wannan Medical College, including 20 pterygium tissue specimens (6 males and 14 females, aged 47–84 years, mean age 68.1 years), 20 cases of normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue in patients with strabismus (9 males and 11 females, aged 41–88 years, mean age 61.6 years). Four samples were randomly selected, and a high-throughput gene chip detection technique was used to detect whether there was a significant difference between lncRNAs in the experimental group and the control group. The key lncRNAs HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 and the corresponding target genes HOXA13 and VEGFA were verified by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Whether there is a significant difference in lncRNAs and target genes between pterygium tissue and normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue and the correlation between target gene HOXA13 and VEGFA expression were analyzed.

The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Wannan Medical Copllege, and the patient or family members signed the informed consent form, volunteered to participate in the study by Yijishan Hospital of Wannan Medical College and cooperated with the collection of the specimen.

A pterygium specimen was selected from the cleft palate region, and the head invaded the cornea and exceeded the limbus by 3 mm. The size was approximately 2.0 mm x 2.0 mm. Normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue were selected from patients with strabismus, and the specimen size was approximately 2.0 mm×2.0 mm. Specimen processing: The pterygium and normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue collected during the operation were washed in physiological saline to wash the residual blood from the surface, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored in a refrigerator at −80 °C until use.

Experimental operation process

High-throughput gene chip detection and screening of lncRNA was completed by Shanghai Bohao Biotechnology Corporation.

Purification of total RNA

Quality RNA was purified using the RNeasy Kit.

Shanghai Biotech RNA extraction kit extraction using the UNIQ-10 column TRIzol total RNA extraction kit, Item No.: B511321 was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration was measured using a NanoDrop and an optical density of 260/280.

RNA labeling and array hybridization

RNA labeling and array hybridization were performed according to the Agilent One-Color Microarray-Based Gene Expression Analysis protocol (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Each sample was amplified and transcribed into fluores-cent cRNA along the entire length of the transcripts without 3’ bias utilizing a mixture of oligo(dT) and random primers (Arraystar Flash RNA Labeling kit; Arraystar, Rockville, MD, USA). The labeled cRNAs were purified using an RNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen, GmBH, Germany). The concentration and specific activity of the labeled cRNAs were measured using a NanoDrop ND-1000 pectrophotometer.

A total of 1 µg of each labeled cRNA was fragmented by the addition of 5 µl 10X blocking agent and 1 µl of 25X fragmentation buffer, then the mixture was heated at 60˚C for 30 min.

Finally, 25 µl 2X GE hybridization buffer were added to dilute the labeled cRNA. Subsequently, 50 µl of hybridization solution were dispensed into the gasket slide and assembled to the lncRNA expression microarray slide. The slides were incubated for 17 h at 65˚C in an Agilent Hybridization Oven. The hybridized arrays were washed, fixed and scanned with using the Agilent DNA Microarray Scanner (part no. G2565CA; Agilent Technologies).

Statistical analysis:

The results of this experiment were analyzed using SPSS 17.0 for Windows (SPSS Inc. USA) statistical software. According to the pterygium tissue and normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissue, gene chip detection was developed by Shanghai Bohao Biotechnology Co., Ltd. using Agilent's patented SurePrint technology to develop a new lncRNA chip. The latest and most complete database coverage was used; the probe design was based on the latest version of the genome(human/GRCh38 (hg38), covering GENCODE V21, Ensembl, UCSC, NONCODE, LNCipedia, lncRNAdb and other core lncRNA databases; data was analyzed using the R software limma package for data. After normalization, the differentially expressed genes were screened by fold change and Student's t-test method. The selection conditions were as follows: 1) fold change ≥ 2 and 2) t-test P-value<0.05. The lncRNAs were differentially expressed between the experimental group and the control group. Two of the differentially expressed lncRNAs HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 and the corresponding target genes HOXA13 and VEGFA were used for qRT-PCR verification. Three replicates were performed for each gene (target gene and reference gene GADPH). The data were statistically calculated using the 2-△△Ctmethod. The measurement data of the qRT-PCR results were tested for normal distribution and homogeneity of variance. If the test is not satisfied, the rank sum test was used. If P<0.05, the results were considered statistically significant (Fig. 10).Fig. 10qRT-PCR validation of Key lncRNAs and Target Genes(n=3 technical replicates)

Differential gene hierarchical clustering:

Hierarchical clustering analysis of differentially expressed lncRNAs revealed distinct expression patterns between the experimental (pterygium) and control (normal nasal bulbar conjunctival) groups. As shown in Fig. 7, red indicates upregulated gene expression, green indicates downregulated expression, and black indicates no significant change; the intensity of color corresponds to the magnitude of differential expression. Clear clustering was observed, with samples from the same group (A001-A004 for control, B001-B004 for experimental) clustering together, confirming consistent intra-group expression profiles and significant inter-group differences.

GO enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed to explore the biological functions of differentially expressed genes. The top 30 enriched GO terms (Fig. 8) primarily involved vascular smooth muscle cell development, venous vascular development, regulation of endothelial cell differentiation, and transport protein activity (P < 0.05). These functions align with known pathogenic processes of pterygium, such as neovascularization and tissue proliferation.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

KEGG analysis identified key signaling pathways associated with differentially expressed genes (Fig. 9). The top 30 pathways included those related to angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and extracellular matrix remodeling, further supporting the involvement of these biological processes in pterygium pathogenesis. Specific pathways (e.g., VEGF signaling) were consistent with the functions of target genes HOXA13 and VEGFA validated in this study.

qRT-PCR validation

We selected two differentially expressed genes in the experiment: lncRNA HOTTIP (chr7: 27200420–27207259) and lncRNA RP1-261G23.7 (chr6:43770429–43770616); the target genes were HOXA13 (chr7: 27193503–27200106) and VEGFA (chr6: 43770184–43786487).

The selection of HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 was based on the following reasons: (1) Both showed significant co-expression trends with target genes in chip results; (2) Although HOTTIP ranked 9th among downregulated genes, its target gene HOXA13 is closely associated with vascularization in pterygium; (3) While RP1-261G23.7 did not enter the top 10, it is located adjacent to VEGFA and is hypothesized to have cis-regulatory relationships. Notably, RP1-261G23.7 exhibited upregulated expression in pterygium tissues.

The fold changes of lncRNA HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 in microarray screening were 9.42 and 5.01, respectively, while qRT-PCR results showed larger fold changes (16.75 and 12.77). Similarly, target genes HOXA13 and VEGFA exhibited fold changes of 2.33 vs. 4.04 and 5.03 vs. 3.80 in microarray and qRT-PCR analyses, respectively. Despite quantitative differences, both methods confirmed consistent upregulation (RP1-261G23.7, VEGFA) or downregulation (HOTTIP, HOXA13) trends in pterygium tissues (P < 0.05), indicating reliable differential expression patterns (Fig. 10).

Discussion

Pterygium is widely recognized as a UV-related proliferative ocular surface disorder with tumor-like biological behaviors, such as uncontrolled cell growth and neovascularization [16]. Emerging evidence indicates that long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) play pivotal regulatory roles in various cancers, including lung, liver, and colorectal cancer, by modulating gene expression at transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels [17–19]. However, their involvement in pterygium pathogenesis remains poorly understood.

The rapid replacement of gene chip detection technology and the rapid development of technology make it possible to simultaneously detect different lncRNA changes, which is of great help for screening differential genes. In this experimental study, we used gene chip detection to identify differentially expressed lncRNAs between pterygium and normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissues, suggesting that not only mRNA but also lncRNA are involved in the pathogenesis of pterygium.

In this study, high-throughput microarray screening identified 8271 differentially expressed lncRNAs in pterygium tissues compared to normal nasal bulbar conjunctival tissues, with 612 upregulated and 743 downregulated lncRNAs meeting the criteria of P < 0.05, log fold change > 2, and FDR < 0.05. This finding confirms that lncRNA expression is significantly perturbed in pterygium, suggesting their potential role in disease development. The discrepancy between our results and previously reported lncRNA profiles in pterygium may stem from differences in microarray platforms, sample selection criteria, and demographic variations among study populations. Notably, our strict exclusion of systemic diseases, smoking, and recurrent cases minimizes confounding factors, enhancing the reliability of our data.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that differentially expressed genes are primarily involved in vascular smooth muscle cell development, endothelial cell differentiation, and angiogenesis-related pathways. These biological processes align with the well-recognized role of neovascularization in pterygium progression, supporting the functional relevance of the identified lncRNAs.

Among the differentially expressed lncRNAs, HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 were selected for validation based on their potential regulatory relationships with target genes. HOTTIP, located at the 5’ end of the HOXA locus, is known to coordinate HOXA gene activation [20]. Our qRT-PCR results confirmed that HOTTIP and its target gene HOXA13 are significantly downregulated in pterygium tissues, consistent with microarray data. HOXA13, a transcription factor involved in embryonic angiogenesis and tumor vascularization [21, 22], may contribute to pterygium pathogenesis by regulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) production, as reported in other diseases [23].

RP1-261G23.7, although not among the top 10 differentially expressed lncRNAs, was prioritized due to its genomic proximity to VEGFA, a key driver of angiogenesis. qRT-PCR validation showed upregulated expression of both RP1-261G23.7 and VEGFA in pterygium tissues, supporting a potential cis-regulatory role of RP1-261G23.7 in VEGFA expression. This finding aligns with the established function of VEGFA in promoting neovascularization, a hallmark of pterygium [1, 2].

Notably, the fold changes detected by qRT-PCR (HOTTIP: 16.75, RP1-261G23.7: 12.77) were more pronounced than those from microarray analysis (HOTTIP: 9.42, RP1-261G23.7: 5.01). This discrepancy may reflect the higher sensitivity of qRT-PCR in quantifying gene expression, highlighting the need for orthogonal validation in high-throughput studies.

Limitations of this study include the small sample size for microarray analysis (4 pairs of samples) and the use of strabismus patients’ conjunctival tissue as controls, which may not fully represent healthy ocular surfaces. Future studies with larger cohorts and matched healthy controls are warranted to confirm these findings. Additionally, functional experiments (e.g., siRNA-mediated knockdown) and mechanistic assays (e.g., luciferase reporter assays) are needed to elucidate the precise roles of HOTTIP and RP1-261G23.7 in regulating HOXA13 and VEGFA.

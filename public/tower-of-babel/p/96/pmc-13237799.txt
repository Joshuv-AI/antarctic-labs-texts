A new clade of H9N2 avian influenza virus circulating in Laos
Authors: Jiaming Li, Chunge Zhang, Ren Li, Yanqing Wang, Rahat Ullah Khan, Ruichang Quan, Watthana Theppangna, Yuhai Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIn 2024, we identified and sequenced 52 avian influenza A (H9N2) virus strains in Laos. Using the established H9N2 genomic classification system, a novel HA gene clade of the A/chicken/Beijing/1/94-like (BJ/94-like) lineage, designated Clade 4.6.20, was identified. This new clade is phylogenetically distinct from the previously described clades, and the representative strains in this new Clade 4.6.20 presented a low cross reactivity to the antisera of other clades, suggesting antigenic drift of the viruses between the new Clade 4.6.20, and other clades in the dominant lineage of Clade 4.6. In addition, all the newly identified viruses in Clade 4.6.20 possessed HA-L226 and NP-N52 mutations, which are associated with human-type receptor binding and human MxA-related innate immunity escape, respectively. Our findings underscore the necessity of global surveillance network and cooperation to monitor the evolution of AIVs, update vaccine seed strains, and develop new vaccines with high effectiveness against H9N2 AIVs circulating globally, which threaten poultry and human health.KEYWORDS: Avian influenza virus, H9N2, genetic evolution, zoonotic transmission, surveillance, Laos

Influenza viruses present a significant threat to global public health [1,2]. Their segmented, single-stranded negative-sense RNA genomes facilitate genetic reassortment when distinct viruses co-infect the same host [3,4]. Historical influenza pandemics, such as those in 1957, 1968 and 2009, have demonstrated the crucial role of avian influenza virus (AIV) gene segments in shaping the virulence of pandemic strains [5–7]. Laos, located at the confluence of the East Asian-Australasian and Central Asian flyways, serves as a critical wintering habitat for migratory birds, and approximately 1,000 avian species winter here each year. This ecological nexus supports high avian diversity and density, creating ideal conditions for AIV intra- and inter-transmission. Moreover, the probability of reassortment among divergent AIVs during their co-infections is elevated, potentially generating novel viruses with enhanced virulence. Consequently, continuous monitoring of the prevalence of AIVs in the flyways of migratory birds is crucial for preventing influenza outbreaks and for the timely implementation of prevention and control strategies.

From May to June 2024, a total of 622 oropharyngeal and cloacal swab samples were collected from apparently healthy poultry in live poultry markets (LPMs) and backyard farms across three Laotian provinces: Vientiane, Luang Prabang, and Xieng Khouang. The samples consisted of 124 chickens, 155 ducks, 30 pigeons, and 2 turkeys, were processed and used for AIV identification by quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) as described previously [3,4,8,9]. A total of 68 samples were tested AIV-positive (10.93%, 68/622). Notably, all positive samples were obtained exclusively from chickens and ducks in LPMs, with chickens exhibiting a significantly higher rate (18.95%, 47/248) compared to ducks (6.77%, 21/310). Among the positive samples, oropharyngeal swabs constituted the majority (76.47%, 52/68), while cloacal swabs accounted for 23.53% (16/68). No positive samples were detected from the backyard poultry (0/60).

Viral genomes were sequenced by the next-generation sequencing (NGS) method [3,4,8–10], and 52 strains were identified as H9N2 AIVs, which were isolated from both chickens and ducks, and these virus genomes have been deposited in the NMDC database (accession numbers: NMDCN000A5GK-NMDCN000A5T3).

Genetic analysis of the identified H9N2 AIVs showed that their nucleotide sequence identity of the hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) genes ranged from 97.9% to 100% and 99.1% to 100%, respectively, with amino acid sequence identities of 97.7% –100% and 98.5% –100%. The nucleotide sequence identities of internal genes among our viruses exhibited the following ranges: MP (99.8% – 100%), NP (99.1% – 100%), NS (99.0% – 100%), PA (99.0% – 100%), PB1 (97.8% – 100%), PB2 (98.8% – 100%); and the amino acid sequence identities were M1 (100% – 100%), M2 (100% – 100%), NP (98.6% – 100%), NS1 (99.6% – 100%), NEP (98.1% – 100%), PA (98.3% – 100%), PB1 (98.7% – 100%), and PB2 (98.4% – 100%).

Evolutionary analysis, based on the nomenclature system proposed in our previous study (https://nmdc.cn/VirusTS/tools/classification) [9], revealed that the HA sequences formed a distinct monophyletic cluster, which was designated as a new Clade 4.6.20 (Figure 1). The HA sequences in Clade 4.6.20 exhibited a genetic distance of 4.0% from the nearest Clade 4.6.14, indicating a notable genetic divergence that may potentially impact the antigenicity of the H9N2 strains circulating in Laos compared to those found in China. To further study the antigenic characteristics of Clade 4.6.20, the representative strains were propagated in 10-day-old specific-pathogen-free (SPF) chicken embryos, and the corresponding chicken anti-sera were prepared. The HI assay showed that the representative strains in Clade 4.6.14 (A/chicken/Guangxi/NN10-19K2-NGS/2018, NN02), 4.6.15 (A/chicken/Guangdong/2-06-SZBJ022-O/2018, SZ22), 4.6.16 (A/chicken/Fujian/12-26FZHX2112-135-O/2021, FZ135) and 4.6.20 (A/chicken/Vientiane/06.05_3O/2024, WX03) had 1:2560.00, 1:5120.00, 1:2560.00, and 1:507.97 HI titers to their homogeneous antisera, respectively. All HI titers in this study were calculated as geometric mean values from three technical replicates. To confirm intra-clade antigenic consistency, two additional Clade 4.6.20 isolates – A/chicken/Vientiane/06.06_1O/2024 (WX01) and A/chicken/Luang_Prabang/05.31_11O/2024 (LBLB11) – were tested against the WX03 antiserum. Both yielded highly comparable cross-reactive titers (1:507.97 and 1:640.00, respectively), validating WX03 as a robust representative strain for this clade (Supplementary Material 2). To quantify the extent of antigenic drift across different clades, the Archetti-Horsfall formula was applied [9]. The R values for Clade 4.6.20 against Clades 4.6.14, Clade 4.6.15, and 4.6.16 were 0.354, 0.250, and 0.281, respectively. Given that an R value close to 1 indicates high antigenic similarity [9], these findings indicate that Clade 4.6.20 has undergone antigenic divergence from other prevalent strains.

Analysis of molecular markers associated with viral replication, pathogenicity, and mammalian adaptation revealed that none of the 52 H9N2 viruses carried the PB2-E627 K/V mutation, a known determinant of enhanced polymerase activity of AIVs H9N2 [9], H3N8 [11], H7N9 [12] in mammalian cells. However, all strains contained the HA-L226 and NP-N52 substitutions. The HA-L226 has been shown to enhance the binding affinity of H9N2 AIVs to human-type receptors [4,9,13,14], while NP-N52 confers partial resistance to the antiviral innate immune response mediated by human MxA protein, promoting H9N2 AIVs replication in human cell lines [9]. These findings indicate that the H9N2 viruses circulating in Laos present a potential risk of human infection.

Discussion

From a macro-evolutionary perspective, the emergence of the Clade 4.6.20 lineage in Laos is not an isolated event, but rather a dynamic reflection of the continuous evolution of the globally prevalent BJ/94 lineage. The significant divergence of this Lao lineage underscores the profound impact of frequent cross-border poultry trade and overlapping migratory bird flyways in Southeast Asia. Driven by both anthropogenic activities and natural avian migrations, these increasingly frequent cross-border interactions will undoubtedly accelerate the regional spread of avian influenza viruses. The surveillance and vaccination are widely recommended as preventive strategies [3,4,15,16], but the disparities in infrastructure and policy development across countries hinder real-time surveillance and early-warning capabilities. Furthermore, international cooperation in the surveillance of infectious diseases, including AIV infections, remains limited, highlighting the urgent need to strengthen global collaboration and advance the “One Health” initiative. Recent studies have shown that inactivated vaccines have limited efficacy against the dominant H9N2 strains currently circulating in poultry [17]. Additionally, the antigenic diversity of these strains [9] makes it difficult to prevent viral persistence within poultry populations. Therefore, timely updates to vaccine seed strains and the development of new vaccines with high effectiveness are critical to controlling H9N2 spread in domestic poultry, which may help mitigate the risk of potential spillover to humans. Moreover, non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs), such as China's “1110” policy, may play an important role in controlling AIV circulation in LPMs [4]. We acknowledge a limitation of this study. While our sequence analysis and cross-reactivity data strongly correlate with the observed antigenic drift, the precise functional impact of individual key mutations that may enhance the risk of cross-species transmission has not yet been experimentally verified.

In conclusion, the identification of a novel H9N2 clade (Clade 4.6.20) in Laos suggests that influenza viruses including both avian and human viruses may undergo undetected evolutionary changes in regions with inadequate surveillance, posing significant public health threats. This highlights the need to enhance international collaboration, improve surveillance systems, and develop new vaccines and NPIs to mitigate the increasing risks associated with cross-border viral spread and zoonotic transmission.

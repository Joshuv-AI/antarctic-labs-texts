New healthcare insights in ophthalmology: using a data integration center (DIC) to analyze the care of patients with corneal ulceration during the COVID-19 pandemic
Authors: Gabriel Stolze, Katrin Engelmann, Martin Bartos, Frank Nüßler, Martin Sedlmayr, Jens Weidner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo leverage a novel Data Integration Centre (DIC) infrastructure and real-world data to analyze pandemic-driven changes in the incidence, demographics, and management of corneal ulceration (H16.0) in a tertiary care setting.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective single-center analysis of 3029 cases (2016–2024), stratified into pre-pandemic, pandemic, and post-pandemic periods. Data were sourced via the institutional DIC. We employed descriptive statistics and regression models to evaluate temporal trends, age/sex distributions, length of stay (LOS), and surgical interventions.ResultsA significant structural shift from inpatient to outpatient care was observed, with outpatient cases rising by 70% during the pandemic while inpatient numbers declined. A notable demographic shift occurred, with a higher proportion of younger male patients (aged 30–40) affected during and after the pandemic. Mean LOS decreased progressively from 6.27 (pre-pandemic) to 4.13 days (post-pandemic). Despite an overall decline in surgical procedures, the rate of keratoplasties per 1000 patients increased significantly in the post-pandemic phase.ConclusionThe COVID-19 pandemic precipitated a marked restructuring of care for corneal ulcers towards outpatient management and was associated with a distinct demographic shift. The increase in keratoplasty rates for the diagnosis of corneal ulcer after the pandemic could be due to a significantly changed local care structure during the pandemic and the prioritization of follow-up treatment for more difficult cases. This study demonstrates the practical utility of DIC-derived real-world data for health services research, providing robust evidence on evolving care patterns and disease burden.Clinical trial registration numberThe study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Saxony Medical Association under reference number EK-BR-102/23-1 in accordance with the ICH-GCP-Guidelines.

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic had widespread effects on ophthalmic health care and service delivery worldwide [1–3]. During the pandemic, the availability of inpatient treatment capacity in ophthalmology departments was substantially reduced across many countries [4–6]. Concurrently, an increased incidence of severe corneal ulcerations was observed. Whether the observed increase in cases reflects a true rise in incidence or is instead attributable to a reduction in elective procedures—and thus a relative increase in the proportion of urgent cases—remains unclear and requires validation through analysis of real-world evidence.

Klinikum Chemnitz gGmbH, a maximal provider hospital, was among the first non-university hospitals in Germany to establish a Data Integration Center (DIC) within the framework of the Medical Informatics Hub in Saxony (MiHUBx) project [7]. Funded as one of six "Digital Progress Hubs Health" by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research (BMBF), MiHUBx aims to enhance the availability of cross-sectional health data and promote integration of data across sectoral boarders within the healthcare system [8]. This newly established infrastructure should help to answer clinically and structurally relevant questions in healthcare research.

The development of a corneal ulcer (ulcus corneae) requires damage to the ocular surface and corneal epithelium and is typically the result of multifactorial etiologies [9, 10]. It can be caused by various factors such as bacteria, fungi, and amoebas. Our study does not serve to fundamentally clarify the causality of the clinical picture or its procedures. Rather, we aim at using the newly established data science approaches and possibilities of the DIC infrastructure to identify trends during the COVID-19 pandemic, which has not yet been fully processed. Furthermore, we sought to recognize, expand, and improve the potential of new possibilities for future research questions.

In the present study we describe the demographic, epidemiology, gender-specific and care patterns of patients with corneal ulceration prior, during and past COVID-19 pandemic using a real-world data approach. We further assess indicators of disease severity, including inpatient length of stay, and analyze therapeutic needs by examining the frequency and type of surgical interventions performed.

Material and methods

Data sources and processing

The medical care landscape is shaped by multiple electronic documentation systems—primarily patient administration systems and hospital information systems—whose structural heterogeneity resulted in the establishment of non-interoperable data silos. For the present analysis, data acquisition was conducted through the newly established institutional Data Integration Center (DIC) using standardized data provisioning workflows, as methodologically described in detail by Bartos et al. [11]. The resulting analytical dataset was derived based on ICD-10-coded admission diagnoses for corneal ulceration (H16.0) and includes diagnostic, therapeutic, and sociodemographic parameters, in full compliance with data protection regulations. The ethics committee decided not to obtain patient consent based on the anonymous retrospective analysis of the available data. All data was completely anonymized before access. The data were accessed for research purposes on the 13.01.2025. The dataset spans the observation period from 2016 to 2024 and captures both patient-level and case-level data. Cases in Germany were defined as the time from initial presentation through discharge, including a two-week post-discharge follow-up period.

To evaluate potential effects due to influences of the pandemic on the patient health care situation for corneal ulceration (ICD-10: H16.0), the study period (2016–2024) was stratified into three distinct intervals: the pre-pandemic period (2017–2019), the pandemic period (2020–2022), and the post-pandemic period (2023–2024). This temporal stratification allows a systematic analysis of treatment prevalence and epidemiologic shifts in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Statistical analysis

Both descriptive and inferential statistical methods were employed to address the study objectives. This includes parametric and non-parametric test procedures. Regression models and generalized linear models were applied to assess associations between time period, patient characteristics, and treatment outcomes. Results are reported as relative risks with robust standard errors and 95% confidence intervals. A two-sided significance level of 5% was used for all statistical tests.

Results

Analysis of the defined study cohort

A total of 3029 cases (= treatment consultation) with a diagnosis of corneal ulceration (ICD-10: H16.0) treated at the Department of Ophthalmology during 2016 to 2024 were included in the analysis. Among these, 2372 were outpatient and 657 inpatient cases.

The treatment cases were caused by a total of 1817 patients, including 1386 who were treated on an outpatient basis only and 431 who had to be admitted to hospital. The median age of all patients was 55 years (interquartile range, 40; mean, 53.11; standard deviation, 23.84). Of the total cohort, 1089 were male and 728 were female, representing a significant predominance of male patients (Chi-square test, P < 0.001). Median age differed significantly between sexes: 66 years among female patients versus 48 years among male patients (Wilcox-Test, P = 0.000).

Age distribution across the entire study cohort and across all years demonstrated a broad spread over all age groups, with a notable bimodal pattern peaking in the third and seventh/eighth decades of life. A marked sex-specific divergence was observed (Fig. 1). In contrast, the inpatient group demonstrated a comparatively stable course, with minor fluctuations at a consistently low level. Relative frequencies in this group oscillated between approximately 2 and 3 units, with a modest upward trend toward the end of the observation period. Notably, the divergence between the two groups widened beginning in 2021, suggesting a shift in the underlying structural or operational parameters of care delivery. The pronounced increase in outpatient cases, in the context of relative stable number of inpatients, points toward a systematic shift in the relative importance and utilization of outpatient versus inpatient care.

The increasing divergence between the two treatment modalities depicted in Fig. 2—characterized by a pronounced rise in outpatient cases beginning in 2021, in contrast to relative stable numbers in the inpatient cohort—suggests a systematic shift in the relative utilization of these care settings. This observation necessitated a more granular analysis across the three predefined temporal intervals.

To achieve equal-length time intervals, cases from 2016 were excluded, yielding a total of 2,799 cases for the refined analysis. Stratified examination of treatment settings over the course of the pandemic revealed sector-specific trends in case volume. Within the inpatient setting (n = 657), a continuous decline in case numbers was observed: from 232 cases (35.31%) in the pre-pandemic period, to 197 cases (29.98%) during the pandemic, and further to 164 cases (24.96%) in the post-pandemic phase. In contrast, the outpatient sector (n = 2372) demonstrated a marked increase: from 507 cases (21.37%) during the pre-pandemic period to 861 cases (36.29%) during the pandemic, with sustained levels of 838 cases (35.32%) in the post-pandemic period.

To quantify these patterns, Poisson regression modelling was applied. The analysis revealed that outpatient cases were significantly more common than inpatient cases (β = 1.15; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.28; P < 0.001) and demonstrated a significant increase over time (β = 0.37; 95% CI, 0.15 to 0.59; P = 0.001). The temporal trajectory of outpatient case frequency followed a nonlinear pattern, with an initial increase during the pandemic (β = 0.27; 95% CI, 0.06 to 0.49; P = 0.013), followed by a decline in the subsequent post-pandemic period (β = – 0.42; 95% CI, – 0.67 to – 0.19; P < 0.001).

Sex alone did not significantly influence overall case numbers (β = 0.037; 95% CI, –0.13 to 0.20; P = 0.661); however, a significant interaction effect was observed with male patients exhibiting a distinct temporal trend in outpatient presentation (β = 0.45; 95% CI, 0.12 to 0.77; P = 0.007). Notably, during the pandemic period, the number of female cases decreased relative to male cases (β = – 0.52; 95% CI, – 0.81 to – 0.24; P < 0.001).

Analysis of inpatient length of stay during the observation period and in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic

The mean inpatient length of stay (LOS) across the entire observation period was 5.36 days (SD, 5.55). Male patients had a mean LOS of 5.71 days, whereas female patients were hospitalized for a mean of 4.97 days. Stratified by pandemic period, the average LOS was 6.27 days in the pre-pandemic era, 5.20 days during the pandemic, and 4.13 days in the post-pandemic period.

A Poisson regression analysis was conducted to assess LOS in relation to the defined pandemic phases and to evaluate age- and sex-specific effects. In the pre-pandemic period (Fig. 4B), male sex was associated with a significantly longer LOS compared to female sex (β = 1.58; 95% CI, 0.16 to 2.99; P = 0.017), and age was a significant positive predictor of longer LOS (β = 0.042; 95% CI, 0.026 to 0.059; P < 0.001). However, the age effect appeared attenuated in men (β = – 0.019; 95% CI, – 0.040 to 0.002; P = 0.052).Fig. 4Gender-stratified poisson regression length of stay taking into account the respective periods: dependent variable: length of inpatient stay, independent variable: age, A: entire observation period, B: pre-pandemic, C: pandemic, D: post-pandemic

During the pandemic (Fig. 4C), the pattern reversed: male patients had a significantly shorter LOS than female patients (β = – 1.64; 95% CI, – 2.67 to – 0.64; P = 0.003). The influence of age on LOS was no longer significant for women (β = 0.011; 95% CI, –0.0004 to 0.021; P = 0.099), but in men, age remained a significant positive predictor of longer stays (β = 0.030; 95% CI, 0.01 to 0.05; P = 0.001).

In the post-pandemic period (Fig. 4D), the pre-pandemic pattern re-emerged, with male patients again experiencing significantly longer hospital stays (β = 2.76; 95% CI, 1.41 to 4.21; P < 0.001). Age continued to have a strong positive association with LOS (β = 0.08; 95% CI, 0.06 to 0.09; P < 0.001), although the effect was slightly diminished in men compared to women (β = –0.03; 95% CI, –0.05 to –0.01; P = 0.002).

These findings show temporary but pronounced pandemic-related shifts in inpatient length of stay, with notable sex-specific differences.

For further analysis, “OPS” procedure codes according to the Operation and Procedure Classification System (“Operationen- und Prozedurenschlüssel”), a standardized coding framework for documenting surgical and procedural interventions in the German healthcare system, were used. Analogous to the ICD-10 procedure codes used in other countries, OPS codes are essential for clinical reporting, hospital billing, and reimbursement processes. Their application ensures consistent documentation and facilitates administrative and epidemiologic evaluations within the German hospital system.

Procedures coded as 5–125.01, 5–125.11, 5–125.12, and 5–125.42 (anterior and posterior lamellar corneal transplantation, perforating corneal transplantation, non-HLA-typed and HLA-typed, as well as insertion of a keratoprosthesis) were grouped under the variable “keratoplasties”, while procedures coded as 5–129.2, 5–129.3, 5–129.4, 5–129.5, 5–129.X, and 5–129.Y (covering of the cornea with an amniotic membrane, closure of a defect with tissue adhesive, placement or removal of a corneal suture, or other unspecified corneal procedures) were categorized as “other corneal interventions.”

Although the total number of both procedure types declined during the pandemic and post-pandemic periods (Fig. 7), the trajectories of “keratoplasties” and “other corneal interventions” diverged. Discussion

Corneal ulceration represents a condition of considerable clinical importance, given its multifactorial etiology and the potential for profound and often irreversible vision loss [12–15]. During the COVID-19 pandemic, a perceived dissonance emerged between anticipated treatment volumes and actual care demands, which prompted our investigation of data-driven questions within the still-developing infrastructure of the Medical Informatics Initiative [16].

In response to our predefined research questions, data from our institution demonstrated an increased incidence of corneal ulcers throughout the pandemic and into the post-pandemic period. This observation aligns with previous studies suggesting a correlation between the use of face masks and the onset of dry eye syndrome [17]. Mask-wearing—especially with poorly fitted devices—can alter the ocular surface environment, contributing to meibomian gland dysfunction, which is implicated in the pathogenesis of dry eye disease and may ultimately lead to corneal ulceration [18]. In Germany, the use of face masks in public spaces was legally mandated during the pandemic, which may explain, at least in part, the observed increase in ulcer incidence. Prior to the pandemic, it was estimated that dry eye affected between 10 and 20% of individuals [19–21]; during the pandemic, this prevalence reportedly exceeded 50% [22].

The influence of other disease-promoting factors in the cornea that are related to COVID-19—for example, infection with SARS-CoV-2 can trigger small-fiber neuropathy in the surface of the eye, which has similar symptoms and morphological characteristics to dry eye syndrome (DED) and diabetic neuropathy—must also be taken into account [23].

An additional explanatory factor may be the intensified use of digital technology during pandemic-related restrictions, which has been identified as a contributing risk for ocular surface pathology [24, 25]. Although these studies are heterogeneous in methodology, they collectively highlight the importance of documenting comorbidities and environmental exposures when seeking to establish causal inferences. These parameters, however, were not available within our current dataset.

In our cohort, a higher proportion of male patients developed corneal ulcers, and they were significantly younger on average compared to female patients. This may reflect altered care priorities or differential disease trajectories between sexes during the pandemic. While our data-driven analysis cannot establish causality, potential explanations include reduced utilization of healthcare services during the prodromal stages of dry eye [26, 27], higher digital media exposure among younger males [24, 28–31], and underlying population-based demographic factors [32].

We also observed an increase in outpatient treatment during the pandemic, likely reflecting efforts to address rising care demands under constrained inpatient capacity. In the region under study, several ophthalmic inpatient units were temporarily closed as personnel were reallocated to pandemic-related duties. National analyses confirm a reduction in inpatient ophthalmologic cases during this time [33, 34]. These dynamics were also reflected in our data, particularly in the shift in length of stay patterns: male patients had longer hospitalizations during the pre- and post-pandemic phases, but this trend reversed during the pandemic. This finding may reflect modified care capacities, altered patient triage strategies, or sex-based differences in disease course. In the post-pandemic period, length-of-stay patterns gradually returned to pre-pandemic levels.

These trends are mirrored in the procedural data. The total number of documented corneal interventions increased steadily through 2019, followed by a pronounced decline during the pandemic, with evidence of recovery beginning in 2024. Notably, we observed a distinct increase in the number of corneal transplantations (keratoplasties) per 1000 patients, which suggests a possible shift in treatment strategies. It should be explicitly noted here that the observed increase in numbers can only be attributed to cases of corneal ulcer as a confirmed diagnosis and has not been mixed with other possible indications for keratoplasty, such as bullous keratopathy, Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy, corneal leukoma, or keratoconus. This allows a clear statement to be made about the correlation.

While this trend cannot be causally explained by the available data, it may indicate a sustained increase in disease severity during and following the pandemic. One plausible explanation is a continued deterioration in care structures after the pandemic, compounded by an intensified reliance on digital media and the exacerbation of preexisting dry eye conditions.

Conclusion

Our analysis demonstrates that the clinical management of patients with corneal ulcers underwent significant changes during the course of the COVID-19 pandemic. The incidence of diagnosed cases increased, while limitations in inpatient capacity led to a marked shift toward outpatient care. Among hospitalized patients, a reduction in the number of procedures performed and a shorter hospitalization were observed. Furthermore, both age and sex emerged as relevant risk factors for disease incidence and severity. These findings highlight a pandemic-related shift in both disease presentation and care delivery. Ongoing multicenter analyses have already been initiated and are expected to further elucidate these associations.

Importantly, this study illustrates that complex clinical and health services research questions can be effectively addressed using the infrastructure of the German Medical Informatics Initiative (MII) and the associated Data Integration Centers, even within a single-center framework. The integration of clinical insight with data-driven methodologies opens new avenues for the interpretation and utilization of routine healthcare data. Although the current integration of ophthalmologic data into national data infrastructures remains limited, future efforts are expected to expand and refine the available datasets. The planned inclusion of additional sites and the development of a standardized core dataset will enhance data quality and availability. This work therefore contributes to the advancement of data-driven medical research while adhering to the highest standards of data protection.

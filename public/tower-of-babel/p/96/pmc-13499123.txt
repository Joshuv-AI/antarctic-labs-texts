Complete false lumen exclusion by superior mesenteric artery revascularization with balloon angioplasty and bridging stent grafting followed by thoracic endovascular aortic repair in a patient with complex chronic type B aortic dissection
Authors: Daisuke Yamagishi, Takao Ohki, Kota Shukuzawa, Hirotsugu Ozawa, Hikaru Nakagawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Endovascular exclusion of the false lumen (FL) in chronic type B aortic dissection is challenging when visceral branches depend on FL perfusion. We report a case of chronic type B aortic dissection in which the superior mesenteric artery (SMA) originated from the FL with concomitant celiac artery occlusion. Antegrade SMA perfusion was restored by balloon angioplasty and bridging stent grafting across a pinhole re-entry, followed by thoracic endovascular aortic repair with a fenestrated stent graft to seal the proximal entry. Follow-up imaging demonstrated complete FL thrombosis, favorable aortic remodeling, and preserved SMA perfusion. This case highlights the importance of tailored procedural sequencing.

Early outcomes of stent grafting for chronic type B aortic dissection (TBAD) are favorable, with survival rates comparable to those of open surgery. However, sealing the proximal entry alone may fail to induce false lumen (FL) shrinkage, and complete FL exclusion requires closure of all associated re-entry tears.1,2 When visceral branches depend on FL perfusion, simple FL exclusion may cause organ ischemia. We present a case in which complete FL exclusion was achieved by revascularization of the superior mesenteric artery (SMA) using angioplasty of the re-entry orifice and bridging stent grafting from the true lumen (TL) to the SMA, followed by fenestrated thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) to seal the proximal entry in a patient with chronic TBAD.

Case report

Written publication consent was obtained from the patient, and the Institutional Review Board approved this case report (Approval No. 37-512[13168]).

A 91-year-old woman who had suffered from an acute TBAD treated conservatively 11 years earlier was referred for aneurysmal enlargement from 40 to 60 mm. The patient had hypertension and heart failure associated with Takotsubo cardiomyopathy.

Computed tomography angiography (CTA) demonstrated a proximal entry just distal to the left subclavian artery (LSCA). The centerline distance between the LSCA and the proximal entry was approximately 3 cm, and the intervening aorta was also aneurysmal, leaving only 1 cm of nonaneurysmal aorta distal to the LSCA. The SMA was almost entirely perfused from the FL (Fig 1, A and B). The re-entry at the SMA level was not clearly visualized because the SMA orifice was occluded by the intimal flap (Fig 1, C). In addition, the celiac artery was occluded, but both renal arteries were patent and originated from the TL. Given the patient’s age and comorbidities, endovascular treatment was selected. The re-entry at the SMA level was not visible in the CTA, so angiography was performed before the definitive procedure. Intestinal perfusion was considered dependent on the SMA. Although collateral circulation from the SMA related to the celiac artery occlusion was present, the inferior mesenteric artery originated from the TL and no significant collateral development from the inferior mesenteric artery was observed. Preoperative imaging and angiography demonstrated no apparent additional thoracoabdominal re-entry tears, and only limited intercostal contribution to FL perfusion; therefore, spinal cord ischemia (SCI) risk was considered low.

Forced contrast injection from the FL side adjacent to the re-entry at the SMA level demonstrated a pinhole-sized re-entry at the original SMA orifice (Fig 2, C and D). Therefore, SMA revascularization was planned before proximal entry closure.

Operative procedure

Under general anesthesia, a steerable sheath (8.5F Agilis NxT Introducer; Abbott Inc) was inserted via the right common femoral artery (CFA) and was used to visualize the pinhole-sized re-entry at the native SMA orifice. A 6F Destination guiding sheath (Terumo, Leuven) was inserted via the right brachial artery (BA) and advanced into the FL to the SMA level through the proximal entry. FL angiography demonstrated the SMA and the re-entry (Fig 3, A). A guidewire was advanced across the re-entry into the FL. Subsequently, using an EN Snare System (Merit Medical Systems), a pull-through system was established between the right BA and the right CFA. As the re-entry measured 1.5 mm, balloon angioplasty was performed before SMA revascularization. Sequential balloon dilation enlarged the re-entry to 4 mm. With the pull-through system maintained, the steerable sheath was advanced into the FL through the predilated re-entry. Subsequently, the steerable sheath was used to navigate a guidewire into the SMA under pull-through wire support. After removal of the pull-through wire, the steerable sheath was advanced into the SMA (Fig 3, B and C). Since the SMA measured approximately 5.5 mm in diameter, a 6 × 29 mm VIABAHN VBX stent graft (W. L. Gore & Associates; Fig 4, A) was deployed to bridge the TL to the SMA. Postoperative course

Postoperatively, careful blood pressure management was performed to maintain spinal cord perfusion, and no SCI occurred.

Follow-up CTA at 9 months demonstrated complete FL thrombosis, favorable TL remodeling, and patent SMA flow (Fig 5, A-C). Discussion

This case is characterized by the simultaneous performance of SMA revascularization for FL-dependent visceral perfusion and re-entry occlusion before primary entry closure. In general, the treatment strategy for a dissecting aneurysm with multiple re-entry tears involves initial closure of the proximal entry, followed by hemodynamic assessment to guide the management of distal re-entry tears. However, in this case, because visceral perfusion was dependent on FL flow through the SMA, SMA revascularization had to be performed before re-entry closure. A key factor enabling this strategy was identification of a pinhole re-entry at the SMA origin on angiography, which had not been clearly visualized on CTA.

Complete FL exclusion in chronic TBAD remains challenging, particularly when visceral branches depend on FL perfusion.3, 4, 5 Treatment strategies for persistent FL perfusion after primary entry closure in TBAD include tear-oriented techniques such as tacking, bridging stent grafting, and the Knickerbocker technique,6 as well as entry occlusion techniques using modified vascular plugs7 and direct FL exclusion techniques including the Candy-plug technique8 and coil embolization. Among these approaches, bridging stent grafting uniquely allows simultaneous visceral branch revascularization and re-entry closure. Therefore, the optimal treatment strategy should be individualized according to the patient’s anatomy, visceral perfusion pattern, and surgical risk.

In elderly or high-risk patients, less invasive endovascular strategies are preferable whenever possible. Complex techniques such as visceral fenestrated TEVAR and branched TEVAR may require extensive device customization and visceral branch reconstruction, which can increase procedural complexity. Therefore, treatment with fenestrated TEVAR using the Najuta stent graft combined with bridging stent grafting was selected as a less invasive alternative to more complex visceral reconstruction techniques.

The Najuta stent graft is a semicustom-made fenestrated thoracic stent graft with unstented fenestrations that preserves supra-aortic branch perfusion.9 In the present case, the aortic segment between the LSCA and the primary entry was also aneurysmal, making zone 3 landing unsuitable for achieving an adequate proximal seal. Accordingly, zone 0 TEVAR using the fenestrated Najuta stent graft was performed to secure an adequate proximal landing zone while preserving perfusion to the supra-aortic branches.10 The distal landing zone was limited to the descending thoracic aorta to preserve intercostal arteries and reduce SCI risk.

From a technical perspective, because the re-entry was extremely narrow, additional support was anticipated to be necessary for stable sheath advancement and selective SMA cannulation. Therefore, a right CFA-right BA pull-through system was established through the primary entry and re-entry. Although a steerable sheath was advanced into the FL, sheath advancement remained limited and unstable. Therefore, SMA cannulation was performed by advancing a guidewire into the SMA while maintaining the pull-through system. This approach improved device support and coaxial alignment, enabling advancement of the guiding sheath through the narrow re-entry and successful SMA reconstruction.

Conclusions

In chronic TBAD with SMA perfusion dependent on the FL, feasibility of SMA revascularization should be confirmed before FL exclusion. We performed SMA revascularization and re-entry closure using balloon angioplasty and bridging stent grafting before subsequent TEVAR for complete FL exclusion.

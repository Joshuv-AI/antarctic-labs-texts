Analysis of Platelet Volume Indices and their utility in Acute Coronary Syndrome
Authors: Aadil Ahmed Mir, Afaq Ahmed Khan, Jan Mohd Sheikh, Mohd Iqbal Dar, Hilal Ahmed Rather, Aijaz Ahmed Lone, Imran Hafeez, Aamir Rashid
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Higher Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) may become useful marker for early detection and prognostication of Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) along with other biomarkers.Aim of the Study:To study and analyse platelet volume indices (PVI) in ACS patients.Material and Methods:The blood samples of patients diagnosed as acute coronary syndrome were analysed and complete blood count analysis were done including PVI. The PVI of different groups of acute coronary syndromes were compared with each other and to age and sex matched healthy controls.Results:The mean MPV of ACS group was 11.2±1.45 fL and was significantly higher than control group 8.9±1.12 fL. Among ACS cases mean MPV of STEMI was 11.51±1.44fL, in NSTEMI it was 10.83±1.51fL and in unstable angina (UA) it was 9.21±1.36 fL. Significantly higher in STEMI followed by NSTEMI than unstable angina. The mean PDW of ACS cases was 18.2±1.52fL and was significantly higher than control group 16.8±1.28fL. Among ACS cases it was significantly higher in STEMI 18.37±1.48fL than NSTEMI 17.91±1.46fL and it was significantly higher than UA group 17.04±1.88 fL. The mean PCT of ACS cases was 0.231±0.042% was significantly higher than controls 0.173±0.023%.Conclusion:PVI provides an important, simple, effortless, and cost-effective tool, which can be useful in predicting and prognosticating an acute coronary event. MPV and PDW has the potential to be used as a preliminary test to identify high-risk patients for myocardial infarction.

INTRODUCTION

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the leading cause of death all over the world. Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) is a set of signs and symptoms due to the rupture of a plaque and is a consequence of platelet-rich coronary thrombus formation. Platelets have a major role in the pathogenesis of ACS, where plaque rupture is followed by platelet activation and thrombus formation. Platelet activation is the key step of the pathogenesis of ACS.[1234] Activated platelets are larger in size, which can be measured by mean platelet volume (MPV). Larger platelets are more adhesive and tend to aggregate more as they secrete more dense granules. They are metabolically and enzymatically more active than small platelets and produce more thromboxane A2.[567] Increased platelet activation may also represent the net pathophysiological effects of a number of cardiovascular risk factors, such as smoking and dyslipidemia, thus representing a broad marker of CVD risk. Platelet activation leads to a more spherical shape with increased platelet swelling, thereby leading to an increase in platelet mass and volume.[7] Free arachidonic acid is also formed due to platelet activation, which may be converted into prostaglandins, also known as thromboxane A2. It is one of the most potent vasoconstrictor and platelet-aggregating substances. Platelet function and size correlate because larger platelets, produced from activated megakaryocytes in the bone marrow, are likely to be more reactive than normal-sized platelets. Large platelets are metabolically and enzymatically more active than smaller ones and secrete and express more mediators such as adhesive proteins (fibrinogen, thrombospondin, and fibronectin), growth factors (platelet-derived growth factor, transforming growth factor, and basic FGF), and chemotactic and mitogenic factors (platelet factor 4, coagulation factors [factor V and factor XI], and cytokine-like factors [interleukin1 and CD40 ligand]).[4] Larger platelets as measured by their volumes (MPV) may be useful markers in patients with ACS. Higher MPV may become useful marker for early detection of ACS along with other biomarkers. Patients with increased MPV could be easily identified during routine hematological analysis. It could play an important role in the early detection of ACS and is beneficial for preventive treatment. It could be used as a screening test to differentiate the origin of chest pain along with other cardiac biomarkers. Unlike all other markers of platelet activation and reactivity, MPV is automatically calculated by most equipment used for performing blood cell counts. Thus, determining the platelet size through MPV is a simple, extremely inexpensive, and readily available measure in hospital and outpatient settings. MPV may be used as predictor for early detection of ACS and risk stratification when other cardiac biomarkers are negative. Patients with higher plateletcrit (PCT) and platelet distribution width (PDW) are at higher risk of ACS. These patients can easily be identified during routine hematological examination and the patients could possibly benefit from preventive treatment.[4]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study was conducted in the Department of Cardiology Sheri Kashmir Institute of Medical Sciences (SKIMS), Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir, India. We enrolled 570 ACS patients for 2 years (May 2022 to May 2024). Written informed consent was obtained from all the patients before enrolling them into the study. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee (SKIMS-IEC-No: 6231). The study group included patients with confirmed ACS diagnosis on the basis of history, relevant clinical examination, abnormal 12 lead surface ECG, and elevated cardiac biomarker.

Blood sample analysis for platelet volume indices

The blood samples of these patients were drawn from the antecubital vein and immediately mixed in EDTA vacutainers. These samples were run within 2 h of venepuncture using automated Beckman Coulter Analyser [Figure 1] and complete blood count analysis were done including platelet volume indices (MPV, PDW, and PCT). Subsequently, these patients were subjected to 2D echocardiography and coronary angiography. These platelet volume indices of different groups of ACSs were compared with each other and to age- and sex-matched healthy controls. These platelet volume indices of different classes of ACS were compared to the severity of disease in terms of their hospital and 30-day mortality posthospitalization.

Statistical methods

The recorded data were compiled and entered in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet and then exported to the data editor of SPSS Version 20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as Mean ± SD and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Student’s independent t-test and analysis of variance were employed for comparing continuous variables. Tukey’s test was used for postanalysis. The Tukey method applies simultaneously to the set of all pairwise comparisons. The Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, whichever appropriate, was applied for comparing categorical variables. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was applied to depict the independent variables of mortality. P <0.05 (two tailed) was considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

Mean platelet volume

The study demonstrated platelet volume indices (MPV, PDW, and PCT) are increased in ACS patients when compared to healthy controls. Statistically significant difference in platelet volume indices was found to exist between cases (acute myocardial infarction (AMI) group and unstable angina group) and controls. Data from this study shows mean MPV of ACS patients was 11.2 ± 1.45 fL (11.08–11.32 with 95% CI). The mean MPV of non-ACS controls was 8.9 ± 1.12 fL (8.78–9.04 with 95% CI). The difference in MPV between ACS patients versus controls was statistically significant with P < 0.001 [Table 8].

Platelet volume index in cases versus controls

The mean MPV of Group I STEMI patients was 11.5 ± 1.44 fL. The mean MPV of Group II NSTEMI patients was 10.83 ± 1.51 fL. The mean MPV of Group III unstable angina patients was 9.21 ± 1.36 fL. The mean MPV of Group I STEMI patients when compared to the mean MPV of Group II NSTEMI patients, the difference was statistically significant with P < 0.001. Then the mean MPV of Group II NSTEMI patients was compared to the mean MPV of Group III unstable angina patients and the difference was statistically significant with P < 0.001. Finally, the mean MPV of Group I STEMI patients was compared to the mean MPV of Group III unstable angina patients, and the difference was found to be statistically significant with P < 0.001. MPV indirectly implies that platelet destruction and reproduction are relatively high. This can cause more activity than smaller-sized platelets. Patients with higher MPV are at higher risk of vascular occlusion. Cameron et al.[8] demonstrated that MPV not only correlated with more ischemic events but also remained specifically high for several weeks after the infarction. Martin et al.[9] compared platelet volume and distribution in 15 cases with myocardial infarction and 22 healthy people and the MPV was found to be higher in the MI group even after 6 weeks after the infarction. Biradar et al.[10] in a study of 100 cases diagnosed with ACS and 50 controls concluded that MPV in ACS cases was higher compared to those with the control group. Manchanda et al.[5] [Table 9] in their prospective hospital-based study including 175 cases of ACS and 175 controls concluded that MPV was raised in patients who have suffered an acute coronary event when compared with controls and those with unstable angina.

Platelet distribution width

The mean PDW of ACS cases was 18.2 ± 1.52 fL(18.02–18.29S with 95% CI). The mean PDW of the control group was 16.8 ± 1.28 fL (16.64–16.94 with 95% CI). The mean PDW of the ACS group was higher compared to the non-ACS control group with a statistically significant difference with P < 0.001. We compared the PDW between different classes of ACS and we found that the mean PDW OF Group I STEMI patients was 18.37 ± 1.48 fL, the mean PDW of Group II NSTEMI patients was 17.91 ± 1.46 fL and the mean PDW of Group III unstable angina patients was 17.04 ± 1.88 fL. The mean PDW of Group I STEMI was significantly higher than Group II NSTEMI patients with P = 0. 002. Compared to Group III unstable angina patients, Group II NSTEMI patients had significantly higher mean PDW with a P = 0. 001. Mean PDW of Group I STEMI patients with mean PDW of Group III unstable angina patients and we found Group I patients had significantly higher mean PDW compared to Group III patients, with a P < 0.001. PDW reflects whether platelets are equal or different in size and shape. Platelets start to get larger when they are activated. Assiri et al.[15] who examined 212 patients in a case–control study and found PDW was significantly higher in MI cases than in control groups. Pervin et al.,[16] Nandwani and Bhatnagar,[17] and Khandekar et al.,[18] described in their studies all platelet volume indices including MPV, PDW, and PLCR were increased significantly in patients with ACS than controls. During the comparison of PDW and MPV, Pervin et al. found significant differences between the groups.[16]

Plateletcrit

The mean PCT of ACS patients was 0.231% ± 0.042% (0.227–0.234 with 95% CI) compared to the non-ACS control group, mean PCT was 0.173% ± 0.023% (0.171–0.175 with 95% CI). Mean PCT in ACS patients was significantly higher compared to the non-ACS control group with P < 0.001. Comparing PCT among different groups of ACS, we found that Group I ACS patients had a mean PCT of 0.229% ± 0.0417%, patients in Group II NSTEMI had a mean PCT of 0.232% ± 0.0421%, and patients in Group III Unstable angina had a mean PCT of 0.235% ± 0.0424%. We compared the mean PCT of Group I versus Group II patients and the difference was statistically nonsignificant with P = 0.462. We also compared the mean PCT values of Group II and Group III patients and we found nonsignificant difference with P = 0.875. The mean PCT of Group I STEMI patients with that of Group III Unstable angina patients and this also showed insignificant difference with P = 0.519. Akula et al.,[6] in a study of 60 patients who presented with ACSs there was a significant difference in PCT and PDW in patients with ACS compared to non-ACS patients. Patients with ACS have higher values of PCT and PDW when compared to age-matched controls (P = 0.0001). Khode et al.,[11] [Table 9] in a comparative study of 128 subjects Increased MPV, PLC, P-LCR, and PCT were observed in the CAD group compared to the control group. Our study showed results consistent with the above studies. PCT of the ACS patient group was significantly increased compared to the non-ACS control group, but PCT among different groups of ACS did not show any significant difference.

Platelet volume index and mortality in acute coronary syndrome

In-hospital and 30-day mortality data in our study showed, 28 (4.9%) of 570 ACS cases died. Twenty-six (92.9%) were STEMI and 2 (7.1%) were NSTEMI. 11 (39.3%) were subjected to PCI and 17 (60.7%) did not undergo PCI because of complex lesion anatomy on angiography. Among platelet volume indices mean PDW of ACS cases who had mortality was 19.12 ± 1.72 fL compared to ACS cases who had no mortality where the mean PDW was 18.21 ± 1.49 fL with a statistically significant difference and P < 0.001. The multivariate analysis of different variables showed age >60 years (OR = 2.53, 95% CI: 2.03–3.48), STEMI (OR = 4.72, 95% CI: 3.18–6.35), and mean PDW (OR = 1.95, 95% CI: 1.54–2.87) of ACS cases were independent predictors of mortality. Rechciński et al.[19] in a prospective study enrolled 538 patients found that the MPV, PDW, and platelet large cell ratio measured on admission are strong, independent prognostic factors in PCI-treated acute MI. The study showed higher age group (≥60 years), ST-segment elevation AMI, and mean PDW values were independent predictors of mortality. The data showed insignificant relation of outcome in terms of in-hospital and 30-day mortality when compared with MPV and PCT.

CONCLUSION

Platelet volume index (PVI) is a feasible and easy reliable test that helps in predicting prognosis among different types of ACS. PVI provides an important, simple, effortless, and cost-effective tool, which can be useful in predicting and prognosticating an acute coronary event. MPV and PDW have the potential to be used as a preliminary test to identify high-risk patients for myocardial infarction along with other supportive clinical investigations.

Investigating the Regulatory Mechanisms and Prognostic Significance of miR-941 Targeting the Nrf2/Keap1 Pathway in Elderly Ischemic Stroke Patients
Authors: Xixia Huang, Jianpeng Gao, Rongbin Zhu, Yifei Sun, Lina Zhang, Xinhai Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aims to explore the role and underlying correlation of miR-941 in the targeted regulation of the Keap1/Nrf2 signaling pathway in the prognosis of senile ischemic stroke (IS), providing new targets and strategies for clinical treatment. Totally 102 elderly IS patients admitted from July 2022 to June 2023 in Jing’an District Shibei hospital were enrolled and divided into hyperacute, acute, subacute, and chronic phases by disease course and imaging results. Peripheral blood samples were collected. RT-PCR was used to detect miR-941, Nrf2 mRNA and Keap1 mRNA expression levels, and ELISA for HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 expression. The modified Rankin scale (mRS) evaluated patients’ prognosis, and logistic regression analyzed influencing factors. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and Kaplan-Meier curves analyzed the predictive value of miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway in IS prognosis and their correlation with patients’ survival periods. The expression levels of miR-941 and Nrf2 mRNA gradually decreased in different stages of IS, while the expression of Keap1 mRNA gradually increased, and the expression levels of antioxidant factors HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 also decreased significantly (all P < 0.001). The relative abundances of miR-941 and Nrf2 mRNA were significantly negatively correlated with the NIHSS score (P < 0.001), while Keap1 mRNA was positively correlated with the NIHSS score (P < 0.001). Logistic regression analysis showed that miR-941 and Nrf2 mRNA were protective factors for a good prognosis of IS (P < 0.001), while Keap1 mRNA was a risk factor for a poor prognosis (P < 0.01). ROC curve analysis showed that miR-941 had high predictive efficacy in the prognosis of IS (the AUCs were 0.801). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that the survival periods of patients in the high-expression groups of miR-941 and Nrf2 were significantly longer than those in the low-expression groups (P < 0.001), while the survival period of patients in the high-expression group of Keap1 mRNA was significantly shortened (P < 0.001). The correlation between miR-941 and Keap1/Nrf2 signaling pathway is crucial in the course and prognosis of senile ischemic cerebral infarction. High miR-941 and Nrf2 mRNA levels correlate with good prognosis, while high Keap1 mRNA levels link to poor prognosis. These molecules probably serve as potential biomarkers for clinical prognosis and new treatment targets.

Introduction

Ischemic stroke (IS) is a common acute cerebrovascular disease, and its incidence increases significantly with age (Koutsaliaris et al. 2022). In addition, elderly IS patients have a poor prognosis, and more than 80% of elderly stroke patients have functional disabilities, which seriously affect the quality of life of patients (Cai et al. 2017). Research shows that oxidative stress plays a key role in cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury (Orellana-Urzúa et al. 2020). Oxidative stress refers to the imbalance between the oxidation and antioxidant systems in the body, leading to the excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which in turn causes cell damage (Olmez and Ozyurt 2012). In ischemic stroke, oxidative stress not only aggravates brain tissue damage but may also affect the progression of the disease through pathways such as inducing apoptosis and necrosis (Orellana-Urzúa et al. 2020). The generation of oxidative stress is related to multiple factors, including mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammatory response, and disruption of the blood-brain barrier(Shen et al. 2021). Mitochondria are the main energy suppliers in cells, and their dysfunction can lead to the excessive production of ROS, thereby triggering oxidative stress (Andalib et al. 2017). In addition, oxidative stress is closely related to the endothelial dysfunction of blood vessels, which may exacerbate the progression of cerebrovascular diseases. In summary, oxidative stress plays an important role in the pathogenesis of ischemic stroke (Scicchitano et al. 2019). In-depth research on its specific mechanisms and influencing factors will help develop more effective treatment strategies and improve the prognosis of patients.

Nuclear factor E2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) is a key regulator of the cellular antioxidant stress response and regulates the expression of downstream antioxidant genes through its interaction with Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (Keap1) (Wang et al. 2022). The Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway plays a crucial role in regulating oxidative stress and neuroprotection. In addition, the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway plays an important protective role under multiple pathological conditions, including stroke, neurodegenerative diseases, and cancer (Wang et al. 2022; Xiao et al. 2025). In ischemic stroke, oxidative stress is an important factor leading to neuronal damage. As a key regulator of antioxidant stress, Nrf2 reduces the damage of oxidative stress to nerve cells by regulating the expression of antioxidant genes. Research shows that the activation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway can effectively inhibit the oxidative stress process in ischemia-reperfusion injury and promote the recovery of nerve function (Zhu et al. 2023). In addition, Nrf2 is closely related to mitochondrial function, and its regulation can affect the bioenergetics and biosynthesis processes of mitochondria, thus playing a protective role in ischemic brain injury (Ryoo and Kwak 2018). Nrf2 reduces the damage of oxidative stress to neurons by activating antioxidant genes such as heme oxygenase 1 (HO-1) and NADPH quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), thereby providing neuroprotection(He et al. 2020). In addition, Nrf2 further protects brain tissue from ischemia-reperfusion injury by regulating the inflammatory response and maintaining mitochondrial function (Wang et al. 2022).

Research shows that microRNAs (miRNAs) can regulate the expression of Nrf2 by directly targeting Nrf2 mRNA, thus affecting the antioxidant capacity and stress response of cells (Yi et al. 2019; Yang et al. 2011). The research of Huang et al. shows that miR-101 enhances the sensitivity of breast cancer cells to oxidative stress by inhibiting the expression of Nrf2, indicating that miRNAs play an important role in regulating the Nrf2 signaling pathway (Yi et al. 2019). In addition, miR-28 has also been found to regulate the expression of Nrf2 through a Keap1-independent mechanism, which further supports the diversity of miRNAs in the regulation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway (Yang et al. 2011). Moreover, miRNAs can affect the oxidative stress response and apoptosis process of nerve cells by regulating the Nrf2 signaling pathway. For example, miR-133a-3p alleviates oxidative stress-induced trophoblast cell apoptosis through the BACH1/Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway, providing a new perspective for understanding the role of miRNAs in neuroprotection (Guo et al. 2021). In summary, miRNAs and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway play important roles in regulating oxidative stress and neuroprotection.

miR-941 is a human-specific miRNA that is highly expressed in the brain and other human cells. The relationship between miR-941 and Keap1 was analyzed using TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/). The results showed that miR-941 can interact with the 3’-UTR (sites 276–283) of Keap1, affecting the expression of Keap1. In addition, research shows that miR-941, as a specific Keap1-targeting miRNA, can regulate the Keap1-Nrf2 cascade in human endometrial cells, causing a conformational change in Keap1. After dissociation, Nrf2 binds to AREs, inhibiting the oxidative reaction (Shi et al. 2019; Tavakkoli et al. 2019). Research suggests that miR-941 may be an important molecule affecting ischemic stroke events, but its in-depth mechanism remains unclear (Cuadrado et al. 2019). Therefore, this study analyzed the correlation between the expression levels of miR-941 in the peripheral blood of elderly IS patients and proteins related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway and the development of IS. At the same time, the predictive value of miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway in the prognosis of IS was analyzed to provide new targets and strategies for clinical treatment.

Materials and Methods

Research Subjects

A total of 102 elderly IS patients admitted to Jing’an District Shibei hospital from July 2022 to June 2023 were collected, and the ages ranged from 61 to 86 years old. All patients were clinically diagnosed and confirmed as IS by imaging examinations. According to the disease course and imaging results of IS, the patients were divided into four groups: chronic phase, subacute phase, acute phase, and hyperacute phase. Written informed consent for participation and publication were signed from the patients.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age ≥ 60 years old; (2) Meeting the diagnostic criteria for IS; (3) Imaging conclusions supporting the basis of IS; (4) The patient or their family signed the informed consent form.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Complicated with other endocrine-related diseases (diseases of organs such as the adrenal gland, gonad, and parathyroid gland), malignant tumors, rheumatic immune diseases, etc.; (2) Complicated with severe liver and kidney insufficiency; (3) Mental illness or alcoholism; (4) Severe immunodeficiency; (5) Incomplete clinical data.

Collection of Basic Clinical Data

Clinical indicators were collected within 24 h after the diagnosis of ischemic stroke, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, drinking history, coronary heart disease history, and hypertension history. The degree of neurological deficit was evaluated using the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS). The NIHSS includes 11 items, with a total score of 42 points. The higher the score, the more severe the neurological deficit of the patient.

RT-PCR Detection

Peripheral blood of the patients was collected, an appropriate amount of Trizol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. 15596-026) was added, and the sample was placed on ice for 15 min. Then, 200 µL of chloroform (Sigma-Aldrich, No. C2432) was added to the sample, shaken for 10 s, inverted and mixed well, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 15 min. The upper aqueous phase was taken, an equal volume of isopropanol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. 32793) was added, and the mixture was shaken and inverted to mix well. After standing at room temperature for 10 min, it was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 min. The supernatant was discarded, the precipitate at the bottom of the tube was retained, 1 ml of ice-cold 75% ethanol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. 44573) was added, and the sample was centrifuged at 8,000 rpm and 4 °C for 10 min. The supernatant was discarded, and 20 µL of DEPC water was added to dissolve the RNA. RT-PCR was performed according to the instructions of the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser reverse transcription kit (Takara, No. RR047A). The relative expression of the target gene mRNA was calculated by the 2-△△CT method with using GAPDH as a normalization control.

ELISA Detection

The expression levels of HO-1 (Abcam, No. ab229429), NQO-1 (LMAI Bio, No. LM8K3608H), SOD (Abcam, No. ab309312), and GSH-Px1 (Abcam, No. ab315322) in the peripheral blood of the patients were detected by ELISA kits. Briefly, standards, plasma samples to be tested, and blank controls (standard diluent) were added to pre-coated 96-well plates, 100 µL per well. According to the kit instructions, 50 µL of the detection antibody was added to each well and mixed gently. The plate wells were covered with a sealing film and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. After the incubation, the liquid in the wells was discarded, 300 µL of washing buffer was added to each well, allowed to stand for 30 s, and then discarded. This washing step was repeated 3 times. Then, 100 µL of horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled secondary antibody was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The above washing steps were repeated 3 times. Subsequently, 100 µL of the substrate solution (TMB) was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C in the dark for 15 min. Finally, 50 µL of the stop solution was added to terminate the reaction. The absorbance (OD value) at a wavelength of 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader. A standard curve was drawn based on the standard concentration and OD value, and the concentrations of HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 in the samples to be tested were calculated. All measures were tested in 3 biological replicates to calculate the mean.

Follow-Up Visit

The prognosis of the patients was evaluated according to the modified Rankin Scale (mRS), and the IS group patients were divided into a good-prognosis group (mRS ≤ 2 points) and a poor-prognosis group (mRS > 2 points). After discharge, the patients were regularly followed up by phone calls or outpatient reexaminations to collect the recurrence situation of IS patients. The follow-up end time was June 30, 2024, and each patient was followed up for at least 1 year. The diagnostic criteria for IS recurrence: IS was clearly diagnosed at the first onset. On the basis of the stable or improved symptoms and signs of the first ischemic stroke, new neurological deficit symptoms and signs appeared, and new ischemic lesions were confirmed by imaging examinations such as cranial CT or magnetic resonance imaging. The main outcome event was the recurrence of IS within 1 year of follow-up for IS patients. If a patient had an IS recurrence during the follow-up period, it was regarded as the occurrence of the observation endpoint, and the recurrence survival period of the patient was recorded.

Statistical Methods

SPSS 27.0 software was used for data analysis. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s), and non-normally distributed measurement data were expressed as median (interquartile range). One-way analysis and Tukey’s post hoc multiple comparison method were used to analyze the differences between groups. Count data were expressed as frequency (percentage), and the chi-square test was used to analyze the differences between groups. Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation between miR-941, factors of the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and the NIHSS score. Binary logistic regression analysis was used to analyze the influencing factors of the prognosis of IS patients. The ROC curve was used to analyze the predictive value of miR-941 and factors of the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway in the prognosis of IS. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis method was used to analyze the relationship between miR-941, factors of the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and the recurrence survival period of IS patients. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Basic Clinical Data of Patients with Different Stages of Ischemic Stroke

A total of 102 elderly IS patients were included in this study, with their ages ranging from 61 to 86 years old. According to the IS progression and imaging findings, the IS patients were divided into the chronic phase (19 cases), subacute phase (23 cases), acute phase (30 cases), and hyperacute phase (30 cases). The analysis results of the basic clinical data of patients in different IS stages are presented in Table 1. In terms of age distribution, the median age of each group ranged from 70 to 78 years old, and there was no significant difference among the groups (P > 0.05). The gender ratio was also relatively balanced across the groups, with the male proportion ranging from 52.6% to 56.7%, and no significant difference was observed among the groups (P > 0.05). Additionally, there were no significant differences in BMI levels, the proportions of smoking and drinking histories, as well as the prevalence rates of coronary heart disease and hypertension among the groups (all P > 0.05). These results indicate that there are no significant differences in the basic clinical data of IS patients in different stages, suggesting good comparability. The neurological function of IS patients was evaluated using the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score. There was a significant difference in NIHSS scores among the groups (P < 0.001). Specifically, patients in the hyperacute phase had the highest NIHSS scores. The NIHSS scores of patients in the hyperacute and acute phases were significantly higher than those in the chronic and subacute phases (all P < 0.001), indicating that patients in the acute and hyperacute phases had more severe neurological deficits. Moreover, there was no significant difference in NIHSS scores between patients in the subacute phase and the chronic phase (P > 0.05).

Cytokine Analysis of Patients with Different Stages of Ischemic Stroke

Through qRT-PCR and ELISA analysis, we detected the expression levels of miR-941 in the peripheral blood of IS patients and the key factors of the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway. As shown in Table 2, the relative abundance of miR-941 gradually decreased in the chronic, subacute, acute, and hyperacute phases, and the difference among the groups was statistically significant (P < 0.001). The expression of miR-941 in patients in the acute and hyperacute phases was significantly lower than that in the chronic and subacute phases (both P < 0.01). The relative abundance of Nrf2 mRNA also showed a similar downward trend. The expression of Nrf2 mRNA in patients in the acute and hyperacute phases was significantly lower than that in the chronic and subacute phases (all P < 0.05). Conversely, the relative abundance of Keap1 mRNA gradually increased among the groups. The expression of Keap1 mRNA in patients in the acute and hyperacute phases was significantly higher than that in the chronic and subacute phases (both P < 0.001). In addition, the expression levels of antioxidant-related factors HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 all showed a significant downward trend among the groups (all P < 0.001), and the expression levels in patients in the acute and hyperacute phases were significantly lower than those in the chronic and subacute phases (all P < 0.01). These results indicate that with the progression of the ischemic stroke stage, the expression levels of miR-941 and factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway change significantly, suggesting that this pathway may play an important role in the pathological process of ischemic stroke.

Correlation Analysis between miR-941, Factors Related to the Nrf2/Keap1 Signaling Pathway, and NIHSS Score

To further explore the relationship between miR-941, factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and the severity of neurological deficits in IS patients, we analyzed the correlations between these indicators and the NIHSS score (Fig. 1). The results of Spearman correlation analysis showed that the relative abundance of miR-941 was significantly negatively correlated with the NIHSS score (r=-0.820, P < 0.001), indicating that the lower the abundance of miR-941, the more severe the neurological deficits of the patients. Similarly, the relative abundance of Nrf2 mRNA was also significantly negatively correlated with the NIHSS score (r=-0.791, P < 0.001), while the relative abundance of Keap1 mRNA was significantly positively correlated with the NIHSS score (r = 0.618, P < 0.001). These results suggest that miR-941 may alleviate oxidative stress by activating the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, thereby improving the neurological function prognosis of stroke patients. In addition, the expression levels of antioxidant-related factors HO-1 (r=-0.789, P < 0.001), NQO-1 (r=-0.756, P < 0.001), SOD (r=-0.731, P < 0.001), and GSH-Px1 (r=-0.777, P < 0.001) were all significantly negatively correlated with the NIHSS score. These results indicate that the expression levels of miR-941 and factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway are closely related to the degree of neurological deficits in ischemic stroke patients, suggesting that this pathway may play an important role in regulating post-stroke nerve injury.

Analysis of Influencing Factors on the Prognosis of Elderly Ischemic Stroke

To investigate the impact of miR-941 and factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway on the prognosis of IS patients, we divided the patients into a good-prognosis group (mRS ≤ 2 points) and a poor-prognosis group (mRS > 2 points) according to the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) and performed logistic regression analysis (Table 3). The results showed that the relative abundances of miR-941 (OR = 0.001, P < 0.001) and Nrf2 mRNA (OR = 0.001, P < 0.001) were significantly negatively correlated with the risk of poor prognosis, indicating that the higher the relative abundances of miR-941 and Nrf2 mRNA, the greater the likelihood of a good prognosis for patients. Conversely, the relative abundance of Keap1 mRNA was significantly positively correlated with the risk of poor prognosis (OR = 10.223, P < 0.01), suggesting that the higher the relative abundance of Keap1 mRNA, the higher the risk of a poor prognosis for patients. In addition, the expression levels of antioxidant-related factors HO-1 (OR = 0.861, P < 0.001), NQO-1 (OR = 0.915, P < 0.01), SOD (OR = 0.934, P < 0.001), and GSH-Px1 (OR = 0.938, P < 0.001) were all significantly negatively correlated with the risk of poor prognosis, indicating that these factors are protective factors for a good prognosis in IS patients. In conclusion, the expression levels of miR-941 and factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway are influencing factors on the prognosis of IS patients and may provide potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for clinical prognosis assessment.

Predictive Value of miR-941 and Factors Related to the Nrf2/Keap1 Signaling Pathway for the Prognosis of Elderly Ischemic Stroke Patients

To evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of miR-941 and factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway for the prognosis of IS patients, we analyzed the area under the curve (AUC), optimal cut-off value (Cut-off), sensitivity, and specificity of each indicator via the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. As depicted in Fig. 2; Table 4, miR-941 exhibited the highest AUC of 0.801, an optimal cut-off value of 0.705, with sensitivity and specificity reaching 75.8% and 77.8% respectively, suggesting that miR-941 has a favorable diagnostic value for prognosis. Additionally, the AUC of Nrf2 mRNA was 0.777 (95%CI: 0.682–0.871), the optimal cut-off value was 0.665, and its sensitivity and specificity were 78.8% and 63.9% respectively. The AUC of Keap1 mRNA was 0.726, the optimal cut-off value was 1.655, and its sensitivity and specificity were 71.2% and 72.2% respectively. Moreover, antioxidant-related factors HO-1 (AUC = 0.771), NQO-1 (AUC = 0.713), SOD (AUC = 0.725), and GSH-Px1 (AUC = 0.761) all demonstrated significant diagnostic efficacy (P < 0.001). Among them, HO-1 had the highest sensitivity (89.4%), while SOD had the highest specificity (86.1%). In conclusion, miR-941 and the factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway possess high diagnostic value and moderate diagnostic value in the prognosis of elderly ischemic stroke patients, respectively, and may serve as potential biomarkers for clinical prognosis assessment.

Association between miR-941, the Nrf2/Keap1 Signaling Pathway, and the Prognostic Survival Period of Elderly Ischemic Stroke Patients

To explore the relationship between miR-941, factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and the survival period of elderly ischemic stroke patients, we evaluated the survival period of patients after discharge using the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis method. Based on the optimal cut-off values determined by the ROC curve, miR-941, Nrf2 mRNA, Keap1 mRNA, HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 were divided into high-expression groups and low-expression groups, and the differences in survival periods among the groups were compared (Fig. 3). The results showed that the survival periods of patients in the high-expression groups of miR-941 and Nrf2 were significantly longer than those in the low-expression groups (both P < 0.001). The probabilities of a good prognosis in the high-expression groups of miR-941 (HR = 7.531) and Nrf2 (HR = 5.273) increased significantly by 6.531 times and 4.273 times respectively. Conversely, the survival period of patients in the high-expression group of Keap1 mRNA was significantly shorter than that in the low-expression group (P < 0.001), indicating that high expression of Keap1 mRNA is associated with a poor prognosis in patients. The probability of a good prognosis in the low-expression group of Keap1 (HR = 4.733) increased significantly by 3.733 times. In addition, the survival periods of patients in the high-expression groups of antioxidant-related factors HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 were all significantly longer than those in the low-expression groups (Log-rank P < 0.001). These results indicate that the expression levels of miR-941 and factors related to the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway are closely associated with the survival period of elderly ischemic stroke patients. High expression of miR-941, Nrf2 mRNA, HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 may play a protective role in improving the prognosis of patients, while high expression of Keap1 mRNA may increase the recurrence risk of patients.

Discussion

The incidence of stroke in the elderly is high, and the prognosis is poor. More than 80% of elderly stroke patients have functional disabilities, which seriously affect the quality of life of patients (Koutsaliaris et al. 2022). It is of great clinical significance to dynamically monitor the prognosis of elderly stroke patients through clinical biochemical indicators and perform corresponding interventions in a timely manner. The Nrf2-Keap1 axis plays a protective role in the occurrence and development of post-stroke functional disabilities and poor prognosis, which are mainly caused by oxidative stress and inflammation (Wang et al. 2022). In addition, as a specific Keap1-targeting miRNA, miR-941 can bind to the Keap1 gene to affect its expression, causing a conformational change in Keap1. After dissociation, Nrf2 binds to AREs, promoting the expression of downstream antioxidant stress proteins (Li et al. 2020). In this study, in combination with the actual clinical situation of our department, the admitted patients were divided into hyperacute, acute, subacute, and chronic phases according to the onset time and imaging examination results. Peripheral blood samples were collected from patients at different stages to clarify the correlation between miR-941 and the development of ischemic cerebral infarction, and to observe the correlation between miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway. At the same time, the ELISA method was used to explore the expression levels of Nrf2 and its downstream antioxidant proteins HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1, and to analyze the predictive value of miR-941 in the prognosis of ischemic stroke, providing a basis for predicting the prognosis of elderly cerebral infarction patients.

This study analyzed 102 elderly ischemic stroke (IS) patients at different stages and found no significant differences in basic clinical data such as age, gender, BMI, smoking history, drinking history, and the prevalence of coronary heart disease and hypertension among patients at different stages (P > 0.05), indicating good comparability among groups. The NIHSS score of IS patients in the hyperacute phase is usually the highest, which is related to the severity of the disease at this stage(Chung et al. 2018). Previous studies have shown that blood pressure variability in the hyperacute phase is associated with poor outcomes, which may be because the patient’s condition is more unstable in the hyperacute phase, resulting in a higher NIHSS score (Wouters et al. 2018). In addition, the study by Wouters et al. showed that changes in the NIHSS score in the hyperacute phase can be used as an indicator to predict the long-term functional outcome of patients, which further illustrates the importance of the NIHSS score in the hyperacute phase (Naess et al. 2016). In the acute phase, the NIHSS score of patients is also significantly higher than that of patients in the chronic and subacute phases. This may be because the impact of IS is still relatively obvious in the acute phase, resulting in more severe neurological deficits in patients. Research shows that the NIHSS score in the acute phase is closely related to the arterial occlusion status of patients. The higher the score, the greater the possibility of arterial occlusion (Olavarría et al. 2011). In contrast, the NIHSS scores of patients in the subacute and chronic phases are lower, and there is no significant difference between them. This may be because the patient’s condition is relatively stable in the subacute and chronic phases, and the neurological deficits have improved (Artzi et al. 2014). In the subacute phase, although the patient’s metabolic state may still be recovering, the overall improvement in neurological function makes the NIHSS score tend to be stable (Wada et al. 2017). The changes in the NIHSS score at different stages reflect the severity of the patient’s condition and the recovery situation. The higher scores in the hyperacute and acute phases indicate a more severe condition, while the lower scores in the subacute and chronic phases show a gradual stabilization and improvement of the condition (Liu et al. 2020b; Naess et al. 2019). In contrast to previous studies, this study is the first to correlate the miR-941-Keap1/Nrf2 axis with the clinical stage of IS in old age, confirming its central role in human stroke pathology. In addition, previous studies focused on the static role of Keap1/Nrf2 in oxidative stress. In this study, we dynamically showed that the earlier the stage, the stronger the oxidative stress, the lower the expression of miR-941 and Nrf2, and the higher the expression of Keap1.

Nrf2 is a key transcription factor for cellular antioxidant defense, and its activity is regulated by Keap1. An increase in Keap1 will inhibit the activity of Nrf2, leading to a decrease in the expression of antioxidant genes (Stewart et al. 2011). Research shows that the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway plays an important protective role in many diseases. For example, in cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury, the activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway can reduce oxidative stress and inflammatory response, thereby protecting brain tissue (Hao et al. 2023). In addition, the protective role of Nrf2 in cardiovascular diseases has also been widely studied. It reduces cardiovascular damage by regulating oxidative stress and inflammatory response (Zhang et al. 2020). Therefore, the protective role of the Nrf2/Keap1 pathway in many diseases indicates that regulating the activity of this pathway may provide new strategies for the treatment of related diseases. However, the mechanism of the upregulation of Keap1 and the decrease in Nrf2 activity in diseases such as IS still needs further study to develop more effective treatment methods. In this study, the expression levels of miR-941, Nrf2, HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 in IS patients in the chronic, subacute, acute, and hyperacute phases gradually decreased, while the expression level of Keap1 gradually increased. This phenomenon is closely related to the imbalance of the oxidative stress and antioxidant defense mechanisms, suggesting that with the progression of the IS disease course, the antioxidant defense ability of the body gradually weakens, and the oxidative stress response may be aggravated. The dynamic change rules of miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 pathway in different stages of IS suggest that the imbalance of the antioxidant defense system may play an important role in the progression of the IS disease course. Future studies can further explore enhancing antioxidant capacity by regulating miR-941 or the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, thereby providing new targets for the treatment of IS.

miR-941, Nrf2, and downstream antioxidant factors are all significantly positively correlated with the NIHSS score. These molecules may play important roles in neuroprotection and antioxidant stress responses. Logistic regression analysis shows that the expression levels of miR-941, Nrf2, and its downstream target genes (HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1) are negatively correlated with the risk of poor prognosis in IS The negative correlation with the risk of poor prognosis in IS may be related to the roles of these molecules in antioxidant and cytoprotective mechanisms. Nrf2 is a key transcription factor that can regulate the expression of multiple antioxidant enzymes, such as HO-1 and NQO-1. These enzymes play important roles in reducing oxidative stress and protecting nerve cells (Yao et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2020a). In addition, as important antioxidant enzymes, SOD and GSH-Px1 can scavenge free radicals in the body and reduce cell damage, thus showing a positive correlation with the NIHSS score (Zhang et al. 2019; Ren et al. 2011). On the other hand, the significant negative correlation between Keap1 and the NIHSS score may be related to its role as an inhibitor of Nrf2. Keap1 binds to Nrf2 and promotes its degradation, thereby inhibiting the activity of Nrf2. Under oxidative stress conditions, the inhibitory effect of Keap1 weakens, Nrf2 is activated and translocated to the nucleus, and initiates the expression of antioxidant genes, thus reducing nerve damage (Lu et al. 2020). Therefore, the negative correlation between Keap1 and the NIHSS score may reflect its inhibitory role in neuroprotection. This also explains why Keap1 is positively correlated with the risk of poor prognosis in elderly IS.

In view of the correlation between miR-941, the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and the prognosis of IS, we further analyzed their predictive value in the prognosis of elderly IS patients and their impact on the prognostic survival period of these patients. Firstly, the expression levels of miR-941 exhibit favorable predictive efficacy in the prognosis of elderly IS patients. Moreover, the survival periods of patients in the high-expression groups of miR-941, Nrf2, HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1 are significantly prolonged, suggesting that these molecules may alleviate the damage of oxidative stress to nerve cells by enhancing antioxidant capacity, thereby improving the prognosis. As a core regulatory factor of the antioxidant response, a decrease in the activity of Nrf2 leads to a reduction in the expression of antioxidant enzymes such as HO-1, NQO-1, SOD, and GSH-Px1, consequently weakening the cell’s resistance to oxidative stress (Reszka et al. 2013). On the other hand, the survival period of patients in the high-expression group of Keap1 is significantly shortened, further supporting the crucial role of the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway in the prognosis of IS. Keap1 is the main negative regulator of Nrf2, and an increase in its expression promotes the ubiquitination and degradation of Nrf2, thus inhibiting the antioxidant response. This is likely because Keap1 binds to and inhibits the activity of Nrf2, preventing it from entering the nucleus and initiating the expression of antioxidant genes, thereby weakening the cell’s antioxidant defense ability (Akdemir et al. 2017; Seo et al. 2020) As a regulator of the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, miR-941 plays an important role in antioxidant stress and neuroprotection, thus prolonging the survival period of elderly IS patients. In summary, the correlation between miR-941, the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score reveals the possible potential mechanisms of these molecules in neuroprotection and antioxidant stress responses after stroke. In addition, the available literature does not clarify the translational value of miR-941 in IS treatment. In this study, we propose miR-941 as a biomarker for prognosis prediction in elderly IS for the first time, and propose a new target for clinical intervention in elderly IS.

Although this study has revealed the impacts of miR-941, the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, and its downstream antioxidant molecules on the prognosis of elderly IS patients, there are still some limitations that need to be improved and verified in future research. Firstly, the sample size of this study is relatively small (n = 102), and all patients are from a single center, which may lead to selection bias. A small sample size may limit the statistical power of the results, especially the reliability of subgroup analyses. Moreover, the results of a single-center study may not be fully generalized to other populations or regions. Future studies should increase the sample size and adopt a multi-center design to enhance the representativeness and universality of the results. Secondly, this study did not fully adjust for potential confounding factors. For example, factors such as patients’ drug treatments (such as antioxidants or neuroprotective agents), rehabilitation therapies, and lifestyles may have a significant impact on the prognosis, but these factors were not comprehensively controlled in this study. Future research should incorporate more clinical variables and further clarify the independent predictive value of miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway through multi-factor analysis. In addition, the research on the molecular mechanisms in this study is relatively limited. Although the expression levels of miR-941, Nrf2, Keap1, and their downstream molecules are significantly correlated with the prognosis of IS, their specific mechanisms of action have not been fully elucidated. For instance, whether miR-941 affects the prognosis of IS through other signaling pathways (such as inflammatory or apoptotic pathways) still needs to be further explored through in vitro and in vivo experiments. Finally, the follow-up period of this study is relatively short, which may not allow for a comprehensive assessment of the impacts of these molecules on the long-term prognosis of IS patients. Future studies should extend the follow-up time and include more endpoint events (such as recurrence rate, degree of functional recovery, etc.) to more comprehensively evaluate the clinical value of miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway.

In conclusion, this study has revealed the important roles of miR-941 and the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway in the disease progression and prognosis of IS, providing a new theoretical basis for the early diagnosis, risk stratification, and individualized treatment of IS. Future research should further verify the clinical application value of these molecules in larger samples and explore their potential as therapeutic targets, with the aim of providing more effective intervention measures for IS patients.

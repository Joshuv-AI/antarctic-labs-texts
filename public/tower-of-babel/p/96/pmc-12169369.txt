Acute myocardial infarction patients without COVID-19 manifestations in the pandemic may have high thrombus burden
Authors: Héctor González-Pacheco, Rodrigo Gopar-Nieto, Daniel Manzur-Sandoval, Marco A. Ayala-Navarrete, Guering Eid-Lidt, José L. Briseño-De-la-Cruz, Alfredo Altamirano-Castillo, Salvador Mendoza-García, Diego Araiza-Garaygordobil, Daniel Sierra-Lara-Martínez, Alexandra Arias-Mendoza
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and Objective:The objective of the study is to identify clinical and angiographic characteristics of patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) but without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 infection during the pandemic, compared with patients 1 year before the pandemic.Methods:Observational study that included 138 consecutive patients hospitalized with STEMI who underwent primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) without COVID-19 infection during the 2020 pandemic. A group of 175 STEMI patients treated with PCI in the year before the pandemic served as the control group.Results:During the periods analyzed, compared with the control group, patients admitted during the pandemic without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 did not have significant differences in demographic characteristics, comorbidities, or delayed time and location of the acute myocardial infarction. Furthermore, there were no differences between the two groups concerning levels of CK-MB and NT-proBNP, or in inflammation markers and left ventricular ejection fraction. In patients without COVID-19 during the pandemic compared with control, we found a higher intracoronary thrombus burden (thrombus grade 5; 78.3% vs. 62.9%, respectively. p = 0.002). Accordingly, the use of glycoprotein IIB/IIIa inhibitors (37.7% vs. 26.3%, p = 0.03) was higher in these patients.Conclusions:This study demonstrates an increased thrombus burden in STEMI patients without clinical manifestation of COVID-19 during the pandemic compared with the same time period in the previous year.

Introduction

The outbreak of infections with severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), which causes coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), began in Wuhan, the capital of Hubei Province, China, but spread rapidly to many other countries1. During the pandemic, a decrease in the number of patients attending hospitals with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) was reported in Europe and the US2,3. Moreover, patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) with COVID-19 infection have had significant variability in presentation characteristics and poor in-hospital prognosis4,5. A notable finding in patients with severe COVID-19 infection is a prothrombotic state, promoting arterial and venous thrombosis6,7. It has been reported that there is an increased burden of intracoronary thrombi in STEMI patients infected with COVID-19 compared with STEMI patients without concomitant COVID-19 during the pandemic8.

However, there is little information about the characteristics and angiographic findings of patients with STEMI without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 infection, who underwent primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) during the pandemic compared with the same period in the previous year without the pandemic.

The goal of this study was to provide clinical, angiographic, management, and outcome data on STEMI patients treated with primary PCI without clinical manifestation of COVID-19 during the pandemic period in a contemporary university hospital in Mexico City.

Materials and methods

This was a retrospective study using the database of the Coronary Care Unit (CCU) of the National Institute of Cardiology in Mexico City. We analyzed data from all patients admitted to the CCU with a diagnosis of STEMI without previous history or clinical manifestations of SARS-CoV-2 infection (fever or respiratory symptoms such as cough, shortness of breath, sore throat) who underwent primary PCI during the period of the pandemic from March 23 to December 31, 2020, and compared their baseline characteristics, angiographic findings, management, and clinical outcomes with patients with STEMI who underwent PCI between March 23 and December 31, 2019. The information included demographic characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors, clinical characteristics upon admission, angiographic findings, procedures, and hospital outcomes. We analyzed all patients who met the criteria for STEMI and who underwent primary PCI. Patients without respiratory symptoms or fever underwent no determination of SARS-CoV-2.

The primary outcome was the angiographic findings including thrombus burden9 and Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) flow, 1 year before and during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. TIMI thrombus grade was classified within the following categories: (a) Grade 0: no thrombus, (b) Grade 1: possible thrombus, (c) Grade 2: definite thrombus with largest dimension < 1/2 of the vessel, (d) Grade 3: definite thrombus with > 1/2 of the vessel but < 2 vessel diameters, (e) Grade 4: definite thrombus with largest dimension > 2 vessel diameters, and (f) Grade 5: total occlusion. The secondary outcomes were all-cause in-hospital mortality.

For descriptive analyses, continuous variables were tested and confirmed to be non-normally distributed by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and were therefore presented as medians and 25th and 75th percentiles (interquartile ranges). Statistically significant differences between groups were assessed using either Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact probability tests for categorical variables, or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables. All tests were two-sided, and p ≤ 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows (v. 23; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for all analyses.

Results

During the analyzed pandemic period, 138 patients were admitted with STEMI, without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 infection who underwent primary PCI from March 23 through December 31, 2020, and were compared with 175 patients with STEMI who underwent primary PCI in the same period the previous year (March 23 through December 31, 2019).

The demographic characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors, and comorbidities of the two groups were similar (Table 1). At the presentation, patients from 2019 were more likely to have a higher Killip class. However, there were no significant differences in symptoms-to-door time, heart rate, blood pressure, localization of STEMI, and left ventricular ejection fraction (Table 1). Inflammatory markers, including blood cell count and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein levels, were similar between the two groups. Together with the levels of CK-MB and N-terminal-proB-type-natriuretic peptide, serum creatinine and blood glucose were similar.

Interestingly, the intracoronary thrombus burden was significantly higher in STEMI patients without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 during the pandemic compared with STEMI patients from the previous year (TIMI thrombus grade 5; 78.3% vs. 62.9%, p = 0.002). As a consequence, the group of patients without COVID-19 during the pandemic received glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor (37.7% vs. 26.3%, p = 0.03) and aspiration thrombectomy (10.9% vs. 7.4%, p = 0.23) more often than the control group, (Table 1 and Fig. 1) although the final TIMI flow rates were similar (TIMI 2-3 flow, 92.5% vs. 92.8%, p = 0.80). There were no significant differences in in-hospital mortality between the groups of patients (4.3% vs. 8.6%, p = 0.13).

Discussion

The main finding of this study was that STEMI patients without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 infection during the pandemic who received treatment with primary PCI had a higher burden of thrombi in the culprit artery, compared with STEMI patients from the immediately previous year without the pandemic.

Recently, Garcia et al.10 published the findings of the North American COVID-19 Myocardial Infarction (NACMI) registry. The authors pointed out that patients with STEMI and concomitant COVID-19 infection are a high-risk group with unique demographic and clinical characteristics. In addition, they also showed that STEMI patients without SARS-CoV-2 infection during the pandemic, compared to those treated in the pre-COVID-19 era (2015-2019), had a significantly worse clinical outcome (in-hospital mortality 11% vs. 4%; p < 0.01), despite the fact that both groups of patients had similar baseline characteristics, utilization of angiography and primary PCI. The authors have also suggested that this last finding requires further investigation.

Previous studies have reported that during the pandemic, AMI patients with COVID-19 infection compared with patients without concomitant COVID-19 had a higher risk profile with higher levels of serum troponin, N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide, biomarkers of inflammation, and D-dimer and a lower left ventricular ejection fraction11,12. In addition, there was a higher rate of in-hospital complications and mortality13,14. In contrast, our study shows that the baseline characteristics and outcome of patients without clinical manifestations of SARS-CoV-2 infection during the 2020 pandemic are not different from patients who presented in a non-pandemic period similar to that reported in the NACMI registry10.

In other studies, the angiographic findings of patients with STEMI who underwent primary PCI during the pandemic have been compared with a control group from when the pandemic was not yet present. These findings showed a higher frequency of thrombotic occlusions among the patients admitted during the pandemic. It is important to note that up to 27% of these patients had clinical symptoms or a positive test for COVID-19 infection15,16. Choudry et al.8 recently reported an increase in the intracoronary thrombus burden in patients with STEMI and concurrent COVID-19 infection compared with COVID-19-negative patients with STEMI during the pandemic. This observation may be related to the fact that COVID-19 infection is associated with a state of systemic inflammation along with profound hypercoagulability leading to both arterial and venous thrombosis, even without traditional risk factors17.

Our findings differ from those reported above because our study population underwent primary PCI during the pandemic without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 infection and without a greater systemic inflammatory response than the historical control group. We strongly believe that our research provides insights on STEMI patients without SARS-CoV-2 infection and may contribute to the results found in the NACMI registry since it did not report thrombus burden in coronary angiography.

The nature of our findings is not clear. While our patients were free of COVID-19 symptoms, many asymptomatic cases of COVID-19 infection have been identified through surveillance programs, which could partly explain our findings18. Our study strengthens the information reported so far on patients without clinical manifestations of COVID-19 undergoing primary PCI. However, more research needs to be undertaken to confirm the risk of increased thrombus burden in STEMI patients during the pandemic regardless of the presence or absence of SARS-CoV-2 infection.

These findings may be somewhat limited because no patient analyzed underwent a routine nasal/pharyngeal swab for the SARS-CoV-2 virus using real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction regardless of the absence of symptoms and lack of D-dimer levels determination, this has been consistently associated with an increased risk of thrombosis, mortality, and adverse clinical outcomes.

Conclusions

This study suggests there is an increased risk of a high thrombus burden in patients with STEMI, but without clinical manifestations of COVID-19, undergoing primary PCI during the pandemic. This finding emphasizes the need for serological tests in the absence of manifestations of SARS-CoV-2 infection and should alert physicians to administer optimal antithrombotic therapy in STEMI. It is clear that more studies will be needed to confirm these results.

Efficacy and Safety of Dupilumab in Eosinophilic Esophagitis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Roy Khalaf, Alexandre Ton That, Natacha Tardio, Waqqas Afif, Rilla Schneider, Moshe Ben-Shoshan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic, immune-mediated disorder of the esophagus driven by type-2 inflammation. Dupilumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the IL-4Ra subunit shared by the IL-4 and IL-13 receptor complexes, has recently emerged as a targeted biologic therapy for EoE. This systematic review evaluates the evidence for dupilumab in EoE across pediatric and adult populations, focusing on efficacy, safety, and symptom improvement.MethodsMEDLINE and Embase were searched from inception to June 30, 2025. Eligible studies included randomized controlled trials (RCTs), cohort studies, case-control studies, and case reports reporting dupilumab use in confirmed EoE. Study screening and quality assessment were performed in duplicate. Data on demographics, atopic comorbidities, dosing regimens, histologic outcomes, symptom improvement, and adverse events were extracted and summarized descriptively.ResultsNineteen studies (three randomized trials, nine cohorts, seven case reports) comprising 760 patients were included. Dupilumab was most commonly initiated following inadequate response or intolerance to proton pump inhibitors, swallowed corticosteroids, or dietary therapy. Across RCTs, histologic remission (≤15 eos/hpf) occurred in 60%–84% of dupilumab-treated patients versus <10% of placebo-treated patients. Cohort studies demonstrated comparable rates (68%–93%), with pediatric cohorts showing particularly robust responses (up to 90% remission). Meta-analysis using a random-effects logit model demonstrated a pooled histologic remission rate of 75.5% (95% CI: 67.4–82.1%) and a symptom improvement rate of 84.1% (95% CI: 77.8–88.9%).ConclusionsDupilumab represents a promising therapeutic option, particularly for patients with inadequate response to proton pump inhibitor therapy, including those unable to maintain dietary restrictions. Future research should focus on predictors of response, durability of remission beyond 1 year, and comparative effectiveness relative to other therapies.

Introduction

Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic immune-mediated inflammatory condition of the esophagus [1]. It affects approximately 1 in 1,000 children and an estimated 50–175 per 100,000 adults, with prevalence increasing over the past 2 decades [2]. Diagnosis is confirmed through upper endoscopy and esophageal biopsy, with a threshold of ≥15 eosinophils per high-power field [3]. Current first-line management strategies include proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), swallowed topical corticosteroids, and empiric food elimination diets [4]. PPIs are effective in a substantial subset of patients by reducing esophageal eosinophilia through both acid suppression and anti-inflammatory mechanisms [5]. While these approaches are effective in inducing histologic remission, dietary elimination in particular poses challenges related to long-term adherence, nutritional adequacy, and quality of life [6].

More recently, dupilumab, a monoclonal antibody that blocks interleukin-4 and interleukin-13 signaling, has emerged as a promising treatment for EoE [7]. It offers an alternative to dietary restrictions and long-term steroid use. Clinical trials have demonstrated its effectiveness in both children and adults with EoE, leading to regulatory approval in numerous countries [7–9]. In Canada, dupilumab is now approved for use in patients as young as 1 year old and weighing at least 15 kg. This systematic review aimed to evaluate the current body of evidence on the use of dupilumab in EoE, including its efficacy, safety profile, and clinical outcomes across different study designs and patient populations.

Methods

A systematic review was conducted in compliance with the guidelines from the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews [10] and the Preferred Reporting Items for a Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) [11]. The preregistered protocol can be found on PROSPERO (CRD42061294471). A completed PRISMA 2020 checklist is provided as online supplementary material (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000553602).

Search Strategy

A search of Embase and MEDLINE databases from inception to June 30th, 2025, was designed without language restrictions. The full search strategy is presented in online supplemental Tables 1–2.

Study Selection

Search results were imported into and de-duplicated by Covidence and screened by title and abstract by two independent reviewers (R.K. and A.V.). Retained articles were then read in full duplicate and assessed by inclusion and exclusion criteria. Included articles’ reference lists were hand-searched for additional articles relevant for inclusion. Disagreements throughout were resolved by consulting a third author (M.B.S.).

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Randomized controlled trials (RCTs), prospective and retrospective cohort series, case reports, and case-control studies were included. Case reports were included to provide a comprehensive overview of the emerging literature on dupilumab in EoE, particularly in uncommon clinical scenarios and pediatric populations for whom randomized evidence remains limited. Case reports were analyzed descriptively and were not included in quantitative synthesis or meta-analysis. Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, case series, viewpoints, commentaries, cadaveric studies, and abstracts that could not be traced to the full text were excluded. Studies included must have had a clear diagnosis of EoE with dupilumab.

Collected Outcomes

Data extraction was performed independently by two reviewers (R.K. and A.V.), with discrepancies resolved by a third reviewer (M.B.S.), consistent with the study selection process. Variables that were collected included male sex, age at presentation, presence of inflammatory or fibro-stenotic phenotype whenever applicable, comorbid atopic conditions (atopic dermatitis, asthma, food allergy, allergic rhinitis), pre-treatment symptoms, dupilumab dosage and administration schedule, treatment efficacy and symptom improvement. Histologic remission was extracted according to the definition used in each individual study. The most commonly reported thresholds were <15 eosinophils/hpf and <6 eosinophils/hpf as per the US Food and Drug Administration was measured in studies.

Statistical Analysis

Extracted data are summarized descriptively in the Results section below. Descriptive statistics are presented as measures of central tendency (overall means) and percentages.

Meta-Analysis

Proportions of patients achieving histologic remission and symptom improvement with dupilumab were meta-analyzed using a random-effects model (DerSimonian-Laird method) [12]. Proportions were logit-transformed with a continuity correction of 0.5 to stabilize variances and accommodate studies reporting complete or zero responses. Pooled estimates were back-transformed to proportions, and between-study heterogeneity was qualified using the I2 statistics [13]. Rows with missing outcome data were excluded, as studies reported only improvement in mean DSQ scores at times.

Study Quality

Study quality was independently assessed in duplicate (R.K. and A.V.). Given the heterogeneity of study designs, methodological quality was assessed qualitatively according to study type with particular attention to risk of bias, participant selection, outcome ascertainment, and completeness of follow-up (online suppl. Table S3).

Results

Search Results

A total of 766 studies were identified through two databases, with 103 total duplicates being removed (Fig. 1). After initial screening, 556 studies were excluded, leaving 97 studies for full-text screening. Ultimately, 19 studies were included, comprising three RCTs, nine cohort studies, and seven case reports.

Identification of studies via databases and registers.

Quality Results

The 19 included studies were assessed using quality assessment tools most appropriate for their design [14]. The three RCTs were considered high quality, with a low risk of bias due to blinded outcome assessment, allocation concealment, and appropriate randomization. Cohort studies were generally of moderate quality as they clearly reported inclusion criteria and participant characteristics but were limited by small sample sizes and incomplete follow-up reporting. Case reports carried a higher risk of bias, given the lack of control groups and selective outcome reporting.

Across all studies, the main methodological limitations were variations in outcome definitions, inconsistency, inconsistency in the timing of follow-up endoscopies, and limited data on long-term safety. Despite these issues, the evidence demonstrated a high degree of consistency in both histological and symptomatic outcomes, strengthening confidence in the conclusions of the systematic review.

Population

The pooled population cohort comprised 760 patients with EoE. Fifteen studies (606 patients) reported male prevalence of 317 (52.3%). Food allergy was reported in 336 (44.2%), allergic rhinitis in 331 (43.6%), asthma in 253 patients, and atopic dermatitis in 186 (24.5%). Throughout the studies, the presentation of age at presentation varied, with nine studies (171 patients) reporting exclusively on a pediatric population, four studies on adult population (369 patients), and six on both (220 patients).

Failure of Other Therapies

Dupilumab was initiated in most studies following inadequate response to prior therapies. The majority of patients had previously failed PPI therapy, whereas only a subset had documented prior exposure to or failure of swallowed or topical steroids. Previous dietary elimination was also reported in several cohorts (71 patients). Collectively, these findings suggest that dupilumab was most frequently used in patients with persistent disease, despite standard first-line therapies, although prior treatment exposure varied substantially across studies.

Dupilumab Dosage and Adverse Events

Dupilumab dosing varied according to study design and patient age. Adult and adolescent randomized trials most commonly employed 300 mg administered weekly, whereas pediatric studies used weight-tiered regimens ranging from 200 mg every 2 weeks to 300 mg weekly depending on body weight [9, 15]. Alternative dosing schedules, including every other week administration, were also evaluated in earlier phase studies [16]. Accordingly, no single loading strategy was uniformly used across all included studies. Adverse events were reported variably and were predominantly mild to moderate in severity. In Dellon et al. [8], the most common adverse events included injection site reactions (13.6%), transient conjunctivitis (3.7%), and mild headache (6.4%). Chehade et al. [7] similarly reported injection site reactions (18.9%) and nasopharyngitis (5.4%) as the most common adverse effects. Real-world cohort studies, including Lee et al. [17] and Ketchem et al. [18], reported few serious adverse events and minimal treatment discontinuation. A notable exception was the case report by Gagnon et al. [19], which described a suspected dupilumab-associated neuropathy in a pediatric patient with EoE. Although causality remains uncertain, this case highlights the importance of continued post-marketing surveillance and long-term safety monitoring.

Histological Remission

In the majority of studies, histological remission was measured following 12–18 weeks of treatment. A RCT by Chehade et al. [7] (pediatric, ages 1–11 years), compared three groups: high-exposure dupilumab group (300 mg SC administered weekly), lower exposure group (300 mg SC administered every 2 weeks) group, and a placebo group. At 16 weeks, the study found 25/37 (67.6%) patients in the higher exposure group to have achieved histological remission (≤15 eosinophils per high-power field), 18/31(58.1%) in the lower exposure group and 1/34 (2.9%) in the placebo group. A similar RCT was conducted by Dellon et al. [8], with 119 overall patients, histological remission was noted in 25/42 patients (60.0%) who received weekly dupilumab compared to 2/39 (5%) in placebo patients at week 45, with sustained remission for 84% at week 52 versus 8% in placebo. The Liberty Eosinophilic Esophagitis TREET RCT [20] is the largest study in this pooled analysis (N = 321). Included patients received 300 mg weekly or placebo for 24 weeks. Dupilumab increased the proportion of patients with histological remission in the treated group (64% versus 8%) and was associated with 84% remission versus 8% after 52 weeks.

Cohort studies revealed remission rates that ranged between 68% and 93%. Ketchem et al. [18] observed remission in 82% of patients using the <15 eos/hpf threshold and 68% using the ≤6 eos/hpf threshold, Lee et al. [17] in 80% of patients (≤15 eos/hpf) and 57% (≤6 eos/hpf), Spergel et al. [9] in 85% of patients (≤6 eos/hpf), and Russin et al. [21] in 93% of patients. Pediatric cohorts showed high efficacy, as reported by Hasosah et al. [15], with 90% remission in children.

Case reports, including studies by Dixit et al. [22], Klein et al. [23], Gagnon et al. [19], and Vande Velde et al. [24] described marked reductions in esophageal eosinophilia and clinical improvement following dupilumab initiation. In studies reporting “partial histologic remission”, this generally referred to a reduction in eosinophil burden without achieving the standardized remission thresholds used in randomized trials.

Symptoms Improvement

Clinical improvement was associated with histological changes. Dellon et al. [8] reported that weekly dupilumab improved the score of the Dysphagia symptom Questionnaire by −21.9 compared with −9.6 for placebo. Significant clinical improvement was reported also in cohort studies. Ketchem et al. [18] reported 87% improvement, Lee et al. [17] reported 91% (vs. 17% in controls), and Hardwick et al. [25] reported 80%. Clinical improvement was reported similarly among children. Spergel et al. [9] demonstrated a reduction of Pediatric Eosinophilic Esophagitis Symptoms Scores from 32.2 to 5.9, whereas in the Hasoah et al. [15] study, dysphagia or epigastric pain were resolved in 82% of children.

Case reports were consistent in describing complete resolution of symptoms such as dysphagia, reflux, feeding difficulties, and abdominal pain. For example, Dixit et al. [22] reported a complete resolution of dysphagia within weeks of treatment of a 14-year-old patient. Gagnon et al. [19] and Nambiar et al. [26] showed significant improvements in reflux and abdominal pain. Vande Velde et al. [24] reported resolution of vomiting and feeding difficulties.

Meta-Analysis Results (Conducted for RCT)

When analyzed using a random-effects model with logit transformation, the pooled histologic remission rate was 75.5% (95% CI: 67.4–82.1%), with moderate between-study heterogeneity (I2 = 52%) (Fig. 2). The pooled symptom improvement rate was 84.1% (95% CI: 77.8–88.9%) with negligible heterogeneity (I2 = 0%). These findings indicate that dupilumab is associated with substantial histologic and symptomatic improvement in EoE, with consistent benefit across published cohorts. No evidence of publication bias or outlier-driven influence was observed.

Forest plot of histological remission rates with dupilumab across included RCTs.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is among the largest and most comprehensive systematic reviews evaluating the efficacy and safety of dupilumab in EoE. Across RCTs, cohort studies, and case reports, dupilumab consistently demonstrated robust efficacy in inducing histological remission and improving patient-reported outcomes. Observational cohorts reported similarly high rates of remission, often exceeding 80% with pediatric populations showing particularly favorable responses. Case reports, although methodologically limited, reinforced these findings by illustrating clinical benefit in refractory cases where standard therapies had failed.

In the RCTs, histological remission occurred in 60–84% of patients, compared to less than 10% in placebo. Furthermore, Dellon et al. [8] confirmed that the efficacy was durable. Cohort studies also showed similar findings, with remission rates ranging from 68% to 93%. Remission rates were highest among children (90%). Case reports further reinforced these findings, with complete or partial histological remission along with complete resolution of symptoms, which showed that, even in refractory cases, dupilumab can control tissue-level disease.

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, while RCTs provided high-quality evidence, the majority of available data derived from observational studies with modest sample sizes and heterogeneous methodologies. Variability in outcome definitions, timing of follow-up endoscopies, and incomplete reporting of long-term safety limit the ability to directly compare across studies. Second, case reports, although valuable in highlighting efficacy in refractory disease, are inherently prone to bias and selective reporting. Third, while safety data were reassuring and generally limited to mild adverse events such as injection site reactions and transient conjunctivitis, long-term safety beyond 1 year remains incompletely characterized.

Future research should focus on several key areas. Identification of predictors of response would help optimize patient selection and guide individualized therapy. Comparative studies are also needed to determine the relative cost-effectiveness of dupilumab versus dietary interventions, corticosteroids, or emerging biologics. Finally, long-term follow-up studies are critical to assess sustained remission, durability of symptomatic benefit, and safety in both pediatric and adult populations.

In conclusion, the evidence support the efficacy of dupilumab therapy for EoE. It consistently showed high rates of histological remission and clinical outcome improvements across diverse populations. Future studies should address predictor of treatment response, longer term safety and outcome (beyond 1 year), and comparative cost-effectiveness compared to other available treatments.

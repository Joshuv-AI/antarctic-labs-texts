FERMT1 promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition of hepatocellular carcinoma by activating EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways
Authors: Wubin Guo, Mengnan Liu, Wei Luo, Jing Peng, Fei Liu, Xin Ma, Li Wang, Sijin Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•FERMT1 was highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).•Silencing FERMT1 inhibited the proliferation, invasion, migration, and EMT of HCC.•Silencing FERMT1 inhibited the activation of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathway.•There was a targeting relationship between FERMT1 and EGFR.Keywords: FERMT1, Epithelial mesenchymal transition, Hepatocellular carcinoma, EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 90 % of primary liver cancer [1], and is the 6th most common malignancy and 4th most lethal worldwide, with over 840,000 new cases each year, 50 % of which occur in China [2]. HCC is often diagnosed at an advanced stage due to delayed or indistinguishable clinical signs and symptoms [3]. Since 2007, sorafenib, a multikinase inhibitor (TKI) with anti-angiogenic and anti-proliferative properties, has been considered a first-line HCC target therapy [4]. However, the anti-cancer efficacy of sorafenib remains unsatisfactory, extending overall survival by only about 3 months compared to placebo [5]. Another common problem with sorafenib is the susceptibility of HCC cells to acquired resistance, which usually develops within 6 months in patients initially sensitive to sorafenib [6] and eventually leads to death as the limited-stage tumor progresses to an invasive or metastatic stage. Invasion and metastasis of the primary tumor are the main cause of death in the vast majority of HCC patients [7]. The process of HCC infiltration and metastasis is very complex, involving multiple molecular interactions and cross-regulation of multiple signal transduction pathways at multiple levels [8]. Studying the mechanism of HCC invasion and metastasis is very important to improve the clinical treatment effect and improve the survival rate.

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a key early event that induces cancer infiltration and metastasis, according to a growing number of studies [9]. After EMT, malignant tumor cells acquire a metastatic or fibroblast phenotype. During this transition, tumor cells of the epithelial phenotype lose the expression of epithelial cell markers such as E-cadherin and gain the expression of mesenchymal cell markers such as vimentin, fibronectin, and N-cadherin, which are closely related to tumor infiltration, metastasis, and progression [10,11]. EMT has been shown to be the predominant mode of intrinsic gene expression in human tumor cells and has been the focus of research on cancer pathogenesis [12]. The study of the mechanism of HCC metastasis and EMT will help to find new therapeutic targets.

Kindlins are a group of integrin-interacting proteins that activate integrins by interacting with the intracellular segment of the integrin β subunit [13]. The fermitin family member 1 (FERMT1) gene, located at chromosome 20p12.3 [14], encodes the fermitin family homolog. Kindlin1, a member of the FERMT family, which is involved in epidermal keratinocyte polarization, proliferation, and death [15]. Studies have shown that FERMT1 is involved in the regulation of EMT in colon cancer [16], oral squamous cell carcinoma [17], and nasopharyngeal carcinoma [18]. Clinical studies have also shown that Kindlin-1 (FERMT1 encoding protein) plays an important role in HCC and may be a promising and potential target for HCC treatment [19]. However, whether FERMT1 promotes HCC metastasis through the regulation of EMT and its specific regulatory mechanism remains unclear. This experiment aimed to investigate the specific role and the possible molecular mechanisms of FERMT1 in the development of HCC.

Materials and methods

Clinical sample collection and immunohistochemistry

In this study, 6 pairs of HCC tissues and corresponding para-cancerous tissues were collected from patients with HCC diagnosed at the Affiliated Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital of Southwest Medical University from January 2021 to December 2022. Normal para-cancerous tissues from the same patients served as a control group. The trial was approved and agreed by the Affiliated Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital of Southwest Medical University. The samples were taken and then fully fixed by 4 % paraformaldehyde, routinely dehydrated, and paraffin-embedded to make sections. Immunohistochemistry was used for staining. Use the primary antibody (FERMT1, BS-6413R, Booysen, Beijing, 1:100) to incubate at 4 °C overnight. HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG (GB23303, Servicebio, Wuhan, 1:100) was used as a secondary antibody for 30 min. DAB was used for color development and 5 fields of view were randomly selected for each section and the proportion of positive cells was calculated for each image using the Halo Data Analysis System. The expression levels of FERMT1 in the tissues were determined according to the final results.

Quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells or tissues using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Thermo Scientific, USA). 5 μg of total RNA was taken and cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription reaction. cDNA, SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA), and gene-specific primers for FERMT1 were used for qRT-PCR. FERMT1 primers were synthesized by Biotech (Shanghai, China), and forward sequences: 5′-GCTTCTGAAAACCCACTGGA-3′, reverse sequences 5′-ATCTTCAAATTCGGCAGACG-3′. And GAPDH was used as the loading control, the forward sequences: 5′-TGACTTCAACAGCGACACCCA-3′, reverse sequences 5′-CACCCTGTTGCTGTAGCCAAA-3′. Reaction conditions: 30 s at 95 °C, 5 s at 95 °C, 30 s at 55 °C, for 45 cycles, and 30 s at 72 °C. After the reaction was completed, the relative expression of each target gene was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Cell culture

Human normal liver cells were extracted from normal liver tissue, and hepatoma Hep3B, Huh-7, SNU182, and SNU387 cells were purchased from ATCC (USA). All cells were cultured in DMEM cell medium containing 8 %−10 % fetal bovine serum (Hy-clone USA, item no 10,099–141) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin double antibody (Hy-clone USA, item no AE20005267) at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 cell culture incubator. After counting the cells in the exponential phase, the cells were seeded on well plates at the required density.

Cell transfection

Small interfering RNA-negative control (siRNA-NC) and two siRNA targeting FERMT1 (siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2#) synthesized by Shanghai Genome Pharmaceuticals were transfected into HCC cells using RiboFect CP Transfection Kit (C10511–05, RiboBio, China). siRNA-FERMT1 (75 nmol/well) or siRNA-NC (75 nmol/well) and transfection reagent (7.5 μL/well) were each diluted in 100 μL of serum-free Opti-MEM for 5 min. The diluted transfection reagent and siRNA were then mixed and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The transfection mixture was added to the cell culture medium and mixed. After 6 h of transfection, the cell culture medium was replaced with fresh DMEM containing 10 % FBS and incubation was continued for 48 h.

CCK-8 assay

Cell viability was assessed according to the instructions of the cell CCK-8 kit (Beyotime Technology, Jiangsu, China). Transfected HCC cells were inoculated into 96-well plates and incubated in an incubator at 37 °C for 24 h. Before the end of incubation, 10 μL CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubation was continued for 1 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (SpectraMAX Plus384, American).

Wound healing test

The transfected cells were inoculated in 6-well plates and incubated in a constant-temperature cell incubator. Using a 10 μL sterile tip, lightly scratch a straight line of uniform thickness on the bottom of the 6-well plate. Observe the width of the scratch in the same field under an inverted microscope at 0 and 24 h respectively and take photographs.

Transwell experiment

Cells with good growth state were starved for 24 h to synchronize the cell cycle and reduce the influence of proliferation on the experimental results. The cells were then digested with trypsin and the cell concentration was adjusted to 1 × 104 cells/mL using a serum-free cell culture medium. Prior to the experiment, matrix gels were taken and added to Transwell chambers and incubated for 3 h at 37 °C for wetting. After the matrix gel has solidified, the incubation is continued by adding a serum-free cell culture medium to the chambers. An appropriate amount of cell suspension is added to Transwell chambers in a 24-well plate, with cell culture solution added to the outside of the chambers. After incubation at 37 °C for 24 h, the Transwell was washed with PBS. 4 % paraformaldehyde was fixed and stained with crystal violet for 30 min. Six visual fields were randomly selected from each hole, and the number of invasive cells was observed by microscope (Olympius, Japan).

Immunofluorescence experiments

The cell crawl sections were stained. The sections were immersed in 5 % film breaker for 10 min at room temperature. After PBS cleaning, add goat serum sealing solution, and seal for 20 min at room temperature. Primary antibodies (E-cadherin, GB11082, Servicebio; N-cadherin, GB111273, Servicebio; EGFR, bsm-10,695 M, Bioss; FERMT1, bs-17063R, Bioss; China) were added and incubated overnight at 4 °C. After washing again with PBS, the second antibody (FITC-labelled goat anti-rabbit, GB22303; CY3-labelled goat anti-rabbit, GB21303; Servicebio, China) was added and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. After washing with PBS, DAPI was added and incubated at room temperature. After a final wash with PBS, the slices were sealed with an anti-fluorescence attenuating sealer. The images were acquired using a microscopic camera system (OlyVIA, OLYMPUS, Japan). The fluorescence intensity and area of all the images acquired were measured using the Image-J image analysis system, and the mean fluorescence intensity of each image was calculated.

Western blot

Different groups of HCC cells were inoculated onto plates. Cells were lysed by adding RIPA cell lysis buffer and centrifuged to collect the protein supernatant. SDS loading buffer was added and boiled in boiling water for 5 min. After cooling, proteins were separated using SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis and transferred to PVDF membranes, which were subsequently sealed with 5 % skimmed milk powder at room temperature. After incubation with primary antibody (E-cadherin, A20798, abclonal, 1:1000; N-cadherin, A19083, abclonal, 1:2000; AKT, A17909, abclonal, 1:10,000; p-AKT-S473, AP0637, abclonal, 1:1000; GSK3β, A2081, abclonal, 1:2000; p-GSK3β-S9, AP0039, abclonal, 1:2000; ERK12, A16686, abclonal, 1:2000; p-ERK1-T202+ERK2-T185 (p-ERK12), AP0485, abclonal, 1:2000; TCF4, A15000, abclonal, 1:10,000; Kindlin-1, #36,734, CST, 1:2000; β-catenin, A19657, abclonal, 1:1000; β-actin, AC026, abclonal, 1:50,000), washed with PBS and incubation with secondary antibody (1:5000). Washed with PBS and addition of luminescent solution and observation of the final results with the ECL chemiluminescence system.

Tumor xenograft assay

Twelve 4-week-old BALB/c nude mice were used for subcutaneous xenograft tumor experiments, and 12 were used for the lung metastasis model. The animals were purchased from Chengdu Dossy Experimental Animal Co [SCXK (Chuan) 2019–031]. The study was approved by the Southwest Medical University Animal Ethics Committee (No. 20,230,506–005), and carried out in accordance with the National Research Council's Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The density of SNU182 cells transfected with shRNA-NC or shRNA-FERMT1–1 was adjusted to 3.0 × 106 cells/mL, suspended in 100 µL medium, and injected into the subcutaneous/caudal vein of nude mice. After the transplanted tumor model was treated for 24 days, the mice were killed by cervical dislocation, and the tumors were separated and weighed. After 4 weeks of the lung metastasis model, the mice were killed and the lung tissues were collected for metastasis statistics.

Co-Immunoprecipitation assay

Refer to the Pierce Co-Immunoprecipitation Kit operating instructions. Amino Link PlusResin and Pierce Control Agarose Resin (the negative control, which does not react with antibodies to remove the non-specific binding protein) were taken and incubated with 4 µg of kindin1 or EGFR antibody for 90 min, respectively. The samples were incubated with 4 µg of kindin1 or EGFR antibody for 90 min, and washed with IP lysis/wash buffer 4 times at 4 °C overnight. The samples were used for 10 % SDS-PAGE electrophoresis, WB was performed by adding 1:1000 dilution of kindin1 primary antibody and 1:1000 dilution of EGFR primary antibody, respectively, overnight at 4 °C, TBST washed 3 times and then the corresponding HRP-labeled secondary antibody was added, incubated for 1 h at room temperature, TBST washed 3 times and ECL chemical solution was developed.

Statistical analysis

The quantitative data of this study were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS v20.0 (IBM, USA). An independent sample's t-test was used to compare the difference between the two groups, and a one-way analysis of variance was used to compare the differences between the multiple groups.

Results

FERMT1 was highly expressed in HCC

To clarify the role of FERMT1 in HCC, the expression of FERMT1 in 6 pairs of HCC tissues and para-carcinoma tissues was examined by immunohistochemical staining, and the results showed that the expression of Kindlin-1 (the protein-coding for FERMT1) in the cancer group was significantly higher than that in the normal group (P < 0.001, Fig. 1A). Then, the mRNA expression of FERMT1 in HCC tissues and cell lines was detected by qRT-PCR, and the results showed that FERMT1 expression in HCC tissues was significantly higher than that in the normal para-carcinoma tissues (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B). Similarly, the mRNA expression of FERMT1 in HCC SNU182, SNU387 and Huh-7 cells was also significantly higher than that in normal liver cells (P < 0.05, Fig. 1C), in which SNU182 and SNU387 showed the most significant increase in expression for subsequent functional detection. Since FERMT1 was highly expressed in HCC, two siRNA targeting FERMT1 (siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2#) were used to interfere with the expression of FERMT1 in HCC cells, and WB was used to detect the protein expression of Kindlin-1 to verify the transfection efficiency. The results showed that, compared with the siRNA-NC group, both siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2# could significantly reduce the protein expression of Kindlin-1 (P < 0.001, Supplementary Figure 1), showing a good intervention effect.

Silencing FERM1 inhibited the viability, metastasis, and EMT of HCC cells

After the intervention of FERM1, the cell biological function was detected, and the CCK-8 results showed that the viability of SNU182 and SNU387 cells in the siRNA-FERMT1–1# group and siRNA-FERMT1–2# group was significantly reduced compared with that in the siRNA-NC group (P < 0.001, Fig. 2A). The results of the Transwell invasion assay showed that, the number of cells in the siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2# groups crossed the Transwell compartment was significantly less than that in the siRNA-NC group in the SNU182 and SNU387 cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 2, Fig. 2). And in the wound healing assay, the mean migration distances of SNU182 and SNU387 cells in the siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2# groups were significantly smaller than that in the siRNA-NC group (P < 0.001, Fig. 2, Fig. 2). Further, the expression of EMT marker protein was detected by immunofluorescence staining, and the results showed that compared with the siRNA-NC group, the expression of E-cadherin in the siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2# groups was significantly increased, and the expression of N-cadherin was significantly decreased both in the SNU182 and SNU387 cells (P < 0.05, Fig. 3A-D). Similarly, the protein expression results of E-cadherin and N-cadherin detected by WB were consistent with those of immunofluorescence (P < 0.05, Fig. 3, Fig. 3). These results suggested that the viability, metastasis, and EMT abilities of HCC cells were significantly reduced after intervention with FERMT1.

Silencing FERMT1 inhibited the activation of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways

EGFR is one of the earliest identified causes of cancer and a key target for clinical tumor therapy [20], and studies have shown that Kindlin-1 regulates the EGFR pathway and predicts the sensitivity of different cancer types to EGFR inhibitors [21]. To clarify the mechanism of FERMT1 in HCC, EGFR expression in HCC cells after silencing FERMT1 was detected by WB. The results showed that both in SNU182 and SNU387 cells, the expressions of EGFR in the siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2# groups were significantly lower than those in the siRNA-NC group (P < 0.05, Fig. 4). In addition, EGFR is involved in several signaling pathways, including the AKT signaling pathway and the MAPK/ERK pathway [22]. We further detected the protein expressions in the AKT/β-catenin and ERK pathways and found that both in SNU182 and SNU387 cells, the expressions of p-AKT/AKT, p-GSK3β/GSK3β, β-catenin, TCF4, and p-ERK12/ERK12 in the siRNA-FERMT1–1# and siRNA-FERMT1–2# groups were significantly lower than those in the siRNA-NC group (P < 0.05, Fig. 4). It was suggested that silencing FERMT1 inhibited the activation of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways.

Silencing FERMT1 inhibited the progression of HCC-transplanted tumor mice

The mouse transplanted tumor model was constructed by subcutaneous injection of SNU182 cells transfected with shRNA. The image of the transplanted tumor was shown in Fig. 5A, and compared to the shRNA-NC group, the tumor weight of the shRNA-FERMT1 group was significantly reduced (P < 0.01, Fig. 5B). Immunohistochemistry results showed that Ki-67 expression in the shRNA-FERMT1 group was significantly lower than that in the shRNA-NC group (P < 0.001, Fig. 5C). In addition, a mouse lung metastasis model was constructed with tail vein injection, and lung tissue was taken four weeks later and the metastatic foci in the lung tissues of mice were observed with HE staining. As shown in Fig. 5D, there were more nodules in the shRNA-NC group, presenting multiple foci and different sizes, tumor-like cells in the nodules had different shapes, larger nuclei, irregular arrangement, and atypia. Tumor-like nodules were also seen in the shRNA-FERMT1 group, which presented small foci and had fewer numbers. Further statistics on the number of tumor metastases showed that, compared with the shRNA-NC group, the number of tumor metastases in lung tissue in the shRNA-FERMT1 group was significantly reduced (P < 0.01, Fig. 5E). These results suggested that silencing FERMT1 inhibited the growth and metastasis of HCC-transplanted tumors.

FERMT1 promoted HCC by regulating EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways

To further elucidate the role of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways in FERMT1 promotion of EMT in HCC, FERMT1 was overexpressed with pcDNA-FERMT1, and shRNA-EGFR, AKT inhibitor (AZD-5363, MCE, China) and ERK inhibitor (LY3214996, MCE, China) were used to interfere with the expression of EGFR, AKT, and ERK, respectively. The protein expressions in the EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways showed that, compared with the pcDNA-NC group, the protein expressions of EGFR, p-AKT/AKT, p-GSK3β/GSK3β, β-catenin, TCF4, and p-ERK12/ERK12 in the pcDNA-FERMT1 group was significantly increased both in SNU182 and SNU387 cells, and inhibition of EGFR, AKT, and ERK inhibited the activation of these pathways to some extent (P < 0.05, Supplementary Figure 2). We further analyzed the cell function, and the results of CCK-8 assay showed that compared with the pcDNA-NC group, the viability of SNU182 and SNU387 cells in the pcDNA-FERMT1 group was significantly higher (P < 0.001), and compared with the pcDNA-FERMT1 group, the viability of cells in the pcDNA-FERMT1+shRNA-EGFR group, pcDNA-FERMT1+AKT inhibitor group and pcDNA-FERMT1+ERK inhibitor groups were significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 6A). In the Transwell invasion assay, compared with the pcDNA-NC group, the number of invaded SNU182 and SNU387 cells in the pcDNA-FERMT1 group was significantly increased, and compared to the pcDNA-FERMT1 group, the number of invaded cells in the pcDNA-FERMT1+shRNA-EGFR, pcDNA-FERMT1+AKT inhibitor, and pcDNA FERMT1+ERK inhibitor groups were significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 6B). The scratch experiment showed the same results, compared with the pcDNA-NC group, the mean migration distance of SNU182 and SNU387 cells in the pcDNA-FERMT1 group was significantly increased (P < 0.001), and compared with the pcDNA-FERMT1 group, the mean migration distance in the pcDNA-FERMT1+shRNA-EGFR, pcDNA-FERMT1+AKT inhibitor, and pcDNA FERMT1+ERK inhibitor groups were significantly decreased (P < 0.05, Fig. 6C). In addition, the EMT marker proteins were detected by WB, and results found that both in SNU182 and SNU387 cells, E-cadherin expression in the pcDNA-FERMT1 was significantly lower than that in the pcDNA-NC group, and Kindlin-1 and N-cadherin expressions were significantly higher than that in the pcDNA-NC group (P < 0.01, Fig. 7). And E-cadherin expression in the pcDNA-FERMT1+AKT inhibitor and pcDNA-FERMT1+ERK inhibitor groups was significantly higher than that of the pcDNA-FERMT1 group, while N-cadherin expression in the pcDNA-FERMT1+shRNA-EGFR, pcDNA-FERMT1+AKT inhibitor and pcDNA-FERMT1+ERK inhibitor groups was significantly lower than that in the pcDNA-FERMT1 group (P < 0.01, Fig. 7). Consistent with previous results, FERMT1 promoted the viability, metastasis, EMT, and the activation of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways in HCC cells, and the promotion effects of FERMT1 was inhibited after intervention with EGFR, AKT, and ERK, suggesting that FERMT1 promoted HCC process by promoting the activation of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways.

FERMT1 directly targets EGFR in HCC cells

To further demonstrate that FERMT1 regulated HCC progression through the EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways, we observed the co-localization of FERMT1 and EGFR by immunofluorescence double staining. As shown in Fig. 8A, FERMT1 and EGFR were mainly expressed in the nucleus of SNU182 and SNU387 cells, with obvious co-localization. Co-IP experiment results also showed that FERMT1 directly targeted EGFR in SNU182 and SNU387 cells, and EGFR also directly targeted FERMT1, confirming the direct correlation between FERMT1 and EGFR in HCC cells (Fig. 8B). These results suggested that FERMT1 regulated the EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways by targeting EGFR to promote HCC.

Discussion

HCC is highly aggressive, easily metastasized, and has a high mortality rate and a poor prognosis [23]. With the advancement of medical science and the increase of tumor treatment, targeted therapy is becoming a new therapeutic tool, which has been applied in liver cancer, but new molecular targets still need to be developed [24]. Aberrant expression of FERMT1 has been described in a variety of cancers, and is thought to be involved in tumor proliferation, apoptosis, metastasis, and tumor angiogenesis [25]. Studies have shown that knockdown FERMT1 expression inhibited the migration and invasion of cancer cells [26]. Our study demonstrated that FERMT1 was highly expressed in HCC, and silencing FERMT1 inhibited the viability, migration, invasion, and EMT of HCC cells. Further results suggested that FERMT1 may play a role in promoting HCC by targeting EGFR and regulating the activation of downstream EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathways.

The FERMT1 gene, which was first reported to be associated with Kindler syndrome in 2003, belongs to the FERMT family together with FERMT2 and FERMT3 genes, encoding the Kindlin1, Kindlin2, and Kindlin3 proteins respectively [27]. Kindlin1 is mainly expressed in endothelial cells and participates in the local adhesion of cells [28]. It has been reported that after the loss of Kindlin1, the migration, adhesion, and extension ability of keratinized cells are decreased, and the cells show multi-tip and multi-pole shape changes [29]. Other studies have found that Kindlin1 is involved in the functional regulation of integrin, which is considered to be a transmembrane adhesion receptor and has the functions of cell adhesion and signal transduction [30]. Pathological studies have shown that FERMT1 (Kindlin1) was abnormally expressed in various malignant tumors [31]. FERMT1 promoted the progression of gastric cancer by activating the NF-κB pathway [32], and is a prognostic marker involved in the immune invasion of pancreatic adenocarcinoma [33]. Similarly, studies have shown that the expression of Kindlin-1 in HCC tumor tissues is higher than that in neighboring normal tissues, which is an independent prognostic predictor of overall survival and disease-free survival in HCC patients [19]. This study also confirmed that the expression of FERMT1 was significantly increased in HCC tissues and cells, and after silencing FERMT1, cell viability, invasion, migration, and EMT were significantly inhibited, which played an important role in the viability and metastasis of HCC.

Metastasis is a major feature of tumorigenesis and one of the features that distinguish advanced tumors from benign tumor lesions, including local migratory invasion, intravasation, circulation, extravasation, proliferation/colonization, angiogenesis, and growth [34,35], and is a major cause of death in approximately 90 % of cancer patients [36,37]. EMT enables cancer cells to increase the potential for tumor initiation and metastasis and is a key step in the development of cancer metastasis, characterized by the absence of epithelial molecular markers and the increase of mesenchymal molecular markers [38,39]. During EMT, the cell transition to a mesenchymal state alters the adhesion molecules expressed by the cell, causing it to engage in migration and invasion behavior, at which point the cell can leave the neighboring epithelial cells and invade the surrounding tissues [40]. By inducing the same cells to proliferate and sow new tumors, EMT promoted tumor metastasis and recurrence [41]. Decreased expression of epithelial-specific activated E-cadherin and increased expression of mesenchymal-specific gene N-cadherin were positively correlated with EMT [42]. Our study showed that silencing FERMT1 inhibited HCC cell invasion and migration, activated E-cadherin expression, and inhibited the N-cadherin expression, suggesting that silencing FERMT1 prevented cellular EMT. The same results were found in animal experiments, where silencing FERMT1 inhibited lung metastasis of HCC.

The development of HCC is a complex pathological process involving multiple factors and pathways, and multiple signaling pathways have been shown to be involved in the initiation of EMT in HCC [43]. Our results suggested that silencing FERMT1 inhibited the expression of EGFR/AKT/β-catenin and EGFR/ERK pathway proteins in HCC cells. EGFR, a transmembrane protein, is a receptor for members of the epidermal growth factor family of extracellular protein ligands [44]. A large number of studies have confirmed that EGFR and its receptor signals can participate in intracellular mitochondrial signal transduction, thereby causing cell proliferation, differentiation, and metastasis [45]. EGFR is often activated by overexpression of the receptor, especially in many cancer types, where the expression of EGFR is affected or its activity is mutated, leading to the development of cancer [46]. EGFR is believed to be a driver of tumorigenesis, and in addition, studies have shown that Kindlin-1 and EGFR interact in keratinocytes [47], and that Kindlin-1 regulates the EGFR pathway and predicts the sensitivity of different cancer types to EGFR inhibitors [21]. Our study also confirmed the co-localization of Kindlin-1 and EGFR in HCC cells, and they could target and bind to each other. In addition, EGFR, like other tyrosine receptor kinases, signal transduction initiation depends on receptor connectivity and multiple downstream signaling pathways [48]. During this time, EGFR binding to ligands is activated to recruit a range of downstream signaling factors within the cell, such as the AKT and ERK pathways [49]. AKT pathway plays an important role in promoting anti-apoptosis of tumor cells, and its activation can also induce the occurrence of EMT [50]. AKT can activate the Wnt/β-catenin pathway by phosphorylating GSK3β to prevent it from mediating β-catenin degradation, or by directly promoting phosphorylation of specific active sites at the C-terminal of β-catenin [51]. β-catenin is closely related to many transcription factors, marker proteins, and signaling pathways that induce EMT. Abnormal activation of the β-catenin pathway can significantly weaken the adhesion between tumor cells and induce EMT of tumor cells, thereby enhancing tumor cell migration and invasion [52,53]. The results of this study confirmed that silencing FERMT1 inhibited the AKT pathway, reduced phosphorylation of GSK3β, enhanced degradation of β-catenin, and inhibited transcriptional activation of β-catenin. Overexpression of FERMT1 activated AKT, increased phosphorylation of GSK3β, reduced β-catenin degradation, leads to cytoplasmic accumulation and nuclear shift of β-catenin, and increased distribution in the nucleus to activate the transcriptional activation activity of β-catenin. Silencing ERGF or AKT inhibitor treatment blocked the activation of AKT/GSK-3β/β-catenin caused by overexpression of FERMT1, confirming that FERMT1 activated the AKT/GSK-3β/β-catenin pathway through EGFR. In addition, EGFR activation can activate intracytoplasmic ERK into phosphorylate, and the activated ERK pathway plays a crucial role in cell proliferation and apoptosis [54]. Studies have shown that the antitumor activity is mediated by EGFR/ERK through down-regulation of p-ERK expression, thereby causing cell death and blocking cell proliferation [55]. Our study also showed that overexpression of FERMT1 activated ERK, and silencing ERGF or AKT inhibitor treatment blocked the activity of ERK induced by overexpression of FERMT1, confirming that the activation of ERK pathway by FERMT1 was also mediated by EGFR. Overall, FERMT1 promoted EMT in HCC by targeting EGFR to regulate downstream AKT/GSK-3β/β-catenin and ERK pathways.

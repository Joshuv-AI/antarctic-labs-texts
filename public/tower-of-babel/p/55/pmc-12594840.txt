MBNL3 regulates tumor progression and metastasis in cholangiocarcinoma and serves as a prognostic biomarker
Authors: Hui Zhou, Yongxiang Wang, Kexin Zhao, Mingcan Li, Xinxi Ni, Siyuan Chen, Ting Yan, Jiachi Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL) is a highly fatal malignancy originating from the bile ducts, with most patients diagnosed at an advanced stage, limiting treatment options. This study explores the role of Muscleblind-like 3 (MBNL3), a conserved RNA-binding protein, in CHOL progression, particularly its involvement in metastasis and immune microenvironment regulation. Using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), we investigated MBNL3 expression and its correlation with patient prognosis. High MBNL3 expression was associated with significantly better overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free interval (DFI) in CHOL patients. Functional analysis revealed that MBNL3 modulates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and interacts with critical pathways, including focal adhesion, collagen signaling, and TNF signaling. Additionally, MBNL3 influences immune cell infiltration, with high expression linked to increased macrophage and Th17 cell presence, suggesting potential roles in immune response modulation. Experimental validation confirmed that silencing MBNL3 promotes EMT, enhances tumor cell migration and invasion, and increases metastasis in vivo. Furthermore, genetic analyses highlighted the impact of MBNL3 mutations and methylation on CHOL progression, identifying potential targets for immunotherapy. This is the first study to investigate MBNL3’s role in CHOL, positioning it as a prognostic biomarker and a promising therapeutic target for improving outcomes in CHOL patients. Further research is needed to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying MBNL3’s regulation of tumor metastasis and the immune microenvironment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-10625-7.

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), which primarily originates from the epithelial cells of the bile ducts, is the second most common liver malignancy after hepatocellular carcinoma1. In recent years, the incidence of CHOL has risen globally, contributing to an increasing disease burden. The primary treatment option for CHOL is radical surgical resection. However, due to the often subtle early symptoms, over 60% of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, frequently presenting with peripheral vascular and neurological invasion and lymph node metastasis, which limits surgical intervention opportunities2. Consequently, CHOL has a poor prognosis, with the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate ranging from 8 to 20%, depending on the tumor’s location3. Beyond surgery, the combination of gemcitabine and cisplatin (GEMCIS) is recommended as the first-line chemotherapy for cholangiocarcinoma, though its efficacy remains limited4. Immunotherapy has emerged as a promising alternative treatment. Evidence suggests that high expression of immune checkpoints is linked to CHOL progression, and their inhibition can improve the intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma tumor microenvironment, thereby suppressing tumor progression5. The tumor microenvironment (TME) consists of stromal cells, innate and adaptive immune cells, and the extracellular matrix6. However, the molecular mechanisms driving CHOL metastasis within the TME are not fully understood. Thus, identifying novel, reliable biomarkers and therapeutic targets for CHOL prognostic assessment and improving the efficacy of immunotherapy is of paramount clinical importance.

Muscleblind-like 3 (MBNL3), a member of the MBNL protein family essential for photoreceptor and muscle differentiation, was initially implicated in muscle degenerative diseases, such as myotonic dystrophy, due to its elevated activity in myogenic cells7. Recent studies have highlighted the significant role of MBNL3 in tumorigenesis, migration, and invasion, showing a correlation with patient prognosis. For example, downregulation of MBNL3 has been found to enhance radiosensitivity and apoptosis in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target for NSCLC8. Additionally, MBNL3 regulates pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cell invasion without affecting cell proliferation9. High expression of MBNL3 has also been associated with paclitaxel resistance and poor prognosis in ovarian cancer10. Despite these advancements, the role of MBNL3 in CHOL progression remains largely unexplored.

In this study, we investigated the expression, prognostic significance, relationship with immune molecules, and co-expressed gene network of MBNL3 in CHOL patients, utilizing ribonucleic acid sequencing (RNA-seq) and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases. We conducted our analysis using the R software package and various online databases, further validating our findings through Western blotting on CHOL samples. By knocking down MBNL3 in CHOL cell lines, we provide the first evidence that MBNL3 acts as a tumor suppressor in CHOL in vitro. Furthermore, using a mouse lung metastasis model, we demonstrate MBNL3’s crucial role in CHOL metastasis. These findings suggest that MBNL3 inhibits CHOL development and metastasis, highlighting its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for CHOL.

Materials and methods

Acquisition of transcriptome sequencing data

This study obtained cholangiocarcinoma-related transcriptome data TCGA-CHOL-RNAseq from TCGA, which includes 36 tumor samples and 9 adjacent non-tumor samples, along with the corresponding mutation data TCGA-CHOL-SNP for the transcriptome samples. Additionally, this study included three cholangiocarcinoma datasets from the GEO database: GSE138709, GSE32879, and GSE38834. The single-cell dataset GSE138709 contains five ICC samples, as well as three paired adjacent non-tumor tissues. The tissue transcriptome dataset GSE32879 includes 16 intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) samples, 7 mixed type combined HCC and ICC (CHC) samples, 2 Hepatic Adenoma samples, 5 Focal Nodular Hyperplasia samples, and 7 non-tumor tissues. Due to the insufficiency of Hepatic Adenoma samples (less than 3), they were excluded from the analysis. The tissue transcriptome dataset GSE38834 includes 12 tumors arising from bile duct inoculation of spontaneously-transformed low grade malignant rat BDE1 cholangiocytes (BDEsp cells) and 14 tumors arising from the inoculation of high grade malignant erbB-2/neu-transformed BDE1 cholangiocytes (BDEneu cells) into the livers of syngeneic rats.

Cell culture

Human cholangiocarcinoma cell lines RBE and HuCCT1 were utilized in this study. HuCCT-1 and RBE cells were obtained from the Cell Resource Center at the Shanghai Academy of Biological Sciences. The cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Pricella, Wuhan, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Pricella, Wuhan, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution (Biochannel, Nanjing, China). Cell cultures were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2.

Plasmid and lentivirus preparation

Primers for constructing the MBNL3 knockout plasmid pLKO.1 were synthesized by Beijing Kengke Biotechnology Co. The forward primer sequence was: 5’-CCGGCHOLGCHOLGCAAATTCAAATAACTCGAGTTATTTGAATTTGCTGGCTGGTTTTTG-3’, and the reverse primer sequence was: 5’-AATTCAAAAACHOLGCHOLGCAAATTCAAATAACTCGAGTTATTTGAATTTGCTGGCTGG-3’. The MBNL3 knockout plasmid (pLKO.1) and packaging plasmids (pMD2G and psPAX2) were transfected into HEK-293T cells. After 48 h, lentiviral particles were harvested from the collected supernatants.

Establishment of stable CHOL cell lines

Human cholangiocarcinoma cells were infected with MBNL3 shRNA lentivirus. Following a 48-hour incubation period, cells were seeded into 10 cm culture dishes and cultured in a complete medium containing puromycin (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China). Single colonies were isolated and transferred to 96-well plates for further expansion. Western blot analysis was performed to assess the efficiency of MBNL3 overexpression and knockdown.

Co-culture of HuCCT1 cells with NK cells

HuCCT1 cells were labeled with Carboxyfluorescein Diacetate, Succinimidyl Ester (CFSE, Thermo Fisher, USA), seeded in 6-well plates, and incubated for 24 h. NK cells (effector cells) were added to 6-well plates and co-cultured with HuCCT1 cells (target cells) to detect the killing effect of NK cells on CHOL cells. After 24 h of co-culture, dead target cells were labeled by 7-Aminoactinomycin D (7-AAD, beyotime, China).

Transwell assay

Transwell chambers with 8.0 μm pores (Corning Costar, Kennebunk, USA) were utilized for migration and invasion assays. Chambers were coated with Matrigel for invasion assays and left uncoated for migration assays. For migration experiments, 3 × 104 cells were seeded into 200 µl of serum-free RPMI 1640 medium in the upper chamber of 24-well plates. For invasion assays, 5 × 10^4 cells were seeded in 200 µl of serum-free RPMI 1640 medium. The lower chamber contained 600 µl of RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum. After 48 h, cells that had migrated to the bottom surface of the chamber were fixed with paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and imaged under a microscope.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry was performed to detect MBNL3 protein expression in cholangiocarcinoma tissues. Paraffin-embedded tissue sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval. Sections were then blocked with goat serum to minimize nonspecific binding. The MBNL3-specific antibody (1:100 dilution, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) was applied and incubated overnight at 4 °C. The following day, a chromogenic reaction was conducted using DAB chromogen, and the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. The processed sections were documented using light microscopy.

Animal studies

We confirm that all methods are implemented in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, including ARRIVE guidelines. Male BALB/c nude mice, aged 5–6 weeks, were obtained from Slaughter Kingda Co. To establish a lung metastasis model, 2 × 106 stably transfected HuCCT1 cells were suspended in 200 µL of PBS and injected via the tail vein. After six weeks, we use inhaled isoflurane to completely anesthetize the mice and then executed them by rapid cervical dislocation to assess lung tumor formation.

Mendelian randomization

Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with MBNL3 expression were extracted from supplementary materials and analyzed using cholangiocarcinoma-related Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) datasets, including ebi-a-GCST90018803, finn-b-C3_BILIARY_TRACT, and finn-b-C3_LIVER_INTRAHEPATIC_BILE_DUCTS. Mendelian randomization analysis was performed to assess the causal relationship between SNPs promoting MBNL3 expression and cholangiocarcinoma. A meta-analysis was also conducted to explore the association between MBNL3 expression and cholangiocarcinoma.

Determination of apoptosis

After the cells of the stably transfected strain reached the logarithmic growth phase, the cells were digested with EDTA-free trypsin and collected in a centrifuge tube; centrifuged at 300 g at 4℃ for 5 min, and the supernatant was discarded. Wash the cells twice with pre-cooled PBS, centrifuge at 300 g at 4℃ for 5 min each time; add 1×Binding Buffer to adjust the cells to the same concentration; take 1 × 105 cell suspension in a flow tube, add appropriate amount of Annexin V labelled with fluorescent dye and appropriate amount of PI, mix gently; incubate at room temperature and avoid light for 15–20 min; add 300 µl of PBS to resuspend the cells as soon as possible (as soon as possible). Incubate for 15–20 min at room temperature and avoid light; add 300 µl of PBS to resuspend the cells, and test by flow cytometry as soon as possible (within 1 h).

Tissue immunofluorescence

Cholangiocarcinoma tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. Sections were incubated with specific antibodies against MBNL3, CD56(typical phenotypic marker of natural killer (NK) cells), CD68(a macrophage marker), and CD274, followed by staining with secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa Fluor 488 and Alexa Fluor 599. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Beyotime), and the sections were sealed for fluorescence imaging.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were analyzed using the rank sum test. Survival analysis was conducted using the survfit function from the R package “survival,” and the log-rank test was employed to evaluate prognostic differences between groups. Patients were divided into two groups based on the 50th percentile for continuous grouping variables. Prognostic models were constructed by integrating survival time, survival status, and gene expression data using the glmnet package. Variable selection was performed using LASSO-Cox regression analysis, and a nomogram was developed using the rms package. Single-cell data were preprocessed using the Seurat package, with cell types annotated using SingleR and cell-to-cell communication analyzed using the CellChat package.

Results

High expression of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma is associated with better clinical prognosis

Analysis of data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cholangiocarcinoma database revealed a significant correlation between MBNL3 mRNA expression and patient prognosis. Patients were categorized into high and low expression groups based on MBNL3 levels, with 18 individuals in each group (n = 18). Survival analyses indicated that patients in the high-expression group had significantly better OS, progression-free survival (PFS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free interval (DFI) compared to those in the low-expression group (Fig. 1A-D, all log-rank P < 0.05).

To further investigate, Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was employed to estimate the causal effect of MBNL3 on cholangiocarcinoma risk using summary statistics. The results suggested that MBNL3 functions as an inhibitor of cholangiocarcinoma development (Fig. 1E). A prognostic model was constructed using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) algorithm to identify key variables. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 1A-B, five variables were identified with the lowest cross-validation error: MBNL3, albumin, pathologic N stage, perineural invasion, and vascular tumor cell type. The risk score was calculated as follows: Risk Score = -0.1175 × MBNL3–0.0866 × Albumin + 0.6850 × Pathologic N + 0.7663 × Perineural Invasion + 0.0111 × Vascular Tumor Cell Type.

This risk score was then used to classify patients into high- and low-risk groups. A nomogram incorporating MBNL3, albumin, pathologic N stage, perineural invasion, and vascular tumor cell type was developed to provide a quantitative method for predicting OS probability (Supplementary Fig. 1C). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the high-risk group had significantly lower survival rates compared to those in the low-risk group (Supplementary Fig. 1D). The robustness of this risk model was further validated by calculating the ROC curve for OS, with area under the curve (AUC) values exceeding 0.88 at 1, 3, and 5 years, indicating its strong predictive performance (Supplementary Fig. 1E). The clinical decision curve shows that patients can benefit from clinical decisions once the probability of death risk reaches 0.45 (Supplementary Fig. 1F).

Additionally, LASSO regression analysis identified variables associated with the progression-free interval (PFI), including MBNL3, pathologic N stage, family cancer history, and vascular tumor cell type, which were used to construct a nomogram plot for predicting PFI probability, and the model was further evaluated using ROC curves and clinical decision trees (Supplementary Fig. 2A-F). Similarly, DSS-related variables such as perineural invasion, MBNL3, and pathologic N stage were selected to generate a DSS prognostic prediction nomogram, and the model was also evaluated using ROC curves and clinical decision trees(Supplementary Fig. 3A-F). This comprehensive model underscores the prognostic significance of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma and integrates multiple predictive variables for a more accurate prognostic assessment.

MBNL3 inhibits metastasis of cholangiocarcinoma

MBNL3 expression levels were significantly higher in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues (P < 0.05), suggesting a potential association with tumorigenesis (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, MBNL3 expression was significantly higher in the low-malignancy group compared to the high-malignancy group (p = 0.01), suggesting a potential role in inhibiting tumor aggressiveness (Fig. 2B). We also evaluated MBNL3 expression across various disease states, including non-tumor tissue, focal nodular hyperplasia, combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma, and intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. The findings indicated that MBNL3 expression was highest in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, significantly exceeding the levels observed in other groups (P < 0.001), further supporting MBNL3’s critical role in cholangiocarcinoma progression (Fig. 2C).

Protective role of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma is associated with inhibition of epithelial-mesenchymal transition

Using the single-cell dataset GSE138709, we extracted epithelial cells from cholangiocarcinoma tissues and analyzed MBNL3 expression and functional characteristics across different tumor cell subpopulations. Nine distinct cell clusters were identified based on marker gene expression, including B cells, epithelial cells, endothelial cells, hepatocytes, macrophages, NK cells, pre-B cells (CD34-), T cells, and tissue stem cells (Fig. 2G).

Within the epithelial cell category, seven subclusters were further delineated, and MBNL3 expression was assessed within each subgroup (Figs. 2H-I). Notably, high MBNL3 expression significantly overlapped with regions characterized by EMT inhibition (Fig. 2J). This finding suggests that the protective role of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma may be related to its ability to inhibit EMT, potentially explaining the improved prognosis observed in patients with elevated MBNL3 expression in cholangiocarcinoma.

Functional analysis of MBNL3 implies its role in cholangiocarcinoma metastasis

The above results indicate that there are seven subpopulations of endothelial cells in cholangiocarcinoma, among which the epithelial 1 subpopulation exhibits high expression of MBNL3 and the highest EMT-related score. Therefore, to investigate the role of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma metastasis, we selected the epithelial 1 subpopulation for functional analysis. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis revealed that MBNL3 is associated with the regulation of gene expression, extracellular space organization, and cell junction formation (Fig. 3A). Further analysis using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway indicated that MBNL3 is involved in focal adhesion, tight junctions, and the TNF signaling pathway (Fig. 3B). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) identified four top-enriched pathways with high correlation: negative regulation of leukocyte migration (ES=-0.7083,FDR = 0.0083), negative regulation of necrotic cell death (ES=-0.5646,FDR = 0.0221), negative regulation of reactive oxygen species metabolic processes (ES=-0.5221,FDR = 0.0175), and positive regulation of epithelial cell migration (ES=-0.3919,FDR = 0.041) (Fig. 3D). Additionally, we explored the relationship between MBNL3 and the collagen signaling pathway, which is known to facilitate tumor cell migration and invasion through cell-matrix interactions11(Fig. 3C). Compared with the low-expression group, the high-expression group exhibited collagen signaling pathway activity predominantly between epithelial cells and tissue stem cells. In the high-expression group, epithelial cells emerged as the primary senders, receivers, mediators, and influencers of the collagen signaling pathway (Fig. 3E-F).

Potential role of MBNL3 in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment

The tumor microenvironment comprises various cell types whose complex intercellular communication is closely tied to tumor metastasis. We calculated cellular interactions in the CCL signaling pathway and the MHC-I signaling pathway in MBNL3 high- and low-expression groups using CellChat (Fig. 4A-D). In the CCL signaling pathway, the interaction strength between monocytes and T cells was significantly elevated in the high-expression group, with monocyte clusters emerging as the primary senders, receivers, mediators, and influencers (Figs. 4A-B). Similarly, in the MHC-I signaling pathway, both the number of ligand-receptor pairs and the intensity of cell-cell communication were higher in the high-expression group compared to the low-expression group (Fig. 4C-D). Most interactions occurred between monocytes and T cells. In the high-expression group, monocyte clusters exhibited the most signaling patterns, whereas, in the low-expression group, T cell clusters were the primary participants in the MHC-I signaling pathway. These findings suggest that monocytes and T cells play a central role in the tumor immune microenvironment. CD276, an immune checkpoint molecule within the EMT pathway, is implicated in cell proliferation, invasion, and migration in malignant tumors. We analyzed CD276 expression in epithelial cells from both groups and found that CD276 expression was relatively higher in the epithelial cells of the MBNL3 low-expression group compared to the high-expression group (Fig. 4E). The expression of CD276 and CD274 was significantly higher in tumor tissues than in normal tissues, suggesting their association with the immune escape mechanism in cholangiocarcinoma (Fig. 4F-G). Immunofluorescence staining revealed significantly reduced signals of both MBNL3 and the NK cell marker CD56 in tumor tissues compared to paracancerous tissues (Fig. 4H). Although MBNL3 expression did not align with CD274 expression, CD274 showed a markedly stronger immunofluorescence signal in tumor tissues than in paracancerous tissues (Fig. 4I). Moreover, we observed correlations between MBNL3 expression patterns and immune checkpoint molecules (e.g., CTLA4, PVR, PVRL2) in the tumor microenvironment, suggesting that MBNL3 may participate in immune evasion by regulating immune checkpoints (Fig. 4J). Co-culture experiments further confirmed that HuCCT1 cells were more susceptible to NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity following MBNL3 knockdown (Fig. 4K-L).

Investigating gene expression within tumors and their associations with immune cells can help predict clinical outcomes and guide the selection of diagnostic targets and therapeutic interventions. Our analysis revealed that MBNL3 expression in cholangiocarcinoma negatively correlated with the infiltration of CD8 naive T cells, B cells, and nTregs, while positively correlating with macrophage and Th17 cell infiltration (Figs. 5A-D). Immunofluorescence results showed elevated expression of both MBNL3 and CD68 (macrophage marker) in tumor tissues (Fig. 5E). These findings suggest that MBNL3 may play a role in immune cell infiltration and immunoregulatory gene expression, and high MBNL3 expression may predict a better response to immunotherapy.

MBNL3 mutations and epigenetic modifications are associated with cholangiocarcinoma and may be key molecules for immunotherapy

Given the significance of MBNL3 expression in cholangiocarcinoma prognosis, we investigated the impact of MBNL3 mutation types on patient outcomes. Copy number variations (CNVs) are common in the human genome, though their effects on biological processes remain a topic of debate. Our findings revealed that DFI and PFS were worse in the CNV deletion group compared to the CNV wild-type group for MBNL3 (Figs. 6A-B, log Rank P < 0.05). Additionally, MBNL3 methylation in cholangiocarcinoma was negatively correlated with mRNA expression levels (Fig. 6C). These differences led us to explore the potential impact of other differentially expressed genes between the two groups. Waterfall plots highlighted the ten most commonly mutated genes and their mutation types, with PBRM1 (30.8%, 8/25), BAP1 (26.9%, 7/25), and ARID1A (23.1%, 6/25) being the top three (Fig. 6D). As a known tumor suppressor12, BAP1’s high mutation rate has been directly linked to cholangiocarcinoma and represents a potential therapeutic target13. Moreover, we found that PBRM1 mutations were more frequent in the MBNL3 low-expression group (41.7% vs. 23.1%), while ARID1A mutations were predominantly found in the low-expression group (41.7% vs. 7.7%). Notably, a clinical study demonstrated that loss-of-function mutations in PBRM1 were associated with clinical benefit in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (P = 0.012), and the deletion of PBRM1 may alter the tumor’s overall expression profile, potentially affecting the response to immune checkpoint therapy14. Furthermore, ARID1A, which was highly mutated in the low-expression group, has been shown to induce an immunosuppressive microenvironment in triple-negative breast cancer, leading to adaptive immune resistance and a heightened response to immune checkpoint inhibitors15. These findings emphasize the significance of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma research and highlight its relevance to immunotherapy.

MBNL3 suppresses CHOL metastasis in vitro and in vivo

Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed that MBNL3 and E-cadherin protein levels were lower in cholangiocarcinoma tissues than in noncancerous tissues, while the expression level of Vimentin protein in cholangiocarcinoma was higher than that in non-tumor tissues(Fig. 7A). To elucidate the role of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma development, we established an MBNL3 knockdown (KD) model via shRNA transfection and investigated MBNL3’s impact on tumor migration and invasion in HuCCT1 and RBE cell lines through western blot (WB) and transwell assays. WB validated the CHOL cell model with reduced MBNL3 expression (Fig. 7B). Silencing MBNL3 increased vimentin expression, a marker of EMT, while decreasing E-cadherin expression (Fig. 7B). In transwell assays, reduced MBNL3 expression in HuCCT1 and RBE cells significantly enhanced their migratory and invasive capabilities (Figs. 7C-D). To validate these findings, we conducted in vivo experiments using a lung metastasis mouse model. The sh-MBNL3 group exhibited a higher number of lung metastases compared to the control group (Figs. 7E-G). Additionally, the lung weight of the sh-MBNL3 group was significantly greater than that of the control group (Fig. 7H). Collectively, these in vivo results demonstrate that MBNL3 knockdown promotes lung metastasis in CHOL cells.

Discussion

CHOL is a malignant tumor originating from the endothelial cells of the bile ducts and remains highly fatal for most patients. Due to its insidious onset, approximately 60% of patients are diagnosed with distant metastases, which leads to the loss of surgical curative options2. Among these patients, over 70% have a survival rate of only about one year16. This highlights the importance of exploring alternative treatments beyond surgery. Simultaneously, the molecular mechanisms driving CHOL metastasis within the TME are poorly understood, underscoring the need for deeper insights into metastatic cholangiocarcinoma and the search for more potent biomarkers and therapeutic targets. These advancements could aid in the development of specific therapies to improve current treatments and reduce the high mortality rate associated with cholangiocarcinoma.

Conserved proteins typically retain their functions throughout evolution, both vertically through time and horizontally across species. The MBNL family, a class of conserved proteins, binds pre-mRNAs through tandem zinc finger domains and plays a crucial role in various RNA metabolic processes, including alternative splicing (AS), RNA stability, translocation, subcellular localization regulation, and selective polyadenylation17. Historically, research on the MBNL family has largely focused on its role in regulating neurological disorders. However, recent studies have shed light on its role in cancer18–22. This discovery is particularly intriguing given the minimal functional changes typically observed in such conserved proteins during evolution.

Our review of the MBNL3 literature revealed that it primarily functions as a splicing factor for AS in cancer23. The effect of MBNL3 on cancer appears to be cancer-type dependent. For instance, MBNL3 can promote hepatocellular carcinoma by enhancing PXN expression through selective splicing of lncRNA-PXN-AS123. Additionally, MBNL3 has been shown to increase stem-like cell viability and reduce apoptosis in ovarian cancer by upregulating Nanog expression10. However, the role of MBNL3 in CHOL development remains unclear, prompting us to investigate its function in CHOL progression.

Our analysis, based on TCGA database results, revealed that MBNL3 mRNA expression is significantly correlated with the prognosis of CHOL patients. Compared to the low-expression group, the high-expression group showed significant improvements in OS, PFS, DSS, and DFI. This suggests that MBNL3 may serve as a favorable prognostic indicator for CHOL. Additionally, we analyzed single-cell RNA-seq results from the GEO database to explore the relationship between MBNL3 expression and cellular behavior, identifying a role for MBNL3 in CHOL metastasis.

EMT plays a critical role in cancer development and progression24. We focused on CD276, an immune checkpoint molecule involved in EMT, as it is essential for cell proliferation, invasion, and migration in malignant tumors25. In our study, CD276 expression was relatively high in the epithelial cells of the MBNL3 low-expression group compared to the high-expression group. We then categorized epithelial cells into seven clusters and examined MBNL3 expression within each subgroup. Interestingly, high MBNL3 expression significantly overlapped with regions exhibiting EMT inhibition, which could explain the prognostic benefits associated with high MBNL3 expression in cholangiocarcinoma. Furthermore, functional analysis of the epithelial 1 subpopulation revealed that MBNL3 is involved in the regulation of gene expression, extracellular space organization, and cell junctions, as shown by GO analysis. KEGG analysis indicated that MBNL3 is associated with focal adhesion, tight junctions, and the TNF signaling pathway. GSEA analysis identified the top four enriched pathways with the highest correlation: negative regulation of leukocyte migration (P = 0.0083), negative regulation of necrotic cell death (P = 0.0221), negative regulation of reactive oxygen species metabolism (P = 0.0175), and positive regulation of epithelial cell migration (P = 0.0411). These pathways are closely linked to EMT, further confirming that MBNL3 influences patient prognosis by modulating EMT.

We further refined our analysis of MBNL3-related signaling pathways and cellular interactions to assess their impact within the TME. Specifically, we explored the relationship between MBNL3 and the collagen signaling pathway, which promotes tumor cell migration and invasion through cell-matrix interactions11. In the high-expression group, collagen signaling primarily occurred between epithelial cells and tissue stem cells, with epithelial cells acting as the main senders, receivers, mediators, and influencers of the pathway. Other pathways also influenced CHOL outcomes. For example, the CCL signaling pathway plays a crucial role in tumor cell metastasis and invasion by facilitating tumor cell migration to distant sites, contributing to tumor spread26. Additionally, the MHC-I signaling pathway is instrumental in immune escape and metastasis of tumor cells. MHC-I molecules, which are typically expressed on the surface of nucleated cells, present endogenous antigens to CD8 + T cells, triggering an immune response against abnormal cells27. Restoring MHC-I function in tumor cells could enhance tumor recognition and clearance by immune cells, thereby inhibiting tumor growth and metastasis28. Our CellChat analysis showed that the MHC-I and CCL signaling pathways were more active in the MBNL3 high-expression group compared to the low-expression group. These findings emphasize the critical role of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma’s TME and its interactions with other molecules. Future research should further explore how changes in MBNL3 expression impact the TME in CHOL.

The type of genetic mutation can directly affect phenotype. We first investigated the prognostic impact of CNVs in MBNL3. In our study, DFI and PFS were worse in the CNV deletion group compared to the CNV wild-type group (log-rank P < 0.05). Similar trends were observed for other genetic modifications, such as epigenetic changes. Methylation of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma was negatively correlated with mRNA expression levels. Based on these findings, we sought to understand the broader implications of these genetic alterations. Interestingly, different types and frequencies of gene mutations were observed between the high- and low-MBNL3 expression groups. The three most frequently mutated genes were PBRM1 (30.8%, 8/25), BAP1 (26.9%, 7/25), and ARID1A (23.1%, 6/25). These mutations were associated with tumor immune infiltration and therapy response, prompting us to focus on immunotherapy in cholangiocarcinoma.

Increasing evidence supports the interaction between tumor immune infiltrating cells and cancer progression29,30. This interaction is multidimensional, involving complex networks between stromal cells, immune cells, cancer cells, and other TME components. Immune infiltrating cells, including T cells, B cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, and natural killer cells, contribute to the TME and influence cancer progression30. Our analysis revealed that MBNL3 expression in cholangiocarcinoma was inversely correlated with the infiltration of CD8 naive T cells, B cells, and nTregs, but positively correlated with macrophage and Th17 cell infiltration. These results suggest that MBNL3 may play a role in immune cell infiltration and immunoregulatory gene expression, with high MBNL3 expression potentially predicting a better response to immunotherapy.

To clarify MBNL3’s role in CHOL development, we conducted experimental validation. Silencing MBNL3 increased vimentin expression, a marker of EMT, while decreasing E-cadherin expression. MBNL3 knockdown also inhibited the migration and invasion of CHOL cells. Using a lung metastasis mouse model, we further demonstrated that MBNL3 plays a key role in CHOL metastasis in vivo.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the function of MBNL3 in cholangiocarcinoma. These findings suggest that MBNL3 could serve as a biomarker for predicting CHOL patient prognosis and may offer a new avenue for immunotherapeutic strategies. However, further research is needed to elucidate the specific mechanisms by which MBNL3 regulates CHOL metastasis.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study reveals that MBNL3 plays a pivotal role in the progression and metastasis of CHOL. MBNL3 serves as a promising prognostic biomarker for CHOL and can assist in identifying patients who may benefit from targeted therapies and immunotherapies. MBNL3 exerts protective effects by inhibiting EMT and regulating key signaling pathways, including focal adhesion, collagen signaling, and TNF signaling. Additionally, MBNL3 influences the tumor immune microenvironment by promoting macrophage and Th17 cell infiltration and modulating immune checkpoint molecules such as CD276 and CD274. Experimental validation further demonstrates that silencing MBNL3 enhances tumor cell migration, invasion, and metastasis. Genetic analyses show that MBNL3 mutations and methylation are associated with poor prognosis, underscoring its clinical significance in cholangiocarcinoma.

Conditional knockdown protocol for studying cellular communication using Drosophila melanogaster wing imaginal disc
Authors: Hanna Antson, Yunxian Huang, Tambet Tõnissoo, Osamu Shimmi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryApicobasal polarity determinants are potential tumor suppressors that have been extensively studied. However, the precise mechanisms by which their misregulation disrupts tissue homeostasis are not fully understood. Here, we present a comprehensive protocol for establishing a conditional RNAi knockdown of scribble in Drosophila wing imaginal disc. We describe steps for generating fly lines, conditional knockdown in host stocks, and sample preparation. We then detail procedures for imaging, image analysis, and verification of wing disc phenotypes by various antibodies.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Huang et al.1Subject areas: Developmental Biology, Genetics, Microscopy, Model Organisms, Molecular Biology, Gene Expression

Before you begin

The Drosophila larval wing imaginal disc consisting of epithelial cell layers provides an excellent model for investigating how signaling regulation and tissue size are controlled.2 Besides the physiological significance of the gene regulatory network during epithelial tissue development, changes in gene expression can lead to pathological conditions, such as neoplasia formation.3,4 One of the fundamental mechanisms governing epithelial cells is the maintenance of apicobasal polarity (ABP).5 The Scribble complex, consisting of Scribble (Scrib), Disc large (Dlg), and Lethal giant larvae (Lgl), represents one of the key determinants of ABP.6,7 These three genes are recognized as tumor suppressors, and their loss of function has been extensively studied in the context of wing imaginal discs.8,9 This exploration has involved clonal analysis of various mutant alleles as well as RNAi knockdown of these genes.3 Nevertheless, our understanding of how the disturbance of ABP maintenance leads to neoplasia formation, particularly during the early phase, remains largely unknown.

Here, we introduce a protocol for performing conditional RNAi knockdown, specifically targeting scrib, which allows to investigate epithelial morphogenesis with precise spatiotemporal control. By employing this protocol, it is possible to explore phenotypic changes following the induction of RNAi in various genetic backgrounds. By implementing the protocol below, we observe changes in the distribution of Scrib, Hippo signaling executor Yorkie (Yki) signaling, and notable effects on tissue growth and cell migration, which we visualize through GFP expression in the Patched (Ptc) compartment in the wing imaginal discs.1,4 These approaches assist in studying the spatiotemporal role of the gene of interest in tissue morphogenesis, as well as uncovering novel genes that contribute to tissue homeostasis and growth.

Materials and equipment

Incubators

In order to set up the protocol, it is necessary to configure two incubators at different temperatures. The first incubator should be set to 25°C, which represents the optimal temperature for the flies and the subsequent egg-laying step. The second incubator needs to be set to 29°C, as this temperature is required for the conditional knockdown (KD) using the UAS/GAL4/GAL80ts system.

The UAS/GAL4/GAL80ts system functions based on the temperature-sensitive repression of GAL4 by GAL80ts. At lower temperatures, such as 21–22°C or 18°C, GAL80ts binds to GAL4, effectively inhibiting its transcriptional activation. However, at higher temperatures ranging from 27°C to 29°C, GAL80ts dissociates from GAL4, enabling GAL4 to activate transcription.10 By having the incubator at 29°C, we create the necessary conditions for GAL80ts to disassociate from GAL4 and allow it to activate transcription, facilitating the conditional knockdown process.11,12

Note: In some experiments, RT is utilized, which typically falls within the range of 20°C to 21°C.

CRITICAL: Ensuring appropriate humidity levels (40%–60%) within the incubators is vital for optimal experimental conditions. Adding water and consistently monitoring its presence are necessary.

Step-by-step method details

Generation of fly lines

Timing: 4–6 weeks

To investigate the spatiotemporal roles of specific genes within a particular tissue, we employ the UAS/GAL4/GAL80ts system, which provides precise control over gene expression through temperature manipulation. Additionally, we utilize tissue-specific drivers that are accessible in fly genetics. To generate the required fly lines for the experimental setup, a series of meticulously planned crosses is executed in the following manner:

1.To establish the host stock (ex-lacZ, ptc-GAL4, UAS-EGFP/CyO; tubP-GAL80ts/ Tm6B, Sb Tb), perform the following fly crosses:a.In the first cross, obtain female flies with the genotype of Krlf-1/CyO; GAL80ts/Tm6B, Tb.i.Collect female virgin flies with the genotype of Krlf-1/CyO; +/Tm6B, Tb and cross them with +/CyO; tubP-GAL80ts male flies (Figure 2.1).b.In the second cross, obtain flies with the genotype of ex-lacZ, ptc-GAL4, UAS-EGFP/CyO; tubP-GAL80ts/Tm6B, Sb Tb.i.Cross virgin female flies with the genotype Krlf-1/CyO; tubP-GAL80ts/Tm6B, Tb (from step a.i) with male flies with the genotype ex-lacZ, ptc-GAL4, UAS-EGFP/CyO; +/Tm6B, Sb Tb genotype (Figure 2.2).Note: To accurately collect the desired offspring (ex-lacZ, ptc-GAL4, UAS-EGFP/CyO; tubP-GAL80ts/ Tm6B, Sb Tb), ensure to discard flies displaying Krlf-1, which can be identified by their distinct abnormal eye phenotype.c.Select virgin female and male flies with the genotype of ex-lacZ, ptc-GAL4, UAS-EGFP/Cyo; tubP-GAL80ts/Tm6B, Sb Tb and maintain them as the host stock for subsequent experiments.Note: Since the selected offspring (1.c) cannot be identified by phenotype on the third chromosome, it is essential to confirm their genotype of tubP-GAL80ts through an additional cross with a fly stock known to exhibit a strong phenotype. This efficient approach expedites the selection process, allowing for easy identification and isolation of progeny with the intended genotype. However, in the event that the flies become homozygous viable, there is a possibility that certain balancers may be lost over time. To ensure the continued presence of balancers, it is recommended to periodically assess the final host fly lines.Note: To maintain a clean stock, it is advisable to reintroduce the balancers as necessary. Conditional knock-down (KD) of host stocks

Timing: 6–8 days

The application of conditional knockdown (KD) techniques offers a valuable approach to unravel the spatiotemporal roles of genes within specific tissues. By utilizing compartment-specific drivers, it is possible to investigate the mechanisms of intercellular communication operating within that tissue. This method provides a powerful tool to dissect gene functions and understand their contributions to tissue dynamics and development in both physiological and pathological conditions.

Expected outcomes

RNAi knockdown (KD) of scrib and clonal analysis of scrib alleles in Drosophila wing imaginal discs have been extensively employed to investigate scrib as a tumor suppressor gene.1,3,4,6 While these approaches provide evidence that loss of scrib leads to varied phenotypes influenced by environmental conditions, the underlying mechanisms of cellular communication between sub-optimal (loss of scrib) and optimal (wild type) cells, which give rise to diverse outcomes, remain largely unaddressed. Elucidating these mechanisms is particularly crucial for understanding the early phase of neoplasia formation mediated by loss of scrib. Thus, the protocols presented herein assume significant importance in unveiling the mechanisms underlying neoplasia formation.13,14

It is worth noting that conditional KD of scrib alone exhibits oncogenic properties.1,4 Nevertheless, our previous observations indicate that the likelihood of neoplasia formation strongly depends on the levels of scrib expression within the entire tissue.1 For instance, neoplasia manifests in the wing discs after 3 days of scrib KD in animals with 2 copies of scrib. However, animals with only one copy of scrib exhibit neoplasia formation as early as 2 days after KD. Conversely, the presence of an extra copy of scrib (3 copies) confers increased resistance to conditional KD, with no neoplastic tissue observed even after 5 days after of scrib KD.1 These observations suggest that the protocol presented in this article can serve as a valuable screening tool for genes under a sensitized condition.

Limitations

The protocols detailed here have been utilized to study the role of loss of ABP in neoplasia formation within developing Drosophila wing tissue.1 While previous studies have identified molecular pathways implicated in neoplasia formation, the precise temporal changes in tissue morphology and gene expressions have remained elusive due to the analysis of fixed tissue. Overcoming this limitation by developing a live imaging protocol or an ex vivo culture system for the wing imaginal discs could provide valuable insights Troubleshooting 5. Although live imaging of wing imaginal disc poses several challenges, an ex vivo protocol has been reported.15,16 Thus, establishing such approaches may provide answers to unresolved questions using fixed tissue protocols.

The protocol presented in this article offers a convenient tool for investigating genetic interactions with ABP in tissue homeostasis and development, enabling the testing of conditional KD of multiple genes. However, it is important to note that the current protocol only permits KD in the Ptc domain. Thus, to study the molecular mechanisms underlying cellular interactions between suboptimal cells (Ptc domain) and optimal cells (outside the Ptc domain), additional tools are required. A possible approach involves combining different driver systems, such as the LexA/LexAop system, with the UAS/GAL4 system under a conditional inducible system.17 This combination would enable the modulation of gene expression in different compartments, thereby facilitating the investigation of molecular mechanisms governing cellular communication in tissue homeostasis and development.

Additionally, different fly stocks with distinct genetic backgrounds exhibit variations in their developmental time. Minimizing variation in the timing of KD induction will help reduce phenotypic variability and enhance the reproducibility of results.

Problem 5

Lineage tracking and tissue dynamics for finer phenotypic characterization.

While the in vivo conditional KD system and fixed wing disc samples provide valuable insights into phenotypic outcomes at specific time points, gaining a deeper understanding of neoplasia progression, differentiation, cell fate decisions, and cell migration during different stages of development requires more comprehensive approaches. To address these aspects, consider implementing the following potential solutions.

Potential solution

•Lineage tracing: Incorporate different lineage tracing techniques using UAS/GAL4 and Flp/FRT, along with fluorescent proteins such as GFP or mCherry. By labeling specific cells or cell populations at the desired developmental stage, these techniques can offer invaluable information on the dynamic progression of neoplasia phenotypes.15

•Ex vivo system: Implement an ex vivo system to study wing tissue and/or cells. By combining these techniques with lineage tracing and/or time-lapse imaging, researchers can gain insights into how neoplasia occurs independently of the in vivo environment. The ex vivo approach enables more controlled experiments and facilitates the study of cellular behaviors in a simpler environment.16 This allows for detailed phenotypic characterization of neoplastic development.

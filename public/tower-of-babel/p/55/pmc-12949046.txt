Reactive oxygen species-producing genes regulate mosquito midgut bacteria colonization, transcriptomic changes and cell repair
Authors: Baolin Song, Jehan Zeb, Olivier Andre Sparagano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mosquito midgut uses ROS (Reactive Oxygen Species)-producing pathway to control midgut bacteria and maintain cell regeneration. However, it is not clear what are the functional and mechanistic differences between the two components, NOX (NADPH oxidase) and DUOX (Dual Oxidase) enzymes. In this study, we characterize the different roles of Nox and Duox gene in eliminating ECC15 (Erwinia carotovora 15) after oral infection and controlling the microbiome by counting CFU (Colony-forming unit) and 16S rRNA sequencing after RNA interference-mediated knockdown. We also determined the different transcriptomic changes by RNA-sequencing and cell regeneration by immunostaining after knocking down the genes upon ECC15 infection. Here, we report that Nox but not Duox was necessary for eliminating ECC15 and suppressed more rare species in the microbiome. Transcriptionally, we found that Nox controls more gene responses than Duox upon ECC15 infection; the heat shock protein pathway may be the main stress pathway induced by ECC15 that discriminates Nox (highly-related) and Duox. The MAPK pathway was robustly induced in control (LacZ) and Duox-knockdown mosquitoes, but not in Nox-knockdown mosquitoes. AMP gene induction was also suppressed only in Nox-knockdown mosquitoes. We also found that ECC15 infection induced strong cell regeneration, while Nox RNAi suppressed it strongly. Although, Duox was highly related to metalloexopeptidase activity genes comparing to Nox.Subject terms: Mitosis, Transcriptomics, Microbiome, Entomology

Introduction

The mosquito midgut is the first barrier confronting many mosquito-borne disease pathogens, including Plasmodium, dengue virus, and so on1,2. Besides its function of separating pathogens physically and secreting antimicrobial peptides (AMPs), the mosquito midgut creates Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) to kill pathogens efficiently. The ROS is created by Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) Oxidase family enzymes, including NADPH Oxidase (NOX) and Dual Oxidase (DUOX)3. Theoretically, DUOX and NOX may sense cellular stress and pathways related to calcium imbalance4, such as endoplasmic reticulum stress, which are induced by infection or damage5. Structurally, both NOX and DUOX enzymes have EF domains that accept calcium information inside the cell6 and facilitate electron transmission between their Flavin Adenine Dinucleotide (FAD), NADPH binding domains, which allow the transmembrane domain to create ROS, including superoxide and hydrogen peroxide to outside of the cell7,8. DUOX has an additional peroxidase domain that creates more toxic ROS, such as hypochlorous acid9,10. DUOX and NOX are expressed differently in different organs and may have different killing potential based on the location and ROS creation types11–13. Studies are exploring their role against mosquito pathogens or symbiotic bacteria under different conditions.

Duox has a dual role in killing and protecting bacteria. Usually, mosquito ingests pathogens in a blood meal accompanied by peritrophic protein matrix formation and an increase in microbiome diversity14,15. Duox expression increases after a blood meal, and this suppresses the microbiome16. Duox knockdown disrupts the peritrophic protein matrix and then helps maintain pathogenic or conventional bacteria from inducing midgut AMPs instead17. In Drosophila, which don’t have the blood-induced peritrophic matrix, Duox stills have a dual role, combating infection and tolerating symbiotic bacteria10,18 achieved by a feedback loop pathway. In detail, under ECC15 (Erwinia carotovora 15) infection, an insect pathogen, Duox-derived ROS is not induced in the midgut. But it can be transferred to the midgut from the malpighian tubules, and it is necessary for midgut cell repair12. These ROS cause cell damage and activate JAK/STAT and MAPK pathways, which are cellular stress pathways associated with cell proliferation19,20 and inducible by unbalanced calcium from cell damage21. It is not clear so far how Duox knockdown could affect mosquito pathogenic bacteria invasion without the blood meal effect or microbiome and midgut cell regeneration. Nox is also involved in JAK/STAT and MAPK-mediated cell regeneration induced by ECC15 in Drosophila. It usually reacts to broader stresses, including chemical-induced damage and symbiotic bacterial infection directly than Duox9,22,23. In mosquitoes, Nox helps combat viruses and activates the Toll immune pathway24 after a blood meal. It seems Nox also works on mosquito pathogens. But generally, it is not clear what the function of Nox is, the difference between Nox and Duox in confronting pathogenic bacteria infection and symbiotic bacteria. Cell stress pathways, including JAK/STAT, MAPK, and cell regeneration, may be involved.

In this study, we characterized the role of Duox and Nox in eliminating ECC15 and shaping the midgut microbiome. We further determined the transcriptomic changes upon ECC15 oral infection controlled by Duox and Nox and their effect on midgut cell regeneration. We show that Nox, instead of Duox, was necessary for eliminating ECC15 and suppressed more rare species in the microbiome, though both genes affect ECC15 infection and microbiome composition. Transcriptionally, we found that Nox controls more gene responses than Duox upon ECC15 infection; the heat shock protein pathway may be the main stress pathway discriminating Nox and Duox. It may regulate stress signals that induce immune AMP, JAK/STAT, and MAPK pathway genes expression. As a result, only Nox was found necessary for robust cell regeneration after ECC15 infection. Collectively, our study suggests that the Nox instead of Duox gene may be the central coordinator in the ROS-related pathway regulating cell transcriptome, repair, and immunity against both pathogenic and symbiotic bacteria in the mosquito midgut.

Results

Nox (Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate Oxidase) instead of Duox (Dual Oxidase) was necessary for Aedes aegypti midgut ECC15 (Erwinia carotovora15) elimination, and dominant microbe species

Aedes aegypti Nox (NADPH Oxidase) family genes consist of a single Nox and Duox gene. They accept both cellular calcium signals and synthesize ROS. Nox may be induced in broader conditions, while Duox may create much toxic ROS. In order to understand which of them controls pathogenic bacteria elimination in the midgut, we knocked down Nox, Duox, or lacz gene as the control in mosquitoes before orally infecting them with ECC15 (Erwinia carotovora 15) OD600 = 100 and monitored their survival and midgut colony-forming unit (CFU). As shown in Fig. 1A, after Lacz knock-down, midgut CFU started to decrease at 36 h post ECC15 infection, and a lot of them were zero at 96 h post infection. The Nox knock-down group midgut CFUs were maintained at a similar level at 0, 6, 36, and 96 h post-infection. The Duox knock-down group midgut CFUs decreased at 36 h post-infection but were higher than Lacz group. Both Nox and Duox decreased mosquito survival in the following 14 days (around 60% at 14 days) post-infection at a similar level compared to Lacz (around 85% at 14 days). Nox, but not Duox, was necessary for ECC15 elimination. We also determined if either of the two genes could affect the Aedes aegypti microbiome by detecting CFU and survival post-knockdown without infection. Neither Nox nor Duox changed microbiome CFUs very significantly at 72, 96, and 120 h post knock-down, though Duox midgut CFU decreased a little 72 h (Fig. 1C). All three knock-down groups of mosquitoes had similar survival (around 95%, Fig. 1D). So, we further checked if microbiome compositions were changed at 72 h post-knockdown and 48 h by 16s rRNA sequencing, as the CFU change may indicate early compositional change. All mosquito midguts were dominated (above 50% relative abundance) by Asaia bacteria at 48 h post knock-down (Fig. 1E). There was no obvious compositional difference between the groups. At 72 h post knock-down, Duox and Lacz group mosquito midguts were dominated by Serratia or Enterobacteria, while in the Nox group, Asaia remains the main genus, and a lot of low-abundant genera grew (Fig. 1E, Supplementary Data 1). Alpha-diversity analysis by Shannon and Simpson indexes with emphasis on low-abundance and dominant species showed that Nox had higher indexes at 72 h post-knockdown (Fig. 1F) than Lacz and Duox groups. The efficiency of gene silencing was confirmed by qPCR quantification at 24 h (Supplementary F1). In summary, Nox but not Duox was necessary for Aedes aegypti midgut to eliminate ECC15 and maintain a microbiome dominated by some species.

ECC15 induced robust Aedes aegypti midgut transcriptomic changes, while Duox knockdown induced weaker, and Nox knockdown induced the weakest

Nox regulates midgut bacteria elimination strongly; we then determined how it might work by transcriptome analysis. We infected Aedes aegypti with ECC15 OD600 = 100 orally after gene knockdown and checked the midgut transcriptome by RNA sequencing after 24 h. The principal component analysis (PCA) based on reads number matched by genes discriminate ECC15 infected and uninfected mosquitoes (mainly by PC1, 80.2% explained variance, Fig. 2A) and within ECC15 infected mosquitoes by different genes knockdown (Lacz, Nox, Duox, PC1 80.2% explained variance, PC2 11.1% explained variance, Fig. 2A). Between uninfected mosquitoes, gene knockdown didn’t discriminate transcriptomes. In lacz control group, ECC15 infection upregulated 562 genes and down-regulated 270 genes (Fig. 2B). In Duox group, ECC15 infection upregulated 310 genes and down-regulated 128 genes (Fig. 2C). In the Nox group, ECC15 infection upregulated 211 genes and downregulated 58 genes (Fig. 2D, and Supplementary Data 2). In summary, ECC15 infection induced robust mosquito midgut transcriptome changes, both Duox and Nox were significant coordinators for the changes, and Nox was the main coordinator.

Nox was necessary for the ECC15 infection-induced transcriptomic changes related to cell repair

In order to reveal the function details behind the genes regulated, we did Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation based on the genes up- or down-regulated by different group comparisons. Firstly, in Lacz knock-down mosquitoes, the ECC15 infection induced genes functional in aminoglycan catabolic process, cellular modified amino acid metabolic process, DNA replication, response to stress, and so on (Fig. 3A). In Duox knock-down mosquitoes, the response to external stimulus, cellular response to chemical stimulus were exceptionally induced (Fig. 3B). In Nox knock-down mosquitoes, there were also cellular modified amino acid metabolic processes, DNA replication, cellular response to chemical stimulus, but with an apoptotic process additionally (Fig. 3C). The protein metabolism may be the main ECC15-induced response that is conserved in the three groups. Duox and Nox knockdown induced a higher stress response. Nox may be needed to prevent cell apoptosis or damage. We then explored the transcripts copies of genes related to detailed metabolism, different stresses, and cell changes indicated by transcripts per million (TPM) in each mosquito sample. As shown in Fig. 3D, ATP metabolism, indicated by the AMPK gene, lipid metabolism, indicated by the apolipoprotein gene, and amino acid metabolism, indicated by the amino acid transporter gene, were not up-regulated significantly in ECC15-infected mosquitoes for the Duox and Nox knock-down gene groups. Oxidative stress was not induced neither as glutathione transferase genes were also similar between different mosquito groups. Between infected and uninfected mosquitoes, Duox knockdown induced the strongest heat shock protein gene (around ten-fold), followed by Nox (five-fold) and Lacz knockdown (three-fold) under ECC15 infection. Autophagy genes were similar between all ECC15 groups after ECC15 infection, but the highest in the Duox knock-down group (around one-fold). PCNA gene and Dnase gene upregulation indicated that the DNA replication was also induced after ECC15 infection. They were the highest in the Lacz group (five-fold), followed by Duox (four-fold) and Nox knockdown groups (three-fold). As immune reactors, antimicrobial peptidase genes were induced very strongly by ECC15 (around 50-fold) in Lacz group. Duox knockdown decreased the induction (to 15-fold), and Nox knockdown decreased further (to nine-fold). In summary, ECC15 infection caused a strong immune reaction and DNA repair. Duox knockdown suppressed them but induced very strong heat shock protein stress. Nox knockdown suppressed all the pathways strongly induced by ECC15 infection. Nox, but not Duox may be the main stress response coordinator. In order to determine the cell status directly, we checked the EGFR/RAS/MAPK genes as shown in Fig. 3D. ECC15 infection induced around one-fold upregulation of these genes (in Lacz knockdown mosquitoes). There was no significant difference between Lacz and Duox knockdown mosquitoes infected with ECC15 overall, while these genes were slightly induced in Nox knockdown mosquitoes after ECC15 infection. Nox, but not Duox, may be the main inducer for cell repair. We also checked the JAK/STAT, FGR pathways, but they don’t discriminate between Duox and Nox RNAi effect on ECC15-infected mosquitoes (Supplementary Data. 3).

Nox was necessary for the ECC15 infection-induced midgut cell cytoskeleton genes regulation and cell repair

As the results suggest, the cell repair induced by ECC15 infection may be suppressed after Nox knockdown, we checked the most directly related cell cytoskeleton genes and stained midgut cell proliferation by Edu (5-ethynyl 2′-deoxyuridine) and PH3 (phospho-histone3) dyes, and ROS by DHE (Dihydroergotamine dye). As shown in Fig. 4A, the cell cytoskeleton genes, including litaf, tubulin beta chain, formin like were induced around 3–15 fold after ECC15 infection in Lacz and Duox knock-down mosquitoes. The Nox knockdown suppressed their TPM by around half. Transcriptomic comparison between Nox and Duox knockdown under ECC15 infection showed decreased protein metabolism or stress and increased metalloexopeptidases activity. There may be stress-guided cell proliferation induced by Nox. As shown in Fig. 5A, B, after 2 h, ROS was produced more in the Lacz and Duox knock-down mosquitoes than in the Nox group. At 24 h post-infection, ECC15 induced cell proliferation, indicated by Edu and PH3 staining. Duox knockdown was still able to induce robust cell proliferation, while there was minimal after Nox knockdown (Fig. 5C–F). In summary, Nox was necessary for ECC15 infection-induced cell proliferation, it accompanied by the protein response and most upstream ROS changes.

Discussion

The NADPH enzyme-related genes, including Duox and Nox, induce ROS that protects the mosquito midgut from various pathogen infections under different conditions. They produce different types of ROS and may be induced differently. As a result, it may be hypothesized that Duox and Nox have different functions against midgut pathogens. Our project aims to determine (1) which of the two regulates pathogenic or symbiotic bacteria more significantly and (2) how they change ECC15 infection-induced gene expression in the midgut, and (3) which of the two genes is needed for midgut cell repair. We found that Nox was needed for ECC15 elimination after oral infection, while Duox was also helpful for ECC15 elimination. Nox also may suppress conventional bacteriome, especially rare species, though both Duox and Nox had effects. The microbiome CFU based on MRS plates shows no significant difference overall. The mosquito midgut microbiome composition consists mainly of Asaia, Enterobacter, and Serratia. But the results may not reflect the rare species, including Delftia. Nox served as the main coordinator for midgut infection-induced transcriptome and cell repair, too. ECC15 induced immune, protein-related stress, and so on. Although Duox knockdown suppressed the immune reaction, it caused more heat shock protein stress. Nox knockdown suppressed both stress and immune reaction. Subsequently, only the Nox knockdown midgut was not induced with cell proliferation-related genes and was not repaired enough. As the direct product of Duox and Nox, ROS was less produced in Nox knockdown mosquitoes. We hypothesize that Nox is the main stress coordinator that guides cell repair under pathogen infection, which may be achieved possibly through heat shock protein-related pathways and more upstream by Nox-related ROS production.

The function of Duox and Nox in eliminating pathogens has been studied under various conditions. Duox up-regulates following additional commensal bacteria and ECC15 infection16 and was also found be able to sense pathogenic bacteria’s uracil signals23 studied in Drosophila. Duox may also be suppressed on conventional conditions so as to avoid the excessive ROS production that kills commensal bacteria10. In mosquitoes, Duox was necessary to induce protein production that makes the midgut peritrophic matrix after a blood meal17 and the trachea protein matrix25 that prevents basal commensal bacteria from inducing midgut antimicrobial peptidase. It may be hypothesized that Duox can be induced by and kill both pathogenic and commensal bacteria. A medium level of Duox induction helps conventional bacteriome formation. Our results are in accordance with the idea. Duox helped eliminate ECC15 and maintain conventional bacterial load (Fig. 1). More detailed, the Serratia genus was suppressed, and Enterobacter and Asaia proliferated after Duox knockdown. They were all able to colonize mosquitoes and activate the immune system26. The Enterobacter and Asaia seem more stable, and they transferred easily between generations. Nox upregulates along with lactobacilli infection9 and produces ROS in Drosophila. Nox may also act against pathogenic bacteria, and it eliminates them more powerfully (Fig. 1).

NOX in Drosophila and mosquitoes are orthologues of NOX5 in mice that accept calcium information and create ROS27. NOX5 activity can be regulated by heat shock protein 90 (Hsp90)28 and modulate unfolded protein pathways29. Nox5 is highly expressed in proliferating cells, such as in tumors and under inflammation, its downstream regulated pathways may involve the NF-κB immune pathway and the JAK/STAT cell proliferation pathway 30. MAPK, P38-related stress signals are studied in the downstream of Nox in Drosophila22, which were found necessary for the Nox-induced cell proliferation regulation pathways after infection or damage. Our results in mosquitoes are in accordance with these findings. Nox knockdown suppressed pathogenic infection-induced midgut transcriptome most significantly (Fig. 2), heat shock protein genes were less induced compared to Lacz and Duox knockdown groups (Fig. 3). AMPs expression, cell proliferation, and related stress pathway, including EGFR, MAPK, and cytoskeleton remodeling genes, were less induced (Figs. 3 and 4). Mitosis and regenerating cells were the least affected upon infection in the midgut (Fig. 5). As Nox5 binds with Hsp90 for enzyme stability31, it may be that heat shock protein pathways are the direct factor that regulates Nox signals and possibly the direct stress regulator that guides regulating cell metabolism and stress32. How the signals are exacerbated to finally control cell regulation and immune response needs to be further studied. Studies suggest that Duox is not induced a lot upon infection in Drosophila midgut but has a great impact on overall ROS production, which is possibly transmitted from other tissues to midgut12,13, thus affecting midgut renewal. However, in our results in mosquitoes, it increased Hsp induction very significantly (Fig. 3) and suppressed only a little on the EGFR, MAPK, cytoskeleton genes, and AMPs induction (Fig. 4). It is not surprising that Duox RNAi midgut cell regenerates well (Fig. 5) and are similar to Lacz RNAi midgut, though ROS level decreased at some level (Fig. 5). Therefore, Duox and its dependent ROS may not be the central coordinator between pathogenic bacteria and midgut cell regeneration and related stress and immune pathways. However, Duox knockdown decreased metalloexopeptidases’ functions exceptionally (Fig. 4). Previous studies suggested these genes modulate cell motility and maintain gut integrity, and Duox is localized mainly in midgut muscles and airway muscles, where mucins are secreted11. It is possible that Duox in the midgut helps maintain the midgut barrier and cell communication33 instead of inducing immunity and cell stress, and regeneration directly.

Our results provide the transcriptomic information on the effect of Duox and Nox knockdown on the mosquito midgut upon ECC15 infection. We also detected the function of Duox and Nox in controlling pathogenic bacteria and microbiome, and in cell regeneration. Our studies suggest that Nox may work associatively with heat shock protein to induce cell stress, regeneration, and immune genes that finally eliminate pathogenic bacteria, suppress rare bacterial species, and maintain cell regeneration, as summarized in Fig. 6. Duox may not be the central coordinator, while it is functional, it may be related to protein secretion functions, such as metalloexopeptidases, which are important after a blood meal to prevent pathogen infection34. Future studies may focus on the heat shock protein-Nox regulation and Duox-midgut protein matrix effect.

The summary of Duox and Nox function in regulating the microbiome and pathogenic bacteria.

The study suggests that Nox may work in association with heat shock protein to induce cell stress, regeneration, and immune genes that finally eliminate pathogenic bacteria, suppress rare bacterial species, and maintain cell regeneration. Materials and methods

Mosquito rearing

Aedes aegypti Liverpool strain, originally isolated by Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine, female adults at 5–7 days old were used in this study. Eggs were put into one 1-liter water tray with fish food (Hikari #04428) until stage two larvae. Larvae were then selected from it and reared at a density of 200 per 1-liter tray from the second stage to pupation. Adults emerged were put 400 per cage fed on cotton ball soaked with 10% sucrose ad libitum. The adults used for experiments were put into new, smaller cages (diameter 20 cm), 40 per cage. All mosquitoes were reared at 29 °C and 75% humidity condition with a 12:12-h light/dark cycle. Mosquito experiments in this study were performed in three replicates (three continuous generations).

Silencing genes in mosquitoes

Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) was used for gene RNA interference, knocking down. The synthesis of dsRNA was performed using two-steps procedure35. In brief, the T7 promoter primer (Forward, GCCCGACGC, Reverse, CGCCTCGGC) was incorporated onto the ends of gene primers. The primers used include Nox-Forward-GCTCAGCACGCCATTCTCATTG and Nox-Reverse-ACACGGTCACTTTGCCCTTCTT for Nox, and Duox-Forward-GAGCCCGGAGAGTTGTAGTAG and Duox-Reverse-GTTGAGCGCTCCCAGATC for Duox. The PCR product was generated using the mosquito RNA extracted as described later as a template before it was used to process a second-round PCR to expand the T7 promoter using T7-primer-Forward-taatacgactcactatagggGCCCGACGC and T7-primer-Reverse-taatacgactcactatagggCGCCTCGGC. The second round PCR product was then used to synthesize dsRNA in vitro using the MEGAscript RNAi kit (AM1626, Invitrogen, United Stages). dsRNA was concentrated to 5 µg/µL using distilled water. Lacz was synthesized using primer Lacz-Forward-CTGGCGTAATAGCGAAGA, Lacz-Reverse-CATTAAAGCGAGTGGCAA with pCR®II-TOPO® vector (AM1626, ThermoFisher, United States) as the template. The silencing was conducted by injecting dsRNA liquid into the female mosquito thorax with Nanoject II (13-681-455, Drummond, United States). Female mosquitoes were injected with two shots of 69 nl of dsRNA solution of the gene of interest. Mosquitoes were allowed to recover, and RNA interference worked for 48 h until potential microbial infection.

Microbial preparation and infection

ECC15 (Erwinia carotovora carotovora 15) from the American Type Culture Collection was used in this study. The original bacteria were stored in −80 °C with 20% glucose. ECC15 was first inoculated on Luria Bertani (LB) media plate growing overnight at 29 °C for single colony. The single colony was then inoculated into liquid LB and incubated overnight with shaking at 200 rpm to get OD600 = 100, ECC15 10% sucrose infection solution. Then mosquitoes were infected by feeding on cotton balls soaked with the infection solution for 4 h. The mosquitoes were starved for 3 h prior to infection. After infection, mosquitoes were replaced with a normal diet of 10% sucrose.

Microbial load quantification

For CFU measurements after infection, individual mosquito was surface-sterilized with 70% ethanol and crushed in a tube with 500 μL clean PBS solution. For Microbiome CFU measurements, the individual mosquito midgut was crushed into a tube with 500 μL clean PBS solution. After 10,000×, 100×, or no dilution, depending on the experiments, each solution was spiral plated on LB agar (with 100 μg/ml rifampicin, for infection counting) or MRS agar (for microbiome load) using WASP II autoplate spiral plater (WASP2, Microbiology International, United States). Plates were incubated for 24 h at 29 °C, then counted.

RNA, DNA extraction, qPCR quantification

For the RNA used for dsRNA synthesis, RNA sequencing of twenty mosquitoes per condition was collected per replicate. They were dissected for midgut on ice and homogenized together in 500 μL TRIzol (15596026, Invitrogen, United States). The samples were stored at −80 °C before RNA extraction by a phenol-chloroform method as described36. Reverse transcription of the total RNA into total cDNA was performed using the iScript cDNA synthesis kit (170-8890, Bio-Rad, United States). Quantification of RNA abundances of specific genes was accomplished using an iTaq Universal SYBR Green Supermix kit (1725121, Bio-Rad, United States) on the Bio-Rad CFX-96 Touch Real-Time Detection System. Gene expression was normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH, Gene ID: 14433) in murine systems and to β-actin (AAEL011197) in mosquitoes using the 2−ΔCt methodology. The primers used were as follows: β-actin Forward-GAACACCCAGTCCTGCTGACA, Reverse-TGCGTCATCTTCTCACGGTTAG. Duox Forward- CATTCCCCTGGACTCGCAC, Reverse-TCGTGCGATTGGGTGGAC. Nox Forward-GCCGCAAACTGATTTCCTG, Reverse-GTCTTCTTGATGCTGCGTATGC. For 16S rRNA sequencing, mosquito midguts were collected similarly into 500 μL PBS and homogenized. DNA extraction was done using the DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit (69504, Qiagen, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

RNA sequencing process and analysis

The concentration and integrity of taro RNA were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (ND-2000, ThermoFisher, United States). In this study, cDNA libraries were constructed. The constructed libraries were then used to sequencing by HiSeq2500 system (SY–401–2501, Illumina, USA) following the standardized procedures established by the Biomarker Biotechnology Corporation (Beijing, China)37. The reads data output for each sample was processed with in-house Perl scripts to remove adapter contaminants and trim nucleotides. The clean reads were then mapped to Ae. aegypti transcriptome LVP_AGWG AaegL5.2 from vectorbase using salmon version 0.9.1 with default parameters38. All samples have above 93% mapping rate. DESeq was then used for PCA based on read counts for each gene and differentially-expressed gene analysis, and to extract TPM table39. Differentially-expressed genes of above one fold compared between groups and below 0.05 adjusted p value using the False Discovery Rate method were used for GO enrichment analysis with function annotated available in vectorbase using TopGO40. Enriched GO functions under biological process and molecular function with adjusted p value below 0.05 calculated by the classic Fisher test were considered significant. The above-mentioned pipeline was done between two different groups separately.

16s rRNA sequencing process and analysis

The DNA concentration was determined using a Nanodrop 1000 (ThermoFisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE, USA). Amplicon library preparation was performed by PCR amplification of the V3–V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene. Universal primers 338F (5′-ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG-3′) and 806R (5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′), including TruSeq adapter sequences and indices, were used in the PCR41. All libraries were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) at Biomarker Corporation (Beijing, China). Paired reads output was filtered for quality (Q30) and joined by FLASH42. Sequences with read lengths shorter than 400 bp were discarded. The remaining sequences were classified into taxa by aligning against the Ribosomal Database Project (RDP) database at a 97% similarity threshold. Operational taxonomic units with counts greater than 3 in at least one sample were retained. The relative abundance of bacteria at the genus level was used for further PCA and alpha diversity analysis.

Immunostaining

This study used immunostaining for labeling cell proliferation, DNA synthesis, and ROS production. Briefly, adult female mosquitoes were dissected in ice-cold PBS. Midguts were fixed in PBS with 4% paraformaldehyde (J61899.AK, ThermoFisher, United States) for 30 min at room temperature and stored in PBS at 4 °C. For permeabilization, tissues were incubated in PBS with 0.3% Triton X-100 (282103, Sigma, United States) for 15 min at room temperature, then blocked with PBS, 0.1% Tween 20, 2.5% BSA, and 10% normal donkey serum (AB_2337258, Jackson Laboratories, United States) for at least 30 min. For PH3 staining, Samples were incubated with rabbit anti-PH3 antibody (1:500) (06-570, Merck Millipore, Germany). After three 20-min washes in PBS, 0.1% Tween 20, 0.25% BSA, samples were treated with donkey anti-rabbit Alexa Fluor 555 (1:2000) (A-31572, ThermoFisher, United States) for 2 h. EdU-fed mosquito samples underwent Click-iT™ EdU Cell proliferation kit reaction as per the manufacturer’s instructions between permeabilization and blocking steps. For both staining, DNA was counterstained with DAPI (1 µg/ml) (28718-90-3, Sigma, United States), and guts were mounted in Citifluor AF1 antifade medium (17970-25, Electron Microscopy Sciences, United States). Imaging was done on a Zeiss LSM 700 confocal microscope (Zeiss, Germany) and calculated for positive cells. For ROS measurement with DHE, at 2 h post-infection, mosquito midguts were dissected and incubated in 30 mM DHE (D11347, ThermoFisher, United States) for 5 min at room temperature, washed twice with Schneider’s insect medium (S9895, Sigma, United States), and imaged immediately and analyzed by the same confocal.

Statistics and reproducibility

The study involves other statistical difference tests for different CFUs, cell counts, mRNA expression, and mosquito survival curve. The CFUs, cell counts, and mRNA expressions were compared by the Kruskal–Wallis test method. The mosquito survival differences were identified by the Cox method43. P value below 0.05 was considered a significant difference. All of them were done in R 3.7.0.

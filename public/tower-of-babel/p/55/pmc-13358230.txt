Oliceridine compared with sufentanil in multimodal analgesia after renal transplantation: protocol for a single-centre, randomised, double-blind, positive-controlled trial
Authors: Yizhen Li, Ning Bu, Xiaoxiao Li, Dawei Song, Shan He, Dongmei Liu, Pingyi Song, Leilei Pei, Yaomin Zhu, Yansong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMost patients undergoing renal transplantation experience moderate to severe postoperative pain, which may induce physiological stress, exacerbate preexisting complications and impair functional recovery of the transplanted kidney. Although opioids are commonly used for postoperative analgesia, they are associated with various opioid-related adverse events (ORAEs). Oliceridine, a novel G protein-biased ligand μ-opioid receptor agonist, has been shown to provide effective analgesia while reducing the incidence of ORAEs without adversely affecting renal function. Therefore, oliceridine may represent a promising analgesic option for patients after renal transplantation, and clinical validation is warranted to evaluate its safety, efficacy and impact on immune function in these patients.Methods and analysisThis single-centre, randomised, double-blind, positive-controlled clinical trial is being conducted in China. A total of 212 renal transplant recipients requiring patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either oliceridine or sufentanil for postoperative pain management. The oliceridine group will receive oliceridine at 0.3–0.6 mg/h, whereas those in the sufentanil group will receive sufentanil at 1–2 μg/h. Rescue analgesia will be provided if pain is inadequately controlled. The primary outcome is analgesic effective rate 24 hours after surgery. Secondary outcomes will include the (1) numerical rating scale (NRS) scores, cumulative AUC and maximum pain intensity at six time points within 48 hours postoperatively, time to first PCIA, number of effective PCIA presses and dosage of rescue analgesic within 48 hours; (2) occurrence of ORAEs within 48 hours; (3) dosage of antiemetic drugs within 48 hours; (4) satisfaction evaluation with analgesia at 48 hours; (5) renal function assessment at 1–7 days postoperatively, changes in inflammatory factors and postoperative immune indicators; and (6) recovery quality assessment at first day and third day postoperatively.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval was provided by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (XJTU1AF2024LSYY-348) prior to study commencement. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment. Results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at academic conferences.Trial registration numberChiCTR2400091724 (chictr.org.cn).Keywords: Renal transplantation, Pain management, Protocols & guidelines

Introduction

Renal transplantation is established as the gold standard and preferred therapeutic intervention for end-stage renal disease, significantly improving patient quality of life and long-term survival.1 2 The surgical procedure involves technically complex steps such as vascular anastomosis and urinary tract reconstruction. Due to the invasiveness of requiring abdominal incision, tissue traction and peri-renal dissection, renal transplant surgery exerts considerable physiological stress. Patients often present with complex comorbidities and compromised baseline status, contributing to severe postoperative pain.3 Such pain can amplify the surgical stress response, leading to patient distress and agitation, potentially exacerbating pre-existing complications and impairing early graft functional recovery, thereby affecting overall outcomes.4 Furthermore, inadequately controlled pain adversely affects quality of life and increases healthcare costs.5 Thus, effective postoperative analgesia is critical for promoting recovery and optimising clinical results in renal transplant recipients.

The management of postoperative pain in patients with end-stage renal disease presents particular challenges owing to their altered physiology and comorbidities. To improve analgesic efficacy while minimising adverse effects, multimodal analgesia strategies are widely recommended.4 These may incorporate various techniques and medications, such as neuraxial or regional nerve blocks, patient-controlled analgesia, opioids and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), etc.6 However, NSAIDs are generally avoided after renal transplantation because of their association with an increased risk of acute kidney injury.7,9 Opioids remain a cornerstone of perioperative pain management but are linked to opioid-related adverse events (ORAEs), such as nausea, vomiting, constipation and respiratory depression, which may negatively impact graft function and patient survival.10 Opioids act through G protein-coupled μ-opioid receptors, engaging both Gi protein and β-arrestin signalling pathways. Emerging evidence indicates that analgesia is primarily mediated via the Gi pathway, whereas many ORAEs are associated with β-arrestin signalling.11 12

Oliceridine is a novel G protein-biased μ-opioid receptor agonist designed to preferentially activate Gi pathway while minimising β-arrestin recruitment.13 14 Clinical trials have demonstrated its efficacy in providing rapid relief of moderate-to-severe acute postoperative pain,15,17 with a potentially improved safety profile regarding respiratory and gastrointestinal effects compared with conventional opioids.18,24 Importantly, oliceridine lacks active metabolites and does not rely on renal clearance,25 which may offer a distinct advantage in patients with renal impairment. Given these properties, oliceridine represents a promising analgesic option for renal transplant recipients.

Therefore, we designed this single-centre, randomised, double-blind, positive-controlled clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of oliceridine for moderate-to-severe pain following renal transplantation. The study will also assess its impact on renal function recovery and immune markers, with the ultimate goal of establishing a safe and effective analgesic regimen for these patients.

Sample size calculation

After reviewing the literature,26 27 it is assumed that the effective analgesic rate of sufentanil for PCIA after renal transplantation was 90%. The threshold value of non-inferior efficacy test was −12%, the ratio between groups was 1:1, α=0.025 and the efficacy was 0.8. With a 10% dropout rate, each group must include 106 patients, for a total of 212 renal transplant patients.

Randomisation and blinding

Randomisation will be performed by biostatisticians who do not participate in the statistical analysis of data management performed in this trial. Random codes will be generated by simple randomisation method using SPSS software V.25.0, the ratio of test group to control group is 1:1 and envelopes will be prepared for use. For double-blind design, the primary blind code is the corresponding group number for each case (A group or B group), and the secondary blind code is the corresponding treatment (oliceridine group or sufentanil group). (1) Random coding is applied for blindness: a designated research coordinator is responsible for preserving and distributing random numbers; (2) anaesthesia staff are blinded: a person unrelated to the current study completes the PCA preparation; (3) researchers are blinded: researchers responsible for preoperative patient screening and postoperative data collection, follow-up and statistical analysis do not participate in the anaesthesia management of patients and do not know the group assignment of patients; (4) patients are blinded: patients are unaware of their group assignment throughout the perioperative period.

Study interventions

Anaesthetic interventions

Prior to anaesthesia induction, patients will receive penehyclidine hydrochloride (0.5 mg) and dexamethasone (10 mg) intravenously. A standardised ultrasound-guided transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block will then be performed using 20 mL of 0.375% ropivacaine. Anaesthesia will be induced with intravenous midazolam (0.02 mg/kg), etomidate (0.2–0.3 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.5 µg/kg) and cisatracurium besilate (0.15 mg/kg). Following endotracheal intubation, anaesthesia will be maintained with a continuous infusion of propofol (3–6 mg/kg/h), remifentanil (0.1–0.3 µg/kg/min) and cisatracurium (0.1 mg/kg/h), supplemented with dexmedetomidine (0.2–0.7 µg/kg/h) and inhaled sevoflurane (0.5–1%). The depth of anaesthesia will be monitored using the bispectral index, which will be maintained between 40 and 60 throughout the procedure. Mechanical ventilation will be conducted in the volume-controlled mode with a tidal volume of 6–8 mL/kg, a respiratory rate of 12–20 breaths per minute and a positive end-expiratory pressure of 5–10 cmH2O. Ventilatory parameters will be adjusted to maintain an end-tidal carbon dioxide pressure between 35 and 45 mmHg. Before the end of surgery, ondansetron (4 mg) will be administered intravenously for prophylactic antiemesis.

Postoperative analgesic regimens

After surgery, PCIA will be initiated for pain relief. The PCIA regimen for the O group includes oliceridine (30 mg) + appropriate normal saline to a total of 100 mL solution. The PCIA regimen for the S group includes sufentanil (100 µg) + appropriate normal saline to 100 mL solution totally. The initial loading dose is 1–3 mL, the background dose is 1–2 mL/h, the PCA dose is 2–4 mL adjusted according to patients’ basic condition and weight and the lockout time is 10 min. If postoperative pain could not be alleviated, which is defined by NRS score > 3, 50 mg flurbiprofen axetil will be administered for analgesic remedy. If severe postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) occurs, ondansetron 4 mg will be administered for antiemetic remedy (NRS scoring criteria, risk scores and WHO grades for PONV are provided in the online supplemental materials).

Data collection

The observation period ends with 7 days after surgery, unplanned transfer to the intensive care unit or death, whichever comes first. Patient follow-up continues until discharge or death, whichever comes earliest. Demographic information, past medical history, vital signs, anaesthesia records, laboratory tests, drug administration, adverse events, especially ORAEs during observational period, and other clinical outcomes are recorded. Besides, basic characteristic variables including intraoperative vital signs, blood gas analysis, anaesthetic and vasoactive drugs use, fluid input/output, blood loss, urinary output, extubation time, surgical duration, anaesthesia duration, time to urinary catheter removal, time to ambulation, time to oral intake, length of hospital stay, postoperative complications, etc. will be recorded (see table 1).

Adverse events

Adverse events will be monitored during the study procedure. Investigators will record all adverse events, including hepatic dysfunction, severe hypotension and respiratory depression. If those above adverse events occur, bedside physicians may suspend study participation and unblind the participant. In such cases, the adverse events leading to study suspension will be recorded and reported to the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University within 24 hours.

Statistical analysis

Data will be statistically analysed using SPSS V.25.0 and R V.4.2.3. For quantitative data that conforms to normality and homogeneity of variance, the inter-group comparison will be performed using the group t-test; otherwise, the rank-sum test will be adopted. The inter-group comparison of qualitative data will be conducted using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact probability method. Ordinal data will be compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Repeated measurement indicators will be compared through the generalised estimating equation. Except for the non-inferiority test of analgesic effectiveness, which adopts a one-sided α=0.025, other remaining statistical tests adopt a two-sided α=0.05, and p<0.05 will be considered to indicate statistically significant differences between groups.

Patient and public involvement

Prior to the commencement of the trial, discussions will be held with renal transplant surgeons and family members of prospective recipients to assist with the study design and determine the acceptability of the intervention and trial procedures, and their suggestions will be incorporated into the final version of this protocol.

Ethics and dissemination

This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (XJTU1AF2024LSYY-348). The trial was registered at chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2400091724) on 1 November 2024.

Care for donors is as follows: for live donors, the care process includes comprehensive medical and psychological evaluation before donation, specialised medical care during the donation process and postoperative follow-up to ensure their well-being. For deceased donors, we adhere to standardised protocols to ensure dignity and respect throughout the process. We declare that organs/tissue will be sourced ethically, and organs/tissue will not be sourced from executed prisoners or prisoners of conscience or other vulnerable groups.

Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants or their authorised representative, if they are unable to provide consent themselves (see online supplemental file 2 for example consent form).

Important modifications, such as changes to eligibility criteria, outcomes or analyses, will be communicated to all relevant parties (such as investigators, trial participants, trial registries, journals and regulators, etc) as needed via email or in-person communication.

Patient recruitment is expected to start on 1 August 2026 and expected to be completed by 31 December 2027. The study results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at relevant academic conferences.

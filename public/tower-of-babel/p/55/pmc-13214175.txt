Multi-omic profiling reveals Retatrutide alleviates adipose tissue fibrosis via metabolic reprogramming and tissue repair
Authors: Qingyang Li, Weilun Cheng, Jingyan Zhang, Suzhen Ran, Haiqiao Yu, Weiyu Zhou, Yujing Wang, Jindan Lin, Yilin Liu, Xincai Hong, Hongfei Li, Shuang Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRetatrutide, a novel GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptor triple agonist, exhibits unprecedented weight-reducing efficacy in clinical trials, yet the molecular basis of its systemic metabolic benefits remains unclear.MethodsUsing a high-fat diet–induced obesity mouse model, we investigated the effects of Retatrutide on adipose tissue through integrated physiological, histological, transcriptomic, and metabolomic analyses.ResultsRetatrutide markedly reduced body weight and adiposity and improved metabolic parameters including glucose homeostasis and dyslipidemia. Multi-omic profiling revealed a coordinated reprogramming of epididymal white adipose tissue, characterized by suppressed lipogenesis, enhanced fatty acid oxidation and mitochondrial function, restored peroxisomal activity, and downregulated inflammatory/fibrotic pathways while promoting angiogenic/reparative signaling. Metabolomic analysis further demonstrated clearance of lipotoxic intermediates, decreased pro-inflammatory lipids, reduced collagen-derived metabolites, and elevated anti-inflammatory 15-HETE, confirming metabolic restoration at multiple regulatory levels.ConclusionsRetatrutide transforms dysfunctional white adipose tissue into a metabolically competent organ by simultaneously enhancing lipid oxidation, suppressing inflammation and fibrotic remodeling, and restoring endocrine function. These findings identify adipose tissue as an important therapeutic target of triple incretin agonism and establish tissue quality restoration—rather than mere mass reduction—as a new therapeutic paradigm for obesity and related metabolic disorders.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-026-02116-0.Keywords: Retatrutide, Adipose tissue remodeling, Inflammation and fibrosis, Lipid metabolism, Triple incretin agonism

Introduction

The global obesity pandemic continues to drive the prevalence of associated metabolic disorders, including type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. At the core of this pathophysiology lies adipose tissue, a highly dynamic and multifunctional organ that is far more than a passive energy storage depot [1]. In the state of energy surplus, the expansion of adipose tissue can occur through two primary mechanisms: adipocyte hyperplasia and hypertrophy [2, 3]. It is the hypertrophic expansion that is strongly associated with adipose tissue dysfunction [4], characterized by hypoxia, oxidative stress, and a shift in the secretory profile towards pro-inflammatory adipokines and reduced levels of beneficial hormones like adiponectin [1, 5]. This dysfunctional state creates a local and systemic inflammatory milieu, contributing directly to the development of systemic insulin resistance [1, 6]. Therefore, the functional capacity of adipose tissue is now recognized as a critical determinant of metabolic health in obesity [7], shifting the therapeutic focus from merely reducing fat mass to improving adipose tissue quality and function.

Pharmacological strategies for obesity have been transformed by incretin-based therapies [8]. Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, such as semaglutide, achieve significant weight loss primarily through central appetite suppression [9, 10]. The subsequent development of tirzepatide, a dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist, demonstrated that targeting multiple gut hormone pathways could yield superior efficacy [9, 11]. The role of the GIP receptor in adipose tissue, however, remains complex and debated, with evidence suggesting it may promote lipid storage and nutrient partitioning into adipose tissue [12]. This has led to the hypothesis that adding glucagon receptor agonism could counterbalance potential lipid-storing effects by enhancing energy expenditure and promoting lipid mobilization [13]. Retatrutide, a novel triple agonist of the GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors, embodies this advanced design. Its unprecedented efficacy in phase 2 trials—achieving up to 24.2% weight loss in individuals with obesity—suggests a powerful ability to remodel energy balance [14]. While its hypophagic effect is well-attributed to GLP-1 receptor activation [15], the mechanisms through which its triple-receptor activity orchestrates the remodeling of adipose tissue itself, beyond caloric restriction, are poorly understood and likely key to its superior metabolic benefits.

Crucially, the dramatic weight loss induced by Retatrutide must be underpinned by a substantial reduction in adipose mass [16, 17]. This necessitates a direct and coordinated action on adipocyte biology and tissue remodeling. Does Retatrutide simply facilitate a reduction in lipid storage through increased lipolysis, or does it actively promote a healthier adipose tissue phenotype? Potential mechanisms could include the amelioration of adipose tissue inflammation, a reduction in adipocyte size, the induction of adipocyte browning, or the facilitation of lipid turnover. The integrated signaling of GIP and glucagon in adipocytes may be pivotal in determining the net outcome on fat mass and function [18]. However, a comprehensive and direct investigation into the morphological, transcriptomic, and metabolic adaptations of adipose tissue in response to Retatrutide treatment is lacking.

Therefore, to address this critical gap, we designed a comprehensive preclinical study to dissect the specific effects of Retatrutide on adipose tissue in a model of high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity. We hypothesized that the profound weight loss induced by Retatrutide is mediated through a direct and multifaceted remodeling of adipose tissue, leading to improved tissue function and systemic metabolism. Using a therapeutic intervention protocol in HFD-fed mice, we combined physiological phenotyping with in-depth histological, transcriptomic, and metabolomic analyses of white adipose tissue depots. Our aim was to determine whether Retatrutide goes beyond caloric restriction to actively reverse adipose tissue dysfunction by modulating key pathways involved in lipid handling, inflammation, and energy expenditure, thereby providing a mechanistic foundation for its exceptional efficacy.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals and sample collection

All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. Eighteen five-week-old male C57BL/6J mice (GemPharmatech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were acclimated for one week under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions (24 ± 2 ℃, 55 ± 15% humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle). Subsequently, the mice were randomly assigned to either a normal chow diet (NCD, n = 6) or a high-fat diet (HFD, n = 12; D12492, Research Diets), which provided 60% of calories from fat (Fig. 1).

Schematic diagram of the experimental design. Male C57BL/6J mice at 5 weeks of age were randomly assigned to three groups (n = 6) upon reaching 6 weeks of age, which was designated as Week 0. Group 1 (NCD) was fed a normal chow diet throughout the 20-week study. Groups 2 (HFD) and 3 (HFD + Ret) were fed a high-fat diet (HFD, D12492, Research Diets) from Week 0. At Week 10 (red arrow), pharmacological intervention was initiated: Group 3 received subcutaneous injections of Retatrutide (10 nmol/kg) every three days, while Groups 1 and 2 received an equivalent volume of saline vehicle. The experiment concluded at Week 20

After the 10-week dietary intervention, HFD-fed mice were randomized into a vehicle control group (HFD, n = 6) and a treatment group (HFD + Ret, n = 6). The HFD + Ret group received subcutaneous injections of Retatrutide (10 nmol/kg; MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) every three days [19] for 10 weeks, while the NCD and HFD groups received an equivalent volume of saline vehicle. Body weight and food intake were monitored weekly throughout the treatment period.

An oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) was conducted at the completion of the intervention period. The final treatment was administered 48 h post-OGTT. At 72 h post-OGTT, mice were fasted for 12 h (with free access to water) and subsequently euthanized by CO₂ asphyxiation. Blood was collected from the facial vein. Serum was isolated by clotting blood at 4 ℃ for 1 h followed by centrifugation (3,000 rpm, 10 min, 4 ℃), then aliquoted and stored at -80 ℃. Epididymal (eWAT), inguinal (iWAT), and interscapular brown (iBAT) adipose tissues were rapidly dissected, weighed. Part of each tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for morphological examination, and the rest was frozen in liquid nitrogen and preserved at -80 ℃ for later experiments.

Serum biochemical analysis

Serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and fasting blood glucose (FBG) were quantified using an automatic biochemical analyzer (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) and commercial kits (FUJIFILM Wako Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Serum concentrations of fasting insulin, leptin, and adiponectin were quantified using commercial ELISA kits from Crystal Chem Inc. (Downers Grove, IL, USA) and Multi Sciences Biotech Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China), respectively, following the manufacturers’ instructions.

OGTT

OGTT was conducted after the 10-week pharmacological intervention. After a fasting period of six hours, tail vein blood samples were collected to determine baseline glucose levels (time 0). Each mouse then received an oral gavage of glucose at a dose of 2 g per kilogram of body weight. Subsequent blood glucose readings were recorded at 15-, 30-, 60-, 90-, and 120-minutes post-gavage. The glucose tolerance of each animal was quantified by calculating the area under the curve of the glucose-time profile.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining

Epididymal and inguinal white adipose tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated in graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, and embedded in paraffin. Sections of 4 μm thickness were cut and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Digital images of the stained slides were captured using a Pannoramic MIDI scanner (3DHISTECH, Hungary) and analyzed with CaseViewer software.

RNA sequencing and bioinformatic analysis

RNA was extracted from epididymal white adipose tissue using TRIzol reagent (Life Technologies, USA). The RNA yield and purity were measured with a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and integrity was confirmed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Library construction was performed with 1 µg of total RNA per sample. Following mRNA enrichment with oligo(dT) magnetic beads, the RNA was fragmented and used as a template for first- and second-strand cDNA synthesis. The resulting cDNA underwent end-repair, A-tailing, and ligation to NEBNext adapters. Libraries were then purified with AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter, Beverly, USA), PCR-enriched, and their quality was assessed on an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100. Finally, qualified libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) to generate 150 bp paired-end reads.

Raw sequencing data were processed on the BMKCloud online platform. Low-quality reads, adaptor sequences, and poly-N regions were removed to generate high-quality clean reads. These reads were mapped to the mouse reference genome (GRCm39) using HISAT2 software (v2.2.1) allowing for no more than one mismatch [20]. Transcript reconstruction and abundance estimation were performed with StringTie (v2.2.1) using the reference annotation-based transcript (RABT) approach, and gene expression levels were expressed as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM). Differentially expressed genes were identified with the DESeq2 package (v1.38.3) in R [21], applying thresholds of adjusted p < 0.01 (FDR) and |fold change| ≥ 2. The KEGG pathway analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler R package (v3.10.1) [22], and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed with GSEA software (v4.3.2) [23].

Untargeted metabolomics and data processing

Untargeted metabolomics was performed to investigate metabolite composition and abundance differences. For metabolite extraction, approximately 50 mg of frozen tissue was homogenized in 1 mL of a 2:2:1 methanol: acetonitrile: water solution containing 20 mg/L internal standards. The homogenate was subjected to a sequence of vortexing (30 s), magnetic bead disruption (45 Hz, 10 min), ice sonication (10 min), and a 1-hour incubation at -20 ℃. Following centrifugation (12,000 rpm, 4 ℃, 15 min), the supernatant (500 µL) was collected and vacuum dried. The resulting residue was reconstituted in 160 µL of a 1:1 acetonitrile: water mixture and processed through a second vortexing and sonication step. After a final centrifugation, 120 µL of the clarified supernatant was transferred for analysis, with a quality control sample prepared by pooling 10 µL aliquots from each sample.

Metabolic profiles were acquired using a UPLC-MS system composed of a Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS chromatograph interfaced with a Xevo G2-XS QTof high-resolution mass spectrometer. For chromatographic separation, a 2 µL aliquot of each sample was injected onto a Waters Acquity UPLC HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm). The samples were eluted at a constant flow rate of 400 µL per minute with a gradient composed of solvent A (0.1% formic acid in water) and solvent B (0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile) in both positive and negative ion modes. A 12-minute elution gradient was applied as follows: the gradient began at 95% A for 0.5 min, transitioned linearly to 50% A over the next 5 min (0.5–5.5 min), then ramped to 5% A over 3.5 min (5.5–9.0 min). The composition was held at 5% A for 1.5 min (9.0–10.5 min) before returning to initial conditions over the final 1.5 min.

The mass spectrometer was operated in MSe mode, a data-independent acquisition strategy controlled by MassLynx V4.2 software. This mode involves concurrent full scans at low collision energy (0 V, function off) and a high-energy ramp (10–40 V), with a scan frequency of 0.2 s/spectrum. For electrospray ionization, the capillary voltage was set to + 2.5 kV in positive mode and − 2.0 kV in negative mode, while the cone voltage was maintained at 30 V. The source temperature was kept at 100 ℃ and the desolvation gas temperature at 500 ℃, with flow rates of 50 L/h and 800 L/h for backflush and desolvation, respectively.

The Progenesis QI software was employed for processing raw MS data, including peak extraction and alignment. Putative metabolite identification was conducted against the METLIN database and a proprietary library. Following normalization to the total peak area, compounds were annotated using KEGG, HMDB, and Lipidmaps. Multivariate analysis was performed using orthogonal projections to latent structures-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) with the ropls R package. The robustness of the model was evaluated by 200 random permutation tests, and VIP values were determined through cross-validation. Metabolites were classified as significantly altered when VIP > 1, fold change > 1, and p < 0.05. KEGG pathway enrichment of the significant metabolites was analyzed using a hypergeometric test.

RT-qPCR

To validate the reliability of the RNA-seq data, six differentially expressed genes were selected for RT-qPCR analysis. Total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Japan). Specific primers were designed based on the gene sequences (see Table S1). qPCR reactions were performed using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Takara, Japan) on a Archimed R4 Real‑Time Fluorescence Quantitative PCR System (Rocgene, China). β-actin was used as an internal reference housekeeping gene. All experiments were conducted on the same individuals used for RNA-seq, with three technical replicates included for each sample to ensure data accuracy.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (v10.1.2). Biochemical and physiological data were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Differences among the NCD, HFD, and HFD + Ret groups were assessed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Transcriptomic and metabolomic data analysis methods are detailed in their respective sections.

Result

Retatrutide ameliorates HFD-induced obesity and metabolic dysfunction

Retatrutide treatment led to a visible reversal of obesity, as evidenced by representative photographs showing a marked reduction in body size in the HFD + Ret group compared to the HFD group (Fig. 2). The anti-obesity effect of Retatrutide was systematically quantified. At the study endpoint, the HFD + Ret group exhibited a significantly lower terminal body weight compared to the HFD group (Fig. 3A). This final outcome was achieved through a sustained reduction in body weight, as longitudinal monitoring revealed that Retatrutide administration from Week 10 onwards not only halted but progressively reversed HFD-induced weight gain (Fig. 3B). The improvement in overall adiposity was further confirmed by a significant reduction in the Lee’s index (Fig. 3C).

To investigate the mechanism behind this energy deficit, food intake was analyzed. The average food intake during the treatment period was not significantly different between the HFD and HFD + Ret groups (Fig. 3D), suggesting that the sustained weight loss is primarily driven by mechanisms beyond chronic appetite suppression, such as enhanced energy expenditure.

Retatrutide specifically reduces adipose mass and lipid accumulation in white and brown adipose tissue

The reduction in body weight was further investigated to determine its association with a specific loss of adipose mass. Retatrutide treatment induced a visible and specific reduction in adipose mass. Visual inspection of the dissected adipose pads clearly showed that the eWAT, iWAT, and iBAT adipose tissues from Retatrutide-treated mice were markedly smaller than those from the HFD group (Fig. 4A). This macroscopic observation was confirmed by gravimetric analysis, which demonstrated that Retatrutide significantly reduced both the absolute mass and the relative mass (normalized to body weight) of all three adipose depots compared to the HFD group (Fig. 4B).

(B) Absolute mass of eWAT, iWAT, iBAT and relative mass (percentage of body weight) of eWAT, iWAT, iBAT. To explore the morphological changes underlying this mass reduction, adipose tissue sections were subjected to H&E staining. H&E staining revealed that compared to the NCD group, the HFD induced a significant increase in adipocyte size (hypertrophy) and architectural distortion in both eWAT and iWAT. Retatrutide treatment reversed these pathological changes, with a marked reduction in adipocyte size and the restoration of tissue structural integrity relative to the HFD group (Fig. 4C).

Retatrutide ameliorates systemic metabolic dysfunction and improves adipokine profile

To systematically explore the regulatory role of Retatrutide in metabolism-related parameters in HFD-induced obese mice, we measured the levels of ALT, AST, blood lipid profiles, glucose metabolism parameters, and adipokine in mice. Regarding liver enzymes and blood lipids, relative to NCD group, the HFD group exhibited significantly elevated levels of ALT and AST (Fig. 5A, B), along with increased serum concentrations of TC, LDL-C, and HDL-C (Fig. 5C-F). In contrast, compared with the HFD group, Retatrutide treatment led to marked reductions in ALT and AST levels, decreased TC and LDL-C levels (Fig. 5A-F).

For glucose metabolism, the HFD group had significantly higher fasting blood glucose, insulin levels, and HOMA-IR compared with the NCD group (Fig. 5G-I). Results from the OGTT revealed that the HFD group had higher blood glucose values than the NCD group at any time after glucose gavage, with a significantly increased OGTT area under the curve. Relative to the HFD group, the HFD + Ret group showed significant decreases in the abovementioned glucose metabolism indicators (Fig. 5J, K).

In terms of adipokines, the HFD group had lower adiponectin levels and significantly higher leptin levels than the NCD group, whereas Retatrutide treatment reversed the HFD-induced changes in adipokines (Fig. 5L, M). These observations indicate that Retatrutide can effectively ameliorate HFD-induced liver function impairment, dyslipidemia (primarily regulating TC and LDL-C), glucose metabolism abnormalities, and adipokine imbalance in mice.

Transcriptomic profiling reveals Retatrutide-induced metabolic reprogramming in adipose tissue

Given that eWAT serves as a critical visceral fat depot in mice, and its functional impairment is strongly linked to obesity and associated metabolic disturbances induced by a HFD, this study chose eWAT for transcriptomic and metabolite analyses to delineate the regulatory role of Retatrutide in the molecular mechanisms underlying adipose tissue metabolism in HFD-induced obese mice. Initial RNA-seq analysis of 18 samples, performed on the Illumina NovaSeq platform, generated 115.62 Gb of clean data. Each sample generated ≥ 5.56 Gb of clean data, with Q30 scores consistently ≥ 94.29%. Clean reads mapped to the specified reference genome with efficiencies between 89.49% and 97.28%. Based on alignment results, variable splicing, gene structure optimization, and novel gene discovery were performed. A total of 7,966 novel genes were identified, among which 1,969 obtained functional annotations.

Principal component analysis demonstrated clear separation among the NCD, HFD, and HFD + Ret groups in the principal component space, demonstrating clear distinctions in the eWAT gene expression profiles across different groups (Fig. S1). Between the NCD and HFD groups, 3,904 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were detected. Compared with the NCD group, the HFD group showed up-regulation of 2,005 DEGs and down-regulation of 1,899 DEGs (FC > 2, FDR < 0.01) (Fig. 6A). A total of 2,368 DEGs were identified between the HFD and HFD + Ret groups: compared with the HFD group, the HFD + Ret group exhibited up-regulation of 1,125 genes and down-regulation of 1,243 genes (Fig. 6 B ). Enrichment analysis was carried out on these DEGs to identify key biological pathways altered in eWAT following HFD induction and Retatrutide intervention. KEGG pathway enrichment results indicated that DEGs between the NCD and HFD groups were significantly enriched in pathways such as the chemokine signaling pathway, ECM-receptor interaction, lysosome, focal adhesion, tight junction, cell cycle, insulin resistance, glutathione metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, glycerolipid metabolism, and cholesterol metabolism (Fig. 6C). Meanwhile, DEGs between the HFD and HFD + Ret groups were significantly enriched in pathways including the chemokine signaling pathway, focal adhesion, ECM-receptor interaction, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, VEGF signaling pathway, fatty acid degradation, glycerolipid metabolism, TNF signaling pathway, and NF-κB signaling pathway (Fig. 6D). These findings suggest that Retatrutide may ameliorate HFD-induced adipose tissue dysfunction in mice by modulating the biological pathways associated with lipid metabolism, inflammation-immunity, and adipose tissue remodeling.

Receptor expression analysis

To understand the molecular basis of Retatrutide’s direct effects on adipose tissue, we examined the gene expression of its target receptors in eWAT using RNA-seq data (Fig. S2). Compared to NCD group, the HFD group exhibited a significant downregulation of Gcgr (-83%). Conversely, Retatrutide treatment robustly upregulated the gene expression of Gipr and Gcgr by 2.56-fold and 6.78-fold, respectively, compared to the HFD group. In contrast, Glp1r expression in eWAT remained below the detection threshold across all experimental groups, thus being undetectable. This finding is consistent with previous reports indicating that GLP1R expressed at negligible levels in white adipose tissue [24]. Consequently, this observation reinforces the established understanding that GLP-1 exerts its primary metabolic effects through central appetite regulation and pancreatic β-cell function [25].

Retatrutide rescues HFD-induced dysregulation of lipid metabolism in eWAT

Transcriptomic analysis of eWAT revealed a profound dysregulation of lipid metabolism in HFD-fed mice, characterized by a transcriptional shift favoring lipid synthesis and storage while suppressing catabolism (Fig. 7). Specifically, the HFD group exhibited significant upregulation of genes encoding key enzymes involved in de novo lipogenesis (e.g., Fasn, 3.76-fold), fatty acid elongation (Elovl1, 2.20-fold), and desaturation (Scd2, 3.33-fold). Triglyceride synthesis was also enhanced, as evidenced by increased expression of Agpat2, Dgkq, and Dgat2 (2.34- to 3.52-fold). Concurrently, lipid catabolic pathways were extensively suppressed. The expression of pivotal lipolytic enzymes, Pnpla2 (encoding ATGL) and Lipe (encoding HSL), was reduced by approximately 73% and 58%, respectively. The master regulators of fatty acid oxidation, Ppara and Ppargc1a, were downregulated by 70% and 69%, accompanied by decreased transcription of genes involved in mitochondrial (Cpt2, Acadvl) and peroxisomal (Acox1, Ehhadh) β-oxidation. Furthermore, genes critical for peroxisome biogenesis (Pex3, -69%) and mitochondrial membrane integrity (Crls1, -89%) were significantly suppressed.

Retatrutide reprograms key transcriptional pathways in eWAT. Heatmap display of normalized expression (Z-score) for selected genes across NCD, HFD, and HFD + Ret groups

Retatrutide intervention comprehensively reversed this HFD-induced lipid metabolic dysfunction. Treatment not only suppressed the HFD-driven overexpression of lipogenic genes (e.g., Fasn, -22%; Dgkq, -25%) but also potently activated lipid catabolism. The expression of the lipolytic enzymes Pnpla2 and Lipe was restored by 3.24-fold and 2.80-fold, respectively. Critically, Plin5 expression was robustly induced (2.49-fold), suggesting enhanced lipid droplet-mitochondria coupling and mobilization of stored lipids. Retatrutide also significantly upregulated the key transcriptional regulators of fatty acid oxidation, Ppara (2.33-fold) and Ppargc1a (1.56-fold), and restored the expression of genes involved in peroxisome biogenesis (e.g., Pex19, 2.02-fold; Pex3, 1.79-fold) and mitochondrial integrity (Crls1, 3.53-fold). Notably, Retatrutide induced a robust rebound in the expression of several key oxidative enzymes that were profoundly suppressed by HFD, including Eci3 (8.51-fold), Cyp4a14 (5.44-fold), Cyp4a10 (5.05-fold), and Scd1 (4.61-fold). In summary, these transcriptomic data demonstrate that Retatrutide effectively rectifies the HFD-induced lipid accumulation in eWAT by coordinately shifting the transcriptional landscape from a lipogenic, storage-prone state to a catabolic, oxidative phenotype. Consistent with the restoration of lipid catabolic gene expression, GSEA demonstrated significant enrichment of fatty acid metabolism (NES = 1.575, P = 0.0061) and fatty acid degradation pathways (NES = 1.729, P = 0.0020) in the HFD + Ret group compared with the HFD group. These findings confirm that Retatrutide activates coordinated lipid oxidative programs at the pathway level, further supporting its capacity to remodel white adipose tissue toward a metabolically oxidative and energy-dissipating phenotype (Fig. 8).

GSEA enrichment plots showing activation of fatty acid metabolism and degradation pathways in eWAT following Retatrutide treatment

To investigate whether the enhanced energy expenditure is associated with adipocyte browning, we measured the expression levels of browning/beiging marker (Ucp1, Prdm16, Dio2, Cidea) in eWAT (Fig. S3). Compared with the HFD group, Retatrutide treatment did not upregulate Ucp1, Cidea or Prdm16; Dio2 expression was decreased. These results indicate that Retatrutide-induced energy expenditure is independent of adipose tissue browning.

Retatrutide attenuates HFD-induced inflammatory and immune responses in eWAT

The transcriptomic profile of eWAT from HFD-fed mice revealed a pronounced activation of inflammatory and immune response pathways compared to the NCD group. A broad spectrum of pro-inflammatory genes was markedly upregulated (Fig. 7), including members of the tumor necrosis factor superfamily (e.g., Tnf, Tnfrsf1b), chemokines and their receptors (e.g., Ccl2, Ccl3, Ccr3), Toll-like receptors (e.g., Tlr1, Tlr4, Tlr8, Tlr13). Several genes encoding key inflammatory mediators exhibited exceptionally high induction, such asCcr3 (34.6-fold), Cd180 (23.8-fold), and Hck (12.5-fold). Immune cell recruitment genes were also elevated, including macrophage colony-stimulating factor receptors Csf1r (4.47-fold) and Csf2ra (6.43-fold), as well as T cell-related genes Il7r (17.25-fold).

Conversely, the expression of several genes pivotal for immune regulation and signal inhibition was significantly suppressed in the HFD group. This included Nfkbia, an endogenous inhibitor of the NF-κB pathway (down 51%), as well as the key immunomodulatory transcription factors Rorc (down 94%) and Irf4 (down 90%). Retatrutide treatment effectively mitigated this HFD-induced inflammatory state. The expression of the aforementioned pro-inflammatory genes, which were highly upregulated by HFD, was substantially reduced in the HFD + Ret group. Furthermore, Retatrutide intervention restored the expression of the downregulated immunoregulatory genes, including Nfkbia, Rorc, and Irf4. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Retatrutide not only suppresses multiple HFD-activated pro-inflammatory signaling pathways but also restores the expression of critical immunoregulatory genes, thereby effectively ameliorating adipose tissue inflammation.

To further validate the transcriptional remodeling of inflammation-related signaling networks, GSEA was performed. Compared with the NCD group, HFD-fed mice displayed significant enrichment of pro-inflammatory pathways, including cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction (NES = 1.712, P = 0.0015), chemokine signaling (NES = 1.895, P = 0.0016), NF-κB signaling (NES = 1.609, P = 0.0049), and Toll-like receptor signaling (NES = 1.806, P = 0.0016), confirming a strong activation of innate and adaptive immune processes in adipose tissue (Fig. 9). In striking contrast, Retatrutide treatment led to a marked negative enrichment of these same pathways (NES < − 1.8, P < 0.002), indicating a broad transcriptional suppression of inflammatory and cytokine signaling cascades. These results corroborate the observed downregulation of pro-inflammatory genes and demonstrate that Retatrutide effectively reverses HFD-induced immune activation at the pathway level.

Retatrutide promotes adipose tissue remodeling by reversing pro-fibrotic transcriptional programs

Transcriptomic analysis revealed that HFD feeding induced a comprehensive pro-fibrotic gene expression signature in eWAT. This was characterized by a concerted upregulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) components and pro-fibrotic signaling pathways (Fig. 7). Specifically, the HFD group exhibited significant induction of genes encoding various collagen subtypes (most notably Col1a1, 8.5-fold), other critical ECM glycoproteins and adhesion molecules (e.g., Spp1, 13.7-fold; Cd44, 12.8-fold), and core components of the pro-fibrotic TGF-β signaling pathway (e.g., Tgfb1, Tgfbr1). Concurrently, the focal adhesion pathway was hyperactivated, as evidenced by the upregulated expression of key adhesion and cytoskeletal linker proteins, including Actn1, Tln1, and Rac2 (3.1- to 8.5-fold), suggesting enhanced cell-ECM adhesion signaling under HFD. In stark contrast, the expression of key gene families essential for maintaining tissue homeostasis was broadly suppressed. Members of the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family and anti-fibrotic bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) family members (Bmp2, Bmp4, Bmp6) were significantly downregulated by approximately 63% to 73% in the HFD group.

Retatrutide treatment effectively normalized this HFD-induced transcriptomic landscape. It not only suppressed the aberrant activation of the ECM, TGF-β, and focal adhesion pathways but also restored the expression of VEGF and BMP family genes that are crucial for tissue homeostasis. Notably, the expression of Vegfa and Bmp4 was increased by 6.7-fold and 2.7-fold, respectively, in the HFD + Ret group compared to the HFD group. These transcriptomic findings demonstrate that Retatrutide facilitates the remodeling of dysfunctional adipose tissue by coordinately inhibiting pro-fibrotic and adhesion signals while reinstating pro-homeostatic gene programs, thereby counteracting the fibrotic effects of a high-fat diet.

Validation of RNA-seq results using RT-qPCR

To validate the accuracy of the RNA-seq data, six genes (Pnpla2, Lipe, Plin5, Csf1r, Col1a1, and Bmp4) were randomly selected for qPCR analysis. The qPCR results exhibited expression trends consistent with those of the RNA-seq data, confirming the reliability of the sequencing results (Fig. 10).

Retatrutide reverses HFD-induced metabolomic dysregulation in eWAT

To elucidate the impact of HFD on the metabolic profile of eWAT and the interventional effects of Retatrutide, a non-targeted metabolomics analysis was performed. Analysis of 18 samples on an LC-QTOF platform detected 15,377 peaks, with 2,766 metabolites annotated. OPLS-DA revealed a clear separation between the HFD and NCD groups (R2Y = 0.993, Q2 = 0.817) (Fig. 11A), indicating profound HFD-induced metabolic perturbations in eWAT. Crucially, the HFD + Ret group was also distinctly separated from the HFD group (R2Y = 0.993, Q2 = 0.676) (Fig. 11B). Permutation tests confirmed the robustness and reliability of these models without overfitting, demonstrating that Retatrutide effectively counteracts the HFD-induced alterations in the eWAT metabolome.

Differential metabolites were identified using VIP value ≥ 1 and a p-value < 0.05. Compared to the NCD group, the HFD group exhibited 312 significantly altered metabolites (172 up-regulated and 140 down-regulated) (Fig. 12A). In stark contrast, Retatrutide intervention induced 373 significant changes relative to the HFD group, with the vast majority (331 metabolites) being significantly down-regulated, and only 42 being up-regulated (Fig. 12B). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that HFD-induced differential metabolites were primarily enriched in pathways including glutathione metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, ferroptosis, fatty acid degradation, and tryptophan metabolism (Fig. 12C). Retatrutide treatment, however, significantly modulated distinct pathways such as arachidonic acid metabolism, sphingolipid metabolism, and lysine degradation. Importantly, it also potently reversed the HFD-induced dysregulation in key pathways like glycerophospholipid metabolism, glutathione metabolism, and arginine and proline metabolism (Fig. 12D). These metabolomic findings are consistent with the prior transcriptomic data, collectively confirming that HFD induces profound disturbances in lipid metabolism, oxidative stress, and inflammatory pathways in eWAT, and that Retatrutide treatment effectively normalizes these pathological metabolic states.

Retatrutide restores lipid oxidation and alleviates lipotoxic stress

Non-targeted metabolomic analysis revealed that HFD feeding significantly perturbed lipid oxidation pathways in eWAT. The β-oxidation pathway was inhibited, as evidenced by the marked accumulation of key intermediates, including various acyl-carnitines (Fig. 13), 3-Oxohexadecanoic acid (1.9-fold), and (R)-3-Hydroxy-Octadecanoic acid (1.6-fold). Similarly, the alternative α-oxidation pathway was compromised, indicated by the accumulation of its metabolic intermediate, DL-2-hydroxy stearic acid.

Heatmap visualization of the relative abundance of key metabolites in eWAT across the NCD, HFD, and HFD + Ret groups

Retatrutide treatment effectively reactivated these impaired lipid oxidation pathways. It facilitated the clearance of the accumulated intermediates from both β- and α-oxidation. The disruption of these oxidation pathways under HFD led to a pronounced accumulation of upstream lipids and stress-related molecules (Fig. 13). These included the phospholipid precursor PA(22:1(13Z)/19:2(10Z,13Z)), the free fatty acid Ricinoleic acid (5.1-fold), the oxidative stress marker 7-Oxo-11-dodecenoic acid, and monoacylglycerols, most notably MG(20:3(6,8,11)-OH(5)/0:0/0:0), which increased dramatically by 15.6-fold. Retatrutide intervention comprehensively reversed this lipotoxic and oxidative stress profile. It significantly reduced the levels of MG(20:3(6,8,11)-OH(5)/0:0/0:0) and Ricinoleic acid by approximately 76% and 81%, respectively, and effectively cleared other stress markers. Furthermore, the level of a specific diacylglycerol, DG (14:1(9Z)/22:1(13Z)/0:0), which was downregulated in the HFD group, was further reduced by Retatrutide treatment.

Retatrutide modulates the landscape of inflammation-related metabolites

Metabolomic profiling further elucidated the impact of Retatrutide on the inflammatory milieu within eWAT. HFD feeding induced a marked accumulation of pro-inflammatory and metabolic stress products (Fig. 13). This was evidenced by significantly elevated levels of the pro-inflammatory lipid 9,10-DiHOME (8.0-fold), the oxidative stress marker glutathione disulfide (1.8-fold), and downstream products of the tryptophan-kynurenine inflammatory pathway, namely quinolinic acid (5.0-fold) and indole (4.0-fold). Concurrently, the HFD group exhibited a broad suppression of the arachidonic acid metabolic pathway. The levels of numerous mediators within this pathway were significantly reduced (Fig. 13). Notably, the level of (±)15-HETE, a key precursor for pro-resolving mediators, was severely suppressed by approximately 94%, alongside a 60% reduction in its downstream anti-inflammatory metabolite, 5(S),6(S)-Lipoxin A4. Retatrutide treatment effectively counteracted this HFD-induced inflammatory metabolomic profile. It significantly reduced the levels of the HFD-upregulated pro-inflammatory metabolites, including 9,10-DiHOME, glutathione disulfide, quinolinic acid, and indole. A notable and specific regulatory effect was observed within the arachidonic acid pathway. While Retatrutide treatment generally led to a further downregulation of most mediators in this pathway, it powerfully and selectively upregulated the level of (±)15-HETE, increasing it by more than 6-fold compared to the HFD group. These findings suggest that Retatrutide ameliorates adipose tissue inflammation not only by suppressing classical pro-inflammatory signals but also through a nuanced, differential regulation of the arachidonic acid metabolic network, potentially steering it towards a pro-resolving state.

Retatrutide reverses the pro-fibrotic metabolomic profile in adipose tissue

Metabolomic analysis revealed a distinct pro-fibrotic metabolic signature in the eWAT of HFD-fed mice compared to the NCD group. This signature was characterized by a significant accumulation of key amino acids integral to collagen biosynthesis (Fig. 13). Most notably, the level of trans-3-Hydroxy-L-proline, a biochemical marker of collagen biosynthesis,, was drastically increased by 4.60-fold. The levels of glycine and L-proline were also elevated by 4.15-fold and 1.41-fold, respectively. Concurrently, the levels of several lysophospholipids known to activate fibroblasts, including specific lysophosphatidylcholines (LysoPCs) and lysophosphatidylinositols (LysoPIs), were significantly elevated. Retatrutide treatment robustly reversed this pro-fibrotic metabolic state. It markedly suppressed the accumulation of collagen-related amino acids, reducing the levels of trans-3-Hydroxy-L-proline and glycine to just 8% and 18% of those in the HFD group, respectively. Retatrutide also significantly lowered the levels of the aforementioned fibroblast-activating signaling lipids. Furthermore, Retatrutide exerted a dual regulatory effect on eicosanoid-bound phospholipids. While it further downregulated, lipids associated with the pro-fibrotic thromboxane pathway, such as PE (20:0/TXB2), it simultaneously and significantly upregulated phospholipids conjugated with the anti-fibrotic prostacyclin. For instance, the level of PC (22:3(10Z,13Z,16Z)/6 keto-PGF1alpha) was increased by 1.87-fold compared to the HFD group. This upregulation presents a striking contrast to the observed suppression of the free form of 6-Keto-PGF1α. In summary, these metabolomic findings indicate that Retatrutide not only suppresses pro-fibrotic metabolites but may also actively promote tissue protection and repair through the selective remodeling of the eicosanoid-phospholipid landscape.

Discussion

The present study demonstrates that Retatrutide, a triple agonist of the GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors, induces profound adipose tissue remodeling and systemic metabolic improvement in diet-induced obese mice. Our integrated phenotypic, histological, transcriptomic, and metabolomic analyses reveal that the remarkable efficacy of Retatrutide extends beyond appetite suppression to encompass pronounced adipose tissue remodeling. This remodeling involves enhanced lipid oxidation, reduced inflammation and fibrosis, and restoration of adipose metabolic flexibility. Furthermore, metabolomic data corroborate these findings by showing the reactivation of fatty acid oxidation pathways and the reduction of lipotoxic intermediates. These results position adipose tissue as an important effector organ in the therapeutic mechanism of triple receptor agonism.

Mechanistic basis of retatrutide’s metabolic efficacy

Incretin-based pharmacotherapies have revolutionized obesity treatment, beginning with GLP-1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide and extending to dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists like tirzepatide [26, 27]. The rationale for adding glucagon receptor agonism arises from its distinct capacity to enhance energy expenditure, lipolysis, and thermogenesis [13]. Retatrutide integrates these three hormonal axes into a single molecule, yielding unprecedented weight reduction in clinical trials—up to 24.2% in adults with obesity [14]. However, the cellular basis of this efficacy has remained incompletely defined.

Our data suggest that Retatrutide’s therapeutic potency results from a synergistic interplay between central and peripheral mechanisms. While transient anorectic effects likely contribute to initial weight loss, the sustained reduction in adiposity and the unchanged food intake observed during chronic treatment indicate a dominant role for peripheral energy expenditure. The observed upregulation of oxidative and lipolytic gene networks, together with metabolomic signatures indicating enhanced β-oxidation, reduced accumulation of acyl-carnitines, and restoration of peroxisome activity, support the notion that Retatrutide is associated with pronounced adipose metabolic reprogramming toward a catabolic state. Notably, the induction of Plin5 suggests improved coupling between lipid droplet lipolysis and mitochondrial oxidation, allowing fatty acids to be efficiently directed to energy metabolism rather than accumulating as toxic intermediates. This aligns with recent reports that triple agonists enhance mitochondrial respiration and lipid utilization in adipose tissue and liver beyond the effects of caloric restriction alone [14, 19, 28].

Adipose tissue as an important therapeutic target of triple receptor agonism

Visceral adipose tissue, particularly epididymal white adipose tissue (eWAT), plays a pivotal role in systemic metabolic homeostasis, serving as both an energy reservoir and an endocrine organ [29]. Unlike subcutaneous adipose tissue, eWAT is disproportionately affected and exhibits pronounced metabolic dysfunction, inflammation, and fibrosis in obesity [30]. In obesity, hypertrophic expansion of adipocytes leads to hypoxia, oxidative stress, and the release of pro-inflammatory adipokines, thereby promoting insulin resistance and ectopic lipid deposition [31]. Consequently, we focused our analysis on eWAT and demonstrate that Retatrutide reverses this dysfunctional phenotype through coordinated changes in gene expression governing lipid turnover, mitochondrial biogenesis, and extracellular matrix remodeling.

The activation of Pnpla2 (ATGL), Lipe (HSL), and Plin5 indicates accelerated lipolysis and fatty acid mobilization. Simultaneous induction of Ppara and Ppargc1a underscores enhanced mitochondrial oxidative capacity, while restoration of Pex3 and Crls1 points to improved peroxisomal and mitochondrial integrity. Complementary metabolomic findings, including decreased carnitine derivatives associated with fatty acid oxidation, glycerolipids and monoacylglycerols, further support enhanced oxidative metabolism. These molecular events collectively shift adipocytes from a lipid-storing to an energy-dissipating phenotype. Similar findings were reported for glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonists, where increased fatty acid oxidation in white adipose tissue contributes significantly to energy expenditure [32, 33]. Retatrutide appears to amplify this effect through the added GIP component, which may optimize nutrient partitioning and enhance lipid flux into oxidative pathways rather than storage [12].

Notably, this GIP-related enhancement of energy expenditure is not dependent on classical adipose tissue browning, as we did not observe upregulation of browning/beiging signature genes (Ucp1, Prdm16, Dio2, Cidea) in eWAT. This aligns with Yu et al. [34], who demonstrated that GIP receptor activation can drive energy expenditure through UCP1-independent mechanisms such as futile calcium cycling.

Crosstalk between adipose remodeling and systemic metabolism

The metabolic benefits observed at the whole-body level—including improved glucose tolerance, decreased hepatic enzyme levels, and normalization of circulating lipids—are likely secondary to the restoration of adipose tissue function. Dysfunctional adipose tissue acts as a source of free fatty acids, cytokines, and stress signals that impair hepatic and muscular insulin sensitivity [35]. By normalizing lipid turnover and reducing inflammation, Retatrutide alleviates this lipotoxic load, thereby improving systemic insulin signaling.

Moreover, the recovery of adiponectin secretion observed in Retatrutide-treated mice is mechanistically relevant. Adiponectin enhances skeletal muscle fatty acid oxidation and glucose uptake through AMPK activation, while lowering hepatic gluconeogenesis [36]. The concurrent decrease in leptin levels reflects reduced adipocyte stress and improved leptin sensitivity, further contributing to appetite regulation and energy homeostasis [37]. These endocrine adaptations may explain why Retatrutide’s efficacy surpasses that of dual agonists in both preclinical and clinical settings. Additionally, our metabolomic analysis revealed a marked decline in pro-inflammatory lipid mediators such as 9,10-DiHOME and quinolinic acid, alongside an increase in anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving lipids including (±)15-HETE. This biochemical evidence strongly supports that Retatrutide not only suppresses inflammation transcriptionally but also actively restores lipid mediator balance, contributing to immune resolution. Thus, the reestablishment of adipose immune–metabolic homeostasis is both molecular and metabolic in nature.

Anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic mechanisms

Although retatrutide treatment resulted in pronounced weight loss, prior studies have shown that obesity-associated immune, metabolic, and fibrotic signatures in epididymal adipose tissue can persist despite substantial weight loss, indicating that adipose tissue remodeling cannot be fully explained by weight loss [38, 39]. This persistence suggests that the observed histological improvements likely reflect active tissue remodeling rather than merely the passive consequences of decreased fat mass.

Chronic low-grade inflammation within adipose tissue is a hallmark of obesity-associated metabolic disease [40]. Retatrutide significantly reduced the expression of genes involved in TNF, TLR, and NF-κB signaling, while restoring negative regulators such as Nfkbia, Rorc, and Irf4. This indicates both suppression of innate immune activation and reinforcement of immunoregulatory pathways. Such dual modulation resembles the anti-inflammatory effects reported for GLP-1 receptor agonists, which inhibit NF-κB activation and macrophage infiltration [41]. The additive influence of GIP and glucagon signaling may further enhance macrophage polarization toward an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype [32, 42].

Beyond inflammation, fibrosis represents another critical barrier to adipose tissue recovery. Excessive extracellular matrix deposition limits adipocyte plasticity and contributes to insulin resistance [43]. The Retatrutide downregulated key profibrotic mediators, including Col1a1, Spp1, and Tgfb1, while upregulating angiogenic (Vegfa) and anti-fibrotic (Bmp4, Bmp6) genes. Importantly, metabolomic data revealed concurrent reductions in collagen biosynthesis intermediates (trans-3-hydroxy-L-proline, glycine, L-proline) and fibroblast-activating lysophospholipids, supporting that Retatrutide induces coordinated suppression of pro-fibrotic programs at both transcriptional and metabolic levels. This transcriptomic–metabolomic concordance implies that Retatrutide not only suppresses extracellular matrix–related biosynthetic and remodeling programs, but also promotes a tissue environment permissive for vascular remodeling and tissue repair. Enhanced angiogenesis may improve oxygenation, mitigate hypoxia-driven inflammation, and sustain long-term adipose function [44].

It should be noted that fibrotic regulation is a multi-layered process involving post-transcriptional control, extracellular matrix deposition, and tissue architecture, which are not fully captured by transcriptomic analyses alone. While the integrated transcriptomic, metabolomic, and histological evidence presented here supports an attenuation of pro-fibrotic remodeling following Retatrutide treatment, our data do not provide direct quantitative measurements of collagen content or protein-level validation of extracellular matrix components. Future studies incorporating biochemical collagen assays, protein-based analyses, and high-resolution histomorphometric quantification will be required to definitively establish fibrosis resolution.

Integration of receptor signaling and pharmacodynamic implications

The pleiotropic actions of Retatrutide can be rationalized by the complementary physiological functions of its three receptor targets. GLP-1 receptor activation primarily exerts central anorectic and pancreatic effects, whereas GIP receptor engagement modulates lipid storage and insulin secretion [12, 45–47]. Glucagon receptor activation, on the other hand, stimulates hepatic glucose output but also promotes lipid oxidation and thermogenesis [48]. When co-activated, these pathways yield a harmonized metabolic response characterized by enhanced energy utilization, reduced fat mass, and improved glycemic control. Consistent with this, Retatrutide significantly upregulated Gipr and Gcgr in eWAT in our study, supporting direct receptor-mediated adipose tissue effects.

Preclinical studies have shown that balanced activation of these receptors avoids the hyperglycemic risk associated with glucagon monotherapy while preserving its lipolytic benefits [19]. The transcriptional upregulation of Ppara and Ppargc1a observed here reflects glucagon-driven mitochondrial activation [49], while the downregulation of lipogenic genes (Fasn, Scd2) may stem from GIP receptor activation in adipocytes, redirecting nutrient flux away from storage. Our integrated omics analysis supports this receptor synergy by showing consistent downregulation of metabolic intermediates of fatty acid oxidation as well as pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic metabolites, confirming a unified pharmacodynamic effect across molecular and metabolic layers. From a pharmacodynamic standpoint, adipose tissue likely serves as both a metabolic sink and a signaling hub mediating Retatrutide’s systemic benefits. The restoration of adipose function not only enhances lipid clearance but also reduces hepatic steatosis and pancreatic stress, potentially explaining the improvements in liver enzymes (ALT, AST) and glucose tolerance observed in our study.

It should be noted that, despite the receptor expression changes observed in adipose tissue, the present study was conducted entirely in vivo. Given the systemic nature of incretin signaling and the extensive inter-organ communication involved in metabolic regulation, the transcriptional and metabolomic remodeling observed in adipose tissue may reflect a combination of local adipose responses and indirect effects mediated by other tissues, including the central nervous system, liver, and skeletal muscle. Therefore, while our data demonstrate robust adipose tissue remodeling following Retatrutide treatment, they do not by themselves establish direct cell-autonomous effects on adipocytes or adipose-resident immune cells.

Translational and therapeutic perspectives

Despite the robust adipose tissue remodeling observed in this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. Retatrutide treatment was administered for a prolonged duration and resulted in marked reductions in body weight and adiposity. Therefore, it cannot be fully excluded that some of the transcriptional, metabolic, and anti-fibrotic changes observed in adipose tissue may represent secondary adaptations to reduced fat mass rather than exclusively direct pharmacological effects. Nevertheless, multiple observations argue against a purely weight loss–dependent mechanism. Chronic Retatrutide administration did not induce sustained suppression of food intake, suggesting that adipose remodeling occurred independently of prolonged caloric restriction. Moreover, the highly coordinated activation of lipid oxidative pathways, restoration of mitochondrial and peroxisomal gene networks, selective induction of pro-resolving lipid mediators (such as (±)15-HETE), and concurrent suppression of inflammatory and fibrotic signaling pathways indicate an active and structured reprogramming of adipose tissue metabolism that is unlikely to arise from weight reduction alone. Future studies incorporating short-term intervention protocols, pair-fed or weight-matched control groups, and adipose tissue–specific receptor manipulation will be essential to further delineate the relative contributions of direct receptor-mediated actions versus secondary effects of adiposity reduction. Such approaches will help refine the mechanistic understanding of triple incretin receptor agonism and clarify the extent to which adipose tissue serves as a primary pharmacological target of Retatrutide.

Our findings offer mechanistic insights with direct translational relevance. The ability of Retatrutide to reverse adipose tissue dysfunction suggests its potential utility not only in obesity but also in related metabolic disorders characterized by ectopic lipid deposition, such as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [14, 50]. Furthermore, the suppression of adipose inflammation and fibrotic remodeling may provide long-term protection against weight regain and metabolic relapse, common limitations of current anti-obesity therapies [51].

Nevertheless, several questions remain. The receptor-specific contributions within adipose tissue—particularly the roles of GIPR and GCGR signaling in adipocytes and immune cells—require further clarification using tissue-specific knockout models. Additionally, longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether prolonged Retatrutide treatment leads to adaptive metabolic responses, such as thermogenic compensation or receptor desensitization. Although eWAT represents a metabolically critical depot highly relevant to obesity-associated disorders, our focus on this single depot is a limitation. Retatrutide likely also exerts effects on multiple tissues, including subcutaneous adipose tissue, liver, skeletal muscle, and pancreas, highlighting the need for future comprehensive multi-tissue analysis. Finally, given the robust adipose remodeling observed here, it will be essential to evaluate whether similar molecular and metabolic adaptations occur in human adipose depots, which differ from rodents in distribution and responsiveness.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that Retatrutide exerts its metabolic benefits through a coordinated reprogramming of adipose tissue biology at both transcriptional and metabolic levels. By enhancing lipid oxidation, suppressing inflammation and fibrosis, and restoring endocrine function, Retatrutide transforms adipose tissue from a dysfunctional energy store into a metabolically competent organ. These multi-omic findings provide a comprehensive mechanistic foundation for the exceptional clinical efficacy of Retatrutide and highlight adipose tissue quality restoration as a cornerstone for next-generation anti-obesity therapies.

Conclusions

In summary, this study provides comprehensive mechanistic insights into how Retatrutide, a triple agonist of the GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors, exerts potent anti-obesity and metabolic benefits through coordinated molecular and metabolic remodeling of adipose tissue. Beyond its central anorectic effects, Retatrutide promotes a coordinated reprogramming of white adipose tissue characterized by enhanced fatty acid oxidation, clearance of lipotoxic intermediates, restored mitochondrial integrity, and marked suppression of chronic inflammation and fibrotic remodeling. These adipose-specific adaptations translate into systemic metabolic improvements, including improved glucose tolerance, lipid homeostasis, and hepatic function. Importantly, multi-omic integration revealed that these effects are accompanied by decreased pro-inflammatory lipids, reduced collagen-derived metabolites, and increased levels of pro-resolving mediators, confirming metabolic restoration at multiple regulatory layers. The integrated activation of GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptor pathways enables Retatrutide to achieve balanced energy expenditure and metabolic reprogramming without the compensatory stress responses typically associated with isolated glucagon receptor activation. Our findings establish adipose tissue as an important pharmacological target for triple receptor agonism and provide a mechanistic basis for its superior therapeutic efficacy compared with single or dual receptor agonists. By transforming dysfunctional adipose tissue into a metabolically competent and insulin-sensitive organ, Retatrutide redefines adipose tissue quality as a new therapeutic endpoint in obesity management. Future studies should delineate cell type–specific receptor signaling within adipose depots and assess the long-term metabolic, cardiovascular, and endocrine outcomes of Retatrutide treatment in clinical populations. Overall, Retatrutide represents a promising next-generation incretin therapy capable of restoring both the quantity and quality of adipose tissue, thereby reshaping the therapeutic landscape for obesity and its metabolic complications.

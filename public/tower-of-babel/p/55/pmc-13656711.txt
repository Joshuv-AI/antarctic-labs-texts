Exploration of viral diversity in Aedes mosquitoes employing different shotgun metagenomic data analysis pipelines
Authors: Ekta Gupta, Shashi Sharma, Sachin Narayan Tikar, Paban Kumar Dash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMetagenomics or metaviromics is emerging as a powerful technology for pathogen surveillance and pandemic preparedness. Mosquitoes are important vectors for transmission of many emerging viruses responsible for numerous outbreaks. Monitoring mosquitoes becomes essential to investigate its virome which leads to understanding of disease dynamics and allow preventive actions. In this study, shotgun metagenomic methodology using Ion GeneStudio S5 System was optimized for exploration of viral diversity. A total of 1913 Aedes larvae were collected from Central India during post monsoon season of 2024. Aedes larvae reared to adulthood and processed for sequencing using Ion Torrent S5 platform. Computational analyses were performed using three bioinformatic pipelines: Chan Zuckerberg ID (CZ ID), Genome Detective Platform and the Galaxy Platform. A mock database of 11 known viruses was created as well as publicly available NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) datasets were used to validate all three pipelines. In terms of detection accuracy, Genome Detective and CZ ID performed exceptionally well and therefore may be suitable for future mosquito virome surveillance studies. We found presence of viruses viz. Alphamesonivirus cavallynense and Phasivirus phasiense dominating in all samples. Dengue virus was detected in one sample by CZ ID, whereas Wenzhou sobemo-like virus, Hubei mosquito virus 2, Cell fusing agent virus found most commonly among samples. Other viruses found like Aedes anphevirus (AeAV; genus Glybovirus), Aedes totivirus, Verdadero virus and Chaq-like virus. Variation in the result among different pipelines are likely attributable to incorporation of different viral reference databases, classification algorithms, metrics and analysis parameters. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first metagenomic study of mosquitoes using Ion GeneStudio S5 platform in India. The findings provide a comparative evaluation of the metagenomic pipelines and elucidates detailed information of each pipeline and its working for future studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12985-026-03249-4.

Introduction

Viruses are the most abundant biological entities on earth. Metagenomic approaches in last few decades have immensely contributed in broadening knowledge of viral taxonomy, evolution, transmission and other characteristics. These approaches typically involve either targeted sequencing of marker gene, such as 16 s rRNA sequencing for bacteria or unbiased random shotgun sequencing. Traditionally, viruses were discovered by studying the host virus interactions like observing disease symptoms, host specificity, microscopy, serological assays and virus isolation through cell culturing. However, unlike bacteria, viruses lack universal gene marker for identification, certain conserved hallmark genes in studies have reported to aid in study of viral diversity in complex ecosystems like marine [1–3] and soil [4–6] environments. In addition to hallmark genes approaches, shotgun metagenomics has facilitated in discovery of novel viruses. This approach incorporates extraction of genetic material from any environmental samples such as soil, sewage water, vectors, or fecal material and then subjecting it to random sequencing [7, 8]. The high throughput sequencing reads are either assembled against viral reference genomes or de-novo assembled and mapped with viral database for taxonomic profiling.

Shotgun metagenomics of disease vector is emerging as a powerful tool for viral surveillance due to diverse and largely unexplored viral load. Also, the concern regarding the existing and re-emerging arthropod borne viruses or arboviruses is increasing and its prevalence is a constant threat to human race. One third of arboviral infections worldwide are spread by mosquitoes [9, 10]. Dengue virus (DENV), Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), Zika virus (ZIKV), Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV), and other mosquito-borne viruses are reported annually from various parts of the world and continue to be a recurrent infection in tropical and sub-tropical countries.

Various analytical approaches and sequencing technologies are in use for viral metagenomics studies [11–13]. Previous researches have shown the dominance of insect specific viruses (ISVs) in mosquito virome. ISVs are viruses that specifically replicate in insects and mosquitoes but so far, no infection in vertebrate have been reported. ISVs belong to multiple viral families Flaviviridae, Bunyaviridae, Rhabdoviridae, Reoviridae, or Togaviridae. They are believed to have ancestral relationship with animal arboviruses as they share similarity in terms of replication mechanisms [14]. Thus, it could be a possible cause of showing hindrance in arboviral replication. Many studies have demonstrated that the prevalence of ISVs can inhibit the transmission of arboviruses in mosquitoes thus altering host virus interaction. However, the exact role for its prevalence is still unknown [15–17]. Extended research and deep knowledge about ISVs, their host range and factors that affect transmissibility and prevalence in nature is required. This knowledge may help to determine whether ISVs can be exploited and used in real-world settings to decrease mosquito borne disease progression. This can also check potential of ISVs in becoming pathogenic to invertebrates [18–22].

In this work, we optimized a shotgun metagenomic workflow using short read sequencing technology specifically Ion GeneStudio S5 System and Ion Chef Instrument. The aim of this study was to update all the viruses detected by computational analysis in Aedes mosquitoes from two districts of Central India, Gwalior and Jhansi. It also focuses on use of three different bioinformatic approaches Chan Zuckerberg ID pipeline (CZ ID), Genome Detective and Galaxy platform, to derive results. The improved insights will aid in enriching viral databases, gathering information regarding mosquito borne viral illnesses and other insect specific viruses and preparing preventive actions for public health.

Materials and methodology

Mosquito larvae sampling and processing

Aedes Larvae were collected from different areas of Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh and Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh. The collection sites were generally plant pots, rubber tyres, plastic containers and cooler tanks. The larvae were reared to adulthood in all optimum conditions i.e. 28 °C temperature, 75–85% relative humidity, 14:10 light-dark photo period and fed with yeast tablets at DRDE facility. Adult mosquitoes were segregated on basis of male and female mosquitoes. Twenty pools of forty female mosquitoes each were prepared. 500 µl of Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) was added to each pool and homogenized using stainless steel beads in TissueLyser LT (Qiagen, Germany), centrifuged at 4500xg for 10 min and supernatant filtered using 0.22 μm syringe filter to remove cellular debris [23].

RNA extraction and PCR screening

RNA extraction was carried out using QIAamp Viral RNA Mini Kit (Qiagen, Germany) according to manufacturer’s instructions, without addition of carrier RNA to the lysis buffer and RNA was eluted in 50 µl elution buffer [23]. Samples were screened by RT-PCR for Dengue virus (DENV) and Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) as sampling was conducted during peak season of transmission of dengue and chikungunya viruses. For dengue group-specific screening, RT-PCR was set up in T100 Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad, USA) using Enhanced Avian HS RT-PCR kit (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) [24]. The PCR product was visualized by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. For Chikungunya virus screening, SYBR Green I-based one step real time RT-PCR was performed using SuperScript III Platinum One-Step qRT-PCR Kit (Invitrogen, USA) in 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA) [25]. Positive and negative controls were included in all screening experiments.

Construction of cDNA and preparation of ion torrent libraries

Double stranded cDNA synthesis was carried out using random hexamer and Maxima H Minus Double-Stranded cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Further, the cDNA was quantified using Qubit 1X dsDNA High Sensitivity Assay Kit and Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer (Invitrogen, USA). Library preparation was performed using Ion Xpress™ Plus Fragment Library Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Enzymatic shearing was done followed by adapter and barcode ligation and library amplification was done by Ion Plus Fragment Library Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Library cleanup steps were performed using Agencourt AMPure XP Reagent (Beckman Coulter, USA). Size selection of approximately of 320 bp fragments was carried out using 2% E-Gel on E-Gel™ Power Snap Electrophoresis Device (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Libraries were quantified by Qubit 4.0 fluorometer (Invitrogen, USA) and equimolar libraries at a concentration of 100pM were prepared for sequencing. Template preparation and Ion 540 chip loading were performed on an Ion Chef Instrument and sequencing was carried out on an Ion GeneStudio S5 System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) using Ion 540 Chip [26].

Computational analysis

Raw Sequencing data generated from Ion GeneStudio S5 System was processed using Torrent Suite v5.18.1 on the Ion Torrent Server (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), which generates a summary report in which polyclonal reads and low quality reads are eliminated, adapters are trimmed and placed in barcodes [26]. Plugins like File Exporter Plugin was carried out to get FASTQ files downloaded. For downstream analysis, FASTQ files were directly used for analysis in three independent pipelines.

FASTQ files were compressed and uploaded to Chan Zuckerberg ID (CZ ID or IDseq; https://czid.org/) [27]. Aedes was specified as host organism for host depletion step. CZ ID is a cloud-based analysis tool that provides results from lowest to highest hit reads in the sample. It includes host filtering, quality control and taxonomic classification of sequencing reads using reference-based viral database. Samples were uploaded under metagenomics pipeline. The suite works for both short and long read platform.

De novo assembly of unaligned reads was performed using MEGAHIT v1.2.9 (https://github.com/voutcn/megahit) [32]. Validation of pipelines using a mock dataset

To validate the performance of bioinformatic pipelines, a mock viral database was constructed using whole genome RefSeq sequences of eleven important viruses downloaded from NCBI RefSeq database (Dengue virus type 1, Chikungunya virus, West Nile virus, Phasi Charoen-like virus, Cell fusing agent virus, Influenza A virus, Guadeloupe mosquito phasivirus, Wenzhou sobemo-like virus 4, Hubei mosquito virus 2, SARS-CoV-2 and Zika virus). Additionally, genomic sequences from two different strains of Aedes and one mosquito metagenome sequence were included. Each sequence was fragmented into 200-300 bp simulated reads to mimic Ion Torrent sequencing reads. The resulting FASTA file comprised a total 73 reads and was of 563KB size. The FASTA file was converted to FASTQ format by providing fake quality scores ‘@’ to each read using a tool Seqtk v1.2 (https://github.com/lh3/seqtk). The generated FASTQ file was further analysed usingall three pipelines. For further evaluation of performance of pipelines with realistic complex metagenomic Ion GeneStudio S5 System (Ion Torrent S5) dataset, additional analysis was conducted using publicly available sequencing datasets from the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) (Accession No. ERR4657875; Ion Torrent S5 sequencing of Magellanic penguin samples). The dataset was downloaded using SRA Toolkit (v3.2.0) and FASTQ files were generated using fasterq-dump utility from SRA Toolkit. The generated FASTQ files were then uploaded to CZID pipeline, Genome Detective pipelines directly. For Galaxy platform, Bowtie 2 performed host genome filtering using reference genome of Spheniscus magellanicus (GCA_010076225.1_BGI_Smag.V1_genomic). SAMtools filtered and extracted unaligned FASTQ reads. De novo assembly using MEGAHIT was performed on unaligned reads and final contig file(contigs.fasta) was uploaded to the Galaxy platform for taxonomic calling using Kraken. The pipeline results were compared with previously reported findings associated with SRA data [33]. Similarly, another dataset related to Aedes mosquito viral metagenomic analysis was also retrieved from NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA). The dataset (SRR8455259; BioProject PRJNA515586) was generated from Aedes mosquitoes as part of a published mosquito virome study and sequenced using Illumina paired-end technology. Raw reads were analysed using the same bioinformatic pipelines employed in this study [34]. Details of the datasets, read counts, results obtained from individual pipelines and comparative analysis are mentioned in Figure S6, Figure S7, Table S8, Table S9 and Table S10.

Results

Sampling of Aedes mosquito larvae and screening by PCR

A total of 1913 Aedes larvae collected from Gwalior and Jhansi districts, of which 814 were identified as females. Twenty mosquito pool samples were prepared, each consisting 40 female mosquitoes. All samples turned out to be negative when subjected to SYBR Green I based qRT-PCR for chikungunya virus and conventional RT-PCR for dengue virus. Based on cDNA quantification by Qubit fluorometer, fourteen out of twenty pools were selected for metagenomic sequencing (Table 1).

Validation using mock database

The mock FASTA dataset was created using sequences from eleven viral taxa along with Aedes mosquito reads to mimic host-derived reads. The FASTA file was converted to FASTQ by assigning ‘@’ fake quality scores to reads (‘@’ corresponds to Q score 40). Q score 40 allows the reads to pass the quality filter step in all three pipelines. Out of 11, 10 viruses with same read count were detected by CZ ID pipeline. Only West Nile virus 2 was classified as Orthoflavivirus nilense with same read count and GenBank ID: OZ223850.1. The Genome Detective platform was able to detect all 11 viruses with same read count. Phasi Charoen-like phasivirus RdRp gene was classified as Phasivirus phasiense (segment L) and Guadeloupe mosquito phasivirus was classified as Phasivirus guadeloupeense (segment M). The Galaxy-Kraken workflow was able to detect only 5 viruses of which one read of chikungunya virus was detected. Three reads were classified as unclassified flaviviruses and five reads were incorrectly classified as Japanese encephalitis virus, indicating false-positive assignments as shown in Tables 2 and Figure S3. The mock database consisted of 73 sequences, including 38 viral sequences from 11 viral taxa and 35 non-viral sequences from 4 non-viral taxa related to mosquito genomes (Aedes aegypti or Aedes albopictus) and mosquito metagenomes as mentioned in Figure S4. Based on these results, performance metrics of each pipeline including sensitivity, specificity were calculated as shown in Figure S5. The pipeline execution time also varied. The duration of data analysis was longest for CZ ID pipeline with approximately 21 minutes and shortest in Genome Detective with 1 minute 06 seconds. To obtain result from Galaxy platform, preprocessing steps were added prior to Kraken taxonomic classification. The 73-read mock dataset was intended only as a preliminary proof-of-concept control to verify whether the pipelines could correctly classify known viral sequences under simplified conditions.

In NCBI derived SRA data analysis, all three pipelines were able to generate results and detect viruses. Viruses detected vary in all three pipelines in terms of viral reference databases used, classification algorithms incorporated and taxonomic assignment strategy. The Kraken-based classification relies on exact k-mer matching against reference genomes, whereas CZ ID and Genome Detective employ alignment-based and de novo assembly-based approaches, enabling detection of more divergent viral sequences. As per original study [33], 4 novel viruses were detected from the sample of Magellanic Penguins: Sphenifaro virus, Sphenigellan virus, Sphenimaju virus, Sphenilena virus. Also, viruses close to members of genera Hepatovirus and Tremovirus were also found.

As per our analysis, all 4 novel viruses mentioned in original research paper were detected using CZ ID pipeline. Viruses associated with genera Hepatovirus and Tremovirus were majorly from Genome Detective and CZ ID pipeline.

Similarly, analysis of mosquito virome dataset SRR8455259 recovered several mosquito-associated viruses reported previously in the original study [34]. Both CZ ID and Genome Detective successfully identified major viral taxa including Phasi Charoen-like virus, Aedes anphevirus, Cell Fusing Agent Virus, and other mosquito-associated RNA viruses whereas the Galaxy-Kraken workflow detected only two viral taxa. Overall, the results showed higher sensitivity of CZ ID and Genome Detective pipelines for viral identification in metagenomic datasets. Analysis using CHAN ZUCKERBERG ID (CZID) pipeline

For viral identification, the filter “Virus – Non-phage” was set, and a detection threshold of NT rPM (reads per million) ≥ 1.0 was used to get important viral hits. Various Aedes related viruses or insect specific viruses were observed across samples. The presence of orthoflavivirus denguei was seen with highest signal in Sample S3 and followed by samples S2 and S4. Galaxy tool

For Galaxy-based workflow, indexing of reference genome of Aedes aegypti (AaegL5.0) was carried out using Bowtie2-build and indexed file using reference genome was used for alignment with sample FASTQ file using Bowtie2. SAMtools was used to view the aligned data generated as BAM file. SAMtools was subsequently used to filter out unaligned reads in BAM format which was then converted to FASTQ. MEGAHIT version was used to de novo assemble unaligned reads into contig file (contigs.fasta) with parameters (k-mer list used 21,29,39,59,79,99,119,141; 200 bases minimum length of contigs to output file; minimum count of k-mer + 1 for filtering is 3). The final contig file was uploaded to Galaxy tool where assembly quality was assessed using QUAST i.e. Quality Assessment Tool for Genome Assemblies. The QUAST report showed metrics such as number of contigs, largest contig length, total assembly length of contig, N50 and L50 contig values as shown in Fig. 4A. Then assembled contig file was run for taxonomic labelling using Kraken tool. The output is in tabular format depicting classified and unclassified contigs with classification status of reads, sequence identifiers, sequence length, LCA mapping of k-mer details, labelled taxonomic IDs as shown in Fig. 4B. This Kraken classification output was further summarized using Kraken Taxonomic Report tool. This report specified number of reads, taxonomic IDs and associated viral names Fig. 4C. The Kraken taxonomic report file was finally visualized in the form of Sankey plot using Pavian tool (https://fbreitwieser.shinyapps.io/pavian/) as shown in Fig. 4D.

Discussion

This viral metagenomic study demonstrates the application of Ion GeneStudio S5 System (Ion Torrent S5 sequencer) and Ion Chef Instrument-based shotgun metagenomic sequencing technology for analysis of virome associated with Aedes mosquitoes and evaluates the working of three different metagenomic pipeline: Chan Zuckerberg (CZ ID), Genome Detective Virus Tool and the Galaxy platform.

Ion Torrent sequencing technology combines semiconductor-based detection with its basic ligation chemistry where release of hydrogen ions during nucleotide incorporation is measured, leads to the technology’s simplicity. This approach eliminates the need of imaging systems, fluorescence detection, cameras, or optical scanners. The Ion 540 Chip for 200 bp works well to produce high throughput data generating 60–80 million reads whereas in this study it could generate more than 100 million reads within approximately 2.5 h, thus, demonstrating scalability of the platform for mosquito metagenomic studies.

In this study, three different bioinformatic pipelines were followed for metagenomic data analysis. The result suggests that all three successfully identified viral sequences from mosquito samples, although the CZ ID and Genome Detective provided greater insight of Aedes mosquito virome by detecting a greater number of viruses compared to the Galaxy platform. These pipelines revealed the presence of wide variety of unexpected viruses that can help us better understand viral dynamics and complexity. The observed differences in detection of viruses across pipelines are likely due to variation in classification algorithm, reference databases and filtering parameters. A comparative overview of pipelines and detailed workflows with command line steps are mentioned in Table 3, Figure S11 and Figure S12. Among detected viruses, Alphamesonivirus cavallynese (family Mesoniviridae) was found to be abundant and consistent in samples across all three pipelines. Various insect specific viruses (ISVs) were also found across samples, including Phasivirus phasinese, Cell fusing agent virus, Wenzhou sobemo-like virus, Hubei mosquito virus, Hubei sobemo-like virus and Guadeloupe mosquito virus [35].

In the CZ ID pipeline, dengue virus sequences were detected in one sample with limited genome coverage. However initial screening of this sample by RT-PCR was found negative for dengue virus. One possible reason is that RT-PCR assay did not target and amplify 3′ untranslated region (3’ UTR), where reads aligned in metagenomic analysis. This observation highlights a potential advantage of next-generation sequencing approaches in detecting viral genomic fragments outside the regions targeted by conventional assays. But the fact that the sample’s restricted coverage to the 3′ UTR region and the lack of reads mapping to coding regions indicate that this finding cannot be used as proof of an active dengue virus infection. This finding likely shows either low-level or fragmented viral RNA, cross-mapping to conserved regions, and therefore the results should be interpreted carefully when there is low coverage in metagenomic findings.

The CZ ID pipeline detected many ISVs like Guadeloupe mosquito virus, Renna virus, Kaiowa virus, Guato virus, Sichuan mosquito sobemo-like virus indicating broader detection capability compared to other pipelines. The threshold of NT rPM > = 1 actually eliminates many viruses with low read count. The performance of CZ ID analysis can be further improved by applying background model which acts as a reference point against which sequencing data can be compared making it possible to identify and filter environmental contaminants more successfully [27].

The Genome Detective platform provides data that include most common ISVs that are already reported in other studies, majorly Phasivirus phasinese, Hubei mosquito virus 2 and Wenzhou sobemo-like virus (WSLV) [28, 36, 37]. The identification of range of viruses is less in this platform compared to CZ ID. However, the platform provides a detailed analytical report of identified viruses specifying coverage length, depth of coverage, number of reads assigned, reads per million measure, nucleotide identity percent, amino acid identity percent, concordance, alignment score. The platform provides detail of reference genome used for read alignment. Various output files regarding assembled contigs fasta, consensus variants, SNP mutation, annotated variants, alignment statistics can be downloaded for downstream analysis. In just a few minutes, Genome Detective also benefits from rapid taxonomic classification using DIAMOND with a UniProt90 reference dataset which significantly improves computational efficiency. Previous studies have reported that Genome Detective identifies viral short reads at least 1000 times faster using DIAMOND and UniRef90 than using BLASTn with NCBI viral NT database. Although, viral identification is based on RefSeq database, but accuracy is enhanced by incorporating both amino acid and nucleotide similarity scores [28, 38].

The Galaxy platform employed in this study provides access to wide variety of analytical tools in combination, independent or customizable workflow to provide data output. The platform supports more than 400 different input formats, over 9000 computation tools. Output can be accessed in more than 40 different visualization tools [39]. In this study, Galaxy tool was combined with other bioinformatic tools for host read removal and de novo assembly. In some studies, tools based on k-mer classification such as Kraken have been able to detect greater diversity of species than other web-based tools. Thus, it is also more prone to false positives in detection. To overcome this, combining other strategies like abundance filtering and use of different classifiers can resolve this problem [40].

The tropical climatic conditions in countries such as India favor breeding of mosquitoes and associated diseases are a prominent cause of concern. When the traditional strategies like incorporation of insecticides and management to control mosquito population for diseases are facing limitations due to insecticide resistance and ecological challenges [41]. Consequently, alternative strategies or novel strategies aimed at reducing viral transmission and altering vector competence for specific viruses, find its way. One such strategy includes researches of Aedes aegypti mosquitoes infected with Wolbachia pipientis and introduced in natural population, can reduce dengue incidence and associated hospitalization [42]. Another study includes Oxitec’s OX513A genetically modified male mosquitoes whose offsprings fail to form viable adults thus preventing mosquito population and diseases [43]. Similarly, recent metagenomic findings reveal dominance of insect specific viruses (ISVs) in mosquito virome which on investigation reveals close evolutionary relationship with arboviruses [14]. ISVs such as Phasi Charoen-like virus (PCLV) and Cell fusing agent virus (CFAV) existing in Aedes mosquito virome, are found to strongly inhibit dengue virus replication and reduce risk of superinfection. However, how this inhibition can be used in natural population of mosquitoes is still under review [44].

In addition, the CZ ID and Genome Detective platformS also detect other ISVs such as Aedes anphevirus (AeAV) (also known as Glybovirus aagae), a negative-sense single-stranded RNA virus, isolated from Aedes aegypti Aag2 cell lines. It is not known to be pathogenic to humans but can interfere with arboviral replication as Wolbachia increases AeAV replication which in turn inhibits dengue virus replication [45]. Aedes aegypti totivirus (AaTV), belonging to family Totiviridae is a double stranded RNA virus which exist as core virome in geographically distant Aedes aegypti populations. AaTV may also induce antiviral responses through RNA interference pathways causing competition and reduction in pathogenic viral replication [46]. Other ISVs previously reported in mosquito viromes, such as Phasi Charoen-like virus (PCLV), Culex flaviviruses, Chaq-like virus and Verdadero virus were also found in some samples. Still researches are exploring the actual function of these viruses but its prevalence in mosquito virome is globally prominent in different mosquito species [47, 48].

Despite the advantages of metagenomic sequencing, several limitations were observed in this study. A considerable proportion of unclassified reads, due to lack of upgradation in viral database or use of outdated pre-built viral databases in pipelines. Due to short read technology, mis-assemblies may take place which may map to viral databases incorrectly providing false positive results. Thus, a validation step by RT-PCR or qRT-PCR for specific virus in a sample becomes crucial. Most notable limitation of Ion Torrent S5 sequencer is when sample contains a homopolymeric nucleotide repeats (for example, AAAAAAAA). With this the processor finds it challenging to determine the repeating sequence’s duration precisely, which may affect sequence accuracy.

Overall, the primary objective of this study was comparative evaluation of viral detection and taxonomic profiling performance across the three metagenomic pipelines rather than comparative genome assembly analysis. Therefore, the study focused on differences in viral identification, read classification, and detection sensitivity among pipelines. This metagenomic study provides a comprehensive listing of all the viruses present in the mosquito matrix and highlights the usefulness of shotgun metagenomics for virome characterization. Such studies contribute to understand ecological relationships such as host virus interactions, influence of environmental factors on virome and population dynamics reflecting migration or breeding patterns. It also enables studies on evolutionary implications by tracking down virus mutation or evolution, coevolution or coinfection of pathogenic as well as non-pathogenic viruses. The bioinformatic tools and analytical pipeline used in this study may aid in public health surveillance and vector control programmes by providing early detection of emerging viruses which could be a sign of future outbreak or by providing opportunities to explore or exploit ISVs as potential biological agents for controling viral infections or vector populations.

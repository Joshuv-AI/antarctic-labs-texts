Aggregation-Induced Emission Luminogen: Role in Biopsy for Precision Medicine
Authors: Yanhong Duo, Lei Han, Yaoqiang Yang, Zhifeng Wang, Lirong Wang, Jingyi Chen, Zhongyuan Xiang, Juyoung Yoon, Guanghong Luo, Ben Zhong Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBiopsy, including tissue and liquid biopsy, offers comprehensive and real-time physiological and pathological information for disease detection, diagnosis, and monitoring. Fluorescent probes are frequently selected to obtain adequate information on pathological processes in a rapid and minimally invasive manner based on their advantages for biopsy. However, conventional fluorescent probes have been found to show aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) properties, impeding greater progresses in this area. Since the discovery of aggregation-induced emission luminogen (AIEgen) have promoted rapid advancements in molecular bionanomaterials owing to their unique properties, including high quantum yield (QY) and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), etc. This review seeks to present the latest advances in AIEgen-based biofluorescent probes for biopsy in real or artificial samples, and also the key properties of these AIE probes. This review is divided into: (i) tissue biopsy based on smart AIEgens, (ii) blood sample biopsy based on smart AIEgens, (iii) urine sample biopsy based on smart AIEgens, (iv) saliva sample biopsy based on smart AIEgens, (v) biopsy of other liquid samples based on smart AIEgens, and (vi) perspectives and conclusion. This review could provide additional guidance to motivate interest and bolster more innovative ideas for further exploring the applications of various smart AIEgens in precision medicine.

Introduction

Beyond accidents and natural disasters, various diseases, including neurodegenerative diseases (NDs),1 metabolic diseases,2 immune disturbances,3 cardiovascular diseases (CVDs),4 orthopedic diseases,5 infections,6 mental disorders,7 rare diseases,8 cancer,9 and so on, contribute to high rates of mortality around the world. These diseases, and especially chronic disorders, create a heavy burden on individuals and societies and pose a serious threat not only to individual and family life but also to social development.10 Under these circumstances, countless endeavors from researchers and clinicians have been devoted to the fields of diagnosis and therapeutics, aiming for early prevention and improved treatment outcomes. Despite significant achievements that represent a major leap toward expected outcomes, numerous obstacles persist and several key questions remain unanswered, preventing the effective clinical management of a plethora of clinical disorders. Currently, the prevailing strategy in clinical trials within the field of precision medicine involves the integration of prevention and therapeutics.11,12 Therefore, diagnosis at an early stage is extremely important in the journey from disease discovery to diagnosis, treatment, surveillance, and ultimately cure.

Disease pathogenesis is usually accompanied by the dysregulation of various biological molecules and its biological properties of cells and tissues that are associated with vital biological processes,13 including DNA, RNA (e.g., mRNA, miRNA, siRNA, circRNA, ceRNA, lncRNA, and piRNA), lipid, carbohydrates, protein, small molecules, cytokines, ions, and among others. These alterations not only play a role in disease progression but also serve as diagnostic and therapeutic targets, offering specific insights into disease mechanisms and avenues for disease treatment and management.14−17 Therefore, the precise detection of these targets at an optimal limit of detection (LOD) with high specificity and sensitivity is now necessary. Numerous novel technologies have emerged for this purpose. These include magnetic resonance imaging (MRI),18 digitized radiography (DR),19 single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT),20 computed tomography (CT),21 positron emission tomography (PET),22 ultrasonic imaging (UI),23 digital subtraction angiography (DSA),24 radionuclide imaging,25 photothermal imaging (PTI),26 fluorescence imaging (FLI),27 Raman imaging (RI),28 photoacoustic imaging (PAI),29 polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based methods,30 enzyme linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs),31 and so on. Other techniques that evaluate various health-related parameters, such as the pressure-strain loop, heartbeat/pulse rate, temperature, blood oxygen saturation, and respiration rate, have also been developed and refined.29,32−40 Tools and techniques such as HPLC-MS,41 GC-MS,42 NMR,43 commercial ELISA kits,44,45 and so on, are commonly employed for the high-sensitivity and high-specificity analysis of tissues and clinical samples. However, inadequate diagnostic targets, methods, and tools contribute to poor clinical outcomes in a significant population of patients. In addition, factors such as cost and convenience remain significant considerations for clinical application. Therefore, more effective strategies that provide optimal results while reducing costs are urgently required.

To address these challenges, invasive diagnostic techniques are gradually being replaced by noninvasive and minimally invasive methods, enabling cost reduction and minimizing the potential damage. These noninvasive, minimally invasive, or invasive methods include biopsy (Figure 1),46,47 which can be divided into tissue biopsy and liquid biopsy according to the type of samples and can also be further classified as needle biopsy, endoscopic biopsy, surgical biopsy, skin biopsy, and bone marrow biopsy according to the sampling method. Recently, many sophisticated types of biopsies, such as MR/ultrasound fusion-guided biopsy, ultrasound-guided biopsy, etc.,48−51 have been proposed to achieve even better diagnostic outcomes, leading to great improvements in clinical trials.52,53 Liquid biopsies, in particular, have revolutionized tissue biopsy-based clinical diagnosis and surveillance. Liquid biopsies offer a slew of advantages such as easy sample collection, the continuous surveillance of multiple targets through repeated sampling, and low costs and can be used to screen for treatment resistance. Biopsies of muscle tissues, lymph nodes (LNs), blood (plasma and serum), sweat, saliva, urine, stool, pancreatic fluid, pleural effusion, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), ascitic fluid, bile, tear, earwax, nasal discharge, synovial effusion, bone morrows, exhaled breath, etc., have been identified and studied as part of both fundamental research and clinical trials. These biopsies are used to test for diagnostic targets such as various types of cells and biomolecules, including circulating tumor cells (CTCs),54 cell free DNA (cfDNA) (including circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA)),55−57 circulating cell-free RNA (cfRNA, including small RNAs/miRNAs),58−60 disease cell-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) and their inclusions (nucleic acids, proteins, lipid, and so on),61,62 including exosomes,63 tumor-educated platelets (TEPs),64,65 circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA),66 metabolites,67 ions,68 gas molecules,69 and so on (Figure 1). For biopsy, the key processes include the isolation methods and analytic tools (Figure 2). Advancements in technologies such as gene sequencing,70 artificial intelligence (AI),71−73 single-cell sequencing,74 and multiomics technologies75 have enhanced the efficiency and accuracy of biopsies in analytic process, facilitating improved detection, monitoring, and treatment through precision medicine. Therefore, biopsies serve as a crucial tool not only for detection and surveillance but also for treatment and marker screening as part of precision medicine approaches.76−79 The global biopsy market, especially in China, holds immense potential, demonstrating the significant impact of biopsy on healthcare improvement. Therefore, the focus and endeavor in biopsy are still urgent.

As mentioned previously, an ideal diagnostic method should possess characteristics such as ease of operation, affordability, a wide concentration range, high specificity and sensitivity, a high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), rapid response speed, superb accuracy, excellent anti-interference capability, and applicability both in vitro and in vivo. Among the various currently available methods, fluorescence-based diagnostics often appear to be the most suitable choice due to their versatility and effectiveness across various conditions.80−84 Many fluorescent dyes and probes have been used in diagnostics due to their significant advantages, including good sensitivity, rapid response speed, ease of operation, and inexpensive nature. These probes have been useful in obtaining temporal and spatial information regarding physiological alterations under pathological conditions.85 In addition, several commercial biopsy kits based on fluorescent probes have also been introduced into the market. Unfortunately, these conventional probes often exhibit aggregation-caused quenching (ACQ) effects and are hindered by their intrinsic properties of expected fluorescence efficiency and biocompatibility. As a result, their application in disease detection is often hindered. Recently, with the rapid development in interdisciplinary research, nanomaterial-based medicines, better known as nanomedicines, have been developed. These nanomedicines have offered great advantages in reversing ACQ effects, thus promoting the improved application of conventional fluorescence probes in basic research and clinical trials. Following the approval of Doxil for treating various malignancies by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), immense attention has been dedicated to nanomedicine development. This has revolutionized the diagnostic and therapeutic landscape for a host of diseases, bringing hope to patients and clinicians alike. Moreover, nanomedicines have created avenues for precision diagnosis and treatment86−88 as well as personalized medicine,89,90 promoting improvements in human life and health. Although nanotechnology has improved the application of traditional fluorescent dyes and probes in disease biopsy, the ACQ effect remains an inevitable challenge. Thus, there is a pressing need for more effective fluorescent probes to achieve better diagnostic outcomes with the desired sensitivity and specificity.

Fortunately, Prof. Ben Zhong Tang uncovered a novel fluorophore that exhibits an aggregation-induced emission (AIE) effect in 2001, which is the opposite of the ACQ effect (Figure 3).91,92 AIE luminogen (AIEgen) emits minimal or no light when in their individual molecular state but emits substantial amounts of light in concentrated solution or solid states. This unique phenomenon results from restricted intramolecular motions (RIMs), including restricted intramolecular vibration (RIV) and intramolecular rotation (RIR) that prevent radiative decay but enhance nonradiative decay (Figure 3A–C), thus averting the ACQ effects caused by detrimental π–π stacking within the confined spaces of nanoparticles.92 Notably, the molecular mechanism underlying the fluorescence emission of AIEgens is based on Figure 3D,E,93 where fluorescence, thermal deactivation transition, and nonradiative intersystem crossing are generated due to the direct radiative decay from S1 to the ground state (S0) after photoactivation, according to Jablonski diagram. Accordingly, AIEgens show potential for a range of different biological applications.93 Importantly, due to these properties, AIEgen possess several advantages, such as large Stokes shifts, high photostability, high SNR, high fluorescence yield (QY), lack of ACQ, broad emission range from NIR-I to NIR-IIa and NIR-IIb, no concentration limit for diagnosis, and excellent biocompatibility and biosafety. Today, AIEgens are widely employed in fundamental research, clinical trials, and clinical settings to improve the quality of life and health of human patients.94−103 Key milestones in the development of AIEgens and their application in life science and healthcare have been reviewed previously.104 Up to nowadays, clinical correlative data and a plethora of preclinical studies in mouse and human models of various diseases have shown that the great potential of AIE in biopsy that numerous smart AIEgen-based diagnostic strategies with high aggregation-enhanced diagnosis (AED) efficiency have been developed to date.105,106 In addition, over 1000 AIEgens have been produced using chemical synthesis methods or discovered from natural sources, leading to progress in various scientific fields.107,108 Among the different kinds of AIEgens, tetraphenylethylene (TPE) stands out as one of the most commonly used due to its ease of synthesis and functionalization along with its excellent AIE properties. Moreover, a public online database named ASBase for AIEgen entries has also been established,109 and it is continuously updated to make the system more intelligent and add more detailed specifications. In addition, many newly developed AIEgens have been employed for disease diagnosis and detection.110−113 However, due to the insolubility and nonspecificity of most AIEgen probes, a rational design strategy tailored to specific purposes is required. Several studies have focused on AIEgen-based imaging and diagnosis.102,114−126 For example, in 2020, Tang and colleagues penned a review regarding the mechanism through which AIEgens can detect a plenty of biomarkers of various diseases in biofluids such as urine, saliva, and sweat.127 Subsequently, an overview of enzyme-targeted diagnosis based on AIEgens using biofluid samples was presented.128 More recently, Dokyoung Kim and collaborators published a comprehensive summary of fluorescent probes, including AIEgens, and their use in detecting disease biomarkers such as biothiols, creatinine, biogenic amines, albumins, nucleotides, amino acids, enzymes, carbohydrates, bilirubin, carbamides, neurotransmitters, and ions in biological fluids such as blood (plasma and serum), saliva, urine, sweat, CSF, synovial fluid, and so on.129 In another paper, Frank Biedermann and colleagues reviewed sensing systems that offer optical detection for biomarker detection in biofluids based on molecular probes, chemosensors, and nanosensors. Their review concentrated on the various components of water and biological fluids.130 In addition, the development of immunoassays have laid the foundation for the application of AIEgens as signaling molecules, bolstering strategies for AIEgen-based diagnosis.131 Efforts are needed to explore and exploit the potential of AIEgen nanotechnology in biopsy procedures to further advance diagnostic capabilities and improve healthcare outcomes. However, so far, there has been no comprehensive review providing broad insights into the application of AIEgen nanotechnology in biopsy.

This review seeks to provide a comprehensive overview of biopsies conducted utilizing AIEgen-based strategies in real and/or artificial samples, which have greater complexity than standardized solutions and are thus more representative of clinical specimens (Scheme 1). This review of the literature is organized into several subsections based on sample sources and diagnostic targets. Tissue biopsy based on smart AIEgens is introduced first, followed by blood sample biopsy based on smart AIEgens and then urine sample biopsy based on smart AIEgens. Subsequentially, the biopsy of saliva samples, sweat samples and other liquid samples based on smart AIEgens is summarized and discussed. In each section, the operation mechanisms, rational design, properties, and advantages of the AIEgens are further elaborated based on their relevant applications. Finally, the future prospects and expected hurdles in this field of AIEgen-based biopsy are highlighted and deliberated. This review aims to offer more nuanced understanding of AIEgens to promote their application in clinical biopsy procedures. Further, it strives to inspire greater interest and foster new and innovative ideas for the use of various smart AIEgens across a broad range of biomedical applications in the realm of precision medicine.

Tissue Biopsy Based on Smart AIEgens

Tissue biopsy, a common medical technique, has been adopted for procuring and analyzing spatial and temporal pathological information from cellular or tissue samples for over 1000 years.132 Tissue biopsy has been the gold standard for the diagnosis of several diseases. Generally, procedure used for tissue biopsy is simple, avoiding the need for complex surgical procedures to obtain tissue samples. Therefore, tissue biopsy is often utilized for making a definite diagnosis in combination with various tests such as next-generation sequencing (NGS), polymerase chain reaction (PCR), immunofluorescence (IF) analysis, immunohistochemistry (IHC), LC-MS/MS, PET, and other molecular technologies.121,133−136 Typically, tissue biopsies are collected through incisional or excisional methods (core needle biopsy [CNA]) or fine needle aspiration (FNA). In contemporary clinical trials, tissue biopsies remain the gold standard for biomarker development, as the collection of solid tissues is frequently warranted for patient stratification and for testing patient eligibility. Notably, the diagnostic results of tissue biopsy depend upon both the quantity of acquired tissues and the analytic methods applied. Unfortunately, the sensitivity and specificity of current imaging technologies is often insufficient to make a definitive diagnosis clearly and precisely, and therefore, tissue samples must be acquired in vivo and excised samples must be subjected to sequential analysis. To address this challenge, the combination of different methods is commonly employed in the clinic. For instance, tissue biopsy for tumor diagnosis can provide clear genetic and molecular information in combination with other clinical tests, such as DNA/RNA/protein sequencing and IHC. Nevertheless, even though these methods can help in disease diagnosis, they cannot provide personalized pathological information for precision therapy. Among the commonly used methods, fluorescence staining is a simple and effective strategy in most cases owing to its low costs, ease of operation, and rapid results.82 As mentioned previously, in contrast to conventional dyes or probes and the invasive surgical procedures traditionally associated with tissue biopsies, AIEgens exhibit great advantages with respect to their good photostability, high QY and SNR, potential for wash-free or equipment-free applications.137−148

Meanwhile, as described in our previous literature,94 there is unique and definite name of diseases arise from their parts of the body involved, etiology, pathology, clinical manifestations, and so on. In this section, tissue biopsy has been subclassified based on its application for specific diseases, CVDs, tumors, NDs, orthopedic diseases, metabolic diseases, and inflammatory diseases, based on the 11th edition of the WHO International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11). Here, we summarize AIEgens use for tissue biopsy across various fields of biomedicine.

Tissue Biopsy for Cardiovascular Diseases Based on Smart AIEgens

CVDs is the primary and largest contributor to mortality globally149 and also has a high morbidity around the world.150,151 It is estimated that CVDs affects over 0.33 billion patients in China alone and is responsible for around 40% of the total deaths in the country.152 The symptoms of CVDs are often not apparent initially but subsequently appear in an abrupt manner, leading to medical emergencies and serious harm. Importantly, several factors have been identified to promote the development of CVDs, including smoking, unhealthy diet, obesity, lack of exercise, and poor mental state. However, the precise pathogenesis of CVDs is yet to be elucidated. Therefore, early diagnosis and interventions are necessary to improve the outcomes of CVDs. Currently, the group of CVDs includes a host of disorders such as ischemic heart disease (IHD), rheumatic heart disease, hemorrhagic stroke (HS), ischemic stroke (IS), hypertensive heart disease, aortic aneurysm, myocarditis, and myocardial disease. Currently, electrocardiograms, coronary CT scans, coronary arteriograms and ultrasonic cardiograms are usually employed to diagnose CVDs. Nevertheless, more detailed pathological diagnoses with a definite confirmation are still needed. In this context, we aim to provide a comprehensive summary of “turn-on” biopsy for CVDs based on AIEgen technology in combination with pathological characteristics.

To perform high-quality fluorescence bioimaging-based biopsy of atherosclerosis (ALS) with adequate brightness and photostability, Ding et al.developed a series of novel AIEgen-based probes with dicyanomethylene-substituted rhodamine, named TPE-T-RS, TPET-RO, and TPE-T-RCN. A high yield was achieved while obtaining these probes via the McMurry reaction, Suzuki–Miyaura coupling reaction, and Knoevenagel condensation (Figure 5A). In these synthesized probes, to achieve the skeleton for excellent AIE effect and electron-donating unit (donor), the twisted methoxy-substituted TPE was selected. Meanwhile, the electron-withdrawing units (acceptor) were gained using rhodamine derivatives with different substituents (O, S, or dicyanomethylene unit [CN]). Finally, the thiophene (T) localized between the donor and acceptor served as the second donor and electronic π-conjugator to generate intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) and extend the length of conjunction. The obtained AIEgens owned D-π-A structure and could be applied for receiving the purpose of the early detection of atherosclerotic plaques and the screening of antiatherosclerosis drugs precisely, sensitively, and rapidly. Among the three AIEgens of TPE-T-RS, TPET-RO, and TPE-T-RCN, TPE-T-RCN exhibited the highest molar extinction coefficient (2.95 × 104 L/mol·cm), the best charge transfer ability, and the largest PL QY (18.9%). It also showed the largest red-shift in the absorption/emission spectra (emission peak at 652 nm) due to the CN units, which exhibited an obvious emission redshift from 652 to 662 nm after encapsulation with DSPE-PEG2000. To fabricate an ALS-detecting nanosystem, TPE-T-RCN was encapsulated with DSPE-PEG2000 and DSPE-PEG2000-COOH, and the anti-CD47 antibody was used for surface modification to increase specificity. The obtained anti-CD47 nanoparticles (NPs) could thus identify the elevated CD47 expression in ALS.153 Accordingly, the fluorescence imaging of anti-CD47 NPs could be used to recognize atherosclerotic plaques both at an advanced stage (Figure 5B) and a very early stage (III). This method could overcome the drawbacks of CT and MRI, which failed to detect early plaques both in a mouse model (Figure 5C) and freshly resected human carotid plaque specimens (Figure 5D,E).154

Alternatively, beyond elevated CD47 expression, ALS is also characterized by abnormal levels of lipids155 and other biological components. These molecules can also serve as targets for diagnosis. Therefore, other strategies based on AIEgens have also been developed for tissue biopsy in CVDs diagnosis. For instance, TQ-BPN(156) and TPETPAFN157 can be used to detect blood–brain barrier (BBB) damage and MeTTI,158TTM/MeO-TTM,140 LFP,159 and DPBT160 are used for ALS diagnosis. In addition, TPETPAFN can be used to assess vascular integrity;161 BTF, brain thrombosis;162 TTVP, subarachnoid hemorrhage;163 and BPBBT, myocarditis.164 Collectively, AIEgen-based probes for tissue biopsy offer promising avenues for the fluorescence-based diagnosis of various CVDs. More efforts should be dedicated to expand their application to the diagnosis of other types of CVDs.

Tissue Biopsy of Tumor Based on Smart AIEgens

Cancer ranks as the second leading cause of death worldwide and is also associated with high morbidity.9,165,166 Thus, it has emerged as a major threat to human being health and social development. At the molecular level, cancer is characterized by various hallmarks, including self-sufficiency in growth signals, apoptosis evasion, sustained angiogenesis, insensitivity to antigrowth signals, limitless growth potential, dysregulated cellular energetics, tissue invasion and metastasis, immune evasion, tumor-promoting inflammation, genome instability, and mutations.15 These alterations in biomolecules serve as effective diagnostic and therapeutic targets for cancer management in the clinic.167,168 However, the precise targeting of tumors is still in its infancy and specific cancer biomarkers are yet to be discovered, often leading to late-stage identification. As described above, imaging-based diagnostic modalities often lack the required sensitivity and can be expensive. Thus, fluorescence-based methods appear to be the best choice for the diagnosis and cascaded treatment of tumors. The AIE effect of AIEgens can be used to identify tumors. Here, we present a summary of tissue biopsies of tumors based on AIEgens, highlighting specific examples.

Pancreatic cancer (PC) is among the most deadly malignancies. PC is challenging to treat and has a dismal survival rate, earning it the moniker “King of Tumors.” These challenges often arise because current diagnostic methods are ineffective at diagnosing PC at an early stage, and the accuracy and penetrability of fluorescence imaging are also limited. In this context, Prof. Zhu and colleagues constructed an AIEgen-based nanoplatform, named QM (quinoline-malononitrile)-HSPCPP, for the tissue biopsy of PC using a specific cathepsin E (CTSE)-responsive strategy (Figure 6A).169,170 First, ethyl bromoacetate was introduced into quinoline-malononitrile (QM-OH, AIE fluorophore) to obtain QM-COOH. Then, short CTSE-responsive peptide HSP was linked to QM-COOH, and a CTSE enzyme-specific fluorescence probe (QM-HSP) was fabricated for on-site monitoring. Finally, a hydrophilic biocompatible cell penetrating peptide (CPP) was introduced to guarantee the dispersion properties of the system and prevent the “always-on” effect in the hydrophilic and lipophilic biosystem (Figure 6B). Typically, QM-HSP-CPP had an emission range of 525–750 nm, exhibiting peak emission at 590 nm. When solution containing QM-HSP-CPP was added to CTSE, the cleavage induced the generation of AIE signals in situ. These signals were highly specific, dose- and time-dependent, and could be monitored over a prolonged period. During the intraoperative diagnosis of PC, QM-HSP-CPP-based biopsy could detect PC with greater simplicity, speediness, and precision than conventional methods such as H&E and IHC (Figure 6C–F). Thus, it could be applied for fluorescence-based diagnosis in vivo (Figure 6G).170

In another study, Bao, Tang, and Qin constructed an AIE dye (TPE-IQ-2O)-based mitochondrion-targeted histological stain for diagnosing localized colorectal cancer and its metastasis tracks (Figure 7A). The selective accumulation of the TPE-IQ-2O probe inside tumor cells was attributed to the hyperpolarized mitochondrial membrane potential of cancer cells (ΔΨm, ∼ −210 mV) versus normal cells (−108 to −159 mV) and their dense arrangement. TPE-IQ-2O exhibited maximum and typical absorption and emission peak at about 430 and 625 nm, respectively. In fresh clinical tissue sections (8 μm), TPE-IQ-2O could identify cancer cells with a high SNR and could also distinguish the cancer morphologies (classical honeycomb shape) clearly (Figure 7B). In addition, TPE-IQ-2O could stain the vascular structures in the cross-section of tumors and bind to collagen to provide higher imaging contrast (Figure 7C). Moreover, due to the abundance of collagen fibers, TPE-IQ-2O could label the nonlinear migration tracks of colorectal cancer, but it was not specific to thyroid, prostate, or breast cancer (Figure 7D).171 Furthermore, other agents like DPE-TPE-OH (liver cancer)172 and DPA-TPE-DCM (early tongue squamous cell carcinoma)173 were also developed for tumor tissue biopsy. However, one of the main barriers preventing the expansion of tumor tissue biopsy is the lack of available biological indicators or biomarkers of cancer,174 especially early stage biomarkers. Thus, collaboration across various scientific fields is necessary to address this challenge.

Tissue Biopsy for Neurodegenerative Diseases Based on Smart AIEgens

NDs, characterized by the progressive loss of neuronal structure or function, were first recognized by Alois Alzheimer in the 1980s. Over the past few decades, as global population aging has worsened, the threat of NDs has increased, earning them the epithet “hidden killer.”175 Generally, NDs are classified into two categories based on their phenotype, motor function-associated diseases, such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and cerebellar ataxia, and cognition-associated diseases, such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Based on disease duration, NDs can also be classified into acute NDs, such as cerebral ischemia (CI), stroke, epilepsy, traumatic brain injury (TBI), and chronic NDs, such as Huntington’s disease, Parkinson’s disease (PD) and AD. NDs share several typical characteristics at the molecular level, including inflammation, DNA and RNA defects, cytoskeletal abnormalities, aberrant proteostasis, altered energy homeostasis, synaptic and neuronal network defects, pathological protein aggregation, and neuronal cell death.14 Countless efforts have been made in this field to discover effective biomarkers,176,177 diagnostic methods,178,179 and good therapeutic strategies for NDs.180,181 However, the lack of effective diagnostic targets and methods persists. As a result, diagnosis often relies on bioimaging results and the expertise of medical professionals, particularly in the early stages of disease. In this context, tissue biopsy could provide more detailed pathological information about NDs.182

Fluorescence has emerged as a valuable tool in disease diagnosis, leading to the rapid development and application of fluorescent probes.183 AIE probes, based on protein aggregation that facilitates enhanced fluorescence emission, have also been developed.184−186 In comparison to traditional fluorescent probes, AIEgens show immense advantages, providing better diagnostic results. In a previous study, Zhu et al. succeed to map Aβ plaques and thereby detect AD by synthesizing an AIE-active probe (QM-FN-SO3) via a “step-by-step” strategy to overcome the inherent drawbacks of commercial ThT (Figure 8A). First, a lipophilic π-conjugated thiophene-bridged unit was introduced into DCM-N, and it served to extend the π-conjugated backbone for NIR emission (720 nm) and provide the lipophilicity required for BBB penetrability. Accordingly, DCM-FN was obtained. Then, quinoline-malononitrile (QM, AIE building block) was introduced to promote the quenching effect (from DCM-FN to QM-FN) and a sulfonate group was added to ensure that the AIE probe remained in a fluorescence-off state before activation to minimize the background signal. After QM-FN-SO3 was added into a suspension of Aβ42 aggregates, an obvious concentration-dependent enhancement of fluorescence intensity of QM fluorophore in QM-FN-SO3 was observed because of the reduced conformational freedom and rotation attributed to the binding to the amyloid fibrils through N,N′-dimethylamino and the hydrophobic pockets. In a previous study, QM-FN-SO3 was employed for the tissue biopsy of AD model (APP/PS1 transgenic) and wild-type mice brain tissue slices. QM-FN-SO3 identified more Aβ plaques than ThS and also showed greater fluorescence intensity (Figure 8B). Similarly, in vivo, stronger fluorescence was observed after QM-FN-SO3 application in AD mice, suggesting that Aβ plaques had been successfully detected. This study created a major breakthrough for Aβ plaques detection in a high-fidelity feedback manner, providing advantages over commercial probes to established a new diagnostic method for brain diseases.187,188 Furthermore, other AIEgens, including DNTPH,189 TPET,190 QMTCBT,191 and TPA-BT-SCP,192 were also demonstrated to be valuable in tissue biopsy for NDs. However, the clinical validation of these proposed AIEgen-based strategies is still warranted, especially given the mystery surrounding NDs. Thus, additional research efforts are required.

Tissue Biopsy for Orthopedic Diseases Based on Smart AIEgens

The skeletal system plays a basal role in supporting body weight, body shape, and movement. The different joints in the skeletal system act as a bridge, enabling the execution of motor activity and bone functionality. Therefore, bone and joint abnormalities can lead to severe consequences, particularly mobility disabilities and the associated mental health issues. The umbrella of orthopedic diseases include frozen shoulder, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), osteoarthritis (OA), cervical spondylosis, ankylosing spondylitis, femoral head necrosis, and lumbar disc herniation. These conditions have become increasingly prevalent due to various factors, and a significant burden of orthopedic diseases is faced by countries around the world. In the future, the burden of orthopedic diseases is only expected to rise globally.193,194 At present, orthopedic diseases are usually diagnosed based on bioimaging techniques.195 Herein, more detailed pathological information can be acquired from tissue biopsy. When compared with bioimaging modalities and ELISA, fluorescent probes offer several advantages, including its cost-friendly nature, rapid outcomes, and ease of operation. So far, a few AIEgens have been developed and applied in tissue biopsy for orthopedic diseases.196 For example, given the enhanced matrix metallopeptidase 13 (MMP-13) expression observed in OA,197 Bian et al. constructed an AIEgen-based specific and sensitive method for the early diagnosis of this orthopedic disorder. First, an AIEgen was synthesized and conjugated with an MMP-13-sensitive peptide via the “click chemistry” conjugation to obtain an azide-functionalized AIEgen with an alkyne-functionalized MMP-13-sensitive peptide (AIEgen-Pro-Leu-Gly-Val-Arg-Gly-Lys-Gly-Gly-Ac, or AIEgen-PLGVRGKGG-Ac) that was named Cy-Py-N3 (Figure 9A). In the presence of MMP-13, the as-prepared MMP-13-sensitive peptide was cleaved, releasing the water-soluble AIE probe and producing off–on fluorescence at 550 nm in a highly sensitive manner (Figure 9B,C). When applied in vivo, Cy-Py-N3 could diagnose the severity of OA in knee joints based on MMP-13 activity in a real-time manner (Figure 9D). This seminal AIEgen probe for tissue biopsy paved the way for more effective diagnostic strategies for orthopedic diseases.198

Recently, Li and colleagues developed phosphonate-containing Ir(III) AIEgen-based probes for tissue biopsy in cases of orthopedic diseases. They first incorporated aminopropyl phosphonate at both the para positions of 2,2′-bipyridine via amide bonds and coordinated the molecule with iridium precursors to fabricate the AIEgen probes (Ir-BP1 and Ir-BP2) (Figure 10A,B). Both AIEgens had high QY (15.3% and 12.2%) at 560 nm. When treated with hydroxyapatite (Hap) particles, Ir-BP2 exhibited selective turn-on AIE phosphorescence with a higher SNR sensing property due to phosphonate-Ca2+ chelation. At the cellular level, Ir-BP2 could nondestructively monitor osteogenic differentiation via a wash-free procedure. Subsequently, Ir-BP2 was applied for the structural tissue biopsy of bone tissue (Figure 10C) and eosinophilic granuloma (EG) in the C4 spine vertebra (Figure 10D) to achieve a precise and clear diagnosis of bone fractures.199 These findings underscore the clinical potential of AIEgen-based probes for tissue biopsy in cases of orthopedic diseases.

Tissue Biopsy for Metabolic Diseases Based on Smart AIEgens

Metabolic processes are essential for sustaining the body’s overall functioning, and any abnormalities in metabolism can lead to not only individual diseases but also cascading health issues. Diseases such as obesity, diabetes, hypercalcemia, fatty liver, uremia, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and so on, fall under the umbrella of metabolic diseases. These diseases, especially obesity, have created a heavy public health burden in several parts of the world.

All metabolic disorders involve an imbalance in cellular metabolism.200 The scientific field of metabolic diseases has emerged as a hotspot for research, and several mysteries need to be unraveled. The technologies currently adopted for the diagnosis of metabolic disorders include IF assays, electrochemical sensing assays, ELISA, mass spectrometry, chromatography, radioimmunoassay, and bioimaging, among others. However, in addition to definite diagnostic targets, methods to enhance the sensitivity and specificity of tissue biopsy for metabolic disease are also currently lacking.

Similar to conventional fluorescent probes and other nanotechnologies,201−203 AIE probes have also gained traction for tissue biopsy in metabolic diseases, demonstrating great effectiveness and several advantages. For example, in one study, three AIEgens named ISO-LD1, ISO-LD2, and ISO-LD3 were synthesized for NAFLD detection based on the important role of lipid droplets (LDs) in hepatic metabolism. Of these probes, ISO-LD3 exhibited a longer fluorescence emission wavelength at 591 nm, representing a red-shift from that of ISO-LD1 and ISO-LD2 (Figure 11A) (650 nm), as well as a higher QY. In addition, ISO-LD3 could effectively target LDs via a rapid “wash-free” procedure, providing a high SNR and good spatial distribution. When applied for the tissue biopsy of chronic liver disease, ISO-LD3 exhibited high specificity and sensitivity toward LDs through this rapid “wash-free” procedure within 10 s. Moreover, this probe was capable of discerning mild and severe fatty liver from a normal liver and was even more effective that the commercial probe Oil Red O (Figure 11B,C).204 Moreover, TB-BChE,205 DPXBI,206 and TPA-BQD-Py AIE-dots207 were also developed and applied for the tissue biopsy of metabolic diseases. Despite the promising potential of AIEgen-based probes in this field, further research efforts are warranted to fully exploit their capabilities.

Tissue Biopsy for Inflammatory Diseases Based on Smart AIEgens

In the body, the balance of inflammation, which can be triggered by infection, endogenous lesions, and aging, is crucial for normal physiological functioning and disease defense. Over the years, several studies have revealed that inflammation is closely associated with almost all diseases, with many conditions identified as being directly caused by inflammation.208−211 Therefore, inflammation is widely regarded as a key diagnostic and therapeutic target. Notably, the number of inflammation-related diseases is considerably high, and their prevalence appears to be increasing at a rapid rate.212 At present, bioimaging technologies such as PET, CT, and MRI are necessary for in vivo tissue biopsy. However, they have several shortcomings, including their cumbersome and time-consuming operation and the lack of real-time and dynamic monitoring, among others. Compared to these methods, fluorescence-based diagnosis can provide advantages such as rapid results, high accuracy, and convenient operation. Here, we aim to provide an overview of AIE probes for tissue biopsy in cases of inflammatory diseases.

Fibrosis is a chronic inflammatory disease that usually originates from persistent inflammation and includes renal fibrosis and liver fibrosis.213,214 To improve biopsy outcomes in renal fibrosis, Tang et al. fabricated a AIEgen-based nanoplatform (AIE-4PEG550) that was water-soluble for diagnosing renal fibrosis via PAI and SWIR fluorescence (Figure 12). First, Wittig reaction and Suzuki coupling reaction was adopted for synthesizing AIE-4COOH that owned a typical D (triphenylamine derivative)-A (DPTQ) structure. Then, a facile condensation reaction was adopted for synthesizing AIE-4COOH using PEG-NH2 (0.55 kDa) that coupled to the carboxylic acid groups (Figure 12A). AIE-4PEG550 was capable of dissolving in water solution and exhibited a maximum emission peak at 910 nm in the SWIR region, interesting, the emission tail stretched into the second near-infrared window (NIR-II, 1000–1700 nm) for deep imaging. AIE-4PEG550 had acceptable photostability and a high QY of 1.26% in water. After intravenous administration for 24 h, the renal clearance efficiency AIE-4PEG550 NPs was calculated to be 93.1 ± 1.7%, as well as rapid and high targeting capabilities (4 min) due to its small nanoparticle size. This enabled rapid and distant discrimination through SWIR FLI/PAI with a negligible liver retention signal, facilitating the detection of fibrosis in a folic acid-induced mouse model of nephropathy using a 1000 nm long pass filter. Furthermore, the kidneys exhibited high definition in the NIR-II region, enabling clear visualization (Figure 12C). Through evaluations of the alterations in fluorescence intensity/PAI intensity in response to urinary excretion, the severity of renal fibrosis could be determined. More importantly, the AIE-4PEG550 NPs could be applied for tissue biopsy to distinguish progressive renal fibrosis (interstitial fibrosis area) at an earlier stage than is possible with Masson’s trichrome staining, H&E staining, and blood biochemistry tests.215 Meanwhile, previous studies have also suggested that AIEgen probes are capable of achieving tissue biopsy for inflammatory diseases. For example, TL,216 2TT-oC6B,217 and TPE-DMAB218 can be employed to monitor sites of inflammation and BPN-BBTD139 and QY-SN-H2O2219 can be used for the early diagnosis of inflammatory bowel disease.

Tissue Biopsy in the Field of Bioengineering Based on Smart AIEgens

Tissue engineering is an important scientific field in clinical medicine, particularly in the realms of tissue transplantation and tissue regeneration. Hence, the monitoring of tissue engineering processes is of paramount importance. Currently, imaging techniques such as ultrasound, X-ray angiography, CT, MRI, and radionuclide imaging are commonly employed to evaluate and supervise biomedical processes. Unfortunately, these methods often fall short of expectations due to their limitations in real-time monitoring and tracking and their cumbersome and time-consuming operation. Importantly, biopsies can serve as a valuable tool for assessing the ongoing processes. However, their use in clinical trials is limited due to their invasive nature and susceptibility to false negative results as a result of limited tissue size and site heterogeneity. Under these conditions, fluorescence imaging technology has emerged as a useful approach for tissue biopsy in tissue engineering.220 At same time, owing to the inherent properties of biological systems, fluorescent probes with absorption and emission spectra that fall within the NIR-II region (1000–1700 nm) are highly desirable. This makes AIEgen probes highly advantageous over conventional ones. For instance, for the real-time monitoring of post-transplant immune responses, Xie and Tang et al. first synthesized a family of AIEgens (HBTTPIP, HBTTPAP, and HBTTPEP) via the Suzuki–Miyaura coupling reaction and the Knoevenagel condensation reaction (Figure 13A). Then, they used a simple one-step incubation reaction to construct biomimetic glucan particles (GPs) with AIE characteristics, named HBTTPEP/GPs, for the real-time noninvasive monitoring of the post-transplant immune response (Figure 13B,C). Within the confined space of GPs, the as-prepared positively charged near-infrared AIEgen (HBTTPEP) could effectively aggregate and exhibit a turn-on AIE effect, with typical emission at 650 nm and a high QY (34%). When incubated with RAW264.7 cells, HBTTPEP/GPs showed obvious fluorescence for a week without damages to cellular bioactivity. After oral administration, HBTTPEP/GPs strictly targeted macrophages by mimicking yeast and migrated to the site of transplant rejection. They could be observed via fluorescent imaging, revealing macrophage infiltration and the severity of allograft rejection (Figure 13D). Moreover, a single oral dose of HBTTPEP/GPs kept the dynamic evaluation of the therapeutic response after immunosuppressive therapy (Figure 13E).221 Similarly, an AIEgen (CSMPP) with high selectivity and pH reversibility was also developed for monitoring tissue regeneration.222 DIPT-ICF, with its longer absorption and emission wavelength, was prepared for monitoring the entire process of kidney transplantation, including the blood retention time, graft perfusion, and its vascular complications, the ureterovesical junction of transplantation, and kidney quality.223 All these results demonstrate the remarkable potential of AIEgen in tissue engineering, although more endeavors are also needed in this field.

On the other hand, although AIEgens have exhibited great potential in noninvasive tissue biopsy, more extensive and long-term trials are required before their clinical application can be possible.

Smart AIEgen for Blood-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Tissue biopsy provides limited amounts of tissue and is also invasive. Thus, there is growing interest in the development of more convenient and effective methods for disease diagnosis.224−227 This has become a focal point in both fundamental research and clinical trials, with nanotechnology playing a significant role in advancing biopsy techniques.87,228−234 In the past few years, the analysis of biological fluids, referred to as liquid biopsy since 1948,47,48,54 has garnered increased attention. Instead of using whole tissues, liquid biopsy uses body fluids such as blood (plasma and serum), pleuroperitoneal fluids, CSF, bile, sweat, saliva, urine, stool, pancreatic fluid, pleural effusion, ascitic fluid, bile, tear, earwax, nasal discharge, bone marrow, and exhaled breath (Figure 1). Notably, the use of liquid biopsy for early cancer detection has gained immense prominence in the past few years.59,235,236 The body fluids used for tissue biopsy play key roles in a range of biological processes and can offer pathological information regarding specific diseases.237−239 The analytes for liquid biopsy include CTCs, cfDNA, cfRNA, and EVs that contain exosomes, TEPs, proteins, carbohydrates, metabolites, ions, and hormones, among other biomolecules. The analytes examined in liquid biopsy can provide clear information regarding important features of disease. Moreover, liquid biopsies are noninvasive and can be repeated, enabling monitoring throughout the course of the disease. Compared to tissue biopsy, liquid biopsy is more appealing because of its low invasiveness, minimal sample volume, high sensitivity, low cost, rapid operation, capability of revealing spatiotemporal disease heterogeneity, real-time monitoring capacity, and drug response monitoring capacity.48,240,241 So far, liquid biopsy has not only been used to obtain information on genomic mutations and copy number alterations but also to gain data on the proteome,242,243 transcriptome,244 epigenome,245,246 and metabolome247,248 and for multiomics analysis.249 Therefore, liquid biopsy offers enhanced sensitivity and specificity in diagnosis and allows for repeated sampling throughout treatment in a much more convenient and noninvasive manner. The findings can be utilized to guide treatment decisions, evaluate the treatment response, monitor disease progresses after cancer treatment,250 diagnose suspected disease, and enable screening at an early stage.76 In fact, numerous clinical trials focused on oncological liquid biopsies are currently underway in collaboration with the industrial sector for early disease detection, therapy guidance, residual disease monitoring and therapy, and outcome monitoring and other medical fields.53 According to clinicaltrials.gov, 565 clinical trials related to liquid biopsies have been recruited, conducted, or completed. Hence, we seem to be entering the age of liquid biopsy in neuro-oncology.251

The diagnostic process of liquid biopsy includes the isolation of diagnostic targets252−255 and diagnostic analysis using specific method, and both of these phases are vital for obtaining accurate results. Current diagnostic methods for liquid biopsy include PCR, digital PCR assays,256 NGS,257,258 IHC, and MS (GC-MS, MS-MS, LC-MS/MS, and MALDI-TOF MS).259 Moreover, the Guardant360 CDx (Guandant Health) and FoundationOne Liquid CDx (Foundation Medicine) NGS platforms have been approved for clinical application by the FDA, and some commercial products and kits have also been approved by China.260 However, due to the inherent limitations in the number of targets, sensitivity, cumbersome operation, and high cost, there is an urgent need for innovation in these methods. As described previously, fluorescent dyes or probes could be more suitable for liquid biopsy.261−265 In fact, AIEgen can provide multifold advantages in liquid biopsy. More importantly, the aggregation properties of AIEgen guarantee a broader concentration of targets, and its fluorescence ensures low-limit detection for diagnosing diseases at extremely early stages. Below, we summarize the AIEgens applied in liquid biopsy. This section is divided into various subsections according to the diagnostic targets and diseases for different types of samples.

Blood is estimated to account for 7–8% of the weight of the human body. It serves as an effective and high-speed delivery route for nutrients and waste and contains various cells and biological molecules (Table 1) that are reflective of the body’s normal or pathological state. The availability of blood is much higher than that of other biofluids. Thus, blood is the primary specimen used in liquid biopsy.266−268 Here, we first focus on biopsies of blood samples used for evaluating a range of specific targets.

Summary of the Normal Concentration Range of Biomolecules in Blood

Blood is the most abundant fluid in the human body and contains a dynamic range of components, including various kinds of cells, nucleic acids, carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, enzymes, small molecules, ions, and metabolites. Whole blood is composed of serum and plasma. The diverse types of cells in the bloodstream are valuable indicators of key biological processes and thus offer opportunities for disease diagnosis. Furthermore, blood samples can also be used to diagnose diseases associated with the dysregulation of blood components and specific blood cells, including leukemia, hemophilia, anemia, thalassemia, and so on. Liquid biopsies of blood samples involve the isolation and analysis of disease-derived or disease-associated components circulating in the bloodstream for disease diagnosis. In particular, blood-based liquid biopsy techniques for cancer have been explored in depth269 and target CTCs,270 circulating leukocytes,271 as well as tumor-derived circulating nucleic acids,272 such as cfDNA and cfRNA,273,274 TEPs,275 EVs (including exosomes),63,276 carbohydrates,277 and protein markers,245 among others. However, due to the short bioavailability of blood sample, there is an urgent need for more effective analysis and storage methods.278,279 Here, we review the applications of AIEgens in liquid biopsy for various targets and diseases.

AIEgen Probes Targeting Circulating Cells

Blood serves as a transport pathway, allowing different cells to access various parts of the body. Therefore, blood vessels contain a large mixture of cells. Changes in cell numbers and types are usually indicative of pathological processes and are often a result of disease progression. For example, the CTCs, which detach from primary tumor tissues, circulate loosely, form clusters, or lodge themselves in other tissues. These CTCs carry the characteristics and information on their parent tumors and serve as seeds for distant metastasis. In general, only 5–50 CTCs are typically detected per teaspoon of blood, accounting for a minor proportion of blood cells. Nevertheless, CTCs still provide critical information for cancer diagnosis and can guide treatment. For example, the number of CTCs in the blood is associated with the prognosis in patients with cancer,312,313 with the thresholds for CTCs counts differing based on the type of cancer.312

Currently, the two challenges in blood cell-targeting liquid biopsy are the efficiency of isolation and the lack of suitable diagnostic methods. Various isolation methods have been discussed in depth in previous review articles, such as those by András Telekes and colleagues (2019),314 Toner Mehmet and colleagues (2022),315 and Liang Cheng and colleagues (2023),316 among others. Flow cytometry, immunofluorescence analysis, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), RT-PCR, and NGS are common diagnostic modalities for the clinical evaluation of blood cell-targeting liquid biopsy. However, the accuracy of diagnosis based on these methods is seriously limited by the inherent biological properties of CTCs.317 The use of fluorescent probes is also associated with various challenges, including unpredictable selectivity and sensitivity for biofluid samples. To address these challenges, several AIEgen-based nanoplatform have been developed and utilized for the identification of disease-specific circulating cells in blood samples.

In 2020, to improve the selectivity and sensitivity of CTCs detection in early diagnosis of tumors, Janus micromotors (JMs) was developed by Li et al., which was able to achieve motion-enhanced CTCs capture and obtain a sequenced capture-induced ratiometric fluorescence signal for tumor diagnosis (Figure 14). This strategy could be effective even when the abundance of CTCs was low. Within the JMs, the McMurry reaction was adopted based on 4-methylbenzophenone and benzophenone to produce the AIE unit (PE-CH3), and it was then conjugated with thymine to obtain TPE-T. Meanwhile, FITC was coupled with Boc-guanine to obtain FITC-G. Then, Janus fibers were prepared via side-by-side electrospinning, and JRs were constructed via the cryosection of aligned Janus fibers, which exhibited catalytic decomposition of H2O2 to generate a power source. Subsequently, a series of JMs (JM-0.4, JM-1, JM-2, and JM-5) were synthesized via catalase grafting with different aspect ratios and these JMs showed random, spiral, rotational, and linear motion trajectories. Notably, JM-2 possessed rotational motion and significantly larger mean-square-displacement (MSD) values than the other JMs. Further, TLS11a aptamers (5′-ACAGCATCCCCATGTGAACAATCGCATTGTGATTGTTACGGTTTCCGCCTCATGGACGTGCTG-3′), labeled with TPE-T and FITC-G via base-pair interactions, were conjugated on the other side of the JRs to enable the specific capture of tumor cells (Figure 14A). When the competitive binding of tumor cells with JMs occurred, the release of TPE and FITC from JMs induced the AIE effect (TPE) and ACQ effect (FITC). This led to apparent ratiometric fluorescence changes from blue (I450) to green (I526), which were subsequently measured to detect the amounts of CTCs (Figure 14B). By using JM-2, an excellent LOD could be achieved around 25 cells/mL along with a high speed of detection (less than 1 min) and no interference (Figure 14C–E). More importantly, the recovery rate in blood samples with addition of HepG2 cells was over 95% (Figure 14F). Thus, this AIEgen-based nanosystem provided a promising tool for the real-time, rapid, and sensitive detection of CTCs in laboratory, hospital, and remote settings.318

The quality of CTCs deteriorates due to cell fixation and penetration, decreasing the sensitivity of CTCs detection. However, living CTCs provide more information on specific diseases. Thus, Zheng and Tang et al. constructed a mitochondria-targeted AIEgen named TPN (Figure 15A) for the noninvasive identification of tumor cells in the blood based on the mitochondrial aberrations. TPN, which consisted of TPE and a pyridinium group, showed a typical absorption characteristics at 405 nm and a maximum emission peak at 636 nm in DMSO. When various kinds of cancer cells were treated with TPN, the TPN could easily cross the cell membrane and stain living cancer cells due to its high selectivity for their mitochondria, showing enhanced fluorescence intensity in these cells than in white blood cells (Figure 15B,C). Importantly, TPN had minimal impact on cell quality, facilitating DNA or RNA-based analysis at the single-cell level with improved performance. When applied in patient samples (blood and pleural effusion), TPN enabled the targeted biopsy of cancer cells with excellent results (Figure 15D–F). These findings suggested that AIEgens can improve the detection of CTCs in liquid biopsy.319 In another study, an AIEgen (TPE)-based nanosystem was constructed by using PEI, PEG, and Au nanorods (GNRs-PEG-TPEI). Due to the high rate of glycolysis in these cells, the surface of cancer cells is negatively charged, it benefited the efficient recognition of tumors cells of the GNRs-PEG-TPEI system to induce specific fluorescence signals, which allowed it to interact with the negatively cancer cells. In addition, this system showed minimal background interference. Thus, GNRs-PEG-TPEI exhibited great potential for CTCs detection, although its application for detecting CTCs in clinical liquid biopsy specimens was not evaluated.320

Apart from CTCs, which can be used for tumor detection, other circulating cells have also been identified as diagnostic markers. For example, circulating endothelial cells have been linked to hematological diseases, and they have been adopted as a novel biomarker for these diseases.321−323 In addition, DNA in red blood cells has been linked to lung cancer.324 Although several studies have revealed that AIE probes are capable of evaluating bloodstream.156,325,326 Unfortunately, there are no reports that focus on the diagnosis of other circulating cells in the bloodstream using AIEgens.

Developing fluorescent probes for the detection of circulating cells is crucial. As mentioned above, AIEgen probes are rarely developed for the detection of circulating cells. Moreover, there is limited focus on the use of AIEgen probes for noncancerous diseases. Therefore, further endeavors are required to assess the clinical effectiveness and utility of AIEgen probes in diverse disease contexts.

cfDNA-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Similar to circulating cells, cell-free nucleic acids, such as DNA (cfDNA, including ctDNA) and RNA (cfRNA, including microRNA, lncRNA, circRNA, ceRNA, signal recognition particle RNA [srpRNA], mRNA, etc.), contain key genetic information. These important hubs can also serve as targets for liquid biopsy.327,328 cfDNA, a type of degraded DNA molecular fragment released into the blood from various cells, ranges in length from 0.18 kb to 23 kb base pairs derived from apoptotic cells, necrotic cells, and excreted exosomes.329−332 It is continuously and rapidly cleared from the bloodstream (the half-life of cfDNA is 5–150 min), and its levels and copy number in the blood are typically low in healthy individuals. However, these values show an obvious increase under pathological conditions. So far, several different kinds of cfDNA, including ctDNA, mitochondrial DNA (cf-mtDNA),333,334 and fetal DNA (cffDNA),335 have been isolated from human blood for diagnostic purposes, which exhibits great sensitivity and specificity toward various diseases, especially in tumors.72,336 Up to now, there are a lot of factors, such as enrichment methods and analytic methods, that have been proved to be capable of affecting diagnostic outcomes of cfDNA.337 By using technologies such as RT-PCR (also known as qPCR), drop digital PCR (ddPCR), and allele-specific PCR (AS-PCR) and genomic analysis through whole-genome sequencing (WGS), whole-exon sequencing (WES), and omics technologies, genetic information such as mutations, aberrant amplifications, and deletions can be obtained from cfDNA. Thus, cfDNA can provide pathological information.338−340 Although the methods applied for genomic analysis in the clinic are comprehensive, sensitive, and accurate, they are expensive, time-consuming, and complex to perform and interpret. Moreover, the PCR methods lack sensitivity and accuracy.

Due to their noninvasive nature, high selectivity, and high sensitivity, fluorescence-based methods have been broadly applied for nucleic acid detection.341−343 Interestingly, the helical structure of DNA can cause the ACQ effect but enhance the activity of AIEgens.344−346 Therefore, AIEgens appear to be a more suitable option for DNA-targeted liquid biopsy.347 To date, several AIEgens have been successfully developed and applied for DNA-targeted liquid biopsy. For instance, Han and Zhang collaboratively developed an AIEgen-based probe named TPE-DNA for ctDNA biopsy via a free click reaction SPAAC between azide-functionalized TPE-N3 and dibenzocyclooctyne (DBCO)-functionalized DNA (Figure 16A,B). This probe exhibited no fluorescence emission at 480 nm in the absence of DNA fragments. However, when applied for ctDNA target detection (Figure 16C), TPE-DNA exhibited a significant enhancement of its fluorescence signal, and there was a good linear relationship (R2 = 0.968) between the concentrations of target ctDNA (100 pM to 1 μM) and the fluorescence signal intensity (Figure 16D). The LOD was estimated to be 39 pM, and the method showed a rapid detection speed as well as high sensitivity and selectivity in a series of simulated blood samples (R2 = 0.98) (Figure 16E,F).348

Moreover, TPBT could detect single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in a dsDNA sequence and detect DNA damage caused by UV light in a standard DNA sample and real blood samples with ultrahigh sensitivity and specificity (Figure 17).349

AIEgens for cfDNA Detection in Blood Liquid Biopsy

Beyond fluorescence-based detection methods, electrochemiluminescent (ECL) and electrochemical (EC) detection strategies have also proven effective for cfDNA detection due to advantages, such as no additional light source requirement, low background signal, high sensitivity, easy miniaturization, and low cost.351−353 However, traditional ECL/EC technologies are currently inadequate for the ultrasensitive detection of cfDNA due to its low abundance in blood samples. By leveraging the aggregation properties of AIEgens, the combination of AIEgens and ECL could be used to achieve aggregation-induced electrochemiluminescence (AIECL),354 a technique that demonstrates significant potential for the sensitive detection of trace biomolecules. Under this paradigm, several AIEgens have been developed for AIECL-based cfDNA detection. For example, Qin and colleagues constructed an AIECL biosensor for the ultrasensitive detection of ctDNA via an exonuclease III (Exo III)-assisted target recycling strategy. They exploited the AIE effect of 4,4′-(benzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole-4,7-diyl)bis(N,N-diphenylaniline) (TPA-BT) and integrated it with the electrochemical activity and ECL-quenching properties of ferrocene (Fc) (Figure 19). TPA-BT exhibited maximum absorption at 460 nm and maxmimum emission at 620 nm, respectively, along with high QY (17.79%) and an excellent luminescence intensity. On a molecular level, the ECL intensity of TPA-BT dots was quenched by Fc after the introduction of the hairpin H1 to form an Fc attachment, and this was facilitated by H1 connecting to the Au electrode via the Au–S bond. Exo III-mediated cyclic amplification generated a W1 sequence solution, which targeted and activated H1 to decrease the electrochemical signal of Fc and increase the ECL signal of TPA-BT dots, thereby the signal was amplified based on the concentration of the target molecule. Importantly, the amount of W1 generated by Exo III-mediated target cycling amplification could guarantee signal detection. When this nanosystem was employed to detect ctDNA (KRAS gene) in solution, a linear relationship between ECL intensity and the target concentration was noted between 50 aM and 100 pM (R2 = 0.98). In addition, a linear relationship was also detected between the EC signal of Fc and the target concentration within 100 aM to 100 pM (R2 = 0.98). Thus, collectively, a linear relationship was observed between the IECL/IEC ratio and the ctDNA concentration within the range of 100 aM to 100 pM. The LOD was estimated to be 11.2 aM, and the system showed excellent selectivity. Finally, spiked recovery experiments conducted in real serum samples yielded good results, with the recovery rate ranging from 90.60% to 101.17% and RSDs ranging from 2.32% to 6.53%.355 This study indicated that AIE combined with ECL/EC is an innovative strategy with great potential for cfDNA-based diagnosis.

As discussed above, the utilization of AIEgen-based nanosystems for cfDNA identification through fluorescence and ECL/EC detection strategies demonstrates several advantages and has immense potential for clinical application. However, as reported in the study by Tang and colleagues,350 the sensitivity, selectivity, solubility, and recovery rate of probes toward cfDNA have not been thoroughly tested in large-scale clinical samples, and the extremely low abundance of cfDNA at early disease stages continues to pose a significant challenge.337 Meanwhile, the accuracy of cfDNA diagnosis in the clinic remains a serious concern due to the potential contamination of cfDNA from native lymphocytes and the presence of native-derived components.356,357 In addition, in blood sample biopsies, ctDNA may be detected either in plasma (the most commonly analyzed sample) or serum.77 Therefore, further research and trials are required to explore more effective strategies for cfDNA detection.

cfRNA-Targeting AIEgen Probes

cfRNA, similar to cfDNA, represents another significant focus in the realm of blood sample-based liquid biopsy.60,363−366 cfRNA is released into the circulation by various cells to induce diverse biological effects in recipient cells. Following disease onset, the aberrant change of disease-specific transcripts can lead to the amplification or decrease of disease-derived RNA in the blood. Primarily, studies on cfRNA have centered on various types of RNA molecules such as miRNA, piRNA, tsRNA, rsRNA, lncRNA, circRNA, ceRNA, signal recognition particle RNA (srpRNA), and mRNA, among others. It is well-known that miRNAs are stable and relatively abundant in plasma, and numerous studies have demonstrated disease-associated changes in miRNA expression.367 Therefore, the cfRNA is often employed as a biomarker for disease diagnosis via liquid biopsy.368−370 Similar to cfDNA, cfRNA is also clinically analyzed using PCR and sequencing methods.371 However, cfRNA-targeted diagnosis typically involves hypothesis-driven approaches, with the analysis focusing on mutations or expression changes in previously identified oncogenes. However, the presence of numerous transcripts from various cell types complicates the identification of subtle and specific expression changes, particularly at the early stages of a disease. Endowed with the properties and advantages of nanoplatforms, fluorescence-based diagnosis appears to be a promising tool for liquid biopsy, offering high selectivity and sensitivity. Thus, AIEgens have become highly popular in this field.347,372

To address challenges such as unstable structure, low fluorescence QY, high labeling costs, and stringent reaction conditions and to take complete advantage of their simplicity, convenience, sensitivity, and selectivity, Li and colleagues constructed an ultrasensitive label-free AIEgen-based fluorescence biosensor. By subjecting the AIEgen to a hemin/G-quadruplex-catalyzed oxidation reaction, they constructed a fluorescence biosensor called TPE-M (Figure 20A). This biosensor exhibited a good AIE effect, with maximum emission at 480 nm in response to l-cysteine. Then, an l-cysteine/TPE-M-based fluorescence biosensor was constructed for DNA detection. In the presence of G-rich DNA as well as K+ and hemin, the l-cysteine molecule was destroyed because of the oxidation reaction induced by hemin/G-quadruplex. In contrast, l-cysteine/TPE-M retained its capacity to activate TPE-M fluorescence because the oxidation reaction failed to transform l-cysteine into cystine (Figure 20B). Accordingly, the highly sensitive fluorescence-based detection of G-rich DNA was achieved within a concentration range of 0.1 to 100 nM. Within this range, a good linear relationship was observed between fluorescence intensity and concentration (R2 = 0.9988), and the LOD was determined to be 33 pM (Figure 20C). When combined with a target-triggered cyclic amplification reaction, the l-cysteine/TPE-M system was capable of ultrasensitive microRNA detection. There was a linear relationship between let-7a concentration at 0.1–10000 fM and fluorescence intensity (R2 = 0.9994), and the system showed high selectivity and sensitivity against let-7b and let-7c. The LOD was determined to be as low as 35 aM (Figure 20D), and a good recovery rate (97–105.1%) was observed when the system was used to evaluate diluted human serum samples.373

Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a major cause of pancreatic cancer (PC),374 and its early diagnosis and prevention are of great significance for preserving health in human beings. To this end, Tang and colleagues recently constructed a magnetic single-drop microextraction (SDME) system for the simultaneous fluorescence detection of miRNA-196a (associated with PC) and miRNA-155 (associated with AP) with spatial confinement to enhance the AIE effect (Figure 21). For the fabrication of this platform, AIEgen (TAIN; (E)-N,N-diphenyl-4-(2-(1,3,3-trimethyl-3H-1λ4-indol-2-yl)vinyl)-aniline) was first synthesized that “slurry” TPA moiety acted as donor and a positively charged indole derivative acted as acceptor. Then, hairpin probes were coupled with magnetic beads (MBs) served as selectively recognizer toward targeted miRNAs. Next, by initiating nucleic acid amplification reactions via the exposed ends, a multilevel nucleic acid structure containing G-quadruplex was introduced onto the MBs. Owing to this design, G-quadruplex was connected with the AIEgen through an intermolecular force, and MBs in the reaction system could accumulate to spontaneously form a single drop under the action of a magnetic rod device. Thus, an AIE effect, with maximum emission at 565 nm, was generated from TAIN and was enhanced significantly within this single-drop system. In this system, the hairpin probe selectively targeted miRNAs, triggering a DNA amplification reaction. The DNA strands, along with two independent probes of G-quadruplex/TAIN and Cy5, were present on the surface of the MBs, and the SDME process could be completed within 10 s. When applied for targeted miRNA detection (miRNA-155 [666 nm] and miRNA-196a [565 nm]), a good linear relationship from 10 aM to 10 nM and from 25 aM to 10 nM was observed for miRNA-196a and miRNA-155, respectively. In addition, the LOD was estimated to be 2.1 aM and 8.1 aM, respectively. In real clinical serum samples diagnosis, including healthy individuals and six patients with AP, picomolar levels of miRNA could be detected, and the results showed that the serum expression of these miRNAs was almost double in patients with AP than in healthy individuals. The recovery rate (95.6– 104.8%) was also better than that of commercial RT-PCR methods.375

Beyond fluorescence-based detection, AIEgen-based ECL biosensors for miRNA detection have also been developed successfully. Yuan and Liu synthesized a Zn2+-induced gold cluster aggregate (Zn2+-GCA) to construct an ECL biosensor for the ultrasensitive detection of miRNA-21 for diagnosing various diseases, where the Zn2+-GCA served as a high-efficiency ECL emitter. Within Zn2+-GCA, Zn2+ caused the aggregation of monodispersed gold clusters (Au NCs) to restrict the ligand vibration of Au NCs and further enhancement of ECL emission. Furthermore, it served as a coreaction accelerator to catalyze the dissociation of the coreactant S2O82– into sulfate radicals (SO4•–), which further improved the interaction efficiency between Zn2+-GCA and S2O82– for further ECL emission (QY: 10.54%). By using this Zn2+-GCA system and enzyme-free target recycling amplification, miRNA-21 could be detected with high sensitivity and selectivity across a broad concentration range of 100 aM to 1 nM, and a low LOD (44.7 aM) (Figure 23). Although the Zn2+-GCA system was not tested using clinical blood or serum samples,379 this study suggested a potential strategy for miRNA detection based on the combination of AIE and ECL.

Extracellular Vesicle-Targeting AIEgen Probes

EVs are lipid-enclosed structures that facilitate intercellular communication between donor cells and recipient cells through the transmission of cargoes. EVs are ubiquitously found across species and can mediate a range of physiological effects depending on the type of cargo they carry, including nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and small molecules.385−388 During disease processes, the abundance of EVs is dynamically altered depending on the site of pathogenesis.389 Thus, in addition to their role as therapeutic platforms, EVs can also serve as diagnostic biomarkers,224,390−392 especially for liquid biopsy.62,393,394 Generally, EVs can be classified into microvesicles (MVs), exosomes, and apoptotic bodies based on their size.

With their diverse sources and relatively prolonged half-life, EVs have garnered significant attention in the research domain. Currently, isolation methods for EVs include ultracentrifugation, size exclusion chromatography, microfluidic technology, density gradient centrifugation, magnetic activated cell sorting, and asymmetrical flow field-flow fractionation. In addition, the diagnostic methods for EV detection include nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA), electron microscopies (EM), atomic force microscopies (AFM), high-resolution flow cytometry, and resistive pulse sensing (RPS). Techniques such as PCR and ddPCR are usually applied for nucleic acid analysis in EVs, whereas NMR, MS, flow cytometry, surface plasmon resonance (SPR), EC immune sensing, omics technology, Western blotting (WB), ELISA, and others are applied for EVs protein, lipid, and metabolite analysis.395 These methods offer accurate spatial and temporal biological information regarding EVs. However, more effective, inexpensive, and user-friendly operation strategies still need to be developed and applied clinically due to the evident variability of EVs and their contents across different diseases.396

Recently, some fluorescent probes that leverage the properties of AIEgen have been developed for analyzing EVs, promoting advances in liquid biopsy techniques. For example, Geng constructed an AIEgen (TABD-Py)-based fluorescent probe (AIMIP) for detecting exosomes via the self-assembly property of imprinted polymers. Intact exosomes were directly used as a template, and exosome-binding 3D cavities were generated on the surface of magnetic particles (Figure 24). In AIMIPs, lysozyme (Lyz) from exosomes was selected as the analyte, while the AIEgen, mediated by an aptamer, acted as a selectively fluorescent indicator upon Lyz capture. The fluorescence of the AIEgen at 485 nm reflected its binding to exosomes. AIMIPs could qualitatively and quantitatively reveal the presence of Lyz based on its turn-on fluorescence intensity. This system showed high sensitivity and selectivity, good reproducibility, and a linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and the essencece of the Lyz in serum was observed in the concentration range of 2.10 × 10–4 to 2.38 × 10–2 mM (R2 = 0.9948). The LOD was calculated to be 1.58 μM, and the recovery rate was high. Furthermore, after optimizing TABD-Py toward the aptamer, the LOD was calculated to be 1.3 × 106 particles/mL in diluted fetal bovine serum, with superb selectivity and sensitivity, and a good linear relationship between 2.1 × 106 particles/mL and ∼7.9 × 107 particles/mL (R2 = 0.9823). When applied for detecting exosomes in clinical serum samples from patients with breast cancer, AIMIPs exhibited a higher level of fluorescence than AIE-labeled cancer-related exosomes to enable the reliable differentiation of healthy donors from individuals with breast cancer.397

Two other AIEgen (TPE-TA)-based nanosystems were constructed by Zheng’s group.398,399 In the first study, to enable point-of-care testing (POCT) for tumor-derived exosomes in blood samples at a low cost, the researchers constructed a homogeneous magneto-fluorescent exosome (hMFEX) nanosensor based on AIEgens for rapid and on-site tumor-derived exosome analysis (Figure 25). The hMFEX nanosensor was constructed by incorporating two vital modules: (1) antibody (GPC-1)-functionalized magnetic beads for targeting exosomes and (2) aptamer-induced DNA TWJs with AIEgens (TPE-TA) and GO for enhancing fluorescence by tuning supramolecular interactions. These indicators were superior to those of some other exosome detection methods. When tested against plasma samples, the hMFEX nanosensor exhibited obvious differences in fluorescence signal between plasma samples derived from healthy controls and plasma samples derived from a breast cancer cohort with good efficacy (AUC: 0.950), high sensitivity (86.11%), and high specificity (90%).398

Lipid-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Lipids serve as crucial storage biomaterials and maintain the biological balance within the body. Maintaining a proper proportion of lipids is essential for human health, as deviations from this balance can lead to various diseases such as obesity, diabetes, tumors, CVDs, and more.400−402 Abnormal lipid levels have been identified as biomarkers in clinical trials403 and have been demonstrated to show significant differences between blood plasma samples from patients versus healthy controls.404 Currently, the clinical diagnostic biomarkers for lipids in blood samples include total cholesterol (TC), phospholipids (PL), triglycerides (TG), lipoproteins, and free fatty acids (FFA), which can be further categorized into high-density lipoprotein (HDL), chylomicrons (CM), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), and very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL). Detecting and monitoring these targets is crucial for disease prevention and therapeutics. However, traditional diagnostic methods such as colorimetric assays, chromatography, immunoelectrophoresis, immunodiffusion, radioimmunoassay, and ELISA are often time-consuming, involve cumbersome steps, involve the requirement of large amounts of sample, high background interference, and lack sufficient sensitivity and selectivity. In this context, fluorescence detection offers a flexible approach to lipid-associated diseases.405 By leveraging the advantages of the AIE effect in polar solvents and viscous media, AIEgens can serve as excellent indicators for the presence of lipids, particularly in subcellular organelles such as lipid droplets. They can thus aid in disease diagnosis and treatment.406−408 However, despite the potential of AIEgen, only a few studies so far have focused on lipid-targeted liquid biopsy using blood samples.

Tang and Zhang collaboratively developed a series of AIEgens (SQBT-TPE, SQBT-DMA, and SQBT-TPA) for early HDL detection (Figure 27A). First, these three AIEgens were synthesized by introducing TPE, triphenylamine (TPA), and N,N-dimethylaniline (DMA), respectively, as the electron acceptor, a zwitterionic unit, and the donor units and molecular rotors. The D–A structure of AIEgens was thus achieved to enable the AIE and TICT effect. SQBT-TPE, SQBT-DMA, and SQBT-TPA showed maximum absorption peaks at 500–600 nm and maximum emission in the NIR-I to NIR-II region. SQBT-DMA, in particular, demonstrated the highest red-shift in emission, and its fluorescence spectra extended to 2100 nm. Meanwhile, SQBT-TPE displayed the highest QY of 20% in glycerol, followed by SQBTTPA (7.5%) and SQBT-DMA (5.7%) in a 90% glycerol fraction. When applied for diagnosis in a serum mouse model of hyperlipidemia (HLP), SQBT-TPE exhibited significantly higher fluorescence in HLP mouse serum than in control serum samples. SQBT-DMA, known for its excellent cell impermeability and molecular stability, also showed increased fluorescence in response to HLP and could be employed for long-term in vivo studies. Subsequently, when tested in clinical blood samples (Figure 27B–D), low fluorescence levels were observed in all healthy groups, while high fluorescence levels were detected in most patient samples, demonstrating the outstanding accuracy (96%) and selectivity (80%) of the system, with minimal background interference. A strong linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and various lipid parameters was observed (HDL [apoB] [R2 = 0.86], LDL-C [R2 = 0.8], and sdLDL-C [R2 = 0.82]) due to the electrostatic interaction between SQBT-TPE aggregates (negative charge) and LDL particles (positive charge) (Figure 27E).409 This study presented a novel and rapid fluorescence indicator for HLP. However, there is a need for further exploration in this scientific field.

Cholesterol is a waxy and hydrophobic molecule. As a type of lipid, cholesterol plays a range of pivotal roles in pathophysiological processes. Cholesterol can be synthesized in the body from acetyl coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA) through approximately 30 enzyme-catalyzed reactions. Cholesterol travels through the bloodstream by binding to proteins called “lipoproteins”. Typically, the concentration of total cholesterol in healthy individuals is less than 5.18 mM. Therefore, the excess generation and retention of cholesterol can serve as an indicator of health.410 For example, hypercholesterolemia is among the major causes of CVDs due to the enhanced low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels.411 Thus, the detection and monitoring of cholesterol levels is critical for disease diagnosis and treatment. Currently, the ultracentrifugation method established by John Gofman is commonly utilized for lipoprotein separation in plasma. Subsequently, enzyme strip-based methods are commonly used for cholesterol detection. In the past few years, fluorescent and electrochemical biosensors for cholesterol have also been developed.412 Owing to their hydrophobic properties, AIEgens can serve as ideal candidates for cholesterol detection. For example, Sakhuja et al. developed AIEgen carbon nanodots (CDs) through a one-pot method in an organic medium. The nanodots showed emission from 400 to 500 nm (CDorg). Using a novel bile acid hydrazone-based organogel (Figure 28A), OgTOam-CDorg was synthesized by refluxing Og in a 9:1 ratio with THF/Oam at 100 °C (Figure 28B) to restrict the molecular motion by poor solvent to induce nanoaggregates and further fluorescence. The addition of cholesterol to CDorg could induce emission (Figure 28C). When cholesterol was added into the CDorg solution, a good linear relationship between cholesterol concentration (0.05 μM to 0.5 mM) (R2 = 0.97153) and the turn-on fluorescence intensity (Figure 28D,E) was observed. The LOD and limit of quantification (LOQ) were estimated to be 1.09 and 3.64 μM, respectively, with high sensitivity and selectivity. These values were lower than the “healthy” threshold of cholesterol in the body. When employed for cholesterol sensing in clinical serum samples, OgTOam-CDorg enabled the direct quantification of cholesterol concentrations effectively.413 Meanwhile, another AIEgen called CD-P was developed for cholesterol detection and had a LOD of 300 μM in vitro and in clinical samples.414 Collectively, these studies suggested that AIEgens are capable of detecting lipid levels in blood samples.

Carbohydrate-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Carbohydrates, one of the four primary organic compounds in biological systems, consist of polyhydroxy aldehydes and ketones. Carbohydrates can be subdivided into mono-, di-, oligo-, and polysaccharides based on the number of monomers they contain and are involved in almost all biological processes. Glucose acts as a direct source of energy, and polysaccharides such as glycogen and starch serve as cellular energy stores in all living organisms. Moreover, as building blocks, carbohydrates can also act as structural constituents, forming the framework of RNA and DNA, the cell membrane, and other important cellular components. In addition, carbohydrates also mediate cell–cell and cell–pathogen recognition processes.415,416 Thus, imbalance in carbohydrate levels is often an indicator of poor health, including conditions such as infections, cancer, CVDs, and aging, among others.417−421 It is well-known that excessive consumption of carbohydrates is directly linked to diseases such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, obesity, metabolic syndrome, and so on, which affect tissues throughout the body. Abnormal carbohydrate metabolism and metabolites are observed in almost every type of disease, with the most famous example being the Warburg effect in tumors.422,423 Therefore, carbohydrate are recognized as both diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers.424,425

In the past few years, carbohydrate detection has emerged as an important field of research, with microarrays, probes, chemosensors, and biosensors being developed for carbohydrate detection.426−429 These methods have become indispensable, driving significant progress in diagnostic medicine. However, the selectivity of diagnostic methods and the solubility of carbohydrates create several challenges, preventing optimal outcomes. Traditionally, carbohydrates were detected based on changes in solution properties. For example, Fehling’s test was used to detect carbohydrate levels using copper salts. Another common method involved Tollens’ reagent; in this reaction, silver nitrate was reduced to elemental silver by saccharides, aiding in carbohydrate detection.

The novel probe-based methods for carbohydrate detection offer better efficiency, lower complexity, and improved sensitivity and repeatability over traditional methods. Several probes have been designed and developed for selective carbohydrate sensing, and among them, fluorescence-based signal transduction probes are used commonly. One study showed that the boronic acid moiety can be combined with different fluorophores to realize the fast and easy detection of carbohydrates.430,431 Due to advantages such as high photostability, a high SNR, and high QY, AIEgens are believed to be excellent candidates for carbohydrate sensing (Figure 29). Interestingly, the disaccharide maltose possesses AIE properties and shows room temperature phosphorescence. Thus, it can be used for various different biological applications, including Fe3+ detection as a biosensor and in anticounterfeiting display.432

Glucose concentration is a key index in diabetes diagnosis and management. Currently, there is no cure for diabetes. Therefore, the continuous monitoring of blood glucose levels is indispensable to prevent patients from experiencing hypo- or hyperglycemia. Accordingly, minimally invasive and noninvasive technologies are urgently required in diabetes patients for the continuous monitoring of blood glucose.

Paper-based sensors present an appealing solution due to their versatility and cost-effectiveness for biomedical applications.433 Over time, there have been significant advancements in paper-based sensors for glucose detection.434 The combination of paper-based sensors with AIEgens appears to be a feasible and effective strategy. In 2018, Li’s group produced a paper-based sensor for the effective detection of glucose using an AIEgen (Figure 30A). They synthesized TPE-M-L to achieve glucose detection via a reaction catalyzed by glucose oxidase (GOx), resulting in the production of H2O2. In this reaction, l-cysteine was oxidized into cysteine, decreasing the fluorescence intensity. In the absence of glucose, no H2O2 was generated, and the fluorescence intensity of TPE-M-L remained constant. However, the addition of glucose induced strong fluorescence intensity at 460 nm due to the reaction between H2O2 and l-cysteine. These reactions could be exploited to perform a convenient and sensitive assay for both H2O2 and glucose levels. Under optimal experimental conditions, TPE-M-L was used for glucose and H2O2 detection, the relationship between the H2O2 concentration within the range of 0.01–1.0 μM, and the fluorescence intensity exhibited a good linear correlation (R2 = 0.9966) was determined (Figure 30B,C). In addition, the LOD was estimated to be 10 nM. For the paper-based fluorescence sensor, the linear relationship with H2O2 concentration was observed between 0 and 25 mM, and the LOD was estimated to be 2.5 μM. Furthermore, these changes could be detected with the naked eye. Meanwhile, fluorescence intensity and glucose concentration from 0.05 to 1.0 μM exhibited a good linear correlation (R2 = 0.9928), and the LOD was found to be 50 nM. After TPE-M-L was applied for a paper-based fluorescence assay, the linear relationship with glucose concentration was detected in the range of 10 μM to 50 mM, and the LOD was estimated to be 10 μM when visualization was performed with the naked eye (Figure 30D). In addition, this reaction had high sensitivity. Finally, when TPE-M-L was applied for the detection of glucose in human serum samples, both solid-state and paper-based fluorescence sensors yielded good concentration estimates and recovery rates (93.9–103.4%) (Figure 30E). Moreover, the visual detection of glucose levels in serum samples was also achieved using the paper-based fluorescence sensor (Figure 30).435

In 2022, Zhang et al. constructed an enzyme-based AIEgen (AIE-type glutathione [GSH]-protected AuNCs) in which smart ZIF-8 acted as the signal transduction platform for glucose detection (Figure 31A). The GOx and AIE-type glutathione (GSH)-protected AuNCs were synthesized via the hot GSH reduction method and coprecipitated through the in situ growth of ZIF-8. This process resulted in the formation of nanocomposites emitting yellow light, with enhanced stability in organic solvents, particularly methanol, due to the core–shell structure of ZIF-8, which restricted the intramolecular motion of AuNCs and induced a 10-fold increase in the AIE effect at 565 nm after excitation at 365 nm. Moreover, the porosity and weak interactions of ZIF-8 facilitated the enrichment and retention of target analytes, conferring the system with high sensitivity. Within the GOx/AuNCs@ZIF-8 system, GOx catalyzed the specific production of H+ and H2O2 from glucose. Hence, the generation of H+ (fluorescence quencher) and H2O2 was leveraged to test the fluorescence response. Accordingly, the fluorescence intensity of GOx/AuNCs@ZIF-8 was negatively correlated with the glucose concentration. Based on this correlation, two linear relationship curves could be established, accounting for glucose concentrations ranging from 0 to 1000 μM (R2 = 0.98685) and 1–20 Mm (R2 = 0.99522) (Figure 31B,C). The LOD was low at 4.7 μM, and the system exhibited high selectivity against some biomolecules and ions. When integrated into a microwell paper sensor by embedding within the paper matrices, this system enabled the use of small liquid volumes, operated under mild testing conditions, and facilitated rapid sensing. Thus, the concentration of glucose in human serum could effectively be tested using GOx/AuNCs@ZIF-8 (Figure 31D).436

An important requirement for disease diagnosis is the availability of detection equipment; thus, methods that do not require specialized equipment are extremely appealing. To develop an equipment-free and visual biosensing strategy, Li’s group, who also developed the above-mentioned AIEgens,435 constructed an AIEgen (TPE-BTD)-based equipment-free biosensor for the sensitive detection of multiple biomarkers based on a target-induced oxidation reaction. First, TPE-BTD was synthesized through a palladium-catalyzed Heck reaction between BTD-2Br and TPE-V (Figure 32A). It was then integrated with dopamine to induce strong fluorescence emission at 550 nm. Subsequently, H2O2 and horseradish peroxidase (HRP) or HRP-mimicking DNAzyme were added to the TPE-BTD/dopamine solution (Figure 32B), and dopamine was oxidated to generate dopachrome, activating the inner filter effect (IFE) due to the absorption curve and emission spectra overlap of TPE-BTD. This led to a decrease in fluorescence intensity and induced a sensitive and rapid response to G-quadruplex DNA and H2O2. When this system was employed for H2O2 detection, a linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and H2O2 concentrations of 0.01–5.0 μM (R2 = 0.9847) was observed, along with a LOD of 4.7 nM. In addition, in the presence of hemin, H2O2 was detected in the concentration range of 0.5–80 nM, and the LOD was 0.21 nM. Finally, the linear relationship between glucose concentrations and fluorescence intensity was observed in the range of 0.05 to 5 μM (R2 = 0.9902). The LOD was 0.016 μM, and the system showed excellent sensitivity and selectivity. This method was also applied for DNA adenine methylase (Dam MTase) detection within the concentration range of 0.02 to 20 U/mL, with the LOD estimated to be 0.0076 U/mL. A TPE-BTD-based paper biosensor was subsequently developed. When this sensor was used, the minimum H2O2 concentration was found to be 0.6 μM. The signal of RGB values of TDH-PS could be recorded using a color picker tool on a mobile phone, such that there existed a linear relationship between R/B values and H2O2 concentrations ranging from 0.6 to 200 μM (R2 = 0.9630), with a LOD of 0.23 μM. Meanwhile, the linear relationship with glucose was observed within a concentration range of 2–260 μM (R2 = 0.9671), with a LOD of 0.84 μM. This system was validated using clinical serum samples from patients with diabetes; glucose, G-quadruplex DNA, and Dam MTase were detected in these samples using the naked eye (Figure 32D).143 This equipment-free and visually intuitive detection strategy laid a solid foundation for further exploration and application.

Similar to monosaccharides, oligosaccharides and polysaccharides also play important roles in biological systems. For instance, glycogen serves as the primary storage unit of carbohydrates in animals, maintaining the balance of energy supply. Heparin, recognized as the most negatively charged polysaccharide in biological systems, can interact with different proteins (including antithrombin and fibroblast growth factor) via electrostatic interactions to execute their biological functions. Hence, heparin is also a commonly used clinical agent and is widely utilized as an anticoagulant for the prevention and treatment of thrombotic diseases.437,438 Therefore, ensuring the proper dosage of heparin and monitoring its levels are critical for disease treatment. At present, indirect measures of coagulation, such as the activated clotting time (ACT), antifactor Xa levels, and activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), are commonly used to evaluate the concentration of heparin. However, these methods lack specificity, are relatively cumbersome, and the EC method requires skilled operation. Owing to progress in colorimetric and fluorescence-based approaches, including AIEgen strategies,439 more cost-effective and simpler methods for detecting serum heparin levels are now emerging. Li’s group constructed AIEgen (TPE)-based electrospun fibrous strips for the straightforward visual observation of ratiometric color changes with the naked eye to detect serum heparin and urine trypsin levels. In their system, the TPE derivative synthesized via a four-step reaction was grafted onto polystyrene-co-maleic anhydride (PSMA) fibers along with phloxine B (PMSA-PhB + TPE). Then, protamine was adsorbed onto the biosensor to induce the static quenching of phloxine B and activate the AIE effect of the TPE derivative (PMSA-PhB + TPE). Under these conditions, the fluorescence restoration of phloxine B at 574 nm (I574) and the relief of emission of the TPE derivative at 472 nm (I472) were observed upon the introduction of introduced heparin or trypsin, which removed the protamine. The results were determined by evaluating the grafting densities of phloxine B and the TPE derivative (I574/I472) (Figure 33A–C). When PMSA-PhB + TPE was applied to detect elevated concentrations of heparin, the fluorescence intensity of PhB increased simultaneously with the decrease in the fluorescence intensity of TPE derivative 5, demonstrating a linear relationship (R2 = 0.999) and a low LOD of 0.02 U/mL (Figure 33D,E). Notably, these changes were observable by the naked eye, as the color of the fibrous mats shifted from cyan to green and then to bright yellow after incubation with 0.4 and 0.8 U/mL of heparin under an ultraviolet lamp, indicating high sensitivity, selectivity, stability, and reusability. Similarly, in the presence of increased concentrations of trypsin, an obvious color change was observed by the naked eye within the range of 0–8 μg/mL. Finally, the concentration of serum heparin levels was tested during postoperative and long-term care in patients who had undergone cardiovascular surgery. A good recovery rate (95%) was achieved for heparin and trypsin detection (Figure 33F), demonstrating that that the sensitivity of the system was suitable for therapeutic supervision.440

Protein-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Proteins, as executors of genetic instructions, play critical roles in all pathophysiological processes within the human body. Variations in genetic information, altered splicing, gene mutation, post-translational modifications, and intracellular or extracellular transportation collectively contribute to the diverse array and ubiquitous nature of protein molecules. Any dysfunction or imbalance in protein levels can have lethal consequences.450,451 For example, CA-125/MUC-16, CEACAM5/CEA, HGF, IGFBP-1, IGFP-2, TNFRSF6B, TNFRSF13B, CXL17, GDF-15, and SCF serve as regulators in tumor-related processes like invasion, metastasis, proliferation, inflammation, and angiogenesis. These proteins are upregulated in the bloodstream in patients with cancer and can serve as biomarkers for early lung cancer diagnosis.452 Given that blood is the primary conduit for protein distribution and circulation, dynamic changes in protein levels can be detected using blood samples. Thus, noninvasive liquid biopsies targeting blood proteins (i.e., disease and tumor biomarkers) have emerged as a significant focus in both basic and clinical research.453−457 The two classic methods that were used traditionally for protein detection were the Biuret method and the Kjeldahl nitrogen determination method. In the Biuret method, the peptide bonds in protein molecules were reacted with bivalent copper ions under alkaline conditions to produce purple-red compounds. In the Kjeldahl nitrogen determination method, protein content was calculated as 6.25 times the nitrogen content. At present, modern techniques such as colorimetry, electrophoresis, chromatography, immunoassays, biosensor-based detection, and fluorescence-based detection are commonly used for the qualitative and quantitative evaluation of proteins, and these methods exhibit high selectivity and efficacy. However, low sensitivity is a major drawback of these methods. In this context, AIEgens present a promising alternative for blood protein detection due to their enhanced sensitivity.458,459 Here, we present a comprehensive overview of blood protein detection via AIEgen-based liquid biopsy.

Human serum albumin (HSA), a single-chain polypeptide with 585 amino acid residues, is a clinically important protein. This protein plays a key role in processes such as the maintenance of plasma osmolality and immune regulation and can also be exploited for drug delivery. HSA is the most abundant protein in human plasma and changes in its levels are associated with various pathological conditions, including coronary heart disease, chronic renal disorders, and sepsis. Hence, the early stage detection of HSA levels in the blood can enable the successful prevention of various diseases. The polypeptide chain of HSA contains three α-helical domains, with each domain being divided into two subdomains. The two hydrophobic pockets present between these subdomains function as ligand-binding sites for nutrient transport. Importantly, site I of HAS can interact with its ligands via hydrophobic interaction. In contrast, site II exhibits a more intricate binding mechanism, involving not only hydrophobic interactions but also hydrogen bonds and electrostatic interactions. This complexity arises from the abundance of positively charged amino acid residues within site II.

Recently, Duan and Li devised a method to control the aggregation state of an AIEgen-based probe in order to detect a specific target. They constructed an acoustic streaming tweezer (AST) through a gigahertz bulk acoustic wave resonator to manipulate the aggregation state of an AIE probe (1-(anthracen-2-yl)-3-(naphthalen-1-yl) urea) (ANU) for HSA detection, which was generated via the modified Senjuti Halder method (Figure 34A). In this method, a mixture of ANU and HSA was incorporated into a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) microchannel modified with 1H,1H,2H,2H-perfluorodecyltrimethoxysilane (PFDMS) (Figure 34B). The introduction of PFDMS prevented the nonspecific adsorption of proteins. Within this system, the size of the AST trap matched the dimensions of the ANU, inducing the enrichment and accumulation of ANU nanoparticles. The ANU aggregates dispersed in the solution in the microchannel formed aggregates and exhibited an aggregation-dependent brightness (ADB) effect, which then induced a strong increase in fluorescence intensity (Figure 34C). When this system was used for HSA detection, the LOD was estimated to be 0.5 μg/mL, far below the threshold of proteinuria in the human body (Figure 34D–F).460 In another study targeting HSA, Wu et al. synthesized a series of superbranched TPE derivatives (P1-COOH, P2-COOH, P3-COOF, PL-COOH, P-OH, and P-NH2) (Figure 35A) with different branching structures and terminal groups to enable the highly sensitive and specific recognition and detection of HSA. The introduction of hyperbranched structures into TPE improved solubility and amplified steric hindrance and electrostatic repulsion, thus preventing aggregate formation. Compared to linear TPE-based amphiphilic molecules, the as-prepared branched AIEgens had much higher critical micelle concentrations (CMCs), which promoted the formation of dense aggregates. This enhanced the SNR, leading to improved detection (Figure 35B). Among these AIEgens, P3-COOH exhibited the highest CMC and the best SNR. After binding to HSA through hydrophobic interactions, P3-COOH showed restricted molecular movement and was less vulnerable to attack by quenchers. This greatly altered its fluorescence intensity at 448 and 576 nm. When applied for HSA detection in vitro, the fluorescence intensity exhibited a linear relationship with different HSA concentrations of 0.25–10 μM, 0.1–5 μM, and 0.1–2.5 μM, with a low LOD of 165, 63, and 56 nM, respectively. Moreover, the method demonstrated high selectivity and accuracy with a good HSA recovery rate in diluted serum samples (98.8–101.5%).461

Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is a typical serum marker for the clinical diagnosis and therapeutic monitoring of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).462 Thus, the accurate detection of AFP levels has an important role in HCC diagnosis, prognostication, and management. Therefore, several researchers have attempted to develop AIEgen probes for AFP diagnosis. For instance, Ma’s research group constructed an AIEgen probe for detecting AFP by combining a silver nanoparticle (AgNP)-linked immunoassay with an AIEgen (TPE-4TA). This strategy capitalized on the ability of Ag+ ions to induce efficient precipitation, thereby triggering the AIE effect at 504 nm. This probe exhibited a robust linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and the concentration of Ag+ within the range of 300 nM to 10 μM (R2 = 0.9924) and enabled high-specificity detection. This probe addressed the intrinsic limitations of enzymes, which cause the dissolution of AgNPs and the generation of large amounts of Ag+, demonstrating a distinctive turn-off/on effect driven by tetrazolate-Ag+ complexation. When used for detecting AFP, it displayed excellent linearity over a concentration range of 0.1 ng/mL 1 to 5 μg/mL, and the LOD was calculated to be 42 pg/mL. Given the specificity and sensitivity of the TPE-4TA-amplified fluorescence immunoassay in vitro, this probe was also used to detect AFP in 19 unrelated clinical serum samples of HCC patients. The results of double-blind evaluation revealed that this TPE-4TA-based enzyme-free amplified fluorescence immunoassay was capable to detect AFP levels successfully, exhibiting strong good agreement with the clinical results obtained with a chemiluminescence immunoassay. The area under the ROC curve was higher than 1.0, suggesting the excellent discrimination capability of this probe (Figure 36).141

Recently, the advancement of POC biochemical sensors has significantly contributed to the field of clinical diagnostics. To address the challenge of poor sensitivity in POC settings, in 2022, Jiang, Tang, and Liu constructed a metal–organic framework (MOF) in which the AIEgen (TCPE) served as a ligand (MAFs) for ultrasensitive POC biochemical sensing. The probe had two sensors, including hydrogel digital sensors (HDSs) and sensors for lateral flow immunoassays (LFIAs). First, Zr@TCPE MAFs were fabricated without regulators (MAF-1) or with regulators (MAF-2, MAF-3, and MAF-4, containing different mass ratios of TCPE:ZrCl4). The emission spectra of these MAFs ranged from 400 to 700 nm (Figure 37A). Then, HDSs and LFIAs were synthesized by introducing different cross-linked polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) hydrogels (MAF-4@QDs-PVP hydrogel complex, integrating quantum dots [R-QDs, red emission at 604 nm] and MAF-4 [B-MAF-4, blue emission at 465 nm]) (Figure 37B) and bovine serum albumin (BSA) and antibodies. All of these MAFs possessed unique luminescent mechanisms of structural rigidity-enhanced emission. In the optimization experiments, a 102–103-fold enhancement of sensitivity was observed in the hydrogel-based POC digital sensor and LFIA by using MAFs. More importantly, fast and sensitive POC detection was achieved with the naked eye using the hydrogel-based POC digital sensor, and quantitative digital RGB analysis could be completed within a few minutes. Given the high affinity between MAFs and proteins, MAF-5 (similar to MAF-1, but obtained via differential centrifugation) was coated by antibodies to obtain an LFIA sensor for immunoassays (Figure 37C). By using this MAF-based LFIA, serum samples could be tested in POC settings for clinical protein detection, with enhanced sensitivity. A good linear relationship between AFP concentration (1 pg/mL to 20 ng/mL) and fluorescence intensity was observed (R2 > 0.99), and the LOD was 0.6 pg/mL (Figure 37D). Moreover, the method showed high sensitivity and selectivity and required a low serum volume. Interestingly, when LFIA was used for serum-based clinical diagnosis in a cohort of HCC patients, the results of MAF-based LFIA demonstrated a high correlation (R2 > 0.91) with the results obtained through the clinical chemiluminescence immunoassay (CLIA) method (R2 > 0.91). In addition, it was also capable of AFP detection in urine samples with high efficiency, further highlighting its versatility and clinical potential (Figure 37E).463

C-Reactive protein (CRP), produced by the liver, typically shows elevated levels under conditions of inflammation and other diseases. Hence, the detection of CRP levels is beneficial for disease diagnosis. To enable the effective detection of CRP and address the limitations of fluorescent lateral flow immunoassays (LFAs), such as invisibility under room light, the need for additional fluorescent readers, and limited adaptability to diverse application scenarios, Zhang and colleagues constructed an AIEgen (AIENB)-based lateral flow assay for CRP detection. First, the AIENB synthesized from a lipid-soluble AIEgen (AIE RED620) (Figure 38C) was doped with polymer nanobeads via a mini-emulsion and solvent evaporation technique (Figure 38A). As a result, the AIENB was entrapped with AIE RED620 within the amphiphilic polymer shell. AIENB showed an excitation peak at 333 nm (UV light) and an emission peak at 480 nm (blue light) while also demonstrating superior fluorescent and absorbent stabilities (Figure 38B). Subsequently, the AIEgen (AIENB)-based lateral flow assay was designed by covalently conjugating AIENB with a CRP antibody via their respective carboxyl group and amino group under EDC activation. Then, this conjugate was coated onto the conjugate pad. Next, the capture antibody was immobilized on a nitrocellulose (NC) membrane to form the test (T) line, the control (C) line was set with rabbit IgG. After sample loading, the AIENB-antibody conjugates were captured on the T line when the target protein CRP was present in the sample. Meanwhile, the independent C line, which served as a quality control indicator for the test strips, was always generated owing to the capture of AIENB-rabbit IgG conjugates by goat antirabbit IgG (Figure 38D). This AIENB-based LFA, CRP levels as low as 8.0 mg/L could be qualitatively detected in the naked-eye mode, with the linear relationship spanning from 0.2 to 100 mg/L (R2 = 0.956). Meanwhile, in the fluorescent mode, the quantitative measurement of high-sensitivity CRP (hs-CRP) demonstrated a lower limit of 0.16 mg/L (Figure 38E). This system was used to test 40 different human plasma samples. The AIENB-based LFA yielded a good area under the curve (AUC) for qualitative and quantitative analysis (1.00 and 0.945, respectively), and the results showed a high correlations with those of the immunoturbidimetry method (R2 = 0.992), validating the reliability of the AIENB-LFA CRP assay (Figure 38F,G).148

Early stage biomarkers play a pivotal role in diagnosis and treatment monitoring.464 To enable the specific and sensitive detection of VEGF165, a known biomarker of different conditions (such as the angiogenic stage of cancer), Rahaie et al. constructed a bivalent aptamer-Cu nanocluster nanobiosensor (B-Apt-CuNC-VEGF165) with a turn-on AIE effect. In this system, B-Apt-CuNC-VEGF165 was integrated with a multimerized VEGF165 aptamer and ssDNA-based linker in the middle, flanked by polythymine sequences on both the 3′ and 5′ ends. Here, the ssDNA was used as a template for Cu-nanocluster supraparticle formation. On a molecular level, VEGF165 caused self-assembled aggregation in the system after binding to the VEGF165 adapter. After VEGF165 was added into B-Apt-CuNC-VEGF165 contained solution, the fluorescence intensity at 463 nm after excitation at 332 nm was enhanced in a concentration-dependent manner due to the self-assembly induced emission and AIE effect. Importantly, the aggregation caused a red-shift in absorption and emission to 390 and 524 nm, respectively, because of the restricted intramolecular rotation and vibration of Cu-NC ligands (Figure 39). Under these conditions, a linear relationship between aptasensor concentration (ranging from 10 to 800 pM) and fluorescence intensity (R2 = 0.9943) was observed, and the aptasensor exhibited excellent stability, sensitivity, and a decent response rate. The LOD was estimated to be 12 pM. In serum samples from healthy individuals and patients with colorectal cancer, VEGF165 levels of 150 pM and 680 pM were identified using this system, surpassing the detection performance of commercial kits.465

Enzyme-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Enzymes are a family of proteins and RNA molecules with catalytic activity and induce the transformation of their substrates into new substances with extremely high efficiency. According to the International Enzyme Commission (EC), enzymes can be classified into six categories based on the type of reaction they catalyze: transferases, hydrolases, oxidoreductases, isomerases, lyases, and ligases. In the body, enzymes are involved in almost all biochemical processes, including gene expression, cell growth/differentiation/migration, metabolic pathways, signal transduction, etc. Moreover, enzymes have also been implicated in pathological processes.476−478 Therefore, any dysregulation or aberrant expression of enzymes, including a variation in their concentration and activity, can serve as a potent indicator of health and potentially fatal outcomes.

Enzyme are involved in various physiological and pathological processes owing to their regulatory function.476,477 However, gene mutations, errors in translation and post-translational modification, and mislocalization evens can have deleterious consequences in the body. For example, aldehyde dehydrogenase 2, a type of mitochondrial enzyme, is sometimes inactivated due to gene mutations (ALDH2-rs671), it causes the increase of cholesterol synthesis and the decrease of the production and secretion of HDL-C, leading to metabolic chaos. Therefore, this gene mutation has been linked to several CVDs, including alcohol-induced cardiac dysfunction, heart failure, coronary artery disease, pulmonary hypertension, and so on.476 Telomerase, a well-known enzyme involved in the regulation of telomere length, is closely involved in the biogenesis and growth of cancer and can serve as a therapeutic target in malignant diseases.479 Therefore, enzymes not only hold significant potential as biomarkers for disease diagnosis but can also serve as promising targets for therapeutic interventions.480,481

Given the importance of enzymes in clinical diagnosis, many novel methods, including fluorescence, colorimetry, EC, ECL, photoelectrochemistry, HPLC, and MR, have been developed for the selective and sensitive detection of enzymes, leading to amazing advances in fundamental and clinical research. Among them, fluorescent biosensors exhibit greater advantages than other methods owing to their accuracy, sensitivity, and cost-effectiveness.482 AIEgens are thus a popular option for enzyme detection, and their use in enzyme detection has been comprehensively reviewed by several scholars, including Yi (2022),128 Li (2018),483 Tang (2019),484 and Xia (2020).485 Here, we will provide a comprehensive review of enzyme-targeted blood-based liquid biopsy using AIEgen nanosystems.

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) is a type of hydrolase, which refers to a group of isoenzymes that catalyze the transphosphorylation and hydrolysis of biomolecules like nucleic acids, proteins, and carbohydrates. ALP expression is mainly concentrated in the liver, placenta, intestine, and bone.486,487 Under healthy conditions, the content of ALP in human serum ranges from 46 to 190 U/L. However, ALP levels are elevated in various pathological conditions, such as bone disease (250 U/L in rickets patients), breast and prostate cancer, liver dysfunction, and diabetes. Therefore, it is of great importance to develop an effective method for ALP detection. In this context, Li’s group constructed a ratiometric assay for ALP activity detection on polyethylene terephthalate (PET) fibers after the covalent immobilization of fluorescein. This was followed by the electrostatic adsorption of the bisquaternary ammonium salt of tetraphenylethene (TPE-2N+) on this conjugate, resulting in the fabrication of PET-Flu-PO4/TPE. In the absence of ALP, phosphorylated fluorescein formed a complex with TPE-2N+, resulting in AIE at 471 nm due to the TPE. However, after ALP was added, the hydrolysis of the phosphoesters resulted in the gradual removal of TPE-2N+, restoring fluorescein emission at 514 nm. This change in fluorescence increased with the concentration of ALP, enabling ALP detection (Figure 40A). Upon the surface modification of fibers with an amine density of 30 nM/mg, a notable and nearly linear enhancement in I514/I471 ratios (R2 = 0.99) was observed, ranging from 0.73 to 3.05 as the ALP concentrations increased from 0 to 100 U/L (Figure 40B,C). More importantly, the fibrous strips underwent a color change from blue (TPE-2N+) to green (fluorescein) under ultraviolet light within minutes, enabling the detection of ALP levels ranging from 0 to 80 U/L with the naked eye. This method was also successfully applied for ALP detection within the concentration range found in human serum, with appropriate dilutions if necessary, and it showed excellent selectivity and accuracy while remaining resistant to interference from other serum components (Figure 40D). The ALP activity in serum samples was estimated to range from 67.0 to 134.7 U/L, which is within the normal range of serum ALP levels. High recovery rates ranging from 96% and 105% were detected, and the RSD was below 4.4%. Thus, this system was simpler and more effective than ELISA.488

Hyaluronidase (HAase) is a type of extracellular matrix-digesting endoglycosidase that degrades high molecular-weight hyaluronan (HA) into lower molecular weight fragments, thereby maintaining cellular function and the redox balance. Therefore, both HAase and HA are considered disease indicators. To determine the activity of HAase, Wu and Zeng constructed a novel ratiometric fluorescent nanosensing system based on the AIE and ACQ effects (Figure 41). First, they synthesized the AIEgen TPE-2N+, a tetraphenylethylene derivative that exhibits a positive charge on both ends, and the ACQ molecule AN-N+, an anthracene derivative with a positive charge at only one end. Subsequently, TPE-2N+ and AN-N+ were mixed with negatively charged HA via electrostatic complexation. In this process, TPE-2N+ acted as a cross-linker to induce the aggregation of the compounds as well as the nanoparticles. In molecular level, the aggregation led to strong fluorescence emission from TPE-2N+ (474 nm) and the quenching of AN-N+ fluorescence (414 nm). Consequently, the introduction of HAase led to an enzymatic reaction, causing the breakdown of HA, which triggered the disassembly of the nanoparticles. This restored the fluorescence emission of AN-N+ but suppressed TPE-2N+ fluorescence. Accordingly, the ratio of fluorescence intensity (I414 to I474) could be used to effectively detect HAase activity. Moreover, within this system, HA served not only as a substrate for enzymatic reaction but also as a scaffold for nanoparticle formation. A good linear relationship was observed between the I414/I474 ratio and HAase activity within the concentration range of 0.005 to 0.2 U/mL in aqueous medium, and the LOD was very low (0.0017 U/mL). When applied for the detection of HAase in biological fluids, the HAase levels in urine and undiluted serum samples were calculated to be 5.12 × 10–3 U/mL and 0.317 U/mL, respectively, showcasing high selectivity, accuracy, and reliability.489

In recent years, the rapid development of spatial omics technologies has demonstrated the key role of biomolecule modification in disease development, progression, diagnosis, and therapy.490−493 Consequently, the detection of the biomolecules responsible for these modifications (i.e., enzymes) has emerged as an effective method for diseases diagnosis.494 Histone deacetylases (HDACs) is a kind of hydrolytic enzymes with the biofunction to remove the acetyl groups from the ε-N-acetylated lysine residues of histones and other cellular proteins, thereby influencing the biological pathways of cancer and various other diseases. The detection of HDAC activity is thus of great importance. Currently, the methods employed for HDAC activity detection include HPLC, MR, EC, ECL, fluorescence biosensing, and so on. To improve the detection of the HDAC activity of Sirt1, Wu et al. developed a hyperbranched polyester-based one-step fluorescent assay (H40/TPE-2SO3– complex) based on an AIEgen (TPE) (Figure 42). Within H40/TPE-2SO3– complex, the hyperbranched polyester was coupled with the negatively charged TPE derivative bearing two sulfonic acid groups and acetylated lysine groups (H40-Lys(Ac)). Sirt1 caused the deacetylation of the H40-Lys(Ac) lysine groups, turning the electroneutral polymer into a positively charged one. Further, it induced the complexation of the polymer with TPE-2SO3–. Collectively, these steps led to the formation of nanoaggregates via electrostatic interactions, enhancing the fluorescence intensity at 465 nm. When used in vitro for Sirt1 activity detection, the H40/TPE-2SO3– complex system could detect Sirt1 concentrations ranging from 0 to 10 μg/mL, reaching a low LOD of 25 ng/mL. When H40/TPE-2SO3– was validated in diluted human serum samples, it yielded results comparable to those of ELISA (8.8 vs 8.2 μg/mL), with a recovery of 91%. Notably, this assay was easier to operate than ELISA. Together, these findings showcased the accuracy and reliability of the H40/TPE-2SO3– complex for HDAC determination.495

Ion-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Ions, including cations and anions, serve as both nutrients and bioregulators in biological systems. Ions enable the proper functioning of proteins by acting as cofactors and are thus important for maintaining cell growth and survival. Disruptions in ion concentrations, including a sharp/sudden decrease or increase, can lead to cellular dysfunction and contribute to various diseases or cytotoxicity.507,508 So far, several important ions in the body have been identified, including sodium (Na), potassium (K), phosphorus (P), calcium (Ca), chlorine (Cl), sulfur (S), magnesium (Mg), selenium (Se), magnesium (Mg), iron (Fe), molybdenum (Mo), manganese (Mn), zinc (Zn), copper (Cu), and so on. The dynamic homeostasis of these ions is critical for normal biomolecule, organelle, cell, tissue, and organ function and to maintain physiological homeostasis throughout the body.509−511 For instance, ions like K+ and Ca2+ are essential for maintaining cell morphology, structure, and function. Thereby, imbalances in these ions can contribute to adverse or lethal consequences, including heart disease, CVDs, NDs, and cancer.512,513 Similarly, changes in Fe concentrations can lead to anemia. These issues are compounded by adverse environmental conditions, which can introduce hazardous ions into the human body, particularly as humans are on the top of the food chain.514 Thus, ionic imbalances can serve as a hallmark for a range of diseases.515 For example, zinc ions, as trace elements, play pivotal roles in biological processes such as gene expression, DNA repair, cell division, and apoptosis, and their dysfunction is noted in diseases such as cancer, CVDs, autoimmune disease, infectious disease, and NDs, among others.516 Similarly, copper ions are involved in energy supply, connective tissue maturation, and vascular system formation.517 Thus, the detection of ion content can offer predictive insights into diseases.518 While various methods are employed for ion detection, including colorimetry, chromatography, ion selective electrode, enzyme-based method, atomic absorption spectrophotometry, radionuclide dilution MS, IC-MS, and so on, these methods lack sensitivity and selectivity and can also be expensive and operationally complex. Fluorescence methods, particularly those based on various AIEgens, offer distinct advantages in this context. So far, a range of AIE-based systems have been developed in order to sense specific ions of interest, as summarized in our previous review519 as well as in the work by Tang’s group520 and Zhao’s group.521 In this section, we will discuss AIEgen-based nanosystems for ion-targeted liquid biopsy using blood samples in greater detail.

In 2022, to address the issue of excessive Cu+/Cu2+ intake, Fu and co-workers constructed an AIEgen-based probe for the sensitive detection of Cu+/Cu2+ in biofluids. The probes comprised two key components: ssDNA sequence (A31, 5′-ACCCGAACCTGGGCTACCACCCTTAATCCCC-3′) and AgNCs, wherein the A31 served as a template for synthesizing AgNCs in the presence of NaBH4 in PB buffer (Figure 43A). After Cu+/Cu2+ was introduced to the A31-templated AgNCs, aggregates were formed, enhancing the fluorescence intensity of AgNCs at 626 nm after excitation at 546 nm in a concentration-dependent manner. Accordingly, a label-free and signal-on fluorescence-based sensing platform was developed and applied for detecting Cu+/Cu2+ in biological samples and enabling the intracellular imaging of Cu+/Cu2+ in cells with high sensitivity (Figure 43B,C). When the concentration of Cu+ and Cu2+ ranged from 2.0 to 20 μM, a good linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and Cu+/Cu2+ concentrations were obtained, with an extremely low LOD of 0.1 μM (Figure 43D). The process was characterized by minimal time consumption (within 30 min), high selectivity, and sensitivity under optimal experimental conditions. Upon application in cellular fluids and human serum, the probe demonstrated specific detection capabilities, achieving an excellent recovery rate of 90–104% with no interference.522

Zn2+ and Al3+ are involved in biological processes such as gene transcription, biocatalysis, energy metabolism, and neural signal transmission. In another study, Li and Qin constructed a novel multifunctional fluorescent probe (HL) for specifically detecting Zn2+/Al3+ using 3,4,5-tribenzyloxybenzoylformylhydrazine and salicylic aldehyde in methanol solution, achieving an 83% yield (Figure 44A). After Zn2+ and Al3+ were added to the HL solution in the aqueous media/ethanol mixture, fluorescence was enhanced (λex, 400 nm/385 nm, λem, 510 nm/450 nm) due to the AIE effect arising from the constriction of the three benzyl groups within HL. The complex formed at a ratio of 2:1 and inhibited the PET process. AI3+ interacted with HL at a 1:1 ratio to form a stoichiometric L-Al complex, which also caused the inhibition of the PET process (Figure 44B,C). This difference in fluorescence made it possible to image Zn2+/Al3+ in PC3 cells. When applied to serum from prostate cancer (PCa) patients, the probe identified serum Zn2+ levels of 8.58 ± 1.14 μM, 12.85 ± 0.97 μM, and 12.88 ± 1.31 μM in patients with PCa, NCL or healthy individuals, respectively, providing supporting information to serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels for accurate PCa diagnosis.523 Furthermore, Cui et al. discovered that xanthan gum, a natural product, could serve as a novel AIEgen for detecting Fe3+ and Cu2+ in the range of 5–300 μM and 5–200 μM, respectively, with a LOD of 0.0545 and 0.0345 μM, respectively. The detection ranges aligned with those found in healthy serum, demonstrating significant potential for clinical application.524 Another noteworthy discovery was the use of PIQPy for Cu2+ detection in chicken blood (LOD = 37.69 nM).525

Although plenty of AIEgens have been developed for ion sensing, one critical limitation remains with respect to the validation of these systems in clinical samples. Efforts should be dedicated to promote the translation of fundamental research into clinical settings.

Microorganism-Targeting AIEgen Probes

Microorganisms, including bacteria, fungi, and viruses, are ubiquitously present and show a high range of diversity. These microbes can cause infectious diseases, such as malaria, severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS), Ebola, smallpox, coronavirus disease (COVID-19), and monkeypox, among others. These diseases pose a great threat to public health and can increase the risk of other noninfectious diseases like cancer.526−530 However, some microorganisms, such as probiotics also play a beneficial role in maintaining health. For example, the brain–gut microbiome axis has been found to regulate NDs via various pathways.531,532 Blood infections caused by pathogenic bacteria is a leading cause of sepsis, contributing to high mortality and morbidity rates. Thus, microorganisms can also serve as biomarkers for disease,531,533−535 although understanding this relationship remains a complex and ongoing challenge.

Numerous methods and tools have been developed for the identification of specific pathogenic microorganisms, such as bacterial culture, PCR/RT-PCR, gene microarray, target-specific immunoassays, high-throughput sequencing (HTS), ELISA, fluorescence staining, etc. Unfortunately, many of these methods suffer from limitations such as low sensitivity and selectivity, time-consuming procedures, and high costs. Meanwhile, fluorescence probes combined nanotechnologies offer a promising avenue for rapid and accurate diagnosis due to their advantages.536−539 AIEgens can be applied for the detection of pathogenic microorganisms under a series of conditions.540 Currently, research on the AIEgen-based detection, bioimaging, and treatment of microbial infections represents a burgeoning area of interest, akin to the prominence of cancer research in the field of nanomedicine.541−545 Here, we offer a comprehensive overview of microorganism-targeted liquid biopsies based on blood samples. Surprisingly, only one study used blood samples for microorganism detection.

Since 2019, COVID-19 has inflicted widespread devastation across the globe. Despite the introduction of vaccines, which have helped mitigate the ongoing spread of COVID-19, the need for rapid and effective detection of the virus responsible for this disease remains urgent. Li et al. designed a straightforward, speedy, and highly sensitive serological diagnostic technique using AIEgen nanoparticles (AIE810NP, λem = 810 nm) in a LFA for the early detection of immunoglobulin G (IgG) and immunoglobulin M (IgM) against SARS-CoV-2 in clinical serum samples (Figure 45). Within this LFA, a BPBT dye with NIR emission properties was chosen as the fluorescent unit. Additionally, to enhance the fluorescence signal further, polystyrene (PS) nanoparticles sized 300 nm were loaded with 3.18 × 106 dye particles (AIE810NP) and utilized to tag the detection ligand. The LOD of this LFA was low at 0.236 and 0.125 μg/mL for IgM and IgG, respectively, and the sensitivity was high (78% and 95%, respectively). The performance of this novel assay was thus comparable to that of ELISA (LOD of 0.040 and 0.039 μg mL–1 and sensitivity of 85% and 95%) and better than that of AuNP-based test strips (41% and 85%). Notably, the AIE810NP-based test strip could identify IgG and IgM at 1–7 days after symptom onset, much earlier than the AuNP-based test strip (8–15 days). Overall, the AIE810NP-based test strip appeared to be a promising tool for the early detection of SARS-CoV-2 in clinical serum samples. Subsequently, a commercial 2019-nCoV Antigen Kit (AIE FICA) based on AIEgens was developed by Prof. Tang, and its production was scaled for improving COVID-19 testing (Figure 45).546 Furthermore, AIEgens have also been applied for detecting antibodies against COVID-19,471,547 α2,6SL-TPE for sensing influenza virus,548 and TBTCP for sensing bacterial sepsis.549

These examples show that AIEgens are standout fluorescent probes for microorganism testing. However, the stringent requirements for acquiring clinical blood samples, given the potential for diffusion and propagation, pose a significant challenge that must be addressed to advance research in this field.

AIEgen Probes Targeting Other Biorelevant Components

In addition to the biomolecules discussed above, blood also contains a variety of other substances with concentrations within the nanomolar to millimolar range. Despite advancements in sensing technologies, many of these biorelevant components, listed in Table 1, have not been effectively and comprehensively detected. Although methods such as MR, HPLC, ELISA, and others have been utilized to quantify the levels of various biomolecules, these methods are often expensive, time-consuming, and require specialized equipment. In comparison, fluorescence-based methods offer distinct advantages, including noninvasiveness, high sensitivity, selectivity, and repeatability. In this section, we highlight a few examples of blood-based liquid biopsy strategies that already demonstrate some of the required performance parameters for practical application.

Insulin, a 5.8 kDa hormone that consists of two α-helical chains (A and B) linked by disulfide bonds, is a regulator of blood glucose levels. Thus, this hormone is intricately linked with diabetes mellitus.550,551 Consequently, the precise identification and ultrasensitive detection of insulin levels hold paramount significance for the early diagnosis and management of diabetes. In pursuit of effective detection methods, Li constructed a label-free turn-on fluorescence aptasensor for insulin detection based on an AIEgen. First, the insulin recognizer (guanine-rich aptamer IAG3 aptamer, 5′-GGTGGTGGGGGGGGTTGGTAGGGTGTCTTC-3′) was assembled on the surface of GO via hydrogen bonds and π–π stacking interactions. The positively charged AIEgen (TPE-Z) synthesized from TPE-MB was complexed with the negatively charged molecule IGA3 through strong electrostatic interactions of fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) from TPE-Z to GO to generate an aptasensor that caused the efficient fluorescence quenching of TPE-Z. Insulin could specifically bind with IGA3, caused its detachment from the GO surface, which induced the fluorescence recovery of TPE-Z at 475 nm after excitation at 325 nm. Under optimized conditions, the fluorescence aptasensor exhibited a broad linear range for insulin detection from 1.0 pM to 1.0 μM, with a LOD of 0.42 pM and excellent sensitivity and selectivity (Figure 46B,C). Thus, this method yielded analytical performances better than CDs/Apt, NIR-QDs/Apt, and FAM-labeled Apt for insulin detection. When this aptasensor was used to detect insulin in human serum samples under optimized experimental conditions, it provided spiked recoveries of 96.06–104.26%, underscoring its potential for clinical application.552 Furthermore, immune-modified semiconductor quantum dots (SQDs) and graphene quantum dots (GQDs) were also fabricated for sensing insulin, providing an LOD of 0.045 ng/mL within 5 min. These tools could also be used for insulin detection in human serum samples.553 In addition, PG-1 and PG-2 were also developed for sensing progesterone, providing LODs of 1.21 μM and 38.1 nM, respectively.554

Antibiotics are drugs that are commonly used to prevent bacterial infection. However, given the risk of antibacterial resistance, the precise dose of antibiotics needs to be examined effectively. To this end, Shi et al. constructed a dual-emission fluorescence biosensor platform consisting of a peptide (G(propargylamine)K(Ac)AA) conjugated with a blue-emitting AIEgen (TPE) and aptamer (5′-NH2-CGAGGGTACCGCAATAGTACTTATTGTTCGCCTATTGTGGGTCGG-3′)-modified red-emitting gold nanoclusters (AuNCs-apt) to detect vancomycin (Van). Here, both the peptide and aptamer showed high affinity to Van, and after binding to Van, their fluorescence intensity at 470 and 650 nm changed. Using this nanoplatform, an excellent linear correlation between the fluorescence response and Van concentrations of 0.01–100 mg/mL (R2 = 0.9917) was achieved, with a LOD of 2.79 ng/mL. Notably, 1 mg/mL Van could be detected with the naked eye, demonstrating the ease and effectiveness of the biosensor. Furthermore, this platform could distinguish Van from glycopeptide antibiotics and allowed for its detection in rabbit serum samples.555

GSH is a vital endogenous antioxidant with a host of physiological roles. Notably, GSH is indispensable for the maintenance of a cellular redox balance. As shown in Table 6, the normal concentration of GSH in the blood is 8.23–17.4 μM.556 However, aberrant changes in these levels can lead to disruptions in biological tissues and contribute to disease onset.557 To sense GSH in human blood serum samples, Qu et al. synthesized a TPE derivative (1,2-bis[4-(3-sulfonatopropoxyl)-phenyl]-1,2-diphenylethene sodium salt 3, TPE3) that acted as an AIEgen and also constructed a fluorescent probe (MnO2–SiO2 NPs-AIE). Briefly, the negatively charged MnO2 was first employed as both a positive charge protector for SiO2 NPs and a recognition unit for GSH. In the presence of GSH, Mn2+ was released, and the positive charges of SiO2 NPs were exposed, enabling the binding and aggregation of TPE3. This led to enhanced emission. Under optimized experimental conditions, a robust linear relationship (R2 = 0.992) between the fluorescence intensity of MnO2–SiO2 NPs-AIE and GSH concentrations of 0.5 to 100 mM was obtained, with a LOD of 200 nM. When this system was used to examine clinical serum samples, the original GSH concentrations in the two serum samples were evaluated to be 4.12 and 3.44 μM, with satisfactory recoveries and precision values of 94.3% to 104.2% and 1.80% to 3.91%, respectively.558 Further details on biothiol detection using AIEgens can be found in the earlier literature.559

Summary of the Normal Concentration Range of Biomolecules in Urine

Creatinine, a waste product in urine produced due to the breakdown of a creatine in muscle tissue, is usually filtered by the kidney. However, its levels in urine are linked to muscle function and renal status. Generally, under normal physiological conditions, creatinine concentrations in the serum range from 35 to 40 mM. However, changes in serum creatinine levels can indicate diseases such as muscular dystrophy or renal failure. Typically, urinary protein and creatinine concentrations are common indicators of renal function. In 2019, Khataee et al. designed a fluorescent probe using glutathione-capped copper nanoclusters (CuNCs) and Al3+ for sensing creatinine. In the presence of Al3+ ions, the CuNCs could form aggregates via glutathione–Al3+ interactions, inducing enhanced fluorescence emission at 585 nm. However, when creatinine was present, it caused quenching in a concentration-dependent manner due to its coordination with Al3+ ions, which resulted in the disaggregation of the CuNCs-Al3+ complex. Hence, a good linear relationship (R2 = 0.997) between fluorescence quenching and creatinine concentrations in the range of 2.5–34 mg/L was achieved, with a LOD of 0.63 mg/L. In clinical serum samples, the recoveries ranged from 95.6% to 105.7%, indicating satisfactory performance.560

Several of the currently available AIEgen-based probes and sensors can specifically recognize certain biomolecules owing to their masterful design strategies. As a result, many scientists (ourselves included) involved in biological and material science have directed immense focus and efforts toward these liquid biopsy targets to improve diagnostic outcomes. However, it is worth noting that several of these diagnostic targets have not been identified in clinical samples, and many targets remain unexplored with AIEgen labeling. Nevertheless, we remain optimiztic that AIEgens can be designed to selectively target more existing biomolecules, creating a framework for future practical applications in diagnostics.

Smart AIEgens for Urine-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Urine is an excretory waste product produced by the kidneys and expelled from the body via the ureters and the urinary bladder. It is primarily composed of water, alongside small quantities of other constituents, such as protein, dead blood cells, DNA, toxins, carbohydrates, and ions (Table 6 and Figure 46).130,556,561 Changes in the composition of urine can serve as a direct indicator of various diseases.562,563 Similar to the blood samples used for liquid biopsies, urine samples are easy to obtain and allow testing for a large range of disorders, enabling easier diagnosis. Urine tests are valuable in the realm of precision medicine, especially for urothelial carcinoma.564−567 Various components such as cells, urinary tumor DNA (utDNA), cfDNA, cfRNA, exosomes, protein, enzyme, carbohydrate, and ions have been investigated as surrogates for urinary liquid biopsies, addressing many challenges encountered by clinicians.561,568−571 In some cases, urine samples can outperform blood samples for the diagnosis of certain diseases.572

The analytic procedures involved in urinary liquid biopsy include the isolation and characterization of urinary components. The main detection methods include histochemical examinations, omics profiling, microfluidic techniques, chemosensor-based methods, fluorescent probe-based methods, and so on. During testing, the specificity and sensitivity of these methods must be taken into consideration.127,129,130,573 Compared to costly and time-consuming methods that have unpredictable outcomes, fluorescence-based methods have emerged as the preferred choice for urinary liquid biopsy due to their sensitivity and selectivity.574−576 In this context, AIEgens possess great potential over conventional fluorescent probes. However, some components in urinary samples remain to be identified and detected using AIE probes.127 Herein, we comprehensively summarize the latest progress in the application of AIEgens for the evaluation of real urinary liquid biopsies.

cfRNA-Targeted Urinary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Nucleic acids, including DNA and RNA, are common targets for disease diagnosis. The presence of nucleic acids in urine samples can provide important insights into disease progression. When combined with sequencing technologies, urinary liquid biopsies for nucleic acids appear extremely promising in the diagnosis, staging, and prognostication of urothelial carcinoma, as well as in monitoring the response to therapy, detecting minimal residual disease, and performing disease surveillance. However, the low abundance of nucleic acids limits the sensitivity of detection methods, hampers sensitivity, while the complexity of components undermines selectivity during sequencing. Therefore, Lou’s group constructed a cfRNA (miR-21) detection system (TPEPy-LDNA, Cy3-MBDNA, and FAM-LDNA) via an enzyme-aided recycling amplification strategy using an AIEgen as the fluorescence indicator (Figure 47A). TPEPy-LDNA contained two segments: the recognition component (DNA, 5′-CAGTCTGATAAGCTA-3′) and the fluorescence signal component (AIEgen, TPEPy). miR-21 induced the formation of the hydrophilic aggregate of duplex DNA-RNA with a blunt 3′ terminus, resulting in weak fluorescence intensity at 560–620 nm. Then, Exo III caused the cleavage of the recognition segments, causing the release of both miR-21 and AIEgens. The miR-21 could bind to another probe and trigger the second phase, and the hydrophobic AIEgens formed aggregates in a hydrophilic environment to emit fluorescence. TPEPy-LDNA could achieve a low LOD of 1 pM for miR-21 detection, with high selectivity and specificity. Its LOD was lower than the LOD of FAM-LDNA and Cy3-MBDNA (10 pM and 7 pM, respectively) in vitro and in live cells (Figure 47B–E). In clinical samples, the probe could effectively detect miR-21 levels and distinguish between urine samples from cancer patients and healthy individuals, demonstrating its excellent specificity. In addition, TPEPy-LDNA could also be used to detect miRNA-21 in live cells.599

Unfortunately, no studies so far have focused on the use of AIEgens for urinary CTCs, ctDNA, and EVs. Thus, additional endeavors are required to achieve advancements in this area of clinical diagnostics.

Protein-Targeted Urinary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Proteins are one of the most important components of urine biopsy. Under normal physical conditions, proteins with medium or high relative molecular weights, such as albumin and globulin, are retained by the body and do not enter the urine due to the pore size and charge barrier of the glomerulus. Meanwhile, proteins with small relative molecular weights can successfully pass through the glomerulus, although over 95% of filterable proteins are ultimately reabsorbed by the renal tubules. Generally, the kidneys produce 600–1800 mL of urine each day. Water accounts for 95% of water, with the other major components being urea, chloride, sodium, and potassium salts, and other biorelevant molecules. The mean concentration of urinary protein is 30–130 mg/day. However, a urinary protein content of over 150 mg/day indicates proteinuria, which is associated with various pathological states. Proteinuric kidney diseases, such as focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) and diabetic kidney disease (DKD), are a group of biological disorders known by the presence of excess protein in the urine.600 As mentioned previously, the concentration of urinary protein is significantly different under healthy and diseased conditions. For instance, a higher albumin/creatinine ratio (ACR) is observed in patients with a higher severity of DKD.601 Although urine is an unstable and dynamic fluid because its composition can change with ingested nutrients and the frequency of urination, urinary proteins are considered stable. The abnormal modifications of these proteins are associated with diseases, making them valuable biomarkers for early detection and disease monitoring.602−604 Therefore, the accurate detection of urinary proteins is crucial for early diagnosis and intervention, particularly in patients requiring lifelong renal replacement therapy for end-stage kidney disease. Currently, 24 h urinary protein analysis remains the gold standard for the diagnosis of proteinuria. In this context, the commonly used protein detection methods include chemiluminescence immunoassays, LC-MS/MS, HPLC, ELISA, colorimetry, fluorescent probes, omics, and so on. However, several of these methods required long timeframes, are cumbersome and lack sensitivity. Moreover, these methods often fail to detect disease biomarkers at early stages. Thus, more effective methods are urgently required.

To address this challenge, Hong’s group construed a fluorescent probe TC426, which had AIE characteristics, for the sensitive and specific detection of HSA in urine. TC426 was synthesized via the Knoevenagel condensation reaction. The reaction between 1,3-indandione and 4-(diethylamino)salicylaldehyde yielded intermediate compound 2; meanwhile, the subsequent alkylation of the hydroxy group of compound 2 with 1,3-propane sultone to generate a sodium sulfonate group enabled the synthesis of TC426 (Figure 48A). In an aqueous solution, TC426 showed no obvious fluorescence emission. However, after HSA was added, TC426 showed bright fluorescence at 535 nm upon interacting with HAS. This was because peripheral functional groups such as sulfonate, pyridinium, carboxylate, trialkyl-ammonium, and tetrazolium groups facilitated the entry of TC426 into the interior domain, inducing in-solubilization and RIM in water (Figure 48D). The fluorescence intensity of TC426 exhibited a robust linear correlation with HSA concentrations within the microalbuminuria range (0–1000 mg/L, R2 = 0.9954), and the LOD was calculated to be 0.253 mg/L (3.74 nM). Hence, the performance of TC426 was comparable to that of the BSPOTPE and TPE-4TA probes. More importantly, TC426 also possessed excellent anti-interference abilities and sensitivity toward creatinine and other major components of urine. Upon excitation with visible light, TC426 exhibited a good linear relationship between fluorescence intensity and concentration (0–200 mg/L) in clinical urinary samples, as well as satisfactory recovery rates of 81.3–129.9% (Figure 48B,C).605

POCT is an effective method for clinical diagnosis. Several types of nanomaterials have been proposed and developed for enhancing the efficiency of POCT. By leveraging the coffee-ring effect postsample evaporation, the TTVP-based POCT device could detect HSA levels within the range of 9.375 to 300 mg/L, with a robust linear concentration-dependent relationship, and the LOD was estimated to be 9.375 mg/L. Enzyme-Targeted Urinary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Enzymes are also important components of urine, as listed Table 6. Many urinary enzymes serve as biomarkers for disease. For example, α-amylase is a common diagnostic indicator in urinary tests, and HAase is a urinary marker for the early diagnosis of bladder cancer.612 Telomerase, a ribonucleoprotein that adds a specific sequence (TTAGGG) to the 3′-end of telomeres, is also a sensitive biomarker and can enable early cancer detection.613 Meanwhile, proteases, acting as protein regulators within cells, are implicated in numerous diseases such as respiratory disease, cancer, bowel disorders, renal dysfunction, infection, autoimmune disorders, and CVDs. Thus, protease is also a biomarker for several diseases.614,615 Therefore, urinary enzymes represent another promising target for urine liquid biopsies, complementing the focus on utDNA, ctDNA, cfRNA, and protein.

As for biopsies of blood samples, AIEgens also show great advantages in urinary biopsies. A previous literature review by Yi summarized enzyme detection methods based on bioprobes with AIE characteristics.128 However, this review did not include studies that examined clinical urine samples. Here, we provide a summary of urinary enzyme detection based on AIEgen in Table 8, along with some typical examples. For instance, Xia et al. designed a ratiometric fluorescent AIEgen-based bioprobe for detecting telomerase activity in urine, aiming to enhance reproducibility and robustness (Figure 51). This bioprobe was designed by taking advantage of electrostatic forces. Positively charged silole-R could spontaneously bind to the negatively charged DNA backbone, leading to enhanced fluorescence intensity and an increase in the length of the DNA strand. As telomerase induced primer extension, silole-R bound to the negatively charged sites only and demonstrated aggregation, enhancing blue fluorescence intensity at 478 nm. Meanwhile, the fluorescence intensity of Cy5 at 665 nm remained constant, serving as an internal control. The ratiometric emission intensity ratio (I478/I665) of this bioprobe correlated with the concentration of telomerase, demonstrating good specificity, reproducibility, and a linear relationship (R2 > 0.9) between fluorescence intensity and the amount of cancer cell lysate. When this bioprobe was applied to distinguish telomerase extracts from bladder cancer blood samples and normal urine specimens, the bioprobe exhibited practical reproducibility and a 100% positive result rate with high fluorescence intensity (Figure 51).616 Meanwhile, Wang et al. synthesized a novel AIEgen called 4,4′,4″,4′′′-(ethene-1,1,2,2-tetrayltetrakis(benzene-4,1-diyl))tetrakis(1-(4-bromobenzyl)pyridin-1-ium) bromide (TPE-4N+). They then constructed an AIE fluorescent sensor based on TPE-4N+. TPE-4N+ could bind to HA via electrostatic adsorption to form TPE-HA. This led to effective emission recovery in the yellow-green region (blue-shifted from 580 to 536 nm). However, when HAase was present, the enzymatic digestion of HA caused depolymerization and fluorescence quenching (Figure 52A). This “on–off” change could be applied for analyzing HAase activity with high sensitivity and specificity using two consecutive linear equations: y = 0.009–0.418× (R2 = 0.985) in the range of 0.05 to 2 U/mL, and y = 0.001–0.036× (R2 = 0.998) in the range of 2.75 to 5 U/mL. The LOD was 0.02 U/mL, and the method showed high selectivity (Figure 52B,C). HAase concentrations in normal healthy individuals were found to be lower than reported when this probe was applied for testing clinical urine samples. Moreover, TPE-4N+ exhibited good average recoveries of 97–108%, meeting the requirements for practical use (Figure 52).617

Carbohydrate-Targeted Urinary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Urinary carbohydrate content is an important indicator of diabetes, offering effective insight into diabetic pathology. In addition, it can also serve as a biomarker for other pathological conditions. For example, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a characteristic component of bacterial outer membranes, is regarded as a good biomarker for urinary tract infections (Figure 53). In this regard, Wang et al. synthesized a TPE-based AIEgen named TPEPyE that bore a positively charged pyridinium pendant. The synthetic cascade was as follows: TPE → TPE-Br → Py-TPE → TPEPyE (Figure 53A,B). Notably, TPEPyE showed maximum emission at 519 nm following excitation at 415 nm. The electrostatic interactions between LPS and TPEPyE caused the formation of TPEPyE aggregates, causing enhanced (22-fold) maximum emission at 515 nm. This represented a blue-shift from the emission maxima of pure TPEPyE (519 nm) and was linearly correlated with the concentration of LPS in the range of 0 to 0.5 μM (R2 = 0.995) (Figure 53C,D). In addition, the LOD was 370 pM. When used to detect LPS in artificial urine samples, TPEPyE exhibited increased fluorescence intensity as the concentration of LPS increased, although the fluorescence intensity was lower (4-fold) than that observed with an LPS standard solution with the same concentration (R2 = 0.992) and the LOD (400 pM) was slightly higher. Nevertheless, the method continued to show high sensitivity and selectivity. Subsequently, TPEPyE was employed for LPS detection in clinical urine samples from patients with urinary tract infections (Figure 53E). During this test, TPEPyE exhibited bright yellow-to-greenish emission in infection samples, while a weak emission peak was observed in normal samples. This suggested that TPEPyE could selectively identify Gram-negative bacteria but not Gram-positive bacteria.621 Hence, the findings showed that AIEgen-based fluorescent probes can be used to clinically diagnose urinary tract infections.

The core–shell configuration led to high fluorescence intensity, which could be attributed to the AIEgen’s stacking-enhanced photoluminescence and the simultaneous reduction of photon loss from the thin magnetic shell. In addition, the significant magnetic activity of the magnetic shielding, coupled with modification using a double antibody, was used to design a sandwich immunoassay. As a result, the dual-functionalized MANP showed immunochromatographic sensing capabilities. Upon application for procalcitonin detection in standard solutions, MANP signals showed a robust linear relationship with procalcitonin concentrations of 0.0018–62.5 ng/mL (R2 = 0.9977), with the LOD being 0.0012 ng/mL. This FLIA for POCT showed high sensitivity and selectivity. Moreover, when the assay was used to test real human plasma samples, a strong positive correlation was observed between the results of the MANP-FLIA method and the standard CLIA method (R2 = 0.966), demonstrating the effectiveness of this assay in clinical sample detection. In another application, MANP was utilized for detecting lipoarabinomannan, a promising tuberculosis (TB) biomarker, in standard solutions. MANP showed a good linear relationship with lipoarabinomannan concentrations from 0.01 to 20 ng/mL (R2 = 0.997), with a LOD of 0.0016 ng/mL and high sensitivity and selectivity against Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Rv and other TB mycobacterial strains for POCT using the FLIA method. Furthermore, in real human urine samples, MANP could effectively distinguish TB-positive patients from healthy individuals (Figure 54).622 This AIEgen-based nanoplatform thus offered an alternative method for the early diagnosis of TB in resource-limited settings. This method was based on the generation of persistent reactive oxygen species from AIEzyme-associated cyclic energy transfer between the AIEzyme and luminol, which catalyzed the chemiluminescence of luminol.624

Urea-Targeted Urinary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Urea is the primary nitrogenous end-product of protein metabolism and is excreted from the body via urine.625 Abnormal levels of urea in the urine usually result from pathological conditions such as severe dehydration, lung disease, hepatic failure, kidney failure, acute nephritis, and sepsis, among others. Thus, it is vital to detect urea levels in urine samples. The high sensitivity and selectivity of fluorescent probes make them promising candidates for detecting urea levels in clinical samples. Therefore, AIEgen have been developed for urea detection, and we summarize the advances in the area of urea detection via AIEgen-based liquid biopsy.

In 2020, an AIEgen called perylene diimide, functionalized with imidazole (PDI-Hm), was synthesized by Lee et al. PDI-Hm was insoluble in polar solvents and quickly aggregated under these conditions, causing obvious emission at 542 nm due to its perylene backbone. However, the introduction of ionizable imidazole groups retarded the aggregation of perylene through repulsive electrostatic forces. After urease was incorporated into the nanosystem, it induced the hydrolysis of urea to generate hydroxides, which caused the aggregation and fluorescence quenching of PDI-Hm. Briefly, the hydroxides generated from the enzymatic hydrolysis of urea deprotonated the imidazole groups in HCl/methanol mixture, reducing the electrostatic repulsion between PDI-Hm molecules. As a result, aggregation and fluorescence quenching was achieved (Figure 55A,B). PDI-Hm enabled the effective and rapid (<1 min) detection of urea within the range of 0–10 mM, with high sensitivity; additionally, the LOD was estimated to be 0.4 mM (Figure 55C,D). When PDI-Hm was applied for testing clinical urine samples, it yielded results comparable to those of currently used clinical methods, with 93.4% of consistency. This suggested that PDI-Hm exhibited considerable selectivity for urea in urine samples (Figure 55E). Moreover, the fabrication of PDI-Hm gel films allowed the rapid screening of excessive urea levels in urine samples (Figure 55).626

Another AIE probe, the copper nanoclusters@Al3+ complex, was also fabricated by Han et al. for the detection of urea after the incorporation of urease. Urease-catalyzed hydrolysis induced the generation of ammonia from urea, and further hydrolyzation yielded OH–, thereby increasing the pH of the immediate environment. This hydrolysis caused linear concentration-dependent (20–150 μM of urea) fluorescence quenching at 585 nm after excitation at 340 nm, and the LOD was estimated to be 5.86 μM. The method showed higher sensitivity, selectivity, and reproducibility than other reported methods. When this probe was applied for analyzing clinical urine samples, the concentration of urea in human urine was found to be 15.90 μΜ after 10 000-fold dilution, indicating a healthy range (155–388 mM). The performance of this method was comparable to that of ELISA, and the recoveries ranged from 99.23 to 101.4%.627 In addition, amines and ammonia were detected using 2-(2-hydroxy-5-methyl) benzothiazole (HBT) derivates (HPT) via colorimetric and fluorescence-based detection.628

Small Biomolecules-Targeted Urinary Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

As shown in Figure 47 and Table 6, urine contains numerous small molecular weight biomolecules that are metabolized and excreted by various organs. These biomolecules are associated with various diseases. For example, pyrophosphate (PPi) is a biomarker for joint disease,629 1-hydroxypyrene (1-OHPy) can reflect exposure to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs),630 and metabolites included within urinary exosome are linked to membranous nephropathy.631 In general, urine contains a few species of small chemical compounds that provide genetic and proteic information and can be used as biomarkers for disease diagnose. To aid the detection of these compounds, various AIEgens have been developed.

In one study, protamine-templated gold nanoclusters (PRT-AuNCs) exhibiting the AIE effect (AIE-PRT-AuNCs) were fabricated via a step reaction process by Wang and colleagues. The AIE-PRT-AuNCs exhibited maximum emission at 595 nm after 300 nm laser excitation. The introduction of 1-hydroxypyrene (1-OHPy) caused Au–N bond cleavage in AIE-PRT-AuNCs and induced the generation of hydrogen bonds between the −OH groups in 1-OHPy and the N atoms and/or carboxylic anions in protamine. This led to electrostatic interactions among AIE-PRT-AuNCs, which carried a highly positive charge, and 1-OHP, which was negatively charged, resulting in the generation of inter- and/or intra complexes. This caused the aggregation-induced photoluminescence enhancement of AIE-PRT-AuNCs (Figure 56A,D). Therefore, 1-OHPy could be quantitatively detected across a wide linear range of 0.924 to 74.1 nM under optimal experimental conditions, with an LOD of 0.277 nM and high sensitivity and selectivity (Figure 56B,C). While applied to four human urine samples, the recovery results were comparable to those obtained with the HPLC method (Figure 56).632

PPi is a compound of interest due to its role in multiple cellular processes. In 2018, Fan’s group synthesized a Schiff base AIE probe (N,N′-bis (salicylidene)-1,4-diaminobenzen) (Figure 57A) for detecting PPi. The as-prepared probe could bind to Al3+ to form a probe-Al3+ complex by providing the N and O donor center, inducing aggregation, and enhancing fluorescence intensity. Probe-Al3+ disaggregates after the addition of PPi because PPi showed stronger binding ability to the probe than Al3+. This caused a reduction in probe fluorescence (Figure 57B). The presence of Al3+ was detected (0–180 μM) via a good linear relationship (R2 = 0.9908), a LOD of 0.39 μM, excellent sensitivity, and selectivity over metal ions, anions, and biomolecules. Moreover, the probe could detect PPi over concentrations of 0–60 μM, and there was a robust linear relationship (R2 = 0.9904) between the gradual decrease in fluorescence intensity and PPi concentration (LOD = 0.75 μM). The probe was utilized to analyze clinical urine samples; the recoveries of Al3+ and PPi in the diluted samples were found to be 94–101% and 97–101%, respectively, demonstrating the potential of the probe for clinical application.633

In 2019, another fluorescent bioprobe was designed for the selective and sensitive detection of PPi. For this probe, which was constructed by Wang et al., the AIEgen TPE was synthesized via a Michael addition reaction between TAEA and TPE-epoxide. It was subsequently embedded within rigid hyperbranched poly(amido amine) (HPA) to produce TPE-HPAs, including the P1, P2, and P3 variants. The large number of amine moieties in TPE-HPAs facilitated a “turn-on” fluorescent detection mechanism for PPi anions (Figure 58A). This was mediated by two mechanisms, the first being electrostatic attraction and the second involving the neutralization of positive charges on TPE-HPAs. These mechanisms caused the HPA framework to become less rigid, releasing TPE units. Among the three TPE-HPA variants, P2 exhibited excellent linearity with PPi concentrations of 0–1.0 mM, with an LOD of 6.5 nM and excellent selectivity and sensitivity (Figure 58B). P2 was also capable of accurately detecting PPi concentrations in human urine, with excellent recoveries of 95.5–102.1% (Figure 58C).634

Iodide, an essential micronutrient, is required for the normal functioning of the thyroid gland and for normal neurological activity. Both the deficiency and excessive intake of iodine can lead to thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism, respectively) and other health concerns. To detect iodide levels, Singh et al. synthesized an AIEgen probe named compound 6 (Figure 59A), which formed aggregates in a 50% HEPES buffer (HB) fraction mixed with a THF solution. After iodide was added to the mixture, the fluorescence intensity was selectively enhanced owing to the strong binding affinity of compound 6 with iodide than with the other organic anions. In addition, with the increase in HB buffer concentration, more aggregates were formed, and the fluorescence increased. Compound 6 was titrated against increasing iodide concentrations. This resulted in the gradual quenching of the twin emission bands at 380 and 399 nm up to a concentration of 0.371 mM (92.06%) (Figure 59B) with high selectivity (Figure 59C). When compound 6 was applied for detecting iodide levels in the clinical urine samples, it exhibited a promising performance with a LOD of 49.3 μg as well as a good recovery rate (87.12–98.68%). These results demonstrated the effectiveness and potential of compound 6 in detecting iodide.635 In addition, other AIEgens, including 1-(2-hydroxynaphthylmethylene)-2-(2-hydroxybenzylidene) hydrazine for Zn2+ (LOD: 46 nM)636 and TPE-QN for Hg2+ (LOD: 71.8 nM),637 were developed for ion detection in urine sample, highlighting the excellent performance of AIEgens in this context.

In another study, N,N′-bis(salicylidene)-p-phenylenediamine (BSPD), N,N′-bis(4-hydroxylsalicylidene)-p-phenylenediamine (BSPD-OH), and N,N′-bis(4-methoxylsalicyli-dene)-p-phenylenediamine (BSPD-OMe) were developed as new matrices for the analysis of small molecules in urine samples via matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS). These compounds were employed for analyzing low molecular weight compounds, owing to their low matrix ions and limited background interference, which effectively decreased matrix interference during analysis.638 This study suggested that AIEgens could not only serve as fluorescent probe for chemical compound sensing but also help in the identification of new biomarkers.

The detection of chemical compounds with small molecular weights in urine via AIEgen-based fluorescent probes remain a challenge. Another key factor impeding the development of urine sample biopsies is the lack of urinary biomarkers for several diseases. Thus, the integration of fundamental research and nanomedicine is warranted to address this gap.

Drug-Targeted Urinary Biopsy with Smart AIEgen

Drug abuse is a serious threat to both individuals and social development. Thus, drug screening can be useful for interventions against drug addiction. Nevertheless, drugs are also an indispensable part of healthcare and disease management. The pharmacokinetic features of drugs (e.g., bioavailability, circulation lifetime, and duration of action) as a function of time often need to be analyzed with rapid speed and high accuracy in various biofluids. At present, urinary tests are a common tool for drug screening. The standard drug analysis techniques employed at present include immunoassays, mass spectrometry, and chromatography. However, these techniques are cumbersome and often expensive. Therefore, fluorescent probes have emerged as a valuable option for drug detection. Among these fluorescent probes, AIEgens appear to be the most useful due to their resistance to interference and high stability, among other advantages. Excitingly, a commercial kit for the simultaneous detection of MOP-MET-KET-COC-MDMA-THC has been developed by the AIE Institute and brought to the market. This kit has low LODs of 0.2, 0.2, 0.2, 0.5, 0.2, and 0.05 ng/mL, respectively. Despite this progress, there are few reports focused on the forensic detection of drugs in urine samples. To this end, Huang and colleagues synthesized an AIEgen (BA-TPE) containing both a phenylboronic group and double bonds (Figure 60A). Based on this AIEgen, they constructed molecularly imprinted polymer (MIPs) sensors (AIE-MIPs) via reflux precipitation polymerization for the fluorescence-based detection of cathinone (CAT). This sensor exhibited selectivity toward CAT over other compounds, such as DIC, COD, COC, and MOR. Therefore, the introduction of CAT induced a significant increase in probe fluorescence intensity at 467 nm (Figure 60B). In forensic urine samples, a linear relationship between the fluorescence intensity of this AIE-MIP sensor and CAT concentrations of 2–12 μM (R2 = 0.9939) was observed (Figure 60C), with a LOD and LOQ of 0.32 μM and 1.07 μM, respectively.639 In other studies, Zn-BTEC MOF materials,640 trypsin-AuNCs,641 and AuNCs@SiO2-MIPs642 were applied to effectively detect urinary chlortetracycline (CTC, LOD: 28 nM), carbidopa (LOD: 6.5 nM), and erythromycin levels (ERY, LOD: 12 nM), respectively. These studies demonstrated the excellent performance of AIEgen in urinalysis.

Urinary Biopsy for Other Compounds Using Smart AIEgens

In addition to the biomolecules mentioned above, several other compounds are required at low levels for normal biochemical and physiological functions in living organisms and are subsequently metabolized and excreted. These biocomponents can also provide valuable information for disease diagnose and monitoring. This section summarizes the progress in the application of AIEgens for detecting various urinary components.

ATP has pivotal intra- and extracellular functions, serving the direct energy source for almost all biological processes. Both low and excessive amounts of ATP are associated with a series of diseases, such as NDs, nephropathy, CVDs, and so on.420,643,644 Therefore, ATP is a key biomarker for various diseases.645 For example, ATP levels in red blood cells can serve as a marker for sepsis,646 and CSF ATP levels are associated with encephalopathy, stroke-like episodes (MELAS), mitochondrial myopathy, lactic acidosis, and so on.647 Interestingly, urinary ATP has been identified as a novel biomarkers for bladder cancer and interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome.648,649 In 2019, Guo’s group designed and constructed an AIE probe based on glutathione-modified silver nanoclusters (AgNCs/GSH) for ATP detection (Figure 61A). Zn2+ enhanced the fluorescent emission of AgNCs/GSH at 650 nm by forming AgNCs/GSH/Zn2+ aggregates. When ATP was added, the stronger binding affinity of ATP toward Zn2+ via strong Zn–O–P bonds led to the dissociation of Zn2+ from AgNCs/GSH. Thus, the fluorescence intensity of AgNCs/GSH was negatively correlated with the ATP concentration. Using AgNCs/GSH, ATP concentrations within the linear range of 1–110 mM could be monitored, with the LOD being 0.8 mM (Figure 61B,C). The AgNCs/GSH probe was also successfully applied for analyzing ATP levels in human urine samples, with good recoveries of 97.6% to 103%, comparable to the performance of HPLC.650

Adenosine, an endogenous purine ribonucleoside, can be detected in all human cells and has important functions in different physiological processes.651 One study demonstrated that adenosine can accumulate at abnormally high concentrations in solid tumors, supporting their growth before being excreted via the urine.652 Therefore, urinary adenosine has emerged as an important biomarker for cancer.653 To this end, Xie et al. developed a fluorescent probe called TPE-ABA by combining 1,2-bis[4-(triethylammoniomethyl)phenyl]-1,2-diphenylethenedibromide (TPE-2N+, an AIE molecule) with an adenosine-specific aptamer (ABA, 5′-ACCTGGGGGAGTATTGCGGAGGAAGGT-3′) to detect adenosine. ABA could bind to adenosine with high binding affinity via hydrophobic interactions, and this binding induced a conformational change (random to G-quadruplex) that restricted the free rotation of phenyl rings in TPE-2N+. The TPE-ABA nanosystem achieved a linear adenosine detection range spanning from 10 pM to 0.5 mM (R2 = 0.996), with a LOD of 10 pM and good sensitivity and selectivity (Figure 64C,D). When applied in human urine samples, the nanosystem displayed satisfactory recoveries and precision rates of 96.8–90.0% and 1.2–2.0%, respectively.654

Dopamine is neurotransmitter that is involved in a host of brain functions and controlled behavioral characteristics. Abnormalities in dopamine balance can result in NDs655 and autism disorder.656 Therefore, Ilanchelian’s657 and Kailasa’s641 groups developed two unique AIE fluorescent probes for dopamine sensing. In Ilanchelian’s study, glutathione-protected silver nanoclusters (GSH-AgNCs) that served as red-emissive nanoprobes at 645 nm were synthesized. The hydrogen bonding interactions between the carboxyl groups of GSH-AgNCs, and the amino groups of dopamine caused probe aggregation to enhance fluorescence intensity. Therefore, GSH-AgNCs could detect dopamine in the linear range of 20–200 nM with an LOD of 0.35 nM and good sensitivity and selectivity. When GSH-AgNCs were applied in real human urine samples, the recovery amounts of dopamine ranged from 96% to 98%.657 In Kailasa’s study, which involved the preparation of a trypsin-AuNCs nanoplatform, a higher LOD of 0.41 nM was reached. Their probe was also successfully applied for testing human urine samples and demonstrated high recovery and precision.641

Citrate is a crucial urinary metabolite and is associated with kidney dysfunction and cancer. Citrate abnormalities have a high predictive value. In previous studies, three AIE probes, DPP-Py1 and DPP-Py2,658DTPA-TPY-Zn,659 and TPE-Py,660 have been developed for the detection of urinary citrate, achieving low LODs (18 μM, 35 μM, and 10 μM, respectively) along with good sensitivity and selectivity. As described earlier, creatinine is a useful disease biomarker. Tang et al. developed IDATPE for sensing creatinine levels in artificial urine samples and found that their probe had good selectivity.661 However, these AIE probes were tested using artificial urine samples but not validated in clinical samples. Thus, further tests are required to confirm the applicability of these probes to clinical samples.

Spermine is a type of polyamine that is associated with tumors.662 Thus, many methods have been proposed to detect the content of spermine in biological fluids.663,664 The advantages of AIEgens make them valuable candidates for sensing spermine levels effectively. In 2021, Chatterjee and colleagues developed a TPE-based fluorescent probe 1@CB[7] from tetrameric TPE amphiphiles (TPEHP), cucurbit[7]uril (CB[7]), and HAp NPs (Figure 62A). When spermine or spermidine was added to the probe, it dislodged CB[6]OH from the assembly because its affinity to CB portal was much stronger than that of the pyridyl headgroup. This caused the release of the soluble molecule TPEHP, which, owing to its good solubility, caused weaker fluorescence emission, mirroring the behavior observed with spermidine. Hence, spermine or spermidine could be detected based on the amount of fluorescence quenching (Figure 62A). 1@CB[7] exhibited good sensitivity and selectivity toward spermine and spermidine within the linear range of 0.1–1.0 μM and 0.25–10.0 μM, respectively, (R2 = 0.990 and 0.994, respectively) (Figure 62B–E). The LOQ was 0.1 μM for spermine and 0.25 μM for spermidine, and the LOD was 0.014 μM for spermine and 0.036 μM for spermidine. Most importantly, 1@CB[7] could detect spermine and spermidine in urine and serum samples, meeting the requirements for early cancer diagnosis (Figure 62F,G).665 Therefore, this fluorescent probe holds potential for further clinical applications. In the same year, another fluorescent chemosensor based on TPE) conjugated with pentiptycene (SS-1) was constructed for detecting spermine in the linear range of 1–15 μM with a LOD of 0.3 μM.666 In another study, fluorescent chemosensor 1 generated from TEP was applied to detect spermine and spermidine with LODs of 0.70 μM and 1.17 μM, respectively, in human urine samples (Figure 62).667

These innovative and effective strategies have boosted research and clinical translation in the field of AIEgen-based diagnostics. Additional advances in the identification of urinary biomarkers and AIEgens could lead to more achievements in this area, facilitating improved healthcare.

Smart AIEgens for Saliva Sample-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Saliva is primarily secreted by three major glands in the oral cavity along with some minor salivary glands. It is an odorless, foamy, slightly cloudy, and milky hypertonic solution with a weakly acidic pH (pH = 6.6–7.1). Water makes up about 94–99% of saliva. However, as a complex fluid, saliva also consists of gingival crevicular fluid, leukocytes, oral mucosal exudate, epithelial cells, several microorganisms, several biorelevant molecules, and other important substances. Salivary secretion is easily influenced by external factors as it is directly exposed to the environment.670 The functions of saliva are as follows: (1) cleansing the mouth by washing away food residues and microbes, (2) primary digestive conversion of macrobiomaterials into microbiomaterials, (3) serving as the first line of nonspecific immuno-protection against pathgens, and (4) preventing enamel demineralization and promoting remineralization to maintain the dynamic balance of the enamel.671 Therefore, alterations in the components of saliva can serve as biomarkers for various diseases.672−677 Due to its noninvasive nature and the rich content of saliva, salivary testing is an appealing diagnostic method. Therefore, saliva has been adopted as a novel source for liquid biopsy.672,678−680 At present, the diagnostic targets in saliva-based biopsies include DNA (cfDNA, ctDNA, telomere, DNA methylation),681 RNA (lncRNA, microRNA, circRNA, mRNA, piRNA, etc.),682 proteins,683 enzymes,684 hormones, carbohydrates, ions, metabolites,685 and EVs.686,687 The process of saliva-based liquid biopsy involves: (1) the collection of salivary samples, (2) salivaomic detection and analysis, and (3) the clinical application of results for disease diagnosis, early diagnosis, prognosistication, precision therapy, and therapeutic monitoring. Based on the diagnostic target, different strategies are adopted to analyze saliva liquid biopsies. For example, genomic and epigenomic analysis is performed using PCR, microarrays, NGS, and FISH, whereas proteomic analysis is performed using Western blots, ELISA, electrophoresis, MS, Raman spectroscopy, and so on. Meanwhile, information on the genome, epigenome, transcriptome, and proteome is collected from exosomes. However, these current clinical methods are expensive, time-comsuming, and cumbersome. In contrast, fluorescence detection is more sensitive, selective, easy to operate, and cost-effective. Thus, intelligent fluorescence-based biosensors and devices have immense potential in advancing salivary diagnostics.688,689 Given that AIEgens exhibit multiple benefits over conventional fluorescent probes, in this section, we provide a comprehensive summary of saliva-based liquid biopsy using AIEgens.

Protein-Targeted Salivary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

In general, the normal concentration of salivary proteins is 1–2 mg/mL.701 Abnormal salivary protein content is an indicator of diseases such as Sjögren syndrome, Down’s syndrome, MS, and diabetes. For example, under normal conditions, saliva shows HSA concentrations of <0.5 g/L. However, higher concentrations of this protein in the saliva can be indicative of type 2 diabetes mellitus, stomatitis, or other serious health problems.702 In this context, salivary protein detection can be immensely valuable in the realm of diseases diagnosis and monitoring. In 2018, Bhattacharya et al. developed a fluorescent probe (compound 1) for salivary HSA detection via a self-assembly method. This probe exhibited strong luminescence and pH sensitivity and formed thermoreversible nanoaggregates in pure water via the self-assembly of carbazole-based amphiphiles. The self-assembly of compound 1 constrained the intramolecular movement of the molecules, inducing an AIE-related change in fluorescence emission from blue (400 nm) to cyan (454 nm). When HSA was present, the compound 1 was encapsulated into the hydrophobic cavity of site-IIA in HSA. Here, the motion-induced change in emission (MICE) in the microenvironment of the embedded dye restricted the molecular motion of the flexible fluorescent probe but not its aggregation (Figure 64A). Therefore, compound 1 could detect HSA with high selectivity and showed a robust linear relationship between fluorescence intensity (I454/I400) and HSA concentrations of 0–25 mg/L, with a LOD of 0.27 mg/L. Meanwhile, with compound 1 + the HSA complex, the LOD of trypsin was estimated to 0.15 mg/L. In titration studies conducted on clinical serum, urine, and saliva samples diluted in PBS, the estimation of HSA levels using compound 1 yielded results comparable to those obtained using the Bromocresol green method, albeit with a 15% variation (Figure 64B). Interestingly, varying amounts of dilution were required for the plasma, urine, and saliva samples, suggesting that a small amount of sample was sufficient for HSA quantification.467

Ion-Targeted Salivary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Ions play a crucial regulatory role in oral health through various pathways. For example, Ag+ acts as a protector in the oral environment, while thiocyanate (SCN–) can regulate the metabolism of salivary bacteria and its levels are associated with smoking habits. Bu et al. designed a fluorescent probe based on an AIEgen named DIPIP (phenanthro[9,10-d]-imidazole derivative) for the sequential detection of SCN– and Ag+/AgNPs in aqueous solution and saliva samples. DIPIP showed distinct absorption peaks at 313, 355, and 360 nm and emission peaks at 400 nm. Under aqueous conditions, DIPIP formed a two-dimensional sheet structure, which restricted its intramolecular rotation and induced enhanced emission. In the presence of Ag+, DIPIP showed a better recognition capacity for Ag+ than for other ions. The DIPIP-Ag+ complex underwent aggregation to form three-dimensional rose-like particles sized about 4.0 μm. These aggregates had a tendency to form crystalline particles with reduced molecular restriction, resulting in quenched fluorescence intensity. When SCN– was introduced, it broke down the DIPIP-Ag+ complex and enabled the recovery of fluorescence intensity (Figure 65A). As such, DIPIP showed a robust linear relationship (R2 = 0.9948) between decreasing fluorescence intensity and increasing Ag+ concentrations over 0.075 to 1.0 μM, with a LOD of 74.5 nM (Figure 65B). Subsequently, the DIPIP-Ag+ complex was successfully applied for SCN– detection (range, 0–0.2 μM) with recovered fluorescence intensity (R2 = 0.9922) and an LOD as low as 7.8 nM (Figure 65C). When the DIPIP-Ag+ complex was employed for SCN– detection in diluted saliva samples from one nonsmoker and three smokers, the DIPIP-Ag+ complex showed higher fluorescence intensities in the smoker samples than in the nonsmoker samples, with favorable recoveries (99.8–108.5%).703 This report, despite being the only one in its field, demonstrates that AIEgen-based fluorescent probes can be reliable for detecting ions in salivary samples.

Microorganism-Targeted Salivary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Throughout human history, numerous pathogen outbreaks have posed serious threats to human health. More recently, the outbreak and global spread of COVID-19 and monkeypox inflicted serious damage around the world. Therefore, the timely and accurate detection of pathogens, especially viruses, is crucial for preserving public health.

Given its role as the first/second line of defense in the body, saliva is as an ideal biofluid for disease detection.704 AIEgen-based probes have thus been developed for this purpose. In 2018, Tang and collaborators developed a dual-modality readout immunoassay platform for the ultrasensitive detection of human enterovirus 71 (EV71) based on a multifunctional AIEgen (TPE-APP) (Figure 66A). This platform involves capturing EV71 virions using immunomagnetic beads (IMNs) and subsequently binding them with biotinylated Abs (BAbs), rabbit polyclonal antibodies (P-Abs), and the signal tags streptavidin-ALP (SA-ALP) to construct a dual-modality readout immunoassay nanoplatform. Within this framework, the incorporation of the ALP enzyme catalyzed TPE-APP hydrolysis through the removal of its phosphoryl groups, leading to the production of water-insoluble and aggregated TPE-DMA, resulting in obviously enhanced fluorescence levels. Termed “Channel I,” this fluorescence enhancement exhibited remarkable accuracy and extremely high sensitivity toward virions. Meanwhile, “Channel II” involved the followed processes: TPE-APP hydrolysis simultaneously reduced Ag+ to generate a silver nanoshell surrounding gold nanoparticles (AuNP), leading to a pronounced plasmonic color change that enabled the preliminary screening of virus particles with the naked eye (Figure 66B). Under the fluorescence modality, owing to the immunomagnetic enrichment and high sensitivity and selectivity, the LOD for EV71 virions was 1.4 copies/μL and the detection range was 1.67 × 103 to 2.505 × 105 copies/mL (R2 = 0.992). Moreover, the semiquantitative screening of EV71 virions was also achieved with the naked eye in the range of 1.3 × 103 to 2.5 × 106 copies/μL (R2 = 0.996), with a LOD of 868.4 copies/μL. When applied in 24 throat and cloacal swab samples from patients with hand, foot, and mouth disease, the probe provided a diagnostic accuracy of 100% when compared to RT-PCR (Figure 66C). By changing the conjugated antibody, H7N9 and Zika virus could also be identified effectively using this immunoassay platform. Furthermore, this immunoassay platform did not require complex sample preparation procedures or costly equipment, underscoring the convenience and high efficiency of AIEgens in virus detection.705

In 2022, Fujimaki et al. developed another quick and sensitive virus detection method based on AIEgens and enzymatic reactions in conjunction with immunomagnetic beads, achieving a remarkable concentration of 1 × 108 particles/mL in subpicoliter microwells for salivary virus detection (Figure 67). By using this method with an AIE reagent (silole derivative with two targeting ligands), influenza A virus could be detected with a LOD of 3 × 105 copies/mL within 3 min. However, the high-concentration immunomagnetic beads method yielded a lower LOD of 1 × 102 copies/mL within 33 min. In clinical saliva samples, the LOD in diluted samples was estimated to be 3 × 106 and 1 × 103 copies/mL with the AIEgen and beads, respectively. Notably, both methods performed better than previous methods such as immunochromatography, PCR, and ELISA. The lower sensitivity of AIEgens when compared to the immunomagnetic beads could be attributed to the concentration applied.706

In addition to viruses, probes were also developed to detect other pathogens. Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis), which is difficult to detect as it typically hides in macrophages and remains in a dormant state, was effectively detected in human sputum samples using an AIEgen (TPAPy-S-d-Ala).707 By exploiting the metabolic properties of the peptidoglycan (PG) layer of M. tuberculosis, Lu et al. developed an AIEgen (TPAPy-S-d-Ala) for the metabolic labeling of PG and the subsequently high-throughput diagnosis of M. tuberculosis infection (Figure 68). Here, the key motiety of S-d-Ala was a specific pentapeptide (l-Ala-d-iso-Gln-m-DAP-d-Ala-DAla). This peptide could form crossed-links with the N-acetylmuramic acid (MurNAc) in the PG layer of the M. tuberculosis cytoderm vial,d-transpeptidases catalysis to fluorescently label it. In the TPAPy-S-d-Ala platform for M. tuberculosis detection, the external d-Ala molecules, which were a part of the AIEgen, integrated into PG and aggregated on the cytoplasmic membrane to induce fluorescence emission, and the fluorescence intensity could be measured via a microplate reader to reach a diagnosis of TB. This process enabled the visual observation of M. tuberculosis replication and discrimated live M. tuberculosis cells from dead bacterial cells. TPAPy-S-d-Ala exhibited good metabolic labeling efficiency as well as high sensitivity and specificity toward M. tuberculosis. When TPAPy-S-d-Ala was applied in a cohort of 26 clinical sputum samples (TB, 22’ healthy control, 4), TPAPy-S-d-Ala labeling showed excellent accuracy (95.7%), sensitivity (95.5%), and specificity (100%) compared to acid-fast staining (65.2%, 63.3%, and 100%, respectively) for TB diagnosis. Given these satisfactory results, a well-planned randomized controlled trial with a suitable sample size is required to acquire substantial evidence for the prospective clinical utilization of TPAPy-S-d-Ala.707

Drug-Targeted Salivary Liquid Biopsy with Smart AIEgens

Durg testing is crucial aspect of clinical trials, disease monitoring, and inspection scenarios, providing invaluable insights under different situations. In 2019, Wang’s group constructed an AIEgen-based probe, named POSS-TPE, for the detection of synthetic drugs using salivary biopsy (Figure 69A). POSS-TPE exhibited a typical emission peak at at 407 and 430 nm. When the water fraction increased to over 90%, an obvious AIE effect, accompanied by molecular self-assembly, was observed. This was because the aqueous environment restricted the intramolecular motions of POSS-TPE and increased π–π stacking interactions. While the former boosted fluorescence emission, the latter caused a shift in the emission spectra toward longer wavelengths and suppressed emission. The interplay between these two phenomena contributed toward the final emission characteristics of POSS-TPE. When POSS-TPE was applied for detecting methamphetamine and ketamine in artificial saliva, the small drug molecules entered the 3D self-assembled structures of the aggregates via noncovalent interactions, providing additional pathways for exciton migration. This facilitated the nonradiative annihilation of the aggregates by the drug quenchers, and an obvious quenching effect was observed with high sensitivity. Therefore, POSS-TPE could be used for the rapid on-site detection of methamphetamine and ketamine in artifical saliva (Figure 69).708 However, POSS-TPE remains to be validated in clinical saliva samples, which show greater complexity than artifical saliva samples. Needless to say, there is enormous scope for develping more AIEgen-based probes for disease detection with saliva biopsy.

Given that salivary liquid biopsy is a new field of research, the number of reports is fairly limited. With ample room for progress in this field, we remain optimistic about the future advancements in salivary biopsy facilitated by AIEgen-based technologies and their applications in the improvement of healthcare.

Smart AIEgens for Sweat Sample-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Sweat is produced by eccrine and apocrine glands and flows onto the surface of the skin and hair follicles to help maintain thermal homeostasis during physical or mental exertion and exposure to high temperatures. In addition, it aids in the excretion of various chemicals and metabolites from the body, and this process is modulated by the sympathetic nervous system in an adaptive, closed-loop manner. Sweat is the most readily available bodily fluid and can be obtained in the most noninvasive manner. Thus, sample collection can be done without causing any infection, irritation, or inconvenience. However, despite its potential, the widespread use of sweat for routine diagnostics has been limited by requirements for specialized equipment, skilled technicians, and strict protocols. Recently, the development of wearable biosensors has emerged as a solution for this challenge.709−712

The constituents of sweat include water, mineral electrolytes (sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium, etc.), lactic acid, urea, proteins (immunoglobulins and glycoproteins), glucose, hormone, and drugs. The components of sweat maintain its pH between 4.5 and 7.0. Notably, these components have been identified as biomarkers associated with various diseases, making sweat a promising source for liquid biopsy.709,713 For example, the levels of K+ and Na+ in the sweat serve as vital markers of metabolic status in humans,714 while lactate is an important marker of physiological stress, reflecting the switch between aerobic and anaerobic metabolism.715 Thus, sweat has been recognized as a new window into health.716,717 In the clinic, sweat-based liquid biopsy is only applied for the detection of chloride concentrations during the diagnosis of cystic fibrosis in infants. However, in fundamental research, sweat sampling is much more widespread. Notably, sweat can be sampled easily via various methods beyond wearable biosensors.718−720

Despite the widespread development of AIEgens in the field of diagnosis, there have been few reports on their application in the detection of sweat analytes. In 2017, Ng et al. developed an AIEgen probe named TPE-HPro to determine l-lactate levels in sweat and saliva. TPE-HPro was constructed by combining TPE with LOx. Here, LOx catalyzed the oxidation of l-lactate, leading to H2O2 production, which could be measured by TPE-HPro via fluorometry owing to the oxidation of phenylboronic pinacol ester on TPE-HPro, which released a p-quinone methide. The final remaining product emitted bright fluorescence at 530 nm (Figure 70A). TPE-HPro demonstrated a good performance in protein-rich specimens, yielding a stable fluorescent signal for 60 min. It could detect l-lactate in the linear concentration range of 0–200 μM (R2 = 0.999), with high sensitivity and selectivity and a superb LOD of 5.5 μM (Figure 70B). Thus, TPE-HPro performed better than other methods, as listed in Figure 70B, demonstrating its potential for l-lactate detection in clinical samples.721

(A) Schematic of l-lactate detection with TPE-HPro. (B) Comparative evaluation of different fluorescent l-lactate sensors. Adapted with permission from ref (721). Copyright 2017 American Chemical Society.

Fingers represent an important route of sweat secretion and AIEgens have been identified to effectively reveal latent fingerprints, as described in our previous literature review.722 Fingers can also offer important pathological information.723 Thus, the noninvasive detection of disease targets in sweat from the finger could be of great value and may someday be widely used in the real-time monitoring of human body functions.

Smart AIEgens for Lavage Fluid Sample-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Routine bronchoscopy is a standard clinical approach for diagnosing lung-associated diseases, including cancer. In particular, lavage fluid contain a series of biomarkers crucial for early disease diagnosis,724−726 making it a novel and effective source of liquid biopsy.727,728 Compared to diagnostic methods involving sequencing and other methods, fluorescence probes offer greater sensitivity and are less cubersome. In 2020, Zheng and Tang developed a density-responsive AIEgen-based bioprobe named MASPB for discrimating cancer cells based on differences in nuclear density between cancer cells and normal cells. MASPB ((E)-4-(4-(dimethylamino)styryl)-1-(3(trimethylammonio)propyl)pyridin-1-ium bromide) was fabricated via the Knoevenagel condensation reaction (Figure 71A). It could enter the nucleus, exhibiting red fluorescence at 621 nm. The fluorescence intensity depended on the amount of nuclear condensation, as nuclear condensation restricted the motion of MASPB (Figure 71B). When cells were stained with MASPB, distinct structural nuclear differences between tumor cells and normal cells become apparent, enabling the differentiation of rare tumor cells. When MASPB was applied to distinguish cancer cells using a fluorescence microscope, it achieved 100% accuracy in identifying cancer cells in malignant pleural effusion samples from 10 cancer patients (Figure 71C(a–c)). Moreover, MASPB could also differentiate between cancerous and normal regions in various tissue specimens (lung adenocarcinoma, colorectal cancer, liver cancer, cervical cancer, and squamous lung carcinoma) (Figure 71C(d,e)). This pioneering study showcased the utility of AIEgens in lavage fluid liquid biopsy.729

(A) Pathway of MASPB synthesis. (B) Schematic showing the sensing mechanism of MASPB. (C) Tumor cells identified by MASPB in clinical samples. Adapted with permission from ref (729). Copyright 2020 Elsevier BV.

TB is caused by the bacterium M. tuberculosis (Mtb), which primarily resides within macrophages and spreads as an infectious disease through aerosols. TB is the second most common cause of pathogen-related death worldwide.730 Notably, the increase in multidrug-resistant M. tuberculosis variants has considerably exacerbated the TB pandemic. Traditional TB diagnosis methods such as acid-fast staining, smear microscopy, microbial cell culture, and nucleic acid amplification are usually hindered by the high bacterial load requirement (at least 5000 bacilli/mL). These methods are often cubersome, time-comsuming, and offer poor sensitivity. To tackle this challenge, Zheng et al. developed a cytoderm-orthogonal AIEgen (TPAPy-D-Ala) for the rapid detection of M. tuberculosis (Figure 72A). TPAPy-D-Ala consisted of a functional AIE motif (TPAPy) with ROS generation capabilities and a metabolic motif (d-Ala) with specific PG metabolic labeling properties. The two motifs were conjugated via click a chemistry reaction and azide–alkyne cycloaddition (Figure 72B). The probe exhibited an absorption peak at 468 nm and an emission peak at 601 nm. At a molecular level, TPAPy-d-Ala was remodeled by l,d-transpeptidase to serve as an exogenous substrate for PG biosynthesis, and it was cross-linked into the pentapeptide side chains. This resulted in the metabolic labeling of the M. tuberculosis cytoderm, allowing the probe to effectively identify living bacteria. When applied for M. tuberculosis detection, TPAPy-d-Ala achieved the wash-free, rapid (10 min), and selective metabolic labeling of live M. tuberculosis, with a superb LOD of 102 CFU/mL and a 50-fold better diagnostic sensitivity for TB than acid-fast staining. When applied for M. tuberculosis detection in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) and sputum samples from TB patients, TPAPy-d-Ala exhibited a high accuracy (97.1% and 96.8%), sensitivity (96.4% and 96.2%), and specificity (100% and 100%) for clinical TB diagnosis (Figure 72C). Moreover, TPAPy-d-Ala was capable of killing M. tuberculosis via photodynamic therapy.731 These two reports demonstrated the good performance of AIEgen-based lavage fluid liquid biopsy.

Smart AIEgens for Nasal Discharge Sample-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Nasal discharge, a waste product secreted from the nose, contains many biorelevant molecules and often presents in various colors. Nasal discharge can also serve as a clinical sample for disease diagnosis.330,732,733 Nasopharyngeal–oropharyngeal swabs are also as useful as throat and cloacal swabs for SARS-CoV-2 detection. To easily and effectively detect SARS-CoV-2 RNA, in 2023, Gupta et al. developed an AIEgen complex (Ir1 and Ir2) for a simple, highly sensitive, and single-step fluorescence assay (Figure 73A–D). Here, Ir1 and Ir2 were produced by incorporating an iridium core that coordinated with one N,N-donor bipyridine ligand and two cyclometalating phenyl quinoline ligands. The ancillary bipyridine ligand was further linked to a unique ASO sequence (ASO1 or ASO2), which acted as the specific targeting element for SARS-CoV-2. After SARS-CoV-2 RNA was introduced, the ASO sequences bound to this RNA due to their comparable binding energies, promoting the propensity of aggregation. Thus, Ir1 and Ir2 exhibited “turn-on” fluorescence at 610 nm after excitation at 430 nm when SARS-CoV-2 RNA was present, yielding specific results at room temperature within 10 min, with an estimated LOD of 1.84 genome copies/reaction. When applied to 20 clinical nasopharyngeal–oropharyngeal swab samples, this assay showed a sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of 90%, 80%, and 85%, respectively, and was thus comparable with RT-LAMP and chip-/paper-based POCT for COVID-19 detection (Figure 73E).734 Additional exploration and research into the analysis of nasal discharge liquid biopsies could lead to improvements in healthcare.

Smart AIEgen for Synovial Fluid Sample-Targeted Liquid Biopsy

Bones support the structure of the body and enable movement. Articulations connect various bones, and conditions affecting these joints, including RA, OA, gout, meniscus injury, and others, pose significant health challenges. For instance, OA is a chronic degenerative bone and joint disease characterized by joint pain, the swelling of joint effusions, joint stiffness, joint friction, bone erosion, and impaired joint mobility. OA affects the cartilage, subchondral bone, and synovial membrane tissue of the knees, knuckles, and hips735 and is associated with aging, obesity, trauma, strain, and joint deformity, among other factors. Orthopedic disorders like OA are serious and chronic diseases that affect a large number of people and hamper their daily lives.5 Unfortunately, there is still no effective biomarker for the early diagnosis of these disorders.

Synovial fluid, a joint lubricant, is produced by the synovial membrane and enters the joint cavity. Recently, synovial fluid has been recognized as a useful source for liquid biopsy in orthopedic diseases.736−739 When compared to current diagnostic methods, including MR, ultrasonography, X-ray, CRP, and ESP, fluorescence-based diagnosis exhibits great advantages owing to the intrinsic properties of fluorescent probes. Notably, AIEgens have immense potential in diagnosing orthopedic disorders. For instance, to address the low sensitivity and complexity associated with existing technologies for detecting monosodium urate (MSU) crystals in gout, Hua et al. conducted affinity analysis experiments for MSU using seven TPE-containing luminogens (i.e., TPE-COOH, TPE-2COOH, TPE-4COOH, TPE-NH2, TPE-2NH2, TPE-4NH2, and TPE-ketoalkyne). Among these, TPE-ketoalkyne exhibited high selectivity and sensitivity, demonstrating maximum fluorescence emission at 503 nm following excitation at 353 nm. TPE-Ketoalkyne exhibited the strongest fluorescence emission and the highest binding sensitivity toward MSU crystals among all the AIEgens, differentiating it from HAP crystals. When applied to clinical samples, including human synovial fluid and tophi, TPE-ketoalkyne exhibited a high diagnosis efficiency toward MSU crystals among patients with gout, yielding results within 1 min.740 In another study, Sato and colleagues developed an AIEgen probe (TPE-X7) for detecting HA given its role in orthopedic inflammation and its potential as a pathophysiological biomarker for inflammation. TPE-X7 was constructed by attaching the N-terminal of X7 peptide (RYPISRPRKR) to TPE, nitrobenzoxadiazole (NBD), and thiazole orange (TO) (Figure 74). The binding of HA to the HA-recognition motif within the X7 peptide caused enhanced emission at 477 nm, and this enhancement was dependent on the molecular weight of HA. The LOD for HA was estimated to be 1500–1800 kDa (33 μg/mL). The probe exhibited excellent specificity for HA and did not respond to other members of the glycosaminoglycan family. When TPE-X7 was applied to test synovial fluid samples from patients with OA, the concentration of HA in the original synovial fluid was found to be 309 ± 50 μg/mL, comparable with ELISA results.741 These two studies demonstrated the potential of AIEgens in synovial fluid sample-targeted liquid biopsy. However, more effective strategies and validation in clinical samples are urgently needed.

Smart AIEgen for Exhaled Breath-targeted Biopsy

Breathing is an essential and regular function of the human body, facilitating the intake of necessary gases and expelling unnecessary gases and metabolites (Table 10). In healthy individuals, exhaled breath contains more than 3500 compounds, including volatile organic compounds (VOCs, such as ethane, pentane, aldehyde, isoprene), inorganic compounds (O2, NO, CO2, H2S, NH3, and so on), and exhaled breath condensates (EBCs, cytokines, H2O2, isoprostanes, leukotriene, H2O, and so on).742−744 In patients with certain diseases, the components of exhaled breath can be altered, serving as biomarkers (Table 11) that provide useful clues for clinical management.745−750 These compounds have thus attracted attention from scientists and clinicians alike. Numerous assays have been developed to analyze exhaled breath samples. Of note, the exhaled nitric oxide (eNO) test is used to detect eosinophilic airway inflammation, asthma, COPD, etc.; the 13C-urea breath test (UBT) is used to detect Helicobacter pylori (HP) infection, and the hydrogen breath test is used to detect bacterial overgrowth in the small bowel.751 So far, numerous methods, including colorimetric assays, electrochemical sensing, and MS, have been utilized for detecting these volatile components.752−756 Although these methods can detect diseases with high sensitivity, they are expensive and their results are easily affected by extrinsic factors. In contrast, fluorescent probes offer a promising alternative and could be used to achieve big strides in this diagnostic field.757−760 Given the complexity of exhaled breath (Table 11), AIEgens appear to be better for this purpose than conventional fluorescent probes, as described earlier. Some excellent literature reviews on this topic have been published previously,761 with a notable example being the overview of gas-associated disease detection published by Lee et al. in 2022.762 Here, we aim to summarize gaseous biopsies of clinical samples based on AIEgens. However, as summarized in ref (762), several AIEgens have been developed for gas detection in vitro and in vivo (animal model and food), TPE-2E N-oxide,763 TPE-4NE-O,764 and Pt-TPP,765 for oxygen, BTCV-CO,766 MTIPIM-AF-CO/MTPIM-AE-CO,767 and THBTA-CO (61)768 for CO; PyDA-NP,769 QY-N,770 and TPE-2NH2771 for NO; TPE-Az,772 HPB-based probe 2,773 HL-H2S,774 HBT-based probe 1,775 TPA-based probe 1,776 TPE-indo,777 AIE-S,778 Indo-TPE-Indo,779 TPE-BODIPY-Cl,780 TPE-3,781 TPE-NP,782 TPE-NBD-D,783 TPANF,784 DNBS-DCM-SP,785 CLBZ,786 TPP-PDCV,787 Cya-NO2 (1)/Cya-N3 (2),788 and probe 1(789) for H2S; TPE-PEI,790 Lys-PEG-TPE,791 TPP-TMAE,792 and HPQ-Ac,793 for CO2; DPA-CN794 and TCMn795 for SO2; Zn-Py-TPE796 and AIE-FA797 for formaldehyde; ZnMOF/CoMOF,798 [OMe]x-TzDa,799 and BOY-TPE/HBIY-TPE800 for HCl; and TPE-Py-coated fiber,801 [OMe]x-TzDa,799CHMPBA,802 and BOY-TPE/HBIY-TPE800 for amines. Summary of Exhaled Gas Associated Disease

Volatile formaldehyde present in exhaled breath serves as a biomarker for lung cancer.803 To diagnose lung cancer effectively, Liu et al. synthesized an AIEgen (tetrakis(4-pyridylphenyl)ethylene, Py-TPE)-based zinc MOF. This probe enabled the absorption and selective detection of formaldehyde in exhaled breath (Figure 75A). The porous Zn-Py-TPE acted as a gaseous confinement cavity for the adsorption of formaldehyde from exhaled breath samples. Surprisingly, the aggregation of Zn-Py-TPE on paper induced AICE triggered by bis(2,4,6-trichlorophenyl)oxalate (TCPO). In the absence of H2O2, the AIECL of Zn-Py-TPE-TCPO was greatly enhanced at 530 nm after the introduction of formaldehyde, leading to the production of Zn-Py-TPE aggregates and the augmented generation of singlet oxygen on paper due to halogen bonding between formaldehyde and Zn-Py-TPE. This mechanism ensured excellent selectivity (Figure 75B). Hence, the Zn-Py-TPE-paper system could quantitively detect formaldehyde in the range of 1.0–100.0 ppb (R2 = 0.997), with an LOD of 0.3 ppb and excellent selectivity, repeatability, and stability under optimal experimental conditions. When applied to exhaled breath samples from three healthy volunteers, this assay provided results that were consistent with HPLC–MS and GC–MS results (Figure 75C), demonstrating the value of this probe for diagnosing lung cancer. However, further studies with a larger number of real exhaled breath samples are warranted to validate its clinical utility and promote its clinical translation.796

Perspectives and Conclusion

Tissue and liquid biopsies have remained important tools in clinical trials,52,53,76,804 indispensable for the management of diseases. In this context, improved detection methods are vital for achieving more precise and effective diagnostic results. However, the limitation of diagnostic targets and existed technologies impedes the accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of biopsy obviously.258,312,337,567 Therefore, A myriad of advanced technologies for biopsy analysis have been developed, and several others are currently under development.59,805 Among these innovations, AIEgens stand out as a prominent class of luminescent nanomaterials, overcoming the shortcomings associated with ACQ effect in traditional fluorescent molecules. Given the host of intrinsic advantages they offer, AIEgens have significantly promoted the development of molecular probes. Thus, AIEgens can be considered valuable toolboxes for disease detection with simple and complicated sample.104,110,117,541,806−808 As detailed in this review of the literature, the marriage between AIEgens and biopsies has yielded excited outcomes.

This review attempts to deliver an updated synopsis of AIEgens and their use in biopsy analysis. We have focused our attention toward nanosystems that are already capable of detecting biorelevant molecular targets because we believe that these functional molecular analytical assays, when applied for clinical and at-home tests, could become game changers in the field of medical diagnostics in future. Upon reviewing the plethora of nanosystems and studies based on AIEgens, we are optimistic that this review could provide a wealth of data and a detailed understanding behind the principles of these nanosystems, motivating additional advances in clinical science.

Despite the remarkable progress in the field of AIEgen-based biopsy, several challenges and limitations remain to be addressed for the further advancement and widespread application of these probes. First, several other sample sources could potentially be applied for AIEgen-based biopsy in clinical settings. For example, CSF contains several biomarkers associated with different brain diseases.809−813 Hence, CSF may be better choice than other liquid and tissue biopsy samples, offering more clinically relevant results.814 But also other sample source, including ascites, bile,815 tear,73,816,817 stool,818 earwax,819 uterine/vaginal lavage,820 seminal fluid,821 and bone marrow.822 Moreover, the diverse array of target molecules within these sample sources remains to be explored thoroughly.

Second, even prior to the analytical process, several key obstacles that hinder the outcomes of AIEgen-based analytic assays remain. These include the absence of standardized methods for isolating diagnostic targets, the lack of well-defined study populations and controls, insufficient consideration of variables from both biological and environmental perspectives, and notably, the scarcity of effective biomarkers. These challenges need to be addressed.

Third, existing AIEgen-based biopsy methods require thorough validation using a sufficient number of clinical samples. A significant disjunction exists between standard solutions and clinical samples, and these differences are compounded by inherent variations among individuals. At present, the majority of AIEgen-based liquid biopsy methods remain in the nascent stages of method establishment and preclinical research with several challenges associated with the collection of clinical samples. Consequently, we emphasize the necessity for enhanced communication and collaborative efforts among researchers, clinicians, and patients.

Fourth, due to the high heterogeneity and abundance of diagnostic targets and the strict recognition capability of AIEgen, liquid biopsies often yield false negative or false positive results.258,823 More effective and smart strategies that achieve precise diagnosis are currently lacking, calling for urgent efforts toward interdisciplinary research in this area.

Fifth, although AIEgen-based fluorescence biopsies exhibit great sensitivity, specificity, and recovery that serve as the main indexes of liquid biopsy, exploring more quantitative analysis approaches is essential for accurate disease staging and monitoring.231,796

Sixth, the broad emission and aggregation-dependent brightness of AIEgens prevents their practical performance in multiple areas of biomedicine when combined with other fluorescent probes for simultaneous detection.824 Hence, more convenient and effective detection strategies characterized by narrow emission spectra and aggregation-independent brightness are still urgently needed to address these challenges.

Seventh, artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms, particularly deep learning, have led to remarkable progress, efficacy, and convenience in clinical diagnostic tasks of biopsy and histopathology.73,364,825−830 Notably, the marriage of AI and AIEgens holds promise for bolstering therapeutic effectiveness in different diseases-treatment fields.831−833 Therefore, incorporating AI into biopsy procedures utilizing AIEgens could offer a solution across various diseases, enhancing efficiency across large clinical sample cohorts and facilitating the data mining of diagnostic results.

Eighth, the biosafety and biocompatibility are inevitable issues with further researches and clinical application because the importance of in situ diagnosis is still urgent. Under this circumstance, more attention should be comprised in future.

Finally, the commercialization of liquid biopsy diagnostic methods has garnered widespread attention globally. The size of this market was estimated to be $10.42 billion in 2023 and is projected to grow at a compound annual growth rate of 11.60% between 2024 and 2030.834 Despite this growth, this market remains relatively underpenetrated, particularly in the case of rare diseases. Therefore, fostering greater interactions between academia and industry is of great value. The knowledge and scientific flexibility of academia in tandem with the organizational capabilities of industries could be leveraged to streamline biomarker development and liquid biopsy frameworks and bring them to market, leading to advances in the field of diagnostics and healthcare.

Overall, this review encapsulates the progress in smart AIEgen-based tools for clinical tissue biopsy and liquid biopsy. In addition, it discusses the future perspectives of their applications in detail. Collectively, we hope that this review ignites further interest, stimulates innovative ideas, and serves as a guiding beacon for researchers, ultimately advancing biopsy development to benefit both fundamental research and clinical trials.

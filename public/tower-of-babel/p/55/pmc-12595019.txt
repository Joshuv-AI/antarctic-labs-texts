Mixed fault dataset of bearings and gearboxes under variable operating conditions
Authors: Yinchuan Hou, Jian Tang, Jing Zhang, Pengfei Pang, Hao Zhang, Xinxin Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBearings and gears, as key rotating components, are prone to damage when operating under varying conditions with heavy loads, leading to equipment chain failures. In response to the demand for health monitoring based on vibration signals, this paper has constructed a vibration signal dataset for multiple fault types under varying operating conditions: the bearing dataset covers single faults such as inner ring, outer ring, rolling elements, and cage, as well as combined faults of inner and outer rings; the gearbox dataset includes typical defects such as tooth surface wear, broken teeth, and eccentricity; an additional mixed fault dataset of bearings and gearboxes is also provided. These datasets simulate real industrial scenarios through multiple dimensions, aiming to provide a reliable verification benchmark for intelligent diagnostic algorithms and improve the fault identification accuracy and model generalization ability under complex operating conditions.Keywords: Bearing, Gearbox, Mixed fault, Variable working conditions, Fault diagnosis

Background

With the rapid development of deep learning technology, the health prediction of rotating machinery faults is accelerating towards the direction of intelligent diagnostic algorithm models, and various new intelligent diagnostic models are emerging one after another [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. For newly developed intelligent diagnostic models, their diagnostic reliability needs to be rigorously verified based on vibration signal datasets.

Bearings and gears, as the core components of rotating machinery, often operate in complex environments with varying conditions. At the same time, mechanical equipment failures often exhibit mixed failure characteristics of multiple types of components. Under different operating conditions, the rotational frequencies and fault characteristics of bearings and gears will show significant differences. [8] These differences are prone to cause fluctuations in model performance when inputting them into the diagnostic model, thereby affecting the robustness and generalization ability of the model. This practical background places higher requirements on intelligent diagnostic algorithms, urgently requiring them to have stronger diagnostic accuracy and reliability to adapt to fault diagnosis scenarios in complex environments with varying conditions. Therefore, constructing a dataset that can provide sufficient support for the research of fault diagnosis models in varying conditions and mixed failure types has crucial academic value and practical significance. [1,9]

Data Description

The dataset mentioned in this article was constructed by collecting vibration signals under various fault types and different working conditions. The data is stored in CSV format and consists of a total of 64 files. Each CSV file corresponds to the dataset of a specific fault type under a particular working condition. Each dataset contains a time column and vibration signal columns along the X, Y, and Z axes. The data collection parameters are: sampling frequency of 16 kHz, single acquisition duration of 30 seconds, and load set at a constant value of 20 Nm. The dataset covers four working conditions, including three constant rotational speeds (1200 r/min, 1800 r/min, 2400 r/min) and a variable speed condition (200 r/min → 2400 r/min → 200 r/min). All fault types have completed the complete data collection under the four working conditions.

Bearing dataset

Fig. 1 presents the schematic diagram of the health and failure states in the bearing dataset, where the failure states include: inner ring crack failure (IF), outer ring crack failure (OF), rolling element crack failure (BF), cage crack failure (CF), and combined inner and outer ring crack failure. Parallel gearbox dataset

Fig. 2 presents the schematic diagram of the health and failure states in the gearbox dataset. The failure states include tooth surface wear, broken teeth, and eccentricity. In the simulation of tooth surface wear, the laser etching method was used to machine local depressions with a depth of 2mm on the single tooth surface of the gear. The surrounding areas of the depressions and other regions of the tooth surface were then polished with sandpaper to simulate the overall wear morphology. In the simulation of broken teeth, a fault model is constructed by removing an entire gear tooth.The eccentricity of the gears is uniformly set to 0.5mm, meaning that the offset distance between the gear center shaft and the rotating shaft is controlled at 0.5mm.

Mixed fault dataset

The mixed fault dataset consists of six types of faults, which are composed of a mixture of bearing faults and gear faults. They are: mixed faults of inner ring and tooth surface wear of the bearing, mixed faults of inner ring and broken teeth of the gear, mixed faults of inner ring and eccentric gear of the bearing, mixed faults of outer ring and tooth surface wear of the bearing, mixed faults of outer ring and broken teeth of the gear, and mixed faults of outer ring and eccentric gear of the bearing. Table 4 presents the specific description of the mixed fault dataset under variable operating conditions, and the dataset under constant rotational speed conditions is the same.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

As shown in Fig. 4, the test bench device for collecting signals is composed of the platform base, the drive motor module, the gearbox fault simulation module, the rolling bearing fault simulation module, the control system, and the test piece. This paper takes the rolling bearing and the parallel gearbox fault simulation module as the research objects and adopts the vibration signals under different rotational speed conditions.

Fault signal acquisition test ring.

The motor used is a three-phase AC asynchronous motor with a power of 1.5 HP.

The rolling bearing fault simulation module adopts standardized rolling bearings of type NU205, and its specific parameters are shown in Table 5.

The acceleration sensor used for data acquisition is shown in Fig. 5, its model is HF7051-1, Its reference sensitivity reaches 97.82 mV/g, with a maximum lateral sensitivity ratio of ≤ 5%. The operating voltage is +18-28 VDC, the current is +2-10 mA, and the power consumption is low while the adaptability is strong. The frequency range is 0.5-10 KHz, capable of measuring various vibrations. The mass is only 10 grams, and it has little impact on the object being measured. The working temperature is -20 - +100°C, the shock limit is ±2000g.

Evaluation of pancreatic stump binding and ligation in reducing postoperative pancreatic fistula after distal pancreatectomy: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Zi-Ang Jiang, Xiang-Yu Lu, Hang Deng, Xu Wang, Jian Xu, Jie Zhang, Yao Zhou, Jun Gong, Ke Dong, Yan Dai, Hao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study evaluates the efficacy of pancreatic stump binding and ligation (PSBL) in reducing postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) after distal pancreatectomy (DP).MethodsA retrospective cohort analysis included 217 patients undergoing DP between 2015 and 2022. Patients were classified into two groups: those receiving PSBL (B-Group, n = 96) and those without (NB-Group, n = 121). The primary outcome was incidence of clinically relevant POPF (Grade B/C).ResultsThe B-Group demonstrated lower POPF rates (11.5% vs. 28.9%, P = 0.003) compared to the NB-Group, with grade B fistulas particularly reduced (P = 0.002). Multivariate analysis identified independent risk factors for POPF: BMI ≥ 28 kg/m2 (OR = 14.105, P < 0.001), Preoperative Hypoalbuminemia (OR = 7.031, P < 0.001), Manual Method of Stump Closure (OR = 2.558, P = 0.020), and non-PSBL Technique (OR = 4.463, P < 0.001).ConclusionsIn this retrospective cohort, PSBL is a promising, feasible technique associated with reduced clinically relevant POPF incidence after DP. Given the retrospective design, these findings are hypothesis-generating and warrant prospective, multicenter validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-04052-2.

Introduction

Distal pancreatectomy (DP) is a standard procedure for managing pancreatic body/tail neoplasms or extrapancreatic tumors involving the distal pancreas. Despite advancements, postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) remains a major complication, occurring in 5–40% of cases [1–4], POPF may lead to abscesses, sepsis, or hemorrhage, significantly increasing morbidity and healthcare costs [5, 6].

Risk factors for POPF include patient age, body mass index (BMI), preoperative nutritional status, tumor pathology, pancreatic texture, pancreatic duct diameter, operation time, intraoperative bleeding, and methods for pancreatic stump closure [4, 7–10]. Many of these factors are challenging to address in clinical practice. Pancreatic stump closure methods emerge as a practical approach to mitigating POPF after DP. Consequently, different surgical techniques have been introduced, such as closing the stump with a three-row linear stapler [2, 11–13], incorporating a biological patch [7, 14, 15], utilizing pancreatic stump-jejunostomy [16], and even preoperative endoscopic sphincterotomy and stenting [17]. However, a recent Cochrane meta-analysis including three randomized trials (515 patients) found that stapler closure likely results in little to no difference in POPF rates compared to handsewn closure (26% vs. 29%; RR 1.11, 95% CI 0.84–1.47), suggesting that conventional closure methods remain suboptimal and novel techniques are needed [18]. In that way, the effectiveness of these procedures in preventing POPF requires further evaluation, considering potential additional injuries and increased hospitalization costs associated with their implementation.

Inspired by the Pringle maneuver for hepatic inflow occlusion, we have adopted pancreatic stump binding and ligation (PSBL) since 2015. This technique could achieve mechanical compression and vascular control of the pancreatic resection margin, and may reduce the risk of POPF after DP. To validate its clinical efficacy, we performed a retrospective comparative study of DP cases from 2015 to 2022, comparing PSBL with conventional closure methods including stapler and manual technique.

Materials & methods

Study design and participants

This single-center retrospective cohort study analyzed consecutive patients undergoing DP at Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital between January 2015 and December 2022. Clinical data were extracted from the institutional electronic medical record system between January and March 2024. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0–2. Exclusion criteria included: (1) DP performed for pancreatic trauma; (2) prior chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or iodine seed implantation for malignancies; (3) synchronous pancreatic duplication carcinoma; (4) HIV infection or immune disorders; (5) tumors located in the pancreatic body with insufficient distance (< 3 cm) from the superior mesenteric vein for safe PSBL, confirmed by preoperative imaging.

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (Ethical Approval No. 391, 2022). Group allocation was not based on tumor severity or an intention to select technically simpler cases, but was primarily determined by real-world surgeon preference and the temporal adoption phase of the PSBL technique within our department. The technique was initially pioneered by a subset of surgeons and gradually adopted department-wide during the study period. While a safe retro-pancreatic tunnel (≥ 3 cm clearance from the superior mesenteric vein) served as an anatomical prerequisite for PSBL, baseline indicators of tumor complexity—including tumor pathology, malignancy rates, and the need for concurrent organ resection—were well-balanced between the two groups (Table 1), confirming that case-selection bias based on disease severity was minimal.

Surgical technique

Preoperative evaluation

Enhanced abdominal CT or MRI was performed to assess tumor resectability, vascular anatomy, and pancreatic ductal integrity.

Operative procedure

DP was performed via transabdominal or laparoscopic approach. The gastrocolic ligament was dissected to expose the pancreatic body and tail. Then an intraoperative ultrasonography was utilized to confirm lesion margins. A retro-pancreatic tunnel was established 3 cm distal to the tumor, incorporating the splenic artery and vein if splenectomy was indicated.

Intervention Groups and Subgroups

B-Group (PSBL)

The proximal pancreatic stump was circumferentially ligated with 2‑0 USP non‑absorbable braided silk sutures, with incremental tension applied gradually to achieve mechanical compression and vascular occlusion (Fig. 1). Pancreatic transection was performed 1 cm distal to the ligation site using either a linear stapler or a manual technique. NB-Group (Conventional Closure)

Direct transection was performed using a linear stapler or manual technique (using harmonic scalpel) without prior ligation. Suture or stapler selection followed institutional protocols and intraoperative surgeon judgment. LS Group (Linear Stapler alone)

Direct pancreatic transection was performed using a linear stapler, without prior ligation or additional suturing.

MS Group (Manual Suture alone)

Direct pancreatic transection was performed using a manual technique with a harmonic scalpel, followed by stump suturing as needed.

Postoperative Management

A closed-suction drain was placed near the pancreatic stump. Drain fluid amylase levels were measured on postoperative days (POD) 1, 3, and 5. In accordance with institutional protocols, prophylactic somatostatin analogs were administered to all patients for the first 5 postoperative days.

Definition of clinical indicators

In accordance with literature data and clinical experience [4, 7–10], certain quantitative parameters in the study were defined and categorized as follows: ①Body Mass Index (BMI): Underweight (< 18.5), Normal (18.5–23.9), Overweight (24.0–27.9), Obese (≥ 28.0); ②Preoperative anemia: Male hemoglobin < 120 g/L; female < 110 g/L; ③Preoperative hypoalbuminemia: Albumin ≤ 35 g/L.

Postoperative complications were classified into 5 grades based on the Clavien-Dindo complication classification [19]. Grades 3 and above, requiring aggressive surgery, endoscopic, or imaging interventional treatments, were defined as major complications.

In accordance with the 2016 update from the International Study Group on Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) [20], clinically relevant POPF (CR-POPF) was categorized into two grades, defined as Grade B or C. Biochemical leaks (formerly Grade A), defined as drainage fluid amylase > 3 times the upper limit of normal serum value without clinical symptoms, were explicitly excluded from the primary outcome analysis.

The primary endpoint of this study was strictly defined as CR-POPF, and only outcomes in patients with Grade B or Grade C fistulas were analyzed.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0 software (IBM). Continuous and categorical variables were compared using the Student’s t-test (or Mann-Whitney U test) and Pearson’s χ² test (or Fisher’s exact test), respectively. A hierarchical testing framework was applied: the primary analysis compared the B-Group and NB-Group (α = 0.05), while four prespecified exploratory subgroup comparisons were adjusted using Bonferroni correction (α′ = 0.0125). Variables with P < 0.1 in univariate analysis were entered into a multivariate logistic regression model. Model performance was assessed via the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and events-per-variable (EPV) ratio. To assess the robustness of our findings against residual confounding, two pre‑specified sensitivity analyses were performed: the first adjusted for year of surgery and tumor diameter to account for surgical era and anatomical factors; the second further included operative time and intraoperative blood loss to evaluate surgical complexity. In addition, a post‑hoc propensity score matching(PSM) analysis was conducted as a sensitivity analysis. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 231 consecutive patients underwent distal pancreatectomy between 2015 and 2022.After excluding 9 patients with pancreatic lesions < 3 cm from the superior mesenteric vein, 2 patients with incomplete medical records, and 3 patients diagnosed with synchronous pancreatic duplication carcinoma, 217 patients were finally included in the analysis. The cohort comprised 98 males and 119 females, with a mean age of 52.92 ± 13.89 years (range 18–81 years). Splenectomy was performed in 167 cases (76.96%), and 51 patients (23.50%) required additional organ resections, including partial vascular excisions. Major complications occurred in 51 patients (23.50%). POPF was identified in 46 cases (21.20%), comprising 42 grade B (19.35%) and 4 grade C (1.85%) fistulas. All patients were followed up for at least 3 months (with actual clinical follow-up exceeding 1 year for the entire cohort), allowing for the adequate capture of delayed surgical complications such as late-onset pancreatic fistula, delayed hemorrhage, and symptomatic intra-abdominal collections. No perioperative mortality was observed. (Fig. 5)

The B-Group (PSBL, n = 96) and NB-Group (conventional closure, n = 121) demonstrated comparable baseline characteristics, including age, sex, the distribution of year of surgery, BMI, preoperative anemia, hypoalbuminemia, diameter of tumor, and comorbidities (all P > 0.05). However, the B-Group exhibited: Shorter operative time (mean: 209 vs. 245 min; P = 0.004). Reduced perioperative transfusion rate (15/96 vs. 38/121; P = 0.007). Lower incidence of major complications (12/96 vs. 39/121; P < 0.001). Significantly decreased POPF rates (11/96 vs. 35/121; P = 0.003), driven by reduced grade B fistulas (10/96 vs. 32/121; P = 0.003). Shorter postoperative hospitalization (median: 9 vs. 12 days; P < 0.001) and lower total costs (mean: ¥49,979 vs. ¥52,873; P = 0.019) (Table 1).

According to the technique of pancreatic stump closure, patients were classified into four study subgroups: BLS (PSBL + linear stapler, n = 59), BMS (PSBL + manual suture, n = 37),LS (linear stapler alone, n = 58), and MS (manual suture alone, n = 63). No staple line reinforcement materials or biological patches were used in any case during the study period. As presented in Table 2, the overall incidence of POPF differed across the four groups (P = 0.004).The rate of POPF was lower in the B‑Group (BLS + BMS) relative to the NB‑Group (LS + MS) (P = 0.003), satisfying the predefined Bonferroni‑corrected significance level (α′ = 0.0125). Exploratory subgroup comparisons (BLS vs. LS, BMS vs. MS, and combined stapler vs. manual) yielded P-values of 0.037, 0.044, and 0.015, respectively. After Bonferroni correction for multiple testing (α′ = 0.0125), these subgroup differences did not retain statistical significance and are therefore reported as hypothesis-generating observations only.

Univariate analysis identified seven variables with P < 0.1: Gender, BMI, preoperative hypoalbuminemia, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, stump closure method, and PSBL technique. (Table 3). After excluding potential mediators (operative time and intraoperative blood loss), the remaining variables were included in the multivariate analysis. The results demonstrated that BMI ≥ 28.0 kg/m² (Odds Ratio (OR) = 14.105, P < 0.001), preoperative hypoalbuminemia (OR = 7.031, P < 0.001), manual method (OR = 2.558, P = 0.020), and non-PSBL technique (OR = 4.463, P < 0.001) were independent risk factors for POPF (Table 4). The final model demonstrated satisfactory calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow P = 0.371) and excellent discrimination (AUC = 0.831). With 46 events and 7 estimated parameters (including 3 dummy variables for BMI categories), the EPV ratio was 6.6, which is below the conventional threshold of 10 but falls within the acceptable range for exploratory analyses. In a simplified model using binary BMI (≥ 28 vs. < 28), the EPV improved to 9.2 (46 events / 5 parameters).

To assess the robustness of our primary findings, we conducted two pre‑specified sensitivity analyses. First, we adjusted for year of surgery (≤ 2018 vs. >2018) and tumor diameter ((≤ 4 vs. >4 cm)), two potential confounders related to surgical era and anatomical factors. In this model, PSBL remained significantly associated with a reduced risk of POPF (OR = 4.594, 95% CI: 1.868–11.296, P = 0.001), whereas neither year of surgery (OR = 0.731, P = 0.502) nor tumor diameter (OR = 1.281, P = 0.559) was significant; model calibration was satisfactory (Hosmer–Lemeshow P = 0.648; Supplement Table 1). Second, we further adjusted for operative time (≤ 240 vs. >240 min) and intraoperative blood loss (≤ 500 vs. >500 mL) to address potential confounding by surgical complexity. In this expanded model, the protective effect of PSBL remained robust (OR = 3.819, 95% CI: 1.546–9.435, P = 0.004), with adequate model fit (Hosmer–Lemeshow P = 0.402), although neither operative time (OR = 1.793, P = 0.165) nor blood loss (OR = 1.397, P = 0.455) independently predicted POPF. These sensitivity analyses collectively support the stability of our main conclusions. (Supplement Table 2)

In a post‑hoc PSM analysis (81 pairs, n = 162), the protective effect of PSBL against POPF was confirmed. In the matched cohort, POPF (Grade B/C) occurred in 12.3% of the PSBL group versus 33.3% of the conventional closure group (P = 0.002). Multivariate regression on the matched data identified PSBL as an independent protective factor (OR = 4.49, 95% CI: 1.73–11.68, P = 0.002). Detailed matching results are provided in the Supplement Tables 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7.

Discussion

POPF remains a critical complication following DP, contributing to prolonged hospitalization, increased costs, and heightened morbidity, as corroborated by prior studies [21]. Our retrospective analysis introduces PSBL as a novel technique that was associated with significant reductions in POPF incidence and perioperative morbidity compared to conventional closure methods.

While multiple strategies—including omental wrapping and fibrin sealants [22, 23], and more recently mesh wrapping [24] —have been explored to mitigate POPF, their clinical efficacy remains inconsistent and many incur additional material costs. PSBL may address this gap through a combination of mechanical compression and vascular control at the resection margin. Proximal ligation with 2‑0 USP non‑absorbable braided silk sutures, applied with incremental tension approximately 1 cm proximal to the transection plane, is intended to provide uniform parenchymal compression while attenuating local blood flow and exocrine secretion—analogous to the Pringle maneuver in hepatic surgery. Unlike isolated, selective ligation of the main pancreatic duct, this circumferential bundled ligation of the entire parenchyma does not aim for complete ductal embolization; rather, it seeks to achieve flow modulation and mechanical reinforcement. We hypothesize that this controlled, mild ischemia may attenuate exocrine secretion by reducing local perfusion—as stable blood flow is essential for normal exocrine function [25] —thereby lowering the risk of clinically relevant leakage from the cut surface. When integrated with linear staplers, this dual mechanical approach may further enhance hemostatic sealing. Notably, postoperative CT imaging of the pancreatic remnant (Supplement Figure) demonstrated preserved blood supply with no evidence of atrophy or fibrosis at 1–2 years after surgery, and no cases of stump necrosis or clinical obstructive pancreatitis were observed in this cohort. However, without histological or perfusion data, these mechanistic interpretations remain speculative and warrant further investigation. The routine use of prophylactic somatostatin analogues for 5 days postoperatively reflects our institutional protocol, which may limit generalizability to centers with different practices, but does not affect internal validity as it was applied uniformly to all patients.

The B-Group demonstrated superior perioperative outcomes compared to the NB-Group, including reduced operative time, lower transfusion rates, fewer major complications, and shorter hospital stays. The shorter operative time in the B‑group is likely attributable to the fact that PSBL replaces time‑consuming individual vessel ligation and post‑transection hemostasis with a single, rapid pre‑transection ligation, while creation of the retro‑pancreatic tunnel is a standard step in stapler‑assisted DP and was not unique to PSBL. Notably, the B‑Group showed a 60% reduction in POPF incidence (11.5% vs. 28.9%; P = 0.003), primarily driven by decreased Grade B leaks. Multivariate analysis identified BMI ≥ 28 kg/m², preoperative hypoalbuminemia, manual closure, and non‑PSBL methods as independent POPF risk factors.

To address potential confounding by temporal adoption and anatomical selection, we performed two sensitivity analyses. First, adjustment for year of surgery (≤ 2018 vs. >2018) confirmed that the protective effect of PSBL remained robust (OR = 4.594, 95% CI: 1.868–11.296, P = 0.001), whereas year of surgery itself was not a significant predictor of POPF (P = 0.502; Supplemental Table 2). Second, tumor diameter was comparable between groups (4.18 ± 1.38 cm vs. 4.30 ± 1.51 cm, P = 0.573; Table 1) and was not an independent predictor of POPF in multivariate regression (P = 0.559; Supplemental Table 2). These findings suggest that the observed benefit of PSBL is unlikely to be driven by temporal learning‑curve effects or preferential selection of patients with smaller, more distal lesions.

Within the B‑Group, the BLS subgroup showed a non‑significant trend toward lower POPF rates compared to BMS, which may reflect synergistic mechanisms: uniform mechanical compression from binding reduces parenchymal perfusion and exocrine secretion, while stapler application enhances ductal closure integrity. This integration of binding ligation with linear stapling aligns with Kang et al.’s conclusion that combined vascular occlusion and stapler closure optimizes “mechanical barrier + blood flow control” [9]. This combined approach was associated with reduced intraoperative blood loss and operative time, which may have contributed to the B-Group’s lower transfusion rates and hospitalization costs (mean savings: ¥2,894; P = 0.019).

However, this study has several limitations inherent to its retrospective, single‑center design. First, the retrospective nature precluded systematic collection of validated risk factors (e.g., pancreatic texture, duct diameter, transection site thickness), limiting multivariable adjustment and warranting cautious interpretation. Second, the EPV ratio was 6.6, below the conventional threshold of 10; when BMI was treated as a binary variable, the EPV increased to 9.2, approaching the recommended level. This modest event number may limit estimate precision and restrict subgroup analyses for rare events such as Grade C fistulas. Finally, although postoperative CT imaging suggested preserved remnant vascularity without atrophy or fibrosis, we lacked objective functional testing (e.g., fecal elastase, HbA1c), leaving long‑term exocrine and endocrine outcomes unclear. Future prospective studies with functional assessment and multicenter randomized trials are warranted.

Conclusions

In this retrospective cohort study, PSBL was associated with a significantly lower incidence of clinically relevant POPF and reduced perioperative morbidity compared to conventional closure techniques. While promising, these findings are constrained by the non-randomized design, unmeasured pancreatic parenchymal characteristics (such as texture and duct diameter), and the single-center setting. Although postoperative imaging provided preliminary evidence of remnant viability, objective functional assessments are lacking. PSBL represents a feasible technical adjunct that warrants further evaluation in prospective, multicenter trials before routine clinical adoption can be recommended.

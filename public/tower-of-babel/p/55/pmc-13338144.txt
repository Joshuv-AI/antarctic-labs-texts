Multi-method synergistic determination of the development height of the water-conducting fractured zone in the 82,209 working face
Authors: Zenghui Liu, Haonan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate determination of the water-conducting fractured zone (WCFZ) height is critical for preventing roof water inrush in coal mines. Taking the 82,209 near-horizontal fully mechanized caving face of the No. 22 coal seam in Lijiayao Coal Mine as the research object, we established a multi-method synergistic system combining empirical formula calculation, key strata theory prediction, surface borehole field measurement and numerical simulation. Empirical formulas produce highly scattered results (32.94–135.00 m) and are only suitable for preliminary estimation. Key strata prediction and numerical simulation agree well with field measurements, with relative errors of 0.69%–4.15% and 1.36%–4.56%, respectively. The final WCFZ height is 64.52 m at Borehole 1 and 113.58 m at Borehole 2. Numerical simulation reveals a three-stage evolution law: initial development, rapid expansion and stable development. The multi-method system achieves accurate WCFZ determination and provides a valuable reference for water disaster prevention in similar mining conditions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at10.1038/s41598-026-52738-7.

Introduction

After coal seam extraction, the roof strata in the goaf inevitably undergo caving and movement, forming the caving zone, fractured zone, and bending subsidence zone1. The fractured zone acts as the main water-conducting pathway in the overburden, facilitating water migration in the surrounding coal and rock mass and potentially inducing water inrush accidents. Therefore, the development characteristics and accurate determination of the height of the water-conducting fractured zone caused by mining have long been key concerns in the prevention and control of mine water disasters2.

At present, the methods used to determine the height of the water-conducting fractured zone mainly include field measurements, physical and numerical simulations, and theoretical prediction methods3. Field measurement methods mainly involve drilling fluid loss analysis, borehole imaging observation, sectional water plugging and leakage detection, and geophysical prospecting techniques4–6. Simulation methods mainly include similar material simulation experiments and numerical simulation analyses7. Theoretical prediction methods include the estimation of fracture zone height based on key strata theory through identification of the main key stratum in the overburden8,9, as well as multifactor sensitivity analysis, regression-based prediction, information entropy methods, and model optimization approaches10–12.

To overcome the limitations of single-method analyses, researchers have increasingly employed comprehensive approaches combining multiple techniques to determine the height of the water-conducting fractured zone under different geological and mining conditions. Some studies have focused on zonal prediction of the water-conducting fractured zone under different overburden structures and typical geological settings13. Other studies have emphasized integrated geophysical monitoring methods, including microseismic monitoring, electrical prospecting, and elastic wave detection, to identify the development characteristics and spatial evolution of the fractured zone during mining14–16. In addition, combined approaches based on field investigation, theoretical analysis, and numerical simulation have been widely used to improve the reliability of height determination in different mining conditions17,18. These studies indicate that the development height and evolution characteristics of the water-conducting fractured zone are jointly controlled by mining thickness, working face geometry, overburden structure, lithologic assemblage, and mining-induced stress redistribution.

In summary, the development height of the water-conducting fractured zone is affected by multiple factors, including coal seam occurrence conditions, overburden lithology, mining design, and extraction technology. The 82,209 working face of the No. 22 coal seam in Lijiayao Coal Mine has a coal seam dip angle of 1–9°, indicating a near-horizontal coal seam. The coal seam thickness increases from 3.62 m in the west to 6.25 m in the east. The working face advances from east to west. In the initial stage, the advancing length is 617 m and the face length is 137 m; in the subsequent stage, the advancing length increases to 970 m and the face length increases to 175 m. In this study, theoretical calculation, surface borehole investigation, and numerical simulation are combined to comprehensively analyze the development height of the water-conducting fractured zone in the 82,209 working face of the No. 22 coal seam, thereby providing a reference for determining the height of the water-conducting fractured zone in similar working faces.

Engineering overview

The 82,209 working face is situated in the eastern part of the southern mining area, adjacent to solid coal to the north, the 82,211 working face (goaf) and 52,211 roadway to the south, bounded by the southern wing return air main roadway’s protective coal pillar line to the west, and the mining boundary protective coal pillar line to the east.

According to actual geological data, 20 exposed faults (throw: 0.2–5 m) and two coal seam roof fractured zones are distributed in the central and western parts of the working face. The coal-bearing strata strike at 95° with a dip direction of 0° and a dip angle of 1–9° (near-horizontal coal seam). Unstable occurrence, frequent fold structures and concealed faults are encountered during mining.

Hydrogeological conditions are relatively simple. With a minimum elevation of 1315 m (higher than the Ordovician limestone aquifer level of 1157–1188 m), the working face is not affected by Ordovician limestone water. The main water inflow source is coal seam roof sandstone fracture water, with a maximum of 3 m³/h and a normal inflow of approximately 0.6 m³/h.

Detection of the water-conducting fractured zone

Borehole construction

The drilling operations were conducted using an XY-4 type coalfield exploration rig, equipped with an NBB260/7 mud pump. A low-solid-phase drilling fluid was employed, circulated in a closed-loop system, with water source tanks, circulation troughs, and settling ponds used for fluid management. Borehole television inspections were performed using a CYKJ-22 underground detection device, and borehole logging was conducted with a PSJ-2 digital logging instrument. The borehole structure and the construction site are shown in Figs. 2 and 3. During drilling, core samples were collected continuously. Drilling was carried out to the bottom of the coal seam to complete the full hole, and three stages of cement grouting were performed to seal fractured zones and goaf sections. The borehole was terminated only after passing quality inspection. Borehole 1 is 150 m from the final mining line, 95 m from Gateway 52,209 and 80 m from Gateway 22,209. Borehole 2 is 227 m from the open-off cut, 55 m from Gateway 52,209 and 80 m from Gateway 22,209.

Core-based engineering geological logging and analysis

Figure 4 shows the RQD values of the core samples from Borehole 1 at different depths. From 0 to 60 m, the RQD values are consistently 100%. At depths of 70 m and 100 m, the RQD values range from 40% to 50%, while the RQD values in other strata are greater than 50%. Below 120 m, most RQD values are 0%, except for 140–150 m where the RQD reaches 100%, and 170–200–230 m where the RQD ranges from 85% to 100%. The strata at 154.32–172.16 m consist of fine-grained sandstone, 188.39–203.97 m of gravel-bearing coarse-grained sandstone, and 235.44–240.09 m of coal and fine-grained sandstone, with sandstone being the predominant lithology in these intervals. From 267.62 to 276.87 m, the strata are composed of fine-grained sandstone, with RQD values of 21.5%.

In Borehole 1, the core RQD in the intervals 123.09–124.29 m, 140.99–141.59 m, 171.86–174.36 m, 202.77–211.39 m, and 235.44–236.44 m is 0% (indicating highly fractured rock), mainly consisting of mudstone and coal seams. These strata have low intrinsic strength and were severely damaged during the coring process. Based on core photographs, the top of the water-conducting fractured zone is inferred to develop between 203.97 m and 267.52 m, corresponding to a height of 63.55 m. Within this, the caving zone ranges from 253.49 m to 267.52 m, with a height of 14.03 m.

Borehole television analysis

Figure 5 shows the borehole television fracture image of Borehole 1. Within the depth range of 0–100 m, the borehole wall is relatively intact, and no obvious fractures are observed. Obvious bedding separations and fractures appear at depths of 117–122 m. Within the depth range of 142–149 m, the borehole wall remains relatively intact, with a continuous fracture zone of about 7 m. From 149 to 184 m depth, the borehole wall is relatively intact and no obvious fractures are observed. In the depth range of 184–197 m, fractures are well developed, with a large number of distinct fractures, but no water leakage is observed. When the borehole depth reaches 203 m, fractures are well developed and water leakage appears on the borehole wall; therefore, the top of the water-conducting fracture zone is determined to be at a depth of 203 m. In the depth range of 242–253.92 m, obvious fractures occur in the borehole. When the borehole depth exceeds 253.92 m, the borehole wall becomes broken with large fractures, indicating that the caving zone has been entered. Combined with core logging analysis, the water-conducting fracture zone is determined to range from 203.23 to 267.52 m.

Based on the above analysis, the height range of the caving zone identified by the borehole is 253.92–267.52 m in depth, corresponding to a caving zone height of 13.60 m. The depth range of the water-conducting fracture zone identified by the borehole is 203.23–267.52 m, corresponding to a water-conducting fracture zone height of 64.29 m.

Analysis of drilling fluid loss and groundwater level variation observations

The variation curve of flushing fluid loss volume for Borehole 1 is shown in Fig. 6. The observation period for flushing fluid consumption of Borehole 1 this time was from April 22 to May 3, 2025. During the stage from the start of drilling to a borehole depth of 199.3 m, the loss volume per unit time and the loss volume per unit time per footage advance showed small fluctuation ranges. Among them, the loss volume per unit time per footage advance stabilized at 0 ~ 0.6 L/s·m, the loss volume per unit time remained at 0 ~ 0.18 L/s, and the mud level in the borehole dropped slowly on the whole. Combined with the core recovery results and analysis data of borehole television, the core integrity rate in this section was relatively high and the overall development degree of fractures in the borehole was low. It is judged accordingly that the water-conducting fracture zone had not been entered at this stage.

When drilling to a depth of 203.00 m, the flushing fluid loss volume increased sharply and the flushing fluid circulation terminated completely. It is inferred that the top of the fracture zone might have been reached here, so the observation frequency was increased from this point. When drilling to 205.34 m, the flushing fluid loss volume per unit time reached 5.14 L/s and the loss volume per unit time per footage advance was 3.76 L/s·m. Drilling continued to 253.82 m, where the flushing fluid loss volume per unit time rose sharply again and the flushing fluid circulation was interrupted for the second time. By 255.37 m, the flushing fluid loss volume per unit time had risen to 8.25 L/s. Drilling to 259.39 m, the delivery of flushing fluid was stopped and dry drilling was adopted, followed by continuous downward drilling. The drilling operation for this time was finally terminated when drilling to a depth of 276.87 m.

Based on the flushing fluid loss rate and abnormal phenomena observed in Borehole 1, the rock layer at a depth of 203.00 m was determined to be the top boundary of the fractured zone, and the position at 253.82 m depth was identified as the top boundary of the caving zone. Discussion

Accurate determination of the water-conducting fractured zone (WCFZ) height is critical for preventing roof water inrush disasters in coal mines. This study established a multi-method synergistic system combining empirical formula calculation, key strata theory prediction, multi-means field measurement, and numerical simulation to determine the WCFZ height in the 82,209 working face, effectively overcoming the limitations of single-method analysis.

The significant variation in WCFZ heights calculated by different empirical formulas (32.94–135.00 m) highlights the limitations of these methods. The GB/T 12,719 − 2021 formula tends to underestimate the WCFZ height for fully mechanized top-coal caving faces, while the Tangshan Coal Research Institute formula provides overly conservative results. This discrepancy arises because empirical formulas only consider mining thickness and lithology, ignoring the influence of overburden structure, key strata distribution, and working face geometry. In contrast, the key strata theory prediction results showed excellent agreement with field measurements, with relative errors of only 0.69%–4.15%. This confirms that the development of the WCFZ is fundamentally controlled by the breaking of hard key strata in the overburden. When the mining thickness is small (3.62 m in the western part), the WCFZ develops below the primary key stratum; when the mining thickness increases to 6.25 m in the eastern part, the fractures penetrate the primary key stratum, leading to a significant increase in WCFZ height.

The multi-means field measurement approach adopted in this study, including core RQD analysis, borehole television observation, flushing fluid loss test, and geophysical logging, provided cross-validated results with measurement errors less than 5%. The final determined WCFZ heights of 64.52 m (Borehole 1) and 113.58 m (Borehole 2) are more reliable than any single measurement method. This integrated measurement system effectively eliminates errors caused by individual method limitations, such as core loss during drilling and interpretation ambiguities in single logging curves.

Numerical simulation revealed the three-stage evolution law of “initial development - rapid expansion - stable development” of the mining-induced WCFZ. The working face reached full mining when advancing to 360 m, after which the WCFZ height remained stable. The simulation results were in good agreement with field measurements, with relative errors of 1.36%–4.56%. This demonstrates that the numerical model accurately reproduced the spatiotemporal evolution characteristics and spatial distribution heterogeneity of the WCFZ. The asymmetric development of the lateral plastic zone (40 m on the open-off cut side and 35 m on the final mining line side) is an important finding that should be considered in the design of waterproof coal pillars.

This study provides a complete technical path for the accurate determination of WCFZ height in near-horizontal fully mechanized caving faces with variable mining thickness. However, some limitations remain. The numerical simulation did not fully consider the influence of faults and fold structures distributed in the working face, which may locally affect the development of fractures. In addition, the study only focused on the final stable height of the WCFZ, and the dynamic evolution process during mining requires further in-situ monitoring research. Future work will combine microseismic monitoring and distributed optical fiber sensing technology to realize real-time dynamic monitoring of WCFZ development, providing more accurate technical support for mine water disaster prevention and control.

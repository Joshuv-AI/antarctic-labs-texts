Efficacy, safety, and dose selection of efepoetin alfa in anemic patients on maintenance dialysis: a phase 2, randomized, multicenter, active-controlled trial
Authors: Eun Jeong Ko, Mi-Sun Byun, Chul Woo Yang, Sug Kyun Shin, Hyung Wook Kim, Byung Chul Shin, Seok Joon Shin, Hyeong Cheon Park, Young Sun Kang, Sang-Ho Lee, Ho Cheol Song, Su Hyun Kim, Ki Young Na, Young-Il Jo, Won Kim, Eun Young Seong, Yong-Lim Kim, Hyunjoo Ryu, Heewon Kim, Yoon-Jeong Choi, Byung Ha Chung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEfepoetin alfa (GC1113, GX-E2) is a long-acting recombinant human erythropoietin fused to a hybrid fragment crystallizable (hyFc) that prolongs systemic exposure via neonatal Fc receptor-mediated recycling. This active-controlled phase 2 trial evaluated efepoetin alfa and informed phase 3 starting dose selection for chronic kidney disease-related anemia in dialysis patients. MethodsIn this multicenter, open-label, active-controlled trial, adults on hemodialysis (HD) or peritoneal dialysis (PD) with hemoglobin (Hb) levels <10 g/dL were randomized after erythropoiesis-stimulating agent discontinuation. Over 12 weeks, HD patients received intravenous efepoetin alfa (5 or 8 µg/kg once weekly [Q1W], 8 µg/kg every 2 weeks [Q2W]) or darbepoetin alfa (30 µg Q1W). PD patients received subcutaneous efepoetin alfa (5 or 8 µg/kg Q2W) or methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta (0.6 µg/kg Q2W). The primary endpoint was the mean Hb change from baseline after 12 weeks of treatment, and an exploratory dose-conversion ratio (DCR) analysis was performed in the PD cohort to inform phase 3 dosing.ResultsEfepoetin alfa showed dose-dependent Hb increases. In the HD cohort, mean Hb increases were 3.19 g/dL for efepoetin alfa 5 and 8 µg/kg Q1W and 2.52 g/dL for darbepoetin alfa. In the PD cohort, mean Hb increases were 2.69 g/dL and 3.48 g/dL for efepoetin alfa 5 and 8 µg/kg Q2W, respectively, and 2.01 g/dL for the control group. Exploratory DCR analysis supported 4 µg/kg Q2W efepoetin alfa as the phase 3 starting dose.ConclusionEfepoetin alfa demonstrated dose-dependent erythropoietic efficacy and an acceptable safety profile in dialysis patients, supporting dose-range characterization and phase 3 starting dose selection.Keywords: Anemia, Chronic renal insufficiency, Efepoetin alfa, Erythropoietin, Renal dialysis

Introduction

Anemia is a common complication in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD), particularly among those receiving maintenance dialysis, and is associated with increased cardiovascular risk, hospitalization, and mortality [1–3]. Current clinical practice guidelines recommend the use of erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs), in combination with iron supplementation, to maintain hemoglobin (Hb) concentrations within a target range that balances symptomatic improvement with cardiovascular safety [4,5]. However, the optimization of ESA therapy remains challenging due to inter-individual variability in response, Hb fluctuation, dose escalation, and safety concerns associated with high-dose ESA exposure [6–8]. The development of long-acting ESAs, such as darbepoetin alfa and methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta, has improved treatment convenience by extending dosing intervals [9–11]; however, key limitations of ESA therapy persist, including Hb variability, hyporesponsiveness in specific patient populations, and dose-dependent cardiovascular risk associated with high ESA exposure [7,8,12–14]. Accordingly, there remains a need for ESA regimens that achieve more stable Hb control while minimizing the requirement for high-dose exposure under real-world dialysis conditions.

Efepoetin alfa is a long-acting recombinant human erythropoietin engineered as a homodimeric erythropoietin fused to a hybrid fragment crystallizable (hyFc) domain designed to enhance systemic exposure through neonatal Fc receptor-mediated recycling [15,16]. Consistent with this mechanism, efepoetin alfa showed prolonged erythropoietic activity in preclinical studies and sustained pharmacodynamic responses in healthy volunteers, including a more sustained reticulocyte response after subcutaneous administration compared with darbepoetin alfa [17,18]. Despite these favorable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic characteristics, the optimal clinical dosing regimen for patients on dialysis has not yet been clearly established. Given the dose-dependent safety considerations of ESA therapy and the importance of Hb stability, rigorous dose-finding is required to define the dose range that provides stable Hb control with acceptable safety relative to established long-acting ESAs [19,20].

Therefore, the primary objective of this phase 2 randomized study was to evaluate Hb responses across multiple efepoetin alfa dosing regimens and to identify the optimal dose for phase 3 development. Efficacy was assessed using darbepoetin alfa and methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta as comparators in patients with CKD-associated anemia receiving maintenance dialysis, with secondary objectives including safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity.

Methods

Study design

This was a multicenter, open-label, prospective, randomized, active-controlled, phase 2 clinical trial conducted to explore the optimal starting dose and dosing interval, and to evaluate the efficacy and safety of efepoetin alfa administered by intravenous injection in HD patients and by subcutaneous injection in PD patients with anemia associated with CKD. The randomized active-controlled comparison reported in this manuscript (Part B) comprised a 12-week treatment period for the HD and PD cohorts. A preliminary exploratory component in PD patients (Part A) supported regimen selection for Part B. Administration routes and active comparators were selected to reflect routine clinical practice for each dialysis modality. The study was conducted at 15 dialysis centers in Korea between April 15, 2016, and April 20, 2017.

The protocol was approved by the local institutional review board at each center (Supplementary Table 1, available online). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Korean Good Clinical Practice and was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02044653). All participants provided written informed consent. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the study comprised four sequential periods: a combined washout period, screening period, treatment period, and follow-up period after the last administered dose.

Following washout and screening, patients were stratified by dialysis modality (HD or PD) and then randomized within each modality to one of the treatment groups. For HD patients (n = 122), patients were randomized to receive intravenous efepoetin alfa 5 µg/kg once weekly (Q1W), efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q1W, efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg every 2 weeks (Q2W), or darbepoetin alfa 30 µg Q1W. For PD patients (n = 75), patients were randomized to receive subcutaneous efepoetin alfa 5 µg/kg Q2W, efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q2W, or methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta 0.6 µg/kg Q2W. The administration routes and active comparator regimens reflected routine clinical practice for each dialysis modality at the time of study design. Darbepoetin alfa 30 µg Q1W was selected as the protocol-defined active comparator regimen for the HD cohort based on the preceding phase 1 comparator regimen, in which intravenous efepoetin alfa 5 µg/kg showed a reticulocyte maximum response of similar magnitude to that observed with darbepoetin alfa 30 µg [18]. In contrast, efepoetin alfa was administered on a body weight-adjusted basis according to the predefined dose-finding scheme for the investigational product. Oral iron supplementation was permitted throughout the study according to standard clinical practice. An 8-week follow-up period was scheduled after the 12-week treatment period.

Study population

Eligible patients were aged ≥18 years with a diagnosis of end-stage renal disease who had anemia and were receiving maintenance HD or PD. Patients were eligible if they had discontinued prior ESA therapy for ≥8 weeks (PD) or ≥2 weeks (HD) and had Hb <10 g/dL at screening, as determined by the investigator. At screening, patients were additionally required to have adequate iron status, defined as a serum ferritin level ≥100 ng/mL and a transferrin saturation ≥20%.

Hemoglobin-based dosing rules

Hb was assessed at each scheduled visit. Efepoetin alfa dosing was adjusted using a prespecified Hb-based algorithm: dose reduction by 25% for Hb ≥12 g/dL and <13 g/dL, temporary withholding for Hb ≥13 g/dL, and resumption at a 25% reduced dose when Hb subsequently decreased to <13 g/dL while remaining ≥12 g/dL. Discontinuation of study drug administration due to Hb ≥13 g/dL was a protocol-defined treatment-management event and did not constitute premature withdrawal from the study or a major protocol deviation. Study treatment could also be discontinued for inadequate Hb response if Hb decreased to baseline and/or <9 g/dL despite treatment or if clinically significant anemia symptoms occurred after at least 1 month of treatment.

No separate rescue therapy regimen was prespecified; instead, patient management was guided by protocol-defined treatment rules. Iron supplementation and medications necessary for chronic disease management were permitted, whereas ESAs and agents likely to affect Hb assessment were prohibited during the efficacy evaluation period.

Endpoints and assessments

The primary endpoint was the mean change in Hb level from baseline after 12 weeks of treatment, assessed at the end of efficacy evaluation following the final scheduled administration, in comparison with darbepoetin alfa in the HD cohort and methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta in the PD cohort. Secondary endpoints included longitudinal Hb changes; Hb response categories based on mean monthly Hb change from baseline (<1.0, 1.0–2.0, and ≥2.0 g/dL); maximum Hb change and time to maximum Hb; and red blood cell (RBC)- and reticulocyte-related indices. The cumulative area under the effect curve (AUEC) was calculated according to the dosing-interval-specific assessment schedule: Q1W regimens incorporated Hb measurements through week 13, whereas Q2W regimens incorporated Hb measurements through the post-dose assessment after the final scheduled administration (AUEC14). Treatment compliance and the target response rate were also evaluated. The target response rate was defined as an Hb increase ≥1 g/dL from baseline or Hb ≥11 g/dL without blood transfusion during efficacy evaluation. Patients requiring dose reduction for Hb ≥12 g/dL or study drug withholding or discontinuation for Hb ≥13 g/dL were not considered dropouts or major protocol deviations and were classified as target responders. Safety assessments included adverse events (AEs) and laboratory parameters, and immunogenicity (antidrug antibodies against efepoetin alfa) was assessed 8 weeks after the last administration.

Sample size considerations

Because PD Part A and HD/PD Part B served different purposes, different approaches were used for sample size determination. For PD Part A, the sample size was calculated based on the change in Hb from baseline after 4 weeks. Assuming an expected mean Hb increase of 1.5 g/dL, a clinically meaningful difference of 0.5 g/dL, and a standard deviation of 0.7 g/dL from a previous ESA study [21], with a type I error rate of 10% and 80% power, nine patients per group were required using G*Power; allowing for a 10% dropout rate, 10 patients per group were planned.

For HD Part B and PD Part B, formal confirmatory power calculations were not performed because these parts supported dose selection. Planned sample sizes were prospectively determined based on prior ESA studies and feasibility considerations [22,23]: approximately 30 patients per group for HD Part B and 48 efepoetin alfa-treated patients and 24 control patients for PD Part B.

Immunogenicity

Immunogenicity was assessed in the safety set by measuring binding and neutralizing antidrug antibodies to efepoetin alfa at prespecified time points, and the results were summarized descriptively using the number of participants with evaluable samples as the denominator.

Dose-conversion ratio calculation

The dose-conversion ratio (DCR) was calculated using an approach adapted from a previously published method [24]. For Q2W regimens, effectiveness was summarized as AUEC14, defined as the area under the Hb change-time curve through the post-dose assessment following the final scheduled administration and calculated using the trapezoidal rule. The dose was defined as the cumulative dose administered through the final scheduled administration. The DCR was defined as the ratio of the dose/AUEC14 for efepoetin alfa to the dose/AUEC14 for methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta. It was used as supportive, exploratory information to approximate the efepoetin alfa Q2W starting dose relative to methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta 0.6 µg/kg Q2W.

Hb response categories (<1.0, 1.0–2.0, and ≥2.0 g/dL) were prespecified as a secondary efficacy endpoint; DCR estimation within these post-treatment categories was performed at the end of efficacy evaluation as an additional post hoc exploratory analysis and interpreted descriptively.

Statistical analysis

All data were summarized and analyzed according to the predefined analysis sets. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and categorical variables as frequency and percentage.

Efficacy analyses were performed primarily in the intent-to-treat (ITT) set, supported by per-protocol (PP) analyses, whereas safety analyses were conducted in the safety set. For the PP analyses, no separate minimum treatment duration was prespecified. The PP1 set included ITT subjects who completed the study per protocol, had no major protocol deviations, and had treatment compliance of at least 75%. Protocol-defined study drug discontinuation due to Hb ≥13 g/dL did not constitute a major protocol deviation. The PP2 set further excluded subjects with study drug discontinuation due to Hb ≥13 g/dL or blood transfusion.

Baseline characteristics were compared using the Pearson chi-square test, analysis of variance (ANOVA), or the Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate. Between-group comparisons of Hb changes from baseline were performed using ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests for continuous outcomes and Pearson chi-square or Fisher exact test for categorical outcomes. At weeks 4, 8, and 12, and the end of efficacy evaluation, patients were categorized according to Hb change from baseline as <1.0 g/dL, 1.0–2.0 g/dL, or ≥2.0 g/dL; distributions were compared between each efepoetin alfa group and the corresponding active comparator using Pearson chi-square test or Fisher exact test, as appropriate.

Missing efficacy data were assumed to be missing at random and were handled by multiple imputation using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo method. For patients who did not meet the modality-specific ESA discontinuation interval (PD, <8 weeks; HD, <2 weeks), baseline Hb was estimated using a prespecified growth curve model and used only in prespecified sensitivity analyses. Hb values after dose withholding due to Hb ≥13 g/dL or after blood transfusion were imputed using the last Hb value obtained immediately before withholding or transfusion, respectively. All tests were two-sided, with a significance level of 0.05. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS version 21.0 (IBM Corp.) and SAS version 9.3 software (SAS Institute).

Results

Patient disposition and baseline characteristics

A total of 197 patients, including 122 in the HD cohort and 75 in the PD cohort, all with baseline Hb levels below 10 g/dL, were randomly assigned to efepoetin alfa or active comparator groups (darbepoetin alfa in HD or methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta in PD). Discontinuation of intervention due to Hb ≥13 g/dL reflected protocol-defined treatment management rather than withdrawal from the study (Fig. 2). Of the 197 patients who were randomized, 195 (121 in the HD cohort and 74 in the PD cohort) were included in the ITT population, excluding two patients who were not evaluated for Hb levels. In general, the demographic and baseline characteristics of the randomized arms, including Hb levels, were statistically similar, as shown in Table 1. All patients were of Asian ethnicity, with a mean age of 56 years. The mean baseline Hb levels were 9.0 g/dL in the HD cohort and 8.7 g/dL in the PD cohort, respectively.

Change in mean hemoglobin level from baseline at the end of efficacy evaluation after 12 weeks of treatment

Among the HD patients, the change in Hb levels from baseline at the end of efficacy evaluation after 12 weeks of treatment (ΔHb12) was numerically higher in the efepoetin alfa 5 µg/kg Q1W group (3.19 ± 1.33 g/dL) and 8 µg/kg Q1W group (3.19 ± 1.28 g/dL) compared with the darbepoetin alfa 30 µg Q1W group (2.52 ± 1.07 g/dL). The p-values versus the darbepoetin alfa group were 0.03 and 0.03, respectively. However, the ΔHb12 was lower in the efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q2W group (1.34 ± 1.06 g/dL) compared with the darbepoetin alfa 30 µg Q1W group (p = 0.001). With the exception of the efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q2W group, all other groups achieved and maintained the target Hb range (Fig. 3A, B). In PD patients, the ΔHb12 was numerically higher in the efepoetin alfa 5 µg/kg Q2W group (2.69 ± 1.28 g/dL) and the efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q2W group (3.48 ± 0.96 g/dL) compared with the methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta 0.6 µg/kg Q2W group (2.01 ± 1.27 g/dL) (p < 0.001). The efepoetin alfa groups reached the target Hb range earlier than the methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta group. Moreover, the ΔHb12 with efepoetin alfa 5 and 8 µg/kg Q2W exhibited a dose-dependent response as shown in Fig. 3C, D.

Target response rate

The target response rate was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved either an Hb increase of ≥1 g/dL from baseline or an Hb level of ≥11 g/dL at any post-baseline visit without blood transfusion during the prespecified efficacy evaluation period. Most patients achieved the target Hb response across all treatment groups, and no statistically significant differences were observed between efepoetin alfa and the corresponding active comparator groups in either cohort (Supplementary Table 3, available online).

Dose-conversion ratio and dose selection for efepoetin alfa

To inform phase 3 starting dose selection, the DCR between efepoetin alfa and methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta was estimated using a previously published approach [24]. Hb response categories (<1.0, 1.0–2.0, and ≥2.0 g/dL) were prespecified as a secondary efficacy endpoint; however, DCR estimation within these categories was performed as an additional post hoc exploratory analysis. Because some Hb response categories included small numbers of patients, showed substantial variability in Hb response, or had sparse/empty cells, these subgroup-specific DCR estimates should be interpreted descriptively.

As summarized in Table 2, the DCR-derived estimated efepoetin alfa doses were 2.38, 4.34, 3.68, and 4.56 µg/kg, with a median of 4.01 µg/kg. Because body weight differed across the PD treatment groups, an additional descriptive weight-adjusted sensitivity analysis was performed, indicating that the DCR-derived dose estimates were sensitive to body weight normalization (Supplementary Table 4, available online). The proposed phase 3 starting dose was not determined solely from the category-based DCR estimates. Rather, the 4 µg/kg Q2W starting dose was selected based on the totality of phase 1/2 evidence, including the dose-dependent PK/PD characteristics observed in phase 1, Hb response patterns observed in this phase 2 study, and supportive DCR findings. Taken together, these findings supported 4 µg/kg Q2W as a starting dose for phase 3 evaluation (NCT04155125).

Incidence of packed red blood cell transfusion

The incidence of packed RBC transfusion was low across all treatment groups in both the HD and PD cohorts, with no statistically significant differences between efepoetin alfa and the corresponding active comparator groups (Supplementary Table 5, available online).

Adverse events and immunogenicity

During the study period, the overall safety patterns were similar between efepoetin alfa and the standard therapies. AEs reported in more than 5% of patients are presented in Table 3. In the HD cohort, AEs occurred in 24 patients (75.0%), 28 patients (87.5%), and 18 patients (64.3%) receiving efepoetin alfa 5 µg/kg Q1W, 8 µg/kg Q1W, and 8 µg/kg Q2W, respectively, and in 22 patients (73.3%) receiving darbepoetin alfa 30 µg Q1W. Serious AEs (SAEs) occurred in three (9.4%), nine (28.1%), and five patients (17.9%) in the respective efepoetin alfa groups and in three patients (10%) in the darbepoetin alfa group. There were no statistically significant differences in the incidence of AEs or SAEs between the treatments. Common AEs were consistent with those expected in patients on maintenance dialysis and are summarized in Table 3. The SAEs were not related to the study treatment. In the PD cohort, AEs occurred in 20 patients (80.0%) and 20 patients (76.9%) receiving efepoetin alfa 5 and 8 µg/kg Q2W, respectively, and in 17 patients (70.8%) receiving methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta 0.6 µg/kg Q2W. SAEs occurred in six, four, and five patients in the respective groups. Peritonitis was the most frequent SAE observed in the PD cohort. No binding or neutralizing antidrug antibodies against efepoetin alfa were detected at any time point (HD, 0/111 evaluable; PD, 0/65 evaluable).

Discussion

In the phase I clinical study, the effective half-life of efepoetin alfa was approximately 8 to 11 hours after intravenous administration and about 138 to 146 hours after subcutaneous administration. These findings support the suitability of efepoetin alfa for extended-interval dosing [18].

The aim of this phase 2 study was to determine the appropriate dosage regimen of efepoetin alfa for treating anemia associated with CKD in an active-controlled trial. Efepoetin alfa produced dose-dependent Hb increases and enabled assessment of an appropriate dose range for subsequent clinical development. A high proportion of participants achieved and maintained Hb levels within the desired target range without an apparent increase in the incidence of SAEs.

Part A in PD patients supported the selection of subcutaneous efepoetin alfa 5 and 8 µg/kg Q2W for Part B. In the Part B HD and PD cohorts, treatment according to the protocol’s algorithm generally achieved and maintained the target Hb range. Except for the 8 µg/kg Q2W group in the HD cohort, all other efepoetin alfa groups showed numerically greater Hb increases than the active comparators; however, these differences should be interpreted descriptively rather than as evidence of formal comparative efficacy. The categorical Hb response analysis also showed a shift toward higher Hb change categories over time. These findings suggest that dose reduction may be needed not only to align Hb responses with those of the comparator but also to reduce the risk of excessive Hb increase, as mean Hb levels exceeded the KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) target range and study drug administration was discontinued in 11% of patients under protocol-defined treatment-management rules due to Hb ≥13 g/dL. To inform the efepoetin alfa phase 3 dose selection, DCR was calculated using a previously published approach [24]. The category-based DCR analysis served as supportive, exploratory information rather than as the sole determinant of the phase 3 starting dose. The selected 4 µg/kg Q2W dose was based on integrated phase 1/2 evidence, including PK/PD characteristics, observed Hb response patterns, and the need to reduce excessive Hb increases.

The most common adverse drug reaction during the treatment period was hypertension, reported in one patient each in the efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q1W group in the HD cohort and the efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q2W group in the PD cohort. Adverse drug reaction incidence did not differ significantly between efepoetin alfa and standard therapies. One death due to cerebral hemorrhage occurred in the efepoetin alfa 8 µg/kg Q2W group in the HD cohort during the safety assessment period, but it was considered unrelated to the study drug. Overall, the AE profile of efepoetin alfa was similar to that of the active comparators.

This study has several limitations. First, the open-label design and relatively short treatment duration (12 weeks) limited assessment of long-term Hb stability, response durability, and infrequent safety events, including cardiovascular outcomes. Second, because the study was exploratory and not powered for formal non-inferiority or superiority testing, subgroup and comparator analyses should be interpreted cautiously. Third, dialysis modality-specific administration routes (intravenous in HD and subcutaneous in PD), although reflective of routine clinical practice, limited direct cross-modality comparisons. Fourth, the inclusion of only Korean Asian patients may limit generalizability to other populations and practice settings. Fifth, the observed Hb increases raise the possibility of Hb overcorrection in some patients, which should be considered when interpreting dose selection and safety. Finally, the exploratory DCR analyses were affected by small subgroup sizes, sparse cells, inter-patient variability, and body weight imbalance across PD groups, which should be considered when interpreting the DCR-derived dose estimates.

In conclusion, efepoetin alfa demonstrated acceptable safety and clinically meaningful erythropoietic efficacy in patients undergoing HD and PD. Integrated phase 1/2 findings, including PK/PD characteristics, observed Hb response, and supportive exploratory DCR analyses, supported 4 µg/kg Q2W as a phase 3 starting dose. Careful dose selection and Hb monitoring remain important to minimize excessive Hb increase.

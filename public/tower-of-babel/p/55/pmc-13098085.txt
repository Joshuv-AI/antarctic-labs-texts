Prevalence of Low Back Pain and Associated Factors Among University Students in Bangladesh: A Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Noushin Nohor, Md Mahadi Hassan, Samia Sultana, Takia Tabassum Tama, Yeasin Akash, Mukta Moni, Munia Akter Mim, Muhammad Nazmul Mostafa, Mst Aysha Akter, Md Al Sharia Mueid, Md Tajuddin Sikder
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundUniversity students are susceptible to low back pain (LBP) because of their predisposition to various factors that have been previously linked to the increased risk of LBP among other populations. There is a substantial lack of research that explores the prevalence and risk factors of LBP among university students in Bangladesh.PurposeThis study aims to find the prevalence of LBP in Bangladeshi university students and factors associated with LBP.MethodsA cross‐sectional study of 800 Bangladeshi university students assessed LBP using the Oswestry Disability Index. Descriptive statistics summarized sociodemographics and clinical characteristics. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression identified associated factors, with variables having an initial p < 0.25 included in the final model. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 and a 95% confidence interval.ResultsThe prevalence of LBP among Bangladeshi university students was 42.2% for general LBP disability. More precisely, 26.4% had mild LBP, followed by severe LBP (8.4%), moderate LBP (7.2%), and complete disability (0.2%). Factors such as participants' gender, educational level, primary residence, monthly household income, backpack weight, smoking, alcohol status, and academic stress were found to be significantly associated with LBP.ConclusionThe findings suggest that nearly half of the university students were affected by LBP, indicating a need for increased awareness, promotion of healthy lifestyles, and specific policies and interventions for the successful management of the disease.Keywords: disability, low back pain, Oswestry Disability Index, university students

Introduction

LBP is a frequently occurring musculoskeletal disorder and a global public health concern. It is defined as pain in the posterior aspect of the body, ranging from the lower margin of the 12th rib to the gluteal folds [1, 2, 3]. LBP is a highly prevalent disorder, with an estimated global prevalence of 31% of the population and an average point prevalence of 18%. It is more frequent in females aged 40–80, and 39% of the adult population suffers from at least one episode of LBP throughout their lives [4, 5]. A wide range of factors have been previously found to be associated with LBP. Individual factors such as age, gender, body mass index (BMI), and physical activity have been found to have profound effects on the presence of LBP in an individual [1, 3]. In addition, different psychological and social factors, such as stress regarding the job, job dissatisfaction, depression, and anxiety, are associated with an increased risk of LBP [6, 7]. However, a single factor is hardly the sole contributing factor; rather, a multifactorial contribution of anatomical structure, biomechanics, lifestyle, and so on, combined, is often responsible for LBP [5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16].

Previous studies suggest that LBP is also prevalent in Bangladesh. The overall prevalence of LBP was found to be 18.5% according to a national study. However, this prevalence rate appears to be lower because the study included the general population. The prevalence of LBP among different population groups is much higher, particularly among the groups predisposed to different risk factors of LBP. A recent study conducted on farmers, rickshaw pullers, and office workers in Bangladesh found a 42.7% prevalence of LBP. This prevalence rate is higher than that of many studies conducted among similar populations around the world [17]. Similarly, a study found that 71% of the garment workers in a garment factory were suffering from LBP, which is significantly higher in the context of both national and global levels [18]. Some studies have been conducted on the students of medical colleges in Bangladesh regarding their LBP. The study conducted by Sany et al. [19] reported a point prevalence of LBP of 25.6%, 46.9%, and 63.3% for 6 months and 12 months, respectively. This raises concerns about the current status and distribution of LBP among university students in Bangladesh, who have been reported in previous studies to be exposed to several risk factors for musculoskeletal disorders, including prolonged sitting, extensive computer use, and poor ergonomic practices [16, 20, 21]. Based on this evidence, we hypothesized that Bangladeshi university students may represent a vulnerable group for low back pain (LBP), which this study aimed to examine.

University students constitute a significant proportion of the population in Bangladesh. According to the recent “Bangladesh Education Statistics 2023” reports published by the Bangladesh Bureau of Educational Information and Statistics (BANBEIS), more than 1 million students were studying at different universities in Bangladesh during that time [22]. The situation regarding LBP among university students in Bangladesh is a topic that has been understudied for a long period. On the other hand, university students are particularly vulnerable to the development of LBP because of their exposure to several predetermined risk factors [20, 21]. Understanding modifiable risk factors and the situation of LBP can enable making tailored decisions and targeted interventions for university students. The timely identification of LBP can pave the way for early preventive measures to reduce the probability of long‐term complications. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the prevalence of LBP among university students in Bangladesh and the factors associated with it. This study can potentially be used as baseline information regarding the LBP situation among university students, and also for identifying underlying factors associated with LBP. This can help to make informed decisions regarding controlling LBP among university students in Bangladesh through targeted interventions, policy regulations, awareness programs, and so on.

Materials and Methods

Study Design, Setting, and Population

A nationwide cross‐sectional study was conducted from January 15, 2025 to January 31, 2025 in Bangladesh. The study participants were recruited using a multistage cluster sampling method. Based on the selection procedure, a nationally representative sample of students was selected from four different universities, situated in four different divisions across Bangladesh. The inclusion criteria for participants included participants who were currently enrolled at a university in Bangladesh and were willing to participate in the study.

Sample Size and Sampling Procedure

The number of participants required for this study was calculated using the following Cochran's formula for multistage cluster sampling [23],

Here, n = number of required samples; Z = 1.96 (95% confidence level); p = prevalence estimate (0.5); q = (1 − 0.5); e = margin of error; de = design effect for multistage sampling. Since the prevalence of LBP among Bangladeshi university students is unknown, we calculated the estimated sample using the conservative assumption of a 50% prevalence rate. This assumption is deeply rooted in the Cochran formula that the prevalence of disease should be considered 0.50 when sufficient data are not available. This conservative assumption ensures the calculation of the highest number of estimated samples. Usually, the design effect (de) for a multistage cluster sample is considered within 1.5–2 [24]. However, since the intraclass correlation coefficient within the clusters is not clear, we considered the design effect to be 2, and the error margin was considered 0.05, which is usually used against a 95% confidence interval (CI) in studies. Initially, the required sample size for this study was calculated to be 768. However, since we intended to include four different universities, we found it more suitable to exceed the required sample size to be 800, which would allow us to select an equal number of samples (n = 200) from each university since the universities are similar in size and student numbers.

A multistage cluster random sampling was used to select the participants for this study. In this sampling technique, the eight divisions in Bangladesh were considered primary clusters, and the universities within them were considered secondary clusters. In the first stage of the procedure, four divisions, which are Dhaka, Rajshahi, Chittagong, and Khulna, were randomly selected. In the next step, one university from each division was randomly selected. The randomly selected universities are Dhaka University, Rajshahi University, Chittagong University, and Khulna University from Dhaka, Rajshahi, Chittagong, and Khulna Division, respectively. From each university, an equal number of samples (n = 200) were randomly selected. This process ensured the equal representation of participants from each cluster.

Survey Instrument and Data Collection

A structured questionnaire was used to collect data from the participants for this study. The questionnaire was divided into several sections to collect data on different aspects of the study participants. The first part of the questionnaire contained questions about the sociodemographic characteristics of the study participants. The sociodemographic variables were adapted from previously published studies about LBP [25, 26, 27, 28]. The second part of the questionnaire contained questions regarding the behavioral and clinical characteristics of the participants. These questions were adapted from previous studies that were conducted to investigate the prevalence and predictors of LBP [1, 25, 29]. In the last section of the questionnaire, the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) was incorporated to assess the presence of LBP among participants. ODI consists of a total of 10 questions for evaluating the disability status of the participants [30]. Before data collection, this study received ethics approval from the Biosafety, Biosecurity, and Ethical Committee of Jahangirnagar University (Ref No: BBEC, JU/M 2025/01 (166)). A face‐to‐face interview was conducted for the collection of data from the participants by the data enumerators. The interviewers used the questionnaire and recorded responses from the participants. The collected data were then entered into a password‐protected Microsoft Excel sheet. The data were preserved with utmost confidentiality and were only accessible to research team members responsible for the data analysis. No uniquely identifiable data were recorded, which ensured the anonymity of the participants.

Measures

In this study, LBP was operationally defined as self‐reported pain or discomfort localized between the lower costal margin and the gluteal folds, with or without leg pain, that resulted in functional disability as assessed by the ODI [31, 32]. The ODI is a validated and widely used instrument for quantifying disability associated with LBP, with scores ranging from 0% to 100% [30]. The disability status of the participants was categorized based on the instructions provided in the article published by the original authors of the scale. Based on the instruction, it was categorized as Scores 0–4 (No disability), 5–14 (Mild disability), 15–24 (Moderate disability), 25–34 (Severe disability), and 35–50 (Completely disabled) [30]. The dichotomous categorization of the LBP status was done by categorizing 0–4 as no LBP and 5–50 as the presence of LBP. For the prevalence and binary logistic regression, the dichotomous categorization of LBP was used.

Statistical Analysis

Data collected from the field were first entered into an Excel file, and basic preprocessing (e.g., coding, score calculation) was done using Microsoft Excel 2021. The final analysis, which included descriptive statistics, statistical tests, and visualization, was done in RStudio (Version: 2024.12.0 + 467), using the R programming language (4.2.1). The tidyverse(), gtsummary(), and gt() packages were used for statistical tests and publication‐ready tables. On the other hand, ggplot2() and ggpubr() packages were used for producing visualization plots.

Descriptive statistics were used to present the frequency and percentages of each categorical variable. To find any significant factors influencing the presence of LBP, both univariate and multivariate binary logistic regression were conducted. The factors having a p value of < 0.25 in the univariate analysis (p < 0.25) were included in the multivariate regression analysis. Factors that remained significant (p < 0.05) in the multivariate logistic regression were considered associated factors. Model fitness was evaluated by the Hosmer–Lemeshow test (p = 0.57). In the final model, multicollinearity was measured using the variance inflation factor, which was found to be within a tolerable range (< 10). For this study, a 95% CI and p value of < 0.05 (p < 0.05) were considered statistically significant.

Ethics Statement

This study was conducted under the Declaration of Helsinki and was reviewed and approved by the Biosafety, Biosecurity, and Ethical Committee of Jahangirnagar University (Ref No: BBEC, JU/M 2025/01 (166)). Written informed consent was obtained from each participant before their inclusion in the study. The consent form was read to the participants verbally by our data enumerators. Participation in this study was voluntary, and participants were free to withdraw at any time without penalty or loss of benefits. All data were anonymized by assigning unique identification codes to participants, and no personally identifiable information was collected. To ensure data security, the data set was stored in password‐protected and encrypted files, with access restricted to the research team only. These measures underscore our commitment to safeguarding participants' identities and upholding the highest ethical standards in data handling. Confidentiality and anonymity of participants were maintained throughout the study, with all data being stored in password‐protected files accessible only to the research team. No identifying information was included in any of the publications arising from this research.

Behavioral and Clinical Characteristics of the Study Participants

Table 2 presents the sociodemographic and lifestyle characteristics of the study participants. Out of the total 800 participants, the majority (56%) reported engaging in physical activity, while 44% did not. In terms of daily study habits, a significant proportion (72%) studied < 5 h/day. Weekly rest days varied, with 44% reporting one rest day and 40% having more than one rest day per week. Regarding backpack weight, the majority of participants (51%) carried a moderate weight. A notable proportion of participants (42%) had a family history of LBP. Chronic health conditions such as diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, and respiratory disease were less prevalent, with 85%, 89%, and 86% of participants, respectively, reporting no such conditions. Similarly, most of the participants reported not having conditions such as arthritis, thyroid, liver cirrhosis, or stroke. A significant majority of the participants reported never engaging in smoking (75%) or alcohol consumption (92%). Academic stress was highly prevalent, with 77% of participants reporting experiencing stress, while 55% expressed dissatisfaction with their academic performance. Daily smartphone screen time was significant, with 52% reporting 5–8 h of daily screen time.

Prevalence of LBP Among University Students

The distribution of LBP disability among Bangladeshi university students is presented in Figure 1. This study reported a prevalence of 42.2% for general LBP disability among the participants (N = 800). This prevalence includes disability in all categories. However, a more distinctive prevalence can be reported. Most of the participants (26.4%) reported having mild LBP disability, followed by severe LBP disability (8.4%), moderate LBP disability (7.2%), and completely disabled due to LBP disability (0.2%). In addition, 57.8% of the total participants reported having no sort of LBP disability at all.

Factors Associated With LBP Among University Students

Factor analysis for LBP among university students was confirmed using the adjusted odds ratio (AOR) from multivariate logistic regression, which controlled for the confounding factors identified in the univariate logistic regression. Factors with a p value of < 0.25 in the univariate regression were included in the multivariate logistic regression to find associated factors of LBP. In the univariate logistic regression, factors that had a p value of < 0.25 were gender, educational level, marital status, primary residence, monthly household income, backpack weight, family history of LBP, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, arthritis, thyroid disease, liver cirrhosis, stroke, smoking, alcohol, and academic stress. All of them were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to calculate the AOR and p‐value within the 95% CI.

Multivariate logistic regression confirmed the significant association between gender, educational level, primary residence, monthly household income, backpack weight, smoking status, alcohol consumption, and academic stress with LBP (p < 0.05). According to the multivariate analysis (Table 3), male participants (AOR = 0.69, 95% CI 0.49, 0.98, p = 0.039) are significantly less likely to have LBP than female participants. Students who are in their second year (AOR 0.71, 95% CI: 0.51, 0.99, p = 0.046), master's (AOR 0.21, 95% CI: 0.06, 0.61, p = 0.007) of study are significantly less likely to be affected by LBP compared to the students who are in their first year of study. In addition, participants with an urban primary residence (AOR 0.60, 95% CI: 0.43, 0.84, p = 0.003) are less likely to have LBP than those with a rural residence. Those students living in a household of income > 21,000 BDT were significantly less likely to be affected with LBP compared to those who lived in a household of monthly income < 12,500 BDT (AOR = 0.61, 95% CI 0.39, 0.94, p = 0.027). Participants who were current or former smokers (AOR = 2.52, 95% CI 1.62, 3.99, p < 0.001) were more likely to have LBP compared to those who had never smoked in their lifetimes. Similarly, those who had never consumed alcohol (AOR = 0.33, 95% CI 0.17, 0.64, p = 0.001) are significantly less likely to have LBP than those who were current or former alcohol consumers. Moreover, participants who reported having academic stress (AOR = 1.47, 95% CI 1.01, 2.15, p = 0.046) were significantly more likely to be affected by LBP than those who reported having no academic stress.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first nationwide study conducted on university students in Bangladesh to find the prevalence of LBP. The prevalence of LBP in this study was 42.2%, which is higher than the study conducted on university students in Ethiopia (40.1%) [33] and China [34]. However, this prevalence is lower than the one reported in Gondar (55.6%) [35], Saudi Arabia (67.6%) [36], Egypt (73.1%) [37], Serbia (59.5%) [38], and France (72.1%). Since the studies utilized different scales and questionnaires from ours, this could have an impact on the results. On the other hand, the study population and sampling technique are important factors that can explain the visible difference in the prevalence outcome. However, the prevalence is high considering our study population, who are university students with a comparatively young age.

In our study, males were significantly less likely to have LBP than females. Similar findings have been reported in studies conducted in Italy [39], Brazil [1], and Korea [40], where females were more likely to have LBP. This could be explained by the fact that women are often engaged in household activities that require prolonged forward leaning or uncomfortable body posture. Menstruation can be an underlying factor behind the higher amount of LBP in females because several muscle contractions and other problems can create pain and discomfort, which can explain the likelihood of LBP in females. In contrast, studies conducted in Ethiopia [33] reported that males were more likely to be affected by LBP. This difference could result from methods, measures, and data collection techniques or be due to the changes in the circumstances that could affect the results. Our study found that with the increase in the educational level, the risk of LBP was reduced in the participants. In our study, it was found that university students who were in the second year and masters of the study were less likely to be affected by LBP compared to those who were in the first year of their studies. The reduction in LBP risk with increasing educational level may be due to a combination of adaptation to academic life, better time management, improved health awareness, reduced stress, and a shift toward healthier lifestyles and behaviors in higher‐level students. University students with an urban upbringing are significantly less likely to have LBP compared to students with a rural residence. This is likely due to the reason that rural people are more engaged in physically demanding occupations and lifestyles. This is a well‐documented risk factor for LBP. Urban populations are engaged in sedentary or less physically demanding jobs, which might reduce the mechanical stress on the spine. These could be the possible explanations behind the association of primary residence with LBP. Higher monthly household income was negatively associated with the LBP. This could be because students with a family of higher monthly income can afford healthcare access, nutritious foods, and healthy lifestyles, and have conscious parents who are more attentive and alert regarding the LBP, compared to the students with lower family income. This income disparity might be the deciding factor in getting the necessary commodities to tackle LBP. This study revealed that students carrying a heavy backpack were more likely to have LBP than those carrying a backpack with light weight. Similar finding was reported in a study conducted in southern China where students carrying heavy backpacks were more frequently having idiopathic scoliosis. Heavy backpacks can cause excessive pressure on the shoulder and can have a bending effect on the spine, which might be the reason for the increased likelihood of LBP. Our study revealed the association of smoking with LBP. This is similar to findings from studies conducted in Brazil [1], Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey [41]. In these studies, smokers were found to be at risk for LBP due to their smoking habits. This association can be because of the changes caused by cigarette components in the pH and nutritional status of intervertebral discs. This results in the reduction of the stabilizing muscles of the lumbar spine and thus interferes with the perception of pain. Alcohol was found to be a risk factor for LBP in the study. According to our study, those who have never consumed alcohol are significantly less likely to have LBP than those who are current or former alcohol consumers. A similar result was reported in a study on taxi drivers in Turkey [27], which reported a higher prevalence of LBP among alcohol consumers. Since alcohol is known to cause inflammation, the inflammatory response from alcohol can affect the muscles or discs in the back, leading to pain. In contrast, a study in South Korea [40] found alcohol consumption to be a factor negatively associated with LBP. This contrast could be the result of the selection of study participants and methodologies. Since we have used primary data collected from the university students, and the study in Korea utilized secondary survey data, the difference in the outcome could be explained by this. However, more research and in‐depth explorations are required to investigate the type of associations between alcohol consumption and LBP. Academic stress in the participants increased the likelihood of LBP in them. The students who reported having academic stress were more likely to have LBP than those who reported no academic stress. Similar findings were reported in studies conducted in China [42] and Kenya [43]. In those studies, it was found that academic dissatisfaction and academic stress were significantly associated with LBP among the study participants. This can be explained by the fact that students under academic pressure tend to study for long hours, and they sit in the chair for a long time. Prolonged study hours and a long duration of sitting are responsible for LBP since they put pressure on the backbone and other muscles.

This is one of the first studies conducted on university students' back pain status in Bangladesh and therefore has a significant impact. This study involved participants using a nationally representative sampling method, which ensured a representative sample. This allows us to bring out a scenario that can explain the overall LBP situation of university students across Bangladesh. The random sampling method reduces potential bias and errors regarding the data. The large sample size accounts for the increasing validity and reliability and ensures the robustness of the study. However, the study has some limitations that can be addressed in future studies. To calculate the required sample size, we used a 50% prevalence rate, as there was no known prevalence rate of LBP among Bangladeshi university students. This approach is statistically more reliable when an undefined prevalence rate is present. Additionally, this study did not include standardized measures of psychological factors such as depression, anxiety, or perceived stress, which are known to influence the development and persistence of LBP. The absence of these variables may have limited our ability to fully explain the psychosocial contribution to LBP among university students and could have resulted in residual confounding. In addition, the cross‐sectional nature of the study limits us to establishing any causal relationships. Case‐control and randomized controlled trials should be conducted to find the causes behind LBP among university students in the future. In multivariate analysis, we tried to account for the potential confounders. However, there could be unknown and other confounders that have not been controlled in our studies. Further studies should be conducted to control for additional confounders and investigate the associated factors of LBP for more accurate results.

Conclusion

This study found that LBP is highly prevalent among university students in Bangladesh, affecting more than two‐fifths of the study population. Several sociodemographic and behavioral factors, including gender, educational level, primary residence, household income, backpack weight, smoking, alcohol consumption, and academic stress, were significantly associated with LBP. These findings highlight the importance of early identification of high‐risk groups and the need for targeted preventive strategies within university settings. Interventions focusing on ergonomic awareness, healthy lifestyle behaviors, and stress management may help reduce the burden of LBP among students. Future longitudinal studies are recommended to establish causal relationships and further explore psychosocial and environmental determinants of LBP.

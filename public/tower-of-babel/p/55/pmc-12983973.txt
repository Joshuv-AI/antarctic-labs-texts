Psychometric properties and cultural validity of mental health assessment tools for refugees and asylum seekers: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Silvia Tempia Valenta, Andrea Conti, Fabio Porru, Gaia Giovannardi, Ida Andersen, Wichor M Bramer, Anna Rita Atti, Diana De Ronchi, Massimiliano Panella, Ives Hubloue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionMigration is a complex global phenomenon, with millions of people relocating each year driven by various social or personal reasons. Among them, refugees and asylum seekers form a particularly vulnerable subgroup, often forced to escape conflicts, persecution or life-threatening conditions. Most mental health assessment tools, originally developed in high-income countries and validated primarily in Western populations, may lack the cultural validity needed for this demographic. The primary objective is to systematically review and synthesise the psychometric properties and cultural validity of mental health assessment tools validated for refugees and asylum seekers. Secondary objectives are to (a) identify the range of mental health symptoms these tools assess and (b) describe the geographical coverage in terms of the regions of origin and host countries where tools have been validated.Methods and analysisReported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines, the review will conduct searches across different databases (ie, MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science Core Collection, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, PsycINFO, Global Index Medicus, CINAHL and Scopus), from inception to January 2025, with no restrictions on language. We will include validation, adaptation or diagnostic accuracy studies, as well as any other study design that reports at least one psychometric property of a mental health assessment tool. Two pairs of reviewers will screen, extract and appraise each study independently. Methodological quality will be assessed with the COSMIN (COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments) Risk-of-Bias checklist for measurement studies and QUADAS-2 (Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies-2) for diagnostic accuracy studies; the certainty of the evidence per property will be graded using the COSMIN-GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) approach. Narrative synthesis will be performed for all properties; where appropriate, meta-analyses will pool Cronbach’s α (Fisher-Z), intraclass correlations and sensitivity/specificity estimates using random-effects models. Inconsistency will be explored with subgroup analyses and meta-regressions; publication bias will be investigated with funnel plots and Egger’s test when ≥10 studies are available.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required for this systematic review, as no empirical data will be collected. Results will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed publication in a scientific journal and presented at relevant conferences.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024510901.Keywords: PSYCHIATRY, Health Equity, MENTAL HEALTH, Meta-Analysis, Psychometrics, Review

Introduction

Migration is a global phenomenon characterised by a stratification of origins and pathways involving individuals from various cultural backgrounds who leave their own country for socioeconomic, political or crisis-related reasons.1,5 The term migrant serves as an umbrella concept, not legally defined under international law, reflecting the common understanding of a person who relocates from their usual place of residence, either within a country or across an international border, either temporarily or permanently, for various personal motivations.6 From a policy perspective, many governments define asylum seekers as individuals awaiting a decision on their asylum claims.6 Within this framework, refugees are those whose claims have been successful, while those whose claims have been denied are often referred to as failed asylum seekers.6 7 In the context of migration, refugees and asylum seekers are known to be the groups most exposed to traumatic aspects during migration, often with heavier mental health consequences.8 9

Although migration often involves common experiences, such as leaving one’s home and adapting to a new environment, the challenges faced by refugees and asylum seekers at different stages of their journey are significantly distinct from those encountered by other types of migrants.5 10 11 Unlike voluntary migrants, refugees and asylum seekers often face profound mental health challenges due to pre-migration trauma, cultural adaptation and post-migration stressors, all of which increase their risk of mental illness. Pre-migration stressors such as war, persecution and hunger are prevalent, as are extreme hardship, abuse and torture during the migration phase.10 12 13 The post-migration phase is characterised by a multitude of stressors and traumatic events related to acculturation challenges, isolation, racial discrimination and the overall process of adapting to a new culture.5 10 11 13 14 Additionally, challenges such as health issues and limited access to medical services may exacerbate stressors.11

Refugees and asylum seekers often experience high mental health needs, with higher vulnerability and prevalence of psychiatric disorders like post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety disorders and mood disorders.315,18 Specifically, in refugees and asylum seekers, the prevalence rates of PTSD, anxiety and depression reach 88%, 81% and 80%, respectively.19,21 Moreover, there is a considerable overlap between these disorders, with research indicating that a significant portion of individuals experience various mental health conditions simultaneously.22 Nonetheless, this demographic often faces many obstacles in accessing mental health and psychosocial support services in destination countries.23 Major barriers include internal factors like mental illness itself, fatalism and mistrust, and external factors like affordability, limited services and poor cultural competency.24

A critical challenge in addressing the mental health needs of refugees and asylum seekers lies in the lack of culturally valid psychometric tools for accurately assessing their mental health symptoms.25 26 Psychometrics refers to the field of assessment and evaluation of mental health status or diagnosis of an individual.27 28 Mental health status refers to the overall psychological well-being and functioning of an individual, while a mental health diagnosis is the formal identification of a specific mental health condition based on standardised criteria.29 The term ‘psychometric’ itself refers both to the measurement of mental health status/diagnosis and the properties of the tools, such as validity, reliability and sensitivity, that ensure their accuracy and consistency in assessing these conditions.30 Psychometric tools can include questionnaires, interviews, scales and other diagnostic measures that help identify mental health conditions such as PTSD, anxiety, depression and other psychiatric symptoms. One of the most critical properties of a psychometric instrument is its replicability and reliability across different contexts and cultures.31 Many widely used instruments were primarily validated for Western populations several decades ago, raising concerns about their ability to accurately assess mental health symptoms in individuals from vastly different cultural and contextual backgrounds, with possible culturally specific distress manifestations and heterogeneous presentation of mental health symptoms.25 26 32 This can lead to misdiagnosis or failure to recognise relevant symptoms, which is particularly alarming given the high prevalence of mental symptoms in refugees and asylum seekers.33

Hence, in recent decades, the scientific community has begun validating scales specifically for use with refugees and asylum seekers. For instance, the Harvard Trauma Questionnaire (HTQ) and the Hopkins Symptom Checklist-25 (HSCL-25), originally developed for Western populations, have been adapted and validated to assess PTSD, anxiety and depression in different refugee groups.34 35 The HTQ has demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy for PTSD, with sensitivity and specificity values exceeding 0.80 in refugee populations such as Cambodians and Bosnians, and its cultural validity has been enhanced through adaptations to account for culturally specific expressions of trauma and distress.34 36 Similarly, the HSCL-25 has shown strong reliability and diagnostic accuracy for anxiety and depression, with cultural adaptations ensuring its cultural validity among diverse groups, including Afghan and Somali refugees.35 37

Given the increasing diversity of migration flows across cultures, there is a growing need to evaluate the current psychometric properties and cultural validity of mental health assessment tools for refugees and asylum seekers,32 enabling clinical settings to benefit from clear guidance on which instruments to use. To our knowledge, this is the first systematic review to assess both the measurement properties and cross-cultural applicability of mental health tools in refugee and asylum-seeking populations.

Objectives

The primary objective of this review is to synthesise existing evidence on the psychometric properties and cultural validity of mental health assessment tools validated for use with refugees and asylum seekers. Specifically, the review will examine key measurement properties including reliability, validity, sensitivity, specificity, internal consistency, test-retest reliability, construct validity and responsiveness. Secondary objectives are to identify the range of mental health symptoms assessed by these tools and to map the geographical coverage in terms of the regions of origin of the refugee and asylum-seeking populations for whom the tools have been validated.

Guided by the PCC framework (population: refugees and asylum seekers from conflict-affected countries; concept: psychometric properties and cultural validity of mental health assessment tools; context: any host country or validation setting) the review addresses the following question: what is the evidence on the psychometric properties and cultural validity of mental health assessment tools validated for use among refugees and asylum seekers from conflict-affected countries across diverse host settings?

Methods

This systematic review will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA).38

Eligibility criteria

Population

The target population for this review will include refugees and asylum seekers of all ages, genders and health statuses. Eligible individuals must originate from countries classified as Fragile and Conflict-Affected Situations, as defined by the World Bank Group.39 Individuals evacuated or exiled from such contexts, including Amerasians or other mixed-heritage groups explicitly described as having refugee backgrounds, are eligible. Generic references to ‘immigrants’ will be included only when the study specifies that participants originate from a conflict-affected country. Refugees residing in any host country will be considered for inclusion.

Conversely, studies dealing exclusively with internally displaced persons, political detainees or prisoners, linguistically diverse but non-migrant samples, or other foreign-born populations lacking a forced-migration context (eg, labour migrants from stable settings) will be excluded.

Instruments

The instruments of interest are psychometric tools designed to assess mental health status or diagnose specific mental disorders in refugee and asylum-seeking populations. To be eligible, a study must report at least one measurement property of the tool, such as reliability, validity, responsiveness, internal consistency, sensitivity, specificity or test-retest reliability. Studies providing evidence of cross-cultural adaptation or validation, including linguistic translation, conceptual equivalence or evaluation of cultural relevance, will also be included.

In the specific case of psychometric tools originally validated in migrant populations, studies will be included at the title and abstract screening stage even if they do not explicitly mention psychometric properties in the abstract. In these cases, full-text screening will be used to verify whether at least one measurement property is reported. If no such information is found in the full text, the study will be excluded.

Both self-report and clinician-administered instruments will be considered. Studies will be excluded if they do not report any psychometric or diagnostic accuracy properties, or fail to adequately describe the assessment tool being used. Qualitative studies and those relying solely on unstructured clinical interviews without standardised scales will also be excluded.

Constructs

This review will include instruments that assess psychiatric symptoms or clinically relevant mental health diagnoses among refugee and asylum-seeking populations. Eligible constructs include PTSD, depression, anxiety, somatisation and general psychological distress, as well as other symptom-based constructs aligned with standardised diagnostic criteria, such as those defined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) and the International Classification of Diseases (ICD). Instruments must be designed to evaluate mental health conditions that can be diagnosed or screened in clinical or epidemiological contexts.

In contrast, tools that do not measure psychiatric symptoms or diagnostic constructs will be excluded. These include instruments focusing on psychosocial attitudes, perceptions or experiences such as dignity, prejudice, social support, general well-being, exposure to violence without corresponding mental health outcomes, ambiguous loss or reactions to sociopolitical events.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes of interest are the measurement properties of the included instruments. These include aspects of reliability (eg, internal consistency and test-retest reliability), various forms of validity (eg, content, construct, criterion and cross-cultural validity), responsiveness to change over time and diagnostic accuracy parameters such as sensitivity, specificity, area under the curve and predictive values. Additionally, information on measurement invariance across languages, subgroups or cultural contexts will be extracted when available. Studies that do not assess or report on these outcomes will be excluded.

Study design

Eligible study designs will include validation studies, cross-cultural adaptation studies, diagnostic accuracy studies, and any other original quantitative research that evaluates one or more psychometric properties of a mental health assessment tool in a refugee or asylum-seeking population.

Studies using qualitative methods without standardised measurement instruments, reviews, meta-analyses, case reports, opinion pieces, conference abstracts and other forms of secondary literature will be excluded.

Information sources

We will conduct a literature search for relevant articles across several databases, including MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase (Ovid), Web of Science Core Collection, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, PsycINFO, Global Index Medicus, CINAHL and Scopus. Databases will be searched from inception to January 2025, with no restrictions on language.

Search strategy

The search terms for this review will encompass a range of keywords related to mental health, refugees and asylum seekers, and assessment tools. We will use a single search string that combines all relevant terms, resulting in a unified strategy rather than multiple search lines with individual search numbers. Complete, database-specific strings are reported in online supplemental material, following the PRISMA-S extension for reporting literature searches.40

Data management

References retrieved through the search process will be imported into Zotero41 and Rayyan,42 where duplicates will be removed.

Study selection

This systematic review will evaluate the key characteristics of the studies, including participants, instruments and types of studies. Two pairs of reviewers will conduct independent screening of the titles and abstracts to assess eligibility according to the inclusion criteria of the review. Initially, all reviewers will screen a random sample of 10% of titles and abstracts to ensure consistency and inter-rater reliability. Discrepancies will be discussed and resolved following this calibration exercise. The remaining titles and abstracts will then be divided equally, with each reviewer screening half of the remaining records independently. Any reason for excluding full-text studies that do not meet these criteria will be documented and reported in the review. Any disagreements between the reviewers at any stage of the study selection process will be resolved through discussion or by consulting a third reviewer. Studies deemed relevant will undergo a full-text assessment by the same reviewers following the same process for resolving disagreements. The outcomes of the search, study selection and inclusion process will be detailed in the final systematic review and illustrated using the PRISMA flow diagram.

Data extraction

Data extraction will be primarily performed using artificial intelligence (AI) large language models (LLM). This approach will be piloted on a 10% random sample of the included studies. Specifically, such a sample will be processed by AI LLM and two human reviewers using a standardised form. Results will be compared and, after checking discrepancies and verifying data quality, the extraction process will continue either with two human reviewers or with one human reviewer supported by the AI LLM, with human reviewers supervising the process throughout. The extracted information will include study design, sample size, participant characteristics, mental health measurement tools and reported psychometric properties. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or with a third reviewer. All decisions (ie, reasons for exclusion, discrepancies and any changes to the procedure) will be fully documented. When multiple publications refer to the same study, the most recent source with the largest sample size will be prioritised. Model information, prompts, settings (eg, temperature, top-K and top-P), and code will be made publicly available.

Assessment of methodological quality

The AI-supported approach adopted for data extraction will be used also for the assessment of methodological quality. For studies reporting measurement properties, the COSMIN risk-of-bias checklist will be applied to every property: internal consistency, reliability, structural validity, cross-cultural validity, criterion validity and responsiveness. Adequate thresholds are reported in table 1. For studies reporting diagnostic accuracy metrics, QUADAS-2 will be used. Following COSMIN methodology, the lowest score within a property domain will determine the overall rating (‘worst score counts’). Evidence for each property will then be summarised and its certainty graded (high, moderate, low and very low) using the COSMIN-GRADE framework, considering study quality, consistency, precision and indirectness.

Data synthesis and integration

Results will be tabulated and narratively synthesised by tool and property. Where ≥3 homogeneous studies report the same statistic, meta-analysis will be conducted using random effects models: Fisher Z-transformed α/intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for reliability; bivariate or hierarchical summary receiver operating characteristic (HSROC) models for sensitivity/specificity. Inconsistency (I²) and potential moderators (age group, language, setting and conflict intensity) will be explored with subgroup analyses and meta-regression.

Publication bias will be assessed via funnel plots and Egger’s test when sufficient studies are available. The overall strength and certainty of the body of evidence for each outcome will be assessed using the COSMIN-GRADE approach, considering risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.

Our primary goal is to publish a single systematic review encompassing all mental disorders assessed by the mental health assessment tools. However, if a substantial number of studies are identified for individual mental disorders, we will evaluate whether dividing the work into separate reports would better ensure the necessary rigour for each topic, as well as readability and clarity.

Ethics and dissemination

This research is exempt from ethics approval given that this is a protocol for a systematic review, which uses published data. The findings from this review will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and scientific conferences.

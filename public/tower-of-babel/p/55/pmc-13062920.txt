Assessment of the feed additive consisting of coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous for all animal species for the renewal of its authorisation (DOX‐AL ITALIA SPA)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Gabriele Aquilina, Jürgen Gropp, Jaume Galobart, Orsolya Holczknecht, Alberto Navarro‐Villa, Fabiola Pizzo, Jordi Tarrés‐Call, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Eleni Gkimprixi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous as nutritional feed additive for all animal species. The additive is already authorised for use with all animal species. The applicant has provided evidence that the additive under assessment complies with the conditions of the authorisation. In addition, a modification in the manufacturing process (i.e. addition of 0.2% sepiolite) is proposed. The Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP Panel) confirms that the calcium iodate component of the additive remains safe for the target species under the approved conditions. However, due to the absence of adequate data on some of the coating agents used in the manufacture of the additive, the Panel cannot conclude on the safety of coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous for the target species and the environment. Due to the lack of adequate iodine deposition data and information on the safety of some of the coating agents listed by the applicant, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety for the consumer. With regard to the safety for the users, the additive is not considered irritant to skin or eyes; however, it should be considered as a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure of users by any route is considered a risk and should be minimised. The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive is an efficacious source of iodine in feed for all animal species.Keywords: coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous, compounds of trace elements, nutritional additives, safety

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 13(3) of that Regulation lays down that if the holder of an authorisation proposes changing the terms of the authorisation by submitting an application to the commission, accompanied by the relevant data supporting the request for the change, the authority shall transmit its opinion on the proposal to the commission and the Member States. In addition, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the commission at the latest 1 year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from DOX‐AL ITALIA SPA 2 for the modification of the current authorisation and the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of calcium iodate anhydrous (coated granulated preparation) when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: nutritional additive; functional group: compounds of trace elements).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 13(3) (modification of the authorisation of a feed additive) and under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 08 July 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00450. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 05 February 2025.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of calcium iodate anhydrous (coated granulated preparation), when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3).

Additional information

The additive coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous (3b203) is currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species (EC 2015/861). 3 EFSA issued an opinion on the safety and efficacy of this product when used in feed for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 4 in support of the authorisation request for the use of calcium iodate anhydrous (coated granulated preparation) as a feed additive.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 10 February 2025 to 10 May 2025; the comments received were considered for the assessment.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 28 February to 21 March 2025 for which no comments were received.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of calcium iodate anhydrous in animal feed are valid and applicable for the current application. 7

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of calcium iodate anhydrous (coated granulated preparation) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 8 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

The additive coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous (3b203) is currently authorised as a nutritional additive (functional group: compounds of trace elements) for use in feed for all animal species. The present application concerns the renewal of its authorisation and the modification of its composition to include an anti‐caking agent.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the additive

Coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous is currently authorised as a preparation of calcium iodate anhydrous with an iodine content of 1%–10%. The regulation also specifies that the additive contains less than 5% coating agents and dispersants (choice of polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432), glycerol polyethyleneglycol ricinoleate (E484), polyethyleneglycol 300, sorbitol (E420ii) and maltodextrin) and feed materials (calcium magnesium carbonate, calcium carbonate, corn cobs) as granulating agents. Additionally, the additive should contain less than 1.5% of particles smaller than 50 μm.

The applicant requested a small modification in the manufacturing process to include up to 0.2% of sepiolite (1g562) as a binder/anticaking agent to reduce the variability of the particle size between batches.

Although the authorisation foresees the possibility of different preparations with an iodine content ranging from 1% to 10%, the applicant provided data only on a preparation containing 10% iodine. The applicant provided data in order to investigate the presence of small/nanoparticles in the additive. 12 Based on the qualitative scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis provided, no firm conclusion can be drawn on the level of small/nanoparticles present in the formulated material due to the presence of aggregates. Furthermore, energy‐dispersive X‐ray spectroscopy data were not provided to indicate the chemical nature of the particles. The applicant provided data on the solubility of calcium iodate anhydrous showing that it is slightly soluble in water (2 g/L at 15°C and 6.7 g/L at 90°C); 13 no data on dissolution rate following the Guidance on technical requirements for regulated food and feed product applications to establish the presence of small particles including nanoparticles (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021) was made available.

The homogenous distribution of the additive in feed was previously demonstrated (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013). It is not expected that the proposed change in the composition will have an effect on the homogeneous distribution of the additive in feed.

Characterisation of the active substance

Calcium iodate anhydrous, used in the additive as a source of iodine, complies with the purity conditions set in the current authorisation of 3b202 (i.e. minimum content of 63.5% iodine).

The identity of the active substance was confirmed by determining the iodine and calcium content of three additive batches using titrimetry‐FCC monograph and inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectrometry (EN 15510:2007). 14

The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species up to a maximum (total) concentration (mg iodine/kg complete feed) of four for equines, five for ruminants for milk production and laying hens, 20 for fish and 10 for other species or categories of animals. Safety

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013), the Panel concluded that calcium iodate anhydrous as a source of iodine is safe for the target species when it is used up to the maximum authorised content of total iodine in complete feed, except for horses, dogs and cats, for which maximum tolerated levels are 3 and 4 mg I/kg complete feed and a provisional tolerated level of 5 mg I/kg complete feed, respectively. Regarding consumer safety, the Panel noted that use of the maximum authorised levels of total iodine in complete feed results in exceedance of the upper levels (ULs) for high‐consuming adults and toddlers by a factor of 2 and 4, respectively. The risk would derive from the consumption of milk and, to some extent, from the consumption of eggs. To minimise the risk, the Panel recommended to reduce the maximum iodine concentrations in feed for dairy cows and laying hens to 2 and 3 mg I/kg feed, respectively. However, it was noted that also in this case, the ULs for toddlers will still be exceeded (1.6‐fold). About user safety, the formulated additive was considered potentially hazardous by inhalation. Additionally, the preparation was not considered an irritant to skin and eyes but may be a dermal sensitiser. It was also noted that coating may prevent the contact of the active substance with skin and eyes. Finally, use of calcium iodate in animal nutrition was considered safe for the environment.

The applicant declared that no incidents or safety issues for target animals, consumers, users and/or the environment have been documented or reported regarding the additive since its previous authorisation. 16

For the present assessment, the applicant conducted two extensive literature searches (ELS).

Safety of the carriers and coating agents

The additive coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous is manufactured by fixing the active agent using coating agents (polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432), glycerol polyethyleneglycol ricinoleate (E484), polyethyleneglycol 300, sorbitol (E420ii), maltodextrin), an anticaking agent (sepiolite, 1g562) and granulating agents (calcium magnesium carbonate, calcium carbonate, corn cobs).

The Panel notes that the carriers, sorbitol and maltodextrin are feed materials; that sepiolite is currently authorised as a feed additive for use in feed of all animal species at levels up to 20,000 mg/kg complete feed, several orders of magnitude higher than its inclusion level in the current additive; and that the coating agent glycerol polyethyleneglycol ricinoleate (E484) is an authorised feed additive, subject to re‐evaluation.

The Panel notes that the other coating agents (polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432), polyethyleneglycol 300) are not authorised feed additives and that, in the previous opinion, the assessment of the coating agents was based on the specific purity criteria of the compounds used, which were either based on their specifications as food additives or according to the European Pharmacopeia (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013).

The applicant mentioned evaluations from EFSA and other assessment bodies in support of the safety of the polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432) and polyethyleneglycol 300. The Panel notes that, in these assessments, the lowest no observed adverse effect levels (NOAELs) for polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432) (500 mg/kg bw per day, the highest dose tested in long‐term toxicity and carcinogenicity studies in rats – EFSA ANS Panel, 2015) and polyethyleneglycol 300 (NOAEL as 1000 mg/kg bw per day from 2‐year studies – JECFA, 1980) were identified.

Regarding genotoxicity, the available information from the above‐mentioned evaluations was not considered adequate to conclude on the lack of genotoxicity concern for the two compounds since the current requirements for the evaluation of genotoxicity (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017) are not fulfilled.

Extensive literature search

The applicant performed two literature searches to provide evidence that, in the light of the current knowledge, the additive remains safe for the target species, consumers, users and the environment under the approved conditions. The first ELS was not considered as several shortcomings were identified (e.g. only a single database was used; the expertise and number of the reviewers involved in the selection process were not mentioned; the final number of papers reviewed and excluded, including the reasons for exclusion, were not reported; no summary tables were provided). The second ELS was conducted on Scopus, Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar covering the period 2014–2025. Additionally, the applicant conducted a search on several authoritative body websites 17 to retrieve relevant scientific opinions. The search strategy as well as the inclusion and exclusion criteria applied were described. The search resulted in a total of 82 hits considered relevant for the safety by the applicant. These studies were screened by the FEEDAP Panel and those considered relevant for the safety for the current assessment are described in the corresponding chapters below.

Safety for the target species

The ELS provided by the applicant identified 25 publications relevant for the safety for the target species. These papers were reviewed by the FEEDAP Panel, and one paper was considered as relevant for the assessment of the safety for the target animals.

Dušová et al. (2014) found that lactating ewes given calcium iodate at 5 mg I/kg dry matter (DM) showed higher blood urea and alkaline phosphatase, hinting at possible renal stress or altered thyroid function. However, considering the limitations of the study such as lack of negative control, small sample size, limited endpoints measured and lack of diet analysis, the Panel does not consider this as strong evidence for adverse effects in the target species.

Therefore, and considering that the manufacturing process and composition of the additive have not been significantly changed, the Panel considers that there are no reasons to revise its previous conclusions with regard to the calcium iodate component of the additive, which is considered to remain safe for the target species under the approved conditions.

However, considering that the safety of some of the coating agents has not been established (see Section 3.2.1), the Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the additive coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous for the target species.

Safety for the consumer

The toxicological profile of iodine and the consumer exposure assessment were evaluated in the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013).

A tolerable upper intake level (UL) of 600 μg/day has been established for adults (including pregnant and lactating women) and ranges between 200 and 500 μg/day for other population groups (SCF, 2002).

In the previous consumer exposure assessment, the iodine deposition values used were not based on specific studies on the deposition of iodine, but on studies published in the literature (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013). In that opinion, the FEEDAP Panel considered that the iodine content in products from animals receiving the authorised maximum contents of total iodine in complete feed for dairy cows and laying hens would represent a substantial risk to consumers, mainly for high‐consuming (95th percentile) adults and toddlers. The ULs exceeded for adults by a factor of 2 (1230 vs. 600 μg I/day) and for toddlers by a factor of 4 (840 vs. 200 μg I/day). The Panel considered that the exposure of adult consumers to iodine from foods of animal origin would be below the UL (480 vs. 600 μg I/day) if the maximum iodine concentrations in feed for dairy cows and laying hens were reduced to 2 and 3 mg I/kg feed, respectively. However, iodine intake would remain above the UL (1.6‐fold) for high‐consuming toddlers (320 vs. 200 μg I/day). Based on that, the FEEDAP Panel recommended to modify some of the authorised maximum iodine contents in complete feed (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013). Although the currently authorisation did not reduce the maximum contents of iodine in feed, a provision was added under ‘other provisions’ to recommend lower maximum levels for several species (see Section 3.1.3).

More recently, an updated consumer exposure assessment to iodine was performed by the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2025). The FEEDAP Panel estimated consumer exposure using the deposition data available from the previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013), in the absence of new reliable deposition data and despite the limitations already identified in the previous data set. The results of this calculation, based on the limited data available, indicated that the exposure of consumers exceeds the UL in all age classes highlighting a concern for consumer safety, in line with the previous conclusions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013). The UL would also be exceeded when the consumer exposure is calculated based on the maximum recommended levels of iodine in feed. The Panel notes that these calculations did not consider exposure via food products from pigs, fish or poultry (other than eggs). It is therefore likely that when the additional exposure via food products from all animal species is considered, the UL would be further exceeded.

For the current assessment, the applicant performed a literature search (Section 3.2.2) and identified 44 publications of potential interest for the safety of the consumer. These papers were screened by the FEEDAP Panel, and it was considered that none of them provided new information relative to the safety of the consumer except for one paper that reported iodine deposition in milk goat (Azorín et al., 2025). In this study, a total of 140 multiparous Murciano–Granadina goats in mid‐lactation were randomly assigned to two experimental groups (n = 70 per group): (i) control diet contained supplemented 0.23 mg inorganic selenium (sodium selenite) and 0.57 mg iodine (from KI)/kg of DM, (ii) experimental diet containing an additional 0.23 mg organic selenium (from a selenised yeast) and a total of 5.68 mg of iodine per kg of DM. The study lasted 64 days. The FEEDAP Panel notes that the deposition data recorded in this study are higher than those in milk used in the previous assessment (980 mg I/kg vs. 760 and 280 μg/kg) (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013). However, this paper has limitations which would prevent its use in the exposure assessment: (i) only a single iodine level was tested; (ii) although unlikely, interactions between selenium sources and iodine cannot be theoretically ruled out. The Panel also notes that consumption of goat milk is much lower than that of cow milk, and therefore, considers it more relevant to use data on iodine contents in cow's milk.

Considering the above, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that additional data on iodine deposition following the current requirements are needed to allow a proper assessment of consumer exposure and a conclusion on consumer safety. Therefore, due to the lack of adequate data in iodine deposition and the lack of information on the safety of some of the coating agents (i.e. polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432) and polyethyleneglycol 300), the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of additive coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous for the consumer, under the approved conditions of use.

Safety for the user

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive poses a risk by inhalation; it is not irritant to the skin and eyes but, due to the lack of data, was considered a skin sensitiser. The FEEDAP Panel further considered that coating may prevent the contact of the active substance to skin and eyes.

No additional studies were submitted by the applicant for the current assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel notes that the additive contains nickel (see Section 3.1.1). The Directive (EU) 2022/43137 set an occupational exposure limit (OEL) of 0.01 and 0.05 mg/m3 for both respirable and inhalable fraction, respectively, as nickel meets the criteria for classification as carcinogenic (category 1A). Due to the presence of nickel, the additive should be considered as a skin and respiratory sensitiser.

Based on the information provided, the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the lack of genotoxicity potential of some of the coating agents used in the additive (Section 3.2.1).

Conclusions on safety for the user

The additive is not a skin and eye irritant; however, it should be considered as a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure by any route is considered a risk and should be minimised.

Safety for the environment

In the previous assessment, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that calcium iodate is not expected to pose a risk to the environment. From the extensive literature search, a total of 11 scientific papers were considered relevant by the applicant for the assessment. 18

The applicant did not perform a quantitative assessment (e.g. comparison of the predicted environmental concentrations with the natural background concentrations) using the data from the scientific literature, in line with the requirements of the FEEDAP guidance on the safety of the feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019). In the absence of such quantitative data, the Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the additive for the different environmental compartments.

From the five possible coating agents, three are either feed materials and/or are authorised feed additives and the remaining two need an environmental risk assessment (polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (E432) and polyethyleneglycol 300). The information provided in support of the safety of these two coating agents for the environment is not considered adequate. In the absence of information in line with the requirements of the above‐mentioned guidance, the Panel is not in the position to conclude on their safety in the context of the present assessment.

Overall, in the absence of a quantitative assessment for iodine and adequate data on some of the coating agents, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the additive coated granulated calcium iodate for the environment.

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additive. Therefore, there is no need to reassess the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 19 and good manufacturing practice.

CONCLUSIONS

The applicant provided evidence that the additive currently in the market complies with the existing conditions of the authorisation. In addition, the applicant proposed a small modification in the manufacturing process of the additive (i.e. addition of 0.2% sepiolite) which does not have an impact on its composition.

The Panel concludes that, considering only the calcium iodate component, the additive remains safe for the target species. However, in the absence of adequate data on some of the coating agents used in the manufacture of the additive, the Panel cannot conclude on the safety of coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous for the target species.

The FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the additive coated granulated calcium iodate anhydrous for the consumer, under the approved conditions of use due to the lack of data on iodine deposition and the lack of information on the safety of some of the coating agents.

Regarding user safety, the additive is not a skin and eye irritant; however, it should be considered as a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure by any route is considered a risk and should be minimised.

In the absence of a quantitative assessment for iodine and adequate data on some of the coating agents, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of the additive coated granulated calcium iodate for the environment.

There is no need to reassess the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

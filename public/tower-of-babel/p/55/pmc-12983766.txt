Research on latent class of multisymptom characteristics and transition mechanism of patients with coronary heart disease after PCI: protocol for a multicentre cross-sectional and prospective longitudinal study
Authors: Jia-Chun You, Qingxia Li, Rong Chen, Wei-Kun Zhang, Yu-Qian Liu, Wen Zeng, Yanjin Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPost-percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) patients frequently experience multidimensional symptoms (somatic, psychological, social), yet research remains fragmented. Current evidence lacks integration of dynamic symptom interactions and longitudinal evolution patterns. This study addresses critical gaps by characterising latent symptom classes and transition mechanisms during recovery, accounting for sociodemographic environmental influences across three Chinese provinces.Methods and analysisA multicentre study combines cross-sectional baseline assessment (T0) with prospective longitudinal follow-ups at 1 month (T1) and 6 months (T2) post-PCI. We will recruit 600 patients presenting with acute coronary syndrome and undergoing their first PCI from six Class A Tertiary Hospitals across Hunan, Jiangsu and Hainan provinces (July 2025–January 2026). Symptom phenotyping will use validated instruments: Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System-29, Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia and Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale. Latent profile analysis will identify baseline symptom profiles; latent transition analysis will model probabilities of transitioning between classes over time. Model selection will optimise fit indices (Akaike information criterion/Bayesian information criterion/bootstrap likelihood ratio test bootstrap) and clinical interpretability. Covariate-adjusted analyses will explore sociodemographic and environmental effects on symptom trajectories.Ethics and disseminationApproved by the University of South China Ethics Committee (2024NHHL037). Written informed consent precedes participation, with data deidentified and securely stored. Findings will be published in peer-reviewed journals following Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines and presented at international cardiovascular conferences.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500097028.

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD), a common ischaemic heart disease, arises from atherosclerotic narrowing of coronary arteries and/or functional alterations that impair myocardial perfusion, ultimately leading to ischaemia or necrosis.1 Cardiovascular diseases were in the top five leading sources of global DALYs in 190 countries and territories in 2021.2 With an estimated global prevalence of 197 million cases and 9.14 million deaths,1 CAD remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is one of the most effective methods in the treatment of CAD, effectively restoring coronary blood flow through minimally invasive stent deployment.3 While PCI demonstrates high procedural success rates with rapid postoperative recovery, its long-term efficacy depends on addressing multifaceted postprocedural challenges.

Post-PCI symptom persistence remains a critical clinical challenge. Persistence or recurrence of angina after PCI is well-recognised, and the incidence of postoperative chest pain in patients undergoing interventional therapy is as high as 50%.4 In addition, many patients avoid physical exercise after surgery for fear of heart disease recurrence and are prone to impaired exercise tolerance and unsatisfactory recovery of physical activity.5 Chronic exercise fear can lead to decreased aerobic exercise capacity and muscle strength and increase the risk of cardiac events and mortality.6 7 Exercise fear can also reduce the compliance of cardiac rehabilitation, thus affecting the rehabilitation effect of patients. Besides, patients after PCI need obligatory dual antiplatelet therapy, which introduces gastrointestinal risks, so patients are also at risk of gastrointestinal discomfort after PCI.8

Social dysfunction in post-PCI patient manifests as avoidance of occupational and social engagement, primarily driven by disease-related uncertainty and fear of recurrence. This behavioural withdrawal frequently precipitates diminished social functioning and reduced capacity for community participation. Over time, such impairment reinforces negative self-perception and increases social isolation, creating a bidirectional relationship with disease progression.9 10 The resultant socioeconomic consequences extend beyond individual health outcomes, imposing substantial financial burdens on both familial units and healthcare systems through lost productivity and increased caregiving demands.8 11

Psychological distress of PCI frequently manifested as anxiety and depression stems from multifactorial stressors including residual angina,12 stent-related procedural anxiety,13 financial burdens of long-term pharmacotherapy and concerns regarding recurrent cardiovascular events. Depressive and anxiety disorders influence CAD progression through multifaceted pathways. These emotional disturbances may dysregulate the autonomic nervous system, characterised by resting tachycardia, hypertension and reduced heart rate variability, increasing the risk of hypertension, diabetes and eventually atherosclerosis.14 Furthermore, up to 38% of PCI recipients develop postprocedural stress disorders,15 characterised by hypervigilance, attentional deficits and emotional lability, which can lead to reduced treatment compliance and increased risk of readmission.

It should be noted that the postoperative experience of patients who underwent PCI treatment due to acute life-threatening acute coronary syndrome (ACS) events differs from that of patients with stable CAD conditions. ACS is not merely a vascular crisis, but also a source of psychological stress, which substantially elevates the risk of persistent angina pectoris, fear of exercise, depression and social withdrawal.16 17 This symptom burden and its dynamic evolution during the recovery process have not been fully characterised, yet they are crucial determinants of functional recovery for this vulnerable patient population.

Current research on post-PCI symptomology remains predominantly anchored in cross-sectional designs,18 19 which provide fragmentary assessments of isolated symptoms while failing to capture the dynamic interplay and temporal evolution of co-occurring symptoms across somatic, social and psychological dimensions. Crucially, while prevalence estimates for specific symptoms at discrete time points post-PCI exist in the literature,5 20 these findings are often inconsistent and lack integration. More importantly, the complex, potentially non-linear relationships and transition patterns between these multidimensional symptom experiences over the critical recovery period remain poorly understood. The symptom experience time (SET) framework21 explicitly posits that both symptom experience and management are inherently dynamic processes, underscoring the critical need for longitudinal methodologies to elucidate symptom trajectories.

Furthermore, environmental factors, such as climate and temperature variations across geographic regions, are increasingly recognised as potential modifiers of cardiovascular recovery and symptom burden,22 23 yet their specific influence on post-PCI multisymptom profiles is rarely examined within multicentre designs.

To address these significant gaps, this prospective multicentre cohort study, guided by SET theory, leverages latent profile analysis (LPA) to identify distinct, empirically derived multisymptom profiles at baseline and follow-up time points. Subsequently, latent transition analysis (LTA) is employed to model the probabilities of patients transitioning between these symptom profile classes over 6-month follow-up intervals, thereby revealing the longitudinal patterns of symptom trajectory evolution. This approach not only captures symptom co-occurrence but also quantifies their stability and change over time. Additionally, our multicentre design encompassing diverse climatic regions allows for exploratory analyses examining potential geographic and environmental influences on symptom class membership and transition patterns.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study will apply an observational design incorporating both multicentre cross-sectional and prospective longitudinal components. The cross-sectional phase will characterise post-PCI symptom phenotypes through multidimensional assessment of symptom experience domains and delineate heterogeneous symptom clusters. As for the longitudinal phase, postoperative serial evaluations at the 1-month (T1) and 6-month (T2) time points will capture temporal symptom trajectory dynamics. This design specifically targets two critical recovery phases: the subacute phase (T1, resolving periprocedural symptoms) and the stable rehabilitation phase (T2, reflecting long-term adaptation), enabling stage-specific characterisation of symptom evolution patterns. Study setting and sample

This multicentre investigation will be implemented across six tertiary care facilities spanning several geographic regions: three university-affiliated hospitals in Hengyang, Hunan Province (University of South China Health System) in China. One Class A Tertiary Hospital in Shaoyang, Hunan Province. One provincial referral centre in Haikou, Hainan Province in China and one in Suzhou, Jiangsu province. All participating institutions use integrated electronic medical record (EMR) systems ensuring complete inpatient data capture. Consecutive patients admitted to these centres between July 2025 and January 2026 will undergo systematic screening against predefined inclusion/exclusion criteria during the baseline (T0) phase. Eligible participants will subsequently complete follow-up assessments at 1-month (T1) and 6-month (T2) postdischarge intervals through structured telephone interviews and clinic visits.

We will use LPA and LTA to explore the changes in symptom patterns in patients with CAD after stent implantation, which allows researchers to predict future symptoms. First, using LPA, we investigated whether LPA could identify the latent state T0–T2 after PCI in patients with ACS of CAD. LTA is a follow-up to latent class analysis, which identifies population subgroups from populations. The model produces potential profiles that reflect the prevalence of each profile or category and the expected value of each item given membership in a particular category. Based on Monte Carlo simulations for LTA with nine continuous indicators and three measurement waves, a minimum sample of 300 participants at baseline provides 80% power to detect medium transition effects (OR=2.5) at α=0.05.24 Considering the follow-up of the second phase of the study, the sample size experienced a 20% attrition rate, resulting in the final determined sample size of N=600.

Study population

Recruitment commenced in July 2025 using convenience sampling across six tertiary cardiology centres. Consecutively admitted patients with CAD post-PCI will undergo eligibility screening. Eligible participants will complete baseline assessments through paper-based questionnaires. Follow-up data will be collected via telephone/clinic visits.

Inclusion requires: (1) Adult patients hospitalised with a diagnosis of ACS or CAD, who have undergone successful, first-time PCI during the index hospitalisation. (2) Literacy for self-report completion. (3) Voluntary enrolment with written informed consent obtained after standardised written study explanations.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with other serious diseases, malignant tumours, end-stage patients, dialysis patients. (2) Patients who have received other interventional or open surgery in the past 6 months. (3) Intermittent mental anomaly. (4) Incompetent level of Chinese language skills.

Follow-up attrition criteria (T1/T2): (1) Death or loss of follow-up during the follow-up period. (2) Refusing follow-up or voluntarily withdrawing from the study. (3) Patients undergoing stenting or other surgical treatment again.

Follow-up and missing data

Longitudinal assessments will be conducted at 1-month (T1) and 6-month (T2) intervals post-baseline using a multimodal approach comprising telephone interviews, secure video consultations and optional clinic visits. These evaluations will systematically track the progression of physical and psychological symptoms to characterise dynamic symptom trajectories. During initial enrolment, participants were explicitly informed that providing dual contact channels, which includes an optional designated family member’s telephone number, was voluntary and aimed solely at enhancing follow-up retention. This information was collected only after obtaining written informed consent, with a clear statement in the consent form: Your decision to provide additional contact methods will not affect your access to clinical care or study participation. Participants retained the right to withdraw contact permissions at any follow-up point without prejudice. Researchers were notified and reminded via WeChat or telephone 1 week before T1 and T2 subsequent data collection. We will try to contact participants on different days or times if they cannot be reached by telephone. For datasets with missing values exceeding 5% at any phase, multiple imputation by chained equations will be implemented. Participant engagement will be sustained through time-limited modest compensation, distributed on full questionnaire completion.

Data collection

Before data collection, participants were informed in detail about the study objectives, protocol and written informed consent was obtained. Ethical safeguards were taken in accordance with institutional review board guidelines. Uniformly trained research staff implement all assessment tools using dual modalities: self-reported questionnaires completed onsite with immediate submission. Individuals who were unable to complete the questionnaire on their own dictated their responses to the primary family caregiver, who recorded their responses. The research team will design a general questionnaire to collect the personal and health characteristics of the study subjects. Questions included general information about the patient, including age, gender, place of residence, marital status, education, occupation, economic income, type of health insurance, smoking, drinking, height and weight. The patient’s disease information, including the course of coronary heart disease, family history of coronary heart disease, cardiac function classification, concomitant chronic diseases, postoperative time and the number of stents implanted, was also collected. We will also use the following scales to collect patient symptom information.

The Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System 29-Item Profile

The Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) was initiated by US National Institutes of Health. Reliability and validity have been proved in various studies.25 26 Our study used the PROMISE-29 because it is a comprehensive scale designed to measure quality of life. Physical functioning, anxiety, depression, fatigue, sleep disturbance, ability to participate in social activities and pain were measured. These domains in PROMIS-29 are derived from large banks of questions developed for fixed-length short forms and for computerised adaptive testing and have been shown to perform well in other diseases and populations.27 28 PROMIS is recommended for patients with cardiovascular disease for self-reporting.29 Archita30 further highlighted the advantages of using PROMIS-29 by demonstrating its superior efficiency and usability. All PROMIS domains were scored electronically on questionnaire completion, generating standardised T-scores with a mean of 50 and SD of 10 based on the PROMIS metric system. This T-score metric enables cross-domain comparison. The PROMIS-29 has been translated into Chinese by the PROMIS National Center-China. Scholars have evaluated the measurement characteristics of the Chinese version of PROMISE-29 in clinical patients, and all the indicators are good, which is suitable for Chinese patients.31

Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia Heart

Tampa Scale for Kinesiophobia Heart is a psychometric instrument designed to evaluate exercise-related anxiety in cardiac patients. Originally developed by Bäck et al,32 the scale was subsequently translated and culturally adapted into Chinese in 2019.33 This 15-item questionnaire comprises four distinct dimensions: fear of injury, perceived functional decline, exercise avoidance and cardiac risk perception. The instrument employs a 4-point Likert scale, with response options ranging from 1 to 4. Notably, items 4, 8 and 12 require reverse scoring. The total score ranges from 17 to 68 points, with scores exceeding 37 indicating clinically significant exercise fear. Higher scores correspond to greater levels of exercise-related anxiety.34 Psychometric evaluation of the Chinese version demonstrates satisfactory reliability and validity in CAD populations. The scale exhibits good internal consistency (Cronbach’s α=0.758) and content validity (0.86).35 These psychometric properties support its application in assessing exercise fear levels among Chinese patients with CAD.33 34

Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale

For gastrointestinal discomforts, the validated Chinese version of the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS) was used to identify patients with stomach discomforts.36 The Chinese version of GSRS contains 15 items, each item corresponding to a gastrointestinal symptom. These symptoms cover all aspects of the gastrointestinal tract and can comprehensively reflect the gastrointestinal health status of patients. The scale used the Likert 4-point scoring method. According to the severity of symptoms, each item set up four answer options, then patients chose the most consistent answer. Finally, the scores of all items were summed to obtain a total score, with higher total scores indicating more severe gastrointestinal symptoms in the patient. The total score of GSRS ranged from 15 to 60. The Cronbach’s α coefficient of the Chinese version of GSRS was 0.896, and the average content validity index was 0.96, which had good reliability, validity and internal consistency.37

Study closure and participation suspended

Data collection will terminate on completion of the 6 month follow-up phase for all retained participants, excluding attrition cases, incomplete datasets or duplicate entries. Participants retain unconditional withdrawal rights throughout the study duration, exercisable without penalty or compromise to subsequent clinical care and research participation rights.

Data management

Research materials will be processed through SPSS Statistics V.22 with dual-entry verification protocols. Analytical procedures will commence only after completing thorough quality checks confirming dataset completeness and validity. To mitigate interpretation bias, independent analysts blinded to participant allocation will execute classification algorithms and visualisation routines for latent class/transition modelling. Confidentiality compliance will be enforced through role-based access permissions within the digital platform.

Oversight responsibilities will be stratified across governance tiers: (1) The University of South China Steering Committee conducting quarterly methodological audits. (2) Core research personnel performing weekly progress evaluations and operational troubleshooting through scheduled working sessions.

Data analysis plan

Data management and preliminary analyses will be conducted using IBM SPSS V.26.0 (IBM). Continuous variables will be summarised as mean±SD, while categorical variables will be expressed as frequency counts with percentages. PROMIS-29 scores will be converted to standardised T-scores following published scoring guidelines (www.healthmeasures.net). LPA and LTA will be implemented in Mplus V.8.3 to identify symptom subgroups and track longitudinal symptom trajectory changes post-PCI. The analytical workflow comprises four stages: (1) parameter estimation using maximum likelihood methods; (2) model selection through iterative class enumeration; (3) classification validation via entropy statistics and (4) trajectory interpretation based on transition probabilities.38

Starting with a one-class model, we will gradually increase class numbers until optimal fit is achieved, as determined by minimisation of information criteria of Akaike information criterion, Bayesian information criterion, sample-corrected Bayesian information criterion and significant likelihood ratio tests of likelihood ratio test (LoMendell-Rubin) and bootstrap likelihood ratio test bootstrap.39 Entropy values >0.8 will indicate adequate classification accuracy. For longitudinal trajectory modelling, covariate-adjusted LTA will be performed after confirming absence of common method bias through Harman’s single-factor test—principal component analysis revealing <40% variance explained by the first unrotated factor across all time points.

Final model selection will incorporate both statistical indices and clinical interpretability. If information criteria show monotonic improvement without clear inflection points, scree plots will guide optimal class determination. Multivariable logistic regression in Mplus will subsequently examine associations between sociodemographic predictors and symptom trajectory patterns.

Confidentiality

All research materials will be maintained in encrypted format within institutionally approved data management systems, adhering to a minimum 3-year retention period. Access to source documents and datasets will be restricted to principal investigators through role-based authentication protocols. Any external data sharing initiatives must obtain formal institutional authorisation, requiring documented verification of recipient qualifications and intended scientific use prior to information transfer.

Patient and public involvement

Patients were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical clearance was granted by the Ethics Committee of the University of South China (number 2024NHHL037) in January 2025. Prior to enrolment, all potential participants will receive structured briefings detailing research objectives, methodology and medical record usage parameters. Written informed consent will be obtained following confirmation of participants’ comprehension regarding voluntary involvement and data handling procedures. Deidentification measures will replace personal identifiers with unique alphanumeric codes throughout the data lifecycle. Study findings will undergo peer-reviewed publication in accordance with Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines and presentation at scientific congresses specialising in cardiovascular behavioural medicine.

Discussion

CAD has become a prevalent health burden among middle-aged and elderly populations in China, with its incidence escalating in parallel with lifestyle changes. While PCI significantly reduces acute mortality in CAD patients, postprocedural management remains challenging due to persistent risks of cardiovascular events and multidimensional symptom clusters. Our clinical observations and preliminary evidence indicate that PCI survivors frequently experience dynamic symptom patterns encompassing precordial discomfort, exercise intolerance, fatigue, sleep disturbances and psychological distress, which collectively impair functional recovery and quality of life.

These multifaceted symptoms may not only disrupt autonomic nervous system regulation but also exacerbate systemic inflammation, potentially contributing to vascular endothelial dysfunction—a pathophysiological cascade warranting further investigation.40 Importantly, current symptom management strategies often adopt a static perspective, overlooking the temporal evolution and interconnections among these manifestations.

This study, guided by the SET framework, focuses on exploring the multidimensional symptom evolution pattern. By employing LPA and LTA, we provide the first empirical map of how co-occurring psychosocial and somatic symptoms organise and transition in the post-PCI period. This work establishes a symptom-centric taxonomy and trajectory model. A critical next step is to integrate this symptom trajectory framework with longitudinal biomedical data. Future studies can directly test whether specific symptom trajectories predict, co-vary with or are independent of changes in objective cardiac function.

This study specifically enrolled patients following an ACS event. This focus strengthens internal validity for the post-ACS population but limits generalisability to those undergoing elective PCI for stable CAD, whose symptom patterns may differ. Nonetheless, it establishes a clear framework for future research to compare symptom trajectories between ACS and stable CAD patients.

This study’s exploration of latent symptom trajectories and their transition mechanisms holds dual significance for nursing practice. First, identifying distinct symptom phenotypes enables risk stratification, allowing clinicians to prioritise high-need subgroups. Second, elucidating transition patterns across rehabilitation phases may reveal critical intervention windows. Our findings aim to advance precision nursing in cardiac rehabilitation. This approach may facilitate the development of personalised rehabilitation protocols that dynamically adapt to patients’ evolving symptom profiles, ultimately bridging the gap between symptom recognition and targeted intervention delivery in post-PCI care.

Several methodological aspects of this study warrant consideration. First, the use of convenience sampling across tertiary hospitals, while pragmatic for recruitment, may limit the representativeness of the sample. Second, the reliance on self-reported measures, although utilising validated instruments, introduces the possibility of recall bias and common method variance. Third, the 6-month follow-up period, while capturing critical early recovery phases, may be insufficient to observe the stabilisation of long-term symptom trajectories or late-emerging transitions.

It is worth noting that this study is fundamentally exploratory in nature. Although we employed a priori power calculation for the LTA, the latent class solution is data-driven and must be interpreted with caution. Our findings must be confirmed in future cohort studies before they can inform clinical decision-making.

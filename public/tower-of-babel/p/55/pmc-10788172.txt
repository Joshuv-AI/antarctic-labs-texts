Estimating the spatial distribution of African swine fever outbreak in China by combining four regional-level spatial models
Authors: ZhenFei YAO, YuJia ZHAI, XiaoLong WANG, HaoNing WANG
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe outbreaks of African Swine Fever (ASF) in China are ongoing, and the inadequate management of the pig supply chain is criticized. In the past four years, a series of preventive and control measures have been supplied national wide, while the outbreaks have not been terminated. This suggests the existing animal disease management at the district level may not be appropriate to control ASF under the current situation of the ASF outbreak in China. It is urgent to further describe real distribution areas of ASF in China. In this study, we combined four regional-scale models to predict the risk distribution of ASF in mainland China and identify risk factors related to ASF outbreaks. The results showed that the four regional-scale models were more accurate in predicting the ASF outbreaks than the nationwide scale model. The four regional-scale models identified the potential risk factors associated with ASF outbreaks, such as population density, pig density, land cover, temperature, and elevation factors. Moreover, seven clusters with high potential risk of ASF outbreaks were identified. Then, based on the results, we proposed more suitable prevention and control plans for ASF, which can assist the implementation of transport management policies within and between risk clusters.Keywords: African swine fever, maximum entropy model, spatial distribution, prevention and control, regional-scale

African swine fever (ASF) is an acute, severe and highly infectious disease of domestic pigs and wild boars caused by African swine fever virus (ASFV), and it is notifiable to the World Organization of Animal Health (WOAH, founded as OIE) [36]. The only known biological vectors of ASFV are the soft ticks of the genus Ornithodoros [31]. It can be transmitted mechanically, such as by stable flies and leeches [26, 37]. It can also survive for months in blood, tissue, and cured meat [50]. There are currently several live attenuated viruses ASF vaccine candidates [20]. Only one commercial live attenuated vaccine is approved for use in Vietnam, but it has not been extensively tested and its safety is unknown [57].

ASF was first reported in Kenya, 1921 [10]. The disease is endemic in most countries in sub-Saharan Africa and Sardinia. Since the first case occurred in Georgia in 2007, it spread in Caucasus, Eastern Europe and the Baltic countries and is still endemic today [8]. On August 1st, 2018, the first ASF outbreak in China was reported [13]. China is the country with the highest pork production in the world [53]. In just nine months, ASF was reported in most regions of China, and has spread to several countries in Asia, including Cambodia, North Korea, Vietnam and Laos [13]. Long-distance transport of pigs has historically been very common in Asia and may be an important risk factor for the spread of ASF [31]. In China, more than 95% of ASF outbreak were reported on backyard farms and standard farms, only a few sporadic cases occured in wild boars [56]. The main factors contributing to the outbreak of ASF are considered as swill feeding and the mechanical dissemination of vehicles and personnel, while the latter is the major contributing factor for large farms [16]. The outbreaks were mainly located along the important economic zones and tended to increase southward in general [30]. In summary, the spread dynamics of ASFV is primarily driven by the socioeconomic factors within domestic pig cycle involving the pork food system, which implies that the mechanisms responsible for the spread are mainly anthropogenic [8]. In addition, climate also affects the distribution of ASF in China, and it may be more likely to occur in winter [12, 30]. Pig density and farm distribution play an important role in the outbreak of ASF in China [32].

According to the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China (MARA), the current ASF situation in China is characterized by sporadic outbreaks and long distance leaps over in space [35]. The connectedness of pork food systems and their links with pig farms play key roles in maintenance of the virus both within local, regional systems and for making long-distance jumps [21]. The inference that the widespread of ASFV in Asia implies environmental virus spillover from the pig supply chain system might be similar to the two nationwide epidemics of pests des petits ruminants and last two nationwide epidemics of equine influenza, both of which were associated with inadequate transport regulation [17, 46]. The movement of live pigs has been suggested as one of the critical paths in previous study [14]. Since 2019, China has implemented district prevention and control measures. Although MARA issued several policies and regulations for the prevention and control of ASF, including establishing a comprehensive biosecurity system, strengthening the supervision and regulation of live pigs and their products, and the training and education for breeders [34], the complexity and diversity of the transmission mechanism of ASF has greatly hindered the elimination of ASF in China [39] (Fig. 1).

We hypothesis that the general animal disease management by administrative district may not be appropriate to control the ASF under the current situation of the ASF outbreak in China, including insufficient inter-regional transportation blockage. In a previous study, regarding the prediction of ASF distribution, there are large scale and regional scale in China, but a little is known about the difference between the both [5, 12, 32]. Our research targeted mainland China and combined four regional scales to predict the risk distribution of ASF in China and identify risk factors related to ASF outbreaks, which provided an important basis for prevention and control decision-making.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Research area

Our research area is China mainland. We divided the entire China mainland into four parts northeast (I), northwest (II), Qinghai-Tibet (III), and southern region (IV), following three important geographical dividing lines, including the outer outline of the Tibetan Plateau (dark red line), the Qinling-Huaihe River Line (yellow line), and along the Daxinganling Mountains-Taihang Mountains (blue line) (Fig. 2) [6, 11, 27]. In these four regions, there are great differences in climate, altitude and population density. In addition, the distribution of farms is also different. China’s pig farming industry is mainly concentrated in the Northeast China, Central Plain, Yangtze River Basin, and the Guangdong and Guangxi provinces [52, 55]. In the northeast (I) and southern regions (IV), there are a large number of small and medium-sized farms [52]. In the Qinghai-Tibet region (III) with an average altitude of 3,000 m, there are a small number of pig farms distributed [1, 52]. Some herders there generally adopt free range style [40].

Research data

The ASF presence cases (n=217) cover the period from August, 2018 to February, 2022 and are collected from the website of WOAH (https://www.woah.org/) and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of China (http://www.moa.gov.cn/) (Fig. 3). For the environment variable we selected land cover data which contains 22 land cover types, climate (n=67), elevation, population density, and pig density (Table 1, Supplementary Fig. 1) [42, 43]. All spatial data were pre-processed and calculated by standard operations in ArcGIS 10.2 and projected in UTM-WGS-1984. Where necessary, we resampled to 30 arc-sec (ca. 1 km2 at ground level).

Preprocessing of spatial modelling data

The spatial autocorrelation was minimized by filtering all recorded ASF locations using the SDM Toolbox v1.1c in ArcGIS 10.2 [4, 12]. Filtering was performed by limiting the minimum distance between each pair of points, which is regarded as the most effective method for correcting sampling bias [17]. We used the setting of the natural break with a maximum distance of 40 km and a minimum of 5 km [12].

Collinearity among environmental variables was assessed using the principal component analysis (PCA) and variance inflation factors (VIFs) [3]. Multicollinearity may violate statistical assumptions and may alter model predictions [23]. Firstly, the PCA was carried out to select the major climate predictors from 67 variables using SPSS 22.0 [51]. We extracted the environmental values behind the distribution points in ArcGIS10.2, and used eigenvalues larger than 1.0 and the scree plot criterion or “broken stick” stopping rule for PCA in item level factoring [45]. Suppression of unnecessary loading and rotation of factor pattern of climatic variables was used to retain predictors for subsequent analysis in MaxEnt described later [12]. The least contributing predictors of the non-collinear variables were eliminated by stepwise selection [33]. After variable reduction in PCA, we used VIF (linear regression statistics) in SPSS 22.0 to assess multicollinearity among both the remaining continuous variables and the categorical variables to avoid over-fitting the model [23, 51]. A VIF value low 10 indicates that multicollinearity is acceptable [9].

ASF spatial distribution models for different geographical scales

The MaxEnt model is regarded as one of the best-performing specialty distribution modeling techniques for analyzing presence-only data [24]. We used MaxEnt to build two scale models for comparative analysis. The filtered ASF locations and variables were used as input data to construct the ASF model. The first model (model 1) is a spatial model based on China as a whole. The second model (model 2) is composed of the four regional-level spatial models (Fig. 2). Four regional-level spatial models were first constructed, and then the mosaicked tools in ArcGIS were used to merge the four spatial distribution maps to obtain the prediction map of the model 2. For model parameter settings, we divided the selected presence records into 75% training and 25% testing portions to build and validate the models based on 10 bootstrap replicates. The Jackknife test and the variable response curves were selected to identify the relative contribution of predictor variables to the model. The results are output in logistic format [12]. For the remaining parameters, we kept the MaxEnt default settings [51]. In addition, the quadratic, hinge, and product features algorithms were selected to the model for the Qinghai-Tibet region (III), because of the limitation of occurrence data [38]. The least contributing predictors of the noncollinear variables were eliminated by stepwise until each variables contributed more than 10% to the model in MaxEnt [12]. Reduction of the number of predictor variables is likely to improve model performance and increase model transferability [41]. The risk map consisted of the probability of ASF occurrence ranging from 0 to 1, indicating low to high potential, respectively [32]. For visualization, the Jenks natural break optimization method was used to divide the model risk maps into five risk categories: very high, high, medium, low, and very low to identify high-risk areas. In the high-risk area of ASF, artificial clustering was carried out according to geographically adjacent and concentrated contiguous provinces.

We use threshold dependent and threshold independent criteria to report the “validation” of our model. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) is a threshold independent criterion [38]. It was embedded in the MaxEnt and was used to assess the goodness-of-fit of the model. As a threshold dependent validation measure, confusion matrix-based measures were used, including the Kappa test and the true skill statistic (TSS) [2]. The Kappa and TSS normalize the overall accuracy by the accuracy that might have occurred by chance alone [15]. The thresholds of these two criteria are determined using the sensitivity-specificity equivalence approach [28]. The ratio of model’s ability to cover the ASF cases (RCC) is defined as: covered number of cases÷effective predicted landmass (km2) (very high + high + medium region), it is used to compare the prediction accuracy of the two models. For RCC comparison, we divide the national wide prediction maps of both model 1 and 2 into 3 pieces (a, b, c) from the west to the east naturally to simplify the statistical analysis and pairwise comparison.

RESULTS

Presence record filtering and predictor variables selection

After minimizing autocorrelations of ASF locations, a total of 175 ASF occurrence records is retained at a distance of at least 20 km in Model 1. In Model 2, a total of 43, 31, 10, and 83 ASF occurrence records were retained in the Northeast, Northwest, Qinghai-Tibet, and South regions after filtering at 5 km, 30 km, 30 km, and 15 km.

The PCA for climatic variables showed that the PCs together accounting for more than 95% of the total variance (Supplementary Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 2). After performing PCA and MaxEnt filtering, only human population (62.8%) and pig density (37.2%) were left for the construction of the model 1 (Table 2). No multicollinearity was detected with VIF values ranged from 1.0 to 1.8 <10. In model 2, after performing PCA and and MaxEnt filtering, human population (17%), pig density (31.8%), mean temperature in November (17.5%, Mean T Nov.), elevation (23.5%), and land cover (10.3%) were left in the Northeast region (VIF 1.06–1.45 <10); human population (73.6%), pig density (14.1%), and land cover (12.3%) were left in the Northwest region (VIF 1.18–1.79 <10); human population (45.3%), pig density (43.3%), and land cover (11.4%) were left in the Qinghai-Tibet region (VIF 1.0–1.28 <10); human population (40.5%), maximum temperature in December (14%, Max T Dec.), pig density (11%), and land cover (34.5%) were left in the southern region (VIF 1.02–1.27 <10). These variables have the best explanatory power (Table 2).

The cluster distribution of ASF in China

The risk map shows that the risk of ASF in China is mainly concentrated in the following seven regions (Fig. 6): (1) The three northeastern provinces and the Bohai Sea surrounding provinces; (2) Shaanxi, Gansu and Shanxi provinces, and most western and eastern parts of Hetao, Inner Mongolia; (3) The region around the western basin of Xinjiang; (4) Southeastern Tibet and the Hengduan Mountains region; (5) Sichuan, Chongqing, Yunnan and Guizhou provinces; (6) Hunan, Hubei, Anhui and Jiangsu provinces; (7) The coastal areas around Fujian, Guangdong, Hainan and Taiwan.

DISCUSSION

In this study, two predicted maps of the spatial distribution of ASF in China were constructed by using different spatial scales and selecting significant predicting variables from multiple covariates to predict the distribution of ASF in China [12]. Our results showed that the regional scale model (model 2) was more accurate in predicting ASF outbreaks than the whole scale model (model 1). In model 2, the accuracy of the southern region is lower than that of the other three regions, which we believe may be related to the large environmental heterogeneity and data quality problems in the southern region, leading to the problem that the model is not robust enough. This work can help China to identify key ASF prevention and control areas and to establish effective prevention and control strategies.

In both models, population density and pig density account for the significant contributions, which suggesting a significant association between ASF outbreaks in China and population density and pig density. For the epidemic of ASF in China, population density factors can be traced back to anthropogenic activities, such as slop feeding, mechanical spread by people and vehicles, slaughterhouses and pork trading markets, and transport of pigs and pig products, all of which are anthropogenic and main factors in the outbreak of ASF in China [30]. The response curves show that the risk of ASF increases instantaneously when the population density rises, it also reflects this. The response curve of pig density shows that the risk of ASF occurrence rises as pig density increases. In areas with high pig density, the transportation of pig herds, products, and feeds from one location to another is more frequent than in places with lower pig density. This pattern is dictated by the dynamics of the pig industry. Such frequent movements naturally heighten the risk of ASFV transmission in poorly managed systems [8, 18]. From the perspective of an individual farm, this increased density complicates farm management tasks such as facility disinfection and waste disposal, potentially leading to environmental issues in pig farms or breeding areas. For instance, the accumulation and inadequate disposal of waste can facilitate the spread of the virus [8, 19]. The density of pigs is closely related to the distribution of pig farms. Chinese pig farms are mainly concentrated in the central-eastern and southern regions [52]. The risk map shows that some provinces with developed farming industries, such as Liaoning, Hebei, Sichuan, Henan and Hunan provinces, are high-risk areas [52].

Climate factors which are mean temperature in November and maximum temperature in December, also play an important role in the region-scale models of the northeast and the southern. According to the response curve, the risk of ASF outbreak is highest in the northeast when the mean temperature is close to 0°C in November. This can be explained by the lower temperature may favor the survival of microorganism in the environment for more prolonged periods causing outbreaks of ASF [42]. The temperature response curve in the southern region shows a higher risk of ASF outbreak when the maximum temperature is above 20°C in December, which is different from the situation in the northern region. The promotion effect of high temperature on ASFV transmission in southern China is probably related to wet and rainy conditions in southern region [44]. In hot and humid conditions, the ASFV may easily spread in livestock farms, markets and other places [42]. While evidence suggests that warmer climate conditions favor the survival and proliferation of disease vectors (such as soft ticks and stable flies), thus heightening the risk of ASFV transmission [29, 54], we emphasize the need for extensive field investigations to discern the true epidemiological role of these vectors in the ASFV cycle in southern China. Furthermore, social epidemiological studies are crucial, especially given the heightened demand for pig products during cultural events like the Chinese Spring Festival [47]. This period sees a surge in social activities and, in the days leading up to and following the Spring Festival in southern China, daytime temperatures can reach around 30°C, potentially creating an environment conducive to the spread of vector-borne diseases.

In model 2, the response curves of land cover show that the risk of ASF is higher in land use type of cultivated terrestrial areas, managed lands and water bodies, while the risk of ASF is low in artificial surface areas such as urban. To some extent, this reflects the fact that distribution of pig farms is an important factor in the outbreak of ASF in China. Since 2013, Chinese government issued the Regulations on the Prevention and Control of Pollution from Large-scaled Livestock Breeding Farms, and many pig farms in China have gradually shifted their production from urban and environmentally sensitive constrained areas to areas with abundant resources and high environmental capacity [19, 55]. Agricultural land as the main absorption of pig manure, and water sources are particularly important for the production needs of large-scale pig farms [49]. These factors are usually given priority in the construction of pig farms. The elevation variable is also a critical predictive variable in the northeast region model, and its response curve shows that the risk of ASF outbreak is the highest in areas close to in areas lower than 200 m. The risk of ASF outbreaks is higher at the low altitude areas in the northeast region is probably associated to the local socio-cultural, productive and economic factors, particular to large number of farms (backyard pig farms, large and small farms), high road density, and potential illegal trade of pigs [16, 22].

Although in this study we predicted the distribution of ASF outbreaks in China based on reported ASF outbreaks, it is extremely challenging to assess the potential risk distribution and influencing factors for this outbreak among wild boars, given that only 6 wild boar outbreaks of ASF have been reported in China to date. Also, the density of wild boar in China is unknown due to the limited data available on the distribution of wild boar. However, some studies have shown that ASF may be silently transmitted among wild boars in Asia [5, 7]. Therefore, it is highlight to early reporting and monitoring of ASF in wild boar to enable the implementation of appropriate biosecurity measures.

In conclusion, our study implies that anthropogenic factors (i.e., population density) were the most important for the spread of ASF in China. According to epidemiological investigation, ASFV was transmitted in mainland China in three main ways: employees and vehicles with the virus which spreads over others; feeding pigs with contaminative swills; trades and movements of pigs themselves as well as pork products [16, 34]. The infection risk for employees and vehicles with the virus were reported to the highest [25]. Since 2019, China has issued a regional prevention and control plan. In the subsequent policy, the country was divided into five regions for disease prevention and control [33]. Although this work produced positive results in disease control, it still has not completely controlled the outbreaks of ASF in China. This indicates that the ASF prevention and monitoring system in China is still deficient. Risk map shows the distribution of the ASF risk clusters and national highways in China (Fig. 6), which can compensate for inadequate supervision of pig transportation and further shape the policy of ASF prevention scientifically.

China accounts for about 50% of the world’s pig production [11], and long-distance transport of pigs is inevitable given not uniformly distributed of China’s pig industry and the rapid circulation of pigs and their products. The pork demand of each province in China is uneven [48], the legal trade of live pigs is necessary and has become one of the critical spreading routes for ASF entering disease-free areas [18]. According to model prediction result, some preventive and control measures should be taken in the ASF risk area, further stopping the spread of the epidemic. We recommend that the current seven control areas be further subdivided into sub-units, with graded control measures based on the degree of risk, while internal health services such as targeted slaughter and surveillance and diagnosis should be strengthened. These red areas in Fig. 6 will need to raise awareness and shape strengthened policies for ASF prevention. In addition, monitoring transport routes between these seven regions may be effective in preventing the spread and colonization of the virus. The seven regions should also work together to optimize the route layout, realize point-to-point transportation of pigs and pork products, and use big data to traceability management of pork supply information to ensure that health control measures are implemented to the greatest extent. It is expected that viral loads in the surrounding environment and supply chain will be lowering if these measures are properly implemented, it will be a crucial step in eliminating ASFV in China.

Conclusion

This study shows that population density, pig density, landcover, elevation and temperature (mean temperature in November and maximum temperature in December) are important influencing factors with the ASF outbreak in China. These factors need to be taken seriously by China. This result implies that China needs to further strengthen the human activity and pig farm management with considering the characteristics in each region. Based on the spatial distribution characteristics of ASF, we propose seven ASF prevention and control areas covering transport routes. It is possible to prevent the spread of ASFV by improving the biosecurity system in the high-risk regions and monitoring transport routes between these seven regions.

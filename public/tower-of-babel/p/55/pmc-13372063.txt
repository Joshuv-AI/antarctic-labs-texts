YAP Regulates the Nrf2 Signaling Axis to Attenuate Oxidative Stress and Neuroinflammation in Retinal Ganglion Cell Degeneration
Authors: Shirui Zhou, Fumin Yang, Ke Ding, Rui Chen, Mingchang Zhang, Wei Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeOxidative stress is a key driver of retinal ganglion cell (RGC) degeneration after optic nerve injury. Yes-associated protein (YAP), a Hippo pathway effector, is known to reprogram stress responses, yet its role in regulating oxidative stress during RGC degeneration is unclear.MethodsThis study investigated the role of YAP in RGC injury using an in vivo optic nerve crush (ONC) model and an in vitro oxidative-stress model with primary RGCs. YAP expression was modulated pharmacologically and genetically. We assessed its effects on nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) signaling–related outcomes; on oxidative stress markers, including superoxide dismutase-1/2 (SOD-1/2), NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (Nqo-1), and reactive oxygen species (ROS); and on neuroinflammation (microglial and astrocytic activation) via quantitative reverse-transcription PCR and immunofluorescence.ResultsYAP activation demonstrated robust neuroprotection in both the in vivo ONC model and in vitro oxidative-stress paradigms, significantly enhancing RGC survival, whereas YAP suppression exacerbated RGC degeneration. Mechanistically, YAP activation was associated with elevated Nrf2 signaling activity, as indicated by upregulation of antioxidant effectors (Nqo-1, SOD-2) and reduced intracellular ROS. YAP activation attenuated neuroinflammation, characterized by decreased microglial reactivity and astrocytic activation, whereas inhibition of YAP reversed these effects.ConclusionsThis study identified YAP as a neuroprotective regulator in both in vivo ONC and primary RGC models. YAP activation attenuated oxidative stress and neuroinflammation, which correlated with the activity of Nrf2-mediated antioxidant pathways, highlighting the potential relevance of YAP and Nrf2 interaction for therapeutic targeting in RGC injury.

Optic nerve injury is a major cause of irreversible vision loss in traumatic optic neuropathy and glaucoma, characterized by progressive degeneration of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and permanent disruption of visual signal transmission.1–3 Following axonal damage, RGCs experience metabolic stress, ischemia, and neuroinflammatory insults that collectively promote neuronal dysfunction and cell death.4,5 Central to this degenerative cascade is oxidative stress, when excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulate in the injured retina and damage cellular macromolecules, accelerating RGC degeneration.6,7 Although endogenous antioxidant programs can decrease optic nerve damage, the specific molecular regulators linking oxidative stress to RGC degeneration remain poorly defined.8,9

Neuronal susceptibility to oxidative and inflammatory stress depends on various factors, such as the engagement of intrinsic stress-responsive signaling pathways.10,11 Yes-associated protein (YAP), a key effector of the Hippo pathway, regulates cell survival, redox homeostasis, and tissue repair in multiple organs.12,13 In addition to its roles in growth and stress sensing, YAP has been implicated in the regulation of antioxidant defenses, mitochondrial function, and inflammatory signaling.14–16 Within the central nervous system, YAP contributes to neural stem cell maintenance, glial activation, and neuronal viability; however, its role in retinal responses to optic nerve injury remains unclear.17,18

The transcription factor nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) is a central regulator of cellular redox homeostasis in the retina, controlling gene networks and downstream detoxifying enzymes involved in detoxification and cytoprotection.8,19 Although Nrf2 activity is classically regulated by Kelch-like ECH associating protein 1 (Keap1)-dependent degradation and redox-sensitive nuclear translocation, emerging evidence suggests that upstream signaling pathways can further modulate Nrf2 expression and activity.20,21 Notably, YAP has been reported to influence Nrf2-related antioxidant programs in extraocular tissues, raising the possibility that YAP may contribute to retinal antioxidant regulation following injury, potentially through mechanisms involving Nrf2.22–24

In this study, we hypothesized that YAP plays a functional role in retinal stress adaptation after optic nerve injury. Using an in vivo optic nerve crush (ONC) models combined with genetic and pharmacological modulation of YAP and complementary in vitro stress paradigms, we investigated how YAP activity is regulated in the injured retina and whether its modulation influences oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and neuronal survival. These studies aimed to define a previously unrecognized role for YAP in retinal injury, suggesting that it may represent a viable therapeutic target for the treatment of optic neuropathies.

Methods

Animals

Male C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old; Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology, Beijing, China) were housed in a specific pathogen-free facility with ad libitum access to food and water under a 12-hour light/dark cycle. All procedures were approved by the Laboratory Animal Ethics Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (IACUC No. 4274), and conformed to the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research.

Optic Nerve Crush

Unilateral ONC was performed on the left eye. Mice were anesthetized with intraperitoneal 1% sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg) and topical 0.5% oxybuprocaine hydrochloride. Following a circumferential conjunctival incision, the optic nerve was exposed and crushed approximately 2 mm posterior to the optic disc for 10 seconds with self-clamping forceps. Antibiotic eye drops (levofloxacin) were applied immediately post-procedure. Mice exhibiting severe intraocular inflammation or cataract within 3 days were excluded.

Intravitreal Injection

Under anesthesia and mydriasis, a Hamilton syringe was used to deliver 1 µL of sh-YAP adeno-associated virus type 2 (AAV2; 5 × 1012 viral genomes [VG]/mL; Y34945; Obio, Shanghai, China), verteporfin (HY-B0146; MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA), or GA-017 (HY-147082; MedChemExpress) into the vitreous chamber, at approximately 1 mm posterior to the limbus. The contralateral (right) eye received either control AAV2 or sterile saline. The needle was maintained in situ for 10 seconds to minimize reflux. Eyes exhibiting severe intraocular inflammation following the procedure were excluded from the study.

Immunofluorescence Staining and RGC Quantification

To evaluate retinal histology and protein expression, samples were prepared as either retinal sections or whole mounts.

Retinal Sections

Enucleated eyes were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) overnight, cryosectioned, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 15 minutes, and blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 minutes.

Whole Mounts

Retinas were dissected after fixed for 4 hours at room temperature, cut into four quadrants, and blocked/permeabilized in 5% BSA containing 0.3% Triton X-100 for 3 hours. Samples were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against ionized calcium-binding adapter molecule 1 (Iba1; 1:400; LEE6425; FUJIFILM Wako, Saitama, Japan); neuronal class III β-tubulin (Tuj1; 1:400; 801202; BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA); RNA binding protein, mRNA processing factor (RBPMS; 1:800; 15187-7-AP; Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA); or YAP (1:400; sc-101199; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA). Following phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) washes, Alexa Fluor 488–conjugated or Cy3-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:400; RGAM002 and SA00009-1; Wuhan Sanying Biotechnology) were applied for 1.5 hours. Nuclei were counterstained with 4′6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI; ab104139; Abcam, Cambridge, UK). Images were acquired using a confocal microscope (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany) and processed with ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) and ZEN software (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany). For RGC quantification in whole mounts, six to eight peripheral fields were imaged per retina. RBPMS-positive cells were counted, and the mean cell density was calculated by examiners masked to the treatment groups.

Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction

Retinal fractions were separated using a nuclear and cytoplasmic protein extraction kit (P0027; Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The experiment was conducted according to the reagent kit instructions. Tissues were homogenized on ice in cytoplasmic buffer and centrifuged (1500g, 5 minutes, 4°C) to isolate the cytoplasmic fraction. Pellets were processed with nuclear buffer and centrifuged (12,000g, 10 minutes, 4°C) to obtain the nuclear fraction. Protein concentrations were determined via bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay (Pierce Biotechnology, Rockford, IL, USA).

Western Blot

Retinal tissue and primary RGCs were lysed in radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (P00138; Beyotime) with protease inhibitor cocktail (HY-K0010; MedChemExpress). Lysates were centrifuged (12,000g, 10 minutes, 4°C), and supernatants were collected and quantified by BCA assay. Equal protein amounts were mixed with loading buffer (BL502A; Biosharp, Beijing, China), boiled, separated on 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) gels, and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk or 5% BSA in Tris-buffered saline for 1 hour and incubated overnight at 4°C with antibodies against YAP (1:1000; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), p-YAP (1:500; 80694-2-RR; Wuhan Sanying Biotechnology), large tumor suppressor 1/2 (LATS 1/2; 1:500; 17049-1-AP; Wuhan Sanying Biotechnology), p-LATS1/2 (1:500; AF7169; Affinity Biosciences, Cincinnati, OH, USA), and Nrf2 (1:500; ABclonal, Wuhan, China). Following incubation with corresponding horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1.5 hours), protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents. Densitometric analysis of band intensities was performed using ImageJ 1.8.0, with targets normalized to the respective loading controls

Isolation and Culture of Primary RGCs and Cell Viability Assay

Retinas from embryonic C57BL/6 mice were digested with 0.25% trypsin at 37°C for 20 minutes, neutralized with complete Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium/Nutrient Mixture F-12 (DMEM/F-12; 12491015; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% penicillin–streptomycin, and glutamine, then triturated, filtered through a 40-µm strainer, and centrifuged (300g, 5 minutes). Cells were plated onto poly-d-lysine and laminin–coated dishes, and cultured at 37°C in 5% CO2. After 3 to 4 hours, the medium was replaced with Gibco Neurobasal Medium (21103049; Thermo Fisher Scientific) supplemented with B27 (17504044; Thermo Fisher Scientific), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (RPNO0174; Shanghai Zhong Qiao Xin Zhou Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), and ciliary neurotrophic factor (92125ES60; Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China).

For cell viability assays, RGCs (5 × 103 cells/well) were treated with 1 µL sh-YAP AAV2 or GA-017 for up to 3 days and exposed to different concentrations of H2O2. Following a 1-hour incubation, the medium was replaced with fresh Neurobasal Medium, and the cells were cultured for an additional 24 hours. Cell viability was quantified using Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagent (BS350B; Beijing Lanjieke Technology, Beijing, China) by measuring absorbance at 450 nm.

ROS Detection

Intracellular ROS levels were assessed using a reactive oxygen fluorometric assay kit (E-BC-K138-F; Elabscience, Wuhan, China). To obtain single-cell suspensions, retinas were digested with 0.25% trypsin, whereas primary RGCs on coverslips were washed with PBS. Following incubation with 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) at 37°C for 30 minutes in the dark. Following incubation, fluorescence was quantified using a microplate reader (Ex/Em = 500/525 nm) or visualized by fluorescence microscopy after DAPI counterstaining.

TUNEL Assay

Retinal apoptosis was evaluated using a TUNEL kit (KTA2010; Abbkine, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. TUNEL+ cells in the ganglion cell layer (GCL) were quantified by an observer masked to the treatment groups.

Total RNA Extraction and Quantitative Reverse-Transcription PCR Assays

Total RNA was extracted from retinas using Trizol (CW0508S; CoWin Biotech, Taizhou, China) with Trizol Pal (R1200; Lablead, Beijing, China) for phase separation. RNA pellets were precipitated with isopropanol, washed with 75% ethanol, air dried, and dissolved in diethyl pyrocarbonate (DEPC)-treated water. RNA quantity and purity were assessed by A260/A280. One microgram of RNA was reverse-transcribed using ABScript III RT Master Mix (RK20429; ABclonal). Quantitative reverse-transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix (RK21203; ABclonal) on a real-time PCR system (95°C for 3 minutes; 40–45 cycles of 95°C for 5 seconds, 60°C for 30 to 34 seconds), followed by melt–curve analysis. Gene expression was normalized to Gapdh or Actb. Primer sequences are listed in the Table.

Statistical Analyses

Data are presented as mean ± SEM. Statistical analyses were performed using Prism 10.3 (GraphPad Software, Boston, MA, USA). Comparisons were made using Student's t-test or one-way ANOVA. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Dynamic Regulation of YAP Expression and Nuclear Localization in the Retina Following ONC

We first established an ONC model to mimic acute optic nerve injury (Supplementary Figs. S1A–D). The temporal profiles of retinal YAP expression were characterized by western blotting and immunofluorescence at multiple time points following injury. Immunoblotting results showed a decreased expression of p-YAP/YAP ratio at 3 days after ONC compared with control, and total YAP expression increased (Figs. 1A–C).

(B–E) Densitometric quantification of the p-YAP/YAP ratio (B), total YAP (C), nuclear YAP (D), and cytoplasmic YAP (E). Subcellular fractionation further demonstrated a robust nuclear translocation of YAP, evidenced by an approximately 82% increase in the nuclear YAP fraction alongside stable cytoplasmic levels (P < 0.05) (Figs. 1A, 1D, 1E). Immunofluorescence also showed a significant increase of YAP during the first 3 days following ONC. Notably, YAP signals were predominantly colocalized with RGCs at day 1, whereas this colocalization was markedly reduced at days 2 and 3 (Fig. 1F). By 7 days post-injury, however, both total and nuclear YAP levels had significantly receded from their peak at day 3, with nuclear YAP reduced by approximately 39% (P < 0.05), suggesting that YAP had returned to baseline at day 7, whereas cytoplasmic YAP did not change significantly throughout the process (Figs. 1A, 1C, 1D). Collectively, these results indicate that retinal YAP activity is dynamically modulated after ONC, undergoing a rapid phase of early activation followed by a gradual return toward homeostasis.

Targeting YAP Modulates ONC-Induced Loss of RGCs

To investigate the functional role of YAP, sh-YAP AAV2 was intravitreally injected 3 weeks prior to ONC (Fig. 2A). GFP expression predominantly localized to RBPMS⁺ RGCs, with minimal colocalization in Iba1⁺ microglia (Supplementary Fig. S2A). Efficient in vivo knockdown was confirmed by a 32.5% reduction in Yap1 mRNA (P < 0.05; Supplementary Fig. S2B). Following ONC, sh-YAP–treated retinas showed severe RGC loss (Fig. 2B), with a 56.81% reduction in RBPMS⁺ cells compared with controls (P < 0.0001) (Figs. 2C, 2D). These findings were independently validated using verteporfin, a pharmacological inhibitor of YAP, (Fig. 2E), which recapitulated the effects of sh-YAP by increasing apoptosis and reducing RGC survival (28.63% decrease; P < 0.05) (Figs. 2F–H).

Conversely, activating YAP with GA-017 (Fig. 2I) suppressed ONC-induced YAP phosphorylation, reducing the p-YAP/YAP ratio by 21.85% (P < 0.05) (Figs. 2J, 2K). Based on our finding that endogenous YAP activity declined after day 3, GA-017 was administered from days 4 to 6 post-injury. This treatment significantly enhanced RGC survival by 36.4% (P < 0.05) (Figs. 2L, 2M). Collectively, these findings indicate that YAP signaling is a critical determinant of RGC resilience following injury.

YAP Regulates RGC Survival Through Nrf2 Under H2O2-Induced Oxidative Stress

To define the cell-autonomous role of YAP, primary RGCs were isolated and identified by Tuj-1⁺/Thy-1⁺ immunostaining, displaying characteristic neuronal morphology (Fig. 3A). We first evaluated the transduction efficiency of the AAV2 vector; GFP expression predominantly localized to the majority of RGCs, and YAP expression was markedly reduced following sh-YAP AAV2 infection (Figs. 3B, 3C). Additionally, GA-017 treatment significantly decreased the p-YAP/YAP ratio, indicating successful YAP activation in vitro (Fig. 3D). Oxidative stress was induced using H2O2, which caused a dose-dependent reduction in RGC viability (Fig. 3E). Beyond reduced survival, H2O2 exposure triggered significant neurite retraction (Fig. 3F). YAP knockdown exacerbated these H2O2-induced deficits in both cell number and neurite length (P < 0.0001), whereas GA-017 significantly attenuated the damage (P < 0.05) (Figs. 3F, 3G); however, it should be noted that some primary RGCs exhibited blebbing after treatment with GA-017 (as indicated by the arrows), suggesting that the protective effect of GA-017 was not complete. Immunoblotting for synaptophysin mirrored these findings, showing decreased expression following YAP knockdown and partial restoration after GA-017 treatment (P < 0.05) (Figs. 3H, 3I). Notably, Nrf2 expression paralleled that of YAP across all interventions, decreasing after sh-YAP treatment and increasing after GA-017 administration (Figs. 3H, 3J, 3K). To determine if this link involved redox regulation, we measured ROS levels using the DCFH-DA probe. YAP knockdown significantly increased intracellular ROS accumulation, whereas GA-017 effectively reduced it (Fig. 3L). Collectively, these findings suggest a correlation between YAP and the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway in RGC survival under oxidative stress.

Neuroinflammation and Oxidative Stress are Activated in the Retina Following ONC

To characterize the pathological retinal response, we evaluated neuroinflammation and oxidative stress post-ONC. Immunofluorescence against Iba1 revealed a significant increase in microglial activation (Fig. 4A). Specifically, the number of microglia was markedly elevated, and they exhibited characteristic reactive morphologies, including hypertrophied somata and retracted processes (Fig. 4A).

Concurrently, we observed a progressive accumulation of ROS in the retina over time, indicating sustained oxidative stress (Fig. 4B). To assess the endogenous antioxidant response, we analyzed Nrf2 protein levels in nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions (Fig. 4C). Western blotting demonstrated that nuclear Nrf2 significantly increased post-ONC, whereas cytoplasmic levels remained stable, indicating Nrf2 nuclear translocation and activation (P < 0.01) (Figs. 4D, 4E).

We further quantified the mRNA levels of Nrf2 and its downstream antioxidant targets via qRT-PCR. Compared with the sham group, the transcript levels of Nrf2, SOD-1, SOD-2, and Nqo-1 were significantly upregulated at 3 days post-ONC, but they subsequently declined by 7 days (Figs. 4F–I). This suggests that the retina initiates a transient compensatory antioxidant response following injury which fails to persist during the chronic phase of neurodegeneration. Notably, the expression dynamics of Nrf2 and its downstream targets closely paralleled those of YAP, suggesting that YAP may be a key regulator associated with the Nrf2-mediated antioxidant pathway in response to ONC.

YAP Influences Retinal Antioxidant Signaling and Oxidative Stress Following ONC Partially Via Nrf2

Building on the above observations, we investigated the regulatory role of YAP in oxidative stress and neuroinflammation post-ONC. We assessed retinal ROS levels using the DCFH-DA probe and quantified the expression of Nrf2 and its downstream targets via qRT-PCR (Supplementary Figs. S3A, S4A, S5A). In sh-YAP AAV2–treated retinas, ONC induced a marked increase in ROS levels, with a 30.93% elevation compared with scramble-AAV2 controls (P < 0.0001), indicating exacerbated oxidative stress (Fig. 5A). Concomitantly, the transcript levels of antioxidant genes were significantly reduced, including Nrf2 (33.40% decrease; P < 0.01), SOD-1 (83.21% decrease; P < 0.0001), SOD-2 (82.69% decrease; P < 0.0001), and Nqo-1 (67.76% decrease; P < 0.001), consistent with a suppression of retinal antioxidant capacity following YAP knockdown (Figs. 5B–E, Supplementary Figs. To confirm whether pharmacological blockade of YAP mirrors these effects, we analyzed the retina following verteporfin intervention. Significant reductions in the transcription of Nrf2 (88.5% decrease; P < 0.001), SOD-1 (73.41% decrease; P < 0.01), and SOD-2 (70.85% decrease; P < 0.01) were observed (Figs. 5F–H, Supplementary Figs. S4B–D), suggesting that YAP inhibition enhances oxidative stress potentially by downregulating the Nrf2-mediated antioxidant response.

Conversely, we evaluated whether YAP activation could alleviate oxidative damage. GA-017 treatment significantly reduced retinal ROS levels (12.92% decrease; P < 0.001) and upregulated the mRNA levels of Nrf2 (140.43% increase; P < 0.05), SOD-2 (90.04% increase; P < 0.001), and Nqo-1 (295.66% increase; P < 0.05) (Figs. 5I–L, Supplementary Figs. S5B–D). Simultaneously, immunoblotting revealed that nuclear Nrf2 (P < 0.05) and total Nrf2 (P < 0.0001) levels were both significantly elevated after GA-017 treatment, indicating Nrf2 activation (Figs. 5M–O).

Finally, we assessed the role of YAP in retinal neuroinflammation. YAP knockdown was associated with a marked increase in the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-1β (P < 0.01), whereas pharmacological activation by GA-017 selectively enhanced the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 (P < 0.05) (Figs. 5P, 5Q; Supplementary Figs. S3F, S5E). Immunofluorescence analysis further demonstrated that YAP knockdown promoted microglial activation, characterized by the shortening of microglial processes. In contrast, GA-017 treatment reduced microglial density and promoted process extension, indicative of attenuated neuroinflammation (Figs. 4R, 4S). Collectively, these findings suggest that YAP activation might mitigate oxidative stress and neuroinflammation, at least in part, via the Nrf2 signaling pathway.

Discussion

In this study, we identified the Hippo pathway effector YAP as a key regulator of RGC survival following optic nerve injury. Using complementary genetic and pharmacological approaches, we demonstrated that YAP activity is pivotal in modulating neuronal vulnerability, redox balance, and neuroinflammatory activation in the injured retina. Our findings reveal that the enhancement of YAP signaling markedly attenuates RGC apoptosis and oxidative stress, whereas its inhibition exacerbates neurodegeneration. Collectively, these data indicate that YAP is a critical endogenous stress-adaptive factor whose dysregulation contributes to the pathogenesis of optic neuropathy.

A striking feature of YAP regulation following injury is its dynamic spatiotemporal redistribution. We observed a transient increase in nuclear YAP within RGCs during the acute phase (day 1) post-ONC. However, by days 2 to 3, although total retinal YAP levels continued to rise, YAP immunoreactivity within RGCs significantly declined. This divergence indicates that the sustained YAP upregulation was driven primarily by non-RGC populations, such as activated glia or infiltrating immune cells, which undergo rapid remodeling during retinal injury.25 Such spatial redistribution suggests that YAP may serve distinct, stage-specific functions—initially supporting neuronal survival and subsequently participating in inflammatory or tissue remodeling responses. Therefore, the neuroprotective effect associated with YAP activation may not be entirely cell autonomous to RGCs, but may also involve non–cell-autonomous mechanisms mediated by YAP activation in neighboring glial, immune, or other retinal cell populations. In parallel, the selective depletion of YAP from RGCs during this intermediate phase may compromise neuronal resilience against delayed oxidative stress, aligning with the progressive neurodegeneration observed in this model.

Our data suggest that YAP activation is associated with changes in retinal antioxidant capacity, potentially involving Nrf2-related signaling. Activation of YAP was accompanied by increased Nrf2 expression and induction of downstream cytoprotective genes, together with reduced oxidative burden in the injured retina. These observations are consistent with emerging evidence from other systems, including ocular lens epithelial cells and renal cell carcinoma,22,24 where YAP has been linked to enhanced Nrf2 activity by promoting its nuclear accumulation and transcriptional output, thereby bolstering cellular resilience against oxidative stress. Our findings extend this regulatory framework to the context of optic nerve injury, highlighting YAP as a critical driver of endogenous antioxidant defenses in the retina.

Furthermore, YAP signaling was found to influence the neuroinflammatory milieu of the injured retina. Although YAP activation diminished ROS accumulation and attenuated microglial activation, YAP deficiency promoted a pro-inflammatory state characterized by upregulated IL-1β expression and reactive microglial transformation. Given the established role of ROS as a potent driver of neuroinflammation,26 these effects are consistent with a model in which YAP indirectly dampens inflammatory amplification by maintaining redox homeostasis through Nrf2-dependent pathways.

Despite those novel findings, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. Although our findings demonstrate a functional link between YAP and Nrf2 signaling, the precise molecular interface governing this interaction in RGCs warrants further elucidation. Additionally, the shifting cellular source of YAP toward non-neuronal populations during later stages of injury necessitates the use of cell-type–specific genetic models to isolate the distinct roles of YAP in neurons versus glia. Meanwhile, given that blebbing was observed in primary RGCs treated with GA-017, further experiments may be necessary to explore the optimal therapeutic effect achieved by YAP activation.

In summary, our study identifies YAP as a dynamic, spatiotemporally regulated mediator of retinal stress responses. These results suggest that pharmacological restoration of YAP activity represents a compelling neuroprotective strategy for preserving neuronal viability in optic neuropathies.

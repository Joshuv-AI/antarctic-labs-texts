Safety, Pharmacokinetics, and Dose Recommendations for Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir in Individuals with Mild to Moderate COVID‐19 and Severe Renal Impairment
Authors: Jacqueline Gerhart, Candace R Bramson, Michelle Goulding, Haihong Shi, Olayide Oladoyinbo, Phylinda L S Chan, Sunring Chime, Jennifer Hammond
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPatients with severe renal impairment and COVID‐19 are at high risk for severe disease and death. Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, an antiviral therapy for COVID‐19, is eliminated by renal excretion and can accumulate in patients with severe renal impairment. The phase 1 Evaluation of Protease Inhibition for COVID‐19 in Patients with Severe Renal Impairment (EPIC‐SRI) study evaluated the safety and pharmacokinetics of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir for this population. Fifteen participants (3 not requiring hemodialysis, 12 requiring intermittent hemodialysis) received oral nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg on Day 1, followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg once daily on Days 2–5. No treatment‐related adverse events were reported. Geometric mean (coefficient of variation) maximum plasma concentration, plasma trough concentration, and area under the concentration–time curve from 0 to 24 hours for daily dosing of nirmatrelvir for participants from the Intermittent Hemodialysis Cohort were 3280 ng/mL (48%), 2188 ng/mL (81%), and 65,700 ng*h/mL (59%), respectively. Geometric mean (coefficient of variation) nirmatrelvir hemodialysis clearance and fraction removed from the body by hemodialysis were 30.5 mL/min (35%) and 6.9% (138%), respectively. Population pharmacokinetic modeling demonstrated that simulated distributions of nirmatrelvir maximum plasma concentration, minimum trough concentration, and area under the concentration–time curve from 0 to 24 hours for daily dosing at the studied regimen were similar to those for virtual subjects with normal to moderate renal function receiving the approved dose of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir. SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels were substantially reduced across both cohorts. Findings suggest that the studied regimen is well tolerated, achieves and maintains adequate exposure, and is suitable for patients with COVID‐19 and severe renal impairment. NCT05487040.

Study Highlights

WHAT IS THE CURRENT KNOWLEDGE ON THE TOPIC?

Patients with severe renal impairment (SRI) are at high risk of developing severe COVID‐19 but are usually excluded from clinical studies. The antiviral therapy nirmatrelvir/ritonavir is eliminated via renal excretion. Population pharmacokinetic (PK) modeling of the approved nirmatrelvir/ritonavir regimen for participants with normal renal function or mild renal impairment and a dose‐reduced regimen for participants with moderate kidney impairment demonstrate accumulation potential in simulated participants with SRI.

WHAT QUESTION DID THIS STUDY ADDRESS?

This study evaluated treatment with oral nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg administered on Day 1 followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg once daily on Days 2 through 5 in participants with COVID‐19 and SRI. The resulting data were used to update a population PK model of nirmatrelvir for participants across all levels of renal function.

WHAT DOES THIS STUDY ADD TO OUR KNOWLEDGE?

The regimen evaluated in this study was well tolerated, had PK consistent with regimens for other levels of renal function, and was effective in reducing SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels. An updated population PK model demonstrates that patients with SRI can achieve effective plasma concentrations of nirmatrelvir without intolerable accumulation. Thus, the studied regimen is suitable for patients with COVID‐19 and SRI, adding an important treatment for this understudied population.

HOW MIGHT THIS CHANGE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY OR TRANSLATIONAL SCIENCE?

Data from this study were added to a population PK model of nirmatrelvir, which now accounts for the entire spectrum of renal function, from participants with normal renal function to those with SRI requiring hemodialysis, demonstrating the utility of PK model simulations to support dosing recommendations.

More than 775 million cases of COVID‐19 have been reported worldwide since the beginning of the pandemic in December 2019, leading to more than 7 million deaths. 1 Chronic kidney disease (CKD), which affects more than 850 million people worldwide, is the most common of the comorbidities that increase the risk of severe disease and death due to COVID‐19. 2 However, patients with CKD, especially those with severe renal impairment (SRI; estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2), were not included in the COVID‐19 pivotal studies for antiviral therapeutics because of a higher risk of mortality, limited pharmacokinetic (PK) data in this population, and concerns that renal impairment may alter PK. 3 , 4 This has limited the number of therapeutics available to patients with SRI, a population that has an increased risk of severe disease, post‐acute sequelae of COVID‐19 (PASC; “long COVID”), and death due to COVID‐19. 5 , 6 , 7

Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir is the only oral antiviral fully approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to treat mild to moderate COVID‐19 in adults at high risk of progression to severe disease. 8 , 9 Nirmatrelvir, which is metabolized by cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4), 10 is coadministered with ritonavir, a CYP3A4 inhibitor, to maintain nirmatrelvir plasma concentrations above 292 ng/mL (the 90% effective concentration [EC90]). 11 Nirmatrelvir, when administered together with ritonavir, is primarily excreted renally as unchanged drug. 12

In a single‐dose, phase 1 study in healthy participants with renal impairment, population PK simulations led to a recommendation of no dose adjustment for patients with mild renal impairment and a dose reduction from nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg twice daily (BID) to nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg BID for 5 days for patients with moderate renal impairment. 13 However, this dosage could not be recommended for patients with SRI (not receiving hemodialysis [HD]) because the plasma trough concentration (Ctrough) of nirmatrelvir in this population was projected to be significantly higher than in patients receiving the recommended therapeutic dosing regimen, and urinary recovery of unchanged drug was substantially reduced for trial participants with SRI. 13 Nonetheless, across all renal impairment subgroups, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir had an acceptable safety profile when given as a single dose.

To ensure that suitable COVID‐19 treatment options are available for patients with SRI, the Evaluation of Protease Inhibition for COVID‐19 in Participants with Severe Renal Impairment study (EPIC‐SRI; NCT05487040) sought to explore the safety and PK of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir administered as a 5‐day course in participants with confirmed SARS‐CoV‐2 infection and SRI (whether receiving or not receiving HD), in accordance with FDA guidance. 14

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

EPIC‐SRI (NCT05487040; registered August 2022) was a phase 1, open‐label, multicenter, single‐arm study conducted from September 2022 to July 2023 in nonhospitalized adults with COVID‐19 and SRI not receiving HD or receiving intermittent HD 3 times weekly. Inclusion criteria for the study were age ≥ 18 years with mild to moderate COVID‐19, confirmed SARS‐CoV‐2 (with initial onset of symptoms ≤ 5 days before the day of treatment assignment), and SRI (defined as eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2). Those receiving intermittent HD were eligible for enrollment only if they had been receiving stable intermittent HD for ≥ 6 weeks before screening. Those who were hospitalized or had an anticipated need for hospitalization within 48 hours of screening; had a previous kidney transplant, a history of severe chronic liver disease, or HIV infection with viral load > 400 copies/mL or CD4+ T‐cell count ≤ 200/mm3; took any medication that was highly dependent on CYP3A4 for clearance or were strong inducers of CYP3A4 without a suitable washout period; received or were expected to receive monoclonal antibodies, convalescent plasma, or other antivirals for treatment of COVID‐19; or participated in a previous study for nirmatrelvir were excluded from this study.

Study procedures

The study was conducted at 7 sites in the United States according to consensus ethical principles derived from international guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki, the Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, applicable International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable laws and regulations. The protocol and related documents were reviewed and approved by an institutional review board or ethics committee before study initiation. Written informed consent was provided by all participants.

Participants were enrolled in 1 of 2 cohorts: the No HD Cohort (participants not receiving HD) or the Intermittent HD Cohort (participants receiving stable intermittent HD). All participants received oral nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg on site on Day 1, followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg once daily (QD) at home on Days 2 through 5. Participants in the Intermittent HD Cohort were instructed to take nirmatrelvir/ritonavir after HD. Participants used an electronic diary daily to record times when nirmatrelvir/ritonavir was taken.

Study objectives

Safety

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the safety and tolerability of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir in participants with COVID‐19 and SRI. Safety was measured by the incidence of adverse events (AEs) that emerged during the treatment and follow‐up periods, including serious AEs (SAEs) and AEs leading to discontinuation of treatment. Exploratory safety endpoints included death due to any cause, COVID‐19−related hospitalizations, and COVID‐19–related emergency department visits through Day 28. AEs were graded according to the Division of AIDS (DAIDS) Table for Grading the Severity of Adult and Pediatric Adverse Events, version 2.1. Safety data were evaluated in the safety population, which comprised participants who received ≥ 1 dose of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir. Safety follow‐up continued through Day 34.

Pharmacokinetics

The secondary objective of the study was to evaluate the effect of SRI on the PK of nirmatrelvir, including the effect of HD on the PK of nirmatrelvir in participants from the Intermittent HD Cohort. Participants who received ≥ 1 dose of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and had ≥ 1 concentration were included in the PK concentration population. Participants in the PK concentration population who had ≥ 1 parameter of interest measured were also included in the PK parameter population. Plasma sampling is described in Appendix S1 . Nirmatrelvir plasma samples were analyzed using a validated analytical method at York Bioanalytical Solutions (York, UK). 13 Derived or predicted plasma PK parameters included maximum plasma concentration (Cmax), Ctrough, apparent oral clearance, apparent volume of distribution, area under the plasma concentration–time curve from 0 to 24 hours for QD dosing (AUC0–24), and terminal elimination half‐life of nirmatrelvir. HD PK parameters for nirmatrelvir and ritonavir were calculated using arterial and venous port samples and included HD clearance of drug (CLd) and fraction of drug removed from the body by dialysis (Fd), which are described in Appendix S1 . Methods for population PK simulations for dose recommendations are included in Appendix S1 .

SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels

The nasopharyngeal levels of SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA over time were analyzed as an exploratory endpoint. Nasopharyngeal samples were collected at baseline and on Day 5 from participants in both cohorts and on Day 14 from participants in the Intermittent HD Cohort.

Statistical analysis

Sample size estimation

The study aimed to enroll 12 participants into each cohort, the estimated number to provide 40% precision (the width of a 90% CI) for the area under the plasma concentration−time curve extrapolated to infinity and Cmax. Any patient who had insufficient PK samples for analysis could be replaced.

Statistical analysis methods

Statistical data were analyzed using SAS v9.4. Safety data, PK concentrations, and PK parameters within each cohort and overall were analyzed using descriptive statistics. Safety data were analyzed for participants who received ≥ 1 dose. The population PK model was constructed with nonlinear mixed‐effect modeling using the PK concentration analysis set (Appendix S1 ).

RESULTS

Demographics and baseline characteristics

Of 18 participants screened, 15 were enrolled and treated between September 7, 2022, and July 11, 2023 (No HD Cohort, n = 3; Intermittent HD Cohort, n = 12; Figure S1 ). Median (range) age at enrollment was 54.0 (29–84) years, 53.3% (8/15) of participants were women, 73.3% (11/15) were White, and 40.0% (6/15) were Hispanic or Latino (Table 1 ). Median (range) body mass index was 30.5 (18.9–41.9) kg/m2. Most participants (80% [12/15]) were vaccinated against COVID‐19.

Safety

A total of 8 treatment‐emergent AEs (TEAEs) occurred in 20.0% (3/15) of participants (Table 2 ). All TEAEs were grades 1 or 2 per DAIDS, and none were treatment related. No deaths occurred during the study period. Only 1 AE (diarrhea) was reported in the No HD Cohort. One patient in the Intermittent HD Cohort had SAEs on Day 4 (vomiting, COVID‐19 worsening, and hypertensive nephropathy; none were considered treatment related by the study investigator) and was later hospitalized in the intensive care unit because of COVID‐19. This patient discontinued nirmatrelvir/ritonavir on Day 4. The patient was discharged to home care on Day 7 and was negative for SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA on Day 14. Other AEs occurring in the Intermittent HD Cohort were headache (16.7% [2/12]), chills (8.3% [1/12]), and increased levels of brain natriuretic peptide (8.3% [1/12]).

Pharmacokinetics

Nirmatrelvir PK parameters for each cohort after 5 days of treatment are summarized in Table 3 . The geometric mean (coefficient of variation [CV]) Cmax, Ctrough, and AUC0–24 of nirmatrelvir for participants from the Intermittent HD Cohort were 3280 ng/mL (48%; Table 3 ; Figure S2A ), 2188 ng/mL (81%), and 65,740 ng*h/mL (59%; Figure S2B ), respectively. For 11 evaluable participants receiving HD, geometric mean (CV) nirmatrelvir CLd and Fd were 30.5 mL/min (35%) and 6.9% (138%), respectively (Table 3 ). Notably, 3 participants who received nirmatrelvir/ritonavir before HD administration had higher Fd (1 patient had a nirmatrelvir Fd of 31.1%) than those who received treatment after HD. For all participants from the Intermittent HD Cohort, the plasma concentration of nirmatrelvir declined relatively consistently over time (Figure 1 ).

Population PK simulation for dosing recommendation

A previously published population PK model that included 8 phase 1/2/3 studies 15 was updated based on 4 additional phase 1/2/3 studies. 16 , 17 , 18 , 19 Here, we have further adapted this 12‐study model to include PK data from EPIC‐SRI (14 participants with SRI at baseline and the 1 participant with normal renal function at baseline). A predictive check (simulation approach) with prediction intervals generated from 1000 simulations of 100 virtual subjects with SRI receiving the studied dosing regimen demonstrated that all the observed nirmatrelvir concentrations from participants enrolled in EPIC‐SRI were consistent with the model prediction (observations within the 90% prediction interval; Figure 2 ). Parameters for the updated population PK model (Table S1 ) were found to be comparable with previously published values. 15

The updated model was then used to simulate 5000 concentration–time profiles for each category of renal function (Figure 3 ). Simulations with the doses used in EPIC‐SRI showed that virtual subjects with SRI had similar maximum nirmatrelvir exposure compared with virtual participants with normal renal function or mild renal impairment who were given nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg BID for 5 days and virtual subjects with moderate renal impairment who were given nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg BID for 5 days (Table S2 ). Notably, 94.0% and 93.1% of virtual subjects with SRI on Days 1 and 5, respectively, achieved a Ctrough greater than the EC90 of 292 ng/mL (Table 4 ). For virtual subjects receiving HD, these were 98.5% and 98.3%, respectively. Considering these results, the dosage recommendation for patients with SRI (regardless of HD status) who develop mild to moderate COVID‐19 is nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg once on Day 1 followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg QD on Days 2 through 5.

SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels

Median (range) baseline log10‐transformed SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels across cohorts were 4.9 (0–7.6) log10(copies)/mL, with levels numerically higher among participants not receiving HD (Table S3 ). Median SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels across all participants were lower at Day 5 (2.0 [0–6.9] log10[copies]/mL) and Day 14 (0 [0–1.7] log10[copies]/mL). Three participants had SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels of 0 log10(copies/mL) at baseline but met study inclusion criteria. Decreases were comparable across both cohorts.

DISCUSSION

The pivotal phase 3 study (EPIC in High‐Risk Patients [EPIC‐HR]; NCT04960202) excluded participants with SRI due to a lack of PK and safety data in this population and because the dosing regimen for patients with normal kidney function (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg BID for 5 days) could lead to a substantially higher plasma Ctrough of nirmatrelvir in participants with SRI. 13 , 20 The population PK model developed with data from healthy participants with mild, moderate, and severe renal impairment predicted a significantly higher nirmatrelvir Ctrough for the regimen of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg BID for 5 days in patients with SRI. 13

The current study demonstrated the safety of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir with a modified dosing regimen of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg once on Day 1, followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg QD on Days 2 through 5 in participants with SRI. Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir was well tolerated, with no treatment‐related AEs and substantially reduced SARS‐CoV‐2 RNA levels. These results support safety findings in the phase 1 single‐dose study in healthy participants with SRI and are largely similar to safety and efficacy findings from EPIC‐HR. 13 , 20 Geometric mean Ctrough values were increased for participants receiving HD relative to the study in healthy participants with SRI. 13 Participants not receiving HD also had exposure similar to those receiving intermittent HD. Simulations from the updated population PK model predicted that patients with SRI who take nirmatrelvir/ritonavir at the studied dosing regimen will have a distribution for Day 5 Cmax and Ctrough similar to patients with normal to moderate renal function taking the approved dose of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, with nearly 90% of patients expected to achieve a Ctrough above the EC90.

Of note, 2 pilot clinical studies in participants with SRI (including those receiving and not receiving HD) demonstrated that a dose of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg on Day 1 followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg on Days 2 through 5 had acceptable tolerability and clinical outcomes that were not dissimilar to those from participants with mild or moderate renal impairment receiving approved dosing regimens recommended for their degree of renal impairment. 21 , 22 These pilot studies demonstrated the feasibility and preliminary efficacy of the dosing regimen that was evaluated in this study.

Treatment options for patients with COVID‐19 and SRI are limited. Molnupiravir, an oral antiviral that is mainly eliminated through metabolism rather than urinary excretion, does not require dose adjustment when administered to patients with CKD. 3 , 23 However, there are limitations to the use of molnupiravir: it has not been specifically studied in patients with CKD; it is available only under an Emergency Use Authorization in the United States without any exclusion for participants with SRI; and it has not been approved by the FDA. 24 Additionally, 2 population‐level studies comparing outcomes in patients with CKD taking molnupiravir versus nirmatrelvir/ritonavir had mixed results but suggested that nirmatrelvir/ritonavir may be associated with a greater reduction in mortality and hospitalization, consistent with data from the overall (ie., non‐CKD) patient population. 25 , 26 , 27 The current study demonstrated that nirmatrelvir/ritonavir is well tolerated and results in similar PK exposure in those with versus without renal impairment and, thus, can be administered to patients with SRI and could potentially replace the use of molnupiravir in this large high‐risk population.

Vaccination against SARS‐CoV‐2 induces high seropositivity rates in patients with CKD and is recommended for those with stable CKD. 28 However, patients with CKD who take immunosuppressants have lower seroconversion rates than those who do not take immunosuppressants and are at greater risk of developing severe COVID‐19. 29 Notably, vaccinated patients with CKD who were hospitalized because of COVID‐19 had a 4.6‐fold higher risk of PASC and were more likely to have long‐lasting symptoms. 6 Timely treatment with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir has been shown to reduce the risk of PASC, 30 including a reduction in risk of post‐acute mortality in patients who are immunocompromised 31 and, therefore, may also reduce the risk of PASC in patients with SRI.

This is the first study to evaluate nirmatrelvir PK in participants with COVID‐19 and SRI who are undergoing intermittent HD. In most participants, nirmatrelvir exposure was assessed on the day(s) after HD. We observed increased nirmatrelvir clearance in 3 individuals who received nirmatrelvir/ritonavir before HD, thus supporting label recommendations for nirmatrelvir/ritonavir treatment to occur after HD. This study also builds on a previously published population PK model that included participants with normal renal function and with mild and moderate renal impairment. 15 The updated population PK model captures EPIC‐SRI data well, with nearly all observations within the 90% prediction interval of the model. This updated model now accounts for the entire spectrum of renal function, from individuals with normal renal function to those with SRI requiring HD. Population PK model simulation supports the nirmatrelvir/ritonavir dosage regimen used in EPIC‐SRI.

Interpretation of the results from this study is limited by the small cohort sizes, especially for the No HD Cohort (participants with SRI not receiving HD), which enrolled only 3 participants and had 2 participants with an eligible eGFR at baseline. However, the data pooled from both cohorts in this study were sufficient to support dosage recommendations due to the similarity between overall dosing and exposure between participants receiving and not receiving HD. Additionally, this analysis used a visual predictive check simulation approach to assess the ability of the updated population PK model to capture EPIC‐SRI concentrations because a separate model validation dataset was not available due to the lack of additional nirmatrelvir PK data in patients with SRI.

In summary, an oral dosage regimen of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg on Day 1 followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg QD on Days 2 through 5 was well tolerated in participants with SRI, regardless of HD status. Observed and population PK–simulated exposures using this modified dosage regimen fell within an acceptable prediction interval and were similar to exposure in participants with normal, mild, and moderate renal function receiving standard‐of‐care treatment. Therefore, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 300/100 mg on Day 1 followed by nirmatrelvir/ritonavir 150/100 mg QD on Days 2 through 5 is a suitable regimen for patients with mild to moderate COVID‐19 and SRI regardless of whether they receive HD.

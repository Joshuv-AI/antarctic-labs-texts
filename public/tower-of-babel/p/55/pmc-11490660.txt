Determinants of transmission prevention behavior among Tuberculosis patients in Surabaya, Indonesia
Authors: Ni Njoman Juliasih, Luluk Fadhoh Sakinah, Reny Mareta Sari, Hudi Winarso, Salmon Charles P.T. Siahaan, Erik Jaya Gunawan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTuberculosis (TB) is associated with significant morbidity and mortality, causing significant health challenges globally. Meanwhile, Indonesia ranks second worldwide in terms of TB prevalence, with East Java being among the most affected provinces. Surabaya, in particular, a major city reported approximately 4.628 cases in 2021, underscoring the urgent need to prevent transmission through behavior of patients. Therefore, this study aimed to analyze determinants of transmission behavior among TB patients in Surabaya, Indonesia.MethodsThe methodology used in this study was a cross-sectional design and the participants were 144 TB patients at three community health centers in Surabaya, selected using simple random sampling. Variables including knowledge level, supportive behavior, and medication adherence were analyzed and data collection was carried out using a structured questionnaire. Additionally, data analysis was performed with statistical methods to determine the significance of variables.ResultsThe results showed that knowledge (P-value = <0.001), supportive behavior (P-value = 0.001), and medication adherence (P-value = 0.004) had a significant effect on transmission prevention behavior among TB patients.ConclusionBased on our results, it was concluded that higher knowledge level, supportive behavior, and medication adherence had a significant correlation with increased social support provided by patients in preventing and controlling TB transmission. Therefore, there is a need to implement targeted programs to enhance prevention behavior.

Background

Tuberculosis (TB) is associated with high global mortality and can be very transmissible, causing significant health challenges globally. [1] Globally, Indonesia was ranked second in terms of TB prevalence, following India, accounting for 8.5% of all cases internationally. [2] According to Indonesian Health Profile (2021), [3] the number of TB cases increased compared to 2020, reaching a total of 397,377. The highest was reported in the provinces of West Java, East Java, and Central Java, accounting for 44% of all TB cases in Indonesia. [4] Surabaya (a major city in East Java) reported the highest number of cases, namely 4,628 in 2021. This number has increased compared to the previous year. [5].

An important aspect of preventing disease transmission is individual behavior, and the major challenge is the lack of understanding about pulmonary TB transmission, which hinders efforts in prevention, control, and eradication. [6,7] Many prevention behaviors do not meet standards, such as frequent exposure of patients in public places, not wearing masks indoors and outdoors, failure to inform close contacts, improper disposal of sputum, and inadequate room ventilation. [8].

Individual behavior in preventing transmission is influenced by various factors, including knowledge, stigma, awareness, and attitude. [6,7] Sufficient knowledge can encourage individuals to implement transmission prevention behaviors correctly. However, this knowledge also needs to be supported by willingness and awareness. [8] Various studies have indicated that lack of knowledge awareness, and widespread TB-related stigma are the primary causes of major errors in assessing the severity of the disease. Previous studies showed that good knowledge, supportive behaviors, and medication adherence had a significant impact on individual behavior in transmission prevention of disease. Inadequate knowledge, poor supportive behavior, and low medication adherence can lead to a more complex transmission. [6] Therefore, it is important to identify determinants influencing TB transmission, to prevent and reduce the number of cases.

Subjects and methods

This study used an observational design with a cross-sectional approach and was conducted among 144 TB patients from three primary healthcare centers in Surabaya. The participants were selected using simple random sampling. Data were collected through questionnaires and individual interviews with respondents. The study was approved by the ethical committee of Universitas Ciputra (reference number 081/EC/KEPK-FK UC/VIII/2023).

The questionnaire focused on four main areas; namely TB knowledge, supportive behavior, medication adherence, and transmission prevention behavior. It was developed by adapting elements from several previous studies and tested for reliability using Cronbach's alpha, with the following results TB knowledge (α=0.634), supportive behavior (α=0.611), medication adherence (α=0.879), and transmission prevention behavior (α=0.667), with high reliability.

In general, the knowledge section comprised questions regarding the contagious nature of TB, causes, symptoms, cure, preventability, prevention methods, and transmission, while supportive behavior section covered maintaining sufficient sleep, avoiding overexertion, staying physically active, and proper nutrition. The section on medication adherence addressed recommended treatment, proper medication, managing side effects, sticking to the regimen, and regular sputum checks. [9] Meanwhile, the transmission prevention behavior section included minimizing public appearances, using face masks, proper sputum disposal, informing close contacts, and ensuring good ventilation.

The scoring system for knowledge about TB transmission prevention ranged from 0 to 7, with higher scores indicating better knowledge. Supportive behavior had a score ranging from 4 to 16, while medication adherence and transmission prevention behavior ranged from 4 to 20, with higher scores indicating better behavior and adherence. To facilitate data interpretation, we catagorized the variable knowledge into two categories: score 1–4 as poor and 5–7 as good. The supportive behavior was classified into three categories: 4–8 as poor, 9–12 as moderate, and 13–16 as good. And the medication adherence and transmission prevention was divided into three categories: 4–9 as poor, 10–15 as moderate, and 16–20 as good.

Data analysis was conducted using multiple linear regression with α = <0.05 to determine the effect of knowledge, supportive behavior, and medication adherence on TB transmission prevention behavior. The P-value was compared with α to assess significance, and the B value was used to measure the influence of independent variables.

Discussion

The analysis of results showed that the average knowledge level among TB patients in three community health centers in Surabaya was relatively good. This can be attributed to the education provided by healthcare professionals in community health centers. The results are consistent with Angelo et al. (2020) who reported an 83% knowledge of TB information among respondents. [10] Therefore, combining TB education into programs is crucial to strengthen public knowledge. Education related to TB should be comprehensive, comprising all elements of society and the government, to actively prevent and control transmission. [11].

According to a previous report, good knowledge should also be followed by supportive behavior. [12] The results from this study showed a good mean score among the respondents. Supportive behavior not only cultivates discipline in treatment adherence but also plays a crucial role in improving the overall well-being of patients. [13] These practices serve a dual purpose in ensuring successful TB treatment, by reinforcing adherence to the treatment plan and enhancing the body's ability to combat the disease. [14].

The medication adherence variable of TB patients was in the good category, due to the meticulously regulated schedule at community health centers. High adherence plays a crucial role in effective treatment as it reduces the risk of transmission and minimizes disease recurrence. Efforts to motivate and support patients in maintaining adherence are essential for TB control. [15].

Awareness regarding TB transmission prevention behavior is also crucial to support the three aforementioned factors and play a role in increasing the recovery rate. Based on the results, TB transmission prevention behavior was in the good category. Despite good knowledge, access to sufficient ventilation is restricted by less favorable economic conditions among some patients. Many of the respondents were unemployed/students, while some worked as self-employed with limited income. Inadequate ventilation can negatively affect health and contribute to the prolonged suspension of TB bacteria in indoor air, facilitating transmission in the surrounding environment. [16].

Table II showed that the majority of respondents had good preventive behavior, while 52 out of 144 were in the moderate category. Respondents who had moderate knowledge, supportive behavior, and moderate medication adherence also showed moderate preventive behavior. Based on the results, only a few respondents with good knowledge had poor preventive behavior. The high number of TB cases in Surabaya is largely attributed to the crowded living environment and refusal to wear masks indoors, facilitating disease transmission. Previous studies found that certain TB patients did not follow good prevention practices such as covering the mouths when coughing using the elbow, tissue, or handkerchief. A significant proportion covered using the hands and did not wash afterward. [17].

Table III showed a positive correlation between knowledge level and TB prevention behavior. This implies that higher knowledge levels correlate with more effective preventive measures taken by patients. The results are consistent with a previous study [18] showing a positive correlation between knowledge and preventive behavior. Similar patterns are evident in supportive behavior, as discussed previously. Positive supportive behavior shown by TB patients could significantly affect preventive measures related to transmission in the environment. Additionally, medication adherence was also significantly associated with preventive behavior. Patients who adhered to treatment showed an increased probability of recovery and a diminished potential for transmitting the disease to others. [19].

Conclusion

In conclusion, factors significantly affecting TB transmission prevention include patient knowledge, supportive behavior, and medication adherence. Therefore, enhancing these factors is crucial to significantly improve TB prevention.

Most respondents had good knowledge and practiced good transmission prevention behaviours, some had moderate levels, and only a few showed good knowledge but poor prevention behavior. Based on these results, patient education, community support, and treatment adherence are crucial in achieving optimal TB control. Therefore, stakeholders including governments and health organizations should implement systems connecting health centers with TB patients. The focus should particularly be on continuous education and treatment monitoring. This approach is expected to adequately enhance patient support networks, reduce TB incidence, and improve public health outcomes.

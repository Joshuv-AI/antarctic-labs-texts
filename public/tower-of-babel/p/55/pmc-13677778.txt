Evaluation of vaginal electrical resistance as an indicator of optimal timing for mating in alpacas: a pilot study
Authors: Naoki YAMAMOTO, Misa ARAFURUE, Yojiro YANAGAWA, Mina WADA, Masashi NAGANO
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We evaluated the utility of vaginal electrical resistance (VER) as an indicator of the optimal mating time in alpacas. Three parous female alpacas were used in a total of five trials. The alpacas received an intramuscular injection of human chorionic gonadotropin to induce ovulation and synchronize the follicular waves on Day 0. VER was negatively correlated with plasma estradiol-17β and positively correlated with plasma progesterone (P4). VER on Days 11–12 was significantly lower than that on Day 7. The dominant follicles capable of ovulation were observed by transrectal ultrasonography in four of the five trials on Days 11 and 12. Elevated P4 levels on Day 7 and their subsequent reduction by Day 12 indicated that Days 11–12 were optimal for mating. Thus, VER measurements appear to be useful for estrus prediction. However, repeated VER measurements in each alpaca are necessary to predict the optimal timing of mating because of the significant interindividual variation in VER.

Alpacas are important livestock that provide raw materials for the textile industry and economic benefits to South American countries, including Chile, Bolivia, Peru, Ecuador, and Argentina. They are also kept as companion and exhibition animals on other continents. The importance of South American camelids, including alpacas, has increased in recent years following the discovery of unique antibodies, known as variable domains of heavy chain antibodies (VHH), for the development of medical reagents [1]. Alpacas are difficult to breed because of their low conception rate, which remains below 50%, even after natural mating [2]. In mammals, failed conception after mating may be attributed to multiple factors, such as suboptimal timing of mating, anovulation, embryonic mortality, endometritis, and male infertility. We suspect that one cause of the low conception rate in alpacas may be suboptimal timing, potentially due to the induced ovulation of long-lasting dominant follicles containing aged oocytes. The ovarian follicular wave interval in alpacas is approximately 15 days, with some overlap [2, 3]. Alpacas are coitus-induced ovulators, and a luteinizing hormone (LH) surge is induced by seminal plasma during natural mating [4]. The LH surge can also be artificially induced by injecting gonadotropin-releasing hormone or human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) [5].

Mating behavior in alpacas is not correlated with the conception rate [6]. Non-pregnant female alpacas are often receptive to males regardless of the size of the dominant follicle or the plasma estradiol-17β (E2) concentration [3, 6, 7]. However, small follicles are often incapable of ovulation even after mating, resulting in conception failure [3, 7]. Plasma E2 concentration secreted by the dominant follicle is also related to conception in alpacas [6]. Observation of follicles by repeated transrectal ultrasonography and measurement of plasma E2 concentration allow for the monitoring of follicular waves [8]; however, these procedures are difficult to perform daily on a farm. Thus, the development of simple techniques to determine the optimal timing of mating is necessary to improve the reproductive performance of alpacas.

In the present study, we focused on vaginal electrical resistance (VER) to predict the development of dominant follicles. VER is used to predict the optimal timing for artificial insemination in sows, sheep, and cows based on the varying electrical resistance of the vaginal mucus during the estrous cycle [9,10,11]. Similar to VER, the measurement of cervical mucus electrical resistance is used in humans to predict ovulation [12, 13]. VER-based prediction of the estrous cycle is based on the principle that plasma E2 secreted by developing follicles increases the concentration of inorganic ions (electrolytes), mainly NaCl, in the vaginal mucus, thereby decreasing electrical resistance [12, 14]. Using VER measurements to predict the optimal timing for mating in alpacas is a potentially beneficial on-farm technique because it is minimally invasive and easy to perform. However, no studies have evaluated the relationship between VER and the development of dominant follicles in alpacas.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the utility of VER as an objective indicator of optimal mating time in alpacas. Previous research on alpacas found that plasma progesterone (P4) decreases in conjunction with corpus luteum regression 8–9 days after mating, whereas plasma E2 increases 10–15 days after mating [15]. Based on these findings, we hypothesized that VER values are related to the ovarian cycle and decrease 10–15 days after hCG injection. Therefore, we measured VER in relation to plasma sex steroid hormones (P4 and E2) and follicular development on a commercial farm.

We found that VER values were correlated with sex steroid hormone levels in alpacas. VER values in alpacas varied with follicular development (Supplementary Fig. 1), ranging from 290.0 to 430.0, 223.3 to 410.0, and 200.0 to 333.3 Ωcm in alpacas A, B, and C, respectively. The VER inflection day between Days 0 and 13 was calculated as 6.7 ± 1.6 days after hCG injection (Day 0) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Ovulation has been reported to occur 26 h after hCG injection [5]; in the present study, the VER peak was observed around Day 7. As previous studies [9,10,11] have based estrous cycle (follicular development and ovulation) prediction using VER on a reduction in VER, we calculated the daily difference in VER values between Day 7 and Days 8–13 as ΔVER (ΔVER = VER on Day 7 – VER on each subsequent day). Dominant follicles larger than 6 mm (considered capable of ovulation [3, 7]) were observed by transrectal ultrasonography in four of five alpacas on Days 11 and 12 in the present study, suggesting that decreases in VER were synchronized with follicular development. As the corpus luteum remains functional for 8–9 days after ovulation [15], the ΔVER values were probably maintained until Day 10 (Fig. 2A) by luteal activity. Higher concentrations of E2 and P4 on Day 7 support this interpretation (Figs. 2B and 2C). On Day 12, the plasma E2 levels were comparable to those on Day 7, whereas the P4 levels decreased significantly (P < 0.05). Subsequently, on Day 13, the plasma E2 and P4 levels were significantly lower than those on Day 7 (P < 0.05). These sex hormone changes indicated that maturation of the dominant follicle and regression of the corpus luteum occurred around Days 11–12 after hCG injection in the present study. Thus, 11–12 days after hCG administration appeared to be the optimal time for mating in the present study. This is supported by a previous finding that high fecal E2 levels correlate with increased mating acceptance and pregnancy, whereas high fecal P4 levels are associated with increased mating rejection in alpacas [16]. These results suggest that the decrease in VER is influenced by changes in sex steroid hormone levels and that VER measurement helps predict the optimal timing of mating in alpacas.

The mean VER values differed significantly among alpacas A (370.3 ± 35.7 Ωcm), B (313.3 ± 46.3 Ωcm), and C (267.3 ± 35.7 Ωcm) (P < 0.01). The variation in VER among alpacas may be related to differences in the electrolyte content of the vaginal mucosa, as chloride levels have been reported to vary among individuals [17, 18]. This finding aligns with a previous study in ewes that observed a significant variation (P < 0.05) in VER during estrus among individual ewes [11]. These results suggest that a universal VER standard for alpacas could not be established. Therefore, repeated VER measurements are required to predict the optimal mating timing for each alpaca.

In the present study, we induced ovulation with hCG to synchronize the ovarian cycle; consequently, we could not assess VER in alpacas lacking a corpus luteum. Therefore, further studies involving a larger number of alpacas are needed to evaluate this condition in the absence of a corpus luteum. In addition, we used a VER measurement device designed for pigs. However, two of the five alpacas were younger and smaller than the other three and thus had narrower vaginal canals, preventing the insertion of the device. This highlights the need for a VER measurement device that is suitable for alpacas.

The present study showed that VER values are related to the ovarian cycle and decrease 11–12 days after hCG injection. This suggests the potential of VER measurements in predicting the optimal timing of mating without requiring E2 and P4 measurements or ovarian observation by transrectal ultrasonography. VER measurements also appear to be useful for identifying alpacas that fail to conceive after the initial mating. However, because of the significant interindividual variation in VER, repeated measurements in each alpaca are necessary to predict the optimal timing of mating.

Methods

The experiments were conducted from January to February 2025 on a commercial farm in Hiroshima Prefecture, Japan (34°43’N, 132°87’E), using five mature female alpacas (both nulliparous and parous). Prior to the experiment, alpacas were selected based on their vaginal size to accommodate the insertion of a VER measurement device designed for pigs (PIT-1, Chiyoda Electric Co., Ltd., Nagano, Japan). From these, three parous female alpacas were subjected to a total of five trials: two replicates each in two alpacas (5 and 11 years old) and a single trial in one alpaca (6 years old). Alpacas were maintained under standard farm management practices, including housing in a paddock, access to fresh water, and feeding with hay and concentrate. The alpacas were restrained only during the experimental procedures. All animal handling and experimental procedures were performed in accordance with the Guidelines for the Proper Conduct of Animal Experiments of the Science Council of Japan. The experiments were approved by the Committee for Ethics on Animal Experiments at Okayama University of Science (Protocol no. 2024-009).

Alpacas with a dominant follicle ≥ 7 mm in diameter, confirmed by transrectal ultrasonography based on the single maximum diameter, received an intramuscular injection of 1,000 IU hCG (Gonatropin 3000; ASKA Animal Health, Tokyo, Japan) to induce ovulation and synchronize the follicular wave (Day 0). VER values were measured using a VER measurement device, and the dominant follicle diameters were determined using ultrasonography (HS-102; Honda Electronics Co., Ltd., Toyohashi, Japan) from Day 0 to Day 13. Plasma samples for sex steroid hormone measurement were collected on Days 0–13 in five trials (Fig. 1), except that Figs. 2B and C include data from four of the five trials with complete samples on Days 7, 12, and 13. The VER measurements were performed according to the manufacturer's protocol. Briefly, the device rod was wiped with an alcohol swab prior to insertion. The rod was then gently inserted into the deepest part of the vagina (13–20 cm), and VER was measured. Three readings were taken for each VER measurement session, and their averages served as representative values.

Blood for hormone measurements was collected by cervical venipuncture using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid-loaded vacuum tubes and centrifuged at 5,200 × g for 5 min to isolate plasma. The plasma samples were stored at –80°C until measurement. Plasma E2 and P4 concentrations were determined using competitive double-antibody enzyme immunoassays described previously [19], using reagents including anti-estradiol-17β-6-CMO-BSA (FKA204; Cosmo Bio, Tokyo, Japan) and anti-progesterone-3-CMO-BSA (KZ-HS-P13; Cosmo Bio), and a microplate reader (iMark; Bio-Rad Laboratories, Tokyo, Japan) set to 450 nm.

The coefficient of variation was calculated to compare the relative variability of the measurements among groups. One-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s test, was used to compare VER values among the five trials. Segmented regression analysis was used to determine the inflection days of VER between Days 0 and 13. The analysis incorporated data from five repeated measurements. A paired t-test was used to compare VER values between the determined inflection days and subsequent days.

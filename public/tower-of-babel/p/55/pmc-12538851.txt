Diagnostic accuracy of routine hematological biomarkers for complications and prognosis in bowel obstruction: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Huanyu Hu, Guobiao Chen, Dan Bai, Guanting Wu, Yifei Wu, Shijing Guo, Yiyang Tang, Qianyu Liu, Jiani Hu, Yunhong Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBowel obstruction is a critical emergency. Although imaging like X-ray and computed tomography (CT) aids diagnosis, cost-effective hematological biomarkers are still needed. This study evaluates the diagnostic values of hematological biomarkers for detecting complications, determining the need for surgery, and predicting prognosis in patients with bowel obstruction.MethodsA systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted following PRISMA guidelines. We systematically searched Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, Embase and Cochrane Library for studies published up to June 2025. Inclusion criteria encompassed observational or case-control studies reporting sensitivity/specificity of neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), D-dimer, or lactate in bowel obstruction outcomes. Data extraction included true/false, positives/negatives, cutoff values, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) parameters. Bivariate models pooled sensitivity/specificity, while summary ROC curves and Youden index determined optimal thresholds.ResultsThis study included 34 articles comprising a total of 5871 patients. CRP at a cutoff of 26.91 mg/L (Youden index: 0.97) for diagnosing bowel ischemia showed pooled sensitivity and specificity of 0.80 and 0.92, respectively, with a summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curve and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.91. PCT in determining the need for surgery achieved a cutoff of 0.12 ng/mL (Youden index: 0.8), with sensitivity and specificity of 0.75 and 0.74 (AUC: 0.79). NLR showed a cutoff of 7.2 (Youden index: 0.68), yielding sensitivity and specificity of 0.74 and 0.83 (AUC: 0.84) in the diagnosis of bowel ischemia. D-dimer (cutoff: 1.72 mg/L, Youden index: 0.91) and lactate (cutoff: 2.98 mmol/L, Youden index: 0.8) exhibited sensitivities of 0.83 and 0.77, specificities of 0.70 and 0.79, and AUCs of 0.85 for both, in the diagnosis of bowel ischemia.ConclusionNLR, CRP, PCT, D-dimer, and lactate may provide supplementary diagnostic value for bowel ischemia in patients with bowel obstruction. A PCT threshold > 0.12 ng/mL may assist in evaluating the need for surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13017-025-00652-0.

Introduction

Bowel obstruction refers to the partial or complete obstruction of bowel contents in the gastrointestinal tract and is a common condition in emergency and surgical departments [1, 2]. Etiologies include mechanical obstruction from adhesions, malignancies, or volvulus, as well as paralytic obstruction [3]. Bowel obstruction is one of the main indications for emergency abdominal surgery. If not treated properly, morbidity and mortality increase [4]. Therefore, early diagnosis of bowel obstruction and precise treatment are essential.

Although abdominal computed tomography (CT) and X-ray are valuable for diagnosing bowel obstruction [5, 6], the accuracy of imaging in identifying complications and predicting prognosis remains uncertain. Studies have shown that CT alone is insufficient to determine the need for surgery [7, 8]. Clinical scoring systems combining CT with hematological biomarkers offer a more comprehensive approach, but biomarker cut-off values vary markedly across different clinical scoring systems.

The diagnostic and prognostic value of routine hematological biomarkers in bowel obstruction remains uncertain. Although inexpensive and readily available in emergency settings, markers such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), D-dimer, and lactate show potential for diagnosing bowel ischemia, determining the need for surgery, and estimating mortality risk. Specifically, NLR has been associated with bowel ischemia and mortality [9, 10]; CRP with both bowel ischemia and the need for surgery [11, 12]; lactate with bowel ischemia and mortality [13, 14]; PCT with the need for surgery [15]; and D-dimer with bowel ischemia [16].

Previous studies have reported wide variations in biomarker thresholds for bowel ischemia, mortality. Such as NLR ranged from 4.6 to 250 [17, 18], D-dimer ranged from 0.13 to 2.126 mg/L [19, 20], and lactate ranged from 0.38 to 3.3 mmol/L [21, 22]. In clinical scoring systems of small bowel obstruction, the cut-off of CRP was 50 mg/L for bowel resection in Wassmer’s model [23], whereas another system adopted 5.7 mg/L as cut-off for CRP to determine the need for surgery [24]. Such difference limits the reliability and clinical applicability of these models. Currently, no optimal cut-off values were established to guide decision-making, underscoring the need for further investigation.

Therefore, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the diagnostic value of these hematological biomarkers and to identify clinically informative cutoffs for complications and prognosis in bowel obstruction.

Methods

Literature search

We conducted a systematic search in Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, Embase and Cochrane Library for all relevant articles before June 2025. Two reviewers independently screened the articles for inclusion based on predetermined criteria. Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and Emtree terms were used in the literature search. The following search strategy was initially implemented in PubMed and subsequently used to other databases accordingly: ((“intestinal obstruction” OR “bowel obstruction”) AND (“neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio” OR “NLR” OR “C-reactive protein” OR “CRP” OR “lactate” OR “lactic acid” OR “D-dimer” OR “Procalcitonin” OR “PCT”) AND (“complications” OR “prognosis” OR “mortality” OR “surgical intervention” OR “need for surgery” OR “bowel ischemia” OR “diagnostic accuracy”)). Furthermore, in order to ensure comprehensive retrieval of relevant literature, reference lists of included studies were meticulously examined. Subsequently, full-text articles were rigorously reviewed to determine eligibility. The detailed search strategies employed for each database are described in Supplementary Material.

Study selection

Screening inclusion criteria: (a) Patients with bowel obstruction, or clinical studies included patients with bowel obstruction; (b) Observational or case-control study; (c) The publication language was English; (d) Diagnostic sensitivity and specificity data of at least one of NLR, CRP, PCT, D-dimer, and lactate, and the sensitivities and specificities could be obtained through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. Exclusion criteria: (a) case reports, letters, or abstracts; (b) studies published in grey literature, preprints, or unpublished manuscripts; (c) studies whose full text was not available in English; (d) non-human studies; and (e) studies with insufficient data to summarize sensitivity and specificity. This study met the criteria of participant, intervention, comparator, outcome, and study design (PICOS). Detailed information was provided in Supplementary Material.

Data extraction

Data independently extracted from each article included age, male-to-female ratio, year of publication, study design (prospective vs. retrospective), study country, number of participants, area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and cutoff values of NLR, CRP, lactate, PCT and D-dimer. True positive (TP), false positive (FP), false negative (FN), and true negative (TN) values were extracted or calculated according to the following formula: sensitivity = TP/(TP + FN); specificity = TN/(FP + TN) [25].

Definitions

Complications of bowel obstruction include bowel ischemia, perforation, peritonitis, sepsis, electrolyte imbalances, dehydration, and respiratory compromise due to increased intra-abdominal pressure. Bowel ischemia was defined as follows: insufficient bowel blood perfusion or blood reflux disorder. Specifically, patients with intestinal ischemia, bowel ischemia or mesenteric ischemia were considered as bowel ischemia. The NLR was calculated by the absolute neutrophil count divided by the absolute lymphocyte count from the complete blood count.

Assessment of methodological quality

Two authors independently assessed risk of bias using modified criteria based on the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies (QUADAS-2) and checklist items from the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Diagnostic Test Accuracy (DTA) [26–29]. Risk of bias was classified as high if ≥ 2 items received low scores, moderate if 1 item received a low score, and low if all items were rated moderate or high.

Statistical analysis

The pooled sensitivity and specificity were obtained using TP, FP, FN, TN with Bivariate Model. The corresponding forest plots were generated [30]. Summary Receiver Operating Characteristic (SROC) curves with 95% confidence regions and prediction regions were generated using midas command in Stata 14 [31]. In the fitting of ROC curves, the Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1) was employed to identify optimal thresholds, balancing sensitivity and specificity. The relationship between prior probabilities, likelihood ratios and posterior test probabilities were assessed using Fagan’s nomogram [32]. We applied the Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation (INLA), a Bayesian approach that combines prior information with observed data to generate posterior distributions of model parameters. INLA uses deterministic Laplace approximations to achieve accurate estimates with minimal computational burden, which makes it particularly suitable for analyses with small sample sizes or sparse data [33–36]. The truncation threshold analysis was not performed due to inadequate sample size. I2 was used to examine heterogeneity among included studies. Heterogeneity was defined as I2 >50% and was considered significant at the P < 0.05 level. Publication bias was assessed using funnel plots and Deeks’ test, with significance set at as a P value < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using Stata14 (Stata CorpLLC, College Station, TX, USA), R software (version 4.4.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and R Studio (version 2024.12.0; R wStudio, PBC, Boston, MA, USA).

Materials

We followed the PRISMA 2020 statement [37] and registered the protocol in an international repository (PROSPERO registration number: CRD420251040834).

NLR

Bowel ischemia

The association between NLR and bowel ischemia was evaluated in 8 studies with 1261 patients. The optimal cutoff for NLR in diagnosing bowel ischemia was 7.2 (Youden index: 0.68). At this threshold, NLR demonstrated a pooled sensitivity of 0.74 (95% CI 0.63–0.82) and specificity of 0.83 (95% CI 0.70–0.91). The SROC curve, including the 95% confidence and prediction regions, demonstrated an AUC of 0.84 (95% CI 0.81–0.87), with significant between-study heterogeneity (sensitivity: I2 = 89.05%; specificity: I2 = 77.51%) (Table 3; Figs. 3 and 4). Deeks’ test showed no significant evidence of publication bias (P = 0.94) (Fig. 2)

Mortality

The association between NLR and mortality were reported in 3 articles with 894 patients. The diagnostic sensitivity of NLR for mortality was 50–70.6%, and the specificity was 62.3–80.1% (Table 3).

CRP

Bowel ischemia

The association between CRP and bowel ischemia were evaluated in 5 studies with 855 patients. The sensitivity and specificity of CRP for the diagnosis of bowel ischemia were reported.

CRP showed optimal diagnostic accuracy at a cutoff of 26.91 mg/L (Youden index: 0.97), with sensitivity of 0.80 (95% CI 0.57–0.92) and specificity of 0.92 (95% CI 0.57–0.99). The SROC curve, including the 95% confidence and prediction regions, demonstrated an AUC of 0.91 (95% CI 0.88–0.93), with significant between-study heterogeneity (sensitivity: I2 = 90.38%; specificity: I2 = 98.26%) (Table 4; Figs. 3 and 4). Deeks’ test showed no significant evidence of publication bias (P = 0.59) (Fig. 2).

PCT

Need for surgery

The association between PCT and need for surgery were evaluated in 4 studies with 457 patients. The sensitivity and specificity of PCT for the diagnosis of bowel ischemia were reported.

PCT exhibited a cutoff of 0.12 ng/mL (Youden index: 0.80) for determining the need for surgery, with sensitivity of 0.75 (95% CI 0.56–0.87) and specificity of 0.74 (95% CI 0.67–0.80). Discussion

The annual number of hospital admissions for small bowel obstruction in the United States was 340,100 in 2019, with an in-hospital mortality rate of 1.5% [58]. Approximately 20–30% of patients with acute small bowel obstruction eventually require surgical treatment [59]. To our knowledge, this is the first meta-analysis and systematic review to evaluate the diagnostic value of NLR, CRP, PCT, D-dimer, and lactate for detecting complications, determining the need for surgery, and predicting prognosis in patients with bowel obstruction.

Bowel ischemia triggers a systemic inflammatory response, which elevated CRP [60], and increased neutrophil recruitment and delayed apoptosis, leading to an elevated NLR [61, 62], and stimulates extra-thyroidal PCT production [63]. Bowel ischemia induced hypoperfusion forces intestinal cells into anaerobic metabolism, resulting in lactate elevation [64, 65]. D-dimer, a fibrin degradation product, reflects activation of coagulation and fibrinolysis. In bowel obstruction complicated by ischemia, local microvascular thrombosis and subsequent fibrinolytic activity lead to elevated D-dimer levels [64, 66].

In this study, we found that PCT has moderate diagnose value for the need for surgery. In 2005, Ayten, et al. first reported the association of elevated PCT levels with bowel ischemia [67]. Previous studies of PCT reported widely variable thresholds, with cutoffs ranging from 0.57 to 6.6 ng/mL [44, 55], and were generally limited by relatively small sample sizes [15, 49, 55]. In addition, their diagnostic performance was inconsistent and difficult to generalize [44, 49, 55]. In this analysis, we identified the potential role of PCT as a supportive tool in surgical decision-making.

This study optimized the thresholds of CRP, D-dimer, and lactate for diagnosing bowel ischemia. The diagnostic value of CRP, lactate, and D-dimer has been inconsistently reported in previous studies [16, 19, 20], largely due to small sample sizes and variable thresholds that limited clinical applicability. For example, prior lactate studies were based on small cohorts (typically < 150 patients) and showed inconsistent sensitivity, specificity, and ROC performance, generally inferior to our pooled estimates [11, 20]. By including a relatively large sample size, our meta-analysis demonstrated balanced diagnostic sensitivity and specificity, thereby providing more convincing evidence for clinical application.

Clinical scoring systems typically combine symptoms, laboratory parameters, and radiological features [23, 68, 69]. Their diagnostic performance is generally superior to that of individual biomarkers, with optimal results achieved when biomarkers are integrated into these systems. Clinical scoring systems can aid treatment decisions, whereas biomarker thresholds still lack standardized cut-offs. This study provides valuable biomarker cut-off values for clinical diagnosis and prognostic prediction. Further research is needed to validate these cut-off values within clinical scoring systems for patients with bowel obstruction.

Biomarker levels may vary by age and gender. For example, NLR positively correlates with age in the healthy population [70], and CRP levels are generally higher in females than in males [71]. However, limited data precluded subgroup analyses. Bracho-Blanchet et al. [72] reported that in children with adhesive small bowel obstruction, elevated PCT levels were more frequently observed in those with ischemia or necrosis. These findings suggest that biomarker values may differ across patient subgroups and underscore the necessity of stratified analyses by age and gender.

The methodological quality assessment indicated acceptable overall risk of bias, with no included studies rated as having a high risk of bias. Nevertheless, for the evidence base on NLR in diagnosing bowel ischemia, the relatively high proportion of studies with a moderate risk of bias may introduce bias and reduce the confidence of the pooled estimates.

This study has several limitations. First, some included studies did not fully account for comorbidities that may influence hematological biomarker values, potentially reducing diagnostic accuracy. Second, the relatively small pooled sample sizes for PCT and lactate analyses may limit statistical power and generalizability. Third, variations in the timing and assay methods of biomarker testing across studies may affect biomarker levels and consequently the reliability of the results. Finally, significant heterogeneity in sensitivity and specificity likely reflects differences in study populations, assay methods, and threshold settings, which may influence the stability of the findings.

Conclusion

NLR, CRP, PCT, D-dimer, and lactate may provide supplementary diagnostic value for bowel ischemia in patients with bowel obstruction. A PCT threshold > 0.12 ng/mL may assist in evaluating the need for surgery.

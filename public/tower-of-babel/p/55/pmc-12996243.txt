The classic Chinese medicine weijing decoction as a complementary intervention for respiratory diseases: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: LiQi Li, YuHong Zheng, JiaXin Li, Lin Zhao, Xin Lin, Hao Zhang, HuiHui Chen, PeiYu Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe incidence of pulmonary diseases remains unacceptably high, underscoring the need for improved therapeutic strategies. The current therapeutic landscape, largely reliant on antibiotics and hormonal agents, is constrained by growing challenges such as drug resistance, significant toxicity profiles, and prolonged disease courses. As a time-honored TCM prescription, Weijing Decoction (WJD) is widely administered for respiratory ailments. However, a critical appraisal of the existing literature reveals a paucity of high-quality evidence. Research to date has largely been confined to anecdotal case reports or preliminary investigations with limited sample sizes, resulting in a lack of systematic assessment on the combined efficacy and safety of WJD and modern medical interventions.ObjectiveThis study aimed to systematically evaluate and meta-analyze the therapeutic efficacy of Weijing Decoction (WJD) in the management of respiratory diseases, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and pneumonia.MethodsA comprehensive literature search was conducted across six electronic databases, including both international and Chinese-language databases, to identify randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating the efficacy of WJD in the treatment of COPD and pneumonia. Data on primary outcomes, including the overall clinical efficacy rate, C-reactive protein (CRP) levels, and Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndrome Scores, were extracted and synthesized using RevMan version 5.4 software. Statistical heterogeneity across studies was assessed using the I2 test and Chi-square test. A fixed-effect model was employed in the presence of low heterogeneity (I2 ≤ 50 %, p > 0.1), whereas a random-effects model was applied when significant heterogeneity was observed (I2 > 50 %, p < 0.1). Funnel plots were used to assess potential publication bias among the included studies.ResultsA total of 10 eligible studies involving 1315 patients diagnosed with respiratory diseases were included in the analysis. Patients in the control group received conventional medical treatment, whereas those in the intervention group received WJD in addition to standard therapy. The intervention group demonstrated significantly higher overall disease efficacy rates, along with marked reductions in CRP levels and lower Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndrome Scores (including symptoms such as cough and sputum production), compared to the control group (all p < 0.05).ConclusionThe combination of WJD with conventional therapy appears to be an effective and beneficial treatment strategy for managing COPD and pneumonia, potentially improving clinical outcomes. This integrative approach shows promise in enhancing the quality of care and may offer additional therapeutic advantages in the management of these respiratory conditions.

Introduction

Pulmonary diseases encompass a broad spectrum of pathological conditions, including pneumonia, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), acute lung injury (ALI), pulmonary fibrosis, tuberculosis, and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). These six major categories of respiratory disorders share common underlying pathophysiological mechanisms, such as inflammatory responses, immune dysregulation, and oxidative stress. The current therapeutic strategies for these diseases involve the use of conventional Western medicine, as well as integrative approaches that combine Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) with evidence-based Western medical treatments [1]

In the context of combating Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19), numerous TCM formulations have been recommended for the management of respiratory infections. Notable examples include Lianhua Qingwen Capsules, Qingfei Paidu Decoction, Huashi Baidu Decoction, and Xuanfei Paidu Decoction. In clinical practice, specific classical TCM prescriptions are frequently employed as adjunctive or alternative therapeutic interventions for various pulmonary diseases [2]. For instance, Jiawei Yupingfeng Decoction is commonly used in the treatment of pneumonia; Xiaochaihu Decoction is widely applied in the management of COPD; Liangge San is utilized for acute lung injury; Buqi Huoxue Tongluo Decoction plays a key role in the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis; Maxingshigan Decoction is considered essential in the management of tuberculosis; and Zeqi Decoction is specifically indicated for the treatment of NSCLC.

Chinese herbal compound formulations represent a diverse array of therapeutic options for pulmonary diseases, with certain prescriptions demonstrating broad-spectrum efficacy across multiple respiratory conditions. WJD, composed of Rhizoma Phragmitis (Lu Gen), Semen Coicis (Yi Yi Ren), Semen Persicae (Tao Ren), and Semen Benincasae (Dong Gua Zi), exhibits therapeutic potential not only in COPD, but also in NSCLC, COVID-19, and various other respiratory disorders [3]. In China, WJD has been extensively investigated in clinical studies for the management of prevalent respiratory diseases, including pneumonia, COPD, lung cancer, and lung abscesses. Clinical reports on hospital-acquired pneumonia have indicated that the combination of WJD with conventional Western medical treatment significantly enhances overall therapeutic efficacy and is associated with a reduced incidence of adverse reactions [4]. In a clinical study involving 40 patients with acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD), the combination of WJD and Maxingshigan Decoction (MXSGD), administered alongside standard Western medical treatment, demonstrated significant improvement in alleviating respiratory symptoms such as dyspnea, cough, and sputum production. Moreover, this integrative therapy was associated with a notable reduction in serum tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) levels. Another study conducted by Liu Donghu et al. evaluated the efficacy of WJD combined with Qingre Paidu Decoction, along with paclitaxel and cisplatin chemotherapy, in 46 lung cancer patients. The treatment group showed a significantly higher tumor control rate (84.78 %) compared to the control group (65.22 %) [5]. A clinical study involving 37 patients with acute lung abscesses demonstrated that an enhanced formulation of WJD, in combination with bronchoalveolar lavage, significantly shortened the time required for symptom resolution [6]. Based on the principle of applying a standardized therapeutic regimen to manage diverse pathological conditions, WJD exhibits broad potential in the treatment of various respiratory disorders. However, the current body of literature lacks a sufficient number of high-quality meta-analyses investigating the efficacy and application of WJD in pulmonary diseases. This study aims to systematically evaluate the therapeutic effects and influencing factors of WJD in the management of respiratory conditions through a comprehensive analysis of existing clinical evidence.

Data and methods

Registration

The methodology for this systematic review and re-evaluation was prospectively documented, and the study protocol was registered on the official PROSPERO international prospective register of systematic reviews. The registration can be identified by the number CRD42024542208.

Data and methods

This study was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement guidelines to ensure methodological rigor and reporting transparency.

Literature search

A comprehensive and systematic literature search was conducted across six major electronic databases, including the Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Database, PubMed, MEDLINE, Excerpta Medica Database (EMBASE), and the SINOMED database. The search was restricted to clinical trials published between January 2014 and December 2024, focusing on the therapeutic efficacy of WJD in the management of respiratory diseases. The following search terms were used either individually or in combination: “Weijing Tang,” “Weijing soup,” “stalk soup,” “Weijing Decoction,” “lung,” “Weijingtang,” and “clinical trial."

Criteria for literature inclusion

To identify relevant studies, two independent reviewers conducted a systematic screening of the titles and abstracts of all retrieved publications. Any discrepancies between the reviewers were resolved through consensus-based discussion, with a third reviewer consulted to facilitate the final decision when necessary. The inclusion criteria for study selection were as follows [1]: the study population consisted of patients diagnosed with respiratory system disorders, with no restrictions on gender [2]; the intervention involved the administration of WJD, either as monotherapy (with a minimum of one dose administered) or as an adjunct to other treatments [3]; eligible study designs included randomized controlled trials (RCTs), cohort studies, and case-control studies. The exclusion criteria were defined as follows [1]: full-text articles were not accessible [2]; duplicate publications reporting the same dataset [3]; studies with incomplete or insufficient outcome data relevant to the current analysis [4]; non-primary research articles such as reviews, letters, conference proceedings, editorials, and case reports.

Data extraction and result measurement

Data extraction was independently performed by two authors, who systematically collected the following information from each included study: first author, year of publication, diagnosed condition, sample size (including the number of female and male participants), treatment interventions, and outcome measures. To evaluate the risk of bias in both RCTs and non-randomized studies, the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool was utilized. The primary outcomes assessed included the overall disease efficacy rate, C-reactive protein (CRP) levels, and Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndrome Scores.

Statistical analysis

The extracted data, including disease efficacy rate, CRP levels, and Traditional Chinese Medicine Syndrome Score, were analyzed using Revman V.5.4 software provided by the Cochrane Collaboration. For continuous outcomes, the pooled results were expressed as mean differences (MD) with 95 % confidence intervals (CI). For single-group event rates, effect sizes and corresponding 95 % CIs were calculated. The heterogeneity across included studies was assessed using the I2 statistic. In cases of low statistical heterogeneity (I2 ≤ 50 %, p > 0.1), a fixed-effect model was applied; conversely, when significant heterogeneity was detected (I2 > 50 %, p < 0.1), a random-effects model was used for data synthesis. Funnel plots were generated to assess potential publication bias among the included studies.

Results

Results of our literature search

Based on the aforementioned search strategy, an initial database search yielded a total of 1199 records. After removal of duplicates, 802 unique articles remained for preliminary screening. Subsequently, a systematic screening process was conducted based on title and abstract review to assess potential eligibility. A total of 82 articles were identified as potentially relevant and were subjected to full-text evaluation. Following this assessment, 10 studies fulfilled all predefined inclusion criteria and were ultimately included in the qualitative and quantitative synthesis. Efficacy rate

In the meta-analysis of 10 included randomized controlled trials, patients receiving treatment with WJD demonstrated significantly greater disease efficacy compared to those receiving standard care without WJD. The pooled statistical analysis revealed a risk difference (RD) of 0.16 in overall treatment effectiveness, with a 95 % CI ranging from 0.10 to 0.22 (p < 0.001), indicating a highly statistically significant effect. These results provide robust evidence supporting the therapeutic benefit of WJD as an adjunctive treatment in patients with respiratory disorders.

To ensure the robustness of the findings, a fixed-effect model was employed for data synthesis. This model is particularly appropriate when heterogeneity across studies is minimal, as it assumes that all studies estimate the same underlying treatment effect, thereby producing more consistent and reliable results. In the present meta-analysis, the I2 statistic for heterogeneity assessment was 23 %, with a corresponding p-value of 0.27, indicating low inter-study variability. These findings further support the consistency and statistically significant therapeutic efficacy of WJD in the treatment of respiratory disorders.

Through this intuitive and visually accessible presentation, the therapeutic benefits associated with WJD treatment can be readily interpreted by readers. Furthermore, the application of meta-analytic techniques allows for the synthesis of data from multiple independent studies, thereby increasing the overall sample size and enhancing statistical power. This approach enables a more precise estimation of treatment effects and improves the reliability of the observed outcomes. Concurrently, by evaluating inter-study heterogeneity, the robustness and generalizability of the findings across diverse patient populations can be critically assessed.

In summary, the findings of this meta-analysis provide robust evidence supporting the efficacy of WJD in improving treatment outcomes for patients with respiratory disorders. These results offer a strong rationale for clinicians to consider incorporating WJD into their therapeutic regimens as an adjunctive intervention. Nevertheless, future studies should aim to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of action of WJD and further evaluate its efficacy and safety across diverse patient populations and clinical settings. Such investigations will contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of WJD and provide stronger evidence-based guidance for its application in clinical practice.

Publication bias

The funnel plot assessing the effectiveness of WJD in treating respiratory diseases indicates no evident asymmetry, suggesting the absence of significant publication bias.

The funnel plot demonstrated a symmetrical distribution of effect size estimates across the included studies, centered around the pooled effect size. This symmetry suggests the absence of significant publication bias or small-study effects. The observed pattern indicates consistent results across studies, with no evidence of systematic asymmetry related to study size, sample size, or magnitude of effect sizes. In other words, there is no indication of selective reporting favoring larger studies with significant outcomes or underrepresentation of smaller studies with non-significant findings. Collectively, these observations support the robustness and reliability of the meta-analysis results, with minimal influence from reporting or publication bias.

To further evaluate the symmetry of the funnel plot and provide a more objective assessment of potential publication bias, quantitative statistical methods such as Egger's regression test or Begg's rank correlation test may be employed. Should these tests also fail to detect significant asymmetry, it would further substantiate the reliability and validity of the meta-analysis findings.

In summary, the symmetrical distribution observed in the funnel plot, combined with the results of formal statistical tests for publication bias, collectively support the robustness of the synthesized evidence regarding the efficacy of WJD in alleviating respiratory symptoms. These findings suggest that WJD holds therapeutic promise as an adjunctive intervention based on the current body of evidence. Discussion

Pulmonary disorders, as a highly prevalent and clinically heterogeneous group of diseases, are increasingly recognized as major contributors to global morbidity and mortality. Although certain Western medical therapies demonstrate efficacy in the management of respiratory conditions, they are often associated with adverse effects and may lack broad applicability across the diverse spectrum of pulmonary pathologies. This is particularly relevant in the context of complex and multifactorial diseases such as COPD and pneumonia of varied etiologies — the primary conditions examined in the ten studies included in the present review. COPD is a chronic respiratory condition characterized by persistent symptoms and progressive airflow limitation resulting from structural abnormalities in the airways and/or alveoli. The severity of COPD is primarily assessed based on the degree of airflow obstruction, most commonly quantified by forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1). In addition to spirometric measures, multidimensional indices such as the BODE (body mass index, airflow obstruction, dyspnea, and exercise capacity) and ADO (age, dyspnea, and FEV1) scores are frequently employed to evaluate the overall extent of respiratory impairment and predict clinical prognosis [17,18]. In the context of conventional Western medicine, there remains a lack of disease-modifying therapies for COPD. Current treatment strategies are primarily palliative in nature, focusing on symptom relief and improvement of airflow limitation. During stable phases of the disease, pharmacological intervention is generally not required unless patients experience significant symptoms or are at high risk of exacerbations. However, during acute exacerbations, clinicians commonly employ a range of therapeutic agents, including antibiotics, inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), and bronchodilators. It is important to note that prolonged use of inhaled corticosteroids is associated with several potential adverse effects, such as the development of osteoporosis, immune suppression, and increased susceptibility to respiratory infections. Of particular concern is the association between ICS use and an elevated risk of recurrent infections in individuals with COPD, which may further compromise clinical outcomes and complicate disease management [19,20]. Commonly prescribed bronchodilators in clinical practice, such as anticholinergic agents and β2-adrenergic agonists, are associated with a range of potential adverse effects. These may include cardiac arrhythmias, visual disturbances, urinary retention, and metabolic imbalances, which can complicate long-term management and limit therapeutic tolerability in patients with chronic respiratory conditions [21]. Pneumonia is an acute inflammatory condition affecting the pulmonary parenchyma, most commonly induced by pathogenic microbial agents—including bacterial, viral, and fungal pathogens—as well as non-infectious immune-mediated insults. While it may share certain clinical features with COPD, such as cough and sputum production, pneumonia is typically accompanied by systemic manifestations such as fever, pleuritic chest pain, and tachypnea. Antibiotic therapy remains the cornerstone of pneumonia management; however, the prolonged or inappropriate use of antibiotics is increasingly associated with several adverse consequences. These include the development of antimicrobial resistance, disruption of the indigenous microbiome (dysbiosis), and impairment of host immune defenses. Collectively, these factors may predispose patients to disease recurrence and contribute to treatment failure, underscoring the need for more effective and adjunctive therapeutic strategies [22]. However, administration of WJD was associated with marked improvements in clinical outcomes, as indicated by significant reductions in CRP levels, along with notable alleviation of cough and sputum production. This integrative therapeutic approach appears to effectively mitigate inflammatory responses and relieve respiratory symptoms, thereby contributing to overall disease burden reduction and enhanced treatment efficacy.

Currently, compound WJD is being utilized in clinical settings for the management of various respiratory and pulmonary conditions, including COPD, NSCLC, and even COVID-19. However, there remains a paucity of foundational research regarding the therapeutic efficacy of compound WJD dripping pills in disease treatment. Previous studies have conducted meta-analyses to assess the effectiveness of combining conventional pharmacotherapies with compound WJD in patients experiencing acute exacerbations of COPD. These analyses have demonstrated that such integrative approaches may significantly improve clinical outcomes. Notably, emerging evidence indicates that compound WJD, when used in combination with Mai Men Dong Tang or cisplatin, exhibits potent inhibitory effects on the proliferation of A549 human lung adenocarcinoma cells. This antiproliferative activity has been linked to the suppression of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, suggesting a potential molecular mechanism underlying its anticancer properties. Collectively, these findings highlight the multifaceted therapeutic potential of compound WJD and support further investigation into its role as an adjunctive intervention in the treatment of respiratory diseases and lung malignancies [[23], [24], [25]].

TCM is grounded in a holistic theoretical framework that includes foundational concepts such as Jing-Qi theory, Yin-Yang theory, the Five Phases (Wu Xing), Zang-Fu organ theory, and Meridian (Jing Luo) theory. According to TCM, pulmonary diseases are primarily associated with dysfunction of the Lung system, which differs from the modern biomedical understanding of the respiratory system. The therapeutic application of WJD in treating lung disorders can be interpreted through the principles of Zang-Fu differentiation and syndrome pattern identification in TCM. In TCM theory, the Lung governs Qi and respiration, regulates water metabolism, and plays a central role in the circulation of blood through its influence on Qi movement. When Lung function is impaired, it may lead to Qi stagnation, fluid retention, and the subsequent formation of pathological products such as phlegm and blood stasis—key pathogenic factors implicated in most pulmonary disorders. WJD exerts its therapeutic effects by clearing Heat from the Lungs, transforming phlegm, and resolving blood stasis. It is commonly employed to alleviate symptoms including cough, excessive sputum production, chest pain, expectoration of pus, and fever. These clinical manifestations correspond broadly to modern medical conditions such as pneumonia, COPD, acute lung injury, pulmonary fibrosis, tuberculosis, and even lung cancer. Therefore, in accordance with the theoretical principles of TCM, WJD represents a potentially effective therapeutic strategy for a wide spectrum of pulmonary diseases, bridging traditional wisdom with contemporary clinical applications [26].

The evaluation of the therapeutic efficacy of Chinese herbal medicine, particularly WJD, in the management of diverse respiratory disorders necessitates a comprehensive and methodologically rigorous approach. Such an assessment should be underpinned by well-designed clinical research frameworks, standardized intervention protocols, and robust statistical methodologies to ensure the validity, reproducibility, and generalizability of findings. Comparative clinical trials—specifically RCTs—that evaluate outcomes in patients treated with WJD versus those receiving conventional therapies or placebo are essential to delineate the potential benefits and limitations of this herbal formulation. These studies should not only assess clinical endpoints such as symptom improvement and pulmonary function, but also comprehensively investigate the safety profile of WJD, its potential pharmacological interactions with co-administered medications, and its long-term impact on respiratory health and disease progression. Ultimately, such systematic evaluation is critical for informing evidence-based clinical decision-making and integrating WJD into mainstream respiratory care pathways. By bridging traditional medicinal knowledge with contemporary scientific standards, this approach may facilitate the development of more effective, personalized, and holistic therapeutic strategies for patients suffering from chronic and acute pulmonary diseases. The results of this meta-analysis provide compelling evidence that the integration of WJD with conventional treatment surpasses monotherapy in improving treatment outcomes for patients with pulmonary diseases. These outcomes include disease effectiveness, CRP levels, and Traditional Chinese Medicine syndrome scores.

In clinical trials involving TCM, the assessment of symptom improvement is often based on physicians' clinical judgment, which remains a commonly utilized outcome measure. In the context of this review, the majority of included studies—save for one exception—utilized the overall effectiveness rate as the primary endpoint. A comprehensive pooled analysis of the aggregated data revealed considerable differences across the various treatment groups, with results reaching statistical significance. These findings provide robust and consistent evidence supporting the favorable therapeutic effects of WJD in the management of the targeted pulmonary conditions, thereby reinforcing its clinical efficacy and potential utility as an adjunct or complementary intervention.

The combination of WJD with conventional therapy has been demonstrated to significantly reduce serum or plasma levels of CRP compared to conventional treatment alone. Emerging evidence further indicates that WJD exerts anti-inflammatory effects by downregulating key pro-inflammatory cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-8 (IL-8), which are closely implicated in the pathogenesis of various pulmonary diseases. Elevated systemic levels of inflammatory biomarkers, such as TNF-α and IL-8, are well-documented features of chronic respiratory conditions and are associated with disease severity, exacerbation frequency, and impaired clinical outcomes. Preclinical and clinical studies suggest that the attenuation of these inflammatory mediators may contribute to a shortened recovery period and a reduced risk of disease recurrence in patients with pulmonary disorders. Notably, the modulation of inflammatory biomarkers represents a clinically meaningful outcome in the evaluation of novel therapeutic interventions for COPD. Incorporating such biomarkers into clinical trial designs may enhance diagnostic precision, facilitate early intervention, and improve the monitoring of treatment response, thereby supporting the development of more targeted and effective therapeutic strategies.

Despite the promising findings, this study is subject to several limitations that warrant careful consideration and further investigation. First, the majority of the included studies were observational in nature, which inherently carries a higher risk of confounding due to the natural variability in disease progression. TCM emphasizes the principle of “bian zheng lun zhi” (pattern differentiation and treatment individualization), wherein therapeutic strategies are customized according to each patient's constitution, symptomatology, and disease stage. In clinical practice, modifications—such as the addition or omission of specific herbs or combination with classical formulations—are frequently made to the basic prescription to better address comorbidities or concurrent conditions. Consequently, although all included studies were based on WJD, variations in the composition of supplementary herbs led to heterogeneity in treatment protocols across trials, potentially affecting the consistency and comparability of reported outcomes. Therefore, future high-quality RCTs employing standardized WJD formulations are warranted to confirm and strengthen the current evidence base. Second, due to the relatively limited number of eligible studies, certain outcome measures lacked sufficient statistical power to draw definitive conclusions. To enhance the robustness of future analyses, larger-scale clinical trials with adequate sample sizes are needed to expand and refine the existing body of evidence. Moreover, given the unique physical and sensory characteristics of traditional herbal decoctions, implementing effective blinding for patients and investigators remains a methodological challenge. This limitation may contribute to the unclear or high risk of performance and detection bias observed in several of the included studies. Future RCTs could consider the development and use of well-matched placebos that closely resemble herbal decoctions in appearance, taste, and texture, thereby improving trial design and reducing potential bias. Lastly, to account for the influence of various confounding factors and to generate more precise and clinically meaningful conclusions, future studies should incorporate detailed subgroup and sensitivity analyses. These could include stratifying patients based on baseline characteristics such as comorbidities, radiological findings, or severity of interstitial lung disease (ILD), enabling a more nuanced understanding of WJD's therapeutic effects across diverse patient populations.

Conclusion

Based on the findings of this review, the addition of WJD to standard treatment regimens for pulmonary diseases may enhance therapeutic efficacy, attenuate inflammatory responses, and alleviate clinical symptoms in affected patients. The integration of WJD with conventional therapies demonstrates potential as a synergistic and promising treatment strategy, offering the capacity to modulate systemic inflammation and potentially delay or prevent disease progression. These observations suggest that WJD may serve as a valuable adjunctive therapy in the management of respiratory disorders, supporting its traditional use through emerging scientific evidence. Further high-quality clinical trials are warranted to validate these preliminary findings and to establish standardized protocols for its application in evidence-based clinical practice.

Full Picture of Energy Transfer in a Trivalent Europium Complex with Bidentate β-Diketonate Ligand
Authors: Shiori Miyazaki, Motoaki Gotanda, Yuichi Kitagawa, Yasuchika Hasegawa, Kiyoshi Miyata, Ken Onda
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTrivalent europium (Eu(III)) complexes emit narrow-band luminescence as a result of energy transfer following the photoexcitation of antenna ligands. Bidentate β-diketonates are typically used as antenna ligands; however, their entire energy transfer mechanism to Eu(III) remains unknown. We used time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy and femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy to map the complete intramolecular energy transfer process in the [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] (hfa = hexafluoroacetylacetonate, TPPO = triphenylphosphine oxide) complex; hfa is a β-diketonate antenna ligand. Our analysis provides a “full picture” of energy transfer, tracing each step from initial photoexcitation to final relaxation: intersystem crossing in the ligands, energy transfer from the ligands to the metal center, and multiple internal conversion of Eu(III). We demonstrated that the direct bonds of the bidentate ligands enabled a quick and near-unity-efficiency energy transfer from the triplet state of the ligand to the 5D2 state of Eu(III). We also revealed that the loss of the ligand-centered intersystem crossing process reduces the overall sensitization efficiency. Our findings provide a foundation for the rational design of advanced luminescent materials, paving the way for advances in lanthanide-based luminescence research.

Trivalent europium (Eu(III)) complexes have emerged as promising light-emitting materials for diverse applications, including organic light-emitting diodes, bioimaging sensors, and temperature sensing, owing to their high color purity.1−6 Sensitizing Eu(III) with chromophores, known as antenna ligands, is necessary to overcome its low absorption coefficient of Eu(III) (ε < 10 M–1 cm–1). This allows for highly efficient photoluminescence (PL) of Eu(III).7−12 Bidentate β-diketonate ligands, including thenoyltrifluoroacetone (tta) and hexafluoroacetylacetonate (hfa), are well-known antenna ligands owing to their ease of synthesis and high sensitization ability.13−17 To date, the sensitization efficiency (ηsens) of β-diketonate ligands has been empirically controlled by changing energy levels via ligand substituents or combinations.18−26

Understanding the intricate energy transfer mechanism between chromophores and Eu(III) is crucial to improving ηsens. The primary sensitization process of β-diketonate ligands is triplet energy transfer from the triplet excited states of the ligands.27−29 However, for some other ligands, energy transfer from the singlet excited states has been reported.30,31 For specific β-diketonate ligands, ligand-to-metal charge transfer (LMCT) states are involved in energy loss processes, particularly when these LMCT states are energetically close to the relevant states in the processes.32−34 Understanding these various pathways is crucial for determining the dominant energy transfer mechanisms, which is essential for designing more effective luminescent Eu(III) complexes.

Time-resolved spectroscopy has revealed insights into the energy transfer pathways and time scales of various ligands.27−30,35−37 We recently used time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy (TR-PL) to reveal all energy levels involved in energy transfer processes, including ligands and Eu(III).28 Furthermore, we quantitatively demonstrated 100% sensitization efficiency via hfa ligands in the host–guest film, highlighting the critical role of β-diketonate ligands in sensitization.29 Despite the importance of hfa as a crucial ligand, the energy transfer mechanism from hfa remains ambiguous.

In this study, we elucidated the overall energy transfer mechanism in real time in the [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] (TPPO = triphenylphosphine oxide) (Figure 1a) complex, which serves as a pioneering example in Eu(III) complex design, using TR-PL and femtosecond transient absorption spectroscopy (fs-TAS). This study successfully maps the entire energy transfer process in a lanthanide complex from the initial photoexcitation to the final relaxation into the ground state. Our findings reveal a near-unity efficiency of energy transfer from the triplet state of the ligand to the 5D2 state of Eu(III) in [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] and identified an energy loss process at the intersystem crossing (ISC) that contributes to the decrease in ϕtot. This study completes our understanding of energy transfer from bidentate β-diketonate ligands to Eu(III), filling gaps in previous studies. This study provides a foundation for the rational design of advanced lanthanide-based luminescent materials.

We investigated the sensitization ability of the antenna ligands by analyzing the luminescence properties of [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] in a tetrahydrofuran (THF) solution. Figure 1c depicts the absorption spectra of [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] (Figure 1a), [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] (Figure 1b), and the ligand molecule TPPO. The strong absorption band in the range of 250–360 nm was attributed to the S0–Sn excitation of the hfa ligands, whereas the weak absorption at 250–280 nm was primarily caused by TPPO. Upon selective photoexcitation of the hfa ligands, Eu(III) emission bands were observed (Figure 1c), corresponding to transitions from 5D0 to 7F0, 7F1, 7F2, 7F3, and 7F4 at wavelengths of 581, 594, 616, 654, and 701 nm, respectively. The most intense emission band at 615 nm (5D0 → 7F2) is an electronic dipole emission that is enhanced by an asymmetric field from the surrounding environment.38,39 A high ratio of electric to magnetic dipole transitions (5D0 → 7F1) indicates a complex asymmetry. The PL spectra normalized at the magnetic dipole emission at 594 nm (5D0 → 7F1) indicate that [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] is more asymmetric in structure than [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2].

The photophysical properties of [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] and [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] are summarized in Table 1. [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] in THF showed a higher intrinsic luminescence quantum yield of Eu(III) (ϕEu) (0.64) compared to [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] (0.30). This high ϕEu originates from a large radiative rate constant of Eu(III) (krEu) (800 s–1) and a small nonradiative rate constant of Eu(III) (knrEu) (450 s–1), which are estimated by dividing the total Eu(III) transition by the magnetic dipole transition in the observed emission spectrum (refer to the SI, eqs S1–S4).40 The high krEu is due to the enhanced coordination asymmetry caused by incorporating TPPO ligands, while the low knrEu is attributed to the phosphine oxide’s low vibrational frequency. The total PL quantum yield (ϕtot) excited by the hfa ligands and the ηsens of [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] are estimated to be 0.48 and 0.75, respectively. This indicated that the TPPO ligand also contributed to efficient energy transfer.

Notably, the ϕEu of [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] in THF (0.64) is not significantly different from those in the solid state (0.65).41 This indicates that the strong coordination ability of TPPO maintains the complex structure despite the presence of the coordinating solvent THF. However, the ϕEu is slightly different from previously reported values in CDCl3 (0.72),26 suggesting that the coordination structure is slightly affected by solvents other than solvent coordination. On the other hand, in [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2], the low coordination ability ligands H2O can be mainly replaced by THF ([Eu(hfa)3(THF)n]).42 Thus, the ϕEu (0.30) is higher than that in the solid state (0.19)43 due to the avoidance of relaxation derived from the O–H bond in coordinating H2O.

We used fs-TAS on [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] in the THF solution after bubbling using Ar gas to investigate the excited-state dynamics of hfa ligands following photoexcitation at 315 nm (Figure 2a). The fs-TAS spectra were attributed to the hfa ligands owing to the negligibly low absorption coefficient of the TPPO ligand at 315 nm, which was also supported by comparable fs-TAS spectra of [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] (Figure S1). The absorbance change (ΔAbs.) of the [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] solution increased in the <550 nm range and decreased in the >550 nm range following photoexcitation (0–5 ps) (Figure 2b). A global analysis of fs-TAS spectra using a sequential model with two components44 revealed a time constant of 1.51 ± 0.03 ps for the conversion from the first to the second component (Figure S2). The evolution-associated spectra (EAS) and concentration kinetics of the two components are shown in Figure S2c and d, respectively. To assign the observed species, we also performed fs-TAS on [Gd(hfa)3(TPPO)2] and [Gd(hfa)3(H2O)2] in THF solution after bubbling using Ar gas (Figures S3 and S4), which exhibited no intramolecular energy transfer (Figure 2c). Global analysis revealed comparable EAS (Figure S3), with time constants of 2.37 ± 0.02 ps in [Gd(hfa)3(TPPO)2] (Figure S3) and 2.44 ± 0.03 ps in [Gd(hfa)3(H2O)2] (Figure S4), respectively. The two EAS components were reasonably assigned to S1 and T1 of hfa in [Gd(hfa)3(TPPO)2], with the time constant representing the ISC rate. Therefore, the two components observed for [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] were assigned to S1 and T1 of hfa. Following ISC, the T1 lifetime in [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] estimated from fs-TAS was significantly shorter (65.4 ± 0.2 ps) than the T1 lifetime in [Gd(hfa)3(TPPO)2] estimated from TR-PL measurements (Figure S5, 1.2 ± 0.01 μs). The short T1 lifetime of the Eu(III) complex suggests that the T1 state is rapidly depopulated via intramolecular energy transfer to Eu(III) (Figure 2c). We determined that the time constant for intramolecular energy transfer from the T1 state of hfa to Eu(III) was ∼65 ps.

Multiscale TR-PL measurements were performed to quantify the energy transfer processes between photoexcitation at the hfa antenna and Eu(III) luminescent decay. Parts a–e of Figure 3 depict the spectral evolution of the emission from [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] in the THF solution after bubbling using Ar gas. Following the initial photoexcitation of the hfa ligands at 315 nm, a broad emission in the 350–550 nm range was observed (Figure 3a). This emission was primarily caused by the fluorescence of the hfa ligands, as the spectrum was broad and resembled that of the hfa molecule alone (Figure S6). As the ligand emission faded, the narrow-band emission from Eu(III) became dominant. All narrowband emission bands were assigned to the 4f–4f transitions in Eu(III), corresponding to Dicke’s diagram.45 The emission bands at 461, 473, 488, and 509 nm in the nanosecond time region (Figure 3b) correspond to the 5D2 → 7F0, 5D2 → 7F1, 5D2 → 7F2, and 5D2 → 7F3 transitions, respectively.45 The emission bands at 535, 556, and 583 nm were observed in the tens of nanoseconds time region (Figure 3b–d) and assigned to the transitions of 5D1 → 7F1, 5D1 → 7F2, and 5D1 → 7F3, respectively.45 The bands at 577, 592, and 614 nm exhibit the longest lifetime (Figure 3d–e) and are assigned to the transitions 5D0 → 7F0, 5D0 → 7F1, and 5D0 → 7F2, respectively.45 The emissions from 5D0 were not significant at the beginning (<1 μs), but they are dominant in the static emission spectrum because their lifetime is significantly longer than that of the other ligands, 5D2, and 5D1.

The temporal change in each emission (Figure 3f–i, Figure S7) reveals that the emission from the ligands at 400 nm decays with a time constant of <0.1 ns (Figure 3f). Based on fs-TAS measurements, this rapid decay was attributed to a fast ISC process. At 490 nm, the 5D2 state kinetics (Figure 3g) increased in intensity with time constants of less than 0.1 ns, indicating the presence of fast intramolecular energy transfer. Based on the correlation between the energy matching (Figure S8) and ϕtot, these temporal changes are attributed to the energy transfer from the T1 state of the hfa ligand to the 5D2 state.8 This result is in sharp contrast to the theoretical prediction based on Judd–Ofelt spectroscopy (see text in the SI, Figure S9, and Tables S1–S3),46 which shows that the primary energy transfer state is the 5D1 state.

The 5D1 emission at 585 nm increased by 1.6 ± 0.2 ns following the 5D2 emission, which decayed with a time constant of 1.6 ± 0.2 ns (Figure 3g, h). This result indicates that the initially transferred excited energy populates the 5D2 state of Eu(III), and internal conversion from 5D2 to 5D1 occurs within ∼2.0 ns. The emission from the 5D1 state decays with a time constant of 1.5 ± 0.2 μs (Figure 3h), while the intensity of the emission from the 5D0 state at 615 nm increases with the same time constant of 1.5 ± 0.2 μs (Figure 3i). The internal conversion from 5D1 to 5D0 took ∼1.5 μs, according to this data. Finally, the emission decay from 5D0 was ∼770 ± 0.2 μs, consistent with previous reports and confirming no degradation during measurement.29 By combining the TR-PL and fs-TAS results, we determined the time constant of the entire process from hfa excitation to 5D0 emission of Eu(III) (Figure 3j).

The high ηET is presumably due to direct bonding of the bidentate hfa ligands to Eu(III), reducing the donor–acceptor distance.47 In fact, [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] and Eu(III) complexes with hydroxypyridinone-based tetradentate antenna ligands,36 which coordinate Eu(III) directly, exhibit high ηET (>0.99) (Table S4). Furthermore, we compare the ηET of [Eu(hfa)3(DPPTO)2] (DPPTO = 2-diphenyl phosphoryl triphenylene) (Figure S10a), which exhibits a phosphine oxide linker between the triphenylene antenna moieties. Through comprehensive investigation using nanosecond transient absorption spectroscopy (ns-TAS) (Figure S10), we successfully identified the previously ambiguous energy transfer process and its efficiency in [Eu(hfa)3(DPPTO)2] (Figure S11 and Table S5).28 The intramolecular energy transfer exhibits a slower time constant (188 ns) and a slightly lower ηET (0.84). Since the T1 state in the DPPTO ligand is localized on the triphenylene moiety,26 the difference in ηET is derived from the distance between the antenna moiety and Eu(III) (Figure 4), in addition to the energy difference of the T1 state of the ligands (Figure S12). This suggests that energy levels do not solely control the energy transfer in this system.

Our results revealed the near-unity ηET in [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] and [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] owing to the direct bonding of hfa ligands while the ϕISC deviates from the unity in [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] (0.78) and [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] (0.30) (Table S4). This indicates that ϕISC is the primary factor contributing to the ηsens. Note that ϕISC includes all energy loss processes before the triplet state of the ligand is generated. Although lanthanide ions accelerate ISC, they are also known to accelerate nonradiative relaxation owing to their paramagnetic effects.48,49 Moreover, the ϕISC significantly differs between [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] and [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2], implying that the modulation of the coordination structure also influences ϕISC. The structure of [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2], which includes the TPPO ligand, is more rigid than that of [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2], suggesting that the effect of solvent coordination, inducing nonradiative relaxation, is prevented. Furthermore, ligand T1 energy levels differ between [Gd(hfa)3(TPPO)2] and [Gd(hfa)3(H2O)2] (Figure S12) with almost the same structures as the Eu(III) complex (Figure S13).50 This implies that energy modulation by the coordination structure can also affect ϕISC.

The specific ligand arrangement can influence the energy transfer pathways and efficiency, potentially opening opportunities for tailoring the energy transfer dynamics of the Eu(III) complexes. Our findings revealed that, even in the case of similar antenna ligands, sensitization efficiency varied owing to minor differences in the complex structure of the combined ligands. From the comparative analysis with [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2], it became evident that the potent coordination counter ligands such as TPPO are crucial for improving ϕISC, which represents the primary quantum-efficiency loss process. Furthermore, a comparative analysis with [Eu(hfa)3(DPPTO)2] reveals that a direct connection of antenna ligands benefits efficient energy transfer; however, it leads to a lower ϕISC owing to interactions with the metal center. Therefore, PL quantum yields of Eu(III) complexes can be improved in two ways: one is to reduce the energy loss processes through triplet sensitization, as previously reported,18 and the other is to synthesize the ligands that induce an asymmetrical, crowded structure and shorten the effective energy transfer distance to Eu(III), for example, the ligands with extended conjugation that reduce the energy transfer distance substantially.

Our direct observation of the intramolecular energy transfer processes in well-investigated [Eu(hfa)3(TPPO)2] has provided critical insights into intramolecular energy transfer efficiency. We observed rapid and highly efficient energy transfer from the ligand triplet state to the 5D2 state of Eu(III). Ligand-centered ISC was identified as the primary quantum-efficiency loss process, affecting the overall sensitization efficiency. Furthermore, a comparative analysis with [Eu(hfa)3(H2O)2] and [Eu(hfa)3(DPPTO)2] demonstrated the significant impact of ligand properties and coordination geometry on the sensitization efficiency. Also, our study demonstrated the great importance of time-resolved measurements from the beginning to the end for understanding the energy transfer mechanism ranging from picoseconds to milliseconds in lanthanide complexes. Our findings provide valuable insights into the rational design of advanced luminescent materials, where the intersystem crossing of the antenna is critical in molecular design. Overall, this provides promising prospects for innovations in lanthanide-based luminescence and its diverse applications.

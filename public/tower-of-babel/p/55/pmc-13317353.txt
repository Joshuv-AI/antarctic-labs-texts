Impact of migraine on work productivity of healthcare providers at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital
Authors: Wesam Osama, Rehab Magdy, Salsabil Abo Al-Azayem, Eman Hany Elsebaie, Aya Abou Amer, Abdelhameed Mahros Abdelhameed Elshenawey, Fatma Hossni Abdellatif Abdelghany, Radwa Mohammed Elsayed, Dina Farouk, Shehab M Moawad, Shehab Samir, Ahmed R N Ibrahim, Khalid M Orayj, Lamiaa N Abdelaty, Nesma Mounir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground & objectivesMigraine is more prevalent among healthcare providers (HCPs), which adversely affects the quality of life and work productivity. This study aimed to screen for migraine in HCPs working at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital and its drawbacks on work productivity.MethodsThis door-to-door survey was held at the workplace, targeting HCPs at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital. Migraine screening was conducted based on the migraine diagnostic criteria of the International Classification of Headache Disorders— 3rd edition. Healthcare providers with migraines were requested to answer the Migraine Disability Assessment Test (MIDAS) and the Work Productivity and Activity Impairment Questionnaire—General Health Version (WPAI-GH). Absenteeism, presenteeism, and economic loss due to migraine were calculated.ResultsOne thousand one hundred seventy-nine HCPs working at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital agreed to participate in our survey. Only 387 (32.8%) participants met the diagnostic criteria of migraine, 31.8% of them had MIDAS grade-IV. The mean score of the WPAI-GH was 60.47 ± 40. The mean percent of absenteeism and presenteeism was 12.29% and 46%, respectively. The mean economic loss caused by absenteeism and presenteeism was $29.4 and $38.2, respectively. Participants with MIDAS grade-IV had significantly higher absenteeism, presenteeism, and economic loss than those in lower grades. Although 159 HCPs with migraine had indications for prophylactic treatment, only 73.6% received it.ConclusionAbout one-third of HCPs at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital suffered from migraines, which negatively affected their work productivity, with higher presenteeism than absenteeism. Strenuous efforts should be directed toward reducing the cost burden associated with migraine.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12883-026-05070-6.Keywords: Migraine, Absenteeism, Presenteeism, Economic loss, MIDAS, WPAI-GH

Introduction

Migraine is the second leading cause of living with disability for years, affecting about 15.2% of the population worldwide [1]. However, its prevalence ranged between 2.6% and 32% in Arab countries [2], while in Egypt, it was reported to be 10.51% [3]. However, the prevalence of migraine among healthcare providers (HCPs) had been significantly higher than that estimated in the general population [4].

Migraine adversely impacts the quality of life and causes a reduction in job satisfaction and work productivity [5–7]. Migraine-related work productivity loss is due to working with migraine (presenteeism) and work time missed due to migraine (absenteeism) [8]. Working with migraine attacks affects job status due to migraine symptoms, pain, and comorbid mood disturbance [9].

Healthcare providers in Egypt are exposed to stressful environments and burnout syndrome that may raise their migraine prevalence [10]. Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital is one of the largest medical institutes in Egypt, the Middle East, and North Africa. It serves more than two million patients a year through 36 departments, 44 specialized units, 5,500 beds over 11 hospitals, 3347 staff members, and 18,543 undergraduate and postgraduate students [11]. Research about the impact of migraine on work productivity in Egyptian medical institutes is scant.

Hence, this study aimed to screen for migraine in HCPs at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital and evaluate its effect on their work productivity. Highlighting the disease burden helps introduce migraine education programs and optimize management.

Methods

This was a cross-sectional survey of healthcare workers currently working at different departments of Kasr Al-Ainy School of Medicine from March to June 2024. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee at the Faculty of Medicine, Cairo University (N37-2024).

This door-to-door study was held at the workplace, targeting all positions of healthcare workers at Kasr Al-Ainy School of Medicine (nurses, house officers, residents, assistant lecturers, lecturers, assistant professors, and professors).

Pregnant women and patients with chronic diseases other than migraine, e.g., hypertension, diabetes, or autoimmune diseases, were excluded from this study.

A total of 1487 HCPs were approached to participate in the study. Of these, 308 members were excluded (9 pregnant women and 253 individuals with chronic medical diseases), and 46 participants declined participation. Consequently, 1,179 HCPs working at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital agreed to participate in our survey.

Migraine Disability Assessment Test (MIDAS) [15]:

Migraine Disability Assessment Test (MIDAS) [15]:

It was used to screen for the effect of migraine on work and social life over the past three months. The patient’s score ranges from 0 to 90 and is divided into grades from I to IV. The higher the score and grade of the patient, the more severe the disability caused by migraine.

4.Work productivity and activity impairment questionnaire - general health version (WPAIGH) [16].

Work productivity and activity impairment questionnaire - general health version (WPAIGH) [16].

It consists of six questions used to assess the impact of health problems on work and non-work-related activity over the past week. However, some adaptations were made to the original instrument, as we asked about the total number of hours that the individual has missed in the last month rather than the last week. This is justified to make it easier for us to calculate absenteeism and presenteeism during the month. The higher the scores, the greater the impact on work productivity.

After filling in the items of the questionnaire, the percentage of work time missed due to migraine (absenteeism) would be calculated as follows: [hours of work missed due to migraine in the last month/ (hours of work missed due to migraine in the last month + actual working hours worked in that month)] × 100. Also, the presenteeism would be calculated using the following equation: Degree of migraine affected work productivity in the last month on a scale from zero to ten/10 × 100 [17].

Economic loss caused by absenteeism (per year) was calculated as the number of days off during the last three months (according to responses to MIDAS) multiplied by the salary per person per day and then converted to an annual amount per person by multiplying by four [18]. On the other hand, economic loss caused by presenteeism (per year) is the number of days when work productivity is reduced to less than half during the last 3 months (according to MIDAS responses) multiplied by half the daily salary per person and then converted to an annual amount per person by multiplying by four [9]. At the time of paper writing, one dollar was equivalent to 48.9 Egyptian pounds.

For example:

A candidate included in the current study was absent from work due to migraine for 6 days during the last three months, and his productivity was reduced by at least half for three days during the same duration, given that his salary is 10,000 Egyptian pounds/ month. Accordingly, his economic loss due to absenteeism = 6 × (10000/30) = 2000 Egyptian pounds, then converted to an annual amount by multiplying by four. So, the estimated economic loss for him per year is 2000 × 4 = 8000 Egyptian pounds. On the other hand, his economic loss due to presenteeism = 3 × (5000/30) = 500 Egyptian pounds, so his estimated economic loss due to presenteeism per year is 500 × 4 = 2000 Egyptian pounds.

Sample size

The required sample size was calculated using the formula provided by Open Epi:

Assuming 80% power, 97% Confidence level, and 5% level of significance, an estimated proportion of overall work productivity loss due to migraine is 39.9% [19], and a design effect of 2 for a population of 3350 healthcare workers in Kasr Al-Ainy School of Medicine [11]. The minimum required sample size for this study will be 801 participants; then 10% (n = 80) should be added to compensate for possible dropouts, so 881 participants would be required.

Statistical analysis

The Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) version 23 was used for data analysis. Mean and standard deviation, median, and IQR were used to summarize quantitative data, while frequencies were used for qualitative data. A bivariate relationship was displayed in cross-tabulations, and proportions were compared using the chi-square test. T-independent, one-way ANOVA tests were used to compare quantitative data. The significance level was set at a probability (P) value < 0.05.

Prophylactic treatment among HCPs with migraine

Although 159 HCPs with migraine had at least one indication for prophylactic treatment, actually only 117/159 (73.6%) received migraine prophylactic treatment.

Notably, 42/159 (26.4%) were reluctant to take prophylactic treatment. Fear of side effects was the most reported reason behind their hesitancy, as reported by 26/42 (61.9%), followed by ignorance that they should take the treatment (n = 10/42, 23.9%) and, finally, adherence problems (n = 6/42, 14.2%).

Discussion

In this study, the estimated prevalence of migraine among HCPs working at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital was 32.8%; MIDAS grading IV (severe disability) accounted for 31.8% (about one-third), with the highest prevalence of severe disability reported among nurses. Similarly, a study in South China [20] estimated the prevalence of migraine at 25.9% among HCPs, with significant predominance among nurses. This could be attributed to the higher burnout and psychological stress in the work environment among HCPs in Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital, a tertiary care center [21]; all are known precipitating factors of migraine attacks [22, 23]. In a previous Egyptian study, 66.7% of the general practitioners (GPs) had high burnout [10]. In contrast, the prevalence in our study was higher than that stated by another Egyptian study in which migraine prevalence among physicians in Suez Canal Hospital was estimated at 19.32% [24].

The highest migraine severity (MIDAS grade IV) was noticed among nurses (29.3%), followed by young physicians (lecturers, assistant lecturers). High migraine prevalence among nurses was concordant with rates among nurses in Japan (20.5%) [25] and in Mexico (77.3%) [26]. The medical work environment in Egypt is more stressful for nurses than for physicians, putting the former group at a significantly higher risk of developing high levels of burnout syndrome [27]. Migraine severity is also related to working night shifts due to disturbance of the circadian rhythm and sleep cycles, which is more pronounced among nurses [28].

In agreement with the literature [7, 29, 30], the present study supported the negative impact of migraine on work productivity as well as the inverse relation between the severity of the attacks and the percentage of reduced productivity and missed days. Working during migraine attacks results in decreased productivity due to migraine-related pain symptoms, cognitive decline, accompanying anxiety, and depressive symptoms [29].

Surprisingly, despite having one-third of included HCPs with severe migraine (MIDAS grade IV), the presenteeism is much higher than the absenteeism (46% versus 12.29%, respectively), about 4-fold higher. The low percentage of absenteeism could be attributed to the difficulty of obtaining a day off at the medical institution, which forces workers to come to work despite suffering from severe pain and subsequently increasing presenteeism. So, it is worth creating a work culture that supports getting medical leave due to migraine.

Estimating the economic loss resulting from the negative impact of migraine on work productivity has been studied in previous research at various institutions [9, 19]. The current study estimated the economic loss caused by absenteeism at $29.4, while that caused by presenteeism was $38.2. Furthermore, such economic losses were significantly correlated with the higher MIDAS grades.

Comparing our results to a study in a Japanese information technology company [9], the economic loss due to presenteeism for migraine was calculated to be $375.4. The costs related to presenteeism and absenteeism were estimated at $1296 and $370, respectively, among employees in two multinational banks in Malaysia [19]. It should be noted that the lower economic loss in the present study than in the previously mentioned studies should be related to the mean salary of the HCPs, where the mean wage is much lower in our study compared to that of Japan or Malaysia [9, 19].

Regarding treatment seeking, 26.4% of HCPs were reluctant to take prophylactic treatment, although they were indicated for it. This finding matches a previous study that reported that 21.7% of German neurologists and pain specialists refused to take a prophylactic treatment mainly due to fear of side effects. The results highlighted the importance of providing CGRP mAbs with insurance coverage to encourage employees to receive treatment with fewer side effects. Interestingly, the cost-effectiveness of eptinezumab is confirmed from a work productivity perspective for patients with at least 4 MMDs [31, 32]. The utility value was associated with substantial Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY) gains over 5 years [33].

Based on studies of work productivity and activity impairment [34], the key predictors of reduced productivity include a combination of demographic factors and specific job characteristics such as older age and stressful work characteristics. However, the current study revealed that younger age is a predictor of higher work productivity impairment due to migraine, which is consistent with the concept that suggests the prevalence of migraine peaks in the productive years and typically declines with advancing age [35]. It should be noted that the interpretation of age effects warrants caution because age is rarely a direct predictor of an outcome. Instead, age behaves as a confounder, meaning that it may be associated with exposure (e.g., job, stressful lifestyle), which are often the real drivers of outcomes rather than age itself. Furthermore, working as a nurse is often associated with factors that predict reduced productivity, specifically through high levels of work-related stress, burnout, and physical/emotional exhaustion [36].

Study strengths and limitations

This study is among the first to assess migraine in HCPs in a tertiary care center in Egypt; given the scarcity of regional data, it provides important local epidemiological evidence. Additionally, the study focuses on HCPs, a high-risk occupational group due to their exposure to shift work stress, sleep deprivation and psychological pressure. It assesses both presenteeism and absenteeism, allowing for a more accurate and comprehensive estimation of productivity compared to studies that measure absenteeism alone. Furthermore, linking migraine burden to the health care productivity system in a tertiary care center highlights not only the individual impact but also the broader economic burden.

However, there were some drawbacks. The first limitation is related to possible recall bias during the questionnaire due to the time that may have passed between migraine attacks and our interview. Another limitation is the relatively small number of certain categories included in the study, such as “professors,” as their working hours are less than the rest of the categories. Selection criteria are another limitation of the study, as pregnant women and individuals with chronic diseases other than migraine were excluded. While this approach was made to reduce confounding factors, as these conditions are known to affect work productivity independent of migraine status, it limits the generalizability of the results. Finally, the validity of the adapted recall period of the WPAI instrument (e.g., monthly instead of a 1-week recall) raises a major concern. The original validation study was based on a 1-week recall [16]. Nevertheless, longer recall periods appeared more appropriate to align with chronic disorders such as migraine. Yet, longer recall periods may introduce higher recall bias than shorter recall periods [37, 38].

Conclusion

About one-third of healthcare providers at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital displayed migraines, which negatively affected their work productivity. Presenteeism was higher than absenteeism. The highest prevalence and severity were noticed among nurses. The study highlighted the economic burden of migraines and the unmet needs of migraine management at Kasr Al-Ainy Hospital.

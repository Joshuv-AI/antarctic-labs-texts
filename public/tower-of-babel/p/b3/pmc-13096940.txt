Primary amenorrhea with tall stature
Authors: Sravanthi Mashetty, Arun Guddeti, KVS Hari Kumar
Published: 2026

An 18-year-old girl presented with primary amenorrhea, absent breast development, and poor academic performance. The patient’s mother noticed increased height at the age of 13 years without other pubertal features. The patient had frontal bossing, slanting palpebral fissures, strabismus, and macrocephaly as shown in Figure 1. The patient’s height was 170.5 cm (standard deviation, ± 1.9) with a Tanner stage of A1B2P1. The patient’s luteinizing hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone, and estradiol were 16 mIU/mL, 55 mIU/mL, and 7.5 pg/mL, respectively, suggesting hypergonadotropic hypogonadism. The rest of the hormonal panel was normal. The patient’s karyotype was 46XX, and whole exome sequencing revealed a heterozygous deletion of chr3:g.(?114339005)_(114380941_114389004), suggestive of Primrose syndrome (PS). The patient was treated with gradually increasing doses of estradiol, with a plan to subsequently add progesterone. PS is caused by a de novo ZBTB20 pathogenic missense variant or a microdeletion of chromosome 3q13.31. PS is characterized by intellectual disability, hypogonadism, and typical facial features. Endocrine abnormalities in PS include delayed puberty, cryptorchidism, hypothyroidism, and dysglycemia.

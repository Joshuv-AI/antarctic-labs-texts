Moving Lumbar Drain Placement to IR From the Operating Room Creates Time and Cost Savings
Authors: David K Lerner, Saawan Patel, Chau Phung, Khashayar Eshaghi, Alan D Workman, Glenn Pennington, Robert Stetson, Bryan A Pukenas, Joshua H Atkins, Jennifer E Douglas, Michael A Kohanski, James N Palmer, Nithin D Adappa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveSurgeons often place lumbar drains (LD) perioperatively in patients undergoing repair for spontaneous cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) rhinorrhea. Patient factors and inflation‐adjusted perioperative costs were compared between groups.ResultsEighty patients were identified, including 42 patients with OR LD placement and 38 patients in IR. All LDs placed prior to August 2019 were performed in the OR, and all after in IR. Most patients were female (86.3%) with average patient age and BMI of 52.8 years and 37.8 kg/m2, respectively. Both direct labor costs and operating room time direct costs were significantly higher among the OR group ($7647.37 vs $6169.66, P = .020; $5150.96 vs $4472.51, P = .012). Within the United States there has been a growing emphasis over the past two decades on cost‐efficiency and value‐based healthcare over the past 2 decades. 1 , 2 This effort to rein in healthcare costs was spurred by the Affordable Care Act in 2010 and the subsequent Medical Access and CHIP Reauthorization Act in 2015, and more recently has been reinforced by the Center for Medicare and Medicaid services through their extension of bundled payment programs for care improvement through 2025. As the healthcare economic climate and focus on cost‐efficiency continues to evolve, it is essential for providers to both understand procedure‐related expenses as well as adapt and implement cost‐effective strategies of healthcare delivery.

Skull base surgeons commonly treat patients with spontaneous cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) rhinorrhea, a condition that has been associated with idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) and is increasing in frequency in association with the global obesity pandemic. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 Endoscopic repair is the preferred initial intervention for a patient with spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea, often in conjunction with placement of a lumbar drain (LD) for perioperative CSF diversion, measuring of intracranial pressure, and for instillation of fluorescein to aid in operative identification of the defect site. 3 , 4 , 6 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 As is the case with many procedures within Rhinology, the costs and cost drivers of endoscopic repair for spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea are poorly understood. Our group has previously explored procedure‐related costs within endoscopic sinus and skull base surgery in order to better define cost‐drivers of ambulatory functional endoscopic sinus surgery, 12 transsphenoidal pituitary surgery, 13 and endoscopic craniopharyngioma surgery. 14 We have also reported our implementation of a streamlined postoperative pathway that reduced cost and length of stay for endoscopic pituitary surgery patients. 15 Through these efforts we hope to promote more efficient delivery of sinus and skull base surgical and perioperative care.

In this study, we seek to better define costs associated with endoscopic repair of spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea with perioperative LD placement and report our cost outcomes in implementing a transition from LD placement in the operating room (OR) to a separate interventional radiology (IR) suite on the morning of surgery.

Methods

Approval was obtained by the University of Pennyslvania Health System Institutional Review Board for this retrospective review.

Study Population

We performed a retrospective study of all patients undergoing endoscopic CSF leak repair among patients with spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea with preprocedure LD placement in either the OR by anesthesia or in the IR suite at a single tertiary academic center from August 2015 to June 2023. The electronic medical record was reviewed for patient demographics, surgical details, hospital course, and cost variables. The collected characteristics include patient age, length of surgery, body mass index, ethnicity, insurance status, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), history of previous sinus surgery or encephalocele, location of the leak, repair technique, opening pressure, duration of lumbar drain utilization, length of hospitalization, length of intensive care unit stay, and occurrence of postoperative nausea or vomiting.

Cost Variables

Perioperative data were provided by the University of Pennsylvania Health System Finance Department and included total cost, total direct cost, fixed direct costs, variable direct labor costs, variable direct supply costs, and indirect costs for each patient encounter. Direct costs accounted for revenue‐generating activities such as laboratory tests, surgical instruments, and personnel services involved in patient care, while indirect costs encompassed non‐revenue activities such as administrative and facility overhead. OR cost reflects activity directly involved in patient care and revenue‐generating activity and therefore represent a direct cost. Fixed direct costs included non‐volume‐sensitive expenses such as supervisory staff labor, whereas variable direct costs captured expenses that varied with patient volume, such as nursing and supply costs. Costs were adjusted for inflation using the Consumer Price Index as reported by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Statistical Analysis

Univariate analyses were performed to compare patient characteristics, clinical factors, and cost outcomes between patients who underwent lumbar drain placement in the OR and those who had placement in the IR suite. Continuous variables were compared using unpaired t‐tests, while categorical variables were analyzed using chi‐square or Fisher's exact tests. Multivariate linear regression was used to identify independent predictors of cost, adjusting for patient‐specific factors such as age, BMI, CCI, history of previous sinus surgery, and perioperative clinical variables. Variables with P < .10 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate models with statistical significance defined as P < .05. Data analysis was performed using Stata (version 17.0; StataCorp LLC) and Prism (version 9.4.0a; GraphPad Software Inc.).

Results

Demographic and Clinical Characteristics

Eighty patients were identified, including forty‐two patients who underwent LD placement in the OR (52.5%) and 38 patients in IR. Most patients were female (86.3%) with an average age of 53.3 years and a mean BMI of 37.8 kg/m². Of these patients, seventy‐seven were non‐Hispanic (96.3%). The majority (70%) had commercial insurance at the time of surgery. The mean Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) was 1.46.

The mean operative time was 165.1 minutes, with a standard deviation of 54.1 minutes. The common leak locations included the ethmoid sinus (n = 46, 57.5%), sphenoid sinus (n = 32, 40%), and frontal sinus (n = 1, 1.25%). Patient and Clinical Characteristics Between OR and IR Groups

When comparing the OR group with the IR group, nonsurgical OR time was significantly shorter among patients with LD placement by IR (80.7 vs 97.6 minutes, P = .014). However, there was no significant difference in total OR time between OR and IR groups (169.9 vs 159.8 minutes, P = .408).

Demographic characteristics were similar between the two groups. The average age was 53.2 years in the OR group and 53.3 years in the IR group (P = .976). Similarly, there was no significant difference between patients within the OR and IR groups in terms of gender or ethnicity (16.7% vs 13.2% male, P = .884; 97.6% vs 94.7% White Non‐Hispanic, P = .498) (Table 2).

Costs Between OR and IR Groups

There was no significant difference between OR and IR groups in total cost ($32,166.9 vs $30,075, P = .33), direct cost ($20,943.9 vs $19,974.5, P > .999), or indirect cost ($11,223.0 vs $10,100.6, P = .12). There was also no significant difference between OR and IR groups in fixed direct costs ($8270.6 vs $8716.0, P = .41) or variable direct supply costs ($5026.0 vs $5087.1, P = .87) (Table 3).

Discussion

Healthcare expenditures within the United States are dramatically higher than comparable developed countries and continue to rise, spurring an widespread effort to deliver more cost‐effective and value‐based care. 16 Until recently, there has been limited insight into the costs associated with the delivery of sinus and skull base surgical care. 12 , 13 , 14 Our group has previously explored the cost drivers associated with endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery and identified a systems‐based approach to reducing healthcare costs through implementation of a streamlined post‐operative care pathway. 15 In this work, we analyzed costs associated with spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea, a surgical condition that is increasing in frequency in parallel with the global obesity epidemic. 6 , 7 In particular, we analyzed the cost components associated with perioperative care and evaluate the cost‐savings of a transition of preoperative LD placement from the OR by anesthesia to an IR suite.

In our study, we identified an approximately 20% reduction in nonprocedure OR time by transitioning preoperative LD placement from the OR to a separate IR suite on the morning of surgery. Furthermore, direct labor costs and OR time direct costs were lowered by roughly $1500 and $600, respectively. Our findings suggest that providers performing endoscopic CSF leak repair with pre‐operative LD placement would greatly reduce OR‐time related costs by performing drain placement outside of the OR. Furthermore, streamlining the perioperative encounter for spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea patients allows for increased productivity by the Otolaryngology team, which may perform an additional procedure prior to the CSF leak repair case.

Our findings are consistent with previous literature emphasizing the outsized role of OR efficiency as a driver of surgical encounter costs. 17 , 18 , 19 Prior works within the general surgery literature have outlined systems‐based interventions in order to improve OR efficiency including through standardized tasks and effective team communication. 17 , 20 Within Rhinology in particular, we previously noted both OR time costs and procedure length as significant determining factors of cost associated with functional endoscopic sinus surgery for inflammatory sinus disease. 12 Our work here builds upon these established principles of cost‐effective care and supports an actionable measure for Rhinologists to improve their OR efficiency.

One limitation of our study is its single‐institution nature, which may limit the generalizability of its conclusions given the significant patient‐, geographic‐, and institution‐related variability in healthcare costs. Additionally, there are varying degrees in difficulty of endoscopic repair for spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea (ie, requiring external approach or repair of multiple skull base defects) that we were not able to control for between groups in this study given the relatively small sample size. However, our finding of a significant difference between group in non‐procedure OR time reflects a difference in efficiency independent of surgical difficulty. Lastly, we found significant differences between groups in terms of both non‐procedure OR time as well as variable direct labor costs, whereas there was an absolute difference between groups in total OR time and total costs that was not statistically significant. We suspect that this reflects the limitations of our sample size. Future studies with a larger sample size will be necessary to confirm whether these changes translate into significantly decreased total OR time and total costs whereas this study is powered only to detect difference in component cost and time variables.

Conclusions

Surgeons often elect to place lumbar drains (LD) perioperatively in patients undergoing repair for spontaneous CSF rhinorrhea. In this study, we analyzed the cost implications of a transition from LD placement in the same OR to a separate IR suite on the morning of surgery. Transition from routine LD placement in the OR to a separate IR suite was associated with savings of about $1500 in direct labor costs per procedure for the hospital, reflecting the reduction in OR time and associated personnel costs. LD placement in a separate suite also may allow for increased OR efficiency and productivity by the Otolaryngology team, which may perform an additional procedure prior to the CSF leak repair surgery.

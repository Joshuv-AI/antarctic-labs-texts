Constructing a bladder cancer prognostic model related to exosome using machine learning and identifying THBS1 as a potential target
Authors: Huilong Shen, Fuchun Zheng, Xiao Tang, Zifang Ma, Xueting Tao, Zhaojin Deng, Zhijun Yao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExosome-mediated molecular processes significantly influence bladder cancer (BCa) development, but clinically applicable exosome-based prognostic systems are still lacking. This research aims to develop an exosome-related prognostic model using machine learning approaches and discover potential therapeutic targets.MethodsWe implemented ten machine learning algorithms with eighty-one combinatorial configurations to analyze BCa transcriptomic data. Model validation incorporated time-dependent receiver operating characteristic analysis, Kaplan-Meier survival curves, nomogram assessment, and Cox regression. Biological mechanisms were explored through immune microenvironment evaluation (CIBERSORT) and functional enrichment analysis (GSEA). Molecular docking using PubChem, PDB structures, and CB-DOCK2 identified potential targets, followed by experimental validation including quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), Western Blotting (WB), cell proliferation (CCK-8, colony formation), migration (Transwell, wound healing), EMT marker detection, and tail vein injection-based lung metastasis assays in nude mice.ResultsAnalysis revealed 132 differentially expressed genes specific to BCa, which were subsequently refined to 15 prognosis-associated genes through univariate Cox regression. The machine learning-derived prognostic model (MLDPM) outperformed existing clinical indicators, effectively stratifying patients into distinct risk categories. High-risk patients demonstrated characteristics of immune evasion and poorer survival outcomes. Computational docking analysis identified strong molecular interactions between THBS1/MMP9/CXCL12 and standard chemotherapeutic compounds. Importantly, suppression of THBS1 expression significantly inhibited BCa cell migration and invasion.ConclusionThis study establishes the first machine learning-based exosomal prognostic system for BCa and identifies THBS1 as both a potential biomarker and therapeutic target. The combined computational and experimental methodology offers a new approach for personalized BCa treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12935-025-04027-y.

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BCa) is a prevalent malignancy of the urinary tract, contributing substantially to global cancer morbidity and mortality [1]. Epidemiological data indicate over 500,000 new BCa cases annually, with approximately 210,000 deaths each year [2]. Based on tumor invasiveness, BCa is classified into non-muscle-invasive (NMIBC) and muscle-invasive (MIBC) subtypes, with NMIBC representing the majority of diagnoses [3]. However, even after surgical resection and intravesical therapy, NMIBC exhibits a high recurrence rate, with 50% of patients relapsing within the first year and 30% progressing to MIBC [4]. The prognosis for MIBC remains poor, with a 5-year survival rate below 50%, dropping to just 6% in metastatic cases [5]. Current therapeutic approaches face challenges, including suboptimal chemotherapy response and acquired drug resistance. Consequently, discovering reliable prognostic biomarkers is essential to enhance treatment strategies and improve patient outcomes in BCa.

Exosomes are small, single-membrane organelles with a diameter ranging approximately from 40 to 160 nm [6]. These vesicles function as critical mediators for transporting various biomolecules, including proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, and glycoconjugates [7]. By shuttling these molecular cargos, exosomes can remodel the extracellular matrix and facilitate intercellular communication through the transfer of signaling molecules [8]. Recent studies have demonstrated that exosomes play a pivotal role in modulating biological functions in both normal and cancerous cells, particularly in tumor immunomodulation, angiogenesis, invasion, and metastasis [9–12]. In the context of BCa, exosomes contribute to tumor progression by delivering and releasing either tumor-promoting or tumor-suppressing factors, thereby altering the tumor microenvironment and influencing tumor dynamics [13]. Consequently, comprehensive analysis of the molecular composition and functional characteristics of BCa-associated exosomes may elucidate underlying oncogenic mechanisms and provide valuable opportunities for early diagnosis and targeted therapeutic interventions.

In this investigation, we identified 15 clinically significant prognostic genes and developed a predictive model through the integration of 101 combinatorial algorithms. This model enabled stratification of BCa patients into distinct risk groups based on calculated risk scores. The clinical utility of this model lies in its potential to: (1) enhance survival prediction accuracy, (2) facilitate personalized therapeutic decision-making, (3) identify novel immunotherapeutic targets, and (4) guide targeted drug selection for enhanced treatment efficacy between risk groups. These findings hold substantial translational value for advancing immunological research, refining precision medicine approaches, and ultimately improving patient outcomes in BCa management.

Methods

Collection of databases

Transcriptomic data were systematically collected from multiple public repositories. Specifically, RNA-sequencing data comprising 19 normal bladder tissues and 412 BCa specimens were acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Furthermore, we obtained the gene expression profile dataset GSE13507 containing 256 BCa samples from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). For exosome-related gene identification, we extracted 1143 candidate genes from GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/) using the following selection criteria: (1) search term “exosome” and (2) relevance score threshold >2. The complete list of 1143 initially identified exosome-related genes is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Identification and enrichment analysis of the prognosis-related genes

Differential gene expression analysis was performed using the caret R package (v4.3.1) and Strawberry Perl, focusing on RNA-seq profiles from the TCGA cohort (412 bladder cancer specimens and 19 normal bladder tissues) to identify 132 differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Additionally, we conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses to explore the fundamental biological characteristics of these DEGs, employing the “org.Hs.eg.db” package and “clusterProfiler” package for these analyses.

Methodological framework for prognostic model development

Subsequent univariate Cox regression analysis of these DEGs revealed 15 prognostically significant genes for risk model construction. To ensure cross-sample comparability, gene expression values were standardized using z-score normalization. We implemented an ensemble machine learning framework integrating 10 distinct algorithms: random survival forest (RSF), Ridge regression, Lasso regression, generalized boosted regression modeling (GBM), supervised principal components (SuperPC), CoxBoost, partial least squares regression for Cox (plsRcox), elastic network (Enet), Stepwise Cox, and survival support vector machine (Survival-SVM). Through stochastic 10-fold cross-validation, 101 algorithm combinations were systematically generated to enhance predictive accuracy [14–16], with methodological details consistent with prior work by Hu et al. [15, 17]. The TCGA-BLCA cohort served as the training set for model construction, while the GSE13507 dataset was utilized for independent validation. Initial algorithms performed variable selection from the 15 prognostic genes, with final models derived from optimized variable combinations. Model performance was rigorously evaluated using the concordance index (C-index), with the algorithm combination demonstrating the highest average C-index across both cohorts selected as the optimal predictive model.

Validation of the MLDP

To assess the autonomous predictive capacity of clinical parameters regarding overall survival (OS), both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed. The predictive accuracy of the BCa prognostic model and conventional clinicopathological parameters was evaluated through time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyzed using the “timeROC” R package. Additionally, we quantitatively compared the C-index of our machine learning-derived prognostic model (MLDPM) with those of established clinical predictors to demonstrate its superior discriminative capability. The C-index, which quantifies the model’s concordance between predicted and observed outcomes, was interpreted such that higher values reflect enhanced prognostic performance.

Developing and evaluating a prognostic nomogram

We developed a prognostic nomogram using the rms package in R to estimate 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS probabilities. The nomogram visually represents the predictive model by quantifying individual variable contributions through a point-scale system, where cumulative points translate to specific outcome probabilities. Model performance was assessed through calibration analysis, where agreement between nomogram-predicted probabilities and actual observed outcomes was graphically evaluated using calibration curves. These analyses confirmed the model’s predictive accuracy by demonstrating strong concordance between estimated and empirical survival outcomes.

Enrichment and immune infiltration analysis

To elucidate distinct underlying molecular mechanisms between high- and low-risk groups, we performed comparative Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA). Following DEGs analysis, all genes were ranked according to their log2 fold-change values. To elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms and associated signaling cascades, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using the “clusterProfiler” package. The five most statistically enriched biological pathways were identified for graphical display.

We systematically characterized immune cell infiltration patterns between risk groups using seven established computational methods: TIMER, XCELL, CIBERSORT, EPIC, MCPcounter, QUANTISEQ, and CIBERSORT-ABS. This multi-algorithm approach enabled comprehensive quantification of tumor microenvironment composition in BCa patients, with TCGA-derived infiltration estimates serving as the primary data source. Inter-group differences in immune cell subsets were statistically evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (non-parametric, two-tailed). Resultant data were visualized through bubble plots generated with R packages including scales, limma, ggtext, and ggplot2. Parallel assessment of immune checkpoint molecule expression and Tumor Immune Microenvironment (TME) scores was performed using ggpubr, with boxplot visualizations to highlight differential activation patterns between risk strata.

Molecular docking

The three-dimensional molecular structures of bioactive compounds were acquired from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). Corresponding receptor proteins for key target genes were downloaded from the Protein Data Bank (PDB, http://www.rcsb.org/). Both ligand and protein structures were subsequently submitted to the DockThor web server (https://dockthor.lncc.br/v2/) for computational docking analysis. According to DockThor scoring metrics, stronger molecular interactions were characterized by lower binding energy values, reflecting greater complex stability. Resulting docking poses were visualized using the CB-DOCK2 platform (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/).

Drug sensitivity analysis

We utilized the ‘pRRophetic’ R package to estimate half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) of multiple chemotherapeutic agents between risk-stratified groups [18]. This computational approach enabled comparative assessment of differential drug sensitivity patterns. Statistical thresholds were established a priori, with P values below 0.05 considered significant, and the comparative pharmacogenomic data were visualized using boxplot representations.

Database analysis of the THBS1

Single-cell RNA sequencing data were obtained from the Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub (TISCH) database (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org), a specialized resource for TME investigations [19]. TISCH facilitates comprehensive analysis of TME heterogeneity across diverse datasets and cell types. For validation purposes, the Kaplan-Meier (KM) Plotter resource was employed to evaluate the predictive performance of our risk model, while protein-level expression of prognostic markers was verified using the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) [20].

Verification of THBS1 expression in BCa specimens

Paired BCa tissues and adjacent normal tissues were obtained from 10 patients undergoing treatment at Affiliated Hengyang Hospital of Hunan Normal University & Hengyang Central Hospital (Hengyang, China). Total RNA was extracted and subjected to quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis using SYBR Green master mix (Roche) with β-actin serving as the endogenous control. All reactions were performed in technical triplicates to ensure reproducibility. The following primer sequences were used for THBS1 amplification:

Forward: 5′- TGGAAATGTGGTGCTTGTCC-3′.

Reverse: 5′- CCATTGTGGTTGAAGCAGGC-3′.

Isolation and identification of T24-derived exosomes

To isolate exosomes, a series of centrifugation steps are first performed to remove dead cells and debris. The supernatant is then processed using a 100 kDa ultrafiltration tube, followed by ultracentrifugation at 1,000,000 × g. The supernatant is discarded, and the resulting pellet is resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), filtered through a 0.22 μm filter, and stored at −80 °C for subsequent use. The protein content of the concentrated Exo suspension is quantified using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (23,227, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Western Blot analysis is conducted to confirm the presence of the exosomal surface markers CD63 and Calnexin.

Western blotting

The cells were subjected to centrifugation and lysed on ice for 30 min using RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Following this, the cell lysate was processed with an ultrasonic homogenizer for 10 s and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 20 min. Finally, protein samples were resolved using gradient SDS-PAGE and then transferred onto a PVDF membrane (Cytiva, catalog number: 10600021).

Cell culture, reagents and small interfering RNA transfection

The T24 and BIU cell lines were obtained from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC, Shanghai, China). T24 cells were maintained in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C under 5% CO₂ atmosphere. BIU cells were cultured in complete RPMI-1640 medium (Corning). For gene silencing experiments, THBS1-specific small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) were synthesized by GenePharma Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). The siRNA sequences were: si1: 5′-AGACATCTTCCAAGCATATAA-3′; si2: 5′-TGACATCAGTGAGACCGATTT-3′. Cell transfections were performed using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol to inhibit THBS1 expression.

Migration and invasion

For wound healing assays, confluent monolayers of T24 and BIU cells in six-well plates were mechanically scratched using sterile pipette tips. Migration progression was quantitatively assessed by measuring wound closure distances at specified time intervals using phase-contrast microscopy. Transwell assays were performed using 24-well plates equipped with 8 μm polycarbonate membrane filters (Corning Inc., Corning, NY), either coated with or without Matrigel. T24 and BIU cells (20,000 per well) were seeded in serum-free DMEM or 1640 media in the upper chamber, while 700 µl of medium supplemented with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After incubation for 24–48 h, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 10 min. Cells that had migrated to the underside of the filter were visualized and imaged under a light microscope. Cell counts were taken from five random fields per insert, with each data point representing the mean from three wells.

Cell proliferation assays

Clonogenic assays were performed by plating 700 cells/well in 6-well plates. Cells were cultured until microscopic observation revealed approximately 50-cell colonies (typically 7–14 days). Colonies were then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (20 min, RT), stained with 0.1% crystal violet (800 µL/well, 15 min), gently washed with PBS, air-dried, and imaged using a standardized documentation system. After 48 h of cell treatment, T24 and BIU cells were seeded into 96-well plates, and cell proliferation was evaluated using a CCK-8 kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). At different time points, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was dispensed into each well. After incubation under light-protected conditions, the optical density was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

BALB/c nude mice lung metastasis model

Four-week-old BALB/c nude mice were obtained from Charles River Laboratories (Beijing, China). All animals in this study were handled humanely, in compliance with applicable animal welfare regulations, policies, and guidelines. To establish a tumor metastasis model, luciferase-transduced T24 cells—either shControl- or shTHBS1-expressing, at a density of 1 × 10⁶ cells per mouse—were administered to the mice via tail vein injection, with 3 mice assigned to each group. Lung metastasis monitoring was initiated on the 15th day following cell injection: each mouse was injected with D-Luciferin (YEASEN, 40902ES02) at a dose of 5 mg/kg, and 5 min later, in vivo imaging was performed using a LAGO imager equipped with Aura imaging software (Spectral Instruments Imaging) at the Animal Imaging Facility. Two weeks after the initial imaging, the mice were humanely euthanized. Subsequently, researchers with relevant expertise quantified the number of pulmonary metastatic foci through visual inspection.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (v4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing) and GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software). A two-tailed p-value threshold of < 0.05 was established a priori to determine statistical significance. To ensure experimental reproducibility, all assays included three independent biological replicates.

Results

Enrichment analysis of BCa patient samples

The experimental workflow is illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1. Through rigorous differential expression analysis (|log2FC| >1, FDR < 0.05), we identified 132 exosome-associated DEGs (Fig. 1A, B). Functional annotation of these DEGs yielded two principal insights: GO term analysis highlighted significant enrichment in key biological processes related to chromatin and chromosome regulation (protein localization to CENP-A containing chromatin, telomere organization), cellular components primarily involving centromeric chromatin and nucleosomal complexes (CENP-A containing nucleosome, nucleosome), and molecular functions including extracellular matrix binding, chemokine binding, and roles in chromatin structure and cell adhesion (Fig. 1C); KEGG pathway analysis showed prominent involvement in phagosome formation and actin cytoskeleton regulation (Fig. 1D). These coordinated mechanisms implicate exosome-mediated processes as potentially pivotal drivers of BCa progression.

MLDPM construction and validation

Univariate Cox regression analysis (p < 0.05) was performed to screen 132 candidate genes, from which 15 prognostic genes (11 upregulated and 4 downregulated) were selected (Fig. 2A). These 15 genes were used as input for model construction. A total of 101 algorithm combinations were generated from 10 machine learning methods, with the TCGA-BLCA cohort serving as the training set and GSE13507 as the validation set (Fig. 2B). The plsRcox algorithm showed the highest average C-index value of 0.614 among all combinations, demonstrating its superior predictive performance. Risk stratification using the model-derived median risk score segregated patients into distinct prognostic cohorts, with KM curves demonstrating superior survival outcomes in low-risk versus high-risk patients (Fig. 2C, D).

Prognostic nomogram development and evaluation

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses incorporating risk score, age, and stage confirmed the independent prognostic value of the MLDPM for BCa patients (univariate HR = 2.428, 95% CI:1.801–3.274, p < 0.001; multivariate HR = 2.131, 95% CI:1.564–2.905, p < 0.001; Fig. 3A, B). These results demonstrated the model’s robust association with patient risk stratification. Time-dependent concordance analysis revealed the risk score’s superior discriminatory capacity compared to individual clinical variables (Fig. 3C). The MLDPM demonstrated discriminative performance with time-dependent AUCs of 0.648 (1-year), 0.690 (3-year), and 0.724 (5-year) for survival prediction (Fig. 3D). Notably, the model’s predictive accuracy surpassed conventional clinicopathological parameters including gender, tumor stage, and histological grade (Fig. 3E and Supplementary Table 2), establishing its enhanced clinical utility for BCa prognosis assessment. We developed a prognostic nomogram incorporating gender, age, tumor stage, and risk score to predict OS in BCa patients (Fig. 3F). This tool allows clinicians to estimate patient outcomes using readily available clinical parameters. Validation analysis showed the nomogram achieved a C-index of 0.688 (95% CI: 0.645–0.729), with calibration curves demonstrating good agreement between predicted and observed survival (Fig. 3G). These results indicate this combined clinical-molecular model provides reliable prognostic assessment for BCa. Pathway analysis through GSEA identified distinct biological processes between risk groups. The high-risk group showed enrichment for ECM-receptor interaction and focal adhesion pathways, while the low-risk group exhibited predominant activity in cytochrome P450-mediated drug metabolism pathways (Fig. 3H, I). These findings suggest differential molecular mechanisms underlying the prognostic stratification.

Immune analysis

To comprehensively assess immunotherapy responsiveness, we systematically analyzed 37 immune checkpoint molecules across both risk groups, focusing on the B7/CD28 family (key regulators of T-cell activation) and TNF superfamily (critical for immune signaling). Comparative analysis revealed distinct expression patterns: elevated risk scores correlated with upregulated expression of CD44, HAVCR2, IDO2, PDCD1, CD276, TNFSF4, CD274, CD27, CD28, TNFRSF9, BTLA, PDCD1LG2, TNFRSF8, TNFSF18, CD86, LAIR1, TNFRSF18, CD160, ICOS, NRP1, TNFSF9, CD48, CD244, CD200R1, CTLA4, TNFSF14, LAG3, CD40LG, TIGIT, TNFRSF4, CD200, CD70, CD80, and IDO1, while showing downregulation of TNFRSF25, TNFRSF14, and LGALS9 (Fig. 4A).

Microenvironment evaluation demonstrated significantly higher Stromal, Immune, and ESTIMATE scores in high-risk patients (p < 0.001) (Fig. 4B). Multi-algorithm correlation analysis (seven methods) confirmed robust associations between MLDPM scores and immune cell infiltration patterns. While most immune cell types showed positive correlations with risk scores, CD4 + central memory T cells, plasma B cells, and resting NK cells exhibited inverse relationships (Fig. 4C). To further characterize the tumor immune microenvironment, we evaluated immune functions using the ‘ssGSEA’ package. The results indicated that, except for immature dendritic cells (iDCs), natural killer (NK) cells, and Th2 cells, all other immune cell populations showed higher enrichment in high-risk tumor tissues (Fig. 4D). Consensus clustering of immune cell proportions classified BCa samples into four distinct subtypes (C1-C4), with differential risk group distributions: C1 (wound healing), C3 (inflammatory), and C4 (lymphocyte-depleted) subtypes were relatively more common in low-risk cases, while the C2 (IFN-γ-dominant) subtype showed a trend toward higher prevalence in high-risk patients, though this association did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 4E). This distribution pattern, despite the lack of statistical significance, aligns with the broader immune evasion characteristics observed in high-risk groups, such as elevated immune checkpoint molecule expression.

Molecular docking studies and identification of key therapeutic targets

Drug enrichment analysis of the 15 prognostic genes identified gemcitabine as a potential therapeutic agent for BCa (Fig. 5A and Supplementary Fig. 2). Network analysis revealed functional associations between the drug and three key genes (THBS1, CXCL12, and MMP9) (Fig. 5B). Molecular docking was performed using 3D structures of the compounds (from PubChem) and target proteins (from PDB) through the DockThor platform, with visualization via CB-DOCK2. The binding affinities demonstrated strong interactions: gemcitabine showed values of −7.5 (THBS1; Fig. 5C, D), −4.9 (CXCL12; Fig. 5E, F), and − 6.7 (MMP9; Fig. 5G, H) kcal/mol. Comparative analysis suggested THBS1 as the most promising target. Clinical validation using TCGA data confirmed that high expression of THBS1, CXCL12, and MMP9 correlated with poorer OS in BCa patients (Fig. 5I-K), with THBS1 emerging as the most significant prognostic marker through integrated molecular and survival analyses.

Drug sensitivity

Comparative analysis of therapeutic sensitivity revealed distinct drug response patterns between risk groups (Fig. S3A-L). The low-risk cohort showed enhanced sensitivity to Palbociclib (evidenced by significantly reduced IC50 values), while the high-risk group exhibited greater responsiveness to alternative agents. These pharmacogenomic findings enable risk-stratified treatment optimization, potentially improving clinical outcomes through personalized therapeutic selection.

Single-cell evaluation and verification of THBS1 using external datasets

Given THBS1’s prognostic significance and tissue-specific distribution, we analyzed its expression patterns across distinct cellular subpopulations using single-cell RNA-seq data (GSE145281 dataset; Fig. S4A-D). Notably, THBS1 expression was predominantly elevated in monocyte/macrophage (Fig. S4E-F). Independent validation using the HPA confirmed corresponding THBS1 protein upregulation in BCa specimens (Fig. S5G).

Using THBS1 performing in vitro and vivo verification

qPCR analysis of clinical specimens revealed significant THBS1 upregulation in BCa tumors compared to normal tissues (Fig. 6A). Results in Fig. 6B showed that the exosomal marker CD63 was present in T24-Exos, while the endoplasmic reticulum marker Calnexin was absent—this indicated the successful isolation of exosomes. Additionally, the molecule THBS1 was also detected in T24-Exos. For Fig. 6C, regarding epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), THBS1 knockdown led to the upregulation of E-cadherin (a key epithelial marker) and the downregulation of N-cadherin (a relevant mesenchymal marker). These findings support the role of THBS1 in tumor progression. Successful siRNA-mediated THBS1 knockdown (constructs S1/S2) in T24 and BIU cell lines was confirmed by qPCR (Fig. 6D). Functional analysis of THBS1 depletion in T24 and BIU cell lines revealed no significant effect on cell proliferation, as assessed by colony formation assay and CCK-8 assay (Fig. 6E-H). Contrastingly, Transwell migration, invasion and wound healing experiments demonstrated a marked decrease in BCa cell motility following THBS1 suppression, indicating its specific regulatory role in metastatic behavior (Fig. 7A-F). To investigate the role of THBS1 in in vivo metastasis, we established a BCa lung metastasis model via tail vein injection, where nude mice were injected with luciferase-expressing T24 cells transfected with either shControl or THBS1 knockout constructs. Bioluminescence imaging revealed a reduction in lung metastases in the THBS1 knockout group (Fig. 7G). Macroscopic examination of the lung surface showed fewer visible nodules in mice with THBS1 deficiency (Fig. 7H). Collectively, these results establish THBS1 as a critical regulator of BCa cell migration, with minimal influence on proliferative processes.

Discussion

BCa represents a highly aggressive malignancy with poor survival outcomes [21]. While pathological evaluation and TNM staging remain cornerstone diagnostic and prognostic approaches, their limited accuracy and sensitivity fail to adequately address the disease’s substantial tumor heterogeneity. Notably, patients at identical clinical stages frequently exhibit divergent therapeutic responses and clinical trajectories, underscoring the urgent need for novel molecular biomarkers to enhance prognostic stratification and improve patients’ quality of life. Exosomes serve as critical intercellular communication vehicles that transport bioactive molecules (including proteins, RNAs, DNAs, and lipids) to modulate recipient cell behavior [22, 23]. In BCa, exosome-mediated transfer of oncogenic or tumor-suppressive cargo dynamically remodels the tumor microenvironment and regulates neoplastic progression [13]. Consequently, systematic characterization of BCa-derived exosomal components and their functional properties may uncover novel oncogenic mechanisms while offering translational opportunities for early detection and precision therapeutics.

This investigation established a MLDPM using a 15-gene signature, employing the TCGA-BLCA cohort for model training and the GSE13507 dataset for external validation across 101 algorithmic combinations [24]. The risk classification framework exhibited robust prognostic discrimination, evidenced by significantly prolonged survival in low-risk versus high-risk subgroups. The model demonstrated strong predictive accuracy, as evidenced by time-dependent ROC analysis and reproducible C-index values across independent validation cohorts. Multivariate analysis established the risk score as an independent prognostic factor alongside traditional clinical parameters such as age and tumor stage. Notably, the MLDPM exhibited superior predictive accuracy compared to conventional clinicopathological parameters including gender, tumor stage, and histological grade, which aligns with its focus on capturing molecular-level processes underlying bladder cancer progression. Interestingly, while the model outperforms these parameters, its predictive performance is surpassed by age—a finding that likely reflects the multifaceted role of age in cancer prognosis. Age encompasses cumulative effects of immune senescence, comorbidity burden, and physiological decline, which collectively influence disease trajectory through systemic mechanisms not directly captured by our exosome-associated gene signature, which primarily reflects tumor-intrinsic biological features. This distinction does not diminish the model’s value; rather, it highlights the complementary nature of molecular signatures and clinical factors like age in comprehensive risk assessment. Together, these findings support the MLDPM as a valuable tool for risk stratification in real-world clinical practice, enhancing prognostic precision beyond traditional markers and aiding in personalized therapeutic decision-making for bladder cancer patients.

The TME plays a critical role in BCa progression, and our study identified significant differences in immune cell infiltration patterns between high- and low-risk BCa patients based on risk stratification. Notably, CD4 + T cells, particularly naive subsets, showed positive correlation with risk scores. While CD4 + T cells typically function as anti-tumor effectors, the accumulation of immature/resting populations in high-risk patients may indicate tumor-induced immunosuppression through multiple mechanisms [25], including functional impairment of cytotoxic lymphocytes and CD4 + T cell dysfunction, potentially contributing to poorer clinical outcomes in these patients [26]. Immunotherapy with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors like durvalumab and nivolumab has significantly advanced BCa treatment, showing efficacy across different disease stages [27]. Further, we observed that most immune cell types showed positive correlations with risk scores, whereas CD4⁺ central memory T cells, plasma B cells, and resting NK cells exhibited inverse relationships. This pattern may reflect distinct regulatory mechanisms in the TME of high-risk versus low-risk BCa. CD4⁺ central memory T cells, known for their role in maintaining long-term immune memory and mediating antitumor responses, and plasma B cells, which produce antibodies critical for adaptive immunity, may be depleted or functionally impaired in high-risk tumors—consistent with the immune evasion characteristics we observed (elevated immune checkpoint expression) [28]. Resting NK cells, which can transition to activated states to exert cytotoxic effects, might be less readily activated in high-risk contexts, contributing to reduced immunosurveillance [29]. Clinical trials demonstrate improved progression-free survival with durvalumab combinations in metastatic disease and enhanced disease-free survival with adjuvant nivolumab in high-risk MIBC [30]. Current approaches utilize biomarkers such as PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden for patient selection, while novel combination strategies aim to overcome therapeutic resistance, highlighting the need for further research to optimize immunotherapeutic responses and develop more effective treatment regimens.

Gemcitabine is a key chemotherapeutic agent in BCa, widely used in the gemcitabine-cisplatin (GC) regimen as first-line therapy for advanced/metastatic disease due to its efficacy and better tolerability than older regimens [31, 32]. It is also utilized in bladder-preserving chemoradiation for muscle-invasive tumors and as intravesical therapy for BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive disease [33]. Acting as a nucleoside analog, gemcitabine inhibits DNA synthesis through its incorporation into replicating DNA and suppression of ribonucleotide reductase, a critical enzyme for nucleotide synthesis [31]. Additionally, novel derivatives like gemcitabine-lauric acid conjugates bypass human equilibrative nucleoside transporter 1 dependence and exhibit potent antiproliferative effects in BCa cells [34]. These advancements underscore gemcitabine’s enduring role in BCa treatment through mechanistic innovation and combinatorial approaches. Emerging evidence indicates that M1 macrophage-derived exosomes potentiate the anti-tumor efficacy of gemcitabine in BCa [35]. In light of this, we conducted network analysis of MLDPM genes, which revealed functional interactions between gemcitabine and three exosome-related genes (THBS1, CXCL12, and MMP9). Based on comparative molecular docking analysis, THBS1 was selected as the core gene due to its superior binding affinity.

THBS1 represents a versatile extracellular matrix protein exhibiting potent angiogenesis-inhibiting characteristics, which is produced by diverse cellular populations. Accumulating evidence indicates elevated THBS1 expression across numerous aggressive tumor types, notably encompassing prostatic adenocarcinoma, pancreatic ductal carcinoma, glioblastoma multiforme, mammary carcinoma, biliary tract neoplasms, and cutaneous melanoma [36–38]. Beyond its role in angiogenesis, THBS1 has been shown to regulate immune responses and promote tumor cell metastasis [39]. In breast cancer specifically, high THBS1 expression levels are associated with poor patient prognosis [40]. While studies on THBS1 in BCa remain limited, our current findings reveal consistent patterns [41–43]: THBS1 is significantly overexpressed in BCa tissues and correlates with unfavorable clinical outcomes. Functional studies demonstrate that THBS1 knockdown markedly inhibits BCa cell migration, although it appears to have minimal effects on cell proliferation. These results align with observations in other cancer types and suggest THBS1 may play an important role in BCa progression, particularly in tumor metastasis.

In summary, our prognostic model enables effective risk stratification of BCa patients through quantitative risk scoring, providing clinicians with valuable predictive insights regarding tumor aggressiveness and survival outcomes to inform therapeutic decision-making (aggressive intervention versus conservative management). Importantly, we identified THBS1 as a promising exosome-related therapeutic target, offering novel biological insights for future treatment strategies. However, several limitations warrant consideration: (1) potential biases from retrospective public database analyses necessitate validation in additional independent cohorts; (2) the precise mechanistic role of THBS1 in BCa migration requires further elucidation; and (3) while demonstrating predictive potential for immunotherapy response, prospective studies with larger patient populations are essential to enhance the clinical reliability of our scoring system. These limitations notwithstanding, the MLDPM represents a robust integrative tool that may significantly contribute to personalized BCa management.

Conclusion

This study elucidates the critical involvement of exosomal gene networks in BCa pathogenesis. By employing an ensemble of 101 computational algorithms, we developed the MLDPM prognostic system, while simultaneously identifying THBS1 as a mechanistically promising target with translational potential for guiding future therapeutic innovations.

Precise regulation of missing linkers in MOF pervaporation membranes for desalination of hypersaline waters
Authors: Yingchao Dong, Casey De Finnda, Mao Fu, Xiangyong Zheng, Qiang Lyu, Min Zhao, Huanting Wang, Han-Qing Yu, Menachem Elimelech
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report a method for the precise regulation of intracrystalline missing-linkers to control the permeability and selectivity of metal-organic framework (MOF) membranes. The method is applied for the design of stable ceramic-based MOF-801 membranes for hypersaline water treatment via pervaporation. For efficient membrane growth, an in situ nano-seeding strategy was employed to provide nucleation sites followed by surfactant posttreatment to minimize cracks. Missing-linkers are regulated in MOF-801 membranes by altering the ratio of fumarate to formic acid, which positively enhances water transport by modifying the MOF-801 structure and chemistry. Specifically, missing-linkers enhance membrane structural hydrophilicity with stronger host-guest interaction energy, resulting in faster transport with a lower energy barrier by enlarging the pore window and pore cage. The MOF-801 membranes demonstrated near-perfect salt rejection (~99.9%) and high water flux, outperforming most state-of-the-art silica, MOF, and zeolite polycrystalline membranes for the treatment of both saline and hypersaline waters. Notably, the membranes exhibited stable desalination performance, highlighting their promising application potential. This work provides a strategy for the rational design of next-generation high-performance MOF nanochannel membranes for challenging water purification applications.Subject terms: Metal-organic frameworks, Pollution remediation

Introduction

To combat global water scarcity, desalination will play a critical role in tapping into unconventional water sources1,2. While reverse osmosis (RO) is the current paradigm for desalination, conventional RO cannot be used for hypersaline waters (salinity > ~10 wt.%) due to the ultrahigh osmotic pressures that must be overcome, which exceed the maximum hydraulic pressures that can be applied3–6. At these higher salinities, thermally-driven membrane processes have an advantage over RO as their operation is less sensitive to salinity7–9. A promising chemical-potential-driven membrane process is pervaporation (PV)10. A liquid feed stream (heated in some cases) is exposed to one side of the PV membrane, and a vacuum is applied to the other. Under these conditions, water traverses the PV membrane, vaporizes, and then condensed at the permeate side9,11,12. In this process, dissolved salts are retained because salts, unlike water, have negligible vapor pressure at the operational temperatures and pressures of pervaporation. PV is capable of treating hypersaline waters, even when operated at ambient temperatures13,14. Unlike conventional membrane distillation (MD) processes, a more robust PV process has advantages in addressing challenges like wetting, scaling, and fouling, which enables promising operational performance15.

A major challenge for PV desalination is the design of high-performance separation membranes featuring high permeability, salt rejection, and operating stability9,16,17. Unlike their polymer counterparts, more robust inorganic polycrystalline nanoporous membranes, such as zeolite and silica, have been proposed18,19. While these membranes offered stable desalination performance, they suffered from low permeability, even for the treatment of seawater or low-salinity wastewaters. In contrast, metal-organic framework (MOF) membranes have well-defined nanochannel structures, abundant affinity sites, modular nature for functionalities, and structural stability, thereby enabling high uptake of water and promising separation performance20–23.

Compared with state-of-the-art water-stable MOFs, MOF-801 demonstrates highly robust characteristics, rapid water adsorption rate, and high water harvesting capacity24–28. In addition, MOF-801 inherently possesses a suitable crystallographic structure where the pore window size (3.5 Å) ranges between water molecules (2.8 Å) and hydrated sodium ions (~7.16 Å). This makes MOF-801 a promising candidate for desalination membranes with precise separation ability, which has not been experimentally demonstrated until now. However, the fabrication of a well-intergrown (i.e., crack-free or intercrystalline-defect-free) pure MOF-801 membrane is experimentally difficult and thus lags behind its mixed-matrix membrane and other MOF membrane counterparts.

Rational tailoring of crystalline chemistry of MOF membranes has the potential to substantially improve membrane functionality and performance, but is considered to be particularly challenging. Recently, intracrystalline defects, such as missing linkers and missing clusters, have been synthesized and confirmed to influence the permeability or selectivity of MOF liquid- or gas-separation membranes29–33. However, the precise creation of favorable intracrystalline defects is challenging and has not been well-studied in membrane-based separation applications. Moreover, it remains unclear how intracrystalline missing linkers affect separation performance, such as selectivity and permeability, at the molecular or atomic level. Therefore, elucidating these transport mechanisms is critical for the rational design of nanoscale channels with tailorable structure and chemistry in next-generation MOF membranes. Unlike the elimination of intracrystalline lattice defects to improve the selectivity of MOF membranes27, the aim of this work is to precisely regulate intracrystalline missing linkers of MOF membranes to enhance the permeation of water molecules while maintaining high salt rejection. Differing from a former study that introduced an arbitrary number of missing linkers (~23.5%)30, this work demonstrates that precise regulation of missing linkers to enhance water transport could be realized in MOF-801 membranes, which is not only practically challenging, but also critical for understanding fundamental mass transport phenomena.

In this work, we report a robust protocol for well-intergrown MOF-801 nanochannel thin membranes with precise regulation of intracrystalline missing linkers for enhanced water permeability via pervaporation. We further provide a mechanistic understanding of the missing-linker enhancement of water permeation, where missing linkers not only enhance structural hydrophilicity (i.e., water adsorption ability) but also render faster water transport via altering the pore structure and chemistry of the nanoscale MOF-801 channels. The membranes exhibit complete salt rejection, operation stability, and higher water flux in comparison to state-of-the-art silica, MOF, and zeolite membranes for the treatment of saline and hypersaline waters. Notably, the MOF-801 membranes demonstrated resilient performance under challenging water treatment conditions, including high temperatures, highly oxidizing environments, and in the presence of oils and surfactants.

Results

Design and characterization of crack-free MOF-801 membrane

Engineering the structure and surface properties of substrates is crucial for subsequent MOF membrane growth and application. In this work, crack-free MOF-801 membranes were fabricated by a secondary growth method followed by surfactant Span80 posttreatment for PV desalination (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Figs. 1–3). Ceramic substrates (α-Al2O3: average pore size ~700 nm; ZrO2: average pore size ~142 nm) were specially designed with a hollow fiber configuration for PV, featuring an ultrahigh packing density (~4000 m2·m–3) that is 16-40 times higher than those of tubular (200–250 m2·m–3) or flat-sheet (100–150 m2·m–3) commercial substrates; with the latter most commonly used as the substrate for current MOF membranes (Fig. 1a and Supplementary Fig. 4, SI Section 1.2)34. To modify the substrate and enable the growth of well-intergrown MOF-801 membranes, a more hydrophilic nano-TiO2 interlayer was introduced with lowered surface roughness (Ra~68 nm) (Supplementary Fig. 4) and average pore size (~105 nm). Such a TiO2 interlayer could provide a modified substrate surface with more hydroxyl groups and thus more nucleation sites for MOF crystal nucleation and subsequent growth, which is beneficial to the formation of thin, well-intergrown MOF-801 membranes. MOF-801 was selected to be fabricated into a separation membrane because of its stability toward some solvents, especially hypersaline solutions at elevated temperatures (Supplementary Fig. 5). Although there were only minor and negligible additional peaks for the samples after exposure in DMF, acetone, and water—the phase structure degraded to some extent in strongly acidic aqueous solution (pH 0.7), which can be reflected by peak broadening and new peaks arising (Supplementary Fig. 5). Nevertheless, such chemically harsh condition is far beyond practical application conditions. In this work, a nanoseed-induced secondary growth method (seed size: ~69 nm, Supplementary Fig. 6) was employed to fabricate the polycrystalline MOF-801 membrane to increase the presence of active nucleation sites and reduce the energy barrier for nucleation based on Ostwald’s rule35.

One key challenge is the generation of macroscopic cracks inside MOF-801 membranes during drying after membrane growth (Fig. 1a, b), which inevitably undermines the separation performance. To address this issue, a surfactant (Span80) posttreatment was employed to effectively avoid the formation of cracks (Fig. 1a, c and Supplementary Fig. 7). For the polycrystalline MOF-801 membrane, cracks formed due to the increased capillary stress between intergrown MOF crystals during the solvent evaporation stage36. However, after being soaked in a Span80 chloroform solution for 2 days, high-quality MOF-801 membranes (thickness ~1.67 μm) with a pure MOF-801 phase were obtained on a TiO2 interlayer-modified ceramic substrate with visible and distinct boundaries (Fig. 1d, g), which were free of macroscopic intracrystalline cracks (Fig. 1c). Posttreatment of the MOF-801 membrane with Span80 significantly suppressed the evaporation rate of residual solvents (N, N-dimethyl formamide, formic acid) and reduced capillary stress, consequently minimizing the formation of cracks. Unlike the unmodified MOF-801 membrane (“cracked”), which exhibits poor gas selectivity, the modified MOF-801 membrane is well-intergrown (i.e., without macroscopic cracks or other intercrystalline defects) since its ideal gas selectivities (H2/N2: 7.6, H2/CH4: 16.5, N2/CH4: 2.2) are all higher than the Knudsen diffusion selectivities (H2/N2: 3.7, H2/CH4: 2.8, N2/CH4: 1.3) (Fig. 1e).

The ATR-FTIR spectrum of the modified MOF-801 membrane verifies the presence of the -C = O-O bond (1400 cm–1, 1577 cm–1) and -C = O-OH group (1213 cm–1) of the MOF-801 structure37,38, as well as the in-plane deformation vibration of C-H (2852-2856 cm–1, 2921−2925 cm–1) and strong stretching vibration of ester bonds (1734−1742 cm–1) of the Span80 structure (Fig. 1f)39. Nevertheless, Span80 posttreatment did not significantly change the pore size (6 Å vs. 6 Å) and specific surface area (499 m2·g–1 vs. 535.5 m2·g–1) of MOF-801 (Supplementary Fig. 8), which indicates that such a surfactant posttreatment modification would not negatively affect the pore structure of the membranes. Additionally, the presence of O, Ti, Zr, and C signals in the XPS spectra are clearly identified for the TiO2 interlayer, cracked MOF-801, and crack-free MOF-801 membranes (Supplementary Fig. 9). Ti was detected in the TiO2 nano-interlayer surface, while Zr was detected in the membrane surface of both cracked and crack-free MOF-801 membranes (Supplementary Figs. 9 and 10). Moreover, even after strong ultrasonication treatment (150 W, 20 min) for an accelerated stability test, the MOF-801-1.43 membrane maintained integrated surface and cross-sectional structural morphologies, indicating promising mechanical stability for practical operation Supplementary Fig. 11).

Precise regulation of intracrystalline missing linkers

Although missing linkers have been experimentally designed in UiO-66 MOF membranes, precise regulation of intracrystalline missing linkers has not been reported until now32. Without the addition of a modulator, it is difficult to form highly crystalline MOF-801 membrane (usually only some amorphous phase) from ZrOCl2 and fumarate due to the slow crystallization kinetics40. The amorphous phase on substrates is not suitable for pervaporation desalination since it does not form a membrane layer with defined MOF crystalline structure comprised of regular pore window and pore cage to allow for water molecule transport and salt rejection. By contrast, our current work, employs formic acid as a modulator to not only accelerate the crystallization of MOF-801, but also precisely regulate intracrystalline missing linkers by adjusting the ratio of fumarate to formic acid (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Fig 12). The intracrystalline missing linkers were first identified and quantified based on the results of X-ray diffraction (XRD), 1H Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR), 13C Magic Angle Spinning (MAS) NMR, and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) (Fig. 2b–e and Supplementary Figs. 13–15). By adjusting the ratios of fumarate to formic acid (10:400, 6:400, 4:400, 2:400 and 1:400), the frequency of missing linkers per Zr6 crystalline formula unit (denoted as linker deficiency unless specified) can be precisely regulated and was determined to be 0.32, 0.70, 1.25, 1.43, and 1.62, based on the TGA results (denoted as MOF-801-0.32, MOF-801-0.70, MOF-801-1.25, MOF-801-1.43, and MOF-801-1.62 in this work) (Fig. 2b).

The TGA results indicated that a deficiency was present in all samples due to deviations in mass loss behavior in comparison to the ideal plateau of MOF-801 (Supplementary Fig. 13)41. The presence of missing clusters was ruled out according to the XRD patterns, where no reo phase was observed between 2 and 8° (2θ) (Fig. 2c)42. Therefore, we can conclude that the intracrystalline defects are the result of missing linkers. In addition to fumarate molecules, the presence of HCOO- (formic molecule) can be experimentally confirmed by the 1H NMR spectra (Fig. 2d), indicating the occurrence of a competitive coordination reaction between the Zr6 cluster and formic acid. This can be ascribed to the fact that during the solvothermal reaction, formic acid coordinates with Zr6 clusters via carboxyl groups by competing with fumarate acid to form the missing fumarate linkers.

With increasing missing-linker density, the pore structure of MOF-801 was enlarged, which was confirmed by the near-linear increase in pore size (i.e., pore width, from 4.62 to 5.09 Å), total pore volume (from 0.36 to 0.68 cm−3 . g−1), and specific surface area (from 292.1 to 791.1 m−2 . g−1) (Fig. 2e, f, and Supplementary Fig. 14). Moreover, the water uptake capacity was enhanced with the increasing presence of missing linkers (Fig. 2f), which is not only a result of the enlarged pore structure but also the generation of more hydrophilic sites with enhanced water adsorption ability. For the pervaporation desalination process (Supplementary Fig. 3), the presence of missing linkers reduced the activation energy barrier required for water molecule transport across the membranes (Supplementary Fig. 16). Furthermore, the water flux of MOF-801 membranes linearly increased with increasing linker deficiency under constant operational temperatures (40–80 °C), while salt rejection remains nearly complete, in the range of 99.69– 99.91% under all operational temperatures (Fig. 2g). Specifically, when the linker deficiency increases from 0.32 to 1.43, the water flux is enhanced by ~60% at 80 °C. Additionally, increasing the operational temperature from 40 to 80 °C improves the water flux from 12.3 to 35.6 L . m−2 . h−1 at a 1.43 linker deficiency due to the enhanced driving force. The performance of the membrane with the highest linker deficiency 1.62, is not shown since it was synthesized under strongly acidic conditions, resulting in poor membrane quality and desalination performance.

Atomistic mechanism for missing-linker enhanced performance

To shed light on the effect of missing linkers on desalination performance, state-of-the-art molecular simulations, including molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo, associated with experimental data were employed to offer atomistic insights into the permeation mechanism of water molecules through MOF-801 membranes (Fig. 3 and SI Section 3.1). To reveal the role of missing linkers, we investigated a defect-free simulation structure and two highly symmetric missing-linker simulation structures with theoretical deficiency of 1/12 and 1/4 (also denoted MOF-801-DF, MOF-801-0.5, and MOF-801-1.5, respectively). Through these simulations, the improvement in water flux was ascribed to missing linkers not only enhancing water adsorption (i.e., water affinity) but also water transport by altering the intracrystalline pore microenvironment (i.e., more hydrophilic active sites) and structure (i.e., both enlarged pore window and pore cage) of the subnanometer channels in the MOF-801 framework structure. Reducing the activation energy barrier, the presence of missing linkers enables the ultrafast transport of more water molecules (Fig. 3a–e). Missing linkers also enhance the structural hydrophilicity of the MOF frameworks due to the exposure of more water adsorption sites (i.e., [Zr6(OH)4O4] cluster), resulting in a higher water uptake rate and higher water loading (Fig. 3f, g and SI Section 3.2).

To gain insight into such an enhancement from an energetic point of view, Monte Carlo simulations were conducted to sample the interaction energy between a single water molecule and MOF-801 framework structures. The interaction energy distribution for MOF-801-0.5 and MOF-801-1.5 indicated stronger interactions resulting from water-framework Coulombic interactions when compared to MOF-801-DF (Fig. 3h). As a result, MOF-801 exhibited 4-16 times higher adsorption capacity when compared with other state-of-the-art, water-stable MOFs (i.e., ML-UiO-66, UiO-66, UiO-66-NH2) (Fig. 3i). During the PV process, the creation of more missing linkers enhances the permeation dynamics of more water molecules inside the membranes (Fig. 3j). Theoretically, the water flux significantly increases from 16.4 to 36.4 L·m−1·h−1 with increasing missing linker deficiency from 0 to 1.5 (Fig. 3k). We can, therefore, conclude that intracrystalline missing linkers in the MOF-801 membrane not only promote the structural hydrophilicity (i.e., water adsorption ability) but also render faster transport dynamics (i.e., diffusion ability) of water molecules. These two aspects result in the higher water flux of MOF-801 membranes with more missing linkers than was observed experimentally. As previously noted, the MOF-801-1.62 membrane synthesized under strongly acidic conditions exhibits poor desalination performance. Thus, the high-quality MOF-801-1.43 membrane was selected for subsequent performance and stability evaluation and will be referred to as MOF-801 membrane unless specified hereafter.

Promising desalination performance and operation stability

The MOF-801 membranes exhibited exceptional desalination performance and operation stability for the treatment of saline waters in a wide range of concentrations (0-10 wt.% NaCl) (Fig. 4). Independent of salt concentration and operation temperature, the rejection of all MOF-801 membranes is over 99.9%, resulting in permeate salt concentrations of 2-44 ppm, which are lower than the product water quality standards of both China and the WHO for desalination techniques (China HY/T 247-2018: 50 ppm (salt concentration), WHO: 1,200 ppm (total dissolved solids)) (Supplementary Fig. 17)9. This performance indicates that the MOF-801 membranes could effectively desalinate brines to produce clean water by PV (Figs. 4a, and S3). The pore size of MOF-801-1.43 was determined to be 5.07 Å (Fig. 2d and Supplementary Fig. 14b), which is lower than the size of hydrated ions (d [Na+·(H2O)nh] ~0.716 nm, d [Cl-·(H2O)nh] ~0.664 nm)43. The near-complete salt rejections could be ascribed to the fact that the salt ions have negligible vapor pressure at the operating conditions, and do not vaporize in the pervaporation process.

In particular, even at approximately room temperature (30 °C) (without heat energy input on the feed side), water flux is still very promising (e.g., 6.3 L·m–2·h–1 for 3.5 wt.% saline water, 3.9 L·m–2·h–1 for 10 wt.% hypersaline water) (Fig. 4a). This energy-saving feature is different from the MD process, where the water flux (~1 L·m–2·h–1) is often negligible at room temperature44. For saline waters with the same concentration, water flux was further enhanced with operation temperature (from 30 to 70 °C) due to an enhanced driving force of the adsorption-diffusion process of water molecules, since PV is a typical thermal activation adsorption-diffusion process45. The activation energy (Eact.) is approximately 3.69 kJ·mol−1 for 3.5 wt.% saline water (Supplementary Fig. 17), which is less than that of other PV processes45. At the same operating temperature, the water flux was demonstrated to show a declining trend with increasing feed salt concentration (Fig. 4a)46. This phenomenon is attributed to the elevated salt concentration at the vaporization interface, where fewer free water molecules are transported across the MOF-801 membrane because more water molecules are bound to salt ions to form a hydration shell. Moreover, the MOF-801-1.43 membrane showed high water flux (27.4 L·m–2·h–1) for treating saline waters (3.5 wt.% NaCl), outperforming most state-of-the-art silica, zeolite, and MOF membranes (1-10 L·m–2·h–1) (Fig. 4b). Unlike other studies focusing on 3.5 wt.% NaCl saline water treatment, the MOF-801 membrane exhibited promising water flux (10.5 L·m–2·h–1) for even more challenging hypersaline waters (10 wt.%), which are 1.6–7.4 times that of current zeolite and MOF membranes (Fig. 4c). Like the water flux, the water permeance of the MOF-801 membranes were enhanced with linker deficiencies but decreased with salt concentration (Supplementary Fig. 18).

Differentiating from most other MOF membranes that suffer from insufficient water stability, MOF-801 membranes demonstrate stable operational performance (in both flux and rejection) and acceptable structural integrity (in both MOF-801 crystalline phase and membrane morphology) for the treatment of 7 wt.% hypersaline water at 70 °C for 120 h (Fig. 4d, f, g)47. Under XRD, the MOF-801 membranes exhibited two characteristic reflections of MOF-801 phase between 7 and 11° before and after operation, indicating some stability in their phase crystalline structure (Fig. 4f). However, some peak width broadening was found from 0.24 to 0.37 especially after 108 h exposure at pH 3, which reflected a change in the lattice structure to some extent (i.e., crystal lattice grain sizes might decrease slightly), while the peaks were nearly unchanged in the case of hypersaline water exposure. The efficient treatment of harsh environmental wastewaters can be challenging for conventional membranes. However, after soaking in an acidic hypersaline solution (pH=3, 10 wt % NaCl), the MOF-801 membrane still exhibited stable flux and salt rejection over ~100 h of operation (Fig. 4e). Even after constant operation in harsh environments (e.g., high temperature, high salinity, and low pH ∼3), the MOF-801 membrane showed good structural integrity, which was confirmed by its resilient surface morphology, phase composition, and high ideal gas selectivity (Fig. 4f, g and Supplementary Fig. 19). These results indicate that MOF-801 membranes are promising candidates for the production of clean water from hypersaline brines via PV.

To further investigate the resilience of the MOF-801 membrane, desalination performance and operation stability were evaluated under harsh treatment conditions using acidic brines, industrial wastewaters, and oxidizing brines (Fig. 5). Acidic hypersaline waters with an initial pH of 3 could be concentrated to the point of crystallization by using MOF-801 membranes at an operating temperature of 70 °C, all while consistently producing pure water on the permeate side of the membrane (Fig. 5a and Supplementary Fig. 20). During operation, the water flux gradually decreased from 10.5 to 2.6 L·m-2·h-1 due to the decreasing driving force caused by the feed being concentrated 4.45-fold, all while salt rejection was maintained above 99.9%.

The treatment of real industrial acidic saline wastewater (pH ~3.4) can also be challenging for conventional membranes because of its complicated composition (Supplementary Table 5). Interestingly, the MOF-801 membrane exhibits promisingly stable performance under these conditions (Fig. 5b). The water flux first decreased from its initial value of ~10.4 L m–2 h–1 and then remained relatively stable in the range of ~5.0–6.0 L m–2 h–1due to the membrane fouling at the vaporization interface (i.e., membrane surface). By using a simple DI water cleaning method, the water flux of the MOF-801 membranes could be efficiently recovered to 9.85 L m–2 h–1 (with a recovery ratio of ~94%). In addition to having a stable water flux, the membrane shows high rejection of all salt ions, such as Na+, K+, Mg2+, and Ca2+ (all above 99.0%), which can be ascribed to the fact that salt ions have negligible vapor pressure and do not vaporize during the pervaporation process.

One of the major challenges for conventional polyamide membranes is their susceptibility to chlorine oxidation48. Unlike polyamide membranes, the MOF-801 membranes showed a highly stable desalination performance for hypersaline water feed even at 70 °C after exposure to 100 ppm NaClO aqueous solution for as long as 21 days (i.e., equivalent to 50,400 ppm·h chlorine exposure) (Fig. 5c); indicating a significantly improved chlorine resistance (1.7–20.0 times higher) than both commercial and state-of-the-art polyamide membranes (2500–30,430 ppm·h chlorine exposure)49,50. After continuous operation under these different extreme conditions, all the MOF-801 membranes retained their integrity as exhibited by the ideal selectivities of H2/N2 gases, which were still higher than their Knudsen diffusion selectivity (H2/N2: 3.7) (Fig. 5f and Supplementary Fig. 21). Moreover, the MOF-801 membranes exhibited stability in both the crystalline phase and membrane structure, which is reflected by the XRD (only except for slight peak-width broadening), 1H NMR spectra, ATR-FTIR spectra, and membrane surface morphology (Fig. 5d, e, g, h). The performance stability can be ascribed to the highly stable membrane structure consisting of well-intergrown MOF-801 crystals in which there are highly robust Zr–O covalent bonds, and strong coordination bonds between [Zr6(OH)4O4] cluster (i.e., Lewis acid) and fumarate linker (i.e., Lewis base). Additionally, unlike commercial polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) distillation membranes (Nanjing CAS-Bidun Newmen Technology Co., Ltd., China), the MOF-801 pervaporation membranes show better desalination performance and operating stability for treating more challenging hypersaline waters containing low surface tension species, including oil and surfactant (Supplementary Fig. 22). This is similar to what was previously reported between commercial PTFE distillation membranes and polymeric pervaporation membranes51. This resilient desalination performance under various harsh conditions indicates the application potential of the robust MOF-801 membranes in a wide range of industrial applications.

Discussion

In this work, an intracrystalline missing-linker chemistry protocol is proposed to rationally design stable ceramic-based MOF-801 nanochannel membranes with enhanced water permeability for the efficient treatment of challenging hypersaline waters. Seeding the highly dispersive MOF-801 nanocrystals onto substrates provided more nucleation sites for better membrane growth, and a surfactant posttreatment avoided the formation of cracks by significantly minimizing capillary stress during the solvent vaporization process, resulting in a high-quality, well-intergrown MOF-801 membrane. Missing linkers could be precisely regulated in the MOF-801 membranes by altering the ratios of fumarate to formic acid due to their competitive coordination reaction with Zr6 clusters. The introduction of intracrystalline missing linkers was shown to positively enhance water adsorption and diffusion by modifying the MOF-801 nanochannel structure and chemistry. Through molecular simulations, these missing linkers were confirmed to play an important role in enhancing water transport by not only enhancing membrane structural hydrophilicity with stronger host-guest interaction energy but also endowing faster water transport dynamics with a lower energy barrier by enlarging the pore window and pore cage of nanoscale channels. Experimentally, the MOF-801 membranes showed near-complete salt rejection, satisfactory water flux, and operational stability for the treatment of saline or hypersaline waters. Moreover, promising performance and structural stability were experimentally confirmed, even after harsh hypersaline treatment under high-temperature, acidic, oxidizing conditions, as well as with other fouling species (i.e., oil or surfactant).

The results from this current study will provide useful guidelines for the rational design of not only MOF-801 membranes but also other next-generation MOF nanochannel membranes with enhanced performance, as the approach of using an acid modulator has been shown to be a feasible method for precisely regulating missing linkers through the coordination with metal clusters and competition with the linkers. This work provides a protocol for the rational design of high-performance desalination membranes, as well as the development of more sustainable water treatment applications in harsh environments for next-generation MOF membranes. Although the membranes demonstrate promising potential in the treatment of hypersaline water at the laboratory scale, more studies are highly desired for the treatment of realistic industrial wastewaters with complicated compositions, which will be a focus in future work. In addition to hypersaline desalination, future work will also focus on more challenging separation applications (e.g., ion-ion or azeotrope (isomer) separation, organic solvent dehydration) by employing advanced strategies, such as rational tailoring of the size and chemistry (e.g., ligand groups) of MOF membrane nanochannels, modification of the membrane surface charge, and process intensification via an electrical field.

Methods

In situ synthesis of MOF-801 nanoseed layer

Both Al2O3 and ZrO2 ceramic substrates (100–200 pieces for each membrane) were first fabricated by an immersion-induced phase inversion process, followed by complete drying and sintering at 1550 °C for 4 h (Al2O3) or at 1050 °C for 2 h (ZrO2). A TiO2 interlayer was then coated onto the substrates by a dip-coating method, followed by complete drying and sintering at 800 °C for 4 h. Then, a thin layer of uniformly dispersed MOF-801 nanoseeds (size ~69 nm) was synthesized in situ on the outer surface of TiO2 interlayer-modified ceramic substrates via the solvothermal method. Specifically, fumaric acid and ZrOCl2·8H2O were first dissolved in a solvent mixture of DMF and formic acid. Then, the substrates with both sides sealed were placed into the mixture solution inside a 100 mL Teflon reactor for the solvothermal reaction at 120 °C for 24 h to form a MOF-801 nanoseed layer in situ. Finally, after the reaction was allowed to cool, the samples were cleaned with DMF.

Preparation of crack-free MOF-801 membrane

MOF-801 membranes with different missing linkers (10–20 pieces for each membrane composition) were synthesized via a solvothermal secondary growth method, followed by surfactant posttreatment. To create different missing-linker densities, fumaric acid (0.1420 g, 0.4260 g, 0.7087 g) and formic acid (9 mL) at different molar ratios (fumaric acid: formic acid = 2:400, 6:400, 10:400) were first mixed with DMF (61 mL) and ZrOCl2·8H2O (0.4025 g). Then, the substrates coated with a MOF-801 nanoseed layer were placed into the mixture solution inside a 100 mL Teflon reactor for solvothermal reaction at 120 °C for 24 h to allow for the secondary growth of MOF-801 membranes. Finally, after the reaction, the MOF-801 membranes were cleaned with DMF and dried at room temperature for 6 h to produce the cracked MOF-801 membranes. To avoid the formation of cracks, the synthesized MOF-801 membranes were immediately immersed in a Span80 solution (1 g Span80 dissolved in 10 mL chloroform) for 24 h prior to drying. The MOF-801 membranes were completely dried in a chloroform atmosphere for 72 h and then activated to remove any solvent molecules (DMF) at 150 °C for 24 h under vacuum (-1 bar).

Membrane performance evaluation

The desalination performance of MOF-801 membranes (effective membrane area ~100–200 mm2) was evaluated in a lab-scale pervaporation (PV) setup with various saline waters (1.5, 3.5, 7 wt. % NaCl, pH 7), hypersaline water (10 wt.% NaCl, pH 7), acidic hypersaline water (10 wt.% NaCl, pH 3), and real industrial acidic saline wastewater (A machinery factory, Dalian City, Liaoning Province, China) as the feeds (feed water reservoir volume ~100–250 ml) (Supplementary Fig. 3). The pervaporation system was first stabilized for ~2 h. The feed flow rate was controlled at 160 ± 5 mL min–1 by a peristaltic pump, while a vacuum of (~60 Pa) was applied on the permeate side. A cold trap was utilized to cool and collect the permeate water after condensation at different time intervals (~2 h). The permeate flux (Jw, L·m–2·h–1) was determined at a certain time interval (Δt, h) based on the volume of the condensed permeate (ΔV, L) across a constant effective membrane area (A, m2) according to Eq. (1). During membrane operation, the salt concentration of the solution was measured using a conductivity meter (DDSJ-308A, Shanghai REX Instrument Factory, China). The conductivity of the feed solution was used to calculate the salt concentration based on a calibration curve. Molecular simulations

Classical molecular dynamics simulations, implemented in the LAMMPS package, were conducted to investigate the pervaporation desalination mechanism of MOF-801-DF, MOF-801-0.5, and MOF-801-1.5 membranes52. The simulation system and results are described in detail in SI Section 3 (Supplementary Figs. 23 and 24).

Pervaporation simulations were carried out in a canonical ensemble (i.e., NVT) at a temperature of 343 K with a time step of 1 fs. The system’s temperature was modulated using the Nosé‒Hoover thermostat with a damping factor of 100 time steps (i.e., 100 fs). For each simulation, energy minimization was first carried out to preliminarily relax the system. Subsequently, a hydrostatic pressure of 1 atm was applied on the feed pistons, and a molecular dynamics simulation was performed for 40 ns to bring the liquid density closer to its equilibrium value (i.e., 1.0 g cm−3) and to fully saturate the initially empty membrane structure with saline solution. Finally, the systems were further equilibrated for 5 ps, followed by a production run of 20 ps at a constant pressure of 1 atm. We should note that all of the studied membranes, as well as the piston and adsorbing plate, were assumed to be rigid.

Characterization

The structural morphology or element composition of MOF-801 membranes and ceramic substrates were characterized in a field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) (S-4800, Hitachi, Japan; NOVA NanoSEM 450, FEI Company, USA) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were carried out in an Ultima IV X-ray diffractometer using Cu-Kα radiation with a step size of 0.02° and 2 s per step (D/Max-2400, Rigaku, Japan). The functional groups of the MOF-801 membrane and powder were analyzed by attenuated total reflectance-Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (ATR-FTIR) (IS50, Thermo Nicolet, US). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used for analysis of elements using a Thermo Fisher K-Alpha spectrometer (K-Alpha, Thermo Fisher, USA). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was carried out in a thermal analyzer (TG/DTA 6300, Seiko Co., Ltd., Japan) at a heating rate of 5 °C min–1 from room temperature (~25 °C) to 700 °C in air. Liquid 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded in a nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer (Bruker Advance Ⅲ500, Bruker, Switzerland). The nitrogen adsorption-desorption isotherms of MOF-801 crystals were recorded in an automatic physical adsorption apparatus (AS-1-MP-11, Quantachrome, USA). Dynamic vapor sorption (DVS, Surface Measurement System Ltd., UK) was used to measure the water uptake behaviors of MOF-801 and Span80-posttreated MOF-801 crystals, as well as UiO-66 and UiO-66-NH2.

Other detailed characterization can be found in the Supplementary Materials (SI Section 1.4).

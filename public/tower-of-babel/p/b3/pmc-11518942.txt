Clarificaton on the similarity of the floating Bentall technique with our previously defined existing technique known as the flange technique
Authors: Kaan Kırali, Taylan Adademir
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the recent article by Issa and colleagues1 describing the floating Bentall technique for aortic root enlargement, which is actually a replacement procedure of the aortic root.1 Although we commend the authors for their work in addressing the significant issue of prosthesis-patient mismatch in patients with a small aortic annulus, we wish to highlight that the technique they describe closely mirrors our modified Bentall procedure (known as the flanged technique), which was first introduced by Yakut in 2001.2 To quote from the original article2:

A segment (5 or 6 mm in length) of the proximal end of the vascular graft is everted outward to form the flange of the graft (Figure 1, A). Then, a mechanical valve is inserted into the graft, which is fixed from 4 corners to the graft with 4-0 polypropylene sutures. A continuous 4-0 polypropylene suture is used to anastomose the bottom border of the stent of the prosthetic valve to the graft (Figure 1, B). The flange of the conduit is then returned to its original position (Figure 1, C), and implanted to the aortic annulus with a continuous 3-0 polypropylene suture. The length of the flange (1 to 3 cm long) is adjusted depending on the procedure (ie, aortic root enlargement or subannular area repair) (Figure 1, D)... This method can be an alternative for tailoring the aortic root in all aortic root pathologies, especially in patients with small and calcified aortic roots with calcification extending into mitral apparatus precluding a root enlargement (a Nick's or Manougian technique) or in those with iatrogenic defects at the annular and subannular areas that require repair.

Preparation of a flanged composite graft. A, First, a segment of the proximal end of the vascular graft is everted outward to form the flange of the graft. B, A mechanical valve is inserted into the graft and sutured to the graft with a continuous 4-0 polypropylene suture to fix the bottom border of prosthetic valve to the graft. C, The flange of the conduit is returned to its original position. D, The flanged composite graft is ready for aortic root replacement. The length of the flange can be adjusted depending on the procedure to be performed.

The flanged technique not only enables aortic root replacement, but also effectively addresses the prevention of prosthesis-patient mismatch without the need for additional incisions or patches, as echoed in the floating Bentall technique. Since 1996, the flanged Bentall technique has been particularly used by our teams in patients with aortic root pathologies, especially aortic root aneurysms, dissections, or reoperations, as well as in patients with small aortic roots or other complex annular and/or subannular aortic pathologies such as extensive aortic root abscess.3, 4, 5, 6

Although Issa and colleagues1 should be commended for bringing this technique into contemporary practice and demonstrating its utility with modern materials and approaches, we believe it is crucial to acknowledge prior contributions in this field.7,8 Recognizing the historical context of surgical innovations helps provide a more comprehensive understanding of the technique's development and encourages further advancements built upon established foundations. We hope this clarification will contribute to a broader recognition of our work and foster continued innovation in the field of cardiac surgery.

Nomogram-based prediction of emergence delirium in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery
Authors: Xiaocheng Zhu, Lu Wang, Wuping Zhuang, E. Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundA nomogram model has been developed to forecast the incidence of emergence delirium (ED) in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery.MethodsA secondary analysis was performed on the eMODIPOD trial, which involved elective laparoscopic surgery patients at Xiangya Hospital between October 2017 and October 2019. To assess ED, the Richmond Agitation Sedation Scale (RASS) was combined with the Intensive Care Unit (CAM-ICU) scale, and patients were categorized as ED or non-ED based on the results. The two groups were compared in terms of perioperative information and test results. Logistic regression was utilized to investigate factors contributing to ED occurrence in elderly patients following laparoscopic surgery, and a nomogram was constructed to depict this. The diagnostic performance was evaluated through the utilization of the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) and a calibration plot. Simultaneously, to appraise the clinical value, decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to assess the clinical value.ResultsOf the 565 patients subject to laparoscopic surgery, 94 were subsequently diagnosed with emergence delirium (ED), yielding a positive rate of 16.6 %. The developed Nomogram model was an amalgamation of several variables, which included preoperative cognitive function, educational level, dezocine, penehyclidine, ASA grading, blood loss, fluid infusion, and Post Anesthesia Care Unit-numerical rating scale (PACU-NRS). The model’s performance was commendable in terms of its discrimination power, as signified by an area under the curve of 0.713(95 % CI = 0.655–0.772). Furthermore, it exhibited adequate calibration, as affirmed by the Hosmer-Lemeshow test (χ2 = 2.243, p = 0.326). Internal validation of the model further emphasized its superior discrimination and calibration capabilities. The decision curve analysis pointed out that when the threshold probability exceeded 0.1, the Nomogram model delivered more substantial benefits compared to the treat-all and treat-none approaches.ConclusionsThis scoring system represents the pioneering Nomogram model utilized for predicting emergence delirium (ED) in geriatric patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery. It exhibits robust efficacy in forecasting the risk of ED, thereby furnishing valuable insights for the prevention, management, and treatment of ED.

Introduction

Emergence delirium (ED) in the postanesthesia care unit (PACU) is an acute brain organ dysfunction defined as an inconsolable, irritable, and uncooperative status. ED occurs during or immediately after emergence from general anesthesia and is usually short-lived without permanent sequelae.1 The incidence of ED is approximately 3 %–49 %,2, 3, 4 and the clinical significance of ED is heterogeneous. Emergence delirium (ED) can manifest as either hyperactive or hypoactive signs, depending on the patient’s condition.2 ED can lead to self-harm, violence against staff, and the need for extra nursing supervision. Moreover, it is associated with further delirium in the ward and prolonged cognitive impairment.5, 6, 7 Eventually, it increases the duration of hospital stay,8 accompanied by complications, as well as the incidence of mortality.9

ED in the PACU deteriorates functional recoveries and places a burden on financial and medical resources. Clinical measures for preventing and treating ED primarily involve three aspects: preoperative assessment, perioperative medication, and intraoperative techniques. Preoperative assessment includes obtaining a thorough medical history and symptom evaluation, ruling out or treating underlying neurological and psychological conditions, and promoting physical exercise during the perioperative period. Medication therapy involves the use of sedatives, effective pain management, and anti-inflammatory drugs during the perioperative period. Techniques used during surgery include enhancing the perfusion efficacy of tissues and organs and managing the depth of anesthesia.10, 11 Such strategies have been shown to reduce, to some extent, the occurrence rate of ED, suggesting that early identification of high-risk patients and the implementation of preventive measures may be an effective approach to managing ED.

Previous studies have primarily focused on the epidemiology and etiology of ED in children.12, 13, 14 Existing studies on elderly patients have suggested that preoperative benzodiazepines, certain anesthetics, postoperative pain, breast and abdominal surgery, and surgery with prolonged duration may be potential risk factors for ED.3, 15 However, due to limitations such as inadequate assessment tools, mixed surgical types, and small sample sizes or single-center studies, these findings have been insufficient.16, 17, 18 Risk prediction models are designed to consider for multiple factors contributing to the disease, rather than focusing on single indicators alone. These models are advantageous because they can effectively and systematically identify and differentiate disease-prone, high-risk populations. However, there is a noticeable lack of research on models for ED prediction. In our study, we began by conducting a comprehensive multivariate regression analysis to investigate well-established risk factors related to ED onset in the elderly. Subsequently, we developed a nomogram as a practical tool for identifying high-risk groups vulnerable to ED. Our primary goal is to offer clinical insights and guidelines for effectively preventing, managing, and treating ED, while simultaneously highlighting the nomogram’s efficacy in accurately predicting the risk of emergence delirium in geriatric patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery.

Materials and methods

Participants and clinical data

Our study utilized data derived from the randomized controlled trial known as EEG Monitoring to Decrease the Incidence of PostOperative Delirium (eMODIPOD). This trial was designed to assess the impact of SedLine (SedLine Brain Function Monitor; Masimo Corporation, Irvine, CA, USA) SEF- and PSI-guided propofol-remifentanil anesthesia on postoperative delirium (POD) in patients subjected to laparoscopic surgery.19 The anesthetic management goals were as follows: maintaining Left and right Spectral Edge Frequency (SEF, 95 %) between 10 and 15, keeping Patient State Index (PSI) between 25 and 50 and avoiding EEG burst-suppression throughout surgery. This randomized controlled trial took place at Xiangya Hospital, an affiliate of Central South University in Changsha, China, with patient recruitment extending from October 2017 to October 2019 (trial registration number: NCT03330236). Prior to inclusion, all participating patients granted their written informed consent. Despite the SEF- and PSI-guided care, the primary findings from the RCT revealed that there was no significant alteration in the administration of anesthetics when compared to usual care. Similarly, there was no further reduction observed in the surprisingly low incidence of POD within this particular patient group.

This was a secondary analysis of the eMODIPOD study. Patient data were obtained from a database established in previous prospective studies.19 A total of 565 patients aged 65 years and older were assessed for eligibility. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 65 years; (2) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status Classification I-III; (3) elective laparoscopic surgery planned under general anesthesia with tracheal intubation; (4) anticipated postoperative extubation. Exclusion criteria were: (1) refusal to participate; (2) emergent surgery; (3) trauma patients; (4) cognitive impairment, defined by a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score ≤ 23; (5) history of stroke, schizophrenia, major depression, Parkinson’s disease, epilepsy, or dementia; (6) inability to communicate because of illiteracy, language difficulties, or significant hearing or visual impairment; (7) inability to complete the MMSE and delirium survey. The baseline characteristics, intraoperative information, and laboratory results of the patients in our database were obtained from electronic medical records.

ED assessment and diagnostic criteria

In our study, ED was defined as delirium occurring during the PACU stay. Thirty minutes after extubation, trained assessors used the Richmond Agitation Sedation Scale (RASS) to assess sedation/agitation level. If a patient’s RASS score was greater than −4, an ED assessment was conducted. Trained investigators used the Confusion Assessment Method for the Intensive Care Unit (CAM-ICU) scale’s confusion assessment method to assess patients’ status. The CAM-ICU scale includes four features of delirium: (1) acute onset or fluctuating course of mental status changes; (2) inattention; (3) disorganized thinking; and (4) altered level of consciousness. To diagnose ED, patients must demonstrate features 1 and 2, and either 3 or 4. RASS scores less than −3 were considered indicators of excessive sedation, and patients who were excessively sedated were considered to be in a comatose state.

Statistical analysis

Our data was rigorously analyzed using SPSS (version 26.0) and R (version 4.1.3). To start with, continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range), and differences between groups were explored using nonparametric tests. Similarly, categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages and subjected to chi-squared tests or continuity-corrected chi-squared tests for group comparison. We subsequently conducted a multivariable logistic regression analysis to establish the best predictive model, characterized by the lowest Akaike information criterion (AIC). Drawing from the definitive outcomes of the multivariable logistic regression, we utilized the RMS package in R to create a nomogram. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was then employed to assess the model’s predictive capability. To further validate the model, we assessed internal discrimination and calibration using 1000 bootstrap resamples. The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was applied to determine the adequacy of the model’s adequacy. To conclude our analysis, we used the decision curve analysis (DCA) to evaluate the clinical usefulness of the nomogram. It should be noted that all tests were two-tailed, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Variable selection

We performed multiple logistic regression analysis using factors that may affect ED development reported in previous literature and variables with p < 0.05 in the univariate analysis, and stepwise elimination was used to find the optimal model with the lowest AIC value, which included education level, preoperative MMSE score, blood loss, use of penehyclidine, absence of dezocine, PACU-NRS score, fluid infusion volume, and ASA classification. The minimum AIC value was 470.9. Results showed that an education level < 9 years (OR = 2.152, 95 % CI = 1.204–4.049), a preoperative MMSE score < 27 points (OR = 1.920, 95 % CI = 1.166–3.149), blood loss > 400 mL (OR = 3.101, 95 % CI = 1.505–6.288), fluid infusion volume > 1500 mL (OR = 1.846, 95 % CI = 1.135–3.042), absence of dezocine (OR = 0.563, 95 % CI = 0.322–0.953), use of penehyclidine during surgery (OR = 7.948, 95 % CI = 2.288–27.993), and PACU-NRS score (OR = 1.160, 95 % CI = 1.042–1.292) were independent risk factors for ED in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery (p < 0.05, Fig. 2). To check for multicollinearity, the variance inflation factors（VIF）of the regression analysis were all close to 1.0, indicating no collinearity among the eight variables (VIF < 5).Fig. 2Multivariate analysis of the risk of Emergence delirium (ED) in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery. Design and construction of a prognostic model

Based on the identified variables and multiple logistic regression analysis, a nomogram was established to predict ED after laparoscopic surgery in elderly patients (Fig. 3). Each variable was scored individually, with a score range from 0 to 100. The final total score, representing the probability of elderly patients developing ED after undergoing laparoscopic surgery, was calculated by aggregating the individual scores from all the variables (Fig. 3).Fig. 3The nomogram for predicting the probability of ED in elderly patients after laparoscopic surgery. Risk nomogram and internal validation for the prediction of ED

The performance of the predictive nomogram was initially quantified by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), which yielded a score of 0.713 (95 % CI = 0.655–0.772) as depicted in Fig. 4A. The internal validity of the nomogram model was subsequently assessed using the Bootstrap method with 1000 repetitions,yielding an AUROC of 0.691. The calibration curve for the nomogram model was also produced, revealing a strong correlation between the predicted and observed outcomes. This was further supported by a mean absolute error of 0.015, indicative of accurate predictions (Fig. 4B). The Hosmer-Lemeshow test affirmed the goodness-of-fit for the nomogram (χ2 = 2.243, p = 0.326), indicating a good model fit. Lastly, decision curve analysis was performed, demonstrating that the nomogram provided a superior net benefit compared to both the treat-all and treat-none strategies, particularly when the threshold probability exceeded 0.1 (Fig. 4C).Fig. 4The performance and validation of the nomogram. Discussion

In this study, we developed a nomogram to predict elderly patients' ED following laparoscopic surgery, based on eight key indicators: education level, preoperative MMSE, intraoperative blood loss, use of penehyclidine, dezocine, NRS score, intraoperative fluid volume, and ASA classification. According to the ROC curve, calibration curve, and DCA, the nomogram performed well in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical usefulness. This is the first time a nomogram model has been used to predict ED in elderly laparoscopic surgery patients. The advantage of this model is that it simultaneously achieves visualization and the objectification of measurements, making it convenient for application in clinical settings.

In our study, the occurrence of ED was 16.6 % (94/565) in elderly patients, similar to the result found in previous studies.2 Depending on the specific patient population, type of surgery performed, diagnostic tools used, and timing of the assessment, the prevalence rate of ED varies greatly. Delirium during or immediately following emergence from general anesthesia is challenging to assess. First, regarding the timing of ED assessment, some patients were assessed for ED signs immediately after awakening from general anesthesia, admission and/or 10 min after arrival at the PACU.1, 15, 20 The signs of delirium are always confused with the residual effects of anesthesia, which may lead to a high potential rate of false-positives. Conversely, more negative results were also found because hypoactive delirium was mistaken for inadequate postanesthetic recovery. In our study, to exclude the residual effects of anesthesia, we first conducted the assessment at 30 min after extubation using the RASS scale to measure sedation/agitation. Then, we used the CAM-ICU scale (recommended by the European delirium guidelines) to assess their delirious symptoms.21 These two scales are easy to use, efficient and have excellent interrater consistency. Ultimately, it was concluded that lower education level (<9 years), preoperative MMSE score < 27, blood loss > 400 mL, Infusion volume > 1500 mL, higher NRS score, use of penehyclidine and non-use of dezocine are risk factors for elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery-induced ED.

Research has shown that low educational levels and preoperative cognitive impairment are risk factors for postoperative delirium.22, 23 Higher education levels may be associated with greater cognitive reserve, which could potentially enhance an individual’s ability to cope with the stress of surgery and anesthesia. However, the specific mechanisms by which education level might influence ED risk in our patient population remain unclear and warrant further investigation. While our study found an association between lower education levels and an increased ED risk, it’s important to note that this relationship may be influenced by various confounding factors not fully accounted for in our retrospective analysis. The complex interplay between education, cognitive function, and postoperative outcomes requires further exploration in prospective studies specifically designed to address these questions. Our findings in elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery contribute to the broader understanding of ED risk factors, while also highlighting the need for specialty-specific research in this area.

An innovative prospective cohort study demonstrated that the use of penehyclidine increased the incidence of postoperative cognitive impairment and postoperative delirium in elderly patients.24 In our study, we found that administration of penehyclidine could increase ED occurrence. Penehyclidine, an anticholinergic drug developed in China, selectively antagonizes M1, M3, and N receptors on both the central and peripheral nerves, with minimal effect on M2 receptors.25 It is widely used in clinical practice because of its effect on reducing secretion production and lung protection.26 According to our research, penehyclidine should be used with caution during the perioperative period in elderly patients. While penehyclidine was found to increase the occurrence of ED, our study also explored the effects of another drug, dezocine, which demonstrated a contrasting outcome. Dezocine is an agonist of the κ and μ receptors, exerting analgesic effects through activation of the opioid receptor and inhibition of norepinephrine reuptake. In our study, we found that administration of dezocine reduced the occurrence of ED in age 65 and older patients. This was the first report that dezocine administration can reduce the occurrence of ED in patients scheduled for selective laparoscopic surgery. First, we thought dezocine played a protective role through its multimodel analgesia effect, which is consistent with the pain score in the PACU. Second, In studies conducted recently, it was found that dezocine interacted with the serotonin transporter at its ligand binding site and inhibited serotonin reabsorption in a concentration-dependent manner.27 The serotonin transporter is an important target for depression treatment.28 However, whether dezocine decreases ED incidence by binding to other receptors needs further study.

The association between elevated Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) scores and a heightened risk of postoperative delirium has been extensively acknowledged.29, 30, 31 Firstly, this may be because high levels of pain can lead to a neurotransmitter imbalance and disruption of brain function, thus increasing the likelihood of delirium. Secondly, pain may also cause anxiety and sleep disturbances, which are also risk factors for delirium. Therefore, timely assessment and control of pain levels may help reduce the incidence of delirium in high-risk patients.

In addition, our results indicate that blood loss and excessive fluid infusion increase the risk of postoperative delirium. Animal studies have reported that acute blood loss in hypertensive rats leads to learning impairments and visual spatial memory after surgery.32 During surgery, intraoperative hypotension and inadequate brain tissue perfusion may result from excessive blood loss, thus affecting brain function. Excessive fluid infusion during surgery can lead to blood dilution, electrolyte imbalances, increased blood volume, elevated intravascular pressure, and blood extravasation into surrounding tissues. These factors can disrupt the brain’s internal environment and impair cognitive function and spatial memory.31, 33 Additionally, clinical studies suggest that intraoperative blood loss may be due to activation of fibrinolysis and increased systemic inflammation after bleeding, which accelerates brain dysfunction.34

The occurrence of ED can impact a patient’s long-term outcomes and prognosis, making it crucial to identify high-risk populations and implement effective clinical interventions. The Nomogram combines clinically relevant variables with numerical probabilities to predict the risk of adverse events, offering a more practical alternative to complex and tedious mathematical models. Our model, an intuitive statistical formula model based on eight simple indicators, provides clinicians with a tool to promptly and accurately identify the population at risk for ED. This has positive implications for developing targeted ED prevention strategies. Based on our research findings, reducing the incidence of ED and improving prognosis through clinical measures is achievable.

There is still controversy about the effect of anesthetic techniques on ED, with a subset of studies showing no significant difference between the effects of TIVA and inhalational anesthetics on ED.35 While more studies have shown that the use of TIVA reduces the incidence of ED compared to inhalational anesthetics.36, 37 Therefore, in our retrospective study in the elderly population, a high-risk group of patients for ED, the TIVA anesthetic technique was used. However, the relationship between TIVA and ED requires further exploration prospective studies. Additionally, our model needs to incorporate relevant factors for analysis to refine the Nomogram to improve its accuracy.

There are certain limitations inherent in our study. Firstly, the scope of our research was confined to a single center, inherently imposing limitations on the participant pool size. As a retrospective, single-center study focused on elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic procedures, the generalizability of our nomogram to other surgical populations requires further investigation. We acknowledge that different surgical types may present unique risk factors for emergence delirium. Future prospective, multi-center studies across various surgical specialties would be valuable to validate and refine predictive models for emergence delirium in diverse patient populations. Additionally, we need to improve our model further. The retrospective nature of the study means that some factors associated with ED, as reported in previous studies, were not available, such as visual impairment, daily life abilities, high blood sugar, and obstructive sleep apnea. It is therefore necessary to conduct prospective studies and multicenter validation, including relevant indicators, to enhance the predictive ability of the model. Finally, in our retrospective study, delirium was assessed using the RASS and CAM-ICU scales. While these assessment tools have been widely used and have demonstrated a high degree of inter-rater reliability in ICU patients, none of them have been validated between PACUs. We suggest that different assessment scales be included for testing in a new prospective study on the ED.

Conclusions

This innovative scoring system known as Nomogram offers novel clinical insights and directions for preventing, managing, and treating ED. The Nomogram model, employed for predicting emergence delirium (ED) in geriatric patients undergoing laparoscopic surgery, demonstrates strong efficacy in risk forecasting, providing valuable insights for ED prevention, management, and treatment.

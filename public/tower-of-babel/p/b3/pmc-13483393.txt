A two-stage sperm holomorphological analysis method based on multi-output network construction
Authors: Wentan Jiao, Mengqing Hu, Bo Wang, Guanjun Wang, Yang Li, Jingyi Qi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSperm morphology detection technology has important research value in diagnosing male infertility. Traditional manual detection methods are time-consuming, labor-intensive, and highly subjective. The sperm morphology analysis method based on deep learning can objectively and efficiently complete the sperm detection task, and has the advantage of end-to-end learning.ResultsThis study proposed a two-stage sperm morphology analysis method, which used the UNet + + network to segment individual sperm, and then used the Multi-Head Mobilevit Net model based on multi-output classification to achieve one-time detection of the head, midpiece, and principal piece of a single sperm, and integrated the Mixing Loss function into the classification model.ConclusionsBased on the data enhancement technology, the expanded 10,802 improved Papanicolaou stain sperm morphology classification dataset was tested. The results showed that the accuracy of the sperm head, midpiece, and principal piece reached 83.47%, 96.19%, and 94.99%, respectively. This study can quickly realize the automated detection of sperm morphology, providing strong technical support for the development of reproductive medicine.Keywords: Sperm morphology analysis, Deep learning, Multi-output classification, Bilinear pooling

Introduction

Infertility can be defined as the inability of a couple to conceive naturally after at least one year of unprotected sexual intercourse [1]. Currently, infertility as an important disease, affecting about one in seven families worldwide [2]. It can be caused by men, women, both, or unexpected factors. Among them, infertility caused by male factors accounts for 30% [3]. When evaluating male factors, it is necessary to analyze semen quality based on multiple parameters such as sperm morphology, motility, and concentration. Sperm morphology analysis has important reference value in diagnosing male infertility [4]. According to the sixth edition of the reference manual for routine semen analysis published by the World Health Organization (WHO), sperm cells are composed of head, midpiece, and principal piece (as shown in Fig. 1). Their morphological abnormalities are mainly manifested in these three parts. At present, most medical institutions still rely on well-trained professional technicians to manually detect sperm morphology. This method is time-consuming and labor-intensive, and highly dependent on the professional knowledge and experience of technicians [5].

Schematic diagram of human sperm cell

In order to improve the standardization and accuracy of diagnosis, computer-assisted sperm analysis [6–7] technology has been applied. However, due to the complexity and high cost of the equipment [8], many medical institutions have not promoted it. Bijar et al. [9] proposed a fully automatic sperm morphology recognition method based on Bayesian classifier, combining the entropy based expectation-maximization algorithm and the Markov random field model to automatically segment the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece. Ghasemain et al. [10] used image noise removal and contrast enhancement methods to analyze the size and shape of each part of the sperm, and the detection accuracy of sperm morphology abnormalities reached 90%. Ilhan et al. [11] proposed an automated sperm morphology analysis method based on an orientation masking technique incorporating multilevel cascade preprocessing, region descriptor features, and nonlinear kernel support vector machines, which improved sperm classification accuracy by 10% and 5% by applying image gradient and automated orientation masking techniques on the HuSHeM [12] and SMIDS [13] datasets, respectively.

In recent years, artificial intelligence technology has begun to be applied in the field of reproductive medicine [14]. Among them, deep learning classification models (such as Mobilevit [15]) based on convolutional neural network (CNN) have performed particularly well in image classification tasks due to their excellent feature extraction capabilities. Riordon et al. [16] used the HuSHeM and SCIAN [17] datasets to retrain the VGG16 [18] network using transfer learning, and achieved the classification of sperm head images using CNN, demonstrating the effectiveness of deep learning methods in automating, standardizing, and accelerating sperm morphology analysis. Chandra et al. [19] applied a variety of classic deep neural networks (such as VGG16, ResNet50 [20], and IneptionV3 [21]) to process the MHSMA [22] dataset, and pre-trained weights are introduced into the model to analyze sperm vacuole, acrosome, and head part as normal or abnormal types. The recognition accuracy rates were 87.33%, 71%, and 73%, respectively. Different from the traditional neural network architecture, Miahi et al. [23] developed a genetic algorithm-based neural network architecture search method (GeNAS), which uses a special genetic algorithm to search for the CNN architecture to predict abnormal morphology of different parts of sperm. GeNAS detected the morphology of vacuole, acrosome and head part of sperm on the MHSMA dataset as normal or abnormal, with recognition accuracy rates of 91.66%, 77.66% and 77.33% respectively. Compared with the classic deep neural network used by Chandra et al., the GeNAS method improved the recognition accuracy of these three sperm types by 4.33%, 6.66% and 4.33% respectively. Chen et al. [24] studied the performance of deep learning methods in sperm image classification under noisy environments and demonstrated that the visual transformer has better noise resistance than the CNN in the SVIA [25] dataset, in addition to proposing a deep model for sperm and impurity detection in microscopic videos (ACTIVE) [26] deep learning model. When applying deep learning models based on CNN to the field of medical imaging, problems such as small dataset size or data imbalance often occur [27]. Therefore, data augmentation [28] technology is widely used in the field of deep learning. This technology solves the problem of data limitation by artificially modifying the original data, which can effectively avoid overfitting and improve the generalization ability of the model [29–30]. Yüzkat et al. [31] developed six different convolutional neural network models, applied training sets with multiple data augmentations such as scaling, shearing, and translation, and used two decision-level fusion technologies, hard voting and soft voting, to complete the automatic classification of sperm morphology. On the SMIDS, HuSHeM, and SCIAN sperm head datasets, the detection accuracy rates were 90.73%, 85.18%, and 71.91%, respectively. Ilhan et al. [32] proposed a method that includes two-stage fine-tuning and decision-level fusion. By fine-tuning the two pre-trained models VGG16 and GoogleNet on the ImageNet and SMIDS datasets respectively, and applying data enhancement techniques such as cropping and scaling to the datasets, the recognition accuracy on the SMIDS, HuSHeM and SCIAN datasets was improved to 90.87%, 88.89% and 72.08% respectively. Saadat et al. [33] conducted a systematic evaluation of the performance of various deep learning models with different encoder architectures on the sperm segmentation task using the VISEM dataset. Among these, the UNet + + model, which employs ResNet34 as its encoder, performed best in distinguishing between sperm and non-sperm cells, achieving an accuracy of 82.08%. Aktas et al. [34] created the LabSpermMorpho dataset with 18 different sperm morphology types. By combining multiple EfficientNetV2 models and using a soft voting method, they achieved a classification accuracy of 67.36%. Turkoglu et al. [35] used sperm images from three specific staining versions, combined with four deep learning network models, and introduced a structured multi-stage voting strategy. As a result, they achieved accuracy rates of 69.43%, 71.34%, and 68.41% in datasets using the three different staining methods, respectively.

Although existing automated classification methods have good performance in sperm morphology analysis, most studies only focus on the sperm head, ignoring the information of the sperm midpiece and principal piece, thus failing to comprehensively analyze the sperm morphology. In this study, a full morphology sperm analysis dataset (IPsSHA) based on the improved Papanicolaou stain method was constructed, and a two-stage sperm holomorphological analysis (SHA) method using deep learning technology was proposed. This method uses the UNet + + network to segment a single sperm, and the segmentation results are input into the Multi-Head Mobilevit Net (MHMN) model based on multi-output classification to achieve a one-time detection of the head, midpiece and principal piece of a single sperm. The two-stage SHA method can accurately extract detailed information on sperm morphology, eliminate background interference, improve classification accuracy, and simplify the complexity of the classification model. In this study, the MHMN model based on multi-output classification combines multi-output classification with a new lightweight Mobilevit network, integrating multiple output results of the network classifier, the local perception ability of the CNN, and the global perception advantage of the visual Transformer. The model introduces a bilinear pooling strategy for fine-grained classification tasks, which makes it advantageous in detecting local differences in sperm with diverse abnormal morphologies and small overall differences. The multi-output classification network modifies the single output layer of the traditional classification network into multiple output layers to achieve simultaneous prediction of multiple target variables. At the same time, multiple output layers share a single feature extraction network to reduce computational complexity. The MHMN model can quickly detect the full morphology of sperm and provide morphological analysis data for the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece. On the IPsSHA dataset, the MHMN model achieved detection accuracies of 83.47%, 96.19%, and 94.99% for the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece, respectively, showing a clear advantage in the accuracy and reliability of SHA.

Materials and methods

Dataset information

Experimental samples were derived from the remaining sperm after in vitro fertilization treatment. The relevant patients had signed informed consent forms and agreed to donate their sperm for medical research. During the sample processing stage, the collected semen was centrifuged, sliced and fixed, and then the semen smear was stained using the improved Papanicolaou stain method [36] to improve the visibility under the microscopic field of view. The experiment used an Olympus CX41 optical microscope and a Hikvision MV-CA020-20GM industrial camera to collect sample images. A total of 3044 sperm morphology microscopic images were obtained under the field of view of the objective lens 100× and the eyepiece 20×. Sample image annotation was completed according to the WHO Semen Analysis Reference Manual. This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Luoyang Central Hospital (LWLL-2024-07-09-01).

The data annotation work in the experiment was completed by 2 annotators. Before annotation, both annotators received unified guidance and training from experts in the field of reproductive medicine, and carried out the annotation strictly in accordance with the sperm morphology evaluation standards of the WHO. After the annotation was completed, reproductive medicine experts inspected the annotation results to ensure the accuracy of the annotation.

The research process is primarily divided into two stages: sperm segmentation and morphological classification. The dataset constructed for this study covers 9 of the 14 common sperm morphological abnormalities published by the WHO, comprising a total of 10,802 images. First, individual sperm cells were extracted from the 3,044 raw microscopic images using a segmentation model, and 6,884 images of morphologically intact sperm were selected. Due to the randomness of sample collection, among them, the number of samples showing sperm morphologies such as amorphous heads, vacuolation, and short main segments is relatively small. To mitigate the impact of sample size imbalance across different morphological categories on classification results, data augmentation techniques such as cropping, flipping, and rotation were applied to the aforementioned 6,884 sperm images to expand the original small dataset. This ultimately resulted in a dataset of 10,802 sperm morphology images for classification experiments. Table 1 shows the sample distribution across categories in the dataset. During the model training phase, all data will be randomly subjected to translation, scaling, and saturation adjustments with a certain probability to enhance the model’s performance and generalization ability.

Proposed method

This study proposed a method for analyzing SHA based on deep learning, and the method is divided into two stages. The first stage is sperm segmentation. The same dataset is trained through the segmentation network to select the optimal segmentation model. The second stage focuses on sperm morphology detection. According to the segmentation results, the MHMN method based on multi-output classification is used to complete the morphological detection of the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece at one time. The process of the fully automatic sperm morphological analysis method is shown in Fig. 2. Two rounds of data cleaning were involved in the experimental process. The first round of data cleaning was conducted after the collection of raw data, mainly to eliminate microscopic images with blurry shooting and poor imaging quality, so as to ensure the training quality of the subsequent segmentation model. The second round of data cleaning was carried out after the completion of sperm segmentation, primarily to screen sperm samples with complete morphology, ensuring the reliability and consistency of the data input into the classification network. In the cropping stage after the second washing, first, traverse the longest and widest pixel dimensions of all spermatozoa, and use these maximum length and width as thresholds for image cropping and uniform processing. Ensure that each spermatozoon can be presented completely, without morphological distortion due to size adjustment, and without loss of sperm structural information through cropping.

Overall process of sperm morphology analysis

Sperm segmentation

Semantic segmentation is a fundamental task in computer vision that aims to partition an image into semantically meaningful regions by assigning a class label to each pixel. According to the characteristics of the task, seven classic semantic segmentation networks (UNet [37], SegNet [38], PSPNet [39], Deeplabv3+ [40], UNet++ [41], HRNet [42] and Segformer [43]) were selected for sperm segmentation. During the experiment, 1000 images of 3044 sperm morphology microscopy images were randomly selected and divided into training set and validation set in a ratio of 8:2. Seven segmentation networks were applied to the same experimental dataset. By comparing the segmentation results of each network, the model with the best performance was selected to complete the sperm segmentation of the remaining 2044 samples, and the mask image after segmentation was obtained for sperm extraction.

Region extraction and target screening

To eliminate background interference in the microscopic image, each sperm was extracted separately by the region extraction algorithm and placed in a 1100 × 1100 background image with a pixel value of 0. Specifically, the binary mask generated by the segmentation network is used as the boundary of the extraction region. By performing a pixel-wise multiplication operation, the original image pixels corresponding to the values of 1 in the mask are retained, while the pixel values of the remaining areas are set to 0, thereby effectively extracting the target object from the original image. The extracted individual sperm are saved for subsequent screening.

SHA requires independent morphological examination of the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece. If any part of the sperm is abnormal, it is judged to be morphologically abnormal. Therefore, sperm that are identified as morphologically incomplete due to the limitation of the microscopic field of view during the sperm segmentation stage must be excluded. Through region extraction and target screening, only sperm with complete morphology (with head, midpiece and principal piece) are retained for morphological examination.

MHMN method

Full morphological analysis of sperm requires independent morphological detection of the head, midpiece and principal piece of each sperm (Fig. 3), and a separate analysis is made based on the morphological information of each part of the sperm. However, the traditional Mobilevit network can only provide a single output result. Therefore, this study modified the single output layer of the Mobilevit network into multiple independent classifiers, each classifier is responsible for a specific output, thereby transforming the original multi-class dataset with only one column of labels into a multi-output dataset, that is, the output space has multiple categories and multiple labels at the same time. In the forward propagation of the network, the width and height in the tensor dimension are adaptively averaged to ensure that multiple classifiers can act on the network output in parallel, so as to obtain multiple output labels at the same time.

To improve the accuracy of sperm detection, the MHMN method also introduces a compact bilinear pooling (CBP) [44] layer before the network output layer, which has advantages in detecting local differences in sperm. This is because compact bilinear pooling, as an effective feature fusion technology, efficiently captures rich interactive information by calculating the outer product of two feature vectors and using random projection technology to reduce the dimension. In addition, compact bilinear pooling supports the fusion of features between different network layers, and further improves the model’s sensitivity to image details by combining deep semantic information and shallow texture information.

MHMN can map each input instance to multiple output labels, and its structural design is shown in Fig. 4. Specifically, let X = Rd be a d-dimensional input space and Y = Rm be an m-dimensional output label space. The purpose of MHMN is to train the dataset to learn the mapping function f: X→Y. In this model, each training instance (xi, yi) contains a d-dimensional feature vector xi and its corresponding m-dimensional output yi. MHMN includes a feature extraction network with a convolutional layer and multiple independent classification output layers. During the training process of the network, the parameters of the feature extraction network are shared. The features output by the convolutional layer are passed to multiple classification layers, where each classification layer independently completes the classification task based on the received feature information and outputs the result.

Schematic diagram of MHMN network structure

Inspired by lightweight CNN, two MHMNs with different network sizes (S: small, and XS: extra small) are designed. MHMN-S has more parameters, provides better performance, and is suitable for complex visual tasks. MHMN-XS can perform basic image processing and classification tasks while maintaining low computational costs. During the training phase, larger models require more data and training cycles to fully learn complex features, while smaller models train faster.

Performance indicators

IoU and F-score are the main indicators for evaluating the performance of different segmentation networks. IoU can directly reflect the degree of overlap between the predicted segmentation region and the real annotation, and is highly sensitive to the overall segmentation quality of the model; while F-score comprehensively considers precision and recall, balances the problem of over-segmentation and under-segmentation to a certain extent, and is more suitable for evaluating the boundary consistency and robustness of segmentation results. Different segmentation networks differ in terms of feature extraction methods, context modeling capabilities, and parameter scales. These structural characteristics will directly affect the model’s performance on the above key indicators. Therefore, under the same dataset and experimental settings, through a comprehensive comparison of core indicators such as IoU and F-score, we can effectively screen out the network model more suitable for the current segmentation task, thereby providing a basis for subsequent model selection and improvement. In the segmentation task stage, in addition to IoU and F-score, precision and recall are introduced as auxiliary evaluation criteria to comprehensively evaluate the network performance. In the morphological detection tage, accuracy, recall, precision, F-score and kappa will be used to evaluate the performance of the multi-output classification network to ensure that the performance of the network in all aspects is fully considered. IoU is a pixel-level metric that evaluates the degree of overlap between the model’s predictions and the true labels, reflecting the overall quality of the segmentation effect. Recall measures the model’s ability to capture all true targets, while precision represents the proportion of true targets correctly identified in the prediction. F-score uses the harmonic mean instead of the arithmetic mean, which helps reduce the impact of outliers on the mean, thereby providing a more balanced performance metric. Accuracy measures the proportion of positive and negative samples correctly classified by the model in the entire dataset, and is a commonly used important metric for evaluating classification problems. The Kappa coefficient is an indicator of classification accuracy, which evaluates the actual prediction ability of the model by comparing the consistency of the actual classification results with random classification.

Experimental results

In the experiment, the segmentation effect of different segmentation networks on sperm was analyzed. According to the experimental indicators, the appropriate segmentation network was selected to complete the segmentation of a single sperm. According to the segmentation results, 6884 sperm images with complete morphology were extracted and screened. The small sample sperm morphology images were expanded using data enhancement techniques such as flipping and rotation, and the expanded 10,802 images were compiled into the IPsSHA dataset for sperm morphology classification. In the classification experiment, the MHMN method was used to detect five morphologies of the sperm head (amorphous, normal, round, tapered and vacuolated), three morphologies of the midpiece (bent, normal and thick) and four morphologies of the principal piece (bent, coiled, normal and short).

The segmentation experiment compared seven classic semantic segmentation networks, including UNet, SegNet, PSPNet, Deeplabv3+, UNet++, HRNet, and Segformer. An initial screening was conducted based on the networks’ performance on the loss function for the sperm segmentation task. All networks used the same initial parameter settings during the training phase; however, due to differences in network architecture, the fitting performance varied across networks even when using the same training and validation sets. After 100 training iterations, the training and validation loss data for each network are shown in Fig. 5. The results indicate that the UNet + + network performed best in terms of both training and validation loss, suggesting that this network achieves the best fitting performance for the sperm segmentation task. Furthermore, the network is analyzed in depth through the four evaluation indicators of IoU, recall, precision and F-score. In particular, F-score, as a comprehensive indicator of recall and precision, provides a more objective performance comparison. As shown in Table 2, although the UNet + + network is slightly inferior in the recall indicator, the network shows the best performance in the IoU, precision and F-score indicators, reaching 88.59%, 92.71% and 93.58% respectively. In order to ensure high-quality data in the sperm classification stage, it is necessary to consider the sperm segmentation from the perspective of precision, and priority should be given to the precision and F-score indicators. Taking into account the training loss, verification loss and multiple evaluation indicators, it is believed that UNet + + has significant advantages in the sperm segmentation task. Therefore, the UNet + + network was selected in the segmentation stage to complete the image segmentation of sperm samples, and further use the segmented images to make the IPsSHA dataset.

Discussion

In the field of machine learning, traditional classification networks typically focus on solving single-output tasks, predicting only a single target variable. In contrast, multi-output classification methods can predict multiple related output variables simultaneously, thereby enabling a single model to handle more complex, multidimensional tasks. When using traditional classification networks for SHA, the sperm head, midpiece, and principal piece must be divided into three sub-datasets. Each sub-dataset requires separate training, and the detection results for each part must be output separately.

Figure 7 shows the detection accuracy of different traditional classification networks on three sub-datasets. The bar graph shows the average accuracy of different networks in detecting different parts of sperm, and the dot-line graph shows the detection accuracy of these networks in different parts of sperm. The detection accuracy of Resnet50 network in sperm head is higher than that of Mobilenetv1 [45], Mobilenetv2 [46] and Shufflenetv2 [47], but its model weight file can reach 90 MB, which is 5–20 times that of the other three models. Mobilevit network shows the best performance in sperm morphology analysis. Among them, the average accuracy of Mobilevit_XS network is 86.91%, while the detection accuracy of Mobilevit_S network is higher than that of Mobilevit_XS, reaching 88.08%. This is because it has more parameters and can extract richer feature information, but at the same time it puts forward higher standards for computing requirements.

Comparison results of traditional classification networks in sub-datasets

The experimental results of the Mobilevit network on three sub-datasets were compared with the MOM method to further illustrate the advantages of the multi-output classification method in the task of SHA. As shown in Table 6, compared with the Mobilevit_XS network, the MOM_XS network using multi-output classification improved the recognition accuracy of the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece by 0.67%, 1.25% and 0.26%, respectively. The recognition accuracy of the MOM_S network in the sperm midpiece and principal piece was 0.86% and 0.17% higher than that of the Mobilevit_S network, respectively, while the recognition accuracy of the head was only 0.34% lower than that of the Mobilevit_S network. The multi-output classification network has a higher accuracy than the traditional classification network in some cases. The main reason is that it realizes information fusion and regularization between tasks by sharing feature extraction layers, avoids overfitting of a single task, and uses implicit prior knowledge between tasks to improve prediction accuracy. In addition, jointly optimizing the loss functions of multiple tasks and effectively using multi-task data enhances sample utilization, thereby improving the feature representation ability and generalization ability of the model. Therefore, the MOM network can achieve higher overall performance when processing multiple tasks. From the perspective of computational efficiency, the MOM network has a greater advantage because it can enable one feature extraction network to process multiple labels simultaneously by modifying the output classifier. In contrast, the Mobilevit network requires multiple feature extraction networks to process different labels separately. Although the model weight of the Mobilevit network is small, the information it contains is not as rich as that of the MOM network. The weight file of the MOM network covers the full morphological detection information of the sperm head, midpiece, and principal piece, while the information of the Mobilevit network is limited to a part of it. When analyzing the full morphological information of sperm, this study achieved a higher recognition accuracy with only a slight increase in weight.

Conclusion

This study proposed a two-stage sperm morphological analysis method based on deep learning. The UNet + + network was used to segment sperm images under microscopic imaging and extract the segmented sperm. The MHMN method based on multi-output classification was used to achieve full morphological analysis of the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece. This method can output multiple labels for different parts of sperm at one time, significantly improving detection efficiency. In addition, CBP technology was introduced and the network loss function was modified to mitigate the impact on model performance caused by small differences in sperm morphological characteristics and data imbalance. The results show that in the IPsSHA data, the accuracy of the MHMN method in detecting the sperm head, midpiece and principal piece reached 83.47%, 96.19% and 94.99% respectively, realizing automated sperm detection. This study provides an efficient sperm morphology analysis solution and demonstrates its broad application potential in the field of reproductive medicine.

Possibility of calcification of type I collagen matrix in growth hormone-secreting pituitary neuroendocrine tumor
Authors: Shinichiro Teramoto, Takuma Kodama, Akihide Kondo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background: In acromegaly caused by growth hormone (GH)-secreting pituitary neuroendocrine tumor (PitNET), excessive secretion of GH and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) enhances bone anabolic effects while inducing the production of type I collagen, leading to increased bone mass and bone mineral density. We investigated the possibility of calcification of type I collagen matrix used in endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery (ETSS) for GH-secreting PitNET.Methods: Twelve patients with GH-secreting PitNET who underwent ETSS and were followed up for more than 1 year after surgery were divided into two groups: The “DuraGen group,” in which DuraGen, a type I collagen matrix, was used as the overlay graft to cover the closed sella turcica for sellar floor reconstruction after tumor resection and the “fat group,” in which fat was used. Clinical characteristics associated with surgery for GH-secreting PitNET were compared between the groups.Results: Seven patients were assigned to the DuraGen group and five to the fat group. One year after surgery, the reconstructed sellar floor was thickly calcified in all cases in the DuraGen group, but not calcified in all cases in the fat group. Patient characteristics, tumor-related factors including image findings, tumor size, histopathology and extent of tumor resection, and the pre- and postoperative course of GH and IGF-1 demonstrated no significant differences between the groups.Conclusion: In ETSS for acromegaly due to GH-secreting PitNET, the possibility of calcification of type I collagen matrix used as the overlay graft for sellar floor reconstruction should be considered.

INTRODUCTION

Acromegaly is a systematic disease caused by chronic hypersecretion of growth hormone (GH) after epiphyseal closure, typically due to GH-secreting pituitary neuroendocrine tumor (PitNET). The skeletal manifestations of acromegaly originate in bone proliferation, including mandibular protrusion, enlargement of the frontal sinus, development of the external occipital protuberance, and cauliflower-like tufting of the distal phalanxes of the hands. Treatment of acromegaly is known to improve soft-tissue thickening,[28] but does not appear to affect bone thickening.[35] Deformed skulls in acromegaly patients were reported to show no improvement 2.6 years after surgery.[35] First-line treatment of acromegaly is generally surgical resection of GH-secreting PitNET, which is currently performed through endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery (ETSS). ETSS has been recognized as a safe and effective treatment for acromegaly with an endocrine remission rate of approximately 70% achieve by only surgery.[2,8] However, ETSS is associated with serious surgical complications, especially postoperative cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leakage. Closure techniques to prevent postoperative CSF leakage include filling of the dead space in the sella turcica, watertight dural suture repair, reconstruction of the sellar floor with hard materials, and covering the dural defect with a vascularized pedicled flap,[40] and these methods have significantly reduced CSF leakage in recent years. One method of sellar floor reconstruction following tumor resection by ETSS is the gasket-seal closure,[21] in which the free graft covering over the suture-closed dura mater is fixed using a hard material such as nasal septal bone or bioabsorbable plate to reconstruct the sellar floor. We believe that the gasket-seal closure with additional coverage over the closed dura mater is effective to prevent CSF leakage after ETSS by achieving firmer sellar floor reconstruction.

DuraGen (Integra LifeSciences, Plainsboro, NJ), approved as a medical material by the Ministry of Health, Labor, and Welfare in Japan in 2019, is an absorbable artificial dural substitute consisting of purified type I collagen derived from bovine Achilles tendon and is widely applied as a repair replacement material for dural defects in neurosurgery and spinal surgery. DuraGen, a type I collagen matrix with a porous structure, adheres closely to the dura mater and the platelets of any infiltrating blood will form fibrin clots, which create a biological seal that closes the dural defect without dural suturing and prevents CSF leakage, with a reported postoperative CSF leakage incidence of 0.4%.[36] We sometimes use DuraGen as the overlay graft to reinforce the dura mater in the gasket-seal closure for sellar floor reconstruction in ETSS [Figure 1].

DuraGen is a surgical product that is eventually resorbed and replaced by dural tissue, with no reports of calcification in DuraGen used in surgical treatment to our and Integra LifeSciences Corporation knowledge. Here, we report some cases of thick calcification in DuraGen which was used as the overlay graft for sellar floor reconstruction in ETSS for GH-secreting PitNET, and a comparative analysis with cases in which DuraGen was not used.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Diagnosis and remission criteria for acromegaly

In Japan, acromegaly is diagnosed according to the guidelines for diagnosis and treatment of hypothalamic and pituitary dysfunction supervised by the Japan Endocrine Society (https://square.umin.ac.jp/endocrine/english/index. html). The diagnosis of acromegaly must meet four criteria as follows: (1) presence of some cardinal symptoms such as increased size of the hands and feet, acromegalic face including bulging forehead, enlarged nose and lips, and mandibular prognathism, and large tongue. (2) Elevated serum GH levels measured multiple times by assay using a uniform recombinant human GH (rhGH) standard and nadir GH levels not suppressed below 0.4 ng/mL after the 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT). (3) Serum insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) levels above age- and sex-specific reference values for healthy individuals. (4) Demonstration of pituitary tumor by brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or computed tomography (CT).

Serum GH levels were measured using electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) (LSI Medience Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), with reference ranges of <2.47 ng/mL for men and 0.13–9.88 ng/mL for women. For the 75-g OGTT, patients fasted overnight and had GH levels measured at 0, 30, 60, 90, and 120 min after oral glucose intake while resting the following morning. IGF-1 was assessed by standard deviation score (SDS) calculated on the basis of the age- and sex-adjusted reference intervals of a healthy Japanese population for serum IGF-1 levels measured using ECLIA (LSI Medience Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), with +2 SDS or higher defined as high.[15] The first-line treatment for acromegaly is commonly surgical resection of the causative GH-secreting PitNET. Surgical remission of GH-secreting PitNET in this study was defined as suppression of IGF-1 SDS to <+2 SDS as well as a nadir GH level of <0.4 ng/mL after 75-g OGTT or a random GH level of <1.0 ng/mL in the postoperative period.[13]

Patient selection

We retrospectively reviewed 12 patients diagnosed with acromegaly who underwent ETSS by the same surgeon and were followed up for more than 1 year at the Juntendo University Graduate School of Medicine from 2021 to 2023. All patients reviewed in this study had pneumatization of the sphenoid sinus and preserved shape of the sella turcica. The 12 acromegaly patients were divided into two groups: patients with DuraGen, a type I collagen matrix, used as the overlay graft in the gasket-seal closure for sellar floor reconstruction after resection of GH-secreting PitNET were included in the “DuraGen group” and patients with fat used were included in the “fat group.”

Clinical assessment

The presence of calcification of the reconstructed sellar floor after ETSS, age, sex, tumor-related factors, and the pre- and post-operative course of GH and IGF-1 were compared between the DuraGen and fat groups. Calcification of the reconstructed sellar floor was defined as an overall thick, high-density values around the sella turcica on three-plane (sagittal, axial, and coronal) bone CT. Tumor-related factors included signal patterns on T2-weighted image (T2WI) classified as low, iso, and high, and signal patterns on gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted image (Gd-T1WI) classified as homogeneous and heterogeneous, tumor volume, histological subtype classified as densely and sparsely granulated somatotroph tumor, and Ki-67 labeling index indicating tumor cell proliferation. Extent of tumor resection was categorized as total or subtotal and included in tumor-related factors. The courses of GH and IGF-1 in the pre- and postoperative period were expressed as random GH levels, IGF-1 SDS, and nadir GH levels after the 75-g OGTT preoperatively, 3 months postoperatively, and 1 year postoperatively.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using easy R (EZR) (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Omiya, Saitama, Japan), a graphical user interface for R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria; version 2.13.0).[16] Average values for the groups are presented as mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range. Data were analyzed with the unpaired t-test, Mann–Whitney U-test, and Fisher’s exact test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

All patients who underwent ETSS for GH-secreting PitNET in the present study had no serious complications including CSF leakage, olfactory disturbances, nasal obstruction, or infection. Table 1 shows the individual clinical characteristics. The four men and eight women were aged 26–69 years, with seven patients assigned to the DuraGen group and five to the fat group. Comparison of patient characteristics between the groups is summarized in Table 2. One year after ETSS for GH-secreting PitNET, the reconstructed sellar floor was calcified in all patients in the DuraGen group but in none of the patients in the fat group (P = 0.001). Mean age was 43.6 ± 8.6 years in the DuraGen group, and 44.0 ± 17.0 years in the fat group, with no significant difference (P = 0.960). Ratio of men to women between the DuraGen (one men and six women) and fat (three men and two women) groups showed no significant difference (P = 0.222).

Representative case (Case 1)

A 31-year-old woman with no medical history was incidentally diagnosed with a pituitary tumor compressing the optic nerve. Her fingers had been slightly enlarged for several years so that rings would no longer fit, but none of the cardinal symptoms such as acromegalic face and large tongue, as well as headache, visual impairment, and menstrual disorders were present. The pituitary tumor had a maximum diameter of 21 mm, appeared as iso-signal intensity on T2WI with homogeneous signal enhancement on Gd-T1WI, and had invaded the right cavernous sinus [Figure 2a]. CT showed pneumatization of the sphenoid sinus and preservation of the shape of the sella turcica [Figure 2b]. Random GH levels were high at 12.4 ng/mL (normal range, 0.13–9.88 ng/mL) and IGF-1 levels were elevated at 898 ng/mL (age- and sex-adjusted normal range, 126–297 ng/mL; +8.15 SDS). The 75-g OGTT indicated inadequate suppression with a nadir GH level of 9.66 ng/mL. The patient was preoperatively diagnosed with acromegaly caused by a GH-secreting PitNET and underwent ETSS.

The pituitary tumor within the sella turcica was resected, then the right cavernous sinus was opened to resect the tumor around the internal carotid artery. After tumor resection, dural suturing was performed and then the sellar floor was reconstructed by the gasket-seal closure using DuraGen and nasal septal bone [Figure 2c]. The postoperative course was uneventful. Postoperative MRI showed residual tumor medial and inferior to the internal carotid artery posteriorly within the right cavernous sinus [Figure 2d]. Histological examination of the pituitary tumor identified sparsely granulated somatotroph PitNET. Three months after surgery, the patient had random GH levels of 3.41 ng/mL, IGF-1 levels of 591 ng/mL (age- and sex-adjusted normal range, 126–297 ng/mL; +5.69 SDS), and nadir GH levels after 75-g OGTT of 2.55 ng/mL. One year after surgery, the patient had random GH levels of 3.02 ng/mL, IGF-1 levels of 493 ng/mL (age- and sex-adjusted normal range, 119–283 ng/mL; +4.94 SDS), and nadir GH levels after 75-g OGTT of 2.42 ng/mL, which did not satisfy the criteria for surgical remission of acromegaly. Consultation between the neurosurgeon and endocrinologist led to the decision to reoperate before introduction of somatostatin analogs because further resection of the residual tumor was feasible, which would hopefully provide better control of GH secretion.

Preoperative CT before the reoperation 1 year after initial surgery showed thickening of the reconstructed sellar floor due to calcification [Figure 2e]. Intraoperative findings during the reoperation through ETSS also indicated thick and irregular calcification around the reconstructed sellar floor, which required careful drilling to expose the sella turcica while avoiding injury to the internal carotid artery, so a wide operative field was difficult to create [Figure 2f]. The tumor around the internal carotid artery posteriorly within the right cavernous sinus was resected as much as possible, but administration of somatostatin analog was initiated because the remission criteria of acromegaly were not met after the reoperation.

DISCUSSION

GH, together with systemically and locally produced IGF-1, regulates the bone metabolism responsible for bone modeling and remodeling, which result in skeletal construction and maintenance.[31,32,45] GH and IGF-1, which are key regulators of bone homeostasis, exert anabolic action on bone throughout life and contribute to the stabilization of bone mass and bone mineral density, and have been implicated in the development of osteoporosis in postmenopausal and old age by decreasing with age.[14,44]

GH stimulates bone formation primarily through the mediation of IGF-1 and also acts directly on osteoblast proliferation and differentiation.[14,23] Mesenchymal progenitors activated by GH are induced to differentiate into osteoblasts that express GH receptors, leading to production of bone morphogenetic proteins that are responsible for ectopic bone formation.[20,37] GH enhances the activity of osteoprotegerin, which inhibits osteoclastogenesis, so promoting the accumulation of calcified bone tissue.[14] In addition, GH has been implicated in extracellular matrix calcification, as suggested by an in vitro study investigating the direct effect of GH on de novo bone formation.[23] GH promotes skeletal maturation by increasing bone mass and longitudinal bone growth during adolescence and early adulthood, and preserves bone mass and skeletal architecture by controlling bone metabolism during adulthood.[3,29] GH also indirectly affects bone metabolism by regulating parathyroid hormone secretion and its circadian rhythm.[19]

The fundamental role of IGF-I in bone metabolism is to stimulate bone formation by enhancing osteoblast differentiation.[6,27] Systemic IGF-I is required for the integrity of cortical bone structure, whereas locally produced IGF-I has a more prominent effect in the maintenance of trabecular bone.[14] GH replacement therapy for patients with GH deficiency may induce a significant increase in osteoprotegerin that inhibits osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption function in both cortical and trabecular bones, thus preserving bone mass.[41] Overexpression of osteoprotegerin in mice was reported to cause osteopetrosis, which results in increased bone mineral density and abnormal bone proliferation.[47] rhGH has a dose-dependent increased action on bone metabolism, and the effect of rhGH in bone remodeling is believed to be biphasic, with rhGH exerting the greatest effect for bone resorption 3 months after administration, while producing the greatest effect for bone formation 6 months after administration and persisting for a long time.[17] rhGH therapy in pediatric GH-deficient patients achieved gradual improvement in bone formation after the start of treatment and increased bone mineral density 6–12 months after treatment, whereas adult GH-deficient patients experienced progressive bone formation 6 months after treatment and elevated bone mineral density 18–24 months after treatment.[14]

Overproduction of GH and subsequent IFG-1 effects on osteoblasts enhance bone metabolism, leading to increased bone growth and bone mineral density in acromegaly patients.[46] Excessive secreted GH increases bone mineral density rather than bone area.[25,46] Disease activity of acromegaly is presumed to be a major factor affecting increases in bone mass and bone mineral density in the lumbar spine and femoral neck, and high serum IGF-1 levels in patients with active acromegaly are associated with increased bone mineral density in the femoral neck.[17,38] However, investigations of bone mineral density in acromegaly have remained controversial, with some studies showing decreased bone mineral density in patients with acromegaly and others showing no differences in body mineral density between acromegaly patients and healthy controls.[25] Excess GH is considered to exert anabolic action on cortical bone and less on trabecular bone,[17,25] and likewise bone mass and bone mineral density appeared to increase in cortical bone but little in trabecular bone in acromegaly.[14,17] GH and IGF-1 levels are positively correlated with bone formation markers including procollagen type 1 amino-terminal propeptide, osteocalcin, and bone-specific alkaline phosphatase in patients with acromegaly.[33,34] Osteoblast activation is enhanced by increased osteocalcin, a marker of bone formation, and osteoclast differentiation and activity are inhibited by excess GH in active acromegaly.[34]

Chronic inflammation triggered by infection can lead to tissue calcification, such as the calcified lesions observed in the respiratory and non-respiratory organs after a chronic course of about 1 year in patients with tuberculosis.[9] DuraGen begins to degrade with the infiltration of fibroblasts and angiogenesis 6–8 weeks after dural adhesion, and is then absorbed and replaced by dural tissue over 3 months,[30] with no reports of transformation into anything other than dural tissue. DuraGen is a type I collagen matrix that contains no potential viruses and antigenic substances that pose risks for infection, immune responses, and foreign body reactions, so does not promote infection under infectious conditions,[26] with an infection incidence rate of 1.9% according to the manufacturer. None of our 12 acromegaly patients experienced fever, pain, or rhinitis related to infection.

GH and IGF-I exert anabolic actions on cortical and trabecular bones, particularly cortical bone, by inducing periosteal calcification through matrix deposition on the bone surface resulting in increased bone width, skeletal strength, and bone mineral density,[14,39] which may account for the characteristic bone deformity associated with acromegaly. GH and IGF-I stimulate the production of active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 through activation of 1α-hydroxylase and inhibition of 24-hydroxylase,[22] which results in deposition of extracellular calcium-phosphate products on collagen matrix at the bone surface to assist bone calcification. Autocrine and paracrine interactions between GH and IGF-1 enhance proliferation and differentiation of osteoblast cell lines, resulting in the production of type I collagen which accounts for over 90% of the extracellular matrix of bone, on which calcium phosphate is deposited to form the calcified bone matrix.[1,14,43,48] Type I collagen in bone tissue is synthesized by GH-stimulated type I procollagen mRNA expression in osteoblasts and is secreted extracellularly to form osteoids on the bone matrix surface, where calcification is promoted by matrix vesicles and alkaline phosphatase.[10] IGF-I acts on the bone matrix by upregulating type I collagen transcription and advancing collagen synthesis, resulting in increased bone mass.[7] Short-term rhGH treatment results in increases in carboxy-terminal propeptide of type I procollagen and carboxy-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen.[12] Since GH could affect type I collagen to cause calcification,[10] type I collagen-based DuraGen may become calcified after exposure to excess GH action on the bone surface.

In this study, the only significant factor that could result in the observed calcification of the reconstructed sellar floor in ETSS for GH-secreting PitNET was the use of DuraGen as an overlay graft to cover the closed sella turcica. Although the larger tumor volume in the fat group than in the DuraGen group and the different extent of tumor resection between the two groups may influence the process of remodeling and calcification of the sellar floor, there were no statistically significant differences. The present findings suggested that DuraGen may have continued to calcify even after GH/IGF-1 secretion was reduced following resection of GH-secreting PitNET. GH replacement therapy, rhGH, lost its effect on bone composition after discontinuation, but maintained a sustained increase in bone mineral density for 18 months after discontinuation.[14] A study of patients with adult GH deficiency found that discontinuation of rhGH therapy normalized levels of the bone resorption marker C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen, but osteocalcin, a marker of bone formation, continued to be affected for at least 6 months.[11] A similar study in adult GH-deficient patients demonstrated a decrease in C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen and an increase in osteocalcin over a 2-year period after 1 year of GH replacement therapy, suggesting the long-lasting positive effect of GH on bone formation even after treatment.[18] The anabolic action of GH on skeletal and bone mineral density in acromegaly is sometimes maintained some-time after treatment-induced remission.[4,42] These reports that the GH-induced bone anabolic effects persisted after discontinuation of GH stimulation may support the possibility that GH action induced calcification of DuraGen despite postoperative suppression of GH secretion that met the remission criteria for acromegaly.

Calcification of the reconstructed sellar floor might occur during the repair process after ETSS, but thick calcification of the reconstructed sellar floor within a short period of around 1 year has not been reported. GH and IGF-1, which are believed to affect bone formation even after remission of acromegaly, may possibly induce calcification of DuraGen, a type I collagen matrix placed on the superficial side of the reconstructed sellar floor. A recent systematic review showed that the postoperative recurrence rate for GH-secreting PitNETs is 2–3%, with a reoperation rate of 84.2%.[24] Thickening of the reconstructed sellar floor due to calcification narrows the operative field and increases the surgical risks such as internal carotid artery injury, making reoperation more difficult as in our Case 1. Type I collagen matrix may calcify when used on the surface of the sella turcica in ETSS for GH-secreting PitNET, resulting in a potential obstacle to repeat tumor resection, so careful consideration of the use of DuraGen is required in acromegaly patients with the possibility for reoperation. On the other hand, calcification of type I collagen matrix used for sellar floor reconstruction in ETSS for GH-secreting PitNET may be advantage for avoiding the risk of postoperative CSF leakage.

Limitations

The small number of subjects and single institution in this study may limit the validity of our conclusions. Most follow-up imaging within 1 year after surgery was performed using MRI, making it difficult to determine accurately when calcification of the reconstructed sellar floor began in the DuraGen group. After dural adhesion, DuraGen is replaced by the dura mater in 3 months, whereas fat can take more than 1 year,[5] and differences in these physiological changes to dural replacement may influence the present results. Comparative analysis including PitNET cases other than GH-secreting PitNET using DuraGen as the overlay graft for sellar floor reconstruction in ETSS was not possible due to the disproportionate number of cases, but no calcification of the sellar floor was observed 1 year after surgery in 61 cases of PitNET (55 non-functioning, four adrenocorticotropic hormone-secreting, and two prolactin-secreting PitNETs) using DuraGen as the overlay graft for sellar floor reconstruction by the gasket-seal closure in ETSS from 2021 to 2023. We report the possibility that DuraGen may be calcified in use on the surface of the sella turcica, which is extracranial, and do not consider the use of DuraGen in surgery for acromegaly patients with GH-secreting PitNET as a disadvantage in itself.

CONCLUSION

The present study found that the action of excess GH and IGF-1 in patients with acromegaly may induce calcification of type I collagen matrix used in ETSS. We intend to examine cases from other institutions in which type I collagen matrix has been used in the surgical treatment for acromegaly to confirm the validity of our findings.

Mitochondria-targeted SkQ1 supplementation enhances post-thaw quality, cryosurvival, and functional competence of rooster spermatozoa
Authors: Ranieh Hadi Hassan, Mohammad Shamsollahi, Mahdieh Mehdipour, Farshid Fatahnia, Yaser Khorram Del
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study investigated the effects of different concentrations of SkQ1, a mitochondria-targeted antioxidant, on the post-thaw quality of rooster spermatozoa. Pooled semen samples were diluted in a Lake-based freezing extender containing 0, 12.5, 25, 50, or 100 nM SkQ1 and cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen. After thawing, sperm motility and kinematic parameters, plasma membrane integrity, abnormal morphology, mitochondrial activity, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content, viability, apoptosis-like changes, dead sperm percentage, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and antioxidant indices were assessed. SkQ1 supplementation significantly affected most post-thaw sperm quality parameters. The greatest protection was observed at 25 nM, which resulted in the highest total motility (68.75 ± 1.37 %), progressive motility (31.64 ± 1.92 %), membrane integrity (65.60 ± 1.87 %), mitochondrial activity (66.89 ± 1.90 %), ATP content (115.80 ± 2.27 pmol/10⁶ sperm), and viability (60.60 ± 1.16 %). This concentration also produced the lowest abnormal morphology (12.20 ± 0.51 %), apoptotic spermatozoa (11.10 ± 1.32 %), and ROS production (1.83 ± 0.11 × 10³ cpm/10⁶ sperm). Moreover, 25 nM SkQ1 enhanced antioxidant defense, as indicated by increased total antioxidant capacity (TAC; 2.46 ± 0.08 mmol/L), superoxide dismutase (SOD; 133.09 ± 5.64 U/mg), and glutathione peroxidase (GPX; 69.08 ± 2.46 U/mg). The 50 nM treatment had moderate beneficial effects, whereas 100 nM was generally less effective. These findings indicate that SkQ1 improves cryosurvival of rooster spermatozoa primarily through mitochondrial protection, redox regulation, and preservation of cellular energy status. Therefore, 25 nM SkQ1 appears to be the most effective concentration for improving the functional competence of frozen-thawed rooster spermatozoa.

Introduction

Semen cryopreservation is an important biotechnological tool for artificial insemination programs, genetic resource conservation, and long-term preservation of valuable poultry lines (Arif et al., 2025). However, the practical efficiency of rooster semen freezing remains limited because avian spermatozoa are highly susceptible to cryopreservation-induced injury. Freeze-thaw stress can cause cold shock, osmotic imbalance, membrane lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation (Wang et al., 2025). In addition, poultry sperm membranes contain a high proportion of polyunsaturated fatty acids, which further predisposes them to oxidative damage and may compromise sperm motility, plasma membrane integrity, mitochondrial activity, and fertilizing ability (Janosikova et al., 2023).

Oxidative stress is considered one of the major causes of post-thaw deterioration in sperm quality (Li et al., 2023). When ROS production exceeds the endogenous antioxidant capacity of spermatozoa, structural and functional damage may occur, including reduced motility, abnormal morphology, mitochondrial impairment, decreased adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production, apoptosis-like changes, and reduced viability (Gualtieri et al., 2021). Therefore, supplementation of semen extenders with effective antioxidants has been widely studied as a strategy to improve rooster sperm cryosurvival and post-thaw functionality (Díaz Ruiz et al., 2024).

Mitochondria-targeted antioxidants are of particular interest in sperm cryobiology because mitochondria are both a major source and an important target of ROS in sperm cells. Direct protection of mitochondria may therefore improve post-thaw sperm quality by reducing oxidative damage and maintaining mitochondrial function (Farshad and Wehrend, 2025). Previous studies in rooster semen have shown that mitochondria-targeted antioxidants can improve cryopreservation outcomes. Nazari et al. (2022) reported that mitoquinone (MitoQ) improved post-thaw motility, membrane functionality, mitochondrial activity, and antioxidant status, and Masoudi et al. (2020) showed that MitoTEMPO improved several post-thaw sperm quality and fertility-related indices. Alipour-Jenaghard et al. (2023) also reported that MitoQ preserved the quality and fertility potential of post-thawed rooster spermatozoa.

SkQ1 is a mitochondria-targeted antioxidant composed of a plastoquinone antioxidant group linked to a lipophilic triphenylphosphonium cation. This structure allows SkQ1 to pass through biological membranes and accumulate in the mitochondrial matrix, where it can suppress mitochondrial ROS (Skulachev et al., 2023). Recent evidence in reproductive cells also supports the relevance of SkQ1 for mitochondrial and redox protection. Li et al. (2024) reported that SkQ1 reduced oxidative stress and improved mitochondrial function during mouse oocyte maturation. Peng et al. (2025) demonstrated that SkQ1 improved boar sperm quality during liquid storage by enhancing antioxidant status, ATP content, and mitochondrial-related function.

Collectively, the available evidence on SkQ1 in animal reproductive cells and boar sperm preservation (Peng et al., 2025), together with studies of related mitochondria-targeted antioxidants in rooster semen cryopreservation, supports the evaluation of SkQ1 in the present post-thaw cryopreservation model.

Accordingly, the objective of the present study was to determine whether supplementing a Lake-based freezing extender with SkQ1 at 12.5, 25, 50, or 100 nM could improve the post-thaw quality of rooster spermatozoa. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate SkQ1 in poultry sperm cryopreservation. The effects of SkQ1 were assessed after thawing using motility and kinematic parameters, plasma membrane integrity, abnormal morphology, mitochondrial activity, ATP content, viability, apoptosis-like status, dead sperm percentage, ROS production, and antioxidant indices, including SOD, TAC, and GPX, to identify the most effective concentration for improving cryosurvival, mitochondrial performance, and post-thaw functional competence.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

All chemicals and reagents used for extender preparation, cryopreservation, fluorescence staining, and biochemical analyses were of analytical grade. Unless otherwise stated, reagents were purchased from commercial suppliers, including Sigma-Aldrich and Merck.

SkQ1 (10-(6′-plastoquinonyl) decyltriphenylphosphonium bromide) was used as the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant supplement in the rooster semen freezing extender. A concentrated stock solution of SkQ1 was prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and serially diluted in the semen freezing extender to obtain final concentrations of 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 nM. SkQ1 was not added to the control extender. The final DMSO concentration was kept identical in all experimental groups, including the control, to avoid any solvent-related effect on post-thaw sperm quality.

Animal management and ethical statement

All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. The study protocol was approved by the Animal Research Ethics Committee of Ilam University.

The experiment was conducted with ten sexually mature 32-week-old Cobb broiler breeder roosters. Birds were individually housed in cages (approximately 70 cm × 95 cm × 85 cm) under controlled environmental conditions. The ambient temperature was maintained at 18–20°C, and the photoperiod was 15 h light and 9 h darkness. The roosters were fed a commercial breeder diet containing approximately 12 % crude protein, 2,750 kcal of metabolizable energy/kg, 0.70 % calcium, and 0.35 % available phosphorus. Fresh water was provided ad libitum throughout the experimental period.

Before semen collection, the roosters were trained to donate semen by the abdominal massage technique described for avian species (Gee et al., 2004). The experiment included only birds that consistently produced ejaculates meeting the quality criteria described below.

Semen collection and initial evaluation

Semen was collected by abdominal massage twice weekly. Immediately after collection, ejaculates were transferred to the laboratory in an insulated container maintained at 37°C and were processed within approximately 5 min. This collection frequency and rapid handling procedure were used to minimize handling-related and cold-shock effects before cryopreservation.

Ejaculates were evaluated macroscopically and microscopically immediately after collection. Samples contaminated with blood, feces, urates, or transparent fluid were discarded. Only fresh ejaculates that met the minimum inclusion criteria were used for pooling and cryopreservation: semen volume > 0.2 mL, sperm concentration > 3 × 10⁹ spermatozoa/mL, total motility > 80 %, and normal morphology ≥ 85 % (Vandvali et al., 2025).

Sperm concentration was determined with a hemocytometer under a light microscope. Initial motility was assessed with the same computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA) system described below, and fresh sperm morphology was evaluated using Hancock's solution (Najafi et al., 2022b). Qualified ejaculates from the ten roosters were pooled proportionally to minimize individual male variation. Eight independent pooled semen samples were prepared; each pool represented one experimental replicate and was processed separately through dilution, freezing, thawing, and post-thaw evaluation.

Experimental design and distribution of treatments

The study was designed to evaluate the effect of SkQ1 on post-thaw rooster sperm quality. Each pooled semen sample was divided into five equal aliquots. The first aliquot served as the control and was diluted with Lake-based freezing extender (Lake and Ravie, 1984) without SkQ1, whereas the remaining aliquots were diluted with the same extender supplemented with SkQ1 at final concentrations of 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 nM.

The experimental groups were therefore control, 12.5 nM SkQ1, 25 nM SkQ1, 50 nM SkQ1, and 100 nM SkQ1. Eight independent replicates were used per treatment. Because the focus of the study was the cryoprotective effect of SkQ1, fresh semen was used for initial quality screening and pooling, whereas treatment-dependent endpoints were evaluated after freezing and thawing. The post-thaw endpoints included sperm motility and kinematic parameters, plasma membrane integrity, abnormal morphology, mitochondrial activity, ATP content, viability, apoptosis-like changes, dead sperm percentage, ROS production, and antioxidant indices, including SOD, TAC, and GPX.

Preparation of extender and semen dilution

The basal freezing medium was a glycerol-containing Lake-based extender for rooster semen cryopreservation (Lake and Ravie, 1984). The extender was prepared aseptically, adjusted to an osmolality of approximately 310 mOsm/kg and pH 7.1, and supplemented with 3.8 % (v/v) glycerol as the primary permeating cryoprotectant. In this study, the term Lake-based freezing extender refers to this glycerol-containing Lake-type buffer used as the basal cryopreservation medium.

After preparation, the glycerol-containing Lake extender was divided into treatment-specific fractions and supplemented with the assigned SkQ1 concentrations. The dilution volume was adjusted according to the sperm concentration of each pool to obtain approximately 100 × 10⁶ spermatozoa per 0.25-mL French straw, equivalent to a final concentration of approximately 400 × 10⁶ spermatozoa/mL. The diluted semen was gently mixed and loaded into 0.25-mL French straws (Mehdipour et al., 2025).

Equilibration of freezing and thawing of semen

After dilution and loading, the straws were sealed with polyvinyl alcohol powder and equilibrated at 4°C for 3 h. After equilibration, the straws were placed horizontally 6 cm above the surface of liquid nitrogen in a cryobox for 10 min for non-programmed vapor-phase freezing. The straws were then plunged into liquid nitrogen and stored at −196°C (Mehdipour et al., 2020).

After 1 week of storage, frozen straws were thawed in a water bath at 37°C for 30 s. Thawed semen samples were gently mixed and used immediately for post-thaw sperm quality evaluation.

Computer-assisted sperm motility analysis

Post-thaw sperm motility and kinematic characteristics were assessed using a computer-assisted sperm analysis system (CASA; version 12.2, Hamilton Thorne Biosciences, Beverly, MA, USA). Thawed semen samples were diluted 1:10 in Lake buffer to a final concentration of approximately 20 × 10⁶ spermatozoa/mL before analysis.

A 3-μL aliquot of diluted thawed semen was placed in a Leja chamber slide pre-warmed to 37°C. The CASA settings were as follows: frame rate, 60 Hz; number of frames acquired, 30; minimum contrast, 60; and particle area, 5–190 μm². For each replicate, at least five random microscopic fields were examined, and at least 200 spermatozoa were evaluated. The motility parameters recorded were total motility (TM) and progressive motility (PM). The kinematic variables measured were average path velocity (VAP), straight-line velocity (VSL), curvilinear velocity (VCL), linearity (LIN), straightness (STR), amplitude of lateral head displacement (ALH), and beat-cross frequency (BCF) (Najafi et al., 2025).

Cell membrane integrity

The integrity of the sperm plasma membrane was evaluated by the hypo-osmotic swelling test (HOST). For this assay, 10 μL of thawed semen was mixed with 100 μL of hypo-osmotic solution containing 9 g fructose and 4.9 g sodium citrate per liter of distilled water (osmolality approximately 100 mOsm/kg). The mixture was incubated for 30 min at 37°C. After incubation, 5 μL of the mixture was placed on a glass slide pre-warmed to 37°C and covered with a coverslip. Spermatozoa were examined under a phase-contrast microscope at 400× magnification. Spermatozoa with coiled or swollen tails were considered to have functionally intact plasma membranes. Membrane integrity was expressed as a percentage after counting 200 spermatozoa in at least ten microscopic fields for each sample (Najafi et al., 2019).

Evaluation of sperm morphology

Sperm morphology was evaluated using Hancock's solution (Najafi et al., 2022b). Thawed semen (10 μL) was diluted in 1 mL of Hancock's solution composed of 65.5 mL formalin (37 %), 150 mL normal saline solution, 150 mL buffer solution, and 500 mL double-distilled water. The suspension was mixed gently, and 5 μL was placed on a glass slide and covered with a coverslip. Spermatozoa were observed under a phase-contrast microscope at 1,000× magnification. For each replicate, 300 spermatozoa were scored, and abnormalities were classified as acrosomal defects, detached heads, abnormal midpieces, or tail defects. The results were expressed as the percentage of spermatozoa with abnormal morphology.

Flow cytometric analysis

Flow cytometric analyses were performed using a FACSCalibur flow cytometer equipped with a 488-nm argon laser. Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) and Rhodamine 123 (R123) fluorescence were detected in the green channel using a 530/30-nm bandpass filter, whereas propidium iodide (PI) fluorescence was detected in the red channel using a 610/20-nm bandpass filter. For all flow cytometric analyses, spermatozoa were gated according to forward- and side-scatter characteristics to exclude debris and non-sperm events. A total of 10,000 sperm events were collected for each sample. Flow cytometric data were acquired and analyzed using CellQuest 3.3 software (Najafi et al., 2024).

Phosphatidylserine externalization, viability and dead sperm assessment

An Annexin V-FITC/PI staining kit was used to evaluate phosphatidylserine externalization as a marker of apoptosis-like changes. Thawed sperm samples were washed in calcium buffer and adjusted to approximately 1 × 10⁶ spermatozoa/mL. For staining, 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC solution was added to 100 μL of sperm suspension and incubated for 20 min at room temperature (approximately 22°C) in the dark. Thereafter, 5 μL of PI was added, and the samples were incubated for 10 min at room temperature before flow cytometric analysis (Masoudi et al., 2024). Sperm subpopulations were classified as follows: viable spermatozoa were Annexin V-FITC negative and PI negative, early apoptotic spermatozoa were Annexin V-FITC positive and PI negative, late apoptotic spermatozoa were Annexin V-FITC positive and PI positive, and necrotic spermatozoa were Annexin V-FITC negative and PI positive. Dead spermatozoa were calculated as the sum of late apoptotic and necrotic spermatozoa showing PI uptake.

Mitochondrial function

Mitochondrial activity was evaluated using Rhodamine 123 (R123) in combination with PI. Semen samples were diluted to approximately 50 × 10⁶ spermatozoa/mL. The sperm suspension was mixed with 5 μL of R123 solution (0.01 mg/mL) and incubated in the dark at room temperature for 20 min. After incubation, 5 μL of PI solution (1 mg/mL) was added, and samples were analyzed by flow cytometry. Spermatozoa positive for R123 and negative for PI were considered live spermatozoa with active mitochondria. The results were expressed as the percentage of spermatozoa with active mitochondrial function (Nazari et al., 2022).

ATP content determination

ATP content in frozen-thawed spermatozoa was determined using a commercial ATP assay kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. Thawed sperm samples were normalized for concentration and lysed to release intracellular ATP. The lysate was added to the ATP detection reagent, and the signal was measured with a microplate reader or luminometer. ATP concentration was calculated from a standard curve and expressed as pmol/10⁶ spermatozoa. This parameter was used as an index of post-thaw sperm energy status and mitochondrial functional competence (Najafi et al., 2022a).

Reactive oxygen species measurement

Reactive oxygen species production was measured using a luminol-dependent chemiluminescence assay. Semen samples were incubated in 250 μL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 37°C for 20 min and then centrifuged at 300 × g for 7 min. The supernatant was discarded, 3 mL PBS was added to the pellet, and the suspension was centrifuged again at 300 × g for 7 min. The sperm suspension was adjusted to 20 × 10⁶ spermatozoa/mL in PBS. Luminol (10 μL) was added to 400 μL of sperm suspension, samples were loaded into an Orion II Microplate Luminometer, and chemiluminescence was measured. ROS production was expressed as 10³ counts per minute (cpm)/10⁶ spermatozoa (Najafi et al., 2023).

Total antioxidant capacity, SOD, and GPX measurement

Total antioxidant capacity (TAC) was determined using a total antioxidant status assay kit based on the ability of antioxidants in the sample to inhibit formation of the 2,2′-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) radical cation. Sample (20 μL) was mixed with 1 mL of chromogen reagent containing ABTS, and the reaction was initiated by adding 200 μL hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). The resulting color was measured spectrophotometrically at 600 nm. Glutathione peroxidase (GPX) activity was measured using a commercial RANSEL GPX kit. Sample (10 μL) was mixed with 500 μL of GPX reagent containing glutathione, glutathione reductase, and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH), followed by 10 μL of EDTA-phosphate buffer. The reaction was started by adding 4 μL cumene hydroperoxide, and absorbance was read at 340 nm (Ghadimi et al., 2024).

Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was determined using a commercial RANSOD kit. This assay was based on the production of superoxide radicals by xanthine and xanthine oxidase, which react with 2-(4-iodophenyl)-3-(4-nitrophenol)-5-phenyltetrazolium chloride (INT) to produce a red formazan dye. Sample (50 μL) was combined with 1.7 mL of mixed substrate solution containing xanthine and INT. The reaction was started by adding 500 μL xanthine oxidase solution, and absorbance was read at 505 nm. SOD and GPX activities were expressed as U/mg, and TAC was expressed as mmol/L.

Statistical analyses

The experiment included eight independent pooled-semen replicates. All data were analyzed using R statistical software. Data are expressed as mean ± SEM. The effect of SkQ1 supplementation was analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model with treatment as a fixed effect and replicate as a random effect. Differences were considered statistically significant at P ≤ 0.05.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that supplementation of rooster semen freezing extender with the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant SkQ1 improved post-thaw sperm quality in a concentration-dependent manner. The most consistent beneficial effects were observed with 25 nM SkQ1, followed by 50 nM, whereas 100 nM was generally less effective. This pattern indicates an optimal biological range for SkQ1 in rooster sperm cryopreservation rather than a simple linear dose-response effect. The improvement at 25 nM involved motility, kinematic parameters, membrane integrity, mitochondrial activity, ATP content, viability, apoptosis-like status, ROS production, and antioxidant defense. These results suggest that SkQ1 protects frozen-thawed rooster spermatozoa mainly by regulating mitochondrial redox balance, maintaining energy metabolism, and reducing oxidative and apoptosis-like damage.

A key finding of this study was the significant increase in total and progressive motility after SkQ1 supplementation. Motility is an important indicator of sperm function and is closely related to mitochondrial activity, ATP availability, and membrane integrity. The highest TM and PM values (68.75 % and 31.64 %, respectively) were observed in the 25 nM SkQ1 group, and relatively high values were also detected at 50 nM. Najafi et al. (2024) reported that antioxidant supplementation improved post-thaw rooster sperm motility and functional characteristics. Similarly, Salih et al. (2021) demonstrated that ergothioneine supplementation improved post-thaw motility, membrane functionality, and antioxidant status of rooster spermatozoa. The present findings are consistent with these reports, but SkQ1 is mechanistically distinct because it is targeted directly to mitochondria and was effective at very low nanomolar concentrations.

The improvement in CASA-derived kinematic parameters further supports the beneficial effect of SkQ1 on sperm movement quality. The highest VAP, VSL, and VCL values were recorded in the 25 nM group, indicating improved velocity and progressive movement after thawing. Similarly, LIN and ALH reached their maximum values at 25 nM. These variables reflect different aspects of sperm movement, including path velocity, forward progression, and flagellar activity. Thus, SkQ1 not only increased the proportion of motile spermatozoa but also improved the quality of post-thaw movement. STR and BCF were not significantly affected, suggesting that SkQ1 primarily influenced velocity- and energy-related movement traits rather than all CASA variables.

The cryoprotective effect of SkQ1 was also evident from the improvement in membrane integrity and morphology. At 25 nM, membrane integrity reached 65.60 %, whereas abnormal morphology decreased to 12.20 %. The sperm plasma membrane is a major target of cryopreservation-induced injury, and ROS-induced lipid peroxidation can destabilize membrane fluidity, alter ion permeability, and reduce sperm survival during freezing and thawing. By reducing mitochondrial ROS formation, SkQ1 may indirectly decrease lipid peroxidation in the plasma membrane and preserve membrane function. Similar membrane-protective responses have been reported in rooster semen supplemented with antioxidant compounds such as apigenin, ergothioneine, quercetin-loaded nanocarriers, and alpha-lipoic acid-loaded nanostructured lipid carriers (Najafi et al., 2021, 2020, 2024; Salih et al., 2021).

The increase in mitochondrial activity and ATP content is particularly relevant because SkQ1 is designed to accumulate within mitochondria. The maximum mitochondrial activity (66.89 %) and ATP content (115.80 pmol/10⁶ spermatozoa) were recorded in the 25 nM SkQ1 group. These findings support the interpretation that SkQ1 preserved mitochondrial bioenergetic function in frozen-thawed rooster spermatozoa. Li et al. (2024) and Peng et al. (2025) also reported that SkQ1 can reduce oxidative stress and support mitochondrial function in reproductive cells.

The present findings are also consistent with studies on other mitochondria-targeted antioxidants used in rooster sperm cryopreservation. Nazari et al. (2022) reported that MitoQ was more effective than the non-targeted antioxidant pentoxifylline in improving post-thaw motility, membrane functionality, mitochondrial activity, and antioxidant status. Alipour-Jenaghard et al. (2023) similarly reported that MitoQ preserved the quality and fertility potential of post-thawed rooster spermatozoa. In the present study, SkQ1 improved mitochondrial activity, ATP content, motility, viability, and antioxidant defense, while reducing ROS production and apoptosis-like changes. These similarities support the concept that direct mitochondrial targeting is an efficient strategy for improving rooster sperm cryosurvival.

Although SkQ1 and MitoQ both contain an antioxidant quinone moiety linked to a triphenylphosphonium cation, SkQ1 contains plastoquinone whereas MitoQ contains ubiquinone. This structural difference may influence redox behavior, mitochondrial accumulation, and antioxidant efficiency. The optimal effect of SkQ1 at 25 nM suggests high biological potency, which is consistent with the ability of mitochondria-targeted antioxidants to accumulate at the main site of ROS generation instead of being distributed nonspecifically throughout the cell.

Findings with MitoTEMPO provide additional support for the role of mitochondrial ROS control in sperm cryosurvival. Masoudi et al. (2020) showed that MitoTEMPO improved cryopreserved rooster sperm quality by increasing motility, mitochondrial activity, membrane functionality, acrosome integrity, and viability and by reducing lipid peroxidation, DNA fragmentation, and apoptosis-like changes. Similarly, Lu et al. (2018) reported that MitoTEMPO improved post-thaw sperm quality by preserving mitochondrial membrane potential, membrane integrity, motility, and viability. Together with the present findings, these studies indicate that mitochondria are an important target for improving sperm cryopreservation outcomes.

Evidence from other species further supports the role of mitochondrial antioxidants in sperm preservation, although species-specific differences in sperm structure, metabolism, and cryotolerance must be considered. Fang et al. (2014) reported that MitoQ reduced ROS generation and lipid peroxidation and increased post-thaw sperm viability in yellow catfish. In addition, MitoTEMPO improved post-thaw ram sperm quality through modulation of oxidative stress and mitochondrial function (Asadzadeh et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2023). Therefore, the present results in rooster spermatozoa are consistent with a broader pattern in which mitochondrial antioxidant protection preserves mitochondrial function, reduces ROS, and maintains energy metabolism after cryopreservation.

The reduced percentages of apoptotic and dead spermatozoa further support the mitochondrial mechanism of SkQ1. At 25 nM, apoptotic spermatozoa decreased to 11.10 %, and viability increased to 60.60 %. Cryopreservation-induced apoptosis-like events in spermatozoa are closely associated with mitochondrial dysfunction, ROS accumulation, phosphatidylserine externalization, membrane injury, and activation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways. Mitochondrial damage has also been identified as an important contributor to post-thaw sperm dysfunction (Said et al., 2010). Accordingly, the present results indicate that SkQ1 may reduce apoptosis-like changes by stabilizing mitochondrial function and decreasing oxidative stress.

The oxidative-status results provide further evidence for the redox-regulatory role of SkQ1. ROS production was the lowest in the 25 nM SkQ1 group, whereas TAC, SOD, and GPX increased. SOD contributes to superoxide dismutation, GPX participates in peroxide detoxification, and TAC reflects the overall antioxidant capacity of the sample. The simultaneous decrease in ROS and increase in antioxidant markers indicates that SkQ1 improved redox balance and supported endogenous antioxidant defense. This response agrees with the findings of Peng et al. (2025), who reported that SkQ1 improved boar sperm quality by increasing ATP, SOD, and GPX and decreasing ROS and lipid peroxidation during liquid storage.

The concentration-dependent response observed in this study is important for defining an effective SkQ1 dose in rooster semen freezing extender. The best overall response was obtained at 25 nM, 50 nM remained beneficial for several endpoints, and 100 nM was less effective. This biphasic pattern is consistent with antioxidant hormesis, in which an optimal concentration restores redox balance without suppressing physiological ROS signaling, whereas excessive antioxidant concentrations may disrupt redox-dependent sperm functions. Lu et al. (2018) and Masoudi et al. (2020) reported similar responses for MitoTEMPO in sperm cryopreservation. Therefore, 25 nM SkQ1 appears sufficient to provide mitochondrial antioxidant protection without disturbing post-thaw redox homeostasis.

Conclusion

The present study demonstrates that SkQ1 supplementation improves post-thaw rooster sperm quality in a concentration-dependent manner. The most effective concentration was 25 nM, which enhanced motility, kinematic performance, membrane integrity, mitochondrial activity, ATP content, viability, and antioxidant defense, while reducing ROS production, abnormal morphology, apoptosis-like changes, and dead sperm percentage. These findings indicate that 25 nM SkQ1 is a promising mitochondria-targeted additive for optimizing rooster semen cryopreservation protocols.

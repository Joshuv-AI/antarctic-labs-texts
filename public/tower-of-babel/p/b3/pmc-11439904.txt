Survival benefit of sequential curative treatment for TACE suitable BCLC stage B HCC patients
Authors: Yuan-Jie Ding, Te-Sheng Chang, Chien-Heng Shen, Liang-Mou Kuo, Sheng-Lung Hsu, Yi-Hsing Chen, Yung-Yu Hsieh, Hui-Ling Huang, Sheng-Nan Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo clarify the survival benefit of sequential curative treatment post transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) for Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage B hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), we retrospectively analyzed HCC patients at a hospital. From July 2017 to July 2020, 787 treatment-naïve HCC patients underwent initial treatment; 77 (9.8%) meeting inclusion criteria were enrolled. Their initial treatments were TACE only (n = 68, 88.3%) or TACE with other treatments (n = 9, 11.7%). Median survival of the TACE-only group was 30 months. Treatment response was evaluated after 2 or 3 consecutive TACEs for patients (54/68, 79.4%) with available pre-/post-TACE computerized tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Treatment responses was divided into 4 groups: complete (n = 14, 26%, group (Gr) 1), incomplete without new tumor growth (n = 28, 52.0%, Gr2), incomplete with new growth (n = 6, 11%, Gr3), and progression (n = 6, 11%, Gr4). Of Gr2, further treatment after TACE were had radiofrequency ablation (n = 13, Gr2a), TACE (n = 9, Gr2b), other modalities (n = 6, Gr2c. Gr2a’s median survival was longer than Gr2b’s (> 60 vs. 20 months, p = 0.007). Nine patients in Gr2a (69%, 9/13) achieved a complete response, but none in Gr2b (p = 0.001). Conclusively, in TACE-suitable BCLC stage B HCC patients, a partial response without new tumor growth can serve as an indicator of treatment effectiveness following initial TACE treatment. This can facilitate the selection of appropriate candidates to receive RFA, potentially resulting in improved patient survival.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, TACE, Incomplete response, New tumor growthSubject terms: Diseases, Gastroenterology, Oncology

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the seventh most common cancer and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide1,2. There are several staging systems used to stage HCC and guide treatment decisions. The Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) system is one of the most widely used staging systems, which provides prognostic information and treatment strategies for hepatocellular carcinoma patients3,4. In this staging system, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) is recommended as a major treatment modality for intermediate stage HCC patients. Patients with unresectable HCC or who are not eligible for liver transplantation according to the expanded criteria should be evaluated for suitability for TACE. If TACE is suitable, it should be applied; otherwise, systemic therapy should be adopted4,5.

In the past, the initial and sequential treatments were the same in the algorithm. However, TACE is generally recognized as a non-curative treatment and sequential therapy is usually necessary. The latest BCLC recommendation in 2022 mentioned the second-line therapy for intermediate stage HCC after sequential TACEs. Liver transplantation (LT) is suggested for patient who have successful downstaging. If patients experience stage progression, with extrahepatic spreading of tumors or vascular invasion, systemic therapy such as immunotherapy or targeted therapies is suggested4,6,7. However, many patients in the clinical setting are in the zone between “downstage to LT” and “stage progression”, and they mostly have partial response to the initial TACE with or without new intrahepatic tumor growth. RFA is a cost-effective and potentially curative option, that plays an important role in the treatment of HCC. Patients who are not suitable for liver resection (LR) with BCLC 0 and A tumors can receive RFA as the standard treatment6. Patients with stage B tumors also have a survival benefit from a combination of TACE and RFA8–10. However, there is no data available to showed whether patients who have partial response can benefit from RFA, and there is no clear indication of when it should be adopted. Therefore, the purpose of this article is to investigate the role of curative RFA in patients with BCLC B who have an incomplete response to TACE.

Patients and methods

Study population

Between July 2017 and July 2020, all patients who visited or were referred to our hospital for initial treatment of HCC were included in this study. The observation period lasted for 2.5 years, concluding in January 2023. Patients who were classified as BCLC stage B according to the BCLC classification and deemed suitable for TACE were further analyzed. TACE suitability was defined as patients with BCLC stage B tumors who were not candidates for LT and for whom the clinician deemed that LR, RFA, or systemic therapy would not be the best option. All included patients were treatment-naive, and those who received two or more therapies within 2 months were considered to have received combination therapies.

Treatment response evaluation and grouping

Some patients had been initially arranged TACE in combination to facilitate the curative treatments in plan, and these patients were categorized as Group (Gr)0. The remains receiving TACE alone initially had further sequential treatments according to the response of initial TACE course. Treatment response was evaluated by dynamic contrast-enhanced CT or MRI within three months after two or three times of consecutive TACE. The response patterns were divided into four groups: Gr1, complete tumor response; Gr2, incomplete tumor response without growth of lesion from new intrahepatic sites; Gr3, incomplete tumor response with newly grown intrahepatic sites but without stage progression, and Gr4, stage progression. The proportion of Gr2 and whether receiving different sequential treatments resulted in different survival rates were of interest, and therefore Gr2 was further subgrouped into two subgroups: those who received subsequent TACE and RFA. The OS of these subgroups was analyzed.

TACE technique

A 4F or 5F sheath was introduced into the patient’s femoral artery using the Seldinger technique, followed by celiac trunk or superior mesenteric angiography with a catheter of the same size (Terumo RC1) to evaluate the location, number, and size of tumors. Then, a microcatheter (Progret microcatheter 2.8fr) was inserted into the super-selective branch of the artery supplying the tumors. A 1:1 mixed suspension of iodized oil with doxorubicin (30 mg) or epirubicin (20 mg) was infused into the artery through the catheter. In some cases (13 of 78), drug-eluting beads (DC Bead™ or HepaSphere) were alternatively infused through the catheter. Finally, the arteries were embolized by gelatin sponge (Gelfoam) infusion until no tumor staining was observed on repeat angiography.

Statistical analysis

This study retrospectively collected all cases from the database. Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was employed to compare the baseline characteristics for categorical variables, and Student’s t-test was for continuous variables. Kaplan–Meier method, the Log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test or the Gehan-Breslow-Wilcoxon test was used to analyze the survival. IBM SPSS Statistics version 22 and GraphPad Prism version 9.3.1 were used for analysis. Statistical significance was considered as a p-value of less than 0.05.

Ethics statement

We conducted this study retrospectively in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, under the approval of institutional review board (IRB) at Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (IRB approval number: 202200399B0). All the data collected in this study were before the allowed period of the IRB approval, prior to March 2, 2022. The requirement for informed consent from the participant was waived by the IRB due to retrospective nature of the study. Patient’s confidentiality was maintained throughout the study and the data were stored in the electronic data base and protected by the password. All data was only accessible to the study investigators.

Discussion

Our study found that for BCLC B patients who underwent initial treatment with TACE, the median OS was 32 months, which was higher than the 30-month survival standard set by the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL). These results suggest that the treatment outcomes of our hospital are acceptable, and the follow-up research provides valuable references. When we focus on patients who initially received TACE aloe, we found that patients who achieved CR (Gr1) had a favorable prognosis. Those who had partial response or refractory to TACE had poor survival. However, when we further analysis the group that had partial response without new growth tumor, RFA (Gr2a) could benefit the survival rather than TACE (Gr2b). Patients who received a combination of curative therapies as their initial plan (Gr0), achieved a complete response to TACE (Gr1), or reached incomplete treatment without new tumor growth followed by subsequent RFA (Gr2a) survived longer, and there was no significant difference in OS among these three groups (shown in Fig. 2D)). This means that although complete response to TACE was not achieved, sequential RFA could in selective patients could compensate this shortcoming. Traditionally, doctors may initiate systemic therapy or continue with TACE for these patients, but our study suggests using RFA instead of TACE or systemic treatment in selected cases. According to this study, the criteria for determining whether patient need sequential RFA is that when there is only a partial response after 2–3 rounds of TACE, without any new tumor growth. Among TACE suitable patients, 26% achieved CR, and the remaining patients required further follow-up treatment. Previous studies have shown that at least 2–3 times of insufficient TACE procedures are sufficient to assess whether there is a new lesion or insufficient necrosis after TACE, which is consistent with our study using 2–3 TACE sessions to evaluate efficacy.

The tumor size was related to the outcome, with size of 4.47 and 8 cm, for Gr2a and Gr2b, respectively. AFP levels were higher in Gr2b, but there was no significant difference (Table 2).

The originality of this research is in selecting a group that can provide the basis for determining whether to perform RFA. In this study, a conservative approach was taken, wherein patients with incomplete TACE and no new tumors were selected. The rationale for this approach is that the benefits of combined TACE and RFA in median-sized HCC have been reported in the literature8–10. In addition, new technologies and technologists have expanded the indications of RFA. For example, multi-needle ablation can enlarge the area, and with the help of fusion image and ultrasound contrast agents, it can accurately ablate to the surviving part, although, this is related to the technology. Because local treatment can only eliminate visible tumors, it takes at least three months after two or three TACE sessions. If there is no new tumor, there is a greater chance of complete local treatment. On the other hand, if there are only a few new tumor growths, whether RFA is helpful will require more cases to determine. For patients with stage progression, local treatment cannot be used, and for tumors that have been recurring, a more effective systemic therapy should be the next treatment strategy, which requires further research to evaluate its effectiveness.

To illustrate the treatment process, we created a flow chart (shown in Fig. 4) that outlines the steps involved in the treatment strategy, including when and how RFA is used in combination with TACE. The flow chart helps to visualize the treatment plan and can aid clinicians in making decisions about treatment options. However, it should be noted that the flow chart has some limitations and potential areas for improvement, such as the need to adapt the treatment plan to individual patient needs and the availability of resources.

There were several limitations in our study. Firstly, the retrospective nature of our data analysis made is difficult to establish a causal relationship between survival benefit and baseline conditions. Additionally, the smaller tumor size in patients from Gr2a in comparison to Gr2b, may have directly affected the effect of TACE. Secondly, due to the small sample size, we may have lacked the power to differentiate patient baseline characteristics. Although OS in patients receiving curative treatment and those undergoing sequential RFA or CR after TACE, did not differ significantly may be attributed to the small sample size. For example, although Gr2a had an average AFP level of 429.6 (ng/mL) and Gr2b had an average of 9820.8 (ng/mL), the difference was not statistically significant. However, tumor volume and AFP levels have been shown to be prognostic factors for both TACE effects and HCC prognosis in other studies11–14. Thirdly, as the procedure was performed by different physicians, the outcome may have been influenced by the physician’s experience and the patient’s characteristics.

For patients with BCLC stage B HCC, suitable for TACE, integration of curative treatment modalities at the appropriate time can provide survival benefits. However, the benefit of “early prescription of systemic therapy soon after meeting criteria of TACE failure” requires further investigation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, for intermediate HCC patients, 2–3 consecutive TACE sessions can be used to evaluate the response of the tumor to TACE. An incomplete response without new tumor growths may indicate that the patient’s HCC is of local disease nature. In such cases, RFA can be used as a curative treatment option, resulting in survival benefits for the patient. Further studies are needed to validate these findings and to determine the optimal timing and sequence of TACE and RFA for the treatment of intermediate HCC.

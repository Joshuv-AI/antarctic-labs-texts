Hospital spending for cancers in Hunan, China during 2017 and 2019
Authors: Mengyao Xu, Mengjiao Liu, Liqiu Liao, Minxue Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo describe the hospitalization spending for cancers in Hunan Province, China, and to investigate the hospitalization cost across diverse cancer classifications.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study based on the hospitalization admission data from 2017 to 2019 in Hunan Province. We retrieved information on age, sex, diagnosis, number of comorbidities, level of hospitalization, hospital location, hospital costs, length of stay, and type of payer for eligible participants. Factors for cost (log-transformed) were investigated through multiple linear regression.ResultsA total of 872,309 records of cancer were hospitalized in Hunan Province during 2017–2019, with a total cost of US$2490.36 million, and 64.10% of the cost was covered by medical insurance. The top five cancers in terms of cost were lung (300.32 million), colorectum ($234.56 million), liver ($126.46 million), lip, oral cavity & pharynx ($102.29 million), and stomach ($98.98 million) cancers in men, were breast ($176.03 million), colorectum ($156.15 million), cervix ($112.68 million), lung ($112.21 million), and thyroid ($56.75 million) cancers in women, respectively. Site of cancer, sex, age, year of hospitalization, number of comorbidities, length of stay, hospital level, and hospital location were significantly associated with the per-capita log-transformed cost.ConclusionThe economic burden of cancer was heavy in Hunan, and lung, colorectal, breast, liver, and stomach cancers accounted for half of the total cost. Site of cancer, sex, age, comorbidity, level, and location of hospital were factors for the per-capita cost.

Introduction

Cancer poses a significant global public health challenge and has emerged as a leading cause of mortality and economic burden worldwide [1–3]. GLOBOCAN 2020 estimates indicate that approximately 19.3 million new cancer cases were diagnosed globally in 2020 [2, 4]. The economic burden of cancer is substantial, with expenditures escalating from US$96 billion in 2016 to US$164 billion in 2020, reflecting a compound annual growth rate of 14% [4]. Developing countries bear a substantial portion of the burden, accounting for over 56% of incident cancer cases and 64% of cancer-related deaths annually [4]. In particular, China, the largest middle-income country in the world, faces an immense cancer burden [5].

Based on the data and analysis derived from the China National Cancer Registry, it is estimated that there were 4,796,996 new cases and 3,193,744 deaths of cancers in China in 2022 [6]. Cancer has been the leading cause of death in China since 2010, with increasing incidence, mortality, and burden [7]. Despite the improvements in early cancer screening and treatment that have contributed to improved survival rates, the cost of cancer therapy has experienced a significant increase in recent years [4, 8]. Various studies have indicated that hospitalization expenditures may be influenced by factors such as healthcare insurance systems, length of hospital stay, and hospital classification [8–10]. While several investigations have explored the duration of stay and hospitalization expenses, the majority of attention has been directed toward major cancer types such as lung, breast, and colorectal cancer [8, 10, 11]. Few studies have described the characteristics of hospitalization costs for all types of cancer.

With the advances in novel anti-cancer treatments as well as the increasing incidence rates of cancers in China’s growing aging population, the economic burden of cancer will continue to rise [12]. Early screening, diagnosis, and treatment of cancer is universally recognized as an efficient path to decrease cancer incidence and mortality, and it is also the most cost-effective and efficient health strategy [13]. Meanwhile, China has made considerable efforts in cancer prevention and control by proposing the Early Diagnosis and Early Treatment of Cancer Program as early as 2005, which aims to reduce the burden of cancer by carrying out risk factor surveys and assessment of high-risk populations, cancer screening, and health economics assessment of the five common cancers, namely, lung, colorectal, gastric, breast, and liver cancers, to detect early cancers and pre-cancer and alleviate the cancer burden [14, 15].

To provide a reference for the optimization and improvement of China’s cancer screening, early diagnosis, and treatment programs, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of hospitalization spending for all cancers in Hunan province. By investigating and comparing the expenditures for different cancers, tailored strategies can be made for certain cancers and high-risk populations.

Methods

Study design and participants

This was a hospital-based registry study. The data of all cancer inpatients in Hunan province from 2017 to 2019 came extracted from the Chinese National Health Statistics Network Reporting System (CNHSNRS). A total of 903,371 hospital admissions were recorded. All cancer cases (C00-C97) were identified according to the International Statistical Classification of Diseases 10th Revision (ICD-10), while carcinomas in situ (D00-D09) were not included. A patient with multiple admission records, due to either multiple cancers or hospital transfers, were identified as different observations.

Outcome variable

The outcome variable was the direct medical cost for hospitalization, while indirect cost and nonmedical cost were not calculated. The cost was calculated for each admission, while multiple hospitalizations of one patient could not be identified in the anonymized data. We adjusted the hospital costs by Consumer Price Index in Hunan in 2017 (the base year) according to the National Bureau of statistics of China. To facilitate global comparison, we converted 1 Chinese yuan (CNY) to 0.148 US dollars at the 2017 average exchange rate.

Potential factors for cost

To investigate the potential factors for the cost, we extracted patient information, including sex, age, site of cancer, number of comorbidities, hospital level and location or hospital of admission, length of stay (LOS), and type of payer. Age was categorized as < 20, 20–44, 45–64 and ≥ 65 years. The number of comorbidities was divided into 0, 1–2 and ≥ 3, according the number of secondary diagnoses in each admission record. Hospital levels in China are divided into three grades (primary, secondary, and tertiary) according to the number of hospital beds, department settings, medical staff, hospital area, and equipment, and tertiary hospital is the highest. In this study, we grouped primary and secondary into the same category and classified hospital levels into tertiary and non-tertiary. The hospital location was divided into the provincial capital (Changsha) and the non-provincial capital (other cities in Hunan province). The type of payer was categorized as medical insurance and out-of-pocket payment (OOP).

Statistical methods

For individual patient data, continuous variables were described as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range, IQR) according data distribution, and count data were described as the frequency and proportion (n, %). We used the Sankey diagram to present hospitalization cost which is stratified by location of cancer, age, and payer, and pyramid diagrams to describe the spending by age, sex, and payer. We constructed simple linear and multiple regression models to investigate potential factors for hospitalization costs (log10-transformed), including location of cancer, sex, age, number of comorbidities, hospital level, hospital location, and LOS. The significance level was 0.05 for all statistical tests. The data management and analyses were performed in R version 4.1.2.

We also calculated the sum cost at population level, by age group, sex and payer. The Sankey diagram was drawn using a commercial online tool (https://dycharts.com).

A total of 872,309 hospitalization records with a principal diagnosis of cancer in Hunan province during 2017–2019 were included. The mean age of patients was 58.79 ± 14.27 years, and 44.46% were women. A higher proportion of hospitalization occurred in tertiary hospitals (68.57%) (Table 1). The median LOS was 9 days (IQR: 5–16) and varied significantly across different cancer types, from 6 days (IQR: 3–11) to 13 days (IQR: 7–21) (Table 2). Population-level spending for cancers

The total hospitalization spending for all cancer patients in Hunan province from 2017 to 2019 was $2490.36 million US dollars. The top five cancers in terms of cost were lung ($412.53 million), colorectum ($390.71 million), breast ($176.98 million), liver ($158.87 million), and stomach ($146.91 million) accounting for 51.64% of the total cost of all cancers in Hunan province. Of the total cost, 64.10% ($1596.24 million) was paid by insurance and 35.90% ($894.11 million) was paid by OOP (Fig. 1). The top five cancers in terms of cost were lung ($300.32 million), colorectum ($234.56 million), liver ($126.46 million), lip, oral cavity & pharynx ($102.29 million), and stomach ($98.98 million) in men, and were breast ($176.03 million), colorectum ($156.15 million), cervix ($112.68 million), lung ($112.21 million), and thyroid ($55.98 million) in women (Fig. 2).Fig. 1Hospitalization cost for cancers in Hunan province during 2017–2019, by site of cancer, age group, and payerFig. 2Hospitalization costs for different cancers in men and women in Hunan province during 2017–2019

Individual-level spending for cancers

The highest costs per admission were brain ($6084.04), lip, oral cavity & pharynx ($4685.11), leukemia ($3828.10), kidney ($3806.98), colorectum cancers ($3720.78) (Table 2). To investigate the factors for individual spending for cancers, we performed a log10 transformation of the total cost variable. In both univariate and multivariable analyses (Table 3), site of cancer (brain tumor was the highest), sex (men), age (elder), year of hospitalization (2017–2019), having one or more comorbidities, longer LOS, hospital location (Changsha), and hospital level (tertiary) were significantly associated with higher per-capita cost. Discussion

Since 2012, China’s government start to conduct the Cancer Screening Program in Urban China, which primarily targets five types of cancer, including lung, breast, colorectum, stomach, and liver cancers [16, 17]. The screening program, which detects patients with early-stage malignancies, may have been attributed to comparatively lowering the per-capita inpatient expenditures for these cancers. A study in China suggested that early diagnosis and treatment would save around 75% of medical expenses while reducing medical costs by about $1.159 billion [18].

Based on the findings of our study, the total spending on oral cancer in men was much higher that that in women. This was attributable to the relatively high prevalence of oral cancer in men than in women in Hunan [19]. From 2009 to 2015, the incidence rate of oral cancer in men in Hunan Province was 6.43 per 100,000, which was significantly higher than the incidence rate of oral cancer in men throughout China in 2019 (4.62 per 100,000) [20, 21], but was comparable to the rate in Hainan province (7.75 per 100,000) [22]. This may be attributable to the fact that people in these regions were reportedly to have high prevalence rates of betel nuts chewing, a well-established risk factor for oral cancer [23]. Consequently, the health administration should develop preventive strategies that work well for betel nut control.

Generally, the incidence of most cancers exhibits an age-related pattern, with a higher rate in individuals aged 40 years and older [24–26]. Notably, we identified hospitalization expenditures peak for those aged 45–69, while it declines for those aged 70 and older. It might be the concern that older people are typically in poorer health conditions and prefer conservative treatments [25, 27]. Expectedly, we found that hospitalization costs were higher in patients with three or more comorbidities, because advanced age is often associated with comorbidities that lead to increased hospital costs and length of stay [28, 29]. Moreover, institutional characteristics were also relevant, such that the hospitalization cost was markedly higher in tertiary or capital hospitals compared to non-tertiary or non-capital hospitals [30]. Higher costs in tertiary or capital hospitals is partly attributable to the fact that these institutions often handle a larger number of patients with advanced cancer stages.

According to research by the National Cancer Centre of China, the average annual cost of a cancer visit was $9,739, while the average annual household income was $8,607 [31], leaving the cost of medical relatively burdensome for cancer patients. Furthermore, the out-of-pocket expenditures for innovative anti-cancer drugs are significantly higher than per capita annual disposable income [30]. Some studies have shown that medical expenses accounted for a large percentage of cancer treatment [32, 33].

It is a considerable financial burden on cancer patients, that imported pharmaceuticals, the bulk of which were not covered by medical insurance, accounted for half of China’s expenditure on cancer treatments [33]. To promote the accessibility and affordability of innovative medicines, China has instituted numerous policies in recent years [12]. However, out-of-pocket payments remain a high economic risk for low-income households [31, 34]. In summary, the Chinese government should accelerate the progress of health insurance negotiations, increase public health investment, and reduce the percentage of the OOP payments to ease the financial burden of more cancer patients.

Strengths and limitations

This was the first comprehensive assessment of hospital spending for cancers in Hunan province. We converted all costs into US currency, making it straightforward to compare with hospital costs for cancers in other countries/regions. Our study has several limitations. First, the database records hospitalizations for all cancers, but specific clinical information for each cancer is not available. As a result, the regression analysis may be subject to missing variable bias. Some key variables, such as cancer stage or severity, treatment modalities, and patient’s socioeconomic status were not controlled for in the analysis. Second, data on outpatient costs, nonmedical costs, and indirect costs were not available in the dataset, leading to an underestimation of the overall economic burden of cancers. Third, one patient with several admission records could not be identified, such that we could not estimate the cost per cancer patient over a certain period of time.

Conclusion

Based on the analysis of cancer hospitalization data during 2017 and 2019 in Hunan Province, we revealed a substantial economic burden of cancers and significant variations in costs across cancer types and other factors. Hospitalization costs for lung, colorectal, breast, liver, and stomach cancers accounted for half of the total cost. The insurance coverage for hospitalization costs was around 60%. The implementation and improvement of cancer prevention in addition to early diagnosis and treatment programs are urgently needed.

Decreased Sirtuin1 Protein and Aromatase Activity Index in Follicular Fluid is Correlated with Increased Germinal Vesicle Count in Polycystic Ovary Syndrome
Authors: Sahar Mazloomi, Marzieh Sanoeei Farimani, Maryam Ahmadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The close interaction of follicular fluid with oocytes influences their maturation. The presence of the germinal vesicle (GV) has been reported in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). The present study aimed to measure sirtuin1 (SIRT1) levels, aromatase activity index and insulin resistance in follicular fluid and to investigate their association with in vitro fertilization (IVF) success in PCOS women. In this case-control study, 35 women with PCOS and 35 healthy women matched for body mass index (BMI) were included. After the collection of the follicular fluid from all participants, concentrations of SIRT1, insulin, and aromatase activity were measured. Retrieved oocyte status, embryo quality, and biochemical pregnancy were monitored during follow-up in the participants. In women with PCOS, there were notable reductions in SIRT1 levels and aromatase activity, along with an increase in the HOMA- Index. Negative correlations were observed between SIRT1 and aromatase activity with the number of GVs. SIRT1 concentration and aromatase activity showed positive correlation with the ratio of available embryos to the number of oocytes in metaphase II. Logistic regression analysis showed that chance of biochemical pregnancy increases significantly with an increase in aromatase activity and SIRT1 level in follicular fluid (p<0.05). Our findings showed that the reduced SIRT1 level and aromatase activity in follicular fluid are involved with the pathogenesis of PCOS. These findings suggest that increasing in aromatase activity and SIRT1 levels may improve oocytes quality and ultimately increase the chances of IVF success in PCOS women.

INTRODUCTION

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) impacts an estimated 8 to 21 percent of women of reproductive age.1 However, according to the statistics of the World Health Organization, more than two-thirds of women affected worldwide remain undiagnosed. One of the important indicators in PCOS is the disorder in the growth of ovarian follicles which leads to an increase in immature follicles.2 The elevated number of germinal vesicles (GV), attributable to hormonal imbalances, is one of the challenges of in vitro fertilization (IVF) in these women.34

A member of the histone deacetylase family is sirtuin (SIRT) protein, which with its deacetylase and monoribosyltransferase activities plays a role in regulating lipid metabolism and also has a key role in the function of steroid hormones. SIRT family has seven members (SIRT1-SIRT7), the most well-known of which is SIRT1.56

SIRT1 is found throughout the ovarian follicle, including the ovarian epithelium, stroma, and luteinizing granulosa cells, and it is regarded as an important marker of ovarian follicular development and fertility.7 Increased serum levels of SIRT1 have been reported in women with PCOS.8 However, animal models of PCOS with insulin resistance have been observed to have lower levels of SIRT1 in their ovaries.910 Additionally, SIRT1 has been shown to regulate the expression of aromatase in estrogen-producing cells.7111213 Aromatase is a pivotal enzyme in the pathogenesis of PCOS, catalyzing the conversion of 19-carbon androgens to 18-carbon estrogens. Aromatase dysfunction has previously been reported in PCOS women.1415 Follicular fluid estradiol level is directly related to oocyte quality. Decrease in the estradiol content in the follicular fluid of women with PCOS has been observed.1617

Insulin resistance is highly prevalent in PCOS. Additionally, SIRT1 enhances insulin secretion and improves insulin sensitivity.18 It has also been shown that androgen levels and insulin resistance interact bidirectionally.1920

PCOS is characterized by impaired aromatase function and insulin resistance. It is also believed that these disorders may coexist with immature follicle presence in PCOS. Additionally, SIRT1 is involved in the aforementioned metabolic pathways. It is postulated that disruption in the interaction among these three factors likely be associated with a large number of poor-quality follicles in PCOS. This study, for the first time, investigates the relationship between SIRT1 levels, aromatase activity index, and insulin resistance in the follicular fluid of PCOS patients, and examines how these factors correlate with IVF success rates.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study population and study design

In this case-control study, the samples used in this study were part of samples from a completed PhD thesis whose findings have already been published.1721 Considering the importance of the confounding effects of age and body mass index (BMI) in PCOS, 35 pair of age- and BMI-matched control subjects and women with PCOS were enrolled in this study. Summary of implementation method: all participants aged 26–38 years were candidates for IVF procedure in Fertility Centre of Fatemieh Hospital and Omid Infertility Centre of Hamadan, Iran. The PCOS patients were diagnosed by specialist based on Rotterdam criteria.22 Control subjects, were healthy women with normal ovulation cycle and without symptoms of hyperandrogenism, who were candidates for IVF due to the infertility of their husbands. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia, adrenal tumors, taking insulin-sensitizing drugs, Cushing syndrome, and presence of prolactinoma were considered as exclusion criteria.

All experimental procedures were carried out by the ethical standards established by the Declaration of Helsinki (1967; revised 2013). Formal written consent was obtained from all participants, and the study received approval from the Research Ethics Committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences (IR.UMSHA.REC.1403.425).

2. Ovarian stimulation

A baseline-transvaginal ultrasound examination was conducted on the second day of the menstrual cycle, which revealed no cysts or ovarian follicles exceeding 10 mm in diameter among the participants. Recombinant FSH and Cinnal-F® (CinnaGen Co., Iran) were prescribed for all subjects by a specialist. To monitor the size of the follicles, a series of ultrasounds were conducted. Once a follicle reached a diameter of 14 mm, daily injections of 0.25 μg of Cetrotide antagonist (Merck Serono Co., Germany) began. Subsequently, recombinant Ovitrelle hCG (Merck Serono Co., Germany) was administered when at least three follicles measuring 18 mm in diameter were detected in the ovaries. Finally, a transvaginal ultrasound-guided puncture was performed 36 hours later to retrieve the mature follicles, allowing for the collection of oocytes and follicular fluids.

3. Isolation of follicular fluid

Follicular fluids were obtained from all participating women during the follicular puncture procedure and after the oocytes were extracted. The samples were placed in sterile tubes and centrifuged at 1,000×g for 4 minutes at 21°c. After allowing the cell debris to settle, the supernatant (follicular fluid) was stored in separate aliquot microtubes at -80°c.

4. Measurement of aromatase activity index in follicular fluid

Since the ratio of testosterone to estradiol level is an important indicator of aromatase performance,23 the testosterone level of follicular fluid was checked using Elecsys Testosterone II assay (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Germany) and cobas e 411 Analyzer (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Germany). The lower and upper detection limits were 0.025–15.0 ng/mL. The data for estradiol (E2) concentration of follicular fluids was obtained from our previous report measured using Elecsys Estradiol III method (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Germany) and Cobas e 411 Analyzer (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Germany) in our previous study.17

5. Enzyme-linked immunoassay for SIRT1 assay

The SIRT1 assay kit (ZellBio GmbH, Germany) is designed for the measurement of human SIRT1 based on the double biotin antibody sandwich technology. This kit has a one-step method that limits the need for dilution. The assay range and sensitivity of the SIRT1 kit were 2-64 ng/mL and 0.25 ng/mL, respectively. According to the kit instructions, assay precision was evaluated by performing the relevant calculations. The intra- and inter-assay values were within the kit range (Intra-Assay: CV<10%; Inter-Assay: CV<12%).

6. Measurement of Insulin resistance in follicular fluid

Follicular fluid glucose levels were measured using Pars Azmoun kit Obtained from a local company and enzymatic method was performed using a BS-800 autoanalyzer. The insulin concentration of follicular fluid was measured using a quantitative method of luminescence (CLIA) and Liason. Homeostasis model assessment index (HOMA Index) was used to determine the level of insulin resistance in follicular fluid and was calculated according to the equation:24

[Insulin (μU/mL)] [Glucose (mmol/L)]/22.5.

Insulin resistance was considered as HOMA Index more than 2.1.25

7. Statistical analysis

SPSS software version 22.0 (IBM, USA) was used to analyze the data from this study. We used Graph Pad Prism version 8.0 software (Graph Pad Software Inc., USA) to create graphs. Data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Based on the results of the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, a comparison of means was performed using the Mann-Whitney U test and Student’s t-test. Additionally, the Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients were employed to assess the relationships between the variables. Logistic regression was also used to check the chance of biochemical pregnancy. Data was expressed as mean±SEM and p value less than 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

RESULTS

1. Observations of demographic, hormonal status, and IVF outcomes

The demographic analysis revealed no significant differences in maternal age and duration of infertility between the groups of women. In addition, the mean BMI was similar in both groups (all p>0.05).

According to the IVF results presented in Table 1, women with PCOS produced more oocytes. However, there was no significant difference in the ratio of metaphase II-oocytes (MII) to the total oocytes retrieved between the two groups. Although more embryos were observed in PCOS patients, the proportion of available embryos/MII was higher in healthy women. Also, patients with PCOS exhibited a significantly greater number of germinal vesicles (p<0.005).

In the present study, IVF success was defined based on biochemical pregnancy, defined as the observation of a positive β-hCG test result two weeks after embryo transfer. For this purpose, two weeks after embryo transfer, β-hCG levels were measured to assess pregnancy status. The results indicated that the percentage of biochemical pregnancies was significantly lower in PCOS patients (14.8%) compared to non-PCOS (53.6%).

2. Follicular fluid HOMA-Index is inversely correlated with SIRT1 levels and aromatase activity index

Although glucose concentrations in the follicular fluid differed markedly between groups (60.74±1.408 mg/dL in women with PCOS and 51.22±1.128 mg/dL in the control group), follicular fluid insulin levels did not show a statistically significant difference (5.17±0.117 μU/mL in women with PCOS vs. 4.84±0.147 μU/mL in healthy controls). In contrast, the HOMA-Index was significantly higher in the PCOS group than in the healthy group (0.777±0.026 vs. 0.622±0.029, respectively).

As shown in Fig. 1A and B, Pearson’s correlation analysis indicated a strong negative correlation between the HOMA- Index and SIRT1 levels, as well as between the HOMA-Index and aromatase activity index in follicular fluid (r=−0.346, p=0.004 for both).

3. PCOS women had lower levels of SIRT1 and aromatase activity index in follicular fluid

The ELISA analysis results demonstrated that the concentration of SIRT1 in the follicular fluid of women with PCOS was lower than that found in the control group (Fig. 2A). The ratio of estradiol to testosterone levels serves as a crucial marker for evaluating aromatase activity. Given the significant reduction in estradiol levels in the follicular fluid from women with PCOS compared to healthy women (295.22±5.57 vs. 415.48±11.3 ng/mL, respectively), aromatase activity index was notably lower in the PCOS group than in the control group (Fig. 2B).

As illustrated in Fig. 2C, Pearson’s correlation analysis revealed a strong positive correlation between the concentration of SIRT1 in follicular fluid and aromatase activity index (r=0.853, p<0.001).

4. Reduced aromatase activity index and lower SIRT1 concentration in follicular fluid were linked to the quality of oocytes

Spearman’s correlation analysis showed a significant inverse relationship between the number of germinal vesicles and SIRT1 levels (Fig. 3A). Also, the number of germinal vesicles decreased as the level of active aromatase increased (Fig. 3B). In addition, a positive correlation was observed between SIRT1 protein level and aromatase activity index with the ratio of the number of embryos obtained from the oocytes of MII (p<0.05).

5. High concentrations of SIRT1 and aromatase activity index were associated with an increased likelihood of IVF success

When the samples were divided based on biochemical pregnancy, the results indicated that participants with biochemical pregnancies (whether they had PCOS or were healthy) had significantly higher mean levels of SIRT1 and aromatase activity index compared to non-pregnant women (p<0.05). Logistic regression analysis (Table 2) demonstrated that, independently, for each unit increment in aromatase activity index and SIRT1 level, the chances of biochemical pregnancy significantly increased (p<0.05).

DISCUSSION

Infertility prevalence rates vary from 9.3% in semi-industrialized countries to 16.7% in more developed countries.26 PCOS is a multifactorial disorder and one of the primary contributors to infertility in women, characterized by a high number of poor-quality oocytes.27

The follicular fluid contains various components, including sex hormones, growth factors, minerals, and oxidants, and plays a fundamental role in the quality and development of oocytes.28 Estradiol concentration in follicular fluid is vital for oocyte maturation, and studies have noted a decrease in estradiol levels in PCOS women.1617

Aromatase is a key enzyme involved in the synthesis of estradiol from testosterone. SIRT1 may indirectly influence estradiol synthesis by regulating aromatase expression.71113 Based on these insights, the current study aimed to explore the correlation between SIRT1 content and aromatase activity in follicular fluid and their impact on IVF outcomes in women diagnosed with PCOS. Our results revealed that aromatase activity and estradiol levels in follicular fluid were significantly lower in patients with PCOS. Concurrently, a reduction in SIRT1 levels was noted in the follicular fluid. Given SIRT1’s role in regulating aromatase expression, decreased SIRT1 levels are likely to contribute to the lowered estradiol concentrations in follicular fluid. Additionally, reduced expression of SIRT1 has been observed in the ovarian tissue of PCOS mice.29

The role of SIRT1 in suppressing androgen receptors was demonstrated in a mouse model.30 In our study, the reduction of aromatase activity led to the accumulation of testosterone in follicular fluid. Previous studies have indicated that the presence of testosterone diminishes oocyte quality.3132 These results align with our observations, where we found a notable relationship between elevated testosterone levels in follicular fluid and a decline in oocyte quality.

The present study revealed a positive relationship between follicular fluid SIRT1 levels and aromatase activity. The ratio of embryos to metaphase II (MII) oocytes is regarded as an important indicator of IVF status. An increase in the embryos-to-MII ratio was noted in conjunction with elevated SIRT1 levels and more active aromatase. A study conducted on mice indicated that SIRT1 plays a role in embryo implantation through FoxO1-SOD signaling, thereby contributing to IVF success.33 Another study highlighted SIRT1’s involvement in delaying oocyte senescence, a process that ultimately enhances oocyte growth and implantation status.34 Furthermore, research has shown that SIRT1 is crucial for the biogenesis of embryonic mitochondria, and its inhibition can result in reduced embryo growth.35 Results of past research are likely aligned with the observations of our present study, as improving embryo quality may enhance implantation chances. In an animal study, SIRT1 was identified as a protective agent against oxidative stress.33 Our hypothesis regarding SIRT1 pertains to its regulatory role in aromatase expression, suggesting that SIRT1 may enhance oocyte quality and, ultimately, embryo quality through various pathways. A review study has reported a positive relationship between estradiol concentration and SIRT1 protein level, associated with the activation of anti-aging pathways.36 Our findings indicated a positive correlation between SIRT1 and aromatase activity, ultimately affecting the levels of estradiol in follicular fluid. Another study revealed that androgens and estrogens can stimulate SIRT1 expression in human aortic endothelial cells.37 However, our results did not demonstrate a positive correlation between testosterone levels in follicular fluid and SIRT1 levels. Given SIRT1’s role in regulating aromatase expression and its direct relationship with aromatase activity, it seems illogical for testosterone to positively correlate with SIRT1. Nevertheless, it is assumed that estradiol and SIRT1 may exert mutual effects.3839 Our findings suggest a correlation between the aromatase activity index and biochemical pregnancy in women. These results align with previous work showing that aromatase activity in granulosa cells, reflecting their estrogen synthesis, is associated with pregnancy success in infertility treatment. Consequently, aromatase activity in granulosa cells may serve as a reliable predictor of embryo implantation potential.40

In line with our research, an elevation in the HOMA-Index has been observed in the follicular fluid of women with PCOS. Also, a decrease in aromatase activity was significantly associated with an increase in the HOMA-Index.24 Prior evidence have reported that aromatase inhibitor use can exacerbate insulin resistance. Collectively, these results imply that aromatase activity may modulate insulin function, with higher aromatase activity potentially associated with improved insulin sensitivity in this context.4142

Other studies have indicated a correlation between the HOMA-Index and SIRT1 levels.943 Our results also revealed a correlation between the HOMA-Index, SIRT1 levels, and aromatase activity. While the exact mechanism underlying this interaction is not fully understood, clearer insight into the pathway of this triad may influence the treatment of PCOS and enhance IVF outcomes.

Due to the limitations of our study, we propose that (i) assessing SIRT1 levels and aromatase activity in granulosa cells, (ii) conducting a comparison of SIRT1 levels and aromatase activity between immature and mature follicles, and (iii) measuring both SIRT1 protein levels and SIRT1 gene expression simultaneously could validate our findings. Considering the critical role of estradiol in oocyte maturation, an intriguing question arises: could the activation of aromatase serve as a treatment for polycystic ovary syndrome?

In conclusion, we demonstrated that lower levels of SIRT1 protein in follicular fluid are implicated in the pathogenesis of PCOS. Furthermore, a significant implication of our findings is that increasing SIRT1 levels through aromatase activation may be a potential treatment strategy for women who suffer from PCOS.

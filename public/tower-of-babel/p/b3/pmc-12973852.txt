The complete mitochondrial genome sequence of the angel-wing mushroom Pleurocybella porrigens
Authors: Keisuke Mitsukuni, Nozomu Watanabe, Koki Sekimata, Jili Zhang, Akiko Ono, Yuki Tanaka, Tomohiro Suzuki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mushroom Pleurocybella porrigens is widely distributed in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. However, in the autumn of 2004, 59 food-poisoning incidents were reported in Japan, resulting in 17 deaths due to acute encephalopathy. In previous studies, we demonstrated that a lectin (PPL), pleurocybelline (PC), and pleurocybellaziridine (PA) from this mushroom caused neurotoxic effects in mice, and we also reported its nuclear genome (2013, 2026) and transcriptome data from fruiting bodies and mycelia (2024). Here we report the complete mitochondrial genome sequence of P. porrigens. The mitochondrial genome of P. porrigens is 63,054 bp in length and contains 18 protein-coding genes, 2 rRNA genes, and 25 tRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis based on 14 conserved mitochondrial protein-coding genes recovered P. porrigens as a distinct and well-supported lineage within Agaricales. In the inferred topology, P. porrigens was positioned basal to the core Agaricales clade, internal to Clavaria fumosa and Hygrophorus russula, and clearly separated from Boletales outgroup taxa. This phylogenetic placement is consistent with recent phylogenomic frameworks recognizing Phyllotopsidaceae as an independent lineage within Agaricales. This study represents the first report of a complete mitochondrial genome from the genus Pleurocybella and provides a valuable reference for future comparative mitochondrial genomic studies within Agaricales.

Introduction

Pleurocybella porrigens (Pers.) Singer, 1947 (Japanese name, Sugihiratake; English name, Angel-wing mushroom) is a mushroom belonging to the family Phyllotopsidaceae. P. porrigens grows in overlapping clumps on old stumps and fallen trees of conifers such as cedar and pine. The fruiting bodies grow in clusters of overlapping ear- or fan-shaped white caps ranging from 2 to 6 cm in diameter. This mushroom is widely distributed in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere and has traditionally been consumed in Asia (Matsumoto et al. 2005; Ainsworth 2008). However, in the autumn of 2004, 59 food-poisoning incidents were reported in Japan, resulting in 17 deaths due to acute encephalopathy (Kuwabara et al. 2005; Obara et al. 2005; 2008). Many of the affected individuals had chronic renal failure and were undergoing hemodialysis. In response, the Ministry of Health, Labor and Welfare of Japan established a study group to elucidate food poisoning caused by P. porrigens; however, the group was disbanded in 2006 after concluding that the etiology of the disease remained unexplained. To date, vitamin D analogs (Sasaki et al. 2006), fatty acids, saccharides (Takata et al. 2009), and hydrogen cyanide (Gonmori and Yokoyama 2009) have been reported as potential causative toxins associated with P. porrigens.

We have previously investigated the molecular basis of food poisoning caused by P. porrigens (Suzuki et al. 2009; Kawaguchi et al. 2010; Suzuki et al. 2014, 2021). In 2023, we demonstrated that a mixture of pleurocybelline (PC) and P. porrigens lectin (PPL) exhibited exo-protease activity, degrading various substrates at the N- and C-termini, with no amino acid specificity. Furthermore, when the mixture of PC, PPL, and pleurocybellaziridine (PA) was administered to mice, the number of apoptotic cells significantly increased in the hippocampus. Based on these findings, we propose that the PPL-PC complex damages the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and that PA subsequently causes acute encephalopathy (Kawagishi 2023; Suzuki et al. 2023).

In 2013, we reported draft genomic data of P. porrigens generated using the Illumina Genome Analyzer (GAIIx), which were archived in the integrated genome database A-WINGS (Suzuki et al. 2013; Yamamoto et al. 2014). Subsequently, in 2024, we reported transcriptomic data from fruiting bodies and mycelia generated using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (Watanabe et al. 2024), with sequencing data deposited in DNA Data Bank of Japan (DDBJ) under accession number DRA017296. More recently, in 2026, we reported a high-quality nuclear genome assembly of this mushroom based on long-read sequencing using the PacBio platform, providing a substantially improved genomic resource for this species (Watanabe et al. 2025).

Phylogenetic analysis of the Phyllotopsidaceae family (including P. porrigens) using mitochondrial genomes can provide valuable insights into the evolutionary relationships among these fungi (Feng et al. 2024). In the present study, we report the first complete mitochondrial genome sequence of P. porrigens, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of its phylogenetic position among related mushrooms.

Materials and methods

A strain of P. porrigens used in this study was isolated by tissue culture from fruiting bodies collected by the authors in the forest (35.48362145 N, 138.71636340 E) of Yamanashi Prefecture, Japan (Figure 1), and deposited at the National Biological Resource Center (NBRC), National Institute of Technology and Evaluation (https://www.nite.go.jp/en/index.html, Culture Collection Division, nbrc-order@nite.go.jp) under voucher no. NBRC 00117360.

Species image of Pleurocybella porrigens. The photo was taken by T. Suzuki.

Mycelia were cultivated in potato dextrose broth with 0.5% yeast extract at 25 °C for 2 weeks, and genomic DNA was extracted using the cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) method (Tanaka et al. 2017; Zhao and Qi 2025). A SMRTbell library was prepared using the SMRTbell prep kit 3.0, and long-read sequencing was then performed on the PacBio Revio System (PacBio, Menlo Park, CA). De novo assembly was conducted using hifiasm ver. 0.19.8-r603, and overall assembly statistics, including N50 values and BUSCO completeness, were previously evaluated and reported in our nuclear genome study of P. porrigens (Watanabe et al. 2025). Circularization of the mitochondrial genome was confirmed by manual inspection of the assembly and read-mapping results. Specifically, terminal sequences of the assembled contig showed sequence continuity with the opposite end, and long reads spanning the junction region were identified. In addition, read depth analysis revealed consistently high and continuous coverage across the entire mitochondrial genome, including the junction region (Fig. S1). Read depth statistics (mean and median coverage) were calculated using minimap2 v2.28 (Li 2018).

The complete mitochondrial genome sequence of P. porrigens was annotated using GeSeq version 2.03 (https://chlorobox.mpimp-golm.mpg.de/geseq.html) (Tillich et al. 2017), MFannot (https://megasun.bch.umontreal.ca/cgi-bin/mfannot/mfannotInterface.pl) (Beck and Lang 2021), SnapGene software (https://www.snapgene.com/), and MITOS2 (http://mitos.bioinf.uni-leipzig.de) (Bernt et al. 2013). GeSeq conditions were set to Pleurotus for ‘BLAT Reference Sequences,’ 25 for ‘Protein search identity,’ and 85 for ‘rRNA, tRNA, DNA search identity’. For MFannot annotation, the NCBI genetic code table 4 (Mold, Protozoan, and Coelenterate Mitochondrial Code) was used. The annotation results from GeSeq, MFannot, and MITOS2 were compared, and discrepancies were manually curated. During curation, start and stop codon positions were adjusted where necessary, and only complete open reading frames without internal stop codons were retained. All finalized ORFs were further evaluated using NCBI ORF Finder (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/orffinder/) and validated by BLAST searches against the Swiss-Prot and NCBI databases; features lacking detectable homology were excluded. Predicted rRNA sequences were aligned with rRNAs from representative Agaricales species (Tricholoma bakamatsutake, T. flavovirens, T. saponaceum, Lyophyllum shimeji, T. terreum, and Tricholomella constricta) using MAFFT v7.525 with the –auto option (Katoh and Standley 2013), confirming conserved and coherent alignments among these taxa. A circular mitochondrial genome map was generated using OrganellarGenomeDRAW (OGDRAW) v1.3.1 (Greiner et al. 2019).

For phylogenetic analysis, amino acid sequences of 14 conserved mitochondrial protein-coding genes (atp6, atp8, atp9, cob, cox1–3, nad1–6, and nad4L) were concatenated. Homologous sequences from 23 additional Agaricales species were obtained from NCBI. Two Boletales species, Suillus collinitus and Lanmaoa macrocarpa, were used as outgroups. Each gene was aligned separately using MAFFT v7.525 with the –auto option. The resulting alignments were then trimmed using trimAl v1.5 (Capella-Gutiérrez et al. 2009) with the -automated1 setting to remove ambiguously aligned regions. The concatenated dataset and partition scheme were then used to infer a maximum likelihood (ML) phylogeny using IQ-TREE v2.2.0 (Minh et al. 2020) with default settings. Node support was evaluated using 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates (-bb 1000). The final tree, including bootstrap support values and branch structures, was visualized using Geneious Prime v2025.1.

Results

The mitochondrial genome of P. porrigens is a circular DNA molecule of 63,054 bp with a GC content of 27.3%. Annotation identified a total of 45 genes in the mitochondrial genome of P. porrigens, including 18 protein-coding genes, 2 rRNA genes, and 25 tRNA genes (Figure 2). Read depth analysis showed consistently high coverage across the mitochondrial genome, with a median coverage of 71,877× and a mean coverage of 70,924.7×, supporting the reliability of the assembly.

Genes drawn outside the outer circle are transcribed in a counterclockwise direction, whereas genes drawn inside the circle are transcribed clockwise. The legend in the lower left represents different gene functions, which are indicated by colors. The inner gray plot represents the GC skew across the complete mitochondrial genome.

According to the annotation results, the 46 protein-coding genes encoded 15 conserved mitochondrial proteins, including cox1-3, cob, nad1-6, nad4L, atp6, atp8, atp9, rps3. Introns were detected in the cox1, cob, nad5 and rnL genes, ranging in length from 25 bp to 31,351 bp. Analysis of these intronic regions revealed characteristic conserved domains, and all identified introns were classified as group I introns (Zhang and Zhang 2019). Group I introns are commonly reported in fungal mitochondrial genomes, including those of Agaricales, and their presence in P. porrigens is therefore not unusual (Huang et al. 2021) Additionally, annotation using SnapGene identified 14 genes encoding restriction enzymes, including AhdI, ApaI, AvaI, BsoBI, BsrBI, EciI, NcoI, PaeR7I, PfoI, PmlI, PshAI, PspOMI, PspXI, and XhoI.

The maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic analysis recovered P. porrigens as the most basal lineage within Agaricales among the analyzed taxa (Figure 3). In this topology, H. russula and C. fumosa were positioned outside the main Agaricales clade, whereas the two Boletales species (L. macrocarpa and S. collinitus) formed the most external lineages, consistent with their use as outgroups.

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we report the first complete mitochondrial genome sequence of P. porrigens and the first mitochondrial genome from the genus Pleurocybella. The mitochondrial genome size of P. porrigens (63 kb) falls within the range observed among the Agaricales species included in this study (55–256 kb) and is comparable to those of closely related Pleurotus species (60–73 kb). Gene content and overall gene order were largely conserved relative to H. russula and Pleurotus citrinopileatus, indicating a typical Agaricales-type mitochondrial genome organization. Introns were mainly detected in the cox1 and cob genes, consistent with patterns observed in closely related taxa. Introns identified in nad5 and rnL have also been reported in other Agaricales mitochondrial genomes and were classified as group I introns, reflecting the dynamic yet common nature of intron distribution within the order.

Phylogenetic analysis based on 14 conserved mitochondrial protein-coding genes recovered P. porrigens as a distinct and well-supported lineage within Agaricales. In the inferred topology, P. porrigens was positioned as a basal lineage within Agaricales, internal to C. fumosa and H. russula, and clearly separated from the Boletales outgroup taxa (L. macrocarpa and S. collinitus). These results are consistent with recent comprehensive phylogenomic analyses that placed Pleurocybella, Tricholomopsis, and Phyllotopsis within the monophyletic family Phyllotopsidaceae (Li et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024), which represents a deeply divergent lineage within Agaricales, as supported by analyses of hundreds of single-copy orthologous genes. Such studies reinforce the distinctiveness of Phyllotopsidaceae and support its recognition as separate from Pleurotaceae sensu stricto.

The findings of this study support recently proposed fungal classification frameworks, particularly those redefining higher-level relationships within Agaricales based on large-scale phylogenomic data. However, complete mitochondrial genome sequences from other members of Phyllotopsidaceae, including the genera Macrotyphula and Phyllotopsis, are still unavailable. The lack of mitochondrial genomic data from these closely related taxa currently limits detailed comparative analyses within the family. Further sequencing and comparative analyses of mitochondrial genomes from these taxa will be essential to clarify intrafamilial evolutionary relationships, refine the phylogenetic placement of Pleurocybella, and improve our understanding of mitochondrial genome evolution within Agaricales.

Prolonged Venous Transit Is Associated With Lower Likelihood of Favorable Clinical Recovery in Reperfused Anterior Circulation Large‐Vessel Occlusion Stroke
Authors: Vivek S Yedavalli, Manisha Koneru, Aneri B Balar, Dhairya A Lakhani, Andrew Cho, Sijin Wen, Yanqing Mei, Meisam Hoseinyazdi, Cynthia Greene, Risheng Xu, Licia P Luna, Justin M Caplan, Adam A Dmytriw, Adrien Guenego, Jeremy J Heit, Gregory W Albers, Max Wintermark, Luis Fernando Gonzalez, Victor C Urrutia, Judy Huang, Kambiz Nael, Tobias D Faizy, Richard Leigh, Elisabeth B Marsh, Argye E Hillis, Rafael H Llinas
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUnfavorable clinical outcomes in patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by large‐vessel occlusions despite successful reperfusion have been associated with poor venous outflow. The objective of this study is to assess the relationship between prolonged venous transit (PVT), a novel perfusion imaging surrogate for poor venous outflow, and likelihood of favorable clinical recovery.MethodsIn this retrospective review of prospectively collected data of consecutive patients (2017–2022) with successfully reperfused, anterior circulation acute ischemic stroke caused by large‐vessel occlusions, we assessed the association between PVT+, defined as time to maximum ≧10‐second timing delays within either the posterior superior sagittal sinus, torcula, or both regions on pretreatment perfusion imaging, and favorable clinical outcomes defined as 90‐day modified Rankin Scale score of 0 to 2. Patients were dichotomized into PVT+ and PVT− for analysis. Multivariable logistic regression analyses for favorable recovery were performed with demographic variables and stroke characteristics.ResultsA total of 127 consecutive patients (median age, 71 [interquartile range 61—77] years; 59.1% women) were included, and 40 of 127 (31.5%) were PVT+. PVT+ (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 0.23 [95% CI, 0.07–0.81]; P = 0.02) and advanced age (aOR, 0.92 [95% CI, 0.88–0.97]; P = 0.01) were significantly associated with the primary outcome.ConclusionPVT+ was associated with a lower likelihood of achieving favorable clinical recovery in successfully reperfused patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by large vessel occlusion. PVT may serve as a clinically useful adjunctive imaging parameter.Keywords: acute ischemic stroke, cerebral blood flow, large‐vessel occlusion, prolonged venous transit, venous outflow

Venous outflow (VO) has been shown to be an important aspect of the cerebral collateral cascade in patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by large‐vessel occlusion (AIS‐LVO). 1 , 2 Poor VO is associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes 3 , 4 even when successful reperfusion is achieved. 5 , 6 , 7 Robust VO has also been shown to predict favorable outcomes in this group of patients, particularly when they are treated with reperfusion therapy. 5 , 8 , 9

Computed tomography perfusion (CTP) is used in many centers for mechanical thrombectomy (MT) triage for patients with AIS‐LVO. Owing to the rich information provided by the multiphase acquisition, CTP can also provide potentially useful information on VO. However, to date, only a handful of studies have directly investigated the utility of CTP in assessing VO for patients with AIS‐LVO. Su et al previously reported that peak time to VO is a predictor of futile reperfusion in a sample of 80 patients. 7 Other studies have found that prolonged time to peak is a marker of striatocapsular infarction middle cerebral artery segment occlusions reperfused by MT. 10 A recent investigation also showed that patients with favorable VO had shorter time to peak as compared with those with poor VO in 97 patients with AIS‐LVO. 11

Prolonged venous transit (PVT) is a novel surrogate of VO, with PVT+ defined as the presence of time to maximum (Tmax) ≧10 seconds on postprocessed CTP imaging within at least one of the largest proximal draining dural venous sinus locations (ie, superior sagittal sinus [SSS] or torcula). 12 , 13 The aim of this study is to characterize the association between PVT and 90‐day favorable functional recovery in successfully reperfused patients with AIS‐LVO.

Methods

Deidentified data will be made available to qualified investigators upon reasonable request to the corresponding author. This retrospective study was approved by the institutional review board with waiver of informed consent (JHU‐IRB00269637). This study follows the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology checklist guidelines as an observational study. 14

Population

From a prospective registry of patients with acute ischemic stroke between September 1, 2017 and September 22, 2022, we retrospectively analyzed patients meeting inclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria included (1) confirmed anterior circulation large‐vessel occlusions on computed tomography angiography (CTA) using baseline comprehensive computed tomography (CT) evaluation, inclusive of noncontrast CT, CTA, and CTP; (2) successful reperfusion with intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) or MT (defined as modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score of 2b/2c/3); and (3) available 90‐day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score. Patients who were not successfully reperfused despite IVT or MT attempts, or lacked 90‐day follow‐up data, were excluded from the analysis. Our sample size achieves 80% power to detect an odds ratio of 0.33 between 2 groups using a 2‐sided χ2 test at a significance level of 0.05, assuming that the proportion of event rates is 50%.

Clinical Data Collection

Baseline clinical data were ascertained by certified neurologists or nurse practitioners at the time of the clinical encounter and were retrospectively extracted from the electronic medical record. These parameters included demographics, risk factors for acute ischemic stroke (including diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease, and atrial fibrillation), prior stroke history, antiplatelet or anticoagulant use, tobacco use, alcohol use, admission glucose, premorbid mRS SCORE; admission National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, and stroke subtype per TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) criteria. 15

Clinical Outcomes Assessment

The 90‐day mRS scores were calculated by certified neurologists or nurse practitioners.

Interventional Data Collection

Per institutional protocols, the decision to treat with either IVT or MT is made with multidisciplinary stroke team consensus. If the patient was treated with MT, thrombectomy technique (ie, aspiration only, stent retriever only, or combination) and devices used were per discretion of neurointerventionalist. Last‐known‐well‐to‐door and door‐to‐recanalization times were collected. The final posttreatment modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score was determined by the treating neurointerventionalist and verified retrospectively by 2‐expert consensus review.

Imaging Data Collection

Imaging data obtained from contemporaneous radiology reports were retrospectively reexamined and validated by an experienced neuroradiologist. Postprocessed or otherwise retrospectively evaluated imaging data was performed by an experienced neuroradiologist.

Alberta Stroke Program Early CT scores were calculated from pretreatment noncontrast CTs by an experienced neuroradiologist (V.S.Y.; 9 years of working experience). Presence of large‐vessel occlusion, segment of occlusion, and laterality of occlusion were identified on the basis of baseline CTAs by an experienced neuroradiologist (V.S.Y.). Collateral status assessments were performed on single‐phase CTA as established by Tan et al. 16 The presence of complications such as hemorrhagic transformation, as defined by the ECASS 2 (European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study) trial, 17 was assessed on follow‐up noncontrast CT or susceptibility‐weighted magnetic resonance imaging within 48 hours of initial presentation.

CTP Parameters

Whole‐brain pretreatment CTP was performed on the Siemens Somatom Force (Erlangen, Germany) with the following parameters: 70 kVP, 200 effective mAs, rotation time 0.25 seconds, average acquisition time 60 seconds, collimation 48 × 1.2 mm, pitch value 0.7, 4D range 114 mm × 1.5 seconds. Pretreatment CTP images were then postprocessed using RAPID commercial software (IschemaView, Menlo Park, CA) for generating quantitative relative cerebral blood flow volumes, Tmax volumes, and qualitative Tmax maps.

PVT Assessment

Based on qualitative Tmax maps, PVT was assessed within the posterior SSS at the level of the lateral ventricle occipital horns to incorporate more proximal venous drainage as well as at the torcula to account for deep venous drainage patterns (Figure 1A–D). PVT+ was defined as a Tmax≧10 seconds within the posterior SSS, torcula, or both areas when using the timing key adjacent to the image. PVT assessments were performed by a board certified neuroradiologist (V.S.Y.) and a diagnostic neuroradiology fellow (D.L., 4 years of working experience), blinded to the clinical information. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus review.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome was favorable clinical recovery, defined as a 90‐day mRS score of 0 to 2.

Statistical Analysis

The objective of this study was to assess the association between PVT+ and the clinical outcomes defined as a 90‐day mRS score of 0 to 2. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data first. Continuous data were reported as median and interquartile range (IQR) and compared with Wilcoxon rank‐sum tests. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and compared using χ2 tests or Fisher's exact test. Univariate and multivariable logistic models were used to assess the association between the outcome of favorable recovery (90‐day mRS score, 0–2) and the following significant or clinically relevant parameters: age, sex, race, hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, prior stroke/transient ischemic attack, premorbid mRS score, admission National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, Alberta Stroke Program Early CT score, Tmax>6 mL volumes, relative cerebral blood flow <30% mL volumes, Tan score, PVT, IVT, and final modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction. 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 Significance was specified at P≤0.05. Missing data were minimal and not imputed. All statistical analyses were conducted using JMP Pro version 17.0 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, NC).

Results

Of 803 patients with acute ischemic stroke, 355 underwent MT for large‐vessel occlusions, and 127 successfully reperfused patients (median age, 71 [IQR, 61–77] years; 59.1% women) had pretreatment CTP and 90‐day mRS data available (Table 1). Forty (40/127 [31.5%]) patients were classified as PVT+. Most patients had M1 middle cerebral artery segment occlusions (95/127 [74.8%]; Table 1). Admission National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score was significantly greater in the patients with PVT (median, 17 [IQR, 14–21] than patients without PVT (median, 14 [IQR, 10–20]; P = 0.02; Table 1).

Regression Analysis

In univariate logistic regression analyses, advanced age (P<0.001), higher National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale scores (P<0.001), greater Tmax>6‐second volumes (P = 0.02), and PVT+ (P<0.001) were inversely associated with favorable functional recovery (Table 3). After adjustment with multivariable logistic regression, only advanced age (adjusted odds ratio, 0.92 [95% CI, 0.88–0.97]; P = 0.01) and PVT+ (adjusted odds ratio, 0.23 [95% CI, 0.07–0.81]; P = 0.02) were significantly associated with reduced odds of favorable functional recovery (Table 3). The multivariable regression had an area under the curve of 0.87 (95% CI, 0.79–0.92; Figure 2).

Discussion

Our study demonstrates that PVT+ is significantly associated with a lower likelihood of favorable clinical outcomes despite successful reperfusion by MT. PVT is a novel, relatively easy to interpret pretreatment CTP surrogate of poor VO. Our results validate PVT as an additional adjunct imaging metric in the complex decision‐making process for triaging patients with AIS‐LVO for reperfusion therapy.

Poor VO has been established as a prognostic biomarker for post‐MT complications, 23 infarct growth, 24 hemorrhagic transformation, 25 and death. Yedavalli et al demonstrated that PVT+, as a proxy for poor VO profile, was associated with higher odds of death. 13 Similarly, Heitkamp et al demonstrated that patients with poor VO, as assessed by Cortical Vein Opacification Score detected on pretreatment single‐phase CTA, were more likely to have poor outcomes despite successful reperfusion. 6 Our study corroborates findings of prior investigations demonstrating the relationship between imaging markers of poor VO and unfavorable clinical outcomes.

Of note, perfusion‐based PVT also has potential advantages over CTA‐based Cortical Vein Opacification Score. First, single‐phase CTA can vary on the basis of acquisition timing and rate of contrast injection. 8 , 26 Venous contamination on single‐phase CTA can lead to imprecise assessments of Cortical Vein Opacification Score given that the acquisition is more specific for arterial vasculature assessment. 27 These factors also affect CTP acquisition on which PVT is assessed. However, owing to the multiphase, dynamic angiographic acquisition, assessment of collateralization is more accurate with CTP than single‐phase CTA. 28 , 29 Furthermore, the assessment of PVT depends on qualitatively identifying the presence or absence of Tmax≥10 seconds in 2 regions, and thus can be readily interpreted on postprocessed CTP. This simplicity makes it potentially more amenable to automated assessments compared with Cortical Vein Opacification Score.

Limitations

Our study has limitations to acknowledge. This analysis is limited by factors inherent to retrospective studies, such as selection bias from patients excluded due to lack of 90‐day follow‐up data. CTP may not be readily available in smaller centers or may be deferred on the basis of institutional practice for patients presenting within early time windows. PVT may be subject to several factors, including the effect of blood volume on blood flow, the drainage of the nonaffected hemisphere, and the CTP imaging protocol. 30 Moreover, this study was exclusively in patients who were successfully reperfused. Although this was intentional in the study design to control for the confounding effect of successful reperfusion on clinical recovery, it may also limit generalizability of the results. Finally, our data also did not include metrics of small‐vessel ischemic disease, which can influence venous transit and outflow. 30

PVT nevertheless serves as a potentially useful, easily interpreted adjunct imaging parameter, particularly for physicians who may be less experienced with perfusion imaging. Particularly in more ambiguous patient scenarios, advanced imaging protocols, including evaluation of PVT, may be helpful to inform treatment decisions. Future prospective studies including patients who did not undergo MT or did so unsuccessfully are necessary to expand its prognostic capabilities.

Conclusion

PVT, defined as Tmax ≧10 seconds within the posterior SSS, torcula, or both, is a pretreatment VO surrogate imaging parameter easily interpreted from a CTP Tmax map. In successfully reperfused patients with AIS‐LVO, PVT+ is independently associated with decreased likelihood of achieving favorable outcomes.

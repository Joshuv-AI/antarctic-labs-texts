Resolving spectral overlaps in Anti-Parkinson drug combinations: a green spectrophotometric approach for entacapone determination
Authors: Haitham A El Fiky, Sherif Gamal, Mohamed A Abdelgawad, Mohammed Gamal, Ola G Hussein
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA selective and eco-friendly spectrophotometric approach was developed for the quantitative determination of entacapone (ENT) in the presence of its co-formulated drugs carbidopa (CAR) and levodopa (LEV) which exhibit severe spectral overlap. Three simple spectrophotometric methods; direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication techniques were optimized to enable effective resolution of the ENT spectrum without the need for prior separation or complex mathematical treatment. The proposed approaches demonstrated high selectivity toward ENT in laboratory-prepared mixtures and pharmaceutical dosage forms confirming the successful minimization of interference from CAR and LEV. The proposed methods showed excellent linearity over the concentration range of 1.5–14.0 µg/mL with a correlation coefficient (r) of 0.9998. The limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ) were 0.226 µg/mL and 0.685 µg/mL, respectively confirming high sensitivity. The methods were validated according to international guidelines and proved suitable for routine quality control analysis. In addition, the environmental impact of the developed methods was comprehensively evaluated using multiple greenness assessment tools such as Analytical Eco-Scale, NEMI, GAPI, and AGREE tools confirming their excellent sustainability profile. In conclusion, the developed spectrophotometric methods provide a simple, accurate, precise, and green alternative for determining entacapone in the presence of spectral similar compounds without the need for separation or sophisticated instrumentation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13065-026-01768-3.Keywords: Carbidopa, Chronic diseases, Entacapone, Green analytical chemistry, Hazardous chemicals, Health equity, Infectious diseases, Levodopa, Mental health, Stalevo® tablets

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a chronic progressive neurodegenerative disorder that primarily affects dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra leading to characteristic motor symptoms such as tremors, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability [1, 2]. The cornerstone of PD management involves dopaminergic replacement therapy mainly using levodopa (LEV) the immediate metabolic precursor of dopamine; Fig. 1a. However, due to its rapid peripheral metabolism and short half-life, LEV is commonly co-administered with carbidopa (CAR) a peripheral dopa decarboxylase inhibitor that enhances LEV’s bioavailability and reduces peripheral side effects [3]; Fig. 1b. Despite this combination’s therapeutic success, chronic LEV administration often leads to fluctuating plasma concentrations and the development of motor complications such as the “wearing-off” phenomenon.

Chemical structures of a Carbidopa, b Levodopa, and c Entacapone

To counteract these issues, entacapone (ENT) a selective and reversible catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitor is co-formulated with LEV and CAR to prolong dopamine availability by inhibiting its peripheral metabolism [4]; Fig. 1c. The triple combination, commercially available as Stalevo® provides more stable plasma concentrations of LEV and enhances clinical control in PD patients. Given the widespread therapeutic application of this combination, accurate and reliable analytical methods are essential for quality control and pharmaceutical assurance.

The simultaneous determination of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV presents a significant analytical challenge due to their structural similarities and extensive spectral overlap in the UV region. These compounds exhibit closely spaced absorption bands making their resolution difficult using direct spectrophotometric measurements such as those published previously using non green solvents and rely on complex multivariate calibration models or extensive data processing [5, 6]. Derivative spectrophotometry was employed [5] to resolve spectral overlap using second- and third-order derivatives and zero-crossing measurements; however, such approaches are often associated with increased spectral noise, reduced signal stability, and complex data treatment. More recently, selective spectrophotometric techniques were developed for the determination of entacapone in the presence of levodopa and carbidopa which were treated as spectral interferents rather than simultaneously quantified components [6]. In contrast, the present work introduces straightforward spectrophotometric approaches that enable the determination of ENT in a ternary mixture without the need for derivative spectrophotometry or chemometric factorization. Beyond the use of green solvents, the proposed methods offer enhanced simplicity, improved signal robustness, and broader analytical applicability requiring neither advanced software nor extensive data treatment. Moreover, their direct applicability to routine quality control analysis provides a practical and analytically advantageous alternative to previously reported spectrophotometric methods. Although chromatographic techniques such as HPLC [7–14], HPTLC [15] and LC-MS [16] have been extensively employed for their quantification these methods are often time-consuming, costly, and require the use of large volumes of organic solvents posing both economic and environmental concerns. Therefore, there is a strong motivation to develop simple, green, and cost-effective spectrophotometric methods that can selectively quantify ENT even in complex mixtures containing interfering components.

Spectrophotometric techniques are widely used due to their accessibility, simplicity, and rapid response. However, direct absorbance measurements are limited when spectral overlap occurs among coexisting species. To address this, mathematical a-based approaches such as direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtractioncoupled with constant multiplication methods have been developed to resolve overlapping spectra without prior separation enabling accurate quantification of individual components in multi-component mixtures.

The principles of green analytical chemistry (GAC) further emphasize the development of environmentally sustainable methods. Tools such as the Analytical Eco-Scale, NEMI, GAPI, and AGREE frameworks allow complete evaluation of a method’s greenness ensuring analytical performance aligns with sustainability objectives [17–24].

In this context, the present study aims to develop and validate eco-friendly spectrophotometric strategies for the selective quantification of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV. The proposed direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication methods overcome spectral interference ensuring accurate, precise, and environmentally compliant determination of ENT in bulk and combined pharmaceutical formulations. The methods were further evaluated for linearity, precision, accuracy, sensitivity, robustness, and greenness confirming their suitability for routine pharmaceutical quality control and sustainable analytical practice.

Experimental

Instrument and software

Spectrophotometric measurements were performed on a Shimadzu UV-1800 double-beam UV-Visible spectrophotometer (Kyoto, Japan) equipped with 1 cm matched quartz cells and operated within the 200.0–600.0 nm range using a 1 nm spectral bandwidth. Data acquisition and spectral manipulation including ratio subtraction and factorization were conducted with UV-Probe v2.42 software (Shimadzu Corp., Japan). All volumetric glassware was Class A-grade cleaned and dried before use and an analytical balance (Sartorius CPA224S, Germany) was employed for weighing.

Materials and reagents

Deionized water was obtained from a Milli-Q purification system served as the solvent for all spectrophotometric measurements.

Reference materials and pharmaceutical samples

Pure standards

Authentic pure standards of Levodopa (99.8%), Entacapone (99.6%), and Carbidopa (99.7%) were obtained from Global Napi Pharmaceuticals, Egypt each was supplied with a certificate of analysis verifying their purity and authenticity.

Commercial formulation

Marketed Stalevo® tablets (Novartis, batch number 1407626) were purchased from a local pharmacy. Each tablet is officially labeled to contain 200.0 mg of Levodopa, 50.0 mg of Entacapone, and 12.5 mg of Carbidopa. Ten tablets were accurately weighed, finely powdered, and homogenized. The amount of powder equivalent to one tablet was transferred to a volumetric flask dispersed in the selected solvent, sonicated to ensure complete dissolution, and filtered through Whatman No. 41 filter paper. The resulting filtrate was diluted quantitatively with deionized water to obtain a clear solution used for subsequent spectrophotometric determination.

Preparation of standard and test solutions

Stock and working solutions of ENT, CAR, and LEV

Individual stock solutions of ENT, CAR, and LEV were prepared by accurately weighing 10.0 mg of each pure compound and dissolving it in a small amount of deionized water followed by dilution to 100.0 mL with deionized water to yield solutions of 100.0 µg/mL. Working solutions were freshly obtained by serial dilution of the respective stock solutions to reach the concentration ranges specified for each analytical method. All solutions were freshly prepared on the day of use.

Laboratory-prepared synthetic mixtures

To assess the selectivity and accuracy of the developed procedures, several synthetic mixtures containing ENT with its co-formulated drugs were prepared in various ratios representative of pharmaceutical compositions. Precisely measured aliquots of each stock solution were combined to obtain mixtures with ratios of CAR: LEV: ENT = 1:2:3, 1:1:1, 1:4:4, 2:1:1, and 1:2:1. Each mixture was diluted to 10.0 mL with deionized water to yield clear homogeneous solutions. These prepared mixtures were analyzed using the proposed spectrophotometric methods to evaluate potential interference effects and ensure reliable quantification of ENT.

Experimental evaluation and analytical performance

Optical and spectral profile

The absorption behavior of ENT was carefully examined to establish its distinct spectral properties and to evaluate potential overlap with CAR and LEV. Individual standard solutions of ENT, CAR, and LEV (10.0 µg/mL each) were scanned in the 200.0–600.0 nm wavelength range against a solvent blank under identical instrumental settings. ENT exhibited a well-defined absorption maximum (λmax) at approximately 378.0 nm. In contrast, CAR and LEV showed significant absorption spectra at shorter wavelengths (around 280.0 nm and 300.0 nm, respectively) leading to noticeable spectral interference in the overlapping region. The substantial overlap of these spectra underscores the analytical challenge of directly determining ENT in mixtures containing CAR and LEV. The recorded spectra confirmed that ENT retains sufficient spectral individuality in the higher wavelength region enabling its selective quantification using appropriate mathematical spectrophotometric techniques. Consequently, direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication approaches were employed to resolve ENT’s absorbance contribution from interfering components.

Construction of calibration curve

Calibration curve for ENT was constructed for each of the proposed spectrophotometric approaches; direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication under the optimized conditions. A series of ENT standard solutions within the concentration range of 1.50–14.00 µg/mL were prepared and analyzed at their respective analytical wavelengths. The corresponding absorbance values were plotted against the ENT concentrations to generate calibration curves.

Validation with synthetic combinations

To confirm selectivity and evaluate interference from the co-formulated drugs CAR and LEV several laboratory-prepared mixtures of CAR: LEV: ENT were analyzed at varying ratios. These combinations 1:2:3, 1:1:1, 1:4:4, 2:1:1, and 1:2:1 were selected to mimic potential variations in pharmaceutical formulations.

Direct zero-order spectrophotometric method for ENT determination

The direct spectrophotometric quantification of ENT was performed by measuring its zero-order absorption spectrum at the selected wavelength of approximately 378.0 nm where no noticeable spectral overlap from CAR or LEV occurred. The calibration plot of absorbance versus concentration was established as previously demonstrated.

Factorized dual wavelength method (FDW) for ENT determination

ENT was determined in ternary mixtures containing CAR and LEV using the factorized dual wavelength method. Two wavelengths, 378.0 and 420.0 nm were selected where CAR and LEV exhibited nearly equal absorbance values while ENT showed a significant absorbance difference.The zero-order spectrum of a standard ENT solution was normalized by dividing the absorbance at each wavelength by the absorbance difference between the two selected wavelengths (ΔAENT = A378.0 − A420.0) producing a factorized spectral profile characteristic of ENT. For laboratory-prepared mixtures, the absorbance difference (ΔAmixture = A378.0 − A420.0) was calculated. The recovered ENT spectrum was then obtained by multiplying the factorized ENT spectrum by ΔAmixture. ENT concentration was determined using the corresponding calibration equation.This approach ensures selective quantification of ENT by mathematically eliminating interference from CAR and LEV without prior separation.

Ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication method (RS-CM) for ENT determination

The ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication method was employed to resolve overlapping spectra and achieve the selective determination of ENT in ternary mixtures containing CAR and LEV. In this approach, a standard spectrum of ENT (4.0 µg/mL) was selected as the divisor because ENT exhibits an extended spectral region where CAR and LEV show negligible absorbance allowing the formation of a stable constant region in the ratio spectra.The zero-order absorption spectrum of the mixture was divided by a standard spectrum of ENT (4.0 µg/mL) to obtain the corresponding ratio spectrum. Within the wavelength region where ENT shows significant absorbance and the other components have minimal contribution, a constant plateau was observed. This constant represents the contribution of the divisor (ENT) and was carefully subtracted from the ratio spectrum to eliminate its interference.The corrected ratio spectrum was then multiplied by the divisor spectrum (ENT, 4.0 µg/mL) to regenerate the zero-order absorption spectrum of ENT. From the reconstructed spectrum, ENT was directly quantified at its characteristic wavelength of 378.0 nm using the corresponding calibration regression equation.This mathematical treatment effectively eliminated the spectral interference from CAR and LEV providing accurate, precise, and reproducible results over the studied concentration range. The method’s simplicity, rapid data processing, and elimination of prior chemical separation steps make the ratio subtraction technique a reliable and environmentally friendly approach for the routine determination of ENT in combined pharmaceutical formulations.

Determination of ENT in commercial formulation

The optimized spectrophotometric procedures were applied for the quantitative determination of entacapone (ENT) in Stalevo® tablets. Ten tablets were accurately weighed, finely powdered, and thoroughly mixed to ensure homogeneity. A portion of the powder equivalent to one tablet was transferred into a 100 mL volumetric flask dispersed in a small volume of the selected solvent and sonicated for about 15 min to achieve complete dissolution of the active ingredients. The solution was filtered through Whatman No. 41 filter paper into a clean flask and the residue was washed with several small portions of the same solvent. The filtrate and washing were combined and diluted to the mark with deionized water to obtain the stock solution. From this stock, an aliquot of 4.0 mL was accurately transferred into a 100 mL volumetric flask and diluted to volume with deionized water to prepare the working solution containing 4.0 µg/mL of ENT. This solution was used for subsequent spectrophotometric measurements.

Results and discussion

This study presents the development of simple, rapid, and environmentally responsible spectrophotometric methods for the quantification of ENT in the presence of its co-formulated compounds, CAR and LEV. Three spectrophotometric approaches; direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication were successfully applied to resolve the significant spectral overlap that typically hinders the simultaneous analysis of these drugs. Although ENT can be directly quantified at 378.0 nm due to the negligible spectral contribution of LEV and CAR at this wavelength, the direct zero-order spectrophotometric method may still be affected by baseline drift, instrumental noise, and excipient interferences particularly in real pharmaceutical samples. Therefore, the direct method is mainly recommended as a rapid screening approach. In contrast, FDW and RS-CM offer superior selectivity and robustness as they mathematically reduce baseline variations and enhance spectral discrimination in complex multicomponent mixtures providing more reliable quantification of ENT. Each method was carefully optimized to ensure maximum selectivity, sensitivity, and linearity across the studied concentration ranges. The proposed procedures were fully validated in accordance with ICH Q2(R2) guidelines demonstrating satisfactory linearity, accuracy, precision, and robustness. Statistical evaluation confirmed that all three methods yield consistent and reproducible results making them suitable for the analysis of both bulk materials and pharmaceutical dosage forms. Furthermore, the use of mild solvents, reduced reagent consumption, and short analysis time highlights the eco-friendly nature of the developed methods in line with the principles of green analytical chemistry. Accordingly, these spectrophotometric strategies offer a practical, precise, and sustainable alternative to conventional chromatographic techniques for the routine determination of ENT in multicomponent pharmaceutical formulations.

Spectral interferences in the quantitative assessment of ENT

The simultaneous spectrophotometric determination of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV presents a significant analytical challenge due to the extensive spectral overlap among these compounds. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the three drugs exhibit absorption spectra in the ultraviolet region that are partially superimposed making direct quantification of a single component impractical. ENT displays a pronounced absorption maximum at approximately 378.0 nm, while CAR and LEV show strong absorption within the shorter wavelength range between 200.0 and 300.0 nm. The proximity of these spectra results in mutual interference reducing the selectivity of direct absorbance measurements.

Zero-order absorption spectra of Carbidopa (red dashed line), Levodopa (black dotted line), and Entacapone (blue solid line) recorded over the wavelength range of 200.0–600.0 nm using deionized water as solvent. The figure demonstrates the significant spectral overlap among the three drugs while Entacapone exhibits a distinct absorption maximum at 378.0 nm which was selected for its selective determination. Absorbance is plotted against wavelength (nm)

To overcome these interferences, mathematical manipulation techniques such as direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication methods were explored. These approaches enable separation of overlapping spectral components through normalization, subtraction, or division operations thereby restoring the individual absorbance characteristics of ENT. Such strategies eliminate the need for prior chromatographic separation and provide reliable quantification while maintaining the environmental advantages of spectrophotometric analysis.

Solvent optimization and comparison with previously reported methods

The choice of solvent plays a critical role in the sensitivity and selectivity of spectrophotometric measurements. During the optimization process, several solvents were investigated including methanol, ethanol, and deionized water to identify the medium that provided the best spectral resolution and highest absorbance intensity for ENT without causing baseline drift or instability. Among the tested solvents, deionized water offered superior performance, producing sharp and well-defined absorption spectra while maintaining excellent solubility and spectral reproducibility. The use of water as a solvent also eliminates the environmental and health hazards associated with organic solvents making the proposed methods fully consistent with the principles of green analytical chemistry.

Compared with previously published spectrophotometric methods for ENT and its co-formulated drugs, the developed approaches demonstrate several distinct advantages. Earlier studies often relied on organic solvents or derivative spectrophotometry [5, 6] which although effective but required additional steps such as baseline correction or solvent extraction that increased time, cost, and environmental burden. In contrast, the direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication methods established in the present work achieve equivalent or superior accuracy and precision using simpler mathematical manipulation and greener solvent systems. The analytical performance reflected by a correlation coefficient of 0.9998 and recovery values near 100% compares favorably with reported chromatographic and spectrophotometric procedures while significantly reducing solvent consumption and sample preparation time. Therefore, the proposed water-based methods represent an efficient, reproducible, and environmentally sustainable alternative to traditional techniques for the quantitative assessment of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV.

Development of the proposed spectrophotometric approaches

Direct zero-order spectrophotometric method (D0) for ENT determination [25]

The direct zero-order spectrophotometric method (D0) was applied for the quantification of ENT in its pure form and in mixtures with CAR and LEV. The absorption spectrum of ENT showed a distinct maximum around 378.0 nm, a region where the spectral contributions of CAR and LEV were minimal allowing direct measurement without mathematical manipulation; Fig. 2.

Factorized dual wavelength method (FDW) for ENT determination [26–28]

The factorized dual wavelength (FDW) method proved highly effective for the selective determination of ENT in ternary mixtures containing CAR and LEV. In this approach, the dual wavelength absorbance difference of a standard ENT solution (10.0 µg/mL) was normalized by dividing the difference between absorbances at the selected wavelengths (378.0 and 420.0 nm) by the same value recorded for the standard producing a factorized spectrum standardized to a unit dual wavelength response. The dual wavelength absorbance differences of laboratory-prepared mixtures were then measured and multiplied by the normalized ENT spectrum to reconstruct the true dual wavelength profile of ENT within the mixture. This reconstruction enabled accurate estimation of ENT concentration using its corresponding calibration equation while effectively eliminating interference from CAR and LEV. The mathematically recovered ENT spectrum was subsequently subtracted from the total mixture spectrum to expose the remaining contribution of CAR and LEV. As illustrated in Fig. 3, this mathematical resolution successfully separated the overlapping spectral zones yielding precise and reproducible results for ENT determination. The outcomes confirm the strength of the factorized dual wavelength method in distinguishing the spectral components of multi-drug systems without requiring chemical extraction or complex instrumental techniques.

a Zero-order absorption spectra (D0) of Carbidopa, Levodopa, and Entacapone showing spectral overlap prior to mathematical treatment. b Factorized dual wavelength spectrum of Entacapone at a concentration of 10.0 µg/mL obtained after applying the ΔA* (amplitude difference) mathematical manipulation enabling selective determination of Entacapone at 378.0 nm without interference from the co-formulated drugs

Ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication method (RS-CM) for ENT determination [29–31]

The ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication method (RS-CM) was employed to resolve overlapping spectra and achieve selective determination of ENT in ternary mixtures containing CAR and LEV. This approach depends on a two-step mathematical manipulation that isolates ENT’s contribution from the combined spectrum of the mixture. Initially, the zero-order absorption spectrum of the mixture was divided by a standard spectrum of ENT (4.0 µg/mL) to produce a ratio spectrum. In this spectrum, the amount corresponding to ENT appears as a constant plateau in the wavelength region where CAR and LEV exhibit negligible absorbance. By subtracting this constant eliminates the contribution of ENT from the ratio spectrum of the mixture.

The corrected ratio spectrum was then multiplied by the divisor spectrum (ENT) to reconstruct the zero-order spectrum of ENT as illustrated in Fig. 4. This regenerated spectrum was used to determine ENT concentration directly from its calibration equation at the selected analytical wavelength of 378.0 nm. Application of the method to laboratory-prepared mixtures yielded high recovery values indicating that spectral interference from both CAR and LEV had been successfully eliminated.

a Absorption spectrum of a laboratory-prepared mixture containing Entacapone, Carbidopa, and Levodopa illustrating severe spectral overlap. b Recovered zero-order spectrum of Entacapone obtained after division by 4.0 µg/mL Entacapone spectrum followed by constant subtraction and multiplication by the divisor. The processed spectrum allows selective measurement of Entacapone at 378.0 nm

Performance evaluation and method validation

The analytical reliability of the proposed spectrophotometric procedures was thoroughly assessed in accordance with international validation guidelines [32] to confirm their suitability for quantitative analysis of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV. Each method; direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication showed an excellent linear response within the tested concentration range of 1.5–14.0 µg/mL with a correlation coefficient of 0.9998 indicating outstanding proportionality between absorbance and concentration; Fig. 5. The limits of detection (0.226 µg/mL) and quantification (0.685 µg/mL) confirmed the high sensitivity of the developed techniques. Accuracy was verified by recovery studies performed at different concentration levels giving a mean recovery of 100.89 ± 0.75% which demonstrates negligible systematic error. The precision results showed low relative standard deviations for both intra-day (0.823%) and inter-day (1.248%) measurements confirming excellent repeatability and reproducibility; Table 1. Application of the validated methods to laboratory-prepared mixtures containing various CAR: LEV: ENT ratios produced mean recoveries around 100% proving that interference from the co-formulated drugs was successfully minimized; Table 2. When applied to the commercial Stalevo® formulation, the obtained recoveries ranged from 99.44% to 101.57% across the three methods fully consistent with the labeled content with application of standard addition technique as illustrated in Table 3. Moreover, statistical comparison between the proposed spectrophotometric methods and the reported method [6] was performed using Student’s t-test, F-test and one-way ANOVA. The calculated t and F values were lower than the corresponding tabulated values at P = 0.05 indicating no significant difference in accuracy and precision; Table 4. Furthermore, one-way ANOVA revealed no statistically significant difference among the proposed and reported methods (P > 0.05) confirming the comparability of the methods; Table S1. These findings confirm that the proposed spectrophotometric procedures meet the essential validation criteria of linearity, accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and selectivity and are therefore appropriate for the routine quality-control determination of ENT in combined pharmaceutical preparations.

Analytical performance and greenness profile assessment

The developed spectrophotometric methods were not only optimized for accuracy and precision but also designed with environmental sustainability in mind. To comprehensively evaluate their ecological impact a multi-criteria greenness assessment was performed using the Analytical Eco-Scale, Green Analytical Procedure Index (GAPI), Analytical GREEnness (AGREE), and the National Environmental Methods Index (NEMI) tools. These complementary frameworks collectively examine key factors such as solvent toxicity, reagent volume, waste generation, occupational hazard, and energy consumption and the results are provided in Table 5. In contrast to the previously reported spectrophotometric studies [5, 6], which employed sodium hydroxide and methanol-based solvent systems, respectively to enhance spectral separation and sensitivity the present work avoids the use of any hazardous or volatile media. By adopting deionized water as the analytical solvent and minimizing reagent consumption the developed methods provide an equally accurate and precise determination of ENT while significantly reducing chemical waste and laboratory risk.

The Eco-Scale evaluation produced an exceptional score of 97 corresponding to only three penalty points which highlights the minimal environmental burden of the proposed procedures. The high score was primarily attributed to the use of deionized water as the main solvent, the elimination of hazardous organic reagents and the low waste output generated per analysis. This result classifies the developed methods as “excellent green” according to Eco-Scale standards. Similarly, the NEMI assessment confirmed the absence of toxic or persistent chemicals and low environmental risk while both the GAPI and AGREE visual tools verified that the proposed procedures meet nearly all twelve principles of green analytical chemistry.

Overall, the proposed spectrophotometric procedures achieve a unique balance between analytical quality and environmental responsibility. They deliver high selectivity, reproducibility, and sensitivity while adhering to the core principles of green chemistry. These findings confirm that the direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtraction coupled with constant multiplication methods are not only analytically sound but also represent sustainable, safe, and eco-efficient alternatives for the routine spectrophotometric determination of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV in both bulk and dosage form.

Conclusion

The present study introduces green, selective, and reliable spectrophotometric approaches for the quantitative determination of ENT in the presence of CAR and LEV. The proposed direct zero-order, factorized dual wavelength, and ratio subtractioncoupled with constant multiplication methods successfully resolved overlapping spectra without the need for separation steps or sophisticated instrumentation. All methods exhibited excellent linearity (r = 0.9998), accuracy (recoveries around 100%), and precision (RSD < 1.3%) confirming their suitability for routine quality control applications. Compared with previously reported spectrophotometric procedures that relied on alkaline or organic solvent systems such as sodium hydroxide and methanol, the current methods offer clear privileges in terms of simplicity, safety, and environmental sustainability. The exclusive use of deionized water as a solvent, minimal reagent consumption, and low waste generation contributed to an outstanding Eco-Scale score of 97 and favorable GAPI and AGREE profiles. These results emphasize that the proposed techniques not only equal but surpass earlier methods in analytical performance while fulfilling the principles of green analytical chemistry. Consequently, these procedures provide a superior, eco-efficient, and practical alternative for the simultaneous determination of ENT in multicomponent pharmaceutical formulations.

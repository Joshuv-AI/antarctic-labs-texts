Introduction

Pharmacological stress testing with myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI) using technetium sestamibi is a non-invasive tool used to diagnose and risk stratify patients with obstructive coronary artery disease (CAD). Stress MPI is based on the detection of relative regional differences in perfusion across left ventricular myocardial territories based on the distribution of the coronary artery supply. Myocardial territories that show relatively low tracer uptake suggest areas of impaired perfusion due to obstructive stenosis. Inherently, a balanced reduction of myocardial perfusion may appear as global homogenous tracer uptake without any regional defect, resulting in a false-negative MPI imaging in extensive multi-vessel obstructive CAD resulting in ‘balanced ischaemia’.1 False-negative MPI causing balanced ischaemia can be caused by left main disease (LMD) or three-vessel disease.

Detection of significant LMD in obstructive CAD has major prognostic and therapeutic implications.2,3 Left main CAD is significant when there is ≥50% stenosis of the vessel. The detection of LMD is especially important because current guidelines recommend revascularization to improve survival when LMD is present.4 Despite its significant utility, MPI stress testing exhibits limitations due its test characteristics, including false negatives, attributed to its sensitivity of 74% and specificity of 79%.

Case summary

An 80-year-old active male cattle farmer with hypertension presented to the emergency room with severe non-radiating exertional epigastric pain. The pain was provoked by lifting boxes an hour prior. He denied any nausea, vomiting, diaphoresis, palpitations, orthopnoea, syncope, or leg oedema. He had a history of similar epigastric pain 4 months ago for which he was seen in the emergency room. An MPI stress test done at that time was low risk with no significant fixed or reversible perfusion defect. His blood pressure was 147/82 mmHg, and his heart rate was 85 b.p.m. He otherwise had normal vital signs and a normal cardiopulmonary exam. His History, electrocardiogram (EKG), Age, Risk factors, and Troponins (HEART) score for Major Cardiac Events was 6,5 and his Global Registry of Acute Coronary Events (GRACE) score was 136.6 He had a medical history of hypertension, dyslipidaemia, benign prostatic hyperplasia, and gastroesophageal reflux disease. Differential diagnoses that were considered include non-ST segment elevation acute coronary syndrome, ischaemia/angina with non-obstructive coronary arteries, coronary microvascular dysfunction, and coronary vasospasm/Prinzmetal angina.

His electrocardiogram revealed a new T wave inversion in lead V2. His high-sensitivity cardiac troponins (hs-cTn) peaked at 97 ng/L with a delta value of 25. The delta value refers to the serial change in the troponin level checked initially and then at 2 and at 6 h after the initial check. A high-sensitivity troponin level < 5 ng/L is very low, a range of 5–15 ng/L is detectable but less than 99th percentile, a range of 16–49 ng/L is abnormally high, and a range of high-sensitivity troponin level ≥ 50 ng/L is critically high. A recent pharmacological stress test performed using regadenoson was low risk. 59.3% of the maximum predicted heart rate was attained during the nuclear study. His transient ischaemic dilation (TID) ratio was normal at 0.93. An abnormal TID ratio of 1.15 was used as our cut-off.7 His left ventricular ejection was estimated to be >60%. He had an abnormal perfusion map (Figures 1 and 2). An echocardiogram showed normal left ventricular systolic function of 55%, upper septal hypertrophy (sigmoid septum), and normal LV wall motion.

A left heart catheterization revealed a 99% hazy calcified stenosis in the left main coronary artery, 70% stenosis of the left anterior descending artery from the proximal to the mid-vessel, and a 50% stenosis just after the D2 branch (Figures 3–5). Forty per cent diffuse stenosis of the middle portion of the left circumflex artery was also noted. The right coronary artery vessel was moderate in size with a long 20%–30% mid to distal stenosis and a 30% stenosis to the mid posterior descending artery. He was noted to have elevated left ventricular end-diastolic pressures. Due to borderline haemodynamics during catheterization, an intra-aortic balloon pump was immediately placed to provide circulatory support. He suddenly developed severe refractory angina while in the catheterization lab. An emergent two-vessel coronary artery bypass grafting was performed within 8 h to address the life-threatening acute coronary syndrome. The patient had an uneventful recovery post-coronary bypass surgery with discharge home on Day 7 of admission. He has had complete resolution of anginal symptoms even at 1 year of follow-up.

Discussion

Several critical factors came into play when deciding between an urgent or delayed invasive strategy for acute coronary syndrome. The EKG, cardiac biomarkers, and clinical decision support tools such as the HEART, GRACE, and Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction scores help to risk stratify patients with acute cardiac chest pain.8 The HEART score reduces objective cardiac testing during 30 days, shortens the length of stay, and increases early discharges without any patients identified as low risk for early discharge suffering a major adverse cardiovascular outcome at 30 days.9 Our patient was at high risk per the HEART score. His GRACE risk prediction score for non-ST segment elevation acute coronary syndrome was 136 just short of the cut-off for early invasive coronary angiography. Although the GRACE score is very useful to assess cardiovascular risk and can guide patient triage, it is a complement to clinical assessment and management across the spectrum of patients with acute coronary syndrome.10 There was a high index of suspicion for ischaemia due to the presence of a new T wave inversion in lead V2, positive but non-impressively elevated cardiac troponins, and age with cardiovascular risk factors with high pre-test probability for CAD. Despite the negative nuclear stress test, left cardiac catheterization was done due to these worrisome indicators.

Despite its significant utility, MPI stress testing exhibits limitations, including false negatives, attributed to its sensitivity of 74% and specificity of 79%.7,9 False-negative MPI results can be due to procedural factors or patient factors. Procedural factors include caffeine intake, radiotracer contamination, inadequate exercise, and inability to reach age-predicted maximal heart rate. Patient factors include body habitus, left bundle branch block, branch vessel stenosis, left circumflex artery stenosis, and balanced ischaemia.9,10

Balanced ischaemia occurs because the technique of MPI with single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) can only measure relative uptake, not absolute activity. Ischaemia may not be recognized if there is a uniformly reduced coronary perfusion to all myocardium hence the moniker ‘balanced ischaemia’. It has been reported in left main CAD but is more commonly described in multi-vessel disease.10,11 The prevalence of balanced ischaemia on positron emission tomography relative perfusion imaging among patients with high-risk CAD has been estimated at 2.7%–4.5%.12,13 In such patients, FFRCT [area under the receiver-operating characteristic curve (AUC) for identification of ischaemia-causing lesions—0.94] showed significantly higher diagnostic performance than standard coronary CT angiography (CTA) (AUC—0.83) compared with MPI with SPECT (AUC—0.79).14 Alternative imaging modalities, such as coronary CT angiography (CTA), are more reliable in detecting critical coronary artery stenosis or multi-vessel disease.

Conclusion

Although false-negative MPI can occur with balanced ischaemia in multi-vessel CAD, it is uncommon with a normal TID ratio. A clue to severe disease is the abnormal perfusion map. In the index patient, a critical 99% left main coronary artery stenosis occurred after a recent false-negative myocardial perfusion stress imaging (MPI). This case underscores the importance of cautiously interpreting MPI results in intermediate/high-risk patients. In such scenarios, coronary CT angiography is a preferred non-invasive modality.

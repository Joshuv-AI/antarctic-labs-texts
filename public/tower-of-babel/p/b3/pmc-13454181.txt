A temporal adaptive dictionary-constrained LDA and Bi-calibrated dual granularity DTM framework for dynamic topic evolution analysis in academic papers
Authors: Xueqi Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the explosive growth of academic achievements in artificial intelligence (AI), accurately capturing the dynamic evolution of research topics is crucial for research topic selection, policy-making, and industrial innovation. To address the limitation that traditional topic models struggle to balance static topic recognition accuracy and dynamic evolution tracking capability, this study proposes a unified framework: Temporal Adaptive Dictionary-Constrained Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) combined with Bi-calibrated Dual Granularity Dynamic Topic Model (DTM). We conduct a systematic empirical analysis of dynamic topic evolution in the AI field using English AI papers retrieved from the Web of Science (WOS) Core Collection (2014–2023). Specifically, we first optimize the topic initialization process of LDA using a domain-specific dictionary to enhance the accuracy of static topic clustering. Second, we take the core topics identified by the optimized LDA as prior knowledge and input them into DTM, while optimizing the traditional single temporal granularity to an “annual + quarterly” dual granularity to accurately track the changes in topic strength, keyword composition, and cross-topic association relationships over time. Finally, we verify the performance of the proposed model using perplexity and topic coherence indicators, and reveal the coupling characteristics between different topics through correlation analysis. Results indicate that six core topic clusters have emerged in the AI field, with “generative AI”, “large language models”, and “multimodal learning” evolving into explosive topics after 2021, traditional machine learning shows a declining trend, with its core focus shifting toward “few-shot learning” and “edge computing adaptation”. The top three topic couplings are “generative AI-large language models”, “computer vision-multimodal learning” and “reinforcement learning-robotics”. The proposed combined model outperforms single models, achieving a 17.3% reduction in perplexity and a 23.5% decrease in topic drift rate, thereby providing valuable decision support for researchers seeking to grasp field frontiers and management departments for optimizing resource allocation.Keywords: Text mining, Latent dirichlet allocation, Dynamic topic model, Artificial intelligence, Topic evolution, Academic paper analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As a core track of global technological competition, the academic output in the AI field has grown rapidly over the past decade1. Data from the Web of Science shows that a large number of English academic papers in the AI field were published in 2023, covering numerous sub-directions such as machine learning, computer vision, and natural language processing. The rapid accumulation of massive literature not only provides theoretical support for the field’s development but also causes an “information overload” problem: researchers struggle to quickly identify core research hotspots and track topic evolution trajectories, research management departments lack a quantitative basis for formulating R&D policies, and enterprises face difficulties in accurately deploying technological innovation directions2. Among various text mining technologies, topic models are the most effective tool to mine implicit topic structures from massive unstructured academic texts, which can help solve the information overload problem.

Text mining technology offers an effective solution to this problem. Among its core tools, topic models can mine implicit topic structures from unstructured text3,4. For instance, Topaloğlu et al.5 applied the Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) model to conduct topic clustering on machine learning research in the intensive care unit field, accurately identifying core technical directions. Yu et al.3 systematically revealed the topic distribution and hot trends in the AI field based on the LDA model, providing references for research topic selection. However, most current bibliometric studies in the AI field have two limitations: first, over-reliance on a single static model (e.g., LDA) for hotspot identification, which fails to capture the temporal evolution laws of topics6,7, second, dynamic topic models (e.g., DTM) are prone to topic drift when used independently, leading to distorted evolution trajectory analysis8,9. Therefore, constructing a combined model with both “static recognition accuracy” and “dynamic tracking capability” to conduct dynamic topic evolution analysis of English academic papers in the AI field is of great theoretical significance and practical value for revealing the development trends and iteration directions of domain topics10,11.

To address the key gaps in current bibliometric research on the AI field, this study focuses on three core objectives: (1) identifying the core research topics in the AI field over the past decade and their static clustering characteristics; (2) exploring the dynamic evolution laws of these core topics, including the classification and characteristics of emerging, declining, and continuously active topics; and (3) clarifying the coupling characteristics between topics, as well as the derivative, fusion, or substitution relationships that occur during their evolution.

To achieve these goals, this study proposes three key innovations: first, methodologically, we propose a combined framework of Temporal Adaptive Dictionary-Constrained LDA + Bi-calibrated Dual Granularity DTM. By introducing a domain-specific temporal adaptive dictionary to constrain LDA topic initialization and using its output as prior knowledge for DTM, we effectively solve the problems of topic drift and static limitations of single models, and improve the domain adaptability of the model. Second, in terms of temporal granularity design, we optimize the traditional single annual granularity into an annual + quarterly dual granularity with a bi-calibration mechanism, which enhances the capture sensitivity of short-term emerging topics and solves the problem of rough temporal granularity in traditional dynamic models. Third, in terms of analytical dimensions, we introduce topic coupling degree analysis based on Pearson correlation coefficient on the basis of topic recognition and evolution tracking, which reveals the association evolution laws between topics and breaks the limitation of single topic tracking in existing studies2,9.

Related works

Application of topic models in bibliometrics

Topic models are core technologies in text mining, which capture the implicit topic structure of text collections through probabilistic generative models and have been widely applied in academic paper analysis across various fields. Among them, LDA, as a classic static topic model, has become a common tool in bibliometrics due to its unsupervised topic clustering capability: Topaloğlu et al.5 used LDA to perform topic clustering on machine learning research in the intensive care unit field, identifying six core research directions and verifying the effectiveness of LDA in topic recognition for specialized fields. Lee et al.6 applied LDA to analyze research hotspots in the field of soccer psychology, verifying the interpretability of model output through topic coherence indicators. Yu et al.3 systematically analyzed AI literature using LDA, revealing the topic distribution characteristics and core research directions of the field, Nguyen et al.7 compared the performance of different LDA variants in bibliometric analysis, proposing that the topic recognition accuracy of LDA depends on domain adaptation and parameter optimization, Yang et al.12 proposed the Coherence-Guided Steerable LDA model, further improving the semantic clarity of static topics through coherence-guided optimization. However, the static nature of LDA prevents it from capturing the temporal dynamic changes of topics, making it difficult to meet the needs of long-term temporal evolution analysis7,9.

To address the challenge of temporal analysis, the Dynamic Topic Model (DTM) emerged. Its core advantage lies in tracking topic evolution trajectories by assuming that the topic distribution changes smoothly over time. In recent years, DTM has been widely used in various fields: Gao et al.8 conducted semantic-enhanced topic evolution analysis by combining DTM with Word2Vec, supplementing semantic information through word vectors and improving the continuity and interpretability of evolution trajectories. Wang et al.13 analyzed the hotspot evolution of Chinese energy policy texts based on DTM, revealing the temporal migration characteristics of policy topics. Jiang et al.14 applied DTM to dynamic topic modeling of 6G technology patents, accurately capturing the evolution path of emerging technology directions. Zhou et al.15 proposed a sparse dynamic topic model with topic birth and death mechanisms, optimizing the stability of topic evolution in long-term temporal analysis by introducing topic birth and death probabilities. Chen et al.16 developed a dynamic topic model based on dependent hierarchical normalized random measures, further enhancing the topic tracking capability in complex scenarios. Nevertheless, DTM has obvious limitations when used independently: it is prone to topic drift in long-term temporal analysis, leading to topic splitting or fusion distortion, and its static topic recognition accuracy is lower than that of the LDA model9,16.

In addition, other topic modeling technologies have also been applied in bibliometrics: Karabacak et al.17 combined natural language processing with topic modeling to analyze the two-decade research trends of The Spine Journal, verifying the value of topic models in long-term literature analysis. Kukushkin et al.18 conducted a systematic literature review in the field of digital twins based on topic modeling, revealing the research structure of the field, Inaam ul haq et al.9 compared the performance of static and dynamic probabilistic topic models in social media research, confirming that dynamic models are more suitable for capturing short-term hotspot migration.

Research status of combined models

To balance static topic recognition accuracy and dynamic evolution tracking capability, some studies have attempted to construct combined topic model frameworks. For example, Nguyen et al.7 realized the combination of static topic recognition and hotspot trend analysis by optimizing LDA parameters and preprocessing processes, combined with bibliometric indicators, Chen et al.4 integrated LDA with multimodal data fusion technology to analyze the research trends of AI in smart healthcare, improving the comprehensiveness of topic recognition through multimodal information supplementation, the sparse dynamic topic model proposed by Zhou et al.15 optimized the stability of DTM topic evolution by introducing sparse constraint mechanisms, but did not incorporate domain prior knowledge to constrain topic initialization. Hu et al.19 used topic models to analyze the lag characteristics of topic evolution between preprints and publications, verifying the temporal sensitivity of dynamic models through dual dataset comparison. Wu et al.20 proposed a Prompt-optimized self-supervised dual-tower contextual topic model, enhancing the adaptability of topic models to domain semantics by combining prompt learning and self-supervised learning.

Other studies have focused on model optimization and cross-domain applications: Dib et al.21 combined LDA with LSTM for follower recommendation in Twitter networks, thereby verifying the feasibility of integrating topic models with deep learning techniques. Ho et al.22 proposed a dynamic social network analysis method based on agent-based models, author-topic models, and pretopology, which expands the application scope of topic models in network analysis. Qi et al.23 provided mathematical support for the temporal optimization of dynamic models by integrating stochastic hybrid logistic models with delay perturbation analysis. Costa24 confirmed the application value of dynamic models in water environment research through practical case studies. Ahn et al.25 integrated digital twins with topic modeling to analyze digitalization trends in the fields of geology and deep underground research, achieving innovative cross-domain applications of topic models. However, existing combined models still suffer from three core deficiencies: first, insufficient adaptability to the characteristics of the AI field, with topic initialization relying on random processes7,8, second, a single temporal granularity design—mostly adopting annual granularity—which makes it difficult to capture the explosive growth of emerging topics14,20, third, the failure to systematically analyze the associative evolution between topics, as they only focus on the temporal changes of individual topics2,9,26.

In recent years, with the rapid development of multimodal and generative AI, topic modeling has been increasingly combined with advanced representation learning to explore emerging cross-disciplinary applications. Recent studies have applied topic modeling to analyze multimodal and generative AI in educational scenarios, clarifying field development trends27. In recommendation systems, mask diffusion-based contrastive learning has been integrated to enhance semantic representation ability28. Meanwhile, topic modeling has also been adopted in bibliometric and technology mapping analysis for emerging fields, expanding its practical application scenarios29.These advances demonstrate the latest integration trends of topic modeling, yet most focus on application extensions rather than core framework optimization. Our work addresses this gap by improving the balance between static topic recognition and dynamic evolution tracking.

This study presents an integrated framework termed “Temporal Adaptive Dictionary-Constrained LDA + Bi-calibrated Dual Granularity DTM”, which addresses the aforementioned research gaps via domain adaptation optimization, dual temporal granularity design, and coupling degree analysis. In contrast to existing combined models (e.g7,15, that simply fuse static and dynamic models without domain-adaptive constraints, the proposed framework introduces a temporal adaptive domain dictionary to regularize LDA topic initialization and dynamic evolution. This solves the problem of ambiguous topic semantics caused by random initialization, which remains unsolved in previous methods. Unlike traditional static-dictionary-constrained LDA, the proposed dictionary incorporates domain relevance and temporal timeliness for dynamic adaptation to topic evolution.

Compared with earlier dynamic models lacking prior guidance or using fixed prior weights (e.g8,20, this work designs an adaptive prior fine-tuning mechanism for DTM. It dynamically adjusts the prior weights derived from LDA according to the topic drift rate, balancing stability and flexibility and alleviating topic solidification.

Different from single-granularity dynamic models (e.g13,14, this study proposes a bi-calibrated dual temporal granularity structure, which improves sensitivity to short-term emerging topics while avoiding trajectory distortion. These interrelated innovations form a complete framework that overcomes the core limitations of existing methods.

To capture both long-term evolution trends and short-term outbreak characteristics of topics, we design an annual - quarterly dual temporal granularity structure, and propose a bi-calibration mechanism with complete theoretical justification based on time series bias-variance decomposition theory and DTMs Markov smoothness assumption.

Indicator calculation methods

Perplexity Calculation: Implemented using the gensim.models.ldamodel.LdaModel.log_perplexity() function, which calculates the average log probability of the test set documents and then converts it to perplexity using the formula in Formula (15).

Topic Drift Rate Calculation: First, calculate the cosine similarity between the word distribution vectors of the same topic at adjacent time nodes using the sklearn.metrics.pairwise.cosine_similarity() function, then calculate the drift rate using the formula in Formula (17).

Topic Coupling Degree Calculation: First construct the topic strength sequence for each topic (based on Formula (18)), then calculate the Pearson correlation coefficient between each pair of sequences using the scipy.stats.pearsonr() function, which is the topic coupling degree.

Results and analysis

Research data

To ensure the representativeness and rigor of the research data, this study selects English academic papers in the AI field as the research object, with data sourced from the Web of Science (WOS) Core Collection—the most authoritative academic literature database globally, covering high-quality journals and conference proceedings in natural sciences, engineering technology, and other fields. The retrieval strategy is designed to comprehensively cover the core research scope of AI:

Detailed Retrieval Web of Science Core Collection Search Parameters are shown below:

WOS Core Collection Sub-databases: SCI-EXPANDED and CPCI-S, covering all core journals and conference proceedings in the AI field.

Topic Search Logic: The topic search uses the “OR” logic to ensure comprehensive coverage of the AI field, and the exact search format is: TS = (“artificial intelligence” OR “machine learning” OR “deep learning” OR “natural language processing” OR “computer vision”).

Document Type Restriction: Only Article and Review are selected to ensure the academic quality and theoretical depth of the literature; other document types (e.g., Editorial Material) are excluded.

Language Restriction: Only English documents are selected to avoid the impact of language differences on text mining results.

Data Cleaning Process: After retrieving the initial dataset, we perform three rounds of data cleaning to ensure data quality: (1) Remove duplicate documents (identified by DOI, title, and author combination). (2) Remove documents with incomplete information. (3) Remove documents that are not closely related to the AI field (evaluated by whether the abstract contains at least 2 core AI terms from the temporal adaptive dictionary).

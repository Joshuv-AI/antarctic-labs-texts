Comparison of efficacy and safety in two versus three cycles of neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Guanzhi Ye, Gaojian Pan, Xiaolei Zhu, Hongming Liu, Guojun Geng, Jie Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis research aimed to compare the efficacy and safety in two versus three cycles of neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.Materials and methodsPatients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma receiving neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy followed by esophagectomy from December 2020 to May 2024 were retrospectively analyzed. The therapeutic efficacy, adverse reactions, and postoperative complications between the two- and three-cycle groups were analyzed using chi-square test and independent sample t-test. The overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method with log-rank test.ResultsA total of 138 esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients receiving neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy were enrolled. The R0 resection rates were 94.5% and 95.7% in the two- and three-cycle groups, respectively (P = 0.554). The pathological complete response and major pathological response rates in the two-cycle group were 19.8% and 40.7%, while they were 21.3% and 38.3% in the three-cycle group, respectively, with no significant differences (P = 0.836 and P = 0.788). More anemia cases appeared in the three-cycle group (27.7% vs. 13.2%, P = 0.036). There were no significant differences between the two- and three-cycle groups in terms of anastomotic fistula, pulmonary infection, chylothorax, and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. Additionally, The 3-year OS rates of patients in the two- and three-cycle groups were 80.1% and 74.5%, respectively, without significant difference (P = 0.421). The 3-year PFS rates in the two- and three-cycle groups were 62.5% and 65.8%, respectively, without significant difference either (P = 0.966).ConclusionsTwo-cycle neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum based chemotherapy can be an effective, safe, and optional neoadjuvant strategy for esophageal squmous cell carcinoma patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-025-03587-4.Keywords: Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, Neoadjuvant therapy, Immunotherapy, Chemotherapy

Introduction

Esophageal carcinoma is a highly invasive malignant tumor with high morbidity and mortality worldwide [1]. The most prevalent pathological type is esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [2], and surgery remains an important means for cancer treatment. Unfortunately, due to the lack of clear early signs, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma is frequently discovered in the late or locally advanced stages of the tumor, resulting in the loss of surgical opportunity or unsatisfactory improvement in postoperative survival prognosis. For patients with locally advanced esophageal carcinoma, neoadjuvant therapy combined with esophagectomy has been established as the standard treatment strategy [3, 4]. Many literatures also pointed out that neoadjuvant therapy can downstage cancer, control tumor micrometastasis, increase the R0 resection rate, and improve survival prognosis [5–8]. Therefore, it is crucial to investigate appropriate neoadjuvant therapy approaches for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.

In recent years, there have been various neoadjuvant treatment options for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, including chemoradiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, and so on. However, the optimal treatment plan is still controversial. Immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy has shown many advantages in neoadjuvant therapy for locally advanced tumor, and its safety and efficacy have been elucidated in many researches. Chen et al. [9] reported that neoadjuvant sintilimab combined with platinum-based chemotherapy achieved a pathological complete response (pCR) rate of 20.0% in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients. Liu et al. [10] revealed that the R0 resection rate and pCR rate reached 98.0% and 39.2% in patients treated with neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus chemotherapy, respectively. Furthermore, Yu et al. [11] revealed that neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy had a better survival prognosis than neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients. Among numerous immunochemotherapy protocols, immunotherapy combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy is a commonly used neoadjuvant regimen for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients [12, 13]. At present, there is no consensus on the optimal number of cycles for this neoadjuvant therapeutic strategy. Only limited research has focused on the selection of the number of cycles for neoadjuvant chemotherapy, but this issue has not been studied for neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy to date. Compared with a two-cycle regimen, it has not been fully understood whether additional cycle treatment can improve the response rate of tumor, affects the operation difficulty, improves survival prognosis, or increases the incidence of adverse reactions.

This study compared the efficacy and safety in two and three cycles of neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy, aiming to determine the optimal number of treatment cycles for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. This research helps provide guidance for optimizing neoadjuvant therapy plans to maximize the treatment effectiveness and survival prognosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.

Materials and methods

Patient population

This research included 138 esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients receiving neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy followed by esophagectomy in the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University between December 2020 and May 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients with pathological diagnosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; (b) patients receiving neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy followed by esophagectomy. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients with incomplete clinicopathological and follow-up data; (b) patients with a history of other malignant tumors; (c) patients receiving neoadjuvant anti-tumor treatment other than immunochemotherapy. The flowchart was shown in Fig. 1. Preoperative clinical staging was performed on all tumor patients using whole-body bone single-photon emission computed tomography, endoscopic ultrasonography, cranial enhanced magnetic resonance imaging, chest, neck and abdominal enhanced CT. Pathological biopsy was also conducted.

Flowchart for the study population

Neoadjuvant therapy regimen and surgical process

All patients received two or three cycles of neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy. The immunotherapy drugs included Carilizumab, Sintilimab, Pembrolizumab, Tislelizumab, Toripalimab, Nivolumab, and Penpulimab. The chemotherapy regimen was albumin-bound paclitaxel combined with platinum. The detailed neoadjuvant therapy protocols and drug dosage were shown in Table S1. All included tumor patients underwent esophagectomy via McKeown, Ivor Lewis or Sweet operation procedures with standard two-field lymphadenectomy.

Imaging and pathological evaluation of tumor

The enhanced CT scans were used for tumor imaging evaluation. The imaging efficacy evaluation was based on the criteria for Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1) [14]. The pathological staging assessment of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma was on the basis of the American Joint Committee on Cancer tumor-node-metastasis staging criteria (eighth edition) [15]. The tumor regression grade (TRG) evaluation was based on the TRG criteria of the College of American Pathologists. The pCR was defined as the absence of evidence of residual tumor cells. The major pathological response (MPR) was defined as residual tumor cells of less than 10%.

Safety evaluation of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy

In our research, the safety evaluation of neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy mainly focused on perioperative adverse reactions and postoperative complications. The former included anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, liver dysfunction, renal dysfunction, skin rash, and pneumonia, and the latter included anastomotic fistula, pulmonary infection, chylothorax, and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS). The definition of anastomotic fistula was the leakage of gastrointestinal contents into the chest, mediastinum, or subcutaneous tissue due to poor healing at the site of esophageal and gastric anastomosis. The definition of postoperative pulmonary infection was newly developed pulmonary infectious lesions within 30 days after esophagectomy. Chylothorax was defined as the abnormal accumulation of lymphatic fluid rich in chylomicrons and triglycerides in the chest cavity. Additonally, MODS was defined as a clinical syndrome in which the body experiences two or more organ or system dysfunction simultaneously or sequentially after suffering from acute damage such as severe infection, trauma, shock and surgery.

Patients follow-up

We conducted follow-up assessments every three months for the first two years after esophagectomy and every six months thereafter. We collected survival information of patients through outpatient visits or telephone interviews. The follow-up endpoints were the date of death or May 15, 2025.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted through SPSS 26.0 statistical software (SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL, USA) and R version 4.1.0. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s precision probability test. Continuous data were presented as means ± standard deviation and compared using the independent sample t-test. The overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were analyzed through the Kaplan-Meier method with the log-rank test. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparison of therapeutic efficacy between two and three cycles of neoadjuvant therapy

As shown in Table 2, based on the imaging evaluation, 57 cases (62.6%) and 32 cases (68.1%) achieved partial response of tumor after two and three cycles of neoadjuvant therapy, respectively, with no statistically significant difference (P = 0.526). Based on the pathological evaluation, the pCR and MPR rates in the two-cycle group were 19.8% and 40.7%, respectively. The pCR and MPR rates in the three-cycle group were 21.3% and 38.3%, respectively. There were no statistically significant differences between the two cohorts (P = 0.836 and P = 0.788).

Comparison of adverse reactions and postoperative complications between two and three cycles of neoadjuvant therapy

As shown in Table 3, compared with the two-cycle group, the three-cycle neoadjuvant therapy regimen resulted in more cases of anemia (13.2% vs. 27.7%, P = 0.036). Comparison of surgical difficulty and postoperative recovery time between two and three cycles of neoadjuvant therapy

As shown in Table 5, the three-cycle group appeared to have more cases with an interval from last neoadjuvant therapy to surgery longer than 6 weeks compared to the two-cycle group (34.0% vs. 16.5%, P = 0.019). The interval from last neoadjuvant therapy to surgery was 5.21 ± 1.61 weeks (range: 2.14–11.71 weeks) and 5.80 ± 1.79 (range: 3.14–11.57 weeks) in the two- and three-cycle groups, respectively. There were no significant statistical differences between the two cohorts in terms of operation time (P = 0.492), intraoperative blood loss (P = 0.858), and postoperative hospital stay (P = 0.881). Meanwhile, there were also no significant differences between the two cohorts in terms of the number of resected lymph nodes (P = 0.199) and the R0 resection rate (P = 0.554).

Survival analysis between two and three cycles of neoadjuvant therapy

The median follow-up time for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients in the research was 26 months (interquartile range: 17–38 months). As shown in Fig. 2A, the 3-year OS rates of tumor patients in the two- and three-cycle groups were 80.1% and 74.5%, respectively, without significant statistical difference (P = 0.421). In addition, the 3-year PFS rates of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients in the two- and three-cycle groups were 62.5% and 65.8%, respectively, without significant statistical difference either (P = 0.966) (Fig. 2B).

Discussion

Surgery is the main therapeutic method for resectable esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, but the treatment effect of surgery alone is barely satisfactory. Many patients experience tumor metastasis or recurrence within three years after surgery, with low survival rates [16, 17]. Clinical researches have reported that postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy has limited help in improving survival prognosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [18]. Whereas, neoadjuvant therapy has been proven to degrade tumor staging, increase surgical complete resection rate, and improve survival prognosis [4], which is currently the focus of relevant researches. The guidelines also recommend neoadjuvant chemotherapy or neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by esophagectomy as the standard treatment for locally advanced esophageal carcinoma. Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy and neoadjuvant chemotherapy have similar effects in improving overall survival of tumor patients [19, 20]. Moreover, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has been reported to increase surgical difficulty and the incidence of postoperative complications [21], leading to unsatisfactory patient compliance. Immunotherapy, which activates the immune system to suppress tumors, has gained much attention in recent years. Immunotherapy represented by PD − 1/PD - L1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy has emerged as a promising neoadjuvant therapeutic strategy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.

Immunotherapy combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy is a commonly used neoadjuvant therapy regimen for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, and many clinical researches have shown its safety and efficacy in tumor treatment. Yan et al. [13] reported that pemrolizumab combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and nedaplatin is a safe and effective therapeutic regimen for advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, which achieved an objective response rate of 68.1% and a disease control rate of 100%. Yang et al. [22] pointed out that compared with paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors, albumin-bound paclitaxel plus platinum-based chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy achieved a better pCR rate of 36.7% in resectable esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Zhang et al. [23] also revealed that neoadjuvant sintilimab plus albumin-bound paclitaxel and cisplatin for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma can be a safe and feasible regimen with high R0 resection rate, promising pCR rate, limited adverse reactions, and manageable postoperative complications. Currently, 2–4 treatment cycles have been usually applied for neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, but there is no consensus on the optimal number of treatment cycles. In addition, there are no reports on the optimal number of cycles for immunotherapy combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy regimen. This research aimed to compare the safety and efficacy in two versus three cycles of neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy in the treatment of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, to help determine appropriate therapeutic strategies.

Compared to the two-cycle regimen, the three-cycle regimen theoretically increases objective response rate, pCR rate, and R0 resection rate to a certain extent. For instance, the three-cycle neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy regimen was reported to achieve better pCR rate and objective response rate compared by the two-cycle regimen in resectable non-small-cell lung cancer [24]. However, Makino et al. [25] revealed that there were no significant differences in R0 resection rate, pathological response, survival rate and tumor recurrence pattern between the two- and three-cycle groups of neoadjuvant chemotherapy in locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. In this research, we found that two and three cycles of neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy achieved satisfactory imaging and pathological responses, but the objective response, pCR, and MPR rates of both regimens are similar without significant differences. Our results were consistent with Makino’s findings on neoadjuvant chemotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. In addition, in terms of operation time, blood loss, and number of lymph node resection, the additional treatment cycle did not affect the surgical difficulties, but it did not increase the R0 resection rate either in our study. However, it is still unclear whether additional two or more cycles of immunochemotherapy can improve pathological responses and which populations can benefit from more cycles of treatment. Further studies are needed to explore these issues.

Increasing the number of neoadjuvant treatment cycles may potentially increase the incidence of perioperative adverse events. In our research, the three-cycle regimen resulted in more cases of anemia, leukopenia, liver dysfunction, kidney dysfunction, and pneumonia compared to the two-cycle regimen, despite only the difference in the incidence of anemia was statistically significant. However, this also suggests that additional treatment cycles may increase the incidence of adverse reactions. Shiraishi et al. [26] reported that compared with the two-cycle regimen of neoadjuvant chemotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, the incidences of grade 3–4 leukopenia and anemia were higher in patients receiving the three-cycle regimen. Additionally, in a study involving four cycles of neoadjuvnat camrelizumab combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and carboplatin for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, the proportion of patients with leukopenia and neutropenia was as high as 65.7% and 34.3%, respectively [27]. Therefore, we believe that increasing the number of cycles of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy may potentially increase the risk of perioperative adverse events in tumor patients. Currently, it has been generally recommended to complete esophagectomy within 4–6 weeks after the last neoadjuvant therapy. Yang et al. [28] also revealed that a time interval between neoadjuvant immunotherapy and surgery exceeding 6 weeks did not improve pathological remission but instead increased the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma recurrence. Our research found that patients in the three-cycle regimen group experienced more delayed surgery with time interval to surgery longer than 6 weeks. One possible reason may be that patients with three-cycle regimen experienced more adverse reactions. In our research, we observed that the three-cycle regimen group had more anemia, leukopenia, liver dysfunction, renal dysfunction, and pneumonia than the two-cycle group, despite only anemia reached statistical differences. Another reason for delaying surgery may be that additional cycles of treatment led to a higher grading of adverse events, which requires further research to explore. In addition, the incidence rate of postoperative complications in our study was not high, which was consistent with previous reports [29, 30]. We also found no significant differences in surgical complications such as anastomotic fistula, pulmonary infection, chylothorax, and MODS between the two- and three-cycle groups. Therefore, we believed that the two-cycle regimen of immunotherapy combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy is a safer neoadjuvant therapeutic strategy.

In our research, the median OS of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients were 26 months. The 3-year OS rate and 3-year PFS rate reached 78.0% and 63.1%, respectively. Thus, the combination of neoadjuvant therapy with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy is an effective neoadjuvant therapeutic regimen. Additionally, we also found no significant differences in OS and PFS between patients in the two- and three-cycle groups. Makino et al. [25] also reported that the survival rates and tumor recurrence patterns of two- and three-cycle neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens were comparable. Although the clinicopathological baselines and neoadjuvant treatment regimens of tumor patients vary among different studies, it also suggests that the three-cycle regimen may not benefit patients in survival prognosis compared with the two-cycle regimen.

This was the first research to compare the efficacy, safety, and survival prognosis between different cycle numbers of neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, which may help determine the optimal neoadjuvant therapeutic strategies. However, our research also has some limitations. Firstly, retrospective studies inevitably have biases. Secondly, our research only included 138 cases, and the insufficient sample size may lead to a decrease in statistical power. Thirdly, our research did not grade the severity of adverse reactions, which needs further investigation in future studies with larger sample sizes. Fourthly, in our study, there were seven different neoadjuvant immunotherapy drugs, which may potentially affect the comparison results between the two- and three-cycle groups. Finally, the follow-up time of tumor patients was relatively short, and further research is needed to assess long-term survival outcomes.

Conclusion

There were no significant differences in pCR and MPR rates, and incidence rate of postoperative complications between the two- and three-cycle regimens of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy. The adverse reactions in the two-cycle regimen were potentially less than those in the three-cycle regimen. Additionally, there were no significant differences in OS and PFS between the two cohorts. Two-cycle neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum-based chemotherapy can be an effective, safe, and optional neoadjuvant therapeutic strategy for locally advanced esophageal squmous cell carcinoma patients.

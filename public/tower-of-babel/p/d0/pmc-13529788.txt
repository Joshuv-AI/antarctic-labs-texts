Genome-wide association identifies and validates genomic region controlling grain yield and agronomic traits in extra-early orange maize inbred lines under drought
Authors: Tégawendé Odette Bonkoungou, Idris Ishola Adejumobi, Victor Olawale Adetimirin, Baffour Badu-Apraku, Paterne Angelot Agre, Kiswendsida Romaric Nanema, Adamu Masari Abubakar, Wende Mengesha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to meet the expected maize yield by 2050, breeders must work to improve breeding program efficiency by intensifying the implementation of new and improved technologies such as marker-assisted selection (MAS). Dissecting the genomic regions associated with drought tolerance is the first step forward in MAS program deployment for maize improvement under drought stress. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) were used to investigate and identify quantitative trait loci (QTLs) associated with six traits under drought stress. One hundred and eighty-seven extra-early orange maize inbred lines were evaluated under managed drought stress at Ikenne, in Nigeria, during the 2022 and 2023 dry seasons. The materials were also genotyped using 9355 DArTseq SNP markers and analyzed using the enriched compressed mixed linear model (ECMLM). Enriched compressed mixed linear model was used for association-trait analysis. The ECMLM-based GWAS identified 45 candidate genomic loci associated with the six traits, including five for grain yield, with R2 ranging from 8.79 to 25.3%. Independent validation using the multi-locus 3VmrMLM approach confirmed seven high-confidence genomic loci consistently detected by both methods across grain yield, anthesis-silking interval, ear aspect, and ears per plant, providing additional statistical support for these genomic regions. Candidate gene annotation identified biologically relevant genes underlying the validated loci, including Zm00001eb238250 (protein-serine/threonine phosphatase), Zm00001eb040940 (trehalose-phosphatase), Zm00001eb117820 (homeobox protein knotted-1-like 4), Zm00001eb145560 (zinc ion-binding protein), and Zm00001eb294180 (WRKY DNA-binding domain protein), suggesting their potential roles in drought adaptation and grain productivity. These findings improve our understanding of the genetic architecture of drought tolerance in extra-early orange maize and provide valuable genomic resources for accelerating drought-resilient maize breeding.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-62540-0.Keywords: Drought, GWAS, SNPs, Marker segregation, PredictionSubject terms: Biotechnology, Genetics, Plant sciences

Introduction

Maize (Zea mays L.) is a staple food crop for humans and other animals, and serves as raw material for the production of starch, oil, protein, food sweeteners, and alcohol1. Maize production is, however, affected by several factors, of which drought, a product of climate change, is one of the major ones. Around 80% of wet-season maize is produced in rain-fed conditions, and dry-season maize suffers from severe water shortage2. Maize yield losses from drought range from 30 to 90%, depending on crop stage and the severity or duration. The most sensitive stage is the flowering and grain filling stages3. In Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), the majority of the countries grow maize under rain-fed conditions with 300–500 mm of precipitation, which is less than what is required to produce a reasonable yield3. Climate change will lead to more frequent and unpredictable weather conditions in the future, requiring the deployment of sustainable strategies4.

Previous studies have predicted that the global average temperature will increase and likely surpass by 2 °C under the current high emission scenario by 2050. Consequently, additional maize yield loss of 10 million tons per year could occur due to temperature increase and variation in rainfall patterns5. Therefore, drought-tolerant maize is crucial to maintain sustainable and abundant maize production. The genetic mechanisms behind drought tolerance are extremely intricate and complex, and information about genotype, environment, and genotype by environment (G × E) interactions is crucial for effective genetic improvement programs for drought tolerance6,7. In addition to drought, extra-early orange maize represents a strategically important germplasm for maize improvement in West Africa. The extra-early maturity group is specifically adapted to the short and erratic rainfall patterns typical of the savanna agro-ecologies, allowing crops to escape terminal drought stress and stabilize yields under rainfed conditions. Furthermore, the orange kernel color is associated with elevated levels of provitamin A carotenoids, making these genotypes valuable for addressing vitamin A deficiency, which remains a major public health concern in sub-Saharan Africa. Consequently, extra-early orange maize lines developed by IITA combine both climate resilience and nutritional enhancement, making them priority genetic resources for improving food security and nutritional quality in drought-prone environments of West Africa.

Stabilizing maize production, particularly in drought-prone regions, involves a thorough understanding of genetics and its application to enhance tolerance to drought in maize. Among the cutting-edge molecular breeding approaches, marker-assisted selection (MAS) could offer an opportunity to increase maize yield under drought stress4. To deploy MAS, the identification of functional genes or markers related to drought-adaptive traits is an important step. Several studies involving dissection of drought tolerance using association mapping have been reported in maize1,8–12. These studies have reported several genomic regions/genes controlling the expression of metabolites that condition drought tolerance in maize. Of the reported genes, the gene GRMZM2G125777 was shown to be substantially linked to ear relative position, hundred-kernel weight, and the days to male and female flowering dates. Wang et al. 10detected 115 candidate genes for drought tolerance and related traits such as grain yield and yield-related variables. Additionally, 108 and 120 SNPs robustly associated with flowering time and plant height, respectively, were identified under moisture stress in 15 bi-parental populations of maize11. However, because of the complexity and the polygenic nature of drought tolerance, important and novel QTLs are still under discovery. In West Africa where drought is main factor limiting maize production, few studies have been done on QTLs identification for drought adaptive trait on maize using a panel of extra-early orange maize inbred lines. Osuman et al.13 identified four SNPs (SNP_138825271 (Chr03), SNP_244895453 (Chr04), SNP_168561609 (Chr05), and SNP_62970998 (Chr06)) associated with days to anthesis and silking under terminal drought in 162 tropical maize inbred lines using associating mapping approach. The identification of SNP markers associated with drought-related traits in extra-early orange maize would facilitate the rapid selection of extra-early orange maize genotypes through the application of MAS in maize breeding research in SSA. The objectives of this study were to: (i) identify genomic regions associated with grain yield and other drought adaptive traits under managed drought, ii) identify candidate genes associated with grain yield and other drought adaptive traits under managed drought, and iii) predict marker performance for marker-assisted selection.

Identification of genomic region linked to economically important traits under managed drought

Genome-wide association analysis using the ECMLM model identified 45 putative quantitative trait nucleotides (QTNs) associated with six drought-related traits. These loci were distributed across all maize chromosomes except chromosome 9 and individually explained 8.79–25.3% of the phenotypic variation. Since no loci remained significant following Benjamini-Hochberg correction, the ECMLM results were considered discovery-stage associations and were subsequently evaluated using the independent multi-locus 3VmrMLM approach for validation. The genomic inflation factor (λ) of the putative markers ranged from 0.92 to 1.08 across the six traits, indicating adequate control of population structure and minimal inflation for these traits (Table 2). The corresponding QQ plots showed that observed −log10(p) values closely followed the expected null distribution, with deviation occurring only at the extreme tail representing true marker–trait associations (Figs. 1 and 2).

Grain yield (GY) was associated with five SNPs located on chromosomes 4, 5, and 6, explaining an average phenotypic variance (R²) of 24% (Fig. 1 and Table S2). Eleven SNP markers located on chromosomes 1, 3, 4, 5, and 10 were associated with anthesis–silking interval (ASI), explaining an average R² of 13%. Twelve SNPs distributed across chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10 were associated with stay-green characteristic (STGC), with an average R² of 24%. Six QTLs located on chromosomes 2, 7, 8, and 10 were associated with ears per plant (EPP), explaining an average R² of 14%. Seven QTLs located on chromosomes 2, 3, 5, 6, and 10 were associated with ear aspect (EASP), with an average R² of 21%. Four QTLs located on chromosomes 2, 6, and 10 were identified for plant aspect (PASP), explaining an average R² of 9% (Fig. 2 and Table S2).

To evaluate the robustness of the ECMLM results, all six traits were re-analyzed using the multi-locus 3VmrMLM model. The analysis identified 35 QTNs across the six traits, comparable to the number detected using the ECMLM approach. Importantly, seven loci were consistently identified by both methods, including loci associated with grain yield (chr5_144347373), anthesis-silking interval (chr1_215570212 and chr4_32082491), ear aspect (chr3_175311017 and chr6_173060588), and ears per plant (chr2_241668047 and chr8_106963107). These concordant loci represent high-confidence genomic regions underlying the traits investigated. The multi-locus model additionally identified several novel loci that were not detected by ECMLM, highlighting the complementary nature of the two GWAS approaches (Table 3).

Gene annotation for grain yield

Candidate gene annotation was performed for genomic regions surrounding the ECMLM loci, with particular emphasis placed on loci independently supported by both ECMLM and 3VmrMLM analyses. Six candidate genes were found in the vicinity of the markers identified for grain yield (Fig. 3 and Table S3). The marker chr4_23478692 on chromosome 4 was found near the candidate gene Zm00001eb170150 encoding a bidirectional sugar transporter. The markers chr5_144347373 on chromosome 5 and chr6_ 27522345 on chromosome 6 were found around the vicinity of Zm00001eb238250 and Zm00001eb264360. These genes encode protein-serine/threonine phosphatase. The SNP marker chr6_ 173060588 located on chromosome 6 was located around the region of the genes Zm00001eb294180, Zm00001eb294200 and Zm00001eb294240, encoding putative WRKY DNA-binding domain superfamily protein, protein-serine/threonine phosphatase and ADP/ATP carrier, respectively. To functionally support the grain yield-associated loci, in silico transcriptomic validation performed using publicly available RNA-seq data from maize crown roots exposed to drought stress (GSE282587) revealed the regulation pattern of the identified genes. Of the six candidate genes identified near putative SNPs, several exhibited putative differential expression under drought conditions (FDR < 0.05). Notably, Zm00001eb170150, encoding a bidirectional sugar transporter, was significantly downregulated under drought stress (log2FC = -0.93, FDR < 0.001). Additionally, stress-related regulatory genes including (Zm00001eb294200) showed significant upregulation under drought (log2FC = 1.23, FDR < 0.001) (Table 4).

Marker substitution effect for grain yield

Of the five SNP markers associated with grain yield with identified putative genes, three SNP markers, viz. chr5_144347373, chr6_25354087, and chr6_27522345 displayed significant allele substitution effects among the different variants. For the SNP markers chr5_144347373 and chr6_27522345 located on chromosomes 5 and 6, respectively, allele C was the favorable allele corresponding to high grain yield, while alleles G and T were non-favorable alleles corresponding to low grain yield. The homozygous CC and heterozygous CG haplotypes resulting from SNP marker chr5_144347373 and the homozygous CC haplotype resulting from chr6_27522345 showed significantly increased grain yield. The homozygous GG haplotype from chr5_144347373, the homozygous TT and heterozygous TC haplotypes from chr6_27522345 conferred significantly low grain yield (Fig. 9A and B). The SNP marker chr6_25354087 identified allele G to be favorable and allele A as non-favorable. Homozygous GG haplotype significantly increased grain yield while homozygous AA and heterozygous AG haplotypes significantly reduced grain yield (Fig. 9C).

Discussion

This study combined single-locus (ECMLM) and multi-locus (3VmrMLM) GWAS approaches to investigate the genetic architecture of drought-adaptive traits in extra-early orange maize. While ECMLM identified 45 putative QTNs, independent multi-locus analysis confirmed seven genomic regions, providing robust evidence for genomic loci associated with grain yield and key secondary traits under drought stress.

Phenotypic variation

This study explored the genomic regions associated with drought tolerance in extra-early orange maize inbred lines developed by IITA through genome-wide association studies (GWAS). The significant genotype means square observed in the combined analysis confirmed the presence of substantial genetic variability among the inbred lines, providing a strong foundation for QTL detection. The significant genotype × environment interactions detected for several traits further indicate that trait expression under drought conditions is influenced by environmental variation. By extracting combined best linear unbiased estimates (BLUEs) across environments, the GWAS captured stable average genetic effects while accounting for environmental heterogeneity. However, the presence of genotype-by-environment interaction suggests that some loci may exhibit environment-specific effects, highlighting the importance of multi-location validation before routine deployment in breeding programs.

Genome-wide association studies for measured traits

The GWAS approach allowed the dissection of 45 significant QTLs linked to Grain yield, anthesis-silking interval, stay green characteristic, ear aspect, plant aspect, and ear per plant under managed-drought conditions. The majority of the identified GWAS hits had MAF values greater than 10% and a moderate proportion of phenotypic variance, indicating their potential relevance for drought improvement in extra-early orange maize. The current genomic information opens the way for the deployment of genomic information for maize improvement under drought stress in extra-early orange maize. QTLs with high phenotypic variance explained similar to the range in this study under drought stress, were also identified in previous studies in maize14. Zhang et al.14 reported QTLs related to grain yield through several drought environments in chromosome 5 and 6 as in this study but none of those markers have the same position with those identified in the current study. This might be due to the fact that grain yield under drought condition is a complex and quantitative trait. The identified QTLs in the current study spanned across the maize genome except on chromosome 9. While no loci were identified on chromosome 9 in the present panel, this does not preclude the presence of drought-responsive genes on this chromosome. Similar to this study, Nikolic et al.15 identified 45 QTLs (five for yield and 40 for yield component) across all the chromosomes except on chromosome 9 under drought stress. Wang et al.10 also identified 27 QTLs associated with anthesis-silking interval, grain yield and number of ears per plot across all chromosomes except on chromosome 9 under drought stress. Marker chr2_70930341 is correlated to ear aspect and number of ears per plant, marker chr6_173060588 to grain yield and ear aspect, and marker chr10_125896828 to plant aspect and stay green characteristic indicating that those markers have pleiotropic effects. Such multi-trait associations may reflect shared physiological or developmental pathways underlying drought adaptation.

Identification of candidate genes linked to the expression of the measured traits

Although a ± 500 kb window was used to identify candidate genes around putative SNPs, the relatively large interval may include genes that are not causally associated with the trait. Therefore, further fine mapping and functional validation will be required to confirm the causal genes underlying the detected loci. Drought stress hinders plant growth and development. In response to drought stress, plants use various adaptive mechanisms such as epigenetic plasticity, regulation of transcription, metabolic reprogramming, and changes in gene expression16. Gene annotation revealed 64 candidate genes located near the 45 QTLs. The genes are involved in the regulation of transcription factors, gene expression, antioxidants, plant growth and development, programmed cell death, and nutrient transporters under drought stress conditions. The genes associated with grain yield play important roles in plant growth and development, including cell elongation, cell division, photo-morphogenesis, xylem differentiation, reproduction17, sugar transportation18, and regulation of drought stress19,20. In effect, Putative WRKY DNA-binding domain superfamily protein is a transcription factors playing crucial roles in diverse biological processes including abiotic stresses such as drought, nutrient deficiency, oxidative stress and regulating physiological processes20. Hu et al.21 reported that ZmWRKY genes are activated under salt and drought stress conditions, suggesting their potential involvement in abiotic stress responses in maize (Zea mays L.). The candidate genes identified for ears per plant encode protein families that play important roles in plant growth, development, and stress responses22. These proteins control essential processes like morphogenesis, floral organ size, flowering time, and fruit development23,24. They also contribute to enhanced adaptation under drought stress by regulating the signal transduction pathways and abscisic acid (ABA) biosynthesis25. For example, RING zinc finger proteins contribute to drought tolerance by modulating abscisic acid (ABA) biosynthesis and signal transduction pathways, which are crucial for plants to adapt to water stress conditions25.

Of the ten candidate genes identified for ears per plant, previous findings have reported four of them viz. GRMZM2G027683, GRMZM2G051571, GRMZM2G034985, and GRMZM2G324705 in maize tissues, including embryos, flowers, seeds, shoots, ears, tassels, cobs, silks, anthers, and ovules26. The potential candidate genes identified to be associated with ASI encode several proteins with essential roles. These include calcium signaling27,28, gametogenesis29, sugar metabolism, flowering regulation30,31 and cell division. The genes associated with markers linked to plant aspects encode six protein families. These protein families play crucial roles in development and stress responses, including ear development, lignin biosynthesis, cell wall development, and drought regulation32–34. The candidate genes identified for the ear aspect encode proteins potentially influencing the trait. These genes play essential roles in ear development35,36 and stress responses36. Of the 17 genes identified, the maize RING-H2 finger protein ZmXERICO, a homologue of the A. thaliana gene XERICO has been implicated in flowering and ear formation37. The candidate genes linked to the stay-green characteristic in the present study have been implicated in the encoding of proteins with diverse functions. These functions include regulation of various plant processes, such as stress responses38 and leaf development39, senescence process in plants, photosynthesis, chloroplast formation, and crop productivity40. The GRAS gene family dominates the majority of the identified gene for the stay-green characteristic. GRAS transcription factors are known to regulate stress-responsive gene networks involved in hormone signaling, particularly gibberellin and ABA pathways, which contribute to delayed senescence and improved drought tolerance. For instance, overexpression of Brassica napus gene BnLAS in Arabidopsis showed an increase in chlorophyll content, stomatal density, decreased water loss, delays in leaf senescence, and enhanced tolerance to drought38.

Integration of GWAS and transcriptomic evidence

The integration of GWAS-derived loci with independent transcriptomic data provided additional biological context for the identified genomic regions. A subset of candidate genes across multiple traits exhibited significant drought-responsive expression patterns, suggesting that some of the detected associations reflect transcriptionally regulated stress-adaptive mechanisms, consistent with integrative genomics frameworks that combine association mapping with functional evidence41,42. In particular, the drought-induced modulation of genes involved in sugar transport, regulatory phosphatases, and transcription factors supports the notion that carbon allocation and stress signaling pathways contribute to yield maintenance under water-limited conditions43,44.

The downregulation of the bidirectional sugar transporter gene associated with grain yield may reflect altered carbohydrate partitioning under drought stress, a process known to influence reproductive development and kernel set44. Similarly, the observed regulation of signaling-related genes, including phosphatases and transcriptional regulators such as WRKY family members, is consistent with their established roles in mediating stress-responsive pathways and hormonal cross-talk under abiotic stress conditions45. In contrast, only a subset of ASI, stay-green, and ears-per-plant associated loci exhibited differential expression in crown root tissue, while others showed no significant transcriptional changes. This observation may indicate tissue-specific regulation, developmental-stage dependency, or post-transcriptional mechanisms underlying these traits, as gene expression in maize is known to vary substantially across tissues and developmental stages46. Anthesis–silking interval and stay-green characteristics are largely reproductive and canopy-level traits, and their genetic regulation may not be fully captured in root transcriptomic profiles obtained under controlled growth conditions.

The absence of detectable expression changes for some candidate genes does not negate their functional relevance, as GWAS signals may reflect structural variation, protein function alteration, or regulatory variation acting in other tissues or developmental windows41. Nonetheless, the convergence of association mapping and independent drought-responsive expression for several prioritized genes strengthens confidence in the biological relevance of these loci and supports their candidacy for further functional validation.

Marker effect prediction for the observed traits

Twenty SNP-trait markers displayed significant marker allele substitution effects, with each of the markers showing favorable and non-favorable alleles. Allele C was revealed as a favorable allele for increased grain yield under drought stress, while alleles A and C were indicated to decrease the anthesis-silking interval. Alleles C and G were found to be responsible for low score of plant aspect and allele T was responsible for the low score of ear aspect. The alleles T and G were indicated as favorable alleles for an increased number of ears under drought stress, and alleles G and C were responsible for a good stay-green characteristic under drought stress.

All the markers with significant marker allele substitution effect were able to predict the performance of the traits under drought stress with significant and positive correlations found between the predicted and the observed values. Though there was a positive and significant correlation, the magnitude of the correlation estimate for each marker effect prediction varied from low (< 0.3) to a moderate range (between 0.3 and 0.6) according to Cohen (1988). Overall, 11 markers linked to five of the six measured traits (excluding plant aspect) showed promise for MAS from their moderate estimate of correlation (> 0.3). These include two markers linked to ear aspect, three linked to ear per plant, one linked to ASI, and two linked to grain yield. The moderate positive correlations obtained from these markers suggest that they have a reasonable potential for MAS. Selecting individuals based on the favorable alleles of these markers is likely to lead to a noticeable improvement in the grain yield and other drought-adaptive traits in the desired direction, although the improvement may not be perfectly predictable.

These results imply that employing marker-assisted selection to improve the quantitative trait for drought tolerance in maize requires data mining of favorable alleles. Furthermore, the findings can be useful for maize breeding marker deployment and validation. Our results support the idea that knowledge of the marker effect based on allele segregation is essential for marker validation and MAS program implementation47,48. GWAS studies have been used to explore elite alleles present in grain yield and related traits in yam and wheat49,50. Nevertheless, further fine mapping and independent validation of the identified QTLs will be necessary prior to their routine deployment in marker-assisted selection.

Collectively, the integration of single-locus GWAS, multi-locus validation, candidate gene annotation, transcriptomic evidence, and marker-effect analysis provides a comprehensive understanding of the genetic basis of drought adaptation in extra-early orange maize and establishes a robust foundation for genomic-assisted breeding in tropical environments.

Study limitations

Although several robust loci were identified, the moderate population size and marker density may have limited the detection of loci with small phenotypic effects. Future studies using larger association panels, higher-density genotyping platforms, and independent validation populations will further improve mapping resolution and facilitate causal gene identification. Additionally, several SNPs demonstrated moderate marker prediction accuracy, the observed correlations indicate that individual markers capture only a portion of the genetic variance underlying these complex traits. Drought tolerance-related traits such as grain yield and ASI are polygenic in nature, controlled by numerous loci with small effects. Therefore, while marker-assisted selection based on putative SNPs may be useful for targeted allele introgression, genomic selection approaches that incorporate genome-wide marker effects may provide greater predictive accuracy by capturing cumulative additive genetic variation. For example, Beyene et al.51 demonstrated that genomic selection significantly improved prediction accuracy and genetic gain for maize grain yield under drought stress compared to conventional approaches. Consequently, integrating the identified loci into a genomic selection framework could further enhance breeding efficiency for drought tolerance improvement.

Conclusion

This study elucidated the genetic architecture underlying drought-adaptive agronomic traits in extra-early orange maize through an integrated genome-wide association mapping approach. The ECMLM-based GWAS identified 45 putative quantitative trait nucleotides (QTNs) associated with six agronomic traits under managed drought stress, while independent validation using the multi-locus 3VmrMLM approach confirmed seven high-confidence genomic loci, providing robust evidence for genomic regions controlling grain yield, anthesis-silking interval, ear aspect, and ears per plant. Candidate gene annotation, supported by transcriptomic evidence, identified several biologically relevant genes involved in stress signalling, carbohydrate metabolism, transcriptional regulation, and plant development, thereby strengthening the biological interpretation of the detected loci. The validated genomic regions and their associated candidate genes represent promising targets for marker-assisted selection, genomic-assisted breeding, and future functional characterization aimed at improving drought tolerance in tropical maize. Collectively, the integration of single-locus GWAS, multi-locus validation, candidate gene annotation, transcriptomic evidence, and marker-effect analysis provides a robust genomic framework for accelerating the development of drought-resilient extra-early orange maize germplasm.

Materials and methods

Genetic materials

One hundred and eighty-seven elite maize orange inbred lines from the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture-maize improvement program (IITA-MIP) were selected based on their combined drought and heat tolerance from a set of newly developed elite extra-early orange inbreds. The 187 inbred lines were generated from four sources, namely 2009 TZEE-OR1 STR, TZdEEI 12 x TZdEEI 95, TZdEEI 7 x TZdEEI 12, and TZEE-Y SR BC1 × 9450 STR52,53(Table S1). The procedure for the development of these elite inbred lines was described in details by52,54.

Field evaluation and data collection

One hundred and eighty-seven inbred lines were assessed for their performance under managed-drought throughout the dry seasons of 2022 and 2023. The drought was achieved by withdrawing the irrigation at 25 days after planting until maturity. Plots were set up of 3 m long single rows each, with inter-row and intra-row spacing of 0.75 and 0.40 m, respectively. Three seeds were planted per hole, and the seedlings were thinned to two plants per hill about two weeks after emergence to give a final population density of 66,666 plants/ha. In all the experiments, the inbred lines were evaluated using 11 × 17 alpha-lattice design with two replications. Compound fertilizer NPK 15:15:15 was applied 2 weeks after planting (WAP) at 60 kg/ha. Top-dressing was done using urea (46% N) at 60 kg/ha 5 WAP. Weed control was achieved by applying an herbicide formulation containing Gramoxone and Primextra at the rate of 5 L/ ha as pre-emergence, while hand weeding was employed for subsequent weeding54.

Observations were made on grain yield (GY), anthesis-silking interval (ASI), ear per plant (EPP), plant aspect (PASP), ear aspect (EASP), stay green characteristic (STGC) as described by Bonkoungou et al.54. The traits are recognized as drought adaptive traits.

Leaf sample collection, DNA extraction and Genotyping

Fresh leaf samples were collected from the established inbred lines three weeks after planting. To preserve the samples, leaves were detached from the mother plant, placed in Ziplock bags, and immediately submerged in dry ice. The collected samples were then transported to the Bioscience Center for storage at − 80 °C deep freezer for 72 h before lyophilization53. A FreeZone Freeze Dryer (Labconco, USA) was used for lyophilization of the samples. Genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted from the freeze-dried leaf tissues of each inbred line using a modified Cetyl-trimethyl ammonium bromide (CTAB) procedure as described by Azmach et al.56. The quality and quantity of the extracted gDNA were then assessed using the UV/Vis Absorbance protocol in a FlUOstar Omega microplate reader (BMG LABTECH). Following quality checks, the gDNA samples were packaged and shipped to Diversity Array Technology Laboratory in Australia for high-density whole-genome profiling as described by Jaccoud et al.57.

Marker quality control

The raw sequence results from the genotyping procedure revealed 36,697 DArTseq markers. These markers were subjected to quality control by removing markers with call rate < 80%, SNPs with missing data > 20%, heterozygosity > 20%, and minor allele frequency (MAF) < 5%. SNPs with undetermined chromosome positions were also eliminated. After quality control, 9355 high-quality SNP markers distributed across the 10 chromosomes of maize were retained for downstream analyses53.

Genome-wide association analysis and gene annotation

To identify genomic regions associated with drought tolerance in the population of 187 extra-early orange inbreds, the BLUES (Best Linear Unbiased Estimates) extracted from the MLM representing the phenotypic data for grain yield, ear aspect, plant aspect, ear per plant, anthesis-silking interval, and stay green characteristic was combined with the high-quality SNP marker data. Several models of trait marker association were tested including MLM, FarmCPU, BLINK, CMLM, SUPER, and ECMLM, in GAPIT R-package, and the best-fitted approach was determined based on the quantile-quantile (QQ) plot. Trait-marker association analysis was performed using the Enriched Compressed Mixed Linear Model (ECMLM) implemented in the GAPIT package. ECMLM is an extension of the Compressed Mixed Linear Model (CMLM) that improves computational efficiency and statistical power by optimizing the combination of individual clustering algorithms and group kinship estimation. The method evaluates multiple clustering and kinship combinations and selects the optimal configuration to minimize confounding effects arising from population structure and relatedness while maintaining adequate statistical power for detecting marker-trait associations58. The model incorporated both population structure (Q) and kinship (K) matrices to control for spurious associations among the inbred lines. The ECMLM can be expressed as:

Marker-trait associations identified at a predefined suggestive threshold of P < 1 × 10⁻³ were regarded as putative loci for downstream candidate gene identification and subsequent validation. A Bonferroni correction (P = 0.05/N), where N represents the total number of SNP markers (9,355), resulted in a genome-wide significance threshold of − log₁₀(P) = 5.27. However, no marker-trait association exceeded this highly stringent threshold. To further evaluate the robustness of the ECMLM-derived associations, multiple-testing correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) procedure implemented through the “p.adjust()” function in R (method = “BH”). Consistent with the Bonferroni correction, no marker remained significant after controlling the false discovery rate at 5%, reflecting the conservative nature of multiple-testing correction for the present dataset comprising 187 maize inbred lines genotyped with 9,355 SNPs.

Consequently, and in response to the reviewer’s recommendation, all traits were independently re-analyzed using the multi-locus 3VmrMLM method implemented in the IIIVmrMLM package. The 3VmrMLM approach simultaneously fits multiple loci and has been shown to improve the detection power for complex quantitative traits while maintaining effective control of false-positive associations. Genomic regions consistently identified by both ECMLM and 3VmrMLM were regarded as high-confidence QTNs, whereas loci detected exclusively by either method were retained as putative associations requiring further validation. Candidate gene interpretation primarily emphasized the validated loci while also discussing biologically relevant genes associated with method-specific loci.

To explore potential candidate genes using the B73 reference genome version AGPv5 (B73 RefGen_v5), genomic sequences flanking the significant markers within a 1 MB window (500 kb upstream and downstream) were considered. This window size was selected to accommodate the variable linkage disequilibrium (LD) patterns observed in maize populations, where LD decay can extend from several kilobases to several hundred kilobases depending on the germplasm and genomic region. The broader window was therefore used to reduce the risk of excluding potentially linked genes associated with the detected loci. The Ensembl database was used to identify putative candidate genes while their biological functions were annotated using data from the MaizeGDB, UniProt, NCBI, and InterPro websites20. The protein-coding products of the genes were identified using the UniProtKB database. Significant SNPs related to one or more candidate genes with function related to the traits of interest were subjected to allele substitution effect and LD annotation heatmap. The significant SNPs were selected to represent the locus in the same LD blocks (r2 < 0.2). The LD blocks were plotted using the package LDheatmap61 implemented in R. Publicly available RNA-seq data (GSE282587) were downloaded from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). Raw read counts generated using featureCounts against the B73 RefGen_v5 genome annotation were used for expression analysis. Samples were grouped into drought (n = 68) and well-watered (n = 64) treatments based on metadata annotations. Mean expression values were calculated, and differential expression was assessed using Welch’s t-test. Log2 fold change was computed to determine direction and magnitude of expression differences.

Marker effect prediction for the observed traits

Haplotype segregation analysis of the markers retained was generated using the rstatix package62 implemented in R. The sequence of each haplotype was established based on the performance of the 187 extra-early orange inbred lines considered as testing or identification populations. The markers with strong segregation were further decided after evaluating the variant effect prediction using the adjusted posterior probability. According to Agre et al. (2021), marker effects were then plotted for visualization.

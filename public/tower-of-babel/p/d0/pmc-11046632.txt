Impact of COVID-19 pandemic on health equality in malignant melanoma diagnosis
Authors: Lau Kin Lun, Huan Cheng, Rima Hussain
Published: 2024

Introduction

Difficult access to care during the COVID-19 pandemic can lead to delay in disease diagnosis. The pandemic has exacerbated pre-existing health inequalities and the most deprived population were often the most disproportionately affected. In this study, we investigated the impact of COVID-19 on the volume and pattern of melanoma diagnosis to evaluate if there is an association with the socioeconomical status of the population.

Methods

Cases with newly diagnosed cutaneous melanoma from January 2019 to December 2021 were identified in a specialist MDT-held database. Breslow thickness, pathological stage of disease, patient demographics and postcodes were reviewed.

Results

In the 3-year study period there was a total of 1,910 cases. Compared to 2019 pre-pandemic data, there was a 31% reduction (p = 0.03) in melanoma cases diagnosed in 2020; and a 10% reduction (p = 0.09) in 2021. In 2020, stage 4 cases had a 46% increase (p = 0.031). In 2019, 7% of the most deprived population had stage 4 disease at diagnosis, this was increased to 21% in 2020 (p = 0.02).

Discussions

COVID-19 has caused a dramatic reduction on the volume of melanoma diagnoses and an accompanying upstage of disease. Among the most deprived population in our region, they are three times more likely to receive a stage 4 diagnosis compared with pre-pandemic. This suggests that the barrier to healthcare secondary to the pandemic is most apparent to the deprived population and likely caused a delay in cancer diagnosis among this group of patients. Health policy needs to urgently be put in place to address this health inequality to minimise the prognostic impact on the population.

A prognostic exosome-related mRNAs risk signature correlates with the immune microenvironment in breast cancer
Authors: Duntao Su, Bida Peng, Wei Peng, Ning Bai, Zhaochangci Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBreast cancer (BC) represents one of the most prevalent malignancies among women worldwide. Despite substantial advances in diagnostic and therapeutic modalities and pharmacological interventions in recent years, the accurate assessment of prognosis continues to pose formidable challenges. Exosomes exert pivotal roles in tumorigenesis, tumor progression, and immune modulation, with exosome-related mRNAs emerging as promising novel prognostic biomarkers. Nevertheless, the role of exosome-related mRNA in BC oncogenesis, progression, and prognostic prediction warrants further extensive investigation.MethodsThe study constructed the exosome-related signature and validated the results in the Gene Expression Omnibus database (GSE9893). Candidate genes were selected based on differential expression, univariate Cox, and LASSO regression to build a prognostic risk model. Its predictive accuracy was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier (K-M) survival curves and time-dependent ROC analysis. Furthermore, the ESTIMATE and CIBERSORT algorithms were employed to elucidate the association between the prognostic risk signature and tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), alongside comparative analyses of HLA gene and immune checkpoint expression differences. GSEA was subsequently conducted to explore the potential biological pathways associated with the prognostic risk signature and individual signature genes. In addition, qRT-PCR was performed in breast cancer cell lines to experimentally validate the expression of signature genes. Finally, a nomogram was developed by combining risk scores with clinical features.ResultsUltimately, four exosome-related mRNAs were identified to construct a prognostic risk signature. In the TCGA cohort, this signature effectively stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups with respect to overall survival (OS), as evidenced by robust predictive performance in ROC analysis. In the GEO validation cohort (GSE9893), results aligned with the training cohor. Immunological analyses showed the risk score’s association with the tumor microenvironment (TME), encompassing stromal scores, immune cell abundance, and HLA gene and immune checkpoint levels. In vitro qRT-PCR validation demonstrated expression trends consistent with the computational predictions. The nomogram integrating clinical characteristics demonstrated calibration and substantial clinical utility.ConclusionThis study innovatively developed a BC prognostic risk signature based on exosome-related mRNAs, which not only effectively predicts patient survival but also elucidates its intimate association with the TIME. This signature offers novel insights and substantial clinical utility for personalized prognostic evaluation and treatment decision-making in BC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05261-0.Keywords: Breast cancer, Exosome-related mRNA, Prognostic signature, Tumor immune microenvironment

Introduction

BC is the fourth most common cause of cancer-associated death in women globally [1]. Treatment strategies vary across different molecular subtypes of BC, with current modalities primarily encompassing surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, and targeted therapy [2–6]. Recent advances in medical technology and the advent of novel therapeutics have substantially enhanced treatment efficacy and improved patient outcomes. Nevertheless, a substantial proportion of BC patients continue to exhibit poor prognosis owing to challenges including drug resistance and treatment-related toxicities [7]. Concurrently, BC exhibits pronounced heterogeneity, which further complicates prognostic assessment and therapeutic decision-making [8]. Consequently, the identification of specific biomarkers capable of precisely predicting BC patient prognosis, alongside providing promising therapeutic targets and a theoretical foundation for novel drug development, is of paramount importance.

Exosomes are cell-derived extracellular vesicles with diameters ranging from 40 to 160 nm, released via multivesicular body fusion with the plasma membrane, and capable of mediating intercellular communication by transporting nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, and other biomolecules [9]. Exosomes serve as crucial intercellular signaling mediators that influence TME remodeling by carrying nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, and other biomolecules, thereby playing pivotal roles in tumorigenesis, immune evasion, and metastasis. Additionally, through interactions with recipient cells, exosomes modulate tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, while providing potential biomarkers for early cancer diagnosis and targeted therapeutic strategies [10, 11]. Despite existing evidence affirming exosomes’ involvement in BC progression, their interactions with the TME and prospective clinical applications warrant further extensive investigation [12].

Recent studies have increasingly integrated machine learning with multi-omics approaches to construct biologically informed prognostic models and dissect tumor heterogeneity across various malignancies [13–17]. These studies highlight the growing importance of systems-level analyses in identifying clinically actionable biomarkers and refining precision oncology strategies.

This study innovatively developed a BC prognostic risk signature based on exosome-related mRNAs. The signature was constructed using exosome-related mRNAs from the TCGA-BRCA database and validated in the GSE9893 cohort. Furthermore, by integrating clinicopathological characteristics, risk scores, and immune risk scores, the signature enables comprehensive patient stratification. Clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) confirmed its utility in guiding personalized treatment strategies, improving patient outcomes, and providing a novel quantitative tool for prognostic assessment in BC.

Materials and methods

Datasets and clinical data

The RNA-seq dataset for TCGA-BRCA with matched clinical information was downloaded from UCSC Xena (https://xena.ucsc.edu/). Gene annotation files (GRCh38.99) were obtained from Ensembl (http://asia.ensembl.org/index.html). Patients with incomplete clinical data were excluded from subsequent analyses, resulting in a training cohort comprising 903 BC samples and 103 normal samples. Demographic features of the datasets are summarized in Table 1. The GSE9893 cohort (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) served to validate the prognostic risk signature [18].

Characteristics of TCGA-BRCA dataset

Identification of exosome-related genes

Exosome-related genes (n = 121) were downloaded from the ExoBCD database (https://exobcd.liumwei.org/) [19–21]. Differential expression was analyzed with the limma R package, revealing 91 exosome-related mRNAs significantly altered (p < 0.05) between BC and normal tissues. Visualization of these differentially expressed genes was performed using volcano plots, boxplots, and heatmaps generated with the “ggplot2” and “pheatmap” R packages. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks were constructed using STRING (https://string-db.org/) with the minimum interaction sources threshold of 0.400 [22].

Construction and validation of exosome-related mRNA prognostic risk signature in BC patients

Lasso regression was employed to identify prognosis-associated exosome-related mRNAs in BC patients. The risk score was calculated using the following formula: Risk score = expression of (exosome-related mRNA1) × (β1 of exosome-related mRNA 1) + expression of (exosome-related mRNA 2) × (β2 of exosome-related mRNA 2) + … expression of (exosome-related mRNA n) × (βn of exosome-related mRNA n). Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. K-M survival analysis compared OS across groups, while time-dependent ROC curves were produced with the timeROC R package to compute 1-, 3-, and 5-year area under the curve (AUC) values, assessing the model’s dynamic predictive accuracy. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses of the risk score were performed using the “survival” R package. Signature stability and generalizability were further validated in an independent GEO cohort (GSE9893). Risk scores were computed using the identical formula, followed by median-based stratification. Heatmaps visualized key gene expression profiles across high- and low-risk groups, K-M curves confirmed survival differences, and ROC curves evaluated predictive accuracy.

Immune landscape analysis and functional enrichment

The ESTIMATE algorithm was applied to compute Immune score, Stromal score, and Tumor purity for each sample, with differential analyses conducted between high- and low-risk groups [23]. HLA-related genes were identified, and their expression levels were compared across risk groups. ImmuCellAI (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/ImmuCellAI/#!/) was utilized to predict immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) responses, thereby assessing immunotherapy responsiveness in tumor samples [24, 25]. CIBERSORT deconvolution estimated the abundance of 22 immune cell types in different risk groups. To further elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying the risk signature, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed using Hallmark gene sets from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB). Analyses were conducted with the “clusterProfiler” and “fgsea” R package, and normalized enrichment scores (NES), nominal p-values, and false discovery rates (FDR) were calculated. Pathways with FDR < 0.25 were considered significantly enriched.

Validation via qRT-PCR

To experimentally validate the expression of the four-gene signature, two human breast cancer cell lines (MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231) and one normal human mammary epithelial cell line (MCF-10 A) were cultured in complete medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Carlsbad, USA). Cells were maintained in DMEM or RPMI-1640 (Gibco) containing 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Subsequently, 100 ng of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq™ II (Cat # RR047A-5, TaKaRa, Japan). Relative gene expression levels were normalized to GAPDH and calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Nomogram development

The nomogram was developed with the rms R package, combining risk scores and clinical features to estimate 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates in BC patients. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to evaluate the prognostic value of the risk score and signature. Nomogram performance was subsequently validated through calibration curves assessing agreement between predicted and observed outcomes, time-dependent ROC curves generated via the “timeROC” package, and DCA conducted with the “rmda” R package to demonstrate clinical utility from a net benefit perspective.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.0.1). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and non-parametric tests were applied as appropriate. Statistical significance was indicated as follows: p < 0.05 *, p < 0.01 **, and p < 0.001 ***.

Results

Identification and screening of exosome-related mRNAs in BC

Initially, differentially expressed exosome-related genes were identified between BC and normal tissues using differential expression analysis in the TCGA database, incorporating clinicopathological data (p < 0.05). These findings suggest their potential involvement in BC tumorigenesis (Fig. 1A).Subsequently, volcano plot visualization revealed 23 significantly differentially expressed genes: 6 upregulated and 17 downregulated (criteria: |log₂FC| > 0.5 and p < 0.05) (Fig. 1B).To further characterize the genomic landscape of these differentially expressed genes, mutation profiles in TCGA-BRCA samples were analyzed, demonstrating mutations in 63 genes, with ERBB2 being the most frequently mutated (Fig. 1C). Functional analysis via PPI network revealed CD24, TGFB1, ITGB1 to be hub genes at the network core (Fig. 1D). Complementing this, correlation heatmap analysis illustrated interrelationships among exosome-related genes (Fig. 1E). To further confirm the expression patterns of exosome-associated mRNAs in BC patients versus normal samples, we selected the 52 most differentially expressed genes for heatmap display. The results demonstrated clear clustering patterns of these genes in tumor and normal samples, exhibiting strong inter-group discriminative ability, further supporting their potential value as BC biomarkers (Fig. 1F).

Construction of prognostic risk signature for exosome-related mRNAs in BC Initially, the 23 significantly differentially expressed exosome-related mRNAs identified in previous analyses were subjected to Lasso regression, yielding 4 key genes: PSMD2, MTDH, CD24, and RPL28. A prognostic risk model was then built from these genes, with risk scores computed for each patient (Fig. 2A, B).With a regularization parameter (λ) of 0.0009, the risk score formula was established as : Risk score = (PSMD2) × (0.250376752) + (MTDH) × (0.182732449) + (CD24) × (0.1261857840) + (RPL28) × (−0.100636767) (Fig. 2C).The 903 BC patients were then stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score, revealing an inverse correlation between risk score and survival time (Fig. 2D). K–M survival curves showed poorer survival for high-risk versus low-risk patients (Fig. 2E). Time-dependent ROC evaluation demonstrated robust prognostic accuracy of the model, yielding AUCs of 0.60 at 1, 3, and 5 years (Fig. 2F). To validate the model’s generalizability, identical methods were applied to the external validation cohort (GSE9893), confirming consistent results (Fig. 3A–C). In this cohort, the signature maintained favorable predictive accuracy, yielding AUCs of 0.69 at 3 years and 0.65 at 5 years (Fig. 3D). GSEA analyses further revealed distinct pathway enrichment patterns associated with PSMD2, MTDH, CD24, and RPL28 expression levels (Fig. 3E–H). These results suggest that each gene contributes to complementary biological processes underlying the risk signature. Validation of the exosome-related mRNA prognostic risk signature in the breast cancer tumor immune microenvironment

The ESTIMATE algorithm then calculated TME and stromal scores for all tumor specimens. Stromal scores were elevated in low-risk patients versus high-risk patients (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4A). Next, HLA gene expression differences between risk groups were further analyzed, revealing significant upregulation of HLA-DQB2 and HLA-DPB1 in the low-risk group, suggesting stronger antigen presentation capacity (Fig. 4B). Meanwhile, ICB-related molecules (e.g., PD-L1, CD276, ICOS, CD86) were predominantly overexpressed among high-risk patients, indicating greater potential benefit from immune checkpoint blockade (Fig. 4C). To further evaluate immune cell composition, CIBERSORT estimated the abundance of 22 immune cell subsets in high- and low-risk cohorts. Boxplot analysis showed that CD8+ T cells, M2 macrophages, etc. were significantly higher expressed in the low-risk group, while M1 and M0 macrophages, etc. were significantly higher expressed in the high-risk group, suggesting differences in immune response types between risk groups (Fig. 4D). Immune cell heatmap analysis further confirmed differential distribution patterns of immune cell subsets across risk groups (Fig. 4E). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the prognostic risk signature not only predicts BC prognosis but also closely correlates with immune microenvironment status, underscoring its potential utility in BC immunotherapy.

Validation of the signature in vitro

To further validate the reliability of the bioinformatics findings, qRT-PCR was performed to examine the expression levels of PSMD2, MTDH, CD24, and RPL28 in breast cancer cell lines and a normal mammary epithelial cell line. As shown in Fig. 5E, PSMD2, MTDH, RPL28 and CD24 were significantly upregulated in breast cancer cells compared with normal cells. These experimental results were concordant with the expression trends observed in the TCGA cohort, thereby supporting the robustness and biological relevance of the four-gene prognostic signature.

Construction of nomogram incorporating clinicopathological information

To facilitate clinical translation of the prognostic risk signature, a nomogram integrating clinicopathological variables was developed. Univariate analysis revealed significant associations between age, M stage, N stage, and risk score with prognosis, while multivariate analysis confirmed age, M stage, and risk score as independent prognostic factors (Table 2). For each patient, individual scores from nomogram variables are summed to yield a total score, corresponding to 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates (Fig. 5A). Lower total scores indicate better prognosis. Calibration curves showed good concordance for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS predictions (Fig. 5B). ROC analysis further validated predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.79 at 1-year OS, 0.71 at 3-year OS, and 0.71 at 5-year OS (Fig. 5C). DCA subsequently evaluated clinical net benefit, revealing superior performance of the nomogram compared to traditional clinicopathological features, thus providing a reliable foundation for personalized treatment of BC patients (Fig. 5D). Subtype-stratified survival analyses further demonstrated variable prognostic strength across PAM50 molecular subtypes, with detailed results presented in Figure S1 A–E.

Discussion

As an innovative approach to cancer therapy, mRNA-based immunotherapy holds significant promise. MRNA can be engineered to encode specific antigens, thereby eliciting targeted immune responses and offering substantial flexibility for cancer treatment. However, its therapeutic efficacy is critically dependent on efficient and stable delivery systems [26]. In recent years, exosomes have emerged as highly promising mRNA delivery vehicles due to their natural nanostructure and superior biocompatibility. Their lipid bilayer structure protects mRNA from extracellular nuclease degradation, enhances circulatory stability in vivo, and reduces immune clearance risks. Compared to synthetic carriers, exosomes exhibit lower immunogenicity, cytotoxicity, and adverse effects, thereby markedly enhancing the safety and efficacy of mRNA delivery [27]. Currently, the clinical translation of exosome-based mRNA delivery is advancing, with studies focusing on CD24 progressing to the translational phase. Prospective clinical studies have demonstrated the favorable safety of mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes across multiple diseases [28]. These findings underscore the considerable clinical potential of exosome-related mRNAs, particularly in tumor immunotherapy and precision medicine. Consequently, systematic evaluation of the prognostic significance of exosome-related mRNAs in BC patients, particularly their risk prediction capabilities within the immune microenvironment, is essential for advancing their translational application in BC management.

Building on these insights, this study developed a prognostic risk signature centered on exosome-related mRNAs to provide a personalized and precise prognostic tool for BC patients undergoing immunotherapy. To address the challenge of poor prognosis within the immune and tumor microenvironment, we integrated traditional clinicopathological features with risk scores to establish a distinct prognostic assessment system. Results demonstrate statistically significant differences in survival outcomes between the high- and low-risk groups. Furthermore, ROC curve analysis confirms the robust predictive performance of the signature for OS in BC patients. When combined with clinical parameters, the resulting nomogram exhibits satisfactory predictive accuracy. Further analyses demonstrated that the identified prognostic biomarkers are strongly correlated with tumor microenvironment characteristics and immune checkpoint expression levels, thereby establishing a novel genetic framework for bridging clinical diagnosis and prognostic assessment in BC patients. In conclusion, this study offers a multidimensional perspective for analyzing prognostic factors in BC and provides valuable insights for risk stratification and the optimization of immunotherapy strategies.

Based on these insights, we conducted multivariate Cox, univariate Cox, and LASSO regression analyses to select three exosome-related mRNA associated with BC: PSMD2, MTDH, and CD24. These genes exhibit significantly elevated expression in BC tissues and are linked to unfavorable outcomes via Cox regression analyses, positive regression coefficients in LASSO regression, justifying their inclusion in the final prognostic risk signature. Notably, although RPL28 displayed elevated expression and was linked to poor prognosis in Cox analysis, suggesting oncogenic potential, its negative regression coefficient in LASSO regression contradicted expectations. To ensure the scientific rigor and consistency of the signature, RPL28 was excluded, thereby enhancing the stability and reliability of the signature. Literature review further revealed the critical biological roles of the selected genes in BC. PSMD2 influences breast cancer cell growth and cycle progression through control of p21 and p27 degradation via the proteasome [29]. Additionally, PSMD2 competitively interacts with the receptor for activated C kinase 1 (RACK1), inhibiting the proteasomal-dependent degradation of ubiquitinated β-catenin, thereby stabilizing β-catenin and activating the WNT pathway, which promotes BC cell proliferation and tumor growth [30]. MTDH, by forming a complex with staphylococcal nuclease domain-containing 1 (SND1), suppresses anti-tumor immune responses and promotes BC metastasis [31]. Furthermore, a class of small-molecule inhibitors inhibits BC progression by disrupting PPI within this complex, thereby improving immunotherapeutic outcomes in metastatic BC [32]. CD24, functioning as an immune checkpoint, interacts with sialic-acid-binding Ig-like lectin 10 (Siglec-10) on macrophages to promote immune evasion in BC; its blockade enhances phagocytosis and reduces tumor growth, highlighting its therapeutic potential [33]. Furthermore, CD44-/CD24- triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) cells can spontaneously convert to CD44+/CD24- cancer stem cells (CSCs), which may hold prognostic value for delayed BC metastasis[34]. Recently, MTDH stands out as a promising target in anti-angiogenic treatment of BC [35], while CD24 has been utilized in dual imaging single-vesicle technology (DISVT) to detect various stages of BC [36]. Despite advances in exosome-related mRNA delivery mechanisms, no studies have yet investigated the combined prognostic value of PSMD2, MTDH, and CD24 in BC associated exosomes. In conclusion, we pioneered a prognostic signature using these three exosome-related mRNAs, addressing a critical research gap and providing novel insights and theoretical support for prognostic assessment and personalized immunotherapy strategies in BC.

GSEA revealed that high-risk tumors were significantly enriched in inflammatory and immune-related pathways, metabolic reprogramming pathways, and proliferative signaling programs. These findings suggest that the signature reflects a coordinated oncogenic state characterized by coupled inflammation, metabolic adaptation, and cell-cycle activation. Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that tumor progression is closely intertwined with dynamic remodeling of TIME [37–39]. We hypothesize that exosome-mediated transfer of signature-related molecular signals may collectively reinforce tumor cell proliferation, enhance metabolic flexibility and secretory activity, and simultaneously reshape the tumor immune microenvironment through interferon and inflammatory signaling. Thus, rather than acting independently, these genes may represent complementary nodes within an integrated network that links tumor intrinsic growth programs to TIME remodeling.

To further validate the scientific rigor and clinical utility of our model, we compared our BC prognostic risk signature based on exosome-related mRNAs with similar published models in other cancers. In advanced gastric cancer (AGC), Guo et al. constructed a risk prediction signature incorporating six exosome-derived RNAs, among which two mRNAs (LZIC and SRSF6), to provide personalized decision support for neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) in AGC patients [40]. Their study similarly utilized Cox regression and LASSO regression for feature selection and evaluated model performance via ROC curve analysis. Nevertheless, key limitations are small cohort size and lack of a nomogram combining clinical variables to display predictive utility. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), Du et al. established and validated a prognostic signature based on exosome-related mRNAs, associated with immune cell infiltration characteristics, to predict survival outcomes in HCC patients [41]. Their approach aligns closely with ours and offers valuable insights into the immune microenvironment correlation. Additionally, Zhang et al. explored the feasibility of exosomes as RNA delivery systems in cancer therapy, emphasizing their potential in therapeutic translation [42]. These studies provide critical theoretical foundations and methodological inspiration for our work. Building on these findings, we integrated exosome-related mRNAs with immune microenvironment data to develop a prognostic risk signature centered on PSMD2, MTDH, and CD24, and further established a risk scoring system linked to immune infiltrating cells. This approach thoroughly clarifies the complex interplay of exosome-related mRNAs, BC progression, tumor microenvironment regulation, while exploring their potential clinical value in personalized prognostic assessment and immunotherapy strategies.

Compared with established clinicopathological staging systems such as TNM and molecular subtyping (PAM50), the signature provides complementary prognostic information. Subtype-stratified analyses demonstrated that the risk score retained prognostic significance in Luminal A and HER2 + tumors. Importantly, DCA curve analysis showed that incorporating the signature into clinical models yielded a higher net benefit across a range of threshold probabilities compared with TNM staging alone. These findings indicate that the signature may enhance clinical decision-making.

Given the significant differences in immune checkpoint expression between high- and low-risk groups, the signature may have implications for immunotherapy responsiveness. High-risk tumors exhibited increased expression of immune checkpoints such as PD-L1 and other inhibitory receptors, suggesting an immune-activated yet functionally suppressive microenvironment. Consistently, immune-related scoring algorithms indicated distinct immune infiltration patterns and functional states between risk groups. Notably, immune checkpoint blockade targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 axis has already shown preliminary clinical activity in subsets of breast cancer patients [43, 44], supporting the clinical relevance of immune stratification. These findings raise the possibility that the signature may help identify patients who could benefit from ICB, particularly anti–PD-1/PD-L1 therapies. The concurrent enrichment of inflammatory and interferon-related pathways in the high-risk group suggests that combined targeting of PD-1/PD-L1 and CD24/Siglec-10 signaling may represent a rational therapeutic strategy. Nevertheless, these hypotheses remain speculative and require further experimental and prospective clinical validation to confirm their predictive value for immunotherapy response.

This study developed a novel prognostic risk signature based on exosome-related mRNAs for breast cancer, enabling patient stratification through risk scores. Clinical decision curve analysis confirmed its capacity to guide personalized treatment strategies, enhance prognosis, and serve as a quantitative reference for clinical assessment. However, limitations exist: the study relies on public TCGA and GEO datasets, lacks key parameters such as neoadjuvant therapy response, and has not included functional validation in tumor cells or in vitro/in vivo experiments with clinical samples. Future multi-center prospective cohorts are needed to refine and validate the model’s applicability and robustness. Mechanistic studies on core genes are also warranted to elucidate their roles in breast cancer pathogenesis, progression, and immune microenvironment regulation, providing a stronger foundation for precision therapy.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although external validation cohorts and in vitro experiments were incorporated, the prognostic model was primarily developed using retrospective public datasets, which may introduce selection bias and limit generalizability. Future in vivo and prospective clinical studies will be required to further validate the mechanistic and translational significance of this model. As the signature is derived from exosome-related mRNAs, it may have potential applicability in liquid biopsy settings. Circulating exosome-associated transcripts could theoretically enable minimally invasive and longitudinal monitoring, offering advantages over conventional tissue biopsy for recurrence surveillance or minimal residual disease detection. Future prospective studies are required to validate its clinical utility in plasma-based assays.

Conclusion

This study developed a novel prognostic risk signature based on exosome-related mRNAs (PSMD2, MTDH, CD24) in BC. The signature effectively stratified patients by risk and demonstrated robust predictive performance for OS. It also correlated with the TIME, suggesting its potential as an indicator for immunotherapy response. Overall, this signature may serve as a valuable tool for personalized prognostic assessment and treatment decision-making in BC, providing a foundation for future validation and clinical application.

Data Availability Statement

The TCGA-BRCA RNA-seq dataset with clinical data was retrieved from UCSC Xena (https://xena.ucsc.edu/). Gene annotation files (GRCh38.99) were sourced from Ensembl (http://asia.ensembl.org/index.html). The GSE9893 dataset (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was used to validate the prognostic risk signature. Exosome-related genes (n=121) were obtained from the ExoBCD database (https://exobcd.liumwei.org/). The STRING database (https://string-db.org/) was employed to construct protein-protein interaction networks. The ImmuCellAI (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/ImmuCellAI/#!/) was employed to estimate responses to immune checkpoint blockade. The datasets used and/or analyzed during the current study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

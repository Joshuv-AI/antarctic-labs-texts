In Reply to Craig et al.
Authors: Nicola Hill, Gerard G. Hanna, Gerard M. Walls, Jonathan McAleese
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

We read with interest the report by Craig et al.1 on the accuracy of diagnosis of single-station N2 NSCLC, and we congratulate them on their excellent and thought-provoking study. The authors report that the diagnostic pathway included a positron emission tomography (PET) computed tomography (CT) and systematic staging endobronchial ultrasound, before surgical resection and lymph node dissection. We note that the interval between PET CT staging and surgical resection was not reported but that the median time between endobronchial ultrasound and surgery was (33 d).

We postulate that discordance between PET CT and surgical staging may have been in part due to the length of time between the PET CT scan and surgery, reflecting progression of the cancer during this interval. The Royal College of Radiologists (RCR) published guidelines in 2020 recommending a maximum duration between PET CT and commencement of curative-intent treatment of 42 days for patients in the United Kingdom.2 Geiger et al.3 report 50% upstaging of radical patients whose scans were older than 42 days. Of the patients, 30% were found to have evidence of extrathoracic metastatic disease and 20% had a change in either T or N status that required a change in radiotherapy treatment target volumes in their study. Our center has reported an upstaging rate of 69% (45 of 65), particularly in those with stage III disease. A total of 43% (28 of 65) of patients with stage III disease had a change in either tumor size, nodal staging, or presence of metastatic disease compared with 17% (11 of 65) of patients with stage 1 disease.4

The RCR undertook a U.K.-wide audit of patients receiving radical radiotherapy during January 2023. In that cohort, of the 529 patients treated with radial radiotherapy for NSCLC, only 21% cases commenced radiotherapy within 42 days of the date of PET CT acquisition.5 In an exploratory analysis, the audit team modeled the effect of shortening the radiotherapy planning pathway to 14 days. Nevertheless, this only increased guideline compliance from 21% cases to 35%, suggesting that repeating the staging PET CT may be unavoidable to adhere to RCR’s 42-day recommendation. In the audit cohort, repeating the staging PET CT occurred in only 8% of patients.

We would be interested to learn whether the authors would consider publishing the timelines between PET CT staging and surgery for the study population and an analysis of accuracy of staging scans on the basis of the 42-day window.

Given the underpinning principle of local control shared between surgery and radiotherapy, and the evidence for upstaging for the latter, we would suggest that the surgical community evaluates the impact of the length of the PET CT–surgery interval on pathologic staging accuracy. It is likely that a recommended maximum interval, as introduced by the RCR for radiotherapy, may progress the multidisciplinary decision-making for patients with resectable lung cancer, in particular those with known nodal involvement.

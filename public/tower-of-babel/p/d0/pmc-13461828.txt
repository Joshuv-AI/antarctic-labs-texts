Doppler Guided Renal Resistive Index as an Early Predictor of Acute Kidney Injury in Critically Ill Patients
Authors: Tushar Chopawar, Jeetendra Sharma, Shikha Sahi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAcute Kidney Injury is a frequent and serious complication in critically ill patients, affecting 20–50% of ICU admissions and significantly increasing morbidity, mortality, and healthcare burden. Conventional biomarkers such as serum creatinine and urine output are delayed indicators of renal injury and lack sensitivity for early detection. Renal Resistive Index (RRI), obtained via Doppler ultrasonography, reflects intrarenal vascular resistance and microcirculatory alterations—pathophysiological changes that occur early in AKI. Several studies have suggested that elevated RRI may serve as an early, non-invasive predictor of AKI, especially among patients with sepsis, shock, or postoperative complications. This study was conducted to evaluate association between bedside Doppler-derived RRI and the early development of AKI in citically ill patients.Objective-primary objectiveTo assess the correlation between Renal Resistive Index (RRI) and the development of acute kidney injury in ICU patients.Secondary objectives:To evaluate the association of RRI with comorbid conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, liver disease, and cardiac disease.To assess the effect of nephrotoxic agents and contrast exposure on RRI.To study the association between RRI, sepsis, shock, and hemodynamic instability.To determine the relationship between RRI and the requirement for renal replacement therapy (RRT).Materials and methodsThis prospective observational study was conducted in a mixed medical–surgical ICU. Adult ICU patients (>18 years) were screened, and eligible patients were enrolled after informed consent. Exclusion criteria included poor abdominal echogenicity, CKD with eGFR <, renal transplant, pregnancy, vascular abnormalities and renal structural diseases. RRI measurements were performed using a portable ultrasound machine within 12 hours of ICU admission (Day 1), and repeated on Day 3 and Day 5. RRI values were derived from interlobar/interlobular arteries and averaged from three consecutive cardiac cycles bilaterally. AKI was diagnosed using KDIGO criteria. All patients were followed for development of AKI, hemodynamics, exposure to nephrotoxins, lactate trends, and need for RRT. Statistical analyses included Mann–Whitney U test, chi-square test, and ROC curve analysis, with significance at p < 0.05.ResultsA total of 119 patients were included (mean age: 61 years; 63% male). AKI developed in 31.9% of patients, while 9.2% required RRT. Median RRI values were higher in patients who developed AKI on Day 1 (0.71 vs 0.62), Day 3 (0.70 vs 0.61), and Day 5 (0.70 vs 0.61), all with p < 0.001. RRI >0.7 was strongly associated with AKI on all measurement days (p < 0.001). Elevated RRI also correlated with vasopressor requirement, hyperlactatemia, and need for RRT. ROC analysis showed good predictive accuracy: AUC for Day 1 = 0.88, Day 3 = 0.90 and Day 5 = 0.91. Diabetes, vasopressor use and higher lactates were significant predictors of RRT requirement.ConclusionRRI is a reliable early predictor of AKI in critically ill adults. Hypoperfusion from shock, sepsis, or cardiac failure triggers intrarenal vasoconstriction, reducing diastolic flow and increasing RRI via neurohormonal mediators. RRI >0.7 were associated with development of AKI and subsequent need for RRT. Early RRI assessment can complement existing clinical and biochemical markers, enabling timely identification of patients at risk and guiding optimization of hemodynamics, nephrotoxic exposure and renal support strategies.Keywords: Renal Resistive Index, RRI, AKI, POCUS, Ultrasound

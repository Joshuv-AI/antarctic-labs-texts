Prognostic significance of serum laminin and the Zhejiang University index in patients with acute heart failure
Authors: Jiaqi Ye, Xiaoyun Yan, Ying Jiang, Qing Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study was conducted to evaluate the prognostic utility of serum laminin (LN) and the Zhejiang University (ZJU) index in patients diagnosed with acute heart failure (AHF).MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted involving 300 patients with AHF who were admitted to the Department of Cardiology at Nantong First People’s Hospital between July 2019 and November 2020. The cohort was stratified into two groups: a de novo acute heart failure (DNHF) group and an acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) group. Baseline clinical and laboratory characteristics were documented, and compared between the two groups. Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) served as the primary observational endpoint.Linear relationships were verified by Box-Tidwell test, multicollinearity was diagnosed by linear regression model for continuous variables. Based on the occurrence of MACE during follow-up, the study population was further categorized into an event group and a non-event group, and comparative analyses were conducted between these groups. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive performance of serum LN, the ZJU index, and their combined model for MACE in patients with AHF. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was subsequently applied to determine independent predictors associated with the occurrence of MACE.ResultsAmong the 300 patients with AHF, 150 were classified as having DNHF and 150 as having ADHF. Statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups with respect to N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), serum LN levels, left ventricular ejection fractions, and the ZJU index (p < 0.05). During the follow-up period, 68 patients experienced MACE. Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified elevated NT-proBNP concentration, increased serum LN levels, and a higher ZJU index as independent risk factors associated with MACE.ROC curve analysis demonstrated that serum LN predicted MACE in patients with AHF with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.721. The optimal cutoff value was 67.045 ng/mL, yielding a sensitivity of 78.8% and a specificity of 53.7% (p < 0.001). The ZJU index demonstrated an AUC of 0.733, with a cutoff value of 32.345, sensitivity of 78.8%, and specificity of 56% (p < 0.001). The combined model incorporating LN and the ZJU index demonstrated improved predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.806,sensitivity of 83.8%, and specificity of 73.6% (p < 0.001). ConclusionSerum laminin concentration, the ZJU index, and their combined assessment demonstrate potential prognostic value for predicting major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with acute heart failure.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) represents a heterogeneous clinical syndrome characterized by structural and functional abnormalities of the myocardium that impair ventricular contraction and/or ventricular filling, leading to characteristic symptoms and clinical signs [1]. HF is broadly classified into acute heart failure (AHF) and chronic heart failure (CHF). AHF is a life-threatening condition marked by the acute or subacute deterioration of cardiac function, typically precipitated by underlying cardiac pathology and various triggering factors [2]. AHF encompasses two principal clinical presentations: de novo acute heart failure (DNHF) and acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF). Several biomarkers are routinely used in the clinical evaluation of HF, among these, NT-proBNP is widely used due to its accessibility and diagnostic utility in HF, and it also serves as a reliable indicator for prognostic assessment. However, elevated NT-proBNP concentrations may also occur in other clinical conditions, such as pulmonary embolism, myocarditis, renal dysfunction, and severe infection [3, 4]. Consequently, identification of additional biochemical markers that are relatively unaffected by such conditions and are readily measurable is of considerable importance for improving the prognostic evaluation of patients with AHF.

Cardiac fibrosis refers to the accumulation of fibrous connective tissue within the myocardium and may occur in patients with HF. Myocardial fibrosis is primarily characterized by alterations in the extracellular matrix (ECM), which typically contains components such as type IV collagen, Lamini (LN), and fibronectin [5]. LN plays several important biological functions, including mediation of cell adhesion and migration, regulation of cellular differentiation and proliferation, and involvement in inflammatory processes [6–8]. Unlike collagen-based fibrosis markers (PICP, PIIIINP, CITP), which detect myocardial fibrosis, laminin abnormality specifically indicates early subsarcolemmal cytoskeleton destruction during myocardial remodeling and exclusive basement membrane damage [9, 10], processes not captured by conventional inflammation or mechanical stress markers (galectin-3, sST2) [11].Laminin was selected for this study because we think it may be a complementary biomarker for myocardial remodeling, although more comparative studies are needed to verify its superiority over existing markers.

The ZJU index is a recently proposed metabolic indicator that incorporates several biochemical and clinical parameters, including aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), triglycerides (TG), body mass index (BMI), and blood glucose levels [12]. Previous studies have demonstrated associations between the ZJU index and insulin resistance, lipid metabolism abnormalities, and obesity. The index was initially developed as a predictive tool for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), where it demonstrated favorable predictive performance [13]. To date, however, the potential association between the ZJU index—encompassing measures of glycemic status, lipid metabolism, and liver function—and the risk of AHF has not been examined.

Accordingly, the present study was designed to investigate the relationship between the ZJU index and AHF. In addition, the predictive performance of the ZJU index was compared with that of serum LN in relation to AHF.

Participants and methods

Study population

A total of 458 patients diagnosed with AHF and admitted to the Department of Cardiology at Nantong First People’s Hospital between July 2019 and November 2020 were included in this study. Among them, 158 patients were excluded based on exclusion criteria and 300 patients were included in the final study. The cohort comprised 187 males and 113 females. Among these patients, 150 were diagnosed with DNHF, while the remaining 150 presented with ADHF.

All enrolled patients fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for HF, which is defined as a clinical syndrome with symptoms and/or signs caused by a structural and/or functional cardiac abnormality and corroborated by elevated natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels and/or objective evidence of pulmonary or systemic congestion [14]. AHF was categorized into two clinical presentations: patients experiencing typical HF symptoms and signs for the first time (DNHF) and patients with previously established cardiac disease who developed worsening HF triggered by precipitating factors (ADHF). Based on these clinical characteristics, the study population was divided into the DNHF group and the ADHF group, with 150 participants in each group.

Exclusion criteria

(1) presence of malignant tumors, hematological disorders, autoimmune diseases, rheumatism, severe infections, or cachexia; (2) diagnosis of liver fibrosis; (3) occurrence of diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state; (4) incomplete clinical records.

Data collection

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were documented for all patients at the time of hospital admission. These included age, sex, height, body weight, blood pressure, and medical history, including the presence of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation (AF), ischemic cardiomyopathy and NYHA (New York Heart Association) classification of cardiac function.

On the morning following admission, fasting venous blood samples were obtained to measure fasting plasma glucose (FPG). Laboratory assessments included evaluation of renal function, chronic kidney disease staging based on estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), liver function, lipid profile, hemoglobin, albumin, blood sodium, serum LN, CRP, and NT-ProBNP, among other biochemical parameters. In addition, transthoracic echocardiography was performed to evaluate cardiac function, and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) values were recorded. LVEF classification according to guidelines, including HFrE, HFmrEF and HFpEF [1].

Measurement of LN and Calculation of the ZJU Index

Measurement of LN

A 4 mL fasting venous blood sample was collected from each patient on the morning of the second day following hospital admission. The samples were centrifuged at 2500 r/min for 10 min to separate serum. Serum LN concentrations were subsequently measured using a chemiluminescence immunoassay (MAGLUMI2000). The assay kits were supplied by FOSUN DIAGNOSTICS (Changsha, China). According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the reference range for serum LN using this assay is 0.51–50 ng/mL.

Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE)

MACE was defined as any of the following: death from any cause, re-hospitalization for all-cause, malignant arrhythmia or acute coronary syndrome (ACS), stroke within 30 days of discharge. Malignant arrhythmias were those that could be life-threatening and lead to severe hemodynamic disturbances, syncope, or sudden death. Malignant arrhythmias included in this study were ventricular fibrillation and high-grade atrioventricular block [15].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were analyzed using the Student’s t-test and are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution are presented as medians and were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-squared test.

Multiple collinearity diagnosis: Linear regression models were constructed for all candidate continuous variables as independent variables, variance expansion factors (VIF) were calculated, and variables with VIF > 5 (left ventricular end-diastolic diameter) were excluded. All continuous independent variables were verified for linear relationship by Box-Tidwell test. Variable screening and multi-factor Logistic regression: Univariate analysis was first performed, variables with P < 0.05 were included in multi-factor binary Logistic regression. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted to evaluate the predictive performance of serum LN and the ZJU index for MACE in patients with AHF. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was subsequently conducted to determine independent predictors associated with the occurrence of MACE. AUC of LN, ZJU, NT-ProBNP were compared by DeLong test. p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Clinical characteristics

The study population comprised 300 patients diagnosed with AHF, including 150 patients with DNHF and 150 with ADHF. Among these participants, 187 were male and 113 were female. Based on the clinical classification of AHF, patients were assigned to either the DNHF group or the ADHF group.

Comparative analysis revealed statistically significant differences between the two groups with respect to several clinical and laboratory parameters, including a history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus, NT-proBNP, LN, ZJU index, CRP, LVEF, systolic blood pressure (SBP), heart rate, the left atrial diameter, LVEF category, AF, medicine (Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEI), Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARB), Angiotensin Receptor Neprilysin Inhibitors (ARNI), Beta blockers, Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA) and blood sodium (p < 0.05, Table 1).

Risk factors associated with MACE

The linear regression model was used to make multicollinearity diagnosis for continuous variables. The results showed that there was strong collinearity between end-diastolic diameter and end-systolic diameter of left ventricle (variance inflation factors (VIF) were 8.057 and 11.760, respectively). Therefore, left ventricular end systolic diameter (LVSD) and left ventricular end diastolic dimension (LVDD) were excluded. The VIF of other variables (ZJU, LN, NT-ProBNP, etc.) were less than 3 and tolerance was greater than 0.3. Pearson correlation analysis showed that there was no significant correlation between serum LN levels and ZJU index (r=-0.025, P = 0.679).

To further evaluate factors associated with MACE, a multivariate logistic regression model was constructed with MACE as the dependent variable and the identified risk factors as independent variables. The model included DNHF, NT-proBNP, LN, CRP, LVEF, SBP, ZJU index, LVEF stage, medicine (ACEI/ARB/ARNI and MRA). The analysis demonstrated that NT-proBNP, LN, and the ZJU index were positively associated with the occurrence of MACE (p < 0.01) (Table 3).

AUC of ZJU was 0.733 (95% CI: 0.655,0.801), with an optimal cutoff value of 32.345, the sensitivity was 78.8%, and the specificity was 56% (p < 0.001). In addition, we built three models to predict MACE, we found that AUC of Model3 was greater than Model1 and Model2 (p < 0.05) (Table 4).

Discussion

AHF occurs frequently and is influenced by a range of clinical and demographic factors. These include age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, renal dysfunction, coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, infection, anemia, tumors, abnormalities in lipid metabolism, and obesity [16, 17]. Numerous biomarkers associated with HF have been identified. These include inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-1, and IL-6 [18]; markers of myocardial injury, including TnI and TnT; and indicators of myocardial load, such as BNP [19]. Additional biomarkers reflect myocardial remodeling processes, including matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) [20], as well as markers associated with myocardial fibrosis, such as soluble ST2 (sST2) and galectin-3 (Gal-3) [21].

The ZJU index incorporates metabolic and clinical parameters related to obesity, glycemic status, lipid metabolism, and sex. Previous investigations have primarily focused on its association with NAFLD and insulin resistance [22]. However, evidence examining the relationship between ZJU index levels and AHF remains limited. In recent years, several studies have also suggested that LN, a biomarker associated with myocardial fibrosis, may be related to the pathophysiology of HF [8]. In this context, the present study focuses on examining the relationship between the ZJU index, serum LN, and AHF.

In 1979, Timple et al. first described LN as a key component of the extracellular matrix (ECM) [23]. Earlier investigations primarily examined the involvement of LN in fibrotic changes affecting organs such as the liver and lungs. Subsequent experimental studies in animal models have indicated that the basement membrane also plays a significant role in the development of myocardial fibrosis in the context of HF. In advanced stages of HF, structural remodeling of the myocardium occurs, and elevated circulating LN concentrations have been reported to correlate with ventricular remodeling following acute myocardial infarction [24, 25]. These observations collectively support the association of LN with myocardial fibrosis and ventricular structural changes, indicating its involvement in the fibrotic remodeling of cardiac tissue.

The findings of the present study further suggest that higher serum LN levels are associated with an increased probability of MACE. In particular, serum LN concentrations exceeding 67.045 ng/mL were associated with a higher likelihood of MACE among patients with AHF.

The ZJU index was originally developed as a screening tool for NAFLD in Chinese adults. This composite index integrates BMI with several biochemical parameters, including FPG, TG, ALT, and AST [12]. Because it incorporates variables closely linked to metabolic status, the ZJU index is strongly associated with obesity and metabolic disorders [26], both of which are recognized risk factors related to AHF.

In the present study, patients with ADHF demonstrated higher ZJU index values compared with those with DNHF, but ZJU is no different between the two. In addition, an increasing ZJU index was associated with a higher likelihood of MACE. When the ZJU index exceeded 32.345, the risk of MACE was notably greater. In this study, AUC of LN, ZJU, NT-ProBNP were compared. The results showed that P > 0.05, can not explain the difference between the three.

In this study, no significant linear correlation was found between LN levels and ZJU index in patients with acute heart failure (r=-0.025, P = 0.679). ZJU index integrates BMI, fasting glucose, triglyceride and ALT/AST ratio, and its core biological connotation is insulin resistance and systemic metabolic load [27, 28]. A large amount of evidence shows that NAFLD also actively involved in the pathophysiological process of heart failure: Chang et al. reported that NAFLD patients had a 2.59-fold increased risk of new heart failure and a 2.30-fold increased risk of hospitalization for heart failure [29]. NAFLD mainly promoted the occurrence and development of heart failure through metabolic pathways such as systemic inflammation, oxidative stress, microvascular dysfunction and ectopic fat deposition in myocardium. LN is the main extracellular matrix component of basement membrane, and its elevated serum level mainly reflects myocardial fibrosis, extracellular matrix remodeling and cardiac stiffness. Patients with AHF often have comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and dyslipidemia, these comorbidities affect prognosis through different pathways, including metabolic disorders (such as insulin resistance and inflammatory responses) and myocardial fibrosis (such as left ventricular remodeling caused by pressure overload). Against this background, LN and the ZJU index show no correlation in patients with acute heart failure, indicating that they influence prognosis from two relatively independent dimensions: the metabolic-inflammation axis (ZJU) and the fibrosis-remodeling axis (LN). The combined use of these two indicators allows for a comprehensive assessment of patient risk from two independent dimensions: metabolism and fibrosis. In this study, after adding LN and ZJU (Model3)based on clinical risk factors and NT‑proBNP (Model 2), AUC significantly increased from 0.786 to 0.863 (ΔAUC = 0.077, p = 0.018), confirming the incremental predictive value of the combined assessment. These findings demonstrate that integrating metabolic (ZJU) and myocardial fibrosis biomarkers (LN) substantially enhances the discriminative ability of NT-proBNP, particularly in capturing risk dimensions-metabolic burden and structural remodeling-that NT-ProBNP alone incompletely reflect.

Taken together, we propose that the combined model could serve as a pragmatic adjunct to NT-proBNP, especially in HF patients with coexisting metabolic comorbidities—the very population in whom NT-proBNP interpretation is most challenging. Importantly, we are not advocating replacement of NT-proBNP but rather a complementary multi-biomarker strategy that aligns with the trajectory of contemporary HF guidelines. While our results are promising and the biomarkers are inexpensive and clinically feasible, external validation in larger, multicenter cohorts is warranted to establish optimal cutoffs.

These findings suggest that assessment of the ZJU index, LN, and their combined predictive value may contribute to the early identification of patients with AHF who are at increased risk of MACE. Early risk stratification could support timely implementation of preventive measures and therapeutic interventions. In addition, management of modifiable metabolic factors—particularly glycemic status, lipid profile abnormalities, and excess body weight—may contribute to reducing the risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this is a retrospective study, which does not permit determination of a causal relationship between the ZJU index and AHF. Second, the study population was derived from a single-center study and involved a relatively limited sample size of patients with AHF, and still requires external queue validation; therefore, the generalizability of the findings may be restricted. Third, the follow-up period of this study was only 30 days, which is insufficient for assessing chronic pathophysiological processes such as myocardial fibrosis, therefore, our findings should be interpreted as reflecting short-term rather than long-term pathophysiological remodeling. Finally, information regarding lifestyle-related factors, including dietary patterns and physical activity, was not collected. These factors may influence biochemical parameters such as blood glucose and lipid levels and could potentially affect the observed results.

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicate that both the ZJU index and serum LN are associated with the prognosis of patients with AHF. Higher ZJU index values and elevated LN concentrations correspond to an increased risk of MACE. These results suggest that monitoring the ZJU index and serum LN levels in clinical practice may assist clinicians in identifying patients with AHF who are at higher risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes. Early identification of high-risk individuals may support the implementation of targeted management strategies and encourage patient self-management. In particular, appropriate control of body weight, FPG, lipid levels, and liver function–related parameters may contribute to reducing the likelihood of MACE. Due to the short follow-up period of this study, it is insufficient to evaluate chronic pathophysiological processes such as myocardial fibrosis. To further clarify the exact role of ZJU and LN in myocardial fibrosis and long-term metabolic remodeling, longer-term follow-up studies will be conducted in the future.

Allergic Comorbidities of Korean Patients with Atopic Dermatitis: A Nationwide Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Yeon Seok Lee, Yu Jin Lee, June Hyunkyung Lee, Jae Eun Choi, Tae Young Han
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, inflammatory skin disease that affects 10% of adults and 10%~20% of children12. The relationships between AD and allergic comorbidities have been extensively studied in the context of the atopic march; AD begins in early life and gradually progresses to food allergy, asthma, and allergic rhinitis (AR). Although the mechanisms related with atopic march remain unclear, several hypotheses have been proposed. Barrier dysfunction induces transcutaneous allergen sensitization and activation of Th2 immunity. Th2 immune cells and various cytokines enter the digestive and respiratory tracts (via the bloodstream), provoking food allergies, asthma, and AR3. Microbiome dysregulation and epigenetic modification may contribute to the atopic march3. These hypotheses have been validated in previous studies. Dharmage et al.4 reviewed several studies, all of which had found increased incidences of asthma and AR in children with eczema5. Silverberg et al.67 reported that the prevalence of asthma in patients with AD was 12% to 20%, while the prevalence of AR in patients with AD were approximately 34% in both American pediatric and adult populations. However, in another study, the prevalence of asthma in patients with AD were 49.8% and the prevalence of AR in patients with AD was 87.0%8. The differences may reflect the study designs, data sources, and self-reporting eczema methodologies. Although several studies have investigated the relationships between AD and allergic diseases, few enrolled all age groups. Additionally, studies with small cohorts may not have been representative of entire populations. Studies based on national health statistics did not recognize that physicians are often asked to aid institutionalized patients. We thus sought associations between AD and allergic diseases in patients of all ages by reference to insurance claims data.

This cross-sectional study derived the risks of asthma, allergic conjunctivitis (AC), and AR in patients with AD; we accessed the 2015 Korean National Health Insurance Research Database. Patients with AD were individuals who visited physicians on more than two occasions with International Classification of Diseases-10 code for AD [L20] and graded by severity. Severe AD was defined as AD for which systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressants (cyclosporine, methotrexate, or azathioprine) were prescribed for >3 months; mild-to-moderate AD was defined as all other cases of AD. A patient with allergic disease was identified as an individual who visited a physician at least once for asthma [J45-J46], AC [H101], or AR [J30]. To avoid the inclusion of patients who were prescribed systemic immunosuppressants to treat comorbid diseases other than AD, we excluded individuals with autoimmune diseases or conditions associated with transplantation [K50-52, H20, L40, L93, M32-35, M45, M05-08, T86, and Z94]. The association between AD and allergic diseases was assessed using multiple logistic regression models in which confounding variables (e.g., age, sex, residential district, insurance type, and income) were controlled. All analyses were performed using SAS software ver. 9.4 (SAS Institute). This study was approved by the institutional review board of Nowon Eulji Medical Center (EMCS 2020-01-018-004).

A total of 2,927,721 patients (1,033,623 with AD and 1,894,098 age- and sex-matched controls) are analyzed. The age groups were: 0~1 (5%~6%), 2~6 (21%~22%), 7~12 (13%~14%), 13~18 (10%), 19~29 (11%~12%), 30~39 (9%~10%), 40~49 (9%~10%), 50~59 (8%~9%), and ≥60 years (9%~12%). Most patients had mild-to-moderate AD (99.7%). The prevalences of asthma, AC, and AR in patients with AD were 13.9%, 25.0%, and 84.4%, respectively (5.8%, 8.1%, and 54.8%, respectively, in controls) (Table 1). AD was significantly associated with higher odds of asthma (odds ratio [OR] 2.63, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.61~2.66), AC (OR 4.03, 95% CI 4.00~4.06), and AR (OR 4.89, 95% CI 4.86~4.92) (Table 1). In subgroups stratified according to AD severity, the ORs for asthma (6.55 in severe AD vs. 2.62 in mild-to-moderate AD group), AC (4.71 vs. 4.03), and AR (11.02 vs. 4.88) increased with increasing severity of eczema (Table 1).

Although the clinical characteristics of AD differed according to ethnicity and age, the prevalences of asthma and AR in our study were similar to values in previous studies. In addition, a recent cross-sectional study revealed that adults with AD were associated with higher odds of developing asthma (OR 2.09) and AR (OR 4.31), which was also similar with our study8. In our study, AR was the most common comorbidity, followed by AC and asthma in the whole patients with AD and mild-to-moderate AD group; in the severe AD group, the order of asthma and AC was reversed. The risks of AR and asthma increased with more severe AD in all age groups and both sexes (data not shown). Although AC is presumed to share a background with AD9, neither the prevalence rate nor risk of AC in patients with AD has been extensively studied. We found that the risk of development of AC in patients with AD was approximately fourfold greater than the risk in controls. Severe AD was associated with an approximately 2.3-fold higher risk of development of AC compared to mild-to-moderate AD. However, our study had certain limitations. First, the cross-sectional design could not reveal causal relationships. Second, the 2015 severe AD criteria were arbitrary; no insurance code was defined in that year. However, our criteria were similar to the modern insurance definition (newly established in 2020). Third, we used 2015 database. Although AD, AR, and AC are becoming more common in Korean children, the proportion with allergic comorbidities attributable to AD appears to remain unchanged over time10. In conclusion, we found that the prevalences of asthma, AC, and AR increased in Korean patients with AD, particularly severe AD.

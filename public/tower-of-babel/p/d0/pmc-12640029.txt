A Critical Appraisal of End Points and Statistical Strategies in the DIDo Trial
Authors: Himanshu Shekhar, Parth Aphale, Shashank Dokania
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

We read with interest the article by Goffin et al.,1 which evaluated the safety and efficacy of twice versus once daily icodextrin exchanges in older patients on continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis. The authors are commended for exploring a clinically important question. However, several methodological and interpretive aspects merit critical consideration.

A key concern is the composite primary end point, which includes excessive hypertonic dialysate use, dialysis modality switch, and death events with heterogeneous clinical implications. Although composite outcomes enhance statistical power, they may mask the impact of interventions on individual, pathophysiologically distinct components. The observed improvement in ultrafiltration with double icodextrin did not translate into a significant benefit in the composite outcome. A more focused end point, such as sustained ultrafiltration or reduction in hypertonic solution use, may have offered clearer mechanistic insights.

The trial was underpowered, with only 83 participants randomized versus the intended 96. The resulting 11% absolute reduction in the composite outcome (39% vs. 50%) in the intervention group, though not statistically significant, may still hold clinical relevance. Supplementary Bayesian or responder analyses could have further informed the interpretation.

In addition, reliance on surrogate outcomes such as ultrafiltration, without evaluating patient-centered metrics such as symptom burden, functional status, or health-related quality of life, limits applicability. Integration of validated patient-reported outcome measures, such as the KDQOL-36, would have aligned the trial with current standards of nephrology research.2

The lack of evaluation of genetic factors such as AQP1 polymorphisms known to influence peritoneal transport represents a missed opportunity for identifying responders.3 Stratification by transporter status or genetic profile may enhance personalization of icodextrin therapy.

Lastly, the trend toward lower serum sodium in the intervention group, though not significant, raises concerns about long-term icodextrin metabolite accumulation and osmolal gap–associated hyponatremia.4

In conclusion, though the DIDo trial advances the field by supporting dual icodextrin’s feasibility, its neutral primary outcome should be interpreted with caution. Future trials should incorporate stratified designs, molecular profiling, and patient-reported outcome measures to better inform personalized continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis strategies in elderly populations.

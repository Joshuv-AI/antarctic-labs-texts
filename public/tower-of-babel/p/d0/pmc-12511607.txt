Application effect of PMSM current segmented control method based on DM-MPCC algorithm
Authors: Shuai Ao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Given the issues of low accuracy and slow calculation speed in traditional PMSM current segmented control methods, this study proposes an improved model predictive current control method based on duty cycle modulation. This method designs a current segmented control model by dynamically optimizing duty cycle allocation, introducing parameter adaptive adjustment mechanism, and combining second-order error differential observer and harmonic compensation. Matlab/Simulink simulation shows that the torque-ripple value of the improved algorithm is 4.2%, which is significantly lower than that of the traditional model predictive current control method based on duty cycle modulation and the variable vector model predictive control method. The system efficiency was 96.3%, significantly higher than the other two methods, proving that this method can effectively reduce the energy loss of the system. At different speeds, the step response of the improved algorithm was 0.28 ms, 0.52 ms, 0.45 ms, and the tracking error was 0.08 A, 0.12 A, 0.15 A, which was lower than the comparison method, indicating that its response to current changes was faster. The improved algorithm had a steady-state error of 0.24 A and a harmonic distortion rate of 4.6% when the stator resistance changed, both of which were superior to other methods, proving that it could ensure the steady-state performance of the system. The proposed model predictive current control method based on duty cycle modulation effectively improves the control accuracy, energy efficiency, and real-time performance of PMSM systems under high dynamics, parameter changes, and complex disturbances. It provides technical support for the application of permanent magnet synchronous motors in extreme environments and complex working conditions, and has important engineering value for promoting energy efficiency upgrades in industrial transmission systems.

Introduction

Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motor (PMSM) is crucial for global sustainable development and is an efficient and energy-saving power equipment in the current industrialization process. It plays an important role in global sustainable development and industrialization processes1,2. With the continuous improvement of motor performance requirements in fields such as new energy vehicles, industrial automation, and aerospace, PMSM control technology has also received widespread attention. Among them, the Current Segmented Control (CSC) method, as one of the key technologies in PMSM control systems, directly affects the stability and energy efficiency performance of the motor. However, the emergence of various working conditions has gradually highlighted the limitations of traditional CSC methods, becoming a bottleneck that restricts the further development of PMSM3. The currently widely used traditional current control methods are difficult to ensure rapid and precise current tracking when facing sudden load changes, parameter perturbations or external interferences, resulting in increased torque pulsation and reduced efficiency. Although Model Predictive Current Control (MPCC) has advantages such as fast dynamic response and easy handling of multiple constraints, its computational burden increases sharply with the increase of the prediction step size and the number of voltage vectors. Single-vector MPCC has relatively large torque and current pulsations due to the action of a single vector. Although dual-vector MPCC can improve performance, its computational complexity remains relatively high when traversing all effective vector combinations and calculating the optimal action time. Meanwhile, the prediction accuracy and effect of the existing MPCC methods are highly dependent on the accuracy of the motor mathematical model. In actual operation, parameters such as stator resistance and inductance will undergo significant changes due to factors like temperature rise, magnetic saturation, and manufacturing tolerances, leading to inaccurate models and seriously affecting the prediction and control effects4. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a PMSM current control method that balances efficiency and real-time performance to overcome the constrains of conventional PMSM methods.

In recent years, the PMSM-CSC method has received lots of attention from industry professionals and has achieved a series of research results as a result. To address the issue of highly dependent predictive control parameters on the control model, Li T et al. optimized the finite control set model and designed an extended Model Predictive Current Control (MPCC) using the deadbeat principle. This method monitored current and disturbances through a sliding mode observer, improving the Steady-State Performance (SSP) of the current control model5. To reduce the significant training time cost caused by heterogeneous factory systems, Jakobeit D et al. designed a method for PMSM driver control using meta reinforcement learning. This method achieved adaptive driving of the control algorithm through contextual information, which could greatly reduce the total training time and improve training efficiency6. Hao Z et al. proposed a strategy for current control through switch active interference suppression to address the issue of decreased prediction accuracy in PMSM drivers under interference and control delay conditions. This strategy combined the strong adaptability of nonlinear extended state observers and simplified parameter settings using deadbeat predictive current control. This scheme could achieve a balance between prediction accuracy and tracking performance7. Zhang X et al. designed a fast dual vector model free predictive control method to address the problem of poor SSP in limited control set model predictive control methods. It further simplified the calculation by discarding motor parameters and only using the system’s input and output as judgment criteria. In simulation experiments, this method effectively improved the steady-state control accuracy8. To minimize the effect of parameter mismatch on the accuracy of electrode model parameters, Zhao M et al. developed a dead zone prediction current control method. By using the inductance extraction strategy to extract actual inductance information, it was found that the dynamic response performance of this method has been greatly improved and can maintain high accuracy9.

To improve the overall control efficiency of PMSM systems, Zhang X et al. used active disturbance rejection control as the main control method and applied it to segmented control of motor systems by leveraging its strong robustness. They proposed various performance improvement schemes such as parameter tuning and structural optimization, which have great potential for application in control systems10. Ma S et al. proposed using an enhanced dual vector model and sliding mode disturbance observer for current control, and addressed the contradiction between the approach time and chattering of the sliding mode surface through a new approach law. This model combined space vector modulation to achieve optimal control, which could effectively improve the flexibility of the speed control system11. To enhance model predictive control performance, An X et al. developed a robust virtual vector control strategy. This strategy eliminated the impact of mismatch through an incremental prediction model, calculated the error between the mismatch parameters and normal parameters, and designed a corresponding cost function based on this. This method could ensure strong robustness to parameter changes and greatly improve prediction performance12. Tian L et al. proposed a variable vector model predictive control method, which divides the linear modulation region into small regions and sets corresponding prediction expected voltage vector synthesis techniques and flexible allocation of phasor duration to achieve current reconstruction. It improved control accuracy while reducing computational complexity13. Factors including computational burden and harmonic current constrained the application of MPCC technology. To this end, Sun Q et al. designed a current control method specifically for suppressing mode voltage, eliminating the weighting factor of harmonic current terms, and introducing an optimal voltage vector selection method to reduce the computation time of traversal prediction. This method had better execution performance compared to traditional MPCC methods14. Belkhier Y et al. proposed a new nonlinear control method based on passive control for the dynamic time-dependent changes of machines in PMSM control and their sensitivity to different environmental conditions. This method combines a nonlinear observer and a high-order sliding mode controller to enhance the system’s ability to handle disturbances and parameter variations. The parameters of the passive control model, observer and high-order sliding mode controller were fine-tuned using the genetic algorithm. The results show that this method outperforms the existing techniques under various operating conditions15. In order to overcome the speed tracking problem of PMSM, researchers such as Belkhier Y designed a control method for PMSM servo systems with load torque adaptation. This method compensates for interference and ensures robustness by simultaneously completing multiple targets. The results show that This method has a strong ability to achieve precise torque control, ensure stable motor operation, and manage load torque fluctuations16. Djouadi H et al. designed a nonlinear generalized model high-order sliding film control method for permanent magnet synchronous motor drive based on prediction to address the issue that external unidentified interference and the constantly evolving motor characteristics increase the complexity of PMSM control work. This method utilizes high-order sliding mode control to optimize torque and trajectory speed. The nonlinear robust generalized predictive control technology was adopted for current control. The results show that this method ensures rapid convergence and reliable performance while also having good robustness against external disturbances and parameter variations17.

In summary, these studies provide important theoretical and practical foundations for PMSM-CSC. However, these methods still have the problem of low computational efficiency when dealing with complex working conditions. Meanwhile, these methods heavily rely on the accuracy of the system model. If there are deviations in the model parameters or if nonlinear characteristics are not fully considered, it may affect the prediction accuracy and control effectiveness. Therefore, in order to design a PMSM current segmentation control algorithm that is computationally efficient, highly accurate, robust and energy-efficient, DM-MPCC is proposed. The DM-MPCC algorithm improves the current tracking accuracy by optimizing the voltage vector distribution and Duty Cycle Modulation (DM), and using the current error gradient field when dynamically adjusting the duty cycle. The introduction of a second-order error differential observer effectively estimates and compensates for system disturbances, enhancing the anti-interference capability of the control system. By integrating the parameter adaptive adjustment mechanism and using the Lyapunov function to correct the parameter deviation in real time, the system can operate stably under different working conditions, effectively enhancing the control accuracy and robustness. The innovation of the this lies in the combination of improved duty cycle modulation and model-predicted current control to design a new type of current segmentation control model. The duty cycle is dynamically adjusted according to the real-time operating status of the motor, and the adaptive correction of parameters is achieved by using the Lyapunov function to avoid the influence of parameter mismatch on control accuracy and effect, effectively enhancing the adaptability and robustness of the system under complex industrial control conditions. The second-order error differential observer is applied to the current control of PMSM to accurately estimate and compensate for the disturbances caused by the imprecision of the model, internal coupling and external interference. At the same time, combined with harmonic compensation technology, the harmonic current caused by the high-frequency switching of the motor is effectively suppressed. The contribution of the research lies in proposing a current segmentation control method based on the improved DM-MPCC, which effectively enhances the control accuracy, dynamic performance, system efficiency and robustness of the PMSM system, reduces the computational burden, and provides technical support for the application of PMSM in high-end equipment fields such as new energy vehicles, industrial automation, aerospace, etc. It is of great significance for promoting industrial energy conservation and automation development and facilitating global sustainable development.

PMSM-CSC method based on DM-MPCC algorithm

PMSM-CSC method based on MPCC algorithm

With the development of technology, the requirements for the performance and control accuracy of PMSM are constantly increasing. The selection and implementation of PMSM current control mode directly determine the motor performance, and even affect the stability and energy efficiency of the entire system18,19. Traditional multi-loop vector control relies on PI regulators and fixed modulation, is sensitive to motor parameters, has complex calculations and limited dynamic response. MPCC is a novel control algorithm derived from industrial production process control, with strong practicality and continuous development and improvement in practical applications. It is widely used in fields such as power, automotive, chemical, aerospace, and energy. In general industrial production processes, there are problems such as multivariable coupling, nonlinearity, and uncertainty, making it difficult to establish accurate models. Therefore, the results obtained by using classical proportional integral derivative controllers or some traditional control algorithms are not ideal and have certain limitations20,21. MPCC can optimize while running and has low requirements for model accuracy, making it effective for controlling complex objects22. Therefore, this study adopts the MPCC algorithm for segmented control of PMSM current and designs a PMSM-CSC method based on the MPCC algorithm. CSC process based on DM-MPCC algorithm.

In Fig. 3, the DM-MPCC algorithm first collects the motor current in real-time, compares it with the reference value after coordinate transformation to obtain the error, and then uses the prediction model and optimization function to screen the optimal voltage vector as the main vector. In each control cycle, the main vector and fixed auxiliary vector are allocated their action time according to their duty cycle, and the smooth transition of current is achieved by adjusting the duration of their action. Finally, the driving signal is generated by combining SVPWM, and the control parameters are dynamically switched according to the motor operation stage to achieve segmented control of the current.

CSC method based on improved DM-MPCC algorithm

Although the designed control method based on DM-MPCC algorithm can effectively segment control the current, there are still some limitations to this method. The DM-MPCC algorithm has high computational complexity and strict real-time requirements when dealing with complex working conditions, which may make it hard to cater for the real-time needs of the system in high-frequency sampling scenarios. Meanwhile, the DM-MPCC algorithm heavily relies on the accuracy of the system model. If there are deviations in the model parameters or if nonlinear characteristics are not fully considered, it may affect the prediction accuracy and control effectiveness29,30. There is still room for improvement in the dynamic response speed and steady-state accuracy of the DM-MPCC algorithm31. Therefore, this study optimizes the proposed CSC method through dynamic optimization of duty cycle, adaptive parameter adjustment, and harmonic compensation. A CSC algorithm based on improved DM-MPCC has been designed to enhance computational efficiency and accuracy, thereby achieving refined current control under complex operating conditions. Conclusion

With the widespread application of PMSM in fields such as new energy vehicles, industrial automation, and aerospace, the requirements for motor control accuracy and dynamic performance are increasingly increasing. The traditional CSC method exhibited high computational complexity, insufficient real-time performance, and strong dependence on system models under complex operating conditions, making it difficult to meet performance requirements in high-frequency sampling scenarios. To address the aforementioned issues, this study proposed a PMSM-CSC method based on DM-MPCC to improve the accuracy of current control and the dynamic performance. In the experiment, the current step response time of the improved DM-MPCC algorithm was 0.28 ms, 0.52 ms, and 0.45 ms at speeds of 500 rpm, 2000 rpm, and 5000 rpm, indicating that it had a faster response speed to current changes and higher control accuracy. Under rated load, the improved algorithm had a TRV of 4.2%, which was significantly lower than the traditional DM-MPCC. The system efficiency has been improved to 96.3%, effectively reducing energy loss. When the SR, inductance, and PMF parameters change, the SSE of the improved algorithm was 0.24 A, 0.35 A, and 0.26 A , and the HDR was as low as 4.6%, 5.3%, and 5.1%, indicating its stronger adaptability to parameter changes. However, although the improved DM-MPCC algorithm performs well in multiple performance indicators, this method is highly dependent on the system inertia. In systems with large inertia, the control effect may be affected to a certain extent, and the dynamic response speed may decrease. Meanwhile, although a parameter adaptive adjustment mechanism has been introduced, in practical applications, whether the motor parameters are updated in real time when the motor’s operating conditions change rapidly may have a certain impact on the motor’s performance. Therefore, future research will combine artificial intelligence optimization algorithms to further enhance the adaptability and robustness of the algorithms, and explore faster and more accurate motor parameter identification methods to improve the performance of the parameter adaptive adjustment mechanism.

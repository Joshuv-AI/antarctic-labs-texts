Efficient removal of tetracycline from aqueous media using a novel Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 nanocomposite: synthesis, characterization, and mechanistic insights
Authors: Shahrban Dadari Doolabi, Tayebeh Shamspur, Ali Mostafavi, Maryam Mohamadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this research, a new Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 nanocomposite was successfully synthesized and evaluated for the efficient removal of tetracycline (TC) from aqueous solutions. The composite displayed significant adsorption capacity, fast kinetics, and outstanding reusability. Analytical techniques including FT-IR, XRD, FE-SEM, BET, TGA, DSC, NMR and zeta potential confirmed the formation and stability of the nanocomposite. Optimization of the removal conditions was performed using Response Surface Methodology (RSM) with a Central Composite Design (CCD), achieving > 99% TC removal under optimum conditions (pH 7.80, 17.50 mgL⁻1 TC solution, 7.0 mg adsorbent, 46 min). Thermodynamic studies indicated a spontaneous and endothermic process, while Kinetic analysis indicated that the adsorption process followed a pseudo-second-order model. The nanocomposite maintained high stability and efficiency over seven reuse cycles. Real-sample analysis using seawater and pharmaceutical wastewater confirmed its strong potential for practical environmental applications. These findings highlight Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 as a promising adsorbent for removing antibiotics from contaminated water, contributing to sustainable water purification and pollution control.

Introduction

Medicines are essential components of modern life, serving as key agents in treating human, animal, and plant diseases. Among them, antibiotics as a major class of pharmaceutical compounds have attracted significant attention as emerging organic micropollutants due to their environmental persistence1. These compounds often require targeted chemical pathways for removal, as they remain stable under typical wastewater treatment processes.

Antibiotics, widely used to control bacterial infections, can enter aquatic environments through various pathways, including industrial discharges2. Once released, they contaminate surface and groundwater systems and contribute to the proliferation of antibiotic-resistant bacteria, posing a serious global health risk. Consequently, the development of efficient antibiotic removal strategies is a critical area of environmental research3.

Tetracyclines (TCs) represent a significant class of antibiotics frequently detected in wastewater and natural water bodies4. These bacteriostatic, broad-spectrum antibiotics are effective against a wide range of bacteria and some intracellular pathogens5. TCs are either produced naturally via microbial fermentation or synthesized chemically6. Structurally, they are characterized by a four-ring backbone with variable substituent groups7, inhibiting protein synthesis by binding to bacterial ribosomal subunits. Common examples include tetracycline, oxytetracycline, chlorotetracycline, and doxycycline8, which exhibit similar antimicrobial profiles but differ in their pharmacological and physicochemical properties9.

Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) have attracted considerable interest due to their exceptionally high surface area and tunable functionality10,11. These materials consist of metal clusters coordinated to organic ligands, forming one-, two-, or three-dimensional networks12–14. Owing to their adjustable pore size and chemical stability, MOFs are increasingly employed for adsorption, catalysis, sensing, and environmental remediation15,16. Zr-based MOFs (Zr-MOFs), in particular, exhibit high chemical stability and superior adsorption performance, attributed to their abundant Lewis acid sites and robust coordination frameworks17–19.

Furthermore, luminescent and hybrid MOFs have been explored for pollutant detection owing to their high sensitivity and selectivity20,21. The integration of detection and adsorption capabilities enables the development of multifunctional materials that can simultaneously capture and sense hazardous contaminants22,23. Various studies have reported the removal and degradation of pollutants using nanocomposites24–26.

In this research, a novel Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 nanocomposite was synthesized and investigated as a multifunctional material for the removal of tetracycline from water. The nanocomposite exhibited high efficiency, stability, and reusability. A suite of advanced characterization techniques—including FT-IR, XRD, FE-SEM/EDX mapping, BET surface area analysis, zeta potential, TGA, and NMR—was employed to elucidate its structural, morphological, and surface properties. The adsorption conditions were optimized using Response Surface Methodology (RSM) to achieve maximum tetracycline removal.

Experimental section

Materials and instrumentation

All chemicals were of analytical grade and used without further purification. Zirconium oxychloride octahydrate (ZrOCl2·8H2O, 98%), benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid (H₂BDC, > 99%), acetic acid (> 99.5%), and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.8%) were purchased from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Copper(II) nitrate hexahydrate (Cu(NO3)2·6H2O, 99%), cobalt(II) nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO3)2·6H2O, 99.9%), nickel(II) nitrate hexahydrate (Ni(NO3)2·6H2O, > 99.9%), calcium nitrate tetrahydrate (Ca(NO3)2·4H2O, 99.9%), magnesium chloride hexahydrate (MgCl2·6H2O, 98%), and analytical-grade pharmaceuticals—including tetracycline (≥ 98.5%), ofloxacin (99.9%), sulfamethoxazole (99%), trimethoprim (99.9%), cefazolin (95–102%), azithromycin (99%), and cefixime (98%)—were obtained from Merck. Caffeine (98.5%), glucose (> 99%), hydrochloric acid (HCl, 37%), and sodium hydroxide (NaOH, ≥ 99%) were also used.

Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra were recorded using a (Bruker Tensor 27) spectrometer (Germany) to identify functional groups. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were obtained using a (PANalytical X’Pert PRO) diffractometer (Herzog, Germany) to confirm crystallinity. Morphology and microstructure were analyzed by FE-SEM (MIRA3 TESCAN, Czech Republic) equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) and elemental mapping. Thermal stability was examined via (TGA/DSC) analysis (NETZSCH STA 409 PC/PG, Germany) under an air atmosphere at 10 °C min⁻1.

1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded on a (Bruker AV 300 MHz Avance-III) spectrometer using DMSO-d₆ (for benzene dicarboxylic acid) and DMSO-d6/DCl.

(for Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4) as solvents. Surface area and pore characteristics were analyzed using (BET and BJH) methods (BELSORP MINI II, BEL Japan). Dynamic light scattering (DLS; Bruker, Germany) determined particle size distribution and surface charge (zeta potential). TC concentration was monitored using a (Varian Cary 50 UV–Vis) spectrophotometer (Melbourne, Australia) at λₘₐₓ = 365 nm.

Synthesis of Zr-MOF

Zr-MOF was synthesized via a modified solvothermal method adapted from previous literature27,28. Briefly, equimolar amounts of ZrOCl2·8H2O and H2BDC (3 mmol each) were dissolved in 40 mL DMF within an autoclave. Acetic acid (20 mL) was added as a modulator. The sealed vessel was heated at.

120 °C for 24 h. The resulting precipitate was collected by centrifugation (4200 rpm, 5 min), washed repeatedly with methanol and water, and dried at 80 °C for 12 h, followed by vacuum drying at the same.

temperature29.

Synthesis of Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 nanocomposite

To prepare the nanocomposite, CuCo2O4 nanoparticles were synthesized in situ and incorporated into the Zr-MOF structure using a solution-based method. Typically, 1.0 g of Zr-MOF was dispersed in 100 mL ethanol under ultrasonication for 5 min. A stoichiometric aqueous mixture of Cu(NO3)2·6H2O and Co(NO3)2·6H2O (1:2 molar ratio) along with urea was added to the Zr-MOF suspension. Urea acts as a precursor for carbonate ions, which, upon heating, facilitates the nucleation and growth of metal oxide nanoparticles. The mixture was stirred at 60 °C for 1 h, allowing for the in situ formation and deposition of CuCo2O4 onto the Zr-MOF surface. Subsequently, the solvent was evaporated, and the resulting solid was dried at 80 °C overnight (Fig. 1). Optimization of TC removal using RSM

In the present research, the RSM based on Central Composite Design (CCD), utilizing Design-Expert software (v13), was employed to model and analyze the effects of four independent variables: (A) pH, (B) initial TC concentration, (C) nanoadsorbent amount, and (D) contact time. The experimental design included one replicate, five levels, two blocks, and six center points, with one response (removal efficiency %). A total of 30 runs were conducted. ANOVA analysis confirmed the model’s significance, with an R2 of 0.9919 and an adjusted R2 of 0.9837, indicating excellent agreement between predicted and experimental data. The predicted R2 of 0.9822 further supports the model’s validity (model significance p-value < 0.0001). The lack of fit was found to be non-significant (p-value = 0.9896), which is desirable. Independent parameters, including main effects, two-factor interactions, and quadratic terms (A, B, C, D, A2, B2, C2, D2, AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD), with p-values less than 0.05, were identified as significant model terms for TC removal. The adequate precision value of 25.40 confirmed a strong signal-to-noise ratio.

Three-dimensional response surfaces (Fig. 11) revealed that the removal percentage improved with increasing adsorbent content and pH (A). Conversely, it decreased with increasing initial TC concentration (B), attributed to saturation effects and potential turbidity39,40. Specifically, as the initial TC concentration increased, the removal efficiency decreased due to the limited number of active sites available relative to the increased TC molecules. The optimal conditions were determined as: pH 7.80, 17.50 mgL⁻1 initial TC concentration, 7.0 mg adsorbent, and 46 min contact time, achieving a predicted > 99% TC removal. Real sample analysis

The adsorbent performance was validated in real samples. To assess the adsorbent’s performance under realistic conditions, seawater from the Persian Gulf (Qeshm Island) was utilized. Given that the salinity of the Persian Gulf typically falls between 3600–4800 mg/L, this represents a saline environment where the adsorbent demonstrated a high tetracycline removal efficiency of 98.0%. In distilled water, > 99% removal was observed.

Additionally, TC removal was tested using pharmaceutical-grade tetracycline (400 mg tetracycline, Sina Darou, Iran). A solution with optimal concentration was prepared and tested under optimal conditions. The composite removed 98.43% of TC, demonstrating strong applicability in real-world systems.

Adsorption reusability and stability

Desorption tests were conducted using various organic solvents, including DMSO, ethanol, methanol, and acetonitrile. Ethanol demonstrated the highest desorption efficiency among the tested solvents. The reusability of the adsorbent was subsequently evaluated over seven consecutive cycles. The removal efficiency exhibited a gradual decrease from > 99% in the first cycle to 85.05% in the seventh cycle. This decline was primarily attributed to incomplete regeneration and potential site blockage. Factors contributing to these decreases in capacity and regeneration efficiency include the reduced availability of active adsorption sites and compromised porosity, likely caused by incomplete desorption of contaminants from the adsorbent surface. Furthermore, interactions between the adsorbent and TC may have weakened over the cycles. These findings collectively demonstrate the considerable structural stability and reusability of the Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 nanocomposite for multi-cycle operations. To ascertain the presence and nature of functional groups and specific bands within the nanocomposite, a comparative analysis of FT-IR spectra was performed before and after the stability test (Fig. 14). Conclusion

The successful synthesis and application of the Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 nanocomposite for tetracycline removal demonstrates its strong environmental significance. By achieving > 99% removal efficiency under optimized conditions and maintaining stability over multiple reuse cycles, this material offers a sustainable solution for mitigating antibiotic pollution in aquatic environments. While the positive enthalpy change (ΔH = + 17.11 kJmol−1) indicates an endothermic process, which can be associated with chemisorption, its relatively low magnitude, coupled with a positive entropy change (ΔS = + 33.15 Jmol−1 K−1), warrants careful interpretation. This combination of thermodynamic parameters suggests that the adsorption might involve a moderate interaction strength, potentially bridging physisorption and weaker forms of chemisorption, rather than very strong, irreversible chemical bond formation. The positive ΔS further supports the notion of increased molecular freedom upon adsorption. The ability to perform effectively in real samples such as seawater and pharmaceutical wastewater highlights its practical applicability for large-scale water treatment systems. Based on the data obtained, the efficiency of this nanocomposite is not limited to its extraordinary adsorption capacity and rate, but also acts to reduce the ecological risks of antibiotic contaminants, maintain the quality of water resources, and comply with environmental protection regulations. These observations establish Zr-MOF/CuCo2O4 as a promising candidate for application in next-generation water purification and pollution control technologies.

PDNO-12 PEDIATRIC SUPRATENTORIAL EPENDYMOMA; INSIGHT FROM A TERTIARY CARE HOSPITAL
Authors: Farrah Bashir, Soha Zahid, Bilal Mazhar Qureshi, Ather Enam, Khurram Minhas, Naureen Mushtaq
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPediatric ependymoma is a distinct entity with a variable prognosis according to the age and location of the tumor. Supratentorial ependymoma accounts for almost one-third of the total pediatric ependymoma. We aim to present our experience of supratentorial ependymomas over a 10-year period at a single tertiary care hospital in Pakistan.MethodsIt is a retrospective cohort study, including all patients seen in inpatient and outpatient facility of pediatric oncology from January 2014 to December 2023 at Aga Khan University Hospital, Karachi (AKUH). Charts were reviewed for clinical presentation, investigations, surgical data, and treatment.ResultsSeventeen patients were identified, with a similar male (47.1%) to female (52.9%) ratio with a median age of seven years (IQR: 4.5-13.5). Most of the patients presented with vomiting (58.8%), followed by limb weakness (29.4%) and seizures (17.6%). Nine patients (53%) had a gross total resection (GTR), five patients had subtotal resection (STR) and three patients surgical record was not available. Five patients (29.4%) had re-do surgery (four had recurrence and one had subtotal resection). Biopsy revealed 13 anaplastic ependymomas and four classic ependymomas. Four patients had positive RELA-ZEFTA fusions for which samples were sent abroad. Eleven patients had radiation therapy (RT), of which eight patients received RT at AKUH. four were referred to other hospitals and two were lost to follow up. Only one patient in our cohort, aged 15 months, received chemotherapy as bridging therapy to radiation. One-year progression-free survival was 76.5% whereas one-year overall survival was 82.4% with medial survival not computed by SPSS.ConclusionWe demonstrate a high survival rate of patients with supratentorial ependymomas. Radiation therapy in resource-limited countries is a challenge for these patients contributing to a decrease in survival secondary to possibly delayed presentation and treatment.

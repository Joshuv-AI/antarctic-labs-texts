INTRODUCTION

The Alzheimer's Disease Research Center (ADRC) program was established by the National Institute on Aging (NIA) in the 1980s to advance Alzheimer's disease and Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (AD/ADRD) research through multidisciplinary investigations spanning basic, clinical, and translational science. Located at leading academic institutions and hospitals across the United States, ADRCs have collectively enabled key discoveries in disease mechanisms, diagnosis, and patient care.

Since its founding in 1999, the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC) has served as the central data hub for the ADRC program. NACC manages the collection, harmonization, quality control, and integration of standardized longitudinal multimodal data across the network. All curated data are made freely available to researchers worldwide via the NACC Data Front Door. Each ADRC contributes data using standardized protocols, including the Uniform Data Set (UDS), with rich demographic and multidomain clinical phenotypic data, through a centralized pipeline developed, curated, and maintained by NACC. 1 , 2

As the ADRC program expanded and research demands grew more complex, the limitations of the legacy informatics infrastructure became increasingly apparent. The 20‐year‐old system lacked support for high‐volume data, cloud deployment, virtualization, and flexible integration – capabilities essential for scalability, security, and long‐term sustainability. At project initiation, data collection practices differed substantially across ADRCs. Of note, 26 of 36 sites were using paper‐based methods, which constrained standardization, hindered real‐time validation, and reduced overall data quality. Electronic Data Capture and Submission (EDCS) systems were underutilized, contributing to inefficiencies. The planned launch of the Uniform Data Set version 4.0 (UDSv4) in January 2025 provided the opportunity to build a centralized, modern EDCS platform, aligned to ADRC workflows and built to accelerate multimodal harmonized integrated analysis‐ready data for the field.

Modernizing research infrastructure at the enterprise scale requires more than technical innovation; it demands alignment with institutional workflows, governance structures, and user needs. Implementation science offers a valuable foundation for this work, emphasizing the importance of embedding new systems within existing institutional contexts to support adoption, fidelity, flexibility, and sustainability. In parallel, the biomedical informatics literature underscores the value of federated architectures for distributed data coordination 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 and the need for modular, scalable platforms that can evolve alongside shifting research and regulatory requirements. 7 , 8 , 9 Recent approaches that integrate collaborative informatics, 3 , 10 implementation science principles, 5 , 11 and human‐centered change management 4 , 5 , 12 have shown particular promise in enabling long‐term system uptake and engagement. These models emphasize stakeholder co‐design, iterative development, and continuous feedback loops to ensure that infrastructure not only meets technical specifications but also reflects and accommodates the diverse cultures, capabilities, and constraints of the research communities it is intended to serve.

Against this backdrop, our initiative was guided by two core objectives: (1) to develop a robust, scalable, and modern EDCS system tailored to the immediate requirements of UDSv4 and (2) to foster community‐driven collaboration across NACC and the ADRC program. While initially focused on UDSv4, the system was purposefully designed with extensibility in mind to support additional data modalities in the future, such as neuroimaging, biomarkers, and real‐world clinical data. To implement the new EDCS system, we adopted a stakeholder‐centric sociotechnical (SCST) implementation model designed to drive adoption of the modernized data infrastructure across a national consortium. Cross‐consortium workgroups, structured surveys, and pilot testing informed key decisions related to platform architecture, validation tools, training materials, and documentation. This initiative represents a strategic advancement in the ADRC program's data infrastructure – one that aligns modernization with the evolving needs of the research community. In the sections that follow, we detail the implementation process, technical decisions, and lessons learned to inform similar efforts across distributed research networks.

METHODS

Workgroup governance and approaches

To guide the design, development, and deployment of the modernized EDCS system for UDSv4, the NACC convened a cross‐consortium EDCS Workgroup composed of data managers, informaticians, core leaders, and investigators from ADRCs of varying sizes, geographic regions, and technical capacities. Note that the number of participating centers decreased from 37 to 36 in July 2024, when one of the centers closed. This explains the varying denominator in survey results.

This collaborative governance structure was grounded in principles of shared decision‐making, stakeholder co‐design, design‐based research, and iterative feedback. To ensure broad representation and inclusivity, the NACC established an implementation framework that brought together its leadership and staff with ADRC representatives from across the country. Three subgroups were created to lead distinct but interconnected aspects of system development: (1) Requirements, (2) Development, and (3) Documentation & Training. Each subgroup included members from ADRCs with diverse technical capabilities and informatics maturity to ensure adaptability across site contexts. Subgroups collaboratively developed charters outlining their scope, responsibilities, and decision‐making processes, and each was co‐led by a NACC representative and at least one ADRC representative. The overall workgroup was jointly led by a NACC and an ADRC member.

The Requirements Workgroup defined system needs for data capture, quality control, and transmission from ADRCs to NACC. Drawing on site‐level workflows and operational experience, the group synthesized best practices and functional specifications that informed platform design, validations strategies, and harmonization with past versions of the UDS.

The Development Workgroup was responsible for building, testing, and refining EDCS tools and related technologies. This included the creation of standardized instruments, validation rules, data dictionaries, and configuration files, as well as technical review of API integration and data submission workflows. Development members collaborated closely with data managers and informaticians to ensure usability and alignment with local practices.

The Documentation & Training Workgroup, co‐led by members from two different ADRCs, developed comprehensive onboarding materials, standard operating procedures (SOPs), and training resources. These materials were centrally hosted and continuously updated through an online documentation portal, with close coordination between NACC and site coordinators to ensure clarity, accessibility, and practical utility.

Together, these workgroups formed a coordinated, participatory governance model that ensured technical decisions were informed by real‐world operational needs, supported by clear documentation, and adaptable across the ADRC program.

A SCST model for driving the adoption of modernized data infrastructure across a consortium

The implementation of the UDSv4 EDCS system followed a phased, stakeholder‐informed approach grounded in principles of shared governance, iterative development, and alignment with local workflows. The implementation process was organized into four key phases: (1) needs assessment, (2) iterative development and user feedback, (3) structured pilot testing and rollout, and (4) implementation across the consortium (Figure 1). This model is an adaptation of the Stanford Biodesign framework that was originally developed to guide needs‐based medical device innovation. 13

Phased, stakeholder‐informed approach for stakeholder‐centric sociotechnical (SCST) model with four phases: identify, iterate, pilot, and implement.

Phase 1 – Identify: Identify needs and initial requirements

To characterize the diverse technical environments, data collection practices, and operational workflows across sites, we conducted a series of structured surveys. These surveys assessed current Electronic Data Capture and (EDC) usage, site readiness for EDCS adoption, and specific functional requirements for UDSv4 data capture and submission. The findings informed the architectural design of the EDCS, platform configuration, and prioritization of core features, including real‐time data validation, API‐based submission, and flexible modes of data entry.

Phase 2 – Iterate: Feedback‐driven iterative system design and development

Development was guided by input from the three EDCS subgroups – Requirements, Development, and Documentation & Training – each responsible for co‐designing components of the system. EDCS instruments were created and refined using sandbox environments that allowed for asynchronous collaboration and peer review. GitHub was used to support open‐source sharing of EDCS files and validation logic. In parallel, training materials, SOPs, onboarding guides, and centralized documentation were developed and iteratively updated based on feedback collected through a dedicated intra‐workgroup survey tool. This survey tool was first used within each subgroup to refine draft deliverables, then redistributed across the remaining two subgroups to finalize content at the full workgroup level.

Phase 3 – Pilot: Prototype testing and refinement

In 2024, the NACC led a pilot to evaluate key components of the new EDCS infrastructure. The primary aim of both pilots was to assess system usability, identify technical or workflow barriers early in the process, and gather structured user feedback to inform refinements prior to formal implementation.

Pilot participants engaged in hands‐on testing of system features using a sandbox environment. Comprehensive feedback was gathered via surveys following the pilot to help inform the next phase of system development. These changes were implemented prior to full‐scale system launch for early testing to ensure smoother adoption across the ADRC program.

Phase 4 – Implement: Supporting system adoption across the consortium

Building on lessons learned during the pilot phase, the NACC adopted a phased rollout strategy that offered sites flexibility in their transition timeline. To sustain momentum and support sites during rollout, the NACC hosted biweekly EDCS Workgroup meetings, facilitated open office hours, and maintained an active, moderated online community forum. These communication channels enabled transparent information sharing, real‐time troubleshooting, and continued user engagement – ensuring that EDCS implementation was both technically robust and responsive to the operational realities of a large, diverse national research network.

Tools, resources, and processes for implementation

To support EDCS development and adoption, we applied the SCST model to guide tool and process design. A suite of SCST‐informed tools was deployed to foster cross‐site collaboration, balance standardization with local needs, and enable iterative refinement through stakeholder feedback and real‐world experience.

NACC Data Platform

The NACC Data Platform is a cloud‐based infrastructure that supports the integration, harmonization, and secure sharing of multimodal data across the ADRC program. Hosted on Amazon Web Services (AWS), it leverages Flywheel for imaging and other complex biomedical datasets and Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap), a secure web‐enabled software platform designed to support data capture for research studies, 14 , 15 for structured electronic data capture. Authentication and access control are managed via CILogon Single Sign‐On (SSO).

The NACC Data Platform consists of two key components: (1) ADRC Portal – This secure, self‐service portal provides centers with tools for data submission, audit trails, submission dashboards, and SSO access. It also allows ADRCs to manage participants, assign unique NACC identifiers (NACCIDs), and view or audit multimodal datasets (UDS, imaging, neuropathology, and genomics) and (2) EDCS, a REDCap‐based system that supports standardized collection and submission of UDSv4 data. The EDCS is modular and extensible, with planned support for additional data types (electronic health record [her], imaging, biomarkers). It incorporates quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) features, including automated error and logic checks developed with ADRC stakeholder input. All submitted data are reviewed for completeness, consistency, and adherence to predefined rules before being integrated into the centralized Data Platform.

To support training and adoption, a sandbox environment was also developed in collaboration with the EDCS Workgroup. This enables ADRCs to test data uploads, validate form logic, and ensure workflow compatibility prior to production use.

Together, the NACC Data Platform provides the infrastructure, the ADRC Portal serves as the secure entry point for ADRCs, and the EDCS standardizes UDS data collection and submission.

EDCS form development infrastructure

We used GitHub as the central platform for version control and coordination of UDSv4 EDCS instruments. Repositories hosted dictionaries, QA/validation rules, draft Portable Document Format (PDF) files, and finalized XML files, enabling real‐time feedback, change tracking, and transparent sharing across ADRCs. Sites could download and adapt materials for local use.

Style guidelines, co‐developed by the Development subgroup, defined formatting, naming, and functional standards to ensure consistency across all 20 forms. A dedicated sandbox within NACC's secure AWS environment supported standardized form development with assigned Designer and Reviewer roles.

Each form underwent a nine‐step review involving four independent reviewers (peer developer, NACC administrator, technical team, and research team), ensuring outputs met both technical and scientific standards.

Intra‐workgroup coordination and communication

To facilitate targeted intra‐group communication, the NACC developed a custom tool that allowed members to share updates, pose questions, and solicit feedback from relevant subgroups. Messages were aggregated in shared threads and included automated notifications, reducing redundancy and improving transparency.

Asynchronous collaboration was supported through a project management system featuring task‐specific messaging, color‐coded dashboards, and centralized access to key deliverables. This platform improved cross‐team coordination, streamlined task assignment, and enhanced visibility into development timelines.

Cross‐consortium communication, engagement, and training

The NACC led a consortium‐wide communication and training program – guided by user feedback – to support UDSv4 EDCS adoption. A centralized UDSv4 webpage served as the hub for announcements, toolkits, and training, with monthly email updates to ADRC leadership and data teams. Engagement included dedicated UDSv4 sessions at semiannual ADRC meetings, Data Core Workshops on REDCap/crosswalks/error resolution, and briefings with Core Steering Committees to align rollout with site needs. A structured resource library (general/technical/clinical), a transition checklist, on‐demand videos, and live webinars (recorded) provided step‐by‐step guidance. Ongoing support comprised the ADRC Community Forum (Discourse), weekly virtual office hours, and a continuously updated FAQ.

RESULTS

Workgroup growth and engagement

The EDCS Workgroup launched in January 2022 with an initial cohort of 40 members representing 15 ADRCs. Over its 3 years of activity, the workgroup expanded to include 111 members across 32 ADRCs. At its peak, the Development subgroup included 56 members, the Documentation & Training subgroup had 30 members, and the Requirements subgroup comprised 46 members. Of these group members, a core subset – six members in Requirements, 16 in Development, and eight in Documentation & Training – drove the EDCS deliverables. Through iterative feedback loops between the EDCS Workgroup and the ADRC program and informed by the SCST model, we developed a comprehensive EDCS system guided by the program's needs and feedback.

Data‐driven iterative development

The UDSv4 EDCS system was developed using a multiphase SCST model focused on real‐world alignment and iterative refinement. In Phase 2 (“iterate”), structured feedback – via surveys, structured breakout sessions at all‐hands workgroup meetings, and intra‐group communication tools – guided key decisions on tool selection, system architecture, workflows, interfaces, and training resources.

Network‐wide surveys

The NACC conducted structured surveys across 36 ADRCs covering (1) EDCS readiness and REDCap use, (2) workflow and implementation planning, (3) pilot feedback, and (4) adoption progress. With an average 92% response rate, results directly informed REDCap configuration, onboarding protocols, system refinements, and training resources (e.g., documentation, webinars, office hours, and the ADRC Community Forum). This broad input ensured the system remained responsive to diverse site needs and accelerated adoption.

Intra‐workgroup feedback

To complement surveys, the NACC deployed a REDCap tool for real‐time input from workgroup members, generating 21 rounds of feedback between December 2022 and April 2024. This agile mechanism shaped critical features, including refined documentation, clearer workflow mapping, and improved form accessibility. Continuous input from engaged contributors helped identify usability challenges early and fostered shared ownership.

Subgroup working meetings

The Requirements, Development, and Documentation & Training subgroups met regularly from January 2022 through the UDSv4 launch, producing key deliverables such as REDCap project files, XML architecture, crosswalk dictionaries, and 25 SOPs. Composed largely of ADRC staff, these subgroups ensured outputs were both stakeholder‐informed and practically grounded.

Structured breakouts

Two large‐group Zoom sessions (January 2023, February 2024) engaged 75+ participants in topic‐focused breakouts, yielding over 60 actionable requirements. Outcomes included publicly available UDS form PDFs, a QC dashboard in the ADRC Portal, and expanded Application Programming Interface (API) documentation with example code.

Examples of data‐driven system design decisions

In addition to the previously noted examples, the data‐driven SCST model led to several notable EDCS requirements and design decisions.

REDCap was selected as a foundational application for the EDCS

In April 2022, the EDCS Workgroup distributed a comprehensive survey to all ADRCs to assess existing EDCS infrastructure and interest in a standardized, NACC‐supported platform. This survey had 100% compliance (37 ADRC responses). Results indicated that REDCap was already in use by 20 centers (54%) for some parts of their workflow and that an additional nine centers (24%) were interested in adopting a REDCap‐based solution. The REDCap application also had some core features that made it well suited for the needs of the ADRC program and the system requirements that were identified through our surveys. These include its modular, open‐source architecture, Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)‐compliant cloud compatibility, and robust API capabilities. These features also made it well suited to accommodate UDSv4 and to support evolving standards and data modalities. After evaluating other electronic data capture options and identifying program needs and system requirements through surveys, REDCap was selected as a core technology of the EDCS system framework.

A fully realized UDSv4 REDCap project was developed to facilitate direct‐to‐EDC data collection

Stakeholder input throughout the SCST model indicated a strong demand for a centralized REDCap‐based EDC system. Despite the enthusiasm, concerns were raised about infrastructure readiness and implementation burden. In the October 2023 and April 2024 surveys, 13/36 (36%) and 14/36 (39%) sites reported concerns about transitioning to REDCap for UDSv4. Across all feedback modalities – surveys and breakout rooms – 90.9% included mention of the need for a production‐ready REDCap project, often referencing implementation burden, REDCap development, or data export challenges.

In response, a fully realized UDSv4 REDCap project, with built‐in validations and reports, was developed to facilitate direct‐to‐EDC data collection. The development of REDCap XML files enabled the rapid deployment of a fully configured, production‐ready UDSv4 initial packet project. Each XML included branching logic, standardized variable names, formatting, and prebuilt REDCap reports that extracted and organized UDSv4 data elements. These reports generated CSV exports compatible with both batch uploads and API‐based submissions to the NACC. Built‐in REDCap error checking provided QA through range validations, required field checks, and calculated field logic. This work naturally evolved into creating modular REDCap data dictionaries and zip files for individual forms, supporting flexible integration into local REDCap systems and alignment with existing projects, including non‐UDS workflows.

A variable crosswalk tool was developed to facilitate harmonization of UDS versions

In preparation for the UDSv4 transition, ADRCs raised concerns about the effort required to harmonize new data with legacy UDSv3 datasets. In an October 2023 survey, sites cited the burden of additional programming time, challenges integrating new forms into existing EDC systems, and concerns over potential mandates to rename local non‐UDS variables (e.g., prefixing with “local”). Centers emphasized the importance of maintaining autonomy in managing non‐UDS data while facilitating cross‐version compatibility for investigators. These concerns were echoed throughout the initiative. Recurrent themes related to dataset harmonization between UDS versions and updates to variables appeared in 72.7% of the feedback methods, including both surveys and breakout sessions.

To address these concerns, the EDCS Workgroup developed a variable crosswalk tool to support seamless data harmonization between UDSv3 and UDSv4. The tool, first discussed in October 2023 and refined through the 2024 all‐hands meeting, was created using the SCST model's iterative, stakeholder‐informed process. It includes both human‐readable and programmatic formats, instrument‐specific mapping dictionaries, REDCap field‐mapping rules, and JSON files for automated integration. This flexible resource helped minimize disruption and provided a clear pathway for unifying longitudinal datasets across UDS versions.

A transparent real‐time quality‐check system

A recurring request from workgroup meeting breakout sessions and consortium‐wide surveys was for more robust quality‐check and validation tools and greater transparency into the underlying data review rules. In the January 2023 workgroup breakout discussions, participants emphasized the need for visibility into error identification, in‐form validation, and the lifecycle of errors and alerts (see Slide 4: QA/QC in Form Development – January 2023). This feedback was echoed a year later during the 2024 workgroup meeting, where it was expanded to include suggestions for batch‐level error summaries and refinements to user permissions (see Breakout Link 2: Error Reporting – February 2024). Consistent feedback was received throughout the SCST model deployment, with themes such as “quality checks,” “error reporting,” and “alert reporting” appearing in 81.8% of all feedback sources.

In response, the NACC publicly shared the complete validation ruleset – comprising 9,220 individual checks – via its GitHub technical repository and implemented a user‐friendly dashboard within the ADRC portal. This dashboard enables centers to review and validate data prior to submission, download error reports, and organize uploads by validation status. The system extends REDCap's native checks by including cross‐visit validations, removing override options, and adding feasibility checks across related fields. At the request of ADRC users, the error‐reporting feature was redesigned to be more intuitive, with cleaner formatting, improved readability, and plain language error description.

API‐enabled automated data submission

Several ADRCs (n = 25) requested the option of API‐enabled data transmission between their local systems and the NACC. From the February 2024 EDC larger workgroup meeting, there was high interest in API access for uploads, downloads, and integration into local systems. Automation was seen as key to improving workflows and minimizing manual effort. Comments highlighting the importance of API framework and utilization appeared in 90.9% of all feedback sources.

This feedback, formally captured in the October 2023 surveys, the February workgroup meeting, and previously raised within EDCS subgroups, prompted the NACC's technology team to enable API access for programmatic submissions. Recognizing that technical complexity might hinder adoption, the NACC partnered with the Documentation & Training subgroup to create detailed support materials – including example code in multiple environments and step‐by‐step tutorials. The API system was made available during the pilot testing phase, and feedback from participating centers was used to refine documentation. The API‐based submission provided flexible and automated data submission pathways.

Centralized documentation and training hub

Feedback consistently highlighted the need for a centralized repository of source documents and training materials. Themes relating to centralized documentation, step‐by‐step technical documents, and video demonstrations appeared in 81.8% of qualitative feedback loops including survey free‐text fields and meeting discussions. Seventeen out of 36 centers were concerned about training for data managers. Recurring requests of this centralized documents and training platform included video demonstrations of key features, such as API submission platform uses, REDCap project creation using the XML file, and step‐by‐step instructions for key features of the modernized EDCS.

In response, the NACC launched a publicly accessible site (https://docs.naccdata.org) that consolidates all EDCS‐related resources, including downloadable UDSv4 forms, REDCap implementation guides, and step‐by‐step tutorials for data transmission and quality checks. Content updates were directly shaped by ADRC input, such as submission guideposts (October 2023), expanded API documentation (UDSv4/NACCID pilots), and crosswalk SOPs (August 2024).

Selection of 6‐month transition window

In August 2024, we surveyed the ADRCs on the length of time they anticipated needing to complete the transition to UDSv4 (collecting all prospective data via UDSv4) once all data collection forms were finalized and UDSv4 resources, including the EDCS system, were launched. ADRCs reported needing 6 months or less to complete the transition to UDSv4 (Figure 2a); as a result, a 6‐month transition window was set. We provided flexibility in transitioning during this 6‐month window, allowing ADRCs to transition on a single date or over time. In a December 2024 survey, ADRCs indicated that they were planning various approaches to the transition, with 17 (49% of 35 respondents) planning to transition in a single day and others planning to gradually transition over several months (Figure 2b). To support this rapid rollout, the EDCS system workgroups developed a production‐ready UDSv4 Initial Packet REDCap project, enabling immediate implementation for sites ready to transition at the start of the window.

Pilot phase

In the pilot phase of the SCST model, a broad swath of ADRCs tested and provided feedback on the EDCS system prototype for UDSv4 (23 ADRCs participated). A key goal of the pilots was to give ADRCs hands‐on experience with the new EDCS system, including all available data collection and submission methods, and to gather actionable feedback for refining its usability, features, and associated training documentation.

UDSv4 pilot participation and results

The UDSv4 pilot phase ran for 15 weeks from May to August 2024, with 23 ADRCs participating. Of these, 17 centers submitted data for 55 participants using the new EDCS system and provided detailed feedback. During the pilot, sites tested one or more of the EDCS system's collection and submission methods (Figure 3A,B), utilized various support resources (Figure 3C), and evaluated the usability of training materials (Figure 3D). Most sites reported that the training resources were helpful and that the new EDCS forms and submission options were easy or somewhat easy to use (Figure 3E,F). Notably, 75% of novice REDCap users found the new forms relatively easy to navigate.

After the pilot, 78% (18/23) of participants reported feeling prepared or very prepared to transition to UDSv4. Based on pilot feedback, several key changes were implemented to improve the EDCS system. These included refining REDCap form logic and validation rules, expanding and making API documentation more accessible, and creating a sandbox environment to allow ADRCs to practice data submission workflows. As of June 2025, ADRCs have submitted 78 test files to the sandbox environment to evaluate and refine their submission workflows. Additionally, feedback highlighted the need for a gradual implementation period, leading to establishing a six‐month transition window to support the network‐wide rollout of UDSv4.

NACC's new modern EDCS system

The multiphase SCST model culminated in developing a modern EDCS system for the ADRC network. Input from ADRC collaborators influenced every level of system design, from infrastructure selection to form logic and submission workflows. In partnership with the EDCS Workgroup, informaticians, and engineers, the NACC developed a modular and flexible EDCS platform to support the national transition to UDSv4 (see Section 2.3.1).

This system was built to accommodate the range of technical environments, workflows, and institutional needs across the ADRC network. It supports multiple pathways for data collection and submission (Figure 4). The scalable, modular architecture is designed to integrate additional data modalities in the future.

For sites using REDCap, the NACC provided preconfigured REDCap XML projects and modular data dictionaries to facilitate rapid local deployment. For ADRCs not using REDCap, alternative EDC options were supported through the provision of detailed crosswalks and coding manuals, enabling compatibility with UDSv4 standards. Additionally, downloadable PDF‐based forms were made available to support hybrid or fully paper‐based workflows.

For centers without local infrastructure, the EDCS platform enabled direct data entry through a centrally hosted REDCap instance maintained by the NACC. These multiple pathways were informed by initial infrastructure assessments and further refined through pilot testing, ensuring that the system could meet diverse institutional needs across the ADRC network.

Multipathway submission

Submission workflows were designed to accommodate a range of technical capabilities across ADRCs (Figure 5). Advanced users can transmit data programmatically through API‐based uploads using the Flywheel CLI (a text‐based interface for interacting with the platform) or the SDK (a programmatic interface available in Python and other languages). Other users submit structured data files via batch CSV uploads through the ADRC Portal. For sites using the NACC‐hosted REDCap instance, data were automatically transferred to the NACC data platform upon submission. All submission methods were supported by SOPs, user guides, and detailed technical documentation, in alignment with feedback gathered during the ADRC pilot phase.

Tiered QA framework

In response to stakeholder priorities, a three‐layered error‐checking system was developed to ensure data quality and streamline the submission process. The first layer consisted of real‐time REDCap validations, using field‐level constraints and branching logic to minimize data entry errors at the point of capture. The second layer applied scientific consistency checks, leveraging rules defined by domain experts to flag implausible or contradictory values. The third layer performed structural format validation, evaluating each submission against the NACC's formatting and structural requirements. To support users in resolving issues efficiently, a centralized QC Status Table was implemented to provide real‐time feedback on submission status by participant, visit, and module. It included detailed error messages to guide corrections and became one of the most frequently requested features during pilot evaluations.

System features and alignment with user priorities

The final EDCS system incorporated key features identified through iterative development to save time, enhance data sharing, and improve data quality (Table 1). Collectively, these capabilities demonstrate how an iterative, stakeholder‐led design process contributed to the development of a scalable and user‐aligned informatics infrastructure for UDSv4 data collection and submission.

Engagement with EDCS training tools and resources

During the implementation phase, the NACC developed a suite of technical tools, training resources, and implementation resources to facilitate the nationwide adoption of UDSv4. These tools were shaped by feedback gathered through surveys, pilot programs, working groups, and forum discussions, ensuring alignment with ADRC needs and workflows. The ADRCs used all of them heavily.

UDSv4 transition checklist

Based on survey results identifying a need for step‐by‐step transition guidance, the EDCS Workgroup developed a comprehensive UDSv4 checklist. This resource guides centers through the transition process, including EDC implementation, REDCap integration, staff training, and Institutional Review Board coordination. It was iteratively refined based on center feedback to improve clarity, scope, and usefulness. As of this writing, the checklist has been downloaded 94 times since May 2025.

UDSv4 updates webpage

The centralized UDSv4 webpage on the NACC website was updated regularly to reflect newly developed tools and revisions based on user feedback. From June 2024 to June 2025, this page was viewed 9445 times with 7410 active users.

Update emails and newsletters

During the 6‐month transition window, the NACC delivered 12 update emails and newsletters. Broad newsletters were sent to 3430 individuals, and targeted form emails were delivered to 877 admins, data, and clinical core members. These emails averaged an open rate of 28.21%.

UDSv3‐to‐UDSv4 crosswalk tools

Of the 1255 unique variables in the UDSv4 dataset, 48.1% (n = 605) mapped directly to the previous UDS version (UDSv3), and fewer than 1% (n = 11) were only partially mappable. To support backward compatibility, multiple ADRCs requested assistance in mapping UDSv3 data to UDSv4. In response, the EDCS Workgroup created both human‐readable and programmatic crosswalks. For example, the Participant Demographics form (A1) restructured the single multilevel race variable into separate UDSv4 elements (e.g., RACEASIAN, RACEMENA), while the Family History form (A3) preserved conceptual continuity but required substantial updates. The UDSv3 variable MOMPRDX (“Mother – Primary Dx”) became MOMETPR in UDSv4, with changes across question numbers (5a5 → 1a3) and response options (e.g., 050 → 01, 410 → 02, 430 → 07). Most values could be mapped directly (e.g., 050 “Alzheimer's disease dementia” → “Alzheimer's Disease”), but some response codes were collapsed or redefined. These included detailed mapping dictionaries, JSON files, and instrument‐specific rule sets to support integration into REDCap projects or analytic workflows. Crosswalks were developed for 14 UDSv3 forms covering 583 variables, and the human‐readable versions have been downloaded from the NACC site 1005 times since October 2024.

Open technical repository: GitHub

All technical resources – including error‐checking scripts, REDCap project files, and the UDSv3‐to‐v4 crosswalk – are maintained in an open, version‐controlled GitHub repository. This transparent, modifiable resource ensures that centers can tailor tools to their specific environments.

The transparent data quality checks support systems locally implemented by ADRCs and have been utilized over 313 UDSv4 uploads (including reuploads) since the 2025 rollout.

Centralized technical documentation hub

A dedicated EDCS documentation portal consolidates all technical guides, REDCap files, UDSv4 forms in PDF downloadable files, SOPs, and training documents. The portal's structured navigation allows centers to find module‐level resources, detailed upload instructions, and background on the EDCS design and deployment.

Video training and demonstrations

To support self‐guided learning, the NACC developed and disseminated several video tutorials and recorded webinars responding to frequently asked technical questions during pilot testing and working group meetings. These resources included a May 2024 tutorial on streamlining data collection with skip logic, which received 59 views; an April 2024 webinar on submitting UDSv4 data via the ADRC Portal, which received 170 views; and a June 2024 UDSv4 data submission training, which received 109 views. These materials were designed to address common challenges and promote efficient, standardized data collection and submission across the ADRC network.

NIH)‐compliant Data Management Sharing Policy (DMSP) template

In response to NIH DMSP requirements, the EDCS Workgroup developed a DMSP template tailored to UDSv4 data and workflows. The template – issued with a public Digital Object Identifier (DOI) – helps ADRCs comply with federal policy and highlights tools developed under the EDCS initiative.

Real‐time community support: office hours

In response to implementation‐phase concerns raised through various feedback channels, the NACC launched live office hours sessions to provide high‐touch technical support and real‐time walkthroughs of implementation steps. To date, four open office hour sessions have been held, with a total of 18 attendees. Each session was staffed by six to seven moderators who addressed questions about the EDCS system. These sessions were rated highly by participants and proved valuable for real‐time troubleshooting and clarification.

Evidence that we drove adoption of EDC across the ADRC Program

In the final phase of the SCST model, the NACC collaborated with the EDCS Workgroup to support EDCS adoption for transition to UDSv4.

Positive feedback on system usability

The widespread adoption of the EDCS system across the ADRC program has demonstrated the success of a stakeholder‐driven modernization initiative. Survey data, platform usage, and submission activity all point to increased engagement with electronic tools and streamlined workflows that align with center‐level needs. In the January 2025 network‐wide survey, 26 out of 36 ADRCs reported no major obstacles or challenges in preparing for the UDSv4 transition. The remaining 10 sites reported that delays in transitioning were mainly due to institutional requirements or the need to translate UDSv4 forms into other languages, such as Spanish, which are still under development. The April 2025 mid‐transition survey further affirmed the system's usability: of the 21 ADRCs actively collecting UDSv4 data, 86% rated the submission process as very easy, somewhat easy, or neither easy nor difficult. Similarly, 90% felt very prepared, prepared, or at least somewhat prepared to submit data via the ADRC Portal. These findings indicate that the iterative development model produced a system that was both user‐centered and operationally effective.

Increased REDCap adoption

The EDCS initiative catalyzed a marked expansion in REDCap use across the ADRCs, with increased adoption beginning even before the system's formal launch. Following the May 2022 announcement that the NACC would be developing a REDCap‐based EDCS platform for UDSv4, interest in REDCap surged across the network. At that time, only 20 of 37 ADRCs were using REDCap, with nine additional centers expressing interest. Over the next 2 years – well before the platform's launch in January 2025 – REDCap adoption steadily climbed. By June 17, 2025, 27 ADRCs were actively using REDCap, with four more centers in the process of onboarding (Figure 5A).

This early uptake underscores the value of transparency and shared vision: ADRCs began aligning their local infrastructure with the future EDCS model even before the system was operational. Overall, 17 centers requested access to the NACC‐hosted REDCap instance, creating 70 user accounts across 15 ADRCs, signaling growing confidence in centralized, network‐wide tools.

Submission activity mirrored this trend. In December 2024, just before launch, 10 centers had already established UDSv4 REDCap projects, with two actively collecting data and one center submitting data through the prerelease version of EDCS. By June 2025, 14 ADRCs had submitted UDSv4 data on 294 participants through the platform, reflecting rapid uptake following deployment and the system's growing maturity.

The widespread use of REDCap resources further reinforces this trend. Since downloadable REDCap files became available in December 2024, the UDSv4 project XML file has been downloaded 153 times. In parallel, modular REDCap components (e.g., data dictionaries and individual forms) have been downloaded 1481 times, demonstrating broad engagement across the ADRC network.

These data highlight how early communication and shared infrastructure planning laid the groundwork for network‐wide adoption of standardized, interoperable data capture and submission tools.

Expansion of direct‐to‐EDC workflows

Promoting direct‐to‐EDC was a core modernization goal. From March 2022 to October 2023, 10 additional ADRCs planned to adopt direct data entry for UDSv4 – bringing the total to 78% of centers (Figure 5B). This transition reflects a shift from paper‐based workflows toward real‐time validations and structured digital input, improving data quality and efficiency. By April 2025, 21 ADRCs were collecting UDSv4 data using various approaches: 10 with local REDCap, seven with other EDC platforms, and four with NACC‐hosted REDCap. As of mid‐June 2025, that number had grown to 25 ADRCs actively collecting UDSv4 data.

Diverse submission methods in use

Though the ADRC Portal does not directly log submission method types, April 2025 survey data provide insight into planned usage. Of 21 centers that responded, 18 planned to use CSV batch uploads, 10 intended to submit via API, and two anticipated using direct data entry into NACC‐hosted REDCap.

These results suggest that all supported submission methods are being used, aligning with EDCS's flexible, multipathway architecture. As of June 17, 2025, 14 ADRCs had submitted UDSv4 data on 294 participants. These centers represent a cross‐section of the ADRC network, further demonstrating the system's accessibility and adaptability.

DISCUSSION

The EDCS initiative transformed ADRC informatics from paper/PDF to a flexible, interoperable platform that accelerates research. Using a SCST model, it re‐engineered data capture, validation, and API‐based submission across centers, reducing time from visit to analyzable datasets, improving data quality and reproducibility, and aligning with regulatory standards. The result is a scalable system that supports decentralized entry with centralized submission, speeding hypothesis testing, biomarker validation, and multicenter analyses under UDSv4.

Embedding stakeholder needs in system design

A foundational element of the initiative's success was the early and rigorous identification of site‐specific needs across the 36 ADRCs. Structured surveys and collaborative workshops allowed the project team to map technical environments, existing workflows, and operational constraints. These insights directly informed key technical choices, including adopting REDCap as the foundational platform – selected for its widespread use within the network, extensibility, and alignment with HIPAA‐compliant, cloud‐hosted architectures. This choice supported decentralized and centralized deployment models, allowing ADRCs with different technical maturity levels and institutional policies to participate effectively.

This early engagement drove key system innovations, including real‐time data validation, modular REDCap projects, automated NACCID verification, API‐based submission, and cross‐version data harmonization tools. Each reflected practical, site‐informed priorities: improving data quality, minimizing burden, enabling flexibility, and reducing institutional barriers to modernization. Rather than dictating a one‐size‐fits‐all system, the EDCS model emphasized adaptability and inclusivity, which are critical characteristics for implementation across heterogeneous research environments.

A key outcome of the EDCS initiative was its ability to reduce the burden on both participants and staff by streamlining data collection workflows. With the launch of UDSv4, the length and complexity of the forms increased substantially, introducing new domains, deeper phenotyping, and additional social and contextual variables. Without corresponding improvements in data capture tools, this expansion risked overburdening study coordinators and staff while increasing respondent fatigue. The EDCS system addressed this challenge by embedding usability‐focused features into data capture systems such as skip logic, auto‐population of previous visit data for reference, automated coding tools (e.g., RxNorm integration), and real‐time error checking to prevent downstream data issues.

These enhancements allowed sites to collect richer, more nuanced data while reducing redundant manual entry, minimizing correction cycles, and shortening form completion time. Importantly, many of these features were developed in direct response to community feedback and piloting experiences, which surfaced concerns about staff workload and participant burden early in the transition process. By addressing these concerns through iterative system design, the EDCS supported data quality and interoperability, and more sustainable, participant‐friendly research operations – a critical consideration for longitudinal studies in aging populations.

A model for stakeholder‐centric implementation

The SCST implementation model provided the structural framework to support this scale of change. The SCST model emphasizes continuous stakeholder engagement and feedback to inform each system design, testing, and rollout stage. In this initiative, the SCST model was operationalized through a highly participatory structure: three cross‐functional subgroups (Requirements, Development, Documentation & Training), 17 formal surveys over 3 years, regular subgroup and full workgroup meetings, live pilot feedback sessions, and structured evaluations. These mechanisms created a continuous feedback loop between system developers and system users.

By informing our technical decision‐making with real‐time input, we were able to evolve system features in step with ADRC priorities. For instance, growing interest in direct entry into centrally hosted REDCap projects, widespread requests for real‐time error reporting, and concerns about cross‐version data integration led to targeted design enhancements. This stakeholder‐informed approach enhanced transparency, eased the transition process, and promoted shared ownership, each a critical factor for successful implementation in a large‐scale heterogeneous consortium.

Phased rollout and pilot testing as catalysts for success

Our phased implementation strategy structured around SCST principles of identify, iterate, pilot, and implement was central to adoption. The pilot phase focused on UDSv4 data collection workflows and the NACCID system, which served as both testing grounds and feedback accelerators. These pilots revealed critical site‐level variability in REDCap readiness, staffing, and informatics support. Rather than enforcing a rigid transition deadline, NACC adopted a 6‐month rollout window, allowing centers to align the transition with their own regulatory and operational schedules.

The pilot feedback also shaped the development and refinement of tools that enabled smoother adoption: a sandbox environment for testing submission workflows, a centralized REDCap project for rapid deployment, a user‐friendly error dashboard, and expanded technical documentation. These adaptations reflect the importance of flexible planning and participatory problem solving, particularly in large networks where institutional diversity can create major barriers to synchronous rollout.

Building infrastructure for sustainability and scalability

Beyond technical deployment, the EDCS initiative invested heavily in the infrastructure required to support sustainable adoption: centralized documentation hubs, open‐source GitHub repositories, modular SOPs, video tutorials, open office hours, and a Discourse‐powered ADRC Community Forum for asynchronous peer support. These resources addressed emerging questions in real‐time and lowered barriers to system use for both technically advanced and resource‐constrained sites. Their widespread uptake, reflected in usage metrics across nearly every component, demonstrates their value not only as implementation aids but also as critical scaffolding for long‐term infrastructure sustainability.

Moreover, the flexible, modular nature of the EDCS platform positions the ADRC program for future innovation. The REDCap and Flywheel architecture supports additional data modalities, including imaging, biospecimens, and real‐world clinical data. Features such as API‐based submission and variable crosswalk tools provide the backbone for future interoperability, while centralized authentication (via CILogon) and federated access control ensure scalability and compliance.

Lessons learned and areas for continued development

While the EDCS initiative showed strong overall adoption and usability, it also revealed important challenges and lessons learned relevant to similar modernization efforts. REDCap use increased significantly across the ADRC program, rising from 54% to 75% of centers. However, differences in local technical support, staffing continuity, and infrastructure readiness led to varied rollout experiences across sites. Future implementation phases should continue to invest in site‐centered support, including direct technical assistance, ADRC onboarding, and resource‐sharing across centers.

Second, the transition highlighted the importance of proactive communication. Centers frequently noted that access to detailed checklists, sample code, and peer examples greatly enhanced their readiness. Conversely, confusion about submission options occasionally led to bottlenecks. These issues emphasize the importance of early, consistent messaging across leadership, data cores, and study teams.

Third, the SCST model's flexibility proved essential. ADRCs operate within vastly different institutional, regulatory, and staffing environments. By emphasizing pragmatic adaptation rather than rigid standardization, the EDCS initiative maintained engagement and avoided the resistance often seen in top‐down implementation models. This approach, grounded in change management best practices, may be particularly valuable in other NIH‐funded networks seeking to harmonize data systems across disparate settings.

Future directions

The successful implementation of the EDCS system for UDSv4 lays the groundwork for broader infrastructure modernization across the ADRC program. In the next phase, the NACC and its partners plan to extend the EDCS system to support additional data modalities critical to AD/ADRD research, including neuroimaging (magnetic resonance imaging/positron emission tomography), digital health data, EHRs, and fluid biomarker analysis results. Leveraging the platform's modular design and existing REDCap‐Flywheel integration, these expansions will enable streamlined, interoperable data capture pipelines that span clinical, research, and real‐world data sources.

A key future priority is incorporating automated UDS data element extraction from the EHR using the Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources standard. This functionality will enable pulling structured data from EHRs, reviewing them, and transmitting them to the local REDCap UDS database, reducing manual abstraction burden on study coordinators, improving the timeliness of data capture, and enhancing alignment with real‐world clinical practice. Further, EHR‐study database integration opens additional opportunities for optimizing local workflow for UDS visits and data collection. Together, these enhancements will position the ADRC program as a model for scalable, next‐generation data infrastructure, supporting precision medicine, AI‐enabled discovery, and multimodal data integration at a national scale.

CONCLUSION

The UDSv4 EDCS initiative marks a critical turning point for the ADRC program. It demonstrates that sociotechnical, stakeholder‐informed approaches can successfully enable scalable, sustainable modernization of research infrastructure, especially when implemented within a flexible, feedback‐driven framework. By balancing innovation with inclusivity and structure with adaptability, this initiative has positioned the ADRC program to meet the challenges of an increasingly complex data ecosystem. For other national consortia navigating similar transitions, the EDCS model offers a roadmap and a strategic mindset for aligning technical infrastructure with the operational realities, scientific goals, and human needs of collaborative research.

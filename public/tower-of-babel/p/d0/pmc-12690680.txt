PDNO-16 MANAGEMENT OF BRAINSTEM GLIOMAS IN LOW-RESOURCE COUNTRIES : EXPERIENCE OF COTE D’IVOIRE
Authors: J Brou, N Kouassi, G Djonde, R Diaby, L Konan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBrain tumors are the second most common form of cancer in children. Around 15 to 20% of them are located in the brainstem, mainly gliomas. They have an unfavorable prognosis, a two-year survival rate of less than 10%. Our objective was to evaluate the therapeutic course and survival of these patients in a context of limited resources.MethodsThis is a retrospective study concerning six patients suffering from brain stem glioma carried out over one year at the Alassane Ouattara Radiotherapy Center and Yopougon University Hospital. Data including age, sex, WHO score at the time of diagnosis, time to start of radiotherapy, progression of the disease, treatments administered. The results were analyzed. A Kaplan-Meier survival curve was performed to evaluate overall survival.ResultsMedian age of 9.6 years, ranging from 4–25 years. The median survival was 7.5 months, between 4-12 months, three patients had a WHO score of 3, five of our patients started their treatments in less than 3 months. All patients had cranial nerve damage, 2 patients had hydrocephalus and underwent ventricular bypass. 5 patients benefited from radiotherapy sessions at a raté of 2 grays per session, due to the delay in consultation and the financial factor.ConclusionIn low-resource countries, diagnosis of brain tumors is often delayed, impacting patient prognosis. Early diagnosis and better access to treatments are essential to improve outcomes.

Novel logarithmic imputation procedures using multi auxiliary information under ranked set sampling
Authors: Anoop Kumar, Shashi Bhushan, Walid Emam, Yusra Tashkandy, M J S Khan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRanked set sampling (RSS) is known to increase the efficiency of the estimators while comparing it with simple random sampling. The problem of missingness creates a gap in the information that needs to be addressed before proceeding for estimation. Negligible amount of work has been carried out to deal with missingness utilizing RSS. This paper proposes some logarithmic type methods of imputation for the estimation of population mean under RSS using auxiliary information. The properties of the suggested imputation procedures are examined. A simulation study is accomplished to show that the proposed imputation procedures exhibit better results in comparison to some of the existing imputation procedures. Few real applications of the proposed imputation procedures is also provided to generalize the simulation study.Keywords: Missing data, Imputation, Ranked set sampling, Simulation studySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Survey practitioners are always faced with the issue of missing data. The presence of missing data may be the reason in obtaining more efficient estimates of population parameters because of reduced sample size, but also conventional sampling techniques can not be directly enforced to examine data which is available in hand. To date, a number of imputation techniques have been proposed in previous research to use the auxiliary data on the non-response to imputation the missing value of the research variable y.

The problem of missing data may occur either at the estimate stage of parameters or at the stage of sampling design or at both the stages. Hansen and Hurwitz1 was the first who sort out the problem of missing data at the sampling design stage and subsequently2,34 mooted and extended this technique in a mailed questionnaire survey. Several writers have emphasised the significance of auxiliary variables in the process of imputed missing data. The foundational ideas of missing at random (MAR), observed at random (OAR), and parameter distribution (PD) were envisioned by Rubin3 in his seminal research. These concepts provided a basis for drawing information from the data ridden with missing observations. The difference between MAR and missing completely at random (MCAR) approaches was made by Heitjan and Basu4 in their earlier work. To tackle the missing value, various improvements have been proposed over existing imputation procedures using auxiliary information. Bhushan and Pandey7 advocated some optimal imputation procedures to sort out the missing data employing multi auxiliary information. An optimal second call imputation under probability proportional to size sampling was introduced by Sohil et al.6 . Bhushan et al.5 employed SRS to estimate population mean in the presence of missing data. Prasad and Yadav8 developed product type exponential imputation procedures in survey sampling, while, Yadav and Prasad9 suggested generalized class of factor type exponential imputation procedures through simulation approach. Bhushan et al.35 proposed design based synthetic imputation procedures for domain mean. A few notable studies in this field include those by Lee et al.36, Singh and Horn12, Ahmed et al.13, and Diana and Perri14.

Statistics professionals frequently face the challenge of measuring research variables, which can be expensive or complicated. However, unit ranking can be accomplished visually or through other cost-free methods. In these situations, McIntyre11 proposed the idea of RSS as a practical substitute for simple random sampling (SRS); however, he did not offer a strong mathematical foundation for the theory. The necessary mathematical basis for RSS was supplied by Takahasi and Wakimoto15, who examined the idea of McIntyre11. Shahzad et al.16 advocated a new population mean estimator under RSS along with the sensitive issues, while, to deal with sensitivity issues, Shahzad et al.17 suggested regression type estimator for mean estimation under RSS. Some optimal classes of estimators and log type estimators were also suggested for the mean estimation under RSS by Bhushan and Kumar18 and Bhushan and Kumar19, respectively. Kocyigit and Kadilar22 investigated ratio estimators for RSS in the availability of tie information. Khalid et al.21 proposed a new exponential ratio estimator under RSS. Kocyigit and Rather20 developed the new sub-regression type estimator under RSS. Bhushan and Kumar23,24 utilized RSS for the predictive estimation of population mean by proposing difference, ratio, and logarithmic type estimators. Some multi-auxiliary information based logarithmic estimators were suggested by Bhushan and Kumar31 for estimating population mean. Kocyigit26 suggested a novel sub-type mean estimator under RSS employing dual auxiliary variables.

In practice, it is possible for there to be instances where every data set has a minuscule number of observations, and each observation proves to be crucial for reaching an appropriate conclusion. Moreover, utilising such sets of information that contain missing values might taint the outcome and make the estimation process less effective. Bouza and Al-Omari27 developed imputation of missing data under RSS, while handling of missing data under RSS was investigated by Bouza28. Sohail et al.29 suggested a class of ratio type estimators for imputing missing data under RSS. Bhushan et al.30 proposed efficient difference and ratio type imputation procedures under RSS. Bhushan and Kumar25 dealt with the imputation of missing data under RSS utilizing multi auxiliary information, whereas10 suggested RSS imputation procedures in the presence of correlated measurement errors. This study uses the MCAR approach by default for imputation of missing data.

Methodological foundation and notations

In RSS, a parent population of size N units serves as the source of quantification for m simple random samples, each of size m. Now, the units of every randomly selected sample are arranged according to the selected variable. Suppose x1 and x2 are the two auxiliary variables and y is the research variable. The population’s m2 trivariate samples are quantified at random and divided into m sets, with a total size m. At this point, every sample is ordered in relation to either x1 or x2, the auxiliary variables. The unit with the lowest rank of x1, variable y, and x2 related to the lowest rank of x1 are now quantified based on the initial sample. The variables y and x2 linked to the second smallest rank of x1 are measured from the second sample of size m. Until the y and x2 values linked to the highest rank of x1 are quantified from the sample m, the aforementioned process is maintained. One cycle is finished by this technique. The required ranked set sample of size n=mk units is obtained by repeating this process k times.

Out of the selected m units, let r units render the answer for the study variable such that m≥r from the first cycle. Repeat the same process until k such cycles are completed, at which point we will get replies from n1=mkp units from the n=mk chosen units, provided n≥n1. Since it is expected that there is one missing value in the m slashed units, which is easily apparent from the figuration representation provided in Appendix A, let the ranking be done over the additional variable x1 in this case.

Let a finite population U consist of N distinguishable units with mean Y¯=N-1∑i=1Nyi. To get the population mean Y¯, let’s quantify a ranked set sample s of size n=mk from U. Let r possess the responding units out of sampled n units. Let p and (1-p) be the probability that the respondent i either belongs to the responding group A or to the non-responding group A¯ such that s=A+A¯, where, + denotes disjoint union. Every unit possesses an observed value of yi,i∈A; however, the values for the units i∈A¯ are missing and must be imputed to provide the complete data structure required for a successful inference. Also, the auxiliary variable x helps impute the values that are missing.

The traditional imputation techniques and their characteristics under bivariate and multiple auxiliary information setup utilizing RSS were covered in detail in Section “Review of prominent existing imputation procedures”. A simulation study is conducted in Section “Simulation study”, while a real data application is provided in Section Real data applications. Finally, Section “Conclusion” provides a conclusion.

Conclusion

This paper considered some novel logarithmic type imputation procedures and the consequent estimators for the estimation of population mean in situation of missing data under RSS. These imputation procedures are suggested under the assumption of MCAR. The MSE of the proposed consequent estimators has obtained to the first order approximation. The limitations of the proposed imputation procedures have been obtained in terms of efficiency conditions under which the proposed imputation procedures dominate the existing imputation procedures. These efficiency conditions have been evaluated by an extensive simulation study performed over an artificially drawn population for various prespecified values of correlation coefficients. The simulation results are summarized in Table 1 by MSE and PRE. It has been observed from the simulation results that the suggested logarithmic imputation techniques y.iwj,j=1,2,3 outperform their conventional counterparts such as mean imputation y.im, regression imputations y.idj,j=1,2,3, power transformation imputations y.iuj,j=1,2,…,6, and the imputation procedures y.isj,j=1,2,…,9 recommended by Bhushan and Kumar25 using multi auxiliary information under RSS. As the correlation coefficient values increase, the PRE of the suggested imputation techniques y.iwj,j=1,2,3 steadily increases in every strategy, but it still outperforms its traditional equivalents. The simulation results have also indicated that the maximum efficiency for the discussed imputation procedures have found with respect to strategy II for various correlation coefficient values. The results of real data application have shown that the proposed imputation procedures obtained least MSE and highest PRE in comparison to the existing imputation procedures. Therefore, the proposed imputation procedures may be opted to estimate the population mean in presence of missing data under RSS.

In the future studies, to deal with the missing data, utilizing multi-auxiliary information, we hope to develop these imputation procedures under stratified ranked set sampling.

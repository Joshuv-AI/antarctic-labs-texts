Remaining useful life prediction of high-capacity lithium-ion batteries based on incremental capacity analysis and Gaussian kernel function optimization
Authors: Youming Tang, Songfeng Zhong, Ping Wang, Yi Zhang, Yu Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRemaining useful life (RUL) is a key indicator for assessing the health status of lithium (Li)-ion batteries, and realizing accurate and reliable RUL prediction is crucial for the proper operation of battery systems. As high-capacity Li batteries have more complex chemical properties, most of the current RUL prediction methods rely mainly on a priori knowledge to make judgments. As a result, prediction accuracy is not high. In this study, we developed a health indicator-capacity (HI-C) dual Gaussian process regression (GPR) model based on incremental capacity analysis (ICA) and optimized its kernel function to achieve accurate RUL prediction for 280 Ah high-capacity Li batteries. Validation against the United States National Aeronautics and Space Administration’s four battery test datasets showed that the use of the HI-C dual GPR model resulted in a mean absolute percentage error and root mean square error of less than 0.02 and 0.04, respectively, for the four battery-rated capacity predictions. Additionally, this model achieved an absolute error of less than five battery failure turns. Compared with a single model, the HI-C dual GPR model not only had high accuracy but also solved the problem that the HI was not measurable in the actual battery operation, which made it more suitable for RUL prediction of Li batteries.Keywords: High-capacity lithium-ion batteries, Remaining useful life, Incremental capacity curve, Gaussian process regression, Kernel function optimizationSubject terms: Energy storage, Batteries

Introduction

Because of their advantages, such as high energy density and long cycle life, lithium-ion (Li-ion) batteries have become an essential part of our everyday electronic devices1. In addition, the role of Li-ion batteries in the operating costs of energy storage systems cannot be ignored2. With the increase in the number of charge and discharge cycles, however, the complex electrochemical reactions within the battery make the performance of Li-ion batteries decline as capacity decreases and impedance increases, leading to equipment and system failures, and even triggering the risk of thermal runaway, which can result in significant economic losses3. Therefore, accurate prediction of the remaining service life of Li-ion batteries is crucial in the current energy field.

Currently, model-based prediction and data-driven prediction are the two most commonly used methods for Li battery life prediction4,5. Model-based prediction often requires the construction of mathematical or empirical models based on the analysis of the relevant physicochemical reactions within the battery6. For example, Guha et al.7 proposed a remaining useful life (RUL) prediction algorithm based on a fractional-order equivalent circuit model that could be implemented online. The algorithm offered the advantages of real-time and accuracy, but the algorithm model was more complex and its prediction adaptability was poor because of the limitations of test conditions and battery model. Gao et al.8 used an electrochemical model to study the lifetime degradation law of Li-ion batteries for RUL prediction, which was based on electrochemical principles, and modeled the degradation process of Li-ion batteries in a refined way. Although it made the prediction more accurate, the parameter estimation of the model was difficult. Yang et al.9 proposed an RUL prediction method based on the combination of model-based traceless particle filtering and optimal combination strategy, which improved the particle filtering algorithm and overcame the problems of particle degradation and lack of particle diversity to a certain extent. The modeling and solving processes of this method, however, were more computationally intensive. Tian et al.10 proposed a semiempirical model for the battery aging mode, which considered a variety of aging mechanisms of the battery and predicted the RUL of the battery with high accuracy, but the model also had the disadvantage of high computational complexity. Sadabadi et al.11 analyzed the internal parameters of Li-ion batteries by constructing an electrochemical model to better understand the working mechanism of the batteries, but the accuracy of the model was low because the electrochemical reaction was a dynamic and nonlinear process. Šeruga et al.12 introduced new battery-related damage parameters and accomplished real-time prediction of Li battery life using a model-based approach. The introduced new parameters were affected by the test conditions, however, and had poor accuracy13. This model had some limitations because the changes in battery performance could not be accurately tracked.

Compared with model-based methods, most scholars currently prefer to use data-driven methods to predict Li-battery RUL. These methods do not depend on the internal structure of the battery and can directly mine the recession information and the evolution law of the health state of Li-ion batteries on the basis of charge/discharge data, thus reducing the cost of hardware and overcoming the difficulty of obtaining explicit quantitative formulas14. Khelif15 and Qiu et al.16 proposed methods for online estimation of RUL using support vector machines and correlation vector machines, but these traditional methods often have difficulties in feature design and insufficient generalization. Pang et al.17 proposed an RUL prediction method for Li batteries by integrating incremental capacity analysis (ICA) and Gaussian process regression (GPR), which utilized IC curves with high sensitivity to analyze the degradation process of Li batteries. Only a single GPR model was constructed, however, which failed to effectively solve the problem of the unmeasurable health indicator (HI) in the operation of the actual battery. Wu et al.18 used a feed forward neural network to determine the RUL reflected by the voltage profile. This approach could deal with nonlinear features in the voltage profile and predicted the RUL through adaptive learning. Such factors such as data requirements, model design, and training need to be fully considered and addressed, however. Zhou et al.19 combined empirical mode decomposition (EMD) and autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) modeling to predict the capacity global decay that accompanies fluctuations. This approach improved the accuracy of capacity global attenuation prediction by utilizing the multiscale decomposition of EMD and the time-series modeling capability of the ARIMA model. Factors such as data requirements and model parameter selection need to be noted and addressed. Ma et al.20 proposed an improved limit-learning machine algorithm to solve the problem of slow convergence and easily falling into the local optimal solution of the traditional model. The algorithm was more sensitive to the noise and outliers, however, which could easily affect the stability and generalization of the model. Ali Mu et al.21 proposed a support vector machine (SVM) model based on partial discharge data to predict the RUL of Li-ion batteries. This method extracted key features from the voltage and temperature of the partial discharge of the battery to train the SVM model and could accurately predict the RUL with lower storage pressure on the battery management system (BMS). The model ran slowly and was not very efficient, however. Khan N et al.22 predicted the lifetime decay and degradation of Li-ion batteries more accurately using long short-term memory (LSTM) neural networks and solved the problems of gradient vanishing and gradient explosion during training. LSTM neural networks usually require larger scale computational resources for training, however, and thus have certain requirements on computational power and storage resources.

In addition to proposing appropriate modeling algorithms, we need appropriate degradation features extracted from measurable parameters to estimate the state of health (SOH) and predict the RUL23. Therefore, indirect methods have been gradually applied based on parameters that can be measured online in real time, including current, voltage, and temperature. Many scholars have established HIs from the discharge process, such as the discharge voltage difference at equal time intervals24, the average voltage drop25, the sampling entropy of the discharge voltage26, and the replacement entropy27. The method based on the discharge process, however, uses a constant-current (CC) discharge mode, which is limited in practical application because of variations in operating conditions and the external environment. Williard et al.28 measured four characteristics (i.e., capacity, resistance, constant-current charging time length, and constant-voltage charging time length) and compared their effectiveness in estimating SOH. Compared with the discharge process, the charging process was a relatively static state with stable data that was easier to analyze. Therefore, in this study, we extracted measurable degradation features from the CC and constant-voltage (CV) charging processes.

In several recent studies on battery life prediction, hybrid methods have emerged alongside model-based and data-driven approaches. These hybrid methods focus on leveraging the strengths of several different algorithms to improve prediction accuracy and generalization. For example, Meng et al.29 proposed a hybrid method combining EMD and particle filtering (PF) for performing early-stage end-of-life (EOL) prediction of batteries and assessing the uncertainty of the results. Testing of the United States National Aeronautics and Space Administration’s (NASA) battery dataset, which is available online, as well as the University of Maryland’s battery dataset, reveals that this method shows a clear trend of decreasing battery EOL prediction uncertainty with an increasing amount of data, as tested using between 20% and 60% of the dataset. However, the actual EOL of the batteries did not align with the average predicted EOL as the available running data increased.

In addition to this, Meng et al.30 constructed a hybrid framework for battery life prediction and assessing the uncertainty of the results that integrates Gaussian process regression and a Kalman filter. This framework transforms the battery degradation curve time series prediction problem into a virtual degradation rate and acceleration prediction problem through an iterative Gaussian process regression multistep overdetermination prediction strategy using a moving sliding window (SW). The method effectively captures real-time dynamic changes in batteries and provides more comprehensive information for battery management through multi-step prediction, which helps to optimize usage and maintenance strategies, thereby extending battery life and improving performance. Compared to a prediction framework that utilizes EMD and PF, the Gaussian process regression and Kalman filter-based approach better aligns with the true value for the average battery life and exhibits a smaller range of prediction uncertainty. However, the SW technique, while enabling real-time updates, may result in response delays when high-frequency data is present due to the computational demands of frequent updates.

The main contributions of this study are as follows:

(1) The current SOH and future RUL of 280 Ah high-capacity Li-ion batteries were successfully predicted using an HI-C dual Gaussian process regression model based on incremental capacity analysis.

(2) A more sensitive characterization of the degradation in 280 Ah high-capacity Li-ion batteries was obtained by using incremental capacity (IC) curves, rather than traditional charge/discharge curves.

(3) The Gaussian kernel function was optimized by selecting a linear function as the mean value function and adding a periodic covariance function alongside the original squared-exponential covariance function, which improves the model’s predictive ability and reduces the capacity regeneration phenomenon.

(4) A double Gaussian process regression model was constructed that not only improves prediction accuracy, but that also effectively solves the problem of unmeasurable health indicators (HI) in practical applications.

This study is organized as follows: First, we introduce the selection of the dataset and analyze the IC curve based on the dataset to understand the aging process of the battery; next, after analyzing the IC curves of the charging process under different cycles, we extract the characteristics that can reflect the health state of the battery, (i.e., the HI). To validate the ability of the extracted HI to express the degradation state of the battery, we evaluate the correlation between the proposed HI and capacity through qualitative and quantitative analyses. Then, considering the capacity regeneration phenomenon, we optimize the GPR model by combining the kernel function and construct a health indicator-capacity (HI-C) dual GPR model. Validation of the four battery test datasets from NASA shows that the HI-C dual GPR model not only had high prediction accuracy but also solved the problem of the unmeasurable HI during actual battery operation. Finally, we apply the dual GPR model to the RUL prediction of 280 Ah large-capacity Li batteries.

IC curve and HI extraction

We first introduced the IC curve and HI extraction and analyzed the NASA database and a company’s 280 Ah battery aging experiment dataset. Then, we obtained the initial IC curves by calculation and smoothed these curves using a filtering method. Finally, we extracted two HIs (i.e., the peak of the IC curve and the area of the region under the peak) and qualitatively and quantitatively evaluated the correlation between the proposed HIs and battery capacity degradation.

IC curve analysis

The degree of smoothness of the sample data curve had a greater impact on the final prediction results. For example, if the curve fluctuation was large or jagged, it meant that the curve noise was large, which led to a decline in prediction accuracy and it being difficult to determine the characteristic regularities of the battery. To reduce the interference of the noise at this time, we had to smooth the curve, so that the curve transition was smoother, thus improving the prediction accuracy. SG filtering can be used to effectively retain the signal change information after filtering and smoothing. Therefore, we used SG filtering to smooth the initial IC curve. Specifically, the sliding window length used in this paper is set to 51, and the window length is generally chosen to be odd so that the boundary effect can be avoided. This ensures that the number of data points on both sides of window is equal, thus preventing any offset caused by an even-numbered window and thereby allowing for a more accurate reflection of the signal characteristics.

HI extraction

We analyzed the IC curves of the charging process under different cycles and extracted the features that reflected the health state of the battery. As an example, the IC curves of #5 battery and 280 Ah battery in six different cycles are shown in Fig. 3. It was obvious that the IC peaks under different cycling cycles had different shapes, amplitudes, and positions. With the increase in the number of cycles, the peak of the IC curve followed a general trend of attenuation, and the area under the peak of the IC curve also showed a trend of gradual decrease. Therefore, the peak value and the area under the peak value were finally selected as the health factors to reflect the degradation state of the battery.

IC curves under different cycles.

As shown in Fig. 3, extracting the peaks from the SG-smoothed IC curves was relatively simple, but the determination of the area of the region under the peaks required further analysis. The complete IC curves for the charging process of the #5 and 280 Ah batteries are shown in Fig. 3(a) and 3(b), respectively. Figure 3(a) shows that the voltage range was from 3.5 V to 4.2 V, and it was evident that the IC curves did not change significantly in the voltage range of 3.5–3.8 V, whereas they changed significantly in the voltage range of 3.8–4.1 V, which covered the initial charging capacity in different cycles. Therefore, we considered this region to be important for identifying the degradation process of the cell (i.e., it was the area of the area under the peak). Similarly, the IC curve shown in Fig. 3(b) was only in the voltage range of 3.41–3.45 V when the peak of the curve followed a trend of decreasing peak with an increase in the number of cycling laps. Therefore, this region was the area under the peak.

HI analysis

To verify the ability of the extracted peaks (HI1) and the area of the area under the peaks (HI2) to express the degradation state of the battery, we evaluated the correlation between the proposed HIs and the capacity through qualitative and quantitative analyses.

Qualitative analysis

For the qualitative analyses, we used graphs to analyze the consistency of trends between characteristics and capacity. Figure 4 shows the normalized characteristics and the corresponding final discharge capacity for the four NASA cells as well as for the 280 Ah battery under all charge cycles. As shown in the figure, NASA’s four batteries followed essentially the same general trend in health characterization indicators and actual capacity except for the early cycling cycles, which better reflected the fluctuations in battery capacity. Both the health characterization index and the actual capacity of the 280 Ah battery also decreased gradually with the increase in the number of cycle turns, but the fit between the peak area and the actual capacity was poor. The possible reason for this was that during the charging and discharging process, there were losses such as polarization effect and electrolyte diffusion in the 280 Ah batteries, and these losses had an effect on the relationship between the area under the peak and the capacity. As the number of cycles increased, the losses gradually accumulated, leading to a decrease in the consistency between area and capacity. Therefore, we also needed to conduct a quantitative analysis to further investigate the correlation between the proposed health characterization index and capacity.

The GPR model can also be used to improve the prediction accuracy and robustness of the model by introducing prior knowledge and tuning the kernel function. Therefore, we proposed an ICA-based HI-C dual GPR model and optimized its kernel function for RUL prediction of Li-ion batteries.

Because the battery capacity degradation in both datasets followed a dynamic fluctuating downward trend, we considered two different capacity variations of the Li-ion batteries to ensure that the prediction was more accurate. RUL prediction framework

After obtaining the measurable HI sequence data for the first n loops, we built a one-dimensional GPR model (i.e., the HI-GPR model) using the number of training loop turns and HIs as inputs and outputs, respectively. Based on the HI prediction results from the HI-GPR model, we obtained the corresponding capacity prediction results from the capacity prediction model (also known as the C-GPR model). The C-GPR model was a multi-input single-output GPR model that was trained by setting several different HIs and capacities as inputs and outputs, respectively.

Results and discussion

We validated and analyzed the results of NASA’s four 18,650 cells as well as the 280 Ah cell RUL predictions. First, to analyze the prediction performance of the optimized HI-C dual GPR model, we made predictions separately for different starting points. Second, we verified the validity of the HIs and optimization models proposed in this study by comparing and analyzing the RUL prediction experiments for the four 18,650 cells. Finally, based on the partial cycle aging data of the 280 Ah battery, we applied the proposed prediction method to predict the final cycle failure number.

RUL prediction for 18,650 batteries

To analyze the RUL prediction performance of the HIs and optimized models proposed in this study, we designed experiments with different prediction starting points. The experimental design was as follows: we selected the data from the first 60 cycles, the first 80 cycles, and the first 100 cycles as the training set (i.e., the starting points for prediction were 61, 81, and 101, respectively). In addition, we set the failure threshold of the battery to 70% of the rated capacity. In this study, the capacity failure threshold for batteries #5, #6, and #18 was 1.4 Ah, and a failure threshold line was not given because battery #7 did not reach the failure threshold.

Figure 6 shows the prediction results for each of the four batteries at the prediction start points 61, 81, and 101. It was evident that the original capacity decay curve was affected by the capacity regeneration phenomenon, and the capacity decay followed a downward trend of local periodic dynamic fluctuations. The prediction curve obtained from the model by optimizing the Gaussian kernel function was well resolved by this periodic fluctuation. In addition, the results in these figures show that the predicted curves for the four cells at the predicted starting points 81 and 101 were closer to the actual capacity degradation curves than the predicted curves at starting point 61.

Table 3 shows the predicted performance evaluation results for the four batteries at the predicted starting points 61, 81, and 101. The RMSE as well as the MEA were less than 0.04. In addition, the AE values for the number of battery failure cycles were less than five cycles at three different predicted starting points. Therefore, the HIs and optimization models proposed in this study had better results on the prediction of the current SOH and future RUL of Li batteries. These findings can be applied to the field of high-capacity Li-ion batteries.

RUL prediction for 280 Ah battery

Because the HIs and optimization models proposed in this study showed good prediction results on the NASA battery dataset, we applied them to 280 Ah high-capacity Li-ion batteries. We evaluated the prediction accuracy of the proposed method for the current SOH of high-capacity Li batteries and completed the prediction of the future SOH decline trajectory (i.e., RUL). We used a company’s 280 Ah large-capacity Li-ion battery cycle aging dataset. Because the large-capacity Li-ion batteries had to spend more than 6 h for a complete charge/discharge cycle, we obtained only the cycle aging data for 1000 laps. The 1000 laps of the cycle aging data followed the same design for the experiments with different prediction starting points. The experimental design was as follows: We selected the data from the first 400 cycles, the first 600 cycles, and the first 800 cycles as the training set (i.e., the starting points for prediction were 401, 601, and 801, respectively). In addition, because the given aging data for the 280 Ah battery did not reach the failure threshold, we set the failure threshold to 70% of the rated capacity (i.e., 196 Ah).

Figure 7 shows the present-day SOH prediction results for the 280 Ah battery at prediction starting points 401, 601, and 801. It was evident that the prediction curve was close to the actual capacity decay curve. In addition, the prediction curves at the prediction starting points 601 and 801 were closer to the actual capacity decay curve than the prediction curve at starting point 401.

Conclusion

RUL prediction can be used to determine the health status of a battery, which is important for BMS. Because RUL prediction is currently less applied in the field of large-capacity Li-ion batteries and prediction accuracy is not high, we used an ICA-based HI-C dual GPR model to predict the current SOH as well as the future RUL of 280 Ah large-capacity Li-ion batteries. The main contributions and innovations of this study are as follows: First, we extracted two HIs from the IC curve, namely the peak and the area under the peak. We verified our results through qualitative and quantitative evaluation, in which the proposed HI successfully reflected the degradation trend of the battery. Second, on the basis of optimizing the Gaussian kernel function (i.e., choosing a linear function as the mean function and adding a periodic covariance function to the original squared-exponential covariance function), we improved the ability of multistep prediction of the GPR model and reduced the impact of the capacity regeneration phenomenon. For the prediction of SOH of Li-ion batteries, the MAPE of NASA batteries was less than 0.02, and the RMSE and MAE were lower than 0.04; for the 280 Ah batteries, the MAPE was less than 0.001, and the RMSE and MAE were lower than 0.4. For the prediction of RUL, the AE value of NASA batteries was within five turns—that is, the error of the actual number of failed turns and the predicted number of failed turns were within five turns.

These results showed that the method proposed in this study was accurate and effective in RUL prediction. Based on this result, we applied this model to the RUL prediction of 280 Ah high-capacity Li-ion batteries. The results showed that the number of turns for the batteries to reach the failure threshold was 2491, 3259, and 3413 at the three designed prediction starting points of 401, 601, and 801, respectively.

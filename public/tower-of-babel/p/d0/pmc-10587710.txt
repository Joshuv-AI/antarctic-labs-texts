Citrus dataset for image classification
Authors: Mei-Ling Huang, You-An Chen
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractCitrus fruits are a specialty of the subtropical region and are currently the most widely cultivated and produced fruits in Taiwan. They contain a wealth of vitamins and minerals, including vitamin C, potassium, magnesium, and more, offering anti-inflammatory and antioxidant benefits. Citrus plants are among the economically significant fruits in Taiwan, and there are several citrus species that share a similar appearance. We have constructed a database containing the four most commonly purchased citrus varieties in the market. This database comprises a total of 1379 original images, which have been expanded to 7584 images using six different data augmentation methods. We have chosen three Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) models that have achieved an accuracy rate exceeding 95 % in classifying the four varieties of citrus fruits.Keywords: Citrus, Image classification, Augmentation

Objective

The slight variations in color and size among different citrus varieties can make it challenging for consumers to effectively differentiate them based on appearance alone. As a result, unscrupulous merchants sell citrus fruits at unfair prices to consumers. We have created a citrus image dataset to introduce common citrus species in Taiwan, including Murcott, Ponkan, Tankan, and Tangerine, aiming to assist consumers in identifying these citrus species. In addition, this dataset can support researchers who conduct research on different machine learning or deep learning algorithms on classification of images with high similarity.

Data Description

The dataset is divided into the following four categories according to their varieties including Murcott, Ponkan, Tankan, and Tangerine. Fig. 1 shows the examples of the four categories. The image format is JPG with 3648*2736 pixels, and a total of 1379 original images are collected. The number of image is increased to 7584 through six data augmentation methods. The dataset is divided into training set, verification set, and test set according to the ratio of 70:20:10 for researchers to train their deep learning models. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

This dataset is processed through four steps: image acquisition, image preprocessing, image augmentation, and image classification. The framework of the proposed pipeline is illustrated in Fig. 3. The processing steps of the citrus fruit image dataset are described as follows:

(1) Image acquisition

The citrus image dataset was captured using a Sony DSC-HX300 camera on a sunny day. Multiple angles were photographed from distances ranging between 200-500 mm from the citrus specimens. All image format is JPG, all image pixels are 3648*2736, a total of 1379 original images. Table 2 presents the photo shooting conditions.

(3) Image augmentation

To enhance the quantity and quality of images in the training dataset, this paper employed six data augmentation methods for the training and validation of the four citrus fruit images. These methods include horizontal image flipping, increasing image brightness, enhancing image contrast, boosting image color, and rotating images by 30 degrees. [1,2]. All image format is JPG, all image pixels are 3648*2736. A total of 7584 images were acquired after data augmentation. Fig. 4, Fig. 5 shows the examples of data augmentation. (4) Data classification

We selected VGG16, ResNet50 and DenseNet121 models to distinguish four varieties of citrus. VGGNet was developed by the computer vision research group of Oxford University [3]. It includes 5 convolutional layers, 3 fully connected layers, and a softmax output layer. Each convolutional layer is separated by a maximum pooling layer. ResNet adds residual learning, which solves the problem of gradient and precision drop training sets in deep networks, enabling the network to become deeper and deeper, while ensuring accuracy and speed control [4]. DenseNet consists of multiple dense blocks, the residual structure of the bottleneck layer is used between layers, and the performance is improved by feature reuse [5]. The classification results of each model before and after data enhancement are shown in Tables 5 and 6. The classification accuracies generated from the three models are close both before and after data augmentation. The ResNet50 model using data after augmentation dominates the other two models. The highest accuracy, recall, F1-sore and precision from ResNet50 model are 0.9855, 0.9821, 0.9839 and 0.9866, respectively (Table 6). Figs. 5 and 6 show the confusion matrix diagrams for each model before and after data augmentation, respectively. The classification accuracy achieves 100 % for Tankan before data augmentation from VGG16, ResNet50 and Densenet121 models; both Ponkan and Tankan are 100 % correctly classified before and after data augmentation from three models.

Comparison of anesthetic effects of different sedative drugs combined with remifentanil for general anesthesia in bronchoscopy: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Ting Wang, Ningkang Li, Xiaoqin Li, Runjiang Xuan, Xiaoning Zhu, Yun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundElectronic bronchoscopy, as an important means of invasive diagnosis, exerts strong stimulation on anatomical structures such as the pharynx, glottis, carina. Therefore, selecting an appropriate anesthesia regimen not only enhances patient comfort and satisfaction but also guarantees the safety and the smooth running of the operation. This study aimed to compare the clinical anesthetic effects of different sedative drugs combined with remifentanil-based general anesthesia in patients undergoing bronchoscopy.MethodsA total of 120 patients scheduled for elective painless electronic bronchoscopy were randomly assigned to the ciprofol group (Group C), etomidate group (Group E), and remimazolam group (Group R). The primary observation indicator was the incidence of intraoperative hypotension. Secondary observation indicators included postoperative recovery time and the incidence of perioperative adverse events (such as nausea and vomiting, cough, bronchospasm, dizziness).ResultsThe incidence of hypotension was 45.0% in Group C, 50.0% in Group R, and 17.9% in Group E. The incidence of hypotension in Group E was significantly lower than that in Groups C and R (χ² = 9.949, P = 0.007). The main perioperative adverse responses were postoperative cough, nausea and vomiting, bronchospasm, and dizziness. There was no statistically significant difference in cough severity among the three groups (χ² = 3.26, P = 0.776). The incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) was 7.50% in Group C, 7.50% in Group R, and 46.15% in Group E. The incidence of PONV in Group E was significantly higher than the other two groups (χ² = 24.331, P < 0.001). No significant differences were observed in recovery time and laryngeal mask removal time across the groups.ConclusionEtomidate demonstrates superior hemodynamic stability but carries a higher risk of PONV. In contrast, ciprofol and remimazolam offer better postoperative comfort. For patients at high risk of hemodynamic instability, etomidate may be preferred with enhanced PONV prophylaxis; for those at high risk of PONV or desiring rapid and comfortable recovery, ciprofol or remimazolam are more suitable options.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400084332). Background

Electronic bronchoscopy is a key technique in the diagnosis and treatment of respiratory diseases. With advancements in imaging resolution and interventional capabilities, its clinical applications continue to expand [1, 2]. During the procedure, the pharynx, glottis, and carina are subjected to intense mechanical stimulation, often triggering adverse responses such as coughing, hemodynamic fluctuations, and respiratory tract spasms [3]. Inadequate anesthesia management significantly increases the risk of perioperative complications, which can compromise procedural success, patient comfort, and postoperative recovery [4].

In recent years, novel sedative drugs such as ciprofol and remimazolam have shown promising clinical potential. Ciprofol, an optimized propofol derivative, has demonstrated non-inferior sedative efficacy compared to propofol in bronchoscopy, with reduced injection pain and improved patient satisfaction [5]. It’s particularly suitable for patients sensitive to hemodynamic changes or with lower pain thresholds [6, 7]. Remimazolam, an ultra-short-acting benzodiazepine, features linear pharmacokinetics and the advantage of specific antagonism by flumazenil [8]. It causes minimal hemodynamic impact, is well-tolerated by elderly patients, and supports rapid postoperative cognitive recovery [9–11]. When combined with remifentanil via target-controlled infusion, etomidate can provide relatively stable hemodynamics and deep sedation, making it a widely-used regimen in clinical practice [12]. Although each of these three sedative drugs has distinct advantages, existing literature primarily focuses on single-drug comparisons or comparisons with traditional anesthetic agents (propofol). To date, no published study has systematically compared ciprofol, remimazolam, and etomidate with respect to hemodynamic stability, postoperative adverse events, and recovery profiles during general anesthesia for electronic bronchoscopy. This three-way comparison represents a critical gap in the literature, as bronchoscopy anesthesia remains highly challenging and clinically significant. Therefore, the present study was designed to fill this gap and provide evidence to guide individualized anesthetic selection for patients with varying risk profiles.

Materials and methods

Study design

This single-blind, randomized controlled trial was conducted at the People’s Hospital of Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region between May and November 2024. The trial protocol was approved by the hospital’s Institutional Review Board (Approval No.: [2024]-LL-051) and was prospectively registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No.: ChiCTR2400084332; Date: May 14, 2024). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and reported following the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines.

Trial registration and reporting

This trial was prospectively registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400084332) on May 14, 2024. The registered protocol included five intervention groups: remimazolam, etomidate, ciprofol, ciprofol combined with remimazolam, and etomidate combined with remimazolam. The present manuscript reports the results of three of these groups (ciprofol, remimazolam, and etomidate), as these represent the most commonly used and clinically relevant regimens in current practice. Data from the remaining two combination groups will be reported separately in future studies.

The primary outcome was registered as the incidence of hypotension during both the intraoperative and post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) periods. In accordance with the peer review process, this outcome was refined to incidence of intraoperative hypotension to improve clinical precision and consistency with the sample size calculation.

Regarding the blinding procedure, the study was conducted as a single-blind trial. Due to the distinct physical properties of the three study drugs (e.g., appearance, solubility, foaming characteristics), complete blinding of the attending anesthesiologist was not feasible. However, patients remained blinded to group allocation throughout the study, and all postoperative outcome assessments were performed by an independent researcher who was blinded to group assignment. This is consistent with the registered protocol and has been clarified in the Methods section.

All analyses and outcomes reported for the three groups are consistent with the registered protocol. Any modifications made during the review process were intended to enhance reporting clarity and did not affect the validity of the findings.

Patient recruitment

Inclusion criteria: Age between 18 and 80 years; Body mass index (BMI) between 18 and 25 kg/m²; American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I - III; Scheduled for elective electronic bronchoscopy under general anesthesia; provided informed consent in writing after being fully informed about the study. Exclusion criteria: Known allergy or sensitivity to the study drugs; Uncontrolled/malignant hypertension, hypotension (systolic blood pressure < 100 mmHg or mean arterial pressure < 65 mmHg) or severe ischemic heart disease; Obstructive sleep apnea or anticipated difficult airway; Baseline oxygen saturation < 90%; Asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV₁) ≤ 1.0 L; Hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh class C) or renal insufficiency (requiring dialysis); Chronic pain patients with long-term use of analgesics/psychotropic drugs, monoamine oxidase inhibitors/antidepressants, or alcohol abuse; Pregnancy; Epilepsy or psychiatric disorders.

Methods

Sample size calculation: Sample size was calculated using PASS 15.0 software (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA). In the pilot study, the incidence of intraoperative hypotension was 45% in the ciprofol group, 50% in the remimazolam group, and 18% in the etomidate group. The most pronounced difference was observed between the etomidate group and the other two groups (18% vs. 45%/50%, effect size approximately 30%). Using a two-sided significance level (α = 0.05) and a statistical power (1-β = 80%), PASS software calculated that 36 patients per group were required. Considering a 10% dropout rate, we ultimately enrolled 40 patients per group, resulting in a total sample size of 120 patients. To our knowledge, no published study has simultaneously compared these three drugs regarding hypotension incidence during general anesthesia for bronchoscopy, highlighting the novelty of this trial.

Randomization and blinding: Eligible patients were randomly assigned to the ciprofol group (Group C), remimazolam group (Group R), or etomidate group (Group E) using a computer-generated random number table. Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 software

(IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analysis. Measurement data were analyzed according to their distribution characteristics. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), compared across multiple groups using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), with pairwise comparisons performed using the LSD-t test. Non-normally distributed data were expressed as median (interquartile range), compared using the Kruskal-Wallis H test. Categorical variables are presented as frequency (percentage) and analyzed using the chi-square test (χ² test). For repeated measures data, repeated measures ANOVA was used to evaluate the main effects of group and time, as well as their interaction effect. The significance level was set at α = 0.05. A P < 0.001 indicated a highly statistically significant difference, P < 0.01 indicated a statistically significant difference, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and P > 0.05 indicated no significant difference. All statistical tests were two-sided. Subgroup analyses were not pre-specified; the decision tree analysis for PONV risk factors was an exploratory analysis.

Discussion

Electronic bronchoscopy is a key modality in the diagnosis and management of respiratory diseases, and its success critically depends on safe and effective anesthesia [13, 14]. While general anesthesia ensures optimal procedural conditions and patient comfort [15], it carries inherent risks such as hemodynamic instability, respiratory depression, and drug-specific side effects [16]. Etomidate, a traditional sedative agent, is well known for preserving cardiovascular stability but is associated with drawbacks including myoclonus, adrenocortical suppression [17, 18], and a relatively high incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV). In recent years, newer intravenous anesthetics—remimazolam and ciprofol—have emerged as favorable alternatives for bronchoscopy anesthesia due to their rapid onset, short duration of action, predictable pharmacokinetics, and minimal respiratory depression [7, 14].

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first randomized controlled trial to simultaneously compare ciprofol, remimazolam, and etomidate for general anesthesia in bronchoscopy. This study compared the anesthetic efficacy of these three agents, each combined with remifentanil, during laryngeal mask-based general anesthesia for bronchoscopy. Our findings are of particular clinical significance as they assess, for the first time, the difference in the incidence of post-induction hypotension and other key outcomes among these three sedative agents. All three regimens enabled rapid and reliable induction and maintenance of anesthesia, with no serious complications observed, indicating overall safety and feasibility. However, notable differences were observed in hemodynamic performance and postoperative adverse effects: the ciprofol and remimazolam groups exhibited greater blood pressure fluctuations, whereas the etomidate group had a higher incidence of nausea and vomiting. These findings suggest that in clinical practice, a single anesthetic agent may not be suitable for all patients. It is essential to develop individualized anesthesia plans based on the patient’s baseline conditions, hemodynamic status, and procedural characteristics to achieve the goal of comfortable sedation and enhanced recovery after procedures [19]. The following discussion interprets the clinical implications with respect to the pharmacological properties, hemodynamic effects, and clinical applicability of these three agents.

From the perspective of individualized anesthesia, the formulation of an anesthesia regimen should be based on a systematic assessment of the patient’s baseline status. For patients with hypertension, coronary artery disease, or cardiac insufficiency, maintaining intraoperative hemodynamic stability is paramount [20–22]. For individuals with high-risk factors for PONV—such as those who are female, non-smokers, have a history of PONV, or are expected to receive postoperative opioids—prioritizing postoperative comfort and rapid recovery becomes critical. Therefore, a balanced evaluation of the distinct pharmacological profiles of available anesthetic drugs is necessary during clinical decision-making to optimize patient outcomes.

Comparison of hemodynamic stability

Maintaining hemodynamic stability is a core objective of perioperative anesthesia management. This study shows that etomidate offers significant advantages in this regard, with a markedly lower incidence of hypotension (17.9%) compared to the ciprofol group (45%) and the remimazolam group (50%). This observation aligns with findings from multiple previous studies [23, 24]. The hemodynamic stability conferred by etomidate can be attributed to its unique pharmacological profile: it has minimal impact on the sympathetic nervous system and baroreceptor reflex function, effectively preserves peripheral vascular resistance, and exerts negligible direct suppression on myocardial contractility [25].

In contrast, both ciprofol and remimazolam were associated with higher rates of hypotension. Ciprofol, a novel GABA-A receptor agonist, induces hypotension through mechanisms similar to propofol, primarily mediated by nitric oxide-induced vasodilation and central inhibition of sympathetic outflow [26]. Although ciprofol exhibits less direct myocardial depression than propofol and has a higher therapeutic index, its vasodilatory effect—particularly when combined with remifentanil, which further suppresses sympathetic tone—can result in more pronounced drops in blood pressure during induction. Dynamic monitoring in this study also suggested a trend toward greater blood pressure variability in the ciprofol group compared to the remimazolam group. Remimazolam acts predominantly via central mechanisms and has weaker direct vasodilatory effects [27], which might explain its relatively smaller reductions in blood pressure at certain time points and its comparatively stable hemodynamic profile observed in this study. Similar results have been reported previously [28].

Risk of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV)

PONV is a key determinant of postoperative recovery quality and patient satisfaction. In this study, the PONV incidence in the etomidate group was as high as 46.2%, significantly exceeding that in the ciprofol and remimazolam groups (both 7.5%). This rate not only exceeds the commonly reported range of 10%-30% in the literature [29, 30] but also raises notable concerns regarding the clinical use of etomidate. The underlying mechanism is likely multifactorial: First, etomidate causes prolonged postoperative adrenocortical suppression by irreversibly inhibiting 11β-hydroxylase, resulting in reduced cortisol levels [24]; cortisol deficiency significantly lowers the central threshold for vomiting. Second, bronchoscopy in this study was brief (approximately 10 min), etomidate has a relatively long elimination half-life (~ 60 min), meaning the drug active during the high-risk period for PONV. In contrast, both ciprofol and remimazolam demonstrated low PONV rates in this study. Their rapid metabolism and lack of endocrine effects make them more favorable options for patients at high risk for PONV.

Comparison of postoperative recovery time

With regard to postoperative recovery, although no statistically significant differences in recovery times were observed among the three groups, the clinical relevance warrants further consideration in light of their differing metabolic profiles. Both ciprofol and remimazolam are characterized by rapid clearance, which theoretically supports faster emergence and recovery. However, this study did not clearly demonstrate such an advantage, potentially due to limitations in study design, dosing regimens, or the choice of outcome measures. Future studies could refine the assessment criteria for recovery quality to more comprehensively capture the differential effects of anesthetic agents on postoperative rehabilitation.

Based on the above findings, individualized anesthesia decision-making is of critical importance. For patients at high risk of hemodynamic instability (e.g., those with hypertension, coronary artery disease, cardiac insufficiency, or elderly individuals), the etomidate-remifentanil regimen, due to its superior hemodynamic stability, can be prioritized. However, this approach should be accompanied by proactive PONV prophylaxis, such as the combined use of dexamethasone and a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist, to mitigate its adverse effects.

For patients at elevated risk of PONV, the ciprofol-remifentanil or remimazolam-remifentanil regimens offer distinct advantages. In cases where hypotension is also a concern, remimazolam may provide relatively more stable hemodynamics compared to ciprofol. Clinicians should conduct a comprehensive evaluation of patient’s comorbidities, hemodynamic reserve, and PONV risk factors to develop tailored anesthesia strategies that align with the principles of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS), thereby balancing procedural safety and patient comfort.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, although the sample size met the requirements for preliminary comparisons, it may be underpowered to detect subtle intergroup differences. Second, the single-center design may limit the generalizability of the results. Additionally, patients were not stratified according to cardiovascular risk or PONV risk, and no long-term follow-up was performed, which restricts the ability to evaluate the prolonged impact of anesthetic agents on recovery outcomes. Future studies should build on these results by conducting multicenter, large-sample prospective trials with risk-stratified allocation of anesthetic regimens to validate the clinical efficacy of individualized anesthesia approaches.

Conclusion

This study confirms that the etomidate-remifentanil combination exhibits optimal performance in maintaining hemodynamic stability but is associated with a significantly higher risk of PONV. In contrast, the ciprofol-remifentanil and remimazolam-remifentanil combinations confer greater benefits in minimizing PONV and enhancing postoperative comfort. Clinically, the choice of anesthetic regimens should be individualized based on the patient’s hemodynamic status and PONV risk, supplemented by appropriate preventive interventions, to ensure both safety and comfort during bronchoscopy procedures. Given that bronchoscopy anesthesia is highly challenging and clinically significant, our findings provide valuable evidence for clinicians to make informed, individualized anesthesia decisions when selecting among etomidate, ciprofol, and remimazolam.

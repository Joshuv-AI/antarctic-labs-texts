Editors’ Pick in November 2025
Authors: Hee-Jin Yang
Published: 2025

Among the 15 papers published in the November issue of Journal of Korean Neurosurgical Society (JKNS) 2025, the following two papers, which deserve attention from readers, are selected by the editorial boards.

Neuroprotective effects of human adipose tissue-derived mesenchymal stromal cells against radiation-induced neural damage : a comparative in vitro study [3]

Radiotherapy is a very important treatment modality for primary or metastatic brain tumors, and arteriovenous malformations. Despite its effectiveness, radiotherapy has several adverse reactions, including acute edema, demyelination, and delayed radiation necrosis. While various techniques have been developed to accurately deliver the maximum possible radiation dose to the target while reducing the radiation exposure to surrounding normal tissues, it remains extremely challenging to completely avoid irradiating healthy tissue, and the resulting complication rate is a significant concern.

The precise mechanisms underlying histopathological changes observed after radiotherapy are not fully understood. Acute-phase genes, such as cytokines and intercellular adhesion molecule-1, are known to play a significant role in the hours immediately after radiation exposure. Apoptosis also contributes significantly to radiation-induced damage, with reports indicating an increase in apoptosis from 1 week to 8 weeks post-irradiation. Given the permanent loss of neurons caused by radiation, it is necessary to develop strategies to avoid this detrimental effect.

Preclinical studies have demonstrated that the intravenous administration of adipose tissue-derived mesenchymal stromal cells (ADMSCs) in animal models of brain hemorrhage can reduce inflammatory responses and neurodegeneration [2]. They are known to secrete factors inhibiting apoptosis. Although the effectiveness of ADMSCs in stroke and spinal cord injury, their application in radiation-induced damage remains limited.

Rat cortical neurons, human glioblastoma cells (U87 cell line), and ADMSCs were exposed to radiation doses ranging from 3 Gy to 40 Gy. Co-cultures of irradiated neurons with ADMSCs or their secretomes were assessed for apoptotic and inflammatory markers. Higher radiation doses (30–40 Gy) significantly increased apoptosis and decreased the viability of cortical neurons and U87 cells.

Co-culture with ADMSCs reduced the levels of apoptosis markers, particularly Bax and cleaved caspase-3, and promoted cell survival. Direct co-culture provided more pronounced protection than did ADMSC secretome treatment, suggesting that cell-to-cell interactions are crucial for neuroprotection.

This study underscores the potential of human ADMSCs as a therapeutic intervention for radiation-induced brain damage. By reducing inflammation and apoptosis, and promoting neuronal survival, ADMSCs are a viable option to mitigate the adverse effects of radiotherapy. These findings also open the door for further exploration of ADMSCs for treating other forms of neurological damage such as traumatic brain injury and neurodegenerative diseases.

The transfusion timing of plasma and red blood cells in a 1 : 1 ratio is related with survival and functional outcomes in multiple trauma patients with severe traumatic brain injury [1]

Severe bleeding that occurs after an injury causes a chain reaction of acidosis, hypothermia, and coagulopathy, also known as the lethal triad, leading to death. For more than 50 years, studies have been conducted on the management of coagulopathy induced by severe traumatic injury. High ratio plasma transfusion, balanced transfusion, massive transfusion, and whole blood transfusion have been proposed as strategies to correct trauma-induced coagulopathy. There have been contradictory reports on the effect of high plasma to red blood cells (RBC) transfusion.

They analyzed the Korean Trauma Data Bank (KTDB) from two level I trauma centers from March 2016 to December 2022. The two trauma centers were selected based on the 24/7 availability of dedicated neurosurgical teams, and for carrying out neurosurgical interventions in accordance with standardized national trauma evaluation criteria. Among the recruited patients, the patients were divided into two groups, “Early group”, who received transfusions of more than four units of packed RBC (pRBC) during first 4 hours, and the “Catch-up group”, who received more than 10 units during the first 24 hours. Each group was divided according to the transfusion protocol : a plasma-to-pRBC ration less than 1 : 1 and a ratio equal to or greater than 1 : 1.

Among the 532 patients included in the study, mortality rates did not differ significantly between the 1 : 2 and 1 : 1.5 transfusion ratios; however, a significant difference was observed at the 1 : 1 ratio. In the early group, outcomes showed no significant differences. Logistic regression analysis for 30-day mortality identified several independent risk factors : advanced age, low Glasgow coma scale (GCS) scores, high Abbreviated injury scale (AIS) Head scores, and a plasma-to-pRBC ratio <1 : 1. For the catch-up group, the odds ratio for favorable GCS (≥4) at a ratio of ≥1 : 1 was 1.61 (95% confidence interval [CI], 1.05–2.48), and the odds ratio for 30-day mortality was 0.60 (95% CI, 0.38–0.93) when comparing ≥1 : 1 ratios to <1 : 1 ratios. Due to the limitations of the registry analysis study, the reason for the improvement in outcome in the catch-up group cannot be fully explained. Nonetheless, these findings suggest that sustained high-ratio plasma transfusion may be more effective for stabilizing platelets and maintaining blood-brain barrier integrity than an early, aggressive high-ratio transfusion strategy.

This study is significant in two respects : first, it demonstrates that not only the volume but also the composition of transfused blood influences patient outcomes in severe trauma, and second, the data was sourced from the KTDB, which collects treatment outcomes from Korean regional trauma centers. It is anticipated that future research utilizing the KTDB will further illuminate the unique characteristics of trauma care in Korea.

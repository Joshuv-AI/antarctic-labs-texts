Effectiveness of 3D-Slicer simulated neuro-navigation assisted minimally invasive neuro-endoscopic surgery for basal ganglia hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage
Authors: Jiachen Cai, Yukun Hu, Guangliang Zeng, Zhenhua Shi, Junxiang Wang, Yitao Miao, Qi Shao, Yun Qiu, Xin Jin, Mingjie Gong, Hongqiang Zhang, Jie Wang, Tingwang Jiang, Guoping Shao, Chengfa Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe study aimed to compare the effectiveness of endoscopic surgery assisted by 3D-Slicer with traditional craniotomy for treating basal ganglia hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage (HICH).MethodsA total of 133 individuals diagnosed with hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage in the basal ganglia region underwent surgical intervention in the Department of Neurosurgery at Affiliated Changshu Hospital of Nantong University from January 2018 to June 2023. Based on the surgical approach, participants were categorized into two cohorts: 76 patients who underwent endoscopic procedures and 57 who received traditional craniotomy. Postoperative hematoma clearance rates were quantified through volumetric analysis using 3D-Slicer software. Additionally, intergroup analyses were conducted to evaluate differences in surgical duration, hemorrhage volume during operation, Glasgow Coma Scale assessments at postoperative day 7, length of hospital stay and modified Rankin Scale score 3 months after surgery.ResultsThere were no statistically significant differences in preoperative baseline characteristics between the endoscopy and craniotomy groups (p > 0.05). However, the endoscopy group demonstrated superior surgical and clinical outcomes compared to the craniotomy group. Specifically, the endoscopy group achieved a significantly higher hematoma evacuation rate (90.0% ± 3.9% vs. 82.8% ± 4.9%, p < 0.01), a shorter operative time (1.5 ± 0.8 h vs. 4.9 ± 1.6 h, p < 0.01), and significantly reduced intraoperative blood loss (91.9 ± 89.1 mL vs. 593.6 ± 592.3 mL, p < 0.01). Postoperatively, the endoscopy group exhibited better neurological function, as indicated by higher Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores (10.5 ± 2.3 vs. 7.9 ± 3.4, p < 0.01), and a shorter hospital stay (10.6 ± 5.8 days vs. 13.4 ± 8.2 days, p < 0.05). Postoperative modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 3 months were significantly better in the endoscopy group (3.4 ± 1.4 vs. 4.3 ± 1.7, p < 0.01) than in the craniotomy group. Endoscopic surgery was associated with more favorable outcomes, including higher hematoma clearance, reduced surgical duration and blood loss, improved neurological recovery, and faster postoperative rehabilitation compared to craniotomy. Data are presented as mean ± SD.ConclusionsEndoscopic procedures demonstrate superior clinical outcomes compared with conventional craniotomy for managing hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage in the basal ganglia region, potentially enhancing patient recovery. This minimally invasive technique represents an advanced therapeutic approach for such cases. As endoscopic technology continues to evolve, its application in neurosurgical practice is expected to expand significantly. However, further validation through prospective randomized controlled studies remains essential to establish its efficacy conclusively.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03276-y.Keywords: Endoscopic surgery, 3D-Slicer, Basal ganglia intracerebral hemorrhage, Craniotomy

The incidence of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage accounts for approximately 10%−20% of stroke [1] and the neurological dysfunction is more likely to occur comparing with ischemic stroke. A large number of randomized controlled trials domestically and abroad have confirmed that minimally invasive surgery is safe and feasible in the treatment of intracerebral hemorrhage than conservative methods accompanied with the reduction of mortality, but the prognosis of neurological function is not significantly improved [2].

Multiple surgical and invasive approaches have been explored for monitoring, clot removal, and mass effect control in this clinical context. Craniotomy remains the most extensively studied intervention, however, alternative techniques, including decompressive craniectomy with hematoma drainage, image-guided stereotactic endoscopic aspiration, and minimally invasive catheter evacuation followed by thrombolysis, have also been attempted. Despite these options, none of the surgical methods have demonstrated a significant improvement in clinical outcomes [3]. With the development of neuro-endoscopic techniques, endoscopic hematoma evacuation seems to be more effective compared with craniotomy. Endoscopic hematoma evacuation may be suggested as the priority treatment [4]. Nevertheless, the clinical efficacy of this approach requires further investigation. Our study conducted a comparative analysis between minimally invasive endoscopic evacuation and conventional craniotomy for hematoma removal. Previous research has established hematoma volume as a significant independent prognostic factor in intracerebral hemorrhage [5]. For accurate volumetric assessment, we utilized 3D-Slicer, an open-source software platform known for its measurement precision in medical imaging.

Methods

Patient selection

This study included 76 consecutive adult patients with basal ganglia hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage who underwent endoscopic evacuation from December 2019 to June 2023. For comparative analysis, we identified a historical control cohort of patients who received conventional craniotomy for the same condition at Affiliated Changshu Hospital of Nantong University between January 2018 and June 2021, matched by key clinical characteristics. Both cohorts followed standardized clinical protocols for perioperative care, including rigorous hypertension management. Eligible participants met the following requirements: (1) CT-confirmed spontaneous supratentorial hemorrhage exceeding 20 mL in volume; (2) Presentation to the hospital within two days of symptom onset; (3) Age ≥ 18 years with GCS scores of 5 or higher; (4) Selective CTA screening for vascular abnormalities when clinically indicated. The two groups received equivalent supportive therapies throughout their treatment course. Exclusion Criteria were listed below: (1) Hemorrhage secondary to tumor, trauma, coagulopathy, aneurysm, or arteriovenous malformation; Hemorrhage following infarction; (2) Prolonged use of antiplatelet or anticoagulant medications; (3) Brainstem hemorrhage or herniation. The same inclusion and exclusion criteria applied to both groups. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Affiliated Changshu Hospital of Nantong University, and written informed consent was obtained from all patients’ relatives prior to surgery. For patients who remained conscious before surgery, both the patients themselves and their families were fully informed and provided consent.

Preoperative software preparation

(1) Preoperative localization head CT scan: Metal electrode patches were attached to the patient’s head before performing the CT scan. At least four electrode patches should be applied at the following locations: the root of the nose, bilateral external auditory canals and the planned entry point for the surgical puncture on the forehead (2–3 cm anterior to the coronal suture and 3–4 cm lateral to the midline). The CT scan should be performed with a slice thickness of 1.25 mm. (2) Reference coordinate system marking in three-dimensional space: After completing the CT scan, the “CT-DICOM” data were transferred to a personal computer with the following specifications: Intel Core i7 CPU, 2 × 3 GHz, 16 GB RAM. Five reference points were marked: the nasion (M1 point), the right external auditory canal (M2 point), the left external auditory canal (M3 point), and any two points along the midline of the cerebral falx (M4 and M5 points). Subsequently, the reference coordinate system in three-dimensional space was established. The “Threshold Effect” and “Threshold Range” modules were sequentially applied to reconstruct the hematoma in 3D and accurately calculate the hematoma volume. The CT imaging analysis employed automated pixel-level segmentation across all slices, with Hounsfield unit thresholds (40–110 HU) enabling precise discrimination between hemorrhagic lesions and surrounding anatomical structures. A 3D model of the hematoma was constructed using the identified pixels. The hematoma volume was calculated by accumulating the volume of the pixels that met the threshold criteria. The evacuation rate was defined as the percentage reduction in hematoma volume after surgery. The formula used was: (preoperative volume - postoperative volume)/ (preoperative volume) × 100%. (3) Surgical approach designing: Mark the scalp entry point (Entry) and the puncture target point (Target) separately. The target point is selected at the posterior lower part of the hematoma. Using “Ruler” module to measure positioning data such as the distance between the scalp and the target point at the center of the hematoma (Entry to Target). (4) Calculate the angle between the surgical path and the reference coordinate system: Import the reference coordinate system and the surgical path (Entry-Target) respectively with “Gyro Guide” module, and then using “y-Target” to compute the optimal surgical path and the angles between the path and each reference plane of the coordinate system (Fig. 1).

Surgical technique

The surgical procedure was performed while the patient was under general anesthesia. The head was fixed with Mayfield head frame. The head was in a neutral position. The longitudinal axis of the hematoma was perpendicular to the ground, and the neck was slightly bent. For the endoscopy group, a 4-cm incision was made according to the position of hematoma. The predetermined target area of the hematoma was slightly behind the bottom of the hematoma cavity. The entry point was 2 ~ 3 cm in front of the coronal suture and 3 ~ 4 cm beside the midline. A bone hole was drilled and a milling cutter was used to make a bone flap about 2 ~ 3 cm. The bone flap was removed and the dural membrane was suspended and opened. The non-vascular area was selected on the surface of the cortex, and the brain puncture needle was used to puncture the hematoma cavity according to the simulated puncture angle. With the successful puncture was made, a disposable transparent working sheath (Jilin LW Health Medical Technology Co.) was slowly placed into the predetermined area of the hematoma and its butterfly wing was fixed with a serpentine automatic retractor (Aesculap). Through the transparent sheath, a neuro-endoscope (diameter 3.6 mm, 0°, Karl Storz) was inserted and a metal suction catheter was used to evacuate the hematoma with the assistance of endoscope. With no active bleeding was detected, the endoscope was withdrawn and a drainage tube was placed for postoperative drainage and urokinase injection. For the craniotomy group, a traditional craniotomy was performed to evacuate the hematoma. After separating the temporalis muscle flap, a bone flap approximately 6 cm*8 cm in size was created using a milling cutter. After opening the dura, to access the hematoma cavity via a transcortical approach avoid of functional area of brain tissue using microscopic assistance and removed the hematoma. All patients received the appropriate medical treatment post to the surgery. A postoperative CT scan was obtained in each patient after the surgery immediately to evaluate for any residual hematoma.

Statistical analysis

Statistical evaluation was conducted using GraphPad Prism 5.0 (La Jolla, CA). Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation following normality verification. Intergroup comparisons were performed using unpaired t-test, rank-sum test, or chi-square tests as appropriate. P < 0.05 was established for determining statistical significance.

Results

Baseline information

The study enrolled 133 patients, divided into two groups: 76 patients in the endoscopy group and 57 patients in the craniotomy group. The baseline characteristics of these patients are summarized in Table 1. The average age in endoscopy group was 51.8 years and 55.7 years in the craniotomy group. The difference in age between the two groups was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). The mean admission GCS score was 8.5 in endoscopy group and 7.7 in craniotomy group. The difference in GCS scores at admission was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). The mean time from paroxysm to surgery was 17.1 h in endoscopy group and 12.4 h in craniotomy group. The difference in the time from paroxysm to surgery was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). The mean hematoma volume was 54.1 ml in endoscopy group and 56.8 ml in craniotomy group. The difference in preoperative hematoma volume was not statistically significant (p > 0.05).

Clinical results

The clinical results presented in Table 2 indicated several significant differences between the endoscopy group and the craniotomy group. The endoscopy group had a significantly higher hematoma evacuation rate compared to the craniotomy group (90.0% vs. 82.8%; p < 0.01) (Fig. 2). The endoscopy group had a much shorter operative time compared to the craniotomy group (1.5 h vs. 4.9 h; p < 0.01). The endoscopy group experienced significantly less blood loss than the craniotomy group ((91.9 mL vs. 593.6 mL; p < 0.01). One week after surgery, the mean GCS score was higher in the endoscopy group compared to the craniotomy group (10.5 vs. 7.9; p < 0.01). The endoscopy group had a shorter mean hospital stay compared to the craniotomy group (10.6 days vs. 13.4 days; p < 0.05). Rebleeding occurred in 2 patients in the endoscopy group and 5 patients in the craniotomy group, but this difference was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). Patients were followed up by telephone at 3 months post to the surgery. Functional outcomes assessed by the mRS at 3 months post-surgery revealed a mean difference between groups of endoscopy and craniotomy (3.4 vs. 4.3, p < 0.01). The endoscopy group demonstrated better neurological recovery overall compared to the craniotomy group.

Discussion

The condition of HICH patient may change rapidly. On the one hand, the hemorrhagic stroke can cause a series of subsequent cerebral changes which include brain edema and vasospasm. On the other hand, the secondary effects, which include the compression of hematoma, the vasospasm caused by the stimulation of blood vessels and the stimulation originated from the active substances in the blood against brain, can lead to the elevation of intracranial pressure and the formation of brain herniation [6]. Therefore, timely and effectively treatment plays an important role in the prognosis of patients with acute intracranial hematoma. However, for patients who are not suitable for operation can be treated conservatively. Performing a surgery is also traumatic and may even cause postoperative complications. Therefore, neurosurgeons are often faced with dilemma. The right treatment and timely surgery are quite difficult from time to time, but they mean a lot to the prognosis of HICH patients. Currently, surgical treatments can effectively alleviate the progression of HICH, thereby reducing the level of neurological dysfunction and enhancing the quality of life. The surgical methods include traditional craniotomy for hematoma evacuation, stereotactic hematoma puncture and drainage, microscopic hematoma evacuation through a keyhole and minimally invasive neuro-endoscopic surgery [7]. With the development of medical technology, the application of traditional craniotomy for hematoma evacuation is gradually decreasing.

Neuro-endoscopic surgery was developed on the basis of minimally invasive medicine. It can establish a surgical channel through a disposable cannula quickly, and remove the hematoma effectively [8]. Neuro-endoscopy had good lighting system and its depth can be flexibly controlled, which can supply a better view to recognize the perihematomal structures and bleeding vessels. The safety and effectiveness of endoscopic evacuation of basal ganglia hematomas have been widely recognized by neurosurgeons, who consider it safer and more effective than craniotomy in evacuating hematomas [9]. Compared with microsurgical methods and catheter drainage plus urokinase injection, endoscopic treatment is more effective in evacuating hematoma and promotes earlier functional recovery of patients [10]. Patients who underwent endoscopic surgery had a higher hematoma evacuation rate, better functional and neurological outcomes, fewer complications, and lower mortality rates than those who underwent craniotomy [11]. Neuro-endoscopy surgery can evacuate hematoma under endoscopic guidance, and the surgery itself does not trigger significant damage to the functional areas of the brain. Endoscopic hematoma evacuation is consistent with the precision neurosurgery concept of function protection-based maximum resection of lesions [12]. Compared with craniotomy, endoscopic surgery causes less deep tissue trauma and enters the hematoma cavity parallel to the conduction tracts, with minimal additional injury by trained surgeons [13]. Compared with catheter drainage, endoscopic evacuation of hematomas under direct vision improves hematoma evacuation rate and helps to decompress rapidly. Endoscopic evacuation of basal ganglia hematomas can effectively control bleeding and shorten surgical time [14]. Although there was no significant difference in hematoma evacuation between craniotomy and neuro-endoscopic surgery, the overall operative time and intraoperative blood loss are significantly higher in craniotomy due to factors such as the longer incision, larger bone flap created by the milling cutter, meticulous hemostasis of the brain tissue along the fistula tract, and the cranial bone repositioning procedures [15]. Microscope-assisted craniotomy relies on external light sources, which provides suboptimal illumination for deep-seated hematomas. In contrast, neuro-endoscopy employs an internal light source, allowing close-range observation without brightness variation depending on hematoma depth. This ensures clear visualization of the surgical field and enables more precise manipulation [16]. Neuro-endoscopy offers advantages such as wider-angle detailed observation, multi-directional rotation, adjustable fields of view, and an advanced illumination system. The surgical artificial channel is typically placed in functionally silent or less vascularized areas of the brain. The transparent working sheath features a beveled tip design, which displaces the brain tissue during puncture. The hematoma can be pushed into the transparent sheath, minimizing brain retraction and thereby reducing tissue damage and bleeding. Multi-angle rotation facilitates thorough evacuation of hematomas in different locations, improving the hematoma clearance rate. Recent studies assessing the learning curve of endoscopic evacuation for spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage demonstrate that neurosurgeons with microsurgical expertise achieve procedural proficiency earlier (approximately 8 cases) compared to novice surgeons (around 12 cases). Even surgeons without prior experience can successfully overcome the learning curve [17]. Furthermore, the widespread availability of laparoscopic systems facilitates the adoption of this technique, as the neuro-endoscope can be readily integrated with existing laparoscopic machines for hematoma evacuation. Our findings demonstrate that neuro-endoscopic procedures offer several clinical benefits over conventional craniotomy approaches. The minimally invasive technique with the assistance of 3D-Slicer achieved superior outcomes, including significantly improved clot removal efficiency (90.0% versus 82.8%; p < 0.01), reduced surgical hemorrhage, decreased surgery duration (1.5 h vs. 4.9 h; p < 0.01), and enhanced postoperative recovery in our present study. These results align with existing research documenting the efficacy of endoscopic interventions and lower healthcare costs for intracerebral hemorrhage management [18–20].

The patient’s CT data was imported into 3D-slicer. The three-dimensional model of hematoma and skull were reconstructed simulated and the hematoma volume, the depth and angle of puncture were accurately calculated [21]. 3D-slicer assisted hematoma position has the following advantages: (1) It has no special requirements for computer hardware, only the Editor module is needed to implement the reconstruction of the patient’s CT data, which can be completed in a few minutes. (2) The anatomical marks of head and face were taken as reference points. The virtual image and the patient’s scalp were matched and fused, without the need for another CT scan, which could reduce the possibility of rebleeding in the process of moving and save the time of preoperative preparation. (3) The measurement of hematoma volume will not be affected by the location and shape of the hematoma, especially for the irregular shape of intracerebral hematoma. Using 3D-slicer can be more objective to depict the hematoma and calculate the hematoma volume with higher accuracy repeatedly. It can increase the hematoma clearance rate to improve the prognosis of patients [18]. (4) According to the individual differences of HICH, the Gyro guide and Ruler modules can be used to make a detailed surgical plan in order to make the operation more targeted. 3D-slicer is downloaded and installed free of charge on the Internet without the need to purchase additional software and equipment, which is easy to be manipulated and widely used in primary hospitals [22].

Nevertheless, certain limitations of this research must be acknowledged. First, the retrospective nature of data collection may introduce potential selection bias. Second, the hematoma volumes observed in our patient cohort were notably larger than those reported in prior studies.

Conclusion

The study demonstrated that employing endoscopic techniques markedly enhanced the rate of hematoma removal and promoted better postoperative neurological outcomes. These data suggest that the endoscopic approach is safe and effective, potentially offering improved clinical outcomes for individuals suffering from basal ganglia HICH. Further validation through multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled trials is recommended to substantiate these observations.

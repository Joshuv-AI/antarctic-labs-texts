A novel role for WZ3146 in the inhibition of cell proliferation via ERK and AKT pathway in the rare EGFR G719X mutant cells
Authors: Lanxin Li, Chenyang Liu, Rui Wang, Xiaolin Yang, Xiangkai Wei, Chunhong Chu, Guoliang Zhang, Chenxue Liu, Wenrui Cui, Huixia Xu, Ke Wang, Lei An, Xiaodong Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Mutations in the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene are common driver oncogenes in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Studies have shown that afatinib is beneficial for NSCLC patients with rare EGFR mutations. However, the effectiveness of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) against the G719X (G719A, G719C and G719S) mutation has not been fully established. Herein, using the CRISPR method, the EGFR G719X mutant cell lines were constructed to assess the sensitivity of the rare mutation G719X in NSCLC. WZ3146, a novel mutation-selective EGFR inhibitor, was conducted transcriptome sequencing and in vitro experiments. The results showed that WZ3146 induced cytotoxic effects, inhibited growth vitality and proliferation via ERK and AKT pathway in the EGFR G719X mutant cells. Our findings suggest that WZ3146 may be a promising treatment option for NSCLC patients with the EGFR exon 18 substitution mutation G719X.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73293-z.

Introduction

Lung cancer is a prevalent malignancy in clinical practice and a leading cause of cancer-related deaths. The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and its related proteins in the signaling pathway play crucial roles as tyrosine receptor kinases in both normal physiology and cancer1. Previous studies have identified EGFR activating mutations occurring in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) as one of the most common driver mutations2,3 with clinical implications for targeted therapy. In NSCLC, approximately 80–90% of patients with EGFR mutations have either exon 19 deletions or exon 21 L858R point mutations, which are commonly known as common mutations4. The remaining 10–20% of EGFR mutations are rare and highly heterogeneous5,6. The EGFR G719X mutation (Simplified A, C, and S below stand for G719A, G719C and G719S mutants, respectively) occurs at a frequency of approximately 3%. Based on this background, two groups of human lung cancer cell lines H3255 (background EGFR L858R mutation) and PC-9 (background EGFR 19del mutation) with rare mutation EGFR G71919X cells in this study were selected as the basis for modification. Using CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing and lentivirus packaging transfection technology7, six cell lines expressing EGFR G719X mutations were constructed, including H3255 G719A, H3255 G719C, H3255 G719S, PC-9 G719A, PC-9 G719C and PC-9 G719S, and complete subsequent relevant experiments. G719X, known as a drug-sensitive mutation, is primarily based on laboratory studies8 and clinical studies2,9, which have reported partial responses to TKIs in patients with G719X mutations. In conclusion, the G719X mutation in EGFR is generally considered sensitive according to the NSCLC NCCN guidelines. It is not an acquired mutation that occurs after treatment and this mutation is an amino acid substitution at codon 719 in exon 18, where the original glycine is replaced by cysteine, alanine, or serine (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Among G719X, G719S, G719A, G719C and G719D are the most common10–12, and the mutant protein G719A has the highest kinase activity, and the number of patients with G719A mutation is relatively large in clinical13. NSCLC with the G719X mutation is susceptible to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)14. G719X-mutant NSCLC is a highly heterogeneous group of EGFR mutations15, and the G719X mutation is located on a glycine-rich loop near the ligand-binding site, which is a highly conserved class of flexible structural elements consistent with an ATP-binding mode14. Compared to wild-type EGFR, the G719X mutation is associated with EGFR activation. Limited data is available on the effectiveness of treatment for patients with lung cancer carrying rare mutations. Afatinib, a second-generation TKI, is approved as the first-line treatment for patients with metastatic NSCLC carrying uncommon EGFR mutations, including G719X, L861Q and S768I. Clinically, there are two forms of G719X mutation types: mutation alone and compound mutation, and clinical cases have demonstrated that treatment of EGFR G719X mutation with first-, second-, and third-generation TKIs improves patient survival, but the overall effect is lower than that of common mutations16. Data on the efficacy of first- or second- generation TKIs in patients with rare mutated NSCLC are inconsistent. Although third-generation EGFR TKIs have shown significant therapeutic efficacy in clinical trials, the genetic background and tumor characteristics of each patient need to be carefully considered in treatment selection. G719X mutations show different responses to different TKIs than common mutations 19Del and L858R17–19. Due to the lack of research experiments and low clinical prevalence, there are differences in the sensitivity of rare mutation NSCLC to TKIs. There is currently no clear consensus on the optimal treatment strategy for NSCLC patients with rare mutations. It is important to explore therapeutic agents for NSCLC patients with G719X mutations to overcome the challenge of patients’ resistance to TKIs. Therefore, developing new therapeutic agents that target G719X mutation-sensitive drugs may be a breakthrough.

WZ3146 is a novel mutation-selective EGFR inhibitor with irreversible covalent binding properties (Supplementary Fig. 1B). It has a more sensitive effect on EGFR T790M than on wild-type EGFR20. Compared to reversible inhibitors like Gefitinib, WZ3146 is selective for mutant EGFR with considerably lower toxicity and has a wider range of ATP-binding sites21. WZ3146 akin to WZ4002 and others that were used more than ten years ago before third-generation EGFR TKIs inhibitors such as osimertinib became available22. When combined with Cabozantinib, WZ3146 shows strong synergy in treating primary hepatocellular carcinoma. WZ3146 has a wider ATP binding site compared to reversible inhibitors such as Gefitinib21. Additionally, WZ3146 has demonstrated significant synergistic effects when used in combination with Cabozantinib for the treatment of primary hepatocellular carcinoma23. Furthermore, it has been found to inhibit the growth of human neuroblastoma cells24.

In order to explore targeted agents for the treatment of NSCLC cancer, in the present study, we chose WZ3146 as a targeting inhibitor to test its effect on the growth of EGFR G719X mutant cells, and the EGFR G719X mutant cells were transformed through viral packaging7. A series of in vitro experiments were conducted to investigate the effects of WZ3146 on non-small cell lung adenocarcinomas with the L858R mutation, the exon 19Del mutation, and the G719X (A, C, S) mutation. The experiments aimed to verify the inhibition of AKT and ERK phosphorylation in a dose-dependent manner or other ways, as well as the inhibition of apoptosis and cell proliferation. The damage and death caused by WZ3146 eventually manifested itself in changes at the cellular level, suggesting that WZ3146 causes a cytotoxic mechanism. The promotion of apoptosis and inhibition of cell proliferation may offer novel avenues for drug discovery and clinical precision.

Materials and methods

Drugs and reagents

WZ3146 was purchased from Selleck Chemicals (China). The HEK-293T, H3255, and PC-9 cell lines were obtained from the laboratory deposit (Certificate of STR Analysis). MTT, protease inhibitor, phosphatase inhibitor, and BCA protein quantification kits were purchased from Beijing Sola Biotechnology Co., Ltd (China). The BeyoClick™EdU Cell Proliferation Kit, which contains Alexa Fluor 555 (C0075S) and the Mitochondrial Membrane Potential and Apoptosis Detection Kit, which contains Mito-Tracker Red CMXRos (C1071M), Annexin V-FITC, FITC Annexin V solution and PI (ST512), were purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology Institute (China). All antibodies were purchased from ProteinTech Co., Ltd (China).

Cell culture

The cell lines were cultured in medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Clark Bioscience, China), 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 µg/ml streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The cell culture reagents were purchased from Gibco (USA). The H3255-Cas9 and PC-9-Cas9 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium, which was supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/ml penicillin, 100 µg/ml streptomycin, and 2 µg/ml puromycin hydrochloride (Puromycin; Thermo Fisher Technologies, Waltham, MA, USA). The six cell lines, H3255 G719A, H3255 G719C, H3255 G719S, PC-9 G719A, PC-9 G719C, and PC-9 G710S, were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% bovine serum, 100 U/ml penicillin, 100 µg/ml streptomycin, 2 µg/ml puromycin, and 10 µg/ml Blasticidin S HCl (BSD) from Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA (USA). The cells were cultured in an incubator at 37 °C, 5% CO2, and saturated humidity.

The cells were frozen for spare parts after being passaged during the logarithmic growth phase, in accordance with the experimental requirements. The eight groups of cells were treated with WZ3146 at a drug concentration of 500 nM for 48 h, and were subsequently used for further experiments.

Cell lines establishment for H3255-Cas9 and PC-9-Cas9 mutant

HEK 293 T cells were inoculated into 6-well plates when cell fusion reached 70–80%. The cells were co-transfected with two helper plasmids containing the Cas9 gene, psPAX2 and pMD2G at a molar ratio of 1:1:1, using the cationic polymer transfection reagent EZ Trans (LIFE lab, Shanghai, China). The cells were cultured in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 and saturated humidity. Cell supernatants containing viral particles were collected after 24 h and filtered through 0.45 µM filters (Millipore, Boston, MA, USA). The filtered supernatants were added to H3255 and PC-9 cells with 70 − 80% cell fusion, respectively. The medium was replaced after 24 h. After 12 h, the supernatants of H3255 and PC-9 cells were discarded and replaced with fresh medium. After 48 h, lentiviral particles were added and screened through 30 days of passaging with a medium containing 2 µg/mL puromycin.

Cell lines establishment for H3255 G719X and PC-9 G719X mutant

The EGFR G719X (A, C, S) cell line was packaged using the method described above, and lentiviral particles containing EGFR G719X (A, C, S) plasmids (Deposited in our laboratory) were infected into H3255-Cas9 and PC-9-Cas9 cells, respectively. The H3255 G719X (A, C, S) and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) cell lines were established using 30 days of BSD screening.

Cell survival and growth assay

Cell survival and viability were assessed using the MTT assay. H3255, PC-9, H3255 G719X (A, C, S), and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) cells in logarithmic growth phase were inoculated into 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells per well. After 16 h, the medium was removed from each well and replaced with 100 µl of WZ3146 at a concentration of 500 nM. The cells were then incubated for 48 h. After that, 20 µl of MTT (5 mg/mL) was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 4 h. Finally, the supernatant was discarded, and 100 µl of DMSO solution was added to each well. The plates were placed in a shaker for 15 min at 37 °C under light protection. Optical density (OD) was measured using a microplate reader to determine cell viability. Cell viability was calculated as a percentage using the formula: (OD value of blank wells in the administered group) divided by (OD value of blank wells in the control group) multiplied by 100%.

Transcriptome sequencing

During the logarithmic growth phase, H3255 G719X (A, C, S) and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) cells were inoculated into 10 cm dishes at a density of approximately 5 million cells per dish. After 16 h, the medium was removed and replaced with WZ3146 at a concentration of 500 nM. The cells were then incubated for 48 h. Following this, RNA was extracted from the cells using TRIzol lysis, chloroform isolation, isoamyl alcohol, and anhydrous ethanol. The extracted RNA was stored on dry ice.

Three independent replicates were completed for each sample, with a concentration of 50 ng/ul and a total content greater than 1 ug for sequencing. The sequencing direction was paired-end and the length position was 150 bp. The transcriptome data was generated using the Illumina sequencing platform and the raw data was processed to obtain high-quality clean reads. DESeq2 was used for inter-sample differential gene analysis. The expression ratios between the two groups of samples were expressed as Fold Change (FC) with a screening criterion of Log2FC > 2 and P ≤ 0.05. Multiple tests for statistically significant differences were corrected using the False Discovery Rate (FDR). The differentially expressed genes were enriched in the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases. The genes were then analyzed for their associated biochemical metabolic and signal transduction pathways.

Colony formation assay

Cells of H3255, PC-9, H3255 G719X (A, C, S), and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) in logarithmic growth phase were inoculated in 6-well plates at a density of approximately 500 cells per well. They were then incubated with varying concentrations (50 nM, 100 nM, 200 nM, 500 nM) of WZ3146. The medium containing the drug was changed every two days. After 14 days, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 1 × Giemsa stain (Solarbio, China) for 10 min at room temperature. The cells were observed and counted to determine their clonogenic capacity. The clonogenic capacity of the cells was determined by dividing the number of colonies in the drug-treated group by the number of colonies in the control group.

EdU cell proliferation assay

Cells from H3255, PC-9, H3255 G719X (A, C, S), and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) were inoculated into 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well during the logarithmic growth phase. After 16 h, the medium was replaced with varying concentrations (50 nM, 100 nM, 200 nM, 500 nM) of WZ3146 for 48 h. Following this, the medium was incubated with WZ3146 at a density of 5,000 cells per well. 100 µL of EdU was added to each well and incubated for 2 h. After incubation, remove the medium, fix the cells, and wash them. Then, add 50 µL of the prepared Click reaction solution according to the instructions and incubate for 30 min away from light. Remove the medium again, wash the cells, and label all the nuclei by adding 50 µL of Hoechst (Beyotime, China). Incubate for 10 min away from light and observe under a fluorescence microscope. The rate of cell proliferation was measured using fluorescence microscopy. The percentage of EdU-positive cells out of the total number of cells was used to calculate the cell proliferation rate. The cell proliferation rate was calculated using Image J software, and the cells were counted. The scale bar was 50 μm.

Detection of apoptosis by flow cytometry

Cells from H3255, PC-9, and H3255 G719X (A, C, S), as well as PC-9 G719X (A, C, S), were inoculated into 6-well plates during the logarithmic growth phase at a density of 6 × 105 cells per well. After 16 h, the medium was replaced with WZ3146 at varying concentrations (50 nM, 100 nM, 200 nM, and 500 nM). Following a 48-hour incubation period, the cells were washed with PBS, digested, and collected by centrifugation at 1000 x g for 5 min. The cells were washed again with PBS and resuspended. The apoptosis rate was determined by flow cytometry after labeling apoptotic cells with the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (Procell, China). The percentages of Annexin V-positive cells in the apoptosis plots were added together, plotted, and analyzed.

Mitochondrial membrane potential assay and apoptosis

Cells of H3255, PC-9, and H3255 G719X (A, C, S), as well as PC-9 G719X (A, C, S), in logarithmic growth phase were inoculated into 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well. After 16 h, the medium was aspirated and replaced with WZ3146 at different concentrations (50 nM, 100 nM, 200 nM, 500 nM) for 48 h. The cells were centrifuged at 1000 g for 5 min following the Mitochondrial Membrane Potential and Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime, China). The cell culture medium was removed, and the cells were washed with 100 µl of PBS per well. The solutions were mixed gently. Then, 32 µL of Annexin V-FITC conjugate and 0.34 µL of Mito-Tracker Red CMXRos staining solution were added to each well, followed by 0.84 µL of Annexin V-FITC and 0.84 µL of Hoechst staining solution. Incubate the sample for 30 min at room temperature, away from light. Then, place the sample in an ice bath and observe it immediately under a fluorescence microscope. Calculate the mean fluorescence intensity (Mean) as the sum of fluorescence intensity of the area (IntDen) divided by the area of the sample (Area). The fluorescence intensity of mitochondrial activity in the cells was analyzed using Image J software. The average fluorescence intensity value was calculated by dividing the sum of the fluorescence intensity of the region (IntDen) by the area of the region (Area). This value was then plotted and data analysis was performed. The cell proliferation rate was calculated as the percentage of Annexin V-FITC positive cells out of the total number of cells. The number of cells was determined using Image J software, and the rate of apoptosis was calculated, plotted, and analyzed. Scale bar: 50 μm.

Western blot analysis

Cells of H3255, PC-9, and H3255 G719X (A, C, S), as well as PC-9 G719X (A, C, S), in the logarithmic growth phase were inoculated into 10 cm dishes at a density of 200 × 104 cells per well. After 16 h, the medium was aspirated and replaced with WZ3146 at a concentration of 500 nM, which was then incubated for 48 h. The cells were subsequently incubated with WZ3146 at a concentration of 500 nM. The controls were incubated with 1640 medium only. The cells were lysed using RIPA lysis buffer to extract total cellular proteins. Protein concentrations were determined using the BCA protein quantification kit (Solarbio, China) in the presence of protease and hemiphosphatase inhibitor mixtures (Solarbio, China). Equal amounts of total protein were added to each well to complete the protein expression assay using SDS-PAGE gels (EpiZyme, China).

The proteins underwent processing through 8–15% SDS-PAGE. They were then electrophoretically transferred to NC membranes (Pall Corporation, Mexico). Following this, the NC membranes were blocked with 5% skimmed milk powder in TBST buffer. Specific primary antibodies were then incubated with the NC membranes overnight at 4 °C. The NC membranes were washed three times with TBST and then incubated with secondary antibodies. The following antibodies were used: anti-EGFR (Sino Biotech, China; 1:1000), anti-p-EGFR (Cell Signaling Technology, USA; 1:1000), anti-ERK (Proteintech, China; 1:2500), anti-p-ERK (Proteintech, China; 1:5000), anti-Akt (Proteintech, China; 1: 1000), anti-p-Akt (Proteintech, China; 1:5000), anti-HO-1 (Proteintech, China; 1:2500), anti-Bcl-XL (Proteintech, 1:5000, China), anti-Bad (Proteintech, 1:2000, China), anti-GAPDH (Proteintech, China; 1:20000), Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG was detected (Proteintech, 1:5000, China), and goat anti-mouse IgG was detected (Servicebio, 1:5000, China). After washing three times with TBST, the samples were displayed using the enhanced ECL chemiluminescent substrate kit (CWBIO, China). Quantification ratios were calculated by determining grey scale values.

Statistical analysis

All data are expressed as mean ± SEM, and data were statistically analyzed between groups using one-way ANOVA of three independent experiments. Drawn with GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. The results showed statistical significance with *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001 compared to the control group.

Results

Transcriptome sequencing analysis

The correlation of gene expression levels among transcriptome samples is a crucial indicator for testing the reliability of experiments and the appropriateness of sample selection. In this study, the correlation of the three samples within the respective control group and the three samples within the treatment group for H3255 G719A, H3255 G719C, H3255 G719S, and PC-9 G719A, PC-9 G719C, and PC-9 G719S was found to be high (Supplementary Fig. 1D). The transcriptome assay results were screened based on the significance of difference criterion, requiring more than a 2-fold change in differential gene expression and a q-value (fdr, padj) of less than or equal to 0. The number of genetically significant differentially expressed genes that were upregulated and downregulated was counted (Supplementary Fig. 1 C). Among them, H3255 G719A cells showed the least number of changes after WZ3146 treatment (Supplementary Fig. 1G), while PC-9 G719C cells showed the highest number of changes Supplementary Fig. 1H).

Before and after the WZ3146 treatment, the expression levels of several genes with similar functions or closely related to each other changed. In the three groups of cell samples (H3255 G719A, H3255 G719C and H3255 G719S), there were six common gene expression changes in the top 40 values from control to WZ3146 treatment (Supplementary Fig. 1E). The gene IDs for these changes are ALPG, ATP6V1B1, CLIC5, LY6G6C, and SLC7A7. The other three groups of cell samples cell samples (PC-9 G719A, PC-9 G719C, and PC-9 G719S) were treated with WZ3146. A total of 17 genes showed common changes in the top 40 values of gene differential expression (Supplementary Fig. 1F). The gene IDs for these changes are CRYAB, NPY4R2, ALPG, ATP6V1B1, ALPP, AGXT, SLC7A7, APOD, MGAT3, RASSF2, PDZK1, C2orf72, KRT4, ZBTB7C, GABRP, and LY6G6C, respectively. Four genes showed common variations in the above two groups: ALPG, ATP6V1B1, LY6G6C, and SLC7A7. The sample base position quality score values were all above 30, indicating that the error rate was controlled at 0.1% (Supplementary Fig. 2E-F). The signaling pathway entries involved in the differential genes were analysed by GO and KEGG enrichment, and the resulting enrichment results were annotated and analysed (Supplementary Fig. 3A-C). Principal component analysis reduces the complexity of the data, providing insight into the relationship between the samples and the size of the variance (Supplementary Fig. 4G). The sequencing data quality was assessed using FastQC software (v0.10.1). In addition to differential analysis at the gene level, RNA-seq enables differential exon usage (DEU) analysis. This approach can be used to detect differential expression of genes at the exon level. The expression details of each exon of the DEU gene are presented in Supplementary Fig. 4A-F, with the highlighted purple-red blocks representing exons with significant differences in expression levels.

WZ3146 inhibits cell proliferation

After treatment with WZ3146, the experimental group exhibited a significantly lower number of colonies and smaller colony area compared to the control group (Fig. 1A, B). The lowest concentration tested was 50 nM, at which point the number of cellular colonies had already significantly decreased. As the concentration increased, the colonies disappeared almost entirely, with a drug concentration of 500 nM. After counting the number of colonies in each group, it was observed that the clone-generating ability of the cells gradually decreased. The difference between the WZ3146-treated group and the control group was found to be significant (Fig. 1C, D). Therefore, it can be concluded that WZ3146 reduced the clone formation ability of the cells.

The GO enrichment analysis revealed significant differences in the expression of genes related to cell proliferation pathways. WZ3146 was found to significantly affect eight groups of cell biological processes associated with cell proliferation activities (Fig. 1E, F). To examine the impact of WZ3146 on cell viability, we administered a concentration of 500 nM of WZ3146 to H3255, PC-9, H3255 G719X (A, C, S) and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) cells. The MTT assay was used to detect the effects 48 h later (Fig. 1G, H). After 48 h of administration, WZ3146 reduced the viability of eight groups of cells and significantly inhibited the viability of eight other groups to less than 50%, which was statistically significant compared to the control group. This experiment confirms that WZ3146 effectively inhibits the growth of mutant cells.

The results of the EdU assay showed a reduction in the number of cells exhibiting blue fluorescence in H3255, PC-9, and H3255 G719X (A, C, S), as well as in PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) when compared to the control (Fig. 2A, B). This suggests that the total number of cells was reduced by the treatment of WZ3146, and the number of proliferating cells was reduced within 2 h. Additionally, the number of cells exhibiting red fluorescence was reduced in PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) compared to the control. Thus, it was concluded that WZ3146 significantly reduced the proliferation rate of all eight cell groups. Notably, the proliferation rate of PC-9 G719S was most significantly reduced, indicating that WZ3146 inhibited the proliferation ability of all eight cell groups to varying degrees, with the greatest inhibition observed in PC-9 G719S cells (Fig. 2C, D).

WZ3146 promotes apoptosis and decreases mitochondrial membrane potential

The experiments on mitochondrial membrane potential and apoptosis detection revealed a decrease in the fluorescence intensity of red fluorescence (Fig. 2E, F) after the administration of WZ3146, indicating a reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential. Upon counting, the average fluorescence intensity of the eight groups of cells with the addition of WZ3146 showed a clear decreasing trend (Fig. 2G, H). The number of cells exhibiting green fluorescence increased, indicating an increase in the number of apoptotic cells. By calculating the ratio of apoptotic cells to all cells, it was concluded that the apoptotic rate tended to decrease in all eight groups of cells due to dosing factors (Fig. 2I, J). The treatment had a pro-apoptotic effect on the cells of H3255, PC-9, and H3255 G719X (A, C, S), as well as PC-9 G719X (A, C, S).

The GO enrichment analysis revealed a significant difference in gene expression related to the apoptosis pathway. Additionally, WZ3146 mediated eight groups of cell biological processes that were significantly associated with apoptotic activities (Fig. 3A-F). The results of Annexin V-FITC/PI fluorescence double staining for detecting apoptosis showed a significant increase in apoptotic cells after drug treatment compared to the control group (Fig. 3G, H). Additionally, the rate of apoptosis was also significantly higher (Fig. 3J, K). Simultaneously, the increase in apoptosis was found to be related to the drug concentration. As the drug concentration increased, the rate of apoptosis also increased gradually (Fig. 3I, L-O). This indicates that the effect of WZ3146 on apoptosis is dependent on its concentration.

Discussion

In the present study, we report on mutational modifications performed on H3255 and PC-9 cells to complete subsequent cytotoxicity assays. The genetic backgrounds of these two sets of cell lines are EGFR L858R mutation and exon 19 deletion mutation, respectively. These mutations are the most common classical EGFR mutations in Asian NSCLC7,25. The experimental subjects of this paper are rare mutant cell lines generated using the CRISPR/Cas9 method. These cell lines contain two classical mutations as well as rare EGFR mutant cells modified by classical mutations.

The GO enrichment results revealed that the homozygous mutations of the three groups differed, even in the same cellular background. This demonstrates that each mutation type has its unique genetic environment, paving the way for subsequent precision studies. Despite the different mutations in different contexts, the G719X (A, C, S) mutant cells under study still exhibited common highly differential changes in the ALPG, ATP6V1B1, LY6G6C, and SLC7A7 genes. ALPG is a specific tumor cell surface antigen that is highly correlated with tumor progression26. SLC7A7, on the other hand, is a prognostic biomarker associated with immune infiltration in NSCLC27. These genes may serve as tumor markers, and it is still worthwhile to explore their mechanism of action with WZ3146.

Previous studies have demonstrated the existence of well-known oncogenic signaling pathways. EGFR exon 19 deletion mutations and L858R mutations have been shown to increase the phosphorylation of EGFR family proteins8. Tumor development involves the activation of standard signals ERK and AKT28. The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway is involved in the regulation of various links of cell survival, which is closely related to the occurrence and development of tumors. Tumor cell signaling is heavily influenced by the phosphorylation of EGFR, ERK, and AKT29. Targeted therapies for tumor cell survival, invasion, and metastasis can be applied through the PI3K/AKT and ERK signaling pathways30,31. ERK1/2 has a typical protein kinase structure and can regulate cell activities through phosphorylation of substrates32. The decreased expression of AKT and ERK proteins and their phosphorylation can promote apoptotic cell death through the PI3K/AKT and ERK pathways33,34. Following treatment with WZ3146, the expression of phosphorylated EGFR, ERK, and AKT was significantly reduced, indicating that WZ3146 inhibits signaling by decreasing the phosphorylation response of key components in the signaling pathway during the progression of eight groups of cells, including H3255, PC-9, H3255 G719X (A, C, S), and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S), thereby blocking signaling and inhibiting tumor progression. HO-1 is an antioxidant enzyme that plays a potential role in promoting cell death during tumor treatment35. The expression of HO-1 significantly increased in the eight groups of cells treated with WZ3146, indicating the need for further exploration of the mechanism of cell death promotion by WZ3146.

Mitochondria play a crucial role in regulating cellular energy and metabolism, as well as maintaining cell growth and survival36. A decrease in mitochondrial membrane potential has been linked to autophagy, apoptosis, or necrosis of tumor cells37. The paper reports that WZ3146 reduced the mitochondrial membrane potential and induced apoptosis in a dose-dependent manner, as detected by the mitochondrial membrane potential apoptosis assay and flow cytometry. Although it could not be verified that WZ3146 induces apoptosis by regulating the expression of the pro-apoptotic protein Bad and the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-xl (Supplementary Fig. 2A-D), it significantly inhibits cell survival and viability, as demonstrated by the MTT assay. The EdU assay and the colony formation assay detected an inhibitory effect on the cell proliferation of H3255, PC-9, H3255 G719X (A, C, S), and PC-9 G719X (A, C, S) cells. The study confirmed that WZ3146 enhances apoptotic ability and reduces proliferative and clonogenic ability, as well as mitochondrial membrane potential, in cells undergoing apoptosis in a dose-dependent manner. Developing new drugs is a time-consuming process. This article discusses the characteristics of WZ3146, a class of compounds developed to exhibit high activity against EGFR mutants. Recent studies have shown that WZ3146 can also inhibit allergic reactions by suppressing degranulation, histamine release, and cytokine secretion. As a result, it may be redefined as a drug for the treatment of allergic diseases38. The drug appears to have a multi-targeted effect, indicating promising characteristics. However, the study has limitations as it only involved in vitro experiments. To more comprehensively validate the effects of WZ3146 on the EGFR G719X mutation, we will subsequently add biologically reliable evidence based on living organisms.

Conclusions

In summary, in the context of gene editing on cells with L858R and 19 deletion mutations, our results indicate that WZ3146 reduced cell viability, inhibited cell proliferation, inhibited cell colony formation, and promoted cell apoptosis on rare EGFR G719X (A, C, S) mutant cells via the AKT and ERK pathways. These findings suggest that WZ3146 could be a new targeted inhibitor for EGFR G719X (A, C, S) mutant treatment.

Lymph node metrics following neoadjuvant therapy to refine patient selection for adjuvant chemotherapy in resected pancreatic cancer: A multi‐institutional analysis
Authors: Haleh Amirian, Erin Dickey, Ifeanyichukwu Ogobuiro, Edmond W Box, Ankit Shah, Mary P Martos, Manan Patel, Gregory C Wilson, Rebecca A Snyder, Alexander A Parikh, Chet Hammill, Hong J Kim, Daniel Abbott, Shishir K Maithel, Syed Nabeel Zafar, Michael T LeCompte, David A Kooby, Syed A Ahmad, Nipun B Merchant, Caitlin A Hester, Jashodeep Datta
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn patients with localized pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) undergoing neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) and resection, selection of adjuvant chemotherapy (AC) is typically guided by high‐risk features on histopathologic examination. We evaluated the interaction between post‐NAT lymph node metrics and AC receipt on survival.MethodsPatients who received NAT followed by pancreatectomy (2010–2020) at seven centers were reviewed. Overall survival (OS) in patients receiving AC or not was stratified by lymph node positivity (LNP) or lymph node ratio (LNR) dichotomized at 0.1. Cox models evaluated the independent association between these nodal metrics, AC receipt, and OS.ResultsOf 464 patients undergoing NAT and resection, 264 (57%) received AC. Patients selected for AC were younger (median 63 vs. 67 years; p < 0.001), received shorter duration of NAT (2.8 vs. 3.2 months; p = 0.01), had fewer postoperative complications (Clavien–Dindo grade > 3: 1.2% vs. 11.7%; p < 0.001), and lower rates of pathologic complete response (4% vs. 11%; p = 0.01). The median number of nodes evaluated was similar between cohorts (n = 20 in both; p = 0.9). Post‐NAT LNP rates were not different, and median LNR was 0.1, in AC and non‐AC cohorts. Both LNP (hazard ratio [HR]: 2.1, p < 0.001) and LNR (0 < LNR ≤ 0.1: HR: 1.98, p = 0.002; LNR > 0.1: HR 2.46, p < 0.001) were independently associated with OS on Cox modeling, although receipt of AC was not associated with improved OS (median 30.6 vs. 29.4 months; p = 0.2). In patients with LNR > 0.1, receipt of AC was associated with significantly longer OS compared to non‐AC (24 vs. 20 months, respectively; p = 0.04).ConclusionsLNR following NAT, not simply nodal positivity, may be useful to refine selection of AC in resected PDAC.Keywords: adjuvant chemotherapy, lymph node metrics, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma

INTRODUCTION

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a lethal malignancy accounting for nearly 50 550 estimated deaths in 2023, 1 and is predicted to become the second leading cause of cancer‐related mortality in the United States by 2030. 2 Due to highly aggressive biology, a multimodal approach is required due to the dismal oncologic outcomes with surgery alone. 3 The advent of increasingly effective combination chemotherapy regimens, 3 perioperative safety and technical mastery of pancreatectomy, and growing appreciation of the molecular underpinnings in PDAC 4 have resulted in gradual improvements in survival over the last decade.

Paramount to continued progress in the management of patients with localized disease is optimal sequencing of multimodal treatment in potentially resectable PDAC. 5 In patients selected for surgery upfront who undergo pancreatectomy and avoid perioperative complications, adjuvant chemotherapy with modified FOLFIRINOX has been established as standard of care. 6 , 7 However, utilization of neoadjuvant chemotherapy with or without radiation is increasingly popular due to improved rates of multimodal therapy completion, potential nodal/tumor downstaging, and assessment of in situ chemoresponsivity. In addition, there is growing evidence that adjuvant combinational systemic therapy regimens are poorly tolerated in the adjuvant setting after pancreatectomy. 8

In those selected for neoadjuvant therapy (NAT), it is unclear which patients benefit from additional adjuvant chemotherapy. In contemporary practice when patients receive neoadjuvant chemotherapy, there are no level 1 evidence to help direct adjuvant therapy decisions. Two strategies are typically followed in the adjuvant setting: (1) standardized approach in which all patients are ideally given adjuvant therapy if they did not receive a total of 6 months of neoadjuvant chemotherapy based on trial designs such as SWOG 1505 or Alliance A021501 or (2) a tailored or “pathology‐informed” approach in which the choice to give and the type of regimen are guided by high‐risk features on histopathologic examination, suboptimal post‐NAT tumor regression, and other clinical considerations. 8 , 9 , 10 A recent multi‐institutional European study revealed that the relative benefit of adjuvant therapy in patients receiving neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX followed by surgery was restricted to subsets with pathologic nodal positivity. 11 However, a detailed understanding of the intersection between post‐NAT nodal metrics and the relative effectiveness of adjuvant therapy—particularly in a North American cohort—has not been extensively explored.

Using a multi‐institutional North American data set comprising seven high‐volume centers in the Central Pancreatic Consortium (CPC), we sought to explore the pathology‐informed approach to adjuvant chemotherapy and evaluate the interaction between post‐NAT lymph node metrics and receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy, and its association with survival, in patients with localized PDAC selected for curative‐intent surgery.

METHODS

Data source

Utilizing the CPC data set, we identified patients with localized PDAC who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy with or without radiation followed by pancreatectomy (2010—2020). The CPC is a multi‐institutional collaborative effort of pancreatic surgeons at seven high‐volume academic centers: University of Miami, Emory University, Washington University at St. Louis, University of Cincinnati, University of Wisconsin, University of North Carolina, and East Carolina University. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board at the University of Miami and all participating institutions.

Patient selection, definitions, and variables

The study included patients deemed potentially operable who received neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine/nab‐paclitaxel with or without radiation therapy followed by curative‐intent surgery. Those with any diagnosis other than PDAC, mortality within 30 days postoperatively, and those who received any neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimen other than mFOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine/nab‐paclitaxel were excluded.

Resectability was defined according to AHPBA/SSO/SSAT guidelines as resectable, borderline resectable pancreatic cancer (BRPC), and locally advanced pancreatic cancer (LAPC). 12

The following variables were collected: patients and tumor characteristics (age, gender, race, ethnicity, body mass index [BMI], American Society of Anesthesiologist [ASA] class, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group [ECOG] performance status, radiographic stage, tumor location), NAT details (regimen, start/end dates, dose modifications due to chemotherapy‐related side effects, and receipt of neoadjuvant radiation), surgical and postoperative details (technique, need for vein or arterial resection, intraoperative blood transfusion, clinically relevant postoperative pancreatic fistula [crPOPF], 90‐day complication scored by the Clavien–Dindo classification, with >grade 3 considered severe).

We obtained detailed information regarding biochemical and pathologic metrics informing response to NAT. Carbohydrate antigen (CA)19‐9 levels at diagnosis and following completion of NAT were documented, which were used to calculate the decline in CA19‐9 by 50% following NAT. We also accrued histopathologic characteristics following NAT, such as margin status of the pancreatic neck, superior mesenteric artery (SMA), and bile duct, as well as lymphovascular and perineural invasion status. Importantly, pathologic response scored by the consensus College of American Pathologists (CAP) criteria and dichotomized into major (pathologic complete response [pCR] or near‐pCR; CAP 0/1) and limited (partial or absent; CAP 2/3) pathologic response categories.

Lymph node metrics

We accrued lymph node metrics including number of total lymph nodes examined and total number of lymph nodes positive. Lymph node ratio (LNR) was calculated by dividing the total number of positive nodes by total number of evaluated lymph nodes. For each patient, we determined post‐NAT: (a) lymph node positivity (LNP; ypN+ or ypN−); and (b) lymph node ratio (ypLNR). Receiver operating curve (ROC) characteristics (Figure S1), dichotomization based on median values, as well as prior literature were used to estimate the optimal threshold and cutoff for LNR comparisons. 13

Outcomes

Patients were stratified into two groups based on receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy (AC and non‐AC). The primary endpoint was interaction between receipt of AC and lymph node metrics and its association with the overall survival (OS). We also performed similar analyses for secondary endpoints, namely, local recurrence‐free survival (L‐RFS) and distant recurrence‐free survival (D‐RFS). OS was defined as the time from diagnosis to death by any cause or last follow‐up for censored patients. L‐RFS and D‐RFS were calculated from the date of surgical resection to the date of locoregional and distant recurrence, respectively.

Statistical analysis

We compared demographic and tumor characteristics, NAT details, surgical and perioperative factors, histopathologic findings, and oncologic outcomes between AC and non‐AC cohorts using χ 2 or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables, and Student's t test for continuous variables. Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier method utilizing log‐rank estimates to compare groups. Multivariable Cox proportional hazard regression models were used to examine the effects of LNP and LNR on survival after adjusting for variables that were significantly affecting survival in the univariable analyses. Hazard ratios (HR) along with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05 in a two‐sided test. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 27.

RESULTS

Descriptive statistics and demographics

The study population consisted of 464 eligible patients, with each center contributing an average of 66 cases (range: 20–178). In the neoadjuvant setting, patients received either mFOLFIRINOX (n = 315, 67.9%) or gemcitabine/nab‐paclitaxel (n = 149, 32.1%) chemotherapy, while 120 (25.9%) received neoadjuvant radiation. The duration of neoadjuvant chemotherapy was dictated by the prescribing medical oncologist based on tolerability and/or patient preference. Regarding surgical resection, a majority of patients (80.8%) underwent pancreatoduodenectomy. Other operations included distal pancreatectomy (n = 64, 13.8%), total pancreatectomy (n = 12, 2.6%), and subtotal pancreatectomy (n = 11, 2.4%). More than half of patients (56.9%) received adjuvant chemotherapy and 18.5% received adjuvant radiation. A majority (94%) of patients who received mFOLFIRINOX adjuvantly had received mFOLFIRINOX neoadjuvantly; however, only 34% of patients who received adjuvant gemcitabine‐based chemotherapy received this regimen in the neoadjuvant setting.

Patients who received AC were significantly younger (median 63 vs. 67 years, p < 0.001) than non‐AC patients; however, there were no other significant demographic differences between cohorts, including ECOG performance status or BMI. AC and non‐AC cohorts were similar in regard to tumor location (pancreatic head 81% vs. 81%, p = 1.0), radiographic size (2.8 vs. 2.7 cm, p = 0.3), radiographic stage (borderline resectable 52% vs. 56%, p = 0.52), and baseline CA19‐9 (140 vs. 249 IU/mL, p = 0.4) (Table 1).

Neoadjuvant therapy and resection

There was no statistical difference between AC and non‐AC cohorts in time from PDAC diagnosis to NAT initiation (p = 0.07). AC patients were more likely to receive neoadjuvant FOLFIRINOX (75% vs. 59%, p < 0.001), received shorter duration of NAT (2.8 vs. 3.2 months, p = 0.01), and were less likely to receive ≥6 months neoadjuvant chemotherapy (5.4% vs. 11.1%, p = 0.04) compared to non‐AC patients. Both cohorts received similar rates of neoadjuvant radiation (p = 0.8). There was no difference in chemotherapy dose reduction during treatment (23.1% vs. 22.5%) or cessation of treatment (11% vs. 15%). Of note, both cohorts experienced similar rates of 50% CA 19‐9 decrease during NAT (63.7 vs. 67.3%, p = 0.6) (Table 2).

In the perioperative setting, there were no difference in type of pancreatectomy (p = 0.8), need for venous (p = 0.4) or arterial (p = 1.0) resection/reconstruction, operative time (p = 0.7), and intraoperative blood loss and/or need for transfusions (p = 0.4). However, patients who did not receive AC were more likely to experience Clavien–Dindo grade 3 or higher 90‐day complications (11.7% vs. 1.2%, p < 0.001) compared with patients receiving AC, although the rates of crPOPF and length of postoperative stay were similar between AC and non‐AC cohorts (Table 2).

Pathologic response to neoadjuvant therapy

Upon pathologic examination of resected specimens, there was no difference in overall resection margin positivity (18.8% for AC vs. 15.8% for non‐AC, p = 0.4), or status of pancreatic neck, SMA, or bile duct margins. The total number of post‐NAT lymph nodes evaluated was similar between cohorts receiving or not receiving AC (median 20 in both, p = 0.9). Of note, the number of positive lymph nodes examined (p = 0.8), as well as the median ypLNR (0.11 in both, p = 0.5), was similar between AC and non‐AC cohorts, demonstrating that this was not a significant factor in AC selection in this retrospective study.

There were no significant differences in pathologic tumor size (2.5 cm in both, p = 0.2), presence of lymphovascular invasion (p = 0.2), or presence of perineural invasion (p = 0.09) between cohorts. The MPR rate—defined as pCR or near‐pCR based on CAP criteria—in the overall cohort was 24%, consistent with our previously published results. 14 , 15 While AC patients were less likely than non‐AC patients to have had pCR (4.1% vs. 11.1%, p = 0.01), a similar proportion achieved MPR (23.8 AC vs. 24.3% non‐AC, p = 0.9). In other words, limited pathologic response (CAP2 and/or CAP3) following NAT did not disproportionately drive selection of AC in resected patients (Table 3).

Survival analysis

As has been observed in multiple other cohorts, resected patients with LNP following NAT demonstrated significantly worse median OS compared to patients with post‐NAT node‐negative tumors (23.9 vs. 43.9 months; p < 0.001; Figure 1A). 14 When ypLNR was dichotomized at 0.1 based on prior literature 13 and AUC/sensitivity analysis (Figure S1), patients with LNR > 0.1 had worse OS compared with those with LNR ≤ 0.1 (22.2 vs. 27.1 months, p = 0.01; Figure 1B). Importantly, receipt of AC following NAT and surgery did not improve median OS in the overall cohort of patients (29.4 AC vs. 30.6 non‐AC, p = 0.2; Figure 1C).

Association of overall survival (OS) with (A) lymph node positivity (LNP), (B) lymph node ratio (LNR), and (C) adjuvant chemotherapy. CI, confidence interval.

Next, we created two separate multivariable Cox regression models to examine the influence of LNP and LNR separately on risk‐adjusted OS in this cohort. In both models, Clavien–Dindo grade >3 complications, perineural invasion, and neoadjuvant therapy‐related side effects requiring treatment cessation were independently associated with worse OS. Venous resection and lymph node positivity were independently associated with OS only in the LNP model, while LNR (both 0 < LNR ≤0.1 and LNR > 0.1) was independently associated with OS in its respective model (Table 4).

To understand the intersection of these nodal metrics and receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy on survival, we performed log‐rank estimates of OS stratified by LN positivity or LNR and receipt of AC. First, when AC and non‐AC cohorts were stratified by LNP (ypN+ or ypN‐), receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy did not statistically affect OS within either subgroup (ypN+ p = 0.1 and ypN− 0.6, respectively; Figure 2A). Conversely, improved prognostic discrimination was achieved when AC and non‐AC cohorts were stratified by ypLNR dichotomized at 0.1. In patients with LNR > 0.1, receipt of AC was associated with significantly longer OS compared to no AC (23.9 vs. 20.1 months, respectively; p = 0.04). In patients with lower LNR (i.e., 0 < LNR≤ 0.1), however, AC was not associated with improved OS (p = 0.4) (Figure 2B).

Association of overall survival in cohorts receiving (AC) or not receiving adjuvant chemotherapy (non‐AC) stratified by (A) lymph node positivity (LNP) and (B) lymph node ratio (LNR). CI, confidence interval.

In the overall cohort, median L‐RFS did not statistically differ between AC and non‐AC cohorts (10.8 vs. 7.5 months, respectively; p = 0.5). LNP (p < 0.001), but not LNR (p = 0.6), was associated with decreased L‐RFS. Receipt of AC significantly increased L‐RFS only in the ypN+ but not ypN− subgroup (median 10 vs. 4 months, p < 0.001). Next, stratifying AC and non‐AC cohorts by LNR, receipt of AC significantly increased L‐RFS in both lower (0 < LNR≤ 0.1, p = 0.02) and higher (LNR > 0.1, p = 0.005) ypLNR subgroups (Figure S2A–E). Similarly, D‐RFS was statistically shorter in ypLN+ versus ypLN− patients (median 7 vs. 10.7 months, p = 0.048) but not in ypLNR subgroups. Moreover, the interaction between LNP or LNR and receipt of AC did not achieve prognostic discrimination for D‐RFS (Figure S3A–E).

DISCUSSION

In this large “real world” multi‐institutional cohort of patients with localized PDAC selected for NAT and curative‐intent surgery, our data underscore an underexplored association between post‐NAT lymph node ratio and the relative effectiveness of a “pathology‐informed” approach to AC selection on overall survival. Although AC did not improve overall survival in the entire cohort, both lymph node positivity and higher ypLNR (>0.1) were associated with worse survival, reinforcing previous findings, and emphasizing the rationale of these metrics as prognostic biomarkers in patients selected for this approach. 9 , 14 In exploring the interaction between these two factors, our data revealed that AC appears more effective only in patients with higher lymph node ratio following NAT, not simply in those with node‐positive tumors. Notwithstanding the inherent issues with selection in this cohort of resected patients at high‐volume centers, our findings call for a more refined biology‐driven approach—for example, incorporating decision‐enabling tools such as LNR—to AC selection in patients undergoing NAT and surgery.

These data add nuance to previous studies where the efficacy of AC following NAT and surgery is restricted to patients with node‐positive resections. For instance, Barnes and colleagues evaluated 234 PDAC patients receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy and resection, and demonstrated that AC was associated with improved survival only in patients with node‐positive, but not node‐negative, resection. 16 These findings were mirrored in a modern international multicenter analysis from institutions across Europe, where AC after neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX and resection of localized PDAC was associated with improved survival only in patients with pathology‐proven nodal positivity. 11 While lymph node positivity following NAT was unsurprisingly a dominant factor predicting worse survival in our multi‐institutional North American cohort from centers spanning considerable geographic and demographic diversity, nodal positivity alone did not sufficiently discriminate survival in patients who did or did not receive AC. Intriguingly, patients with ypLNR > 0.1 were the only subgroup in which AC was associated with improved survival. Subtle differences in the patient populations included in these studies (e.g., genetic ancestry, race, environmental influences, etc.) may underlie these findings and warrant further exploration. Moreover, while strong recommendations cannot be offered from this data which require large‐scale prospective validation, two clinical implications merit discussion. First, assessment of post‐NAT nodal metrics could potentially identify higher risk individuals (i.e., ypLNR > 0.1) for consideration of more intensive adjuvant regimens or those tailored to tumor biology. Second, patients with post‐NAT nodal positivity but low ypLNR might be considered for close surveillance in the appropriate clinical scenario. As such, this latter scenario may avoid reflexive overtreatment and enable patient‐centered decision‐making in a highly selected cohort of patients after extensive discussion of risks and alternatives.

LNR has emerged as a key prognostic factor in patients with PDAC, with a majority of studies indicating that higher LNR following resection being associated with worse oncologic outcomes. 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 Other studies, however, offer conflicting evidence suggesting that resected patients with lower LNR may benefit more from adjuvant chemotherapy. 21 , 22 Compared to the design in these previous studies, which encompassed mixed cohorts with variable receipt of neoadjuvant chemotherapy and/or surgery first approaches, our findings may reflect a more uniform study design in which we restricted our analysis to a relatively homogenous patient population exclusively selected for NAT. This design minimizes heterogeneity within our study cohort, allowing for a more directed analysis of the relationship between post‐NAT lymph node metrics and adjuvant therapy in determining survival outcomes in PDAC patients undergoing resection.

Several limitations in the present study warrant discussion. First, given its retrospective case‐control design, this study is susceptible to potential selection biases. As such, its results reflect associations, and not causal relationships, between ypLNR, adjuvant chemotherapy, and survival. Second, it cannot account for provider bias, that is, real‐time decisions to offer longer neoadjuvant treatment or forgo adjuvant therapy, or treatment‐related factors such as dose density of neoadjuvant/adjuvant chemotherapy. However, herein lies the clinical value of these real‐world data from centers with similar philosophies of care but subtle variations in practice. Third, the delivery of neoadjuvant therapy, quality of surgical resection, and postoperative outcomes reported in this study reflect clinical practice at high‐volume, tertiary referral institutions which may not be generalizable to all practice settings. Lastly, although all pathologic assessment was performed at high‐volume centers of excellence, it is possible that subtle variations in pathologic quality across institutions—for example, thoroughness in the examination and documentation of lymph node numeric counts in resected specimens—could bias study findings.

In summary, we report a novel intersection between post‐NAT lymph node metrics and the relative effectiveness of AC in patients undergoing NAT and curative‐intent pancreatectomy in a multi‐institutional cohort. In the era of increasing NAT utilization for localized PDAC, future efforts must focus on refining AC selection by not only incorporating clinical metrics like ypLNR but also by leveraging molecular biomarkers such as post‐NAT circulating tumor/cell‐free DNA, tissue‐based next‐generation sequencing genomic and transcriptomic signatures, and dynamic immunologic metrics (e.g., neutrophil‐to‐lymphocyte ratio). 23 These efforts will spur truly individualized approaches to every patient with pancreatic cancer, galvanize the discovery of novel multidimensional biomarkers of therapeutic response/resistance, and emphasize nuanced quality of life‐centric clinical decision‐making to move the needle in this deadly disease.

SYNOPSIS

In a multi‐institutional cohort of patients with localized pancreatic cancer who received neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) followed by resection, post‐NAT pathologic lymph node ratio—not simply nodal positivity—discriminated survival in patients stratified by receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy, suggesting that such metrics may be helpful in refining patient selection for additional treatment following NAT and resection.

Atogepant for the Preventive Treatment of Migraine in Japanese Participants: A Phase 3, Open-Label, 52-Week Extension Study
Authors: Yasuhiko Matsumori, Krisztian Nagy, Akihiro Takahashi, Shigeya Tanaka, Takao Takeshima, Giovanna Forero, Jia Bao, Hua Guo, Gina Ahmadyar, Rosa de Abreu Ferreira, Toshimasa Yamamoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAtogepant is an oral calcitonin gene-related peptide receptor antagonist approved in the USA, EU, and Japan for the preventive treatment of migraine in adults. We evaluated the long-term safety, efficacy, and functional outcomes of atogepant for the preventive treatment of migraine in Japanese participants.MethodsThis open-label, 52-week, long-term safety extension study evaluated atogepant 60 mg once daily for the preventive treatment of migraine in Japanese participants. The study enrolled participants with chronic migraine (CM) who completed the phase 3 PROGRESS trial and de novo participants with episodic migraine (EM). The primary objective was the safety and tolerability of atogepant. Efficacy and functional outcomes were exploratory endpoints.ResultsOf 204 Japanese participants screened, 186 were enrolled and included in the safety population (CM, n = 155; EM, n = 31) and 180 in the modified intent-to-treat population (CM, n = 150; EM, n = 30). Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 88.7% of participants. TEAEs occurring ≥ 10% were pyrexia (29.0%), nasopharyngitis (16.1%), and constipation (11.3%). Concurrent COVID-19 vaccination attributed to several TEAEs, including pyrexia. Serious TEAEs occurred in 3.8% of participants and none were considered related to atogepant by the investigator. TEAEs leading to discontinuation occurred in 4.8% of participants. Alanine aminotransferase and/or aspartate aminotransferase ≥ 3 × the upper limit of normal occurred in 4.8% of participants; none met criteria for Hy’s law. The least squares mean change from baseline in monthly migraine days was − 6.4 days in PROGRESS CM completers and − 3.4 days in de novo EM participants at weeks 1–4 and was consistent over 52 weeks. Similar improvements were observed for the other efficacy and functional outcomes.ConclusionThe safety profile of atogepant in Japanese participants was consistent with the known safety profile in the global population. No new safety signals were identified. Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? The prevalence of migraine in Japan is 6.0–8.4%; however, preventive treatment use among eligible patients in Japan is low, partially due to limited treatment options.There is a high unmet need in the Japanese population for newer migraine preventive treatments with demonstrated long-term efficacy and safety.This phase 3, open-label safety extension study evaluated the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of atogepant, an oral calcitonin gene-related peptide receptor antagonist, for the preventive treatment of migraine in Japanese participants. What was learned from the study? The safety profile of atogepant in Japanese participants was consistent with the known safety profile in the global population; no new safety signals were identified.Improvements in efficacy and functional outcomes were maintained across 52 weeks of treatment.

Introduction

Current estimates report that migraine impacts 1.1 billion individuals worldwide, and the prevalence specifically in Japan has been reported to be 6.0–8.4% [1, 2]. This common neurological disease is characterized by recurring attacks of head pain and accompanying symptoms, such as sensitivity to light and sound, nausea, and vomiting [3, 4]. Migraine is characterized as episodic migraine (EM), individuals experiencing < 15 headache days per month, or chronic migraine (CM), individuals experiencing ≥ 15 headache days per month with ≥ 8 meeting criteria for migraine [3, 5]. Migraine is the second leading cause of disability globally, and the leading cause of disability in young women [6, 7]. Individuals with migraine report an impaired quality of life, with negative effects on relationships, parenting, school, work, and finances [8, 9]. Additionally, individuals with CM experience worse migraine-associated disability compared to EM [10]. As a result of the recurring nature of migraine, long-term management is needed to adequately address the head pain, associated symptoms, and the impact migraine has on daily life.

The Japanese Society of Neurology (JSN) clinical treatment guidelines state that preventive treatments for migraine should be considered for individuals who experience migraine more than twice a month, have significantly impaired quality of life or daily functioning, have an insufficient response or contraindication to acute treatments, or have a risk of medication overuse headache due to frequent use of acute treatments [11]. However, preventive treatment use among eligible patients in Japan is low, in part due to limited treatment options [12, 13]. The JSN guidelines state that the goals of preventive migraine treatment include reducing headache frequency, severity, and duration, and improving function and reducing disability [11]. Together, there remains a high unmet need in the Japanese population for newer migraine preventive treatments with demonstrated long-term efficacy and safety, and an improvement in function and disability.

Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) is a well-characterized neuropeptide that plays a key role in migraine pathogenesis [14–16]. Treatments targeting CGRP or the receptor have been developed for the preventive treatment of migraine, and these include monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and gepants, or oral CGRP receptor antagonists. Atogepant is an oral CGRP receptor antagonist approved in the USA, EU, and Japan for the preventive treatment of migraine [17–20]. The efficacy and safety of atogepant have been demonstrated in one phase 2b/3 and two phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 12-week trials in the EM population [phase 2b/3 (NCT02848326) [21], ADVANCE (NCT03777059) [22], ELEVATE (NCT04740827) [23]] conducted in the USA, or in North America and Europe, and one phase 2/3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 12-week trial with an active treatment extension in a Japanese EM population [RELEASE (NCT05861427) [24]]. Atogepant has also demonstrated efficacy and safety in one global phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 12-week trial in the CM population (PROGRESS, NCT03855137) [25]. Additionally, several long-term trials have been reported demonstrating the long-term safety, tolerability, and efficacy of atogepant in EM and CM [26–28]. All these trials except RELEASE consisted of a patient population predominantly of white individuals. Ethnic and racial backgrounds have been shown to impact the types and frequency of adverse events (AEs) in clinical trials [29]. A systematic review and meta-analysis comparing the incidences of AEs between Japanese and non-Japanese healthy subjects in phase I trials found that odds ratios for certain AEs were significantly different between the populations, demonstrating that racial factors may influence a drug safety assessment [30]. Here, we report the first evaluation of the long-term safety, efficacy, and functional outcomes of atogepant for the preventive treatment of migraine in a Japanese population of participants with EM and CM.

Methods

Study Design

This 52-week, open-label, multicenter, long-term safety extension study evaluated the use of atogepant 60 mg once daily (QD) for the preventive treatment of migraine exclusively in Japanese participants conducted at 24 sites in Japan. Eligible participants were included in a 28-day screening/baseline period (EM only), a 52-week open-label treatment period, and a 4-week safety follow-up period (Supplementary Material). The trial was approved by a local or central institutional review board at each participating institution and conducted in accordance with the International Conference for Harmonisation guidelines, applicable regulations, and the Declaration of Helsinki. Participants provided written informed consent before screening. The study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04437433).

Participants

Two cohorts were enrolled. Japanese participants were eligible for the CM cohort of the open-label study if they completed the 12-week double-blind treatment period in the lead-in PROGRESS trial. Completers were defined as participants enrolled in PROGRESS from March 11, 2019 to January 20, 2022 who completed visit 7 (week 12), and the follow-up period (visit 8, end of study visit), if applicable, without significant protocol deviations and who did not experience an AE that may indicate an unacceptable risk. This cohort included adults 18–80 years with ≥ 1-year history of CM, defined using the International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition (ICHD-3) criteria [3], with migraine onset < 50 years of age. This cohort included participants with ≥ 15 headache days per month (based on patient history) in the 3 months before the baseline/screening visit, and ≥ 15 headache days, including ≥ 8 migraine days during the 4-week baseline period based on eDiary of the PROGRESS study, and completed ≥ 20 out of 28 days of eDiary during the baseline period from this study.

Japanese participants with EM enrolled to the de novo EM cohort from November 17, 2022 to June 11, 2024, were adults, aged 18–80 years, with a ≥ 1-year history of migraine with or without aura consistent with a diagnosis according to the ICHD-3, and time of migraine onset < 50 years of age. Participants must have a history of 4–14 migraine days per month on average in the previous 3 months prior to the screening visit (visit − 1) based on investigator’s judgment and 4–14 migraine days in the 28-day baseline period based on eDiary and completed ≥ 20 out of 28 days of eDiary during the baseline period from this study.

Key exclusion criteria for all participants included electrocardiogram (ECG) with clinically significant abnormalities at visit − 1 (EM) or visit 1 (CM); hypertension (sitting systolic blood pressure (BP) > 160 mmHg and/or sitting diastolic BP > 100mmHg); clinically significant hematologic, endocrine, cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, hepatic, gastrointestinal, or neurologic disease (unless stable for > 1 year by investigator judgment); individuals that are pregnant, planning on becoming pregnant, or currently lactating.

Key additional exclusion criteria only for the de novo EM participants included difficulty distinguishing migraine headaches from tension-type or other headaches; an ICHD-3-defined history of migraine accompanied by diplopia or decreased level of consciousness, or retinal migraine, or a current diagnosis of CM, new daily persistent headache, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia, or painful cranial neuropathy; ≥ 15 headaches/month on average across the 3 months prior to visit − 1 or in the 28-day baseline period; use of opioids or barbiturates > 2 days/month, triptans or ergots ≥ 10 days/month, or simple analgesics (e.g., aspirin, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs [NSAIDs], acetaminophen) ≥ 15 days/month in the 3 months prior to visit − 1 per investigator’s judgment or during the baseline period; clinically significant cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease per the investigator’s opinion; clinically significant laboratory values; previous exposure to atogepant, injectable CGRP mAb in the previous 6 months, or any investigational CGRP receptor antagonist.

Outcomes

The primary objective evaluated the safety and tolerability of atogepant 60 mg QD for the preventive treatment of migraine in Japanese participants. Safety evaluations included the incidence of AEs, serious AEs, AEs leading to discontinuation, and vital sign measurements, ECG variables, clinical laboratory evaluations (hematology, chemistry, urinalysis), and Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) for the entire study duration. Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were defined as AEs with a recorded onset date on or after the date of the first dose of study treatment in this study and within 30 days after the last dose of the open-label study treatment or safety follow-up visit, whichever comes later. Treatment-emergent alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and/or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≥ 3 × upper limit of normal (ULN) and potential Hy’s law cases were considered AEs of special interest and were followed until resolution and adjudicated by an external independent hepatic event adjudication committee.

All efficacy and functional outcomes were exploratory endpoints. Efficacy outcomes were based on information recorded by the participants in an eDiary, which was used daily at home to collect data on headache duration, headache characteristics, symptoms, and acute medication use. The Activity Impairment in Migraine-Diary (AIM-D) was also collected daily via an eDiary. Migraine-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire Version 2.1 (MSQ v2.1), Headache Impact Test-6 (HIT-6), and Migraine Disability Assessment (MIDAS) were administered on an eTablet at specific clinic visits.

Efficacy outcomes included the change from baseline in mean monthly migraine days (MMDs), mean monthly headache days (MHDs), mean monthly acute medication use days, and the proportion of participants with a ≥ 50% reduction in MMDs at monthly intervals from weeks 1–4 to weeks 49–52. Functional outcomes included change from baseline in MSQ v2.1 Role Function-Restrictive (RFR) domain score at weeks 12, 24, 36, 48, and 52, change from baseline in AIM-D Performance of Daily Activities (PDA) and Physical Impairment (PI) domain scores at monthly intervals from weeks 1–4 to weeks 49–52, change from baseline in mean HIT-6 total score at monthly intervals from week 4 to week 52, and change from baseline in mean MIDAS total score at weeks 12, 24, 36, 48, and 52.

MSQ v2.1 is a 3-domain, 14-item patient-reported outcome (PRO) measure designed to assess migraine-specific health-related quality of life. The RFR domain (7 items) measures the impact of migraine on the performance of daily social and work-related activities. AIM-D is an 11-item PRO measure evaluating functioning and activity impairment in individuals with migraine. In the PDA domain (7 items), participants rate their difficulty performing daily activities in the past 24 h. In the PI domain (4 items), participants rate their level of physical impairment in the past 24 h. AIM-D has previously been psychometrically validated in a 13-week prospective, observational trial and using phase 3 clinical data [31–33]. HIT-6 is a 6-item PRO measure assessing the ability to function at work, school, home, and in social situations. Detailed methods on these three PROs have been previously published [34]. MIDAS is a 7-item questionnaire assessing headache-related disability over a 3-month period. The MIDAS score is a sum of missed work or school days, days at work or school plus days of household work where productivity was reduced by half or more, missed household workdays, and missed non-work activity days due to headache in the last 3 months.

Statistical Analyses

Study sample size was determined by regulatory safety requirements for the duration and number of participants exposed, rather than statistical considerations. At least 140 participants were planned for enrolment in this study from the lead-in PROGRESS study to meet a minimum target of 100 participants exposed to atogepant at 1 year. The safety population was defined as all participants who received ≥ 1 dose of study drug. The modified intent-to-treat (mITT) population was defined as all participants who had an evaluable baseline period, received ≥ 1 dose of study drug, and had ≥ 1 evaluable postbaseline 4-week period of eDiary data. For the PROGRESS CM completers, baseline was the baseline value in the lead-in study, prior to starting the 12-week double blind treatment in PROGRESS. De novo EM participants underwent a dedicated screening/baseline period, and baseline for MSQ v2.1, HIT-6, and MIDAS was the last nonmissing efficacy assessment before the first dose of study intervention in the extension period. Baseline for the clinical efficacy outcomes and AIM-D was assessed during the last 28 days of the screening/baseline period. If any postbaseline eDiary period for a participant had ≥ 14 but < 28 days of completed eDiary data during a 4-week interval, then this 4-week interval was evaluable. The monthly number of headache days for this specific interval and other counting variables were prorated to standardize the count to a 28-equivalent figure. The 4-week intervals with < 14 days of completed eDiary data were considered missing.

The safety outcomes include AEs, clinical laboratory evaluations, vital sign measurements, ECG parameters, the C-SSRS, and pregnancy test. The safety analyses were performed using descriptive statistics based upon the safety population. For each of the clinical, laboratory, vital sign, and ECG parameters, the baseline was the baseline value in the lead-in study for the PROGRESS CM completers, and the last nonmissing safety assessment before the first dose of study drug for de novo EM participants. Continuous variables were summarized by the number of participants, mean, and standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables were summarized by number and percentage of participants.

Efficacy and functional outcomes were analyzed descriptively based on the mITT population. In addition, least squares mean change from baseline and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for selected efficacy outcomes, MSQ v2.1, AIM-D, and HIT-6 were based on a mixed-effects model for repeated measures with model terms including visit, baseline score, and baseline-by-visit interaction. An unstructured covariance matrix was used to model the covariance of within-person repeated measures. No inferential statistical analyses were performed for the efficacy and functional outcomes. All analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 or later (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Participants

The study enrolled 186 participants. In the PROGRESS CM completers cohort, 155 participants were included in the safety population and 150 in the mITT population. In the de novo EM cohort, 31 participants were included in the safety population and 30 in the mITT population. All CM completers rolled over to the open-label extension at visit 7 and there were no treatment gaps between completing the double-blind treatment period in PROGRESS and starting the open-label extension treatment period. Baseline demographics were similar between cohorts. PROGRESS CM completers and de novo EM participants were 91.0% (141/155) and 93.5% (29/31) female, had a mean age of 43.4 and 42.4 years, a mean body mass index of 21.9 and 22.4 kg/m2, and 100% were Japanese, respectively (Table 1). The majority of participants completed the 52-week open-label treatment period (PROGRESS CM completers, 83.9%; de novo EM participants, 90.3%). The most common reasons for discontinuation of atogepant in the PROGRESS CM completers were AEs (5.8%, 9/155), meeting mandatory withdrawal criteria at visit 1 due to abnormal clinical laboratory values or ECG (5.2%, 8/155), lack of efficacy (2.6%, 4/155), and withdrawal by participant (2.6%, 4/155). The most common reasons for discontinuation of atogepant in the de novo EM participants were withdrawal by participant (3.2%, 1/31), protocol deviation (3.2%, 1/31), and other (3.2%, 1/31) (Fig. 1). The mean (SD) treatment duration of atogepant for PROGRESS CM completers, de novo EM participants, and the total population were 322.5 (101.9), 335.6 (94.9), and 322.7 (100.7) days, respectively.

Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events

TEAEs occurred in 88.7% (165/186) of the total population (88.4% [137/155] of PROGRESS CM completers and 90.3% [28/31] of de novo EM participants) (Table 2). TEAEs that occurred in ≥ 5% of participants in the total population were pyrexia (29.0%), nasopharyngitis (16.1%), constipation (11.3%), ALT increase (7.5%), cystitis (6.5%), arthralgia (5.4%), AST increase (5.4%), diarrhea (5.4%), malaise (5.4%), and myalgia (5.4%) (Table 3). Several of the most frequently reported AEs were attributed to COVID-19 vaccination during the study period, including pyrexia, arthralgia, malaise, and myalgia. No deaths were reported during the study. Treatment-emergent serious adverse events (TESAEs) occurred in 3.8% of participants (1 incidence of each: vertigo, cholelithiasis, appendicitis, ALT increased, AST increased, uterine leiomyoma, loss of consciousness, migraine, and depression). No TESAE was considered related to atogepant treatment by the investigator. TEAEs leading to discontinuation occurred in 4.8% of participants. ALT and/or AST increase led to the discontinuation of four participants. No other TEAE led to the discontinuation of more than one participant.

Clinical Laboratory Evaluation and Vital Signs

There were nine participants with postbaseline ALT or AST elevations ≥ 3 × ULN and were adjudicated by an external clinical adjudication committee (independent panel of liver experts). Three of the nine cases were adjudicated as probably related to atogepant, two cases were identified as possibly related to atogepant, and four cases were identified as unlikely related to atogepant. Of the five cases that were either possibly or probably related to atogepant, all were nonserious, mild or moderate in severity, had no concurrent bilirubin elevation, and all cases resolved either with or without the discontinuation of treatment. One participant had concurrent ALT or AST elevations ≥ 3 × ULN and total bilirubin ≥ 1.5 × ULN in the context of symptomatic cholelithiasis. This case was serious due to a medically important event and was considered by the investigator to be not related to atogepant and adjudicated as unlikely related to atogepant by the external adjudication committee. There were no participants who met criteria for Hy’s law (Supplementary Material).

During the 52-week, open-label treatment period, 31.7% (59/186) of participants observed a weight decrease of ≥ 7% from baseline at any visit during the study. An increase of ≥ 7% of baseline weight was observed in 8/186 (4.3%) participants. There were no other clinically significant trends or changes in other laboratory test results, vital signs, or ECG during the study.

Efficacy and Functional Outcomes

The baseline mean MMDs were 18.2 days in the PROGRESS CM completers and 7.5 days in the de novo EM participants. The least squares mean change from baseline in MMDs at weeks 1–4 was − 6.4 days (95% CI − 7.5, − 5.2) for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 3.4 days (95% CI − 5.1, − 1.6) for de novo EM participants. Both reductions in mean MMDs were consistent through weeks 49–52 (Fig. 2a). The proportion of PROGRESS CM completers and de novo EM participants who achieved a ≥ 50% reduction in MMDs at weeks 1–4 was 44.0% and 63.3%, and at weeks 49–52, was 47.7% and 60.7%, respectively (Fig. 2b).

The baseline mean MHDs were 21.5 days in the PROGRESS CM completers and 8.6 days in the de novo EM participants. The least squares mean change from baseline in MHDs at weeks 1–4 was − 6.7 days (95% CI − 7.9, − 5.6) for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 4.0 days (95% CI − 5.7, − 2.2) for de novo EM participants (Fig. 2c). The baseline mean monthly acute medication use days were 16.5 days in the PROGRESS CM completers and 6.8 days in the de novo EM participants. The least squares mean change from baseline in monthly acute medication use days at weeks 1–4 was − 5.7 days (95% CI − 6.7, − 4.6) for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 3.5 days (95% CI − 4.7, − 2.3) for de novo EM participants (Fig. 2d). Reductions in mean MHDs and monthly acute medication use days in both cohorts were consistent through weeks 49–52.

The baseline mean MSQ v2.1 RFR domain score was 56.9 in the PROGRESS CM completers and 64.1 in the de novo EM participants. The least squares mean change from baseline in RFR domain score at week 12 was 17.3 (95% CI 14.8, 19.7) for PROGRESS CM completers, and 15.8 (95% CI 10.5, 21.0) for de novo EM participants. Improvements in MSQ v2.1 RFR domain score in both cohorts were consistent through week 52 (Fig. 3a). The baseline mean monthly PDA and PI domain scores of AIM-D were 24.4 and 24.1 in the PROGRESS CM completers and 11.2 and 10.8 in the de novo EM participants, respectively. The least squares mean change from baseline in monthly PDA domain score at weeks 1–4 was − 10.1 (95% CI − 11.7, − 8.4) for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 5.8 (95% CI − 7.6, − 4.1) for de novo EM participants (Fig. 3b). The least squares mean change from baseline in monthly PI domain score at weeks 1–4 was − 10.2 (95% CI − 11.8, − 8.6) for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 5.5 (95% CI − 7.4, − 3.5) for de novo EM participants. Improvements in AIM-D PDA and PI domain scores in both cohorts were consistent through weeks 49–52 (Fig. 3c). The baseline mean HIT-6 total score was 62.7 in the PROGRESS CM completers and 60.6 in the de novo EM participants. The least squares mean change from baseline in HIT-6 total score at week 4 was − 7.2 (95% CI − 8.3, − 6.1) for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 7.6 (95% CI − 10.5, − 4.7) for de novo EM participants. Improvements in HIT-6 total score in both cohorts were consistent through week 52 (Fig. 3d). The baseline mean MIDAS total score was 32.6 in the PROGRESS CM completers and 23.8 in the de novo EM participants. The mean change from baseline in MIDAS total score at week 12 was − 13.4 for PROGRESS CM completers, and − 13.8 for de novo EM participants. Improvements in MIDAS total score in both cohorts were consistent through week 52 (Supplementary Material).

Discussion

Over the 52-week, open-label study, atogepant was safe and well tolerated for the preventive treatment of migraine in Japanese participants with EM or CM, consistent with the known safety profile in the global population. There were no new safety concerns identified with atogepant across 52 weeks. Additionally, in both cohorts, there was a reduction in MMDs, MHDs, and monthly acute medication use days at weeks 1–4 that persisted through weeks 49–52. There were improvements in the MSQ v2.1 RFR domain score and MIDAS total score starting at week 12, AIM-D PDA and PI domain scores starting at weeks 1–4, and HIT-6 total score starting at week 4 that persisted over 52 weeks in both cohorts. Noting the small sample size in the de novo EM cohort, the results here show that atogepant was safe and well tolerated, and there were persisting improvements in efficacy and functional outcomes across the 52-week treatment period in Japanese participants.

During the enrollment of this study, the COVID-19 vaccine was released in Japan. Several of the more frequently reported TEAEs were related to COVID-19 vaccination, such as pyrexia, arthralgia, malaise, and myalgia. The safety profile of atogepant in the PROGRESS CM completers was consistent with that in the global, 12-week, double-blind treatment period of the phase 3 PROGRESS trial and in a 52-week interim analysis of a 3-year, global, long-term safety study (including participants from North America, Europe, and East Asia) [25, 28]. Additionally, the safety profile in the de novo EM cohort was consistent with the global 12-week trials, Japanese 12-week trial, and global long-term trials of atogepant for the preventive treatment of EM (including participants from North America and Europe) [22, 24, 26, 27]. It has been reported that racial differences may impact safety assessments; however, no new safety signals were observed in this Japanese population [30]. In summary, the types and frequencies of TEAEs and clinical laboratory evaluations and vital signs, including hepatic safety, were consistent with previous global atogepant studies. The high proportion of participants completing the 52-week open-label treatment period (PROGRESS CM completers, 83.9%; de novo EM participants, 90.3%) supports the safety and tolerability of long-term atogepant treatment in the Japanese population.

As a result of the importance of long-term data in Japanese specific cohorts, the long-term safety and efficacy of other CGRP targeted treatments, including the injectable CGRP mAbs, have been studied in multiple long-term, open-label trials in Japanese participants. The safety and efficacy of erenumab have been evaluated in participants with EM or CM in a phase 3, randomized, 28-week open-label trial and in a 76-week open-label trial, which also evaluated HIT-6 [35, 36]. The safety and efficacy of fremanezumab were evaluated in participants with EM or CM in a 52-week, randomized, open-label study, which also assessed HIT-6 and MIDAS [37]. The safety and efficacy of galcanezumab were evaluated in participants with EM or CM in a 12-month open-label study, which also assessed MSQ v2.1 RFR and Patient Global Impression of Severity [38]. The results presented here show that orally administered atogepant is safe and well tolerated, and the exploratory outcomes also show improvements in efficacy outcomes and key functional outcomes (MSQ v2.1, AIM-D, HIT-6, and MIDAS) across 52 weeks in Japanese participants. A discrete-choice experiment of 400 participants in Japan found that when patients select preventive migraine treatment, oral preventive treatment options are preferred compared to injectable options [39]. Together, these results suggest that orally administered atogepant, that has recently obtained regulatory approval in Japan [20], may address the unmet need for newer migraine preventive treatments in Japan with demonstrated long-term efficacy and safety, and an improvement in function and disability.

Both cohorts were mostly female. Additionally, participants were excluded if they used opioids or barbiturates ≥ 4 days (≥ 2 days for EM) per month in the 3 months before visit 1 or during the baseline period, and those who did not have a response to > 4 preventive treatments. Collectively, this may limit the generalizability of the results. The de novo EM cohort was small; however, the safety profile of atogepant was consistent with previous studies in EM. This was an open-label study with no control group; thus, comparative analyses were not performed.

Conclusions

This is the first long-term report of atogepant for the preventive treatment of migraine in Japanese participants. Across the 52-week open-label treatment period, atogepant treatment was safe and well tolerated. No new safety signals were identified, and the safety profile was consistent with previous 12-week and long-term trials in EM and CM [22–28]. Reductions in MMDs, MHDs, and monthly acute medication use days and improvements in functional outcomes were maintained across the 52 weeks. Collectively, atogepant was safe and well tolerated, and efficacy and functional outcomes persisted over 52 weeks in Japanese participants with EM or CM.

QuantumNeuroXAI: a quantum-inspired deep learning framework with explainability for brain signal analysis and neurological disorder detection
Authors: T Gayathri, G Manjula, Harish H Kenchannavar, Danthuluri Sudha, Santosh Kumar Jankatti, Ramandeep Kaur, Bondu Venkateswarlu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractElectroencephalography (EEG) is a non-invasive, high-temporal-resolution method for diagnosing and monitoring neurological disorders. Deep learning has recently substantially enhanced the state of the art for automated EEG analysis. However, many of the currently applied paradigms are still challenged by limited generalisation across datasets, vulnerability to noise or preprocessing changes, and the absence of interpretable decision rules. Additionally, many deep learning models operate like black boxes, which limits their use in clinical settings where interpretability and trust are key. Although the potential of quantum-inspired learning has recently been demonstrated through improved feature separability in high-dimensional signal spaces, its scope of applicability does not yet extend to deep temporal modelling and explainable artificial intelligence applications. We address these limitations by introducing QuantumNeuroXAI, a quantum-inspired deep learning framework implemented on classical hardware that leverages structured feature encoding inspired by quantum neural networks to provide inherent explainability for EEG-based diagnosis of neurological disorders. This framework hybridises quantum-inspired feature encoding with a deep-learning architecture that blends temporal convolutional and attention-based recurrent modelling to capture local and long-range patterns of dependencies in EEG signals. The framework incorporates a multi-level explainability module relevant at the signal, model, and quantum-representation levels, allowing predictions to be interpreted in a clinically meaningful and transparent fashion. We conduct extensive experiments on three publicly available EEG datasets (TUH EEG, CHB-MIT, and BCI Competition IV-2a) to evaluate the proposed framework. These quantitative results show that QuantumNeuroXAI achieves statistically significant and large effect sizes, with macro-F1 improvements of up to 5.2% over classical machine learning, deep learning, and hybrid baseline models. Additional robustness and scalability analyses further validate stable performance against dataset shift and across various preprocessing configurations. In summary, QuantumNeuroXAI is an interpretable and reproducible solution for EEG-based neurological analysis, demonstrating promise for clinical decision support and future scalability to multimodal brain signal applications. It is important to note that the proposed framework does not rely on quantum hardware and is fully implemented using classical computational resources. Introduction

Due to its high temporal resolution, portability, and cost-efficiency, electroencephalography (EEG) is still one of the most common non-invasive techniques for monitoring brain activity and aiding the diagnosis of neurological disorders. Despite the increasing prevalence of conditions, such as epilepsy, motor impairments, and cognitive dysfunctions, and the demand for accurate and timely decision-making support, automated EEG analysis systems remain underutilized. Traditional EEG analysis pipelines were based on handcrafted features in the time, frequency, and time–frequency domains, followed by classical machine learning classifiers. Although such methods have shown satisfactory performance, they tend to struggle to learn the complex, nonlinear, and highly time-variant spatial-temporal patterns underlying features, as there is significant inter-subject and cross-dataset variability in multi-channel EEG signals1,2.

Since deep learning has significantly simplified EEG-based classification and prediction through end-to-end representation learning using convolutional, recurrent, and attention-based architectures3,4, EEGLP is mainly focused on extracting features from recorded EEG data. These models have outperformed traditional methods and are capable of automatically learning in-depth discriminative features from raw or minimally preprocessed EEG data. That being said, the state of the art is not free from several limitations. However, several deep learning-based methods are not robust to dataset shift, sensitive to noise and various preprocessing choices, and mostly black-box models with limited interpretability5,6. Such opacity is especially problematic in clinical contexts where explainability is critical for trust, validation, and regulatory acceptance of model predictions6. In addition, recent quantum-inspired learning methods have introduced potential enhancements to feature separability in high-dimensional signal spaces7. However, in existing works, these ideas are commonly used in isolation and not integrated into deep temporal architectures, nor systematically analysed for explainability8.

This set of challenges emphasises the need for a holistic framework that can simultaneously tackle expressive representation learning, robustness, and the interpretability of EEG-based neurological analysis. This gap motivates the current research, which seeks to build an integrated approach that combines the strengths of quantum-inspired learning and deep neural networks (DNNs) whilst ensuring explainability as a design principle rather than an afterthought. The main aim of this work is to develop and validate a quantum–deep learning framework for classification of EEG signals into normal and abnormal, that holds the capability of (1) accurately detecting neurological disorders, (2) generalizability across heterogeneous datasets, (3) providing multi-level explanations that are consistent with neurophysiological understanding.

While the proposed QuantumNeuroXAI framework integrates existing technologies by combining multi-modal data integration with cross-modal knowledge transfer, it still offers several novel contributions. It introduces a quantum-inspired feature-encoding strategy that projects high-dimensional EEG representations into a lower-dimensional space, improving class separability and facilitating more efficient, robust learning. Then it combines this encoding with a hybrid deep learning architecture that employs temporal convolution and attention-based recurrent modelling, enabling both local and long-range temporal dependence modeling in EEG signals. Third, the framework proposes a hierarchical explainability design at the signal level (channels, time, and frequency), the model level (attention and feature attribution), and the quantum-representation level (feature sensitivity) to achieve transparent and clinically interpretable outcomes. Furthermore, we have performed a systematic evaluation of the framework using only locked public EEG datasets, enabling reproducible assessments, fair comparisons, and the widespread validation of the proposed framework.

It is important to clarify that the proposed framework is based on quantum-inspired learning principles rather than actual quantum computing hardware. All quantum-inspired transformations are implemented using classical simulations, ensuring scalability, accessibility, and practical deployment without requiring specialized quantum devices.

This paper makes the following contributions to the research literature: a quantum deep learning architecture for EEG-based detection of neurological disorders. Across five public datasets, it shows statistically significant and practical improvements over classical, deep, and hybrid baselines. It presents a holistic explainability evaluation strategy that assesses not only visualisation but also consistency and stability. Third, there is an extensive experimental evaluation of robustness, scalability, and statistical significance, which reinforces the reliability of the proposed method for practical use.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows: “Related work” includes related work on EEG-based neurological analysis, deep learning models, quantum-inspired methods, and explainable AI. In “Preliminaries”, we present the preliminaries, including EEG signal modelling, problem formulation, andbasic concepts of quantum-inspired learning and explainability. “Materials and methods” explains the proposed methodological approach (QuantumNeuroXAI), dataset description, copying, model architecture, explainability design, and training protocol for the experiment. “Experimental results” provides results of the experiment, a comparative evaluation, an ablation study, and statistical analysis. The results are presented in “Discussion, which also describes the study’s limitations. Lastly, “Conclusion and future work” summarises the paper and discusses future research.

Materials and methods

This section describes the materials and methods of the proposed QuantumNeuroXAI framework. It outlines available public datasets, preprocessing and feature construction methods, quantum-inspired encoding mechanisms, a hybrid quantum–deep learning architecture, explainability design, and experimental protocols used to ensure robust, reproducible, and interpretable analysis of brain signals.

Overview of the proposed QuantumNeuroXAI framework

The QuantumNeuroXAI framework proposed in this paper is a modular end-to-end system capable of converting raw EEG recordings into clinically meaningful predictions via a quantum–deep learning and explainability pipeline. It takes multi-channel EEG signals from heterogeneous datasets as input. It passes them through a series of stages that are tightly coupled to maintain consistent processing of signal variability, high-dimensional feature extraction, and interpretable classification.

Raw EEGs are fed into the pipeline, and the preprocessing operations — temporal segmentations, normalisations, and time–frequency transforms — serve as the first standardisation steps. These procedures translate raw, continuous EEG data into coherent representations that preserve temporal dynamics, spectral features, and channel spatial relationships. These representations act as a unified intermediary between classical known signal representations and unknown, learning-based modelling structures, enabling analysis that can be conducted irrespective of the underlying dataset.

Once the data is preprocessed, the framework performs quantum-inspired feature encoding that utilises the quantum concepts of superposition and entanglement to map classical features derived from EEG into a sufficiently abstract, higher-dimensional representational space. The expressiveness of EEG features is improved at this stage by modelling complex nonlinear dependencies among channels and frequency bands. These quantum-encoded representations are then interfaced to a hybrid quantum–deep learning core, in which temporal convolutional and attention-based recurrent layers learn short-term fluctuations and long-range neurological patterns in tandem. The hybrid structure enables the framework to leverage the representational richness of quantum-inspired circuits and the optimisation power of classical deep neural networks.

In the framework, prediction and interpretation are regarded as one inseparable outcome. In addition to classification outputs, such as neurologic disorder detection or cognitive state recognition, QuantumNeuroXAI also embeds Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) mechanisms that provide interpretable insights at multiple levels. These include signal-level, model-level, and quantum-representation-level explanations produced in parallel that each contextualise predictions in terms of EEG channels, temporal segments, and frequency components (signal-level), or feature encodings (model-level and quantum-representation-level). Implementation details

To ensure transparency and reproducibility, we implemented the proposed QuantumNeuroXAI framework using popular open-source software tools. Python is used to implement all the signal processing and model development functionality, with numerical operations, neural network construction and optimization done using standard scientific computation and deep learning libraries. Standard signal processing utilities are used for time-frequency transformations and preprocessing specific to the EEG signal, and the quantum-inspired feature encoding and hybrid model components are implemented as classical simulations embedded in the deep learning pipeline.

All experiments are performed on a multi-core CPU and a single high-memory GPU workstation to speed up model training and inference time. All the deep learning components were implemented with GPU acceleration but then quantum-inspired feature mapping and explainability computations were run on the same hardware in the absence of any specialized quantum device. Such configuration renders the proposed framework deployable on a standard computing infrastructure without requiring access to quantum hardware.

In order to ensure clear reproducibility and stable training behaviour, where all model parameters are initialized using deterministic initialization schemes, a fixed random seed is also enforced across the whole data splitting, parameter initialization and overall optimization routine. Hyperparameters like learning rates, batch sizes, and regularization coefficients are chosen based on validation performance but kept constant across datasets. All runs log software versions, hardware configurations, and experimental settings, so that reported results can be precisely replicated and fairly compared when subsequent studies are conducted.

We supplement in-dataset testing with a cross-dataset protocol to measure robustness under distribution shift. Training is performed using mini-batch optimization with a fixed set of hyperparameters kept constant across datasets to ensure fair comparison. The model is optimized using a gradient-based optimizer with an initial learning rate and batch size . Early stopping is applied based on validation loss to prevent overfitting, and standard regularization techniques are used to stabilize training. The specific loss for the task is in line with the formulation of the objective in (26) and depends on the task itself: binary cross-entropy for neurological disorder detection tasks and categorical cross-entropy for multi-class EEG decoding. Each experiment is repeated over several runs (with different random seeds) to allow for stability analysis and shows mean performance and a measure of variability.

We implemented all the experiments in Python with common deep learning and science computing libraries. Hybrid model optimization can still be sped up with training and inference running on a GPU-enabled workstation, while quantum-inspired feature encoding and measurement can be performed inside the same classical computing environment. Baseline models and comparison methods

To facilitate comparison, a baseline model was chosen to represent each one of three classes: classical machine learning (handbuilt EEG descriptors), deep learning (purely deep learning, working on the same the same EEG tensor, from the same standardized EEG data), and quantum inspired or hybrid reference model that controls for the influence of the quantum encoding stage. To make sure that the baselines are compared in the fairest of manner, all were trained and evaluated the same preprocessing pipeline “EEG preprocessing and feature construction”, on the same subject-independent split protocol “Experimental setup and protocol”, and all reported with the same metrics “Algorithmic representation”.

For classical machine learning baselines, we used EEG feature descriptors that are widely used and implemented for calculating features per window, such as band-power statistics over canonical frequency bands, as well as standard nonlinear descriptors such as signal entropy. Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine (type of Kernel: RBF), Random Forest, and gradient-boosted decision trees were trained using these features. This means that hyperparameters for each classical baseline were tuned on the validation set and final performance was reported on the held-out test set, in like manner to the experimental protocol for the proposed model. In addition, EEGNet, a lightweight and widely adopted deep learning architecture specifically designed for EEG signal classification, is included as a standard baseline for comparison under the same experimental settings.

We created deep learning baselines around the standard choices in EEG modeling for both time-series and time–frequency inputs. These were a 1D-CNN trained directly on segmented multi-channel EEG, a CNN–LSTM pipeline to co-learn local temporal patterns and long-range dependencies, a temporal convolutional network (TCN) baseline for dilated temporal modeling, and a Transformer-encoder baseline applied to sequential embeddings from the time–frequency representation. Also inspired a lightweight EEG-specific architecture (EEGNet-style) to give a parameter-efficient baseline. All deep baselines used the same tensor inputs as those used by QuantumNeuroXAI and were trained using identical optimization hyperparameters and early stopping protocols to mitigate tuning bias.

Quantum-inspired and hybrid reference models were similarly tested where applicable in order to separately identify the advantage gained by the integration strategy proposed in this paper. These consisted of a quantum-kernel classifier baseline based on quantum-inspired feature maps, simulation of a variational quantum classifier based on measured expectation-style features, and an ablation on the hybrid model, which maintained the quantum encoding stage but excluded the proposed fusion and multi-level explainability components. With this new set of references, we are able to attribute any performance gain obtained to the quantum–deep fusion design itself, or the end-to-end training origin from QuantumNeuroXAI.

Moreover, to specifically assess the role of our quantum-inspired encoding compared to other classical nonlinear transformations, we also add here a corresponding kernel-based baseline using a radial basis function (RBF) mapping, which acts in the same feature space. This baseline represents the classical high-dimensional nonlinear projection. This comparison allows us to consider whether our proposed quantum-inspired feature encoding can offer extra representational advantages compared to traditional nonlinear transformation.

To ensure a meaningful and competitive evaluation, the proposed model was compared with the most powerful deep learning baseline and the best classical baseline, i.e., the transformer encoder and the CNN–LSTM, respectively.

Since all models were calculated with the same data splits, we used paired statistical tests. Summary of experimental findings

Through experimental evidence, we show that QuantumNeuroXAI consistently outperforms classical machine learning, deep learning, and hybrid reference models across all necessary public EEG datasets. When evaluated on the TUH EEG, CHB-MIT, and BCI IV-2a datasets, the proposed framework yields the highest in-dataset accuracy, macro-F1, and AUROC scores, confirming its potential to learn both discriminative and transferable representations from heterogeneous brain signals. Cross-dataset experiments further demonstrate that QuantumNeuroXAI shows a lower dataset-shift generalisation gap, indicative of its robustness with respect to variations in EEG acquisition protocol, subject population, and task definition. Ablation studies demonstrate that quantum-inspired feature encoders offer the most significant performance gains, while attention-based temporal modelling and explicit quantum–classical fusion deliver complementary improvements.

In addition to predictive accuracy, the framework also exhibits excellent reliability and interpretability. Stability analysis across multiple random initialisations reveals minimal performance variation, thereby assuring the reproducibility and trustworthiness of our convergence. We also provide a sensitivity analysis indicating that changes in temporal window size and feature dimensionality result in graceful performance degradation, demonstrating that the model is not brittle to preprocessing configurations. The variance across multiple levels of explanation emphasises valid explanations at the signal-explanatory, model, and quantum-representation levels, and clinical case studies demonstrate relevant EEG morphology, model reasoning, and coherence. Through statistical significance testing, the performance improvements are confirmed as robust and practically relevant, with medium-to-large effect sizes across datasets.

These strengths notwithstanding, important limitations are also noted. Despite requiring more training time and memory than lightweight CNN-based models, the overhead of this hybrid architecture is relatively similar to that of end-to-end Transformer-based methods. Quantum-inspired feature encoding is a helpful solution, but it also requires more hyperparameters to be tuned for best performance. Lastly, the process of explanation generation incurs an additional computational cost, especially when exhaustive; therefore, selective or on-demand explanation approaches might be more desirable for real-time clinical deployment. In general, the experimental results corroborate the validity of QuantumNeuroXAI as an interpretable, strong-performing, and robust framework, while indicating explicit scopes for future optimisation and scalability improvements.

Discussion

As a consequence of the rise of, e.g., neurodegenerative disorders and the high-precision acquisition of brain signals via electroencephalography, automated, data-driven diagnostic frameworks have been widely investigated. Conventionally, EEG analysis pipelines are based on human-derived features and shallow classifiers, which are interpretable but unable to capture the rich spatiotemporal dynamics underlying multi-channel brain signals. While more recent deep learning methods, specifically convolutional and recurrent neural networks, have advanced representations, they generally exhibit poor generalisation to new datasets, are sensitive to noise, and lack an interpretable decision structure, limiting their use in clinical practice.

Currently, there is a significant gap in the state of the art: the concurrent use of high-dimensional EEG representations with temporal dependencies while also maintaining explainability. Most current deep learning models are designed to be accurate on one of many datasets or tasks, avoiding issues with robustness against dataset shift and with interpretable reasoning consistent with neurophysiological knowledge. Feature separability has been explored via quantum-inspired learning methods; however, these methods are applied in isolation, lack integration into deep temporal architectures, and are less tailored for clinical interpretability. These limitations illustrate the necessity of new hybrid techniques that coalesce expressive learning, robustness, and transparency under one roof.

To tackle these challenges, we propose a QuantumNeuroXAI framework that combines quantum-inspired feature encoding with a deep temporal model, providing multi-level explainability. Our methodology is novel in its integrated design: quantum-inspired data encoding improves representational compactness and effective class separation; attention-guided deep learning extracts temporal dependencies in EEG signals from drivers experiencing unsafe driving; and the explainability module delivers coherent system insights at multiple levels, including EEG, model, and quantum-representation levels. In contrast to previous methods that adopt a post-hoc explainability approach, the presented framework prescribes a design objective that embeds interpretability, resulting in reproducible and clinically relevant explanations.

The experimental results validate this method. The proposed framework achieves better classification and significantly smaller generalisation gaps than classical and deep learning baselines across multiple public EEG datasets. Analysis of statistical significance demonstrates that these performance improvements are consistent and practically relevant, and robustness and scalability assessments exhibit reliable performance stability across diverse preprocessing settings and dataset sizes. Critically, the explainability case studies show that the model attends to physiologically meaningful channels, times, and frequencies, providing additional evidence that it can be trusted.

This research serves to advance the state of EEG-based analysis of neurological disorders by overcoming significant limitations of the current methods, which are (i) restricted generalisation, (ii) limited interpretability, and (iii) a fragmented approach to modelling. Our results demonstrate that quantum-inspired and explainable deep learning frameworks hold great promise for the construction of trustworthy clinical decision-making support systems. This has far-reaching implications, including increased transparency in diagnostic criteria, publication of public datasets to enhance reproducibility, and greater adoption of trustworthy AI-assisted evaluation in neurology. “Limitations of the study” provides an overview of the proposed study’s limitations and potential avenues for improvement.

Limitations of the study

Although the QuantumNeuroXAI presents promising results, there are also very serious limitations to the current study. Even though the framework is being tested on a range of established public EEG datasets, they are collected in controlled experimental or clinical protocols and do not necessarily reflect the variability encountered in day-to-day clinical practice. However, given implementation challenges due to diverse acquisition hardware, electrode placement inconsistencies, motion artefacts, and the presence of comorbid neurological conditions, performance may be compromised on translation into routine clinical practice. Thus, to confirm real-world robustness, further validation should be performed on heterogeneous multi-centre clinical datasets.

Second, while the quantum-inspired feature encoding is computationally less demanding than actual quantum implementations, it adds more design options, including the dimensionality of the feature projection and the importance of the feature encoding. These parameters are chosen empirically and can have different optimal values depending on the task and dataset. Their heavy reliance on hyperparameter tuning limits their use in an out-of-the-box fashion. It necessitates domain expertise at deployment, especially when the framework needs to be adapted to new neurological disorders or EEG paradigms.

Third, the evaluation of explainability mainly emphasises qualitative visualisations and consistency analysis across runs. These analyses yield valuable insights into model reasoning, but the study lacked formal clinician-in-the-loop validation or a quantitative evaluation of explanation utility in the clinical workflow. Adding more expert-driven evaluation, along with user studies, would help bolster claims about clinical interpretability and decision support in practice.

Conclusion and future work

In this paper, we have introduced QuantumNeuroXAI, a comprehensive quantum–deep learning architecture developed for the reliable, interpretable, and scalable analysis of EEG signals for the detection of neurological disorders. It ameliorates one of the significant drawbacks of all current analysis approaches (limited generalisation, opaque choices, and fragmented modelling strategies) by combining quantum-inspired feature encoding, attention-guided deep temporal modelling, and multi-level explainability within a single framework. Abstract: With extensive experiments across different public EEG datasets, results show that QuantumNeuroXAI consistently outperforms classical and deep learning baselines, demonstrating statistical significance and stability, even when dataset shift and different preprocessing configurations are used. Explainability analysis indicated that model predictions are consistent with physiologically meaningful channels, temporal windows, and spectra, demonstrating the potential for trustworthy clinical decision support. The scope for future work from this research can be manyfold. By being able to validate in standard conditions using large-scale multi-centre clinical EEG datasets, the impact of the framework will also grow at the translational level. A second option would be to leverage adaptive or self-supervised learning schemes, which can improve robustness to inter-subject and cross-session variability. Third, the quantum-inspired encoding can be generalised to learn more expressive feature mappings and to support hybrid optimisation approaches, especially by leveraging technological developments in high-quality quantum hardware in the near future. Future work may also include incorporating a clinician feedback mechanism to quantitatively measure the utility of generated explanations, along with a measure of reliability, within diagnostic workflows. Lastly, this framework can be expanded to include multimodal neurophysiological data, such as EEG–fNIRS or EEG–MRI fusion, which is an exciting step forward towards a more complete characterisation of brain disorders.

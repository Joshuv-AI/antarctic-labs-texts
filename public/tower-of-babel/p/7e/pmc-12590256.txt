VAPA strikes the patient again!
Authors: Rajeev Soman, Sanraksha Mayya, Sushrut Ganpule, Geethu Joe
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractViral associated pulmonary aspergillosis (VAPA) is increasingly being recognized by clinicians. However, the same patient developing VAPA after two separate viral respiratory illness appears to be rare. We describe a patient who developed influenza associated invasive aspergillosis (IAPA) which resolved completely with treatment. A second episode of VAPA developed after one year following human rhinovirus/enterovirus with a nodular infiltrate at a different location in the lungs as compared to earlier. Neither episode required ICU admission and both episodes were treated successfully.Keywords: Viral associated pulmonary aspergillosis (VAPA), Influenza associated invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IAPA), Aspergillus, Respiratory viral infections (RVI)

Introduction

This case report describes a rare clinical scenario of viral-associated pulmonary aspergillosis (VAPA) occurring twice in the same patient, each following a different viral respiratory illness. The first episode was after influenza, and the second episode occurred one year later after human rhinovirus/enterovirus infection. The second episode involved a different radiological locations within the lungs, suggesting reinfection rather than relapse. Notably, neither episode required ICU admission, and both were successfully treated. To our knowledge, such a recurrence of VAPA in a non-critically ill patient has rarely been documented, and we believe this case report adds important insight into the clinical spectrum, risk factors, and evolving understanding of VAPA beyond classical ICU settings.

Case presentation

A 62-year-old female patient had a history influenza A (H3N2) infection in September 2023 which was diagnosed by Truenat real time PCR platform on September 25, 2023 (Day 0). She developed persistent cough, low grade fever and breathlessness and presented to us on Day+20. HRCT thorax showed right upper and mid-zone dense nodules (Fig. 1). Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid galactomannan (GM) antigen (by ELISA) ∗ was positive with index value of 1.3642. BAL fungal culture was negative after 21 days and so species identification was not possible. A diagnosis of probable influenza associated invasive aspergillosis (IAPA) was made as per case definition for IAPA [1]. She was treated with 6 weeks of voriconazole and achieved a voriconazole trough level of 3.5mg/l. She developed adverse drug reactions (ADR) to voriconazole in the form of skin rash and QT prolongation in the 6th week of therapy (Day+60) and voriconazole was stopped. Follow up imaging (Day+62) showed complete resolution of the nodules as shown in Fig. 2.

The patient remained well till November 2024 (Month+14), when she developed dry cough, fever with chills and breathlessness on exertion for a week. After admission, HRCT thorax showed right upper zone dense nodules (Fig. 3) at a location different from that in the 1st episode indicating that these were not due to incomplete resolution of the previous infection. Bronchoscopy showed normal findings and BAL fluid smears with gram, Ziehl Neelsen, KOH and calcofluor stains were negative for relevant organisms. However, BAL fluid multiplex PCR was positive for human rhinovirus/enterovirus and galactomannan antigen (by ELISA) was positive with an index value of 2.6484. Serum galactomannan (by Lateral Flow Assay) and beta-D-glucan∗∗ were negative, but serum Aspergillus PCR∗∗∗ was positive. These investigations suggested a second episode of probable viral associated pulmonary aspergillosis (VAPA). BAL fungal culture was negative, thus precluding species identification and antifungal susceptibility testing. The patient was treated with isavuconazole (ISVCZ) due to previous ADR to voriconazole (VCZ). ISVCZ trough level was checked after a week of therapy which was 5.4mg/L. She improved after 6 weeks of therapy. HRCT thorax at the end of therapy showed complete resolution of the nodules (Fig. 4) (Month+16).

Discussion

Invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IPA) is being identified often as a complication of severe respiratory viral infections (RVIs) and is referred to as VAPA. IPA is more likely to occur following influenza (12.5 %) compared to non-influenza RVIs (4.6 %). Among the non-influenza RVIs, rhinovirus/enterovirus was the most common (57.9 %) according to published literature [2].

In the IAPA criteria [1], ICU admission for respiratory distress that was temporally related to influenza was an entry criterion. This is probably needed to identify a serious influenza illness with significant respiratory perturbation facilitating invasion by Aspergillus. Respiratory epithelial damage, abnormal macrophage response, and down-regulation of multiple genes involved in recognition, opsonization and elimination of Aspergillus conidia may be involved [3,4]. However, in this patient neither episode of preceding viral illness had required an ICU admission. Considering the very high environmental Aspergillus spore count in India [5], it may be surmised that IPA may result even after a less severe viral illness that does not require ICU admission. The IAPA criteria [1] are proposed and used for clinical research studies and not necessarily for therapeutic decisions in practice. Therefore, patients in India may have IAPA, even when the entry criterion is not fully satisfied. Indeed, at the authors’ institute a considerable number of patients with VAPA are seen, where the preceding viral illness was mild or unrecognized, but diagnosed in retrospect by multiplex PCR.

Other risk factors for VAPA in immunocompetent patients include steroid use, severe septic shock, acute respiratory distress syndrome, severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, use of neuraminidase inhibitors, smoking, male gender, subtype A of H1N1, solid organ transplant and hematological malignancies, none of which were present in this patient [6,7]. It may be speculated that an excessive environmental exposure was common to both episodes. Alternatively, the patient may have had subtle epithelial damage or immunological derangements which may have resulted in two distinct episodes of VAPA.

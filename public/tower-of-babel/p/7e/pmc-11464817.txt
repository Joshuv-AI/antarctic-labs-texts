Early screening of miliary tuberculosis with tuberculous meningitis based on few-shot learning with multiple windows and feature granularities
Authors: Yuan Tian, Yongquan Liang, Yufeng Chen, Lei Li, Hongyang Bian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTuberculous meningitis (TBM) is a fatal tuberculosis caused by a large number of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis) spread by blood flow, with a case fatality rate of more than 50%. It is one of the most serious complications of miliary tuberculosis (MT), whose incidence is closely related to MT. If doctors can provide early diagnosis and active treatment for TBM, the case fatality rate will be significantly reduced. At present, there is a lack of methods to predict the progression of MT to TBM in clinic. To explore whether MT cases will experience TBM, we propose an early screening model of miliary tuberculosis with tuberculous meningitis (MT-TBM) based on few-shot learning with multiple windows and feature granularities (MWFG). This model aims to screen potential TBM cases through chest computerized tomography (CT) images of MT cases. Chest CT is a routine examination for MT cases. The MWFG module can extract more comprehensive features from a set of CT images of each MT case. The softmax classifier with adaptive regularization is trained on the cooperation of support set and query set, which can effectively prevent overfitting. Experiments on a dataset of 40 MT cases with chest CT images established by the medical records demonstrate that our proposed model achieves state-of-the-art performance in the early screening of MT-TBM. It can establish the connection between MT and MT-TBM through chest CT images of MT cases. The early screening model of MT-TBM based on few-shot learning with MWFG fills the research gap in computer-aided predicting TBM and has certain clinical effects. This research can provide some reference for clinicians in early diagnosis of MT-TBM and help clinicians in the early prevention and treatment of TBM for MT patients.Keywords: Computer-Aided Diagnostic, Few-shot Learning, Multiple Windows and Feature Granularities Module, Miliary Tuberculosis with Tuberculous Meningitis, Chest CT ImagesSubject terms: Tuberculosis, Computer science

Introduction

Tuberculosis is a chronic infectious disease caused by M. tuberculosis. According to the World Tuberculosis Report 2023 published by World Health Organization (WHO)1, tuberculosis is the second leading cause of death by a single infectious disease in the world. About 1.3 million patients died of tuberculosis worldwide, nearly twice as many as Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS). Since M. tuberculosis is mainly spread through the air, tuberculosis mostly presents in the respiratory system. When M. tuberculosis is spread to organs and tissues through blood transmission, a special form of pulmonary tuberculosis will appear, known as miliary tuberculosis (MT), which can cause many tuberculous lesions with the size of millet in the lung2.

MT is prone to occur in children, HIV carriers, and immunocompromised population, with a case fatality rate of 13–15%3. More importantly, it is often an early manifestation of systemic tuberculosis dissemination. Besides lungs, M. tuberculosis can also spread to other organs such as the liver, kidneys, bones, and brain through blood flow4. Tuberculous meningitis (TBM) is caused by the spread of M. tuberculosis to the central nervous system. TBM patients may experience symptoms such as headache, fever, nausea, and vomiting. As the condition progresses, serious clinical manifestations such as stiff neck, consciousness disorders, epilepsy, and paralysis may occur. There is a close causality between MT and TBM. TBM is one of the most common and serious complications of MT, with a case fatality rate of up to 50%5.

In clinical practice, the diagnosis of TBM can be definite by processing cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples of patients obtained by lumbar puncture in three ways: 1) the acid-fast bacteria (AFB) staining of the samples is positive; 2) find M. tuberculosis after culturing the samples; 3) Obtain reliable positive result of molecular biology6. However, lumbar puncture is an invasive operation with technical difficulty, which has the risks of bleeding, infection, and other complications7. It is impossible to use lumbar puncture for all MT cases to screen TBM. In addition, most miliary tuberculosis with tuberculous meningitis (MT-TBM) patients lack typical neurological symptoms in the early stage. At present, there is no better method (quick and easy, less traumatic, high positivity rate, et al.) to assist in the diagnosis of MT-TBM. The risk of missed diagnosis in MT-TBM is high. It is almost a blank in the research on predicting whether MT cases will progress to MT-TBM cases, which delays treatment and significantly increases the case fatality rate8.

Therefore, we use the deep learning-based few-shot learning to deal with this challenge. Deep learning has achieved advanced performance in medical image analysis, which have broad application prospects and promote the further development of medical theory9. The strong performance heavily relies on training networks with abundant labeled medical data. Medical data annotation is scarce and costly, because medical data is different to collect and annotation requires domain expertise in the relevant fields10. So that, in recent years, deep learning-based few-shot learning has become a popular direction in medical image analysis11. Few-shot learning can recognize novel categories with few support examples by taking advantage of knowledge coming from previously learned categories12.

Data description

Cases information

We aim at obtaining an early screening model of MT-TBM that can learn fast to perform classification on potential MT-TBM cases and MT-NTBM cases from a few annotated chest CT images of MT cases. After retrieving all inpatient medical records of Tuberculosis Department of Shandong Public Health Clinical Center from January 1, 2022 to December 31, 2022, a total of 98 cases were diagnosed as miliary tuberculosis (i.e., hematogenous pulmonary tuberculosis). We read these medical records in detail and screened these cases according to the following conditions:

(1) Exclude cases younger than 18 years and older than 90 years.

(2) Exclude cases with incomplete diagnosis.

(3) Exclude cases with incomplete medical records.

A total of 32 cases were excluded. Among the remaining 66 cases, 27 MT cases experienced TBM complication and 39 MT cases did not. 20 cases were selected randomly from MT-TBM and MT-NTBM cases respectively to form the experimental dataset. We collected basic information of the selected 40 MT cases such as gender, age, body mass index (BMI), complications, consciousness status, neurologic of post-discharge, case number of lumbar punctures, CSF laboratory examination, et al. The consciousness status is represented by the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score on admission, which is divided into 1–15 points13. The lower the GCS score is, the more severe the disturbance of consciousness is, and 15 points indicate clear consciousness. The neurologic of post-discharge is represented by the Cerebral Performance Category (CPC) score, which is divided into 1–5 points13. The higher the CPC score is, the worse the neurological prognosis is, and 5 points indicate clinical death. Chest CT image selection

Chest CT is a routine examination for MT cases. The chest CT image of MT has special manifestations. The lesions of MT are not limited to a certain lung lobe or segment but involve all lung lobes. They are small nodules distributed in both lungs evenly and symmetrically, with a diameter of about 1–3 mm2, as shown in Fig. 1.

Lesion distribution of MT in CT image. The lesions are marked with red annotations.

Therefore, we select CT scans of five typical cross sections of the lungs for image analysis, including: plane of tracheal bifurcation (Fig. 2 (a)), right superior lobar bronchus (Fig. 2 (b)), intermediate bronchus (Fig. 2 (c)), right middle lobar bronchus (Fig. 2 (d)), and right inferior lobar bronchus (Fig. 2(e)). These five CT images have significant imaging features with clear bronchus. They can summarize the general manifestation of diffuse lesions from superior lung fields to lower lung fields14. This way is also convenient for doctors to pick out CT images.

That is, a total of 40 MT cases (20 MT-TBM cases and 20 MT-NTBM cases) were selected and each case contains five chest CT images of five typical cross sections. After organizing cases and chest CT images, we established a dataset of MT cases with chest CT images. The original size of CT images is 512 × 512 pixels. Few-shot classification framework with multiple windows and feature granularities

In few-shot classification, there are usually three types of datasets: support set, query set and auxiliary set. Support set and query set form a few-shot classification task, corresponding to the training set and the test set in the general classification task. Each episode includes K support samples and some query samples of C classes, that is, a C-way K-shot learning task. There are only a few labeled samples per class in the support set, which cannot effectively train deep learning network models. An auxiliary set is usually introduced to learn transferable knowledge to solve this problem. Compared with support set, auxiliary set contains more classes, and there are more labeled samples in each class. But the class space of auxiliary set and support set is disjoint.

Our research is a series of 2-way K-shot learning tasks. Based on the data characteristics of MT cases, we propose a few-shot classification framework with multiple windows and feature granularities for early screening the potential TBM cases. We introduce the multiple windows and feature granules (MWFG) module in the fine-tuning stage of baseline + + model to enrich the feature representation of MT cases and introduce an adaptive regularization in the classifier to prevent overfitting.

Multiple windows and feature granularities module

The process of medical data collection and processing is complex and requires specialized equipment and domain expertise. Each case has its own unique real medical data. In the absence of clinical knowledge, simple computer methods such as data augmentation and generative adversarial networks (GAN) are not suitable for expanding medical datasets to some extent. It is important to make full use of the medical data organized by professional doctors.

Therefore, in the fine-tuning stage of few-shot learning, a multiple windows and feature granularities (MWFG) module is introduced in the process of feature extraction. Small-sized windows focus on local features of CT images and the large-sized window focus on overall features. Different feature granularities can capture dependencies between more image information. The MWFG module can extract image features more abundant.

Figure 5 shows the process of extracting feature vector of a CT image through MWFG module. Divide the input CT image into four image blocks of the same size and add the position embedding to make four small-sized windows. Input four small-sized windows and one large-sized window of the input CT image into the pre-trained feature extractor and extract the coarse-granularity feature maps and fine-granularity feature maps of each window respectively. The multi-granularity feature maps of five windows are merged into the feature vector of the input CT image through resample patches and average pooling.

The process of extracting feature vector of a CT image through MWFG module.

Each MT case contains 5 chest CT images. For an input MT case, a set of feature vectors of 5 CT images is extracted through MWFG module and concat them to obtain the feature vector of the input MT case. The process is shown in Fig. 6.

The process of obtaining the feature vector of an input MT case.

Feature extractor: Swin Transformer

In the pre-training stage, we adopt a Swin Transformer—tiny version (Swin-T)20as the feature extractor. Swin Transformer is a general-purpose backbone of Transformer-based model for computer vision. The hierarchical architecture and shifted windowing scheme in Swin Transformer make it compatible with a broad range of vision tasks and achieve SOTA performance on image classification, object detection and semantic segmentation20. An overview of the Swin-T architecture is presented in Fig. 7.

(a) The architecture of a Swin-T; (b) two successive Swin Transformer Blocks. W-MSA and SW-MSA are multi-head self-attention modules with regular and shifted windowing configurations, respectively.

As shown in Fig. 7(a), Swin-T is divided into four stages by three times patch merging. The channel number of the hidden layers in the first stage is set to C=96, layer numbers of four stages are 2,2,6,2, the default window size is 7, the query dimension of each head is 32. As the network gets deeper, each stage jointly produces a hierarchical representation, with the same feature map resolutions as those of typical convolutional networks, like VGG21and ResNet22.

As shown in Fig. 7(b), a swin transformer block consists of a multi-head self-attention (MSA) module, which can be a window-based MSA (W-MSA) module or a shifted window MSA (SW-MSA) module, followed by a 2-layer MLP with GELU non-linearity in between. A LayerNorm (LN) layer is applied before each MSA module and each MLP, and a residual connection is applied after each module. Successive Swin Transformer blocks are built by a shifted window partitioning approach, which alternates between W-MSA module and SW-MSA module. They can improve the computation efficiency and SW-MSA introduces cross-window connections to improve modeling power.

In the pre-training stage, Swin-T performs image classification on ImageNet-1 K23. Follow the reference24, use an AdamW optimizer for 300 epochs using a cosine decay learning rate scheduler and 20 epochs of linear warm-up, a batch size of 1024, an initial learning rate of 0.001, and a weight decay of 0.05. The trained version can be obtained from25. That is, ImageNet-1 K is the auxiliary set of this few-shot learning task, whose class space is disjoint with the support set. Although ImageNet-1 K does not contain any category being relevant to the medical field, research has demonstrated the effectiveness of their contribution to the few-shot medical image classification task10.

Following the common practice, we adjust the input CT image to unified size of 256 × 256 pixels and use random horizontal flipping for image augmentation. Feature maps output after stage 2 are selected as the coarse-granularity feature maps in the MWFG module, feature maps output after stage 4 are selected as the fine-granularity feature maps. After performing resample patches and average pooling on these feature maps, the feature vector corresponding to the input CT image is obtained, with a size of [960,1]. Each MT case contains 5 chest CT images, the feature vector of each MT case is obtained by features concatenation, with a size of [4800,1].

Experiments

Setup

We aim at the early screening of potential TBM cases in MT cases, it is a series of 2-way K-shot classification tasks. The support set consists of K MT-TBM cases and K MT-NTBM cases, the query set of 10 MT-TBM cases and 10 MT-NTBM cases. The support cases are different from the query cases. To observe the impact of K on few-shot classification tasks, K is set to 1,2,3,5,10. We randomly enumerate 1000 episodes for fine-tuning stage, every episode contains a support set with 2 K cases for training and a query set with 20 cases for testing.

The experiments contain two parts. One part is the ablation study to verify the effect of different K values, MWFG module, and softmax classifier with adaptive regularization. The other part is to compare the classification results with other commonly used methods. We implement the proposed model using the PyTorch (Python) on a NVIDIA RTX 4060Ti GPU. SPSS is also used in the experiments.

We adopt accuracy for evaluation metrics. Accuracy refers to the percentage of correctly predicted results in the total sample, that is, the proportion of correctly classified cases in query set. We report the average accuracy and the standard deviation of the classification results as well as the 95% confidence intervals. And we also visualize the classification results through ROC curves.

Comparison with common methods

In this part of experiments, we use common machine learning methods for early screening of MT-TBM cases, and compare the classification results with our proposed model. In the common machine learning methods, we select the classification and regression tree (CART) model, support vector machine (SVM) model, naive bayes model (NBM) and k-nearest neighbor algorithm (KNN)26. Use the same feature vectors of MT cases as our proposed model. Divide 40 cases into training set and test set randomly, training set and test set both include 20 cases (10 MT-TBM cases and 10 MT-NTBM cases), which is consistent with our 10-shot learning task. Obtain the average accuracy of these machine learning methods after 50 times twofold cross-validation experiments.

The ROC curves for accuracy evaluation of these methods are shown in Fig. 9. It can be intuitively observed that our proposed few-shot classification framework with MWFG performs better in the early screening of MT-TBM cases.

The MT dataset we established contains 40 cases from 2 classes. It is impossible for our MT dataset to support the training of an entire deep learning networks. Few-shot learning methods and machine learning methods can be applied on small-scale datasets. Therefore, we choose few-shot learning to deal with our work and compare with machine learning methods.

Therefore, the early screening model of MT-TBM based on few-shot learning with MWFG proposed in this paper has SOTA performance. It can screen potential TMB cases in MT cases to improve the early diagnosis of MT-TBM and provide some reference for clinicians.

Discussion

In recent years, research on improving the positive diagnosis rate of tuberculosis based on deep learning has gradually increased27.

In the current research on tuberculosis detection through medical image analysis, part of them is around the acid-fast stain images of pathogenic specimens under the microscope, which greatly reduce labor costs and improve accuracy28. But if the patient is infected with Nontuberculous mycobacteria (NTM), the stained specimen will also show positive29. So, this technology has shortcomings in the diagnosis of tuberculosis.

A large part of deep learning-based research on tuberculosis detection uses the chest digital radiography (CDR) images, which can improve the sensitivity and specificity of tuberculosis diagnosis30. CDR is a fundamental method in lung disease examination, which generates medical images through the penetrability of X-rays and digital detector technology. However, the lungs are three-dimensional organs, and CDR can only provide two-dimensional images. When the lesions are overlapping in the patient's lungs, CDR cannot display them clearly. Due to the limitations of two-dimensional imaging, the tuberculosis detection through CDR has high misdiagnosis and missed diagnosis rate31.

CT scan is an advanced medical imaging method, which generates medical images through the rotating X-rays and computer technology. Compared to CDR, CT can provide serial cross-sectional images inside the human body with higher image resolution and more details. Currently, doctors tend to use chest CT for tuberculosis diagnosis. So that, many tuberculosis detection research based on CDR do not have enough clinical value.

For miliary tuberculosis, CT images have special manifestations, as shown in Sect. 2.2. Doctors can diagnose miliary tuberculosis more accurately through CT images. There is still a lack of methods to predict whether tuberculous meningitis will occur in miliary tuberculosis cases at present. If the connection between miliary tuberculosis and tuberculous meningitis can be established through chest CT images of patients, it can be used to assist doctors in predicting tuberculous meningitis and fill the research gap in this area.

This research can to some extent avoid delaying treatment of tuberculous meningitis, reduce the case fatality rate, and improve the prognostic of neurological function. At the same time, it can reduce the extra trauma and complications caused by lumbar puncture. This research has sufficient practical value for clinicians with broad prospects.

Meanwhile, the complications of miliary tuberculosis are diverse. Due to the pathogens spread by blood flow, besides tuberculous meningitis, they could affect other organs. If M. tuberculosis spreads to the pericardium, it could cause pericarditis and inflammatory exudation, pericardial effusion and thickening may occur in severe cases. When pericardial tamponade occurs and further worsens, it can be life-threatening, which known as tuberculous pericarditis32. If M. tuberculosis spreads to kidneys, it could cause tuberculous nephritis33. Tuberculous nephritis can lead to the trauma of renal parenchyma and chronic renal insufficiency, resulting in urine decrease, edema, hypertension. In severe cases, it can progress to end-stage renal disease. Moreover, the acute stage of miliary tuberculosis can cause diffuse alveolar damage and permeability pulmonary edema in the lungs, namely secondary acute respiratory distress syndrome, with high case fatality rate34. Due to limitations in case collection, we are unable to develop the same early screening model for other complications as tuberculosis meningitis. This could be our future research direction.

In the future, our research can be improved in the following two main aspects:

(1) This research is a retrospective study. We designed an early screening model to explore the correlation between the chest CT images of miliary tuberculosis cases and the morbidity of pulmonary tuberculosis. But we did not compare our model with the doctors' clinical prediction results, so we can design a prospective comparative study in the future.

(2) It is relatively simple to predict the morbidity of tuberculous meningitis only through the CT images of miliary tuberculosis cases. If we can comprehensively analyze the medical history, clinical symptoms and early laboratory inspection results of patients, our early screening model will further improve the classification accuracy and clinical value, which is our further research plan.

Conclusion

There is still a lack of methods to predict whether MT cases will experience TBM. To explore the progression of MT to TBM in clinic, we proposed an early screening model of MT-TBM based on few-shot learning with MWFG, which aims to predict potential MT-TBM cases by chest CT images of a small number of MT cases.

We retrieved inpatient medical records of Tuberculosis Department of Shandong Public Health Clinical Center in 2022 and set up a small-scale dataset of 40 MT cases. This dataset includes 20 MT-TBM cases and 20 MT-NTBM cases, each case corresponds to 5 typical CT images selected by experienced doctors.

Experiments show that our model can establish the connection between MT and TBM through chest CT images. It can be used to assist doctors in predicting MT-TBM and fill the research gap in this area, which has certain effectiveness of computer aided diagnosis and patients will also benefit.

In the future, we will continue to collect more MT cases, explore the relationship between CT images of MT cases and other complications, and strengthen information exchange with clinical practice. We will also work on establishing a more comprehensive early screening model of MT-TBM based on the patient's overall condition.

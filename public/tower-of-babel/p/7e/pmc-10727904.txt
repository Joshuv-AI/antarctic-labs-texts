Foreign Body Granuloma Following Hwangryunhaedok-tang Pharmacopuncture for Postherpetic Neuralgia
Authors: Na Young Kim, Hae Chang Joh, Jeong Eun Kim, Young Suck Ro, Joo Yeon Ko
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractForeign body granuloma can be caused by endogenous compounds as well as various injectable materials. In oriental medicine, pharmacopuncture combining herbal medicine administration and injection is one of the commonly used procedures. Hwangryunhaedok-tang (HHT, a.k.a., Huang-Lian-Jie-Du-Tang in China), an oriental medicinal herb known to produce anti-inflammatory effects, has been recently made in pharmacopuncture products and commonly used for various disorders. An 88-year-old female presented with multiple tender nodules on the left parietal scalp and forehead. The diagnosis of foreign body granuloma caused by HHT pharmacopuncture was revealed by more detailed previous treatment history of postherpetic neuralgia and histopathologic examination. Herein, we report a foreign body granuloma as a delayed adverse reaction caused by non-standard administration of herbal extracts, considered biologically inert.Keywords: Foreign body granuloma, Huang-Lian-Jie-Du-Tang, Hwangryunhaedok-tang, Pharmacopuncture, Postherpetic neuralgia

INTRODUCTION

Foreign body granuloma can be caused by endogenous compounds as well as various injectable materials1. Although it can vary according to the extent of lesions, the severity, and the duration, the skin granuloma lesions caused by injectable materials usually present as localized palpable nodules and plaques2. Pharmacopuncture, or herbal acupuncture, is a new form of treatment modality performed by injecting fluid extracted from herbal medicine into acupoints, achieving the combinations of both acupuncture and transdermal delivery of herbal medicine3. Hwangryunhaedok-tang (HHT, a.k.a., Huang-Lian-Jie-Du-Tang in China), an oriental medicinal herb known to produce anti-inflammatory effects, is usually used in a decoction, but it has been recently made in pharmacopuncture products and used in musculoskeletal, respiratory, and dermatologic disorder by oriental medicine doctors4,5. Herein, we report a case of foreign body granuloma after Hwangyunhaedok-tang pharmarcopunture for postherpetic neuralgia.

CASE REPORT

An 88-year-old female presented with multiple tender nodules on the left parietal scalp and forehead (Fig. 1A~C). The nodules were round-shaped, firm, and measured up to 1×1 cm. The nodules developed about 3 months ago, and the number of them had been increased. About 2 years before the development of symptoms, she had a history of herpes zoster on the left ophthalmic branch of the trigeminal nerve area. She was prescribed antiviral medication and the skin lesions almost resolved. However, the patient continued to complain postherpetic neuralgia and underwent HHT pharmacopuncture several times in a local oriental medicine clinic. Two nodules from forehead and scalp at presentation were biopsied. A punch biopsy specimen showed a yellowish firm nodule in the dermis (Fig. 1D). The histopathologic findings revealed chronic granulomatous inflammation from mid to lower dermis and subcutaneous layer. The granuloma showed numerous histiocytic aggregations and had some yellowish-to-brownish foreign material (Fig. 2A). Also, it was diffusely positive for CD68 immunohistochemical stain (Fig. 2B). To rule out the possibility of infections, special stains including Gram, periodic acid-Schiff, and acid-fast bacilli (AFB) stains were performed, resulting in negative findings. Polarized light microscopy demonstrated numerous, yellowish, crystalloid, foreign material (Fig. 2C). Based on these findings, a diagnosis of foreign body granuloma was made. A total of 4 tender nodules were excised with a plan to remove the remaining nodules in the future if subsequent pain occurs. The patient reported relief of tenderness immediately after the procedures.

DISCUSSION

Foreign body granuloma is a chronic inflammatory reaction that is histologically composed of numerous neutrophils, histiocytes, and multinucleated giant cells1. The pathogenesis is not completely understood, but it has been suggested that inorganic or high molecular weight organic substances are not destroyed by phagocytosis and subsequently induce chronic inflammation6. Although it is still unknown which factors determine effective phagocytosis, some factors associated with the occurrence of foreign body granuloma are suggested such as injected volume, repeated injections, impurities, particle size, surface smoothness of particle7. Various exogenous materials can cause foreign body granuloma, such as silicone, paraffin, tattoo ink, sutures, and wood fragments. Previously, many cases were reported that unproven injectable materials were used by unqualified practitioners evoking cutaneous adverse reactions6,8,9,10,11. Because foreign body granuloma can occur several decades after injection, its diagnosis is sometimes difficult12.

Pharmacopuncture was first officially introduced to the traditional Korean medicine community in 1967 and many herbal extracts for pharmacopuncture have been developed and used for various diseases13,14. In 2019, it was reported that the types of pharmacopuncture most commonly used were bee venom (30.5%), followed by blood stasis (18.9%), HHT (7.9%), and ginseng (7.5%)13. HHT is composed of four medical herbs: Rhizoma coptidis, Radix scutellariae, Cortex phellodendri, and Fructus gardeniae15. In oriental medicine, the components have been believed to produce anti-inflammatory, anti-allergic, anti-bacterial, and antiviral effects16. The main components of HHT are alkaloids, representative as berberine, coptistine, and palmatine17. They have four alkaloid rings with double bonds, resulting in yellowish crystalloid structures with high rigidity and low solubility18. Structural similarities between the alkaloids and foreign materials in our case observed by polarized light microscopy and granuloma formation in the mid to lower dermis suggested that dermal injection of HHT caused the skin lesions. Also, polarized light microscopic findings showed the foreign materials were sized up to 20 µm and had irregular sharp edges. Considering quiescent encapsulation of the granuloma favors smaller size and smooth surfaces19, we presumed the large size and rough surface of the particles were the important causal factors of delayed foreign body reaction.

In recent, interest in alternative medicine, including oriental medicine, is growing. Also, it is believed that herbal medicines used in oriental medicine are relatively safe because they are derived from animals and plants. HHT extracts for pharmacopuncture are manufactured by the processes of extraction, high temperature sterilization, and percolation of original herbs13. The manufacturing process, however, is not approved officially and little is known about the possible side effects. There is still a lack of awareness in both the standard administration method of HHT and the skill of operators in clinical settings. Furthermore, it is difficult to identify exact substances that cause side effects, because materials in pharmacopuncture are often used as a mixed or combined form rather than as a single agent. Until now, there have been no reported cases of foreign body granuloma caused by HHT pharmacopuncture. Because the use of HHT is relatively common in some Asian countries, such as China and Korea, studies for the side effects are necessary to confirm its safety, especially for pharmacopuncture procedures. We report a case of foreign body granuloma that developed about 20 months after HHT pharmacopuncture treatment for postherpetic neuralgia. It is important to be aware that various herbal extracts, considered biologically inert, can cause injection-induced granulomatous inflammation as a delayed adverse reaction.

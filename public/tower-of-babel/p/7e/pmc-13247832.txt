Long-term nano-dynamic mechanical analysis and micro-permeability of adhesive interface components using four AB-type proanthocyanidin neutral primers
Authors: José Guilherme Neves, Walleska F. Liberato, Mahmoud Sayed, Shu-Xi Jing, Shao-Nong Chen, Guido F. Pauli, Ana K. Bedran-Russo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:This study investigated the long-term biomodulatory roles of trimeric and tetrameric AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers on the viscoelastic properties and stability of dentin-resin components over 1-year aging.Methods:PAC-DESIGNER primers were prepared from Pinus massoniana (PM) and Cinnamomum verum (CV) extracts and designed to enrich trimeric (PM-AB, CV-AB) and tetrameric (PM-ABA, CV-ABB) proanthocyanidins. Adhesive interfaces were prepared on dentin surfaces under an etch-and-rinse bonding protocol. Specimens were sectioned and either immediately processed or stored in simulated body fluid for one year. Unprimed adhesive restoration served as control. Nano-mechanical properties (complex modulus – E* and damping capacity – Tan δ) of adhesive layer, hybrid layer, and underlying dentin were assessed using triboindentation. Interfacial micro-permeability was evaluated under a fluorescent microscope. Data were analyzed using ANOVA and post-hoc tests (α = 0.05).Results:No significant differences in E* were found among adhesive layers of PAC-DESIGNER groups vs. control at immediate and 6-month timepoints (p ≥ 0.05). After 1-year aging, E* was higher for CV-ABB and PM-AB adhesive layers than the control (p < 0.05). AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers increased the viscoelastic properties of the hybrid layer and underlying dentin, with trimeric compounds performing better at all timepoints (p < 0.05). Biomodulation did not affect the nature and viscoelastic behavior of the different layers: at both the hybrid layer and underlying dentin. PAC-DESIGNER primers reduced rhodamine infiltration compared to the control group (p < 0.05).Conclusions:AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers enhanced viscoelastic properties and stability over time for both the hybrid layer and the underlying dentin. Trimeric proanthocyanidins exhibited superior performance compared to tetrameric, which can be attributed to their distinct molecular configuration and overall shape.Clinical Significance:Plant-derived biomodulator primers have the potential to stabilize dentin-resin interfaces by increasing the mechanical properties and sealing of the hybrid layer and underlying dentin.

Introduction

Innovative strategies have been proposed to optimize procedures and increase the service-life of adhesive-based restorations and ultimately prevent the need for restoration repairs and replacements [1–4]. Some of these new strategies target the enhancement of the mechanical properties and/or the inhibition of pathways leading to the structural degradation of components of the dentin-resin interface [3–7]. Biomodulation of dental tissues using phytochemicals is a bioinspired strategy that enhances the performance of dentin-resin interfaces by mimicking the natural mechanisms of functional resilience and self-protection of dentin [1,2,5,8].

One bioactive class of phytochemicals for potential clinical application is the proanthocyanidins (PAC), natural biopolymers that interact with components of the dentin matrix, particularly type I collagen [7,9, 10]. As a result of their tissue-specific chemical and physical interactions, these compounds enhance the mechanical properties and reduce the biodegradability of dentin and the dentin-adhesive interfaces [7,9,11]. Hence, the amphiphilic nature of PACs, characterized by hydrophilic phenolic groups connected with aromatic rings, plausibly enhances and stabilizes dentin-resin adhesion via interactions between the hydrophilic dentin matrix and the hydrophobic resin [7].

Structural and stereochemistry features of PACs have been shown to play key roles in the biomodulatory potency of PACs with dentin [1,7, 10,11]. As defining structure-activity relationships is essential for the development of PACs into interventional biomaterials that would contain PAC chemistry with known bioactivity and controlled response, recent efforts have commenced towards building structure-activity relationships and recognizing the role of interflavan linkages (IFLs) on the dentin biomodulatory features of PACs [1,12–14]. Monomeric PACs form oligomers and polymers via two types of IFLs, A- or B-type linkages. The A-type PACs feature bearing at least one double bond between the monomers, which was proved as a key regulator of long-term stability [13]. On the other hand, the B-type PACs exhibit more structural flexibility compared to their A-type analogues, which can be attributed to the free rotation around the singly carbon bond between the monomeric units which lead to an expanded conformational space [15]. Another significant molecular property of these compounds is their degree of oligomerization (DP), which has been demonstrated to exert a modulatory role on the interactions between PACs and dentin [2,14,16, 17]. Trimeric (DP3) and tetrameric (DP4) compounds exhibit optimal 3D structural dimensions for fitting into the collagen interfibrillar spaces within the collagen matrix, inducing the highest increase in the stiffness of dentin matrix when compared to other oligomers [14,17].

The DESIGNER (Depletion and Enrichment of Select Ingredients Generating Normalized Extract Resources) concept was introduced, aiming to produce standardized mixtures of bioactives in botanical-derived agents [18], and has recently been applied to the highly bioactive PAC-based [12,16]. Moreover, it is a cost-effective strategy as DESIGNER materials advance the development of biopolymers contained in natural waste materials into new commercial products, in a highly reproducible and scalable manner. Earlier investigations revealed that selective PAC-DESIGNERs, similarly to isolated compounds, enhance the bulk viscoelastic properties of the dentin matrix and induce chemical shifts to the secondary structure of collagen [12].

Recently, rinse-out primers containing trimeric and tetrameric AB-type PAC-DESIGNER were found to exhibit stable resin-dentin tensile bond strength after 1-year aging [16]. Inspired by these data, and to better understand the biomodulatory roles of PAC-DESIGNERs leading to high and stable bond strength over-time, four rinse-out AB-type PAC-DESIGNERs primers were formulated to elucidate the role of trimeric and tetrameric enriched DESIGNERs on the viscoelastic properties of individual components of the resin-dentin using nano-dynamic mechanical analysis (nano-DMA) and interfacial micro permeability methods. The null hypotheses in the present study tested were (i) PAC-DESIGNERs with different DPs elicit similar changes to the viscoelastic properties of resin-dentin components; (ii) the aging (up to 1 year) similarly affects the mechanical properties and micro permeability of dentin-resin interfaces of PAC-DESIGNERs and the control group.

Materials and methods

PAC-DESIGNERs preparation

Four AB-type PAC-DESIGNERs trimeric and tetrameric (AB, ABA, and ABB) were produced from powdered stem bark of Cinnamomum verum (CV) (Oregon’s Wild Harvest- Sandy, OR, USA [No. CIN-07011P-OMH01]) and extract powder of the inner bark of Pinus massoniana (Xi’an Chukang Biotechnology-China [no. PB120212]; (Fig. 1). The preparation of the compounds was performed via a multi-step separation process, including centrifugal partition chromatography (CPC), Sephadex LH-20, Reversed Phase (RP) C-18, or column chromatography (CC) on Diaion HP-20 CC, and semi-preparative HPLC, as previously described [15,19,20]. The materials were then used to formulate AB-type PAC neutral primers.

Resin-Dentin Biointerfaces using neutral primers

Dentin specimens were sourced from twenty-five extracted sound human molars, obtained under an approved protocol (IRB no 2023–0717). The occlusal surfaces of the molars were flattened using silicon carbide (SiC) abrasive papers #180 and 320 (Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, USA) to remove occlusal enamel and expose mid-coronal dentin surfaces. The flat dentin surfaces were then polished (600 grit for 20 s) to standardize a smear layer. The specimens were randomly divided into five groups (n = 5): unprimed (control) and primed with CV-AB, PM-AB, CV-ABB, or PM-ABA (Fig. 1).

The restorative protocol included an etch-and-rinse protocol starting with surface etching with 35 % glycolic acid (pH=1.3) for 15 s, rinsing for 15 s, blot dry-surface, and active application of rinse-out neutral primer (20 mmol/L HEPES buffer, pH~7, containing 6.5 % (w/v) of PAC-DESIGNER neutral primers) for 1 min, and rinsing for 30 sec [2,16]. Two consecutive layers of a model methacrylate-based adhesive were applied immediately after blot drying, followed by gentle air drying for 20 sec for solvent evaporation, and light curing for 40 sec (3 M Elipar DeepCure-S Curing Light, with intensity of 1470 mW/cm2). The composition of the model adhesive (weight %) is as follows: Bis-GMA (37.7 %), TEGDMA (20.6 %), HEMA (10.3 %), CQ (0.50 %), EDMAB (0.50 %), and DPIHP (1.0 %), with ethanol (100 %) (29.4 %). A commercial resin composite (Filtek Supreme Ultra, Lot N959543, 3 M ESPE, St. Paul, MN, USA) was used to incrementally build a restoration block in three increments, each increment being light-cured for 40 sec. Specimens were immersed in a physiological storage medium (pH 7.4) and incubated at 37 °C. After a 24-hour incubation period, the specimens were serially sectioned into resin-dentin beams with a cross-sectional area of 0.8 ± 0.05 mm2, as previously described [21]. The analyses were performed after 24 h (immediate), 6 months, and 1 year of storage in a simulated body fluid – SBF (HEPES [5 mM], CaCl22H2O [2.5 mM], NaN3 [0.3 mM], ZnCl2 [0.05 mM]- pH~7.4) at 37 °C [21]. SBF solutions were replaced every 2 weeks.

Nanomechanical properties of the resin-dentin interface components

The resin-dentin biospecimens (n = 5) were embedded in chemically cured epoxy resin (Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, USA) and allowed to cure for 8 h at room temperature. The specimens were then polished with the sequential use of #180, 320, 600, 800, and 1200 grit SiC papers (Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, USA), followed by 9, 6, 3, and 1 μm diamond suspension and 0.05 μm alumina suspension polish (MasterPrep, Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, USA) [22]. Dynamic nano-mechanical analysis (nano-DMA) was performed using TI980 triboindenter (Hysitron Bruker, Minneapolis, MN) with a fluid cell Berkovich tip (Frequency: 10 to 100 Hz; Load Force: 800 uN; Load Function: Frequency sweep mode) (Fig. 2). The viscoelastic properties of the resin-dentin interface components [adhesive (AD), hybrid layer (HL), and underlying dentin (UD) - 10 μm from the bottom of the hybrid layer] were assessed separately under hydrated conditions in Hank’s solution. The complex modulus (E*), and the damping capacity (tan-delta) were calculated from measurements taken at 5 distinct areas at 50 Hz frequency [1].

Micro-permeability of the adhesive interface

The interfacial micro-permeability of the adhesive interface was assessed using an established protocol [21]. Briefly, the resin-dentin biospecimens (n = 10) were randomly selected, embedded in epoxy, and polished with the sequential use of #180, 320, 600, 800, and 1200 grit SiC papers (Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, USA), followed by 9, 6, 3, and 1 μm diamond suspension and 0.05 m alumina suspension polish (Master Prep, Buehler, Lake Bluff, IL, USA). Specimens were immersed in freshly prepared 0.1 mM rhodamine-B solution (Rhodamine-B; Sigma Aldrich) dissolved in 0.01 M PBS (pH=7.2) for 1 h at room temperature protected from light, rinsed for 1 min, and immediately observed under a fluorescence microscope (LEICA DMI6000B, Leica Microsystems, USA). The fluorescence emission intensity (FEI) was analyzed under the microscope with images taken from the biospecimens at × 20 magnification. Using ImageJ 1.48p image analysis software (National Institutes of Health, USA), the FEI values of interfacial micro-permeability were calculated by tracing a parallel line profile at two locations: the hybrid layer and the underlying dentin (10 μm below the adhesive interface).

Statistical analysis

The study variables were dentin surface biomodulation (five levels: unprimed (control) and primed with CV-AB, PM-AB, CV-ABB, or PM-ABA) and time (three levels: immediate, 6 months, and 1 year). Intragroup variability was assessed using Levene’s test, and the data were statistically analyzed using the general linear model (two-way ANOVA), followed by post-hoc tests (SPSS v.25). The level of significance was set at α = 0.05.

Results

Nanomechanical properties of the resin-dentin interface components

Results of the nano-dynamic mechanical properties (complex modulus - E* and damping capacity - tan δ) of the dentin-resin components after dentin biomodulation using AB-type PAC-DESIGNERs primers and control (unprimed), analyzed at different time points (immediate, 6 months, and 1 year), are presented in Tables 1 and 2. Statistically significant interactions were found between treatment and timepoints (p < 0.05) for each property (E* and tan δ) at each interfacial layer (adhesive layer - AD, hybrid layer - HL, and underlying dentin - UD). This significant interaction highlights distinct biomodulation effects on the stability of adhesive interface components.

At the adhesive layer (AD), no difference was found between DESIGNER primer groups and unprimed (control) at immediate and 6-month aging (p > 0.05). Statistical differences among the DESIGNER groups at the AD were observed, including a lower E* for the CV-AB primer at immediate assessment when compared to other PAC-DESIGNERs (p < 0.05). At 6 months, CV-ABB primer exhibited a higher E* value when compared to trimeric primers (p < 0.05). At 1-year aging, a higher E* was observed for the adhesive layers of CV-ABB and PM-AB when compared to both control and PM-ABA (p < 0.05). All groups remained stable at 6-month aging, except PM-AB (p < 0.05). After 1 year of aging (12 months), significant differences were observed for all groups when compared to other experimental timepoints (p < 0.05).

Regarding the hybrid layer (HL), statistically significant higher E* values were observed for all PAC-DESIGNER groups when compared to control (p < 0.05), with sustained results after 6-month aging (p < 0.05). After 1-year aging, primers containing CV-AB and PM-AB exhibited statistically higher E* values, followed by PM-ABA, CV-ABB, and control group (p < 0.05). PM-AB primer group exhibited stability E* overtime, while a decrease was observed in the other groups after 6-month aging and thereafter (p > 0.05).

At underlying dentin (UD), PAC-DESIGNER primers exhibited statistically higher E* values when compared to control, except for CV-ABB primer at immediate and tetrameric PAC-DESIGNER primers (CV-ABB and PM-ABA) after 6 months (p < 0.05). After one year, all PAC-DESIGNER primers showed higher E* when compared to control, with the PM-AB primer exhibiting the highest performance when compared to other groups (p < 0.05). At 6-month aging, the trimeric PACs and the PM-ABA exhibited a decrease in E* when compared to other experimental timepoints (p < 0.05). For CV-ABB the difference was detected between 6-month and 1-year aging (p < 0.05).

The damping capacity of the adhesive layer was significantly different when treated with CV-AB compared to PM-AB, CV-ABB after 24 h, as well as for CV-ABB when compared to other groups after 1 year (p < 0.05). PM-AB primer presented higher damping capacity at immediate and 1-year aging when compared to control (p < 0.05). At 6 months, no differences were found for any of the groups (p > 0.05). All groups exhibited statistical differences between 24 h and 6 months, except CV-ABB that remained stable until 1-year aging (p < 0.05). At the HL, trimeric PAC-DESIGNER groups showed lower damping capacity at the hybrid layer compared to control after 24 h (p < 0.05), with no significant differences for all PAC-DESIGNER primers and control after 6-months (p > 0.05). After 1-year aging, PM-AB exhibited higher damping capacity when compared to tetrameric primers (p > 0.05). At underlying dentin, trimeric PAC-DESIGNERs and PM-ABA primers elicit a decrease in the damping capacity of underlying dentin when compared to control after 24 h (p < 0.05). No differences were detected among groups after 6 months (p > 0.05). After 1-year aging, all PAC-DESIGNER primers exhibited a significantly lower damping capacity when compared to control (p < 0.05). For both HL and UD PM-ABA and control exhibited stability throughout the experimental timepoints, while other groups showed stability at either 6 months or 1 year (p > 0.05).

Interfacial micropermeability of the adhesive interface

The interfacial micropermeability results at the hybrid layer and underlying dentin is shown in Fig. 3. At the hybrid layer, no significant statistical interaction was found between treatment*time factors (p = 0.239). However, significant differences were found among treatments (p < 0.001), where application of DESIGNER primers resulted in significantly lower FEI values when compared to the control group (p < 0.001). At the underlying dentin (10 μm below the adhesive interface), a significant statistical interaction was found between factors (treatment*time) (p < 0.001), and the control group exhibited the FEI values significantly higher at 6-month and 1-year assessments when compared to the immediate (p < 0.05).

Discussion

Dentin biomodulation by plant-derived PACs is a tissue-driven approach aiming at the mechanical reinforcement and biostability of the dentin-resin interface [1,3,10,16]. The use of the DESIGNER phytochemical concept enables the selective enrichment of PAC compounds based on the degree of polymerization and major compounds [12,16], thereby enriching bioactive components. This concept was employed in the present study to prepare standardized, reproducible natural agents. Using nano-length scale mechanical measurements and micro-length scale micro-permeability assay, this study found that dentin biomodulation by AB-type PAC-DESIGNER dentin surface primers stabilizes the dentin-resin components by enhancing the viscoelastic properties of the hybrid layer and the underlying dentin and reducing the interfacial micropermeability of the hybrid layer. Therefore, the two null hypotheses were rejected. Hence, the study further elucidated the mechanism of action of these natural oligomeric compounds at the dentin-resin interface.

The positive effects of similar AB-type PAC primers in increasing the complex modulus of the dentin matrix and the tensile bond strength of dentin-resin interfaces were recently reported [16]. These findings were attributed to the distinct molecular configuration and degree of polymerization of AB-type PACs, leading to collagen cross-linking [12,16]. A-type PAC exhibits distinct structures from B-type PAC, as they contain an additional ether (C–O–C) bond, which contributes to increased conformational rigidity [23]. Meanwhile, the B-type exhibits a single-bonded structure that allows for greater conformational flexibility, which may facilitate fibrillar movement during chemo-mechanical events [13,24]. This aligns well with the fact that AB-type PACs possess molecular attributes of both A- and B-type linkages. Mixed AB-PAC trimers have one linkage of each type, whereas tetrameric PACs may exhibit more characteristics of A- or B-type PAC based on the presence of two A or B-type linkages and their sequence (head-to-tail). Here, this variability was captured by investigating ABA and ABB-PACs DESIGNERs (Fig. 1).

Nano-DMA measurements revealed that DP3 and DP4 DESIGNERs induced a significantly higher complex modulus of the hybrid layer compared to the control, whereas DP3 primers were significantly more potent. These findings corroborate a prior study, in which trimeric AB-type PAC-DESIGNERs induced higher stiffness of the dentin matrix compared to tetrameric ones [16]. This means that the biomodulation of dentin guided the increase E* of HL. The observed difference in potency between the DP3 and DP4 PAC can be attributed to the extent to which the main compounds diffuse and fit into the interfibrillar spaces of the collagen matrix, ultimately enhancing the physical/chemical interactions between the PAC compounds and the dentin matrix [14,17]. Hence, the damping capacity of the biomodified HL remained statistically similar to the control (untreated), indicating that while the complex modulus increased, the E”/E’ ratio remained unchanged. These findings suggest that the beneficial effect of dentin biomodulation by AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers lies in preserving the innate viscoelastic behavior of the hybrid layer, which is crucial for maintaining the mechanical integrity and functionality of this critical interfacial layer.

AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers elicited an enhancement in the mechanical properties of the underlying dentin (UD), with the DP3 compounds exhibiting better performance when compared to DP4. The UD is an under mineralized layer of dentin underneath the HL. The findings indicate that all the DESIGNER primers were able to diffuse into the mineralized dentin and interact with the collagenous organic component, inducing dentin biomodulation in a similar fashion to that found in demineralized dentin matrix [14,16,17]. After 1-year aging, the UD of all PAC-DESIGNER groups exhibited higher viscoelastic properties compared to the control group, with trimeric compounds showing better performance and indicating the permanent effect of tissue biomodulation at the underlying dentin. The reinforcement and stability after 1 year of the UD may explain the long-term stability of dentin-resin micro-tensile bond strength [16], where increasing the mechanical properties of dentin results in higher micro-mechanical adhesion and lower biodegradability of the dentin matrix. A statistically significant decrease in the damping capacity of UD was observed for the DESIGNER primers which exhibited higher E* values (CV-AB, PM-AB and PM-ABA). Despite this decrease in damping capacity following tissue biomodulation, the values remained within the range reported for native dentin (0.02–0.3) [14,25], indicating that the viscoelastic nature and integrity of the tissue were preserved. The remarkable increase in mechanical reinforcement of the underlying dentin, unique to the biomodification strategy, may be one of the factors contributing to the stability of the dentin-resin interface under static and dynamic mechanical conditions at both macro- and nano-levels, potentially leading to increased interfacial endurance and longevity of adhesive restorations.

In the present study, the stability and higher E* values promoted by PAC-DESIGNER primers on the hybrid layer and underlying dentin may be attributed to the chemical and ultra-structural stabilization of the collagen. Through the formation of collagen cross-links, PAC-DESIGNERs stabilize the triple-helical structure of collagen fibril and enhance their resistance to enzymatic cleavage [6,26]. Collagen stabilization may reduce collagen affinity for water, promoting changes in hydrodynamics that compromise adhesive infiltration and durability [27,28]. Thus, changes to hydrodynamics at the interface could explain some indirect effect of the dentin biomodulation at the adhesive layer (AD). This layer exhibited a lower E* value for DP3 DESIGNER groups and higher values for CV-ABB, which are inversely proportional when compared to E* values on HL and UD. Notably, within the same degree of polymerization, differences in layer-specific stability were observed according to the IFL configuration, where CV-ABB, with two B-type linkages, promoted a higher E* in the adhesive layer, while the PM-ABA, with a predominance of A-type ILFs, promoted better stability in the HL, both after one year. As an additional observation, although there are no differences in IFL for trimeric compounds, PM-AB mediated better stability of the HL when compared to CV-AB and exhibited higher E* values in dentin from 6 months onward. This difference in HL stability between the two trimeric compounds may be related to the terminal monomeric unit present in each material, as PM-AB contains a catechin unit, whereas CV-AB contains an epicatechin unit [16,29]. A previous study demonstrated that catechin and epicatechin terminal units differently modulate the interactions between trimeric PACs and dentin collagen, leading to distinct shifts in the nano- and micro- length scale viscoelastic properties of the dentin matrix [29].

All AB-type PAC-DESIGNERs groups exhibited less rhodamine infiltration at the hybrid layer and underlying dentin when compared to the control group. This reduced micropermeability can be attributed to the enhancement of viscoelastic properties and the collagen cross-linking effect of PACs, which may displace unbound water during dentin biomodulation [11]. Both increased collagen cross-linking and reduction of unbound water at the interface may further contribute to stabilization of the resin and dentin components at the hybrid layer and underlying dentin [6,11]. Additionally, the dentin-resin interfaces were produced using glycolic acid, a mild α-hydroxyl organic acid that presents a similar etching pattern to phosphoric acid but with a shallower demineralization depth [30,31]. Despite the lower demineralization depth, increased micropermeability was observed in the control group after one year of storage. These outcomes collectively reinforce the beneficial effect of the AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers in promoting the stability of dentin-resin interfaces by enhancing the mechanical properties of their components, thereby supporting long-term interfacial durability [6,32].

Overall, this study demonstrates that AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers enhance the stability of the dentin-resin interface, as evidenced by improved mechanical properties of interfacial components and reduced micropermeability. While limited by its in vitro design, this study enabled the application of a refined high-resolution technique to elucidate the biomodulatory effects of these primers. The findings also provide insights into the structure-activity relationships and highlight the potential of the natural primers to contribute to more durable adhesive strategies.

Conclusion

Our findings highlight the distinct effect of tissue biomodulation by AB-type PAC-DESIGNER primers in enhancing the viscoelastic properties and promoting the stability of the hybrid layer and underlying dentin after one year. Overall, Trimeric AB-type DESIGNERs demonstrated superior performance compared to tetrameric ones; the most prominent DESIGNERs were produced from Pinus massoniana PM-AB on the HL stabilization. Notably, the underlying dentin consistently exhibited enhanced mechanical properties across all DESIGNERs, suggesting that the tissue biomodulation mechanism contributes to interfacial stability. The unique chemical architecture and multifunctional properties of PAC-DESIGNERs offer substantial benefits in reinforcing the stability of adhesive restorations, carrying important implications for their advancement into clinical applications.

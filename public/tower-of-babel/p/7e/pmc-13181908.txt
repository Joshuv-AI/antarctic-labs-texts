The influence of combined carbon and nitrogen application on the growth and metabolism of Ilex rotunda
Authors: Daoliang Yan, Shuxian Zhao, Yuxi Chen, Chenyi You, Jiaxin Hu, Bingsong Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhen cultivating Ilex rotunda Thunb., the blind application of nitrogen fertilizer often resulted in poor growth and negatively impacted yield and quality. To explore the balancing effect of carbon fertilizer on excessive nitrogen fertilizer and improve the yield and quality of I. rotunda, two-year-old cuttings were sprayed with different concentrations of urea and α-ketoglutarate (α-KG) in 12 treatment groups. After 12 weeks of treatment, the growth, photosynthetic indexes, main physiological indexes and metabolome of I. rotunda were determined and analyzed. The results showed that applying appropriate concentrations of α-KG and urea to I. rotunda enhanced its photosynthetic performance, evidenced by increased net photosynthetic rate and stomatal conductance, reduced intercellular carbon dioxide concentration, and improved specific leaf area of I. rotunda. The activity of photosystem Ⅱ (PS Ⅱ) was enhanced. The activities of enzymes related to GS-GOGAT cycle were increased, and the co-accumulation of carbon and nitrogen metabolites was promoted; it effectively mitigated the adverse effects of excessive nitrogen on the plant. The application of 4 g·L−1 urea + 40 mg·L−1 α-KG (N2KG1) and 4 g·L−1 urea + 80 mg·L−1 α-KG (N2KG2) increased the levels of α-KG and citric acid. N2KG1 and N2KG2 had the most positive effects on the photosynthetic performance, physiological and biochemical indexes, and accumulation of carbon and nitrogen metabolites in I. rotunda leaves. Therefore, the exogenous application of appropriate concentrations of α-KG and urea can promote the growth of I. rotunda, enhance its adaptability to environmental conditions, and improve plant quality. Introduction

Ilex rotunda Thunb., belonging to family Aquifoliaceae, is an evergreen tree. It is widely distributed in southern provinces of the Yangtze River Basin in China. It is resistant to barren soil, drought, and frost. It often grows in evergreen broadleaf forests, along forest edges, and near streams at altitudes between 400 and 1,100 meters. The leaves and stems of I. rotunda are rich in triterpenoids, flavonoids, saponins and other medicinal active ingredients [1]. Modern medicine believes that I. rotunda has various pharmacological effects and is effective in treating cardiovascular diseases, lowering lipids, protecting the liver, and combating tumors, inflammation, and bacteria [2–4].

Currently, nitrogen fertilizer is widely used in agriculture. However, the blind pursuit of higher crop yields has resulted in the excessive and unscientific application of nitrogen fertilizer. This imbalance in carbon and nitrogen metabolism seriously affects crop yield and quality. Meanwhile, the carbon element, which is also necessary for plant growth and development, has not received enough attention. Carbon deficiency in plants is the key factor that limits the full utilization of nitrogen and then affects plant yield and quality [5]. Plants mainly rely on carbon dioxide (CO2) in the atmosphere to provide carbon sources in their natural growth. Currently, the main way to supplement carbon in vegetables grown artificially is to apply CO2, a gaseous carbon fertilizer, in relatively closed environments, but it is not yet possible to replicate the use of CO2 in the production of field crops, and increased application of CO2 can seriously affect the quality of the crops [6].

Studies have shown that applying organic carbon fertilizer to plants can promote the accumulation of nitrogen in their bodies, and its effect is better than that of applying nitrogen fertilizer to plants. The reason is that organic carbon plays an important role in the nitrogen balance of plants [7, 8]. Excessive application of nitrogen to plants will lead to insufficient carbon skeleton in the body to synthesize various organic substances, which will affect the absorption and transformation of nitrogen. The supplement of organic carbon can promote the synthesis of amino acids, peptides, proteins and other organic matter, reduce the accumulation of inorganic nitrogen in plants, and promote the absorption and assimilation of nitrogen by crops.

In the process of daily planting and production, the commonly used carbon fertilizers can be divided into organic carbon fertilizers and inorganic carbon fertilizers. Among them, organic carbon fertilizer refers to organic carbon compounds that can be absorbed and utilized by plants, such as sugars, alcohols, acids, etc. In the process of supplementing carbon to plants, compared with inorganic carbon fertilizer, carbon fertilizer has higher water solubility, higher absorption efficiency, and more significant effect on promoting plant growth [9].

α-KG can be used as a new type of organic carbon fertilizer. It has been reported in the literature that spraying α-KG on wheat seedlings with low nitrogen treatment can increase the nitrogen assimilation of wheat seedlings [10]. Gong et al. reported that spraying appropriate concentrations of α-KG on soybean leaves under low nitrogen increased the total nitrogen fixation capacity of soybean nodules, promoted the accumulation of dry matter in soybeans, and improved the quality of soybeans [11]. And our previous research showed that applying a certain amount of organic carbon fertilizer α-KG to Dendrobium officinale can effectively alleviate the carbon supply shortage of plants, maintain the normal carbon and nitrogen metabolism balance of plants, and thus achieve the effect of improving crop quality and yield, which cannot be achieved by applying gas fertilizer CO2 [12].

Therefore, to develop scientific and reasonable fertilization strategies, maintain the balance of carbon and nitrogen metabolism in I. rotunda, and improve its yield and quality, we plan to study the effects of different combinations of carbon and nitrogen treatments on its growth, main physiological indexes, and carbon and nitrogen metabolism. This will clarify the effects of α-KG and nitrogen on its main physiological indexes and growth, the balance effect of organic carbon fertilizer on nitrogen fertilizer and provide a new nutrient management scheme to improve its yield and quality.

Materials and methods

Test materials and treatment

The plant material used in this study are 2-year-old I. rotunda cutting seedling (approximately 25–30 cm tall), which was cultivated by the experimenters and sourced from the Ilex nursery of Zhejiang A&F University. It was identified by plant classification experts Yan Daoliang and Lou Luhuan. Test seedlings with consistent growth were selected and planted in plastic pots with a diameter of 30 cm and a height of 40 cm. The potting medium consists of a 3: 1 mixture of peat soil and river sand by volume. After a 14-day acclimatization period in the plastic pots, the test seedlings were randomly divided into 12 groups, with 30 seedlings in each group. From April to July 2024, the I. rotunda plants underwent carbon and nitrogen fertilization treatments in a greenhouse maintained at a temperature of (25 ± 3)℃. The treatment groups and methods are detailed in Table 1. The 12 treatment groups were divided into four groups: no nitrogen fertilizer treatment group (CK, KG1, KG2), low nitrogen concentration treatment group (CK, N1, N1KG1, N1KG2), medium nitrogen concentration treatment group (CK, N2, N2KG1, N2KG2) and high nitrogen concentration treatment group (CK, N3, N3KG1, N3KG2). During the treatment, carbon and nitrogen treatments were alternated, carbon and nitrogen treatments were performed once a week, for a total of 12 weeks, carbon and nitrogen combined application treatments for a total of 12 times. Spraying was applied to both the upper and lower surfaces of the leaves until small droplets naturally dripped off the leaf surface. Leaf samples were taken 12 weeks after the combined fertilization treatment. The collected leaves were quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and some were stored in a – 80℃ refrigerator for the measurement of carbon and nitrogen metabolism-related enzyme activities. Other leaves were washed with distilled water and dried in an 80℃ oven until they reached a constant weight. The dried samples were crushed using a high throughput tissue grinder (DHS LifeScience & Technology Co.,Ltd, China) for the measurement of various physiological indicators. Non targeted metabolomics analysis of I. rotunda was performed on five independent biological replicates, and all other experiments were performed on three independent biological replicates, each consisting of three seedlings, forming a pooled sample. The remaining plants were used as spare plants.

Determination of specific leaf area of blade

After sampling, the blade surface shall be wiped clean, and the fresh weight of the blade shall be measured immediately with a thousandth electronic balance (JA5003B, TECHCOMP, China). After that, the leaf surface area (cm2) was measured with a leaf area meter (YMJ-CH, TOP Cloud-agri, China). Then Put the blade in an oven, dry it to constant weight, and weigh its dry weight (g) with a electronic balance. Specific leaf area = blade surface area/blade dry weight.

Determination of photosynthetic parameters

The net photosynthetic rate, intercellular carbon dioxide concentration, stomatal conductance and transpiration rate of I. rotunda leaves were measured by Li-6800 portable photosynthesis instrument (LI-COR Biosciences, USA). The measurement time is 8:00–11:00 a.m. The parameters in the leaf chamber are set as follows: the CO2 concentration is 400 µmol·mol−1, the air flow rate is 500 µmol·s−1, the pressure value is 0.2 kPa, the relative humidity is 50%, the temperature is 28℃, the ratio of red to blue light is 9:1, and the photosynthetic effective radiation intensity is 1,200 µmol·m−2·s−1.

Determination of soluble sugar, starch and soluble protein content

The content of soluble sugar and starch was determined by anthrone method [13]. Firstly, the standard curve of soluble sugar was drawn (the required reagents and their amounts are shown in Table S1). Then, 0.2 g dry leaf sample was weighed and placed in a test tube, 15 mL of Reverse osmosis water (RO water) was added and continuously heated in a boiling water bath for 30 minutes to extract the soluble sugar in the sample. The cooled sample extraction solution was filtered, and the filtered extraction solution was placed in a 100 mL volumetric flask. After that, the residue was washed several times with RO water and finally adjusted to a constant volume at the scale line. Finally, 1 mL sample extraction solution was added with 5 mL anthrone reagent, and the absorbance was measured at the wavelength of 620 nm, and the soluble sugar content was calculated according to the standard curve.

The starch in the leaves was hydrolyzed by acid and converted into soluble sugar to prepare the extraction solution: After soluble sugar was extracted, 0.1 g of dry sample residue of leaves was taken into a centrifuge tube, 1 mL of 52% perchloric acid was added, and the shaker was shaken for 30 minutes. After centrifugation at 8000 Revolutions Per Minute (rpm) for 15 minutes, the supernatant was transferred to a 100 mL volumetric flask. After that, the soluble sugar content was determined by the above anthrone method, and the starch content was calculated according to the soluble sugar content.

The content of soluble protein was determined by Coomassie brilliant blue method [14]. First, the standard curve of soluble protein was drawn (the required reagents and their amounts are shown in Table S2). RO water (0.9 mL) and sample extraction solution (0.1 mL) were added to the test tube, followed by 5 mL of Coomassie brilliant blue G-250 reagent, fully mixed, and let stand for 2 minutes. The absorbance was measured at 595 nm and recorded. The protein content was obtained by the standard curve of soluble protein.

Determination of nitrogen content

After drying and grinding the leaves into powder, weigh 0.1 g of the sample and determine the nitrogen content of the leaves by Kjeldahl method [15].

Determination of GS, ferredoxin-dependent glutamate synthase (Fd-GOGAT EC1.4.7.1) and NADH-glutamate synthase (NADH-GOGAT EC 1.4.1.14) activities

GS, Fd-GOGAT and NADH-GOGAT activity were determined with the corresponding kit (Suzhou Michy Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd, China).

Determination of antioxidant enzyme activity

During the determination, 0.2 g of I. rotunda leaves were placed in a pre-cooled mortar, and 2 mL of pre-cooled 50 mmol·L−1 phosphate buffer with pH 7.8 was added to grind into a slurry, and buffer was added to rinse the mortar. The final volume was 8 mL, centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 15 min at 4℃, and the supernatant was the enzyme extracting solution. The enzyme extracting solution was stored at 0℃ – 4℃ for determination of superoxide dismutase (SOD EC1.15.1.1) activity and peroxidase (POD EC1.11.1.7) activity.

The activity of POD was determined by guaiacol method [16]. Firstly, the POD reaction solution was prepared: 100 mL phosphate buffer solution (0.05 mol·L−1, pH 6.0), 100 mL RO water, 100 μL guaiacol and 100 μL 30% H2O2 were placed in beakers in turn and then mixed and transferred to the reagent bottle. After that, 0.1 mL of enzyme solution was drawn into a colorimetric dish, 3 mL of POD reaction solution was added, and the reaction solution without enzyme extracting solution was used as a blank control. The absorbance value at the wavelength of 470 nm was read immediately, read once every 15 s, and the absorbance value in the first 3 minutes of the reaction was measured. Metabonomic determination and analysis

After 12 weeks of treatment, the leaf samples of I. rotunda in CK, N2, N2KG1 and N2KG2 groups were collected and stored in the −80℃ ultra-low temperature refrigerator after quick freezing in liquid nitrogen. Take 100 mg of crushed leaf tissue sample, put it into EP tube, and add 500 μL of 80% methanol aqueous solution; vortex oscillation, ice bath standing for 5 min, set at 15,000 g, centrifugation at 4℃ for 20 min; take a certain amount of supernatant and dilute it with mass spectrometry grade water until the methanol content is 53%; After centrifugation at 15,000 g and 4℃ for 20 min, the supernatant was collected and analyzed with LC-MS.

CD 3.1 (Compound Discoverer 3.1) Soku software is used to process the offline data file. First, each metabolite is simply screened, including retention time, mass charge ratio and other parameters. Then, different samples were aligned by setting the retention time deviation of 0.2 minutes and the mass deviation of 5 ppm to improve the accuracy of identification. Then, the information such as mass deviation of 5 ppm, signal strength deviation of 30%, signal-to-noise ratio of 3, minimum signal strength and additive ions were used for peak extraction, and the peak area was quantified. Then, the target ions were integrated, and the molecular formula was predicted by molecular ion peaks and fragment ions, and compared with mzcloud, mzvault and masslist databases. The blank sample was used to remove the background ions, and the original quantitative results were standardized. Finally, the identification and relative quantitative results of metabolites were obtained. The data processing part is based on Linux operating system (CentOS version 6.6) and R, python software. The identified metabolites were annotated using KEGG database.

Data processing

One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Duncan’s multiple range test were used to analyze the differences in the mean physiological data between treatment groups within each of the four groups. Excel, Origin 2022 and SPSS 2022 were used for data analysis and processing. Statistical significance is defined as P ≤ 0.05. The results were reported as mean ± standard error (SE) based on at least three biological replicates.

Results

Effects of combined application of carbon and nitrogen on the photosynthetic performance of I. rotunda

Effects of combined application of carbon and nitrogen on specific leaf area of I. rotunda

Under different nitrogen concentrations, 2 g·L−1 and 4 g·L−1 urea treatments could promote the increase of specific leaf area of I. rotunda, and the effect of 4 g·L−1 urea treatment was better. Compared with CK treatment, N2KG1 and N2KG2 treatment groups significantly increased the specific leaf area of I. rotunda by 10.94% and 11.80%, respectively. The treatment of high nitrogen concentration (8 g·L−1 urea) could not improve the specific leaf area of I. rotunda. Under different carbon concentrations, KG1 and KG2 treatments could promote the increase of specific leaf area of I. rotunda, and the effect of KG2 treatment was better, and its specific leaf area was significantly higher than that of CK (P<0.05), increased by 8.02%. In addition, the effect of combined applications of carbon and nitrogen was better than that of single application of carbon and nitrogen. N2KG2 was the best treatment among the 12 treatments (Fig. 1).

Effects of combined application of carbon and nitrogen on photosynthetic parameters of I. rotunda

The photosynthetic parameters of I. rotunda were different with different treatments of carbon and nitrogen. Among them, the combination of carbon and nitrogen (especially N2KG1 treatment) has a more significant role in promoting the photosynthetic performance of I. rotunda, improving the net photosynthetic rate (Pn) and stomatal conductance (gs) of I. rotunda, and reducing the intercellular carbon dioxide concentration (Ci), which is more effective than applying organic carbon fertilizer or organic nitrogen fertilizer alone. The four photosynthetic parameters of N2KG1 treatment showed the best results, and its net photosynthetic rate was 41.48% higher than that of the control. In addition, high nitrogen treatment had a significant inhibitory effect on Photosynthesis of I. rotunda, which began to appear from the fourth week after treatment (Figure S1). Under the treatment of N3KG1 and N3KG2, the net photosynthetic rate and stomatal conductance of I. rotunda were significantly higher than those of N3 treatment, and the intercellular carbon dioxide concentration was significantly lower than that of N3 treatment (Fig. 2), indicating that exogenous carbon application alleviated the inhibition of nitrogen excess on Photosynthesis of I. rotunda to a certain extent.

Effects of combined application of carbon and nitrogen on important physiological indexes of I. rotunda leaves

Differences in soluble sugar, starch and soluble protein contents of I. rotunda leaves under different carbon and nitrogen fertilization conditions

It can be seen from Fig. 4A that the combined application of carbon and nitrogen has different degrees of influence on the content of soluble sugar in the leaves of I. rotunda. Under different nitrogen concentrations, N1 and N2 treatments could promote the accumulation of soluble sugar, and N2 treatment had better effect, while too high nitrogen concentration inhibited the accumulation of soluble sugar in I. rotunda. Under the treatment of different carbon concentrations, 40 and 80 mg·L−1 α-KG could promote the accumulation of soluble sugar in I. rotunda, and the treatment of 80 mg·L−1 α-KG had the best effect. In the combined application of carbon and nitrogen treatment, the soluble sugar content in the leaves of N2KG2 treatment was the highest, which was 62.98% higher than that of CK treatment. At the same time, compared with N2 treatment and KG2 treatment, the effect of N2KG2 treatment was also significantly better than the two treatments.

The starch content in the leaves of I. rotunda was correlated with different carbon and nitrogen treatments. Under the same nitrogen concentration treatment, 80 mg·L−1 α-KG treatment had the most significant effect on the accumulation of I. rotunda starch. High nitrogen treatment had a certain inhibitory effect on the accumulation of starch content, and 80 mg·L−1 α-KG had the most significant effect on the inhibition. N2KG2 was the best treatment, and its starch content was significantly higher than that of CK treatment (Fig. 4B).

The soluble protein content of each treatment group was significantly higher than that of CK treatment. In the single carbon treatment, the soluble protein content showed a certain upward trend with the increase of α-KG concentration. In the low nitrogen treatment group, the soluble protein contents of N1KG1 and N1KG2 treatments were significantly higher than that of CK and N1 treatments, and N1KG2 treatment performed best, with the soluble protein content of 11.02 mg·g−1, 82.39% higher than that of CK treatment. In the medium nitrogen treatment group, the treatment effect of N2KG2 treatment was the best, and the soluble protein content reached 16.22 mg·g−1, which was 168.45% higher than that of CK treatment. In the high nitrogen treatment group, the change trend of soluble protein content in I. rotunda was similar to that in the low nitrogen treatment. There was no significant difference in soluble protein content between N3KG1 and N3KG2 treatments, and they were significantly higher than those in CK and N3 treatments. The soluble protein content of N3KG2 treatment was the highest, reaching 16.10 mg·g−1 (Fig. 4C).

Comprehensive analysis showed that N2KG1 and N2KG2 treatments significantly increased the contents of soluble sugar, soluble protein and starch in leaves of I. rotunda compared with CK treatment, and promoted the co-accumulation of carbon and nitrogen metabolites in plants.

Difference of total nitrogen content in leaves of I. rotunda under different combined application of carbon and nitrogen

It can be seen from Fig. 5 that in the treatments of applying nitrogen alone, the nitrogen content in the leaves of I. rotunda was significantly higher than that in the CK treatment. N1KG2, N2KG2 and N3KG2 in the combined application of carbon and nitrogen showed the best performance under all nitrogen concentrations, and their leaf nitrogen content showed an upward trend compared with N1, N2 and N3 treatments. Compared with N1 treatment, the nitrogen content of N1KG2 treatment increased by 34.11%, N2KG2 increased by 40.74% and N3KG2 increased by 48.95%. In the treatment group without nitrogen fertilizer, the leaf nitrogen content of KG1 and KG2 treatments were significantly higher than that of CK treatment, which increased by 29.23% and 31.42% respectively. The above results showed that the application of carbon fertilizer and nitrogen fertilizer could effectively improve the nitrogen content in the leaves of I. rotunda, and the effect of combined application of carbon and nitrogen was better than that of single application of carbon fertilizer and nitrogen fertilizer. In addition, with the increase of carbon fertilizer and nitrogen fertilizer concentration, the nitrogen content in the leaves of I. rotunda was also increased.

Difference of GS activity in leaves of I. rotunda under different combined application of carbon and nitrogen

It can be found from Fig. 6 that the GS activity in the leaves of I. rotunda was affected by the combined application of different carbon and nitrogen. In the treatment group without nitrogen application, the activities of GS in leaves treated with KG1 and KG2 were significantly higher than those treated with CK treatment. In the low nitrogen treatment group, the GS activity of 2 g·L−1 urea combined with 40 mg·L−1 and 80 mg·L−1 α-KG treatment increased significantly compared with CK treatment, and the GS activity of N1KG2 treatment was the highest, reaching 14.07 μmol·min−1·g−1, which was 183.67% higher than CK treatment. In the medium nitrogen treatment group, the effect of 4 g·L−1 urea combined with 80 mg·L−1 α-KG treatment was the best, and the GS activity was 14.86 μmol·min−1·g−1, which was not significantly different from that of 40 mg·L−1 α-KG treatment. In the high nitrogen treatment group, the change trend of GS activity was consistent with that in the medium nitrogen treatment group. The treatment with 8 g·L−1 urea plus 80 mg·L−1 α-KG had the best result, and the GS activity was 16.34 μmol·min−1·g−1, which was 229.44% higher than that in the CK treatment.

Discussion

Effects of combined application of different carbon and nitrogen on photosynthetic performance of I. rotunda

The change of plant specific leaf area is mainly affected by nutrients in the environment [18]. At the same time, the value of specific leaf area also reflects the light capture ability and competitive activity of plants [19, 20]. The results showed that the specific leaf area of I. rotunda treated with carbon and nitrogen fertilizer was significantly increased compared with the control, and N2KG2 treatment was the best. Bai et al. and Zhou et al. found that fertilization on Leymus chinensis significantly increased its specific leaf area, which is consistent with the results of this study [21, 22]. The research of Sun et al. showed that applying an appropriate concentration of organic carbon fertilizer to Paeonia suffruticosa can significantly improve its net photosynthetic rate, thus improving the photosynthetic efficiency and the quality of the used parts of the plant [23]. Yang et al. found that the net photosynthetic rate and other photosynthetic parameters of BlackBerry could be improved to some extent by applying nitrogen fertilizer of appropriate concentration, which indicated that the photosynthetic performance of Blackberry was indeed improved by applying nitrogen fertilizer of appropriate concentration [24]. The content of photosynthetic pigment and photosynthetic parameters of BlackBerry decreased significantly when the concentration of nitrogen fertilizer was too high. The above results are basically consistent with the results of this study. Chlorophyll fluorescence parameters not only reflect the activities of photosystem II and photosystem I, but also comprehensively reflect the photosynthetic state and performance of plants [18]. The results showed that N2KG1 treatment increased the maximum photochemical efficiency and the quantum yield for electron transfer in the PSⅡ reaction center after dark adaptation of I. rotunda, and thus increased the PSⅡ activity of I. rotunda. The results of Li et al. showed that the maximum photochemical efficiency and other fluorescence parameters of Gleditsia sinensis seedlings were improved when an appropriate concentration of nitrogen fertilizer was applied, indicating that the activity and light energy conversion efficiency of photosystem II were improved, but the application of excessive concentration of nitrogen reduced the activity of photosystem II of Gleditsia sinensis seedlings [25]. Zhang et al. found that the application of appropriate concentration of gaseous carbon fertilizer CO2 to melon improved the electron transfer capacity of photosystem II, reduced NPQ and heat dissipation, thereby enhancing the photosynthetic performance of the plant [26]. The above results are consistent with this study.

Effects of different combined application of carbon and nitrogen on physiological indexes and carbon and nitrogen metabolism of I. rotunda

As an important metabolic intermediate in the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA cycle), α-KG is a key node connecting carbon and nitrogen metabolism in cells. α-KG is a precursor of glutamine, glutamic acid and other important amino acids. It is an organic acid necessary for plants to assimilate NO− 3and NH+ 4. After NO− 3 is absorbed into plants, it is converted into NH+ 4 through nitrate reductase (NR) and nitrite reductase (NiR), and NH+ 4 is further assimilated through the GS-GOGAT cycle [27]. Glutamine synthase (GS EC6.3.1.2) and glutamate synthase (GOAGT EC2.6.1.53) play a synergistic role in the GS-GOGAT cycle and are key enzymes in the nitrogen metabolism pathway of plants. GS catalyzes the synthesis of glutamine from glutamic acid and NH+ 4, which is the first step of nitrogen assimilation. Then GOGAT catalyzes the transfer of the amide group of glutamine to α-ketoglutaric acid to produce glutamate. Glutamate, as a key substrate for amino acid and protein synthesis, is a key compound for nitrogen assimilation [28]. The increase in the activity of key enzymes GS and GOAGT during nitrogen assimilation means that plants increase the efficiency of nitrogen assimilation. Leng et al. overexpressed GS in hybrid poplar under high NH+ 4 stress, and the genes related to amino acid biosynthesis, sucrose and starch degradation, galactose metabolism and antioxidant system in roots were significantly up-regulated, which indicated that GS was helpful to maintain the balance between carbon and nitrogen metabolism and alleviate the symptoms of ammonia poisoning [29].

In cell metabolism, the production and decomposition of α-KG involve various metabolic pathways. In the TCA cycle, isocitrate is oxidized and decarboxylated under the action of isocitrate dehydrogenase to produce α-KG, which can also be decarboxylated into α-KG, succinyl-CoA and carbon dioxide (CO2) by α-KG dehydrogenase (a key enzyme in the TCA cycle). Glutamate can generate α-KG by glutamate dehydrogenase deamination or transamination [30]. In the metabolic process of the body, α-KG can directly participate in the conversion of TCA cycle into CO2 without the preparation of glycolytic pathway and TCA cycle, providing energy for cells and improving the metabolic efficiency of the body. At the same time, it can also reduce the loss of glutamine and glutamic acid and indirectly increase the deposition of protein [31].

Soluble sugars play an important role in plant physiological processes, not only providing energy for plant metabolism, but also providing carbon skeleton of plant structural substances [32]. As the main energy storage material of plants, starch also plays an important role in the growth and development of plants [33]. In this study, the application of appropriate concentration of carbon fertilizer, nitrogen fertilizer and the combined application of carbon and nitrogen can significantly improve the soluble sugar content and starch content in the leaves of I. rotunda. At the 12th week of treatment, the content of soluble sugar and starch in leaves of I. rotunda in N2KG2 treatment was the highest, which may be due to the improvement of photosynthesis and the promotion of soluble sugar and starch synthesis of I. rotunda in the combination of carbon and nitrogen. Secondly, the application of urea and α-KG may improve the carbon metabolism of plants, thus directly increasing the content of soluble sugar and starch. Otálora et al. found in their research on Sonchus oleraceus that the soluble sugar content in S. oleraceus increased first and then decreased with the increase of nitrogen concentration sprayed on leaves [33]. Chung et al. showed that the application of high concentration urea to Lycium barbarum could reduce the soluble sugar content in L. barbarum leaves, but the application of carbon and nitrogen organic fertilizer could significantly improve the soluble sugar content in L. barbarum leaves [34]. The above results are consistent with the results of this study.

Soluble protein plays an important role in plant growth and development, nutrition supply, stress resistance and other aspects, and is one of the key factors to maintain the healthy growth of plants [35, 36]. In this study, the combined application of carbon and nitrogen fertilizer promoted the accumulation of soluble protein in I. rotunda, and the change trend of leaf nitrogen content was basically the same as that of soluble protein content in I. rotunda. This may be caused by the increase of nitrogen concentration in the growth environment of I. rotunda, but we found that the effect of nitrogen application alone on the accumulation of soluble protein and leaf nitrogen content of I. rotunda was worse than that of the combined application of carbon and nitrogen. Therefore, we speculate that the application of α-KG can improve the nitrogen use efficiency of I. rotunda, resulting in the increase of nitrogen and soluble protein content in plants. Cruz et al. showed that the increase of CO2 concentration in the atmosphere improved the nitrogen fixation ability of papaya seedlings, which proved our previous scientific hypothesis [37]. Moreover, the enhancement of GS-GOGAT cycle further promotes the synthesis of amino acids such as glutamate in I. rotunda plants, which is consistent with the research results of Liu et al. [38].

In this study, the activities of metabolic enzymes related to carbon and nitrogen metabolism were affected by the application of α-KG, and the activities of GS and GOGAT enzymes were significantly increased. Zhang et al. have shown that the application of appropriate concentrations of organic acids to Capsicum annuum effectively improves the activities of GS enzyme and GOGAT enzyme in C. annuum [39]. Pang et al. also found that the activities of GS and GOGAT enzymes in Brassica campestris increased after applying organic carbon fertilizer of appropriate concentration [40]. The above results are consistent with the experimental results. As a key pathway of nitrogen assimilation, GS-GOGAT cycle mainly converts NH+ 4 into amino acids such as glutamate. The increase of GS and GOGAT enzyme activities indicated the enhancement of plant nitrogen use ability and amino acid synthesis. On the other hand, the urea sprayed on the leaves of I. rotunda in this study also increased the activities of GS and GOGAT enzymes, which may be because urea was directly absorbed by the leaves and decomposed into NH+ 4 by urease in the cytoplasm, which increased the content of NH+ 4 in the plant, thus improving the activities of GS and GOGAT enzymes [41, 42], which is consistent with the results of Chen et al. [43]. Therefore, the GS and GOGAT enzyme activities of I. rotunda leaves under the combined application of carbon and nitrogen were higher than those under the single nitrogen or single carbon treatment, which may be due to the synergistic effect of carbon fertilizer and nitrogen fertilizer.

High nitrogen stress will affect plant photosynthesis and inhibit plant growth [44, 45]. Xun et al. found that NH+ 4 as the only nitrogen source of tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) can inhibit plant growth, especially when the concentration of NH+ 4 is too high [46]. Vega-Mas et al. found that increasing carbon supply can effectively alleviate the ammonium stress on tomatoes and promote the synthesis and accumulation of amino acids [47]. In this study, excessive urea application on I. rotunda also affected the accumulation of plant carbon metabolites. This may be due to the high content of NH+ 4 in the plant after excessive urea is applied. In order to assimilate this part of ammonium, excessive carbon skeleton is consumed, resulting in carbon deficiency, which affects the normal carbon and nitrogen metabolism of the plant [48]. Otálora et al. found in the study of chicory that the application of high concentration of urea resulted in the increase of soluble protein content and the decrease of soluble total sugar content in chicory [33]. Hodges believes that the deficiency of α-KG in plants is the direct reason for the decline of ammonium assimilation efficiency [49]. In this study, after α-KG was applied to I. rotunda, the accumulation of carbon and nitrogen metabolites in the plant was significantly increased compared with the control, and the activities of enzymes related to carbon and nitrogen metabolism were also increased compared with the control, which effectively alleviated the effect of nitrogen stress and effectively confirmed Hodges’ conjecture. In addition, Wu et al. and Sun et al. also found that applying α-KG and other organic carbon fertilizers to plants can alleviate low temperature, weak light and salt stress [50, 51]. The analysis of antioxidant enzyme activities in the leaves of I. rotunda showed that high nitrogen treatment increased the activities of SOD and POD in the plant, and there was no significant change in the activities of SOD and POD under low nitrogen and medium nitrogen treatment compared with the control. It further shows that excessive application of nitrogen fertilizer has produced stress on I. rotunda, which is consistent with the research results of Xu et al. [52]. The increase of POD and SOD activity may be due to the damage of plant cell membrane caused by high nitrogen concentration applied to the plant, the destruction of cell structure and the increase of peroxide content [53]. SOD and POD are important antioxidant enzymes in plants. When plants are stressed, the improvement of their activities is conducive to improving the ability of plants to scavenge their own peroxides. In this study, the activities of SOD and POD in the combined application of carbon and nitrogen in the high nitrogen group were significantly higher than those in the N3 group, which indicated that the application of α-KG could further improve the ability of scavenging peroxide of I. rotunda, so as to enhance the resistance of plants. Zhang et al. found that increasing the CO2 concentration in the tomato growth environment can improve the activities of POD and SOD of tomato and improve the plant resistance [54]. This is consistent with the experimental results.

Through non-targeted metabolomics analysis, it was found that among the top 20 up-regulated metabolites in the CK vs N2KG1 comparison group, 13 metabolites were substances that have been proved to have pharmacological activity. Among the top 20 up-regulated metabolites in the CK vs N2KG2 comparison group, 12 metabolites have been shown to have pharmacological activity. The differential metabolites that were up-regulated in the two comparison groups were salvianolic acid B, linarin, medicago sativa 5-o-hexoside, ginsenoside RO and pelargonidin. Regarding the reasons for the upregulation of flavonoids, we speculate that the combined application of carbon and nitrogen accelerates the TCA cycle, generating more glutamate and glutamine. These compounds then provide nitrogen sources via the shikimate pathway for phenylalanine, a key precursor in the synthesis of flavonoids. Among them, salvianolic acid B has been proved to have potential efficacy in the treatment of ischemic brain injury and myocardial infarction [55, 56]. Linarin, ginsenoside RO and pelargonidin have been proved to have the effect of inhibiting inflammation [57–59]. On the other hand, Al Jaouni et al. showed that the increase of CO2 concentration in the environment led to the up regulation of glutamine, L-glutathione, anthocyanin and most flavonoids metabolites in two kinds of herbal medicines — Ocimum basilicum and Mentha piperita, which was consistent with the results of this study [60]. We also found that N2KG1 treatment reduced the content of malonic acid in I. rotunda plants compared with the CK group. Malonic acid is a competitive inhibitor of succinate dehydrogenase and has a blocking effect on the TCA cycle, which indicates that the combined application of carbon and nitrogen promotes the TCA cycle to a certain extent.

Both N2KG1 and N2KG2 treatments with combined application of carbon and nitrogen had an effect on the TCA cycle of I. rotunda. The contents of α-KG, citric acid and glutamic acid were increased and the content of aspartic acid was decreased by N2KG1 and N2KG2 treatment. We believe that the increase of α-KG and citric acid content indicates that the combined application of carbon and nitrogen regulates the TCA cycle of I. rotunda. Glutamic acid plays an important role in amino acid metabolism and protein synthesis in plants. Its accumulation in I. rotunda also shows that the quality of plants has been improved. Aspartic acid and glutamic acid are transformed into each other in plants, and the relationship is complex. The reason why the content of aspartic acid and glutamic acid is relatively down-regulated by the combined application of carbon and nitrogen remains to be studied. Therefore, in the next step, we will further study the effect of carbon fertilizer (α-KG) on the activities of sucrose synthase (SS), sucrose phosphate synthase (SPS), glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH), aspartate aminotransferase (TAS) and other enzymes of I. rotunda under the treatment of carbon and nitrogen combined application, and clarify the effect of carbon and nitrogen combined application on the carbon and nitrogen metabolism pathway of I. rotunda. The second is to explore the internal mechanism of α- KG combined with urea on promoting plant growth of I. rotunda from the perspective of gene pathway, in order to provide reference for directional breeding.

Additionally, the study by Sabir et al. on the chitinase gene family in Hordeum vulgare revealed that key genes exhibit distinct expression patterns in response to various stresses [61]. This provides a gene-level perspective for comprehending the plant stress response network. Subsequent studies can employ genomics and molecular biology methods to elucidate the molecular mechanism by which α-KG regulates the carbon and nitrogen metabolism of I. rotunda. This provides a foundation for the cultivation of high-quality germplasm. Irfan et al. found that exogenous substances can alleviate stress by regulating photosynthesis, providing ideas for future research [62, 63]. Musa et al. identified various bioactive compounds in Justicia adhatoda using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry [64]. Subsequent studies can learn from their analytical methods to further isolate and identify active components in I. rotunda.

Conclusion

This study examined the effects of α-KG and nitrogen on the growth and carbon and nitrogen metabolism of I. rotunda. Photosynthetic performance and physiological indexes were measured in 2-year-old I. rotunda cuttings after 12 weeks of treatment with different concentrations of α-KG and urea. At the same time, the leaves of each treatment group (CK, N2, N2KG1, and N2KG2) were analyzed by non-targeted metabolomics after 12 weeks of treatment at a 0.4% urea treatment level.

The results showed the application of appropriate concentration of α-KG and urea to I. rotunda increased the net photosynthetic rate and other photosynthetic indicators, enhanced the activity of photosystem II, improved the efficiency of light energy conversion, and promoted plant growth. At the same time, it also promoted the accumulation of soluble sugar, soluble protein and starch in I. rotunda, increased the nitrogen content in leaves, improved the nitrogen utilization ability of plants, and promoted the accumulation of nutrients. The activities of GS, FD-GOGAT and NADH-GOGAT enzymes in I. rotunda were increased, which regulated the GS-GOGAT cycle. The contents of α-KG and citric acid in I. rotunda were increased, and the TCA cycle was regulated. Under the combined application of carbon and nitrogen, the carbon and nitrogen metabolism efficiency of I. rotunda was effectively regulated. The N2KG1 and N2KG2 treatment groups showed the most promising results for each treatment. The application of α-KG to I. rotunda under high nitrogen stress increased the activities of POD and SOD, and increased the contents of soluble sugar and soluble protein in I. rotunda, which was beneficial to enhance the adaptation of I. rotunda to the environment. Metabolomic analysis of I. rotunda leaves from different treatment groups further demonstrated that the combined application of carbon and nitrogen sources enhanced both carbon and nitrogen metabolism in the plants, corroborating the physiological experimental findings. Therefore, the application of α-KG and urea can promote the growth of I. rotunda and improve plant resistance, which provides a theoretical basis for the scientific cultivation of I. rotunda.

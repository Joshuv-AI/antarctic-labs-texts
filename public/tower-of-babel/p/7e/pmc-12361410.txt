A Transformer-LSTM-SVR hybrid model for AI-driven emotional optimization in NEV embedded interior systems
Authors: Zongming Liu, Xuhui Chen, Xinan Liang, Zhicheng Sun, Fengqi Yang, Wenwen Ou, Linwei Li, Xiayan Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
With the rapid expansion of the new energy vehicle (NEV) market, a shift from function-oriented toward emotion-oriented interior design has emerged as a key factor in enhancing user experience and brand differentiation. Current research on emotion optimization in new energy vehicle interiors lacks efficient nonlinear modeling methods. To address this gap, a hybrid Transformer-LSTM-SVR model is proposed, which significantly enhances emotion prediction accuracy by integrating attention mechanisms and temporal modeling. This model addresses the complex nonlinear relationship between users’ emotional satisfaction and interior design attributes. Furthermore, interpretability analysis reveals key design feature differences among user groups. The proposed method combines a Transformer module, which captures higher-order interactions among multidimensional design parameters (e.g., sentiment evaluation coefficients and task completion time), and a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, configured to enhance time-series feature capture through adjustments to hidden unit count and sequence length. Potential representations from both modules are combined into high-dimensional vectors via a feature fusion mechanism and subsequently fed into a Support Vector Regression (SVR) module for fitting nonlinear relationships. This hierarchical architecture effectively mitigates the limitations of traditional SVR in modeling dynamic time series and nonlinear relationships, while enhancing model robustness through the synergy between global context-awareness and time-series dependency. Performance comparisons against benchmark models—including traditional SVR, particle swarm optimization (PSO)-tuned SVR, PSO-tuned random forest (RF), Backpropagation Neural Network (BPNN), and Gradient Boosting Regression (GBR)—demonstrate that the proposed model significantly outperforms these baselines, improving prediction accuracy by 12.7% to 23.4%. Compared to traditional KE methods, the synergistic integration of the LSTM’s temporal attention mechanism and the Transformer’s global context modeling improves system robustness against noisy user feedback data by 18.9%. Results indicate that the model significantly enhances the prediction accuracy of users’ emotional needs, offering a viable approach for emotion-oriented and sustainable interior design in new energy vehicles. This study provides practical tools for designers, thereby enhancing the market competitiveness of new energy vehicles and promoting sustainable development.

Introduction

Global industrialization and urbanization have accelerated energy consumption rates and environmental pressures in the transportation sector. Greenhouse gases (e.g., CO2) and pollutants (e.g., NOx, particulate matter) emitted from conventional fuel vehicles significantly contribute to global warming and urban air pollution1. NEVs represent a core direction for transportation decarbonization, offering zero tailpipe emissions, substantially greater energy efficiency than internal combustion engines, and decreasing life-cycle carbon footprints as electricity generation mixes optimize2,3.

Following convergence in core NEV technologies (e.g., three-electric systems), innovation in user experience increasingly supersedes technical specifications as the primary competitive dimension. Automotive interiors serve as the primary physical interface and emotional conduit for user-vehicle interaction, directly influencing driving satisfaction and brand loyalty. J.D. Power’s 2023 empirical research confirms this significance: 72% of NEV consumers cite“emotional resonance”as a primary motivator for their purchase4.

Current research exhibits significant fragmentation: (1) Materials engineering prioritizes lightweight composites (e.g., Caramitu et al. fabricated interior components using PP + CMC composites5; Baskan-Bayrak et al. optimized polypropylene compounds for sustainable production6); (2) Industrial/economic studies emphasize policy and market analysis (e.g., Tan et al. examined NEV development bottlenecks and pathways in China7); (3) Design research develops scenario-based frameworks (e.g., Li et al. proposed a situational-needs-driven NEV interior design process8). These efforts have not established systematic mapping mechanisms between emotional needs and interior design parameters, resulting in quantifiable engineering pathways for emotional design. Consequently, establishing quantifiable methods to fulfill users’ emotional needs through resonant NEV interior design remains critical.

Kansei Engineering (KE) addresses this gap by establishing mappings between user emotions and design parameters9–12. The core KE process comprises three phases: (1) acquisition of product-related perceptual vocabulary; (2) prioritization of design features using perceptual feedback; (3) construction of perceptual evaluation mapping models to inform design programs13–15. During perceptual vocabulary acquisition and feature prioritization, clarifying the importance of design features enhances the efficiency of the development process. Various methods have been employed: Liu et al.16 applied fuzzy AHP (FAHP) to identify core features via requirement importance; Xu et al.17 integrated the Kano model with AHP to categorize critical requirements and develop scoring systems; Yu et al.18 combined AHP and TOPSIS to evaluate sentiment factors for critical indicator identification. Fuzzy TOPSIS (FTOPSIS)19 demonstrates superior accuracy for uncertain, fuzzy sentiment decision-making by effectively capturing variable influences.

While establishing sentiment-to-feature mappings is central to KE, the stochastic and fuzzy nature of user sentiment limits the effectiveness of traditional linear regression in capturing nonlinear relationships. Consequently, nonlinear machine learning techniques are being increasingly adopted to enhance the efficacy of KE in emotion capture. For instance, Li20 integrated KE with machine learning (SVM + SVR proved optimal) to extract emotions from online reviews; Yuan et al.21 utilized SVR to map user requirements to nursing bed features for optimal solutions; Wang et al.22 developed an SSA-LSTM-Attention hybrid model for NEV ingenious cockpit emotional experience optimization. Current research primarily employs SVR, CNN, LSTM, RF, and BPNN for classification/regression tasks. The Transformer-LSTM-SVR architecture addresses traditional SVR limitations for complex, high-dimensional data. The Transformer multi-attention captures long-range dependencies23, the LSTM processes nonlinear features, and ε-SVR performs regression—collectively enhancing prediction, adaptation, and generalization capabilities.

Therefore, this study introduces FTOPSIS and Transformer-LSTM-SVR in perceptual engineering (KE) research and constructs an innovative interdisciplinary methodological framework integrating perceptual engineering with machine learning. The FTOPSIS method is employed to prioritize NEV interior design features. In contrast, the Transformer-LSTM-SVR model is utilized to predict user emotional needs in KE, aiming to develop vehicle interiors that align with consumer emotional requirements. Three key challenges in NEV interior design are systematically addressed through the integration of hybrid Fuzzy TOPSIS (FTOPSIS) and Transformer-LSTM-SVR models: ① ambiguous prioritization of design features; ② quantification difficulties in emotional needs; and ③ inadequate modeling of nonlinear relationships.

FAHP

It helps designers identify key indicators to optimize decision-making by fuzzy evaluating the indicators and assigning weights. Its core steps are as follows.

Step 1: The initial phase involves the systematic construction of the fuzzy judgment matrix. This process begins with pairwise comparison of subordinate elements relative to their parent criteria within the hierarchical structure. Support vector regression model

Support Vector Regression (SVR) operates by identifying an optimal hyperplane that minimizes empirical risk while maximizing geometric margin, enabling reliable prediction of continuous variables. This study presents a novel application of SVR for establishing nonlinear correlations between new energy vehicle (NEV) interior morphological characteristics and aggregated perceptual evaluation scores. During model development, quantifiable interior design parameters from NEV prototypes were operationalized as predictor variables (X1, X2, …, Xn), with user-derived perceptual metrics serving as the response variable (Y) through systematic aggregation of survey data. Long and short time series models

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks represent a specialized recurrent neural network architecture extensively employed for temporal sequence analysis30. Through its gated memory cell mechanism, the gradient instability issues (vanishing/exploding gradients) prevalent in traditional RNN implementations are effectively mitigated while enabling enhanced modeling of extended temporal dependencies within sequential data patterns.

As shown in Fig. 2, the LSTM consists of one or more LSTM cells, and a single LSTM cell internally includes a forgetting gate, an input gate, a cell state update, and an output gate. Among them, the forgetting gate decides to discard part of the information passed from the previous cell state ct−1 to the current cell state ct; the input gate decides to keep the input xt of the network at the current moment passed to the part of the information of the cell state ct. The output gate decides the current output value ht.Fig. 2LSTM network structure.

Transformer models

The Transformer architecture31, initially introduced in the seminal work"Attention Is All You Need"(Vaswani et al., 2017), represents a paradigm shift in neural sequence modeling through its exclusive reliance on attention mechanisms. This innovative neural architecture was specifically engineered to address sequence-to-sequence transformation tasks while resolving the long-term dependency limitations inherent in traditional RNN architectures32.

The model’s computational efficiency stems from its employment of scaled dot-product self-attention mechanisms, which enable parallelized feature extraction across entire sequences. Core architectural components include: 1) multi-head attention layers for contextual relationship modeling, 2) sinusoidal positional encoding for sequence order preservation, 3) residual connections facilitating gradient flow, and 4) layer normalization ensuring training stability.

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the Transformer’s modular design comprises N identical encoder-decoder layers stacked in parallel. Each encoder layer contains self-attention and feedforward sublayers, while decoder layers incorporate additional cross-attention mechanisms for target sequence generation. This architecture has become foundational for state-of-the-art performance in diverse sequence transduction tasks, including machine translation, text summarization, and semantic parsing.Fig. 3Transformer model structure.

Transformer model structure.

① Self-Attention mechanism.

The Self-Attention mechanism is a key feature of the Transformer model, which allows the model to perform weighted aggregation of parts of the input sequence. By calculating the attention scores between queries (Q), keys (K), and values (V), the model can determine the importance of each position relative to the other. The attention scores are scaled, and the value matrix is combined through weighted summation to produce the final output. The calculation formula is:32\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Attention\left( {Q,K,V} \right) = softmax\left( {\frac{{QK^{T} }}{{\sqrt {d_{K} } }}} \right) * V$$\end{document}

② Multi-Head Attention Mechanism.

The Transformer model uses multiple parallel self-attention mechanisms (heads) to capture different types of relationships. Each head captures information by learning different weights, allowing it to be represented across various representation subspaces. The outputs of multiple heads are concatenated and linearly transformed. Transformer-LSTM-SVR modeling

Although LSTM alleviates the long-term dependency problem, it does not entirely resolve it due to its recursive structure32. In contrast, based on the self-attention mechanism, the Transformer model utilizes a parallel attention mechanism that successfully mitigates the long-term dependency issue, allowing for extracting features from more distant temporal sequences and better capturing the dependencies within the sequence33. Additionally, the LSTM model, as a recurrent neural network, is well-suited for modeling sequential data and performs well in time series prediction tasks, effectively capturing dynamic patterns in the perceptual analysis of user emotions.

In Support Vector Regression (SVR), the penalty coefficient (C) enhances the generalization ability of the prediction model and adjusts the error between the measured and predicted outputs. The smaller the error, the more the model’s predictive performance improves. Moreover, the choice of kernel function significantly impacts the SVR model, making it critical to select the appropriate kernel function width.

The Transformer-LSTM-SVR model leverages the Transformer’s feature extraction capability and the LSTM’s ability to capture temporal dependencies, thus improving generalization capabilities. Conversely, SVR enables efficient regression of complex features extracted from the Transformer and LSTM, providing the model with robust nonlinear modeling capacity. Designing the study

Research process

In this study, we first use the KJ method and a web crawler to capture perceptual sample images and perceptual words related to new energy vehicle interiors, obtaining the most representative car interior perceptual words through the screening process. Then, FAHP is employed to calculate the sample perceptual words to obtain the core perceptual words. Subsequently, the relevant car interior samples are deconstructed by the KE method to identify the relevant design features, and the weights of these design features are evaluated using FTOPSIS to determine the design features with a higher perceptiveness coefficient. Finally, a mapping matrix is constructed between automotive interior design features and the users’ perceptual intentions through user evaluation tests, and the Transformer-LSTM-SVR prediction model is utilized to predict and evaluate the perceptual intentions of these features. The prediction results are applied to the design practice of automobile interiors, leading to a design solution that best meets users’ emotional needs. Notably, technological sense and uniqueness share similarities, with technological sense reflecting advanced materials and spatial layout of NEV interiors, while uniqueness emphasizes distinctive designs of interiors to build brand recognition. These aspects encapsulate the“futuristic”nature of NEV interior design, conveying both technological advancement and uniqueness, and highlighting innovative and distinctive design features.

Smooth, fresh, and streamlined attributes are prevalent, particularly in styling design, reflecting the principle of"less is more,"which can be encapsulated in the concept of"simplicity."Human-centric design, safety, and brightness are closely related to user experience in NEV interiors, emphasizing the design’s attention to user needs and occupant safety, thereby reflecting the designer’s dedication to function and experience. This can be characterized as"caring."

In summary, we have divided the eight sensory terms we selected into three categories: futuristic, minimalist, and caring.

NEV interior morphology deconstruction

Deconstruction of interior forms

Morphological deconstruction in perceptual engineering is a crucial technique for elucidating the relationship between perceptual intention and product structure by disassembling and analyzing the structure and form of a product or system34. This method emphasizes the importance of a comprehensive understanding of design elements with respect to user perception and emotion, particularly in terms of user sensation and experience. The basic premise of the morphological deconstruction method involves decomposing a product or system into its component parts and conducting a thorough examination of the form, structure, and function of each element. This deconstruction includes not only an analysis of the physical structure but also a comprehensive study of the design elements, shape, structure, color, and materials. Through such comprehensive deconstruction, designers can gain a deeper understanding of the internal logic and perceptual attributes of a product or system, enabling them to better understand users’ genuine needs, thereby reducing design risks and ensuring that the product aligns more closely with market and user expectations.

In this study, we recruited eight industrial designers with experience in automotive design and formed an evaluation team to analyze 100 NEV interior samples that we had collected. In the process of deconstruction, we identified eight design elements from the NEV interior samples, including the steering wheel, door panel, instrument panel, center console, lights, armrest box, air conditioning vents, and seats. In the prediction process, the Future Sense dataset was used as an example. The data were first normalized to ensure that data with different scales could be compared in the same framework. Discussion

Exploration of methodology and model performance

Within Kansei Engineering, determining design feature weights is crucial for reducing the cognitive load on designers and guiding their practice. However, traditional methods (AHP, Kano model, subjective evaluation) suffer from evaluator subjectivity, which compromises the reliability of the results and their scientific validity. This study, therefore, integrates FTOPSIS with FAHP to evaluate NEV interior features, where fuzzy mathematics mitigates subjective interference and enhances objectivity/accuracy.

Traditional linear methods (QT-II44, PCA45) often fail to capture nuanced customer sentiment. Nonlinear AI methods (neural networks46, ARM47, genetic algorithms48) offer greater flexibility for sentiment analysis. However, neural networks require manual parameter selection (e.g., layer count, neuron count), while ARM handles only discrete variables49, and genetic algorithms yield limited optimal solutions despite their applications in engineering50. Support Vector Regression (SVR) excels in small-sample adaptation, nonlinear processing, and high-dimensional recognition, while avoiding local minima/overfitting, to provide superior sentiment modeling.

In order to systematically verify the advancement of the Transformer-LSTM-SVR model proposed in this study, we selected seven types of representative and widely used benchmark models: SVR as a classical nonlinear regression baseline, which represents the upper limit of the performance of traditional machine learning; PSO-SVR embodies the improvement strategy of intelligent optimization algorithms to enhance the performance of the base model; RF, as a representative model of integrated learning, is good at dealing with complex feature relationships; PSO-SVR is a representative model of integrated learning, which is good at dealing with complex feature relationships; and PSO-SVR is a representative model of integrated learning. Specializes in dealing with complex feature relationships; PSO-RF combines intelligent optimization and integrated learning, and is used to evaluate the impact of optimization techniques on the integrated approach; Transformer-SVR, as a recent typical architecture for the integration of deep learning and traditional models, serves as a key benchmark for comparison due to its structural similarity to the present approach but lack of temporal sequential modeling (LSTM); and BP neural network serves as a basic feed-forward network representative, providing a reference point for deep learning; and GBR and AdaBoost, which together represent the Boosting integration learning paradigm, with their advantages in regression robustness constituting an important benchmark in the integration field.

Compared with these carefully selected benchmark models (SVR, PSO-SVR, RF, PSO-RF, Transformer-SVR, BP, GBR, AdaBoost), the Transformer-LSTM-SVR method proposed in this study demonstrates significant advantages in classification and regression prediction tasks. Compared to traditional SVR and PSO-SVR, Transformer-LSTM-SVR eliminates the need for extensive manual feature engineering and significantly improves prediction accuracy in dynamic data. Compared to PSO-RF51, Transformer-LSTM-SVR is more adept at handling time-series data and is able to comprehend the complexity of data over time. When compared with Transformer-SVR alone, Transformer-LSTM-SVR better manages the time-series information, resulting in superior performance in tracking sentiment changes. Compared with BP52, Transformer-LSTM-SVR addresses the problem of gradient vanishing and possesses a deeper feature learning capability. Compared with GBR53 and AdaBoost54, Transformer-LSTM-SVR demonstrates more robustness and flexibility in noise resistance and dynamic modeling. Therefore, the Transformer-LSTM-SVR method achieves more accurate and reliable prediction results in the sentiment prediction task through its highly automated deep learning framework, which fully combines complex data features and temporal dynamics, and demonstrates superior performance. Error distribution skewness and multi-peak phenomena provide quantitative bases for user group clustering and parameter-sensitive zone localization in interior design.

The Transformer-LSTM-SVR framework has three core limitations: Static feature inputs prevent dynamic interaction capture, inhibiting real-time sensing of driving-induced emotional evolution (e.g., congestion anxiety or high-speed exhilaration) and causing temporal prediction bias; Cultural homogeneity in training data (e.g., East Asian“caring”preference) limits cross-cultural generalizability, reducing adaptability to Scandinavian minimalist or North American sporty styles; and real-time application constraints: LSTM sequential processing and SVR high-dimensional recomposition generate prediction latency (> 1 s), hindering instantaneous design iteration. Future work will focus on three key directions: a) Dynamic Sensing Enhancement: Steering wheel electrodermal sensing and in-cabin expression recognition are integrated to construct spatiotemporal emotion maps for biosignal feedback capture; b) Cultural Adaptation Mechanisms: Hofstede’s cultural dimension indices are injected as prior knowledge and combined with meta-learning to enable rapid geographical preference adaptation; c) Lightweight Deployment: Model distillation (Transformer-SVR teacher) and FPGA acceleration (SVR kernel pipeline) compress parameters by 75% and reduce latency to 50 ms, enabling vehicular incremental learning. This evolution promotes transitioning from static feature-driven to context-adaptive design optimization, establishes scalable emotion-computing foundations for intelligent cockpits, and enables closed-loop affective regulation across automotive, residential, and clinical domains.

Design mechanisms and potential for cross-cutting applications

The center console and seat, as core automotive interior elements, define user experience through a dual functional-emotional mechanism: the console functions as the human–computer interaction hub, with its visual guidance and operating logic directly influencing driving safety and cognitive efficiency; the seat transforms physical comfort into emotional belonging through ergonomic support and tactile design, collectively establishing the cockpit’s physiological comfort and psychological identity basis. This design combination’s essence lies in synergistic functionality-emotion transformation: the console interface sublimates mechanical operation into control perception, the seat morphology extends physical support into a nurturing experience, and material-light-shadow synergy ultimately translates technical parameters into emotional language, forming the"Safety-Comfort-Identity"value chain.

The Transformer-LSTM-SVR model developed in this study effectively quantifies these mechanisms, with potential applications extending beyond the automotive domain. Three major application fields exist: 1) Industrial design – the framework may be adapted to optimize consumer electronics aesthetics, guiding decisions through morphology-emotion mapping deconstruction; 2) Interaction design – time-series data processing advantages enable analysis of dynamic user behaviors (e.g., improving smart cockpit interface fluidity or optimizing service process emotional experiences); 3) Cross-cultural design – multimodal integration potential provides methodological foundations for global product adaptation. Current limitations include a deficiency in dynamic scene response (static features cannot capture real-time emotions) and real-time computational constraints (latency exceeding 1 s).

Beyond technical limitations, despite ethical approval (IRBSUT20250327), perceptual assessment biases require addressing: a) Cultural bias – East Asian groups’“subtle beauty”preferences differ significantly from European/American minimalist standards, causing divergent emotional responses; b) Limited demographic coverage – overrepresentation of student samples may weaken professional designers’ aesthetic judgment representation. To address these challenges and enhance universality, fairness, and application value, future work focuses on: 1) Technical bottleneck resolution – solving dynamic scene response and real-time constraints via biosignal integration and edge computing acceleration; 2) Data diversity enhancement – establishing cross-cultural dynamic datasets incorporating underrepresented regions (e.g., Middle East, Latin America); 3) Algorithmic fairness integration – introducing culturally balanced constraints within feature encoding layers; 4) Assessment standardization – implementing ISO 9241–210 ethical guidelines for standardized vulnerable-group inclusion mechanisms.

Ultimately, an open-source design feature library enables the migration of lightweight frameworks to smart homes, medical, and other fields, promoting cross-industry implementation of the"design feature-user emotion"optimization paradigm and enabling the industrial-scale development of context-adaptive products.

Conclusion

Traditional linear statistical methods predominate in perceptual engineering calculations. However, user emotions represent implicit needs that are often non-explicit, rendering traditional linear prediction methods inadequate for comprehensively capturing the interior perceptual requirements of NEV. A Transformer-LSTM-SVR nonlinear emotion prediction model was developed better to capture drivers’ implicit needs across vehicle interiors, thereby enabling designs aligned with authentic emotional requirements. Automotive interior components were first screened using a combination of FTOPSIS and FAHP to identify critical elements. These components were then employed as variable features to construct an automotive interior emotion evaluation matrix based on expert assessment scores, which was input into the Transformer-LSTM-SVR model to predict optimal interior configurations and generate design matching schemes. The optimal scheme subsequently served as the basis for deriving final design specifications.

Concurrently, SHAP value interpretability analysis was applied to enhance transparency in automotive interior sentiment prediction decisions, with design optimization pathways systematically demonstrated through SHAP swarm plots, feature importance diagrams, and dependency graphs, yielding a 65% improvement in design iteration efficiency. Finally, model accuracy was verified through comparative performance testing against other machine learning models, confirming that Transformer-LSTM-SVR’s prediction accuracy is superior to that of comparable models, including SVR and PSO-SVR. Furthermore, this study presents the following implications:(1) By integrating the advantages of Transformer and LSTM, Transformer-LSTM-SVR is able to effectively capture long time dependencies and dynamic changes, which is suitable for tasks requiring analysis of historical data such as sentiment analysis and user behavior prediction.(2) FTOPSIS combined with FAHP can reduce the subjectivity of decision makers in multi-criteria decision-making and make the decision-making information closer to the real needs, which is suitable for sentiment computing in perceptual engineering.(3) By constructing a nonlinear machine learning emotion prediction model, the combination of NEV interior design features is predicted, and its prediction accuracy is better than that of the traditional linear prediction model. from the perspective of overall industrial construction, the emotional capture of NEV interiors can help to increase the competitiveness of automobile manufacturers and promote the overall sustainability of the industry for both traditional and nontraditional automobile manufacturers.

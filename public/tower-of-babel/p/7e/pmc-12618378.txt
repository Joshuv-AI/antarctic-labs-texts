Value Contribution of Palopegteriparatide in Adult Patients with Chronic Hypoparathyroidism using Multicriteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)
Authors: Juan José Díez, Xavier Badia, Reyes Abad, Carmen Alerany, Mónica Climente Martí, Josep Maria Guiu, Isabel Martín, María Caballero, Manuel Muñoz Torres
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe study objective was to determine the value contribution of palopegteriparatide to the treatment of adults with chronic hypoparathyroidism, a rare endocrine disease, through a multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA).MethodsThe Orphar-SEFH group framework for evaluating orphan drugs was used, along with the weighting performed by 98 national and regional evaluators. A multidisciplinary panel of seven experts individually scored the evidence matrix. The scores were collected and analysed using Microsoft Excel software programmed for MCDA study analysis. The results were discussed in a reflective discussion session.ResultsThe expert panel considered chronic hypoparathyroidism to be a moderate-to-severe disease (mean ± SD: 3.3 ± 0.5) due to its multiorgan impact and associated comorbidities, with significant unmet needs (3.6 ± 1.0), particularly related to the lack of the physiological effect due to insufficient levels of parathyroid hormone. The experts found palopegteriparatide as an add-on treatment to conventional therapy (calcium and active vitamin D) to be much more effective than placebo plus conventional therapy, with this criterion receiving the highest score. The safety profile was considered acceptable (1.6 ± 1.1). The participants highlighted the positive impact of palopegteriparatide on patient-reported outcomes (2.9 ± 0.9) compared to placebo because of improvements in quality of life (QoL). Palopegteriparatide was seen as a potentially disease-modifying treatment, which could lead to savings in "other medical costs" (3.1 ± 1.1) and "non-medical/indirect costs" (2.0 ± 0.8), although no evidence was available, especially in the long term. The quality of the evidence was perceived as high (3.6 ± 0.5). The overall value contribution of palopegteriparatide was 0.58 (+ 1 = maximum value) compared to placebo. The study has some limitations, including a relatively small panel size and the exclusion of treatment cost as a criterion due to lack of pricing information at the time of evaluation.ConclusionAccording to MCDA, palopegteriparatide represents a valuable therapeutic option for chronic hypoparathyroidism treatment, particularly due to its demonstrated efficacy, which had an impact on patients’ QoL, and the current unmet needs in this therapeutic area.Keywords: Chronic hypoparathyroidism, MCDA, Multi-criteria decision analysis, Orphan drug, Rare disease, Health technology assessment, Decision-making

Introduction

Chronic hypoparathyroidism is a rare endocrine disease due to insufficient parathyroid hormone (PTH) secretion from the parathyroid glands leading to hypocalcemia, hyperphosphataemia and elevated urine excretion of calcium [1, 2]. The most common cause of chronic hypoparathyroidism in adults is anterior neck surgery, in approximately 75% of cases. Non-surgical causes comprise 25% and include genetic, autoimmune and idiopathic causes or functional hypoparathyroidism related to magnesium depletion [3]. According to a European-wide meta-analysis of epidemiological studies, chronic hypoparathyroidism affects 32 of 100,000 people [4].

Chronic hypoparathyroidism affects multiple organ systems and causes physical, cognitive and emotional symptoms that can be debilitating and severe, significantly affecting patients' daily lives. In addition, long-term complications can affect vital organs and can be life-threatening [2, 5–8].

Chronic hypoparathyroidism is typically managed with active vitamin D and oral calcium, aiming to maintain calcium concentrations in the lower limit of the reference range with patients free of symptoms or signs of hypocalcemia [1]. However, few therapeutic options are available, and many patients are unable to achieve and maintain serum and urine calcium levels within the normal range and continue to suffer from severe complications and disabling symptoms that drastically limit their quality of life (QoL) [8]. Some of these patients remain inadequately controlled with conventional therapy (CT) and experience iatrogenic symptoms [1] and complications mainly at the renal level (nephrocalcinosis, nephrolithiasis and chronic kidney disease) [2]. The guidelines of the International Working Group 2022 [9] as well as the European ESE Expert Consensus [1] recommend PTH replacement therapy in patients not adequately controlled with CT.

Palopegteriparatide is a recently approved PTH replacement therapy indicated for the treatment of adults with chronic hypoparathyroidism that is administered subcutaneously once daily. This compound provided sustained release of active PTH within the physiological range for 24 h a day [10].

Health technology assessment has traditionally been focused on efficacy, safety and cost [11]. However, there are tools that enable the evaluation of health technologies using additional criteria in a more holistic manner. This approach is particularly relevant in the field of orphan drugs (OD) because of their unique characteristics in clinical development and their epidemiological, clinical and socioeconomic attributes [12]. Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) is a standardized tool that allows for the objective, pragmatic and transparent evaluation of health technologies, supporting decision-making, and it is being used by health technology assessment (HTA) bodies [12–15]. The aim of this study was to determine the value contribution of palopegteriparatide to the treatment of adults with chronic hypoparathyroidism using MCDA methodology.

Methods

The study was conducted according to the steps outlined in the MCDA methodology [15–17]. First, the study objective and the MCDA framework were defined. Then, a literature review was carried out to fill in the evidence matrix. Finally, each criterion in the framework was rated and results were aggregated and discussed in a meeting to assess the value contribution of palopegteriparatide.

Framework Selection

The EVIDEM framework is a validated and standardized MCDA framework for international use, specifically developed for determining the value of innovation in healthcare [18]. The criteria included in the framework are specific to the healthcare decision-making environment and are based on ethical principles. The EVIDEM framework [18] was adapted and validated for evaluation of OD in Spain by a multidisciplinary group of experts [19] and later refined by the Orphar-SEFH group, the working group on rare diseases and OD from the Spanish Society of Hospital Pharmacy (SEFH)[15]. The framework contains 12 criteria organized into 3 dimensions that facilitate a structured and comprehensive reflective discussion on all relevant criteria, aiming to evaluate the value contribution of a drug in the management of a specific disease. Two of these are quantitative dimensions: disease-related criteria (disease severity and unmet needs) and treatment-related criteria [some are comparative (requiring comparison with a reference treatment)—efficacy, safety, patient-reported outcomes—while others are non-comparative: therapeutic impact, other medical and non-medical costs and quality of evidence). In addition, the third dimension comprises qualitative or contextual criteria, providing a broader perspective for assessment. As recommended by the Orphar-SEFH group [15], the criteria "treatment cost" and "opportunity cost and affordability" were excluded because the study was conducted before the drug had obtained price and reimbursement in Spain. The scoring scale for non-comparative criteria ranged from 0 to + 5 (where 0 is the worst score and 5 the best score), while for comparative criteria, it ranged from – 5 (palopegteriparatide is much worse than the alternative) to + 5 (palopegteriparatide is much better than the alternative). Contextual criteria were evaluated based on their potential positive, neutral or negative impact on the National Health System (NHS). Additionally, experts were asked to provide reflections and justifications for each of the scores assigned.

Therapeutic Alternatives

A double‐blind, placebo‐controlled, 26‐week, phase 3 PaTHway trial assessed the efficacy and safety of PTH replacement therapy for hypoparathyroidism individuals with the investigational drug TransCon PTH (palopegteriparatide). Participants were randomized to subcutaneous (SC) once‐daily TransCon PTH or placebo, both co‐administered with CT (calcium and active vitamin D) at baseline, and then followed an algorithm intended to facilitate independence from CT as it was one of the primary endpoint components [20]. The comparator to assess the value contribution of palopegteriparatide in this study was CT plus placebo as it was the comparator of the PaTHway phase III pivotal trial [20].

Literature Review and Evidence Matrix Development

A systematic literature review was carried out to identify evidence on palopegteriparatide and the disease considering all the criteria included in the study, which was then synthesized and structured in the evidence matrix [15]. The literature review was conducted using biomedical databases such as PubMed/MEDLINE, Google Scholar and grey literature [websites of the European Medicines Agency, international clinical trial registries (clinicaltrials.gov), scientific societies and patient associations] [21, 22].

Expert Panel and Study Design

The study was conducted with a multidisciplinary group of experts to gather insights from a broad range of perspectives [23] including endocrinologists with experience in the management and treatment of patients with chronic hypoparathyroidism, hospital pharmacists with expertise in treatment evaluation at the regional and/or hospital level and regional decision-makers in Spain. Members were chosen for their expertise, with an effort to ensure balanced geographical representation across the country.

Participants received 1-h online training on the MCDA methodology.

Ethics Statement

The authors declare that no experiments involving humans or animals were conducted for this research, and no patient data are included in this article. Full consent to participate in the study and to publish the findings was obtained from all contributors.

Matrix Scoring and Reflective Discussion Meeting

After this initial session the experts individually scored the evidence matrix remotely, which had been previously validated by one of the endocrinologists participating in the study. The results were analysed as described below and presented at the reflective discussion meeting which consisted of a face-to-face meeting to discuss the results, scoring and justification behind each rating [24].

Data Collection and Analysis

After the training session, the matrices were sent to the experts for scoring each of the criteria, including their reflections behind each score. The scores were collected individually, entered into a common database and analysed using Microsoft Excel software designed for MCDA study analysis. A descriptive analysis of each criterion was conducted.

For the contextual criteria, the scoring was done on a qualitative scale with three levels: positive, neutral or negative impact; scores were transformed into a numerical scale (+ 1, 0 and − 1 points, respectively). The results were expressed as the percentage of experts who rated each option.

The weighting used to assign the relative importance of each of the quantitative criteria was obtained from a previous study conducted with the participation of 98 Spanish national and regional evaluators [14].

Results

Expert Panel Composition

The value contribution of palopegteriparatide was assessed by a multidisciplinary panel of seven experts: two endocrinologists, four heads of hospital pharmacy services who were experts in drug evaluation and one regional decision-maker.

Quantitative Results

The quantitative criteria results are shown in Fig. 1. The mean score, standard deviation, median and range (minimum and maximum) were calculated for each quantitative criterion.

Quantitative criteria value scoring results (mean ± SD). Max: maximum; Min: minimum; n: number of participants; SD: standard deviation

Chronic hypoparathyroidism was considered as a moderate to severe disease (mean ± SD: 3.3 ± 0.5) due to its multiorgan impact and associated comorbidities such as impaired renal function, chronic kidney disease, impaired bone and mineral metabolism and cognitive dysfunction. Experts found relevant unmet needs in the management of the disease (3.6 ± 1.0) mainly related to the lack of physiological effect due to insufficient levels of PTH, associated with long-term comorbidities, especially in patients not adequately controlled with CT (calcium and active vitamin D).

The highest average score was given to the comparative criterion “efficacy and effectiveness” (3.9 ± 0.9). The entire expert panel considered palopegteriparatide as an add-on therapy to CT (calcium and active vitamin D) to be a treatment with superior efficacy to placebo with strong and consistent results in clinical trials where the composite primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of participants at week 26 who achieved normal albumin‐adjusted serum calcium levels (8.3–10.6 mg/dl), independence from CT (requiring no active vitamin D and ≤ 600 mg/day of calcium) and no increase in study drug over 4 weeks before week 26. “Safety and tolerability” criterion was scored with 1.6 ± 1.1, seen as an acceptable profile by the experts compared with placebo. The experts agreed that palopegteriparatide presents better patient-reported outcome profile than the comparator (2.9 ± 0.9) because of improved quality of life, although no data were available on the long-term effects.

Regarding the “therapeutic impact” criterion, the panel of experts considered palopegteriparatide to be a potentially disease-modifying treatment as it mimics the physiological effect of PTH and can be expected to have long-term effects on bone and kidney function (2.3 ± 0.7). There was a range of scores for the “other medical costs” (3.1 ± 1.1) and “non-medical costs” (2.0 ± 0.8) criteria, and the panel agreed that palopegteriparatide could provide savings within the health system given its efficacy results. However, they noted that no evidence was available to support this hypothesis, especially in the long term.

The quality of the evidence was perceived as high by the experts with very homogeneous scores for this criterion (3.6 ± 0.5), especially highlighting the design of the phase III trial and its open-label extension study considering that it is an orphan drug.

The criteria scores were weighted based on the relative importance of each criterion to estimate the overall value contribution of palopegteriparatide compared to placebo (Fig. 2). The result was 0.58 (scale between − 1 and + 1, with + 1 being the maximum value contribution). The criteria "efficacy and effectiveness" and "quality of the evidence" contributed the most to the overall value contribution with a score of 0.10 and 0.09, respectively. “Disease severity” and “unmet needs” were also relevant to the value contribution with a score of 0.08. Regarding comparative criteria, the "efficiency and effectiveness" criterion provided the greatest added value. The contribution of comparative and non-comparative criteria to the total result was very balanced with an aggregated score of 0.26 and 0.32, respectively.

Qualitative Results

Regarding the contextual criteria (Fig. 3), the impact of introducing palopegteriparatide to the NHS and the appropriate use of the intervention was considered neutral by 100% of participants, as its method of administration and storage conditions did not require any changes, and legislative adjustments, healthcare team training and additional system organization were not needed. Regarding the "common objective and specific interests" criterion, most participants (84%) thought that the inclusion of palopegteriparatide would have a positive impact, as it would be aligned with the specific interests of the NHS and the involved societies, given that no hormone replacement therapy is currently available for these patients. Finally, since chronic hypoparathyroidism is a rare disease, 71% of participants concurred on the positive impact of palopegteriparatide regarding the "Population priorities and access” criterion.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first MCDA study to assess a hormonal replacement therapy. Once-daily SC palopegteriparatide as an add-on therapy to CT was evaluated for the treatment of chronic hypoparathyroidism compared with placebo. The study was conducted by a multidisciplinary group of experts using the MCDA methodology, where the reflective discussion phase allowed for understanding and discussing the results obtained after analysing the scores from the evidence matrix from different perspectives [25]. This approach enabled the holistic evaluation of palopegteriparatide, considering criteria beyond efficacy, safety and cost.

The expert panel considered chronic hypoparathyroidism a moderate to severe disease due to its multiorgan impact, high associated morbidity [6, 8] and unmet needs specifically related to the lack of a hormone replacement therapy targeted for this condition, as CT aims to alleviate hypocalcemia but does not address PTH deficiency [26] and its effects on bone metabolism and at a renal level. In addition, chronic hypoparathyroidism is the only endocrine deficiency disease for which no indicated hormone replacement therapy is available [27]. Patients with the greatest unmet needs were defined by the panel as those who are not adequately controlled with CT (calcium and active vitamin D), for whom European guidelines recommend hormone replacement therapy [1, 9].

The "efficacy and effectiveness" criterion was the most influential concerning the overall value of palopegteriparatide, supporting the product's value and the results from its phase III clinical trial [20] and the open-label extension trial, which provide data consistent with the main study at 52 weeks [28]. The significance of a therapy that replicates the physiological effects of the hormone was underscored, as it plays a crucial role in preventing long-term complications and comorbidities associated with the absence of the hormone's effect and the limitations of CT (tolerability and adherence to high doses of calcium, increased risk of renal complications such us nephrocalcinosis and nephrolithiasis and impaired renal function)[2, 8, 29, 30]. The safety profile was considered acceptable by the participants, although they emphasized the importance of long-term results to better assess it, which is a usual concern regarding OD. Palopegteriparatide was considered a potential disease-modifying treatment due to its mechanism of action, disease control and achievement of independence from CT. In this regard, participants suggested that the treatment with palopegteriparatide could result in savings for the healthcare system related to disease control and modification of its clinical course, as well as savings in indirect costs, although more real-world evidence is needed to confirm this with certainty. The pharmacological cost criterion was not assessed because, palopegteriparatide was undergoing the pricing and reimbursement process in Spain at that time. The positive impact of the treatment on patient-reported QoL in both generic and disease-specific questionnaires was highly valued by the experts, as when compared to placebo, the improvements in QoL could be directly attributed to the drug.

Regarding the contextual criteria, the inclusion of the drug was considered positive and aligned with the specific interests and priorities of the NHS, as well as its priority of access to the population, given that currently no other replacement therapy is available [27]. Finally, it was determined that its impact on the system's capacity to use the drug and ensure its proper use would be neutral, since it is an SC formulation that does not require additional training or organizational adjustments for the teams or the system.

The overall value contribution of palopegteriparatide is comparable and ranks among the highest compared to other ODs such as emicizumab [22] for the prophylaxis of haemophilia A, etracogene dezaparvovec [21] for treatment of severe and moderately severe haemophilia B, tabelecleucel [31] for treatment of the Epstein-Barr virus-positive post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease and olipudase alfa [32] for treatment of non-central nervous system manifestations of acid sphingomyelinase deficiency (0.45–0.73)[21, 22, 31, 32], all diseases with limited or no therapeutic options.

Various methods have been used to assess the value of drugs and support decision making [33]. However, a comprehensive and transparent approach is essential to consistently address the medical and social needs of specific treatments, such as OD and Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products (ATMPs). This approach ensures that all relevant factors, including clinical benefits, patient outcomes and societal impact, are appropriately considered in the evaluation process [25, 33, 34]. The use of the MCDA methodology is on the rise in the context of orphan drug evaluation [35]; it has been used to support healthcare decision making in several countries (Sweden, The Netherlands, Scotland, UK, among others) and also by HTA bodies such as the Institute for Quality and Efficiency in Health Care (IQwig) in Germany and the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in the UK [34, 36]. There are multiple examples showing the use of MCDA for the evaluation of drugs in various therapeutic areas including oncology, haematology, neurology and infectious diseases, among others [21–23, 31, 37–39]. Additionally, the MCDA methodology has been applied in several environments, for example, as a framework for decision making in early access to medications, where a standardized method is needed to ensure equitable access to medicines [40] or to prioritise real-world evidence studies in the oncological field [41].

Undoubtedly, one of the most valuable aspects of this methodology is the reflective discussion conducted after the scoring process, as it facilitates the integration of diverse perspectives into the analysis and enhances the overall evaluation of the drug [15].

The present study has some limitations. The size of the panel could be considered small, although it is consistent with many other MCDA studies published [22, 42] and ensures participation in group discussions, allowing for a deeper understanding of the scores and their rationale. The panel included different profiles, although it would have also been valuable to include a patient representative to capture their perspective. At the time the study was conducted, the price of the drug was not publicly available, which prevented the inclusion of treatment cost as a criterion in the MCDA framework. This could be addressed in the future if a new evaluation is carried out once pricing information becomes available. Lastly, the information presented in the evidence matrix was the one available at the time of the study, so not all long-term data from the extension study of the evaluated drug had been published, meaning the results may evolve as new data are released.

Conclusion

According to MCDA, once-daily SC palopegteriparatide as an add-on therapy to CT could represent a valuable therapeutic option in the management of chronic hypoparathyroidism, with a focus on its demonstrated efficacy, which is also reflected in its impact on patients' QoL and the current unmet needs in this therapeutic area. Furthermore, with the above-mentioned limitations, the participants emphasized the utility and suitability of the MCDA methodology for health technology assessment, as it enables the identification and evaluation of both benefits and limitations by considering multiple criteria and perspectives, thereby supporting decision-making based on a more comprehensive and thorough evaluation.

Adolescent’s anhedonia and association with childhood trauma among Chinese adolescents: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Jing Han, Liu-hong Zhang, Cai-yi Zhang, Liuna Bi, Lin-lin Wang, Yan-xiu Cai
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo estimate the level of anhedonia among adolescents and explore the association between anhedonia and childhood trauma (CT).DesignA stratified random cluster sample of adolescents participated in a survey, which included three questionnaires: Snaith-Pamilton Pleasure Scale, Childhood Trauma Questionnaire and Patient Health Questionnaire-9.SettingThe study was conducted in 60 classes in 10 primary, middle and high schools in five economically developed cities along the Southeast Coast of China from April to October 2022.ParticipantsOne thousand seven hundred and forty-five adolescents with ages ranging from 9 to 18 years participated in the study.Primary and secondary outcomesThe primary outcome was the level of anhedonia, CT and depression among adolescents. The association between anhedonia and CT was determined.ResultsThe mean scores of anhedonia, CT and depression were 24.88 (6.18), 36.75 (8.87) and 4.46 (5.36), respectively. Anhedonia scores of boys (24.24±6.12) and girls (25.62±6.16) were different (t=−4.69, p<0.01). After controlling for sex, age, the presence of siblings and depression, CT was associated with adolescent anhedonia. Emotional abuse (β=0.14), emotional neglect (β=0.15) and physical neglect (β=0.10) positively predicted adolescent anhedonia (p<0.01), whereas physical abuse negatively predicted adolescent anhedonia (β=−0.07, p<0.01). Sex had a moderating effect on the relationship between adolescent anhedonia and emotional neglect during childhood, and the negative effect of emotional neglect on adolescent anhedonia in girls was greater than in boys.ConclusionsCT, including emotional abuse, physical abuse, emotional neglect, and physical neglect, was an independent predictor of adolescents’ ability to experience pleasure in daily life. Therefore, awareness of CT should be promoted. Emotional neglect had a more severe effect on anhedonia among girls than among boys, suggesting that emotional neglect should be paid much attention among girlsKeywords: MENTAL HEALTH, Child & adolescent psychiatry, PUBLIC HEALTH, Depression & mood disorders

Introduction

The mental health and well-being of children and adolescents is an important health issue.1 Mental health problem is the third leading cause of morbidity among children and adolescents worldwide.2 Mental health is ‘a condition of well-being in which each person fulfils his or her own potential, can cope with typical stressors of life, can work successfully and fruitfully and can contribute to her or his community’.3 A meta-analysis of the worldwide prevalence of mental disorders in children and adolescents found that 11–16% of all children and adolescents were affected by one or more mental disorders.4 The prevalence of mental disorders among children and adolescents aged 6–16 years in China was 17.5%, which is higher than that of most countries in the world.5 Since the outbreak of COVID-19, researchers have confirmed that children and adolescents were more likely to experience stress, worry, helplessness and behavioural problems (eg, substance abuse, suicide, relationship problems, academic issues and absenteeism from work or study).6 7

Childhood trauma (CT) is the most important preventable cause of psychological and mental problems among adolescents, accounting for approximately 45% of the causes of mental disease in children and adolescents.8 9 CT refers to acts committed by persons obliged to raise, supervise and nurture children that are sufficient to cause actual or potential harm to the child’s health, survival, growth, development and dignity.10 CT has been divided into five categories: physical abuse, emotional abuse, sexual abuse, emotional neglect and physical neglect.11 Studies have shown that CT has significant effects on brain development, resulting in changes in brain structure and function in the dorsal prefrontal cortex, orbitofrontal cortex, amygdala and striatum, which are involved in the processing of the reward system.8 12

Anhedonia refers to a reduction or loss in an individual’s ability to experience and derive pleasure from daily activities, such as sports, hobbies, eating and social activities.13 Anhedonia is a characteristic of depression, some types of anxiety, as well as substance abuse and schizophrenia14 15 and is an important symptom in the field of mental health research.14 The neural mechanism of anhedonia is mainly related to the abnormal neural circuits of the reward system, involving the prefrontal lobe, amygdala and striatum.16 Adolescence is a sensitive period for the development of the brain’s reward system and a period of susceptibility to anhedonia.17 Anhedonia is a predictor of an adolescent’s suicidal ideation and suicide.14 18 A meta-analysis study found a strong association between anhedonia and suicidal ideation, independent of depression.19

Evidence suggests that CT is a risk factor for anhedonia. Among individuals with mental disorders, those who have experienced CT were more likely to report symptoms of anhedonia.20 A similar relationship between CT and anhedonia has been also found in non-psychiatric individuals.21 However, to our knowledge, the relationship between CT and anhedonia is yet not to be examined in adolescence. This study aimed to evaluate the levels of anhedonia and CT among Chinese adolescents and investigate their relationship while controlling for depression. Our findings may provide reference information for adolescent mental health preventive services.

Methods

Study population

A stratified random cluster sampling method was adopted. Participants were recruited from three middle schools and two high schools in two east-coast provinces of China from April to October 2022. The inclusion criteria for participation were as follows: (1) age ranging from 9 to 17 years and (2) being studying in a school. Adolescents with a history of psychiatric disease were excluded. After obtaining assent from students and written informed consent from parents and teachers, researchers administered paper questionnaires to participants and collected questionnaires on-site. Our group provided 30-min group psychological support for all participants in their various classrooms after data collection. A total of 1900 questionnaires were administered, and 1745 valid questionnaires (91.84%) were obtained after excluding questionnaires with extreme and invalid data.

Questionnaires

The questionnaires consisted of four parts: (1) demographic data, consisting of sex, age, siblings and educational level; (2) Snaith-Hamilton Pleasure Scale (SHAPS); (3) Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) and (4) Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9).

The SHAPS is the gold standard scale for measuring anhedonia. This scale contains 14 items, which are rated on a four-point Likert scale (Lots of pleasure/Average (usual) pleasure/Some pleasure/No pleasure).16 Higher scores indicate more severe anhedonia. The content of the Chinese version of SHAPS was consistent with the original scale, and Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.94 for patients with depression.22 In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.88.

The CTQ is used to assess current or past trauma experiences. This questionnaire contains five subscales: emotional abuse, physical abuse, sexual abuse, emotional neglect and physical neglect. It is rated on a five-point Likert scale from 1 (never) to 5 (always), with higher scores indicating more severe trauma.23 The cut-off values for the presence of CT include emotional abuse ≤8, physical abuse ≤7, sexual abuse ≤5, emotional neglect ≤9 and physical neglect ≤7.24 The content of the Chinese version of CTQ was consistent with the original version and had a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.77.25 The Cronbach’s α of five subscales ranged from 0.41 (physical neglect) to 0.68 (physical abuse),25 which is consistent with the original CTQ, reporting a lower Cronbach’s α coefficient (0.45) for physical neglect.23 The questionnaire has shown acceptable reliability and validity in Chinese adolescents, except for the subscale of physical neglect.26 27 In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of the questionnaire was 0.68, and the Cronbach’s α of five subscales ranged from 0.45 (physical neglect) to 0.73 (physical abuse).

The PHQ-9, developed by Kroenke et al, includes nine items.28 It is rated on a four-point Likert scale, with higher scores indicating more severe depressive symptoms. A total score ≥10 is used as the best cut-off for depression screening in adolescents.29 In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficient of this questionnaire was 0.89.

Patient and public involvement

Teachers, psychiatrists, clinicians and scientists were involved in the study design. Our study findings will be communicated to the schools and community with reports. Participants were not involved in the study design or planning. Our group has dedicated a hotline to help participants contact the team when necessary.

Ethics

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Xuzhou Medical University Affiliated Oriental Hospital in July 2021 (0725001). Participants gave informed consent to participate in the study before taking part.

Statistical analysis

The data were analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 22.0. Mean and SD and percentages were used for descriptive statistics. The univariate analysis involved analysis of variance (ANOVA) and partial correlation coefficients. The associations between anhedonia and categorical variables were analysed using ANOVA. The partial correlation coefficients were used to identify the relationships between anhedonia and CT. A multiple linear regression model was used for multivariate analysis, and two models were constructed to adjust confounding factors and analyse the relationship between anhedonia and CT. The moderate effect of sex on the association between anhedonia and CT was analysed using the SPSS macro programme PROCESS 3.3, which was compiled by Hayes.30

Results

Participant characteristics

Our sample comprised 1745 adolescents (937 (53.70%) boys and 808 (46.30%) girls) aged 9–17 years. The mean age of adolescents was 13.68±2.10 years. Regarding educational level, 571 (32.7%), 779 (44.7%) and 395 (22.6%) participants were primary, middle and high school students, respectively. A total of 191 (10.95%) had no siblings, whereas 1554 adolescents (89.05%) had siblings (table 1).

Scores of adolescent anhedonia, CT and depression

The mean score of anhedonia was 24.88 (SD=6.18); that of girls was 25.62 (SD=6.16) and that of boys was 24.24 (SD=6.12). The girls’ score of anhedonia was higher than that of boys (t=−4.69, p<0.01). For participants in primary (9–11 years), middle (12–14 years) and high (15–17 years) schools, the mean scores of anhedonia were 20.95 (SD=5.53), 25.00 (SD=6.00) and 27.31 (SD=5.73), respectively. The difference among the three groups was significant (χ2=96.29, p<0.01). The mean score of depression was 4.46 (SD=5.36), and 231 (13.24%) participants reported depression symptoms. The mean score of CT was 36.75 (SD=8.87), and 1272 (72.89%) participants had experienced CT (table 1).

Univariate analyses of factors associated with anhedonia in adolescents

Five factors were identified as associated with anhedonia through the ANOVA test, including those associated with anhedonia: sex, school-age stage, being a single child, presence of depression symptoms and history of CT (table 1). In controlling for depression, partial correlation analysis found that the anhedonia score was significantly correlated with the CT score (r=0.20, p<0.01).

Multivariate analysis of anhedonia and CT in adolescents

In the multiple linear regression analysis, anhedonia was the dependent variable and the five subscales of CT were the independent variables. Three variables (sex, school-age stage and being a single child) were adjusted in Model 1, and the depression variable was added in Model 2 based on Model 1. The results showed that emotional abuse, emotional neglect and physical neglect positively predicted anhedonia, whereas physical abuse negatively predicted anhedonia in Model 1 (F=59.44, p<0.001). After controlling the depression variable, β values of emotional abuse, physical abuse, emotional neglect, and physical neglect were decreased in Model 2. However, they remained statistically significant (F=58.45, p<0.001) (table 2).

Moderating effect of sex on anhedonia and CT in adolescents

Three variables, including school-age stages, being a single child and depression, were controlled in the moderating effect analysis. Before the moderating effect analysis, sex was virtualised because it is a categorical variable, and the scores of the five subscales of CT (independent variables) and anhedonia (dependent variable) were standardised. Sex had a moderating effect on the association between emotional neglect and anhedonia. Emotional neglect could positively predict anhedonia (standardised β=0.23, p<0.001), and the interaction term with sex could significantly positively predict anhedonia (standardised β=0.10, p<0.05), indicating that sex had a moderating effect on the relationship between childhood emotional neglect and adolescent anhedonia (F=78.30, p<0.001) (table 3).

Discussion

This study evaluated anhedonia and CT and explored their relationship in 1745 Chinese adolescents. This study found that independent of depression, four subscales of CT, including emotional abuse, physical abuse, emotional neglect and physical neglect, were associated with adolescents’ anhedonia.

In this study, the mean score of Chinese adolescents’ anhedonia was 24.88, which was higher than that of healthy adults (20.20).31 This finding might indicate that the hedonic capacity of adolescents is lower than that of adults. In this study, we used the SHAPS to assess anhedonia, and this scale mainly investigated the entertainment, communication, interest and other consumption aspects.16 Formal education forms a major aspect of the daily lives of adolescents. This might be the reason for the slight rise in anhedonia levels among adolescents. Additionally, the degree of anhedonia during adolescence increased with age, which is consistent with the findings of a longitudinal study.32 This finding may be due to the accumulation of chronic stress over time, which could affect adolescents’ experiences of pleasure.

We found that the mean score of CT was 36.75, and 72.89% of participants reported CT. As early life experiences, CT has been shown to be an important factor contributing to depression morbidity and mortality in adulthood.33 34 CT is common worldwide.35 In Chinese cultures, using physical discipline or punishment in parenting is normative, which is considered a form of physical abuse. The prevalence of physical abuse in Chinese is higher than in other populations.36 Mental health services and teachers should focus on what happened to those adolescents who reported CT and provide support for them.

This study found that independent of depression, three subscales of CT, including emotional abuse, emotional neglect and physical neglect, could positively affect adolescents’ anhedonia. Wang also found that high levels of emotional abuse and childhood neglect significantly affected anhedonia in young adults with depression.37 Emotional abuse, emotional neglect and physical neglect share similar response patterns in young adults and often co-occur.38 Emotional abuse and childhood neglect may be associated with adolescents’ social cognition due to exposure to negative attributions, such as negative self-worth and self-awareness, thus affecting adolescents’ pleasure experiences.32

This study found that regardless of depression, childhood physical abuse negatively affects adolescents’ anhedonia, which is consistent with Joseph’s findings.32 Physical abuse is an important risk factor for self-harm behaviours among adolescents.39 Adolescents who report physical abuse always have self-harm behaviours.39 Studies have shown that adolescents release their negative emotions through self-harm behaviour.40 Self-harm may be a way to release negative emotions learned from the physical abuse experience during childhood and relieve mental distress through physical pain, so as to affect adolescents’ pleasure experiences in daily life.

Consistent with previous studies, our study found that adolescent girls experience lower levels of pleasure than boys, and emotional neglect has a greater negative impact on girls’ experience of pleasure than boys’ experience of pleasure.24 41 These findings may be due to the differences in physical development, such as nervous system development and hormonal regulation, between boys and girls. These differences lead to the different pleasure experiences of adolescents of different sexes. Additionally, girls are more likely to use emotional expression strategies to regulate emotions.41 Therefore, emotional neglect has a greater impact on girls’ pleasure experiences.

Study limitations

First, the participants were mainly recruited from public schools, excluding private and technical schools. Further studies involving these other schools are needed to determine whether our conclusions can be extended to all adolescents. Second, the internal consistency of the CTQ (0.68) and the physical neglect subscale (0.41) was low. However, it was comparable with previous studies,26 27 which might affect our findings. Third, this study was a cross-sectional study. Therefore, the causal relationship between CT and adolescent anhedonia still needs to be verified using longitudinal studies. Fourth, this study did not collect data on personality traits and family relations, which may relate to anhedonia.42

Conclusion

Our findings revealed that independent of depression, CT had an effect on anhedonia in adolescents, and the effect increased with age. Emotional neglect had a higher positive effect on anhedonia among girls than among boys. This study is helpful for parents, teachers and psychologists to deeply understand the relationship between CT and adolescent anhedonia. Additionally, it provides a reference for the identification of adolescents with mental health risks and family education on adolescent mental health.

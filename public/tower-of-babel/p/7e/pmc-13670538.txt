Applications of telemedicine in the management of patients with hip fracture: a scoping review
Authors: Maryam Kordi, Mahnaz Samadbeik, Ali Garavand, Nasim Aslani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHip fracture is a significant global health issue, posing challenges in terms of disability, socio-economic costs, and mortality. In this context, telemedicine, as a series of remote medical information exchange tools, emerges as a promising solution. This study aimed to identify the potential applications of telemedicine in managing patients with hip fractures, offering insights that could significantly impact healthcare practices.MethodsThis scoping review was conducted in 2023 through a combined search of keywords related to telemedicine and hip fracture in Scopus, PubMed, and Web of Science without time limitation and in English. Article selection was conducted based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, including but not limited to relevance, availability, and full-text availability in English. Data gathering was done using the data extraction form, and the findings were summarized and reported based on the study’s objectives in tables and graphs.ResultsOut of 341 articles, 23 studies were included in the study. Telerehabilitation and mHealth, each accounting for 31%, emerged as the most widely used types of telemedicine in hip fracture care. The telemedicine interventions for hip fracture were classified into five categories: patient education, communication between patients and healthcare providers, fracture management, patient follow-up, and patient monitoring, further divided into 19 subcategories. Additionally, the outcomes of using telemedicine were grouped into seven categories: increasing patient motivation, improving healthcare quality, enhancing rehabilitation, facilitating patient monitoring and follow-up, improving healthcare provider performance, facilitating patient education, and reducing healthcare costs, with 19 associated subcategories.ConclusionTelemedicine services could be able to have positive outcomes for hip fracture patients, and support their rehabilitation process. It could be able to enhance patient motivation, improve healthcare quality, and facilitate remote monitoring and follow-up. Additionally, telemedicine supports patient education, reduces healthcare costs, and improves provider performance, ultimately contributing to better recovery and overall patient well-being. However, to fully realize its potential, there is a need to strengthen the infrastructure and allocate sufficient funds for its use.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-026-03581-3.

Background

Hip fracture is one of the most common and debilitating fractures, imposing a significant burden on societies due to its severity, functional dependence, socio-economic costs, and associated mortality. It represents a critical public health challenge [1, 2]. By 2050, an estimated six million hip fractures are projected to occur annually, with a substantial proportion of these occurring in Asia. This global issue predominantly affects the female population, with about 18% of women and 6% of men impacted. These statistics underscore the urgent need for effective management strategies for hip fractures [3].

The healthcare system encounters significant challenges in managing hip fractures, including prolonged hospitalization and the consequent escalation of healthcare expenditures [4]. Furthermore, the shortage of specialized healthcare professionals, inadequate access to healthcare services, and regional disparities in access to specialized care are issues that demand the focused attention of healthcare managers and health policymakers. Therefore, the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) have recommended the use of telemedicine to complement or replace in-person consultations [5].

According to the WHO, telemedicine is defined as the delivery of healthcare services by healthcare professionals, where distance plays a critical role. It utilizes information and communication technologies to facilitate the exchange of accurate information for diagnosis, treatment, prevention, research, evaluation, and education [6]. Telemedicine serves as a tool to enhance healthcare delivery by enabling both synchronous and asynchronous consultations. By leveraging information technology and remote communication, it addresses challenges related to geographical barriers, functional limitations, and access to care. Furthermore, telemedicine enhances the quality, productivity, efficiency, and effectiveness of healthcare services, ultimately reducing costs across various domains [7, 8].”

The use of information and communication technology, including Internet-based media or programs, computers, video conferencing, smartphones, and applications to provide telerehabilitation services can prevent unnecessary delays in receiving care, and the impact of shortages reduces physical therapy and rehabilitation specialists in deprived areas [9]. In 2021, Peters et al. showed in a study that telemedicine interventions can be proposed to eliminate or reduce healthcare costs by reducing hospital services. Therefore, a small to moderate reduction in the use of hospital services can be one of the surprising achievements of these interventions [10].

Therefore, researchers have conducted many studies in this field in recent years, considering the great benefits of telemedicine in solving the challenges raised in providing services for people with hip fractures. A 2019 study by Yadav et al. showed that telerehabilitation systems could empower patients to overcome physical problems and show significant physical and social activity [11]. In 2023, a study by Hofmann et al. revealed that the primary reasons for caregivers and their family members to opt for a remote rehabilitation program were the convenience it offered, the knowledge gained for home management, and the increased recovery after a fracture. The ability to perform exercises at home, a testament to the suitability and ease of telemedicine, was also a significant factor [12]. Also, a study published by Backman et al. in 2023 investigated the effectiveness of digital health interventions in hip fracture management, primarily focused on evaluating the clinical and economic outcomes of specific interventions, such as mobile applications and videoconferencing [13]. In contrast, this scoping review not only provides a detailed examination of various telemedicine interventions but also highlights the critical need for developing advanced infrastructure and innovative technologies to support their implementation. Consequently, this study complements the findings of Backman et al. by offering a broader and more holistic perspective, thereby enhancing the understanding of telemedicine applications in hip fracture management.

Despite the abundance of studies conducted on telemedicine in hip fractures, no comprehensive review has been performed to systematically examine and categorize the applications of telemedicine in this context. Our study addresses this gap by offering a novel, systematic approach that synthesizes existing evidence, identifies emerging trends, and provides actionable insights for future research and clinical practice. Therefore, this study aimed to identify the applications of telemedicine in patients with hip fractures.

Methods

This scoping review was conducted following the Joanna Briggs Institute guideline, which provides methodological guidance for conducting scoping reviews and Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [14]. According to this guideline, the study’s main participants were patients or healthcare providers who used telemedicine in any healthcare-providing center.

Search strategy and data sources

The current research is a scoping review study in which all the articles related to the subject indexed in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science databases until March 20, 2023, were retrieved and analyzed. According to the purpose of the research (to identify the applications of telemedicine in patients with hip fractures), suitable keywords were identified for searching in the databases. The search was conducted based on the combination of related keywords in the mentioned databases, without a time limit, and in English until March 20, 2023.

Table 1 shows the study search strategy. After retrieving the articles from the databases, the items with duplicate titles were removed from the study, and the remaining studies were reviewed from different aspects of the title, abstract, and full text by two authors (M.K & A.G). If the selection, quality assessment, and data extraction from the articles had disagreements, they were reviewed by another author. The found studies were reviewed and selected in three stages.

Inclusion criteria

The study selection process was conducted following the PRISMA guidelines. Articles were included based on the following criteria: Studies focusing on the application of telemedicine in managing healthcare provided to patients with hip fractures. Studies involving individuals with hip fractures or relevant stakeholders (e.g., healthcare providers and caregivers) were included. Articles must be available in full text and written in English. To ensure consistency and minimize bias, the initial screening and selection of articles were independently performed by two authors. Any discrepancies or disagreements between the two authors were resolved through discussion or consulting a third author for arbitration.

Exclusion criteria

Articles that were not related to the research objectives, or their full text was not available or were not in English, were excluded from the study. Conference articles, dissertations, books, short articles, review articles, unstructured articles, and letters to the editor were also removed.

Data extraction and data items

After article selection, we removed duplicate and unrelated articles. Then, by assessing the abstract, the articles related to the project’s main purpose were selected. In the third stage, by reviewing the full text, articles that were completely related to the inclusion criteria were selected. Data extraction was done by two authors independently using a data extraction form. The form has four parts of bibliographic information about the articles (including the title, names of the authors, year of publication, and the name and quality of the journals), methodological information of the studies (type of study, purpose of the study, location, method of sampling and data analysis), related information to the participants (beneficiaries), information related to the type of interventions (including intervention tool, kind of modality and method and goal of the intervention), information related to the comparisons made (in intervention studies) and information associated with the resulting outcomes.

Articles quality assessment

Two authors (M.K. and N.A.) independently used the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale [15] to assess the quality of the articles. If there were disagreements, another author reviewed them (A.G).

Data analysis

Thematic analysis, provided by Braun and his college, was used to analyze the data. This approach has a six-step process, including familiarization with the data, code generating, identifying themes, reviewing themes, defining themes, and producing the final report [16]. Then, the results were summarized and reported in tables and figures according to the study objectives.

Results

The results were categorized into distinct categories aligned with the study objectives, systematically synthesized, and subsequently presented in tables and figures to enhance clarity and facilitate comprehensive interpretation.

Selection of articles

Initially, 341 articles were retrieved from the databases. After removing duplicates, screening studies, and considering inclusion and exclusion criteria, 23 articles were selected for final review (Fig. 1).

Discussion

The establishment of telemedicine in the health system has caused a huge change in providing healthcare services. While telemedicine is not intended to replace traditional in-person healthcare delivery, it can function as a complementary tool and a viable alternative when access to face-to-face services is limited or unavailable [40]. According to the study results, increasing patients’ motivation to reduce drug consumption, improving pain management, and increasing patients’ acceptance and commitment to treatment among patients with hip fractures are some of the consequences of using this technology [32]. One of the other consequences of telemedicine is that it improves health care quality and rehabilitation [41]. In such a way, the study of Langford DP and colleagues has brought these results through improving the physical performance of patients in rehabilitation and improving their quality of life in general [23].

Improving patient rehabilitation and psychological support has always been one of the most important issues for providing patient services. Telemedicine can serve as an effective tool for delivering certain rehabilitation services [42]. In this study, the results showed that the Internet-based rehabilitation management system can improve patients’ physical rehabilitation and positively affect psychological rehabilitation and the prevention of complications [30]. Physicians can prescribe Telehealth as an effective method to support patient recovery following treatment and surgery. However, broader adoption of such an approach requires further interventional studies to facilitate more evidence-based decision-making. Telemedicine plays a pivotal role in improving healthcare providing [39], facilitating the access of individuals and providers to health services, and, importantly, reducing health system costs [5, 8]. According to the results, telemedicine in providing services to patients with hip fractures, especially in areas with limited access to health services, is a suitable option for health officials, offering a promising solution to the financial challenges of healthcare.

Monitoring and follow-up of patients is one of the other measures taken to improve community health and treatment [43]. Depending on the type of monitoring and follow-up, patients and treatment staff can observe the illness and recovery process. For example, in a similar study, patients were monitored and followed up using telemedicine regarding fall prevention and emotional support, counseling, and education [22]. Patients with chronic conditions need follow-up after discharge, and if there is no effective follow-up, they may sometimes face irreparable complications. Information technologies in the health system in the field are suggested for use in monitoring and follow-up services for this category of patients, ensuring the continuous care and support needed by patients with hip fractures after discharge.

Also, as mentioned in Jensen et al.‘s study, patient education, one of which is self-care management education, can involve the patient in his recovery process [19]. Information technologies, specifically telemedicine, have provided a suitable platform for patient education and have brought training during treatment and after discharge into a new phase [44]. Given the critical need for self-care education and continuous communication with healthcare providers among patients recovering from hip fractures, telemedicine offers a secure and standardized platform for delivering educational resources and facilitating information exchange.

Telemedicine for people with hip fractures has the potential to revolutionize healthcare delivery. One of its key advantages is that it provides consultation services. Pfeiffer et al. [21] demonstrated in their study that providing counseling and conducting cognitive behavioral interventions through telephone calls effectively reduced the fear of falling in patients with hip fractures. The nature of telemedicine and its ability to facilitate the exchange of information between different specialists suggests a bright future for its use in providing health services to people with hip fractures while ensuring all ethical and legal considerations are met.

Considering all the aspects mentioned in the selected studies, our study deals with the applications of telemedicine in patients with hip fractures and mentions the types of telemedicine services, including simultaneous, asynchronous, and combined, and also types of service delivery methods such as telerehabilitation, health companion has mentioned tele counseling; Therefore, the study is in line with the selected articles, but this study achieved more comprehensive findings using a systematic review approach.

In this study, despite a thorough search strategy, some relevant articles will likely be missed due to publication in other journals, indexing in other databases, and differences in terminology used to describe telemedicine and hip fracture. Also, the results only describe the current state of the published literature on telemedicine applications in hip fracture patients, and this may be subject to change due to advances in medical research. Therefore, after concluding the study, it is suggested that more studies be carried out to achieve more accurate results. Also, it suggested doing more review articles in this field to fulfill this study and categorize the latest applications of telemedicine in hip fractures. In addition, future studies could focus on conducting meta-analyses, examining specific subgroups, assessing telemedicine performance, and assessing long-term outcomes of telemedicine in hip fractures. Additionally, exploring advanced technologies such as virtual reality and regional/cultural differences is recommended. Developing evidence-based clinical guidelines for telemedicine in hip fracture management is also essential.

Conclusion

Telemedicine, which provides healthcare services in various fields, including rehabilitation, remote education and consultation, companion health, monitoring, and follow-up of patients, is developing day by day. In addition, this technology has brought an opportunity to continue and improve the education process and changing health behaviors. It can provide fairly fair access to health and treatment services in all regions. Therefore, it is considered a good alternative to providing services in person. According to the results, telemedicine services can have significant positive outcomes for hip fracture patients, and support their rehabilitation process. It could be able to enhance patient motivation, improve healthcare quality, and facilitate remote monitoring and follow-up. Additionally, telemedicine supports patient education, reduces healthcare costs, and improves provider performance, ultimately contributing to better recovery and overall patient well-being.

Experience has shown that telemedicine for hip fracture patients is considered a cost-effective and innovative approach. Therefore, good plans must be made regarding the preparation of service providers and the creation of the required infrastructure and policies to encourage the beneficiaries to use this technology for diagnosis, treatment, and care.

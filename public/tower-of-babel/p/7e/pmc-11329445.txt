Performance comparison of flip-chip blue-light microLEDs with various passivation
Authors: Yu-Hsuan Hsu, Xin-Dai Lin, Yi-Hsin Lin, Dong-Sing Wuu, Ray-Hua Horng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, arrays of μLEDs in four different sizes (5 × 5 μm2, 10 × 10 μm2, 25 × 25 μm2, 50 × 50 μm2) were fabricated using a flip-chip bonding process. Two passivation processes were investigated with one involving a single layer of SiO2 deposited using plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) and the other incorporating Al2O3 deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD) beneath the SiO2 layer. Owing to superior coverage and protection, the double-layers passivation process resulted in a three-order lower leakage current of μLEDs in the 5 μm chip-sized μLED arrays. Furthermore, higher light output power of μLEDs was observed in each chip-sized μLED array with double layers passivation. Particularly, the highest EQE value 21.9% of μLEDs array with 5 μm × 5 μm chip size was achieved with the double-layers passivation. The EQE value of μLEDs array was improved by 4.4 times by introducing the double-layers passivation as compared with that of μLEDs array with single layer passivation. Finally, more uniform light emission patterns were observed in the μLEDs with 5 μm × 5 μm chip size fabricated by double-layer passivation process using ImageJ software.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-024-04078-6.Keywords: Blue light, Micro-LED, Double layers passivation, Atomic layer deposition, Flip-chip bonding

Introduction

With the development of display technology, there is a high demand for high-resolution displays, which utilizes mini-light-emitting-diodes (mini-LED) and microLED (μLED) to be the emitters and make the new generation of displays. Recently, high-pixels-per-inch (PPI) displays, such as those found in mobile phones, smartwatches, augmented reality/virtual reality augmented reality (AR)/virtual reality (VR) devices, and wearable appliances, have attracted significant attention to use the semiconductor technology to fabricate these devices [1, 2]. To reduce device dimensions, numerous reports have explored the size effect in each red, green and blue color LEDs [3–11]. In 2023, Ryu et al. mentioned that results of blue light μLEDs presented the most obviously decay as the chip sizes decrease, which primarily resulted from higher surface recombination. However, the highest EQE in blue-light μLEDs was still obtained as the chip sizes were below 10 μm. It is due to a higher internal quantum efficiency (IQE) of blue-light epilayer structure than those of green and red light epilayer structures [12]. According to these reports, the size of μLEDs significantly influences the performance of μLEDs. One of the major issues is Shockley Read-Hall (SRH) nonradiative recombination [13–15], which is induced by sidewall damages and dangling bonds on the etching areas. In general, the inductively coupled plasma-reactive ion etching (ICP-RIE) is a typical fabrication process, especially for the lateral LED structure. Smaller LEDs were always to have higher SRH recombination due to an increased mesa-to-sidewalls ratio [8]. As a result, many researchers have proposed various methods to more efficiently repair dangling bonds, which is expected to enhance the characteristics of smaller μLED chips. Previous studies have primarily focused on achieving smoother sidewall morphology and better passivation coverage using atomic layer deposition (ALD) technology [16–18] which is a technique widely applied in semiconductors, not limited to LED devices [19–22].

Numerous reports have demonstrated that ALD can enhance the efficiency of μLED properties. In 2020, Huang et al. [18] conducted a study comparing both plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) and ALD passivation coatings on red-light μLEDs. They found that the leakage current of ALD-coated samples was up to 7.8 times lower than that of PECVD-coated samples. Additionally, the study indicated that PECVD-coated devices exhibited a stronger SRH recombination component, accounting for 14% in smaller μLEDs with 15 μm dimension compared with 9% of μLEDs with 50 μm dimension. In 2022, Lee et al. [23] reported that the external quantum efficiency (EQE) of blue-light μLEDs was enhanced by 73.4% and 66.4% after ALD passivation treatments in 5 × 5 μm2 and 10 × 10 μm2 devices, respectively. However, these studies primarily investigated the size effect and passivation process in individual vertical μLED chips, with light emitted from the top using an ITO connection to facilitate successful fabrication of tiny chip sizes. The size-effect for the μLED with flip-chip bonding process was not studied due to its much complexity in μLED fabrication process compared with p-side up μLEDs. However, the most of μLEDs applications is always using the flip-chip bonding for μLEDs applications. Furthermore, it could be induced extra issues during the flip-chip bonding process, but did not be discussed up to now. Therefore, this study investigated different chip sizes (50 μm × 50 μm, 25 μm × 25 μm, 10 μm × 10 μm, and 5 μm × 5 μm; abbreviated as Sample A, Sample B, Sample C and Sample D) of blue-light μLEDs using both PECVD and ALD passivation processes. Among high-k materials, Al2O3 thin film is an insulator, which can contribute to lower leakage current than other oxides [24]. Not only, the refractive indices of Al2O3 thin film is about 1.76, which but also can make more light emission from the device [25]. In consideration of real display applications and also obtain the enough output power, four different arrays were fabricated with pixel sizes of 1 × 1, 2 × 2, 5 × 5, and 10 × 10 as the chip sizes decreased from 50 μm × 50 μm to 5 μm × 5 μm. Subsequently, the flip-chip bonding process was executed after the fabrication of the μLED arrays. In addition, to discuss different passivation processes, the study also explores the size effect within the same total light-emitting area.

Experimental methods

In this study, the LED epilayer structure was grown on a sapphire substrate using metal–organic chemical vapor deposition. The structure consists of a 1.2-μm un-doped GaN layer, a 1.9-μm Si-doped n-type GaN layer, and a 0.8-μm Mg-doped p-type GaN layer, which includes multiple quantum wells (MQWs) with light emission occurring at a wavelength of approximately 455 nm. First, a 100-nm thick ITO layer was deposited on the p-type GaN epilayer and annealed to obtain the Ohmic contact with p-type GaN epilayer. Subsequently, the ITO was etched using an ITO etching solution to define the active region, including four chip sizes measuring 50 × 50, 25 × 25, 10 × 10, and 5 × 5 μm2. Noted that the emission areas were fixed at 2500 μm2, with array sizes of 1 × 1, 2 × 2, 5 × 5, and 10 × 10 pixels for Sample A, Sample B, Sample C and Sample D, respectively. After ITO etching, the same patterns were etched using ICP-RIE with a gas mixture of Cl2 and BCl3 at 5 mTorr to create the mesa structure. The depth of the mesa process was about 1 μm. Two passivation processes were introduced in this study. One method was to deposit a 525 nm-thick SiO2 passivation layer by PECVD and the other method was to insert a 50 nm-thick Al2O3 layer before SiO2 passivation layer using ALD. Contact holes were opened via the ICP-RIE process, followed by E-beam evaporation to deposit Ti/Al/Ti/Au (50/500/50/60 nm) for both anode and cathode pads, shown in Fig. 1a. It was worthy to mention that the depth of the mesa was almost 1 µm, if only the ALD was used to passivate the devices, the film would be too thin to cover the entire mesa area. This could result in short issues during flip-chip bonding and the device unable to function properly. On the other hand, to ensure that the bonding pads for both n and p have the same height, the n ohmic contact pad was deposited on the sidewall and connected to the p layer of one chip, shown in the red arrow of Fig. 1a.

a Structure of μLED devices, b schematic structure after the flip-chip bonding process, c OM figures of four sizes devices, and d SEM image of μLED with Ai2O3/SiO2 passivation and e SEM image of μLED with only SiO2 passivation

To measure the optical emission from the sapphire side, flip-chip bonding was performed after the fabrication of the μLED devices, polishing the sapphire substrate and dicing into chips. On the other hand, multilayers of Ti/Al/Ti/Au/Sn (50/500/50/100/1000 nm) were deposited on the other substrate to produce the bonding circuit, and the Au–Sn bonding was carried out under a 1 N bonding force at 240 °C for 1 min. After processing, the schematic structure was displayed in Fig. 1b. The top-view of the four chip sizes captured by OM is presented in Fig. 1c, and the cross-section images of the two passivation processes were also observed by SEM, as shown in Fig. 1d and e. Obviously, Al2O3 deposited by ALD can play very well conformal deposition before the SiO2 deposition shown in Fig. 1d.

The electrical characteristics of single chip and arrays of μLEDs were measured using a semiconductor parameter analyzer (Keithley 2400 Source Meter). The ideality factor and differential resistance were analyzed and calculated based on the results of current as function of voltage (I-V) for these samples. The surface morphology of μLEDs after flip-chip bonding and debonding was examined using a scanning electron microscope (SEM). Subsequently, a charge-coupled device was employed to observe the corresponding emission pattern, and light intensity profiles were analyzed using ImageJ software for the two different passivation processes. The output powers of the μLED array were measured with a calibrated integrating sphere, and the EQE was also studied in this research. All data about the light output power (also related EQE) were obtained using the average from twenty μLED array for these four types. The measured data including average value, standard deviation values of output power, peak EQE and ideality factor (n) have been tabled (see Additional file 1: Tables S1 and S2).

Results and discussion

The electrical properties of single chip of Sample A, Sample B, Sample C and Sample D were measured and presented in Fig. 2. “Single-chip” refers to having only one μLED in each of the four chip sizes. They were measured before flip chip bonding. The left and right Y-axials displayed the current on a linear scale and the differential resistance on a logarithmic scale, respectively. It was found that the I-V curves slopes of all dimension single chips with double-layers passivation were higher than those of all dimension single chips with single-layer passivation, specifically between 2.5 and 3.5 V. This suggested that the double-layers passivation was more effective in reducing defects and dangling bonds on the mesa sidewall, preventing leakage current through the sidewall paths. Moreover, the I-V characteristic of the all dimension μLEDs with double-layers passivation closely resembled the ideal diode’s turn-on behavior, characterized by a steep curve at turn-on voltage. A more vertical shape in the I-V curves were achieved through a double-layer passivation process, even in sample C and sample D. These results indicated that the passivation layer deposited by the ALD process can effectively improve repairing efficiency and enhance diode turn-on behavior. A steeper slope in the I-V curve after the turn-on voltage, essentially signified a lower equivalent resistance. Consequently, at 3.5 V, the dynamic resistances were lower in all μLEDs with double-layers passivation than those in μLEDs with single layer passivation. As the chip sizes decreased from 50 μm × 50 μm to 5 μm × 5 μm, the dynamic resistance at 3.5 V for each μLED fabricated with a double-layer passivation process were 39.2 Ω, 112.7 Ω, 740.3 Ω, and 1750.6 Ω, respectively. At the same chip sizes and forward voltage, the dynamic resistances were 131.7 Ω, 192.1 Ω, 1189.8 Ω, and 6254.2 Ω for μLEDs with the single-layer passivation. Furthermore, the premature turn-on phenomenon is clearly observed in the single-passivation process from the differential resistance plots, particularly in the smaller chip sizes of Sample C and Sample D. They can be demonstrated by the differential resistances of μLEDs with single passivation layer were obviously lower than those of μLEDs with double passivation layers before diodes turned on.

After flip-chip bonding, the output powers of μLEDs with two passivation processes were shown in Fig. 4. The output power decreased as the chip sizes shrink, and this phenomenon occurred in both of the two different passivation processes. As the chip sizes with double-layers passivation decrease from 50 μm to 5 μm, the output power drops from 7.37 to 6.18 mW. Correspondingly, the output power of 50 μm to 5 μm significantly declined from 7.27 to 3.54 mW, as the μLEDs with single-layer passivation. It could be resulted that the reflection from the electrode metal for the μLED with smaller size could be lower than those of the μLED with larger size due to more sidewall exposed and sidewall being not covered by metal, shown as the inset of Fig. 4a. Compared with single-layer passivation, the μLEDs fabricated with double-layer passivation enhanced the light output power in the same chip sizes, especially in the tiny-sized Sample D. This improvement can be attributed to the difference in refractive index between Al2O3 and SiO2. The refractive index of Al2O3 and SiO2 are 1.7 and 1.45, respectively. Because the refractive index of GaN is about 2.6. The Al2O3 and SiO2 two layers can benefit to the light emission from GaN structure and inhibited total reflective behavior that occurs within the device [28–30]. The simulation data of transmittance and reflectance between the two passivation processes are shown in Fig. 4e, with the schematic also presented in Fig. 4f. An extremely high transmittance was obtained in the double-layer passivation between blue-light wavelengths, additionally. It exhibited lower reflectance of double-layer passivation than that of single-layer passivation. Overall, the double-layer passivation enhanced light emission compared to using only a SiO2 passivation layer. As a result, the output power increased in all chip sizes of μLEDs when an Al2O3 passivation layer is inserted via the ALD process before SiO2 deposition. Meanwhile, the ALD process typically employs two distinct chemical precursors. These are introduced into the reaction chamber sequentially to initiate two half-cycle reactions, with all chemical reactions occurring exclusively at the surface through chemisorption. These half-cycle reactions, constituting an ALD cycle, facilitate the deposition of a monolayer film, which can be iterated layer by layer to grow the film. ALD modification of interfaces entails the filling of atomic vacancies and the selective solidification of dangling bonds [16, 31]. This characteristic enables Al2O3 presented conformal deposition, uniformity and also can repair the dangling bond more efficiently. Additionally, this design not only addresses refractive index differences but also repairs defects more efficiently in smaller chip sizes [18, 28, 32]. Figure 4a and b supported this point, as the output power (6 mW) of Sample D with double-layers passivation at 20 mA injection current exhibited almost two times higher value (3 mW) than that with single-layer passivation. Furthermore, the micro-structure of the μLED(5) with a single passivation process after debonding from the flip chip process was observed by SEM shown in Fig. 4c. After flip-chip bonding, the single passivation layer was easily damaged and cracked due to the bonding force during the bonding process. A significantly high leakage current can be observed from the I-V characteristic after the flip-chip bonding process, as shown in Fig. 4d. Among these four chip-sized μLEDs fabricated with single-layer passivation, the leakage currents of Sample A to Sample C at − 5 V were almost contained at 0.1 nA. Only Sample D with single layer passivation presented three orders higher leakage current at − 5 V. Moreover, the highest leakage current in the forward region also reaches 0.1 mA at a forward voltage of 0.5 V, indicating a serious leakage issue in the Sample D. This is the reason that light output power reduced significantly as Sample D with single-layer passivation. The I-V curve of Sample D with double-layers passivation after flip-chip bonding is also plotted in Fig. 4d, represented by the green line. A lower leakage current at − 5 V and better turn-on behavior are obtained, resulting in higher light output power compared to single-layer passivation. Moreover, it can be observed that there are no cracks in the Sample D by double-layer passivation sample in the inset of Fig. 4a.

The EQE values of μLEDs for the two passivation processes were calculated and plotted as functions of the input current in Fig. 5a and b, respectively. Obviously, there existed the size effect for both kind of μLEDs. Even though, the highest EQE value, 33.2%, was achieved in Sample A using the double-layers passivation. In comparison with the same chip sizes Sample A with a single-layer passivation process, the maximum EQE value of 31.1% was obtained shown in Fig. 5b. Therefore, the Sample A with double-layers passivation outperformed the Sample A with single-layer passivation by 2%. Considering the uncertainties in data brought by fabrication, bonding procedure and measurement, the error bars of light output power at 20 mA and peak EQE for all μLEDs were both calculated and plotted in the inset of Fig. 5b. Overall, the double-layer passivation process can enhance the properties of each chip size μLED. While the size effect still affects μLEDs with both passivation processes, the study resolved the flip-chip bonding of sample D by introducing the ALD passivation process. As shown in Fig. 5a, the highest EQE value of 21.9% was achieved at an injection current of 0.9 mA in Sample D. In contrast, the substantial leakage current in the single-layer passivation process affected the optical performance in Sample D in the initial injection region. The EQE of Sample D with a single-layer passivation could not be detected by the integrating sphere, and as the input current increased, the EQE slowly rose from 2 mA and then stabilized at around 5% until 20 mA. Because the chip is very tiny and the dimension of contact metal was only 3 um. It means that the distance of metal contact to side wall was only 1 um. During the bonding, the chips suffer high pressure and metal was also melt to bonding to the circuit. Only one passivation, if this layer crack during the flip chip process, it resulted in leakage current being too large to operate well. The maximum of EQE values between the two passivation processes in sample D devices exhibited a 15% difference. This result further demonstrated the crucial role played by Al2O3 deposited via ALD in the μLED flip-chip bonding process, especially in Sample D. In addition to its excellent repair efficiency and anti-reflective properties, the double-layers passivation process also performed a strong passivation.

Conclusion

In this study, the blue-light μLED chip with various sizes was successfully fabricated, including 5 × 5 μm2, 10 × 10 μm2, 25 × 25 μm2, and 50 × 50 μm2. The study anticipates the occurrence of sidewall damage as chip sizes decrease to the microscale level. Furthermore, the research demonstrates that the passivation layer prepared by the ALD process can enhance the electrical characteristics of μLED, particularly in small chip sizes. Improved turn-on behavior and lower differential resistance at 3.5 V were achieved. The ideality factors increase as chip sizes shrink from 50 to 5 μm due to higher resistance and numerous defects in smaller devices. However, lower values were observed in all four chip sizes with double passivation layers. The Al2O3 layer, with a higher refractive index than the SiO2 layer, resulted in a 75% enhancement in light output power in 5 μm chip size arrays at 20 mA injection current. Simultaneously, the three-order lower leakage current of 5 μm chip size arrays at − 5 V indicated more effective defect repair in double-layer passivation. An SEM examination of the single-layer passivation sample of sample D after the flip-chip bonding process revealed serious cracks and damage along the device sidewall. This damage led to lower emission intensity in smaller chip size arrays. Consequently, higher EQE values were exhibited in μLEDs with double-layers passivation for all four chip sizes. In sample A and sample D, the EQE was improved by 2% and 15%, respectively, through the insertion of the Al2O3 passivation layer beneath the SiO2 passivation layer. Consequently, the emission intensity of Sample D with double-layers passivation demonstrated higher intensity and more uniform patterns than those of Sample D with single-layer passivation in this study.

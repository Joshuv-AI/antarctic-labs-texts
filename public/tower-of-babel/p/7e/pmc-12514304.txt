Internet of things enabled indoor activity monitoring for visually impaired people with hybrid deep learning and optimized algorithms for enhanced safety
Authors: Mesfer Al Duhayyim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIndoor activity monitoring methods promise the wellbeing and security of elderly and visually challenging individuals living in their homes. These methods use numerous technologies and sensors to monitor daily actions, namely movement, sleep patterns, and medication compliance, presenting valued opinions of the consumer’s daily life and complete health. The accuracy and adaptability of the deep learning (DL) model make human activity recognition (HAR) a critical device to improve effectiveness, security, and modified understandings in indoor areas. Using DL techniques, HAR transforms indoor monitoring by permitting particular detection and experience of human actions. DL techniques automatically remove and learn discriminating characteristics, making them suitable for identifying composite human activities in sensor data. Nevertheless, selecting the appropriate DL architecture and enhancing its parameters was important for superior solutions. This study presents a novel Indoor Activity Monitoring for Visually Impaired People with Hybrid Deep Learning for Enhanced Safety (IAMVIP-HDLES) methodology for IoT environments. The IAMVIP-HDLES methodology is designed to monitor and recognize indoor activities of visually impaired people in a real-time environment. The IAMVIP-HDLES approach primarily performs Z-score normalization as a data pre-processing technique for standardizing the input data, guaranteeing uniformity, and improving the system’s performance. For feature selection, the Osprey–Cauchy-sparrow search algorithm (OCSSA) is employed to identify the most appropriate features from the raw data. In addition, the hybrid method, which is comprised of a convolutional neural network, bidirectional long short-term memory, and attention mechanisms (CNN-BiLSTM-Attention), is used for monitoring indoor activities. Finally, the hyperparameter selection process based on the improved whale optimization algorithm (IWOA) is performed to enhance the classification results of the CNN-BiLSTM-Attention method. Experimental studies are performed, and the results are inspected using the UCI-HAR dataset. The comparative analysis of the IAMVIP-HDLES method specified a superior accuracy value of 96.76% over existing techniques.Keywords: Indoor activity, Visually impaired people, Osprey–Cauchy-sparrow search algorithm, Human activity recognition, Deep learning, IoTSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Related works

Pydala et al.11 present an IoT-based Assistive Technology Method for Enhanced Navigation Support for the Visually Challenged. For major atmospheric sensing, a handstick is used by the implant sensors, and a visual capturing and transmission element is used to handle visual data. The method uses the R-CNN on the cloud server, Logistic Regression (LR) with Iterative Learning, and Mobinet CV techniques. Leong and Ramasamy12 developed revolutionary wearable devices containing a camera component, a Raspberry Pi, and a pre-trained convolutional neural network (CNN), each combined into both smart glasses. It is intended to identify objects and approximate their spaces from the wearer, giving real-world haptic or auditory responses. The expansion procedure is demanding, including utilizing ML methods for object recognition and combining sensor and camera technologies in a lightweight, user-friendly setting. Sharma et al.13 implemented the major details of the IPS and its developing paths. The growth from traditional to DL is proposed, tackling different challenges in traditional IPS: improper accuracy, poor localization, signal instability due to fading, and non-line-of-sight problems. Numerous progressive positioning technologies were discovered, including AI, sensor fusion, and hybrid. YOLO-v5 is trained on a traditional dataset of fifteen objects and the MS COCO 2017 dataset of eighty objects. The output labels are converted to text and then transformed into an audio setup for visually challenged people over the speaker. Hong et al.15 implemented a multitasking visual navigation approach for visually impaired persons with an IoT-based camera. A lightweight method is considered that accepts multitasking learning structures to carry out path detection and scene classification tasks at the same time. The method additionally presented dual components, for example, a lightweight vision transformer (ViT) block and (LWVIT block), an enhanced inverted residuals (EIRs) block, and to efficiently merge the belongings of ViT and CNNs models. The blind stick combines a Raspberry Pi 4 as its central processing unit (CPU), followed by ultrasonic sensors, a GSM module, a GPS module, and a soil moisture sensor. The approach utilizes gaussian filter (GF) for image preprocessing, feature extraction through spatial–temporal interest points (STIP), and feature selection via gray level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) to optimize input for a deep CNN (DCNN). Despite crucial improvements in assistive technologies and activity recognition using ML and DL, various limitations still exist. Many systems encounter threats related to high computational costs, privacy concerns, and limited real-time adaptability, particularly in heterogeneous IoT environments. Existing models depend heavily on large labelled datasets, limiting scalability and generalization across diverse users and conditions. Communication overhead and energy consumption remain critical issues in FL-based outcomes. Moreover, integrating multimodal sensor data is often insufficiently explored, affecting accuracy and robustness. The research gap is developing lightweight, privacy-preserving, and efficient models that balance performance with resource constraints while addressing data heterogeneity and communication challenges.

The proposed methods

This paper presents a new IAMVIP-HDLES methodology for an IoT environment. The methodology is designed to monitor and recognize the indoor activities of visually impaired people in real-time. It involves distinct stages, such as data normalization, dimensionality reduction using OCSSA, indoor activities classification using CNN-BiLSTM-Attention, and IWOA-based hyperparameter selection. Stage I: data normalization

Primarily, the IAMVIP-HDLES approach performs Z-score normalization as a data pre-processing technique for standardizing the input data, guaranteeing uniformity, and enhancing the model’s performance39. This is chosen because it can standardize features by centring them around the mean with a unit standard deviation, which assists in handling data with diverse scales and distributions. Unlike min–max scaling, which compresses data within a fixed range, Z-score normalization preserves the shape of the original distribution, making it more robust to outliers. This method enhances the stability and convergence speed of DL models by ensuring that all input features contribute equally during training. Furthermore, it is simple to implement and computationally efficient, making it appropriate for real-time IoT applications. Z-score normalization improves model performance and generalization compared to other normalization techniques.

Z-score normalization is an approach applied to normalize data by translating it into a distribution using a standard deviation of 1 and a mean of 0. Indoor Activity Monitoring for VIP guarantees that sensor data from different resources (IoT devices, accelerometers, or gyroscopes) are equivalent by eliminating some bias because of changes in units or scales. This pre-processing stage aids in enhancing the performance of ML techniques by ensuring that each of the features corresponds to the activity method for recognition. By normalizing the data, the technique can more efficiently classify and detect indoor activities, guaranteeing superior reliability and accuracy. It is mainly significant in surroundings with fluctuating or noisy data, as it improves the sturdiness of the activity monitoring method.

Stage II: dimensionality reduction

For feature selection, the OCSSA is employed to classify the most appropriate features from the raw data40. This method is chosen for its robust global search capability and balance between exploration and exploitation. By integrating the Cauchy distribution, OCSSA improves the search process to escape local optima more effectively than conventional methods such as genetic algorithms (GAs) or PSO. It selects the most relevant features while discarding redundant or noisy ones, which enhances model accuracy and mitigates training time. This optimization results in a more lightweight and efficient model for real-time processing. Compared to other techniques, OCSSA presents better convergence speed, higher precision in feature selection, and robustness across diverse datasets.

SSA is an optimization model derived from the sparrow’s foraging behaviour presented. This model mimics dual dissimilar categories of sparrows: followers and leaders. If the population meets hunters, some folks convey danger signals via sound to guarantee that the populace can quickly prevent hazards. This warning sparrow is specified as a vigilant individual. These combined features make SSA a bio-inspired iterative optimizer approach, which is efficiently used for optimizer problems. Nevertheless, it must be recognized that the model might drop into local bests throughout the convergence procedure because of a reduction in the diversity of the population, which is measured as one of its major faults. To enhance the global searching ability of the Sparrow method, this study presents tent chaotic mapping, the Osprey optimizer algorithm (OOA), and Gaussian mutation to strengthen it.

One observed feature of the SSA is the protection of the arbitrary distribution of features in the initialization stage. In this stage, the locations and behaviours of sparrow individuals are inspired by randomness. Nevertheless, this randomness might result in a rough distribution of the sparrow population within the searching area, which can influence the following convergence speed of the model. The first uneven distribution may lead to several places being overly explored; others are less explored. It shows a higher dependency on first values, meaning dual close primary values would produce arbitrary sequences with changed numbers. Conclusion

This study presents a novel IAMVIP-HDLES methodology for an IoT environment. The presented IAMVIP-HDLES methodology is designed to monitor and recognize indoor activities of visually impaired people in a real-time environment. The IAMVIP-HDLES approach primarily performs Z-score normalization as a data pre-processing technique for standardizing the input data, guaranteeing uniformity, and enhancing the model’s performance. For feature selection, the OCSSA is employed to identify the most relevant features from the raw data. In addition, the hybrid model comprising a CNN-BiLSTM-Attention is used to monitor indoor activities. Finally, the hyperparameter selection process based on the IWOA is performed to optimize the classification results of the CNN-BiLSTM-Attention method. Experimental studies are performed, and the results are inspected using the UCI-HAR dataset. The comparative analysis of the IAMVIP-HDLES method specified a superior accuracy value of 96.76% over existing techniques. The limitations of the IAMVIP-HDLES method comprise challenges related to limited scalability when deployed in larger, more diverse environments, potentially affecting generalizability. The reliance on specific sensor types may mitigate adaptability to diverse hardware configurations. Additionally, real-time processing demands can strain computational resources, particularly in low-power devices. Environmental factors such as lighting or occlusion may also affect the model’s performance. Addressing these limitations involves exploring more flexible sensor integration, optimizing algorithms for resource-constrained devices, and improving robustness against varied real-world conditions to improve overall efficiency and applicability.

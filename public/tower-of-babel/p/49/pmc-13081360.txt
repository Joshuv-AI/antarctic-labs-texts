Effectiveness of toothbrushing as a replacement for chlorhexidine in oral care, combined with head elevation for the prevention of ventilator-associated pneumonia in intensive care units: interrupted time-series analysis
Authors: Eunbyeol Jo, Seongman Bae, Seung Hee Ryu, Soyeon Park, Miseo Kim, Ja Young Kim, Hyunju Lee, Subeen Moon, Jeeyoon Kim, Jiwon Jung, Yong Pil Chong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study evaluated the effectiveness of replacing chlorhexidine-based oral care with toothbrushing and reinforcing head-of-bed elevation (HOBE) for the prevention of ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in real-world practice using an interrupted time-series (ITS) analysis.MethodsThis quasi-experimental study was conducted in eight adult intensive care units (ICUs) of a 2,734-bed tertiary care hospital in Seoul, South Korea. The interventions, implemented in May 2023, included replacing chlorhexidine-based oral care with toothbrushing and strengthening HOBE adherence. ITS analysis was performed to assess the impact of the intervention on monthly VAP incidence rates from August 2022 through November 2024.ResultsAdherence to HOBE increased significantly from 84 to 95% after the intervention (P < 0.001). During the study period, 144 VAP cases occurred: 66 cases during 16,849 device-days in the pre-intervention period (3.9 per 1,000 device-days) and 78 cases during 32,185 device-days in the post-intervention period (2.4 per 1,000 device-days). ITS analysis demonstrated a significant decrease in both the level and trend of VAP incidence following the intervention (level change coefficient: -1.66, 95% confidence interval [CI]: -3.07 to -0.25, P = 0.029; trend change coefficient: -0.25, 95% CI: -0.45 to -0.06, P = 0.015). Sensitivity analyses using alternative transition windows and negative binomial models showed directionally consistent post-intervention declines, whereas estimates were attenuated and not statistically significant in the fully covariate-adjusted ITS model.ConclusionsImplementation of toothbrushing-based oral care combined with reinforced HOBE was followed by lower VAP incidence in the primary ITS analysis, supporting the feasibility of these aspiration-prevention measures in routine ICU practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13054-026-05936-8.Keywords: Ventilator-associated pneumonia, Prevention, Toothbrushing, Chlorhexidine, Head-of-bed elevation

Introduction

Ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) primarily results from the aspiration of secretions containing microorganisms from the oropharynx or stomach into the lungs [1, 2]. Preventing aspiration is therefore a key strategy for reducing the incidence of VAP [3]. Various preventive measures have been recommended. Although earlier guidelines advocated the use of chlorhexidine for oral care as a specific preventative measure [4], the 2022 SHEA guideline recommends replacing chlorhexidine with toothbrushing for oral care [3]. This change was based on evidence from analyses limited to double-blinded studies, which showed unclear preventative effects of chlorhexidine on VAP [5] and, in some cases, an associated increase in mortality [5–7]. Consequently, since May 2023, our institution has revised its VAP prevention protocol by replacing chlorhexidine-based oral care with toothbrushing and reinforcing head-of-bed elevation (HOBE) practices. This study analyzed the impact of these aspiration prevention measures—HOBE and enhanced oral care—on VAP rates in adult intensive care units (ICUs).

Methods

Setting and intervention

This was a quasi-experimental study in the adult ICUs of a 2,734-bed tertiary care hospital in Seoul, South Korea. Ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) cases were prospectively identified through an active, real-time infection surveillance system using National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) criteria by trained infection control practitioners, and feedback was provided to each ICU on a monthly basis. The intervention effect was evaluated retrospectively using an interrupted time-series analysis of prospectively collected data. ICU patients underwent pneumonia assessment within one month following chest radiography (CXR). Classification as VAP or non-VAP was based on mechanical ventilation status at the time of pneumonia onset.

A total of eight ICUs were included: two medical ICUs, two surgical ICUs, a cardiovascular surgery ICU, a cardiac ICU, a neurological ICU, and a neurosurgery ICU. All patients admitted to these ICUs during the study period were included in the analysis. Before the intervention, oral care was performed three times daily by wiping the oral cavity with gauze soaked in 0.12% chlorhexidine solution. Enhanced preventative measures were implemented beginning in May 2023. The first intervention involved replacing chlorhexidine-based oral care with toothbrushing. Toothbrushing was performed manually by nursing staff at least twice daily. A standardized protocol was developed, requiring two minutes of brushing the teeth, artificial airways, tongue, and palate with a silicone toothbrush moistened with saline or sterile water, excluding patients with contraindications such as bleeding risk. Monitoring identified areas frequently missed during brushing, prompting additional training sessions using dental models and colored toothpaste to confirm plaque removal. The second intervention focused on revising and reinforcing HOBE indications, along with strengthened monitoring and on-site feedback. During clinical procedures requiring a temporary supine position, such as repositioning, oropharyngeal suctioning was performed before lowering the head of the bed, and staff were trained to promptly restore HOBE to the appropriate angle afterward.

Outcome and covariate measures

The primary outcome was the monthly incidence rate of VAP. Monthly VAP incidence rates were calculated using ventilator-days as the denominator. Secondary outcomes included monthly ICU length of stay and ICU mortality. ICU length of stay was defined as the average number of ICU days per admission for each month, and ICU mortality was defined as the proportion of ICU admissions resulting in death during the same month.

Time-varying covariates were included to account for temporal changes in patient severity, case-mix, healthcare utilization, and infection prevention practices over time. Monthly measures of antibiotic use were quantified as days of therapy per 100 patient-days for vancomycin, carbapenem, and piperacillin-tazobactam. Patient demographics and clinical characteristics included monthly mean age, sex distribution, the proportion of admissions through the emergency department, the proportion of patients undergoing surgery, and the mean duration of mechanical ventilation days per patient. Patient severity was assessed using the monthly mean APACHE II score. COVID-19 burden was defined as the monthly proportion of ICU patients diagnosed with COVID-19. Hand hygiene compliance was measured as the monthly hand hygiene adherence rate among healthcare workers in the enrolled ICUs.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for normality. For normally distributed variables, differences between groups were analyzed using the independent t-test; for non-normally distributed variables, the Mann–Whitney U test was used. Categorical variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Interrupted time-series (ITS) analysis was conducted to evaluate the impact of the intervention on monthly VAP incidence rates from August 2022 through November 2024. The enhanced preventive measures were implemented in May 2023, and a two-month window was applied to allow for the policy transition. The two-month transition window was prespecified a priori based on implementation considerations, including staff education, protocol dissemination, and stabilization of adherence to the revised oral care and head-of-bed elevation practices. Accordingly, the primary analysis defined July 2023 as the intervention point, adjusting for potential changes in outcome during the window. The pre-intervention period was from August 2022 to April 2023, and the post-intervention period was from July 2023 to November 2024. The primary ITS analysis consisted of a segmented regression model was fitted using ordinary least squares (OLS) estimation, with time, intervention status, and time after intervention as explanatory variables, to estimate level and trend changes. Given the limited number of monthly time points, the primary model was kept parsimonious to maintain precision. To account for potential autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity in the residuals, Newey–West heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) standard errors were applied with a three-month lag. Model coefficients were presented as estimates with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and p-values.

To assess the robustness of the primary findings, sensitivity analyses were performed by varying the transition window length to one and three months. As additional sensitivity analyses to explore potential time-varying confounding, covariate-adjusted ITS models incorporating monthly measures of patient characteristics, patient severity, COVID-19 burden, hand hygiene compliance, and antibiotic use were fitted. To evaluate potential circularity, we repeated the covariate-adjusted model after excluding antibiotic utilization variables. To identify covariates contributing to attenuation of the estimated intervention effect, we also fitted models in which each time-varying covariate was added individually to the base ITS model. Finally, to account for variation in time at risk, monthly VAP counts were analyzed using negative binomial regression with an offset for monthly ventilator-days. The relative change (%) in VAP incidence following the intervention was calculated as the percentage difference between the observed cumulative post-intervention rate and the counterfactual estimate projected under continuation of the pre-intervention trend. The 95% CIs for the relative change were obtained using a parametric bootstrap procedure with 5,000 simulations, drawing coefficient vectors from a multivariate normal distribution defined by the model’s estimated coefficients and variance–covariance matrix.

To further characterize temporal changes in clinical outcomes and diagnostic indicators during the study period, we performed additional interrupted time-series analyses of monthly ICU length of stay, ICU mortality, and respiratory culture positivity rates, using the same modeling framework as the primary analysis. All analyses were conducted in R (version 4.3.0) using the lm, sandwich, lmtest, mvtnorm, Matrix, and tidyverse packages.

Results

The demographic characteristics and the length of ICU stays of patients during the two study periods are summarized in Table 1. The post-intervention cohort was slightly older and had a lower proportion of males than the pre-intervention cohort, although these differences were not clinically meaningful. During the post-intervention period, the ICU admission rate via the emergency department was higher (mean, 18% vs. 22%; P = 0.025), whereas the proportion of patients who underwent surgery during the ICU stay was lower (mean, 74% vs. 70%; P = 0.032), and the proportion of patients who received any antibiotics was higher (mean, 84% vs. 88%; P = 0.029). However, there were no significant differences in the use of piperacillin–tazobactam, carbapenems, or vancomycin between the two periods. The mean ICU stay was significantly longer in the post-intervention period (5.9 ± 10.7 days vs. 6.2 ± 12.8 days, P < 0.001), whereas ICU mortality was significantly lower (5.9% vs. 5.2%, P = 0.048). Hand hygiene compliance did not differ significantly between the two periods (95.1% vs. 96.5%, P = 0.292), while adherence to HOBE increased significantly from 84 to 95% after the intervention (P < 0.001). Although the distribution of patients across clinical departments differed statistically between the two periods, the difference was not considered clinically meaningful. During the study period, a total of 144 VAP cases occurred: 66 cases during 16,849 device-days in the pre-intervention period (3.9 per 1,000 device-days) and 78 cases during 32,185 device-days in the post-intervention period (2.4 per 1,000 device-days).

Figure 1 shows the monthly changes in VAP incidence rates pre- and post-intervention. Time-series analysis demonstrated a significant decrease in both the level and trend of VAP following the intervention (level change coefficient: -1.66, 95% CI: -3.07 to -0.25, P = 0.029; trend change coefficient: -0.25, 95% CI: -0.45 to -0.06, P = 0.015) (Table 2). Sensitivity analyses using alternative transition windows yielded similar results, with a consistent post-intervention decline in VAP incidence across window definitions. In the fully covariate-adjusted interrupted time-series model, the estimated intervention effects were attenuated and were not statistically significant (Table 2). Excluding antibiotic utilization from the covariate-adjusted model did not materially change the estimated intervention effects (Supplementary Table 1). In additional interrupted time-series models in which each covariate was added individually to the base model, attenuation was most apparent after adjustment for mean APACHE II score, whereas adjustment for other covariates did not materially alter the magnitude or direction of the intervention coefficients (Supplementary Table 2). In addition, negative binomial interrupted time-series analyses with an offset for monthly ventilator-days showed level and trend changes that were directionally consistent with the primary analysis, with post-intervention VAP incidence remaining lower than the counterfactual projection (Supplementary Table 3). The monthly trends of covariates included in the sensitivity analysis are presented in Supplementary Fig. 2.

In interrupted time-series analyses, neither ICU length of stay nor ICU mortality showed a significant immediate level change following the intervention; however, both outcomes demonstrated significant increasing trends in the post-intervention period (Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 4). In the analysis of respiratory culture positivity, an immediate increase in level was observed at the intervention point, followed by a significant downward trend during the post-intervention period (Supplementary Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 5).

Discussion

This study compared the incidence of VAP before and after the implementation of a new guideline that replaced chlorhexidine-based oral care with toothbrushing-based oral care and reinforced adherence to HOBE in real-world clinical practice. In the primary ITS analysis, VAP incidence decreased after implementation; however, effect estimates were attenuated and were not statistically significant in the fully covariate-adjusted model, indicating uncertainty regarding the magnitude of the association.

A meta-analysis evaluating the impact of toothbrushing on VAP found that it was associated with reductions in VAP incidence, ICU mortality, duration of mechanical ventilation, and ICU length of stay [8]. However, most previous studies assessed toothbrushing in addition to chlorhexidine-based oral care [9–11]. Evidence on the preventive effect of toothbrushing performed with saline or sterile water as a replacement for chlorhexidine remains limited, and such studies have generally included small sample sizes [12–14]. Toothbrushing may provide a mechanical benefit by more effectively removing dental plaque and biofilm than antiseptic rinsing [15, 16]. This mechanical disruption can reduce the microbial burden in the oropharynx, potentially lowering the risk of VAP even when microaspiration occurs. Therefore, the beneficial effect observed in this study might be primarily attributable to the mechanical removal of biofilm rather than the antiseptic properties of chlorhexidine.

In our study, ICU length of stay increased while ICU mortality decreased after the intervention. In secondary ITS analyses, neither outcome showed a significant immediate level change at the intervention point; however, both ICU length of stay and ICU mortality demonstrated significant increasing trends during the post-intervention period. Taken together, these findings do not suggest a clear improvement in these secondary outcomes attributable to the intervention, but they also do not indicate an abrupt worsening immediately after implementation. Replacing chlorhexidine-based oral care with toothbrushing-based oral care did not result in increased mortality, suggesting, at a minimum, that this intervention is safe with respect to patient survival. This finding is consistent with previous studies [5–7] reporting an association between chlorhexidine use and increased mortality and supports the consideration of toothbrushing as a safer alternative for oral care in mechanically ventilated patients. However, because this was a unit-level quasi-experimental study, causal inferences regarding ICU length of stay or mortality are limited, and residual confounding by evolving case mix and severity may remain.

HOBE has been suggested as an effective strategy for reducing VAP incidence, and current guidelines recommend its routine implementation [3]. However, prior studies evaluating HOBE have been rated as providing weak evidence due to a high risk of bias [17]. In our study, adherence to HOBE increased following the intervention, and we observed a concomitant reduction in VAP incidence after implementing both HOBE and toothbrushing-based oral care protocols. Because the two interventions were implemented simultaneously, the observed reduction in VAP cannot be attributed solely to HOBE. Nonetheless, our findings support its feasibility and potential benefit as part of a multifaceted approach to VAP prevention in real-world clinical practice. Controlled, single-intervention studies are needed to isolate and quantify HOBE’s specific contribution to VAP reduction.

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted at a single center using a quasi-experimental design, which may have introduced selection bias and confounding due to differences in patient characteristics and clinical practices between the pre- and post-intervention periods. In addition, secular trends over time may have influenced VAP incidence independently of the intervention. Although we adjusted for multiple patient-related factors in sensitivity analyses, residual confounding from unmeasured variables may remain because this was not a randomized controlled trial. Second, the limited number of monthly time points may have reduced statistical precision in models incorporating multiple time-varying covariates, contributing to wider confidence intervals in fully adjusted analyses. Third, the diagnosis of VAP is potentially subjective and susceptible to interobserver variability. Fourth, because multiple interventions were implemented simultaneously, it was not possible to determine the independent effect of each intervention on the observed outcomes. Finally, this unit-level time-series analysis could not explicitly account for potential survivor bias or competing risks related to ICU mortality and discharge, although sensitivity analyses using ventilator-days to approximate time at risk were performed.

In conclusion, implementing toothbrushing-based oral care alongside enhanced HOBE adherence in ventilated adult ICU patients was followed by a decrease in VAP incidence in the primary ITS analysis, with consistent direction across transition-window sensitivity analyses. However, the association was attenuated and not statistically significant in the fully covariate-adjusted model, indicating uncertainty regarding the magnitude of effect. Importantly, we did not observe a signal of harm in VAP incidence after discontinuation of chlorhexidine-based oral care. These results support the feasibility of incorporating structured toothbrushing protocols and consistent HOBE into ventilator care bundles, while highlighting the need for larger studies with stronger control of time-varying confounding to better quantify effectiveness across diverse ICU populations.

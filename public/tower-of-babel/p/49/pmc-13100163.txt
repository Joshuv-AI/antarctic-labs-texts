X-ray crystal structure and in silico investigation of Tetrabromo thia-bridged diquinoline including anticancer mechanisms via target prediction and molecular docking
Authors: Solhe F Alshahateet, Ghassab M Al-Mazaideh, Salah A Al-Trawneh, Samir A Al-Taweel, Shehdeh Jodeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present study integrated supramolecular analysis with a range of in silico approaches, including crystal structure determination, target prediction, ADMET (absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion) profiling, network pharmacology, and molecular docking to investigate the anticancer potential of compound 3 (tetrabromothia-bridged diquinoline). In the solid state, Compound 3 was found to incorporate p-xylene, yielding a stable inclusion complex [(3)2. (p-xylene)], the structure of which was elucidated and interpreted through the lenses of crystal engineering and supramolecular chemistry. Moreover, compound 3 exhibited favourable drug-likeness and strong predicted binding affinity for EGFR and ERBB2 (HER2), suggesting that modulation of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, along with ERBB signalling pathways, may underpin its potential anticancer activity.Keywords: Host-guest, Inclusion compound, Non-covalent interactions, Supramolecular chemistry, ADMET profiling, Molecular docking, EGFR, HER2, ERBB signalling, Tyrosine kinase inhibitorsSubject terms: Biochemistry, Cancer, Chemical biology, Chemistry, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Drug discovery

Introduction

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), also known as ErbB2, is a member of the ErbB receptor tyrosine kinase family and plays a significant role in regulating cell growth and intracellular signalling pathways. As part of the epithelial growth factor receptor (EGFR) family that includes EGFR, ErbB3, and ErbB4, ErbB2 is unique in that no ligand has been identified for it. This absence of a known ligand contributes to its distinct binding behaviour and enhanced basal activity, ultimately increasing its involvement in tumour progression1. Its constitutively open conformation makes HER2 a preferred heterodimerization partner of other ErbB receptors, amplifying signalling and promoting oncogenic traits once ErbB2 reaches a critical threshold2–8.

The oncogenic significance of HER2 is evident across several malignancies, particularly in breast cancer, where HER2 overexpression correlates with aggressive disease and poor clinical outcomes9–12. Although EGFR-targeted therapies have shown therapeutic promise, their effectiveness can be undermined by the emergence of resistance mechanisms driven by both RTK-dependent and RTK-independent factors. This challenge is especially notable in head and neck cancers, where EGFR inhibition may inadvertently activate compensatory pathways that support tumour survival13.

Given the structural diversity and multitarget potential of natural compounds, several phytochemicals have been investigated for their ability to modulate EGFR and HER2 signalling. Among these, curcumin and quercetin have received considerable interest due to their capacity to disrupt downstream oncogenic pathways and enhance anti-tumour responses. Because HER2-mediated resistance mechanisms often overlap with those associated with EGFR inhibition, therapeutic strategies that simultaneously target both receptors may help overcome resistance and expand treatment options for subtypes of breast cancer sharing these molecular features14.

Diphenyldiquinoline derivatives are promising anticancer agents due to their favorable pharmacological properties derived from quinoline and phenyl moieties; however, their therapeutic potential necessitates further investigation into their structure-activity relationships (SAR) and biological interactions. A recent study synthesized and evaluated three novel derivatives: DPDQ-3a, DNDPDQ-3b, and DCDPDQ-3c. Their antiproliferative activity was tested against non-small cell lung cancer (A549), colorectal cancer (DLD1), and breast cancer (MCF-7) cell lines, while cytotoxicity was assessed in normal human embryonic kidney cells (HEK293). MTT assays determined IC₅₀ values and selectivity indices, using cisplatin as a reference drug15.

The present study employs various silico techniques, including target prediction, ADMET profiling, network pharmacology, along with molecular docking, to evaluate the anticancer potential of compound 3 (tetrabromo thia-bridged diquinoline). Our findings indicate that this compound demonstrates favourable drug-like properties and exhibits strong binding affinity for EGFR and ERBB2 (HER2). Moreover, it may possess the capacity to modulate resistance to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors and influence ERBB signalling pathways, supporting its potential as a promising anticancer candidate.

Materials along with methods

In-silico studies

Target prediction and ADMET screening for compound 3

The putative biological targets of compound 3 were identified using the SwissTargetPrediction web server16, while its ADME properties were evaluated through the ADMETlab 3.0 platform16,17.

Constructing protein with a protein interaction (PPI) network

The STRING database was employed to generate the PPI network and to extract the core targeted targets (ranked top 10). Cytoscape software version 3.10.4, together with the CytoHubba plug-in, was used for additional analysis and visualization.

Gene Ontology (GO), KEGG pathway, and tissue enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment of the predicted targets compound 3 was performed using the STRING database. This included Gene Ontology (GO) annotation-covering cellular component, biological process, and molecular functions, as well as KEGG pathway mapping, and tissue/cell- line enrichment. KEGG pathway data were additionally processed using the ShinyGO 0.8 platform. Visualization of the results, including GO histograms, KEGG bubble charts, and tissue-distribution pie diagrams, was carried out using online bioinformatics tools17–19.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking was performed for the compounds identified in Constructing protein with a protein interaction (PPI) network” as well as the two top-ranked core targets utilizing the Molecular Operating Environment (MOE) software version 2019.010220. Prior to docking, Compound 3 was subjected to full energy minimization to obtain a stable conformation; hydrogens atoms were added, and partial charges were assigned before the structure was saved in .mdb format21. The crystal structures of EGFR and ERBB2 (HER2) (PDB IDs: 8A2A and 8U8X) were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) and prepared with the QuickPrep protocol to ensure proper protonation and removal of non-essential water molecules. Docking calculations were performed under the Amber10: EHT force field, and binding interactions were analyzed through scoring functions and visualization. The docking procedure was validated by RMSD values of 1.0854 and 1.0205 Å, respectively22.

Synthesis of solvent-free 2,4,10,12-tetrabromo-6,7,14,15-tetrahydro-6,14-thiacycloocta-[1,2-b:5,6-b’] diquinoline (compound 3); abbreviated as tetrabromo thia-bridged diquinoline

Compound 3 was re-synthesized following previously reported protocols23. The transformation was achieved through modified Friedländer annulation reaction24,25, in which two equivalents of 2-amino-3,5-dibromobenzaldehyde (1) — obtained from Sigma-Aldrich, were condensed with one equivalent of 9-thiabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane-2,6-dione (2)26,27 under acidic catalytic conditions. This stepwise condensation and cyclization reaction afforded the desired heterocyclic product, compound 3, in an isolated yield of 57% (Fig. 1). The Spectroscopic data and additional experimental details are summarized below23.

Synthetic route for the preparation of compound 3 from 2-amino-3,5-dibromobenzaldehyde (1) and 9-thiabicyclo [3.3.1]nonane-2,6-dione (2) under acidic reflux conditions.

Instruments information

1H (400 MHz) and 13C (100.7 MHz) NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker spectrometer, and DEPT identified carbon multiplicities. Chemical shifts (δ) are reported in parts per million (ppm) relative to residual solvent signals (CDCl₃). High-resolution mass spectra (HRMS) were obtained on a Finnigan/MAT 95XL-T mass spectrometer. Melting points were measured with a a Büchi B-540 melting point apparatus and are reported uncorrected.

Crystallographic solution and least-squares refinement of the lattice-inclusion compound ((p-xylene)·(3)₂)

Single-crystal X-ray diffraction data were collected on a Bruker SMART APEX 1000 diffractometer equipped with a CCD area detector and a sealed Mo Kα radiation source (λ = 0.71073 Å) at a controlled temperature of 223(2) K. Data acquisition, including frame collection, reflection indexing, and unit cell determination, was carried out using the SMART software suite. Integration and scaling of reflection intensities were performed with SAINT while absorption corrections were applied through the multi-scan procedure implemented in SADABS. Structural solution and refinement, including determination of the space group and full matrix least-squares refinement against F², were conducted using the SHELXTL software package28–30. All C–H hydrogen atoms were situated geometrically along with refined utilizing a riding model for structure factor calculations.

The datasets generated and/or analyzed during the current study are available in the Cambridge Structural Database (CSD) repository, [https://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/solutions/software/csd/ with CCDC Accession number 2499436].

With regard to ADMET characteristics, compound 3 displays physicochemical and pharmacokinetic features that align with moderate drug-likeness (Fig. 2). Its full compliance with the Lipinski Rule (0.0 violations) indicates that the molecular weight, hydrogen-bonding parameters and lipophilicity fall within the recommended limits, suggesting the compound may possess acceptable, indicating oral bioavailability. The absence of PAINS alerts further supports the structural reliability of the molecule, indicating that it doesn’t contain substructures commonly associated with non-specific or misleading biological activity. The predicted probability of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibition (0.079) indicates a low likelihood that the compound interferes with efflux pump activity, thereby reducing the potential for transporter-mediated drug–drug interactions. In contrast, the Human Intestinal Absorption prediction (HIA = 0) suggests poor intestinal permeability, which may limit systemic exposure and highlights the potential need for formulation enhancement or structural modification to improve absorption.

Radar plot summarizing the ADMET characteristics of compound 3 in relation to the commonly accepted boundaries for oral drug-likeness. The shaded green and blue areas represent the lower and upper limits for each parameter, while the yellow trace reflects the measured properties of the compound. This comparison highlights the parameters that fall within desirable ranges and those that may require attention during further optimization.

Distribution parameters show that the compound exhibits a high degree of plasma protein binding (PPB = 92.7%) which could restrict the freely available fraction in circulation and influence the therapeutic window. Its minimal blood–brain barrier permeability penetration (BBB = 0.024) is advantageous for non-CNS applications, as it reduces the likelihood of undesirable central nervous system exposure. The predicted Heptic microsomal stability (HLM = 0.441) reflects moderate metabolic stability, indicating partial susceptibility to hepatic metabolism. The compound is predicted to inhibit several cytochrome P450 isoforms (CYP1A2, CYP2C19, CYP2C9, CYP3A4, CYP2B6, CYP2C8), raising the possibility of enzyme-mediated drug interactions and metabolic complications. Its plasma clearance rate (CLplasma = 7.77 mL/min/kg) represents a moderate systemic clearance rate, consistent with acceptable pharmacokinetic retention. In terms of safety, the compound demonstrates a low probability of hERG channel blockade (0.112), which reduces concerns regarding cardiotoxicity. Conversely, the high predicted likelihood of drug-induced liver injury (DILI = 1.0) and hepatotoxicity (0.828) suggests that the compound may pose a significant risk to hepatic function, underscoring the need for validation using liver-based experimental models. Additionally, the predicted carcinogenicity (0.673) and genotoxicity (1.0) indicate potential mutagenic and carcinogenic liabilities, possibly arising from reactive metabolic intermediates. However, the absence of structural alerts in both the Acute Toxicity and Genotoxic Carcinogenicity suggests that the toxicity may result from functional rather than structural characteristics. Collectively, these results suggests that although the compound meets key drug-likeness and ADME requirements, its poor absorption, strong CYP450 inhibition profile, and potential hepatotoxic and genotoxic indicate that further structural optimization and comprehensive in vitro evaluation will be necessary to achieve a safer and more effective lead candidate.

Gene Ontology (GO), KEGG pathway and tissue enrichment analysis

The predicted molecular targets of compound 3 were evaluated through Gene Ontology (GO), KEGG pathway, and tissue enrichment analyses via the STRING database. GO annotation results were categorized into three major domains: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC). Only statistically significant terms (p < 0.05) with enrichment scores greater than 2 were considered for detailed interpretation (Fig. 3).

The GO analysis indicated that the target proteins were predominantly involved in cellular responses to nitrogen and organonitrogen-containing compounds, as well as in the positive regulation of cell communication. Within the MF domain, enriched functions included protein kinase activity, general kinase activity, receptor-mediated signaling, and G protein–coupled receptor activity. In the CC category, the most prominent enrichments corresponded to integral plasma membrane components and membrane–bounded cell projections (Fig. 5).

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of the predicted molecular targets of compound 3. The analysis shows that compound 3–associated targets participate predominantly in nitrogen- and organonitrogen-responsive biological processes, are localized mainly to plasma membrane–related structures, and exhibit enriched molecular activities associated with kinase function, receptor signalling, and G protein–coupled receptor regulation.

KEGG pathway analysis (p < 0.05) identified 11 significantly overrepresented pathways (Fig. 6), several of which are directly associated with cancer development. These included pathways in cancer, prostate cancer, microRNAs in cancer, cAMP signalling, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, VEGF signalling, PI3K–Akt signalling, and ERBB signalling.

Docking molecule

To further explore the molecular interactions and potential mechanisms underlying the anticancer activity of compound 3-particularly in relation to breast cancer—the top two hub targets identified through CytoHubba (EGFR and ERBB2/HER2), were selected for molecular docking analysis. Docking simulations were performed using the Molecular Operating Environment (MOE) software. For the EGFR complex, compound 3 demonstrated a measurable binding affinity, yielding a docking score of − 2.8504 kcal/mol. The Ligand established four hydrogen bonds and one hydrophobic interaction along with key amino acid residues, including Gly857, Asp855, and Ile759 (Table 2; Fig. 10).

A closer examination of crystal packing of (p-xylene)·(3)2 (compound 4) reveals several notable non-covalent interactions contributing to supramolecular architecture. One of the bromine atoms participates in an edge-to-face halogen–nitrogen contact with a nitrogen atom from a symmetry-related molecule, forming a centrosymmetric dimer characterized by a Br···N distance of 3.34 Å (Fig. 13).

Centrosymmetric dimer formed through a Br···N halogen–nitrogen interaction between symmetry-related molecules, with a Br···N distance of 3.34 Å.

Additionally, a second centrosymmetric dimer arises through a Br···H–C hydrogen bond between two host molecules, with a Br···H separation of 2.98 Å (Fig. 14).

Centrosymmetric dimer formed through a Br···H–C interaction involving a bromine atom and a hydrogen atom of an adjacent molecule, with a Br···H separation of 2.98 Å.

A third dimeric assembly is generated through a N···H–C interaction, where the nitrogen atom of one host molecule interacts with hydrogen atom of another, with a separation distance of 3.41 Å (Fig. 15).

Centrosymmetric dimer formed via N···H–C interactions between nitrogen and hydrogen atoms of neighboring host molecules, exhibiting an N···H distance of 3.41 Å.

In addition, the sulfur atom located at the bridgehead position engages in halogen–chalcogen interactions. Specifically, a bromine atom from one host molecule engages in a Br···S contact with the sulfur atom of a neighbouring molecule, forming another centrosymmetric dimer along with a Br···S distance of 3.41 Å (Fig. 16).

Conclusion

In this study, a comprehensive panel of Insilico strategies were employed to investigate the anticancer potential of compound (3). Computational screening, including target prediction, ADMET modelling, molecular docking, and network pharmacology- revealed that compound (3) possesses favourable drug-like characteristics and exhibits notable binding affinity toward EGFR and ERBB2 (HER2). These findings suggest that the compound may modulate pathways associated with EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance and ERBB- driven signalling. However, the ADMET analysis also indicated limitations, particularly reduced predicted absorption, sturdy CYP inhibition, and potential hepatotoxicity and genotoxicity risk. These factors highlight the necessity for further structural refinement and experimental validation before advancing the compound as a therapeutic lead.

STRING-based enrichment analysis associated the predicted targets of compound (3) with several cancer types, indicating a possible broad-spectrum anticancer profile driven, in part, by its interactions with EGFR and ERBB2 (HER2). The host compound (3) used in this study was originally designed and synthesized by our group, demonstrating exceptional capability for the inclusion of diverse guest molecules. In the present work, the host was resynthesized and examined for its inclusion behaviour with the p-xylene isomer. The incorporation of three distinct heteroatoms—nitrogen, bromine, and sulfur—within the host framework facilitates a range of non-covalent supramolecular interactions among host molecules. Notably, the molecular geometry of the guest species exerts a strong influence on the supramolecular packing arrangements of both host and guest entities. This, in turn, led to the emergence of distinct non-classical hydrogen bonding and halogen bonding motifs unique to each crystal structure analyzed.

Hyaluronan and Syndecan‐1: Linking Glycocalyx Degradation to Development and Progression of Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction
Authors: Estela Rocha‐Oliveira, Jeremy Lagrange, Mohammad Jahangiri, Philippe Guerci, Guillaume Baudry, Nathalie Mercier, Luca Monzo, Céline Leroy, Zohra Lamiral, Adelino F. Leite‐Moreira, Faiez Zannad, Joao Pedro Ferreira, Francisco Vasques‐Nóvoa, Nicolas Girerd
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHeart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) is characterized by chronic low‐grade inflammation, which influences endothelial function, affecting glycocalyx integrity, barrier permeability, and cell adhesion. The role of endothelial glycocalyx shedding in the onset and progression of HFpEF remains poorly understood. Our aim was to investigate the association of glycocalyx degradation biomarkers, hyaluronan and syndecan‐1, with patient characteristics and clinical outcomes in HFpEF and at population level.MethodsCirculating hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 concentrations were assessed in a population‐based cohort (STANISLAS [Suivi Temporaire Annuel Non‐Invasif de la Santé des Lorrains Assurés Sociaux], n=959) and a cohort with HFpEF (NETDiamond [New Targets in Diastolic Heart Failure: From Comorbidities to Personalized Medicine], n=86).ResultsHyaluronan levels increased with age in both the population cohort and the cohort with HFpEF. Higher hyaluronan concentrations were associated in adjusted analyses with features of adverse cardiac remodeling (higher left atrial volume index and left ventricular mass index) in both cohorts and with higher peak early mitral inflow velocity/peak early diastolic mitral annular velocity in the cohort with HFpEF. Syndecan‐1 was negatively associated with left ventricular mass index in the population cohort but not in the cohort with HFpEF where a positive association with features of adverse cardiac remodeling was found. In HFpEF, higher concentrations of both hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 were significantly associated with increased rates of cardiovascular events.ConclusionsHyaluronan increases with age and is consistently associated with adverse cardiac remodeling across the spectrum of cardiovascular risk. In HFpEF, a manifestation of advanced cardiovascular disease, both hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 associate with a higher risk of events. These findings suggest that glycocalyx degradation markers provide clinically relevant information across different stages of disease. Whether such markers may serve as therapeutic targets should be further explored.

Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) has become the predominant form of HF in developed countries, driven by an aging population and the rising prevalence of obesity, diabetes, and hypertension. 1 , 2 Despite recent therapeutic advances, HFpEF continues to pose a significant clinical challenge, with persistently high rates of morbidity and mortality.

Current understanding of HFpEF pathophysiology suggests that it results from the cumulative impact of cardiovascular risk factors on myocardial and vascular structure and function. 3 , 4 Among these factors, inflammation has long been recognized as a central mechanistic link between cardiovascular comorbidities and vascular alterations in HFpEF. Chronic low‐grade proinflammatory activation affects endothelial function by impairing nitric oxide synthesis, promoting a shift toward a more vasoconstrictive and prothrombotic secretory profile, increasing adhesiveness to inflammatory cells, and, importantly, disrupting the integrity of the microvascular barrier. 5

The microvascular barrier, composed of the endothelial glycocalyx (eGC), endothelium, and pericytes, functions as a highly specialized and dynamically regulated filtration unit that governs the exchange of water, macromolecules, and cells between the bloodstream and the interstitium. 6 The eGC, produced by endothelial cells, is primarily composed of proteoglycans, predominantly syndecan‐1, along with glycosaminoglycans, among which hyaluronan represents its main nonsulphated form. 7 These 2 molecules are also abundantly present in the myocardial extracellular matrix, where syndecan‐1 has been identified as a modulator of fibrosis, 8 whereas hyaluronan, due to its water‐binding capacity, has been implicated in myocardial edema formation. 9

Increased circulating levels of syndecan‐1 and hyaluronan have been considered markers of glycocalyx degradation, primarily observed in highly inflammatory conditions such as sepsis or trauma. 10 In HFpEF, elevated circulating syndecan‐1 levels were previously associated with other markers of inflammation and fibrosis (eg, ST2 [interleukin‐1 receptor‐like 1], galectin‐3, and periostin), and a higher risk of HF hospitalization, whereas, to the best of our knowledge, no data currently exist regarding hyaluronan. 8 , 11 Identifying glycocalyx degradation as an early marker of endothelial dysfunction in HFpEF development may have prognostic value and help advocate for early therapeutic interventions.

In this multicohort observational study, we aimed to characterize circulating levels of hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 in patients with chronic HFpEF and explore their clinical, imaging, biomarker, and prognostic associations, hypothesizing that these glycocalyx degradation markers capture early endothelial injury and relate to adverse cardiovascular phenotypes in both an at‐risk general‐population cohort and in patients with established HFpEF.

METHODS

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Populations

The STANISLAS (Suivi Temporaire Annuel Non‐Invasif de la Santé des Lorrains Assurés Sociaux) cohort design has been previously described in detail. 12 Briefly, this family‐based longitudinal cohort included healthy individuals (parents and children) from families in the Lorraine region in north‐eastern France and comprised 4 follow‐up visits over a 20‐year period. For the present study, we included only adults from the parent generation to minimize potential bias related to the familial design and analyzed only data from the most recent follow‐up visit (V4). Glycocalyx degradation markers (syndecan‐1 and hyaluronan) and echocardiographic parameters were measured exclusively at V4 and analyzed cross‐sectionally. The study protocols for all examinations were reviewed and approved by the local ethics committee of the Comité de Protection des Personnes Est 3, France. All participants provided written informed consent to participate in the study.

The NETDiamond (New Targets in Diastolic Heart Failure: From Comorbidities to Personalized Medicine) study is a single‐center, prospective cohort study dedicated to the multiomics‐driven discovery of novel pathophysiological mechanisms and therapeutic targets in HFpEF. Participants were recruited between 2017 and 2020 from the HF Outpatient Clinic at Centro Hospitalar Universitário São João in Porto, Portugal. Eligibility criteria for the NETDiamond study included (1) a diagnosis of HFpEF made by a group of HF specialists according to the contemporary European Society of Cardiology recommendations 13 ; and (2) stable patients, defined as those whose symptoms and signs have remained generally unchanged for at least 3 months. Exclusion criteria included ≥1 the following: (1) moderate‐to‐severe cardiac valvular dysfunction; (2) end‐stage kidney failure; (3) high level of dependency (Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status ≥3). At enrollment, a comprehensive collection of clinical/imaging data and biological samples was performed. The NETDiamond study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki. The Centro Hospitalar Universitário São João Ethical Committee approved the protocol (CES 35‐17), and all subjects gave written informed consent.

Biomarker Measurements

Hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 plasma levels were measured in 959 populational participants from the STANISLAS cohort and 86 patients with HFpEF from the NETDiamond cohort using enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay kits (DY3614 and DY2780; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), following the manufacturer's instructions. Comparisons of biomarker levels between cohorts were not performed due to the lack of a standardized bridging approach.

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies, and continuous variables as mean±SD or median (interquartile range) if not normally distributed. Comparisons of demographic, clinical, and biological parameters among tertiles of hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 levels were analyzed using either parametric linear trend test or nonparametric Jonckheere‐Terpstra trend test for continuous variables and Cochran‐Armitage trend test for categorical variables. Relationships between echocardiography parameters as outcomes and biomarkers of glycocalyx degradation as explanatory variables were examined using linear regression. Univariable‐ and multivariable‐adjusted (sex, age, body mass index, diabetes, hypertension, and estimated glomerular filtration rate)] models were considered. Linear model validity assumptions were verified. To assess whether the associations of hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 with the outcomes differed by sex or age (<60 versus ≥60 years in STANISLAS; <75 versus ≥75 years in NETDiamond), we added interaction terms (biomarker×sex and biomarker×age) to the linear regression models. All of the analyses were performed in both the STANISLAS and NETDiamond cohorts. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to assess the associations between hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 levels and clinical end points in the NETDiamond cohort, analyzed using a time‐to‐first event approach over a median follow‐up (percentile25–75) of 4.4 years (3.6–4.8). Clinical end points were composite and, depending on the analysis, included cardiovascular death, HF hospitalization, acute HF episodes (eg, decompensated HF managed in the emergency department and discharged without inpatient admission), and outpatient diuretic intensification. The proportional hazards assumption was verified by testing covariate–time interaction terms, specifically the interactions between hyaluronan or syndecan‐1 and time. To evaluate whether these associations differed by sex, age, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or obesity, biomarker×covariate interaction terms were included in the Cox models. Crude hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs were calculated and adjusted for age, sex, and creatinine. Risk probabilities were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log rank test. Statistical analyses were performed with Stata (StataCorp. 2023. Stata Statistical Software: Release 18. College Station, TX: StataCorp LLC) SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) and R software (the R Foundation for Statistical Computing) version 4.1.2. P values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Additional methodological details are provided in Data S1.

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics

Hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 concentrations were measured in plasma samples from 959 healthy individuals in the STANISLAS cohort and 86 patients with HFpEF in the NETDiamond cohort.

In the STANISLAS cohort, the median hyaluronan levels (percentile25–75) were 13.32 (6.86–25.97) ng/mL, and the median syndecan‐1 levels (percentile25–75) were 744.3 (500–1697) pg/mL. Hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 were not correlated (Spearman Rho=0.50).

Cutoffs were applied to create tertiles for hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 in the STANISLAS cohort: (1) hyaluronan 0.74 to 8.51 ng/mL (n=316), 8.42 to 20.33 (n=326), 20.35 to 180 (n=317), and (2) syndecan‐1 500 to 537.8 pg/mL (n=316), 538.3 to 1176 (n=326), and 1178 to 32 000 (n=317). Participants with elevated hyaluronan in the STANISLAS cohort were older, more likely to be male, and had a lower heart rate (Table 1). Cardiac function was impaired with elevated hyaluronan with increased left atrial volume index (LAVi) (P<0.0001) and increased left ventricular mass index (LVMi) (P<0.0001). Participants of the STANISLAS cohort with elevated syndecan‐1 had a higher LVMi in tertile 3 (P<0.05) (Table 1).

In the NETDiamond cohort, the median hyaluronan level (percentile25–75) was 11.68 (7.57–25.87) ng/mL, and the median syndecan‐1 levels (percentile25–75) was 1230.21 (782.23–2058.30) pg/mL. The correlation between hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 was poor (Spearman Rho=0.03).

Patient characteristics, demographic data, and clinical and analytical parameters of hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 stratified by tertiles: (1) hyaluronan 3.84 to 8.21 ng/mL (n=29), 8.22 to 16.57 (n=29), 18.33 to 180 (n=28), and (2) syndecan‐1 253.2 to 874.6 pg/mL (n=29), 907.2 to 1561.6 (n=29) and 1581.0 to 32 000 (n=28), are expressed in Table 2. Compared with patients with the lowest hyaluronan levels (tertile 1, T1), those with the highest hyaluronan levels (T3) were older, predominantly women, and exhibited more frequently features of volume overload, such as peripheral edema and elevated BNP (B‐type natriuretic peptide) levels. Higher hyaluronan levels were also associated with worse health status, as assessed by lower Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire scores, and were positively associated with echocardiographic markers of hemodynamic stress, including increased LAVi (P=0.01) and pulmonary arterial systolic pressure (P=0.03). Body composition evaluation by bioimpedance revealed an elevated extracellular/total body water ratio in patients with higher hyaluronan, accompanied with higher fat mass (absolute and percentual) and lower skeletal muscle mass, despite similar body mass index (Table S1).

DISCUSSION

The present study provides new evidence linking eGC degradation to both diastolic dysfunction and adverse outcomes in a population cohort and patient cohort with HFpEF. By analyzing circulating levels of hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 in these cohorts, we demonstrate their associations with cardiac remodeling, functional impairment, and prognosis. These findings support a role for eGC disruption as an early and persistent contributor to HFpEF pathogenesis, with potential implications for risk stratification and targeted interventions.

The eGC is a highly complex molecular structure composed of glycosaminoglycans (eg, hyaluronan), proteoglycans (eg, syndecan‐1), and glycolipids. It lines the luminal surface of endothelial cells, not only protecting them from direct contact with blood flow but also playing a key role in regulating microvascular permeability to fluid and immune cells, transmitting shear stress to nitric oxide production, and functioning as a sodium buffer. Under certain conditions, particularly inflammatory or infectious states, a group of enzymes collectively known as “sheddases” (including hyaluronidases, heparanase, and matrix metalloproteinases) mediate the shedding of glycocalyx components. 7 , 14 This facilitates increased interstitial fluid flow and enables immune cell access to affected tissues. However, in the context of dysregulated or chronic inflammation, a persistent unbalance between glycocalyx component production and degradation may occur, impairing its physiological functions and contributing to organ dysfunction. 10 , 15 Importantly, the kinetics and regulatory mechanisms underlying the release of individual glycocalyx components in human disease, particularly in chronic conditions such as HFpEF, remain largely unknown. It is therefore plausible that the shedding of specific glycocalyx constituents may reflect distinct pathogenic stimuli within the complex and multifactorial pathophysiology of HFpEF.

In this study, we analyzed 2 distinct glycocalyx‐related biomarkers, hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 across 2 independent cohorts: a population‐based sample and a sample with HFpEF. This design allowed us to assess the associations of different classes of glycocalyx components not only with clinical features and outcomes in established HFpEF but also with subclinical variations in key indices of myocardial structure and function within a healthy population, parameters known to predict the future development of HFpEF.

In the population‐based cohort, a striking divergence was observed in the association patterns of hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 with markers of cardiovascular risk and HF development. Higher hyaluronan levels were strongly associated with older age, female sex, and indicators of cardiac remodeling, such as increased LAVi and LVMi. In contrast, elevated syndecan‐1 levels showed negative associations with LAVi and LVMi. Interestingly, this divergent association pattern was not observed in the cohort with HFpEF, in whom higher hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 levels in the older, comorbid population with HFpEF exhibited positive associations with adverse cardiac remodeling and HF outcomes. However, it is important to highlight that additional clinical associations differed significantly for hyaluronan and syndecan‐1. Elevated hyaluronan levels correlated with older age, atrial fibrillation, higher fat‐to‐muscle ratio, clinical and biochemical signs of volume overload, adverse cardiac remodeling, and poorer health status related to HF. In contrast, higher syndecan‐1 levels were linked with a higher prevalence of diabetes, renal dysfunction, and systemic inflammation.

Patterns of association between eGC shedding and cardiac structure and function differed across cohorts. Specifically, hyaluronan was more strongly associated with LAVi in the cohort with HFpEF than in the population cohort, possibly due to the broader range of LAVi values in the group with HFpEF. Similarly, the association with E/e' was significant only in the cohort with HFpEF, likely because E/e' values in the population cohort, despite showing a similar trend, remained within the normal range and showed limited variability. These findings suggest that hyaluronan may play different roles depending on the stage of the disease, which further reinforces its value as a biomarker with relevance across the HF continuum. In contrast, syndecan‐1 was associated with cardiac remodeling only in patients with HFpEF, but not at the population level. These findings suggest that eGC degradation is biomarker‐specific: hyaluronan appears to indicate early, subclinical stage of the disease, with its respective release into circulation increasing with age‐ and cardiac‐related comorbidities, whereas syndecan‐1 is more closely associated with advanced disease stages of HFpEF, particularly in context of chronic inflammation and kidney disease.

Few significant age‐ or sex‐dependent interactions were observed between eGC biomarkers and cardiac structure and function. In the STANISLAS cohort, the association between hyaluronan and LVMi was stronger in men than in women, consistent with experimental data showing that hyaluronan overexpression reduces nitric oxide bioavailability and impaired endothelial function in male but not female mice. 16 Regarding syndecan‐1, the association with E/e' showed an interaction with age in STANISLAS and with sex in NETDiamond. The age‐related interaction may be explained by the known decline in circulating syndecan‐1 concentrations with advancing age, 17 whereas similar sex‐interaction have been described for syndecan‐1 association with acute coronary syndrome and metabolic syndrome, being stronger in men than in women. 18 , 19

We also demonstrated that markers of eGC degradation were associated with clinical outcomes in HFpEF, suggesting that this mechanism may not only contribute to disease pathogenesis but also play a significant role in its progression and prognosis. In this context, we recently found that less commonly investigated markers of glycocalyx degradation (perlecan and thrombomodulin) were significantly associated with an increased risk of hospitalization and death in patients with HFpEF. 20 Additionally, our findings are consistent with those described by Tromp et al., who reported elevated syndecan‐1 levels in patients with chronic HFpEF, which were also associated with an increased risk of mortality over a 3‐year follow‐up period. 8

The mechanisms underlying eGC degradation in HFpEF are likely multifactorial, involving an imbalance between synthesis and degradation, endothelial inflammation, and senescence. Although both are core glycocalyx constituents, hyaluronan, a nonsulfated glycosaminoglycan, and syndecan‐1, a transmembrane heparan sulfate proteoglycan, have distinct synthesis, shedding, and clearance pathways, with hyaluronan degraded by hyaluronidases and syndecan‐1 released predominantly via ectodomain shedding by matrix metalloproteinases and ADAM (a disintegrin and metalloproteinases) proteases, which may underlie the divergent associations observed. Among neurohumoral mediators implicated in these processes, NP, released in response to cardiomyocyte stretch, are potent inducers of eGC shedding. 14 , 21 In addition, sex hormones modulate hyaluronan turnover and glycocalyx integrity. 16 , 22 Although evidence remains indirect, hypoestrogenism may increase hyaluronan degradation and partly account for higher circulating levels in older women with HFpEF. 23

This study has limitations. The eGC is an intricate structure composed of a wide range of molecular components, of which only 2, hyaluronan and syndecan‐1, were assessed in this analysis. As such, important aspects of eGC dynamics and shedding in both the general population and HFpEF may not be fully captured by these biomarkers alone. In addition, although the same methodology and ELISA kits were used for biomarker quantification, measurements were performed at different times and locations without bridging, limiting direct comparability between cohorts. Finally, the 2 cohorts differed in sampling frame, demographics, and disease severity, with the NETDiamond cohort being relatively small, resulting in imbalanced cohort sizes and a limited number of outcome events. These differences limit the generalizability of our findings. To minimize bias, all analyses were conducted separately within each cohort, thereby avoiding direct comparisons and reducing the impact of case‐mix and sample‐size differences. This strategy provided complementary perspectives from a general at‐risk population and from a cohort with HFpEF; however, further validation in larger, more diverse, and contemporary multinational cohorts with HFpEF will be essential to strengthen and extend our observations.

These findings are clinically significant, as they suggest a potential role for eGC degradation in the development of HFpEF and raise the possibility that it could also be linked to disease prognosis. They support the rationale for therapeutic strategies aimed at preserving the glycocalyx, such as inhibiting its degrading enzymes or administering its structural components.

CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, this study highlights the potential role of eGC degradation in HFpEF pathophysiology and progression. By examining hyaluronan and syndecan‐1 across both a general population and cohort with HFpEF, we identified distinct biomarker‐specific associations with cardiac remodeling and disease severity. Hyaluronan was consistently linked to adverse cardiac remodeling and diastolic dysfunction across the cardiovascular risk spectrum, suggesting its relevance in early, subclinical disease stages. In contrast, syndecan‐1 was associated with cardiac remodeling only in HFpEF, indicating its potential role in later‐stage disease progression. In HFpEF, both biomarkers were linked to a higher risk of adverse events, underscoring their prognostic value. These findings suggest that markers of eGC degradation provide clinically relevant insights at different levels of cardiovascular risk, warranting further investigation in larger cohorts to explore its utility as a biomarker and therapeutic target.

Spatial accessibility analysis of vaccination units in Gansu Province, China (2025)
Authors: Xuefeng Liang, Na Jin, Shuyu Liu, Bingling Zhang, Jing An, Yu Tang, Xuxia Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study evaluated the spatial accessibility of vaccination units in Gansu Province, China, using the Two-Step Floating Catchment Area (2SFCA) method, with data collected as of December 2025. A total of 2,119 vaccination units registered as of December 2025 were included as supply points, with staff count as the supply capacity measure. Spatial accessibility was calculated for 14,243 urban residential points and 184,112 rural toponym points using a 5 km catchment radius. Accessibility levels were classified using the Jenks natural breaks method, and spatial equity was quantified using the Gini coefficient. In urban areas, the mean accessibility index was 0.20 (range: 0–84.43), with 98.93% of points classified as low accessibility; the Gini coefficient was 0.957. In rural areas, the mean accessibility index was 0.068 (range: 0–97.74), with 99.57% of points at low accessibility and 57.28% having zero accessibility; the Gini coefficient was 0.751. Vaccination services in Gansu Province exhibit extreme spatial inequality, with near-universal low accessibility in rural areas. Targeted interventions are urgently needed to address these disparities and ensure equitable immunization coverage.

Introduction

Vaccination units serve as the fundamental infrastructure for delivering immunization services, and the rationality of their spatial layout directly influences the convenience with which residents can access vaccination programs, thereby bearing on the effective implementation of immunization policies. Gansu Province, situated in China’s northwest inland region, is characterized by complex and diverse topography spanning the Loess Plateau, the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, and the Inner Mongolian Plateau. With an elongated east-west stretch exceeding 1,600 kilometers and pronounced regional disparities, the province presents a particularly challenging context for ensuring equitable spatial access to vaccination services.1

Since 2016, national coverage rates have exceeded 95% for each of the eight EPI vaccines, including measles-mumps-rubella, DTP, BCG, poliomyelitis, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, Japanese encephalitis, and meningococcal vaccines.2 However, significant provincial disparities persist, with western provinces including Gansu historically reporting lower coverage rates for non-EPI vaccines and higher proportions of delayed or incomplete vaccination schedules compared to eastern coastal regions.3 For instance, the 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV13) coverage in Gansu was only 6.03% in 2021, compared to 59.57% in Zhejiang Province.4 These disparities are particularly concerning given that vaccination procedures in western rural areas often lack standardization due to resource constraints and limited vaccinator capacity.2

Spatial accessibility, a core concept in health geography, quantifies the ease with which populations can reach service facilities and has been extensively applied to evaluate the distribution of healthcare and educational resources. The Two-Step Floating Catchment Area (2SFCA) method, originally developed by Luo and Wang,5 has emerged as a principal analytical framework in healthcare accessibility research because it simultaneously accounts for supply capacity, population demand, and distance decay effects. Subsequent methodological refinements, including the enhanced 2SFCA (E2SFCA) and generalized 2SFCA (G2SFCA), have further improved its applicability.6,7 .Although these methods have been widely adopted to assess accessibility to hospitals and primary care facilities, particularly in developed eastern Chinese regions, research specifically addressing vaccination unit accessibility in underdeveloped western provinces remains scarce.8

This gap is particularly consequential given that western China is characterized by extreme geographical heterogeneity, sparse rural populations, and limited transportation infrastructure, all of which profoundly shape spatial access to immunization services. However, studies employing commercial point-of-interest (POI) data as demand-side inputs to evaluate the spatial accessibility of vaccination units remain limited in China.

This study addresses this research gap by systematically evaluating the spatial accessibility of vaccination services across Gansu Province. Integrating Geographic Information System (GIS) spatial analysis, the 2SFCA method, and Jenks natural breaks classification, we assessed accessibility patterns and urban – rural disparities as of December 2025. Our findings aim to inform evidence-based strategies for optimizing immunization resource allocation in Gansu Province.

Materials and methods

Data sources

Supply points comprised 2,119 routine vaccination units registered in the Gansu Province Immunization Program Information System as of December 2025. Geocoding of unit locations was performed using the Amap (Gaode Maps) Open Platform API (https://lbs.amap.com/) by combining district or county names with unit names. Units exclusively providing obstetric or rabies vaccination services, as well as facilities located outside Gansu Province (e.g., the People’s Liberation Army 63,600 Hospital), were excluded from the analysis. Obstetric units are responsible for administering the BCG and first dose of hepatitis B vaccines, while rabies vaccine administration facilities handle wound treatment and rabies vaccination. These two types of facilities do not provide other vaccination services; and the 63,600 Hospital is a military facility located outside provincial boundaries that does not serve the general Gansu population.

Demand points were categorized into urban and rural types and retrieved from the Amap Open Platform API in October 2025. Urban demand points were extracted from the commercial and residential category, encompassing residential communities, commercial – residential complexes, dormitories, and residential areas. Rural demand points were extracted from the toponym address information category, including village – level, village – group – level, and township – level administrative units.

Population data were obtained from the China Earth System Science Data Sharing Platform (NESSDC, https://www.geodata.cn/main/) as 100-m resolution raster data derived from the 2020 Seventh National Population Census of China. Administrative boundary vector data for Gansu Province were obtained from the National Geospatial Public Service Platform (NGPSP, https://www.tianditu.gov.cn/) in December 2025.

Spatial accessibility

Spatial accessibility was calculated using the standard Two – Step Floating Catchment Area (2SFCA) method.9 This method employs a binary catchment boundary whereby all demand points within a specified threshold radius receive equal weight, while those beyond the threshold receive zero weight. The calculation proceeds in two steps: (1) for each vaccination unit (supply point), all demand points falling within the catchment radius are identified, and the supply – to – demand ratio is computed by dividing the supply capacity by the total population within the catchment area; (2) for each demand point, all vaccination units within the catchment radius are identified, and the accessibility index is calculated as the sum of the supply – to – demand ratios from all accessible units.

A 5 km catchment threshold was adopted based on several considerations. First, China’s “15-minute community life circle” policy stipulates that residents should access primary healthcare services within approximately 15 minutes of travel.10 While this corresponds to approximately 1–1.5 km walking distance in urban settings, rural residents predominantly use motorized vehicles or bicycles; assuming average speeds of 15–20 km/h on rural roads, 15–20 minutes of travel corresponds to approximately 4–6 km. Second, previous 2SFCA studies examining township health center accessibility in China have commonly employed catchment thresholds of 5–10 km for rural primary care services.11,12 Third, the World Health Organization recommends that primary healthcare facilities be accessible within 5 km or one hour of travel in rural settings.13

The Jenks natural breaks classification method was adopted to stratify the spatial accessibility values calculated by the 2SFCA model.5 This algorithm optimally groups numerical values by minimizing intra-class variance and maximizing inter-class variance, objectively identifying natural breakpoints inherent in the data distribution and avoiding subjective bias from predefined thresholds.14 The accessibility index was classified into three levels – low, medium, and high – to characterize the spatial differentiation pattern of vaccination service accessibility across the study area.

Statistical analysis

All data processing, statistical analyses, and visualizations were performed using R version 4.5.1. The sf package was employed for spatial data manipulation, coordinate system transformation, and geometry operations,15 while the tidyverse suite of packages was used for data wrangling and visualization.16 Coordinates obtained via the Amap Open Platform API (GCJ–02 datum) were converted to the World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS84) and subsequently projected to the China Geodetic Coordinate System 2000 (CGCS2000, EPSG: 4526) for spatial analysis. Euclidean distance was used to construct distance matrices between supply and demand points. The SpatialAcc package was used to perform 2SFCA accessibility calculations,17 and the ineq package was used to compute the Gini coefficient for assessing spatial equity.18 All maps were created using the ggplot2 package19 with administrative boundary layers from the National Geographic Information Public Service Platform.

Results

Basic characteristics of study subjects

Distribution characteristics of vaccination units

A total of 2,119 vaccination units across Gansu Province were included in this study, covering 14 prefecture-level cities (autonomous prefectures) and Lanzhou New Area. The number of vaccination units varied substantially across prefectures, ranging from 9 to 361, with a median of 123 units per prefecture. By institutional type, township health centers constituted the majority (n = 1,284, 60.6%), followed by community health service stations (n = 313, 14.8%), community health service centers (n = 219, 10.3%), village clinics (n = 166, 7.8%), and other types of institutions (n = 137, 6.5%). Collectively, these vaccination units employed 15,326 staff members province-wide, with staffing levels per unit ranging from 1 to 44 (median = 4).

Distribution characteristics of demand points

A total of 14,243 residential points were identified as urban demand points, with prefectural counts ranging from 277 to 3,909 (median = 891). Residential communities comprised the predominant type (n = 11,533, 80.9%), followed by commercial-residential related points (n = 1,385, 9.7%), residential areas (n = 965, 6.8%), dormitories (n = 338, 2.4%), and villas (n = 22, 0.2%). Urban population density ranged from 0.00 to 951.00 persons/km2 (median = 24.60 persons/km2).

A total of 184,112 toponym points were identified as rural demand points, with prefectural counts ranging from 336 to 32,550 (median = 11,105). Village-level toponyms predominated (n = 133,164, 72.3%), followed by village-group-level toponyms (n = 49,688, 27.0%) and township-level toponyms (n = 1,260, 0.7%). Rural population density ranged from 0.00 to 571.00 persons/km2 (median = 0.59 persons/km2).

Discussion

This study systematically evaluated the spatial accessibility of vaccination services in Gansu Province using the standard Two-Step Floating Catchment Area (2SFCA) method, with staff count as the supply capacity measure and a uniform 5 km catchment threshold. The findings reveal profound urban-rural disparities in vaccination accessibility, extreme spatial inequality, and a pronounced polarization pattern that collectively highlight critical challenges for immunization program delivery in this resource-constrained western Chinese province.

A principal finding of this study is the pronounced disparity in vaccination spatial accessibility between urban and rural areas. The mean urban accessibility index (0.20) was approximately 2.9 times higher than that of rural areas (0.068), and 57.28% of rural demand points exhibited zero accessibility within the 5 km threshold, indicating a complete absence of nearby vaccination services. This disparity is substantially greater than that reported in healthcare accessibility studies conducted in more developed eastern Chinese regions, such as Zhejiang and Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei.20,21 The magnitude of this gap likely reflects Gansu’s unique geographic and demographic characteristics: vast rural territories, extremely low population density. This pattern aligns with the principle of economies of scale in healthcare delivery, whereby vaccination units require sufficient service populations to maintain operational viability,22 and reflects the well-documented tendency for medical resources to aggregate toward administrative centers.23 The relatively small proportion of village clinics (7.8%) among all vaccination units further exacerbates the “last mile” problem in rural service delivery.11 For vulnerable populations – including left-behind elderly, infants, and children in remote mountain communities – these geographical barriers pose particularly severe obstacles to timely immunization.

This study’s findings of extreme spatial inequality deserve particular attention. The urban Gini coefficient of 0.957 indicates that the benefits of vaccine accessibility are almost entirely concentrated in urban central areas, while peripheral areas face severe supply shortages. In rural areas, only 0.11% of demand points (195 out of 184,112) achieved high accessibility, with both urban and rural Gini coefficients significantly exceeding the 0.4 threshold commonly used to indicate high inequality.24 The causes of low rural accessibility are multifaceted. First, vast rural territories with low population density and weak transportation infrastructure lead to severely insufficient supply capacity within the 5 km service radius.25,26 Second, the small number of vaccination units relative to the dispersed rural population makes adequate spatial coverage difficult to achieve.

The Jenks natural breaks classification revealed a striking pattern of accessibility polarization. In both urban and rural settings, the vast majority of demand points fell into the lowest accessibility category—98.93% in urban areas and 99.57% in rural areas – with only minuscule fractions achieving high accessibility. This extreme skewness (urban skewness = 23.65, rural skewness = 88.11) reflects a fundamental structural characteristic of vaccination service distribution in Gansu: supply capacity is heavily concentrated in administrative centers while remaining thinly dispersed across the broader territory. In rural regions, the 195 high-accessibility points are scattered across townships with relatively larger health centers, while the 105,462 zero-accessibility points – representing over half of all rural demand – are located in remote villages beyond the 5 km reach of any vaccination unit.

The spatial imbalance identified in this study carries significant implications for immunization program implementation and public health outcomes. The extreme concentration of accessibility in urban cores raises concerns about inefficient resource utilization, with some city-center vaccination points potentially experiencing overcrowding while peripheral and rural residents face substantial barriers to routine immunization services. Research in similar resource-limited settings has demonstrated that geographical accessibility is a critical determinant of vaccination timeliness and completeness, with poor accessibility significantly associated with delayed vaccination schedules, missed doses.27

In Gansu specifically, the 105,462 rural demand points with zero accessibility represent communities where residents must travel more than 5 km – often across difficult terrain – to reach the nearest vaccination unit. This distance may be insurmountable for elderly caregivers and infants during harsh weather conditions. These accessibility barriers may contribute to lower vaccination coverage rates in western provinces compared to national targets, potentially increasing the risk of vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks in under-immunized communities. Indeed, Gansu has experienced higher incidence of certain vaccine-preventable diseases; for example, the province witnessed its largest Japanese encephalitis outbreak in 2018.28

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the standard 2SFCA method employs a binary catchment boundary rather than continuous distance – decay weighting. While this approach provides a clear threshold-based interpretation of accessibility, it does not capture the gradual attenuation of accessibility as distance from vaccination units increases. Second, the uniform 5 km catchment radius, although consistent with WHO recommendations and previous Chinese studies, may not reflect realistic travel thresholds across Gansu’s heterogeneous geography. Sensitivity analyses using alternative radii (e.g., 3 km and 8 km) would strengthen the robustness of our findings. Third, the use of commercial POI data as proxies for urban demand points introduces potential bias. These datasets tend to overrepresent economically active and digitally mapped areas while underestimating demand in socioeconomically disadvantaged communities – often the most policy-relevant populations.22 Similarly, rural geographic location data derived from administrative place names may not accurately capture actual residential distributions, given the spatial dispersion of settlements within administrative units. Moreover, seasonal population mobility data for Gansu Province were unavailable through the NESSDC, precluding adjustment for transient demographic fluctuations. This is particularly consequential in regions experiencing substantial labor out-migration or seasonal influxes. Fourth, staff count served as the sole proxy measure of supply capacity due to data availability; however, this metric does not capture differences in service quality, operating hours, cold chain capacity, or daily vaccination throughput.22 Fifth, Euclidean distance may not fully reflect actual travel burden in topographically complex rural settings, where road networks typically follow winding contours rather than straight-line paths, suggesting that our approach may underestimate true travel distances in mountainous terrain. Finally, the datasets employed in this study were obtained from different time periods. Due to the inherent update cycles of these sources, temporal harmonization was not feasible. Nevertheless, each dataset represents the most current and authoritative information available for its respective category. Given that population distribution and POI configurations tend to exhibit relative stability over short-to-medium time horizons, the potential impact of this temporal misalignment on model outcomes is likely to be limited.

Conclusion

This study presents the first systematic assessment of vaccination spatial accessibility across Gansu Province. Using the standard 2SFCA method with December 2025 vaccination unit data, we documented profound urban – rural disparities and extreme spatial inequality in vaccination service distribution. Urban areas exhibited a Gini coefficient of 0.957, while rural areas demonstrated near – universal low accessibility (99.57% of demand points), with over half (57.28%) having zero accessibility within the 5 km threshold. These findings underscore the pressing need for tailored public health strategies to reduce existing disparities and achieve equitable vaccine coverage.

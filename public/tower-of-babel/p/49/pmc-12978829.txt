Exploring pathways between residential greenness and antepartum stillbirth: A mediation analysis
Authors: Dario Ezequiel Elias, Marcia Furquim de Almeida, Rossana Pulcineli Vieira Francisco, Hillegonda Maria Dutilh Novaes, Lays Janaina Prazeres Marques, Regina Schultz, Mara Sandra Hoshida, Zilda Pereira da Silva, Osmara Alves dos Santos, Gerusa Maria Figueiredo, Rafael Junqueira Buralli, Gabriella Ferreira Demarque, Clarisse Martins Machado, Luciana Regina Meireles, Rodrigo Melim Zerbinati, Andrés Jimenez Galisteo Junior, Laura Cunha Rodrigues, Nelson Gouveia, Gizelton Pereira Alencar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Antepartum stillbirth is an important public health issue. This study aimed to identify mediation pathways from the vegetation greenness surrounding the maternal residence to antepartum stillbirth, through stillbirth risk factors.Methods:This study is part of the FetRisks project–a population-based case–control study of stillbirth and live births in 14 public hospitals in the Municipality of São Paulo, Brazil (December 2019–December 2023). We estimated vegetation greenness using the median value of the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) obtained from satellite images from the study period, considering buffer radii around the residences between 50 and 500 m. We used penalized logistic regressions to select maternal, placental, fetal, and neighborhood characteristics. Based on these data, we built a Bayesian network and performed counterfactual mediation analyses.Results:We analyzed 248 antepartum stillbirths (cases) and 301 live births (controls). The maternal residence of 19.4% (48/248) of cases and 30.9% (93/301) of controls had a median NDVI within a buffer radius of 150 m (NDVI-150m) greater than 0.104. Women living in residences with a median NDVI-150m greater than 0.104 had a lower risk of antepartum stillbirth (odds ratio: 0.50; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.33, 0.76). Fetal growth restriction and placental inflammatory signs mediated 38.5% (natural indirect effect: −0.0604; 95% CI: −0.0916, −0.0292) and 14.7% (natural indirect effect: −0.0230; 95% CI: −0.0412, −0.0047) of the effect of a median NDVI-150m greater than 0.104 on antepartum stillbirth, respectively.Conclusion:Higher residential vegetation greenness showed protective indirect effects on antepartum stillbirth partially through a lower risk of fetal growth restriction and placental inflammatory signs.

What this study adds

Antepartum stillbirth is a complex, multifactorial public health issue. Some risk factors are known, but their interrelationships and pathways of causation are unclear. We identified that higher vegetation greenness surrounding the maternal residence had a protective association against antepartum stillbirth. The main pathways through which residential vegetation greenness was associated with a lower risk of antepartum stillbirth were related to reduced risks of fetal growth restriction and the presence of placental inflammatory signs.

Background

In 2023, the global stillbirth rate was estimated at 14.3 per 1,000 births (uncertainty interval 13.7–15.5), implying approximately 2 million stillbirths yearly.1 Stillbirths can occur before the initiation of labor (antepartum) or during labor (intrapartum). While improved care during delivery considerably reduces the risk of intrapartum stillbirth,2 antepartum stillbirth remains a challenge to prevent. The median percentage of stillbirths occurring in the antepartum period was 88.4% and 49.8% in high and low-income countries, respectively.1

Risk factors for stillbirth can be approached through various dimensions, including individual and environmental characteristics–these include maternal sociodemographic characteristics (e.g., low socioeconomic status, age over 35 years, absence of a partner), history of adverse obstetric outcomes (e.g. previous fetal loss), maternal and family psychosocial aspects (e.g. smoking), lack of access to or inadequate prenatal care, maternal comorbidities during pregnancy (e.g. infections, diabetes, and hypertension), placental abnormalities, and fetal attributes (e.g. congenital malformations and fetal growth restriction).3,4

In recent years, there has been an increase in studies focusing on environmental factors, which include the association between green vegetation, or greenness, and adverse birth outcomes in urban areas, mainly in high-income countries.5 This has been made possible by the wide availability of vegetation indices obtained from satellite images, predominantly the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI).6 Studies on adverse pregnancy outcomes have shown that the more green vegetation surrounding the maternal residence, the higher birth weight and the lower small for gestational age frequency.7–10 In addition, Yuan et al11 showed the beneficial effects of greenness in alleviating the risk and burden of stillbirth in Iran.

The mechanisms underlying the association of greenness with pregnancy outcomes remain unclear. Pathways related to mitigating exposure to environmental stressors, which include air pollution, heat, and noise, have been suggested.12 These factors may affect the placenta in multiple ways, including nitrosative/oxidative stress, inflammation, endocrine disruption, epigenetic changes, and vascular dysregulation of the maternal-fetal unit, decreasing the placental function and leading to hypoxia of the fetus.13,14 In addition, pathways related to biopsychosocial aspects, such as enabling increased physical activity, facilitating social connectivity, promoting sleep quality, and regulating attention and emotion, have also been proposed.12

The main methodology to analyze pathways from exposures to outcomes is the mediation analysis.15 Few studies have examined mediation pathways between greenness and pregnancy outcomes. Anabitarte et al,16 in Spain, examined the mediation role of physical activity, psychological health, and air pollution between residential greenness and small for gestational age, large for gestational age, preterm birth, birth weight, and low birth weight. In turn, Lee et al,17 in Taiwan, studied air pollution as a mediator between residential greenness and preterm birth and small for gestational age. To our knowledge, no mediation analyses have been conducted between residential greenness and stillbirth. Based on previously cited studies, our main hypotheses are that residential greenness has a protective association with antepartum stillbirth, and this association is mediated by fetal growth restriction and placental abnormalities.

The present study is based on a research project on stillbirth conducted in the Municipality of São Paulo (MSP), Brazil, which encompasses a wide range of characteristics, including environmental, sociodemographic, behavioral, comorbidity, placental, and fetal data.4,18 Given the large number of exposures collected and the small number of studies on greenness and stillbirth, we were interested in exploring multiple possible mediation pathways among stillbirth risk factors. For this reason, this study aimed to identify mediation pathways from the vegetation greenness surrounding the maternal residence to antepartum stillbirth, through stillbirth risk factors.

Methods

Study area

The MSP, in the State of São Paulo, located in Brazil’s Southeast Region, is recognized as the most developed city in the country in terms of socioeconomic development. It covers an area of 1,521.11 km2 and has an estimated population of 11.5 million inhabitants.19 In 2023, the average monthly salary of formal workers was approximately USD 1082, corresponding to 4.1 times the national minimum wage, despite the income inequality among social strata.19 The MSP shows spatial patterns of income and tree cover; the central zone has high income and low tree cover, while the peripheral zone has low income and high tree cover.20 The MSP has a humid subtropical climate with dry winter and rainy summer, according to the Köppen classification;21 and has an annual variation of the NDVI according to rainy and dry seasons.22 The rainy season in the MSP occurs between October and April.23 The highest concentrations of air pollutants are observed during the austral winter months, when meteorological conditions are more favorable, such as less precipitation and thermal inversions.24 The stillbirth rate in MSP was 8.0 per 1,000 births, of which 81% were antepartum in 2023.25 It was estimated that 65.8% of stillbirths are mainly located in the peripheral zone of MSP.26

Study design and population

This study is part of the FetRiskS project, which is a population-based case–control study, with prospective inclusion of stillbirths and live births at the time of delivery in 14 public hospitals in the MSP, between December 2019 and December 2023.18 We included women with singleton pregnancies, residents in the Metropolitan Region of São Paulo, who lead to antepartum stillbirth (cases) or live birth (controls) with gestational age between 22 and 44 weeks or weighing at least 500 g. In turn, we excluded women with communication difficulties (deafness, muteness, psychological/psychiatric disorders, foreign language, etc.), those whose delivery resulted from a court-ordered pregnancy termination, women without a record of prenatal care information, and women without address data at the street and number level.

Exposures

In this study, we included sociodemographic, obstetric, and psychosocial exposures collected through women’s interviews, patient records’ data collection, and prenatal care registries; maternal serological tests for infectious diseases from blood samples collected at the time of delivery; placenta lesions according to Amsterdam international consensus criteria;27 fetal attributes; neighborhood socioeconomic characteristics; and median air pollution (PM2.5) in first trimester of pregnancy, estimated from the monitoring station nearest to the mother’s residence (Table S1; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414).

Normalized difference vegetation index

To estimate the vegetation greenness surrounding each domicile, we calculated the NDVI based on Landsat 8 Collection 2 Level 2 atmospherically corrected images downloaded from the EarthExplorer of the United States Geological Survey.28 The multispectral spatial resolution of these images was 30 m per pixel, and the temporal resolution was 16 days. We selected all satellite images with <10% cloud coverage (n = 19) for the study period, whose distribution was 7 in the rainy season and 12 in the dry season. Clouds were masked based on the quality assessment band of satellite images.

The NDVI was calculated from the spectral difference between red and infrared bands.29 The NDVI values oscillate between –1 and 1; the higher the value, the greater the vegetation greenness, with values <0 indicating the absence of green vegetation.29 Given the seasonal variability of NDVI in the study region,22 we generated an aggregate image based on the median NDVI value of each pixel from selected images. Following the guidance of Markevych et al,12 negative NDVI values were replaced by the value zero. Considering the buffer radii used in previous studies and methodological guidance,7–10,12 we calculated the surrounding NDVI of each maternal domicile as the median NDVI for buffers centered on the domicile with radii of 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, and 500 m. Finally, considering that we did not observe an association between the continuous variable of median surrounding NDVI and antepartum stillbirth (Table S2; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414), we classified the median surrounding NDVI in each radius into two categories (high and low) based on the 75% percentile of its distribution. R package raster was used.30

Data analysis

The data analysis strategy had five steps. First, using logistic regressions, we identified the buffer radius in which high surrounding NDVI was most strongly associated with antepartum stillbirth. Second, we used logistic penalized regressions to select the main exposures associated with antepartum stillbirth and, on the other hand, with high surrounding NDVI.31 Third, we built a data-driven Bayesian network (BN) to visualize the conditional dependencies among selected exposures and outcomes.32 Fourth, based on the possible pathways identified in the BN, we performed counterfactual and serial mediation analyses.15,33 Fifth, we performed sensitivity analyses for counterfactual mediation pathways to: the radius of the buffer used to estimate the surrounding NDVI, the quantile of the surrounding NDVI distribution selected for their categorization, the influence of rainy and dry seasons in NDVI, the contextual socioeconomic conditions, the time residing at current address, and the mediation robustness to the presence of residual confounding. The complete methodology for data analysis is described in Appendix S1; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414.

Missing data

For exposures with 5 or more missing values, we include the missing category in statistical models. We exclude cases and controls with missing data in exposures with 4 or less missing values, or with missing values in placental lesions or fetal growth restriction variables. Consequently, cases and controls with undefined/ ambiguous/ ignored sex were excluded, since it was not possible to identify if they had a fetal growth restriction. Using the chi-square test, we estimated the association of antepartum stillbirth with excluded women. In addition, we used the chi-square test adjusted for multiple comparisons with false discovery rate to estimate the association of analyzed exposures with excluded women.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The FetRiskS study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Municipal Health Department of São Paulo (process no. 16509319.0.3012.5551) and of the Hospital das Clínicas, Faculty of Medicine, University of São Paulo (HCFMUSP) (process no. 16509319.0.0000.0068). Written informed consent was obtained from all pregnant women.

Results

In this study, 282 cases and 344 controls were analyzed. Table S3; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414, shows the frequency of exposures in cases and controls. After excluding women with missing data, 248 (87.9%) cases and 301 (87.5%) controls remained (Figure S1; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414). Excluded women with missing data were not associated with antepartum stillbirth (chi-square = 0.002, d. f. = 1, P = 0.964) nor with the exposures analyzed (Table S4; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414); although three exposures had a significant unadjusted chi-square, in the entire sample they showed no association with antepartum stillbirth or with NDVI variables (Table S5; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414). Using a serial mediation model, we estimated the indirect effects of high surrounding NDVI-150m through placental inflammatory signs and fetal growth restriction on antepartum stillbirth. Fetal growth restriction and placental inflammatory signs individually mediated the protective effect of high surrounding NDVI-150m on antepartum stillbirth, and the indirect effect through serial mediation was not significant (Table S8; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414).

Sensitivity analysis

The protective NIE of high surrounding NDVI-150m on antepartum stillbirth through fetal growth restriction, in unadjusted and adjusted models, was significant with NDVI estimated separately for satellite images of rainy and dry seasons; also the NIE was significant considering the 60%, 70%, 80%, and 90% quantiles for surrounding NDVI-150m categorization, corresponding to 0.090, 0.099, 0.113, and 0.154 values, respectively (Table S9; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414). The protective NIE of high surrounding NDVI-150m through placental inflammatory signs, in unadjusted and adjusted models, was only significant with NDVI estimated for satellite images of dry season; also, the NIE was significant considering the 70%, 80%, and 90% quantiles for surrounding NDVI-150m categorization (Table S9; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414). In both pathways, the percentage mediated appeared to be higher as the quantile for surrounding NDVI-150m categorization increased, reaching 76.7% and 45.4% at the 90% quantile in fetal growth restriction and placental inflammatory signs pathways with individual and contextual adjustment, respectively (Table S9; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414). Furthermore, in both pathways, the protective NIE remains significant for buffer radii of 50, 100, and 200 m, using the NDVI-150m threshold (0.104) to categorize the NDVI value at each radius (Table S9; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414).

In the mediation analysis conditioned by categories of neighborhood average monthly income of household heads, the protective NIE of high surrounding NDVI-150m through fetal growth restriction, in unadjusted and adjusted models, was significant only in the low category (<R$ 2251; <USD 432); and the protective NIE through placental inflammatory signs was not significant in any category (Table S10; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414). Additionally, Figure S2; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414, shows the geographic distribution of maternal residences according to the categories of neighborhood average monthly income of household heads.

In the mediation analysis conditioned by the categories of the mother’s time residing at current address, the protective NIE of high surrounding NDVI-150m through fetal growth restriction and through placental inflammatory signs were only significant in the “9 months or more” category (Table S11; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414), which involves most of the studied sample (85.5%).

In relation to residual confounding, the protective NIE through fetal growth restriction and through placental inflammatory signs, in unadjusted and adjusted models, showed sensitivity to unobserved confounders that affect mediator-outcome in the same direction, to unobserved confounders that affect exposure-mediator in opposite directions, and showed no sensitivity to unobserved confounders for exposure-outcome (Table S12; https://links.lww.com/EE/A414).

Discussion

In this study, high surrounding NDVI-150m was associated with lower risks of antepartum stillbirth, fetal growth restriction, and placental inflammatory signs; and was positively associated with neighborhoods with low average monthly income of household heads. Fetal growth restriction mediates between 38.5% and 41.5% the protective effect of high surrounding NDVI-150m on antepartum stillbirth, while placental inflammatory signs mediate between 14.4% and 16.7%.

The protective association of residential vegetation greenness against both antepartum stillbirth and fetal growth restriction agrees with previous studies.7–11 High residential vegetation greenness could mitigate the effect of environmental stressors that are associated with decreased placental function, which can lead to hypoxia of the fetus and fetal growth restriction.12–14 In concordance, Queiroz Diniz Oliveira et al34 showed a negative association between NDVI and air pollution in the MSP, Brazil, suggesting that regions with higher vegetation density have lower air pollution levels. Additionally, in the MSP, the higher density of vegetation is associated also to a lower ambient temperature,35 and extreme ambient temperature values can increase the risk of fetal growth restriction.36 These antecedents, along with the strong association between fetal growth restriction and stillbirth,37 support the mediation role of fetal growth restriction between residential vegetation greenness and antepartum stillbirth observed in our study.

On the other hand, the higher residential vegetation greenness also showed a protective association against placental inflammatory signs. Previous studies have reported that air pollution increases the presence of maternal inflammation biomarkers, such as interleukins and Tumor necrosis factor alpha.38 In particular, based on analyzing these biomarkers in the placenta, it was identified that gestational exposure to air pollution and to extreme ambient temperature were associated with placental inflammation.39–41 In turn, placental inflammatory lesions are associated with stillbirth.42 These antecedents support the mediation of placental inflammatory signs between residential vegetation greenness and antepartum stillbirth observed in our study.

In addition to air pollution and ambient temperature, other exposures such as noise and physical activity could be mediators in the identified pathways12; however, evidence regarding these exposures is still inconclusive.16,43 Furthermore, we did not find an independent association between NDVI-150m and physical activity during pregnancy.

We did not observe a serial mediation of placental inflammatory signs and fetal growth restriction between residential vegetation greenness and antepartum stillbirth, but rather individual mediations. This suggests that the protective effect of residential vegetation greenness on antepartum stillbirth would occur to a greater extent through two different pathways: by placental inflammatory signs or by fetal growth restriction. Additionally, the severe impairment of placental function could lead to antepartum stillbirth without sufficient time to affect fetal growth to the point of characterizing the fetus with fetal growth restriction.44

We found that the mediation of fetal growth restriction and of placental inflammatory signs between surrounding NDVI greater than 0.104 and antepartum stillbirth remained significant for buffer radii between 50 and 200 m. In the central zone of MSP, which is the highest income area, the greenness within these radii would be mainly related to the vegetation present in the residences, streets, and urban parks.20 Meanwhile, in the peripheral zone of MSP, which is the lowest income area, it can also be related to the presence of reminiscent Atlantic forests.20

The protective indirect effect of residential vegetation greenness through fetal growth restriction remained significant for NDVI estimated separately with satellite images of rainy and dry seasons. On the other hand, the protective indirect effect through placental inflammatory signs was only significant with images of the dry season, which is the season with the most favorable meteorological conditions for air pollutants.24 Therefore, this protective indirect effect would be mainly related to evergreen vegetation, which retains its foliage throughout the year, a characteristic present in Atlantic forests.45

We identified 0.090 and 0.099 as the minimum surrounding NDVI-150m values at which the indirect effect through fetal growth restriction and through placental inflammatory signs remained significant, respectively. This may be related to the fact that NDVI values less than 0.1 are usually not associated with vegetation, but with barren rock or sand.29 Furthermore, we did not identify an upper limit for the surrounding NDVI-150m; at the maximum value analyzed (0.154) in counterfactual mediation analysis, the indirect effects remained significant, and the mediated percentage appeared to be higher. These findings suggest that residential greenness requires a minimum density of green vegetation to have a protective effect, implying that the association between residential greenness and antepartum stillbirth may not be linear. This may be the reason why we did not observe a linear association between the continuous variable NDVI and antepartum stillbirth; a larger sample size might also be necessary. These observations are consistent with Yuan et al,11 who identified a nonlinear association between NDVI and stillbirth with a pattern resembling an inverted L-shape.

On the other hand, the indirect effect of two mediation pathways (by fetal growth restriction and by placental inflammatory signs) showed sensitivity to unobserved confounders, which affect the exposure-mediator in opposite directions and the mediator-outcome in the same direction. An example of an unobserved confounder for exposure-mediator could be proximity to highways, an indirect indicator of exposure to air pollution, which could be associated with less surrounding vegetation greenness and with a higher risk of fetal growth restriction.46 An example of an unobserved confounder for mediator-outcome could be genetic traits associated with fetal growth restriction or placental injury, which in turn increase the risk of stillbirth.47 Further studies are needed to assess the extent to which these potential unobserved confounders may affect the identified indirect effects.

We identified a positive association between residential vegetation greenness and houses located in social housing that agrees with the spatial distribution of social housing, which is mainly located in peripheral regions of the MSP.48 In addition, we identified a positive association between residential vegetation greenness and more vulnerable socioeconomic conditions, such as neighborhoods with low average monthly income of household heads. This association is consistent with the results of Arantes et al,20 which showed that peripheral zones of the MSP have higher tree cover and lower per capita income compared to the central zone. Furthermore, in the conditioned analysis, the protective indirect effect of residential vegetation greenness through fetal growth restriction was only significant in neighborhoods with low average monthly income of household heads. Previous studies, in Spain and New Zealand, also showed that green spaces only had a beneficial effect on birth weight and gestational age in individuals with lower socioeconomic status.49,50 Although our results are consistent with these studies, future ones are needed due to possible socioeconomic and environmental differences among populations.

Our results, by reinforcing the association between high residential vegetation greenness and lower risk of antepartum stillbirth, and by identifying mediation pathways between them, show the need for further studies both to deepen the characterization of the vegetation and its dispersion around residences and to identify potential confounders, moderators, and other mediators.

The main strength of this study was the inclusion of diverse exposures, including environmental, sociodemographic, behavior, comorbidity, placenta, and fetal data, to assess antepartum stillbirth risk in a population-based design. Another key feature is that this was a study that included data from 14 public hospitals in the MSP, Brazil. The amount and diversity of data give this study a pioneering approach in the stillbirth research area.

Our study had limitations. This is an observational case–control study, and, for this reason, we did not have precise information on the timing of the occurrence of each exposure during pregnancy. However, our objective was to identify potential causal pathways that are also supported by prior knowledge of studied exposures. Confirmation of those potential pathways requires a prospective study design.

NDVI has been widely used to measure the residential vegetation greenness, but it may not differentiate between certain vegetation types.29 Furthermore, climatic conditions can also influence the vegetation characterization, such as temperature and rainfall; to mitigate this limitation, we used the median value of NDVI across different seasons, and we conducted stratified analyses for NDVI estimated separately with satellite images of rainy and dry seasons.

The air pollution exposure variable included did not show an association with NDVI-150m or with antepartum stillbirth, as previously described.4 This lack of association may be related to the fact that it was estimated from the closest station to the mother’s residence, which had a median distance of 6198 m (p25: 4106 m, p75: 9300 m) in the studied sample. This implies less spatial resolution than the NDVI variables studied. Other factors may also influence the lack of association with stillbirth, for example, the median air pollution in the first trimester may not capture the presence of air pollution peaks; and both the mothers in the cases and controls were exposed to high levels of PM2.5 during pregnancy, exceeding the World Health Organization air quality guidelines.4 Future studies with air pollution measurements of greater spatial resolution are needed.

We defined variables that collapsed certain exposures, for example, infections and congenital malformations; however, the individual exposures collapsed would have different pathways to antepartum stillbirth. In the majority of these individual exposures, the number of women exposed was very small, which precluded their inclusion as individual variables.

Our data were collected in public hospitals located mainly in the periphery and deprived areas of the MSP, Brazil. For this reason, the population we studied would have a homogeneous socioeconomic profile that could have limitations in demonstrating the effects of social disparities compared to studies involving more heterogeneous populations.

In the sensitivity analysis, we found that the indirect protective effects were significant only for women who had lived in their current address for 9 months or more. The lack of significance in women who have lived in their residence for less than 9 months may be related both to the low number of women in that group and to a misclassification of the residential context. Additionally, we had no information on the amount of time women spent daily at their residences during pregnancy, and the possible effect of the residential context could be different between those who spend more and less time in their residences. Future studies are needed to assess potential differences related to daily travel to other locations, for example, for work or study.

The period covered by this study spans the COVID-19 pandemic. During the pandemic, changes occurred that may influence the analyzed pathways, for example, less access to health services, more unfavorable socioeconomic conditions, and less air pollution related to the decrease in vehicular traffic.51,52 Given that only one case and one control were recruited in 2019, it was not feasible to perform a sensitivity analysis in relation to the prepandemic period.

The exclusion of individuals with missing data could have induced a selection bias; however, we did not observe an association of the outcome, nor the exposures, with excluded individuals. Although three exposures showed significant unadjusted chi-square with excluded individuals, they were not associated with NDVI variables or the outcome; hence, they may not be found in the analyzed pathways.

Conclusions

We identified that higher vegetation greenness surrounding the maternal residence had a protective association against antepartum stillbirth. The main pathways through which residential vegetation greenness was associated with a lower risk of antepartum stillbirth were related to reduced risks of fetal growth restriction and the presence of placental inflammatory signs.

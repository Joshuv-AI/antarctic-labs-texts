Surgery Versus Surveillance: A Quality-of-Life Analysis of Patients With Moderately Dilated Aortic Root or Ascending Aorta
Authors: Ming Hao Guo, Jehangir J Appoo, Philippe Demers, Hafsa ES, Arminder Jassar, Himanshu J Patel, William Brinkman, Maral Ouzounian, Rony Atoui, Juan B Grau, John Bozinovski, Michael W A Chu, Nimesh Desai, Francois Dagenais, G Chad Hughes, Kevin Lachapelle, Zlatko Pozeg, Elaine E Tseng, Puja Kachroo, Richard Whitlock, Ismail El-Hamamsy, Cristian R Baeza, Anthony L Estrera, Eric Herget, George A Wells, Munir Boodhwani, TITAN:SvS investigators
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe impact of diagnosis and management of ascending thoracic aortic aneurysm (ATAA) on patients’ quality of life (QoL) is not well characterized. This study aims to compare QoL with surgery versus surveillance in patients with moderate ATAAs.MethodsTreatment In Thoracic Aortic aNeurysm: Surgery versus Surveillance is a multicentre randomized trial for patients with ATAA between 5.0 and 5.4 cm and includes a registry of patients who underwent either surgery or surveillance. From May 2019 to December 2022, 157 patients from 15 centres were enrolled in the surveillance (n = 105) or surgery (n = 52) registry and completed baseline and follow-up 36-item short form survey (SF-36) for QoL assessment. The primary outcomes were changes in physical component summary and mental component summary (MCS) scores. Propensity matching was performed to adjust for baseline differences.ResultsPatients in the surveillance arm were older and had higher prevalence of hypertension, dyslipidaemia, and atrial fibrillation (all P < 0.05). The overall mean duration between baseline and follow-up SF-36 questionnaire was 13.2 ± 3.6 months. After propensity match, compared to scores at baseline, patients in the surgery arm experienced significantly greater improvement in scores in MCS (4.1 ± 10.9 vs 0.1 ± 7.7; P = 0.04), general health (7.2 ± 17.6 vs –0.7 ± 14.5; P = 0.02), and mental health (9.5 ± 17.5 vs 1.4 ± 13.4; P = 0.01).ConclusionsIn a multicentre, prospective, non-randomized comparison of patients with moderate ATAAs, surgical treatment was associated with improvement in mental components of QoL at 1 year compared to surveillance.Keywords: thoracic aortic aneurysm, quality of life, mental health

INTRODUCTION

The management strategy of ascending thoracic aortic aneurysm (ATAA) and the corresponding guidelines recommendations has been based on a balance of risk of acute aortic events with surveillance versus the risk of surgery.1,2 While surgery is invasive and involves risks of perioperative mortality, stroke, and other surgical complications, it removes the aneurysmal aorta and eliminates risks of future adverse aortic events. On the other hand, surveillance avoids perioperative risks, but patients are left with longer-term risks of acute aortic syndrome and the associated anxiety and physical limitations of living with the disease. Current recommendations made to patients are driven largely by expected benefits in mortality that are inferred mostly from natural history data derived from single-centre studies and expected perioperative mortality and morbidity. There is little data available from comparative studies of surgery vs surveillance for ascending aortic aneurysms.

However, quality of life (QoL), which may be one of the most important end-points for patients, has not been well studied in this population as most efforts were placed on understanding and improving clinical outcomes. Therefore, QoL has not been included in the decision-making algorithm. Current studies reporting on QoL for patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm largely focused on QoL after aortic surgery, without comparative QoL assessment before surgery and without comparators to those not undergoing surgery.3 Since ATAAs are largely asymptomatic, having invasive surgery might cause a reduction in physical functioning, leave patients with bodily pain, or reduce vitality.4 On the other hand, living with aneurysmal disease could cause anxiety and adversely impact emotional health.5 Thus, the impact of a surveillance or surgical treatment on health-related QoL is unclear.

In this study, we aim to examine the differences in QoL between patients with moderately dilated ATAA who underwent either surgery or surveillance as an initial management strategy.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Patient population

This study was approved by institutional research ethics board (REB Approval: Protocol 20180007-01H, approved on November 25, 2020). Written informed consent for enrolment into the study and publication of the study data were acquired from individual patients, and the study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Helsinki Declaration. Treatment In Thoracic Aortic aNeurysm: Surgery versus Surveillance (TITAN: SvS) is a multicentre trial in which patients with ATAA between 5.0 and 5.4 cm were randomized to surveillance or surgery; the detailed design of the trial and enrolment criteria has been previously described.6 Patients who were not eligible for the trial or refused randomization were enrolled into a parallel prospective registry, which also included a surveillance arm and a surgical arm, and the 36-item short form survey (SF-36) were completed at baseline and at 1-year follow-up. In this as-treated analysis, patients were considered to be in the surgical arm if surgery was performed prior to completion of 1-year follow-up SF-36 questionnaire, and were considered to be in the surveillance arm if no surgical intervention was performed between baseline and 1-year follow-up QoL assessment. Patients who completed both baseline and 1-year QoL assessment were included in this analysis focused on QoL. Patients from the randomized controlled trial were not included in this study.

Data management

All data for the registry were prospectively collected by study staff at each site and case report forms were completed in a web-based trial management platform (DACIMA). Clinical and imaging follow-up was planned annually over the study period.

QoL: SF-36

Patient-reported QoL was assessed with the use of the Medical Outcomes Study 36-Item Short-Form Health Survey (SF-36).7,8 SF-36 evaluates 8 domains of health: physical functioning, role limitations due to physical problems, bodily pain, vitality, general health perception, social function, role limitations due to emotional problems, and mental health. Scores for each domain range from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better health status. In addition, 2 summary scores can be calculated to reflect overall physical (physical component summary [PCS]) and mental health (mental component summary [MCS]) with the use of norm-based methods that standardize the scores to a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10 (higher scores indicate better health status). The minimally clinically important difference is the smallest change in domain score that reflects a clinically meaningful difference in the patient’s QoL; this is usually reflected as a change of 0.2 to 0.5 standard deviation of the mean score, which corresponds to differences of 5 to 10 points for individual domains and differences of 2.5 to 5 points for the summary scores.9

Outcomes

The pre-specified primary outcomes of the study were the change in PCS and MCS score of the SF-36 questionnaire between baseline and at 1-year follow-up. Secondary QoL outcomes included changes in the 8 individual domains of the SF-36 between baseline and 1-year follow-up. Secondary clinical outcomes included short-term post-operative outcomes of the surgery arm, including mortality, stroke, reopening for bleeding, myocardial infarction, and dialysis, as well as mid-term outcomes on follow-up including mortality, acute aortic events, and hospital readmission.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were compared between the surveillance arm and the surgery arm with the use of the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and Student’s t-test for continuous variables, and the standardized difference for each variable was reported (Table 1). Clinical outcomes for the surgery arm were summarized using descriptive statistics with means and proportions. Propensity score matching with 1:1 greedy algorithm and a caliper of 0.1 was conducted with multivariable logistic regression model using pre-operative patient co-morbidities with standardized differences > 0.2, including age, history of hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, dyslipidaemia, diabetes, coronary artery disease, peripheral vascular disease, chronic renal disease, carotid artery disease, as well as baseline score for vitality, social functioning, mental health, PCS score, and MCS score. An absolute standardized difference of < 0.20 was considered as balanced. One-year QoL scores were compared between the 2 groups by mixed effect regression model adjusting for baseline scores. For the primary analysis, the mean difference and 95% confidence interval (CI) in QoL scores between baseline and 1-year were compared in the propensity-matched cohort. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Missing data in the included patients were infrequent (< 2% if present); therefore, they were not replaced with imputation, and the sample size was allowed to vary based on analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Analysis Systems software, version 9.3 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC) and GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA).

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

From May 2019 to December 2022, 225 consecutive patients were enrolled into the registry, of which 157 patients had documented completion of both baseline and 1-year follow-up SF-36 questionnaire, with 52 patients in the surgical arm of the registry and 105 patients in the surveillance arm of the registry (Figure 1). There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between those who were included and those who were not, with the exception that patients included had lower incidence of coronary artery disease (P = 0.03) (Table S1). Overall, 18.5% of patients were female, with no differences between the 2 groups (Table 1). Reason for exclusion from the TITAN: SvS trial included 86 (54.8%) patients who had ATAA of 5.0-5.4 cm but not eligible for randomization, 67 (42.7%) patients who had ATAA of 5.0-5.4 cm and eligible but refused randomization, and 4 (2.6%) patients who had ATAA ≥ 5.5 cm but unfit for surgery. Patients in the surveillance arm were older and had higher prevalence of hypertension, dyslipidaemia, and atrial fibrillation (all P < 0.05). At baseline, compared to the surveillance arm, patients in the surgery arm had lower scores in social functioning (88.7 ± 17.6 vs 80.8 ± 22.5; P = 0.02), mental health (80.5 ± 16.3 vs 73.8 ± 20.7; P = 0.03), and MCS (53.9 ± 8.3 vs 49.4 ± 12.4; P = 0.01), with no differences in other QoL domains. In the surgery arm, no in-hospital mortality, stroke, spinal cord ischaemia, or renal failure requiring dialysis was observed (Table 2).

Post-Operative Outcomes for the Surgery Arm

QoL outcomes

In the surgery arm, mean duration from completion of baseline SF-36 questionnaire to surgery was 2.4 ± 3.4 months, and mean duration from surgery to follow-up SF-36 questionnaire completion was 12 ± 2.1 months. The overall mean duration between baseline and follow-up SF-36 questionnaire was 13.2 ± 3.6 months. On follow-up, after adjusting for baseline SF-36 scores, the MCS score was 55.2 ± 7.2 for patients in the surgery arm and 52.9 ± 7.2 for patients in surveillance arm (P = 0.07), with no difference in PCS score (Table S2).

Propensity match returned 50 pairs of patients in the surgery and surveillance arm. There was no differences in baseline characteristics in the 2 cohorts (Table 1). There was also no significant differences in the baseline SF-36 scores between the 2 groups. On follow-up, compared to scores at baseline, patients in the surgery arm also experienced significantly greater improvement in scores in MCS (4.1 ± 10.9 vs –0.3 ± 7.4; P = 0.02), general health (7.2 ± 17.6 vs –0.8 ± 15.1; P = 0.02), mental health (9.5 ± 17.5 vs 0.4 ± 16.0; P = 0.01), and social functioning (8.5 ± 28.3 vs –1.5 ± 19.3; P = 0.04) (Table 3; Figures 2–4).

DISCUSSION

In this prospective, multicentre registry comparing patients with moderately dilated ATAA who underwent surveillance or surgery, we found a significant improvement in mental health, general health, as well as overall MCS scores at follow-up after surgery compared to surveillance.

Although it is widely believed that patients living with ATAA may experience more emotional burdens associated with the uncertain nature of the aneurysm and risk of acute aortic events, this is one of the first studies to demonstrate improvement in mental well-being of patients with ATAA after surgery compared to surveillance in moderately dilated ATAA. This is an important hypothesis generating study that raises the question of whether QoL as well as the degree of QoL improvement should be included in the decision-making algorithm when considering surgical intervention thresholds for patients with ATAA. Current guidelines for aortic intervention do not include QoL as a potential modifier for earlier intervention, mainly due to a lack of data in this area.1,2

Similar findings have been observed in the abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) literature, where for patients with AAA under surveillance, when compared to the general population, reported more physical and emotional limitations, while patients who underwent open repair had similar SF-36 scores to the general population.10 It has also been observed that patients with smaller AAA who underwent endovascular repair had significant improvement in the mental as well as physical scores at 6 months.11 In addition, previous studies have found comparable postoperative QoL after proximal aortic surgery to that of age- and sex-matched general population.12,13 However, a significant limitation in these studies was that no comparative group of the QoL for patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm followed by surveillance was examined. Ultimately, further studies where confounders are better controlled, such as a randomized controlled trial, would be important to confirm the impact of surgical intervention on QoL of patients with ATAA.

No difference in physical functioning was observed in the propensity-matched cohort between the 2 groups. This demonstrated adequate recovery from surgery in the surgical arm with no impact in daily physical activities and is further corroborated by no difference in bodily pain between the 2 groups. This is also consistent with other trials comparing cardiac surgery to lesser invasive interventions, where patients who underwent cardiac surgery generally experience a decrease in QoL early after surgery, which returns to baseline or even improves at 1-year follow-up.14 However, some previous studies in AAA have reported improvement in physical functioning after intervention, which was thought to be related to physical activity limitations associated with the fear of aneurysm rupture.10 It is possible that differences in physical functioning were not observed due to limited sample size; further studies with larger sample size would be required to better examine the impact of surgical intervention on physical health of patients with ATAA.

In our cohort, at baseline, patients in the surgery arm had lower social functioning, mental health, and MCS scores than the surveillance arm. It is possible that treatment allocation and the prospect of needing to undergo surgery may have had a negative impact on the physical and mental health of patients in the surgery arm. Therefore, balance in baseline QoL scores was attempted via propensity match to ensure differences in improvement in QoL observed was not simply due to lower initial QoL scores in the surgery arm from allocation bias.

This study also has several limitations. First, this is a non-randomized study where blinding is not possible, and therefore patients may report an improvement in QoL scores simply because they have received an intervention. However, as with any cardiac surgical intervention, it would be impossible to completely blind the patient as sham procedure would involve a sternotomy. Propensity match was used to select patients with similar baseline characteristics and scores; however, selection bias from initial treatment arm allocation and exclusion due to lack of follow-up may not be eliminated. Furthermore, other factors such as socioeconomic status and history of mental health concerns may not be accounted for. Second, patients assigned to the surgery arm may initially report lower baseline scores due to prospect of undergoing surgery, leading to a more exaggerated improvement in scores after the procedure. Third, SF-36 is not specifically designed for thoracic aortic aneurysm and therefore may not capture; however, it is well-validated in multiple specialties including patients with vascular disease. In addition, the QoL of the age- and gender-matched general population was not available for comparison. Fourth, the limited sample size may limit the ability to detect clinically meaningful differences in some QoL domains. Lastly, survivor bias may exist as only patients who survived at 1 year completed the 1-year follow-up SF-36 questionnaire and therefore included in the study.

CONCLUSION

In this non-randomized study, we showed that patients with moderately dilated ATAA demonstrate an improvement in the mental health component of QoL after surgery compared to surveillance.

LSTM-CNN hybrid model for E-commerce talent demand prediction and intelligent program optimization in vocational colleges under the double first-class initiative
Authors: Ji Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study develops an intelligent e-commerce talent demand prediction system built on a hybrid Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture, designed to support vocational college program optimization under China’s Double First-Class Initiative. The hybrid model pairs LSTM networks for temporal sequence modeling with CNN components for spatial feature extraction, processing multi-dimensional data drawn from job platforms, industry reports, and employment statistics. Experimental validation confirms that the hybrid approach outperforms traditional methods, with a 32.4% reduction in MAE, a 28.7% improvement in RMSE, and an R2 coefficient of 0.891—compared to standalone LSTM (R2 = 0.764) and CNN (R2 = 0.732) architectures. When pilot institutions adopted the model-guided curriculum adjustments across three vocational colleges, graduate employment rates rose by 15.3% and industry satisfaction scores increased by 21.6%, though these gains should be interpreted as observed associations rather than direct causal effects of the model itself. This work advances evidence-based educational planning and demonstrates practical viability for aligning vocational training with the shifting demands of the digital economy, while contributing to broader workforce development goals under current policy frameworks.

1. Introduction

Research background and motivation

The implementation of China’s Double First-Class Initiative has fundamentally transformed the landscape of higher vocational education, creating unprecedented demands for precision in talent cultivation and program optimization1. Under this policy framework, vocational colleges face mounting pressure to align their educational offerings with rapidly evolving industry requirements, particularly in the dynamic field of e-commerce where technological advancement and market demands shift with remarkable frequency2. The traditional approach of periodic curriculum adjustments based on retrospective analysis has proven inadequate for addressing the real-time talent needs of the digital economy, necessitating the development of intelligent prediction systems capable of forecasting future skill requirements and enabling proactive educational planning3.

The e-commerce sector’s explosive growth has created a complex ecosystem where talent requirements span multiple disciplines, from technical programming skills to digital marketing competencies, supply chain management, and data analytics capabilities4. This multifaceted demand structure challenges conventional educational planning methodologies, which typically rely on static industry surveys and historical employment data to guide curriculum development. The disconnect between educational output and industry needs has resulted in significant skill gaps, with graduates often lacking the specific competencies required by contemporary e-commerce enterprises while simultaneously experiencing difficulty in securing relevant employment opportunities.

Limitations of current prediction approaches

Existing talent demand prediction methodologies in vocational education suffer from several critical limitations that compromise their effectiveness in the rapidly evolving e-commerce landscape5. Traditional statistical approaches, primarily based on linear regression models and simple time series analysis, fail to capture the complex, nonlinear relationships that characterize modern talent markets. These methods typically assume stable relationships between predictor variables and outcomes, an assumption that proves particularly problematic in technology-driven sectors where disruption and innovation continuously reshape skill requirements.

Furthermore, conventional prediction models struggle with the temporal dependencies inherent in talent demand patterns, where historical trends may suddenly become irrelevant due to technological breakthroughs or market shifts6. The inability to effectively process and integrate diverse data sources, including social media trends, job posting analytics, industry reports, and economic indicators, limits the comprehensiveness and accuracy of current forecasting efforts. This data integration challenge is compounded by the heterogeneous nature of available information, which ranges from structured numerical data to unstructured text-based content requiring sophisticated natural language processing capabilities.

LSTM-CNN hybrid model advantages

The application of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks combined with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) represents a significant advancement in addressing the complexities of e-commerce talent demand prediction7. LSTM networks excel at capturing long-term dependencies in sequential data, making them particularly suitable for modeling temporal patterns in employment trends and skill evolution over extended periods. Their ability to selectively retain and forget information through sophisticated gating mechanisms enables the identification of relevant historical patterns while avoiding the degradation issues that plague traditional recurrent neural networks.

Convolutional Neural Networks complement LSTM capabilities by providing superior feature extraction from high-dimensional data sources, including textual job descriptions, industry reports, and social media content. The hierarchical feature learning characteristic of CNNs enables the automatic discovery of relevant patterns and relationships within complex, unstructured data sources that would be impossible to identify through manual feature engineering. When integrated in a hybrid architecture, these complementary strengths create a powerful prediction framework capable of processing diverse data types while maintaining sensitivity to temporal dynamics.

Research objectives and significance

This research aims to develop an intelligent prediction system for e-commerce talent demand that can provide accurate, timely forecasts to guide vocational college program optimization under the Double First-Class Initiative framework8. The primary objective involves designing and implementing a LSTM-CNN hybrid model capable of processing multiple data sources to generate reliable predictions of future skill requirements across different e-commerce specializations. Additionally, the study seeks to establish a systematic methodology for translating prediction results into actionable recommendations for curriculum adjustment, course development, and resource allocation decisions.

The significance of this research extends beyond technical innovation to address fundamental challenges in vocational education planning and workforce development. By providing educational institutions with reliable forecasting tools, this work contributes to reducing skill gaps, improving graduate employment outcomes, and enhancing the overall efficiency of talent cultivation processes. The intelligent adjustment framework developed in this study offers potential for widespread application across various vocational domains, supporting the broader objectives of educational modernization and industry-education integration promoted under current policy initiatives.

Innovation points and contributions

This study presents several key innovations in the intersection of artificial intelligence applications and vocational education planning. The integration of LSTM and CNN architectures in a unified prediction framework represents a novel approach to talent demand forecasting, addressing both temporal complexity and feature extraction challenges simultaneously. The development of an end-to-end system that connects prediction outputs directly to curriculum adjustment recommendations fills a critical gap between forecasting capabilities and practical educational implementation.

The research framework encompasses comprehensive data integration methodologies, advanced model architecture design, and practical deployment strategies for vocational education contexts. The paper systematically presents the theoretical foundation, model development process, experimental validation results, and implementation guidelines, providing a complete roadmap for institutions seeking to implement intelligent talent demand prediction systems. Through rigorous evaluation and case study analysis, this work demonstrates the practical viability and effectiveness of AI-driven approaches to educational planning in the contemporary digital economy landscape.

Related theory and technical foundation

Double first-class initiative and vocational college program setting theory

The Double First-Class Initiative represents a comprehensive policy framework designed to elevate the quality and competitiveness of Chinese higher education through strategic investment in world-class universities and disciplines9. Within the vocational education context, this initiative emphasizes the development of high-level institutions and high-quality programs that can effectively respond to national economic development needs and industrial transformation requirements. The policy framework mandates vocational colleges to establish dynamic program adjustment mechanisms that ensure educational offerings remain aligned with emerging industry demands and technological advancement trends.

The theoretical foundation of vocational program setting rests on the principle of industry-education integration, which requires continuous alignment between educational content and practical skill requirements in the workforce10. This alignment process involves systematic analysis of occupational competency frameworks, industry skill standards, and employer expectations to inform curriculum design and program structure decisions. The dynamic nature of modern industries, particularly in technology-driven sectors like e-commerce, necessitates flexible program adjustment mechanisms that can rapidly incorporate new knowledge areas and skill requirements while maintaining educational quality and coherence.

Program setting adjustment mechanisms in vocational education operate through multi-dimensional evaluation processes that consider market demand signals, employment outcome data, industry feedback, and technological development trends11. These mechanisms typically involve regular curriculum reviews, stakeholder consultation processes, and performance indicator monitoring systems to identify areas requiring modification or enhancement. The integration of predictive analytics and intelligent decision support systems represents an evolution of traditional adjustment approaches, enabling more proactive and data-driven program optimization strategies.

The theoretical basis for matching program settings with talent demand draws from labor economics principles, human capital theory, and systems thinking approaches that emphasize the interconnected nature of education and employment systems12. Human capital theory provides the conceptual framework for understanding how educational investments translate into productive capabilities, while labor market signaling theory explains the role of credentials and skill certifications in facilitating efficient job matching processes. These theoretical perspectives inform the development of program-industry alignment metrics and evaluation criteria used to assess the effectiveness of educational offerings.

International research on dynamic vocational program adjustment reveals diverse approaches to maintaining education-industry alignment, with successful models typically incorporating real-time labor market information systems, employer engagement platforms, and flexible curriculum delivery mechanisms13. European dual education systems demonstrate the effectiveness of close industry-education partnerships in maintaining program relevance, while emerging economies have increasingly adopted technology-enhanced approaches to talent demand forecasting and program optimization. These international experiences highlight the importance of institutional capacity building, stakeholder engagement, and systematic feedback mechanisms in supporting effective program adjustment processes.

Current domestic research focuses on developing intelligent program setting frameworks that can automatically identify skill gaps, predict emerging competency requirements, and generate recommendations for curriculum modifications14. These research efforts emphasize the integration of artificial intelligence techniques, big data analytics, and machine learning approaches to enhance the accuracy and timeliness of program adjustment decisions. The evolution from reactive to proactive program management represents a significant advancement in vocational education planning, supporting the broader objectives of the Double First-Class Initiative through enhanced educational quality and industry responsiveness.

LSTM neural network theory

Long Short-Term Memory networks represent a specialized variant of recurrent neural networks designed to address the vanishing gradient problem inherent in traditional RNN architectures when processing long sequences15. The fundamental innovation of LSTM lies in its sophisticated gating mechanism that enables selective information retention and forgetting through three primary gates: the forget gate, input gate, and output gate. These gates work in conjunction with a cell state vector that maintains long-term memory, allowing the network to capture dependencies across extended temporal sequences while avoiding the degradation issues that plague conventional recurrent architectures.

LSTM networks demonstrate superior performance in time series prediction tasks due to their ability to model complex temporal dependencies and nonlinear relationships within sequential data17. Unlike traditional statistical methods that assume linear relationships and stationary data distributions, LSTM architectures can adapt to changing patterns and capture intricate temporal dynamics that characterize real-world time series. The selective memory mechanism enables the network to retain relevant historical information while filtering out noise and irrelevant fluctuations, resulting in more accurate long-term predictions.

Parameter optimization in LSTM networks typically employs gradient-based algorithms such as Adam or RMSprop, which provide adaptive learning rates for different parameters and improve convergence stability18. Training procedures involve careful consideration of sequence length, batch size, and regularization techniques to prevent overfitting while maintaining generalization capability. Dropout mechanisms applied to recurrent connections and layer normalization techniques enhance training stability and model robustness, particularly when processing high-dimensional sequential data with complex patterns.

Recent applications of LSTM networks in talent demand prediction demonstrate significant potential for capturing labor market dynamics and employment trend forecasting19. These applications leverage the network’s ability to process multiple data sources simultaneously, including historical employment data, economic indicators, and textual job posting information. LSTM-based prediction models have shown notable improvements over traditional forecasting methods in terms of accuracy and temporal stability, particularly for medium to long-term prediction horizons where temporal dependencies play crucial roles.

The integration of attention mechanisms and bidirectional processing further enhances LSTM performance in talent demand forecasting by enabling the network to focus on relevant time periods and incorporate future information when available20. These architectural enhancements address specific challenges in workforce planning applications, where prediction accuracy directly impacts educational resource allocation and curriculum development decisions in vocational training contexts.

CNN convolutional neural network theory

Convolutional Neural Networks constitute a specialized class of deep learning architectures designed to process grid-structured data through hierarchical feature extraction mechanisms that mimic visual cortex processing patterns21. The fundamental architecture comprises multiple interconnected layers including convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers, each serving distinct computational functions in the feature learning pipeline. The network’s ability to automatically learn spatial hierarchies of features from raw input data eliminates the need for manual feature engineering, enabling end-to-end learning from low-level patterns to high-level abstractions.

The convolutional layer performs the core feature extraction operation through discrete convolution between input data and learnable filter kernels, generating feature maps that capture local patterns and spatial relationships. CNN architectures excel in feature extraction and pattern recognition applications due to their ability to learn hierarchical representations that progress from simple edge detection to complex object recognition23. The translation equivariance property of convolutional operations ensures consistent feature detection regardless of spatial positioning, while the shared parameter scheme significantly reduces model complexity compared to fully connected networks. These characteristics make CNNs particularly effective for processing high-dimensional data with spatial or temporal structure.

Fusion methodologies combining CNNs with other deep learning architectures have demonstrated enhanced performance across diverse applications24. CNN-RNN hybrid models leverage convolutional feature extraction capabilities alongside recurrent temporal modeling, while CNN-Transformer architectures integrate attention mechanisms with local feature learning. These fusion approaches address specific limitations of individual architectures by combining complementary strengths and enabling more comprehensive data processing capabilities.

CNN-based data mining has expanded well beyond image classification in recent years, reaching into text analysis, time series forecasting, multi-modal data processing, and even biomedical research where intelligent computational approaches guide targeted drug delivery optimization25,49,50. The successful transfer of convolutional architectures to non-image domains underscores the versatility of learned hierarchical features and their broad relevance for automated pattern discovery.

On the optimization front, contemporary CNN research has turned to depthwise separable convolutions, attention modules, and efficient network design strategies that seek a practical balance between computational cost and predictive power26. These innovations address real-world deployment constraints without sacrificing the core advantage of hierarchical feature learning, positioning CNNs as indispensable building blocks in complex machine learning pipelines for data mining and pattern recognition tasks.

LSTM-CNN hybrid model design for talent demand prediction

Hybrid model architecture design

The proposed LSTM-CNN hybrid model integrates the temporal modeling capabilities of Long Short-Term Memory networks with the feature extraction strengths of Convolutional Neural Networks to address the complex, multi-dimensional nature of e-commerce talent demand prediction27. The architecture design follows a parallel-serial connection strategy where LSTM and CNN components process different aspects of the input data simultaneously before feature fusion occurs in subsequent layers. This design philosophy enables the model to capture both temporal dependencies in historical demand trends and spatial patterns in multi-variate feature representations while maintaining computational efficiency and training stability.

Data preprocessing and feature engineering

The establishment of a comprehensive e-commerce talent demand data collection system forms the foundation for accurate prediction modeling, requiring integration of heterogeneous data sources including online job platforms, industry reports, salary surveys, and educational institution databases31. Specifically, primary data sources include: (1) major Chinese recruitment platforms (Zhaopin.com, 51job.com, and Liepin.com) providing 127,482 job postings through authorized API access spanning 2019–2024, (2) National Bureau of Statistics employment datasets (openly accessible at stats.gov.cn) containing quarterly workforce statistics, (3) China E-Commerce Research Center annual industry reports offering macro-level trend analysis, and (4) institutional employment tracking systems from 15 participating vocational colleges documenting graduate placement outcomes. The data collection framework implements Python-based automated web scraping algorithms with rate-limiting compliance for real-time job posting extraction, REST API interfaces for accessing structured employment databases with OAuth authentication, and scheduled batch downloads from government statistical portals using secure FTP protocols. Data integration employs a multi-stage pipeline: temporal alignment standardizes all observations to monthly intervals through interpolation, semantic field mapping harmonizes categorical variables across sources using manually curated dictionaries (e.g., standardizing job titles and skill terminology), duplicate detection removes redundant postings through fuzzy matching algorithms with 85% similarity threshold, and conflict resolution prioritizes authoritative sources (government data > platform data > survey data) when discrepancies arise.

This systematic multi-source approach covers an estimated 95% or more of regional e-commerce job market activity, with data currency maintained within a 48-h window and cross-source validation yielding 91.3% consistency across overlapping indicators. It is worth noting, however, that online recruitment platform data may overrepresent certain geographic regions, larger enterprises, and technology-intensive skill categories, while underrepresenting informal employment channels and smaller firms that recruit through offline networks. We acknowledge this sampling limitation and have partially mitigated it by supplementing platform data with government employment statistics and institutional tracking records, though residual bias may still influence prediction results in regions or sectors with lower digital recruitment penetration. These considerations should be kept in mind when interpreting the model outputs for educational planning decisions.

The optimization strategy incorporates early stopping mechanisms based on validation loss plateaus and learning rate scheduling that reduces learning rates when validation performance stagnates38. Gradient clipping prevents exploding gradients in the LSTM component while batch normalization stabilizes training in the CNN pathway. The training procedure monitors multiple convergence indicators including loss reduction rates, gradient magnitudes, and parameter update norms to ensure robust optimization across all model components.

Advanced optimization techniques include learning rate warm-up schedules that gradually increase learning rates during initial training phases, followed by cosine annealing schedules that facilitate fine-grained convergence near optimal solutions. The multi-stage training approach enables progressive complexity increase, starting with simplified architectures and gradually incorporating full model complexity as training stabilizes39. Weight initialization strategies are carefully selected based on activation function characteristics and network architecture requirements: Xavier initialization (also called Glorot initialization) is employed for CNN convolutional layers because it maintains consistent variance across layers when using sigmoid or tanh activations, preventing gradient vanishing in deep networks by setting initial weights from a distribution with variance proportional to the inverse of the number of input connections, as demonstrated by Glorot and Bengio (2010). For LSTM recurrent connections, orthogonal initialization is applied because it ensures that the recurrent weight matrix preserves vector norms during forward propagation, effectively preventing both exploding and vanishing gradients that commonly afflict recurrent architectures during backpropagation through time, as validated by Saxe et al. (2013) showing 15–20% faster convergence and improved final performance compared to random initialization. These principled initialization strategies establish favorable starting conditions that promote stable gradient flow during backpropagation, reducing training time by approximately 30% and improving final model convergence quality measured by validation loss plateaus.

Comprehensive parameter sensitivity analysis was conducted to quantify the impact of key hyperparameters on model performance and guide optimal configuration selection. Learning rate sensitivity experiments across the range [0.0001, 0.01] revealed optimal performance at α = 0.003, with performance degrading sharply beyond this value: increasing to 0.01 resulted in training instability with 23.4% higher validation loss due to overshooting minima, while decreasing to 0.0001 caused convergence slowdown requiring 2.8 × more epochs to reach equivalent performance levels. LSTM unit count analysis demonstrated diminishing returns beyond 128 units: configurations with 64 units achieved R2 = 0.862 (3.3% lower than optimal), 128 units reached peak performance at R2 = 0.891, while 256 units provided only marginal improvement to R2 = 0.894 at 1.9 × computational cost, indicating 128 units represents the optimal complexity-performance trade-off. CNN filter number sensitivity testing showed similar patterns: 32 filters yielded R2 = 0.877, 64 filters achieved optimal R2 = 0.891, and 128 filters improved marginally to R2 = 0.893 with 1.6 × training time increase. Dropout rate experiments across [0.1, 0.5] identified 0.3 as optimal, effectively preventing overfitting (training-validation loss gap reduced from 18.7% at dropout = 0.1 to 6.2% at dropout = 0.3) without excessive regularization that would impair learning capacity (dropout = 0.5 reduced R2 to 0.843). Batch size analysis confirmed 32 as optimal for our dataset size, balancing gradient estimate quality with computational efficiency: smaller batches (16) introduced excessive noise reducing R2 by 4.3%, while larger batches (128) provided negligible improvement but increased memory requirements prohibitively. These systematic sensitivity analyses validate the robustness of selected hyperparameter configurations and provide quantitative guidelines for adaptation to different dataset scales and computational resource constraints.

The fusion mechanism proves essential for achieving optimal performance, with alternative combination strategies including simple concatenation or weighted averaging producing inferior results compared to the learned gating approach45.

To shed light on how the model arrives at its predictions—a practical concern for educational planners who must trust and act upon the outputs—we conducted a feature importance analysis using permutation-based methods across the test set. Skill keyword features emerged as the single most influential predictor, with permutation-induced performance degradation exceeding 28%, which aligns with intuition: the specific technical and professional competencies demanded by employers directly shape hiring volumes. Average salary trends ranked second in importance (19.4% degradation), reflecting the signal that rising compensation in particular e-commerce sub-sectors carries about intensifying talent competition. Job posting count contributed 14.7% to prediction sensitivity, serving as a direct proxy for aggregate demand volume. Experience requirements and geographic region each showed moderate influence (8–11%), while company size had comparatively limited predictive weight (below 5%). These findings offer educational planners a transparent basis for understanding which labor market signals most strongly drive the model’s forecasts, enabling more informed curriculum prioritization decisions.

The analysis of prediction stability across different market volatility periods demonstrates the hybrid model’s robustness under varying economic conditions. During periods of high market uncertainty, the model maintains prediction reliability through effective integration of multiple information sources, while traditional methods exhibit increased prediction variance and reduced accuracy. This stability characteristic proves particularly valuable for educational planning applications where consistent forecasting performance enables reliable curriculum development and resource allocation decisions.

Computational efficiency analysis indicates acceptable training and inference costs relative to the performance improvements achieved, with the hybrid architecture requiring moderate computational resources compared to ensemble methods while delivering superior accuracy. The scalability assessment confirms practical applicability for real-world deployment scenarios in vocational education contexts where computational resources may be constrained but prediction accuracy remains critical for effective program planning.

Conclusion

This study set out to explore whether a hybrid LSTM-CNN architecture could meaningfully improve talent demand forecasting for e-commerce vocational education, and the experimental evidence suggests that it can. By combining LSTM temporal modeling with CNN spatial feature extraction, the hybrid approach consistently outperformed traditional forecasting methods and standalone neural network architectures across multiple evaluation metrics. These results affirm the practical potential of deep learning for workforce planning in fast-changing technology sectors, though we recognize that prediction accuracy alone does not guarantee successful educational outcomes.

On the theoretical side, the learnable fusion mechanism that dynamically balances LSTM and CNN contributions represents a meaningful departure from static combination strategies, and the comprehensive preprocessing pipeline offers a replicable template for integrating heterogeneous workforce data sources. Perhaps more importantly from a practical standpoint, the intelligent program adjustment framework translates quantitative forecasts into concrete curriculum recommendations, narrowing the persistent divide between analytical capability and actionable educational planning.

From a practical perspective, the framework equips vocational colleges with data-driven tools for proactive curriculum adjustment and resource allocation, moving beyond the reactive approach that has long characterized program management. Pilot implementations suggest measurable improvements in graduate employment outcomes and industry satisfaction, though these associations warrant cautious interpretation pending larger-scale validation.

Several limitations deserve candid acknowledgment. Model performance hinges on data quality, and as noted earlier, online recruitment platforms may systematically underrepresent certain regions and employment channels, introducing bias that could affect prediction reliability. The hybrid architecture demands non-trivial computational resources and technical expertise that may exceed the capacity of some vocational institutions. The current framework targets e-commerce specifically, and direct transfer to other vocational domains would require retraining with domain-appropriate data sources and revised feature engineering pipelines. Regional economic variation further complicates generalization across diverse geographic contexts.

Future work should pursue several directions to address these gaps. A high priority involves extending the framework to other vocational domains—manufacturing, healthcare, and financial services come to mind—where the core model architecture could plausibly transfer, though each domain would require its own feature ontology, data partnerships, and validation protocols. We believe the modular design of our preprocessing pipeline and the domain-agnostic nature of the LSTM-CNN fusion mechanism provide a reasonable starting point for such adaptations, but the effort required should not be underestimated. On the architectural side, incorporating transformer-based attention mechanisms and exploring real-time data stream integration would sharpen both prediction accuracy and system responsiveness. Federated learning approaches also hold promise for enabling cross-institutional collaboration without compromising data privacy, which remains a sensitive concern in educational contexts.

Beyond the technical contributions, this research speaks to broader societal and educational concerns. The intelligent prediction system tackles workforce development challenges central to China’s digital economy transition by tightening the feedback loop between education and industry, which in turn may reduce structural unemployment and expand opportunity for vocational college graduates who have traditionally faced steeper employment hurdles. Early identification of emerging skill requirements can also help institutions prepare students with competencies that complement rather than compete with automation, an increasingly pressing consideration as AI adoption accelerates.

From an educational standpoint, the framework advances Double First-Class Initiative objectives by replacing subjective program assessments with empirically grounded continuous improvement cycles. The reliance on cloud computing and open-source tools lowers the resource threshold for adoption, potentially democratizing access to predictive analytics that were once the province of better-resourced institutions. We are cautiously optimistic that approaches like the one presented here can strengthen vocational education’s societal relevance and practical orientation without compromising humanistic values or academic rigor—though realizing that potential will depend on thoughtful implementation that respects student agency and maintains pedagogical integrity.

Additional research opportunities worth pursuing include incorporating macroeconomic indicators and policy variables to improve prediction robustness, advancing interpretable machine learning techniques so that educational planners can better understand and trust model outputs, and exploring multi-objective optimization that simultaneously balances pedagogical quality, employment outcomes, and institutional resource constraints. The move toward more intelligent educational planning systems is still in its early stages, but the evidence gathered here suggests it holds genuine promise for supporting evidence-based decision making in vocational education—contributing, ultimately, to more effective workforce development and broader social equity in the digital economy era.

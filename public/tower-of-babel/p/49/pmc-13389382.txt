Correction for Sudhakar et al., Human tumstatin and human endostatin exhibit distinct antiangiogenic activities mediated by αvβ3 and α5β1 integrins
Published: 2026

Correction for “Human tumstatin and human endostatin exhibit distinct antiangiogenic activities mediated by αvβ3 and α5β1 integrins” by Akulapalli Sudhakar, Hikaru Sugimoto, Changqing Yang, Julie Lively, Michael Zeisberg, and Raghu Kalluri, which was first published April 7, 2003; 10.1073/pnas.0730882100 (Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. 100, 4766–4771).

The authors note, “Concerns were brought to our attention regarding blots that appeared similar in Figs. 4C and 5C. The original blots are no longer available, and so we repeated the experiments to confirm the findings that recombinant human tumstatin functions via αVβ3 integrin to inhibit phosphorylation of Akt, while recombinant human endostatin functions independent of the activation of Akt. The results, which are available at https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.09.731223v1, support the original conclusion that the antiangiogenic activity of tumstatin and endostatin use different signaling mechanisms.”

The editors evaluated the new experimental results and approved the correction.

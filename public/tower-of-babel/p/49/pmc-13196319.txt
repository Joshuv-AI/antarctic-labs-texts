Response to the Letter to the Editor entitled “Kidney Outcomes With Rituximab Versus Cyclophosphamide in Crescentic ANCA-Associated Glomerulonephritis: Contextualizing Observational Findings”
Authors: Federica Mescia, Martina Uzzo, Federico Alberici
Published: 2026

The Author Replies:

We thank Brilland and Augusto1 for their thoughtful comments on our work “Kidney Outcomes in ANCA-Glomerulonephritis According to Induction Immunosuppression and Histopathology.”2

As highlighted by Brilland and Augusto,1 and as already discussed in our manuscript, we fully acknowledge that our study has important limitations. We therefore agree that our main finding—namely, that patients with crescentic class antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies-associated -glomerulonephritis receiving rituximab monotherapy may have worse kidney outcomes than those treated with cyclophosphamide-containing regimens—should be considered hypothesis-generating.

Brilland and Augusto also note that our results appear to contrast with several large observational studies comparing rituximab and cyclophosphamide in patients with severe renal involvement, in which no significant differences in kidney outcomes, according to induction treatment, were observed.3, 4, 5, 6 However, we would like to highlight that most of these studies did not include kidney biopsy features in their analysis.3, 4, 5 The only study incorporating histological parameters,6 while clearly demonstrating the predictive value of chronic histological lesions for kidney recovery, did not evaluate kidney outcomes across different induction regimens stratified by histology, which is the approach adopted in our work.

We hypothesize that cyclophosphamide may yield better outcomes than rituximab monotherapy specifically in cases characterized by a substantial proportion of active crescentic lesions, where more rapid and profound control of glomerular inflammation may be critical for optimal recovery of kidney function. Such a signal of superior cyclophosphamide efficacy may only emerge when analyses are stratified according to kidney histology, and may otherwise be obscured in the overall cohort—indeed, this was also the case in our dataset (Supplementary Table S4 in the original article).

In conclusion, we hope that our report will provide a rationale for stimulating further research in this field and contribute to the generation of more data on kidney outcomes in antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies-associated glomerulonephritis, stratified by histological features and emerging biomarkers.

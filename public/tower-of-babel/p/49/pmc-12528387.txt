Spin texture in Bose–Einstein condensates confined in spin-dependent optical lattices
Authors: Shu-Song Wang, Su-Ying Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe investigate the ground state of rotating two-component Bose–Einstein condensates in the combined potential well of one-dimensional spin-dependent optical lattice potential and harmonic potential. The density distribution of the condensate is striped. By defining the generalized momentum operator, we derive the velocity distribution of the condensate. The velocity contours are concentric circles for each stripe, with different centers for different stripes. This velocity distribution corresponds to a specific spin texture structure. In this texture, the azimuthal angle of the magnetic moments varies along the y-direction, while the z-component of the magnetic moments varies along the x-direction. When the lattice period , the texture is divided into two regions. The magnetic moments reverse in a counterclockwise direction and in a clockwise direction as y increases in regions I and II, respectively. Furthermore, when the lattice period , the texture is divided into three regions. The magnetic moments reverse in a counterclockwise direction, do not reverse, and reverse in a clockwise direction as y increases in regions I, II, and III, respectively. Accurate manipulation of the texture can be achieved by adjusting the optical lattice periods and , the relative phase , and the rotational angular frequency .Subject terms: Atomic and molecular physics, Ultracold gases

Introduction

Ultracold atomic Bose–Einstein condensates (BECs) are extremely manipulable, providing a completely new platform for the study of macroscopic quantum phenomena. Topological excitations are essential manifestations of macroscopic quantum phenomena. There are various topological excitations in Bose–Einstein condensates, such as fractional vortices1–5, half-quantised vortices6,7, solitons8–16, monopoles17,18, vortex lattices19,20 and spin textures21–26.

Spin textures are employed in the study of magnetic materials27, director fields of liquid crystals28, field theoretic models of particles29, models of the early Universe30 and other condensed matter systems. Multicomponent Bose–Einstein condensates can act as analogues of these condensed matter systems, enabling the research of phenomena that may be unavailable for experimental observation26. The topologically nontrivial spin texture is a intuitive reflection of the magnetic properties of multicomponent BECs31. Usually, there is a specific correspondence between the texture structure and the vortex structure. For example, the Anderson Toulouse vortex normally corresponds to a skyrmion32, while the Mermin-Ho vortex corresponds to a meron pair33. This provides a feasible method to manipulate spin textures.

The external potential has an essential influence on the vortex structure. In one-dimensional (1D) optical lattice potential, the density of condensate is usually striped31,34, and vortices are usually located at the lattice peaks when the potential confinement is strong enough. In recent years, researchers have successfully generated spin-dependent optical lattices35–42 using two counter-propagating lasers with distinct polarizations, resulting in atoms in different internal states experiencing drastically varied exterior potentials. Spin-dependent optical lattices provide a novel framework for investigating the ground state properties of BECs and generating new textural structures.

In this paper, we study the ground state properties of rotating BECs in spin-dependent optical lattices. We obtain the density distribution of the condensate, which is striped with x-direction in real space and with -direction in momentum space. By defining the generalized momentum operator, we derive the velocity distribution of the condensate. We find that the velocity contours of each stripe are concentric circles, with their center and the coordinate origin exhibiting symmetry with respect to the line where the center of the stripe is located.

This velocity distribution corresponds to a special pseudo-spin texture structure: the azimuthal angle of the magnetic moment is changes along the y-direction, whereas the z-component of the magnetic moment is changes along the x-direction. When the lattice periods and satisfy , the texture is divided into region I and region II, with the magnetic moments reversing in a counterclockwise direction in region I and the magnetic moments reversing in a clockwise direction as y increases in region II. By adjusting the rotation angle frequency , we can manipulate the period of the texture, and by adjusting the lattice relative phase , it is possible to make textures display different periods in the two regions. When the lattice period and , together with the relative phase of the lattice satisfy and , the texture is divided into region I, region II, and region III, with the magnetic moments reversing in a counterclockwise direction as y increases in region I, the magnetic moments not reversing in region II, and the magnetic moments in a clockwise direction as y increases in region III. We calculate the topological charge carried by the spin texture in the system and find that each period of the spin texture carries exactly one unit of topological charge.

This paper is organized as follows. In section “Density and supercurrent velocity”, we discuss the density distribution and velocity distribution of Bose–Einstein condensates in spin-dependent optical lattices by defining the generalized momentum operator. In section “Spin textures and topological charges”, we derive several different spin texture structures corresponding to different periods and relative phases of the optical lattices, and the manipulation of the texture can be realized by adjusting the rotational angular frequency and the relative phase of the initial wave function. We calculate the topological charge carried by the spin texture for each period. Section “Discussion” is the conclusion.

Discussion

We have studied the ground-state specificity of BECs in spin-dependent optical lattices. The condensate density is striped in the x-direction in real space, while it is striped in the -direction in momentum space. By defining the generalized momentum operator, we approximate the velocity distribution of the condensate. We find that in each stripe, the velocity contours are concentric circles centered at for component I and at for component II, where and are the horizontal coordinates at the peak of the density stripes for components I and II, respectively.

Considering the two-component BECs as pseudospin systems, we find that the azimuthal angle of the magnetic moments changes in the y-direction, and the z-component of the magnetic moments changes in the x-direction. For the optical lattice period , the texture is divided into regions I and II. Within region I, where , the magnetic moments reverse in a counterclockwise direction as y increases, whereas within area II, where , the magnetic moments reverse in a clockwise direction as y increases. When , the period of the texture in region I, and the period of the texture in region II satisfy with . When , the textures in region I and region II exhibit different periods. When the inter-component interactions, which can be modulated by Feshbach resonance, are small, the texture periods and conform to the relation for and for . For and the texture is divided into regions I, II, and III. The magnetic moments reverse in a counterclockwise direction, do not reverse, and reverse in a clockwise direction as y increases in regions I, II, and III, respectively. Each period of the spin texture carries one unit of topological charge. The spin textures formed in spin-dependent optical lattice potential are highly manipulable and will be of wide interest to physicists.

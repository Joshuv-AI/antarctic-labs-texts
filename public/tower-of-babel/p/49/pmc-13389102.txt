Strengthening Adolescent E-CIGARETTE Prevention Through Digital Literacy and Responsible Artificial Intelligence
Authors: Darin Jan Tindowen, Djovi Durante, Richster John Macaballug, Flordeliza Gecobe, Joven Jacinto
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the article by Jackman et al, 1 which demonstrated that perceived harm significantly moderates the relationship between social media engagement and past 30-day e-cigarette use among U.S. adolescents. Their findings provide important evidence that educational messaging addressing both social media exposure and the health consequences of e-cigarette use may strengthen tobacco prevention efforts among youth. 1 As digital platforms increasingly influence adolescents’ health behaviors, the study offers valuable insights for developing more responsive prevention strategies.

While we agree with the authors’ recommendation to tailor educational messaging, 1 we believe that effective prevention should also address an upstream determinant of adolescents’ harm perceptions—digital literacy. Digital literacy encompasses the knowledge and skills needed to critically access, evaluate, and apply health information encountered through digital platforms. 2 Adolescents are continuously exposed to vaping-related content through influencers, user-generated videos, targeted advertising, and algorithm-driven recommendations, many of which may normalize or promote e-cigarette use.1,3 Consequently, increasing awareness of e-cigarette harms alone may be insufficient if adolescents lack the competencies to recognize persuasive marketing techniques, identify misinformation, and distinguish evidence-based health information from commercially motivated content.2,3

Schools are uniquely positioned to translate this preventive approach into practice. Beyond delivering conventional tobacco education, schools can integrate digital literacy within health education curricula, enabling students to critically evaluate online health information while fostering informed decision-making regarding tobacco and nicotine products. 2 Such an educational approach complements existing prevention initiatives by addressing the digital environments that increasingly shape adolescents’ beliefs and behaviors.

Responsible artificial intelligence (AI) presents an emerging opportunity to enhance these educational efforts. AI-supported educational platforms can provide interactive learning experiences, personalized feedback, and simulated analyses of online vaping-related content, enabling students to practice evaluating the credibility of digital health information. 4 When implemented with appropriate educational oversight, transparency, and ethical safeguards, AI may strengthen digital literacy while supporting teachers in delivering engaging and evidence-informed tobacco prevention education.4,5 Importantly, AI should be regarded as a complementary educational tool rather than a replacement for comprehensive school-based tobacco prevention programs.

Jackman et al 1 have advanced our understanding of how perceived harm influences adolescent e-cigarette use in the context of social media engagement. Building upon their findings, integrating digital literacy and responsibly implemented AI into school-based tobacco prevention initiatives may further enhance adolescents’ capacity to navigate increasingly sophisticated digital environments and resist online influences that normalize or promote e-cigarette use.

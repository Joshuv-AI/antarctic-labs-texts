Comparative efficacy of sleeve gastrectomy with fundoplication versus standard sleeve gastrectomy in obesity and gastroesophageal reflux disease: A randomised trial
Authors: Pierdiwasi Maimaitiyusupu, Aikebaier Aili, Maimaiti Yisireyili, Yiliang Li, Kelimu Abudureyimu, Xin Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsThis randomised controlled trial compared the efficacy of modified laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy with fundoplication (LSGFD) versus standard laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG) in achieving weight loss and alleviating gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) in patients with obesity.Materials and MethodsEighty patients with obesity (body mass index [BMI] ≥27.5 kg/m2 with comorbidities or ≥32.5 kg/m2) with mild‐to‐moderate GERD were randomised to LSGFD (n = 27) or LSG (n = 53). Assessments included weight, BMI, waist–hip ratio, percentage of excess weight loss (%EWL), GERD Questionnaire scores, endoscopy, lower oesophageal sphincter (LES) pressure and DeMeester scores preoperatively and at 6–12 months postoperatively. Fundoplication type (180° Dor, 270° Toupet or 360° Nissen) was intraoperatively adjusted based on LES pressure. Statistical analyses used t‐tests, analysis of variance and chi‐square tests (SPSS v19.0).ResultsNo significant differences were observed between the two groups in preoperative parameters. However, at 6 and 12 months postoperatively, both groups showed significant reductions in body weight, BMI, waist circumference, hip circumference and waist–hip ratio, along with a significant increase in the %EWL. The LSGFD group demonstrated superior outcomes in treating GERD and other obesity‐related comorbidities, particularly notable improvements in reflux esophagitis.ConclusionsLSGFD provides equivalent weight loss to LSG but significantly superior control of GERD symptoms and resolution of reflux esophagitis. It offers enhanced management of obesity‐related comorbidities without increasing surgical risk, supporting its use in obese patients with GERD.Keywords: bariatric surgery, fundoplication, gastroesophageal reflux disease, obesity, randomised controlled trial

INTRODUCTION

Obesity is one of the major public health challenges facing modern society. It is defined based on body mass index (BMI), with a BMI equal to or greater than 30 classified as obesity. 1 According to the latest projections from the World Obesity Atlas 2025, by the year 2030, nearly 1.1 billion adults worldwide will be classified as obese, and approximately 3 billion adults—around 50% of the global adult population—will be affected by high BMI (including both overweight and obesity), indicating a continuing upward trend in obesity prevalence. 2 Obesity not only reduces the quality of life but is also closely associated with various chronic diseases, including type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, hypertension and several types of cancer. 3 , 4 Surgical treatment has become one of the most effective approaches for managing severe obesity and its comorbidities, especially when other therapies prove ineffective. 5 , 6 , 7 Laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG), a commonly performed bariatric procedure, reduces food intake by resecting a large portion of the stomach and has been widely applied in clinical practice. 8 , 9

Patients with obesity often present with multiple comorbidities, among which gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a common gastrointestinal complication. GERD is characterised by the reflux of gastric acid or stomach contents into the oesophagus, leading to symptoms such as heartburn, retrosternal pain and dysphagia. 10 , 11 , 12 Chronic GERD not only impairs quality of life but may also result in esophagitis or even oesophageal cancer. 13 , 14 Although traditional LSG has demonstrated significant efficacy in weight loss, it may worsen GERD symptoms in some patients. 15 , 16 Therefore, achieving effective weight reduction while simultaneously controlling or alleviating GERD symptoms remains a critical challenge in the treatment of obesity.

To address the issue of GERD potentially caused by conventional LSG, medical researchers have modified the procedure and developed the modified laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy with fundoplication (LSGFD). 15 In the LSGFD procedure, sleeve gastrectomy is followed by fundoplication, which allows for better preservation of the gastric fundus and optimises the anti‐reflux effect. 17 While maintaining the original weight loss efficacy, this surgical innovation reduces the incidence of GERD, notably by adjusting the anatomical structure of the lower oesophagus and the gastroesophageal junction. 18 , 19 Preserving the gastroesophageal junction helps maintain lower oesophageal sphincter (LES) function and reduces gastric acid reflux. Additionally, reducing gastric volume while retaining normal anatomical alignment lowers intragastric pressure, further mitigating GERD. 20 These anatomical and physiological enhancements, absent in traditional LSG, improve safety, patient tolerance and satisfaction. Clinical studies have shown that LSGFD effectively alleviates GERD symptoms while maintaining weight loss efficacy and procedural feasibility. 17 , 21 With ongoing advances in technique and surgical refinement, LSGFD offers a more rational and practical treatment option for patients with obesity.

Although LSGFD has many theoretical advantages, most existing studies have focused on its short‐term outcomes, and there is no widespread consensus regarding its long‐term efficacy or role in managing comorbidities. Moreover, evaluations of the procedure's effects on specific comorbidities, such as GERD, remain insufficiently detailed. Therefore, this study aimed to fill this research gap by proposing a well‐defined scientific question: to compare the efficacy of conventional LSG and LSGFD in treating obesity and managing associated comorbidities through a rigorously designed randomised controlled trial.

The primary objective of this study was to compare the effectiveness of LSG and LSGFD in managing obesity, controlling GERD and addressing related comorbidities. Differences in weight loss, symptom relief and quality of life were evaluated to provide clinically relevant evidence for surgical decision‐making. For patients with concurrent obesity and GERD, LSGFD may offer a more effective treatment option. The findings also support further innovation and optimisation in bariatric surgical techniques, contributing to the development of comprehensive obesity management strategies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and sample size calculation

This study was designed as a prospective, randomised, controlled trial to compare the efficacy of modified LSGFD versus standard LSG in terms of weight loss and GERD control. The primary outcome measures included weight‐related indicators (body weight, BMI and percentage of excess weight loss [%EWL]) and GERD‐related indicators (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease Questionnaire (GERD‐Q) score, LES pressure, endoscopic evaluation and DeMeester score).

Sample size estimation was based on the expected improvement in GERD‐Q scores, which reflect subjective relief from GERD symptoms. According to preliminary observations, the mean improvement in GERD‐Q score at 6 months postoperatively was approximately 2 points in the LSGFD group, with no significant change observed in the LSG group. Based on this anticipated difference (mean difference ≈2.0, pooled standard deviation (SD) ≈2.0, effect size d ≈1.0), the required sample size was calculated using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7). With a two‐sided significance level of α = 0.05 and a statistical power of 80%, the minimum sample size was estimated to be 25 participants per group using an independent‐samples t‐test. The estimation method was referenced from prior research. 22 To account for potential intraoperative conversion of surgical approach and patient dropout during follow‐up, the actual enrollment exceeded the minimum requirement.

Patient selection and grouping

This study ultimately included 80 patients with obesity and mild‐to‐moderate GERD who were hospitalised in the Department of Minimally Invasive Surgery at the People's Hospital of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region between December 2017 and December 2019. All patients underwent bariatric and metabolic surgery voluntarily. Inclusion criteria were based on the Asian diagnostic standards for obesity, with concurrent typical symptoms of GERD. Exclusion criteria included pregnancy or lactation, history of upper gastrointestinal surgery and severe organic diseases.

Patients were randomly assigned to the LSGFD or LSG group using a computer‐generated sequence in a 1:1 ratio. In cases where intraoperative findings revealed poor blood supply to the fundoplication flap or other technical difficulties, the procedure was converted to standard LSG. As a result, 27 patients underwent LSGFD and 53 patients underwent standard LSG. All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment. This trial was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No. ChiCTR1800015952) and approved by the Ethics Committee of the People's Hospital of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region.

Inclusion criteria

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (1) Asian ethnicity with obesity (BMI ≥32.5 kg/m2) or overweight with obesity‐related conditions (BMI ≥27.5 kg/m2) such as obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease, hyperuricemia or polycystic ovary syndrome; (2) diagnosis of GERD with typical symptoms such as acid reflux, heartburn and related manifestations 23 ; (3) absence of organic heart disease, including myocardial infarction and heart failure; (4) absence of acute or chronic infections; (5) failure of prior lifestyle interventions, exercise and conservative medical treatments; (6) willingness to provide informed consent and ability to complete the study.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded from the study based on the following criteria: (1) drug abuse, alcohol addiction, uncontrolled psychiatric disorders or inability to understand the risks, benefits and expected outcomes of bariatric surgery; (2) failure to meet the previously mentioned diagnostic criteria for GERD; (3) presence of severe organic disease affecting the gastrointestinal tract or other systemic infections, or having malignant tumours; (4) surgical contraindications, such as cardiac dysfunction or diseases affecting the lungs, liver, kidneys or other vital organs, which could result in intolerance to general anaesthesia or contraindications to increased intra‐abdominal pressure; (5) history of peptic ulcer, gastric cancer, Barrett's oesophagus, oesophageal cancer or previous gastrointestinal surgery; (6) oesophageal motility disorders, such as spastic motility disorders; and (7) unwillingness to participate in the study.

A flowchart of the inclusion and exclusion criteria for this prospective study is presented in Figure S1.

Treatment methods

LSGFD group

Under general anaesthesia, the LSGFD procedure was performed using a combination of LSG and fundoplication. Patients were supine with the head elevated, legs apart and the body tilted to the right with feet lowered. A four‐port technique was used. An ultrasonic scalpel and non‐traumatic forceps were employed. A 2‐mm skin incision was made below the xiphoid process to insert a self‐made S‐shaped retractor from a thick iron wire. A latex tube was affixed to the tip to retract the left lobe of the liver, exposing the greater curvature of the stomach, the oesophagus and the cardia. Sleeve gastrectomy was performed using a 36‐French bougie as a guide and support, with the resection proceeding along the greater curvature of the stomach. The bougie provided structural support to the stomach and oesophagus (Figure S2). During the procedure, resection margins were carefully controlled, and continuous suturing techniques were used to minimise the risk of postoperative complications.

In addition, to ensure the reliability of the weight loss outcome, the preserved lesser curvature of the stomach was maintained at a width of 3–4 cm and a length of 3–8 cm, or sufficient for fundoplication (as individual variations in oesophageal thickness and different types of fundoplication require different lengths of the preserved lesser curvature). The preserved portion resembled the shape of a fish fin. To prevent postoperative bleeding and fistula formation, the remnant gastric incision was sutured continuously with absorbable sutures, except for the stapling line. Finally, based on the preoperative assessment of reflux severity and LES pressure, the preserved gastric fundus was folded at 180°, 270° or 360°, respectively. Removing the gastric specimen and placing a drainage tube, followed by standard gastrectomy procedures (Figures S3 and S4). LES pressure was measured intraoperatively using a high‐resolution manometry (HRM) system. The pressure catheter was inserted through the nasal passage into the oesophagus and positioned at the LES region. LES resting pressure was measured multiple times while the patient remained relaxed to obtain stable readings and provide objective data for surgical adjustment. 24 Based on intraoperative LES pressure, the severity of reflux symptoms and the patient's swallowing function tolerance, the fundoplication type was adjusted accordingly. If the patient exhibited significant reflux symptoms and low LES pressure, a 360° Nissen fundoplication was chosen to maximise reflux control. If there was a higher risk of dysphagia, a 180° Dor or 270° Toupet fundoplication was preferred to balance reflux control with preservation of swallowing function. 25

LSG group

The general procedure was as follows: pneumoperitoneum was established. After general anaesthesia was administered, the patient was placed in the supine position with the head elevated, feet lowered and the body tilted to the right. Standard surgical disinfection and sterile draping were performed. A 1–2 cm transverse incision was made approximately 3–5 cm above the umbilicus. Through this incision, a trocar was inserted to insufflate gas and establish an artificial pneumoperitoneum, with intra‐abdominal pressure maintained at 12–15 mmHg, as determined by visual assessment of the abdominal cavity after insufflation. Exposure of the surgical field: A 10‐mm trocar was inserted through the 3–5 cm supraumbilical incision to introduce the laparoscope and examine for intra‐abdominal injury, liver abnormalities (e.g., fatty liver changes) and signs of pathology involving the spleen, gastrointestinal tract or pelvic cavity. Subsequently, a 12‐mm trocar was placed approximately 7 cm above the umbilicus at the lateral edge of the right rectus abdominis, serving as the main operating port. This port allowed adequate exposure of the gastric fundus and the greater curvature of the stomach. The greater curvature was transected under the guidance of an orogastric bougie placed along the lesser curvature, starting 3–5 cm from the pylorus and continuing along the greater curvature (Figure S2).

Most of the greater curvature and the gastric fundus were resected using a laparoscopic linear cutting and stapling device. In contrast, the lesser curvature and the antrum were preserved to form a tubular gastric structure. The remaining gastric staple line was reinforced using continuous sutures with absorbable thread to prevent bleeding and gastric leakage. Subsequently, the air was insufflated into the stomach via the gastric tube while the staple line was submerged in water to check for any air leaks or active bleeding along the internal suture line.

Evaluation criteria for weight loss effectiveness

The effectiveness of weight loss was categorised into four levels based on the %EWL: %EWL ≥75% was considered excellent, %EWL ≥50% was considered good, %EWL ≥25% was considered effective and %EWL <25% was considered treatment failure.

GERD symptom relief assessment criteria

Symptom assessment was conducted using the GERD‐Q, which includes six items: heartburn, regurgitation, acid reflux, upper abdominal pain, nausea and sleep disturbance, scored on a 0–3 scale based on symptom frequency over the preceding week. Participants completed the questionnaire at baseline and at 6 and 12 months postoperatively under the guidance of surgeons and gastroenterologists. Objective evaluation included endoscopy, HRM for LES pressure and 24‐h oesophageal pH monitoring at the same time points. Endoscopic esophagitis was graded on a 0–4 scale. Normal LES pressure was defined as 20–40 mmHg, with values below indicating dysfunction. The DeMeester score, derived from pH monitoring, incorporated acid exposure time, reflux frequency and duration; scores >14.72 indicated pathological reflux.

Postoperative comorbidity incidence

Post‐discharge comorbidities primarily included hiatal hernia, gastric leakage, bleeding and residual gastric ulcers. The incidence of postoperative comorbidities was calculated as the number of post‐discharge comorbidity cases divided by the total number of cases, multiplied by 100.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version 19.0, IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range), depending on distribution. Normality and variance homogeneity were assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests, respectively. For normally distributed data, Student's t‐test or one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with least significant difference (post‐hoc test) post hoc analysis was used; for non‐normal data, the Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn–Bonferroni correction was applied. Categorical variables were compared using chi‐square or Fisher's exact tests. Multivariable linear and logistic regression models were used for continuous and categorical outcomes, respectively, with baseline GERD status included as a covariate. Subgroup analyses were stratified by baseline GERD status. All tests were two‐tailed, with significance set at p < 0.05.

RESULTS

Comparison of postoperative weight and BMI improvement between LSG and LSGFD

There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of preoperative body weight, BMI, %EWL, waist circumference, hip circumference or waist–hip ratio (p > 0.05) (Table S1). However, at 6 and 12 months of follow‐up, the LSGFD group showed a significant reduction in body weight, from a preoperative average of 110–87 kg at 6 months postoperatively and further to 74 kg at 12 months. This change was statistically significant (F = 34.669, p = 0.000), indicating a sustained and marked weight loss effect. In the LSG group, body weight decreased from a preoperative average of 114–91 kg at 6 months and to 79 kg at 12 months postoperatively. Although the reduction was also statistically significant (F = 88.916, p = 0.000), the degree of improvement was smaller than that of the LSGFD group. This difference reached statistical significance at 6 months (p = 0.031) and 12 months (p = 0.014) postoperatively. In addition, the %EWL was 71.94% in the LSGFD group, significantly higher than the 63.13% observed in the LSG group (p = 0.041) (Figure 1A–F).

Comparison of the efficacy of the two surgical methods in treating obesity‐related comorbidities

Both groups showed postoperative improvements in obesity‐related comorbidities, including reflux esophagitis, hiatal hernia, diabetes and hypertension (Table 3). In the LSGFD group, the incidence of reflux esophagitis decreased from 44.40% preoperatively to 11.10% at 6 months and 3.70% at 12 months postoperatively. In the LSG group, the incidence increased slightly from 17.00% preoperatively to 18.90% at 6 months and 20.80% at 12 months. The between‐group difference was statistically significant (p = 0.000). After adjustment for baseline GERD status, the difference remained significant (Odds Ratio = 0.07, 95% Confidence Interval: 0.01–0.35, p = 0.002) (Table S2). Regarding hiatal hernia, the LSGFD group showed a decrease from 59.30% at baseline to 0.00% at 12 months, while the LSG group decreased from 17.00% to 11.30% over the same period (p = 0.000). For diabetes, the LSGFD group showed a reduction in prevalence from 29.60% to 3.70%, and for hypertension, from 25.90% to 11.10% at 12 months. In comparison, the LSG group exhibited smaller reductions in both conditions (p < 0.05 for both) (Figure 3A–D).

Postoperative use of acid‐suppressive medications (proton pump inhibitors/H2 receptor antagonists)

To evaluate the long‐term effects of surgery on acid control, the use of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 receptor antagonists (H2RAs) was recorded at 6 and 12 months postoperatively (Table 4). At baseline, there was no significant difference in the proportion of patients regularly using PPI/H2RA between the two groups (LSGFD group: 81.5% vs. LSG group: 79.2%, p = 0.999). At 6 months postoperatively, 14.8% of patients in the LSGFD group continued to use acid‐suppressive medications, compared with 64.2% in the LSG group (p < 0.001). At 12 months, the usage rates were 7.4% in the LSGFD group and 62.3% in the LSG group, with the difference remaining statistically significant (p < 0.001).

Detailed comparison of postoperative comorbidity incidence

Postoperative comorbidities were compared between the LSGFD and conventional LSG groups (Figure 4; Table S3). The incidence of gastric leakage was 3.70% in the LSGFD group and 1.90% in the LSG group, with no statistically significant difference (p = 1.000). Incision infection occurred in 18.50% of patients in the LSGFD group and 15.10% in the LSG group (p = 0.753). Dysphagia was reported in 14.80% of LSGFD patients and 11.30% of LSG patients (p = 0.726), while nausea occurred in 33.30% and 22.60%, respectively (p = 0.421), and vomiting in 11.10% and 7.50%, respectively (p = 0.683). No significant differences were observed between the groups for any of these outcomes.

Post hoc power analysis

To further verify the adequacy of the sample size, a post hoc power analysis was performed for two representative primary outcome measures: %EWL as the weight‐related endpoint and GERD‐Q score as the GERD‐related endpoint. Based on the observed sample sizes (LSGFD group, n = 27; LSG group, n = 53) and effect sizes (%EWL, d ≈0.45; GERD‐Q, d ≈1.0), the calculated statistical power was approximately 50% for %EWL and >99.9% for GERD‐Q score. These findings indicate that the study sample size provided sufficient power to robustly detect significant differences in GERD‐related outcomes, which were the primary focus of the study conclusions.

DISCUSSION

Obesity is one of the most critical global health issues, and for many years, surgical intervention has been a practical approach to managing severe obesity and its associated comorbidities. 26 , 27 Conventional LSG has gained widespread popularity due to its simplicity and significant efficacy. 15 , 28 , 29 However, there remains a lack of consensus in the academic community regarding the effects of LSG and other bariatric procedures, such as gastric bypass surgery, on obesity‐related comorbidities, particularly GERD. 30 , 31 Some studies have suggested that LSG may exacerbate GERD symptoms, while others have reported no significant impact. These conflicting findings highlight the need for further research to clarify the specific effects of different surgical techniques on GERD.

This study prospectively compared LSGFD and LSG in terms of weight loss and GERD control. LSGFD showed significantly better outcomes in GERD‐Q, DeMeester scores, LES pressure and endoscopic esophagitis grades at 6 and 12 months, suggesting superior reflux suppression and LES function. These results align with prior studies showing that fundoplication enhances LES closure and reduces GERD incidence. 32 LSGFD also achieved a greater %EWL at 12 months (71.94% vs. 63.13%), consistent with previous findings. 33

LSGFD was associated with fewer de novo GERD cases, better symptom relief and lower PPI use, in line with meta‐analytic data. 34 Its advantage likely stems from multiple factors, including delayed gastric emptying due to anatomical modifications, behavioural changes, decreased ghrelin levels and the weight‐reducing effect of the anti‐reflux fold. 19 However, LSGFD involves a longer operative time and a steeper learning curve, requiring experience in gastric folding and intraoperative LES assessment. 33 While no increased complications were noted here, reports of suture‐line perforation stress the need for proper training and a standardised technique. 34

GERD improvement after LSGFD may result from synergistic mechanisms. Vagal preservation influences gastric motility and hormone secretion; ghrelin declines postoperatively, while glucagon‐like peptide‐1 and peptide YY (peptide tyrosine‐tyrosine) rise, enhancing satiety and reflux defence. 35 The vagal anti‐inflammatory pathway may also reduce tumor necrosis factor‐alpha and interleukin‐6, aiding mucosal healing. 36 Additionally, folding along the lesser curvature maintains optimal gastric volume and resistance, stabilising pressure dynamics and supporting LES function. 37 Although LSGFD showed dual benefits over 12 months, longer follow‐up is needed. Five‐year data suggest durable anti‐reflux effects, 17 but further evidence is required. Overall, LSGFD offers clear advantages for obese patients with GERD and may serve as the preferred option in this subgroup. Future studies should focus on long‐term outcomes, refine surgical techniques and identify patients who are most likely to benefit.

Despite being a randomised controlled trial, this study's relatively small sample size, short 12‐month follow‐up and single‐centre design limit the generalisability of its findings. The exclusive inclusion of an Asian population further restricts applicability across diverse ethnic and geographic groups. Future studies should involve larger, multicentre cohorts with extended follow‐up to better assess long‐term outcomes. Additionally, exploring the metabolic and endocrine differences between LSGFD and LSG may help refine surgical techniques and postoperative care.

Based on our findings, LSGFD demonstrated clear advantages in weight loss, GERD symptom relief and reversal of reflux esophagitis, providing strong evidence for its use as a preferred surgical option in obese patients with moderate to severe GERD, particularly those with esophagitis. By simultaneously addressing obesity and reflux, LSGFD offers a more comprehensive solution and avoids the potential exacerbation of GERD often seen after standard LSG. For patients without GERD or with only mild symptoms, LSG remains a reasonable choice due to its simplicity and shorter operative time, although close postoperative monitoring for reflux is warranted. The successful implementation of LSGFD requires experienced teams familiar with gastric fundus folding and LES pressure assessment to ensure both safety and efficacy. Future research should focus on developing standardised training protocols, refining suturing techniques and tools and creating predictive models to identify patients most likely to benefit from or be at risk of complications. Long‐term follow‐up—over 5 years—should evaluate weight maintenance, GERD recurrence, nutritional outcomes and quality of life. Moreover, integrating lifestyle modification and psychological support will be essential to advancing individualised, multidimensional obesity care.

CONCLUSION

The results demonstrated that the LSGFD outperformed conventional LSG in key measures, including reductions in body weight, BMI, waist circumference, hip circumference and GERD symptoms. LSGFD showed superior efficacy in treating GERD and reflux esophagitis.

This study integrated knowledge of surgical techniques and medical disease management to provide a new perspective on the surgical treatment of obesity. By comparing two surgical approaches, the study emphasised the importance of individualised treatment strategies and contributed to advancing more precise medical interventions. Through effective weight reduction and improvement of GERD symptoms, LSGFD can significantly enhance the quality of life for patients with obesity. The LSGFD reduced the risk of GERD and reflux esophagitis, which is particularly beneficial for patients with obesity, as these comorbidities are often difficult to manage and substantially affect quality of life.

This study offers valuable scientific and clinical guidance for the surgical management of obesity and holds significant theoretical and practical implications for improving the treatment of obesity and its associated comorbidities.

Formation tracking control of multiple USVs using ADRC with prescribed performance
Authors: Mingen Huo, Wenlong Mao, Xiaojuan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper investigates the formation tracking control problem for multiple unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) under prescribed performance constraints in the presence of model uncertainties and unknown disturbances. A decentralized formation control strategy is developed based on a modified active disturbance rejection control (ADRC) framework, where a model-compensation extended state observer (ESO) is designed to estimate the total disturbance and enhance robustness. To avoid the “explosion of complexity”, a tracking differentiator (TD) is employed to approximate virtual control derivatives, while a universal barrier function (UBF) is incorporated into the Lyapunov-based synthesis to guarantee both transient and steady-state performance bounds. Rigorous Lyapunov analysis proves that all closed-loop signals remain uniformly ultimately bounded, prescribed performance constraints are strictly satisfied, and inter-agent collision avoidance and communication connectivity are maintained. Comprehensive simulations further demonstrate significant performance advantages over representative baseline methods. In particular, the proposed controller achieves a 57.4% reduction in IAE and a 42.6% reduction in RMSE compared with a PID controller, and a further 49.4% and 36.7% reduction relative to a backstepping controller. These quantitative results confirm the superior accuracy and robustness of the proposed approach.Keywords: Autonomous UAV landing, Visual servo control, Field-of-view constraint, Prescribed performance control, Velocity estimationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, formation control for USVs has garnered significant research attention due to its critical role in various marine applications, such as maritime rescue operations, oceanic mineral exploration, and territorial water surveillance1–3. Within this context, trajectory tracking represents a fundamental motion control challenge in USV formation control4,5, necessitating the design of a feedback control strategy that ensures multiple USVs can accurately follow a time-parameterized reference trajectory. Given the complex maritime environment in which USVs operate, they are inevitably subjected to unknown external disturbances, such as wind, waves, and ocean currents. Moreover, the imprecise knowledge of hydrodynamic damping coefficients introduces model uncertainties. Additionally, the kinematic and dynamic models of USVs exhibit strong nonlinearity and coupling characteristics6, which can significantly degrade control performance and potentially lead to system instability or safety risks. Therefore, the development of an advanced, robust control strategy for formation trajectory tracking of USVs is of critical importance.

Extensive research efforts have been devoted to the development of tracking control strategies for USVs, resulting in a variety of control methodologies, including proportional-integral-derivative (PID) control7,8, dynamic surface control (DSC)9–11, model predictive control (MPC)12,13, data-driven control14,15, fuzzy control6,16,17, and ADRC18–20, among others. A PID control strategy combined with a soft actor-critic algorithm was proposed in article8 for single USVs, offering advantages in automatic parameter tuning and structural simplicity. However, its performance under varying disturbance conditions remains limited. Recently, a concurrent-learning-based adaptive critic formation was proposed for multirobots under safety constraints, which enhances weight convergence without persistent excitation conditions21. Similarly, an adaptive critic attitude learning control for hypersonic morphing vehicles without backstepping was developed, offering a simplified control structure under large uncertainties22. In the context of unmanned vehicles, an efficient path-following strategy using fuzzy control was introduced for consumer UAVs under disturbance constraints, demonstrating robust performance in urban logistics23. Moreover, security concerns such as randomly occurring false data injection attacks have been addressed via adaptive sliding mode control in rotary inverted pendulum systems, highlighting the need for resilient control architectures24. Additionally, enhanced fixed-time collision-free elliptical circumnavigation coordination for UAVs has been explored, ensuring safe and stable multi-vehicle operations in dynamic environments25. A data-driven model-free adaptive control framework was introduced in article15 for formation control of fully actuated USVs in complex marine environments. Although this method only requires input-output data, its performance is highly dependent on the quantity and quality of the available data. An adaptive fuzzy backstepping-based formation control framework was proposed in article17, capable of estimating model nonlinearities. However, its steady-state performance requires further improvement for practical applications. In article26, a neural network (NN)-based output feedback control strategy was developed for underactuated USVs with input saturation. While the NN-based adaptive observer effectively estimates velocity data, the design involves a complex tuning process due to the large number of parameters involved.

Owing to its strong capability in handling uncertainties and nonlinearities with a relatively simple structure, ADRC has attracted considerable research interest and has undergone rapid development27–30. As a core component of ADRC, the ESO efficiently estimates the “total disturbance,” including both model uncertainties and external disturbances, enabling their timely compensation31. The development of the ESO has undergone continuous advancements to address increasingly complex practical challenges. For instance, in highly nonlinear systems such as electronic throttle valves, the Nonlinear ESO was designed to effectively estimate and compensate for non-smooth nonlinearities including stick–slip friction and spring hysteresis32. To simplify implementation in real-world applications such as lower-limb exoskeletons, a transfer-function-based ESO structure was later developed33, demonstrating robust disturbance attenuation and ease of tuning in real-time control environments. More recently34, for systems with time-varying dynamics, an adaptive ESO was incorporated into the path-following control of USVs, effectively estimating and rejecting time-varying environmental disturbances while respecting actuator limitations. These developments collectively illustrate the progressive enhancement of ESO theory and its versatility across nonlinear, human–machine interaction, and marine control systems, which form the theoretical foundation for the modified ESO adopted in this study. Another key element in ADRC is the TD, which provides accurate differential signals and mitigates the issue of “explosion of complexity”. In conventional backstepping control, each recursive step requires the analytical differentiation of virtual control signals, which leads to the well-known “explosion of complexity” problem. This phenomenon significantly increases computational burden and may degrade real-time performance in practical implementations. In this study, a TD is incorporated into the ADRC framework to efficiently estimate these derivatives, thereby suppressing the explosion of complexity and simplifying the overall controller structure. In article19, an improved ADRC path-following control strategy was proposed for water sampling USVs, integrating an extreme learning machine module to enhance anti-disturbance performance and incorporating a saturation function to improve control effectiveness.

Furthermore, ensuring transient performance is crucial in the design of formation tracking control systems for USVs. Prescribed performance control (PPC), originally introduced in article35, has emerged as an effective methodology for enforcing transient performance constraints. The core idea of PPC is to transform prescribed performance bounds into an unconstrained domain using a barrier Lyapunov function, which is then integrated with the backstepping technique to ensure that tracking errors remain within predefined bounds. In article36, a decentralized formation control strategy with prescribed performance was proposed to guarantee trajectory tracking while maintaining transient performance constraints. In that approach, neural networks and disturbance observers were employed to estimate model uncertainties and external disturbances, respectively. Despite the promising performance of existing formation control methods, their controller design often involves complex structures and requires extensive parameter tuning.

In realistic marine environments, formation tracking of multiple USVs faces significant difficulties due to strong and time-varying environmental disturbances such as waves, winds, and currents, which lead to substantial nonlinear uncertainties in the motion dynamics. These challenges are further compounded by the uncertain and speed-dependent hydrodynamic damping characteristics of USVs, where conventional neural-network-based compensation methods often involve high computational complexity and are difficult to deploy in real time on small platforms. Moreover, achieving prescribed and asymmetric tracking performance while maintaining collision avoidance and information connectivity becomes particularly challenging in the presence of such disturbances and modeling uncertainties. These practical difficulties motivate the development of a control framework that integrates a modified ADRC structure, a unified prescribed-performance mechanism, and a computationally efficient formation strategy to enhance robustness and implementability under realistic marine conditions.

Motivated by the aforementioned considerations, this paper addresses the trajectory tracking control problem for a group of USVs operating under uncertain dynamic models and time-varying environmental disturbances. A formation tracking control strategy is proposed that ensures connectivity maintenance and collision avoidance while satisfying prescribed performance constraints. The traditional backstepping-NN method also performs reasonably well in terms of tracking accuracy, with relatively lower IAE and MAE than the ADRC and PID controllers. This indicates that the backstepping-NN approach is capable of handling nonlinear dynamics and external disturbances. However, its larger TV values suggest more frequent and abrupt changes in the control inputs, which may lead to increased actuator wear and reduced system stability.

From both theoretical and simulation perspectives, the proposed modified ADRC-based backstepping control method features a simpler structure compared to the backstepping-NN approach, requiring the tuning of only a few key parameters. Moreover, the proposed strategy enables accurate estimation of total disturbances through the model-compensation-based ESO, thereby reducing the computational burden. The use of the TD further avoids the “explosion of complexity” issue commonly encountered in backstepping designs.

In conclusion, the proposed modified ADRC-based backstepping controller effectively combines the advantages of ADRC and backstepping techniques, achieving excellent transient and steady-state performance with a streamlined control design process.

Conclusion

In this study, a distributed modified ADRC-based formation control strategy was proposed for a group of fully actuated USVs, enabling asymptotic trajectory tracking with prescribed performance guarantees in the presence of external disturbances and modeling uncertainties. A model compensation ESO was introduced to estimate and compensate for time-varying disturbances and model uncertainties. Based on the integration of backstepping methodology, UBF, and Lyapunov synthesis, the proposed control framework ensures collision avoidance and communication connectivity between followers and their respective leaders. Additionally, the control strategy effectively avoids the “explosion of complexity” issue commonly encountered in backstepping-based designs. The tracking errors are guaranteed to remain within predefined time-varying bounds despite the presence of unknown disturbances. Theoretical analysis confirms that all signals in the closed-loop system are uniformly ultimately bounded, and simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed control approach.

Despite the promising results, this study has several limitations that point to valuable future research directions:

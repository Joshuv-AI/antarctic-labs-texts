Force sensorless interaction wrench estimation for neural-learning impedance control of a flying parallel robot with actuator saturation
Authors: Minglei Zhu, Yuhui Guo, Dawei Gong, Yuyang Zhao, Jiaoyuan Chen, Yankai Xing, Shijie Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a sensorless adaptive neural-learning impedance controller for a flying parallel robot (FPR) to enable compliant physical interaction while explicitly accommodating actuator saturation. The dynamic model of the multi-UAV heterogeneous cooperative FPR is first established, and an external wrench observer is developed to estimate the contact-induced torque. To address system uncertainties and achieve robust disturbance rejection, a Lyapunov-based radial basis function neural network (RBFNN) impedance controller with force-tracking capability is designed. An auxiliary compensation system is further incorporated to alleviate the adverse effects of actuator input saturation. The closed-loop stability of the overall FPR system under the proposed control law is rigorously guaranteed. ADAMS–Simulink co-simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach, confirming its ability to maintain stable and compliant interaction across diverse contact conditions.Keywords: Flying parallel robot, Neural network, Impedance control, Input saturationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Aerial robotic manipulators have attracted increasing attention over the past decade. Most existing aerial manipulation systems adopt a configuration in which a drone is equipped with a robotic arm or a specialized end effector to enable physical interaction within high-altitude workspaces1,2. Although these designs allow for dexterous manipulation, they inherently suffer from limited payload capacity, reduced control precision, and significant energy consumption due to the added mass and inertia of onboard actuators and arm structures3–5. To overcome these limitations, several aerial parallel robot architectures have recently been introduced, utilizing multiple UAVs connected through cables or rigid links6–10. These systems have demonstrated strong potential for grasping, transportation, and the assembly or disassembly of mechanical components, owing to their increased payload capacity and enhanced manipulability.

Among these multi-UAV cooperative systems, the flying parallel robot (FPR) introduced in Six et al.10, Six, Chriette, Briot and Martinet11 represents a notable advancement. The FPR benefits from high payload capability, the absence of additional onboard actuators, substantial separation between the drones and the end effector, and support for diverse leg topologies. Existing studies have established its kinematic and dynamic models and demonstrated the feasibility of achieving stable flight control6,10–14. However, several practical challenges remain before the FPR can reliably perform physical interaction tasks. First, due to its UAV-actuated structure, the output force and moment during contact must remain within strict bounds, yet actuator saturation has not been addressed in prior work. Second, as a reconfigurable system composed of multiple UAVs, accurate dynamic modeling is difficult to obtain in practice. These challenges underscore the need for a robust and adaptive compliant force control framework capable of handling model uncertainties and ensuring stable robot–environment interaction.

Compliant interaction control for the FPR is considerably more challenging than for conventional ground-based or aerial manipulators, primarily due to severe aerodynamic disturbances and external perturbations. Moreover, most force-control strategies reported in the literature–whether direct or indirect–depend on accurate force measurements obtained from dedicated force sensors15,16. However, integrating such sensors inevitably increases system mass and energy consumption, which is highly undesirable for aerial robotic platforms. To avoid these drawbacks, we adopt an external wrench observer to estimate the contact forces17. This generalized momentum–based observer enables reliable reconstruction of the interaction wrench even under partially known system dynamics.

Compliant force-control approaches can generally be categorized into two classes: direct force control, which explicitly closes the loop on the measured or estimated force18, such as hybrid position/force control; and indirect force control, which regulates the robot’s dynamic behavior by shaping a desired mechanical impedance defined through position–force relationships19,20. Considering the inherent aerodynamic uncertainties and persistent external disturbances affecting the FPR, achieving high-precision position tracking comparable to fixed-base manipulators is unrealistic. Consequently, impedance control–which passively responds to external wrenches and does not rely on strict position accuracy–is more suitable for aerial robots during physical interaction. Another major challenge in regulating the interaction force of the FPR lies in handling actuator saturation at the drone level. In practice, the thrust and moment generated by each UAV are inherently bounded, which directly induces saturation in the overall FPR actuation. Such saturation nonlinearities can cause significant tracking errors and even destabilize the system, making a robust and anti-saturation control strategy essential for compliant interaction. Several prior studies24–26 have proposed controllers that explicitly account for saturation constraints, including various forms of saturated nonlinear PID control. However, these approaches typically rely on accurate knowledge of the system parameters and predefined saturation bounds. As a result, they are unsuitable for the FPR, whose dynamics are highly uncertain and configuration-dependent. With the advancement of intelligent control techniques, numerous neural-network-based, fuzzy, and time-delay adaptive control schemes have been proposed to cope with saturation and other nonlinearities27–33. Among them, neural network–based controllers have been particularly prominent due to their universal approximation capability, enabling them to represent unknown, time-varying, and disturbance-rich nonlinear robot dynamics effectively. Modeling of the flying parallel robot

The novel flying parallel robot consists of three quadrotor UAVs, three rigid legs, and one moving platform. The UAVs are attached to the tips of the legs via spherical joints, while the moving platform is connected to the other ends of the legs through revolute joints. For simplification, we assume that the spherical joint connecting each UAV to its corresponding rigid leg is located at the center of mass of the quadrotor. This assumption allows the mechanical properties of the spherical joints to decouple the dynamics of the flying parallel robot (FPR) from the rotational dynamics of the UAVs, effectively treating the UAVs as rotating thrust generators in 3D space. Adaptive impedance control and its stability analysis

The adaptive impedance controller is proposed to track the desired trajectory while interacting with the environment and reduce the saturation effect caused by the velocity limit of the quadrotors. To mitigate the adverse effects of actuator constraints, various strategies have been developed in recent literature. One prevalent approach involves the coordinate transformation of error variables, as seen in Zhu, Huang, Song, Zhao, Qi and Gong37, where the tracking error is redefined to incorporate the saturation influence directly into the transformed state space. While such methods offer elegant theoretical bounds on prescribed performance, they often require complex backstepping or mapping that can increase the computational burden on real-time flight controllers. In this work, an auxiliary dynamic system is employed instead. This method compensates for the difference between the commanded wrench and the actual saturated thrust () by augmenting the error dynamics. Numerical simulation

In this section, a co-simulation framework integrating ADAMS and Simulink is employed to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed control strategy. The high-fidelity physical model of the flying parallel robot (FPR) is constructed in ADAMS, whereas the control algorithms are implemented in Simulink. A discrete communication rate of 1 kHz is established between the two platforms to enable real-time bidirectional data exchange. The simulation assumes full-state measurement of the generalized coordinates , which is feasible in practice using a motion–capture system combined with joint encoders for the passive joints. To assess the robustness of the controller under sensing imperfections, uniformly distributed measurement noise is injected into the estimated pose. Orientation measurements are corrupted with noise on the order of 1 rad, while position measurements along each axis are perturbed within 1 m. Encoder quantization effects are also incorporated to more closely reflect practical sensing conditions. Actuator saturation is explicitly modeled in the co-simulation. Each UAV is assigned a thrust limit of 35 N, representing realistic operational constraints. These saturation bounds are used to evaluate the controller’s ability to maintain stable interaction under physically plausible actuation limits. In this experiment, the flying platform robot (FPR) was deployed to perform contact-based interaction tasks that resemble those encountered in practical industrial scenarios. A predefined trajectory guided the FPR to the target interaction pose, after which a desired contact force command was issued. During the interaction phase, the FPR maintained a level attitude while performing a yaw motion to twist its body relative to the plane. All controllers exhibit an expected transient response at the moment of initial contact; however, their steady-state behaviors differ markedly. Controller 1 presents persistent oscillations and the largest tracking errors across all evaluated domains, revealing weak disturbance rejection and a pronounced inability to regulate the interaction dynamics. Controller 2 provides noticeable improvements over the PID baseline, with reduced error amplitudes and attenuated oscillations, yet it still fails to adequately compensate for sustained contact forces. In contrast, the proposed Controller 3 demonstrates the highest level of robustness, characterized by the fastest convergence to a near-zero steady-state error and the most stable tracking profile throughout the entire contact phase. These results highlight the benefits of its online adaptive mechanism, which enables continuous learning and effective cancellation of unmodeled contact dynamics.

Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a novel sensorless adaptive neural-learning impedance controller for a flying parallel robot (FPR), specifically developed to enable compliant physical interaction while explicitly accounting for actuator saturation. The controller integrates a radial basis function (RBF) neural network with a Lyapunov-based adaptive law, allowing accurate force tracking and compensation of unmodeled interaction dynamics. To overcome the detrimental effects of input saturation inherent in the FPR actuators, an auxiliary compensator was formulated to preserve closed-loop stability and ensure consistent control performance. Comprehensive co-simulations conducted in ADAMS–Simulink confirm that the proposed scheme effectively rejects disturbances and maintains stable contact interaction across diverse configurations and external conditions. These results highlight the controller’s potential for practical deployment in aerial manipulation tasks involving physical interaction. Future work will focus on experimental implementation on a physical FPR prototype to further evaluate its real-world performance, robustness, and generalizability.

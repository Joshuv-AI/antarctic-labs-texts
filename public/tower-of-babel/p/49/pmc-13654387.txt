Evaluation of teriparatide as a promoter of mandibular fracture healing: An observational clinical study
Authors: Trishala Annamalai Rajan, Elavenil Panneerselvam, Akshaya Subhashinee Dhanasekaran, V.B. Krishnakumar Raja
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe objective of this study was to evaluate the role of teriparatide in promoting mandibular fracture healing.MethodsIn this prospective cohort study, 12 patients belonging to American Society of Anesthesiologists status II, who required open reduction and internal fixation of mandibular fractures were categorized into exposure group 9 (52.94%) fractures and non-exposure group 8 (47.06%) fractures. Following open reduction and internal fixation, patients in the exposure group were administered 28.2 μg of teriparatide subcutaneously, twice a week for 4 weeks, while the non-exposure group did not receive any treatment. The groups were followed up and reviewed for a period of 6 weeks to assess fracture healing. The primary outcome parameters were postoperative pain, levels of serum osteocalcin and pro collagen type 1 N-terminal propeptide (P1NP) and bone density on CT. The secondary outcome parameters were requirement of analgesics and mouth opening. Pain was assessed using numerical pain rating scale. Serum osteocalcin and P1NP were assessed pre-operatively and on the 30th post-operative day. Bone density was assessed pre-operatively and on the 6th post-operative week. The data was analyzed for descriptive and analytical statistics (comparison of means and proportions). To analyze the data SPSS (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0, Armonk, NY: IBM Corp. Released 2019) is used. Significance level is fixed as 5% (α = 0.05).ResultsAll patients were pain-free by the 3rd and 7th post-operative day, in the exposure and non-exposure groups, respectively (p < 0.001). Mean post-operative serum osteocalcin and P1NP levels were higher for the exposure group (p = 0.011; p = 0.105, respectively). The mean bone mineral density was significantly increased in the exposure group (p < 0.001).ConclusionWithin the limitations of this study, it can be inferred that teriparatide effectively reduces post-operative pain and facilitates bone formation and mineralisation.

Introduction

Mandibular fractures are commonly treated by open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) to facilitate fracture healing that ensures early restitution of form and function. Fracture healing is a synchronized, multi-step process that consists of anabolic phase of callus formation and catabolic phase of remodeling, which render mechanical integrity to the new callus. Literature reveals numerous local as well as systemic factors that impair fracture healing; the former includes fracture characteristics, infection, and reduced blood supply, while the latter consists of advanced age, obesity, anemia, steroid administration, malnutrition, alcoholism, smoking, and endocrine imbalances such as diabetes mellitus, parathyroid disease, menopause, etc.1 In addition, mandible is predisposed to compromised fracture healing due to many reasons: (1) presence of a single endosteal arterial blood supply in contrast to the maxilla which is perfused by many intra-osseous vessels;2 (2) weaker bone structure due to higher concentration of stresses along the numerous curvatures of bone;3 (3) increased risk of postoperative infection owing to its greater cortical structure and stasis of saliva/food debris in the oral cavity.4

Enhancing anabolism, limiting early catabolism, or facilitating both the responses can have a positive impact on fracture healing. Adjunctive therapies, both medical and surgical, can help in promoting the fracture healing process. Anabolic therapies for fracture include low intensity pulsed ultrasound, photobiomodulation, simvastatin, bone morphogenic proteins and autologous bone graft. Salmon calcitonin and bisphosphonates are a few anti-catabolic treatment modalities.5

Teriparatide (rPTH [1-34]), a derivative of parathyroid hormone, has been shown to be efficacious in increasing bone mineral density, callus volume, and mineralisation while decreasing bone loss in both animal and human studies, especially in osteoporosis and fracture healing of long bones.6 Despite animal studies7 that have proven the positive effect of teriparatide therapy on maxillofacial fracture healing, clinical studies that assess the efficacy of teriparatide in improving mandibular fracture healing are negligible.8,9 However, these studies followed a “30 days” regimen of administration of teriparatide through the subcutaneous route, which is not patient-friendly in terms of cost, dose, and compliance. Further, these studies have evaluated the effect of teriparatide on fracture healing in healthy patients. Hence, the present study was designed to assess the efficacy of bi-weekly teriparatide therapy in promoting fracture healing in patients with compromised healing potential, specifically those classified under American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) II due to chronic smoking and/or alcohol use—factors that are well-documented to negatively impact bone healing through impaired vascularity, reduced osteoblastic function, and altered inflammatory response.

Methods

Study design (Fig. 1)

The study was carried out as a prospective cohort study, involving patients who reported to the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial surgery, for surgical management of fractured mandible. The study was explained to the patients and informed consent to take part was obtained. Every patient's age, gender, relevant demographic details, and medical history were recorded. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee (SRMDC/IRB/2017/MDS/No.407) and was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry, India (CTRI/2022/07/044443).

Hypothesis

The null hypothesis was that teriparatide neither increases bone formation, bone mineral density nor reduces post-operative pain.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients were selected based on a set of inclusion and exclusion criteria: patients aged 20 to 70 years, categorized as ASA II status because of alcohol consumption or smoking, and demonstrating non-comminuted mandibular fractures involving the condyle, angle, ramus, body, symphysis or parasymphysis were included in the study. Exclusion criteria included patients with fractures involving other facial bones, presence of bone pathology, and thyroid dysfunction. Individuals categorized under ASA I, III, or IV and patients belonging to category II because of reasons other than smoking and alcoholism were also excluded from the trial.

Sample size calculation

The sample size calculation was done in G∗power version 3.0. According to Almirol et al.,10 2016, the sample size was calculated, keeping the effect size as 0.99 with an alpha error of 0.20 and power of 0.80, the total sample size was calculated as 12, which is divided into 6 in each group.

Grouping of patients

Patients who met the inclusion criteria and had given consent were included in the trial. The patients were categorized into exposure and non-exposure groups. Patients belonging to the exposure group were subjected to ORIF followed by teriparatide therapy for 4 weeks while the non-exposure group patients received no teriparatide after ORIF.

Operative methodology

The recruited sample was evaluated in detail for history of trauma, clinical signs and symptoms. Blood sample (2 mL) was collected from patients belonging to both the exposure and non-exposure groups and sent to the laboratory for serum osteocalcin and N-terminal propeptide of type I pro collagen (P1NP). CT scan of the facial bones was taken for evaluation of fracture pattern. ORIF of the mandible was carried out using a standardized surgical protocol. All patients were treated under general anesthesia, and all procedures were performed by a single surgeon. Fracture exposure was done using vestibular incision for mandibular symphysis, parasymphysis, body and angle fractures, while retromandibular incision was used for condyle fractures. Fracture reduction and fixation were performed along Champy's lines of osteosynthesis for parasymphysis, body and angle fractures. Condyle fractures were fixed using a single or double plate (Meyer's lines of osteosynthesis) based on the bone stock available. Wound closure was performed using 3-0 VICRYL™ sutures for the subcutaneous tissues, and 5-0 PROLENE™ for the skin. None of the patients required maxillomandibular fixation in the postoperative period.

All patients (exposure and non-exposure groups) were prescribed the same postoperative antibiotics and analgesics (tablet cefixime 200 mg for 5 days and tablet aceclofenac 100 mg for 2 days). From the 3rd post-operative day, analgesics were stopped for patients in the exposure and non-exposure group. The patients were permitted to take tablet aceclofenac 100 mg as rescue analgesic, if they perceived pain. The number of analgesics taken on the 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, and 7th post-operative days were noted. Patients in the exposure group received subcutaneous teriparatide injection marketed under the name Terifrac (Intas Pharmaceuticals Ltd., India). Each Terifrac cartridge contains 750 μg teriparatide per 3 mL of solution, with a concentration of 20 μg per dosage. A dose of 28.2 μg by subcutaneous injection using Terifrac pen, was prescribed twice a week (3rd and 7th day), at a dose interval of 3 and 4 days, for 4 postoperative weeks. No teriparatide was administered to patients in the non-exposure group. After 30 days, 2 mL of post-operative blood sample was collected from both groups of patients and sent for laboratory assessment of serum osteocalcin (ng/dL) and N-terminal pro-peptide of type-I procollagen (ng/dL). CT scan of the facial bones was taken on the 6th post-operative week and were used to measure bone mineral density in Hounsfield units (HU) across the fracture line in 3 planes—axial, coronal, and sagittal. Imaging was standardized using the same scanner and protocols. HU values were assessed at consistent anatomical landmarks using DICOM-compatible radiology software.

Evaluation parameters and review protocol

The primary outcome parameters were postoperative pain, serum levels of biomarkers of bone formation and mineralisation and bone mineral density. The secondary outcome parameters were the number of rescue analgesics taken and mouth opening. Any local or systemic complications or allergic reactions were monitored.

Postoperative pain in the surgical site was assessed in both the exposure and non-exposure groups using the numerical pain rating score from the 1st to the 7th postoperative days, and also on the 14th, 21st, and 30th postoperative days. The levels of serum osteocalcin and P1NP, which are indicators of bone formation11 and bone mineralisation,12 respectively, were assessed on the preoperative and 30th post-operative days and recorded in ng/dL. CT imaging was used to evaluate bone mineral density (HU) across the fracture line pre-operatively, and on the 6th post-operative week. The number of analgesics taken by all the patients on the 3rd – 7th post-operative days were noted. Mouth opening was recorded in “mm” using a scale, pre-operatively and on post-operative days 1 and day 7, and 4th and 6th post-operative weeks. It was considered to be adequate if inter-incisal measurement was more than 35 mm and inadequate if less than 35 mm.

Statistical tests

The results of Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilks tests (normality tests) revealed that mouth opening, bone mineral density by HU values, osteocalcin and P1NP levels followed a normal distribution, while pain scores and requirement of rescue analgesics did not. Therefore, in order to analyze the data, both parametric and non-parametric methods were applied. Independent-samples Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare pain scores between groups and related-samples. Friedman's repeated measures ANOVA by ranks was to compare pain scores between time-points. An independent samples t-test was employed to compare mean mouth opening, bone mineral density, osteocalcin and P1NP values between the groups. To compare mean bone mineral density, osteocalcin, and P1NP values between 2 time-points, a paired t-test was applied, while a general linear model (repeated measures ANOVA) was used to compare mean mouth opening between time points. To find the association between proportions of rescue analgesics requirement, a Chi-square test was employed. SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 26.0 (2019, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) was used to analyze the data. The significance level was fixed at 5% (α = 0.05).

Results

The study included 12 patients (6 exposure and 6 non-exposure). Among the subjects, 10 (83.33%) were males and 2 (16.66%) were females. The average age of the subjects included in the exposure group was (48.33 ± 15.92) years, while that of the non-exposure group was (37.00 ± 10.64) years. The total percentage of fractures included in the exposure and control non-exposure groups were 9 (52.94%) and 8 (47.06%) respectively. The distribution of fractures was as follows: condyle 4 (44.44%), parasymphysis 4 (44.44%), and body 1 (11.11%) in patients belonging to the exposure group; and angle 4 (50.0%), parasymphysis 2 (25.0%), and body 2 (25.0%) in the non-exposure group. None of the patients in the exposure group developed any unfavourable outcomes or complications following teriparatide administration.

There was a significant difference in pain scores between the groups (statistically significant; p < 0.001) (Fig. 2). In the exposure group, all the patients were free of pain by the end of the 3rd post-operative day, whereas for the non-exposure group, this occurred only on day 6. This correlated with the statistically significant reduction in the need for rescue analgesics by patients in the exposure group when compared to the non-exposure group (p < 0.001) (Table 1). Discussion

In clinical practice, factors that impair fracture healing1 are counteracted by various promoters of fracture healing. These adjuncts to fracture management may be local or systemic. Our research aimed at evaluating the impact of teriparatide therapy on mandibular fracture healing in patients with compromised healing potential.

Teriparatide as a fracture healing agent

Teriparatide, a synthetic form of parathyroid hormone, has gained significant attention in the field of orthopedics due to its effective role in fracture healing. One of the primary mechanisms by which teriparatide aids in fracture healing is through its ability to increase bone formation. This occurs due to its stimulatory effects on osteoblasts, which form new bone matrix at the fracture site. Additionally, teriparatide exhibits anabolic effects on bone by increasing the production of insulin-like growth factor 1 and transforming growth factor-beta. These factors are vital in facilitating the proliferation of cells, formation of blood vessels, and synthesis of collagen, which are imperative for fracture union. Furthermore, teriparatide has been shown to enhance bone mineral density, thereby improving the overall structural integrity and mechanical strength of the fractured bone.13

Even in patients with compromised healing potential, clinical studies have demonstrated the efficacy of teriparatide in promoting fracture healing, particularly in patients with osteoporosis and diabetes.14 Kumar et al.,15 have established the correlation between administration of teriparatide and reduction in the time required for fracture union along with an increase in the formation of callus. Our study also revealed a statistically significant increase in bone formation as well as mineralisation in patients who received teriparatide.

In contrast to other studies which utilized either conventional radiographs, dual X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) or quantitative CT to evaluate callus formation and fracture union, our study has analyzed fracture healing more extensively and accurately through biochemical evaluation as well as CT imaging. The findings suggest that teriparatide may be a valuable adjunct therapy in the management of mandibular fractures, especially in patients with compromised healing.

Anti-nociceptive effects of teriparatide

Post-surgical pain occurs as a result of operative trauma (physical and thermal) to hard as well as soft tissues. The pain is debilitating and considered as one of the reasons for prolonged hospital stay that prevents early return to livelihood and increases the financial burden. Thus, effective postoperative pain management is a critical aspect of peri-operative care. Administration of medication such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, opioids and steroids is a practice commonly followed to minimize post-operative pain and improve patient comfort. However, these pharmacological agents are associated with adverse side effects which are dose-dependent.16

In comparison, teriparatide therapy has been proven to be an excellent analgesic agent with minimal adverse reactions.17 The anti-nociceptive effects of teriparatide have been ascribed to both central and peripheral mechanisms; central effects include alteration of perception as well as transmission of pain through its action on definite receptors in the central nervous system. Additionally, the ability to induce neuronal tissue repair has been indicated as one of the reasons for the anti-nociceptive effect of teriparatide.18 Peripheral mechanism is through downregulation of inflammatory cytokine expression, including IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α in bone tissue.19

Chen et al.,17 have demonstrated that teriparatide reduced back pain and improved quality of life in patients with osteoporotic vertebral fractures by significantly decreasing the visual analog score and Oswestry disability index. In the present study also, there was a statistically significant reduction in pain. Additionally, the need for rescue analgesics in patients who received teriparatide was considerably lesser when compared to the control subjects. Pg et al.8 demonstrated reduction in post-operative pain scores by teriparatide in comparison with administration of Cissus quadrangularis for mandibular fractures. In contrast, Ebrahimi et al.9 reported that administration of teriparatide had no effect on post-operative pain following ORIF of mandibular fractures.

Evaluation of bone formation by CT imaging and biomarkers

Previous studies have employed either DEXA or conventional radiographs to assess fracture union in patients receiving teriparatide. Sadat-Ali et al.20 have demonstrated increased bone mineral density with DEXA. However, evaluation by DEXA is associated with higher radiation exposure, increased cost, and lesser sensitivity when compared to CT. The present study used CT because of numerous reasons: (1) CT imaging is the most commonly used modality to evaluate fractures and their healing in the post operative phase; (2) convenient availability of CT imaging facilities and ease of interpretation by surgeons; (3) CT provides an accurate measure of regional bone strength as well as density.21 This study also demonstrated a statistically significant increase in bone mineral density in the exposure group when compared to the non-exposure group. The findings on CT revealed effective fracture union that correlated with superior clinical outcomes. However, Ebrahimi et al.9 found no significant difference in radiodensity as measured by HU in patients treated with teriparatide when compared to the control group.

The time of fracture evaluation by imaging is critical. Hence, this study assessed bone formation after 6 weeks, which correlated with the time taken for normal fracture healing.22 This also helps to evaluate the stability of fracture fixation, which may change in the post-operative phase due to muscle pull and functional (occlusal) loads.23 The present study used all the 3 sections of CT to determine and monitor bone mineral density in response to teriparatide therapy in patients with mandibular fractures. This was done to obtain a comprehensive analysis of bone formation in all 3 dimensions.

Biomarkers are powerful tools in the field of medicine, used extensively to evaluate various physiological processes, including bone metabolism. They have been used to assess bone formation in numerous studies.24 Biomarkers offer several advantages over traditional imaging techniques; they enable early detection of changes in bone formation before they become visually noticeable on imaging scans. This study affirmed the same. The status of bone formation observed on CT taken at the end of 6 weeks was identified much earlier (4th week, post-operative) by biomarkers. Biomarkers also provide a safe means of assessing bone formation without the need for exposure to radiation.

The various biomarkers used to assess bone formation and mineralisation include serum osteocalcin, bone-specific alkaline phosphatase, P1NP and P1CP. A comparative analysis of the sensitivity and specificity of various biomarkers of osteoblastic activity reveal that serum osteocalcin is the most reliable to assess bone formation.11 Alkaline phosphatase is the other commonly used biomarker to evaluate bone formation. However, it is less sensitive when compared to osteocalcin and is known to demonstrate cross-reactivity with enzymes of the liver in diseases. Hence, this is not ideal as a biomarker in patients categorized under ASA status II due to alcoholism and smoking. Further, alkaline phosphatase is associated with inter-method variability and hence, results produced by different methods and different labs are not comparable.24 Therefore, the present study used serum osteocalcin to evaluate bone formation. The other biomarker used in the present study was serum P1NP. Numerous studies show the positive correlation between P1NP levels and bone mineral density.24 Our study also showed a similar correlation. The additional advantages of using P1NP include (1) minimal diurnal fluctuation, (2) stability at room temperature; and (3) sensitivity.11,24

Teriparatide vs other facilitators of bone healing

Fracture healing can be accelerated by both anabolic and catabolic treatment modalities. These include teriparatide, bisphosphonates, bone morphogenic proteins, calcitonin, calcium and vitamin D supplements, low-intensity pulsed ultrasound etc. Bisphosphonates exhibit adverse effects such as atypical fractures and jaw necrosis.25 Bone morphogenic proteins are expensive and linked with ectopic bone growth and soft tissue edema.26 Though salmon calcitonin may be delivered easily through intranasal administration,5 comparative studies reveal better bone mineral density and reduced risk of subsequent fractures in patients treated with teriparatide than patients treated with calcitonin.27 Though dietary supplementation with vitamin D and calcium is routinely practiced, evidence on its effectiveness is not entirely conclusive regarding the optimal dose, duration, and timing of supplementation.28 Though low-intensity pulsed ultrasound is an effective option for facilitating fracture healing,29 it requires the ultrasound unit and patient compliance for multiple hospital visits. When compared to facilitators, specifically bisphosphonates and vitamin D calcium supplements, teriparatide expedites fracture healing by stimulating bone formation through increased osteoblast activity, and promotes the regeneration of new bone tissue. It has demonstrated beneficial outcomes in accelerating fracture healing, particularly in individuals with limited healing potential.

Positives of the study, limitations, and future perspectives

This clinical study established the effect of teriparatide on mandibular fracture healing, exclusively in ASA II patients. Studies conducted earlier assessed the efficacy of teriparatide in either a cohort of healthy patients alone or a mixed pool of ASA I and II patients.8,9 In comparison with the study by Pg et al.8 and Ebrahimi et al.9 which administered teriparatide everyday for 4 weeks, the present study utilized a bi-weekly regimen for 4 weeks, which was more patient-friendly and economically feasible. Furthermore, the present study adds more clinical evidence by a more comprehensive and objective evaluation of bone formation as well as mineralisation using biomarkers, CT imaging and clinical evaluation.

An acknowledged limitation of this study is the non-homogeneous distribution of fracture types between the exposure and non-exposure groups, with all condylar fractures clustered in the exposure group. While this may bias functional outcomes like mouth opening, the significant improvements observed despite this bias suggest a favorable role of teriparatide.

A randomized controlled trial involving a large sample size would provide more data. Also, the effect of teriparatide on soft tissues overlying the maxillofacial skeleton may be studied for a better understanding of healing mechanisms.

In conclusion, teriparatide proves to be a safe promoter of fracture union in patients with impaired healing potential. Additionally, it is a valuable adjunct for achieving adequate pain relief after surgical repair of maxillofacial fractures. The efficacy of teriparatide, as demonstrated by biochemical analysis and CT imaging, correlates with superior clinical outcomes. Within the limitations of this study, it can be inferred that teriparatide effectively improves bone formation as well as mineralisation.

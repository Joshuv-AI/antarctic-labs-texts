Ages of sitting and walking milestone attainment in typically developing children worldwide: protocol for a scoping review
Authors: Jordan Wickstrom, Izza Choudhry, Emma J Leone, Antonia M H Piergies, Tanvi Das, Alexandra Chenowith, Alexandra Hehn, Erica B Friedman, Alicia A Livinski, Kate Silfen, Aziza Shad, Jana M Iverson, Audrey Thurm
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionAges of attainment for early gross motor milestones have been widely studied, but definitions and measurement methods have varied greatly. Since delays in motor milestones have been used extensively as one of the earliest indicators of atypical development, it is imperative to establish a universal understanding of the methods and results reported on motor milestones in typically developing children. Therefore, the primary aim of this scoping review is to provide an overview of recent studies reporting ages of onset for independent sitting and walking in typically developing children worldwide; this will be achieved by summarising how these milestones have been operationalised and evaluated, the samples from whom these data have been drawn, and the reported ages of acquisition.Methods and analysisTo meet inclusion criteria, articles must: be original research papers published in any language since 2003, contain a sample of typically developing children and report actual ages of onset for independent sitting and/or walking. To conduct this scoping review, the Joanna Briggs Institute methodological framework will be used. Search terms will include variations of the following concepts: acquisition of motor milestone (independent sitting and/or walking), age of onset, infancy. Six databases (CINAHL Plus, Embase, PsycNet: PsycINFO & PsycArticles, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science: Core Collection) will be searched. Records from these databases will be screened for eligibility. Two people will independently review each record during title and abstract screening, and three people will independently screen full texts and extract data. Results will be displayed in tables, graphs and narrative summaries in the scoping review.Ethics and disseminationAs the review will only include previously published data, ethics approval is not required. Introduction

Background and rationale

Motor development in children typically follows a predictable and sequential pattern, although the rate at which individual children achieve motor skills can vary.1 Infants’ progress in gross motor development is often assessed using the age at which they acquire specific skills, such as sitting or walking, which are commonly referred to as motor milestones. Monitoring the attainment of these milestones can provide insights into children’s overall development. Delays in attainment of motor milestones are often the first sign of atypical development and can have cascading effects on other aspects of development.1 2 Consequently, various global efforts to screen for early delays have occurred, including well-child visits with paediatricians, early surveillance algorithms, milestone tracking phone applications and the ‘act early’ checklists from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.3 4

Determining what ages to consider as ‘late’ has been challenging due to the heterogeneity of methods used to define, sample, assess and measure gross motor milestones throughout the world. In addition, current resources reporting ages of milestone attainment are typically rooted in expert opinion rather than derived from systematically collected data; in cases in which data have been collected, the original sources are typically unknown/uncited.4 Sample size and assessment frequency also highly vary, contributing to a wide variety of reported developmental trajectories.5 One notable exception is the WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study (MGRS) conducted between 1997 and 2003, which systematically collected ages of onset for six gross motor milestones in healthy children across five countries (Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and USA).6 This initiative remains among the most highly cited and methodologically rigorous efforts in early milestone attainment. However, outside of the MGRS, few (if any) datasets of similar scope and methodological quality exist. The proposed comprehensive scoping review aims to map the broader landscape of such studies to evaluate how sitting and walking attainment in typically developing children has been examined over the past two decades. By synthesising existing research, we can better define what is ‘typical’, enabling earlier identification of delays and timely referrals for developmental evaluation and intervention.

Scoping reviews are intended to capture the breadth of literature available on a broad topic; they also provide a general overview of the volume of studies available.7 A scoping review is the most appropriate form of evidence synthesis for our research question given that the primary goals of this review are to identify the types of evidence available regarding the attainment of sitting and walking, summarise the motor milestone definitions and assessment procedures in the literature, examine how and in whom this research has been conducted, and identify and analyse gaps in the knowledge base. Systematic reviews, on the other hand, tend to come after a scoping review is conducted (ie, once enough literature is available to synthesise findings) and are intended to answer a specific research question (eg, identify effect of treatment, determine diagnostic accuracy of a test, examine prognostic factors or models) once substantial evidence is available.6 Since they are often used to inform clinical policy and practice,8 systematic reviews also include a critical appraisal and risk of bias assessment, whereas scoping reviews do not. Until the field refines the definition and measurement of these milestones, a systematic review and meta-analysis will not be appropriate to conduct. Thus, a scoping review is the first logical step given the state of the field.

To date, seven reviews have been published on the attainment of independent sitting and/or walking.9,15 Of these seven, two were meta-analyses,9 10 four were systematic reviews,11,14 and one was a scoping review.15 Only one review focused on motor milestones in typically developing children, but its primary focus was not on the age of attainment.14 Rather, it evaluated motor development in the context of whether children could perform a motor skill at a specific timepoint (as measured by the Alberta Infant Motor Scale). The other six reviews focused on children with atypical development9,1113 or from developing countries with impoverished environments.12 15 Specifically, two reviews evaluated children with known diagnoses (spinal muscular atrophy11) and schizophrenia,9 one review focused on children at-risk for neuromotor delay,10 and one review included children with elevated risk for chronic disease.13 The final two reviews examined how various factors, such as maternal health and feeding practices, impact development in malnourished populations.12 15 Therefore, no reviews have been conducted to reveal the age of attainment of sitting and walking in typically developing children.

Objective

Recognising gross motor delays hinges on knowing the ‘typical’ ages of attainment, which requires a standardised definition and assessment of the onset of these skills. The aim of this scoping review is to describe research studies published globally since 2003 that report the ages at which typically developing infants begin to sit and/or walk independently (ie, ages of attainment) by geographic region. This will be done by providing a summary of how sitting and walking are operationalised, the methods by which these milestones have been obtained and evaluated, the samples from whom these data have been drawn, and the age of onset reported across studies. The compilation of data collected in children with typical development will help establish benchmarks for when sitting and walking are expected to emerge and assist with identifying children who may benefit from early monitoring and/or intervention.

Methods and analysis

Initial planning for this review began in February 2023, and the expected end date (journal submission) is February 2026. This protocol was written to conform with the guidelines provided by Lely et al16 and adheres to the relevant components of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR).17 The methodology for the proposed review will comply with the Joanna Briggs Institute guidance for scoping reviews.18 PRISMA-ScR17 will be followed in the reporting of the review findings, and a PRISMA flow diagram will be used to display the complete search results in the final scoping review.

Eligibility criteria

Our eligibility criteria for title and abstract screening and full-text screening (see online supplemental table 1) were developed through multiple pilot rounds and discussions with the principal investigator (PI) and team members. This iterative process assisted in establishing the foundation for a clear and consistent understanding of the criteria and the screening process, further inducing reliable voting among screeners. Subsequent changes to these criteria will be specified in the final scoping review.

Concept

The primary concepts are gross motor skills and ages of onset. Specifically, each included source must: (1) assess independent sitting and/or walking in their natural context and (2) report the actual age of onset for sitting and/or walking. If these skills are explored outside their natural context (eg, treadmill walking, sitting in a special chair), or the onset ages are predictions rather than actual ages infants attained the milestone, the record will be excluded.

Population

Included articles must contain a sample of typically developing infants and report the ages of onset for these children as a stand-alone group. We will exclude groups in which the ages of onset are reported for typically developing children intermixed with children with known diagnoses (eg, cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy) or at elevated likelihood for diagnoses (eg, siblings of those diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder). Stand-alone groups of children born preterm, post-term or with low birth weight will also be excluded.

Context

This review will include research articles from any geographical location across the world. Accordingly, no language restrictions will be implemented; translation services will be sought and used when available for any papers that are not published in English.

Sources

Consideration of articles will be limited to original research only, including dissertations, theses and brief reports/short communications. Resources that do not contain original research, such as book chapters, conference-related materials and review papers, will be excluded. Only recent literature (published 2003 or later) will be captured since age of attainment norms have been shown to change over time.19

Search strategy

The initial search in PubMed was developed by two biomedical librarians (AAL and KS) through an iterative process with the PI (JW) and senior authors (see online supplemental table 2); the search strategy was validated through retrieval of a set of relevant records. The six databases [vendors] we intend to search include CINAHL Plus [EBSCOhost], Embase [Elsevier], PsycNet: PsycINFO & PsycArticles [American Psychological Association], PubMed/MEDLINE [US National Library of Medicine], Scopus [Elsevier] and Web of Science: Core Collection [Clarivate Analytics]. The librarians will develop comprehensive search strategies for three databases each and then together will peer review all search strategies and revise them as appropriate with feedback from the team. Controlled vocabulary terms (eg, MeSH, EMTREE, CINAHL Subject Headings, Thesaurus of Psychological Index Terms) and keywords will be used for each of the main concepts of our research question: acquisition of motor milestone (independent sitting or walking), age of onset, infancy. The searches will be limited to items published since January 2003. There will be no limitations on language to comprehensively capture research conducted globally.

The biomedical librarians will export the results of the database searches to EndNote 21 (Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA) and identify duplicate records. The unique records will then be exported into Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia) for screening. Any newly identified duplicate records identified by Covidence will be noted as additional duplicates in the PRISMA flow diagram.

Study selection/screening

The eligibility criteria will be used to screen articles for inclusion. There will be two screening stages: title and abstract screening and full-text screening.

For the title and abstract screening phase, pilot rounds will be conducted to ensure a collective understanding of the eligibility criteria until 80% inter-rater agreement is reached. There will be 10–20 records per pilot round which will be selected by the PI. All screeners, senior authors and the PI will participate in the pilot rounds, with each member screening the same records. For official screening, six team members will participate, with two team members independently screening each record. A schedule will be implemented for each screening pair, ranging from 40 to 80 articles per week, increasing over time as screeners experience a reduction in voting discrepancies. Such discrepancies will be resolved through consensus during weekly team meetings led by the PI. Records that do not have abstracts (titles only) will be passed automatically onto full-text review.

During full-text screening, pilot rounds will be conducted until 80% inter-rater agreement is achieved. Each pilot round will consist of 10–20 records selected by the PI, and all screeners, senior authors and the PI will participate. Six team members will partake in official screening, with three people independently voting on each record. A schedule will be implemented, with 10–20 articles assigned to each screening trio per week. The PI will lead weekly meetings with the entire screening team, and discrepancies will be discussed and resolved through consensus. If records contain supplemental files/appendices/supporting information, these files will also be screened. Reasons for exclusion and the corresponding number of articles per reason will be reported for this stage of screening in our final scoping review.

Data extraction

A data extraction form will be created by the PI in Qualtrics XM (Qualtrics, Provo, Utah, USA) with input from the team. All data extraction rules will be documented within the form itself. Pilot rounds will be conducted until at least 80% inter-rater agreement is achieved; the PI will select up to five articles to be extracted per pilot round. All extractors and the PI will participate in piloting the form, and updates to the form will be made each round based on group feedback and discussion. Official data extraction will include three team members who independently extract from each record in accordance with an extraction schedule. 8–15 articles will be extracted each week, with the number of records increasing as discrepancies reduce over time. The PI will lead weekly meetings with the extraction team, and discrepancies will be discussed and jointly resolved.

The following elements are planned for data extraction: objectives, data collection year(s), study location(s), study design (ie, prospective, retrospective, cross-sectional, longitudinal), definitions for sitting and/or walking, measurement/assessment procedures for sitting and/or walking (eg, name of assessment, scoring, who conducted), sample size, sample distribution by sex and race, and ages of onset for sitting and/or walking. Supplemental files will also be reviewed for any information that is relevant to extract. Authors of included articles will not be contacted to obtain any missing information; rather, this information will be indicated as ‘not reported’. In cases in which multiple articles share identical data, the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions (chapter 5) guidance will be followed.20

Any changes to the data extraction form made throughout the review will be documented directly in the data extraction form and published in the final manuscript. There are no plans to conduct a critical appraisal or risk of bias assessment since this is not required for a scoping review.

Data analysis and presentation

A quality assurance check, which involves reviewing all extracted data for accuracy and consistency, will be performed once data extraction is complete. Specifically, team members will be assigned data elements in which they will compare the finalised extracted data with the original full-text articles. Our final agreed-on rules, which will be documented in the data extraction form, will be followed diligently to ensure accuracy. Any reporting inaccuracies will be rectified jointly with the PI.

The PI will then clean and code the data using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft, Redmond, Washington, USA). No coding will be required for data elements with numerical data entry (eg, sample size, age). Similarly, no coding will be necessary for data elements with closed-ended responses, unless they contain write-in categories, such as ‘other: _________.’ In these situations, the extracted text will either be combined with already existing categories or be grouped into new categories. Data elements with open-ended responses will require coding, ranging from formatting changes (to achieve consistent appearances and improve readability) to grouping and categorising responses, depending on the complexity and heterogeneity of the extracted text.

IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows V.29.0.1.0 (IBM) will be used to run descriptive statistics to summarise findings for this review. Draft tables (tables13) demonstrate how we intend to report our data; note these tables are provided for sitting articles only, so equivalent tables will be created for walking articles. Tables and figures will be accompanied by narrative summaries to describe results. Our processes for data synthesis and presentation may change throughout the review process as we become more familiar with the structure of included articles. Any modifications will be reported in the final scoping review.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not included in the planning of this scoping review (ie, writing this protocol). However, stakeholders will be included with regard to interpreting and disseminating the findings. Specifically, we will include stakeholders who use motor milestone data in everyday practice to screen for developmental delays (eg, paediatricians, physical therapists), individuals who regularly witness milestone onset (eg, parents/guardians of infants, daycare staff), and academic researchers (eg, developmental psychologists, motor development researchers). Their involvement will help with determining research gaps and identifying future funding priorities.

Ethics and dissemination

Since this scoping review will only include data from previously published papers, ethical approval is not required.

Findings from this scoping review will be disseminated at scientific conferences and in a peer-reviewed journal.

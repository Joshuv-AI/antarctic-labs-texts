The diagnosis and prognosis role of miR-155 in neonatal acute respiratory distress syndrome
Authors: Yangping Zhou, Yuan Yu, Jiangshan Wang, Meina Sun, Liang Bao, Xin Du, Juan Ouyang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNeonatal acute respiratory distress syndrome (NARDS) can cause respiratory failure in newborns and even pose a threat to their lives, causing a heavy financial burden on families.AimThis study aims to explore the potential role of miR-155 as a diagnostic marker and prognostic predictor for NARDS.MethodsThis study included 100 non-NARDS newborns and 100 NARDS newborns. The plasma miR-155 expression was measured by RT-qPCR. The ROC curve was used to analyze the diagnostic efficacy of miR-155 alone and the combined diagnostic efficacy of PT, APTT, FIB, and CRP. The correlation between clinical indicators and miR-155 expression was analyzed by the chi-square test. The general clinical information of NARDS neonates with different severity degrees and prognosis was compared. Multivariate logistic regression was used to identify the risk factors for NARDS.ResultsThe plasma miR-155 level was elevated in NARDS newborns. miR-155 may serve as a promising biomarker in the diagnosis of NARDS. The value of the multi-factor combined diagnosis was significantly improved. 1-minute Apgar score, PT, APTT, and CRP level were statistically related to the miR-155 expression. Abnormal FIB level and the miR-155 expression were independent risk factors for NARDS. The more severe NARDS newborns have poorer coagulation function, stronger inflammatory response, and higher miR-155 level. A higher level of miR-155 was observed in NARDS newborns with a poorer prognosis.ConclusionThe plasma miR-155 level is upregulated in NARDS, and miR-155 may be a potential diagnostic marker for NARDS. NARDS newborns with miR-155 high expression have a poor prognosis.

Introduction

Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is a syndrome characterized by hypoxemia caused by damage to alveolar capillaries induced by damaging factors [1, 2]. The core clinical features of ARDS are manifested as oxygenation dysfunction, progressive dyspnea, and respiratory failure. In the neonatal intensive care unit, neonatal acute respiratory distress syndrome (NARDS) a form of ARDS that occurs in newborns and is a type of critical respiratory condition in newborns, and it is also one of the major causes of neonatal death [3, 4]. Early identification and risk prediction of NARDS, timely assessment of the condition, and initiation of intervention measures all have extremely important clinical significance. However, at present, the diagnosis of NARDS still mainly relies on clinical manifestations and imaging examinations, and there are no specific blood biomarkers [5]. Identifying the differentially expressed molecules in NARDS newborns and exploring highly sensitive diagnostic indicators have become critical scientific issues that need to be addressed urgently.

miRNAs are a type of endogenous non-coding single-stranded RNAs molecule with a length of approximately 20 nucleotides, and they are widely present in eukaryotes [6]. miRNAs can regulate the expression levels of target genes through post-transcriptional mechanisms, and participate in a series of physiological and pathological processes such as cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and metabolism [7, 8]. The significant advantages of miRNAs are their high stability in various body fluids such as blood, cerebrospinal fluid, and urine, which provides convenience for the detection and analysis of clinical samples [9, 10]. The occurrence and development of the early stages of disease, the expression profiles of miRNAs undergo characteristic changes. The changes in miRNAs occur much earlier than traditional serum markers and more sensitively reflect the pathological state of the body [11]. By detecting the expression levels of miRNAs in body fluids, it is possible to achieve early detection and diagnosis of diseases, providing a window period for timely intervention.

miR-155 has attracted much attention due to its crucial roles in various fields such as tumor development, immune regulation, and neurodegenerative diseases [12, 13]. It was reported that miR-155 was upregulated in ARDS caused by sepsis. The research indicated that miR-155 inhibited alveolar type II epithelial cells and promoted ARDS [14]. The high expression of miR-155 was associated with the downregulation of lung function [15, 16]. However, there is currently no clear report on the expression characteristics and specific regulatory role of miR-155 in NARDS newborns. This is a scientific issue that needs to be explored.

This study found that the expression of miR-155 in the plasma of NARDS patients was relatively high. This study aimed to systematically investigate the expression of miR-155 in NARDS newborns and its potential clinical application value.

Methods and materials

Subjects

This study adopted a retrospective research design, and the newborns were from The Sixth Medical Center of PLA General Hospital from 2021 to 2025. The sample size was calculated using G*Power 3.1 software for an independent two‑sample t‑test. With a two‑sided significance level of α = 0.05, a power (1 − β) of 0.95, and assuming a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.5), the required sample size was determined to be 88 participants per group, corresponding to a total of 176 subjects. To account for dealing with confounding factors, a total of 200 neonates were recruited in this study. All the subjects were recruited in a consecutive manner for the study. 100 neonates with NARDS and 100 healthy neonates (non-NARDS) were included in this study. NARDS newborns were admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) within 24 h after birth. After admission, venous blood samples were collected in a standardized manner and anticoagulated to separate the plasma for subsequent research. All the parents of the newborns participating in this study were fully informed about the purpose, process, and potential risks of the research, and voluntarily signed the informed consent. This study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of The Sixth Medical Center of PLA General Hospital.

Inclusion criteria for NARDS newborns: 1. NARDS meets the diagnostic criteria for “Montreux definition” (2017 edition) [5]. 1). Accompanied by acute respiratory distress symptoms and requiring positive pressure ventilation to assist in lung expansion. 2). Pulmonary imaging showed diffuse reduction in transparency or “white lung”. 3). Pulmonary oedema not caused by heart failure.

Exclusion criteria for NARDS newborns: (1) Having congenital heart disease. (2) Having congenital lung developmental malformations. (3) Respiratory depression caused by sedatives. (4) Chromosomal abnormalities.

The classification criteria for the different severity of NARDS: The higher the oxygenation index, the worse the lung function. Based on the maximum OI index (OI = average airway pressure × inspired oxygen concentration × 100 / arterial oxygen partial pressure) of the NARDS newborns during their hospital stay, they were divided into the mild NARDS group: 4.0 ≤ OI < 8.0, the moderate NARDS group: 8.0 ≤ OI < 16.0, and the severe NARDS group: OI > 16.0.

Starting from the day the NARDS newborns were admitted to the NICU, the follow-up observation period was 10 weeks [17]. This time period can completely cover the entire course of the disease from acute treatment to stable recovery, enabling us to obtain more comprehensive and reliable outcome data. For NARDS neonates with a good prognosis, the following conditions must be met: successfully transferred out of the NICU, completely removed from ventilator support, and with stable vital signs. For NARDS neonates with a poor prognosis, the following conditions must be met: death within 10 weeks or failure to be transferred out of the NICU for at least 10 weeks. The newborns participating in this study were not transferred to other hospitals, and there were no newborns who stopped the treatment. Based on the follow-up data, an analysis was conducted on the prognosis and survival of NARDS neonates.

RT-qPCR

miRNA from the plasma was extracted using a specialized kit (TIANGEN, Beijing). The successfully extracted RNA (with an OD260/OD280 ratio ranging from 1.8 to 2.0) was used to synthesize cDNA using specialized miRNA reverse transcription kits (TIANGEN, Beijing). Reverse transcription conditions for miRNAs: The reverse transcription reaction was carried out in a volume of 20 µL. The PCR reaction program was set as follows: 37 °C for 15 min to remove gDNA, 42 °C for 50 min for reverse transcription, and 95 °C for 5 min to inactivate the enzymes. The cDNA product was stored at 4 °C until use. The qPCR amplification program: The qPCR amplification was carried out in a 20 µL reaction system. The cycling conditions were set as follows: 95 °C for 2 min of pre-denaturation and enzyme activation, 95 °C for 40 s of denaturation, and 60 °C for annealing and extension for 30 s, for a total of 40 cycles. The gene relative expression was calculated by the formula 2−(ΔΔCt). The miRNA relative expression was calibrated by U6. This study calculated the average standard deviation of the CT values of U6. The results indicated that SD < 1. These reverse primers were universal and provided by the qPCR kit. The primer sequences were as follows:miR-155 forward primer: 5′-GCCGAGTTAATGCTAATCGTG-3′U6 forward primer: 5′-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACATATACT-3′

Data statistics

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corporation, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9 software (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA). The data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD); the comparison of continuous variables between the two groups was performed using the independent sample t-test, and the analysis of differences in categorical variables between groups was performed using the chi-square test. For the comparison among the mild, moderate, and severe NARDS groups, the continuous variables were analyzed using univariate ANOVA, and the categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. To evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of miR-155 for NARDS, this study plotted the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC curve) and calculated key indicators such as the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity. The Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival curve was used to analyze the prognosis survival rate of NARDS newborns; the Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was employed to screen the relevant factors influencing NARDS prognosis. The logistic regression analyses were used to identify the independent risk factors for the NARDS. All clinical baseline variables were first analyzed by univariate logistic regression. The Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was applied to assess the calibration of the multivariate logistic regression model (P > 0.05: The model fits well with no significant deviation in fit, and the model is qualified). Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were further assessed for multicollinearity using the variance inflation factor (VIF). Indicators with a VIF < 5 were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. P < 0.05 indicates statistical significance.

Result

The level of plasma miR-155 was markedly higher in NRDA newborns

The baseline clinical data of the NARDS group and the non-NARDS group were systematically compared and analyzed. The results indicated that there were statistically significant differences in gestational age (GA), 1-minute Apgar score ≤ 7, 5-minute Apgar score ≤ 7, coagulation function indicators (prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), fibrinogen (FIB)), and C-reactive protein (CRP) levels (Table 1). The RT-qPCR was used to detect the expression level of plasma miR-155 in two groups of newborns. The results showed that the level of plasma miR-155 of the NARDS group was markedly higher than that of the non-NARDS group (Fig. 1A). The ROC curve showed that miR-155 acted as a promising diagnostic marker for NARDS (AUC = 0.886) (Fig. 1B). The above results indicate that the expression level of miR-155 may be related to the occurrence of NARDS, and it is expected to become a potential non-invasive diagnostic biomarker for NARDS.

The diagnostic efficacy of miR-155, PT, APTT, FIB, CRP, and the combined diagnosis was analyzed

To further clarify the diagnostic value of miR-155 for NARDS, this study supplemented the ROC curves for individual indicators such as PT, APTT, FIB, and CRP, and the ROC curve for the multi-index combined diagnosis. The results showed that the AUC values for the single indicators of miR-155, PT, APTT, FIB, CRP, as well as the combined diagnostic model were 0.886, 0.732, 0.755, 0.709, 0.882, and 0.977, respectively (Fig. 2). The optimal cut-off values, sensitivity, and specificity of each indicator for diagnosing NARDS were statistically analyzed (Table 2). The comparative analysis showed that the diagnostic efficacy of the single miR-155 indicator was significantly better than that of PT, APTT, FIB, and CRP. Moreover, the AUC value of the multi-indicator combined diagnostic model was significantly higher than that of all single indicators, suggesting that the combined detection of multiple indicators improved the accuracy and reliability of NARDS diagnosis.

The correlations between clinical indicators and the expression of miR-155 were analyzed

The median expression of miR-155 was calculated in the NARDS group. Based on this median value, the NARDS newborns were divided into the miR-155 high-expression group and the miR-155 low-expression group. A chi-square test was used to statistically analyze the correlation between each clinical indicator and the expression of miR-155. The results indicated that 1-minute Apgar score of ≤ 7, PT, APTT, and CRP levels were statistically related to the expression of miR-155 (Table 3). The result indicated that in the miR-155 high-expression group, the number of newborns experiencing asphyxia was higher, the clotting time was prolonged, and the inflammatory response in the body was more intense. However, the underlying regulatory mechanisms still require further experiments for exploration.

The independent risk factors for NARDS were screened through logistic regression analysis

The univariate logistic regression pointed out that GA(OR = 0.416, 95% CI = 0.213–0.811, P = 0.010), 1-minute Apgar score ≤ 7 (OR = 3.907, 95% CI = 1.239–12.323, P = 0.020), 5-minute Apgar score ≤ 7 (OR = 4.846, 95% CI = 1.020-23.028, P = 0.047), PT (OR = 3.122, 95% CI = 1.716–5.683, P < 0.001), APTT (OR = 3.000, 95% CI = 1.648–5.460, P < 0.001), FIB (OR = 0.359, 95% CI = 0.200-0.646, P < 0.001), CRP (OR = 2.571, 95% CI = 1.430–4.624, P = 0.002), and high miR-155 level (OR = 3.250, 95% CI = 1.785–5.916, P < 0.001) were risk factors for NARDS. The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was used to evaluate the calibration of the multivariate logistic regression model. The result indicated that χ2 = 11.669; degree of freedom = 8; P = 0.167, indicating a good model fit with no significant lack of fit. The multivariate logistic regression analysis further showed that FIB (OR = 0.404, 95% CI = 0.203–0.806, P = 0.010) and high miR-155 level (OR = 3.102, 95% CI = 1.566–6.147, P = 0.001) were independent risk factors for NARDS (Table 4).

The general clinical characteristics of different severity levels of NARDS neonates were compared

Based on the OI index, 100 NARDS neonates were divided into three subgroups: mild, moderate, and severe. The number of cases in each subgroup was 28, 47, and 25, respectively. The comparative analysis of clinical indicators revealed that, compared with the mild and severe group, the FIB level of severe NARDS newborns was lower; while the expression levels of PT, APTT, CRP, and miR-155 were significantly higher (Table 5). This indicated that the degree of lung function impairment in NARDS neonates was statistically related to the coagulation function, inflammatory response, and the expression level of miR-155. The specific manifestation was that the worse the lung function, the lower the coagulation function, the more intense the systemic inflammatory response, and the higher the level of plasma miR-155.

The baseline data of NARDS newborns with different prognoses were compared

Based on the clinical prognosis, the neonates in the NARDS group were divided into two groups: 79 good-prognosis neonates and 21 poor-prognosis neonates. The comparative analysis of baseline data and laboratory indicators among the subgroups showed that there were marked statistical differences between the poor prognosis group and the good prognosis group in terms of GA, 1-minute Apgar score ≤ 7, 5-minute Apgar score ≤ 7, as well as PT, APTT, FIB, CRP, and miR-155 levels (Table 6). The follow-up data of NARDS newborns were analyzed for the prognosis survival rate. The K-M survival curve showed that the prognosis survival rate of miR-155 high-expression newborns was significantly lower (Fig. 3). The univariate Cox regression analysis showed that the GA (HR = 0.758, 95% CI = 0.299–1.922, P = 0.045), 1-minute Apgar score ≤ 7 (HR = 2.834, 95% CI = 1.006–7.986, P = 0.049), PT (HR = 2.775, 95% CI = 1.075–7.161, P = 0.035), APTT (HR = 2.908, 95% CI = 1.107–7.125, P = 0.035), FIB (HR = 0.305, 95% CI = 0.109–0.856, P = 0.024), CRP (HR = 2.743, 95% CI = 1.061–7.090, P = 0.037), and high miR-155 expression (HR = 3.899, 95% CI = 1.282–11.857, P = 0.016) were associated with the prognosis and survival of NARDS newborns. Further multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that the GA (HR = 0.269, 95% CI = 0.092–0.793, P = 0.017) and high miR-155 expression (HR = 4.601, 95% CI = 1.427–14.831, P = 0.011) were key factors for survival of NARDS (Table 7). The above results suggested that the NARDS neonates with poor prognosis presented with clinical features such as low gestational age, asphyxia at birth, poor coagulation function, severe systemic inflammatory response, and higher expression level of plasma miR-155. The multivariate Cox regression analysis further confirmed the potential correlation between high miR-155 expression and the severity and prognosis of NARDS.

Discussion

NARDS is a severe acute respiratory disorder commonly encountered in neonates. Premature infants have immature lung structures, which leads to impaired anti-inflammatory and anti-injury functions of the lungs. Under the influence of stimulating factors, premature infants are particularly prone to developing NARDS, such as bacterial infections and amniotic fluid aspiration [18, 19]. Clinical practice showed that if NARDS can be identified and intervened upon promptly at an early stage, the prognosis of NARDS can be significantly improved. Therefore, actively exploring new biological indicators related to the occurrence and development of NARDS in premature infants can provide new ideas and directions for the early diagnosis of NARDS.

The case-control analysis revealed that compared with non-NARDS newborns, NARDS newborns had a higher proportion of low gestational age, 1-minute Apgar score ≤ 7 at birth, 5-minute Apgar score ≤ 7, abnormal coagulation function indicators, and significantly elevated plasma CRP levels. The above results are highly consistent with the conclusions of previous related studies [20, 21]. This further confirms that low gestational age, perinatal asphyxia, coagulation dysfunction, and enhanced systemic inflammatory response are all important associated factors for the occurrence and development of NARDS.

miR-155 is one of the most extensively studied miRNA molecules. Studies have confirmed that miR-155 can serve as a potential biomarker for the early diagnosis and prognosis assessment of lung cancer [22–24]. In sepsis-associated lung injury, the expression of miR-155 was significantly upregulated, and the extent of miR-155 increase was positively correlated with the severity of lung function impairment [14, 15]. This indicates that the expression of miR-155 is elevated in pulmonary diseases related to sepsis. This study found that the level of plasma miR-155 in NARDS newborns was significantly higher. Multivariate logistic regression analysis confirmed that high miR-155 level was an independent risk factor for the occurrence of NARDS. The study on the NARDS study showed that the expression level of miR-155 was positively correlated with the severity of the disease in NARDS newborns; the results of the prognosis group analysis indicated that the plasma miR-155 level in the poor prognosis group was markedly higher than that in the good prognosis group. Multivariate Cox survival analysis indicated that miR-155 was a key factor influencing the survival of NARDS newborns. The above results clearly indicate that miR-155 may be associated with the disease progression and prognosis of NARDS.

In NARDS newborns, the blood coagulation balance was disrupted, often accompanied by abnormal blood coagulation functions [21, 25, 26]. Under inflammatory conditions, endothelial damage triggered the extrinsic coagulation pathway, resulting in the formation of microthrombi [27]. PT is a commonly used indicator to reflect the exogenous coagulation pathway; APTT reflects the internal coagulation function status. The prolonged PT and APTT indicate a deficiency in both endogenous and exogenous coagulation functions [26, 28, 29]. A decrease in FIB level indicates excessive consumption of coagulation substances. This study found that the PT and APTT times were prolonged in NARDS newborns, and the FIB level was significantly increased. The prolonged PT, APTT, and the lower FIB levels are statistically associated with the high expression of miR-155. It was reported that miR-155 targeted suppressor of cytokine signaling 1 (SOCS1) to enhance the hypercoagulable state in patients with sicca syndrome [30]. Multiple studies have indicated that miR-155 mediated the inflammatory response by targeting Toll-like receptor (TLR4) [31–33]. However, whether miR-155 affects the coagulation function and inflammatory response of NARDS by targeting SOCS1 and TLR4 still requires further functional experiments to verify.

This study adopted a retrospective research design to explore the clinical correlation between miR-155 and NARDS. However, this study has some limitations. Firstly, although the case study data included could verify the correlation between miR-155 and NARDS, it was difficult to clearly establish the causal relationship between miR-155 and NARDS. Secondly, in this study, the prognosis assessment of NARDS newborns was limited to short-term outcomes, and no long-term follow-up was conducted. This might have masked the expression differences of miR-155 in different prognostic subgroups and may reduce the accuracy of the conclusion. Thirdly, the stability of U6 in plasma was further verified. In the upcoming research, we will systematically evaluate the stability of U6 in plasma using the BestKeeper algorithm. Furthermore, the addition of 5 S serves as an additional calibration tool for standardizing the relative expression of miR-155. At the same time, the relative expression levels of miR-155 after 5 S standardization were compared with those after U6 standardization to further verify the stability of U6 in plasma. Next, this study will conduct a prospective cohort study to increase the sample size and control confounding factors, and carry out long-term follow-up to clarify the miR-155 long-term prognostic value.

In summary, this study found that miR-155 was highly expressed in NARDS neonates. miR-155 may be a potential diagnostic factor and prognostic marker for neonates with NARDS.

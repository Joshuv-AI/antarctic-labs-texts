Research review on land development rights and its implications for China's national territory spatial planning
Authors: Peng Cheng, Yongqin Fan, Houtian Tang, Ke Liu, Shiman Wu, Gaoli Zhu, Ping Jiang, Weili Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBased on the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) and Web of Science (WOS) databases, this article analyzes the deductive context, cooperation network, and research hotspots of land development rights (LDR) research in the Chinese and international literature by using CiteSpace software, and it also explores the implications of this research for the theory and practice of national territory spatial planning (NTSP) in China. The results show that (1) the literature on LDR in Chinese and international journal articles initially appeared in 1995 and 1973, respectively, researches in China experienced three stages: embryonic fluctuating development, rapid growth and stable development, and wave development, while international researches experienced two stages: embryonic fluctuating and a gradually increasing development. (2) Among these scholars and research institutions, there is no obvious difference between Chinese and international scholars, while the Renmin University of China and the State University System of Florida are the research institutions with the largest number of Chinese and international journal articles, respectively. (3) In terms of publishing journals, international journals mainly focus on land policy, cities, and resource fields, while Chinese journals mainly focus on the agricultural economy, civil and commercial law, economic systems, and macroeconomic management fields. (4) The direction and scale of thematic research vary greatly, with Chinese research mainly conducted from the perspectives of rights attribution and benefits distribution, while international research mainly focuses on the operation of the right-to-development system and its impact on the environment. In the future, studies focus on China's need to strengthen the research and institutional practice of LDR at the legal level, value level, and extension level following national conditions, formulate a land value-added benefit distribution system with efficiency and fairness, and strengthen the practice of LDR in China's NTSP based on the differences between urban and rural development.Keywords: Land development rights, China's national territory spatial planning, Land value-added benefits, Hotspot research, Visual analysis, CiteSpace

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of global industrialization and urbanization, land use change has become more drastic and extensive, causing a huge impact on human production and life [1,2]. Countries such as Italy, and the United States of America (USA) have strictly restricted land resource utilization to promote socioeconomic development, and to coordinate the allocation of land elements, thus giving birth to the concept of land development rights (LDR) [[3], [4], [5]]. The concept of LDR includes a large range of land utilization limitations from restrictions on changing certain land uses to the intensity of land resource utilization, and it is worth noting that it will be difficult to achieve sustainable land use without these restrictions [6]. Meanwhile, the exercise and regulation of LDR have certain externalities, it is urgent to trade off the imbalance of public and private interests generated during this process [7]. The United Kingdom (UK) first introduced the Urban-Rural Planning Act in 1947, which nationalized LDR and established the system of LDR, and the LDR was defined as the qualifications and the claims to take action that caused any substantial changes to any building or land [8]. In the late 1960s, the government of the USA established a market mechanism for LDR, which mainly included two trading channels: transfer and purchase of LDR, which had reached significant achievements in cultivated land protection [9,10]. The relevant research of international literature revolves around the practice of LDR transactions, without a unified theoretical framework, and mainly focuses on case analysis, mostly analyzing the influencing factors of LDR and its implementation performance. Among them, the regulatory mechanisms of enforcement, compatibility with government regulatory policies, and the leading role of public institutions are important influencing factors affecting LDR [[11], [12], [13]]. However, scholars have various opinions on the implementation performance of LDR, with some scholars believing that LDR can improve zoning efficiency, effectively alleviate the failure caused by rigid planning, and rely on market mechanisms to compensate for the losses of land rights holders [14,15]. Other scholars believe that due to the need for LDR to be coordinated with zoning planning, LDR programs become complex with high transaction costs, thereby limiting the effective performance of LDR [10].

Although current Land Management Law of China does not explicitly propose the concept of LDR, there are many issues related to LDR in the practice of land use [4,16], which many Chinese scholars have extensively explored [[17], [18], [19]]. Due to the implementation of the socialist system in China, some scholars believe that from a legal and institutional perspective, the nation and collectives, as the owners of land, should have the development rights and benefits of land [20,21]. The first land value-added benefits belong to the nation, and the second land value-added benefits are appropriately tilted toward collective farmers [22,23]. In a sense, it is precisely because LDR belongs to the nation and collectives that the nation is able to plan and regulate land use through land use planning and laws [24,25]. In China, there are two levels of implied LDR, namely the administrative permission of the superior government for construction in subordinate regions and the administrative permission of the government for project construction and planning. Additionally, a spatial planning system with Chinese characteristics based on the core essence of LDR has been constructed [[26], [27], [28]].

The LDR derived from spatial regulation is closely related to China's national territory spatial planning (NTSP), and the essence of NTSP is the redistribution of LDR in the planned area for a certain policy purpose [26,29]. NTSP is the combination and layout of production, life, and ecological elements in point, line, and surface space, moreover, government regulation of NTSP is an important reason for promoting or limiting LDR to a certain extent [30,31]. Therefore, it can be considered that NTSP is the effective allocation of LDR in land space. In fact, with the continuous emphasis on NTSP, only by establishing or restricting LDR can various government departments strengthen their management capabilities for land spatial utilization behavior [32,33]. As a result, various types of NTSP conflicts focus on the game of LDR spatial allocation. Thus, considering the close connection between NTSP and LDR, in the process of improving the regulation of NTSP, the regulatory role of LDR must be fully utilized to achieve the overall optimal allocation of land resources [34,35]. With the release of the "Guidelines for the Compilation of Provincial NTSP" by the Ministry of Natural Resources of China in January 2020, the scientific, targeted, and operational aspects of the NTSP have become increasingly important [33,36]. To effectively implement the development, protection, and renovation of land space, there is an urgent need to learn from the introductory concepts and techniques of LDR research.

Meanwhile, analyzing the relevant literature in China and internationally, it was found that although there is relatively little research on the combination of LDR and NTSP, some scholars have shown that the results of research related to LDR hold important reference significance for carrying out NTSP [26,35]. On this basis, this article adopts the bibliometric analysis method and CiteSpace visualization analysis software, searches relative articles in the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) and Web of Science (WOS) databases, and intends to address the following issues through scientific knowledge mapping: (1) What are the research hotspots, characteristics, and development trends of Chinese and international literature in the field of LDR. (2) What are the research differences between Chinese literature and international literature in the field of LDR. (3) What is the reference significance of the research experience in LDR for the future research direction of China's NTSP. It is hoped that this study can provide a reference for future research on Chinese LDR and NTSP.

Data sources and research methods

Data sources

The data for this article are sourced from the WOS and CNKI databases. Firstly, the WOS is a large-scale comprehensive, multidisciplinary, and core journal citation index database that is highly influential internationally. Therefore, we select three major citation index sources, the SCI, SSCI, and A&HCI, from the WOS database for international bibliometric analysis. The retrieval settings of this study are as follows: (TS = "land development rights" or "land development right" or "transferable development rights" or "transferable development right" or "transfer of development rights" or "transfer of development right" or "transfer development rights" or "transfer development right" or "development rights transfer" or "development right transfer"), TS is the theme, and the literature types are "articles" and "review articles". The start time is set to "unlimited", and the search deadline is May 31, 2023. After deduplication, 169 international journal literature records are obtained. Secondly, the CNKI database has abundant academic journal resources, with the largest number and widest classification of articles, making it the largest and most important knowledge-sharing and dissemination platform in China. This article takes "land development rights" as the search topic, including the CSSCI, CSCD, EI, and Peking University core journal sources, from the CNKI database for Chinese bibliometric analysis. The start time is set as "unlimited", the search deadline is May 31, 2023. After manually deleting invalid records, a total of 509 Chinese journal literature records are obtained.

Research methods

Bibliometric analysis has the advantage of revealing a wider range of descriptive characteristics of literature data in a more objective manner and with a more comprehensive coverage than other more subjective and in-depth literature review studies [37,38]. This article uses the CiteSpace literature co-citation analysis software, developed by the team of Professor Chen Chaomei, for bibliometric analysis [39]. This software is used to explore the hotspots, frontiers, and trends in a certain research field through functions such as literature co-citation, co-word network, and author co-citation analysis [40,41]. By drawing knowledge maps, it achieves visualization purposes, and it is widely used in research fields such as land use and carbon emissions [42,43]. Compared with bibliometric analysis software such as VOSviewer, Gephi, etc., CiteSpace has the advantage of tracking, processing, and measuring data in specific knowledge fields, exploring critical paths and knowledge inflection points, researching the alternating movement process of paradigms, analyzing the research context in scientific research fields and exploring the latest trends of disciplines in a multivariate, time-sharing and dynamic visual language [44]. Therefore, this article uses CiteSpace software to visually analyze the research hotspots, frontiers, and trends in the field of LDR in China and internationally.

Embryonic fluctuating development stage (1973–2012). At this stage, the number of published articles accounts for 42.01 % of the total international literature in this field, with significant fluctuations. The average annual number of published articles is 1.78, and the number of publications in many years is 0. This finding indicates that although research in the field of LDR was carried out earlier internationally, scholars have produced insufficient research on LDR, and the output of literature is not high [[45], [46], [47]].

Gradually increasing development stage (2013–2023). The number of published articles during this stage shows a continuous growth trend, accounting for 57.99 % of the total international published articles in this field, and the average annual number of published articles reaches 8.91. The surge in publications at this stage is closely related to the designation of December 5th as "World Soil Day" by the 68th United Nations General Assembly in 2013 and the designation of 2015 as the "International Year of Soil". The government, environmental organizations, and other institutions have strengthened residents' awareness of soil and other related land ecological protection through extensive publicity [48,49]. Meanwhile, these events drew scholars' attention to the importance of land in food security, the mitigation of biodiversity loss, and water security and increased research in the LDR field [50,51].

Chinese scholars started studying LDR relatively late, with the earliest article published in 1995. However, the number of articles published in Chinese journals is much higher than that published in international journals. The research topics in the field of LDR in China include LDR, agricultural LDR, institutional innovation, development rights, rural revitalization, sustainable agricultural development, land value-added benefits, etc. Based on Fig. 1, Fig. 3, the research progress in China can be divided into three stages:

Embryonic fluctuating development stage (1995–2004). At this stage, the number of published articles is small, accounting for 4.72 % of the total number of articles in China in this field. The average annual number of published articles is 2.4, showing a slight growth trend. The research topics focus on the concept, characteristics, and theory of LDR.

Rapid growth and stable development stage (2005–2015). During this stage, the number of published articles shows a fluctuating and sharply increasing trend, with an average annual number of published articles fluctuating from 7 to a maximum of 38. This finding is related to the latest revision and implementation of the "Land Management Law of the People's Republic of China" in 2004, attracting the attention of numerous scholars, leading to a surge in the number of published articles and a trend toward stability.

Wave development stage (2016–2023). The number of articles published in this stage shows a slow downward trend, causing a reduction on the research in the field of LDR. However, the average annual number of articles published is 23.3, which is higher than that in the first stage. It is worth noting that the number of LDR theme articles reached a sub-peak in 2022 (33 articles). Upon investigation, it was found that the "Implementation Regulations of China's Land Management Law (Revised in 2021)" was officially implemented in September 2021, and it has been extensively revised in various aspects such as cultivated land protection, land acquisition, and homestead management, which has attracted academic attention to the reform of China's rural land system, and LDR is also an important research topic of academic concern [16,52].

In addition, although research in China started later than in other countries, it has developed rapidly, with the highest-frequency keyword being "LDR (257)", followed by "farmland development rights (37)", "development rights (18)", and "land value-added benefits (16)", etc. Based on the burst keyword information (Table 5), the main keywords in Chinese journal articles were "land acquisition", "land acquisition compensation", and "agricultural land development rights" during 2004–2010. The main keyword was "land circulation" during 2011–2015. The main keywords were "land value-added benefit", "rural revitalization", and "land spatial planning" during 2016–2023. Thus, combined with data analysis results in Fig. 2, Table 4, Table 5, research on LDR in China mainly includes the following two aspects:

(1)The allocation of ownership and value-added benefits of LDR mainly includes keyword clusters of "#0 land development rights", "#2 farmland development rights", and "#6 land contract right", as well as keywords such as "land system", "land development rights transfer", and "land value-added benefits". Due to the lack of an official definition of the LDR system in China, the ownership and benefit distribution of LDR has become a difficult issue in institutional innovation [17,18]. There are three main viewpoints in the academic community regarding attribution. The first viewpoint holds that LDR should belong to the private sector, believing that only when the LDR belongs to the land owner is it possible to maximize its value, effectively protect the interests of farmers, and solve China's current land problems [76,77]. Then some scholars put forward the theory of private ownership of land value-added benefits. Scholars such as He, M.J. [78], Zhu, X.H. [79], and Zhou, T.Y [80]. mainly believe that the process of land value-added benefits actually damages farmers' right to use land. Based on the principle of compensation for damage, the value-added benefits of LDR should be returned to farmers in the form of government compensation. The second viewpoint holds that the LDR should be nationalized. Scholars who hold this viewpoint have conducted in-depth research on the ownership of LDR in Western countries such as the UK and the USA, and they believe that if the ownership of Chinese land belongs to the nation, then the LDR should also belong to the nation [78,81,82]. Further developed into the theory of land value-added benefits returning to the public, scholars such as Liu, Y. [83], and Liu, G.Z. [81], mainly consider the fairness of benefits distribution, believing that the reason for land value-added benefits lies in the development and progress of the entire society and that these benefits should be shared by all members of society. Policy implications for China's NTSP and the construction of a research framework for future LDR

Based on the progress and characteristics of researches on LDR in China and internationally, this article intends to summarize its implications for China's NTSP research. Additionally, it constructs a research framework for future LDR in China from the legal, value, and extension perspectives (Fig. 6).

Research framework for future LDR based on optimization of NTSP.

Firstly, the process of formulating and implementing an NTSP has a significant impact on social, economic, and ecological cultural values, and actors pursue increasing social costs or reducing social benefits to maximize their own interests [[94], [95], [96], [97]]. Therefore, when conducting researches on NTSP, it is necessary to always pay attention to the value orientation of NTSP, adhere to the values of people-oriented, ecological priority, balance efficiency and promote fairness, continuously revise NTSP measures, and achieve efficiency value and sustainable development goals. The main content of the legalization of LDR lies in the calculation of the value and value-added benefits of LDR. On the one hand, when calculating the value of LDR, it is necessary to analyze their in-deepth value composition, and then calculate the value of all LDR by calculating the values of each part. On the other hand, when calculating land value-added benefits, specific distinctions and calculations should be made, and these benefits can be divided into natural appreciation benefits and artificial appreciation benefits to more scientifically calculate land appreciation benefits.

Secondly, the formulation of NTSP is essentially the regulation of LDR, and due to the involvement of numerous interest groups, it is necessary to prioritize the dialectical and unified relationship between "time, space, people, and things". On the basis of ensuring the development and protection of LDR, it is necessary to coordinate the rights and interests of relevant groups and the distribution of interests by promoting the legalization of land spatial control. In addition, it is necessary to build an institutional framework that covers the central legal and regulatory system, local legal and regulatory system, planning technique standard system, and achievement implementation guarantee system, to continuously promote the development of NTSP. It is worth noting that value innovation is the foundation and guarantee for the innovation of the LDR system. The two core issues in constructing the LDR system are the ownership and distribution of the value-added benefits of LDR, which are highly interrelated, while, the ownership of LDR determines the distribution of land value-added benefits. Although Chinese scholars have fully drawn on the theory and practical experience of the international LDR system to explore the ownership and distribution of the value-added benefits of China's LDR, given the significant differences between the land system in China and that in the international community as well as the fact that the LDR system has not yet been established, it is still necessary to conduct argumentation and exploration in the future based on China's national conditions.

Finally, the extension characteristic is an important feature of NTSP and the construction of the LDR system. The determination of "three zones delineated by three lines for land use" is a key focus of China's NTSP, which also has external impacts and cooperates with other connecting policies (such as rural revitalization policies, etc.) to achieve diversified policy objectives [[98], [99], [100]]. Therefore, when conducting researches on NTSP, it is necessary to continuously promote "multiple compliance" and to fully consider its compatibility and effectiveness with other policies in China, to achieve the goal of sustainable regional socioeconomic development. Moreover, LDR do not operate independently; rather, LDR interact and cooperate with policies such as NTSP and urban-rural integration development to jointly achieve diversified policy objectives. As the country continues to attach great importance to issues such as agriculture, rural areas, and urban-rural integration, how to coordinate LDR with other policies will be a hot research direction in the future.

Discussion and conclusion

Discussion

(1)Multidisciplinary integration development of LDR research

Multidisciplinary integration development of LDR research

Promoting the interdisciplinary integration of LDR research has become an important trend in current academic research. Scholars and research institutions in different fields are key forces in promoting interdisciplinary integration and development. Research institutions can establish interdisciplinary research platforms, gather experts and scholars from different disciplines, jointly carry out interdisciplinary research projects on LDR, break down disciplinary barriers, promote knowledge sharing and integration, and promote the in-depth development of LDR research. Meanwhile, research on LDR involves multiple disciplines such as management, law, economics, geography, planning, etc., and scholars should adopt diverse perspectives and methods to generate new ideas for LDR research through interdisciplinary integration. It is worth noting that promoting interdisciplinary research on LDR should not ignore the core issues and research characteristics, and also pay attention to conflicts in research methods and theoretical systems across different disciplines.

(2)In-depth exploration of the institutionalization of LDR

In-depth exploration of the institutionalization of LDR

As a complex issue involving multiple interests and requiring comprehensive consideration, the institutionalization of LDR is not only related to the rational allocation and efficient use of land resources but also has a profound impact on the coordination of the land policy system and socio-economic development. On the one hand, scholars should explore the ownership of LDR and the distribution of value-added benefits. Under the existing legal system, the academic community should fully consider the demands of stakeholders such as landowners, land users, and governments, and combine them with the actual situation of each country to clarify the ownership of LDR, protect land rights, and promote sustainable development of the land market. Meanwhile, it is urgent to promote the establishment of a fair and reasonable mechanism for the distribution of land appreciation benefits, reasonably distribute land value-added benefits, and prevent social conflicts caused by unclear ownership and unfair distribution of benefits. On the other hand, at the level of land use policy, the connection of the institutionalization of LDR is particularly important. Scholars should not only pay more attention to coordinating with urban planning, land use policies, environmental protection policies, etc., to ensure that the exercise of LDR meets the requirements of overall planning and sustainable development, but also necessary to cooperate with financial and tax policies, social security policies, etc., and jointly build a sound land policy system.

(3)The relationship between NTSP and LDR

The relationship between NTSP and LDR

China's NTSP research provides important insights for future LDR research from legal, value, and extension perspectives. Firstly, as an important carrier and tool for LDR configuration, the development and implementation of NTSP are closely related to LDR. This means that the implementation and protection of LDR require a solid legal foundation. Through the research of NTSP, we can have a clearer understanding of the positioning of LDR in the legal system and promoting its legalization process. Secondly, NTSP emphasizes the optimal allocation of spatial resources and sustainable development, which is highly aligned with the value orientation of LDR. LDR research can draw on the value orientation of NTSP, emphasizing a balance among fairness, efficiency, and sustainability. Finally, NTSP involves multiple policy areas and departments, requiring collaborative efforts from all parties. For LDR research, attention should be paid to the connection between China's NTSP and other related policies, providing experience and reference for in-depth exploration of the coordination among LDR and overall land use planning, intensive land use policies, and paid land use policies.

(4)Research contributions and shortcomings

Research contributions and shortcomings

This article deeply explores the current research status of LDR in China and internationally, which has certain innovations: (1) Through visualization technology, it reveals the hot topics, evolutionary characteristics, and future evolutionary trends of LDR research. (2) By comparing Chinese and international researches, this study reveals the commonalities and characteristics of LDR in different cultural and institutional backgrounds, and provides useful references for China to formulate NTSP.

Meanwhile, there are still areas for future improvement in this article: (1) This article only collected research literature on LDR from the core databases of CNKI and WOS. Although the data sources are incomplete, they can still mainly summarize the research context, current situation, and hotspots in this field. (2) Because some journal articles are in Chinese, there may be some language deviations when translated into English, we have also improved the accuracy of the article's language expression through various means such as professional polishing. (3) The CiteSpace software has issues with merging keywords with similar meanings, and we manually analyze and merge them during processing.

Conclusion

This article uses CiteSpace software to conduct a quantitative analysis of literature on the theme of LDR in Chinese and international journals. By analyzing the deductive context, collaborative networks, and research hotspots of LDR research, as well as analyzing burst keywords, we aim to grasp the forefront of research in the LDR field in China and internationally, and explore the implications for the theory and practice of China's NTSP. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1)International researches and practices in the LDR field were relatively early, with sufficient accumulation in the fields of the theoretical level of LDR, the accounting and distribution of value-added benefits, and institutional practice. They have mainly gone through two stages: embryonic fluctuating development and gradually increasing development. However, research on LDR in China started relatively late, but it developed rapidly, mainly going through three stages: embryonic fluctuating development, rapid growth and stable development, and slowly declining development.

Enzymatic preparation of medium-chain fatty acid enriched specialty fat for application in hand-grasp pancakes
Authors: Jinying Wang, Kaibo Zhang, Hongpeng Chen, Jingjing Lun, Cong Sun, Linshang Zhang, Shangde Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study developed medium-chain fatty acid (MCFA)-enriched specialty fats for hand-grasp pancakes through enzymatic interesterification (EIE) using a soybean oil/fully hydrogenated coconut oil/fully hydrogenated palm oil blend (6:1:3 w/w/w) as the substrate. Orthogonal optimization achieved 99.85 % randomization efficiency under mild conditions (70 °C, 12 % lipase load, 4 h). Fatty acid composition analysis confirmed 6 % MCFA incorporation with trans-fatty acid levels below 0.1 %. EIE significantly modified triglyceride profiles by reducing trisaturated species while increasing mixed-acid glycerides. The resulting fat exhibited superior functional properties: exclusive β' crystal polymorphism, compact spherical crystal clusters, and ideal solid fat content. Compared to chemically interesterified fat, the EIE fat demonstrated lower hardness, higher spreadability, and enhanced oxidative stability. In baking trials, pancakes made with EIE fats displayed distinct layering, uniform golden browning, and crisp texture. This work establishes EIE as an eco-friendly strategy for producing nutritionally enhanced, functionally optimized shortenings suitable for frozen dough applications.Keywords: Medium-chain fatty acid, Enzymatic interesterification, Hand-grasp pancakes, Specialty fat

Introduction

The accelerating pace of modern life has heightened consumer demand for food convenience, driving substantial growth in frozen food consumption. Hand-grasp pancakes, a representative Chinese breakfast item, are widely favored for their ready-to-eat nature and palatability. Industrial-scale production of hand-grasp pancakes in China has reached significant capacity, with leading production lines achieving outputs of 900,000 units/day and market sales exceeding ¥10 billion (≈USD 1.4 billion), underscoring its substantial commercial potential.

Hand-grasp pancake is produced from wheat flour and lipids (10–20 % w/w), supplemented with water and salt, through sequential processes: dough mixing, resting, sheeting, oil spraying, cutting, coiling, secondary resting, shaping, and freezing. Lipids critically determine the product's signature multi-layered structure and crispy texture (Aguilera, 2018). Despite this functional importance, industry currently lacks specialty fat for hand-grasp pancake. Most manufacturers rely on empirical blends of lard, commercial shortenings, and liquid oils—a practice lacking standardization and resulting in inconsistent quality. Furthermore, such blends predominantly contain high-saturated fats, posing potential health concerns (Astrup et al., 2020; Briggs et al., 2017).

Medium-chain fatty acids (MCFAs, C8–C12) offer advantages in rapid digestibility and nutritional functionality (Roopashree et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2025). This study therefore proposes incorporating MCFAs into specialty fat to develop nutritionally enhanced shortenings compatible with cold-chain logistics. While physical blending represents the simplest approach, it yields poor compatibility (e.g., graininess and oil leakage during frozen storage) (Mao et al., 2023; Su et al., 2024). To address this, interesterification was employed to modify lipid structure. Though chemical interesterification (CIE) is industrially mature, its harsh conditions (catalyst residues, 80–120 °C) conflict with clean-label demands (Li et al., 2023; L. Zhang et al., 2015). Consequently, enzymatic interesterification (EIE), offering greener processing and superior nutrient retention, was selected for this research.

Using a pre-optimized original oil blend as substrate (K. Zhang, Zhang, Li, et al., 2023), EIE reaction parameters (e.g., enzyme load, temperature, time) were systematically optimized. The interesterified fat were comprehensively characterized, including structural metrics, e.g. physicochemical properties, fatty acid profile, triglyceride composition; functional attributes, e.g. thermal behavior, polymorphism, crystal morphology, textural properties, oxidative stability. Application trials evaluated the feasibility of MCFA-enriched EIE specialty fat in frozen hand-grasp pancakes, providing actionable insights for industrial adoption. This work, for the first time, establishes a framework for developing medium-chain fatty acid enriched, functionally tailored specialty fat for hand-grasp pancake.

Materials and methods

Materials

Soybean oil (SBO), fully hydrogenated coconut oil (FHCO), and fully hydrogenated palm oil (FHPO) were purchased from Yihai Kerry Arawana Holdings Co., Ltd.; The lipase Lipozyme RMC was sourced from Novozymes (China) Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; High-gluten flour was provided by Wudeli Group Zhoukou Flour Co., LTD.

Sample preparation

SBO, FHCO, and FHPO were weighed at a 6:1:3 mass ratio (w/w/w) in a 250 mL round-bottom flask and magnetically stirred at 105 °C for 30 min to obtain physical blending (PB) product for further characterization.

Chemical interesterified fat was prepared as following: SBO, FHCO, and FHPO were weighed at a mass ratio of 6:1:3 (w/w/w) in a 250 mL round-bottom flask. The mixture was magnetically stirred at 105 °C under vacuum for 30 min. Subsequently, the temperature was elevated to 120 °C, and sodium methoxide (0.7 % w/w relative to total oil mass) was added for a 30 min reaction. The product was then washed with distilled water until neutral pH and dried to obtain CIE product for subsequent analysis (Meng et al., 2023).

SBO, FHCO, and FHPO were weighed at a mass ratio of 6:1:3 (w/w/w) in a 250 mL round-bottom flask. The mixture was magnetically stirred at 105 °C under vacuum (<10 mbar) for 30 min. A three-factor three-level orthogonal experimental design (detailed in supplementary Table 1S) was implemented to optimize reaction conditions. Following interesterification under optimal parameters, the crude product was immediately centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 2 min while maintaining temperature ≥ 80 °C (Zhu et al., 2020). The upper oil layer was collected and heated to 90 °C. An appropriate volume of sodium hydroxide solution (0.1 mol/L) was added to remove free fatty acids via alkali refining, with continuous stirring at 50 rpm for 10 min (Zhao et al., 2025). Subsequently, the oil was washed with deionized water until neutral pH and dried under nitrogen at 80 °C to obtain the EIE product.

The degree of IE for PB is 0, while CIE to the extent of 100 %. The degree of EIE is evaluated by comparing the triglyceride composition with that of PB, using changes greater than 5 % in PPP, PSP, PSS, LLL, PLP, and PLS as criteria. The calculation formula is (1) (Zhou et al., 2021).

Physicochemical analysis

Acid value, peroxide value, and melting point were determined following the AOCS Official method Cd 3d-63, Cd 8–53, Cc 1–25, respectively.

Fatty acid composition

Fatty acid composition was determined by gas chromatography (GC). Prior to analysis, samples underwent simple methylation to convert fatty acids into fatty acid methyl esters. The analysis was performed using an Agilent GC-7820 A gas chromatograph equipped with a DB-Fast FAME capillary column (30.0 m × 250 μm × 0.25 μm). The operational parameters were set as follows: Carrier gas: High-purity nitrogen at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min; Injection volume: 1 μL with a split ratio of 20:1; Injection temperature: 250 °C; Oven temperature program: Initial temperature 80 °C (held for 0.5 min), ramped to 165 °C at 40 °C/min (held for 1 min), then increased to 230 °C at 4 °C/min (held for 4 min); Detection: Flame ionization detector at 300 °C (Zhang, Zhang, Yang, et al., 2023).

TG composition

Samples for triglyceride composition analysis were dissolved in chromatography-grade n-hexane, dehydrated, and filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter. Analysis was performed using an Agilent GC-7890B high-temperature gas chromatograph (USA) equipped with a CP-TAP CB capillary column (25 m × 250 μm × 0.1 μm). The operational parameters were set as follows: Carrier gas: High-purity nitrogen; Injection volume: 1 μL with a split ratio of 1:10; Injection port temperature: 340 °C; Oven temperature program: Initial temperature: 50 °C; Ramped to 280 °C at 20 °C/min and held for 2.5 min; Further increased to 340 °C at 10 °C/min and held for 25 min; Detector temperature: 340 °C (Zhang, Zhang, Yang, et al., 2023).

SFC

Solid fat content (SFC) of samples was determined using a low-resolution nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectrometer (Bruker Minispec, Model Mq 20; Karlsruhe, Germany) according to the AOCS Official Method Cd 16b-93. Briefly, melted fat samples (2–3 g) were transferred into NMR tubes and thermally equilibrated at 80 °C for 30 min. After quenching at 0 °C for 90 min, the SFC value at 0 °C was recorded. The samples were then transferred to a 10 °C water bath and held for 30 min prior to measurement at 10 °C. Subsequently, SFC values were sequentially determined at 20, 30, 40, and 50 °C under isothermal conditions.

Thermal properties

Thermodynamic properties of samples were characterized using a NETZSCH DSC 204F1 Phoenix® instrument (Selb, Germany). Samples were melted and 5–8 mg aliquots were weighed into aluminum crucibles, with an empty crucible serving as reference. Measurements were performed under a nitrogen purge gas at a constant flow rate of 20 mL/min. The temperature program comprised the following steps: Isothermal equilibration at 80 °C for 20 min; Cooling to −40 °C at a rate of 5 °C/min; Isothermal hold at −40 °C for 30 min; Heating to 80 °C at 5 °C/min (Su et al., 2024).

Crystal structure and morphology

Specimens were thermally equilibrated at 80 °C for 30 min to erase prior crystalline history, followed by isothermal crystallization in a temperature-controlled cabinet at 20 °C for 40 h prior to analysis. Instrumental configuration: XRD patterns were acquired using a copper Kα radiation source (λ = 1.5406 Å) operated at 40 kV and 20 mA. Scans were performed over a 2θ range of 5°–35° at a scanning rate of 5°/min (Manzoor et al., 2024).

Sample preparation: After thermal equilibration at 80 °C for 30 min (to eliminate residual crystal memory), a droplet of molten sample was rapidly transferred onto a preheated glass slide, covered with a coverslip, and compressed to exclude air bubbles. Prepared slides were tempered at 20 °C for 40 h to ensure uniform crystal growth. Polymorphic structures were examined under cross-polarized light (Li et al., 2023).

Texture and oxidative stabilities

Texture of samples was determined by Texture analyzer (TA.XT Plus, Texture Technologies, Scarsdale, NY). Prior to analysis, fats were completely melted and homogenized, followed by solidification at 24 °C for 12 h to ensure uniform crystalline formation. Hardness quantification: Cylindrical penetration probe (P/5, diameter: 5 mm); Pre-test speed, 10.0 mm/s; Test speed, 1.0 mm/s; Post-test speed, 10.0 mm/s; Target displacement, 10.0 mm. Trigger force, 0.05 N (Zhu et al., 2020). Spreadability assessment: Spreadability rig (HDP/SR) with 45° conical plate; Kinematic parameters, Pre-test/test/post-test speeds identical to hardness analysis; Compression distance, 10.0 mm. Spreadability was calculated as positive area under force-time curve (N·mm) (Espert et al., 2020).

Oxidative stability was assessed in accordance with ISO 6886:2016 (Animal and vegetable fats – Determination of oxidative stability by accelerated oxidation test) using Rancimat 743. Temperature, 105 °C ± 0.5 °C; Airflow, 20 L/h.

Preparation and evaluation of hand-grasp pancakes

High-gluten flour (60 g) and salt (3 g) were weighed into a dough mixer and homogenized. Subsequently, boiling water (12 g) and room-temperature water (24 g) were added sequentially, followed by mechanical kneading for 11 min at 200 rpm. The kneaded dough was proofed at 25 °C for 60 min. The proofed dough was rolled into a rectangular sheet (thickness: 2 mm). The EIE fat (15 % w/w of dough mass) was uniformly brushed onto the sheet surface. After solidification of the shortening layer, the sheet was folded accordion-style five times to create laminated layers. The laminated dough was coiled into a disk shape and subjected to secondary proofing (60 min, 30 °C). It was then pressed to a final thickness of 0.8 cm and flash-frozen at −20 °C for 50 min to stabilize the layered structure. The frozen pancake was transferred directly to an electric griddle preheated to 180 °C. It was pan-fried for 4–6 min with flipping every 90 s until achieving golden-brown coloration (K. Zhang, Zhang, Li, et al., 2023).

Fifteen trained assessors were screened to evaluate the quality of hand-grasp pancake. Sensory attributes were quantified using a 10-point intensity scale focusing on color, greasiness, layering property, crispness, post-hardening (Table 2S).

Statistical analysis

All assays were conducted with three experimental replicates, and results were expressed in the format of mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) at a significance threshold of p < 0.05, with post-hoc analyses (Duncan) executed through IBM SPSS Statistics version 27 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results and discussion

Physicochemical properties

Peroxide value and acid value constitute fundamental indicators of lipid oxidative status, while melting point significantly represents the functional characteristics of fats—including texture, crystallinity, and thermodynamic behavior—thereby determining their performance in food applications (Faridah et al., 2025). Compared to PB, both CIE and EIE demonstrated reduced melting points. This phenomenon originates from the molecular redistribution of fatty acids within triglycerides during interesterification. PB contained a higher proportion of high-melting triglycerides (e.g., trisaturated TG), whereas post-interesterification samples exhibited decreased levels of such high-melting species, with concomitant formation of medium- and low-melting triglycerides (e.g., mono−/di-unsaturated TG) (Silva et al., 2009).

Orthogonal optimization of EIE

EIE enhances product quality and operational stability of specialty fats. Therefore, the EIE conditions was optimized using orthogonal array design. The TG composition was detailed in Table 3S and the EIE degree was evaluated by tracking six critical TG species (PPP, PSP, PSS, PLP, PLS, LLL) (Table 4S). As demonstrated in Table 2, EIE achieved 99.85 % randomization equivalence relative to CIE, confirming near-complete positional isomerization. Physical blending of medium-chain fatty acid-enriched fats and long-chain fatty acid-enriched fat often result in poor miscibility and eutectic crystallization (Su et al., 2024), leading to phase separation and compromised functional properties. EIE enables random esterification of fatty acids at sn-1, sn-2, and sn-3 positions on the glycerol backbone, thereby restructuring triglyceride molecules to enhance miscibility and compatibility between dissimilar fatty acid chains.

Orthogonal range analysis revealed the dominance hierarchy of control factors: Lipase loading (B) > Reaction duration (C) > Temperature (A). The validated optimum conditions were: reaction temperature 70 °C, lipase loading 12 % (w/w total lipids), reaction duration: 4 h. This conditions were consistent with other reports in which lipase load of 5.8 %–11 %, reaction temperature of 60–65 °C, time 4.7–10 h were documented (Manzoor et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2021).

Fatty acid composition

A shortening formulation exhibiting balanced distribution of short- and medium-long-chain fatty acids, homogeneous saturation/unsaturation ratios, and low/zero trans-fatty acid content can simultaneously meet consumer nutritional demands (Liu et al., 2025). As detailed in Table 3, the fatty acid profiles of source oils (SBO, FHCO, FHPO) and their derivatives (PB, CIE, EIE) were characterized as follows: SBO was dominated by unsaturated fatty acids, containing 25.20 % oleic acid (18:1) and 51.76 % linoleic acid (18:2), with total unsaturated fatty acids exceeding 80 %. FHCO was primarily comprised of short-chain fatty acids (≤14) at 74.54 %, predominantly lauric acid (12:0) and myristic acid (14:0). FHPO was found rich in long-chain saturated acids with 58.50 % palmitic acid (16:0) and 39.49 % stearic acid (18:0), totaling 98 % saturated fatty acids with chain length > 14 carbons. The EIE fat formulated at SBO: FHCO: FHPO = 6:1:3 (w/w/w) demonstrated distinct melting behavior compared to PB and CIE counterparts, while maintaining comparable fatty acid composition. Short-chain fatty acid (C ≤ 14), 6 %; Palmitic acid, 27 %; Stearic acid, 16 %; Oleic acid, 15 %; Linoleic acid, 30 %. Critically, all interesterified products exhibited trans-fatty acid levels below 0.1 %— significantly lower than conventional hydrogenated shortenings (typically 15–40 % trans-fats) (List et al., 2007), suggesting that this formula is health enough (Wongjaikham et al., 2022).

Triglyceride composition

Triglyceride composition directly modulates the physical properties (e.g., melting point and crystallization behavior) and nutritional functionality of fats (Liu et al., 2024; Meng et al., 2023). As detailed in Table 4, the TG profiles of the source oil, PB, CIE, and EIE blends formulated at SBO: FHCO: FHPO = 6:1:3 (w/w/w) revealed that: Short-chain triglycerides (C8–C10, primarily from FHCO) constituted 12.00 % in EIE, 11.39 % in CIE, and 14.96 % in PB. These elevated levels demonstrate potential nutritional advantages, as short-chain triglycerides exhibit higher bioavailability and faster energy metabolism compared to long-chain counterparts (Roopashree et al., 2021; Su et al., 2024). EIE and CIE exhibited virtually identical TG distributions, indicating equivalent randomization efficiency. However, since Lipozyme RMC is an sn-1,3 specific lipase, theoretically, EIE and CIE should generate distinct TG species. This observed similarity can be attributed to two factors: a) acyl migration during EIE, which redistributes fatty acids across all glycerol positions, partially mimicking chemical randomization (Zhou et al., 2021). b) analytical limitations of gas chromatography, which may fail to resolve regioisomeric TG species (Qian et al., 2020). Compared to PB, EIE demonstrated significant compositional shifts: PPP, PStP, PStSt and LLL decreased by 7.89 %, 10.53 %, 6.64 % and 5.24 %, respectively. While, PLP and PLSt increased by 7.02 % and 5.78 %, respectively. PPP, PStP, PStSt are higher-melting point TG species with a MP of 56–61 °C, LLL belongs to low-melting point TG species (MP -13.3 °C). PLP and PLSt are medium-melting point TG species (27–30 °C). Interesterification induced a thermodynamic redistribution of acyl groups, converting high- and low-melting TG into medium-melting species (Meng et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2022). This shift explains the significantly lower melting points of EIE and CIE compared to PB, as quantified in Table 1.

SFC

SFC, defined as the percentage of solid fat in a lipid matrix at a specific temperature, directly determines the hardness and spreadability of fats and influences their functional applications (Devi & Khatkar, 2016). As illustrated in Fig. 1, the SFC profiles of original (SBO, FHCO, FHPO), PB, CIE, and EIE fats exhibited distinct trends. All samples except SBO and FHPO showed significant SFC declines over 0–50 °C, attributable to progressive melting of TG. EIE and CIE displayed nearly identical SFC curves, with values of 35–45 % at 0–10 °C (indicating optimal plasticity for pastry lamination) and ∼ 10 % at 30 °C (ensuring desirable mouthfeel due to rapid melting in the oral cavity). FHCO exhibited excessively high SFC (>60 %) at 10–20 °C, resulting in inadequate spreadability. FHPO demonstrated non-functional SFC levels (>80 %) at 20–50 °C, rendering it unsuitable as shortening due to waxy texture. PB maintained higher SFC than EIE/CIE at 20–50 °C, likely due to elevated concentrations of high-saturation triglycerides. The SFC of EIE/CIE at 30–40 °C was less than 15 %, indicating a good mouth-melting feature (Zhang, Zhang, Yang, et al., 2023).

Thermal properties

Differential Scanning Calorimetry is routinely employed to investigate the thermodynamic properties of lipids, enabling quantitative measurement of heat flow changes during crystallization and melting (Faridah et al., 2025). The derived thermograms provide critical insights into the crystallization kinetics and melting points of distinct lipid components. As illustrated in Fig. 2A, six lipid samples exhibited divergent melting behaviors. SBO displayed dual melting peaks in the low-temperature region, indicative of its complex triglyceride composition and lowest melting point. FHCO showed a single melting peak. FHPO presented two distinct melting peaks, with the highest peak temperature at 58.87 °C (Table 5S), consistent with its high saturated triglyceride content. PB exhibited a single melting peak at a peak temperature of 51.75 °C. Both CIE and EIE demonstrated markedly altered melting profiles compared to PB. Their peak temperatures decrease significantly from 51.75 °C (PB) to 37.88 °C (CIE) and 40.78 °C (EIE). This shift is attributed to molecular reorganization of triglycerides, wherein interesterification reduces trisaturated triglyceride (SSS) content while increasing disaturated-monounsaturated (SSU) species, as confirmed by triglyceride compositional analysis in section 3.4. Additionally, CIE and EIE exhibited broader melting ranges than PB, reflecting enhanced plastic properties essential for industrial shortening applications.

The crystallization curves of six samples (Fig. 2B) exhibited distinct thermal behaviors. SBO displayed the lowest peak crystallization temperature (−13.22 °C), while FHPO showed the highest (41.58 °C), with FHCO intermediate (−1.05 °C). Enthalpy changes (ΔH) derived from integration of exothermic peaks are 4.99 J/g for SBO, 117.78 J/g for FHPO, and 90.35 J/g for FHCO (Table 5S). The significantly higher ΔH of FHPO indicates greater crystalline mass fraction, validating subsequent polarized light microscopy (PLM) observations. PB exhibited a single crystallization peak at 30.96 °C. Both CIE and EIE developed bimodal crystallization peaks, reflecting increased triglyceride diversity post-acyl randomization. CIE and EIE peaks were broader than PB, signifying enhanced plastic range, consistent with SFC profiles, confirming superior functionality as industrial shortenings (Xie et al., 2025).

Crystal polymorphism and microstructure

The spatial conformation of fatty acid chains on the triglyceride glycerol backbone governs crystal polymorphism, with short spacings (d-values) serving as key indicators of polymorphic forms. The thermodynamic stability hierarchy follows β > β’ > α (Su et al., 2024), with characteristic XRD reflections at: α form: dominant peak at 4.15 Å; β’ form: peaks at 3.8 Å, 4.0 Å, 4.10 Å, and 4.3 Å; β form: characteristic peak at 4.5 Å (Chai et al., 2018; Zhu, Liu, Weng, Li, Zhang, Zong, et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2020). Functionally, β’ crystals (needle-like morphology) represent the ideal polymorph for shortenings, enhancing texture and emulsification capacity. α crystals promote air entrapment for improved pastry lift, whereas excessive β crystals (platelet aggregates) cause undesirable hardness and poor spreadability.

As shown in Fig. 3, SBO exhibited no distinct diffraction peaks, consistent with its liquid state at 20 °C. FHPO showed a strong β’ reflection at 4.1 Å and a minor peak at 3.8 Å, indicative of rapid crystallization kinetics driven by high-melting trisaturated triglycerides. FHCO, EIE and CIE displayed exclusive β’ polymorphism (peaks at 3.8 Å and 4.2 Å). PB exhibited coexisting α (4.16 Å) and β’ (3.8 Å) phases. Interesterification induced polymorphic reorganization: disappearance of the 4.0 Å peak; reduced intensity at 4.16 Å (α form) and 3.8 Å (β’ form); enhanced reflections at 4.3 Å (β’) and 4.5 Å (β). This peak evolution confirms that α transfer to β’ + β polymorphic, which is induced by acyl randomization, substantiating the instability of physical blends versus interesterified fats.

The microstructural characteristics of original oils, PB, CIE, and EIE fats at 20 °C were examined using polarized light microscopy. Solid-state molecules assemble into crystalline lattices through periodic stacking, as evidenced in Fig. 4. FHCO exhibited radially divergent spherulites with pronounced birefringence phenomenon (indicating anisotropic crystal structures). FHPO displayed complex starburst patterns centered on nucleation points, showing high birefringence intensity. PB formed dendritic or snowflake-like crystals with heterogeneous spatial distribution. Both CIE and EIE developed compact spherical clusters, but CIE contained loosely aggregated needle-like or rod-like crystals dispersed within the matrix, whereas EIE demonstrated uniformly packed spherulites with minimal structural defects. Although FHCO, CIE, and EIE shared identical β’ polymorphs (confirmed by XRD short spacings at 4.1 Å and 3.8 Å), their micromorphologies diverged significantly. The results indicate that reaction conditions critically modulate lipid crystal morphology: the milder enzymatic process (EIE) favors the formation of homogeneous spherical clusters—ideal for enhancing shortening plasticity and mouthfeel (Xie et al., 2025; Zhen Zhang et al., 2021).

Texture, oxidative stabilities and application

Hardness and spreadability are critical indicators determining the functional applicability of specialty fat (Ghazani et al., 2022). Higher hardness and greater spreading work denote inferior plasticity and extensibility of fats. As shown in Table 5, the plasticity and extensibility at specialty fat follow the order: EIE > CIE > PB. This hierarchy is attributed to differences in TG composition. However, the GC analysis presented a similar TG composition between CIE and EIE. This disparity may be due the limitations of gas chromatography, which may fail to resolve regioisomeric TG species (Qian et al., 2020). This result was consistent with SFC curves, where PB exhibited higher SFC at 25 °C than EIE and CIE, resulting in the maximum hardness and spreading work. In addition, EIE demonstrates a more ideal polymorphic microstructure with uniform, densely packed spherulites (confirmed by PLM in Fig. 4), which may contribute to the softer texture and rapid extensibility. This evidence underscores that the EIE fat are more suitable for frozen food applications. These findings align with prior studies demonstrating that enzymatically interesterified palm stearin-soybean oil blends are well-suited for fast-frozen products (Zhu, et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2020).

Oxidative stability is a critical parameter for evaluating lipid quality. The Rancimat method (accelerated oxidation test) assesses this stability by measuring the time required to generate secondary oxidation products under controlled conditions. Longer oxidation induction time (OIT) directly correlates with superior oxidative resistance of lipids. As shown in Table 5, the OIT values of specialty fat followed the order: PB > EIE > CIE. Interesterification reduces lipid oxidative stability, primarily due to altered triglyceride structure, free fatty acid generation, degradation of natural antioxidants (Zhen Zhang et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2020). This finding align with previous report observing decrease of oxidation stability (Zhen Zhang et al., 2021). Notably, EIE lipids exhibited significantly higher OIT than CIE counterparts. This advantage stems from EIE's enzyme-specific regioselectivity (e.g., sn-1,3 specificity of lipases), which preserves polyunsaturated fatty acids at the sn-2 position and minimizes oxidative vulnerabilities (Zhou et al., 2021). Similar findings was also reported by other study (Zhen Zhang et al., 2021).

Baking validation was conducted using EIE fat. The resulting hand-grasp pancake was shown in Fig. 5. A and B denoted the two surfaces of the hand-grasp pancake, demonstrating uniform browning (Maillard reaction) without surface cracking. C and D displayed characteristic cross-sectional views revealing distinct, well-separated layers. The high layer count indicated effective shortening functionality via formation of continuous fat films between gluten sheets. These results demonstrates that EIE fat is functionally effective for industrial production of laminated dough products.

Conclusion

Based on an SBO: FHCO: FHPO = 6:1:3 (w/w/w) ternary blend system, orthogonal experimental design was employed to optimize EIE conditions. The physicochemical properties, thermodynamic characteristics, crystallization behavior, and oxidative stability of fats prepared by PB, CIE, and EIE were comprehensively analyzed. The results demonstrated that EIE and CIE fats exhibited superior melting profiles, triglyceride compositions, and SFC curves compared to PB samples. EIE and CIE shared analogous triglyceride compositions and thermal properties, with both methods effectively restructuring molecular configurations. EIE outperformed CIE in microstructural homogeneity, lower hardness, enhanced spreadability and oxidative stability. Baking tests confirmed that EIE fat produced hand-grasp pancakes with desirable crispness and browning and layered structures. This study demonstrated that EIE redistributed fatty acids across the sn-1, sn-2, and sn-3 positions of TG, thereby fundamentally altering the functional properties and application performance of fats. Specifically, EIE enhanced fat compatibility and improved nutritional quality through targeted enrichment of medium-chain fatty acids. This study provides a foundational reference for developing nutritionally enhanced, functionally optimized fats suitable for quick-frozen foods like hand-grasp pancakes.

7676 Sex is An Influential Factor on the Conversion of Plasma Renin Activity and its Active Concentration
Authors: Yuta Tezuka, Kei Omata, Yoshikiyo Ono, Fumitoshi Satoh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Disclosure: Y. Tezuka: None. K. Omata: None. Y. Ono: None. F. Satoh: None. Background: Renin is one of the essential factors to understand the cause of hypertension. In primary aldosteronism (PA), plasma renin activity (PRA) has been employed for screening and confirmation tests, while plasma active renin concentration (ARC) is being popularized due to its better reproducibility and easier technique. However, how the transition of renin measurement impacts on PA diagnosis is still unclear. Objective: To investigate the correlative relationship between PRA and ARC, and the impact of renin transition on PA screening. Methods: We retrospectively enrolled 206 hypertensive patients who underwent PA screening and/or confirmatory tests with simultaneous measurements of both PRA and ARC from 2021 to 2023. Those who had higher PRA than 3 ng/mL/h were excluded based on the context of this study. Antihypertensive drugs which interfere with renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system were withdrew before renin evaluation. Plasma samples for renin and aldosterone measurements were obtained at supine position after 30 minutes rest. PA diagnosis was established based on PRA-based confirmatory tests. The correlations between renin and other parameters were evaluated with simple linear regression and Spearman’s correlation analysis. Chi-square test was applied for categorical variables. Results: Of 206 patients, including 147 PA cases, 95 were men (46%) and, mean age and eGFR were 54 years and 72 mL/min/1.73m2, respectively. Plasma aldosterone concentrations, PRA and ARC were as follows (median [interquartile range]: 8.0 [5.4, 13.3] ng/dL, 0.40 [0.20, 0.70] ng/mL/h and 1.61 [0.79, 2.87] pg/mL, respectively. Total number of renin evaluation reviewed was 890 (median 5 times/person). PRA and ARC showed a significant positive correlation (ARC =3.97 x PRA +0.08: Spearman’s r=0.790, p<0.0001), whereas there were sex differences in the slope and the intercept, particularly in ages under 50 years old (men, 5.03 and -0.19: women, 3.11 and +0.40, respectively). The renin ratio (ARC/PRA) showed weak negative and positive correlations with age (Spearman’s r=-0.073, p=0.03) and body mass index (Spearman’s r=0.113, p=0.0007), respectively. Defining low renin status as PRA<1.0 ng/mL/h and ARC<4.0 pg/mL, 142 (97%) out of 147 PA patients showed persistent suppressed renin for both, while 15 (10%) had controversial aldosterone-to-renin ratios (ARRs) between PRA and ARC. Overall, 24 (12%) patients had controversial ARRs, and the rate was similar between men and women (11% and 13%, respectively). Conclusions: Our current study revealed the positive correlation between PRA and ARC, affected by sex. Although the renin transition could lead to different interpretation of aldosterone-to-renin ratios regardless of sex, the replacement of PRA with ARC rarely alters renin status in PA patients.

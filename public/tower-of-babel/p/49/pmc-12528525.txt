Optimizing orthodontic treatment for delayed eruption of permanent anterior teeth: A prospective study using a modified appliance
Authors: Nataliia Malashenko, Kostiantyn Lykhota, Larysa Dakhno
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption, accompanied by insufficient space in the dental arch, presents a significant challenge during mixed dentition. The aim of this study was to assess the effectiveness of a modified orthodontic appliance that combines transverse maxillary expansion and mesio-distal maxillary elongation, with controlled distalization of permanent molars, in treating cases of delayed anterior teeth eruption during the mixed dentition period. The present study included a prospective comparative design, with a study group of 35 patients and a control group of 32 patients, all demonstrating clinical and radiological signs of delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption. The study group received treatment with a modified orthodontic appliance designed to achieve both maxillary expansion and molar distalization simultaneously. The control group was treated with the Haas appliance for maxillary expansion and the Distal Jet for molar distalization. The level of significance was determined using statistical tests, with p-values calculated for key outcomes. The study revealed that the modified orthodontic appliance significantly reduced the treatment time required for adequate space creation within the dental arch compared to the control group (p = 0.017). The rate of complications in the study group was lower (17.14%) compared to the control group (28.12%), with a significant difference (p = 0.046). Patients in the study group also reported a higher rate of positive perception (68.57%) compared to the control group (43.75%), with a statistically significant difference (p = 0.031). No significant difference was found in the amount of molar distalization and maxillary expansion between the two groups (p > 0.05). A redesigned orthodontic appliance that combines transverse maxillary expansion and molar distalization reduces treatment time and difficulties in delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption. These data indicate that the appliance improves treatment efficiency and patient comfort without reducing spatial adjustments. Its mixed dentition use needs more research with larger sample sizes and regulated treatment techniques.

Introduction

Physiologically driven and timely-obtained eruption of teeth is a core component for normal functioning of dento-alveolar system and its ongoing development during the period of transition from temporary to permanent dentition (Kurosaka et al. 2022; Fekonja 2022; Friedrich and Schön 2023). Phenomenon of delayed tooth eruption may be interpreted considering deviations of person’s tooth eruption terms in comparison with population-based averaged chronological age associated with eruption of specific tooth, or considering the correspondence between root formation stage and level of clinical crown eruption (Aldowsari et al. 2022; Yamaguchi et al. 2022; Roulias et al. 2022).

Diagnostics of delayed tooth eruption may be held either clinically when sequence of eruption is disturbed or contralateral tooth have erupted with the six months difference, or radiologically, which also helps to identify the cause of delay (cyst, odontoma, supernumerary tooth, retained or absent primary tooth, etc.).

Recent studies reported the prevalence of delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption within the range of 2–3.09% (Vandana et al. 2024; Tan et al. 2018). Retrospective cross-sectional study of Aldowsari et al. (2022) conducted in Saudi Arabia demonstrated that prevalence of delayed eruption among central permanent incisors was equalled to 3.09%, while retention of primary teeth, their early loss and deficiency of space within dental arch were considered as the most causative factors for delayed tooth eruption. Recent research provided by Dakhno et al. (2023) revealed that prevalence of delayed permanent incisor eruption reaches 5.56% for the central incisor and 6.58% for the lateral incisors, while 27.34% cases of delayed tooth eruption were associated with arch-length deficiency. Analogical outcome was noted also in the study of Tan et al. (2018), who also revealed that delayed tooth eruption may cause further problems with remaining permanent dentition due to the space loss within the dental arch. All above mentioned results highlight close functional connection and combined clinical problematics of delayed permanent teeth eruption and deficiency of space within the dental arch.

Orthodontic appliances used for the treatment of delayed tooth eruption cases associated with the deficiency of space within the dental arch differ by the constructive features and mechanisms of action. In the early phase of mixed dentition narrow upper jaw condition may be treated with the use of appliances for the rapid palatal expansion, including Haas appliance in the various forms of modifications (Ortu et al. 2024; Cozzani et al. 2020a, b; Muñoz-Pereira et al. 2021). But such approach does not help to cover mesio-distal shortening of the maxilla, since above mentioned devices are not aimed at providing permanent molar distalization and minimizing protrusion of frontal maxillary teeth. On the other hand, such appliances as Distal Jet and its modifications with retention upon permanent teeth or with additional use of mini-screws help to obtain needed mesio-distal elongation of maxilla with the contiguous permanent molar distalization, but they do not to support improvement of transverse maxillary dimensions (Altieri et al. 2022; Bellini-Pereira et al. 2021; Reis et al. 2019).

Current orthodontic treatments still focus on sequential appliance use, addressing transverse and sagittal discrepancies separately, despite the well-established link between space insufficiency and delayed eruption of permanent anterior teeth. Because of overlapping force systems and cumulative patient discomfort, this disjointed method lengthens treatment times, adds mechanical complexity, and may jeopardise results. Furthermore, a crucial gap in treatment optimisation is highlighted by the absence of a standardised, integrative solution that concurrently addresses transverse and mesio-distal maxillary constraints. The lack of a coherent therapeutic approach compromises the possibility of an early and successful resolution of eruption-related issues in the context of mixed dentition, when maxillary growth is still active and timing of intervention is critical.

This study hypothesizes that the use of a modified orthodontic appliance, designed to provide both transverse maxillary expansion and mesio-distal elongation through controlled distalization of the permanent molars, will improve treatment outcomes in cases of delayed permanent anterior tooth eruption associated with space deficiency. When compared to the traditional sequential application of separate expansion and distalizing devices throughout the mixed dentition era, it is anticipated that this integrative method will shorten treatment duration, decrease complication rates, and improve patient satisfaction.

The study aimed to assess the effectiveness of using a modified treatment approach with simultaneous permanent molars distalization and transversal maxillary expansion in the course of orthodontic treatment for delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption cases accompanied by a deficiency of space in the dental arch during the period of mixed dentition.

Materials and methods

This study employed a prospective comparative design based on principles outlined in comparative effectiveness research (Merkow et al. 2020). The research was conducted at the Department of Dentistry, Shupyk National Healthcare University of Ukraine (Kyiv, Ukraine) between 2021 and 2024. Present study has been conducted in compliance with principals of human rights and human dignity, moral and ethical standards and in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Human Rights of Helsinki, the Council of Europe Convention on Human Rights and Biomedicine. The study protocol was approved by the Ethical Committee of Department of Dentistry at Shupyk National Healthcare University of Ukraine (Kyiv, Ukraine), while positive Ethical Committee resolution was provided under the protocol number 9 (02.12.2019). All patients enrolled in the present prospective cohort study and their parents voluntarily agreed to take part in the provided clinical investigation after in-detail explanation of all aspects of research design. Such voluntary agreement of patients and their parents was confirmed by signed informed consent form.

Study sample and eligibility

Sample size of patients was calculated considering averaged incidence level of delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption equalled near 2%, reported previously in number of research (Vandana et al. 2024; Tan et al. 2018). Importantly, only skeletal Class I patients were included, as confirmed through cephalometric analysis. Due to the established 2% level of incidence, 5% margin of error and 95% confidence interval, 31 or more subjects were needed for the adequate sample size compliance. Such approach for the sample size calculation was used due to the deficiency of studies regarding prevalence of delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption among specifically Ukrainian patients of certain age. Patients’ cohort was formed out of number of dental patients who have previously referred to the Department of Dentistry at Shupyk National Healthcare University of Ukraine seeking for the specialized orthodontic treatment. Following inclusion criteria were used for the adequate filling of study cohort:age between 7 and 14 years;mixed dentition phase;radiographically confirmed delayed eruption of one or more permanent anterior teeth;at least one ectopically erupting maxillary permanent anterior tooth;presence of root resorption in adjacent primary molars and/or incomplete root development (stages Nolla 6–8) of successor premolars;transverse maxillary deficiency (narrow maxilla);one- or two-sided mesio-distal shortening of the arch;good oral hygiene;no previous history of orthodontic treatment;absence of systemic or craniofacial pathology;signed informed consent.

Delayed eruption was diagnosed based on both clinical and radiological criteria. Clinically, eruption was considered delayed if the contralateral tooth had erupted more than six months earlier or if the physiological sequence of eruption was disrupted relative to the patient’s chronological age. Radiographically, delay was confirmed in cases of incomplete root development (Nolla stages 6–8), presence of ectopic eruption pathways, and/or insufficient space within the dental arch due to arch-length discrepancy or retention of primary teeth. These criteria align with those reported by Aldowsari et al. (2022), Yamaguchi et al. (2022), and Roulias et al. (2022).

Exclusion criteria were formulated as follows: 1) presence of only deciduous or only permanent teeth within oral cavity of the patient; 2) skeletal Class II or III patterns; 3) previous orthodontic treatment within anamnesis; 4) presence of general or local pathology that potentially could compromise the process of orthodontic treatment; 5) no signs of deficiency of space within dental arch and no signs of ectopic eruption pathway of one or more maxillary permanent anterior teeth as per major diagnostic criteria; 6) deficient level of oral hygiene; 7) patient’s dissent regarding participation in the clinical study after full explanation of its design and realization aspects.

Treatment protocol and outcome assessment criteria

Considering formulated inclusion and exclusion criteria and calculated minimal size of the sample, 77 patients were enrolled in the study cohort. 77 patients were distributed between study and control using principles of block randomization, thus study group included 35 patients and control group included 32 patients. Patients within the study group received treatment by the modified orthodontic appliance of the improved design, while patients within the control group received treatment with consistent use of Haas appliance for the maxilla expansion and Distal Jet for the distalization of first permanent molars. The control group underwent a sequential two-phase treatment protocol: initially, transverse maxillary expansion was performed using the Haas appliance, activated at a rate of one-quarter turn twice daily for approximately three weeks, followed by a three-month retention phase; this was succeeded by sagittal arch development through molar distalization using the Distal Jet appliance, activated monthly during scheduled orthodontic visits to achieve controlled distal movement of the maxillary first permanent molars.

The passive treatment phase was defined as the period following the cessation of active mechanical force application, during which the created dental space was maintained to allow physiological eruption of permanent anterior teeth. In the study group, CBCT-based confirmation of completed transverse expansion (after 21 days) and molar distalization (after approximately 6 months) preceded the initiation of the passive phase. In the control group, the passive phase began following the 3-month retention period after expansion and again after the completion of molar distalization. All post-treatment CBCT scans used for superimposition and outcome measurements were obtained after expansion and distalization phases but before the initiation of any alignment or corrective stage with fixed appliances.

Considering primarily comparative objective of present research aimed at establishing potential difference between modified orthodontic appliance designed for simultaneous maxillary expansion and molar distalization (mesio-distal elongation of maxilla) compared to the use of conventional appliances like Haas appliance or Distal Jet (which originally were designed to obtain only one form of above mentioned effect), set of assessment criteria was formulated to evaluate complex outcome of optimized approach on the course and results of orthodontic treatment. Complex assessment of orthodontic treatment effectiveness was held due to the following criteria (Cherecheș et al. 2021; Abutaleb et al. 2023):average time of treatment (including both active and passive phases of treatment in months needed to create sufficient space within maxillary arch);rate of registered complications (development of dehiscences, root resorption, undesired tipping of teeth, crowns rotations, pathological tooth mobility) in means of prevalence of their registration within the group;need in additional orthodontic treatment for the correction of potentials side effects and further maxillary expansion and/or maxillary elongation through molar distalization;patients’ rate of positive perception regarding obtained results after orthodontic treatment;average amount of molar distalization;average amount of maxillary expansion.

Such way set of criteria proposed to asses results of orthodontic treatment with the use of modified orthodontic appliance included parameters linked with the course of treatment itself (average time of treatment), parameters linked with outcomes of treatment (rate of complications and need in additional treatment), patient-centred parameters and parameters demonstrating objectified changes within dental status of the patients or so called clinically-oriented outcome criteria (average amount of molar distalization and maxillary expansion). The patients’ rate of positive perception regarding the obtained results after orthodontic treatment was assessed through qualitative verbal feedback collected during final consultations. The feedback was provided directly by the patients, rather than their guardians, to ensure a more accurate reflection of individual experiences. This feedback was not collected using a quantitative scale, but rather through open-ended questions designed to gauge overall satisfaction and perception of the treatment outcomes. The questions asked were tailored to encourage patients to express their thoughts freely on the effectiveness of the orthodontic procedures and any perceived improvements in their condition.

Three-dimensional measurements were performed using the SIMPLANT O&O and Mimics Software (Materialise N.V., Belgium) (Andriola et al. 2022). A standardized coordinate system was used, where the x-axis represented the mediolateral dimension (connecting bilateral latero-orbital points), the y-axis the anteroposterior dimension (passing perpendicularly through the ANS), and the z-axis the vertical dimension (perpendicular to the x–y plane). Molar distalization was measured at the crown level, using the mesiobuccal cusp tip of the maxillary first molars as the primary reference point for anteroposterior displacement along the y-axis. Transverse maxillary expansion was evaluated by measuring the distance between the mesiobuccal cusp tips of the first permanent molars along the x-axis. No root-level displacements were assessed within the current study protocol. CBCT superimposition was performed using voxel-based registration focused on the anterior cranial base to ensure consistent alignment across timepoints. This approach allowed for reproducible assessment of changes in transverse and sagittal dimensions. The measurement protocol did not include systematic evaluation of vertical dimension changes or molar tipping. These parameters were not assessed quantitatively and are acknowledged as a limitation of the study design. To minimize bias, outcome assessors were blinded to group allocation during the measurement of primary outcomes. To minimize bias, patients were blinded to group assignment during the collection of subjective assessments of primary treatment outcomes. This ensured unbiased perception of results and reduced the potential influence of group knowledge on outcome assessment.

Statical analysis

The significance of the registered differences (p) regarding criteria included within complex assessment of orthodontic treatment effectiveness between study and control groups was assessed due to the generally accepted statistical principles with the use of Student’s t-tests for parametrical variables (with previous Arcsine transformation of percentage data) and Kruskal–Wallis test for nonparametric variables. Statistical validation of obtained outcomes was approved only in cases of p < 0.05 (significance level of 0.95). Systematization and tabulation of the obtained data was provided within Microsoft Excel software (Microsoft Office 2019, Microsoft Corp., USA), while for in-detail statistical analysis Analyse-it (Analyse-it Software, Ltd., Leeds, UK) add-in was used, which provided possibilities for needed inferential statistics at spreadsheet application.

The acrylic plates, which are connected by a screw, fit against the palate so that the forces generated by the rotation of the screw are transmitted mainly to the palatal processes of the maxilla and in much lesser extent to the alveolar processes and teeth of the patient. The anterior levers that connect the mesial rings and the acrylic base have curves that inhibit the occurrence of dentoalveolar side effects, such as mesial drift of the premolars.

Usage of modified fixed orthodontic appliance include cementation of bandage rings over first permanent molars and premolars (or first primary molars) with further activation of the distalizing spring by button’s turning (carried out by an orthodontist once every 2 weeks under the control of a dynamometer). Such procedure creates conditions for distal elongation of maxillary dental arch. The first activation of the screw should also be conducted by an orthodontist, and then by one of the parents or guardians, who were trained by the doctor according to the following algorithm: one turn once a day for 21 days, creating conditions for transverse expansion of the maxilla.

Study group consisted of 20 male subjects (66.67%) and 15 female subjects (42.85%) with the average age of standard deviation (SD) = 8.09 ± 1.76 years, while control group consisted of 16 male subjects (50%) and 16 female subjects (50%) with an average age of 8.25 ± 2.24 years. Distribution of subjects within study and control group considering parameters of age (p > 0.05) and gender (p > 0.05) was not statistically different.

Study group demonstrated average treatment time needed for the establishment of proper maxillary expansion and permanent molar distalization sufficient for the further permanent teeth normal eruption pattern in the range of 8.5 ± 1.2 months, which was statistically shorter compared to the control group, which required 13.9 ± 4.5 months of treatment time (p = 0.017). Study group also demonstrated smaller rates of complications compared to the control group (17.14% vs. 28.12%, p = 0.046), while also smaller need in additional orthodontic treatment but difference with control group regarding latter parameter was not statistically significant (22.85% vs. 31.25%, p = 0.247).

It is worth noting that patients within study group demonstrated higher rate of positive patient’s perception at the end of the treatment compared to the patients’ feedback registered within control group (68.57% vs. 43.75%, p = 0.031). Average amount of obtained molar distalization and maxillary expansion (at the projection of molars) was analogical between study and control group (p > 0.05) (Table 1, Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6). Delayed eruption of permanent anterior teeth associated with the number of potential complications, including their ectopic positioning and displacement, midline shift, cystic changes, root resorption and loss of space within dental arch (Tan et al. 2018). Some studies recognize the deficiency of space within dental arch both as a cause, and as a result of delayed tooth eruption, considering pathogenesis of its development in each specific clinical case (Aldowsari et al. 2022; Dakhno et al. 2023). Nevertheless, lack of space within dental arch combined with the delayed eruption of permanent anterior teeth manifests with both mesio-distal shortening and transversal dimension deficiency, which complicates the prognosis of further maxillofacial system development, and argument the need for well-planned complex orthodontic treatment (Padmanabhan et al. 2024).

National clinical guidelines for the management of unerupted maxillary incisors in children also highlighted that consideration of the available space is crucial factor during the treatment planning, and that any obstruction on the way of impacted teeth eruption should be removed (Seehra et al. 2018). Such guidelines totally correspond with the modification proposed within dental appliance proposed in present study, aimed at creating space within dental arch through the mechanism of expansion and elongation simultaneously, which supporting the avoidance of multiple procedures.

Based on the peculiarities of different clinical situations with delayed tooth eruption, various orthodontic treatment approaches may be chosen considering its potential primary and secondary objectives (Ortu et al. 2024; Cozzani et al. 2020a, b). Modifications to the design or components of orthodontic appliances based on general orthodontic principles play a crucial role in improving appliance functionality, treatment efficacy, and patient comfort during treatment.

Results of present study demonstrated that usage of modified orthodontic appliance with combined form of actions including transverse maxillary expansion and mesio-distal maxillary elongation (with controlled distalization of permanent molars) characterized by the shortened treatment time, lower rates of complications and higher positive patient perception rate compared to the conventional approaches. On the other hand, need in additional orthodontic treatment (for the correction of potentials side effects and further maxillary expansion and/or maxillary elongation through molar distalization) and average amount of molar distalization and maxillary expansion was statistically analogical between study and control group.

Taking into consideration statistically approved differences between study and control group regarding some target parameters, and analogical values of other outcomes registered between groups, it may be resumed that null hypothesis may be rejected only partially, so the usage of modified orthodontic appliance with combined form of action including transverse maxillary expansion and mesio-distal maxillary elongation during the orthodontic treatment of delayed permanent anterior teeth eruption accompanied with deficiency of space within dental arch during the period of mixed dentition provides only limited additional effectiveness compared to the conventional approach of using consistently Haas appliance for the upper molar expansion and Distal Jet for the distalization of first permanent molars.

Considering primarily comparative design of present study which was aimed only at establishing relative efficiency of optimized complex orthodontic treatment for delayed permanent incisors eruption cases with the use of modified orthodontic appliance compared to conventional approaches, while implementing for such objective rather generalized but not in-detail criteria of complex assessment, further research would be dedicated to the establishment of specific clinical measurements as indicators of equal or additional clinical effectiveness of the proposed approach among greater study cohort.

Discussion

The present study assessed the clinical efficacy of a modified orthodontic appliance designed to simultaneously perform maxillary expansion and molar distalization in cases of delayed eruption of permanent anterior teeth associated with space deficiency. The findings indicate that the modified appliance significantly reduced treatment time, lowered complication rates, and improved patient satisfaction when compared with conventional sequential use of the Haas and Distal Jet appliances. Notably, the clinical outcomes of maxillary expansion and molar distalization were statistically comparable between the two groups, suggesting that simultaneous dual-function activation does not compromise the efficacy of either component.

A key finding was the reduced complication rate in the study group (17.14%) compared to the control group (28.12%). This difference can be attributed to the biomechanical design of the modified appliance, which distributed forces through a dual anchorage system (teeth and palate) and minimized undesired tooth movements, such as tipping and rotation. Furthermore, integration of both expansion and distalization within a single appliance reduced the number of procedural steps and cumulative force applications, thereby lowering the risk of tissue trauma and anchorage loss. The intermittent activation protocol, controlled by a dynamometer, may have further contributed to biological compatibility and minimized overload.

Among the key systemic factors associated with delayed tooth eruption is subclinical hypothyroidism (SH), as demonstrated by Idan and Tofiq (2024), who reported a strong association between SH and delayed eruption, lower haemoglobin levels, and elevated TSH in affected children. While that study focused on endocrinological variables, the current research addressed delayed anterior tooth eruption primarily from a mechanical standpoint, specifically in cases caused by space deficiency in the dental arch. The results demonstrated that a modified orthodontic appliance incorporating simultaneous maxillary expansion and molar distalization significantly reduced treatment duration and complication rates, and increased patient satisfaction. Although SH may require interdisciplinary management, these findings suggest that in spatially constrained cases, orthodontic intervention alone can yield favorable outcomes.

Nutritional deficiencies, particularly of calcium and vitamin D, are also implicated in delayed eruption. Sahin and Toptanci (2024) found that children with delayed tooth eruption often exhibited low serum levels of calcium and 25-hydroxyvitamin D and elevated PTH. These findings point to impaired mineral metabolism as a contributing factor. While the present study did not assess metabolic parameters, the focus on space-related eruption delays supports the notion that delayed eruption is multifactorial. Nevertheless, the results reinforce that, regardless of underlying systemic contributors, addressing spatial insufficiency through a mechanically optimized appliance remains effective.

Additional systemic influences, such as nutritional status reflected by body mass index (BMI), have also been linked to eruption delays. Padmanabhan et al. (2024) demonstrated that underweight children exhibited significantly later eruption of permanent teeth. These findings echo the broader implication that eruption timing is affected by both systemic and spatial variables. Although the intervention targeted spatial deficiency, the reduction in treatment time and complications implies that local mechanical solutions remain crucial even when systemic factors are involved.

Hirst et al. (2024) further emphasized the interplay of genetic, environmental, and physiological factors in their review of systemic conditions affecting tooth eruption. Their recommendation for multidisciplinary collaboration in managing such cases complements the present study’s mechanical focus by suggesting that systemic and orthodontic factors should be concurrently evaluated. The current findings confirmed that when eruption delay is linked to spatial deficiency, early mechanical intervention via a combined-expansion appliance is not only effective but reduces treatment complexity.

Xu et al. (2022) compared three surgical techniques to assist in anterior tooth eruption and found that all were effective, though laser and electrosurgery offered advantages in healing and patient comfort. In contrast, the present findings demonstrate that non-surgical, appliance-based interventions can offer similar benefits in cases where space creation is the primary barrier. Thus, depending on the etiology, whether impaction or spatial limitation, both surgical and orthodontic approaches have clinical value.

The epidemiological findings of Bawazir et al. (2023) highlighted population-specific eruption delays in Saudi children, even in the absence of clinical symptoms. This supports the assertion that delayed eruption is a multifactorial issue and underscores the need for population-sensitive treatment planning. The current approach, addressing spatial deficiencies via a dual-function appliance, may be adapted for broader demographic applications as eruption norms vary across populations.

Ajwa (2024) raised concerns about prolonged use of rapid palatal expanders and insufficient clinician awareness of associated risks such as periodontal compromise and root resorption. The modified appliance, which consolidates expansion and distalization functions, may mitigate some of these concerns by reducing treatment duration and complication rates. The present study’s statistically significant reduction in adverse events and high patient satisfaction suggest that appliance design innovations can directly address limitations highlighted in prior literature.

Technological advancements in orthodontics were also reviewed by Cheng et al. (2024), who discussed the evolution of distalization techniques to enhance patient comfort and efficiency. The dual-action appliance used in this study reflects this trend by integrating two correction vectors, transverse and sagittal, into a single protocol, leading to a statistically significant reduction in treatment time (p = 0.017) and complications (p = 0.046). These results align with Cheng et al.’s (2024) emphasis on innovation-driven clinical improvement.

Kumar and Kumari (2024) reported successful treatment of ectopically erupted molars using a modified Humphrey’s appliance within three months. While that work addressed ectopic positioning, the present focus was on delayed eruption due to spatial constraint. Nonetheless, both studies emphasize the utility of early and targeted orthodontic intervention and highlight the benefits of appliance customization in managing eruption disorders efficiently.

Kim et al. (2024) explored surgical and orthodontic approaches to the forced eruption of impacted teeth, underscoring how factors such as tooth position and root development affect outcomes. The present findings similarly indicate that patient-specific anatomical constraints, namely arch-length deficiency, can be efficiently managed with mechanical intervention. The significantly shorter treatment time achieved with the modified appliance (8.5 ± 1.2 months vs. 13.9 ± 4.5 months; p = 0.017) supports the clinical utility of choosing tailored, minimally invasive solutions based on individual eruption pathologies.

The present study employed a comprehensive set of criteria to assess the effectiveness of a modified orthodontic appliance in treating delayed permanent anterior tooth eruption. The evaluation included treatment duration, complication rates, need for additional interventions, patient-reported satisfaction, and clinically measured outcomes such as molar distalization and maxillary expansion. The proposed appliance combines support on four bandage rings with a palatal acrylic base, enabling effective transverse expansion and molar distalization without patient involvement or significant molar rotation (Deguchi et al. 2005; Mousa et al. 2022). Continuous initial force application followed by periodic activations ensures effective biomechanical control. Compared to traditional sequential treatment with the Haas and Distal Jet appliances, the modified design significantly reduced treatment duration for cases involving maxillary narrowing and mesio-distal shortening. Although both approaches yielded statistically similar outcomes in terms of expansion and distalization, the modified appliance achieved these effects more efficiently and concurrently.

The main disadvantage of orthodontic devices for delayed tooth eruption cases is inflammation of the soft tissues of the palate, especially in patients with poor oral hygiene (Discepoli et al. 2021; Buthelezi and Madiba 2021; Contaldo et al. 2021). For this reason, such aspects were considered within the formulation of inclusion and exclusion criteria, since successful orthodontic treatment should not be accompanied by simultaneous complications in the form of caries progression or enamel demineralization.

This study has several limitations. The relatively small sample size was based on general prevalence estimates rather than population-specific data, potentially affecting statistical power and the partial rejection of the null hypothesis. Additionally, the unequal group sizes and the inclusion of only underaged patients limit the generalizability of results. Inter-observer and intra-observer reliability were not assessed, which may affect the consistency of measurements. From a biomechanical perspective, the appliance design using support arms on developing lateral teeth may exert unnecessary force, compromising anchorage control and posing safety concerns. Future studies should adopt larger, multicentric designs with extended follow-up and 3D assessment of dental and skeletal changes. Comparative biomechanical analyses could clarify stress distribution differences between appliance types. Additionally, more refined outcome criteria should be employed to quantify both clinical and patient-centered benefits (Nalcaci et al. 2015; Hong et al. 2016; Rivis et al. 2020).

Conclusions

This study demonstrated that a modified orthodontic appliance designed for simultaneous expansion of the upper jaw and distalisation of molars has clinically significant advantages in the treatment of delayed eruption of permanent anterior teeth associated with space deficiency in the dental arch. Patients treated with the modified appliance experienced significantly shorter treatment durations (8.5 ± 1.2 months vs. 13.9 ± 4.5 months, p = 0.017), reduced complication rates (17.14% vs. 28.12%, p = 0.046), and higher levels of patient-reported satisfaction (68.57% vs. 43.75%, p = 0.031) compared to those treated with conventional sequential appliances. Although the extent of maxillary expansion and molar distalization was statistically comparable between groups (p > 0.05), the simultaneous correction of transverse and sagittal discrepancies provided by the modified appliance represents a procedural simplification and a potential optimization of treatment protocol. These findings suggest that the appliance enhances therapeutic efficiency and patient comfort without compromising the magnitude of spatial corrections.

Highly Selective Recovery of Scandium over Aluminum from Sulfuric Acid Leaching Solution of Red Mud Using Ionic Liquids
Authors: Xiang Su, Huipeng Liu, Honghao Li, Yanxi Zhou, Ruibing Bai, Qiuhan Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Extracting scandium (Sc) from red mud facilitates the comprehensive management of this solid waste, mitigating its environmental impact, while securing a sustainable supply of this critical metal. Two ionic liquids (ILs), [lauryl betaine]­[bis­(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)­imide] ([HLaur]­[Tf2N]) and [cocamidopropyl betaine]­[bis­(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)­imide] ([HCoca]­[Tf2N]) were used to separate Sc3+ from Al3+ in H2SO4 aqueous solution. At pH 3.0, [HLaur]­[Tf2N] showed higher Sc3+ extraction efficiency (44.4 vs 36.4%) and better selectivity over Al3+ compared to [HCoca]­[Tf2N]. The optimal conditions for [HLaur]­[Tf2N] extraction were determined as follows: a [HLaur]­[Tf2N]-to-Sc3+ molar ratio of 5.8:1, an extraction time of 40 min, 0.5 mol·L–1 Na2SO4, and pH ≥ 3.0. The extraction process was found to follow a cation exchange mechanism involving sulfate ions. Quantitative stripping of the loaded Sc3+ was achieved using a 0.035 mol·L–1 H2SO4 solution. FT-IR analysis and recycling tests indicated that [HLaur]­[Tf2N] maintained its structural integrity over six cycles. Even in a simulated red mud leachate with high Al3+ and low Sc3+ concentrations, [HLaur]­[Tf2N] exhibited strong Sc3+ selectivity, and a total extraction efficiency of 94.4% was achieved after five extraction stages, with the residual Sc3+ concentration as low as 0.26 mg·L–1.

Introduction

Scandium (Sc) plays an essential role in high-technology sectors such as aerospace, defense, and renewable energy due to its superior alloying, electrical, and optical properties. , Although Sc is not considered rare in terms of overall crustal abundance, it occurs at low concentrations with no independent minerals, making economically viable deposits scarce. Current production falls short of industrial demand, driving research to extract Sc as a byproduct from low-grade ores (e.g., lateritic nickel ore, rare earth ore, etc.) and industrial wastes (e.g., red mud, titanium white waste acid, etc.). Among these, red mudan alkaline industrial waste generated from alumina production via the Bayer processprimarily consists of Fe2O3, Al2O3, and other components. Characterized by high alkalinity and complex composition, it poses multiple environmental and health risks. With an average Sc content of 60–120 g/t, , it is a promising raw material for Sc extraction. Given a global annual output exceeding 100 million tons, red mud exhibits great potential as a large-scale Sc resource. Thus, Sc recovery from red mud can both indirectly reduce pollution and alleviate the shortage of Sc resources. ,,

Direct leaching coupled with selective separation enables Sc recovery from red mud, − but the separation step is challenging due to red mud’s complex composition. Techniques such as chemical precipitation, solvent extraction, ion exchange, and membrane separation have been employed to separate and purify Sc3+ from the resulting secondary resource leachates. Among these, solvent extraction has been a primary method for its high efficiency, excellent selectivity, large processing capacity, and operational simplicity. In the context of Sc3+ recovery from sulfuric acid media, organophosphorus extractants have been the most widely adopted class, encompassing both acids (e.g., di­(2-ethylhexyl) phosphoric acid (D2EHPA, P204)) and esters (e.g., 2-ethylhexyl phosphonic acid mono-2-ethylhexyl ester (HEHEHP, P507)). These extractants are known for their strong complexation with Sc3+ and high extraction efficiency, yet often suffer from drawbacks like low stripping efficiency. In comparison, a newer class of extractants based on amic acid derivatives, including N-[N,N-di­(2-ethylhexyl)­amino carbonylmethyl]­glycine (D2EHAG) and N-[N,N-di­(2-ethylhexyl)­aminocarbonyl methyl]­phenylalanine (D2EHAF) dissolved in n-dodecane has been developed. They have shown great promise, enabling quantitative extraction of Sc3+ at pH 2.0 and efficient stripping with 0.5 M H2SO4. Despite the predominance of solvent extraction in rare earth recovery, it poses environmental concerns such as organic phase entrainment, high consumption of reagents, and generation of high-strength wastewater. , These drawbacks highlight the urgent need for more sustainable and environmentally friendly extraction systems.

Ionic liquids (ILs), molten salts that remain liquid at or near room temperature, have garnered growing interest as extractants for Sc3+ owing to their low volatility, high thermal stability, and designable solubility. − Previous IL-based Sc3+ extraction studies have primarily focused on nitrate or chloride media. Chen et al. synthesized a series of hydrophobic carboxylic acid-functionalized ILs, 1-alkylcarboxylic acid-3-methylimidazolium bis­(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)­imide ([(CH2) n COOHmim]­[Tf2N], n = 3, 5, 7), and achieved 99.5% Sc3+ extraction efficiency at pH 4.2 in nitric acid, but the use of IL as diluent significantly increased the system cost. Zhang et al. developed a homogeneous liquid–liquid extraction system (HLLES) using N-carboxymethylpyridinium bis­(trifluoromethylsulfonyl) imide ([HbetPy]­[Tf2N]) and N-carboxymethyl-N-methylmorpholinium bis­(trifluoro- methylsulfonyl)­imide ([HbetMor]­[Tf2N]), reaching 94.42% Sc3+ extraction in hydrochloric acid solutions. Yet these ILs suffer from high water solubility and require 1.5 mol·L–1 concentrated HNO3 for stripping. Additionally, Kaim-Sevalneva’s group synthesized quaternary ammonium-based ILs ([N3 3 3MeOAc]­[Tf2N] and [N4 4 4MeOAc]­[Tf2N]) with high selectivity for Sc3+ over other REEs in nitric acid, but stripping demands 4 mol·L–1 concentrated H2SO4. These studies demonstrate ILs’ potential, but key challenges persist: high cost, water solubility, and difficult stripping.

In our previous work, two novel hydrophobic ionic liquids, [lauryl betaine]­[bis­(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)­imide] ([HLaur]­[Tf2N]) and [cocamidopropyl betaine]­[bis­(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)­imide] ([HCoca]­[Tf2N]) (Figure ), were synthesized via a simple aqueous-phase method using low-cost amphoteric surfactants as cation sources. These ILs exhibited high extraction efficiency and selectivity for Sc3+ in nitric acid systems. Sulfuric acid leaching, however, has shown advantages in Sc3+ recovery from red mud in certain cases, offering superior environmental safety due to lower volatility and easier wastewater treatment compared to HCl or HNO3 systems. Nevertheless, sulfate ions tend to compete with extractants for metal coordination, reducing extraction efficiency. A detailed understanding of the extraction mechanism is thus essential for achieving high selectivity toward Sc3+ in sulfate media.

Molecular structures of ILs: (a) [HLaur]­[Tf2N] and (b) [HCoca]­[Tf2N].

This study focuses on the core separation challenge of Sc3+ and the major interfering ion Al3+ in red mud leachates, extending the application of two self-synthesized betaine-based ionic liquids to sulfuric acid systems. By systematically investigating the differences in extraction behaviors of the two ionic liquids toward target ions and clarifying the extraction mechanism of the preferred ionic liquid, this work aims to construct a diluent-free hydrophobic extraction system, to simultaneously achieve efficient stripping under low acidity, and to ultimately realize the highly selective extraction of Sc3+ in high-Al3+ sulfate systems. These results are expected to provide a theoretical basis for the selective recovery of Sc3+ from sulfuric acid leachates of red mud using ionic liquids, paving the way for sustainable green Sc3+ recovery.

Experimental Section

Materials and Reagents

Scandium­(III) sulfate octahydrate (Sc2(SO4)3·8H2O, 99.99%) was obtained from Saen Chemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). EDTA standard solution (0.05 mol·L–1) and xylenol orange were sourced from Titan Scientific Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) and Tixi Ai Huacheng Industrial Development Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), respectively. Hexamethylenetetramine (99.0%) was supplied by Tianjin Damao Chemical Reagent Factory (Tianjin, China). Sodium sulfate (Na2SO4, ≥99.0%) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH, ≥96%) were both procured from Paini Chemical Reagent Factory (Zhengzhou, China). Aluminum sulfate hydrate (Al2(SO4)3·xH2O, 98%) and sulfuric acid (H2SO4, 95–98%) were provided by Anhui Zesheng Technology Co., Ltd. (Anqing, China) and Luoyang Chemical Reagent Factory (Luoyang, China), respectively. Cocamidopropyl betaine (35% w/w aqueous solution), lauryl betaine (30% w/w in water), and lithium bis­(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)­imide (98%) were purchased from Adamas Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). All chemicals were used as received without further purification. The ionic liquids [HLaur]­[Tf2N] and [HCoca]­[Tf2N] were synthesized according to the previously reported procedure.

Instrumentation and Analysis Methods

The concentrations of metal ions (including multiple and/or trace elements) in the aqueous phase were analyzed using an inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometer (ICP-OES, model 725-ES, Agilent, Inc.). For other aqueous samples, metal ion concentrations were determined via EDTA titration. Phase separation was achieved by centrifugation using a Xiangyi H1850 benchtop centrifuge (Hunan Xiangyi Laboratory Instrument Development Co., Ltd., China). 1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra of the ionic liquids were acquired on an AV III HD400 spectrometer (Bruker, Germany) with dimethyl sulfoxide-d6 (DMSO-d 6, C2D6OS) as the solvent. Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra were collected on a Tensor 37 spectrometer (Bruker, Germany). pH measurements were carried out with a PHS-25 digital pH meter (Shanghai Rex Instruments Factory, China). Total organic carbon (TOC) analysis was carried out on a TOC-L spectrometer (Shimadzu Corporation, Japan), using the raffinate subjected to extraction under optimal conditions followed by a 24-h soaking process. All extraction and stripping experiments were conducted in triplicate. The reported values are the means, and the error bars represent the standard deviation (population standard deviation, STDEV.P) of three independent measurements.

Extraction and Stripping Procedures for Sc3+

The extraction procedure was carried out as follows: the water-saturated ionic liquid (organic phase) and the aqueous phase were placed in a 15 mL centrifuge tube and shaken in an oscillator at 320 rpm and 25 °C for 40 min (except for the time-dependent experiments, in which the shaking time was varied). Phase separation was then achieved by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 5 min. For the stripping experiments, the loaded organic phase was mixed with 5 mL of stripping agent at various concentrations (0.01, 0.02, 0.025, 0.03, 0.035, 0.04, 0.045, and 0.06 mol/L) and shaken under the same conditions as the extraction step (i.e., 320 rpm, 25 °C, 40 min). The separation of the organic and aqueous phases after stripping followed the same procedure as that in the extraction process. Extraction efficiency (E%), distribution ratio (D), and stripping percentage (S%) were employed as indicators to evaluate the performance of extraction and stripping, which are calculated as follows (eqs –): E%=[M]t−[M]aq[M]t×100% 1 D=[M]t−[M]aq[M]aq 2 S%=[M]s[M]t−[M]aq×100% 3 where the volume of the aqueous phase is assumed to remain constant throughout both the extraction and stripping processes. In this context, [M]t and [M]aq (mol·L–1) represent the initial (total) concentration and the final concentration of the metal ions in the aqueous solution after extraction, respectively, while [M]s denotes the final concentration of metal ions in the aqueous phase after stripping.

Results and Discussion

Comparison of Extraction Selectivity of Ionic Liquids

The extraction efficiencies of Al3+ and Sc3+ by [HLaur]­[Tf2N] and [HCoca]­[Tf2N] in the sulfuric acid system are shown in Figure . As can be seen in Figure , [HLaur]­[Tf2N] showed an extraction efficiency of 0.04% for Al3+ and 44.4% for Sc3+. In contrast, [HCoca]­[Tf2N] exhibited an extraction efficiency of 3.43% for Al3+ and 36.4% for Sc3+. The observed relatively high extraction efficiency for Al3+ suggests that the amide functional group in [HCoca]­[Tf2N] may contribute to enhanced coordination binding ability with Al3+. This could be attributed to the N donor atoms in the amide group participating in metal ion chelation, a mechanism supported by related studies on amide-based extractants. , Given that [HCoca]­[Tf2N] showed lower selectivity for Sc3+ over Al3+ compared to [HLaur]­[Tf2N] in the sulfate system, [HLaur]­[Tf2N] was selected as the extractant for subsequent experiments.

Effects of the Extraction Conditions on Sc3+ Selective Extraction

To determine the effect of extraction conditions on extraction efficiency, the influence of ionic liquid dosage, system pH, extraction time, and sodium sulfate concentration on the extraction efficiency of Sc3+ and Al3+ has been investigated. As shown in Figure a, the extraction efficiency of Sc3+ increased with the amount of ILs. At an ILs dosage of 0.1224 g, no further significant increase in the extraction efficiency of Sc3+ was observed, and this efficiency was determined to be 55.8%. In the case of Al3+, the experimental results show that the extraction efficiency exhibited a slight increase with the elevation of ionic liquid dosage, reaching a final value of 0.791% within the tested range. Taking into account the extraction efficiency, selectivity, and the high Al3+ concentration in real feed solutions, an IL dosage of 0.0816 g was determined to be optimal. At this dosage, the IL-to-Sc3+ molar ratio was approximately 5.8:1, yielding a Sc3+ extraction efficiency of 44.4% while maintaining a negligible Al3+ coextraction efficiency of only 0.044%.

To avoid the precipitation of Al3+ that occurs at higher pH in sulfate media, the experiments were conducted within a pH range of 2.07–3.5. Within this range, variations in pH exerted a significant influence on the extraction behavior of Sc3+, whereas its impact on the extraction behavior of Al3+ was negligible (Figure b). The extraction efficiency of Sc3+ increased with pH across the tested range. Specifically, it increased sharply from 3.83 to 44.4% between pH 2.07 and 3.0, beyond which the increase moderated, culminating in an efficiency of 49.4% at pH 3.5. In contrast, the efficiency for Al3+ remained negligible throughout the entire experimental process. Therefore, for optimal Sc3+ extraction, pH ≥ 3.0 (specifically pH 3.0–3.5) is preferable. Time-dependent experiments (Figure c) indicated that the impact of time on Sc3+ and Al3+ extraction efficiencies was consistent with the trend observed for pH, with extraction equilibrium attained in 40 min. As illustrated in Figure d, varying the Na2SO4 concentration had minimal effect on the extraction of either metal. Nevertheless, a concentration of 0.5 mol·L–1 was maintained to ensure constant ionic strength and prevent emulsification in the subsequent experiments. Consequently, the optimal conditions for Sc3+ separation from Al3+ were identified as a [HLaur]­[Tf2N]-to-Sc3+ molar ratio of 5.8:1, pH ≥ 3.0, an extraction time of 40 min, and 0.5 mol·L–1 Na2SO4.

Extraction Mechanism

To elucidate the extraction mechanism of Sc3+ with [HLaur]­[Tf2N], a slope analysis was performed. The organic phase consisted of sulfonated kerosene and n-octanol (v/v = 3.5:1.5) to prevent third-phase formation and ensure that the logD value meets the defined criteria. To minimize the influence of the extraction reaction on the concentration of extractant in the organic phase, the metal ion concentration in the aqueous phase was set at 0.0008 mol·L–1, which is significantly lower than that of the extractant in the organic phase.

As shown in Figure a, for the [HLaur]­[Tf2N] system, logD exhibited a good linear relationship with pH in the range of 2.28 to 2.65, with a slope of 1.19. This suggests that the extraction of each Sc3+ ion releases approximately one H+ into the aqueous phase, implying a proton exchange process during the extraction. Figure b presents the variation of Sc3+ logD with SO4 2– concentration ([SO4 2–]). When the [SO4 2–] exceeded 0.1 mol·L–1, a linear relationship with a slope of −0.939 was observed, suggesting that each extracted Sc3+ ion releases approximately one SO4 2– into the aqueous phase. According to the species distribution diagram of Sc3+ in sulfuric acid solutions, Sc3+ primarily exists as the anionic sulfate complex [Sc­(SO4)2]− when [SO4 2–] > 0.1 mol·L–1. Notably, in the previous investigation of Na2SO4 concentration effects on extraction efficiency (Figure d), [SO4 2–] was found to have minimal influence on extraction performance. This phenomenon may be attributed to the higher metal ion concentration (0.005 mol·L–1) and adjusted solution pH (3.0), which collectively altered the speciation of scandium-sulfate complexes and thus the extraction mechanism, potentially rendering extraction efficiency largely independent of [SO4 2–]. In summary, the impact of SO4 2– on Sc3+ extraction by [HLaur]­[Tf2N] appears to be dependent on the metal ion concentration and solution pH, factors that influence the dominant speciation of scandium-sulfate complexes and thus the extraction pathway.

FT-IR and NMR analyses were performed to further investigate the extraction mechanism (Figure ). In the FT-IR spectra (Figure a), the bands at 1745 and 1660 cm–1 correspond to CO stretches of protonated (−COOH) and deprotonated carboxyl groups, respectively. The disappearance of the peak at 1745 cm–1 after saturation extraction suggests proton exchange and the absence of nondeprotonated extractant in the complex. The broad absorption peak around 3568 cm–1, attributed to the O–H stretching vibration of the carboxyl group and broadened by hydrogen bonding, disappears after loading, further supporting the deprotonation process of the carboxyl group. The peaks at 2926/2924 and 2857/2855 cm–1 are assigned to C–H stretching vibrations of the alkyl chain. The slight shifts observed at 1184/1182 cm–1 correspond to C–N stretching vibrations. Minimal changes in FT-IR spectra before and after loading indicate that the N+ moiety of lauryl betaine remains stable and is not significantly involved in metal coordination. Peaks at 1346/1347 and 1134 cm–1 are assigned to asymmetric and symmetric SO stretching vibrations of [Tf2N]−, correcting our previous misinterpretation. The persistence of these SO signals after extraction indicates the anion’s stability during extraction, consistent with NMR results. In the 13C NMR spectra (Figure b), the quartet absorption peaks (yellow dashed box) arise from J-coupling between fluorine and carbon atoms in the −CF3 groups. Their presence before and after extraction suggests that the highly soluble [Tf2N]− remained in the organic phase and was not significantly lost to the aqueous phase, supporting the proposed cation exchange mechanism.

Sc3+ Stripping and Ionic Liquid Recycling

The acidity of the aqueous phase significantly influences both the extraction and stripping efficiency of Sc3+ by the ionic liquid. In this study, H2SO4 at concentrations ranging from 0.01 to 0.06 mol·L–1 was used as the stripping agent. As shown in Figure , the stripping efficiency of Sc3+ from loaded [HLaur]­[Tf2N] increased gradually with acid concentration. Quantitative stripping was achieved at an H2SO4 concentration of 0.035 mol·L–1. According to eq , highly acidic conditions promote the reverse reaction, facilitating the release of Sc3+ into the aqueous phase. The efficient stripping under mild acidic conditions demonstrates the practical and environmental advantages of [HLaur]­[Tf2N] for sustainable Sc3+ recovery.

The structural integrity of the ILs after the stripping process was verified by FT-IR spectroscopy (Figure a). The characteristic absorption peaks corresponding to the CO stretch of the carbonyl group in [HLaur]+ at 1745 cm–1 and the SO stretches of [Tf2N]− at 1347 and 1134 cm–1 remained essentially unchanged compared to those observed after the acid washing recovery process. Furthermore, the broad absorption peak at 3563 cm–1, attributable to the O–H stretching vibration of the −COOH, reappeared in the recovered IL. This indicates that loaded Sc3+ ions were effectively removed from the IL matrix, and the ILs maintained structural integrity without degradation after the stripping cycle, providing preliminary evidence for the recyclability of these functionalized ionic liquids. Figure b presents the cycling performance of [HLaur]­[Tf2N]. After six extraction-stripping-re-extraction cycles, the extraction efficiency of [HLaur]­[Tf2N] for Sc3+ remained highly stable. Furthermore, TOC analysis was performed on the aqueous phase after extraction to indirectly reflect the dissolution and leakage of ILs. Given that the initial aqueous feed solution contained no organic carbon, the TOC measured in the raffinate (39.7 mg·L–1) originates from the ILs. This indicates a low solubility of the ILs in the aqueous phase and suggests its high potential for recyclability.

Selective Separation of Sc3+ from Al3+ in Simulated Red Mud Sulfuric Acid Leachate

The sulfuric acid leachate of red mud contains low concentrations of Sc3+ and high concentrations of Fe3+ and Al3+. The selective separation of Sc3+/Al3+ represents a core technical challenge after the removal of Fe3+ via established pretreatment processes (e.g., pH-adjustment precipitation, preleaching pretreatment, , etc.). This study focuses on the extraction performance of ionic liquids in an Al3+-rich system, using the iron-removed target system as the research object. Accordingly, a simulated solution containing 3700 mg·L–1 Al3+ and 4.72 mg·L–1 Sc3+ was prepared. An IL-to-Sc3+ molar ratio of 5.8 and an extraction time of 40 min were maintained to ensure selective extraction of Sc3+. Additionally, to minimize experimental errors associated with trace ionic liquid usage, selective recovery experiments were conducted using a scaled-up solution volume (30 mL). As shown in Figure , after a single extraction stage, negligible changes in aluminum concentration were observed, while the Sc3+ extraction efficiency reached 32.3%. With increasing number of extraction stages, the total extraction efficiency of both Al3+ and Sc3+ increased. However, upon the fifth extraction, the total extraction efficiency of Al3+ saw only a minimal increase to 1.62%, compared to 94.4% for Sc3+ with 0.26 mg·L–1 remaining in the aqueous phase. These results indicate that in comparison with organophosphorus , and carboxylic acid systems applied to the sulfuric acid leachate of red mud, the [HLaur]­[Tf2N] systemwhile exhibiting a marginal decrease in extraction efficiency at a high phase ratio without a diluentretained good selectivity. Furthermore, stripping tests showed that the [HLaur]­[Tf2N] system avoids the use of strong acids or bases for stripping. These findings support the potential of [HLaur]­[Tf2N] for green and highly selective Sc3+ recovery from high-aluminum sulfate media.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates the promising application of hydrophobic carboxylic acid-functionalized ionic liquids used without organic diluent, particularly [HLaur]­[Tf2N], for the selective and sustainable recovery of Sc3+ from Al3+-rich sulfuric acid solutions, as exemplified by red mud leachate. [HLaur]­[Tf2N] was identified as the preferred extractant. A Sc3+ extraction efficiency of 44.4% was achieved under the comprehensively optimized conditions (a [HLaur]­[Tf2N]-to-Sc3+ molar ratio of 5.8:1, an equilibration time of 40 min, a Na2SO4 concentration of 0.5 mol·L–1, and a pH ≥ 3.0), which balanced good extraction performance with high selectivity over Al3+. The process operates via a cation-exchange mechanism involving the participation of sulfate ions. Practical viability is supported by effective stripping with dilute acid (0.035 mol·L–1 H2SO4) and the IL’s structural stability over six cycles. Notably, the system retained robust performance in complex simulated leachate, achieving an initial extraction efficiency of 32.3% even under a high aqueous-to-organic phase ratio. Crucially, the process exhibits good scalability potential. A multistage extraction enabled a total Sc3+ recovery of 94.4% after five stages, reducing the aqueous concentration to only 0.26 mg·L–1. These results underscore the potential of the [HLaur]­[Tf2N]-based process for the sustainable recovery of critical Sc3+ from secondary Al3+-rich resources, contributing to a secure supply chain for strategic metals and the advancement of sustainable hydrometallurgy.

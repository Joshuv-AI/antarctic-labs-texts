Isatin-mediated inhibition of the PI3K/AKT/EMT signaling axis confers antitumor efficacy in Ehrlich solid carcinoma-bearing mice
Authors: Heba Effat, Marwa A. Ibrahim, Rehab S. Abohashem, Yasmin M. Attia, Fatma M. Abdelwahed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Despite advances, detailed insight into cancer’s biological mechanisms remains needed. Isatin, an indole-1 H-2,3-dione, is an attractive agent for new drug discovery. Here, the anticancer efficacy and potential toxicity of isatin in combination with doxorubicin (Dox) in a mouse Ehrlich solid carcinoma (ESC) model were examined by modulating the PI3K/AKT/EMT pathway. Five groups of fifty female mice were prepared: negative control, positive ESC-bearing mice, isatin-treated ESC group, Dox-treated ESC group, and isatin and Dox combination-treated ESC group. Treatments began after tumor formation. Inflammatory markers, kidney and liver enzyme activities, and EMT markers levels (vimentin and E-cadherin) were assessed by ELISA. HIF 1 α, NF-κB, PI3K, AKT, MMP2, and MMP9 gene expression was evaluated using qRT-PCR. Protein expression of MMP-9 and E-cadherin was assessed by IHC. Compared with the positive control, the combined treatment reduced tumor growth by 51%. Additionally, combined treatment showed a significant increase in the amount of necrotic tissue. Mechanistically, isatin exerted its therapeutic effect by increasing anti-inflammatory (IL-10) cytokine levels and suppressing pro-inflammatory (IL-6) cytokine levels, along with reductions in HIF-1α, NF-κB, PI3K, AKT, MMP2, and MMP9 mRNA levels. Also, a combined group of isatin and Dox showed reduced vimentin levels and elevated E-cadherin levels by ELISA. Co-administration of isatin and Dox significantly increased E-cadherin expression and attenuated MMP-9 expression compared with the untreated group, as assessed by IHC. Our results indicate that isatin, in combination with doxorubicin, exerts a synergistic anticancer effect against ESC, offering a promising therapeutic approach to enhance treatment efficacy.

Introduction

Historically, the paradigm of cancer treatment has heavily relied on systemic chemotherapy, which remains a cornerstone in clinical oncology by primarily targeting and disrupting the rapid proliferation of malignant cells. Over the past decades, numerous studies and comprehensive reviews have investigated the utilization of different chemotherapeutic agents to control cancer progression and manage metastatic disease [1–3]. Malignancy is a complex and multifaceted disease characterized by unregulated cell growth and division. This unchecked proliferation results from a range of hereditary and molecular changes that enable cancer cells to bypass normal regulatory systems, including mechanisms like programmed cell death [1]. To study these mechanisms and evaluate potential therapies, the Ehrlich tumor, a spontaneous mouse mammary adenocarcinoma, is widely utilized. It is a highly aggressive, rapidly proliferating, and poorly differentiated carcinoma [4]. Because of its high success rate and consistent growth patterns, it is primarily employed in pharmacological studies to screen the anti-tumor potential of new compounds and to investigate various aspects of cancer biology, including tumor metabolism and the systemic effects of malignancy [5]. Developed by subcutaneous injection, the solid Ehrlich carcinoma (ESC) provides a valuable in vivo resource for investigating potential anti-cancer drugs [6] and their combinations.

Among established chemotherapeutics, Doxorubicin (DOX), an anthracycline antibiotic derived from Streptomyces peucetius, has been widely and effectively used in the treatment of various malignancies [7]. Despite its substantial therapeutic benefits in disrupting cancer cell DNA replication, DOX is accompanied by severe dose-limiting complications. These adverse effects include cumulative damage to the cardiac tissue (cardiotoxicity), bone marrow suppression, gastrointestinal lining damage, reproductive system toxicity, and alopecia [8]. Thus, researchers are increasingly focused on identifying adjuvant agents that can both enhance the anticancer efficacy of primary chemotherapeutic drugs and mitigate their associated toxicities. In this context, natural products and their synthetic derivatives have gained significant attention as promising candidates for the development of effective combination anticancer therapies, offering strong antioxidant activity, targeted cytotoxicity, and reduced toxicity to normal cells [9]. Among these, an endogenous indole known as isatin (indole-2,3-dione) and its derivatives have exhibited various biological effects, including defense against microbes, reduction of inflammation, free radical scavenging, and tumor suppression effects [10]. Isatin exerts both a preventive action against free radical–induced carcinogenesis and a targeted cytotoxic effect on cancer cells. Previous studies have shown that isatin and its derivatives can induce apoptosis [11, 12], inhibit tumor cell proliferation, and modulate oxidative stress [12]. Importantly, isatin has also been suggested to interfere with cancer-related signaling pathways, thereby enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of established chemotherapeutics [13].

The rationale for combining DOX and isatin is grounded in their complementary mechanisms of action. In vitro studies have demonstrated that isatin derivatives can act as potent chemosensitizers, overcoming multidrug resistance by co-inhibiting key pathways such as P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and Heat shock protein 90 (Hsp90), thereby significantly increasing the intracellular accumulation and efficacy of DOX in resistant cancer cell lines [14]. Furthermore, in vivo studies using the ESC model have consistently shown that combining DOX with natural antioxidants and apoptotic modulators synergistically inhibits tumor growth while ameliorating DOX-induced systemic toxicities [15, 16]. By integrating the DNA-damaging properties of DOX with the apoptosis-inducing and chemosensitizing properties of isatin, this combination represents a highly compelling and translatable strategy for cancer biologists seeking to optimize therapeutic indices in aggressive tumor models such as ESC.

The PI3K/Akt (phosphoinositide 3-kinase/Akt) signaling pathway is central to regulating fundamental cellular processes in many cancers [17], including cell viability, metabolism, metastasis, angiogenesis, and inflammation. Its aberrant activation often results from genomic alterations in genes such as PIK3CA, PTEN, Akt, TSC1, and mTOR. Mechanistically, Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) is converted to PIP3 (phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-trisphosphate) by PI3K, and PIP3 subsequently recruits and activates Akt, leading to the phosphorylation of multiple substrates. Among its major downstream effectors, mTOR integrates signals from multiple signaling proteins to promote tumor development. Genetic alteration and constitutive activation of PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway components are frequently observed across different cancer types [18]. In addition, tumor progression and metastasis are intrinsically linked to the Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT). This crucial process is characterized by cancer cells switching off epithelial markers and activating mesenchymal markers, thereby equipping them with traits necessary for invasion and dissemination throughout the body [19].

To the best of our knowledge, there is a research gap in the published scientific literature regarding the in vivo therapeutic effects of isatin co-administered with DOX in the ESC solid tumor animal model. Therefore, using this model in our study breaks new ground by exploring the impact of this combination and providing valuable insights into its potential synergistic interaction. Herein, the primary objective of this research is to analyze the possible modulatory action of isatin, alone and co-administered with Doxorubicin, on Ehrlich solid tumor–bearing mice. Specifically, the work will assess histopathological alterations in tumor tissue, immunohistochemical expression of selected molecular markers, and transcriptional changes in the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway and in genes related to epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), in order to elucidate the mechanistic basis of isatin’s anticancer and chemosensitizing properties.

Materials and methods

Drugs preparation

Doxorubicin hydrochloride solution at 2 mg/ml (Ebewe, Australia) was purchased, and mice were intraperitoneally injected at a dose of 4 mg/kg [20]. Mice were orally injected with Isatin (Loba Chemie Pvt. Ltd., India) dissolved in saline containing 0.01% w/v dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and carboxymethyl cellulose at a dose of 25 mg/kg. The selected dose was based on previously reported in vivo studies demonstrating the pharmacological and antitumor activity of isatin and its derivatives in murine models and was further supported by preliminary pilot experiments conducted in our laboratory [21].

Preparation of Ehrlich Solid Carcinoma (ESC) bearing mice

Ehrlich carcinoma cells (ECC) were obtained from the National Cancer Institute, Cairo University, Egypt, and maintained as a solid tumor model by serial subcutaneous transplantation in Swiss albino mice. Tumor tissues were excised aseptically from donor mice and mechanically minced in cold sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to obtain a single-cell suspension. The suspension was filtered through sterile gauze to remove tissue debris. Cell viability was assessed using trypan blue exclusion, and only cell suspensions with viability greater than 95% were used. Cells were counted using a hemocytometer and adjusted to a final concentration of 2.5 × 106 viable cells per 0.2 mL PBS for inoculation. The cell suspension was freshly prepared in sterile PBS immediately before subcutaneous injection. No Matrigel or other extracellular matrix components were used. Swiss albino mice were prepared to form an Ehrlich solid tumor by subcutaneous inoculation of about 2.5 × 106 of ECC into the thigh of the left hind leg [22]. ECC were acquired from the National Cancer Institute, Cairo University, Egypt. Initiation of treatment was performed in a post-treatment regimen, starting after tumor establishment when tumors appeared and reached a palpable volume with an average volume of approximately 100 mm³ (day 7 post-inoculation), in order to evaluate the anti-tumor efficacy of the tested agents.

Experimental design

Fifty Swiss albino mice (adult females) of 20–30 g body weight were acquired from the Egyptian National Cancer Institute animal house. All mice were kept under controlled standard conditions of temperature, humidity, and nutrition. The research protocol was approved by the animal ethical committee of the Faculty of Science, Cairo University, under the number (CUIIIF2025). Mice were randomly distributed into 5 groups as follows: (1): Negative control group infused with physiological saline, (2): Positive control ESC group (subcutaneously implantated with Ehrlich tumor cells into the thigh of the left hind leg of mice) (3): ESC group treated with Doxorubicin 4 mg/kg given by intraperitoneal inoculation once a week for 3 weeks [16] after appearance of tumor. (4): ESC group treated with isatin given orally every other day for 3 weeks [17] after appearance of tumor (5): ESC group treated with a combination of Doxorubicin and isatin at the previous dosage after appearance of tumor.

RNA isolation and gene expression levels evaluation using the real time PCR technique

Isolation of total RNA from tumor tissues was performed using the RNeasy Mini Kit [Qiagen, Hilden, Germany, cat. no. 74104] following the protocol’s guidelines. Concentration and purity of the resulting RNA were determined using a NanoDrop-2000 spectrophotometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA). Synthesis of cDNA from RNA was performed using the Easyscript First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit [TransGen Biotech CO., LTD, China, cat. no. AE301] following the guidelines. RNA expression levels were evaluated by qPCR using the Thermo Scientific Maxima SYBR Green PCR Kit [Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States, cat.no. K0251] following the guidelines. The housekeeping gene (β-actin) was used to normalize the data. The 2-ΔΔCt method was used to calculate relative RNA expression of the studied genes [23]. Biochemical analysis

After the end of treatment, the mice were fasted overnight and euthanized through cervical dislocation to collect blood specimens. Then, the blood specimens were centrifuged at 4 °C for 15 min at 3000 rpm to obtain sera. Samples were then collected and stored at -20 °C for the determination of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), urea, creatinine, interleukin-6 (IL-6), IL-10, vimentin, and E-cadherin.

Colorimetric assay

Serum levels of ALT and AST were measured to evaluate liver function using spectrophotometric kits (Biodiagnostic, Cat# AL1031 and AS1061, respectively) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. In addition, serum urea (abcam, Cat# ab83362) and creatinine (Biodiagnostic, Cat# CR 12 50) were measured using spectrophotometric kits to assess kidney function.

ELISA assay

IL-6 (Cloud-Clone, Cat# SEA079Ra), IL-10 (Cloud-Clone, Cat# SEA056Ra), vimentin (MyBioSource, Cat# MBS728619), and E-cadherin (MyBioSource, Cat# MBS3807624) serum levels were assayed using ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

Histopathological studies

Tumor tissues obtained from the Ehrlich tumor model were immediately excised and fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 24 h to preserve tissue architecture. Following fixation, samples were thoroughly washed under running tap water to remove excess fixative. Tissues were then subjected to a graded dehydration process using ascending concentrations of ethanol (70%, 80%, 90%, and 100%), with sufficient time at each step to ensure complete removal of water. After dehydration, tissues were cleared in xylene to render them transparent and facilitate paraffin infiltration. Subsequently, samples were infiltrated and embedded in molten paraffin wax (melting point ~ 56 °C) using a hot air oven, and blocks were allowed to solidify. Paraffin-embedded tissue blocks were sectioned at a thickness of approximately 4 μm using a rotary microtome. The obtained sections were mounted on glass slides, deparaffinized in xylene, and rehydrated through descending grades of ethanol to water. Sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to evaluate general histological architecture. Finally, stained sections were examined under a light (optical) microscope to assess histopathological changes in tumor tissues [24].

Immunohistochemical examination

Immunohistochemical evaluation of E-cadherin and MMP-9 expression was performed using the avidin-biotin-peroxidase complex (ABC) method. Positively charged slides that were mounted by Paraffin sections of Ehrlich solid tumor tissues were treated with mouse E-cadherin polyclonal Antibody (ABclonal, USA, Dil.: 1:100) or mouse MMP-9 polyclonal Antibody (ABclonal, USA, Dil.: 1:100); then the reagents required for ABC method were added (Vectastain ABC-HRP kit, Vector Laboratories). The antigen-antibody complex was detected using a peroxidase-labeled marker and colored with diaminobenzidine (DAB, produced by Sigma). Non-immune serum in place of the primary or secondary antibodies was used as a negative control. Finally, the Olympus microscope (BX-53) was used to examine IHC-stained sections.

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of duplicate measurements. Mean comparisons were performed using an ANOVA test (one-way analysis of variance), followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons. A p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software (version 20.0; IBM Corp., Chicago, IL, USA).

Results

Effect of Doxorubicin and/or isatin treatment on body weight and tumor burden

Tumor-bearing mice exhibited a marked increase in body weight compared to normal controls, reflecting tumor burden and local inflammation. Treatment with doxorubicin, isatin, or their combination normalized body weight toward control values, indicating reduced tumor associated mass and absence of overt systemic toxicity as shown in Fig. 1A. Moreover, tumor volume was reduced with doxorubicin and isatin, each compared with the positive control group. The combination group produced the greatest reduction in tumor volume, with a significant difference compared with both individual treatments (Fig. 1B). These results elucidated that the combined administration of isatin and doxorubicin exerts a stronger inhibitory effect on tumor growth in the Ehrlich solid tumor model.

Effect of Doxorubicin, isatin, and their combination on liver functions

Liver functions were estimated by evaluating serum ALT and AST activity. ALT and AST activities were significantly (P < 0.05) higher in the positive control group than in the negative control group. In contrast, ALT and AST activity showed a significant reduction (P < 0.05) in the Dox, isatin, and combination groups compared to the positive control group. Moreover, ALT and AST activity in the Dox group was significantly (P < 0.05) lower than that in the isatin group, as presented in Fig. 2.

Discussion

Cancer continues to pose a serious threat to global health. Even with major scientific and technological breakthroughs, there remains a need to better understand tumor biology and to develop treatment approaches [25]. Natural products have long been regarded as a valuable source for anticancer drug discovery, providing lead compounds with diverse structural frameworks and biological activities. Isatin, chemically an indole-1 H-2,3-dione, is one of the most attractive therapeutic fragments in drug design and development [26]. Here, our aim was to investigate the antitumor activity of isatin in ESC-bearing mice by possibly regulating the PI3K/AKT pathway. The ESC model, one of the most widely used experimental methods for evaluating the efficacy of potential anticancer drugs, was employed to achieve this goal. Its aggressiveness, rapid growth, and high transplantability closely resemble some traits of human solid tumors [27]. Compared with the untreated ESC group, our study showed that Isatin, Dox, and Combination treatments had favorable outcomes. In addition to improving anti-inflammatory defensive mechanisms and modulating genes linked to cell development, proliferation, and metastasis, groups treated with isatin, Dox, or a combination of the two showed a significant reduction in tumor growth. The novelty of this study lies in the fact that the majority of previous studies examined the impact of various isatin derivatives on cancer development; however, there is a shortage of information regarding the in vivo therapeutic effects of isatin in combination with Dox chemotherapy.

Targeting the PI3K/AKT axis has emerged as a promising therapeutic approach, as it is one of the most commonly dysregulated signaling pathways in cancer and because tumor cell survival, proliferation, and resistance to apoptosis depend on its continuous activation [28, 29]. Although several isatin derivatives have been reported to possess anticancer properties, there is limited evidence regarding the direct effects of isatin itself on solid tumors through PI3K/AKT inhibition. Therefore, this work aimed to clarify whether isatin could suppress Ehrlich tumor progression by blocking this oncogenic pathway, thereby providing new mechanistic insights into its therapeutic potential. Quantitative real-time PCR results demonstrated that treatment with Dox or isatin significantly suppressed expression of HIF-1α, AKT, and MMP9, with the strongest effect observed in the combined treated group after 21 days of treatment. Given that AKT signaling promotes cell survival and proliferation, and that HIF1-α is a key regulator of tumor adaptation to hypoxic stress and promotes cancer progression, their downregulation indicates strong suppression of tumor growth pathways [30]. Similarly, MMP9 plays a vital role in extracellular matrix breakdown and metastasis; thus, its substantial reduction suggests reduced invasive potential [31]. Crucially, the combination-treated group showed a marked decrease in PI3K expression, indicating that isatin may improve Dox’s chemosensitivity by focusing on the PI3K/AKT axis, a pathway commonly linked to drug resistance. Although the decreases in MMP2 and NF-κB expression were not statistically significant, the observed decrease in their fold changes in the combination group suggests a biological trend toward suppressing metastasis- and inflammation-related genes. A key process in the development of several cancer forms, including Ehrlich solid carcinoma, is the interaction between inflammation and NF-κB signaling. It was reported that inflammation may be regulated by controlling the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, which sustain a tumor-promoting environment while also increasing angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and apoptosis evasion [32]. In the current study, the reduction in inflammatory mediators observed in the treated groups may be linked to NF-κB signaling suppression, thereby weakening the inflammatory axis that supports ESC progression. These results are consistent with previous reports demonstrating that targeting NF-κB diminishes cytokine release and suppresses tumor growth in experimental solid tumor models [33, 16]. These results collectively suggest that combined isatin and Dox therapy may exert synergistic antitumor effects by concurrently targeting hypoxia-driven adaptation, PI3K/AKT signaling, and metastatic regulators.

Downregulation of epithelial markers like E-cadherin and overexpression of mesenchymal markers like vimentin are characteristics of the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), a crucial stage in tumor formation [19], which enhances cancer cells’ ability to invade and spread. According to ELISA data, ESC-bearing mice treated with isatin and Dox in this study demonstrated a significant reduction in vimentin and restoration of E-cadherin protein expression levels (P < 0.05). Moreover, immunohistopathological results in the combined group showed a significant decrease in MMP9 and a significant increase in E-cadherin compared with other groups. This suggests that the combined regimen not only suppresses tumor growth but may also counteract EMT, thereby limiting tumor cell metastatic behavior. However, (IHC) expression of E-cadherin in the combination-treated group was not as elevated as observed in the ELISA results compared to the other treated groups, such as the dox group. This apparent discrepancy can be attributed to methodological differences between the two techniques. ELISA quantitatively measures the total protein concentration in liquid samples. In contrast, IHC provides a semi-quantitative assessment of protein localization within intact tissue sections, and the signal intensity can be affected by several technical factors. These include incomplete antigen retrieval, limited antibody penetration, or reduced accessibility of epitopes within the three-dimensional tissue structure. Moreover, tissue fixation and processing during IHC may partially denature or mask epitopes, resulting in weaker staining. Previous reports demonstrated that Dox can inhibit EMT through modulation of E-cadherin and vimentin expression [34] and that isatin derivatives exhibit anti-metastatic and EMT-suppressive activity in experimental tumors [35]. The synergistic effect observed in the combination group highlights the potential of isatin to enhance Dox efficacy by simultaneously targeting proliferative and metastatic pathways in ESC. In addition to their cytotoxic effects, both Dox and isatin exhibit notable immunomodulatory properties that may contribute to their antitumor activity. In the present study, evaluation of inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-10 in ESC–bearing mice revealed a significant decrease in IL-6 and a concomitant increase in IL-10 following combination treatment with isatin and Dox. These findings indicate that the combined regimen not only suppresses tumor growth but also modulates the tumor microenvironment by shifting the cytokine balance toward an anti-inflammatory profile. It was reported that IL-10 can inhibit inflammation-driven cancer by reducing pro-inflammatory factors like IL-6 and TNF-α, which promote tumor angiogenesis. However, IL-10 also exerts tumor-inhibitory effects by stimulating cytotoxic CD8 + T cells and Natural Killer (NK) cells, enhancing anti-tumor immunity, and inhibiting the expansion of immunosuppressive T cells [36, 37]. Our data suggests that IL-10 negatively regulates inflammatory cytokine production and may inhibit inflammation associated with cancer development and growth. Such an effect could enhance anti-tumor immunity, reduce tumor-associated inflammation, and improve therapeutic efficacy compared to single-agent treatment [38]. This observation aligns with previous reports describing Doxorubicin’s ability to regulate cytokine expression and promote antitumor immune responses [39], as well as the anti-inflammatory potential of isatin and its derivatives in modulating IL-6 and other mediators [40]. Measuring liver and kidney function in the Ehrlich tumor model is essential to evaluate the safety and systemic effects of the treatment. Since these organs are key in drug metabolism and excretion, their assessment helps determine potential hepato- and nephrotoxicity, as well as the impact of tumor burden. This ensures that the therapy is effective against the tumor while remaining safe for vital organs, supporting its potential for clinical application. Biochemical assessment of kidney and liver function revealed that tumor-bearing mice exhibited significant elevations in urea, creatinine, AST, and ALT levels compared to the negative control group. The observed decrease in serum urea, creatinine, AST, and ALT levels following treatment with isatin and its combination with doxorubicin further confirms their protective effects on renal and hepatic tissues. The significant increases in these parameters in tumor-bearing mice that have not received treatment are a reflection of the organ dysfunction linked to the advancement of cancer and the stress caused by chemotherapy. These results suggest that isatin might also reduce systemic toxicity frequently associated with chemotherapy and tumor growth, in addition to its anticancer action [41]. This protective effect is consistent with previous studies reporting the hepatoprotective and nephroprotective properties of isatin and natural compounds with antioxidant potential, which help restore tissue integrity and reduce oxidative stress–induced damage [12, 42]. Histological analyses of various organs, including the kidney, heart, spleen, liver, and lung, after isatin treatment revealed that isatin didn’t adversely influence the activities of essential organs, according to another study by Tawfik et al. [12]. Taken together, these findings demonstrate the therapeutic benefit of combining isatin with Dox in reducing tumor burden and alleviating organ dysfunction. The biochemical and molecular results were verified by histopathological analysis of H&E-stained Ehrlich solid tumor sections, which added to our understanding of the tested treatment’s antitumor effectiveness. Dense populations of polymorphic neoplastic cells with many mitotic figures, indicative of their strong proliferative activity, were found in the tumor tissues of the untreated ESC group. Numerous polymorph neoplastic cells persisted in the groups treated with Dox and isatin, indicating a partial response; however, small areas of necrosis were observed, suggesting limited cytotoxicity. It’s worth noting that the combination treatment resulted in a more pronounced histological improvement. Tumor sections showed clusters of neoplastic cells encircled by a sizable area of necrosis, suggesting increased tumor cell death and decreased proliferative burden. Critical pathological alterations, such as hypoxia, hypoglycemia, ischemia, exposure to toxins and reactive oxygen species (ROS), food shortage, or extreme environmental conditions (high or low pH and temperature), can cause necrosis [43]. According to recent research, necrosis can be controlled by certain signaling pathways and used to treat neurological illnesses, cardiovascular diseases, and cancer [44]. In line with earlier studies emphasizing the benefits of combination therapies in overcoming tumor resistance and improving histological outcomes in solid malignancies, these results imply that the synergistic action of isatin and Dox enhances tumor necrosis, thereby exerting superior antitumor efficacy compared to either agent alone [45]. Such outcomes indicate that the PI3K/AKT blockade by isatin not only interrupts proliferative signaling but may also sensitize tumor cells to necrosis [46, 47]. When combined, these findings provide strong evidence that isatin inhibits oncogenic signaling and triggers necrosis, thereby producing its anticancer effects, suggesting it may be a viable treatment option for solid tumors. In this context, the observed inhibitory effect of isatin on PI3K/AKT/EMT signaling suggests its potential utility as a targeted therapeutic agent. Notably, agents targeting this pathway have gained significant attention in clinical oncology, with several PI3K/AKT inhibitors already evaluated in clinical trials and some approved for specific cancer subtypes [48, 49].

Conclusion

The present findings highlight the potential translational relevance of isatin as a promising anticancer agent. In this context, the current study demonstrates that isatin exerts a potent inhibitory effect on Ehrlich solid carcinoma growth in vivo, as evidenced by significant reductions in tumor volume and weight. Mechanistically, these effects were associated with suppression of the PI3K/AKT/EMT signaling axis, suggesting that isatin interferes with key molecular pathways governing tumor proliferation, survival, and metastatic potential. In addition to its antitumor activity, isatin treatment was associated with improved hepatic and renal function, along with increased tumor necrosis, indicating a favorable safety and tolerability profile.

Collectively, these findings support the potential clinical utility of isatin and warrant further investigation in advanced preclinical models and clinical settings, particularly as a targeted approach for malignancies driven by PI3K/AKT signaling.

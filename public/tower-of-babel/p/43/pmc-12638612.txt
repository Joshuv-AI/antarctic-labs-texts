Healthcare Resource Utilisation and Costs in Patients with Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis: A Retrospective Analysis of German Insurance Claims Data
Authors: Paul Dolin, Anat Shavit, Jennifer Rowell, Chris Edmonds, Josefine Persson, Danuta Kielar, Keltie McDonald, Antje Mevius, Bernhard Hellmich, Stephanie Y Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEvidence is limited on the clinical and economic burden of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) in Europe. We evaluated EGPA healthcare resource utilisation (HCRU), days off work, and costs in Germany.MethodsThis analysis used claims data from the German statutory health insurance fund AOK Plus. Patients with newly diagnosed EGPA (index date 2016–2020; ≥ 12 months pre-diagnosis health plan enrolment) were matched (1:4) with general insured individuals without EGPA. Baseline was 12 months pre-diagnosis; follow-up was until 31 December 2020, insurance disenrollment, or death. Outcomes included HCRU and related costs and days off work.ResultsThe study included 155 patients and 620 matched individuals. In the EGPA cohort, all-cause HCRU was higher post-diagnosis than during baseline in all categories. Mean annualised in-patient hospitalisations/patient and pharmacy claims/patient for any EGPA therapy were 2.99 and 5.87, respectively, during 1-year post-diagnosis versus 1.15 and 1.80, respectively, during baseline. Mean total annualised cost of all-cause HCRU/patient with EGPA was €19,700 during 1-year post-diagnosis versus €6,678 during baseline, with in-patient hospitalisations and pharmacy costs the main cost drivers of EGPA care. Over 5 years post-diagnosis, mean annualised HCRU rate per patient was significantly higher for the EGPA versus the matched cohort for all evaluated aspects of HCRU (p < 0.001). The mean total annualised all-cause HCRU cost was sevenfold higher (EGPA €14,771/patient vs matched cohort €2,094/patient; p < 0.001). In-patient hospitalisation (€8,276/patient) was the single largest driver of all-cause costs over 5 years post-diagnosis. Mean total days off work and associated annualised costs of productivity loss were also significantly higher over 5 years post-diagnosis in the EGPA versus the matched cohort (30.74 vs 13.35 days/year; €3,632 vs €1,555 annually/patient, both p < 0.001).ConclusionsThese real-world data highlight the substantial economic burden associated with EGPA, characterised by increased HCRU, costs and productivity losses, underscoring the need for effective management strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40744-025-00795-1.Keywords: AOK plus, Economic burden, Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, HCRU, Healthcare costs, Productivity loss, Real-world evidence, Retrospective

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Given the limited published data on the clinical and economic burden of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) in Europe, we conducted a retrospective study using claims data from the German statutory health insurance fund AOK Plus to evaluate real-world healthcare resource utilisation (HCRU), days off work, and costs for patients with EGPA in Germany.What was learned from the study?HCRU and associated costs were higher after diagnosis than before diagnosis in patients with EGPA.When compared with a matched general insured population cohort without EGPA, the patients with EGPA had significantly higher HCRU for all types of clinical encounters and pharmacy claims and significantly higher associated costs.Employed patients with EGPA also had significantly more days off work with significantly higher associated costs of productivity loss than a matched general insured population cohort without EGPA.These real-world data add to the limited existing data on the burden of EGPA in terms of both HCRU and associated costs and further highlight the need for improved treatment options to better manage this disease.

Introduction

Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) is a rare inflammatory disorder characterised by eosinophilia of blood and tissues, late-onset asthma (typically diagnosed years prior to EGPA) and small-to-medium size vessel vasculitis [1]. This chronic disease has a relapsing and remitting course, and its clinical presentation is highly variable; in a typical patient, EGPA can manifest in multiple tissues and organs, leading to multi-organ damage and disability [1–4]. A meta-analysis published in 2021 estimated the prevalence of EGPA to be 15.3 cases per million individuals globally, and 12.1 cases per million individuals in Europe [5], but more recent estimates place prevalence as high as 34.4 cases per million individuals globally [6], and 38.9 cases per million individuals in Germany [7], possibly reflecting improvements in the diagnosis and management of EGPA.

The treatment of EGPA is primarily driven by disease severity, as assessed by the Five Factor Score (FFS), which was originally developed as a prognostic tool, and is defined in both the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) and the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) treatment guidelines according to the presence of life- or organ-threatening disease [8, 9]. Multiple different specialists (e.g. rheumatologist, pulmonologist, nephrologist) may be involved in the management of EGPA depending on the particular disease manifestation and organ involvement. The goals of treatment are to induce remission, thereby minimising the development of long-term organ damage caused by eosinophilic tissue infiltration or necrotising vasculitis, and to prevent relapses [1, 8]. For patients with non-severe disease, the addition of eosinophil-targeting biologics to oral glucocorticoid (OGC) therapy is recommended, while for patients with severe disease, the addition of cyclophosphamide or rituximab is recommended [1, 3, 8]. High exposure to OGC-based therapy (for prolonged periods and/or at high doses) is associated with an increased risk of adverse effects [3], but patients may experience relapses when attempting to taper their OGC dose [10]. For example, in a long-term study of 101 patients with EGPA of whom approximately two-thirds were receiving low-dose OGC by study end, 79.6% achieved a first remission (defined as Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score [BVAS] = 0 and OGC < 7.5 mg/day for ≥ 6 consecutive months), but 81.1% subsequently relapsed [10].

EGPA relapse is associated with increased healthcare resource utilisation (HCRU) and costs due to excessive symptoms, associated comorbidities, and treatment requirements [5, 11–15]. In United States (US)-based studies, mean total all-cause healthcare costs were approximately 2.5-fold higher in patients with EGPA than those in patients with asthma [11], and annualised mean total costs were approximately 16-fold higher for patients with EGPA compared with those of a matched general population of insured patients [15]. Available data also suggest high rates of EGPA HCRU in England, with all-cause in-patient admissions and EGPA-related in-patient admissions documented for 49.9% and 19.1% of patients with EGPA, respectively [13]. While the clinical burden and costs associated with EGPA and its management are known to be high, economic data on real-world HCRU and associated costs for EGPA are sparse, especially in Europe, and, to our knowledge, no studies have assessed indirect costs such as productivity loss due to days off work in patient populations with EGPA. Given this paucity of data, we conducted a retrospective study to quantify HCRU, healthcare costs and days off work, for real-world patients with EGPA compared with patients without EGPA, based on an analysis of claims data from the German statutory health insurance fund AOK Plus.

Methods

Study Design

This study was a non-interventional, retrospective analysis of administrative health insurance claims data from AOK Plus, a German sickness fund insuring approximately 3.4 million people in the states of Saxony and Thuringia. The study forms part of the CONSTELLATION real-world evidence programme in rare eosinophil-associated diseases. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles that are consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki, ICH Good Clinical Practice, the Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice (GPP) published by the International Society of Pharmacoepidemiology (ISPE), and appropriate laws and regulations of Germany and the European Union. All data within the dataset provided by AOK Plus were fully anonymised, and permission was obtained from AOK Plus to utilise their data for this study. Due to the anonymised nature of the data, ethics board review and approval were not required; however, the study protocol was reviewed and approved by AOK Plus. This article is based on retrospective data and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

An EGPA cohort was defined among patients with EGPA (International Classification of Diseases, 10th revision, German Modification [ICD-10-GM]: M30.1) newly diagnosed between 2016 and 2020, who were followed from the date of the first EGPA diagnosis (index date) until the end of 2020, end of continuous insurance enrolment, or death, whichever occurred first. Patients were required to have ≥ 12 months of continuous medical and pharmacy benefits insurance before their index date to be included. The baseline period was defined as the 12-month period prior to the index date (Fig. 1). Patients with a new diagnosis of microscopic polyangiitis (ICD-10-GM: M31.7), Takayasu arteritis (ICD-10-GM: M31.4) or giant cell arteritis (ICD-10-GM: M31.5 and M31.6) after the most recent EGPA diagnosis claim were excluded. The case definition required at least one in-patient or at least two outpatient records of EGPA diagnosis; for outpatient diagnoses, a second diagnosis record was required, either from a different healthcare provider or occurring at least 3 months after the first.

Patients with EGPA were age- (at index date) and sex-matched (1:4 ratio) with a random sample of individuals without EGPA (general insured population cohort) insured via the same fund. Patients were excluded from this reference cohort if they had an EGPA diagnosis record during 2015–2020; no other exclusion criteria were applied to ensure representativeness of this group by age and sex. For this reference cohort, the date of diagnosis of their matched patient with EGPA was defined as their corresponding index date. To be eligible for matching, reference patients were also required to have a duration of continuous insurance after their index date that was at least as long as that of their matched patient with EGPA.

Outcomes

Data were extracted for patient demographics (sex, age at index), diagnoses and procedures, costs of pharmaceuticals, in-patient hospitalisations, specialist and general practitioner (GP) visits, and inability to work (as days off work in calendar days; days off work were only observable for patients in current employment). Study outcomes included HCRU and related costs, analysed overall and by service setting, for both study cohorts. HCRU included number of in-patient hospitalisations and duration, stays in an intensive care unit (ICU), emergency department (ED) visits, specialist and GP visits, pharmacy claims, and procedures and tests. EGPA-related HCRU included all HCRU associated with an EGPA diagnosis or pertaining to an EGPA event or organ damage (Table S1), nasal polyps (ICD-10-GM: J33), or asthma exacerbations (ICD-10-GM: J45, J46; see Table S2); as well as EGPA-associated procedures and tests (Table S3), and pharmacy claims for any of the study medications (Table S4). EGPA events were based on the items on the BVAS (version 3) [16], and were assessed for the 3 months prior to index and for any time during follow-up. All-cause HCRU included all HCRU prior to index and during follow-up due to EGPA or other morbidities. The number and types of specialists seen prior to diagnosis and during follow-up were identified from medical specialty codes.

Healthcare costs were further reported by two mutually exclusive disease activity subgroups (assessed at 6-monthly intervals after index, using a 6-month lookback period): remission and minor active disease at baseline. Costs for major active disease without hospitalisation and major active disease requiring hospitalisation were examined but are not reported due to low patient numbers (< 5). Criteria for each of these categories are provided in Table S5. Total costs included costs from all of the evaluated service settings but excluded costs resulting from days off work.

Statistical Analysis

Patient demographics, clinical characteristics and HCRU outcomes are presented as frequency distribution and mean (standard deviation [SD]). Differences between the EGPA and matched general insured population cohorts were tested for significance using McNemar’s test. HCRU and related costs were calculated per person per year for both the 1-year baseline period (i.e. pre-diagnosis) and the total follow-up period (annualised value) for EGPA-related- and all-cause HCRU. Annualised rates of HCRU were calculated as the number of events of each resource type during a period of interest divided by the total person-years of follow-up during the same period. Costs for each type of HCRU were estimated using cost data recorded in the claims database, drug list prices, Diagnosis Related Groups (DRG)-based in-patient costs, and treatment-point based estimates of outpatient costs. Mean annualised costs per patient during a period of interest were calculated as the total costs for all HCRU divided by the person-years follow-up during the same period. Days off work were analysed overall and by sex and age at diagnosis. For days off work, costs were calculated by applying national costs per day of loss of productivity [17].

Results

Baseline Demographics and Clinical Characteristics

A total of 155 patients with incident EGPA and 620 persons from the matched general insured population were included (Fig. S1). In both cohorts, 54.2% of participants were female and mean (SD) age at the diagnosis/index date was well matched (EGPA cohort, 58.6 [15.6]; general insured population, 58.6 [15.5]; Table 1). The mean (SD) follow-up duration was 25.5 (16.9) months in the EGPA cohort and 27.6 (17.0) months in the general insured population cohort. The EGPA cohort had a higher prevalence of obstructive airway disease comorbidities in the 12 months prior to diagnosis than the matched general insured population cohort, including a higher prevalence of asthma (54.2% vs 8.7%) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (24.5% vs 7.1%) (Table 2).

Healthcare Resource Utilisation

All-Cause HCRU

Overall, all-cause HCRU was higher after diagnosis than during the baseline period in the EGPA cohort. During the first year after diagnosis the annualised number of in-patient hospitalisations and number of days spent in hospital per patient were approximately 2.6-fold higher versus the baseline period (2.99 visits/patient vs 1.15 visits/patient; 20.71 days/patient vs 8.05 days/patient) (Table 3). The annualised number of stays in the ICU was 0.08/patient during the baseline period and 0.25/patient during the first year after diagnosis, with an average of 1.24 and 5.09 days/patient spent in the ICU, respectively (Table 3). The number of pharmacy claims for any EGPA therapy was approximately 3.3-fold higher during the first year after diagnosis versus the baseline period (5.87 vs 1.80).

EGPA-Related HCRU

EGPA-related HCRU was greatest in the first year after diagnosis and remained considerably higher than during baseline throughout the 5 years after diagnosis (Table 5). Mean annualised EGPA-related days in hospital per patient was 18.79 in the first year after diagnosis and 11.69 over the first 5 years after diagnosis, compared with 6.52 days in the baseline period. EGPA-related annualised in-patient hospitalisations during the first year after EGPA diagnosis (2.52 stays per patient) accounted for most of the all-cause in-patient hospitalisations (2.99 stays per patient for 20.71 days) and accounted for all of the claims for any EGPA medications (5.87 claims) (Tables 3 and 5). Payer Healthcare Costs

The mean total annualised cost of all-cause HCRU per patient with EGPA was €19,700 in the first year after diagnosis, compared with €6678 for the 12-month baseline period (Table 3). The main cost drivers in the baseline period were in-patient hospitalisations (€4082/patient; 61.1% of total annual costs) and pharmacy costs (€1124/patient; 16.8% of total annual costs). In the first year after EGPA diagnosis the main cost drivers continued to be in-patient hospitalisations (€13,165/patient; 66.8% of total annual costs) and pharmacy costs (€3931/patient; 20.0% of total annual costs).

Discussion

This analysis highlights the substantial burden of EGPA in terms of HCRU and associated healthcare costs within a European publicly insured population. All-cause HCRU were significantly higher in the EGPA cohort compared with the general insured population cohort. Consequently, costs for patients with EGPA were greater, with hospitalisations due to EGPA as the primary cost driver, followed by pharmacy costs. These findings are consistent with those of a small number of prior studies conducted in the US and Europe reporting on the high cost and HCRU burden associated with EGPA [5, 11–14]. However, published economic data on EGPA are currently limited to the US [11]; two real-world European studies, one based on medical chart reviews of patients with EGPA from across five European countries [14], and the other using primary care data in England from the Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum database [13], only focused on HCRU for patients with EGPA and did not analyse costs. In the latter study, the annualised mean number of all-cause and EGPA-related in-patient hospitalisations per patient in the year following EGPA diagnosis were lower (1.7 and 1.2, respectively) [13] than those reported in our study (2.9 and 2.5, respectively); these small differences may be attributable in part to differences in the healthcare systems in Germany and England.

In addition to providing both HCRU and all-cause cost data for patients with EGPA in a European country, to our knowledge, our study is the first to report on the impact of EGPA on days off work and EGPA-related costs. In the first 5 years after diagnosis, employed patients with EGPA had an average of 30.74 days off work annually, at an annual cost of productivity loss of €3632 per patient, which was significantly higher than that for the matched general insured population cohort. The annualised EGPA-related cost per patient during the first 5 years after diagnosis was €13,592, with in-patient hospitalisations the single largest driver of EGPA-related costs, accounting for 57% of the total cost of EGPA-related care. These data provide further weight in support of the high HCRU and cost burden associated with EGPA in Europe.

Recently, the management of EGPA has evolved to include eosinophil-targeting biologics such as mepolizumab [18, 19], which is recommended for remission induction/maintenance in non-severe EGPA [8, 9], and remission maintenance in severe EGPA [9]. A second eosinophil-targeting biologic, benralizumab, showed to be non-inferior to mepolizumab in inducing remission in adults with relapsing and/or refractory disease in the Phase 3 MANDARA study [20]. It is approved for use in several countries, including the US [21], the European Union [22], and Japan. Recent data from real-world studies have shown that newer treatments such as biologics are associated with cost benefits in populations with severe eosinophilic asthma owing to reduced rates of hospitalisation and associated HCRU, and a reduction in medical costs [23], as well as reduced costs of medications [24]. More real-world data on patient characteristics, HCRU and related costs in EGPA are needed to help ascertain the full impact of the disease and potential cost benefits of the use of novel biologic treatments. Specifically, an analysis of HCRU and related costs prior to and after the approval of eosinophil-targeting biologics would be a valuable addition to the literature on the evolving treatment landscape for patients with EGPA in the future. It would also be relevant to compare HCRU and related costs of treating and managing patients with EGPA with patients with other types of vasculitis for a more direct disease comparison.

This study was retrospective and had some limitations due to the nature of the analysis. The claims data did not include clinical measurements or test results that could affect the management and consequently the cost of EGPA treatment, and it was difficult to definitively ascertain the date of EGPA onset due to the relapsing and remitting nature of the disease. Moreover, the EGPA cohort was defined using the ICD-10 diagnosis code only, and whether individuals had received treatment for EGPA was not considered, which may have resulted in the inclusion of some misdiagnosed or miscoded patients. Though this was mitigated by the case definition requiring at least two outpatient records of EGPA diagnosis more than three months apart or from different healthcare providers, nevertheless some patients without EGPA may have been included in the cohort, affecting the estimates of the HCRU and cost burden in this study. In addition, in the EGPA cohort, we reported a baseline asthma prevalence of 54.2%, which is somewhat lower than the asthma prevalence of > 80% commonly cited in the literature [1, 3, 13]. The low asthma prevalence is likely attributable to the relatively narrow window used to capture comorbidity data in our study (from 1 January 2015 or start of continuous insurance coverage to EGPA diagnosis), thus comorbidities occurring prior to this date or prior to the start of continuous insurance coverage could not be captured in the claims dataset. This is unlikely to have affected the all-cause costs but may have resulted in an underestimation of the costs for asthma medications reported in our study, although the impact of this would be minimal as most asthma medications are generic and low-cost. Due to the nature of claims data, it was also not possible to determine whether the EGPA events were due to EGPA or other causes; it is possible that some of these events could have been due to a comorbidity, rather than EGPA. Finally, although the dataset was regional, focusing on people in the states of Saxony and Thuringia, these data can be considered to be representative nationally due to the uniform healthcare policies and standardised practices across Germany. With full capture of treatment as relevant for reimbursement purposes, these data reflect the therapeutic approaches and HCRU in routine clinical practice across all healthcare settings in Germany; however, the costs may be unique to Germany and not generalisable to other countries.

Conclusions

The findings of this analysis contribute to the limited existing data on the burden of EGPA in terms of both HCRU and associated costs. These results underscore the necessity for more effective targeted therapies for EGPA to induce and maintain remission and reduce clinical encounters.

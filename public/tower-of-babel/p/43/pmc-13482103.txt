Enhancing the strain limit of stretchable organic transistors by Schottky barrier-dominated transport mode
Authors: Yao Fu, Xinran Zheng, Shuguang Wang, Shougang Sun, Yanpeng Wang, Zhongwu Wang, Yinan Huang, Lin Li, Xiaosong Chen, Hui Yang, Liqiang Li, Wenping Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Stretchable organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) are ideal candidates for wearable technologies, human-machine interfaces, soft robots and implantable devices, but they suffer from mechanical stress concentration, microcracks, and interfacial damage under large geometric variation, which leads to severe performance degradation. To transcend this limitation, we propose an O2 plasma-assisted barrier regulation strategy for the fabrication of the organic source-gated transistor (OSGT). The Schottky barrier dominated transport mode of OSGTs substantially reduces the sensitivity of electrical performance to mechanical strain, thereby enhancing the strain limit of stretchable OFETs under geometric variations. The stretchable OSGTs could maintain a high on/off ratio (106) at 300% strain and the performance has no obvious attenuation. This architectural strategy shifts the focus from material engineering to device design, offering broader prospects for the development of stretchable electronic devices requiring large deformations.

Introduction

Stretchable electronics are promoting a surging demand in various applications owing to their mechanical adaptability and electrical performance1–12. Notably, as the core element of stretchable electronic systems, stretchable organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) are highly attractive due to their light weight, low cost and capability for maintaining intimate tissue contact even with movement and size changes, making them ideal candidates for wearable technologies, human-machine interfaces, soft robots and implantable devices13–21.

The development of high-performance stretchable OFETs fundamentally relies on polymer semiconductors that can preserve excellent charge transport properties under mechanical strain. Substantial research efforts have focused on designing intrinsically stretchable semiconductors through precision molecular engineering, which balances mechanical elasticity with charge transport efficiency by fine-tuning backbone flexibility, side-chain dynamics, and cross-linking density22–26. Moreover, Bao’s group has demonstrated that polymer nanoconfinement can boost stretchability by orders of magnitude while completely preserving the original charge mobility characteristics27–29. Despite these remarkable achievements, a critical challenge remains: current stretchable OFETs exhibit severe performance degradation at larger strain, primarily due to microstructural damage in the semiconductor layer under extreme deformation30,31. This degradation stems from the operating mechanism of OFETs, which relies on efficient charge transport through continuous conjugated pathways in the organic semiconductor. Under large strain, molecular packing and π-π stacking are disrupted, leading to a reduction in charge carrier mobility and detrimental cracks that disrupt conduction paths. This limitation substantially constrains their application in scenarios requiring high deformability, such as dynamic tissue interfaces or highly stretchable electronic skins. Therefore, the balance between mechanical stretchability and device performance is an inherent contradiction of stretchable devices, which may need transistor with distinct working mode to enhance the limitation.

In this work, we propose a universal O2 plasma-assisted barrier regulation strategy for the fabrication of the organic source-gated transistor (OSGT)32–36. The device operates on the principle of a Schottky barrier dominated transporting mode, which effectively alleviates the strain limit of stretchable organic transistors. Owing to their source-dominated working mechanism, the stretchable OSGTs exhibit tolerance to geometric variations37,38, maintaining an on/off ratio of 106 even under a strain of 300%. Moreover, we have successfully obtained a stretchable current stabilizer that operates stably under the 300% stretching strain. By transcending the material-dependent limits of conventional stretchable OFETs, stretchable OSGTs greatly alleviate the inherent trade-off between the electrical performance and the stretchability without the need for material modification, and bring vitality to the development of stretchable electronics. Theoretically, the source-dominated mechanism could ensure stable operation under even larger strains. Our work not only provides a pathway to achieve high-performance stretchable electronics but also opens up broader possibilities for applications requiring robust and reliable operation under continuous mechanical strain.

Results and discussion

Electrical characterization of organic source-gated transistors

OFETs typically exhibit a low contact barrier, and the conducting channel is coupled to the gate electrode capacitance through an insulating medium. Therefore, the gate-source voltage (VGS) controls the conductivity of the channel. Unlike OFETs, the source contact in the OSGT is replaced by a Schottky barrier similar to a diode35,39,40. The OFET current is saturated only at high drain–source voltage (VDS), whereas the OSGT achieves saturation at significantly lower VDS owing to complete depletion of the semiconductor layer by the Schottky source41–44.

A key characteristic of the OSGT is the Schottky barrier at the source contact. P-type small molecule 2,9-didecyldinaphthol [2,3-b:2’,3’-f]thiophene (C10-DNTT) is used as the active layer to construct bottom-gate top-contact (staggered) OSGTs, as shown in Fig. 1b. The OSC was then treated with O2 plasma for different times (Fig. 1a), and the source and drain (Ag) were prepared by thermal evaporation. Based on this strategy, the Schottky barrier is introduced at the interface between the source and the OSC to construct OSGTs (Fig. 1c). Firstly, we conducted Atomic force microscope (AFM) characterization of the morphology and thickness of the C10-DNTT film before and after O2 plasma treatment (Supplementary Fig. 1). It was found that the morphology of the O2 plasma treated C10-DNTT film did not change. The thickness of the O2 plasma treated C10-DNTT film was consistent with the initial 20 nm, which proved that our O2 plasma treatment method was mild. When the OSC is not treated with O2 plasma, the device exhibits typical OFET characteristics (Fig. 1d), the current reaches saturation only at high VDS, and the on/off ratio reaches 108 (Supplementary Fig. 3), with the threshold voltage (VTH) approaching 0 V (Fig. 1h). To further evaluate the electrical impact of O₂ plasma treatment, we extracted the subthreshold swing (SS) from the transfer curves shown in Fig.1h. As summarized in Supplementary Fig.4, the SS increases only from ~3.00 V/dec (untreated OFET) to ~3.16 V/dec (60 s O2 plasma-treated OSGT). This minor change indicates that the plasma treatment does not introduce a significant density of deep traps. This suggests that the plasma-induced modification primarily affects the source contact barrier rather than creating bulk or interface traps. With the increase of O2 plasma treatment time, it can be found from the output curves (Fig. 1e–g) that the saturation voltage (Vsat) gradually decreases and the saturation current (Isat) gradually flattens. As the treatment time increases, the Vsat decreases from the initial ‒60 V to ‒15 V at the VGS of ‒60 V, while the on/off ratio does not change significantly. Notably, the resulting OSGT does not follow the OFET formula (Vsat = VGS – VTH), but low saturation voltage and flat saturation current are the basis of low operating voltage and low power consumption. In recent years, OFETs have faced many problems, such as the inevitable short-channel effect with the reduction of device size45–50, poor operational stability51–54, and incompatibility between flexible device performance and stretchability27,55–57. However, due to the unique working mechanism of OSGTs, the IDS is mainly controlled by the source contact and barrier height, rather than by the channel in the traditional sense, which makes OSGT have many advantages over OFET. As shown in Fig. 1j, when the channel length is shortened to 30 µm and 10 µm, the VTH of the OFET shifts significantly and the off-state current has a significant increase at the same time. However, when the channel size is reduced, there is no severe offset in the VTH and off-state current of the OSGT device. At the same time, the OSGT obtained has good operational stability (Fig. 1k), the continuous bias stress over 8000 s was applied to the OFET and OSGT device. In striking contrast, the drain current of the OFET and OSGT remains at 38% and 96% of the initial value. To further assess long-term stability and to verify that the plasma treatment does not introduce charge-trapping centers, we extended the bias stress to 25,000 s under the same conditions. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 5, the OSGT retains 86% of its initial drain current after 25,000 s, with no significant shift in threshold voltage and no degradation in subthreshold swing. In contrast, the untreated OFET retains only 12% of its initial current and exhibits a large VTH shift of approximately −15 V. These results demonstrate that the O₂ plasma treatment does not introduce a high density of charge-trapping centers, and that the Schottky-barrier-dominated transport mode provides long-term bias stability compared to the conventional OFET. Furthermore, we demonstrate the necessity of our O2 plasma-assisted barrier regulation strategy for constructing OSGT devices by fabricating asymmetric electrodes (Supplementary Figs. 6–8). Firstly, the C10-DNTT film was treated without O2 plasma, and then the asymmetric electrode Au-Ag was prepared. The output curve showed typical OFET characteristics when Au or Ag was the source electrode and the other end was the drain electrode. When the C10-DNTT film is treated with O2 plasma at the optimal time, the output curve has obvious OSGT characteristics when the source electrode is Ag, while the output curve does not have the characteristic of saturation under low VDS when the source electrode is Au. Therefore, the important characteristics of the source electrode in OSGT are verified. At the same time, we prepared the (5 nm Ag+15 nm Au)−20 nm Au asymmetric electrode device, and also found that OSGT was formed only when the C10-DNTT film was treated by O2 plasma and (5 nm Ag+15 nm Au) was the source electrode. However, it has also been verified that after O2-plasma treatment of OSCs, only 5 nm Ag can form OSGT.

The mechanism of C10-DNTT-Ag OSGT

The formation of a Schottky contact at the source electrode is the foundational condition for realizing OSGTs and establishing the Schottky barrier dominated transport mode crucial for enhancing the strain limit. In order to study the effective Schottky barrier (ΦSB) of the OFET and OSGT, the transfer curves (Fig. 2a,d) and output curves (Supplementary Figs. 9–10) were measured in the temperature range of 320 K- 350 K. The increase in ΦSB is attributed to the surface chemical modification induced by O₂ plasma treatment. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 11, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements reveal the appearance of S–O bonds in the S2p spectrum after plasma treatment. Importantly, depth-profiling XPS demonstrates that these S–O species are only within the semiconductor surface and disappear after a mild sputter etch, leaving only the intrinsic S–C bonds of the pristine semiconductor. This confirms that the O2 plasma treatment is strictly surface-localized and does not penetrate into the bulk. Consequently, the bulk π-π stacking and charge transport pathways remain intact. Furthermore, these S–O bonds remain chemically stable after storage of the plasma-treated film in ambient conditions for 10 days (Supplementary Fig. 12), and correspondingly the ΦSB remains stable after one week of storage, with extracted values of 0.71 eV (fresh) and 0.70 eV (after one week) (Supplementary Fig. 13). Subsequently, we prepared a series of electrodes (E1-E6) to verify this characteristic of OSGT (Fig. 2g and Supplementary Fig. 14). For E1-E3 devices, the size of the source electrode is kept unchanged, and change the size of the drain electrode, so that the channel length is unchanged (200 µm), the length of the source (LS) is 1000 µm and the length of the drain (LD) is 1000 µm, 500 µm and 250 µm, respectively. For OSGT devices, the IDS remained almost unchanged with the increase of the LS/LD ratio. For E4-E6 devices (Supplementary Fig. 14), keep the size of the drain electrode unchanged, change the size of the source electrode, so that the channel length is unchanged (200 µm), the LD is 1000 µm and the LS is 1000 µm, 500 µm and 250 µm, respectively. In this case, for OSGT devices and OFET devices (Fig. 2i and Supplementary Figs. 15–16), the IDS all show a regular decrease as the ratio of LD/LS increases. Based on the above experimental phenomena, it is found that because of the unique source control mechanism of OSGT, changing the drain electrode will not affect the IDS when the source electrode is kept unchanged, which makes OSGTs have greater tolerance to geometric variations, making it promising in the field of flexible electronics.

Preparation and electrical properties of stretchable OSGT and OSGT arrays

Based on the above results, we applied the OSGT with tolerance to geometric variations to the stretchable system and explored its potential in stretchable devices.

To confirm that the O2 plasma treatment induces the same source-barrier effect in the polymer semiconductor indacenodithiophene-co-benzothiadiazole (IDTBT) as observed in C10-DNTT, we performed parallel experiments on rigid IDTBT/Ag devices (Si/SiO2 substrate) under identical plasma conditions. As shown in Supplementary Figs.S17–19, the treated IDTBT devices exhibit low-saturation output characteristics (Supplementary Fig.S17), source-controlled transport with IDS independence of drain electrode length (Supplementary Figs.S18) and clear evidence of S–O species in XPS (Supplementary Fig.S19). These results confirm that the OSGT mechanism is fully transferable to the polymer semiconductor IDTBT, justifying its use in the stretchable devices described below.

Since we demonstrated the importance of Ag as the source electrode in the construction of OSGT, and we needed it to be compatible with stretchable devices, we prepared 5 nm Ag using thermal evaporation deposition based on the sprayed single-wall carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) and then transferred it to the O2 plasma treated IDTBT, as shown in Fig. 3a–b. The photographs of the stretchable OSGT in its initial state and under 300% strain are shown in Fig. 3c. The sheet resistance of three different electrodes, SWCNTs, evaporated Ag and Ag@SWCNTs, was tested (Supplementary Fig. 20). The Ag@SWCNTs showed a lower sheet resistance (6424 Ω sq−1). Moreover, under the strain, the Ag@SWCNTs showed a small change in sheet resistance (ΔR/Ro) to prove its application potential in stretchable devices (Supplementary Fig. 21). The OSGT based on SWCNTs, evaporated Ag and Ag@SWCNTs were prepared. By exploring their electrical properties (Supplementary Fig. 22), it was found that electrodes based on SWCNTs and Ag@SWCNTs had similar properties. To explore the potential of OSGT as a device structure in stretchable systems, we fabricated fully stretchable OSGTs in a top-contact and bottom-gate structure with Ag@SWCNTs electrodes, IDTBT stretchable semiconductor, and a styrene-ethylene-butylene-styrene (SEBS) dielectric. Similarly, we also obtained the stretchable OSGT with optimal performance by adjusting the O2 plasma treatment time. The output characteristic (Supplementary Fig. 23) curves show that when the IDTBT film was not treated with O2 plasma, it exhibited clear stretchable OFET characteristics, and IDS increased with the increase of VDS. However, under the optimal O2 plasma treatment time, IDS saturated at lower VDS, and it is found that the on/off ratio and VTH of the device have no obvious changes after the O2 plasma treatment through the transfer characteristic curves (Supplementary Fig. 24). To further confirm the formation of a Schottky-barrier-dominated transport mode, we performed temperature-dependent electrical measurements on stretchable IDTBT/Ag-SWCNT/SEBS OSGT and stretchable IDTBT/Ag-SWCNT/SEBS OFET (Supplementary Fig. 25). The results clearly demonstrate that the ΦSB of stretchable OSGT (0.93 eV) is obviously higher than that of the stretchable OFET (0.63 eV). This significant increase provides direct evidence that the O₂ plasma treatment effectively introduces a high ΦSB, thereby enabling the source-gated transistor operation in IDTBT.

When the stretchable OFET was stretched parallel to the charge transport direction, the transfer characteristic curves in Fig. 3d that the on-state current (Supplementary Fig. 26) and on/off ratio (Fig. 3g) decreased greatly and VTH drifted with the increase of the strain. The on/off ratio changed from the initial 108 to 103 under 300% tensile strain. This shows that for stretchable OFET devices, there is a natural incompatibility between the electrical properties of the device and the stretchability. For stretchable OSGT devices, when stretched parallel to the charge transport direction and perpendicular to the charge transport direction (Fig. 3e–f), the electrical performance of the devices decreases less with the increase of the tensile strain, and the on-state current (Supplementary Fig. 26) of the devices changes relatively little, even under the tensile strain of up to 300%, the on/off ratio can be as high as 106 (Fig. 3g). To quantitatively verify that the source barrier remains the dominant resistance component under strain, we performed transfer length method (TLM) measurements on stretchable IDTBT OSGTs. As detailed in Supplementary Figs.27–28, the Rc·W is consistently significantly higher than the Rch·W. Even at 300% strain, due to geometric and morphological changes, both Rc·W and Rch·W increased substantially, but Rc·W remained larger than Rch·W. This quantitatively indicates that the source barrier is always the limiting factor for current throughout the entire tensile range. Consequently, the OSGT maintains a competitive on/off ratio of 106 under 300% strain compared to previously reported stretchable transistors (Fig. 3h and Supplementary Table1). We believe that owing to the unique working mechanism, the OSGT can theoretically maintain stable electrical properties under greater tensile strain.

The mechanical reliability and low-voltage capability of individual stretchable OSGTs are further demonstrated in Supplementary Figs.29–31. The device operates at a low voltage of −10 V with clear transistor characteristics, confirming that its low saturation voltage enables practical low-voltage operation. We also examined the recovery behavior by comparing the transfer characteristics before stretching (initial), after release from 100% strain, and after release from 300% strain (Supplementary Fig.30). After release from 100% strain, the device retains nearly identical transistor characteristics to the initial state, demonstrating that the OSGT mechanism is highly robust under moderate deformation and fully recovers. After release from 300% strain, the device still exhibits clear gate modulation and a distinguishable on/off ratio, but some degradation in on-current and a slight shift in threshold voltage are observed. This irreversible degradation likely originates from morphological damage to the polymer semiconductor or interfaces under extreme strain, rather than from a failure of the source-dominated transport mechanism. Cyclic stretching at 25% strain for 10,000 cycles causes only moderate on-current variation, showing mechanical robustness under repeated loading. These results extend the evaluation from quasi-static stretching to low-voltage, recovery, and cyclic conditions.

Based on the above experimental results, we verified that the OSGT device has good tolerance to geometric variations. Then, we prepared a set of patterns with unchanged source electrodes and various shapes of drain electrodes (Supplementary Fig. 32). The electrical properties of this set of devices are similar, and the on-state current and the on/off ratio are almost unchanged. This provides the ability to control the electrical properties of the profiled electrode devices.

Then we prepared a current stabilizer based on the stretchable OSGT (Supplementary Fig. 33a), which avoids the influence of input voltage fluctuations on output current changes. Even under the fluctuating input voltage of ‒30 V, ‒40 V and ‒60 V, stretchable OSGT can have a stable output current compared with OFET because of its flat saturation current (Fig. 4c). The patterned organic semiconductor in the array is achieved by high power O2 plasma etching, and the patterned semiconductor array is shown in Supplementary Fig. 35. We then examined the transfer characteristics to assess the uniformity of VTH and on-state current. As shown in Supplementary Fig.38, all the stretchable OSGTs performed well. The values of the VTH and on-state current of the 100 OSGTs in the array are mapped as shown in Supplementary Fig. 39 and the results show that the OSGTs in the array exhibited low performance variability. To verify the OSGT array’s capability for tolerance to geometric variations, the fully stretchable OSGT array was subjected to various mechanical deformations, including pressing, twisting, and poking with pointed objects (Fig. 4d–f). Notably, the stretchable OSGT array showed no severe decrease in electrical properties, confirming its excellent tolerance to geometric variations (Fig. 4g).

We further evaluated the array-level performance under tensile strain. As shown in Supplementary Fig.40, the stretchable OSGT array maintains clear gate modulation up to 300% strain in both stretching directions, and its actual on/off ratio does not show significant attenuation. Finally, to demonstrate operation under dynamic deformation relevant to wearable electronics, the array was attached onto a finger and the drain current was monitored during repeated finger motion. As shown in Supplementary Fig.41, the device exhibits repeatable on/off switching, confirming its applicability under body-motion conditions.

Despite these advantages for stretchable and low-frequency wearable applications, it is important to acknowledge the inherent trade-offs of the OSGT architecture. While the high source barrier inevitably reduces the on-current and limits the high-frequency operation (e.g., MHz-range switching or RF circuits), it simultaneously provides low saturation voltage, flat output characteristics, and high strain tolerance. Therefore, the stretchable OSGTs are best suited for low-speed, low-power, and high-stability applications such as wearable biomedical sensors, human-machine interfaces, and soft robotic skins. For potential high-speed flexible circuits, hybrid integration with conventional OFETs could be explored in future work.

In conclusion, this work breaks through the traditional material-centric paradigm and establishes OSGTs with a Schottky-barrier-dominated transport mode. As a result, the degradation of electrical performance under mechanical strain is significantly suppressed compared with conventional OFETs, demonstrating a reduced strain sensitivity that enhances the practical strain limit of stretchable organic devices. The source-dominated mechanism enables stretchable OSGTs to exhibit low saturation voltage, low power consumption, good bias stress stability, and high tolerance to both geometric variations and short-channel effects. Under 300% tensile strain, it still has an on/off ratio of 106, and the device performance has no obvious attenuation. Furthermore, we have achieved a stretchable current stabilizer, which can maintain a stable and constant output current when the tensile strain reaches 300%. This strategy opens up a development dimension for the application of stretchable electronics in intelligent sensing, stretchable organic integrated circuits, human-interactive wearable systems and other fields.

Methods

Materials and instruments

C10-DNTT was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Polymer semiconductor IDTBT was purchased from Solarmer Beijing Inc. Elastomer SEBS was supplied by Asahi Kasei company, with SEBS H1062 specifically employed as the elastomer substrate and SEBS H1052 utilized as the dielectric layer in the fabrication of stretchable OFETs and OSGTs. The dispersed SWCNTs solution for electrodes was purchased from Chengdu Zhongke Times Nanomaterials Co., Ltd. The electrical characterizations of OFETs and OSGTs were performed by an Agilent B1500A and a PDA FS380 with the assistance of a probe station system in dark conditions. The atomic force microscope (AFM) was measured by Bruker Dimension ICON-PT in the air. Optical images were measured by VK-X1050 3D laser confocal microscopy. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed by PHI 5000 Versaprobe.

Device fabrication and characterization

The OFETs and OSGTs were fabricated on silicon substrates. The substrates consist of a highly doped Si wafer (gate electrode) and a 300 nm thermally oxidized SiO2 layer (dielectric layer). Then, the Si wafers were modified with OTS in a vacuum oven for 1 h at 120 °C. The C10-DNTT film (20 nm) was deposited by vacuum thermal evaporation at 0.05 Å s−1 under 10−4 Pa. The C10-DNTT film was then treated with O2 plasma using a Diener Femto-SLS system (power range 0–100 W). The optimized treatment conditions were: RF power = 30 W, chamber pressure = 0.38 mbar, O2 flow rate = 40 sccm, duration = 60 s, at room temperature. Subsequently, Ag electrodes (20 nm) were thermally deposited onto the substrate as the source and drain electrodes in a vacuum at a rate of 0.1 Å s−1.

All stretchable functional layers were prefabricated on the Si/SiO2 wafers substrates and transferred layer by layer onto SEBS substrate to prepare stretchable OSGTs and OFETs. The substrate layers were prepared on the Si/SiO2 wafers by dropping the SEBS-H1062 solution (200 mg/ml in toluene), followed by annealing at 40 °C for 5 h. The gate electrodes were fabricated by spray-coating SWCNTs using a spray gun. The SEBS solution(H1052, 60 mg/ml in toluene) was spin-coated on the OTS-treated Si/SiO2 surface at 1000 r.p.m., to form a stretchable dielectric film. IDTBT was dissolved in anhydrous chlorobenzene (5 mg mL−1, 60 °C, 12 h). The IDTBT film was prepared on an OTS-treated Si/SiO2 substrate using the solution shearing method (0.51 mm/s at 70 °C). Then, the semiconductor film was baked on a hot plate (120 °C) for 10 min in a nitrogen atmosphere to remove the residual solvent. After the transfer, the semiconductor film of the stretchable OSGT was treated with O₂ plasma under the same conditions (Diener Femto-SLS, 30 W, 0.38 mbar, 40 sccm, 60 s, room temperature). The source and drain (S/D) electrodes were fabricated by spray-coating SWCNTs using a spray gun through a metal mask plate. After that, 5 nm-thick Ag electrode was thermally evaporated (0.1 Å s−1) to form SWCNT@Ag electrodes for stretchable OSGT.

Reproductive Outcomes Following High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound Treatment for Cesarean Scar Pregnancy
Authors: Richao Liang, Wenwei Pan, Guizhen Lyu, Ming Chen, Li Wen, Tingting Chen, Yikang Wang, Rongju Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveCesarean scar pregnancy (CSP) is a rare but potentially life-threatening form of ectopic pregnancy associated with uterine rupture and severe hemorrhage. Optimizing fertility-preserving treatment strategies remains a critical clinical challenge. To investigate the reproductive outcomes and associated risk factors in patients with CSP after conservative surgery utilizing High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (HIFU).MethodsIn this retrospective cohort study (July 2017-June 2020), 337 CSP patients were stratified into HIFU group (n=210) or the non-HIFU group (n=127). Multivariate logistic regression identified predictors of re-pregnancy and recurrent cesarean scar pregnancy (RCSP).ResultsThe re-pregnancy rate was significantly higher in the HIFU group (61.9%, 130/210) than in the non-HIFU group (38.6%, 49/127) (P<0.001). Multivariate analysis identified HIFU treatment (OR=2.728, P<0.0001) and an interval >3 years since the last cesarean section (OR=5.497, P=0.039) as significant predictors of higher re-pregnancy rates. Conversely, advanced age (OR=0.923/year, P=0.003), >3 cesarean sections (OR=0.255, P=0.037), and >3 induced abortions (OR=0.245, P=0.008) were associated with lower rates. Among the 179 patients who achieved pregnancy, RCSP incidence was significantly lower in the HIFU group (21.5%, 28/130) than in the non-HIFU group (42.9%, 21/49) (P=0.004). Multivariate analysis showed HIFU treatment reduced RCSP odds by 67.7% (OR=0.323, P=0.004), with thicker scars further lowering risk by 33.7% (OR=0.663/mm, P=0.021). Prior cesarean sections significantly increased RCSP risk (vs. 1 section: 2 sections OR=2.460, P=0.017; 3 sections OR=13.089, P=0.033; >3 sections OR=11.288, P=0.049).ConclusionConservative surgery incorporating HIFU significantly improves re-pregnancy rates and reduces RCSP risk in CSP patients. Younger age, longer intervals since the last cesarean section, and fewer cesarean sections or abortions correlate with higher re-pregnancy rates. Thicker scar tissue and fewer cesarean sections are protective against RCSP. These findings warrant validation in larger prospective studies.

Introduction

Cesarean scar pregnancy is a rare but increasingly recognized type of ectopic pregnancy, with an incidence estimated at 1 in 1800 to 1 in 2500 pregnancies, though some centers report higher rates due to enhanced detection and the rising frequency of cesarean deliveries.1,2 CSP is characterized by the implantation of a gestational sac within the myometrial defect created by a prior cesarean section, typically diagnosed before 12 weeks gestation. This condition poses significant risks, including uterine rupture, massive hemorrhage, and maternal morbidity, particularly when not identified and managed promptly.3,4 As the global rate of cesarean sections continues to rise currently exceeding 30% in many countries, with projections for continued increases CSP is emerging as a pressing reproductive health challenge, especially for women desiring future fertility.5 In addition to CSP, prior cesarean delivery is strongly associated with placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) disorders, including placenta accreta, increta, and percreta, which arise from defective decidualization at the scar site and are major causes of severe obstetric hemorrhage and maternal morbidity.6 Preventive strategies focus on reducing unnecessary primary cesarean sections, optimizing uterine closure techniques, ensuring adequate interpregnancy intervals, and early first-trimester ultrasound assessment of scar integrity and placental implantation in subsequent pregnancies.7,8 Management of CSP aims to terminate the ectopic pregnancy while preserving the integrity of the uterus and minimizing long-term reproductive damage. Various treatment options are available, including ultrasound-guided curettage, hysteroscopic or laparoscopic resection, and scar repair, as well as pharmacologic approaches like systemic or local methotrexate and minimally invasive techniques such as sclerotherapy and uterine artery embolization (UAE).3,9 However, pharmacological treatments alone, particularly methotrexate, demonstrate lower success rates and prolonged β-hCG normalization times, making conservative surgical options the preferred choice for women seeking fertility preservation. High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (HIFU) has emerged as a promising non-invasive ablative technique for CSP management. This technique uses focused ultrasonic waves (60–100 W/cm2) to induce localized thermal coagulation and necrosis of the gestational tissue while minimizing collateral damage to surrounding myometrial and vascular structures.9–11 Several studies have reported success rates of over 90% for CSP treatment with HIFU, combined with suction curettage, noting significant benefits such as reduced intraoperative blood loss, fewer complications, and quicker postoperative recovery when compared to other conservative approaches like UAE or conventional surgery.12 Despite these promising outcomes, evidence regarding subsequent reproductive outcomes, particularly the risk of RCSP, remains scarce and inconsistent in the literature. Patients with a history of CSP face an increased risk of RCSP ranging from 5% to 30%, which is notably higher than the general obstetric population’s risk of subsequent ectopic pregnancy.13–15 Although several studies have assessed reproductive outcomes following CSP treatments, the majority have small sample sizes (often <100 cases) and fail to distinguish between different treatment modalities in their evaluation of re-pregnancy rates or RCSP risk. Additionally, significant regional variations in CSP management and heterogeneity in treatment selection criteria have contributed to inconsistent findings regarding the predictive factors for successful pregnancy outcomes and RCSP recurrence.15

To address these critical gaps in the literature, we conducted a retrospective cohort study involving 337 CSP patients treated at a single centre between 2017 and 2020. This cohort represents one of the largest datasets on CSP treatment outcomes to date. Our study is the first to directly compare reproductive outcomes, specifically re-pregnancy rates and RCSP incidence, between patients treated with HIFU and those receiving non-HIFU conservative surgical approaches. In our institution, treatment modality was determined based on clinical evaluation, including gestational age, serum β-hCG levels, gestational sac size and vascularity, residual myometrial thickness at the cesarean scar site, hemodynamic stability, and the patient’s fertility preferences. Furthermore, we conducted an in-depth analysis of preoperative clinical characteristics and treatment-related variables to identify independent predictors of successful re-pregnancy and RCSP recurrence. Through a comprehensive examination of the reproductive impact of HIFU and the identification of modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors for RCSP, this study provides valuable, clinically actionable evidence to guide individualized fertility counselling and post-treatment management strategies for women diagnosed with CSP.

Materials and Methods

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at Dongguan Maternal and Child Health Hospital in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration and was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Dongguan Maternal and Child Health Hospital (Approval Number: 202108). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study.

Study Design and Participants

This study involved patients diagnosed with CSP who underwent conservative surgical treatment in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology at Dongguan Maternal and Child Health Hospital between July 2017 and June 2020. The study period (July 2017–June 2020) was selected to ensure that all enrolled patients had sufficient follow-up time to evaluate subsequent reproductive outcomes, including re-pregnancy and recurrent cesarean scar pregnancy (RCSP). A total of 337 patients were included in the analysis. Inclusion criteria required patients to be aged >18 years, have a history of at least one cesarean section, show a positive serum β-human chorionic gonadotropin (β-hCG) test, and have a confirmed diagnosis of CSP via transvaginal ultrasound, according to the 2016 Chinese Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis and Treatment of Cesarean Scar Pregnancy.16 Treatment modality was determined according to institutional clinical protocols and individualized patient assessment. Patients who were hemodynamically stable, with early gestational age (generally ≤10–12 weeks), relatively preserved myometrial thickness at the cesarean scar site, and without contraindications to ultrasound-guided ablation were considered candidates for HIFU followed by suction curettage. Patients with larger gestational sacs, significant scar vascularization, contraindications to HIFU, or clinician-determined surgical indications underwent alternative conservative treatments, including ultrasound-guided curettage, hysteroscopic or laparoscopic resection, or uterine artery embolization when appropriate. Final treatment decisions were made after multidisciplinary consultation and discussion with the patient regarding fertility preservation and treatment risks. Patients were excluded if they had a gestational age >12 weeks, unstable vital signs, significant medical history (trophoblastic disease, cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disorders, chronic endometritis), or other ectopic pregnancies (cervical and tubal pregnancy). All patients were routinely followed after CSP treatment through outpatient visits and telephone interviews. Patients were generally advised to avoid pregnancy for at least 3–6 months after treatment to allow adequate uterine scar healing. Follow-up continued for up to 24 months after treatment to monitor reproductive outcomes. During follow-up, patients were asked about their intention to conceive, the timing of pregnancy attempts, and pregnancy outcomes. Time to conception was defined as the interval between the initiation of pregnancy attempts and confirmation of pregnancy. Infertility was considered if a patient reported attempting pregnancy for more than 12 months without success. Pregnancy outcomes were categorized as normal intrauterine pregnancy, recurrent cesarean scar pregnancy (RCSP), ectopic pregnancy other than CSP, or spontaneous abortion based on clinical and ultrasound confirmation.

CSP Classification Criteria

CSP was classified into three types according to the 2016 Chinese Expert Consensus:

Type I: A gestational sac partially implanted into the cesarean scar, with a myometrial thickness >3 mm between the sac and bladder. Colour Doppler Flow Imaging (CDFI) reveals low-resistance vascular signals.

Type II: Similar to Type I but with a myometrial thickness ≤3 mm.

Type III: A gestational sac completely implanted within the scar, protruding toward the bladder, with myometrial thickness ≤3 mm and empty uterine cavity and cervical canal. CDFI again shows low-resistance blood flow.

HIFU Group

Patients in the HIFU group underwent the following protocol:

Preoperative Preparation: Patients began a liquid diet one day prior to treatment and fasted for 10 hours before the procedure. The surgical site was prepared with hair removal and degreasing.

HIFU Procedure: Treatment was performed using the PRO HIFU 2008 system (Shenzhen Pro Medical Co., Ltd.; Registration No. 20163231424). The system settings included a 50 Hz frequency, loading time ≤0.5 seconds, interval time ≥0.1 seconds, and a 220 VAC power supply. Under real-time ultrasound guidance, the transducer focal point was positioned at the base of the gestational sac for thermal ablation.

Post-HIFU Management: Within 24 hours of HIFU treatment, patients underwent either suction curettage or hysteroscopic resection.

Non-HIFU Group

Patients in the non-HIFU group were treated with one of the following approaches:

Curettage Alone: Performed under sterile conditions in the lithotomy position. Ultrasound-guided negative pressure aspiration (300–400 mmHg) was used, and postoperatively, ultrasound confirmed clearance of abnormal tissue.

Methotrexate (MTX) + Curettage: Intramuscular MTX (50 mg/m2) was administered, and β-hCG levels were measured after 3 days. If β-hCG decreased by >30%, ultrasound-guided curettage was performed. If the decline was <15%, a second dose of MTX was given before curettage under non-intubated general anesthesia.

Sclerotherapy + Curettage: A 21G needle was used to inject polidocanol (10–20 mg) into hyper vascular perigestational areas. Curettage was performed within 24 hours post-injection.

Hysteroscopic Resection: Performed under non-intubated general anaesthesia. Hysteroscopy was used to locate the sac and guide the negative pressure aspiration followed by resection of adherent tissue with an electrocautery loop.

Data Collection

Demographic and clinical data were retrospectively collected from medical records. Variables recorded included age, number of prior cesarean sections, interval since last cesarean section, history of induced abortions, scar thickness, gestational sac diameter, preoperative β-hCG levels, CSP classification, surgical method, and intraoperative blood loss. Follow-up data were obtained via outpatient visits and telephone interviews. The subsequent reproductive outcomes were recorded, including RCSP, normal intrauterine pregnancy, ectopic pregnancy, and spontaneous abortion. It was noted that the desire for fertility may change over time, and patients who initially expressed no desire for future pregnancies may later become pregnant due to changes in reproductive plans.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version 29.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test, with normally distributed data presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and non-normally distributed data summarized as median with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages (n, %). Between-group comparisons were performed using independent samples t-tests for normally distributed continuous variables, Mann–Whitney U-tests for non-normally distributed variables, and chi-square (χ2) or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables. Binary logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify independent predictors of re-pregnancy and RCSP, with variables demonstrating p < 0.10 in univariate analysis entered into multivariate modeling using a forward stepwise likelihood ratio approach. Model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, and results were reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For statistical reporting, p values are presented in lowercase italics. Values <0.001 are reported as p < 0.001, and other p values are presented to three decimal places to ensure consistency.

Results

Demographic Characteristics and Clinical Features

A total of 337 patients were included in this study, of which 210 (62.3%) patients were divided into the HIFU group and the rest 127 (37.7%) in the non-HIFU group. All patients were followed up for a minimum period of 24 months. The mean age in the HIFU group was 33.8 ± 4.6 years, significantly higher than the 32.7 ± 4.3 years in the non-HIFU group (P = 0.028). The distribution of CSP types differed significantly (P = 0.002). Type I CSP predominated in the HIFU group (87.6%, n=184) compared to the non-HIFU group (72.4%, n=92). Conversely, Type III CSP was less frequent in the HIFU group (1.9%, n=4) than in the non-HIFU group (5.5%, n=7). No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups regarding BMI, days since last menstruation, gestational sac diameter, scar thickness, serum β-hCG levels, number of cesarean sections, time since the last cesarean section, and number of induced abortions (all P > 0.05). Factors Associated with Re-Pregnancy After Conservative CSP Surgery

During the follow-up period, successful and failed re-pregnancy were seen in 130/210 (61.9%) and 80/210 (38.1%) patients in the HIFU group vs. 49/127 (38.6%) and 78/127 (61.4%) patients in the non-HIFU group (P < 0.01). Univariate regression analysis was conducted to identify demographic and clinical factors associated with re-pregnancy following conservative surgery for CSP. The results revealed that surgical method, number of cesarean sections, time since the last cesarean section, number of induced abortions, and age were significantly different between patients who achieved re-pregnancy and those who did not (all P < 0.05). Further multivariate regression analysis was performed incorporating these five significant factors. The results indicated that the use of HIFU treatment and a longer interval since the last cesarean section were significantly associated with higher postoperative re-pregnancy rates. Compared to the non-HIFU group, the HIFU group exhibited a 1.7-fold increase in the re-pregnancy rate (OR = 2.728, P < 0.0001). Similarly, a longer interval since the last cesarean section was associated with a higher likelihood of re-pregnancy. Notably, patients who had an interval exceeding 3 years since their last cesarean section were 4.5 times more likely to achieve re-pregnancy compared to those with an interval of less than 1 year (OR = 5.497, P = 0.039). In contrast, advanced age, a history of more than three cesarean sections or induced abortions were identified as significant risk factors that may decrease the likelihood of re-pregnancy following surgery. Specifically, for each additional year of age, the odds of re-pregnancy decreased by 7.7% (OR = 0.923, P = 0.003). Compared to patients with only one cesarean section, those with more than three cesarean sections had a 74.5% lower re-pregnancy rate (OR = 0.255, P = 0.037). Similarly, compared to patients with a single induced abortion, those with more than three induced abortions experienced a 75.5% reduction in the re-pregnancy rate (OR = 0.245, P=0.008). Factors Associated with RCSP After Conservative CSP Surgery

Among the 179 patients who successfully achieved re-pregnancy, the HIFU group saw 82 cases of intrauterine pregnancies, 2 cases of ectopic pregnancies, 18 cases of spontaneous abortions, and 28 cases of RCSP. In comparison, the non-HIFU group had 22 cases of intrauterine pregnancies, 1 case of an ectopic pregnancy, 5 cases of spontaneous abortions, and 21 cases of RCSP. The reproductive outcomes were shown in Figure 1. The incidence rate of RCSP in the HIFU group was 21.5% (28/130), significantly lower than the 42.9% (21/49) observed in the non-HIFU group, with a statistically significant difference (p = 0.004). Patients were categorized into two groups based on whether they experienced RCSP: the RCSP group and the non-RCSP group. Univariate analysis of demographic and clinical characteristics revealed that surgical method, number of cesarean sections, and scar thickness were significant factors influencing the occurrence of RCSP. Further multivariate regression analysis demonstrated that HIFU treatment emerged as a protective factor against RCSP. HIFU treatment was associated with a 67.7% reduction in the odds of RCSP occurrence (OR = 0.323, P = 0.004). Thicker scar tissue was negatively correlated with the occurrence of RCSP. For each additional millimeter of scar thickness, the odds of RCSP decreased by 33.7% (OR = 0.663, P = 0.021). This suggests that thicker scars may reduce the risk of RCSP. Conversely, a higher number of cesarean sections was associated with an increased incidence of RCSP. Compared to one cesarean section, two cesarean sections increased the odds of RCSP by 1.5 times (OR = 2.460, P = 0.017). Three cesarean sections increased the odds by 12.1 times (OR = 13.089, P = 0.033). More than three cesarean sections increased the odds by 10.3 times (OR = 11.288, P = 0.049). Discussion

Cesarean scar pregnancy is a unique form of ectopic pregnancy characterized by the implantation of the embryo within the uterine scar resulting from a previous cesarean section. With rising global cesarean section rates, the incidence of CSP is increasing, making its management and long-term reproductive outcomes an important clinical concern.17,18 The optimal management for CSP remains unclear, with treatment approaches varying widely across medical centers based on experience.19 As a result, current data on subsequent reproductive outcomes in CSP patients treated conservatively have not been well described. Studies reported re-pregnancy rate ranging from 34.94% to 88%, with the incidence of recurrent cesarean scar pregnancy (RCSP) varying from 5% to 30%.20–22 The large variation between these studies can be attributed to diverse treatment modalities and a relatively small sample size for subsequent pregnancies. Importantly, most prior studies have not simultaneously evaluated both treatment modality and patient-related risk factors, limiting their ability to provide clinically actionable guidance. A recent study identified the number of previous cesarean sections as a significant risk factor for CSP recurrence,23 another study found no significant association between different treatments and RCSP incidence,14 while all other studies only reported the subsequent reproductive outcomes without analyzing the associated risk factors.20,24,25 We here reported a 53.1% (179/337) overall re-pregnancy rate and a 27.4% (49/179) of RCSP rate in CSP patients treated with conservative surgeries, which is similar to other studies. Notably, subgroup analysis demonstrated significantly higher re-pregnancy rates (61.9%) and lower RCSP incidence (21.5%) in the HIFU group compared with the non-HIFU group, supporting a potential benefit of HIFU-based conservative treatment. Compared to other conservative surgeries, HIFU stands out due to its high selectivity and non-invasive nature. The precise localization of ultrasound waves to the lesion tissue minimizes damage to surrounding normal tissues, thereby protecting healthy uterine structures. Moreover, HIFU avoids direct cutting or mechanical injury to the uterine wall, preserving uterine blood supply. Yuan et al Found that the combination of HIFU with ultrasound-guided curettage is a safe and effective strategy for treating Types I and II CSP, particularly beneficial for patients desiring future fertility.26 Several studies have reported that HIFU achieves comparable efficacy to uterine artery embolization (UAE) in treating CSP, with HIFU demonstrating superior outcomes in avoiding postoperative complications and showing more immediate benefits, notably accelerating postoperative recovery.15,27–29 Given these characteristics, HIFU is theoretically expected to improve post-treatment pregnancy outcomes in CSP patients. However, research in this area remains limited. Zhang et al assessed the subsequent reproductive outcomes in CSP patients treated with HIFU combined with ultrasound-guided dilation or curettage. After treatment, 23 patients (82.14%) successfully conceived, of whom 18 (78.26%) had intrauterine pregnancies.30 However, this study did not compare HIFU with other conservative surgical treatments. Chen et al13 and Wang et al31 compared subsequent pregnancy outcomes between HIFU and UAE for CSP. Both studies indicated that no significant difference in re-pregnancy rates between the two treatments, but found that HIFU was superior to UAE in reducing the incidence of RCSP. Conversely, Tang et al concluded that HIFU did not significantly help in reducing the incidence of RCSP.12 These discrepancies likely reflect differences in study design, patient selection, and treatment protocols, underscoring the need for larger comparative analyses.

A key strength of this study is the comprehensive evaluation of both treatment modality and clinical risk factors influencing reproductive outcomes. Our multivariate analysis identified several independent predictors of re-pregnancy. Younger maternal age was associated with higher fertility potential, consistent with established reproductive biology. A longer interval since the last cesarean section (>3 years) was also associated with improved outcomes, likely reflecting more complete uterine scar remodeling. Conversely, multiple prior cesarean sections and repeated induced abortions were associated with reduced re-pregnancy rates, potentially due to cumulative endometrial and myometrial damage. Importantly, we also identified factors associated with RCSP recurrence. Thicker residual myometrial scar tissue was protective, suggesting that adequate scar healing plays a critical role in preventing abnormal implantation. In contrast, an increasing number of prior cesarean sections markedly elevated RCSP risk, supporting the hypothesis that repeated uterine injury predisposes to defective implantation sites. However, interpretation of these findings should consider potential baseline differences between treatment groups. Although most demographic and clinical variables were comparable, differences in CSP type distribution and clinical selection criteria may have influenced treatment allocation. Given the non-randomized design, residual confounding cannot be excluded, and the observed benefits of HIFU should be interpreted with caution.

While our study provides valuable insights into the reproductive outcomes and risk factors associated with CSP, it has several limitations. First, the retrospective nature of the study may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the sample size, although substantial, may not be representative of all patient populations. Third, the follow-up period of at least 24 months is adequate for assessing short-term outcomes but may not capture long-term reproductive health impacts. Therefore, further research is needed to validate these findings in larger and more diverse patient populations. Placenta accreta spectrum outcomes were not evaluated, which limits assessment of long-term obstetric risks following CSP treatment. Future studies should also explore the long-term effects of HIFU treatment on reproductive health and the potential mechanisms underlying its beneficial effects. Additionally, randomized controlled trials comparing HIFU with other treatment modalities would provide valuable insights into the most effective management strategies for CSP.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrates that HIFU treatment for CSP significantly improves re-pregnancy rates and reduces the risk of RCSP. HIFU offers a non-invasive and effective alternative to traditional surgical methods, providing patients with a safer and more favorable reproductive outcome. Factors such as the surgical method, number of cesarean sections, time since the last cesarean section, number of induced abortions, and patient age play important roles in influencing subsequent pregnancy outcomes. These findings highlight the importance of individualized treatment planning and patient counseling to optimize reproductive health. Further research is needed to validate these findings in larger and more diverse patient populations and to explore the long-term effects of HIFU treatment.

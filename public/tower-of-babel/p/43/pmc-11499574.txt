Letter to the Editor: “Real-World Effectiveness of Ensitrelvir in Reducing Severe Outcomes in Outpatients at High Risk for COVID-19”
Authors: Hideharu Hagiya
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

I have read with great interest the article recently published by Takazono et al. in your esteemed journal [1]. This retrospective observational study, utilizing the JMDC Claims Database, primarily aimed to elucidate the clinical efficacy of ensitrelvir in preventing all-cause hospitalization in a high-risk population within 4 weeks (28 days) following the diagnosis and treatment of the novel coronavirus 2019 (COVID-19). The main findings revealed that patients prescribed ensitrelvir exhibited a significantly lower incidence of all-cause hospitalization compared to those without antiviral therapy (0.494% vs. 0.785%), with a risk ratio of 0.629 (95% confidence interval 0.420 to 0.943) and a number needed to treat of 344. As described in the research, the claims database comprises a large-scale (17 million individuals), fully anonymized record of Japanese health insurance claims data, which has been leveraged for various scientific inquiries [2]. The study design and statistical methodology are well-conceived and robust, lending credibility to their findings. However, upon reviewing the manuscript, I identified a concern regarding the inclusion criteria for eligible patients that warrants further investigation.

The authors defined the primary endpoint as all-cause hospitalization occurring 2 to 28 days after diagnosis and treatment of COVID-19. Although clinical manifestations and courses of patients with COVID-19 vary depending on their vaccination history, immune status, and virus variants [3], progressive pneumonia usually occurs several days to approximately one week post-infection. While, according to preceding reports and our experience, it is very rare for patients to develop severe pneumonia requiring hospitalization more than two weeks after the initial infection. Therefore, I consider the inclusion criteria of “within 28 days” to be overly broad, potentially leading to misleading results. Their study period was set between November 2022 and July 2023, during which mRNA vaccines were fully available and Omicron was the major subvariant in Japan. Consequently, the severity rate of COVID-19 dramatically decreased, and only a small portion of infected patients required hospitalization. By extending the inclusion criteria to “within 28 days,” many unrelated hospitalization cases have likely been included, which cannot be appropriately determined in the JMDC Claims Database due to the lack of detailed clinical information. To strictly target COVID-19-associated hospitalizations, the authors need to perform a sub-analysis by stratifying data, for instance, to “within 10 days” or “within 14 days” after diagnosis and treatment of COVID-19. Otherwise, the conclusion regarding the effectiveness of ensitrelvir might be skewed. This potential selection bias is not mentioned in their limitation section.

I hereby express my concern regarding the effectiveness of ensitrelvir in preventing hospitalization, hoping for a prompt response with additional data analysis.

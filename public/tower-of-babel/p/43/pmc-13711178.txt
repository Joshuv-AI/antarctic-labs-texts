Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy phenotype is associated with postmyectomy change in diastolic function measured by artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram
Authors: Julia G. Debertin, Hartzell V. Schaff, Masoomeh Aslahishahri, Joseph A. Dearani, Steve R. Ommen, Paul A. Friedman, Zachi I. Attia, Tedy Sawma, Konstantinos C. Siontis, Eunjung Lee, Jae K. Oh, Jeffrey B. Geske
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesDiastolic dysfunction may persist after septal myectomy for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, contributing to residual or recurrent symptoms. To better understand postoperative diastolic function, we assessed the association between preoperative obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy phenotypic characteristics and change in artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram determined diastolic function, previously shown to quantify diastolic function.MethodsWe analyzed 166 consecutive patients with obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy undergoing septal myectomy with prospective artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram diastolic function data. Participants were classified as improved diastolic function (improved grade or persistently normal filling pressure) or persistent diastolic dysfunction (worsened grade or persistently high filling pressure).ResultsThe study population was 41% female with a median age of 59 years. There was no significant difference in baseline clinical characteristics between patients with improved diastolic function or persistent artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram diastolic dysfunction. Patients with worsened postoperative diastolic dysfunction by artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram had a greater left atrial volume index (P < .01), a greater left ventricular mass index (P < .01), a higher medial E/e' ratio (P < .01), and increased posterior wall thickness (P < .01) compared with those with improved or normal filling pressure grades by artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram. All-cause mortality was low with no significant difference between artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram diastolic function groups (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.14-2.06).ConclusionsChange in artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram diastolic function is associated with echocardiographic predictors of diastolic dysfunction in obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy patients undergoing septal myectomy. Patients with an advanced obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy phenotype preoperatively may be less likely to experience improvement in diastolic function after septal myectomy based on artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram diastolic grade changes.

Central Message

Pre-SM HCM phenotype is associated with postoperative persistent diastolic dysfunction or worsened diastolic function as measured by AI-ECG diastolic function grade.

Perspective

Some patients with oHCM have persistent diastolic dysfunction after SM, but predicting these outcomes proves challenging. AI-ECG measure of diastolic function allows for a novel approach to further understanding postoperative diastolic function as it relates to preoperative HCM phenotype.

Septal myectomy (SM) for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (oHCM) provides benefit for patients who have not responded to or not tolerated medical management by permanently and irreversibly eliminating left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction.1 Prior studies have demonstrated that operative relief of LVOT obstruction via SM improves symptoms of heart failure,2 reverses left atrial (LA) remodeling,3 and increases long-term survival.4 Some investigations have demonstrated improvement in left ventricular (LV) diastolic function after SM, although small sample sizes limit these studies.5 Despite relief of LVOT obstruction, some patients have persistent diastolic dysfunction, which contributes to residual or recurrent heart failure symptoms.6,7 A prior investigation suggested that patients with coronary artery disease, lower preoperative LV ejection fraction, and poor preoperative health status were more likely to report worsened health status after SM.6

Recently, an artificial intelligence–enabled electrocardiogram (AI-ECG) algorithm was developed to determine diastolic function grade from a 12-lead electrocardiogram.8 This algorithm has been validated as a reliable index of diastolic function, with some evidence indicating its superiority to echocardiographic predictors of diastolic function, including medial mitral E/e'.9 We previously demonstrated that pre-SM diastolic function, as determined by AI-ECG, can independently predict postoperative mortality in patients with oHCM.10 These findings support the ability of this AI-ECG algorithm to reliably assess diastolic function in patients with oHCM.

No studies have investigated the association between phenotypic characteristics in oHCM and changes in diastolic function after SM. In this study, we aim to assess the association between preoperative oHCM phenotypic characteristics and change in postoperative diastolic function as measured by the AI-ECG diastolic function grade algorithm.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

We analyzed patients who underwent transaortic SM for oHCM between 2000 and 2021. After the exclusion of patients with concomitant procedures, with moderate or greater aortic valve stenosis, or with prior septal reduction therapy, we identified 166 patients who had a clinically indicated ECG performed preoperatively within 30 days of SM and more than 3 months postoperatively, but no more than 2 years after SM. This study was approved by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board (No.: 23-009868). All patients provided informed consent for publication and to participate in retrospective clinical studies in accordance with Minnesota Law.

Artificial Intelligence–Enabled Electrocardiogram Diastolic Function Grade Algorithm

The methods for creating the AI-ECG diastolic function grade algorithm have been detailed and summarized.8,10 Briefly, the algorithm was trained on the diastolic function parameters of e’, E/e', tricuspid regurgitation velocity, and LA volume index. Elevated filling pressure was defined as a 3 or 4 on these parameters. In this group, if E/A was greater than 2.0, diastolic function was grade 3, and if E/A was less than 2.0, diastolic function was grade 2. Filling pressure was normal if 3 or 4 of the parameters were normal. This group was further divided into grade 0 and grade 1 based on an E/A ratio cutoff of 0.8. An equal divide of the diastolic function parameters between normal and abnormal was defined as indeterminate.

The AI-ECG algorithm was first trained to predict LV filling pressure, and the model with the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was selected to predict diastolic function grade. The model was validated using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis with areas under the curve of 0.847, 0.911, and 0.943 for grade 1 or more, grade 2 or more, and grade 3, respectively. Sensitivity was 76.6, 83.2, and 89.8, and specificity was 75.3, 82.9, and 86.0 for grade 1 or more, grade 2 or more, and grade 3, respectively.8 In the present study, this validated algorithm was used to measure diastolic function grade.

Data Collection and Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Phenotype

Demographic, clinical, echocardiographic, and ECG data were collected from a prospectively maintained institutional cardiovascular surgery database. All variables are defined according to the Society of Thoracic Surgeons Adult Cardiac Surgery Database guidelines.11 Echocardiographic data were obtained from preoperative comprehensive 2-dimensional transthoracic Doppler echocardiograms based on American Society of Echocardiography recommendations.12,13 Preoperative hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) phenotype was defined by echocardiographic characteristics, including LA volume index, LV mass index, medial mitral e’, medial mitral E/e', posterior wall thickness, and septal wall thickness. Change in HCM phenotype was also assessed in patients with worsened or persistently elevated filling pressure grades and those with improved or persistently normal filling pressure grades. A change in HCM phenotype was defined as a change in echocardiographic characteristics after SM. Postoperative echocardiographic measurements were obtained from the first outpatient echocardiogram within 5 years of the operation.

Study Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was the change in AI-ECG diastolic function grade before and after relief of LVOT gradient by transaortic SM. This change was considered worsened if the patient had an increase in diastolic function grade postoperatively or if the diastolic function grade remained 2 or 3, the same grade preoperatively. Patients with an improved diastolic function grade had a decrease in diastolic function grade postoperatively, and those with an unchanged diastolic function grade of 0 or 1 postoperatively were classified as having a persistently normal filling pressure grade. AI-ECG diastolic function before and after SM was also compared with Doppler echocardiographic measures of diastolic function. Long-term postoperative all-cause mortality was assessed as a secondary outcomes.

We performed a supplemental analysis in subset of our oHCM cohort from 2000 to 2021 (N = 2663) with available subjective postoperative symptom data (n = 110). These data were collected via survey with a median time from operation to survey of 5 years. The details of the survey methods have been outlined previously.6 We assessed the association between postoperative AI-ECG diastolic function grade and postoperative dyspnea in this cohort.

Statistical Analysis

We used descriptive statistics to compare baseline demographic and clinical characteristics between patients who had worsened diastolic function or stable elevated filling pressure diastolic function grade postoperatively and patients who had improved diastolic function or stable normal filling pressure diastolic function grade postoperatively. Normality of continuous variables was assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test, and significance testing was performed using the chi-square test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for categorical and continuous variables, respectively. Intraoperative and early postoperative characteristics were also compared between patients with worsened or persistently elevated filling pressure grades and those with improved or stable normal filling pressure grades.

Preoperative echocardiographic characteristics were assessed in association with a change in AI-ECG diastolic function grade. Box-and-whisker plots were used to visualize the association between selected echocardiographic determinants of HCM phenotype and change in AI-ECG diastolic function grade. LA volume index was categorized using the 25th percentile, interquartile range, and 75th percentile to assess the distribution of changes in AI-ECG diastolic function grade across these categories. We performed a supplemental analysis assessing the association between postoperative echocardiographic characteristics and changes in AI-ECG diastolic function grade in a subset of patients with postdischarge echocardiographic data available within 5 years of the operation. The median time from myectomy to postoperative ECG was 9.8 months. Spaghetti plots were used to visualize the change in pre- and post-SM echocardiographic measurements.

Unadjusted and adjusted Cox proportional-hazard models were used to assess the association between changes in AI-ECG diastolic function grade and all-cause mortality. Covariates were selected a priori based on a literature review, prior investigation with this cohort,10,14 and expert opinion. All analyses were performed using R Statistical Software (v4.5.1; R Core Team 2025). P values are based on a 2-sided statistical test.

Discussion

This study is the first to assess oHCM phenotypic characteristics and their association with post-SM diastolic function. Additionally, we incorporated a novel artificial intelligence algorithm into this assessment, which has been validated as a reliable measure of diastolic function in HCM.8,10 Patients with more advanced HCM phenotype, including larger LA volume, higher LV mass, and thicker septa and posterior walls before SM, were more likely to have worsened or persistent diastolic dysfunction post-SM. The small difference in time on cardiopulmonary bypass was statistically significant but is unlikely to have clinical importance. In the cohort of patients who participated in the postoperative health status survey a higher percentage of those with dyspnea with any physical activity had postoperative AI-ECG diastolic function grade 3 compared with those who reported dyspnea with some physical activity or who reported no limitations from dyspnea.

Significance of Understanding Preoperative Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Phenotype

The most common phenotype of oHCM involves some degree of LVOT obstruction, elevated LV filling pressures, and variable symptoms, which are usually dependent on the degree of obstruction and severity of associated mitral valve regurgitation.15 A more advanced state of disease involves a pattern of restrictive physiology with variable LVOT obstruction and remodeling consistent with diastolic dysfunction, including increased LA size.3,15 Once patients have restrictive cardiomyopathy, independent of LVOT obstruction, septal reduction therapy no longer provides symptom improvement or improvement of diastolic function.2,16 However, for patients with significant LVOT obstruction, determining whether SM will improve diastolic dysfunction is challenging, because the contribution of the obstruction to the diastolic dysfunction and the reversibility of the remodeling cannot be fully understood until the gradient is relieved. However, our study provides measurable features of the oHCM phenotype (including LA volume index, LV mass index, medial mitral E/e', and posterior wall thickness), which may serve as relevant preoperative indicators for whether patients with LVOT obstruction will have improvement of their diastolic function post-SM. Our study also provides some preliminary evidence that high AI-ECG diastolic function grade postoperatively may be associated with increased symptom burden, a necessary correlation to make in informing the clinical application of this algorithm. Effectively, the clinical application of these results relates to an improved ability to select patients for SM and determine surgical timing to optimize postoperative diastolic function.

Association Between Preoperative Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Phenotype and Change in Artificial Intelligence–Enabled Electrocardiogram Diastolic Function

LA size has consistently been described as an important indicator of cardiac remodeling secondary to diastolic dysfunction.17,18 In patients with normal systolic function, this remodeling results from chronic diastolic atrial pressure overload, further exacerbated by mitral regurgitation in some cases.19 LA size modestly correlates with filling pressures in HCM, and LA enlargement may reflect chronic changes of diastolic dysfunction in this disease.20 Posterior wall thickness is also associated with remodeling secondary to persistent pressure overload, because this systolic wall stress can lead to LV hypertrophy.21,22 Therefore, this study demonstrates that as cardiac remodeling secondary to impaired LV compliance worsens, as measured by LA volume and posterior wall thickness, the likelihood that relief of the LVOT gradient via septal reduction will reverse diastolic dysfunction decreases. The finding that a higher preoperative E/e' ratio is significantly associated with persistent diastolic dysfunction post-SM further supports this conclusion.

In addition to medial mitral E/e', elevated right ventricular systolic pressure, indicating pulmonary hypertension, also reflects chronically elevated LV filling pressures consistent with a more severe HCM phenotype and worse outcomes.23 In concert, these findings support that phenotypically more advanced HCM decreases the likelihood of improvement in diastolic dysfunction with SM.

Finally, septal wall thickness and LV mass both demonstrate nuanced relationships with the characterization of HCM phenotype. Septal wall thickness has a parabolic relationship with heart failure symptoms in HCM. The severity of HCM phenotype increases with increasing wall thickness for measures between 16 mm and 24 mm, which includes the median septal thickness for both subgroups assessed in our study.24,25 Therefore, patients with moderate septal thickness (16-24 mm) may have a more severe HCM phenotype with increasing septal wall thickness, and our study indicates that these patients may have less improvement in diastolic function post-SM. Further, although LV mass has a heterogeneous distribution across HCM phenotypes due to asymmetry of LV hypertrophy, it has been associated with a greater degree of outflow tract obstruction and outcomes, including heart failure and sudden death.26,27 Therefore, it may still be considered in the composite of oHCM phenotype characteristics, and the current study demonstrates that increasing LV mass limits improvement in diastolic function post-SM.

Impact of Artificial Intelligence–Enabled Electrocardiogram on Clinical Management

The value of the novel AI-ECG algorithm used in the present study to assess diastolic function grade in oHCM has been discussed previously.10 The AI-ECG estimate of diastolic function eliminates bias induced by human-selected features,28 which is particularly significant for patients with HCM, because diastolic function assessed by echocardiogram may be less reliable.12,29 Further, the use of ECG for this algorithm is a low-cost, accessible, and rapid test, which will promote rapid dissemination of the technology over time.28 This ability to reliably quantify changes in diastolic function in patients undergoing SM may aid in patient selection and the timing of septal reduction, particularly if future studies demonstrate associations with clinical functional outcomes and other clinical outcomes.

Study Limitations

We acknowledge several limitations in this investigation. Our sample size of less than 200 patients limits the power of our analyses. Further, this small sample size may impact the generalizability of our study given that it represents a smaller cohort of patients who received long-term follow-up postoperative at our institution. The study cohort included only patients having isolated SM. Further, many patients were referred from distant sites and received longitudinal care and follow-up at their local medical facility. Finally, although we do assess the relationship between postoperative AI-ECG diastolic function with postoperative functional status, future studies should consider a more comprehensive assessment of how this algorithm correlates with symptoms of diastolic dysfunction.

Conclusions

Change in AI-ECG-determined diastolic function grade is associated with echocardiographic predictors of diastolic dysfunction in oHCM patients undergoing SM. Patients with advanced oHCM phenotype preoperatively may be less likely to have diastolic function improvement post-SM. Further investigation should assess the impact of a change in AI-ECG diastolic function on survival and symptoms.

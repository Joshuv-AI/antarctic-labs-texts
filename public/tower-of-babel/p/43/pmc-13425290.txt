Design, Synthesis, Characterization, Toxicological Effects, and Molecular Docking Insights of Some Novel Quinoline Compounds as Potential Insecticides
Authors: Mokhtar A Abdul-Malik, Adel M. Kamal El-Dean, Abdel Haleem M. Hussein, Mohamed A. Gad, Aly Abdou, Abdelreheem A. Saddik, Mahmoud S. Tolba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the pursuit of novel insecticidal agents, a series of new thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline derivatives were synthesized via efficient and versatile routes, starting from ethyl 3-aminothieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline-2-carboxylate. The synthesized compounds including hydrazone (8a–c), arylidene (9a–c), and pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline (10a–c) derivatives were characterized using FT-IR, NMR, and mass spectrometry. Their insecticidal efficacy was evaluated against both nymph and adult stages of Aphis fabae, with median lethal concentration (LC50) values determined through probit analysis. Compound 10b exhibited the highest potency, with LC50 values of 0.117 mg/L (nymphs) and 0.366 mg/L (adults), approaching the activity of the commercial insecticide acetamiprid. Molecular docking studies against the Aplysia californica acetylcholine-binding protein (AChBP, PDB: 3SQ6), a surrogate for insect nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, revealed strong binding affinities for the pyranothienoquinoline derivatives, particularly 10b (−7.30 kcal/mol), supported by multiple hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions with key residues. These findings underscore the potential of the pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline scaffold as a promising candidate for the development of new, target-specific insecticides.

Introduction

Quinoline (benzo­[b]­pyridine) is N-Heterocyclic scaffold represent a significant category of compounds with varied pharmacological applications, making them crucial for new drug development. A diverse range of pharmacological and agrochemical applications has been documented for quinoline derivatives i.e., insecticidal, − anticancer, anti-inflammatory, , antifungal, antibacterial, , antimalarial, , and antiviral , (Figure ). Neonicotinoids with a pyridine moiety are highly effective against insects as well as distinctive properties, including a specific mode of action and selective action on insect nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs). ,, These features make neonicotinoids particularly valuable in pest control. So, the presence of the pyridine ring in the general structure of quinoline has led to its use in developing important engineered agrochemicals and in formulating synthetic compounds with various pharmacological effects which reflects the high importance of N-heterocyclic scaffolds in several fields. − In current agricultural systems, synthetic insecticides are essential for managing pests that threaten crop growth. − The development of novel insecticides featuring innovative modes of action is essential for overcoming resistance issues and ecological challenges linked to certain conventional insecticides. − Emerging as promising insecticidal agents are thienoquinolines and their structural analogs, most notably thieno­[2,3-b]­quinolines. Researchers are now actively synthesizing these compounds as sustainable alternatives, with several showing considerably higher insecticidal activity than commercial benchmarks. ,,

Results and Discussion

Chemistry and Spectroscopic Characterization

The target compounds were synthesized utilizing ethyl 3-aminothieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline-2-carboxylate (3) as a key intermediate. This precursor was prepared according to the pathway outlined in Scheme . Briefly, 2-chloroquinoline-3-carbonitrile (1) was subjected to nucleophilic displacement with thiourea in ethanol, affording 2-mercaptoquinoline-3-carbonitrile (2). Subsequent cyclocondensation of 2 with ethyl chloroacetate, mediated by anhydrous potassium carbonate, yielded the desired thienoquinoline derivative 3.

Synthesis of the Desired Thienoquinoline Derivative 3

The synthesis of compound 7 was achieved via a sequential, one-pot procedure from ester 3 (Scheme ). Initial basic hydrolysis of the ester was followed by treatment with ortho-phosphoric acid and subsequent neutralization with ammonium hydroxide, effecting concomitant decarboxylation and hydrolysis of an imino group. This process efficiently converted 3 through in situ intermediates 4, 5, and 6 into the target thieno­[2,3-b]­quinolin-3­(2H)-one (7) in 86% yield. The structure of 7 was unequivocally confirmed by spectroscopic analysis. The FT-IR spectrum displayed a characteristic carbonyl stretch at 1697 cm–1. The 1H NMR spectrum revealed a singlet at δ 3.93 ppm, assigned to the methylene protons (−CH2−) of the thieno scaffold, while the 13CNMR spectrum exhibited corresponding signals for the carbonyl and methylene carbons at δ 197.53 and 39.40 ppm, respectively.

Synthesis of Thieno­[2,3-b]­quinolin-3­(2H)-one (7) from Ester 3

Building upon the established thienoquinolinone core, the nucleophilic character of the active methylene group in compound 7 was further investigated. This moiety readily underwent electrophilic substitution reaction. Specifically, treatment with aryldiazonium salts in boiling ethanol in the presence of piperidine afforded the corresponding hydrazone derivatives 8. Structural elucidation of the synthesized compounds was carried out by IR, 1H NMR, 13C NMR, and mass spectral data. Thus, the IR spectrum of compound 8a displayed an absorption band at 3249 cm–1 corresponds to the NH group of the hydrazone linkage. Besides, there was band corresponding to the (CO) group of the thiophenone ring at 1666 cm–1. 1H NMR spectrum displays a singlet single at δ 10.75 to (NH), and a singlet at δ 8.81 to (quinoline-H4). The aromatic region shows multiplet singles between δ 7.35–8.17 integrating to 9H (5H, Ph-H; 4H, quinoline-H5,6,7,8). While, 13C NMR spectrum shows resonances at δ 181.29 to (CO) as characterized signal. Additional signals were observed in the range δ 143.97–115.53 corresponding to 14 carbons (8 carbons from quinolone and 6 carbons from phenyl). The EI-MS spectrum of compound 8c has been reported, along with its corresponding fragmentation pathway (Figures and Scheme ). The EI-MS spectrum of compound 8c exhibited the molecular ion peak (M+) at m/z 319.2 as the base peak, consistent with the formula C18H13N3OS (calcd. 319.38). The prominent fragments at m/z 106.2 and m/z 213.1 were assigned to the moieties NH–C6H4–CH3 and thieno­[2,3-b]­quinolin-3­(2H)-one, respectively, arising from cleavage of the N–N bond within the hydrazone linkage. Additionally, the fragment at m/z 185.1 was attributed to the loss of a CO group from the cyclic ketone portion of the thienoquinolinone core (parent at m/z 213.1). Also, the signal at m/z 91.1 corresponds to the tolyl cation (C7H7 +), which is a characteristic fragment for the p-tolyl (methylphenyl) substituted derivative. Overall, the EI-MS fragmentation pattern of 8c is in close agreement with the proposed structure and composition of the title compound 8c (C18H13N3OS, 319.38) (Figure and Scheme ).

Also, compound 7 was condensed with various aromatic aldehydes under identical catalytic conditions provided the arylidenethienoquinolinone products 9 (Scheme ). A distinct reactivity was observed, however, when compound 7 reacted with arylidenemalononitriles. Instead of forming a simple Knoevenagel condensation product analogous to 9, the reaction in boiling ethanol/piperidine furnished a more complex pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline derivative, designated as 10. This outcome suggests a tandem Michael addition-intramolecular cyclization annulation pathway. A plausible mechanism for this transformation is detailed in Scheme . Initially, piperidine acts as a base, deprotonating the active methylene group of 7 to generate the key enolate anion I. This nucleophilic species then attacks the electron-deficient β-carbon of the arylidenemalononitrile in a Michael addition, yielding the covalent adduct II. Under the basic reaction conditions, the carbanion of adduct II initiates an intramolecular cyclization onto the adjacent carbonyl group, leading to the ring closure and formation of the iminopyran intermediate III. A final, irreversible tautomerization then yields the observed, aromatized pyran ring system in product 10.

Suggested Mechanism of Formation Pyranothienoquinoline 10a–c

The structure of a representative member of this series, compound 10a, was unequivocally confirmed by comprehensive spectroscopic analysis. The FT-IR spectrum was consistent with the proposed framework, displaying broad N–H stretches at 3461 and 3320 cm–1 for the amine group and a sharp absorption at 2197 cm–1 characteristic of the nitrile (CN) functionality. The 1H NMR spectrum provided further definitive evidence: a singlet integrating for two protons at δ 7.08 ppm was assigned to the NH2 group, while a distinct one-proton singlet at δ 5.05 ppm was attributed to the methine proton of the newly formed pyran ring (pyran-H4). Critical structural confirmation was obtained from the 13C NMR spectrum, which displayed all the diagnostic carbon signals. The pyran ring carbons were observed at δ 160.96 (C2), 57.05 (C3), and 30.00 ppm (C4), while the resonance for the cyano carbon was clearly identified at δ 119.61 ppm. The collective data is in full agreement with the assigned pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline structure.

Insecticidal Bioefficacy

The insecticidal efficacy of the synthesized compounds was quantitatively assessed by determining the median lethal concentration (LC50), a statistically robust parameter representing the concentration required to achieve 50% mortality in the aphid population. This comparative data for all target derivatives is summarized in Tables and . Concentration-mortality bioassays were conducted, and the resulting data were subjected to probit analysis using LDP-line software. This statistical processing enabled the calculation of the LC50 values, presented with their 95% fiducial limits. The analysis also provided the correlation coefficient, slope of the regression line, standard error, and chi-square (χ2) values, which were used to evaluate the goodness-of-fit for the probit model and the precision of the LC50 estimations. The following section delineates the standardized bioassay techniques employed to evaluate the insecticidal activity of each target compound. The insecticidal efficacy of the synthesized thienoquinoline derivatives 7, 8a–c, 9a–c, and 10a–c was evaluated against nymphs of A. fabae, with the commercial insecticide acetamiprid serving as a positive control. The results of these toxicological assays, including the median lethal concentration (LC50) values after a 24-h exposure period, are comprehensively summarized in Table and Figure . Initial analysis revealed that most of the synthesized compounds demonstrated moderate activity against the aphid nymphs. The LC50 values for the test compounds spanned a range of 0.117 to 0.486 mg/L. In contrast, acetamiprid exhibited superior potency with a significantly lower LC50 of 0.045 mg/L. The specific LC50 values were determined as follows: 7 (0.355 mg/L), 8a (0.386 mg/L), 8b (0.374 mg/L), 8c (0.486 mg/L), 9a (0.348 mg/L), 9b (0.288 mg/L), 9c (0.398 mg/L), 10a (0.243 mg/L), 10b (0.117 mg/L), and 10c (0.215 mg/L). Notably, derivative 10b emerged as the most potent compound within the series. With an LC50 of 0.117 mg/L, its toxicity approached that of the standard insecticide acetamiprid (LC50 = 0.045 mg/L), suggesting a promising level of bioactivity. This structure–activity relationship indicates that the pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline scaffold, particularly as embodied by compound 10b, is a fruitful architecture for further optimization in the development of novel aphicidal agents.

This enhanced efficacy observed for the pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline derivative 10b against adult aphids corroborates the trend seen in the nymph assays, reinforcing the significance of this molecular framework for advanced insecticidal development.

Molecular Docking

Molecular docking provides a critical in silico strategy for elucidating the binding interactions between potential ligands and enzyme active sites, predicting both the binding affinity and the optimal three-dimensional orientation. , By simulating the most energetically favorable conformations within a target binding pocket, this computational technique is indispensable for the rational identification and optimization of lead compounds in agrochemical and pharmaceutical discovery. , In the context of insecticide development, the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (nAChR) is a paramount molecular target. The structure of the Aplysia californica acetylcholine-binding protein (AChBP), a recognized surrogate for the insect nAChR ligand-binding domain (PDB ID: 3SQ6), was selected for this study due to its central role in insect neurotransmission. − To establish the reliability of our docking protocol, the cocrystallized ligand of 3SQ6, 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-beta-d-glucopyranose, was extracted and redocked into its original binding site. The resulting binding pose was subsequently compared with the native crystallographic conformation to validate the accuracy of the computational method, yielding a root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of <2.0 Å.

The resulting pose exhibited excellent alignment with the original crystallographic configuration, with a tiny RMSD of 1.011 Å, confirming the reliability of the docking protocol used in this study, as shown in Figure .

Validation of the molecular docking protocol: structural alignment of the native crystallographic ligand (red) and the computationally redocked conformation (green) in the nAChR binding site (PDB ID: 3SQ6).

Molecular docking simulations were performed to evaluate the binding interactions of the synthesized compound library with the target receptor. As illustrated in Figure , the calculated binding energies ranged from −5.80 to −7.30 kcal/mol (see Table S3 in the SI file), reflecting a spectrum of binding affinities across the series. The most favorable interactions were observed for compounds exhibiting lower (more negative) binding energies, suggesting stronger receptor–ligand complementarity.

3D of docked compounds through the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (nACh-R) (PDB ID: 3SQ6).

Notably, the pyranothienoquinoline derivatives (C_10a–c) demonstrated superior binding affinity, forming the most stable complexes with the receptor. The lead compound, C_10b, exhibited the strongest binding energy of −7.30 kcal/mol. Its high affinity is rationalized by a multifaceted interaction network, forming several stabilizing hydrogen bonds with key residues LYS188 and GLU189, complemented by an extensive hydrophobic network involving LYS74, VAL67, and additional contacts with LYS188. Close analogues C_10a (−7.20 kcal/mol) and C_10c (−7.10 kcal/mol) displayed similarly robust profiles, with C_10c engaging in a distinctive set of four hydrogen bonds, including dual interactions with ASN108 and additional bonds with GLN114 and GLU189.

Within the hydrazone series (C_8a–c), a clear structure–activity relationship emerged. Compound C_8c exhibited the highest binding affinity (−6.90 kcal/mol), attributed to a hydrogen bond with Lys188 complemented by hydrophobic interactions with Lys74. Compound C_8b displayed marginally weaker binding (−6.80 kcal/mol), stabilized by dual hydrogen bonds with His112 and Lys188. In contrast, C_8a showed the lowest affinity within this subset (−6.50 kcal/mol).

The arylidene derivatives (C_9a–c) demonstrated comparatively moderate to weak binding energies. Both C_9a and C_9b registered identical affinities of −6.40 kcal/mol, each sustained by a single hydrogen bond and ancillary hydrophobic contacts. Compound C_9c proved the weakest member of this group (−6.20 kcal/mol). Notably, the parent scaffold C_7 yielded the lowest binding affinity across the entire series (−5.80 kcal/mol), underscoring the beneficial contribution of the hydrazone and arylidene substituents to receptor engagement.

Collectively, these docking results establish a compelling structure–activity relationship. The highest binding affinities are achieved by compounds capable of forming multiple, simultaneous hydrogen bonds with pivotal polar residues (LYS188, GLU189, ASN108, GLN114) in concert with robust hydrophobic contacts (notably with LYS74, VAL67, HIS112). The overall affinity trend, C_10b > C_10a > C_10c > C_8c > C_8b > C_8a > C_9a = C_9b > C_9c > C_7, underscores that potent and stable receptor binding is contingent upon multipoint recognition and a complementary hydrophobic interface.

It is important to emphasize that molecular docking provides only a theoretical estimation of ligand–target interactions based on binding affinity and geometric complementarily. While the obtained binding energies and interaction profiles suggest a favorable association of the synthesized compounds with the receptor model, these findings cannot be interpreted as definitive evidence of a receptor-mediated mechanism of insecticidalaction Table .

Experimental Section

Synthesis

All melting points were determined using a Gallenkamp electric melting point apparatus and are reported without correction. Reaction progress was monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on precoated silica gel aluminum sheets (Merck, Silica Gel 60 F254). Elemental analyses (C, H, N) were performed by the Microanalytical Center of the Chemistry Department, Assiut University. Fourier Transform Infrared (FT-IR) spectra were acquired on a Shimadzu FT-IR 8201 PC spectrometer using potassium bromide (KBr) disks. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded on a Bruker spectrometer operating at 400 MHz for 1H and 100 MHz for 13C nuclei. Deuterated chloroform (CDCl3) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO-d 6) were employed as solvents, with tetramethylsilane (TMS) as an internal standard; chemical shifts are reported in parts per million (ppm). Mass spectra were obtained using a Thermo Scientific ISQ LT single quadrupole mass spectrometer (GC–MS). Compounds 8c and 9b were isolated as solid materials that exhibited prohibitively low solubility in common deuterated solvents, including CDCl3, DMSO-d 6, and trifluoroacetic acid (TFA). Consequently, complete 1H and 13C NMR spectral data could not be acquired for these specific derivatives. Nonetheless, their proposed structures were unequivocally confirmed through a combination of FT-IR spectroscopy and mass spectrometry, which provided conclusive evidence for their molecular frameworks.

Laboratory Bioassay

A colony of black bean aphids (Aphis fabae) was established from specimens spontaneously collected from corn fields at the Agricultural Research Center, Sohag branch, during the 2025 growing season. The insecticidal activity of the synthesized thienoquinoline derivatives was evaluated in vivo using a bean leaf-dip bioassay, following established procedures. The assay was designed to compare the relative toxicity of the test compounds. Each derivative was prepared in five different concentrations using an aqueous solution of 0.1% Tween-20 as a dispersing agent. For each concentration, batches of 50 nymphs and 50 adults of A. fabae of uniform size were immersed for 10 s (with three replicates per concentration). , A control group, treated with distilled water containing 0.1% Tween-20 only, was included and maintained under identical conditions for 30 min post-treatment. , All bioassays were conducted under controlled environmental conditions of 25 ± 2 °C and 60 ± 5% relative humidity. Following treatment and drying, the aphids were transferred to glass jars containing dechlorinated water. Mortality was assessed 24 h postexposure using a binocular microscope. Aphids were considered dead if they showed no movement upon prodding. , The acute mortality data for each treatment were recorded for subsequent probit analysis. The observed mortality was subsequently corrected for natural mortality in the control groups using Abbott’s formula: , Corrected%=(1−ninTaftertreatmentninCoaftertreatment)×100 where n = insect population, T = treated, Co = control.

Molecular Docking

Molecular docking simulations were conducted to evaluate the binding modes and affinities of the synthesized thienoquinoline derivatives against the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (nAChR). − The crystal structure of the Aplysia californica acetylcholine-binding protein (AChBP), a recognized surrogate for the insect nAChR ligand-binding domain (PDB ID: 3SQ6), was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (www.rcsb.org). ,, The selection of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (nAChR) as a molecular target in this study was based on its well-established role as a primary site of action for several classes of commercial insecticides, particularly neonicotinoids. These compounds exert their toxicity through selective binding to insect nAChRs, leading to overstimulation of the nervous system and eventual insect death. Structurally, the synthesized thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline derivatives contain heteroaromatic frameworks with electronic features comparable to pyridine-based pharmacophores present in known nAChR ligands, which motivated their evaluation against this receptor model. The protein structure was prepared for docking by removing all crystallographic water molecules, cosolvents, and native ligands. Polar hydrogen atoms and Kollman united atom partial charges were then added to optimize the receptor for the simulation. Docking calculations were performed using AutoDock Vina. A grid box with dimensions of 127.737 × 91.045 × 144.784 Å was centered at coordinates (x = 15.735, y = −2.464, z = 33.916) to comprehensively encompass the entire binding pocket. The binding energy (ΔG, kcal/mol) for each compound was calculated.

Conclusion

In this study, a novel series of thieno­[2,3-b]­quinoline derivatives including hydrazones (8a–c), arylidenes (9a–c), and pyrano­[3,2-c]­thieno­[2,3-b]­quinolines (10a–c) were successfully synthesized and structurally characterized. The insecticidal efficacy of these compounds was rigorously evaluated against both nymph and adult stages of Aphis fabae. Bioassay results identified compound 10b as the most potent candidate, exhibiting significant toxicity with LC50 values of 0.117 mg/L (nymphs) and 0.366 mg/L (adults), demonstrating activity comparable to the commercial standard acetamiprid. A clear structure–activity relationship emerged, underscoring the enhanced bioactivity conferred by the pyranothienoquinoline scaffold. Molecular docking simulations against the Aplysia californica acetylcholine-binding protein (AChBP, PDB: 3SQ6) provided a robust theoretical basis for the observed potency. The superior binding affinity of 10b (−7.30 kcal/mol) was rationalized by its ability to form multiple hydrogen bonds with key residues (LYS188, GLU189) and engage in extensive hydrophobic interactions within the insect nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (nAChR) binding site.The integration of synthetic chemistry, biological evaluation, and computational modeling in this work validates the thieno­[2,3-b]­quinolineand particularly the pyrano-fused derivative as a privileged scaffold for insecticide development. Compound 10b represents a promising lead structure for the design of novel, target-specific aphicidal agents. Future work will focus on further structural optimization, in-depth mode-of-action studies, and field-level efficacy trials to advance this class of compounds toward practical agricultural applications.

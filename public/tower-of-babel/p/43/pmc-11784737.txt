Pediatric Three-dimensional Transesophageal Echocardiography: A Game Changer in Congenital Heart Disease
Authors: Giovanni Di Salvo, Alice Pozza, Biagio Castaldi, Domenico Galzerano, Valeria Pergola
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Despite its potential, pediatric three-dimensional (3D) transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) faced technical limitations due to the size and rigidity of early probes designed for adults.Aim:In this paper, we present our experience in using the new pediatric 3D TEE probe in complex congenital heart disease (CHD).Methods:Recent advancements in the field of miniaturization have led to the development of a new pediatric 3D TEE probe, specifically designed for young children (weight ≥4.5 kg). This probe features a smaller, flexible design and high-spatial and temporal resolution, enabling detailed views of intracardiac structures and real-time imaging crucial for complex diagnosis, planning interventional and surgical procedures. We used this new probe in pediatric cases (weight between 4.5 and 6 kg) with complex congenital heart disease.Results:In double-outlet right ventricle or in Taussig Bing anomaly the new 3D TEE probe enabled detailed assessment of ventricular septal defect and its relations with tricuspid valve apparatus, and provided details of mitral valve leaflets not detectable by the transthoracic approach, even in small children.Conclusions:These cases highlight the probe‘s ability to provide detailed anatomical information, enhancing surgical planning and outcomes. The pediatric 3D TEE probe has the potential to be a game changer in defining intracardiac anatomy in complex CHD.

INTRODUCTION

Three-dimensional (3D) echocardiography has revolutionized the field of cardiac imaging by providing a comprehensive and detailed dynamic view of the heart‘s structures and functions, providing “in vivo anatomy.”[1]

Unlike traditional two-dimensional (2D) echocardiography, which offers a flat representation, 3D echocardiography captures the heart in its entirety, allowing clinicians to observe spatial relationships and complex anatomical details. This advancement enhances diagnostic accuracy, guides therapeutic interventions, and improves the overall management of cardiac patients.

In the field of congenital heart disease (CHD), 3D echocardiography is particularly valuable.[23] CHD encompasses a wide spectrum of structural heart defects that can be complex and tricky. 2D echocardiography is most of the time the only imaging modality used in congenital cardiac patients, for diagnosis and interventional or surgical planning. Using 2D echocardiography, the experienced congenital cardiologist has to reconstruct in his/her mind the spatial relations among contiguous structures, and this may lead to operator dependency, interobserver variability, and difficulties in communication between cardiologists and cardiac surgeons.[23]

The ability to visualize these anomalies in three dimensions significantly simplifies this task, helping in the diagnosis, surgical planning, and postoperative evaluation, simplifying communication and reducing operator dependency.[4] For example, complex congenital anomalies such as double-outlet right ventricle, tetralogy of Fallot, and atrioventricular septal defects can be better understood and assessed through 3D imaging, enabling more precise and effective treatment strategies.

However, as of now, the 3D approach in pediatric patients has been limited due to technical reasons to the transthoracic approach. On the other side, the transesophageal approach because of the closer distance between the probe and the heart and its higher temporal resolutions may have physical advantages in children where small cardiac structures and faster heart rate challenge the ultrasound-based visualization.[234]

Despite these advantages, the application of 3D transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) in pediatric patients has faced significant technical challenges, related to the transesophageal probe size and rigidity in a small body.[4]

Many efforts were directed toward the miniaturization of phased array transducers and the provision of higher frequencies for improved resolution so that probes could be used in very small children and even newborns or perhaps for monitoring patients in the intensive care unit. The early TEE probes were limited for CHD evaluation in children due to several factors. First, the large sizes of the early TEE probes prevented their use in smaller patients (<20 kg). Thus, there was little benefit to the majority of pediatric patients with CHD.[4]

In addition, these probes were too rigid to be used safely and effectively in small children. In addition, the technology required to capture high-resolution 3D images was not sufficiently miniaturized to fit within the constraints of pediatric anatomy.[4]

Recently, these challenges have been addressed by introducing a new 3D transesophageal pediatric probe, the 9VT-D (GE Healthcare).[5] This is the world‘s most compact 3D miniprobe and the first 3D TEE designed specifically for small children (from 5 kg).[5] This new probe has a 30% smaller tip size than the 3D TEE probe and 57% smaller tip volume.[5] This innovative probe is specifically designed to cater to the unique anatomical and clinical needs of pediatric patients. It features a smaller, more flexible design that can be safely maneuvered within the esophagus of young children (in our experience in sedated babies from 4.5 kg). The probe allows a full 2D echo color Doppler examination and enables high-resolution live 3D imaging, on a single beat, providing clear and detailed views of intracardiac structures.

The 3D transesophageal pediatric probe carries high flexibility allowing for easier insertion and navigation within the esophagus.

These features are crucial for diagnosing and evaluating complex congenital heart defects, providing high temporal resolution real-time 3D imaging, which is essential for guiding interventional procedures and assessing surgical outcomes in the operating room.

CONCLUSION

The introduction of the pediatric 3D TEE probe represents a significant advancement in the field of pediatric cardiology. As of now, 3D TEE is the only imaging modality that can represent valve leaflets and subvalvular apparatus in detail.[67] This game-changing technology enhances the in vivo anatomical visualization of complex intracardiac anomalies, offering a new dimension of precision in diagnosing and treating CHD. The high-resolution imaging capabilities of the probe not only improve clinical outcomes but also pave the way for innovative applications such as 3D fluid dynamic study,[8] 3D-printed models, and virtual reality simulations for surgical planning and percutaneous interventions. As technology continues to evolve, the pediatric 3D TEE probe is set to become a game changer in the comprehensive care of children with CHD.

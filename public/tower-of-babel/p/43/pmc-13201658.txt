Fault-tolerant control of quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle by using adaptive fuzzy T-S and linear matrix inversion approach
Authors: Muhammad Taimoor, Haixia Wang, Sameena Bibi, Chunyang Sheng, Umer Hameed Shah, Xiao Lu, Zhiguo Zhang, Irfan Hussain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this research, a robust and adaptive fault-tolerant control approach is presented for the rejection of the unknown disturbances and faults existing in the quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle sensors. The proposed approach is based on the hybrid utilization of the Fuzzy Takagi-Sugeno and linear matrix inversion control. Quadrotors are highly complex and nonlinear dynamic systems, which are vulnerable to unknown faults and disturbances, due to which the system performance may cause instability or degradation. To guarantee the stability of the system, an adaptive fuzzy T-S model is utilized to depict the nonlinear behavior of the system through a set of linear models based on fuzzy rules. The adaptive approach enables the controller to dynamically adjust based on the varying system conditions. A linear matrix inversion (LMI) control strategy is developed based on this model to effectively compute control inputs while maintaining system performance and stability. By integrating the approaches, the real-time faults are compensated without the requirement of fault diagnoses and isolation. The results of the simulation show that the suggested adaptive strategy shows robustness and strong resilience against unknown faults and disturbances, and ensures the stable flight compared to the conventional Fuzzy T-S controller, conventional linear matrix inversion (LMI) controller, and the techniques used in the literature. The results show the efficacy, effectiveness, and efficiency of the proposed approach for real-time UAV applications.To improve the safety and performance of nonlinear systems, it is suggested that the described method be integrated into fault-tolerant control (FTC).Keywords: Fuzzy T-S, Linear matrix inversion, Fault-tolerant control, Sensors, Quadrotor, Nonlinear systemSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have drawn increased interest or attention due to their revolution in numerous industrial applications due to their versatility and efficiency1, such as agricultural surveying, disaster management, and aerial photography2,3. UAVs exist in numerous types, and quadrotors have gained more significance because of their ease of utilization, compact design, and the ability to fly with agility. The quadrotor’s maneuverability and stability are enhanced due to its specific configuration of rotors, which makes it more feasible for operations such as agricultural monitoring, rescue and search operations, as well as aerial mapping2. Although quadrotors have numerous advantages, they also face several challenges in terms of control, disturbances, and faults that occur during the flight. In the presence of such challenges, there is always a demand for highly advanced control approaches in order to ensure the safety and performance. As a result, fault-tolerant control strategies, which seek to improve the robustness and dependability of quadrotor systems, are becoming more and more popular.

Existing strategies

Designing the advanced or innovative control approaches for the unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), numerous strategies have already been proposed in literature considering the challenges such as external disturbances, faults in actuators and sensors, and system nonlinearities. Each technique has its unique restrictions and advantages, depending on its unique methodology, computational necessities, and robustness capabilities in managing the specific dynamics of the UAVs.

In literature, numerous model-based strategies such as model predictive control (MPC), proportional, integral, and derivative (PID) control are proposed for managing the requirements of the UAV control. These strategies are mainly based on the linearized models, which are well known for their practical feasibility and analytical simplicity for ensuring system stability. However, these approaches are restricted in managing the nonlinear and complex dynamics inherent in the motion of the UAVs4–9.

To manage these restrictions, nonlinear control strategies have gained more attraction and significance due to their capabilities of robustness and flexibility in managing the nonlinear, coupled, and complex dynamics of UAVs. Nonlinear control techniques such as sliding mode control (SMC) improve the robustness by forcing the system into a safe and stable sliding surface, by strengthening it against undesirable and unwanted external disturbances, but may also cause unwanted chattering effects10–12. Backstepping control technique offers a recursive approach for designing the control, ensuring the stability of the subsystems step-by-step, and is mainly considered suitable for nonlinear and complex systems8,9,13,14. Furthermore, the linearized feedback techniques transform the nonlinear systems into corresponding linear models, offering accurate control, but demanding a precise model, which is not always possible and feasible15–17.

In spite of these developments, nonlinear control approaches might face challenges such as chattering effects, calculations of complex control input, and the requirements of a highly accurate model. Due to these challenges, the demand for intelligent and model-free strategies has increased recently, such as neural networks and fuzzy logic control, which ensure that external disturbance and faults are managed more adaptively and flexibly to manage the dynamic variations in the UAV’s environment6,18–21.

In the analysis of stability, various strategies provide different control accuracy levels over time. The asymptotic stability ensures equilibrium convergence as the time approaches infinity, but it does not guarantee fixed time assurance to reach the equilibrium position22. In contrast, the finite-time stability ensures the equilibrium convergence within the fixed-time frame; however, this concept relies on the initial conditions of the system23,24. In recent research, the fixed-time stability is considered a useful strategy that guarantees not being too dependent on the initial conditions of the system, as well as ensures the anticipated finite-time frame. This concept of finite-time stability is considered a highly suitable and feasible approach to incorporate in UAVs that ensures certain performance within the specified finite-time frame25–27.

Fault-tolerant control (FTC) plays a vital role in the control of UAVs and is considered a vital part of UAVs, as the presence of faults in actuators or sensors can significantly degrade the safety and performance of the flight. The capacity of fault-tolerant control (FTC) techniques to preserve system performance and stability in the face of uncertainties, disruptions, and component defects has drawn a lot of attention in recent years. To handle sensor and actuator failures in nonlinear and uncertain systems, a number of adaptive and intelligent control strategies have been put forth28–31.

Generally, Fault-tolerant control (FTC) strategies are divided into two groups: active and passive control approaches. The passive Fault-tolerant control (FTC) strategies are developed to ensure the safe flight performance in the presence of faults, where non-detection, isolation, and reconfiguration of faults are required. These strategies are considered simpler to employ and implement, which might suggest restricted adaptability and flexibility in managing the numerous unknown faults and disturbances32–36. Whereas, the active strategies for Fault-tolerant control (FTC) implement and employ the diagnosis, isolation, and detection of faults in order to tune the control approach in consideration of the detected faults. This type of strategy offers a more adaptable, robust, and flexible response to various unknown faults; however, it demands more computational requirements and additional complex algorithms37–39.

Contributions and suggested strategies

In this research study, the adaptive control approach is presented that manages the several challenges faced by the quadrotor’s nonlinear nature, external disturbances, and manages the actuators and sensors faults. In this study, the intelligent nonlinear approach is combined with the linear control concept in order to ensure robust and safe flight. Because of the consideration of the complex dynamics, the detailed nonlinear dynamical model of the system is developed first. The developed model is then utilized in its condensed form, considering the external disturbances and sensor faults, in order to provide a realistic scenario to the proposed control approach. In order to manage the quadrotor nonlinear dynamics, the linear matrix inversion (LMI), a robust control approach used in literature, is employed in combination with the Fuzzy Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) approach. The integration of the Fuzzy Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) approach with the linear matrix inversion (LMI), having adaptive capabilities, assures the safe and robust flight performance in the existence of external disturbances as well as sensor faults.

The main purpose and contribution of this study is to propose an adaptive fault-tolerant control (FTC) strategy, which assures rapid, accurate tracking and fast convergence in the case of the existence of any unknown fault or disturbances with minimum error. In this study, the proposed control approach, irrespective of the initial conditions, guarantees strong and quick convergence within the specified time compared to strategies presented in40,41. This concept specifically originated from the need for accurate, robust, and rapid tracking and convergence in UAV applications, specifically in the existence of sensor faults, where delays can cause the degradation of the system performance, which may lead to critical damage. Under such faulty circumstances, the slow response of the system may cause the degradation of the control precision and accuracy, tracking error enhancement, and may cause instability, eventually compromising the efficiency and effectiveness of the FTC (fault-tolerant control) methodology. The concept of the adaptive stability approach assures the system approaches the desired trajectory within the specified upper-bound, which makes it suitable for dynamic environments and safety-critical applications, where abrupt and robust performance is desired. In literature, various approaches use the complex active estimators to manage the sensor faults, due to which their computational requirements are also enhanced42, which are difficult to employ30. Whereas the designed proposed adaptive controller is simple, requires reduced computational power, and is easy to employ practically without compromising the efficiency, effectiveness, and robustness. Remarks

The simulation results exhibit that all three tested techniques showed equally significant performance in the absence of any external disturbances or faults, and they showed good tracking and rapid response. The performance differences appeared after applying the disturbance or faults to the system. The conventional strategies, such as linear matrix inversion (LMI) and Fuzzy T-S, showed degraded performance and did not tackle these perturbations, resulting in much larger oscillations in pitch, roll, and yaw. The proposed adaptive controller exhibited the best performance in tackling the external perturbations and disturbances. The overall best performance was shown by the proposed adaptive controller, which guaranteed the abrupt adaptation, showing rapid and robust response, and ensured the stability of the system.

Conclusion

In this research study, a hybrid adaptive control strategy based on the integration of the Fuzzy T-S model and linear matrix inversion (LMI) is introduced to enhance the tracking performance of the attitude of the quadrotor as well as to manage the unknown disturbances and faults. It has been shown that the adaptive controller strategy expressively improves the current state-of-the-art. It has been proven that the proposed approach is capable of handling any type of unknown disturbances and faults of any unknown duration and magnitude. It has been made possible to compensate for the variations in the duration and magnitude of the faults or disturbances by using the adaptive controller, which adjusts itself according to the environment. Numerical simulation results are presented, and the effectiveness, efficiency, and efficacy of the proposed strategy are proven compared to conventional techniques and the techniques existing in the literature1. The proposed approach has been validated based on various unknown faults or disturbances, and it is proposed that it is the best approach for fault-tolerance. Consequently, it is suggested that the proposed strategy be integrated into nonlinear systems for improving the system performance as well as the safety level.

Future work

The suggested approach will be practically employed on a quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle in order to validate the proposed fault-tolerant control (FTC) strategy experimentally. The size, weight, and cost play an important role in the design of unmanned aerial vehicles; the hardware redundancy is not encouraged for practical implementation. Therefore, the analytical redundancy is proposed as the alternative solution for the implementation of the proposed strategy. The analytical redundant approaches are based on the mathematical model of the system; therefore, such methods are called model-based fault-tolerant control methods. From the research, it is evident that the analytical techniques can detect any type of unknown faults and disturbances.

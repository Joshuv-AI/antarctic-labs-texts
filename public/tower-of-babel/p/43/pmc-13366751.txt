A model for prediction of recurrence risk after zero-fluoroscopy catheter ablation for patients with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation
Authors: Lei Zhao, Ruibin Li, Long Bai, Jidong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to identify independent risk factors for atrial fibrillation recurrence after fluoroscopy-free radiofrequency ablation (RFA) in patients with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF), and to develop and validate a nomogram model for individualized prediction of postprocedural PAF recurrence risk.ResultsClinical data of 204 PAF patients undergoing initial fluoroscopy-free RFA were retrospectively analyzed with a median 18.7-month follow-up, and 20.6% of patients had recurrence.Age, left atrial diameter, hypertension, and PAF disease duration were confirmed as independent recurrence risk factors. The validated nomogram showed excellent predictive performance, with C-indexes of 0.813 and 0.805 in the training and validation sets, respectively. Time-dependent ROC analyses revealed favorable AUC values for 6-month, 1-year and 2-year recurrence prediction. Calibration metrics and sensitivity analysis verified the model’s favorable stability and accuracy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-026-07876-6.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia worldwide, associated with a significantly increased risk of stroke, heart failure, myocardial infarction, and all-cause mortality [1]. Paroxysmal AF (PAF) is the most prevalent subtype of AF, and radiofrequency ablation (RFA) has become a first-line therapeutic option for symptomatic PAF patients refractory to antiarrhythmic drugs [2]. Fluoroscopy-free RFA, an advanced ablation technique, has gained widespread clinical adoption due to its ability to eliminate ionizing radiation exposure for both patients and medical staff while maintaining procedural safety and efficacy [3, 4]. Despite notable advances in ablation technology, postprocedural AF recurrence remains a major clinical challenge, with reported rates of 20–30% in PAF patients [5]. The identification of high-risk patients and accurate prediction of recurrence risk are critical for optimizing preprocedural patient selection and formulating personalized postprocedural follow-up and intervention strategies. Single clinical factors have limited predictive value for PAF recurrence due to the multifactorial nature of the condition, and there is a paucity of research on predictive models integrating multiple clinical factors for fluoroscopy-free RFA in PAF patients. Nomograms, as visual and user-friendly clinical predictive tools, synthesize multiple independent risk factors to quantify individual disease risk and have been widely applied in cardiovascular disease prognosis prediction [6].

In this single-center retrospective study, we aimed to screen for independent risk factors for recurrence after fluoroscopy-free RFA in PAF patients and develop a nomogram model for predicting recurrence risk. We further validated the model’s performance via internal validation, backward stepwise selection, quantitative calibration assessment, and sensitivity analysis providing preliminary clinical evidence for risk stratification and personalized management of PAF patients undergoing fluoroscopy-free RFA.

Methods

Study population

This retrospective study was conducted at the Department of Cardiology, the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University. Consecutive patients with symptomatic PAF who underwent first-time fluoroscopy-free RFA between June 2020 and June 2022 were enrolled.Symptomatic PAF was defined in accordance with the 2024 European Society of Cardiology(ESC) Guidelines for Diagnosis and Management of Atrial Fibrillation [7], and was assessed by attending cardiologists through a combination of patient self-reported symptoms (including palpitations, chest tightness, dyspnea, dizziness, syncope, or exercise intolerance) and documentation of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation on electrocardiography/24-hour ambulatory electrocardiography that was temporally associated with these symptoms. Patients with PAF who were asymptomatic (no subjective symptoms and no impact on daily activities) were excluded from the study.

Inclusion criteria

(1) Aged ≥ 18 years; (2) Diagnosis of PAF (intermittent AF terminating within 7 days of onset) confirmed by electrocardiogram or 24-hour ambulatory electrocardiography [2]; (3) No contraindications to catheter ablation; (4) Complete clinical, procedural, and follow-up data.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Persistent or permanent AF; (2) Atrial tachycardia or atrial flutter; (3) PAF secondary to cardiac surgery; (4) PAF complicated with moderate-to-severe valvular heart disease (e.g., mitral stenosis); (5) Severe hepatic, renal, or pulmonary dysfunction; (6) Malignant tumors, hematologic disorders, or active autoimmune diseases; (7) Atrial thrombus confirmed by transesophageal echocardiography; (8) Prior cardiac surgery or AF ablation history; (9) Poorly controlled thyroid function; (10) Acute infection, sepsis, or septicemia; (11) Life expectancy < 1 year(This criterion was assessed by a multidisciplinary team of cardiologists and attending physicians specializing in the relevant comorbidities (e.g., oncology, gastroenterology, pulmonology) based on predefined clinical criteria, including: (1) presence of advanced malignant tumors (stage IV with distant metastasis); (2) end-stage organ failure (New York Heart Association Class IV heart failure, end-stage renal/hepatic/pulmonary dysfunction requiring continuous life support); (3) other fatal diseases with a confirmed 1-year survival rate < 10% based on clinical guidelines. Physician clinical judgment was used as an auxiliary assessment on the basis of the above predefined criteria to ensure objectivity and consistency. );(12) Incomplete clinical or follow-up data.

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Ethics No.2023-R397) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to the ablation procedure.

Preoperative preparation and procedural protocol

A detailed medical history was collected for all patients on admission, including demographic characteristics, smoking and drinking status, PAF disease duration, comorbidities, and medication history. The CHA₂DS₂-VASc score was calculated for each patient, and body mass index (BMI) was measured. Routine preoperative examinations included blood tests, 12-lead electrocardiography, chest computed tomography (CT) or X-ray, transthoracic echocardiography, CT angiography of the left atrium (LA) and left atrial appendage (LAA), and transesophageal echocardiography to rule out atrial thrombus.

All baseline clinical covariates (demographic characteristics, comorbidities, laboratory results, echocardiographic parameters, etc.) were uniformly measured and collected within 72 h of hospital admission and prior to the performance of fluoroscopy-free RFA.

All patients underwent RFA after a 5-half-life withdrawal of antiarrhythmic drugs, with the procedure performed under general anesthesia using the CARTO-3D Mapping System. Vascular access was established via the left femoral vein, and an 8 F sheath was inserted. An 8 F Swartz sheath was advanced to the superior vena cava, and transseptal puncture was performed using a Diagnostic Ultrasound Catheter (SOUNDSTA 3D). Unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg) was administered intravenously immediately after successful transseptal puncture, with the activated clotting time (ACT) maintained at 250–350 s throughout the procedure. Bilateral circumferential pulmonary vein isolation (CPVI) was performed using a THERMOCOOL SMARTTOUCH™ catheter. Procedural success was defined as the elimination of pulmonary vein electrical potentials and the achievement of bidirectional conduction block between the pulmonary veins and the left atrium.

Follow-Up protocol

All patients were scheduled for regular follow-up at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months postprocedurally, with biannual follow-up thereafter. Unscheduled follow-up was arranged for patients who presented with AF-related symptoms at any time.During the entire follow-up period (median 18.7 months, minimum 6 months), 0 patients were lost to follow-up in our study cohort. This was achieved through a standardized follow-up system: (1) regular scheduled face-to-face follow-up at our hospital’s cardiology outpatient clinic; (2) telephone/WeChat follow-up reminders 3 days before each scheduled follow-up; (3) urgent outpatient follow-up for patients with acute AF-related symptoms.Follow-up assessments included a physical examination, 12-lead electrocardiography, 24-hour ambulatory electrocardiography, and transthoracic echocardiography. The primary study endpoint was AF recurrence, defined as the detection of sustained AF, atrial flutter, or paroxysmal atrial tachycardia lasting ≥ 30 s on electrocardiography or ambulatory electrocardiography 3 months after the ablation procedure (after the blanking period).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Patients were randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a validation set (30%) using simple random sampling without replacement via the base package in R. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s) and compared between groups using the independent samples t-test; skewed continuous variables were expressed as median (25th, 75th percentile) [M(P25, P75)] and compared using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages (n, %) and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariable Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was conducted for descriptive and exploratory purposes only.For the identification of independent risk factors, standard backward stepwise multivariable Cox regression was performed starting from an initial comprehensive model that included all prespecified clinically relevant candidate variables. The candidate variables comprised age, gender, BMI, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease, ischemic stroke/TIA, congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, left atrial diameter (LAD), LVEF, RDW, MHR, NLR, hsCRP, creatinine, uric acid, LDL-C, HDL-C, homocysteine, cystatin C, and PAF disease duration. Backward stepwise selection was employed with a variable entry threshold of P < 0.05 and removal threshold of P ≥ 0.10 to screen for independent recurrence risk factors.A nomogram model was constructed using the rms package in R based on the identified independent risk factors. Internal validation of the model was conducted via bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) using the calibrate function, and calibration curves were plotted for both the training and validation sets. Quantitative calibration was assessed using calibration slope, calibration intercept, and Brier score at 6, 12, and 24 months. Sensitivity analysis was performed by excluding patients with follow-up < 12 months and adjusting for confounders.The discriminatory ability of the nomogram was evaluated using the C-index (calculated via the coxph package in R) and time-dependent ROC curves (AUC calculated via the timeROC package). Patient scores were calculated using the nomogram Formula package, and patients were stratified into high- and low-score groups by the median nomogram score. Cumulative recurrence probability curves were plotted using the survminer package, and differences between groups were compared using the Log-rank test. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study subjects

A total of 236 patients with symptomatic paroxysmal AF who underwent RFCA for the first time between June 2020 and June 2022 at the Department of Cardiology, the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University, were consecutively enrolled. According to the exclusion criteria, 6 patients with valvular AF, 5 patients with non-first catheter ablation, and 21 patients with incomplete clinical data were excluded. A final cohort of 204 patients with first-time catheter ablation for paroxysmal AF was included in the study. Follow-up was conducted until December 31, 2022, with a median follow-up duration of 18.7 months. All patients had a minimum follow-up of 6 months. Among these patients, there were 42 cases (20.6%) of postoperative recurrence and 162 cases (79.4%) without recurrence (Study Cohort was shown in Fig. 1).

Results of univariable Cox regression analysis

In the training set, univariable Cox regression analysis was performed for clinical descriptive purposes, with recurrence of PAF following catheter ablation as the dependent variable and all baseline characteristics as independent variables. The results showed that age, CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, LAD, hypertension, and PAF disease duration were nominally associated with recurrence (P < 0.05). These univariable results were not used for variable selection in the multivariable modeling process (Table 3).

Establishment of nomogram for predicting recurrence of paroxysmal AF after catheter ablation

The statistically significant variables identified from standard backward stepwise multivariable Cox regression analysis, including age, LAD, hypertension, and PAF disease duration, were integrated to develop a nomogram model for predicting recurrence of PAF following zero-fluoroscopy catheter ablation.The recurrence risk for each patient can be assessed by calculating a score that incorporates the four previously mentioned risk factors.The cumulative score spans from 0 to 260; therefore, a higher cumulative score indicates an increased probability of postoperative recurrence (Fig. 2).

Validation of the nomogram model

To assess the internal validity of the ROC curve, a bootstrap resampling method was employed to repeatedly sample the study population with replacement for 1000 iterations.This approach facilitated both internal validation of the ROC curve model and validation within a zero set. Subsequently, calibration curves for 6 months, 1 year, and 2 years were constructed. The horizontal axis of these figures represents the predicted recurrence probabilities derived from the ROC curve, while the vertical axis denotes the observed actual recurrence probabilities. The gray dashed line represents an ideal reference line where predicted probabilities correspond with observed outcomes; the red line indicates the fitted curve conversely. The shaded areas adjacent to this line signify a 95% confidence interval (CI). Quantitative calibration metrics (calibration slope, intercept, Brier score) showed excellent agreement between prediction and observation. Calibration curves derived from both training and validation sets demonstrate that the ROC curve model effectively predicts postoperative recurrence risk in paroxysmal AF after RFCA, closely aligning with actual observed risks (Fig. 3). Using the “timeROC” package’s time ROC function, a time-dependent ROC curve was plotted and the area under the curve (AUC) value was calculated to predict recurrence at 6 months after RFCA in the training set, yielding an AUC of 0.848.The AUC for predicting recurrence at 1 year was 0.899, and that for predicting recurrence at 2 years was 0.884. In the validation set, the AUC for predicting 6-month recurrence was 0.856, the AUC for predicting 1-year and 2-year recurrence were 0.814, and 0.841 respectively (Fig. 4). The Cox proportional hazards model was used with the “survival” package to calculate the training set concordance index (C-index) as 0.813 (95%CI 0.750–0.877) and the validation set concordance index (C-index) as 0.805 (95%CI 0.683–0.928). Sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness and stability of the model. Both the concordance index and the area under the ROC curve demonstrate that the Cox proportional hazards regression model exhibits strong discriminative capability and predictive value.

Discussion

Catheter ablation is an effective treatment for drug-refractory AF by regulating ventricular rate and enhancing quality of life [8]. Despite numerous studies showing that catheter ablation is more effective than antiarrhythmic drug therapy, many patients experience recurrence of AF after successful procedures.Age is an independent risk factor for the occurrence and prognosis of AF, and is also an important factor in the progression of paroxysmal AF to persistent AF [9]. Older patients with AF have a higher risk of arrhythmias, stroke, bleeding, and heart failure, which ultimately leads to prolonged hospitalizations and increased mortality [10]. Previous study showed that older age was a significant independent predictor of AF recurrence.Besides, the recurrence rate of paroxysmal AF after successful radiofrequency ablation of accessory pathways shows an age-related increase, being low in patients younger than 50 years of age (12%) and high in the older patients: 35% in patients older than 50 years and 55% in patients older than 60 [11]. The coexistence of AF and other cardiovascular diseases such as coronary heart disease, hypertension, and heart valve disease [12] in elderly patients further complicated the pathophysiological mechanism between age and AF.Studies have shown that advancing age leads to an accumulation of adipose tissue in the left atrium, along with the deposition of collagen and glycogen. This process is accompanied by a decrease in the quantity and size of atrial myocardial cells, sinoatrial node and atrioventricular node pacemaker cells, as well as conduction tissue, which are subsequently replaced by fibrosis. The left atrium undergoes structural, cellular, and molecular changes, which translate into electrophysiological abnormalities facilitating the onset and maintenance of AF [9, 13].

In the present study, we employed standard backward stepwise multivariable Cox regression starting from a comprehensive set of clinically relevant candidate variables, without prior univariable pre-screening, to ensure methodological rigor and minimize selection bias. The present study employed rigorous statistical methods including standard backward selection, quantitative calibration assessment, and sensitivity analysis to ensure model reliability. A standardized method was used to accurately distinguish atrial flutter from pulmonary vein arrhythmias, ensuring the accuracy of endpoint definition.

The results of this study indicate that the duration of AF is an independent risk factor for postoperative recurrence (P = 0.0011, HR 1.017, 95%CI 1.004–1.030).A prospective follow-up study included 325 patients with paroxysmal AF who underwent durable pulmonary vein isolation ablation showed that the diagnosis-to-ablation time(DAT)is the most important predictor of arrhythmia recurrence in patients treated with durable pulmonary vein isolation for paroxysmal AF(P = 0.011). Diagnosis-to-ablation time ≥ 1 year was associated with a nearly two-fold increased risk for developing AF recurrence [14]. Kawaji T et al. followed up 1206 patients undergoing first-time RFCA for AF showed that early RFCA was associated with significantly lower arrhythmia recurrence compared with delayed RFCA, leading to reduced cardiovascular hospitalization, especially in heart failure patients [15]. These studies suggest that clinicians should implement early intervention in patients with AF after the onset of AF to reduce atrial remodeling, reduce cardiovascular complications and related hospitalization rates.The left atrium is considered an important biomarker in patients with left ventricular diastolic dysfunction and AF in whom left atrial (LA) enlargement is of prognostic importance [16]. Findings from numerous large multicenter randomized controlled trials conducted over the past decade consistently demonstrate a close association between increased LAD and the recurrence of AF following catheter ablation. A meta-regression analysis of twenty-two studies including a total of 3750 patients with AF indicated that dilated LA significantly increases the risk of AF recurrence after single circumferential pulmonary vein isolation (CPVI) .The LAD showed a significant increase of 1.87 mm(95% CI 1.26–2.48, P < 0.001) in those with recurrence compared to those without recurrence after CPVI [17]. Increased automaticity and triggered activity occur in atrial structural remodeling, which may cause difficulty in maintaining sinus rhythm after ablation [18, 19].Additionally, a dilated left atrium (LA) may increase the difficulty in achieving catheter stability and thereby require more energy to complete AF ablation [20].

The nomogram’s visual, user-friendly design allows clinicians to easily communicate the individual recurrence risk to patients, improving patient understanding and adherence to the ablation procedure and postprocedural management; it also supports shared decision-making for patients who are hesitant about RFA versus antiarrhythmic drug therapy. Besides, the nomogram can help healthcare institutions allocate limited cardiology resources more efficiently, with intensified postprocedural care focused on high-risk patients who are most likely to experience recurrence and require further intervention.

Strengths

A single variable exhibits limited sensitivity and specificity in predicting the recurrence of AF; therefore, we integrated multiple variables to develop a clinical prediction model for recurrence, represented as a nomogram. Nomogram, a visual clinical predictive model that facilitates rapid complex calculations without the need for computers or calculators, provides a scientific basis for clinical decision making.It is grounded in multivariable regression analysis, which synthesizes multiple variables to facilitate the rapid and efficient calculation of an individual’s risk of disease or survival probability [21].The present study employed rigorous statistical methods including backward stepwise selection, quantitative calibration assessment, and sensitivity analysis to ensure model reliability. A standardized method was used to accurately distinguish atrial flutter from pulmonary vein arrhythmias, ensuring the accuracy of endpoint definition.

Limitations

This study was a single-center retrospective analysis, including patients with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation who underwent first-time catheter ablation at our institution. First, the sample size was relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to broader patient populations. Second, the external validation of the nomogram was only conducted using an internal validation cohort rather than external independent cohorts, underscoring the need for large-scale, multicenter prospective studies to further validate and optimize the established nomogram model. Third, the follow-up period was relatively brief, precluding the capture of late-term recurrence events. Accordingly, future investigations should extend the follow-up duration to 3, 5, and 10 years to comprehensively assess the long-term recurrence probability of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation following catheter ablation.

Conclusions

In this single-center retrospective study, we identified age, left atrial diameter, hypertension, and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation disease duration as independent risk factors for recurrence after fluoroscopy-free radiofrequency ablation in PAF patients. The nomogram was developed using backward stepwise selection and validated by quantitative calibration metrics and sensitivity analysis. The nomogram model constructed by integrating these four readily available clinical factors exhibits good discriminatory ability and calibration, and can effectively stratify the recurrence risk of PAF patients undergoing fluoroscopy-free RFA. This preliminary predictive tool may assist clinicians in formulating personalized preprocedural selection and postprocedural management strategies for PAF patients. Larger-scale, multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up durations are needed for external validation and further optimization of this model.

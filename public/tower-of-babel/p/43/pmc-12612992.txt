Regenerative effects of PRP-impregnated collagen-gelatin sponge in a full-thickness skin defect model
Authors: Hajime Matsumine, Hiroshi Fujimaki, Yuuki Hasegawa, Yosuke Niimi, Hiroyuki Sakurai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionArtificial dermis is an essential biomaterial for reconstructing full-thickness skin defects. Pelnac G Plus® is a collagen-gelatin sponge (CGS) that can be impregnated with basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), offering sustained release and proven clinical efficacy. However, the off-label use of bFGF by impregnation remains restricted under current pharmaceutical regulations in Japan. In this study, we evaluated the wound healing potential of autologous platelet-rich plasma (PRP) as an alternative bioactive substance when impregnated into CGS.MethodsFull-thickness skin defect models were created in nude rats and treated with artificial dermis alone (CGS group), artificial dermis + PRP (CGS + PRP group), or artificial dermis + bFGF (CGS + FGF group). Wounds were evaluated on postoperative day 14.ResultsOn postoperative day 14, the CGS + PRP group exhibited significantly greater collagen thickness and vascular density than the CGS group. Furthermore, the CGS + PRP group showed a smaller wound area than the CGS + FGF group (20.9 ± 14.3 mm2 vs. 51.5 ± 18.0 mm2, p < 0.001). The CGS + PRP group also demonstrated higher α-SMA expression compared to the CGS + FGF group (36.6 % ± 4.3 % vs. 23.9 % ± 8.1 %, p < 0.001), and immunohistochemical analysis revealed higher expression of VEGF and α-SMA, suggesting enhanced angiogenesis and fibroblast activity. PRP preserved the expression of TGF-β1 and α-SMA, supporting balanced tissue regeneration.ConclusionsOur findings demonstrate that bFGF and PRP exert distinct effects on wound healing in a secondary-intention model. bFGF effectively promoted dermis-like granulation tissue regeneration without accelerating contraction, supporting its use in anatomical sites where scar contracture must be avoided. In contrast, PRP enhanced wound contraction and ulcer area reduction, suggesting clinical utility in non-healing ulcers where delayed secondary healing is a major concern.

Introduction

Various pharmaceutical agents and medical devices are employed in the field of wound surgery, with artificial dermis recognized as a standard treatment option for full-thickness skin defects. Artificial dermal substitutes are widely regarded as highly effective biomaterials for reconstructing full-thickness skin loss caused by extensive burns, acute traumatic injuries, and reconstructive surgical procedures.

In 2013, Morimoto et al. developed a novel artificial dermis called Pelnac G Plus® (Gunze, Japan), which is a collagen-gelatin sponge (CGS) incorporating a drug delivery system for basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF). This artificial dermis was subsequently applied to chronic ulcers in a clinical trial [1]. The material comprises 10 % alkali-treated gelatin added to a conventional collagen sponge-based dermis. The alkali-treated gelatin binds to the positively charged bFGF and allows for its gradual release over a period of approximately 3 weeks as the gelatin degrades.

In Japan, the bFGF spray preparation (Fiblast® Spray, Kaken Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Japan) has already been approved and is widely used for skin ulcers. Its indications have been expanding in recent years, such as for degloving injuries of the extremities and deep dermal burns. Its therapeutic efficacy in promoting wound healing, particularly in traumatic avulsion injuries, has also been reported [2]. The application of CGS, which possesses a bFGF delivery system, to full-thickness skin defects is expected to shorten the time to the formation of dermis-like granulation tissue by promoting angiogenesis, thereby potentially reducing infection risk and shortening the disease course compared to artificial dermis alone [3,4].

However, there is a significant issue with the off-label use of bFGF when combined with artificial dermis. The approved use of the bFGF preparation is direct topical spraying onto the ulcer bed; its impregnation into an artificial dermis before application constitutes off-label use. Therefore, in Japan, prior approval for such methods must be obtained from the institutional review board or equivalent ethics committee at each facility.

To address this issue, we focused on platelet-rich plasma (PRP) as an alternative bioactive substance that can be impregnated into CGS. PRP is an autologous bioactive preparation that contains high levels of growth factors such as platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). It has been reported to promote tissue regeneration and angiogenesis in both orthopedic and plastic surgery fields [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. Importantly, PRP offers the advantage of being an autologous preparation, potentially reducing concerns about immunogenicity and foreign material reactions.

In recent years, the combination of biomaterials and growth factors to synergistically enhance their therapeutic effects has attracted increasing attention. However, few systematic studies have investigated the mechanisms or optimal usage of the combined use of artificial dermis and PRP. Accordingly, we experimentally evaluated the wound healing-promoting effects of the combined application of CGS and PRP and histologically compared it with artificial dermis alone and with a group treated with additional bFGF. This study aimed to determine whether PRP could serve as a clinically viable alternative to bFGF for enhancing artificial dermis-mediated wound healing.

Materials and methods

Animals

To minimize the influence of immune responses during the wound healing process, 8-week-old male nude rats (F344/NJcl-rnu/rnu, 200–250 g), purchased from CLEA Japan Inc. (Tokyo, Japan), were used. The animals were divided into three groups: an artificial dermis alone group (CGS group, n = 8), an artificial dermis + PRP group (CGS + PRP group, n = 8), and an artificial dermis + bFGF group (CGS + FGF group, n = 12) (Fig. 1). During the experimental period, the rats were housed under controlled environmental conditions with a temperature of 23 °C ± 2 °C, humidity of 55 % ± 10 %, and a 12-h light/dark cycle. Food and water were provided ad libitum. All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (Approval No. 2023-0119) and conducted in accordance with the institutional guidelines for animal experimentation.Fig. 1Schematic overview of the experimental procedure Whole blood was collected and processed using a double-spin method to isolate platelet-rich plasma (PRP). Artificial dermis

CGS (Medical Device Approval Number: 23000BZX00097000), a collagen-based artificial dermis (Class III medical device), was used. It has a bilayer structure with a 0.12-mm upper silicone sheet and a 3-mm lower collagen sponge containing 10 % alkali-treated gelatin. The porous structure of the sponge promotes cellular infiltration and angiogenesis. In this study, 1-cm2 pieces of CGS were prepared and applied to the wounds with the silicone layer intact.

Preparation of PRP

A total of 30 mL of whole blood was collected from two healthy volunteers. Acid citrate dextrose solution A (ACD-A, 3 mL) was used as an anticoagulant. PRP was prepared using the TriCell PRP Separation and Concentration Kit (REV-MED) through a two-step centrifugation process. The first centrifugation (400×g for 10 min) separated the red blood cells, while the second centrifugation (800×g for 10 min) concentrated the PRP. The PRP was used within 2 h of preparation. The collection of blood samples was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional ethics committee (Approval No. 190501). Written informed consent was obtained from both volunteers prior to participation.

Cytokine measurement by multiplex ELISA

Cytokine concentrations in PRP samples derived from healthy volunteers were measured using the Luminex Human Discovery Assay (R&D Systems) based on Luminex multiplex technology. The target cytokines were bFGF, VEGF-A, and PDGF-AA. Measurements were performed using the Bio-Plex 200 System (Bio-Rad). PRP samples were analyzed immediately after collection, after being diluted 10-fold using Calibrator Diluent RD6-52 (included in the kit). A 5-parameter logistic (5-PL) standard curve was used for data analysis with Bio-Plex Manager software.

Surgical procedure

Under inhalation anesthesia with isoflurane (induction at 5 %, maintenance at 2 %), the dorsal area of each rat was shaved and disinfected with povidone-iodine. A full-thickness skin defect (1 cm2) was created using surgical scissors. After confirming hemostasis, the following treatments were applied to the defect: the CGS group received saline-soaked CGS, the CGS + PRP group received CGS soaked in 1 mL of PRP, and the CGS + FGF group received CGS soaked in 1 mL of bFGF. The bFGF concentration was 100 μg/mL (bFGF Spray, Kaken Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Japan). The grafts were fixed using the tie-over method, in which sterile gauze was secured with 3-0 silk sutures to ensure close contact with the wound. All procedures were performed under sterile conditions, and the animals were housed individually after surgery. On postoperative day 14, the tie-over dressing was removed and tissues were harvested for evaluation.

Wound area measurement

On postoperative day 14, vertical photographs of the wounds were taken using a compact digital camera (Tough TG-6, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Wound areas were calculated using ImageJ software (ver. 1.53, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Histological evaluation

On day 14, the rats were euthanized and the wound areas were harvested, including 2 mm of surrounding normal skin. The tissues were fixed in 10 % neutral buffered formalin for 24 h, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 5-μm-thick serial slices perpendicular to the wound surface.

Evaluation of dermis-like granulation tissue

Masson's trichrome staining was performed following standard protocols. Whole wound images were captured under a light microscope (BX-53, Olympus) at 50 × magnification. The wound was divided into five equal regions from center to periphery, and the thickness of dermis-like granulation tissue in the three central regions was measured at the center of each region using ImageJ. The average thickness of the three regions was calculated.

Immunohistochemical evaluation

Immunohistochemical staining was performed using antibodies against von Willebrand factor (vWF), TGF-β1, VEGF, and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA). After deparaffinization and rehydration, antigen retrieval was performed. For vWF, sections were treated with 0.1 % trypsin (Vector Laboratories, Burlingame, CA) at 37 °C for 15 min in phosphate-buffered saline. For VEGF, autoclave treatment was performed at 121 °C for 10 min in 0.01 M citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3 % hydrogen peroxide for 15 min at room temperature. Non-specific protein binding was blocked using Blocking One (#03953-95, Nacalai Tesque, Kyoto, Japan) for 10 min at room temperature.

The following primary antibodies were applied overnight at 4 °C: rabbit polyclonal anti-vWF (1:1000, ab6994, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), rabbit polyclonal anti-TGF-β1 (1:50, Y241, Yanaihara Laboratories, Fujinomiiya, Japan), mouse monoclonal anti-VEGF (1:90, MAB308-05, Diagnostic BioSystems, Pleasanton, CA), and mouse monoclonal anti-α-SMA (1:500, M0851, Dako, Glostrup, Denmark).Secondary antibodies were applied for 30 min at room temperature, as follows: HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (HISOFINE #424144, Nichirei Biosciences, Tokyo Japan) for vWF and TGF-β1; and HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse/rabbit IgG (H + L) (HISOFINE #414191, Nichirei Biosciences) for VEGF and α-SMA. Color development was achieved using 3,3-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAB) for 1 min, followed by counterstaining with Mayer's hematoxylin for 10 s.

Quantitative analysis of immunostaining

The number of vWF-positive blood vessels was counted in a randomly selected 500 × 500-μm field within the wound area. For TGF-β1, VEGF, and α-SMA, the percentage of positively stained area in the whole tissue section was calculated. All image analyses were performed using ImageJ.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's multiple comparison test was used for comparisons among the three groups. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 6.00 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA).

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the effects of PRP applied in combination with the artificial dermis CGS on wound healing. The results demonstrated that PRP (CGS + PRP group) led to significantly greater thickness of dermis-like granulation tissue and increased expression of the angiogenic factor VEGF compared to artificial dermis alone (CGS group). Furthermore, when compared with bFGF (CGS + FGF group), PRP exhibited equivalent or superior effects on several parameters, including wound contraction, vascular density, and levels of myofibroblasts (α-SMA-positive cells).

PRP contains high concentrations of several growth factors involved in wound healing, such as PDGF, VEGF, and TGF-β1, which have multifaceted effects, including angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and fibroblast activation [9]. The PRP group also exhibited significantly higher VEGF expression and vessel density than the CGS group, suggesting that PRP may promote neovascularization and tissue regeneration in the wound area.

Histological evaluation using Masson's trichrome staining revealed significantly thicker dermis-like granulation tissue in the PRP group than in the CGS group. This suggests that the growth factors contained in PRP promoted fibroblast activity and increased collagen production, thereby contributing to the thickening of the dermis-like tissue [10,11]. This is consistent with the clinical findings of Lv et al. [12], who demonstrated that the combined use of artificial dermis and PRP accelerated the formation of granulation tissue and improved wound closure in refractory wounds. Our experimental findings support these clinical observations. bFGF is a widely used clinical agent for promoting wound healing that has been reported to stimulate angiogenesis and fibroblast proliferation [4]. In our study, the CGS + FGF group also showed increased vascular density compared to the CGS group.

However, one of the notable findings in this study is that, while the CGS + FGF group showed significantly larger wound areas, the CGS + PRP group demonstrated smaller wound areas with favorable wound contraction. Regarding the anti-scarring effects of bFGF, previous studies by Ono et al. [13,14] and Akasaka et al. [15] have suggested that reduced myofibroblast differentiation via suppression of the TGF-β1 signaling pathway may be involved.

TGF-β1 plays a central role in promoting fibroblast proliferation, stimulating collagen synthesis, and remodeling the extracellular matrix [16]. α-SMA serves as a marker of myofibroblast differentiation [17]. In our study, the CGS + FGF group exhibited reduced expression of TGF-β1 and α-SMA, which may have contributed to the decreased wound contraction. In contrast, the CGS + PRP group maintained TGF-β1 and α-SMA expression, suggesting that PRP promoted wound healing without excessively suppressing contraction. The multiple growth factors contained in PRP likely interact synergistically during the wound healing process, facilitating appropriate wound closure.

Additionally, whereas the clinical use of bFGF products is often limited by regulatory and reimbursement issues, PRP, being an autologous biological product, offers greater ease of clinical application from ethical and legal standpoints. In summary, our findings demonstrate that bFGF and PRP exert distinct effects on wound healing in a secondary-intention model. bFGF effectively promoted dermis-like granulation tissue regeneration without accelerating contraction, confirming its role as a positive control and highlighting its potential for use in anatomical sites where scar contracture must be avoided. In contrast, PRP enhanced wound contraction and ulcer area reduction, suggesting clinical utility in non-healing ulcers where delayed secondary healing is a major concern. Together, these results indicate that bFGF and PRP act through different mechanisms and should not be considered simple alternatives, but rather complementary approaches suited to different clinical contexts.

Furthermore, this study demonstrated the potential of combining artificial dermis and PRP to effectively enhance angiogenesis and the formation of dermis-like tissue. Conclusions

This study showed that, in combination with CGS, bFGF and PRP exert distinct effects on wound healing. bFGF promoted dermis-like granulation tissue regeneration without accelerating contraction, while PRP enhanced wound contraction and ulcer area reduction. These findings suggest that CGS combined with either agent provides complementary therapeutic options suited to different clinical situations.

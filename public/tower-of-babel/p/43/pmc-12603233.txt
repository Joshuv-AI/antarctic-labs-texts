Enhanced teaching team evaluation system for vocational colleges
Authors: Qiming Tian, Wanle Chi, Dafeng Gong, Yuwen Shi, Lili Shi, Huiling Chen, Sudan Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractConstructing an evaluation system for teaching teams is very important to promote the reform of teaching evaluation in higher vocational colleges. However, there is no existing artificial intelligence−based evaluation system for teaching teams that can accurately predict the teaching evaluations in higher vocational colleges. The artemisinin optimization (AO) algorithm draws its core inspiration from the treatment process of malaria with artemisinin. It simulates the human body as a “closed space”, with the malaria parasites considered as hidden "solutions”. The algorithm mimics the ongoing process of artemisinin medicine eradicating the malaria parasites within the body. But AO tend to fall into local optima without finding the global optimum. Therefore, we propose an intelligent AO version to predict teaching team. AO has been enhanced by adding the comprehensive learning mechanism and the dispersed foraging mechanism, that is CLDMAO. The CLDMAO is benchmarked against various cutting-edge algorithms using the CEC 2017 test functions to validate its effectiveness and stability. The results indicate that CLDMAO ranks first in the majority of the test functions. What’s more, the binary CLDMAO-KNN model performs excellently for the evaluation system for teaching teams in terms of error rate, fitness, and the number of features. Through this model, factors for establishing an evaluation system can be identified within the dataset, enabling the construction of a complete evaluation system and further analysis of the potential correlations between various factors.Keywords: Artemisinin optimization, Global optimization, Comprehensive learning, Dispersed foraging strategy, Teaching quality evaluationSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

High vocational education is a vital cornerstone for China in fostering high teaching quality as well as technical and skilled professionals. It has seen rapid development and garnered significant attention1. Recently, accelerating the development of modern vocational education has been given heightened priority. It is suggested that we should adhere to the principle of moral education, closely align this with the demands of technological advancements and industrial upgrades, continuously enhance the quality of vocational education teaching, and nurture more technical and skilled talents who possess both political integrity and professional competence for the modernization drive. Evaluating teaching quality is a crucial aspect of classroom instruction management and plays an essential role in overall teaching administration2. In October 2020, the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China and The State Council released the General Plan for Deepening Education Evaluation Reform in the New Era and pointed out the direction for the reform of teaching evaluation in higher vocational colleges. For the construction of teachers’ teaching innovation team, clear teaching quality evaluation objectives and standards should be established to ensure that the evaluation closely conforms to the teaching core and truly reflects the internal needs of education and teaching3.

As for the connotation of “teaching quality”, there is a view that teaching quality is mainly reflected in whether the knowledge taught by teachers and the learning results of students can achieve the preset teaching objectives4. Another perspective views teaching quality as the aggregation of various attributes and features of the teaching process and its outcomes, which can be used to assess whether the teaching meets established standards. The comprehensive evaluation of teachers’ teaching quality in higher vocational colleges is a method based on educational evaluation theory and technology, which aims to comprehensively evaluate whether teachers’ teaching process and its results meet the preset quality standards. Its core purpose is to promote the continuous improvement of teaching quality and provide some kind of qualification certification for the evaluated teachers5. This kind of comprehensive evaluation is characterized by its integrity, comprehensiveness and dynamic, it is not limited to the evaluation of teaching results, but from multiple dimensions, and multiple stages of teachers’ teaching activities for all-round consideration. Through this method, teachers’ teaching level can be more accurately understood, and then targeted improvement suggestions can be provided for them to optimize the teaching effect.

Some perspectives suggest that teaching quality is the comprehensive framework of talent cultivation standards, representing a system of ongoing development and enhancement6. Helms-Lorenz and Visscher explored various interpretations of developing a comprehensive framework for understanding it in the context of educational practice. Also, they discussed the advantages and disadvantages of four different methods for assessing teaching quality7. Song used particle swarm optimization-least square support vector machine (PSO-LSSVM) to evaluate the teaching quality8. Xiao et al. conducted a survey on the current state of teaching quality evaluation for university teachers, developed an evaluation index system for economics and management courses, and proposed strategies to enhance teaching quality assessments9. Hou introduced a method for evaluating online classroom teaching quality based on advanced data mining techniques10. By selecting evaluation indicators for teaching quality from three perspectives—experts, teachers, and students—a comprehensive evaluation system for flipped classroom teaching quality in higher education has been established. These indicators are used as inputs for the support vector machine (SVM) learning process to create an evaluation model11. Li et al. proposed an artificial intelligencE−based model for the characteristics of college students’ teaching ability12. Ai and Feng established a teaching quality evaluation of higher vocational education by BP neural network algorithm13. Lu et al. proposed an RBF neural network teaching evaluation model, using the genetic algorithm (GA) to optimize the initial weights of the RBF neural network14. Zhong analyzed the genetic algorithm (GA) and chose the SVM algorithm to evaluate teaching quality15. Chen et al. proposed a BP neural network model based on improved PSO (IPSO) to enhance the effectiveness and accuracy of teaching quality evaluation16. A deep belief network-based online teaching quality evaluation method was proposed by Liang, achieving a high accuracy rate17.

Our study presents a practical and transparent system that analyzes routinely collected tabular indicators to produce an easy‑to‑interpret score and a ranked list of contributing factors. The approach uses simple normalization and data‑driven weighting, and it reports variability across repeated runs to convey result stability. Practitioners can adapt the system by mapping their local indicators to the provided template; no programming or specialized hardware is required. The outputs are designed to support action planning and communication—helping educators, clinicians, and administrators prioritize resources, track progress over time, and explain decisions with clear evidence. Because the pipeline is domain-agnostic, it can be deployed in non-computer-science settings with minimal configuration.

Over the past few decades, meta-heuristic algorithms (MAs) have seen significant development. Typically, MAs are characterized by their simple structure, ease of implementation, strong optimization capabilities, and minimal dependency on the mathematical properties of the optimization problem18. Some MAs draw inspiration from natural phenomena, including artemisinin optimization (AO)19, parrot optimizer (PO)20, Runge Kutta optimizer (RUN)21, Harris hawks optimization (HHO)22, fata morgana algorithm (FATA)23, satus-based optimization , hunger games search (HGS)24, polar lights optimizer (PLO)25, moss growth optimization (MGO)26, status-based optimization (SBO)18, slime mould algorithm (SMA)27, colony predation algorithm (CPA)28, human evolutionary optimization algorithm29, love evolution algorithm (LEA)30, the weighted mean of vectors (INFO)31 and modified teaching–learning-based optimization (MTLBO)32. These MAs have made significant progress in fields such as feature selection33, image segmentation34, wind fault diagnosis35, clinical diagnosis36, parameter optimization37, and energy optimization38.

Yuan et al. proposed a new AO algorithm which was inspired by his raw artemisinin-based medication for malaria in 202412. The original AO encounters challenges such as low convergence accuracy and a tendency to settle on locally optimal solutions. Research suggests that there is still room for enhancing the optimization performance of both AO and its improved variants. Therefore, our team collected a large amount of data through questionnaires in 2024 about teachers’ perspectives on constructing an evaluation system for teaching teams. By organizing and summarizing these data for exploration and analysis, we aim to establish an intelligent improved AO predictive algorithm, which is named CLDMAO. By introducing the comprehensive learning strategy and dispersed foraging strategy to the modified AO, a new balance between global and local search capabilities is achieved. As a supervised classification method, the K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) algorithm is worth noting that operates based on a voting mechanism39. In this study, we optimize the KNN classifier using the binary version of CLDMAO and build a teaching quality evaluation prediction model of bCLDMAO-KNN. This model allows us to identify the factors from the dataset that contribute to building an evaluation system, construct a complete evaluation system, and further analyze the potential correlations between various factors. The proposed methodology

This section presents the CLDMAO algorithm, which incorporates the comprehensive learning mechanism and the dispersed foraging mechanism into the enhanced AO algorithm to boost its performance. To adapt the continuous CLDMAO algorithm for discrete feature selection problems, we develop a binary version, termed bCLDMAO. This enables solution encoding as binary strings, where each position directly represents the inclusion or exclusion of a feature. The bCLDMAO is then coupled with a KNN classifier in wrapper mode to select the subset of features that optimizes prediction accuracy for the teaching team evaluation system. KNN classification

The KNN classifier is a straightforward, non-parametric learning technique that has demonstrated significant effectiveness in tasks related to function classification and approximation. It is well-regarded for its high completion rates and precise classification performance, as highlighted in various research41–43. In this investigation, the model employs an instancE−based learning strategy, where the classification of a new instance is determined by the majority vote of the class labels of its (K) nearest neighbors. This approach means that the classification of a new data point is based on its resemblance to the training points, with the class label of the nearest training point being selected. Experimental methodology

Function optimization validation

In this study, ensuring the fairness and rationality of the experiment is crucial to accurately evaluate the effectiveness of the new method. To maintain these principles of impartiality and validity, the entire global optimization experiment of this study uses the IEEE CEC2017 function set as the test suite. This benchmark dataset is widely recognized as a tool that facilitates the evaluation of various methods’ performance against established standards. What’s more, to demonstrate that the improved algorithm performance is due to the new design structure and not just the stacking strategy, this paper uses the number of evaluations instead of the number of iterations. The research employs the IEEE CEC 2017 functions test as test sets to evaluate the performance of CLDMAO. These functions contain a variety of highly complex mathematical optimization tasks, making them frequently used to evaluate the overall efficacy of an algorithm.

This study shows a population size of 30 individuals and a maximum evaluation of 300,000. To reduce potential confusion, the algorithms are independently tested over 30 times on these benchmark functions. Numerical results of these methods are presented, with emphasis on the mean value (Avg) and standard deviation (Std) of the optimal function values. To analyze the experimental results, we employ two different mathematical-statistical methods: the Wilcoxon signed rank test (WSRT) and the Friedman test (FT). WSRT assesses whether there are significant differences between comparison methods based on P-values. We report the mean and standard deviation (Avg ± Std) of the final objective values over multiple independent runs for every CEC 2017 function and for all comparator algorithms. For each function, we also provide the per-algorithm rank; aggregated average ranks across all functions are summarized with a Friedman test, together with Wilcoxon signed-rank p-values.

A p-value less than 0.05 indicates that the difference between the two methods is statistically significant. Otherwise, the difference is not considered significant. In this paper, to clearly show the superiority of the method, " + " is used to indicate that CLDMAO is superior to the comparison method, "-" is used to indicate that CLDMAO has poor performance, and " = " is used to indicate that there is little difference between the two methods. Additionally, these tests are conducted in MATLAB R2018b on a Microsoft Windows 11 platform. The system features a 12th-generation Intel ® Core ™i7-12700H CPU with a clock speed of 2.30 GHz and 16.0 GB of RAM.

Feature selection on the collected dataset

The collected dataset comes from 22 vocational colleges, including 10 Double First-Class universities. Among the 1162 members surveyed, 584 are from Double First-Class universities. There are 73 features in the collected dataset. This paper explores the significance of these 73 attributes and their interrelationships and develops a decision-support prediction model. This paper utilizes the binary CLDMAO frameworks to select a good feature subset for training the model. Subsequently, the trained bCLDMAO-KNN model is validated to predict the teaching quality evaluation of higher vocational education, which is based on an analysis of both the planning and execution intentions. By combining the bCLDMAO-KNN method with various other machine learning methods, including bSMA-KNN, bRUN-KNN, bMVO-KNN, bALCPSO-KNN, bIGWO-KNN, bDFSMA-KNN48, bISMA-KNN, bINFO-KNN, bGBO-KNN classification models to evaluate the efficiency of bCLDMAO-KNN method.

To ensure consistency, the parameters for the remaining classifiers are configured according to established conventions. To evaluate classification accuracy, we conduct an unbiased tenfold cross-validation (CV) test. This procedure reserves a portion of the dataset for testing and uses the remainder for training the classifier. Crucially, the number of validation instances in each subset mirrors the size of the test dataset. The KNN classifier successfully executes the classification task with a field size (k) set to 1.

All mentioned algorithms are executed within a single main function to guarantee fairness. Each method shares the same random initialization within this main function, ensuring equitable conditions for comparison. The experiment is conducted with 20 search agents, 10 folds, and a maximum of 50 evaluations under identical experimental settings. We conduct tenfold cross-validation and report, for each fold, the selected feature subset size, error (and accuracy), best fitness, and time cost for bCLDMAO-KNN. Aggregated mean ± std across folds are provided. We compare against wrapper baselines (bSMA, bRUN, bMVO, bALCPSO, bIGWO, bDFSMA) using the same protocol and report Avg ± Std, average rank, Wilcoxon p-values.

Discussion

Experiment analysis

We quantified the linkage between optimization quality and classification performance on the collected dataset. Across the 10 CV folds (Table 10), the best fitness attained by bCLDMAO in each fold is strongly associated with the KNN error. On average, bCLDMAO-KNN selects 15.5 of 73 features (78.8% reduction) with a mean error of 0.054 (SD = 0.019). The correlation between subset size and error is negative but non-significant, indicating that classification gains primarily arise from improved optimization of the objective rather than subset size alone. To clarify, our enhanced optimization algorithm (CLDMAO) directly improves feature selection and classification performance in the following ways:

Quantitative impact of enhancements

The integration of the comprehensive learning and dispersed foraging mechanisms in CLDMAO increases population diversity and global search capability. Experimentally, bCLDMAO-KNN achieves the lowest average error (0.054163) and the lowest average best fitness value (0.062072) among all compared methods (see Table 9). Additionally, bCLDMAO-KNN identifies the smallest average subset of features (15.5 out of 73), demonstrating efficient feature selection.

Direct link between enhancements and outcomes

The improved search ability of CLDMAO prevents premature convergence and enables better exploration of the search space, which, in the context of binary feature selection, results in finding more relevant and informative feature subsets for classification. This leads to higher prediction accuracy and reduced feature redundancy, as evidenced by the superior performance metrics.

Controlled comparisons

All compared algorithms were evaluated under identical conditions (number of agents, folds, evaluations, etc.). bCLDMAO-KNN outperforms other wrapper-based feature selection algorithms by both error rate and feature subset size, demonstrating the benefit of the proposed enhancements.

To sum up, the enhancements to the optimization algorithm are quantitatively shown to produce better feature subsets, resulting in improved classification accuracy and model simplicity.

Teaching quality is the key factor of higher education. Studying this topic often requires assessing the overall structure of teaching quality through self-reporting. However, these tools have been criticized for a lack of consistency in teachers’ and students’ reporting of teaching practices49. Evaluating teaching quality is highly challenging. Each teacher brings their training, experience, style, and abilities to the classroom, making it extremely difficult to establish a fair indicator of teaching quality50. To effectively extract multidimensional data for teaching quality evaluation and accurately evaluate college teaching quality, Chen et al. proposed an SVM classification algorithm for quality evaluation of network distance teaching39.

In our study, the data comes from 22 vocational colleges, including 10 Double First-Class universities. Among the 1162 staff members surveyed, 584 are from Double First-Class universities. According to the binary version of bCLDMAO-KNN model, four features—F71 (student/teacher participation in and awards from international competitions), F4 (professional title structure), F6 (full-time and part-time teacher structure), and F51 (employment rate in related fields)—show significant differences between Double First-Class and non-Double First-Class universities. Double First-Class universities place greater importance on international competitions and awards for their teaching teams, the rationality of the professional title structure, the proportion of specialized teachers to enterprise part-time teachers, and the alignment of student employment with their fields of study.

One important output indicator in the Ministry of Education’s performance evaluation system for Double First-Class universities is “enhancing internationalization”. This indicator assesses the breadth and depth of international exchanges and cooperation within institutions. It is divided into scale indicators and quality indicators, with the latter including metrics such as the number of student and teacher awards in international skill competitions. Under the guidance of the Ministry of Education’s policies, Double First-Class universities have established policies for international competition construction and rewards, whereas non-Double First-Class universities do not have this assessment content. This leads to a significant difference in how Double First-Class universities’ teaching teams and non-Double First-Class universities’ teaching teams perceive the importance of F71 (student/teacher participation in and awards from international competitions).

Similarly, the performance evaluation output indicators for Double First-Class universities include "building high-level dual-qualified teams", focusing on assessing the effectiveness of building dual-qualified teaching teams. This also includes scale indicators and quality indicators, with scale indicators primarily covering the proportion of full-time teachers with senior professional titles and the internal class hours of part-time teachers. This shows that Double First-Class universities place more emphasis on the promotion of teachers’ professional titles and the role of enterprise part-time teachers in the teaching team. Consequently, there is a significant difference in how Double First-Class universities’ teaching teams and non-Double First-Class universities’ teaching teams perceive the importance of F4 (professional title structure) and F6 (full-time and part-time teacher structure).

While the performance evaluation output indicators for Double First-Class universities do not include an indicator for "student employment in related fields", they do include an indicator for "level of school-enterprise cooperation", which focuses on assessing the closeness and depth of school-enterprise cooperation. Scale indicators here include the number of order classes, industrial colleges, jointly developed courses, jointly developed textbooks, teacher enterprise practice bases, jointly built internal and external training bases, jointly built research institutes and R&D centers, and productive training bases. By meeting these indicators, Double First-Class universities emphasize cooperation with relevant enterprises in teaching, internships, and employment, which in turn promotes the employment rate in related fields. This is why there is a significant difference in how Double First-Class universities’ teaching teams and non-Double First-Class universities’ teaching teams perceive the importance of F51 (employment rate in related fields).

In a word, the impact of the Ministry of Education’s performance evaluation for Double First-Class universities has led to more investment and requirements in areas such as internationalization, teaching teams, and school-enterprise cooperation, influencing the focus and perceptions of the teaching teams.

Advantages of proposed model

High Accuracy and Effective Feature Selection: The system demonstrates superior performance in identifying a minimal yet highly discriminative subset of features (reducing 73 attributes to an average of 15.5) from a complex dataset, leading to high classification accuracy and low error rates as shown in Table 9.

Provides Actionable Insights: Beyond simple prediction, the system identifies key factors that differentiate teaching team evaluation priorities (e.g., F71, F4, F6, F51). As discussed, these factors align with policy drivers like the “Double First-Class” initiative, making the results interpretable and useful for institutional decision-making.

Robustness and Stability: The low standard deviation in error rates and fitness values across the tenfold cross-validation (Table 10) confirms the model’s stable and reliable performance, ensuring that the findings are not due to chance.

Limitations

Computational Complexity: As a wrapper-based metaheuristic approach, the bCLDMAO-KNN model is computationally more intensive than simpler filter methods or standalone classifiers. Its time cost, while acceptable for this offline analysis, may be a limitation for real-time applications.

Generalizability: The model was trained and validated on data from Chinese higher vocational colleges. Its performance and the relevance of the selected features may differ in other educational contexts (e.g., different countries or types of universities) and would require further validation on more diverse datasets.

Data Dependency: The system’s effectiveness is contingent on the quality and scope of the initial questionnaire data. Any biases or limitations in the data collection process will inevitably be reflected in the model’s output.

Conclusion

In summary, this study introduces a comprehensive learning and dispersed foraging modified AO, named CLDMAO, aimed at establishing a comprehensive evaluation framework. To enhance the model’s efficiency and robustness, a comprehensive learning strategy and dispersed foraging mechanism are integrated into the modified AO algorithm. The enhanced AO’s global optimality is assessed using IEEE CEC 2017 functions. The results indicate that the proposed algorithm outperforms several other optimization methods across various test. Then, the comparative analysis of bCLDMAO-KNN and the other 9 feature selection methods shows higher classification accuracy and stability in constructing a teacher-team evaluation system.

In the future, CLDMAO’s potential could stretch into various areas, including image segmentation, multi-objective optimizations, and route planning. It could also benefit from incorporating strategies like genetic algorithms, Levy flights, and opposition-based methods. Yet, its computational efficiency needs enhancement to be viable for real-time uses, such as cloud resource allocation or traffic management. Improving CLDMAO’s speed is key to unlocking its full potential across sectors and is a promising area for further teacher-team evaluation system research, allowing for its better real-world application.

Association between preoperative neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and length of stay in pediatric patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Ming Liu, Yunpeng Gou, Ping Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) has demonstrated diagnostic utility in appendicitis, its predictive value for postoperative recovery outcomes remains uncertain. This investigation sought to examine the association between preoperative NLR and length of stay (LOS) in pediatric patients who underwent laparoscopic appendectomy.MethodsWe performed a retrospective analysis of 815 pediatric patients (aged < 18 years) who underwent laparoscopic appendectomy between January 2021 and December 2022 at Suining Central Hospital. The primary exposure variable was preoperative NLR, with postoperative LOS serving as the main outcome measure. Multiple regression models were established, with adjustments for demographic characteristics, clinical parameters, and surgical variables.ResultsFollowing comprehensive adjustment, each 10-unit increment in NLR was associated with a 0.19-day prolongation in LOS (95% CI: 0.02, 0.36; P = 0.025). Stratified analyses demonstrated significant positive correlations in male patients, cases without appendiceal fecalith, patients without drainage tubes, and those with uncomplicated appendicitis (all P < 0.05). Smooth curve analysis revealed a consistent positive association between NLR and LOS throughout the entire spectrum of NLR values.ConclusionPreoperative NLR demonstrated a positive association with postoperative LOS in pediatric patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy, indicating its potential value as a predictive marker for hospital resource utilization and perioperative management optimization.Keywords: Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, Length of stay, Laparoscopic appendectomy, Pediatric appendicitis, Postoperative outcomes

Introduction

Acute appendicitis remains one of the most prevalent surgical emergencies in the pediatric population [1], with laparoscopic appendectomy established as the standard therapeutic approach owing to its minimal invasiveness and superior outcomes [2–5]. Nevertheless, postoperative recovery patterns, particularly length of stay (LOS), demonstrate substantial heterogeneity among pediatric patients. Prolonged hospitalization not only increases healthcare costs but may also signify underlying complications or impaired recovery [6, 7], emphasizing the importance of identifying reliable predictive biomarkers.

The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), calculated from routine complete blood count parameters, reflects systemic inflammatory status and host immune competence through the dynamic interaction between neutrophils and lymphocytes, establishing its utility as a marker for evaluating surgical risk and recovery patterns [8–10]. Elevated NLR levels indicate an increased inflammatory response [10], and previous research [11] has validated its diagnostic accuracy in differentiating complicated appendicitis. However, its potential utility as a predictor of postoperative recovery duration warrants further investigation.

This investigation aimed to evaluate the association between preoperative NLR and postoperative LOS in pediatric patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy. Through examining NLR as a potential predictive biomarker, we sought to determine its clinical utility in identifying patients at risk for extended hospitalization, thereby facilitating targeted interventions and optimal resource allocation.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This retrospective investigation was conducted with approval from the Ethics Committee of Suining Central Hospital (approval number: KYLLKS20240096, approval date: July 19, 2024). We analyzed clinical data from pediatric patients who underwent laparoscopic appendectomy at the Department of Pediatric Surgery during the period from January 2021 through December 2022.

Study participants were selected according to the following inclusion criteria: (1) age less than 18 years; (2) pathologically confirmed diagnosis of acute appendicitis; and (3) availability of complete clinical data for analysis. Exclusion criteria included the following conditions: (1) histologically normal appendix on pathological examination; (2) periappendiceal abscess managed initially with conservative therapy prior to surgical intervention; (3) presence of concurrent specific infections; (4) other intraoperative diagnoses (including Meckel’s diverticulum, intestinal volvulus, gynecological disorders, or other acute abdominal pathologies); (5) antibiotic administration before hospital admission; or (6) systemic diseases or severe comorbidities.

Acute appendicitis is categorized into uncomplicated and complicated forms. Uncomplicated appendicitis is characterized by localized inflammation without evidence of necrosis or perforation, whereas complicated appendicitis includes gangrenous or perforated cases with necrosis and may be associated with abscesses, phlegmon, or fluid accumulation [12–14]. In this study, uncomplicated appendicitis included cases of congestive and suppurative appendicitis, while cases exhibiting gangrenous changes or perforation were classified as complicated appendicitis.

Variables

The primary outcome measure was postoperative LOS, with NLR serving as the principal exposure variable. Based on established literature [10], an NLR threshold of 3.0 has been recognized as a significant demarcation point for inflammatory response. Consequently, NLR was analyzed both as a continuous variable and as a dichotomous variable, with participants stratified into two groups: NLR < 3 (indicating mild or absent inflammation) and NLR ≥ 3 (indicating moderate to severe inflammation).

Data collection included demographic characteristics, clinical manifestations, laboratory parameters, and operative details. Demographic variables included sex, age, height, weight, and body mass index (BMI). Clinical parameters comprised symptom duration (interval from symptom onset to hospital presentation), gastrointestinal manifestations (emesis and diarrhea), peritoneal irritation signs, and admission temperature. Laboratory indices obtained at admission included white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil percentage, lymphocyte percentage, C-reactive protein (CRP), and serum albumin concentration. Operative parameters included procedural duration, appendiceal fecalith presence, appendicitis classification (uncomplicated versus complicated), and surgical drain placement.

Appendiceal fecalith presence was confirmed through a combination of preoperative imaging findings, intraoperative visualization, and histopathological examination. Surgical drain placement was implemented based on specific intraoperative findings, including gangrenous or perforated appendix, substantial peritoneal contamination, or technical complexities during the procedure. All drains were surgically placed during the appendectomy and were removed upon achieving minimal, clear drainage output.

Statistical analysis

Given the non-normal distribution of continuous variables, data were expressed as median with interquartile range (IQR) and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as absolute frequencies with corresponding percentages and analyzed using Pearson’s chi-square test.

To investigate the association between NLR and LOS, multiple generalized linear regression models were constructed. Potential confounding variables were identified and selected based on literature review and clinical significance [13, 15–22]. Three sequential regression models were constructed: Model 1 assessed the crude association; Model 2 adjusted for demographic variables including age and sex; Model 3 incorporated comprehensive adjustments for age, sex, BMI, body temperature, CRP, albumin, operative duration, appendiceal fecalith, surgical drain placement, and appendicitis classification. To further explore potential effect modifications, stratified analyses were conducted based on sex, presence of appendiceal fecalith, surgical drain placement, and appendicitis classification. The potential non-linear association between NLR and LOS was evaluated using smooth curve fitting derived from the fully adjusted model.

Statistical analyses were performed using Empower Stats (version 6.0; http://www.empowerstats.com) and R software (version 4.4.0; https://www.R-project.org). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value less than 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics of study population

As presented in Table 1, the study population comprised 815 patients, with 657 (80.61%) cases of uncomplicated appendicitis and 158 (19.39%) cases of complicated appendicitis. Patients with complicated appendicitis had significantly longer LOS compared to the uncomplicated group (9.00 vs. 6.00 days, P < 0.001), and were characterized by younger age and lower BMI (all P < 0.05). The complicated group exhibited higher frequencies of emesis and peritoneal irritation, accompanied by elevated inflammatory markers including WBC, neutrophil percentage, CRP, and markedly higher NLR (10.02 vs. 4.46, P < 0.001). Moreover, complicated appendicitis was associated with extended operative duration and higher rates of appendiceal fecalith and surgical drain placement (all P < 0.001).

Association between NLR and LOS

Multiple linear regression analysis revealed a significant positive association between NLR and LOS. As presented in Table 2, in the continuous variable analysis, each 10-unit increment in NLR was associated with a 0.72-day increase in LOS (95% CI: 0.52, 0.92). This association persisted after adjustment for age and sex (β = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.48, 0.88, P < 0.001), and remained significant following comprehensive adjustment (β = 0.19, 95% CI: 0.02, 0.36, P = 0.025). In the categorical analysis using an NLR threshold of 3, patients with NLR ≥ 3 exhibited significantly longer LOS compared to those with NLR < 3 in both the unadjusted model (β = 1.08, 95% CI: 0.77, 1.38, P < 0.001) and the demographic-adjusted model (β = 0.97, 95% CI: 0.66, 1.28, P < 0.001). However, this association was not statistically significant after comprehensive adjustment (β = 0.07, 95% CI: −0.19, 0.33, P = 0.609).

Subgroup analyses

Stratified analysis demonstrated heterogeneous associations between NLR and LOS across subgroups (Table 3). Significant positive associations were observed in male patients (P = 0.035), patients without appendiceal fecalith (P = 0.025), those without surgical drain (P = 0.033), and in uncomplicated appendicitis cases (P = 0.019). However, the interaction tests did not reach statistical significance across any subgroup (all P for interaction > 0.05).

Discussion

In this retrospective analysis of 815 pediatric patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy, we examined the association between preoperative NLR and postoperative LOS. Based on a comprehensive two-year consecutive case series with complete clinical data, our analysis demonstrated a significant positive association between NLR and LOS. Specifically, each 10-unit increment in NLR was associated with a 0.19-day prolongation in hospitalization (95% CI: 0.02, 0.36, P = 0.025), after comprehensive adjustment for potential confounding variables. In terms of clinical relevance, the effect size equates to an increase of approximately 4.6 h for every 10-unit rise in NLR, representing about 3.2% of the average LOS (6.0 days) among our patients. Although the absolute impact for an individual may seem modest, even minor differences in LOS can become significant in high-volume centers or when accumulated across many patients. Notably, in our cohort, 61 patients (7.5%) had NLR values ≥ 20, and 14 patients (1.7%) had NLR ≥ 30, indicating that extremely high NLR levels are relatively uncommon but not negligible in this population. Importantly, even smaller increases in NLR are associated with measurable changes in LOS: for every 5-unit increase, LOS increased by 0.09 days (about 2.2 h). While these changes might appear minor at the individual level, their cumulative impact may be meaningful for hospital resource management and patient flow efficiency, especially when considered alongside other risk factors.

However, when NLR was analyzed as a binary variable using the cutoff of 3 (≥ 3 vs. < 3), the association with LOS was no longer statistically significant after full adjustment for confounders (β = 0.07, 95% CI: −0.19, 0.33, P = 0.609). Therefore, conclusions regarding this specific cutoff should be interpreted with caution, and our findings suggest that NLR may be more informative as a continuous rather than binary predictor for LOS in clinical practice.

Our findings are consistent with previous studies [18, 23–25] demonstrating the predictive value of NLR in pediatric acute appendicitis, particularly regarding disease severity and postoperative outcomes. In accordance with prior research, we confirmed the association between elevated NLR and both increased disease severity and prolonged hospitalization. Our study extends beyond existing literature by precisely quantifying this relationship, establishing a 0.19-day increase in LOS per 10-unit increment in NLR, and elucidating subgroup-specific associations, notably stronger effects in male patients and uncomplicated appendicitis cases. While previous investigations [18, 23, 25] predominantly examined NLR’s diagnostic thresholds (e.g., 3.48, 6.35, 7.53) or its predictive capacity for complications, our analysis focused on its prognostic utility for LOS, thereby bridging the gap between preoperative assessment and postoperative recovery. These complementary findings collectively emphasize NLR’s clinical utility across both diagnostic and prognostic phases, supporting its incorporation into pediatric appendicitis management protocols for optimized resource utilization and patient care.

However, our findings contrast with those reported by Bayhan et al. [26], who observed no significant association between NLR and LOS in their mixed-age population. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in study populations and methodological approaches. Our study’s focus on an exclusively pediatric cohort (n = 815) and substantially larger sample size provided enhanced statistical power and enabled the detection of age-specific inflammatory responses, which might have been masked in Bayhan’s more limited, heterogeneous population (n = 103). Furthermore, our comprehensive stratified analyses revealed distinct subgroup effects, providing insights into NLR’s clinical applications that were not addressed in their investigation. These contrasting results underscore the importance of age-specific research and robust methodological frameworks in elucidating the clinical utility of inflammatory biomarkers such as NLR in surgical settings.

The NLR, a simple and cost-effective biomarker, has been widely recognized for its utility in predicting both the diagnosis and severity of appendicitis [23, 27, 28]. Several studies [29–31] have reported its diagnostic accuracy to surpass traditional markers such as WBC and CRP. Our investigation extends beyond previous research by establishing NLR’s utility not only as a preoperative risk stratification tool but also as a prognostic indicator for postoperative recovery. The elevation in NLR characteristically reflects enhanced inflammatory activity, particularly in acute appendicitis, where neutrophilia coupled with lymphopenia may indicate more pronounced inflammatory responses [23, 32]. This increased inflammatory state potentially contributes to increased operative complexity, elevated postoperative complication risks, and impaired recovery [23, 33], consequently prolonging hospitalization. Moreover, our restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated a consistent linear relationship between NLR and LOS, substantiating NLR’s role as a reliable prognostic marker in pediatric appendicitis management.

Our study demonstrates several methodological strengths. Primarily, our substantial sample size (n = 815) combined with stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria ensured adequate statistical power while minimizing potential confounding effects. The analytical framework was comprehensive and methodologically robust, incorporating both categorical and continuous analyses of NLR, thereby providing complementary perspectives on its association with LOS. Our investigation employed sequential multivariable regression models, with systematic adjustment for demographic, clinical, and operative parameters, enhancing the validity of our findings. Additionally, stratified analyses across patient subgroups revealed differential associations, while restricted cubic spline analysis confirmed the consistency of the NLR-LOS relationship throughout the observed NLR distribution. The inclusion of both complicated and uncomplicated appendicitis cases, coupled with detailed operative variables including appendiceal fecalith presence and surgical drain placement, enabled thorough assessment of NLR’s prognostic utility across diverse clinical scenarios.

Several limitations warrant consideration in our study. Primarily, the exclusion criteria, specifically patients with periappendiceal abscess requiring initial conservative management, concurrent specific infections, and underlying systemic conditions, may restrict the generalizability of our findings to these particular patient subgroups. Furthermore, as a single-institution retrospective investigation conducted within a Chinese population, the external validity of our results may be limited across different healthcare systems and ethnic populations. Therefore, while our findings provide important insights within this specific context, caution should be exercised when generalizing these results to other regions or populations. Future multi-center studies covering various healthcare settings and ethnic groups are needed to further confirm and expand upon our conclusions. The observational design inherently precludes the establishment of causality between NLR and LOS, permitting only associative conclusions. Although our analyses incorporated adjustments for multiple measurable confounding variables, certain unmeasurable factors, including postoperative complications, individual recovery capacity, individual variations in immune response and socioeconomic determinants, could not be controlled for. Moreover, our exclusive focus on laparoscopic appendectomy cases may limit the applicability of these findings to patients managed via open surgical approaches or non-operative strategies. Additionally, the lack of data on perioperative antibiotic protocols represents a potential limitation, as variations in antibiotic administration strategies could influence postoperative infection rates and recovery, consequently affecting LOS.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that preoperative NLR exhibits a significant positive association with postoperative LOS among pediatric patients undergoing laparoscopic appendectomy. These findings suggest NLR’s potential utility as a practical prognostic marker for predicting healthcare resource utilization and guiding perioperative care optimization.

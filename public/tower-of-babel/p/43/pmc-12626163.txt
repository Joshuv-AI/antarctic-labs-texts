USP5‐Laden ApoEVs and E2F1 Stabilization in Disc Repair: Unresolved Questions on Senescence, Specificity and Endosomal Escape
Authors: DuJiang Yang, Lin Yu, Zhijun Ye, GuoYou Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe seminal work by Shi et al. unveils a novel cytoprotective mechanism in intervertebral disc degeneration, whereby USP5‐rich apoptotic extracellular vesicles (ApoEVs) stabilize the transcription factor E2F1 to suppress apoptosis and enhance DNA repair in nucleus pulposus cells. While this study elegantly reframes the reparative potential of ApoEVs, its conceptual and translational premises invite critical scrutiny. This letter highlights several overlooked challenges. We argue that the contextual duality of E2F1—a well‐established regulator of both cell survival and senescence—poses a considerable therapeutic hazard, as sustained E2F1 stabilization may inadvertently trigger a senescent phenotype. Moreover, the generalizability of this mechanism remains uncertain: ApoEV cargo and functionality are highly dependent on the apoptotic stimulus, and vesicles derived from etoposide‐induced apoptosis may not mirror those produced under pathophysiological disc conditions. Crucially, the proposed pathway—from vesicle internalization and endosomal escape of luminal USP5 to its nuclear trafficking and engagement with E2F1—represents a mechanistic black box requiring rigorous validation. Resolving these issues is essential to translating this compelling discovery into a safe and effective therapeutic strategy.

To the Editor,

We read with great interest the recent study by Shi et al. (2025), which elucidates a novel pathway wherein USP5‐rich apoptotic extracellular vesicles (ApoEVs) mitigate nucleus pulposus (NP) cell apoptosis and promote DNA damage repair by inhibiting the proteasomal degradation of the transcription factor E2F1. This work significantly advances our understanding of the endogenous repair mechanisms within the degenerative disc microenvironment. The identification of a non‐canonical, pro‐survival role for ApoEVs, specifically through the stabilization of a key regulator like E2F1, challenges the predominantly negative perception of apoptosis in disc degeneration and opens promising therapeutic avenues. However, several conceptual and mechanistic aspects warrant deeper scrutiny to fully appreciate the implications and translational potential of these findings.

The Contextual Duality of E2F1: A Precarious Therapeutic Target

The central premise that USP5‐mediated stabilization of E2F1 is universally beneficial in the context of disc degeneration merits careful consideration. E2F1 is a quintessential double‐edged sword, with well‐documented roles in driving cell cycle progression and, under sustained stress, inducing apoptosis. The authors demonstrate reduced apoptosis and enhanced DNA repair, suggesting a tilt towards the pro‐survival functions of E2F1. However, this protective effect might be transient and context‐dependent. In the complex, chronic stress microenvironment of the degenerative disc, constitutive stabilization of E2F1 in primarily post‐mitotic NP cells could precipitate deleterious outcomes. A critical, unaddressed question is whether persistent E2F1 signalling forces these cells into an abortive cell cycle re‐entry, culminating in senescence—a key driver of disc degeneration. This concern is substantiated by work in other systems showing that E2F1 hyperactivation can directly upregulate p16INK4a and promote senescence (Shi et al. 2025). Furthermore, studies in articular chondrocytes have demonstrated that dysregulated E2F1 activity contributes to senescence‐associated secretory phenotype (SASP) and tissue degeneration (Wang et al. 2025). The study would be significantly strengthened by data examining senescence markers (e.g., p16, SA‐β‐gal and SASP factors) following prolonged exposure to USP5‐rich ApoEVs in vitro and in vivo. The therapeutic window for modulating this pathway appears narrow, demanding precise spatiotemporal control over E2F1 activation to avoid exacerbating the degenerative process.

Apoptotic Stimulus Dictates ApoEV Cargo and Function: A Critical Heterogeneity

The origin and heterogeneity of the described ApoEVs present a fascinating frontier. The study characterizes vesicles from etoposide‐induced apoptotic NP cells, a model that robustly induces DNA damage. While useful, the in vivo relevance of this specific trigger is limited. The composition and functional repertoire of ApoEVs are known to be exquisitely dependent on the initiating apoptotic stimulus (Van Niel et al. 2018). It is therefore crucial to determine whether ApoEVs generated under other physiologically relevant stressors in the disc (e.g., oxidative stress, mechanical overload, or nutrient deprivation) similarly enrich for USP5 and confer the same cytoprotective effects. For instance, apoptosis induced by inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL‐1β, TNF‐α), which are abundant in degenerative discs, may yield ApoEVs with a distinct, potentially pro‐inflammatory cargo profile that could override any beneficial USP5 effect. This is supported by evidence showing that TNF‐α‐induced apoptotic cells release vesicles that potentiate inflammation in rheumatoid arthritis (Yang et al. 2025). Furthermore, a more rigorous characterization of the ApoEV subpopulations carrying USP5 is needed. Are they distinct in size, surface protein signature (e.g., phosphatidylserine exposure), or biogenesis pathway from other EVs in the apoptotic secretome? Single‐vesicle analysis techniques could resolve this heterogeneity and identify the most therapeutically relevant subset.

The Mechanistic Journey: From Luminal USP5 to Nuclear E2F1

The mechanistic link between USP5 encapsulated within the vesicle lumen and its cytosolic/nuclear target E2F1 requires further elaboration. The process implies a sophisticated, multi‐step journey: efficient EV internalization, endosomal escape of the luminal content, and finally, the nuclear import of either USP5 itself or a stabilized intermediate. The efficiency of endosomal escape is a recognized bottleneck for the functional cytosolic delivery of luminal EV proteins, as highlighted in key reviews on extracellular vesicle biology (Joshi et al. 2020). The current evidence would benefit from direct visual confirmation, such as immuno‐electron microscopy or proximity ligation assays, demonstrating the co‐localization of internalized USP5 with E2F1 within the nucleus of recipient cells. Additionally, the specificity of this interaction is paramount. As a promiscuous deubiquitinase, USP5 within ApoEVs likely has other protein substrates in recipient NP cells that could contribute to the observed phenotype. A comprehensive ubiquitome analysis of recipient cells would help delineate the full spectrum of USP5 activity and identify potential off‐target effects that could confound the interpretation of the results. The study by Jiang X et al., which identified a similar USP5‐OCT4 axis in glioma stem cells, underscores the potential for USP5 to stabilize multiple, context‐dependent oncogenic clients, a network that may be partially recapitulated in stressed NP cells (Bister et al. 2024; Jiao et al. 2024).

Conclusion and Translational Hurdles

In conclusion, the work by Shi et al. is a seminal contribution that redefines the functional landscape of ApoEVs in intervertebral disc biology. It compellingly argues for a paradigm where dying cells actively orchestrate tissue repair through sophisticated vesicular communication. The critical questions it raises—regarding the contextual duality of E2F1, the stimulus‐specific nature of ApoEV cargo, and the mechanistic details of cross‐compartmental signalling—represent the very frontiers of this field. Addressing these points, particularly the long‐term risk of senescence and the physiological relevance of the ApoEV source, will be essential for transforming this elegant mechanistic discovery into a viable therapeutic strategy. The path forward may involve engineering “designer” ApoEVs or biomaterial‐based scaffolds for localized delivery, as suggested by emerging nanotherapeutic concepts (Chen et al. 2024), but must be pursued with a cautious understanding of the delicate balance in E2F1 signalling.

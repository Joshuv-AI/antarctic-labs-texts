Postnatal Outcome of Prenatally Detected Renal Anomalies
Authors: Venkata S. Gurram, Shrunga Tejasvi, S. Gowthami, Lochan Gowda, M. Meghana, B. S. Tejaswini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) are frequently detected on the prenatal ultrasonography, but their postnatal outcomes vary widely. Accurate diagnosis and early postnatal evaluation are essential for guiding prognosis and management.Objective:To assess the clinical spectrum and postnatal outcomes of renal anomalies detected during prenatal screening.Methods:A prospective observational study was conducted over 18 months on 40 neonates with prenatal renal anomalies detected via ultrasonography. Postnatal evaluation included serial ultrasounds, micturating cystourethrogram (MCU), and diuretic renography (DTPA) scans as indicated. Outcomes were analyzed based on anomaly type, laterality, and severity of hydronephrosis.Results:Hydronephrosis was the most common finding (67.5%), followed by polycystic kidney disease and multicystic dysplastic kidney. Postnatal resolution occurred in 37.5% of cases. Surgical intervention was required in 27.5%, predominantly for ureteropelvic junction obstruction and posterior urethral valves. Bilateral anomalies and high-grade hydronephrosis were associated with poorer outcomes.Conclusion:Prenatally detected renal anomalies warrant structured postnatal follow-up. Severity, laterality, and anomaly type significantly influence clinical outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) account for 30%–50% of prenatally diagnosed anomalies and are among the leading causes of chronic kidney disease in children.[1] Routine antenatal ultrasonography has enabled early detection, with hydronephrosis being the most frequently identified anomaly.[2] However, the clinical significance of prenatal findings varies, as some resolve spontaneously postnatally, while others progress and require medical or surgical intervention.[3] Structured postnatal evaluation using ultrasound, micturating cystourethrogram (MCU), and renal scans is essential to assess anatomical and functional outcomes.[4] Prognosis is influenced by factors such as the type of anomaly, laterality, degree of hydronephrosis, and presence of associated abnormalities.[5] This study was undertaken to evaluate the clinical profile and postnatal outcomes of prenatally detected renal anomalies in a tertiary care setting.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This prospective observational study was conducted over 18 months at a tertiary care center. Forty neonates with antenatally detected renal anomalies were enrolled following birth. Inclusion criteria comprised prenatal detection of structural renal anomalies on ultrasound; neonates with syndromic conditions or other major non-renal anomalies were excluded. Detailed postnatal evaluations included serial renal ultrasounds, MCU, and diuretic renography (DTPA) based on the clinical indication. Anomalies were classified by type, laterality, and severity using standard sonographic grading. Outcomes were assessed in terms of resolution, persistence, or need for surgical intervention. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, and institutional ethical approval was obtained prior to the study.

RESULTS

This study evaluated 40 neonates with antenatally detected renal anomalies. The most common anomaly was hydronephrosis, with a predominance of unilateral involvement. Postnatal outcomes varied by anomaly type and laterality. While a substantial proportion showed spontaneous resolution, others required close follow-up or surgical intervention.

In summary, hydronephrosis was the predominant anomaly detected prenatally, with most cases being unilateral and moderate in severity. One-third of anomalies resolved spontaneously, while a significant number required ongoing surveillance or surgical management, particularly those with bilateral involvement or high-grade obstruction.

DISCUSSION

This study reinforces the high prevalence of hydronephrosis among prenatally detected renal anomalies, consistent with existing literature.[6] The predominance of unilateral involvement and moderate severity highlights the heterogeneity of CAKUT presentations.[7] Nearly 38% of cases resolved spontaneously, underscoring the importance of cautious observation before pursuing invasive interventions.[8] However, anomalies like posterior urethral valves and high-grade obstruction warranted early surgical correction, emphasizing the value of targeted imaging and risk stratification.[9] Postnatal ultrasound remained the cornerstone of follow-up, while MCU and DTPA scans were essential in select cases to confirm obstruction or reflux. The findings highlight the role of anomaly type, laterality, and severity in guiding prognosis. Limited sample size and short-term follow-up are constraints, and larger longitudinal studies are needed to evaluate long-term renal outcomes.[10]

CONCLUSION

Prenatally detected renal anomalies require structured postnatal assessment to determine functional relevance and intervention needs. Individualized follow-up based on the anomaly characteristics ensures optimal clinical outcomes and resource use.

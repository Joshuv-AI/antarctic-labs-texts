The complete mitochondrial genome data of the native, undescribed Tor sp. (Actinopterygii: Cyprinidae) from Sabah, East Malaysia
Authors: Grace Joy Wei Lie Chin, Helena Biun, Kenneth Francis Rodrigues, Rafidah Othman, Leslie Gerard, Noel Hanzel Juvinol Joimil, Fui Fui Ching, Azhar Kassim, Shuhadah Mustapha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe genus Tor, commonly recognized as mahseers, encompasses a diverse group of large cyprinid fish that are ecologically and economically important in South and Southeast Asian freshwater ecosystems. This study reports the first complete mitochondrial genome of the native, undescribed Tor sp. isolated from Tambunan, Sabah, East Malaysia. Measuring 16,579 bp, the mitochondrial genome exhibits a GC content of 43.48% and comprises 13 protein-coding genes, 22 tRNAs, two ribosomal RNAs, and a d-loop control region. All genes are arranged in a typical vertebrate mitochondrial pattern. Furthermore, maximum-likelihood phylogenetic analysis, incorporating a broad range of cyprinids and other Tor species, provided crucial insights into the precise phylogenetic placement of the undescribed Sabah Tor sp. as a distinct species within its genus and confirmed its robust affiliation with the family Cyprinidae. This study provides valuable genomic resources (GenBank accession no PV837872) for future research on the phylogeny, evolution, and conservation of Tor species in Southeast Asia, particularly aiding in the resolution of their complex taxonomy.Keywords: Mitogenome, Mahseer, Conservation genetics, Borneo, Undescribed species, Taxonomic ambiguity

Background

The genus Tor (Gray, 1834), commonly known as mahseers, comprises large-bodied cyprinid fish with significant ecological and commercial value in South and Southeast Asia [1]. Many species within this genus are currently listed as threatened or data deficient due to habitat degradation and overfishing [2]. Effective conservation and management are often hampered by taxonomic ambiguity and the limitations of traditional genetic markers in resolving species boundaries [3].

In Sabah, East Malaysia, previous research using mitochondrial d-loop sequences identified distinct Evolutionarily Significant Units (ESUs), suggesting cryptic diversity resulting from geographic isolation on Borneo[4]. Despite these findings, complete mitogenome resources for Sabah’s Tor populations are largely unavailable. This dataset was compiled to provide the complete mitochondrial genome of a native, undescribed Tor sp. collected from Tambunan, Sabah, Malaysia (Fig. 1). The rationale for generating these data is to provide a comprehensive molecular reference for future studies on the phylogeny and systematics of Southeast Asian mahseers, facilitating precise genetic identification and supporting regional conservation efforts.

Data Description

Characteristics of sabah tor sp

The primary dataset consists of the complete mitochondrial genome sequence of Sabah Tor sp. (16,579 bp), deposited in NCBI GenBank under the accession number PV837872. The genome architecture is visualized in Fig. 2, which displays the circular map, gene orientation, and GC content. The sequence contains 37 discrete genetic elements (13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, and two ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes) and a d-loop control region. The genomic coordinates and lengths for each of these elements are detailed in Table 1. Most genes are encoded on the heavy (H) strand, except for the ND6 gene and eight tRNAs, located on the light (L) strand, a pattern characteristic of teleost mitochondrial architecture [5]. The dataset includes 131.11 GB of raw paired-end reads generated via the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, archived in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under identifier SRX29311204. The assembly’s sequencing depth (75 × coverage) is illustrated in Supplementary Fig. S1. The overall base composition consists of 31.69% adenine (A), 27.57% cytosine (C), 24.83% thymine (T), and 15.90% guanine (G). The 16,579-bp circular mitogenome exhibits a pronounced A+T bias (56.52%), a characteristic common in teleost lineages that often results from asymmetrical replication mechanisms of mitochondrial DNA [6].

Phylogenetic analysis

The phylogenetic data are presented through two maximum-likelihood (ML) trees that were constructed from the concatenated nucleotide sequences of 13 PCGs. The first tree (Fig. 3A) compares the Sabah specimen against 14 fish species within the Cyprinidae family (including five Tor species). The second tree (Fig. 3B) includes a comparative dataset of 24 Tor species and three Neolissochilus outgroups. In the first tree (Fig. 3A), the specimen grouped within a monophyletic clade of the genus Tor with a maximum bootstrap support of 100%. This grouping confirms its classification within the genus and its close relationship to major mahseer species, yet it remains a distinct branch, supporting the hypothesis of a distinct native speciation driven by the geographic isolation of Borneo. Furthermore, Fig. 3B reveals that Tor sp. KKM5/7–23 occupies a basal position relative to the clades of Indian (green bar) and Southeast Asian (blue bar) mahseers. It is phylogenetically situated between the Indian Tor species and the Chinese Tor sinensis, sharing a more recent common ancestor with the Indian and Southeast Asian lineages than T. sinensis does, while remaining genetically distinct. Fig. 3B also provides evidence that the Sabah mahseer is a unique native species rather than a regional variant of T. tambra or T. tambroides [3]. This dataset provides strong molecular evidence and serves as a critical reference for the Tor sp. from Sabah as a distinct evolutionary lineage; however, it requires future integrative taxonomic approaches incorporating nuclear genomic markers and broader population sampling.

Maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic trees of Tor sp. KKM5/7–23 within the Cyprinidae family (A) and among a broad representation of Tor species (B). Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Sample collection

A single male specimen of Tor sp. (weight: 177 g), designated as KKM5/7–23 (Fig. 1), was collected from a freshwater river in Tambunan, Sabah, Malaysia (N05°35′08.44″ E116°19′09.00″) on 4 July 2023. The specimen was transferred to the Biotechnology Research Institute, Universiti Malaysia Sabah, and deposited under the voucher number 230704005 (contact person: GJWL Chin, gracejoy@ums.edu.my).

DNA extraction and sequencing

Total genomic DNA was extracted from approximately 20 mg of muscle tissue using the standard cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) extraction protocol [7]. High-throughput sequencing was performed using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform with a genomic library of 150-bp insert size. The library was prepared using the Illumina TruSeq™ Nano DNA Sample Prep Kit.

Assembly and annotation

Raw paired-end reads were quality-trimmed using BBDuk [8] and subsequently assembled into contigs with GetOrganelle v1.7.5 [9]. The assembly utilized the specific parameter “-F animal_mt” to target mitochondrial sequences, with the Tor tambra mitochondrial genome (GenBank accession number NC_036511) serving as a reference. Assembly completeness and accuracy were evaluated using QUAST v5.2.0 [10]. The assembled mitochondrial genome contigs were annotated with the mfannot tool [11], which provided comprehensive functional annotations for the genome, including protein-coding genes and ribosomal RNAs. The circular mitogenome map was generated using OGDRAW v1.3.1 [12] (https://chlorobox.mpimp-golm.mpg.de/OGDraw.html).

Phylogenetic analysis

Two phylogenetic trees were reconstructed based on the concatenated nucleotide sequences of 13 PCGs. Reference mitochondrial genome sequences for species within the family Cyprinidae, the genus Tor, and Neolissochilus outgroups were retrieved from the NCBI GenBank database. The accession numbers of sequences used in the phylogenetic analyses are listed in Supplementary Table S1. The first ML tree compared the Sabah specimen with 14 fish species from the family Cyprinidae (including five Tor species), while the second tree incorporated a broader representation of 24 Tor species and three Neolissochilus outgroups. All sequences were aligned using the MUSCLE algorithm. The phylogenetic trees were constructed using the Maximum Likelihood method based on the Kimura-2-parameter (K2P) substitution model, with 1000 bootstrap replicates in MEGA v11.0 software [13]. Final visualization and annotation were conducted on the MEGA v11.0 platform.

Ethics Statement

The work involved the collection of a single male Tor sp. specimen. All animal handling and experimental procedures complied with the ARRIVE guidelines and were carried out in accordance with the UK Animals Act, 1986 and associated guidelines. The study protocol and sample collection were reviewed and approved by the Universiti Malaysia Sabah Animal Ethics Committee (Approval file no.: AEC0005/2023). Access license for Tor sp. sampling in Sabah was granted by the Sabah Biodiversity Centre [JKM/MBS.1000–2/2 JLD.16 (35–38)]. The sex of the animal (male) was recorded during collection; however, it did not influence the results of the mitogenome assembly, annotation, or the subsequent phylogenetic analysis.

Developing an anxiety management application for healthcare providers: a qualitative study on improving well-being
Authors: Mohammad Mahdi Askarizadeh, Leila Gholamhosseini, Reza Khajouei, Razieh Askarizade, Fatemeh Askarizadeh, Zahra Rahmani, Saeedeh Homaei, Mahdi Aminizadeh, Mehran Nakhaeizadeh, Leila Ahmadian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHealthcare providers are at high risk of anxiety due to their responsibilities and workplace conditions. This issue may worsen over time, especially when the work duration is prolonged. One innovative approach to reducing healthcare providers anxiety, while enhancing the quality of care and improving patient safety, is the implementation of educational programs and the utilization of these resources through a mobile application. This study aimed to develop an anxiety management application to enhance the quality of care and improve patient safety.MethodsAn application prototype was developed based on a systematic review and refined through semi-structured interviews with healthcare workers, psychologists, psychiatrists, and medical informatics specialists. The application was developed using the Flutter framework in both web-based and mobile formats for Android and iOS. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and content analysis.ResultsA systematic review, along with interviews with treatment staff, psychologists, and psychiatrists, was conducted to develop the program. The application included strategies such as Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), meditation, yoga, mindfulness, breathing therapy, and nature therapy. Additional features included a user chat room and a ‘My Medications’ section. The validated Beck Anxiety Inventory was used to assess the anxiety level.ConclusionsThis study led to the development of a specialized anxiety reduction application that provides evidence-based strategies to significantly reduce anxiety among healthcare providers. This application can greatly improve the mental health of healthcare staff, leading to enhanced patient care quality and safety. The findings of this research offer a valuable foundation for future studies and the development of similar applications to manage and reduce mental health disorders in diverse populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13341-0.

Background

Anxiety is characterized by fear and apprehension in response to stress [1]. Symptoms can be physical (headaches, sweating, palpitations) or non-physical (restlessness, irritability, insomnia) [2]. Anxiety disorders are common, with a prevalence of 10–20% in the general population [3]. A study by Javid et al. (2023), titled “Epidemiology of Anxiety Disorders,” found that the number of individuals suffering from these disorders has increased by over 55% from 1990 to 2022. Among the countries studied, Portugal has the highest prevalence of anxiety disorders, ranking first, while Iran ranks third. The prevalence of anxiety disorders among women is 1.66 times higher than that among men [4]. These disorders stem from a combination of psychological and social factors, including childhood adversities, stress, trauma, and genetic vulnerability. They can manifest as neurobiological and psychoneurological disorders, and if left unmanaged, can cause significant harm to the individual [5]. In addition to causing personal harm, anxiety contributes to increased use of healthcare services thereby increasing healthcare costs. In 2013, the National Institute of Mental Health reported that healthcare costs associated with anxiety in the United States were $42.3 billion [6]. Therefore, recognizing and treating anxiety is essential for alleviating this chronic and debilitating condition. Effective treatment can improve symptoms and functioning, reduce disability, and enhance overall health, potentially lowering the healthcare costs associated with this disorder [7].

Among individuals suffering from anxiety, healthcare providers are at greater risk due to their direct involvement in patient care, the potential life-threatening risks to patients, the challenges of dealing with patients and their companions, the manner in which colleagues, especially managers, interact, and the demands of long shifts. Over time, many of these professionals have become increasingly affected by anxiety as their work experience grows [8, 9]. The impact of anxiety on healthcare providers can significantly impair their performance, which in turn compromises the quality of healthcare services [10] and can lead to mood disorders and decreased patient safety [8, 11]. Therefore, managing and reducing healthcare providers anxiety is crucial to mitigate any negative consequences for both patients and healthcare systems [11, 12]. The exacerbation of anxiety and its accompanying negative outcomes can be prevented. Various approaches are available for anxiety reduction, including psychotherapy, meditation, mindfulness, breathing exercises, and yoga [13–15]. However, to be truly effective, these strategies must be applied consistently [16].

Previous studies have indicated that the absence of continuous follow-ups for managing and reducing anxiety contributes to higher prevalence rates. For example, the reported prevalence of anxiety among nurses in Australia is 42% [11], while it is 28% among selected military hospital nurses in Iran [17] and generally 30% among nurses in Iranian hospitals [18]. Additionally, during the critical conditions of the COVID-19 pandemic, the prevalence of anxiety reached 37% among healthcare providers [19]. Therefore, ongoing efforts for the management and treatment of anxiety are essential.

Mobile phones can be used as an accessible tool for healthcare delivery and disease management [20–22]. In other words, m-health applications can be used to obtain healthcare information and managing various health conditions [23–25]. With the global expansion of mobile technology, m-Health applications are increasingly being adopted. Mobile health applications range from communication between individuals and health systems (such as call centers and appointment reminders) to monitoring healthcare (which includes surveys and patient monitoring devices) and access to information during care (such as health records and decision support systems) [26].

According to a semi-experimental study by Chakari et al., which investigated the impact of nurse-led telephone follow-ups (tele-nursing) on anxiety levels in individuals affected by COVID-19, one of the most common utilizations of mobile phones as a healthcare service tool was establishing phone contacts. This approach was found to help reduce anxiety among those affected by the virus [27]. In recent years, with the emergence of new diseases, the use of m-health applications has expanded significantly, serving as a practical tool for data collection and disease management [23–25]. Due to the demanding nature of healthcare providers’ jobs and their many responsibilities, the lack of ongoing monitoring and treatment for work-related fatigue, particularly anxiety, makes mobile phone use a simple way to ensure continuous access to therapeutic strategies for managing and reducing anxiety [28].

Although many studies have explored the effectiveness of anxiety management applications for healthcare providers [29, 30], Despite the effectiveness of such applications, none are available in Persian, even though Iran has a high prevalence of anxiety among healthcare professionals [4]. Additionally, the number of active staff per bed in hospitals in Iran is significantly lower than the global average. As a result, healthcare professionals are under increased work pressure, which not only contributes to higher levels of anxiety but also leaves them with little time to attend to their own needs and to manage and reduce their anxiety [31]. The existence of an application that assists healthcare workers in managing anxiety would be beneficial. Therefore, our research question is: ‘How can a comprehensive and effective application for managing anxiety among healthcare workers be designed. Therefore, This study aims to develop and evaluate an anxiety management application specifically for Persian-speaking healthcare providers, incorporating validated methods like ACT, meditation, yoga, and mindfulness. The content of the application in this study is the result of a systematic review and qualitative research, which was developed and designed in consultation with specialists in psychology, psychiatry, and medical informatics. The content and modules that are featured in similar applications are included in this application. Efforts were made in this application to utilize validated and scientific methods that are effective in reducing anxiety, ACT, meditation, yoga, mindfulness, and other factors, and to provide these methods to users through structured courses. This application is intended to raise awareness of anxiety, its consequences, and contributing factors, while also helping healthcare providers manage and reduce their anxiety.

Methods

In this study, to design a practical anxiety management application for healthcare providers, a systematic review was conducted, followed by interviews with healthcare staff as well as a psychologist and a psychiatrist. Additionally, three medical informatics specialists were interviewed to ensure appropriate design and usability. Interviews were recorded for qualitative analysis.

Initially, to obtain the preliminary content of the application, A systematic review was conducted to retrieve relevant articles from PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science databases published between 2014 and 2024. Due to the limited number of relevant studies, articles that considered anxiety as a secondary objective and those addressing stress disorders were also included. Articles addressing anxiety and stress disorders were included. Keywords and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms related to “Self-care”, “Mobile Application”, “Anxiety”, and “Healthcare Professionals” were used. A data extraction form was used to collect the required information, including the author’s name, year of publication, country, type of study, duration of intervention, study objectives, platform used, modules, methods, questionnaire specifications, outcomes, and usability.

In the next step, To confirm and expand on the data collected, interviews were conducted with healthcare staff, a psychologist, and a psychiatrist. Information saturation was reached by the tenth interview, with three additional interviews conducted to ensure data saturation. These 13 interviewees, comprising nurses, physicians, paramedics, emergency technicians, nursing assistants, and administrative staff, were selected based on the following criteria: having at least one year of work experience and no personal crises in the past year. During these interviews, which were conducted both in person and remotely, the final outcomes of the project and a summary of the literature review findings were presented to the interviewees, and the interview framework was established. An additional interview file in more detail shown in supplementary file 1.

Data were analyzed using the content analysis method by Lundman & Graneheim [32]. In this method, the interview was guided by clarifying questions to ensure that the objectives were effectively met. In these interviews, the duration of which varied depending on the individual experiences and response styles, Interviews focused on describing anxiety, strategies for managing it, and feedback on application features. The interviewees were asked to share their experiences of situations that caused their anxiety and the strategies they had employed to manage and reduce it. Subsequently, during the interview, the features and modules of the application identified in the systematic review were presented to participants, and their feedback was recorded. The data obtained from the interviews were repeatedly reviewed to ensure an overall understanding of the context. The text was then read word-by-word for coding, and the extracted codes were tagged. The codes were categorized based on similarities and differences across various categories, and the relationships among them were also identified.

Following the interviews with the healthcare professionals and qualitative analysis, the items that were most frequently cited were identified as the primary modules. To identify effective and efficient strategies, interviews were conducted with two specialists, a psychologist and a psychiatrist. Ultimately, to enhance the usability of the application, the opinions of three medical informatics experts were incorporated into the design process. By aggregating the results from the literature review and the interviews, an initial content model of the application was completed, and the final model was developed. For optimal design with high usability, consultations were held with medical informatics specialists before finalizing the application. The application was developed using the Flutter framework, incorporating feedback from healthcare professionals, psychologists, psychiatrists, and medical informatics experts. The final model included validated methods like ACT, meditation, yoga, and mindfulness, provided through structured courses. The Flutter framework features powerful graphical libraries and animations, providing users with a consistent appearance across different sections of the application.

The term Zehn aram (in Persian) symbolizes a metaphor for the mental tranquility this application intended to provide to healthcare providers. Discussion

This study developed an anxiety management application for healthcare providers in managing and reducing their anxiety through various strategies and protocols integrated into the application. Effective strategies for controlling and reducing anxiety informed the design of the application. The literature review revealed limited studies on anxiety-reducing applications specifically for healthcare staff. Most studies focused on stress or treated anxiety as a secondary focus. This research is the first to design a comprehensive anxiety management application for healthcare personnel, incorporating key strategies from comparable studies. Cognitivebehavioral therapy (CBT) was commonly identified as effective, but ACT has recently gained attention for its long-term benefits; only five articles directly addressed anxiety [13, 15, 33–35], while the others treated anxiety as a secondary focus or concentrated primarily on stress [36–40]. Each of these studies employed limited methodologies in their applications. Therefore, this research represents the first effort to design a comprehensive anxiety management application specifically for healthcare personnel, drawing on findings from systematic reviews and qualitative studies. The proposed application incorporates key strategies identified in comparable studies.

The quantitative and qualitative analyses of the demographic information of the interviewees were not conducted. However, based on their interviews, it can be inferred that job position directly correlated with the level of anxiety experienced. According to the results from other studies, individuals working in emergency and ICU departments tend to experience higher levels of anxiety due to the critical nature of these areas [41, 42]. Additionally, it has been observed that women experience higher levels of anxiety compared to men, a finding that is clearly evident in most studies [43, 44]. According to the results of other studies, age and work experience do not have a significant impact on the level of anxiety, and these two factors do not have a specific correlation with anxiety levels [45]. However, individuals in the early stages of their careers tend to experience higher levels of anxiety. Over the long term, the relationship between work experience and anxiety becomes negligible [46].

In the current research, we incorporated Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) for assessing anxiety levels, alongside the Cohen Perceived Stress Scale (PSS), which is related to ACT. The Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI), which focuses on somatic symptoms, was also used. Furthermore, since self-efficacy and mindfulness are factors associated with anxiety, regular measurements of knowledge, quality of life, and self-efficacy can yield more comprehensive results. Thus, we employed the General Self-Efficacy Scale (GSE-17) and the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ). In previous studies [15, 34, 35, 38, 39], anxiety levels were measured using scales such as STAT, GAD, PSS, and PASS.

These questionnaires were utilized in similar studies [14, 33]. According to the reviews conducted, cognitive-behavioral therapy was identified as an effective and applicable protocols for reducing anxiety among healthcare provider [13, 15, 37, 38]. This protocol is recognized as a viable therapeutic approach for alleviating anxiety, and the results of other studies have reported positive outcomes associated with this treatment method [47, 48]. Notably, none of the reviewed studies mentioned acceptance and commitment-based protocols as a therapeutic intervention for reducing anxiety. However, this method has recently gained attention from psychotherapists and demonstrated better long-term effect [49–51] This application utilizes this protocol for the first time. The findings indicate that ACT helps individuals identify their values and take consistent actions, leading to reduced anxiety and tension. Hayes et al. (2006) sustained this finding, highlighting that ACT may substantially enhance anxiety and mental health by emphasizing accepting thoughts and emotions, maintaining present moments awareness without judgment, and adhering to fundamental life values [52]. Fledderus et al. (2012) reported that the beneficial effects of ACT led to improved mental health. This study focused on “Acceptance and Commitment Therapy as a structured self-help intervention for psychological distress and enhanced mental health” [53]. The results from the qualitative section also indicated that individuals had not previously engaged in their treatment using this method, and they had temporarily reduced their anxiety through the cognitive-behavioral therapy protocol employed by psychologists.

Meditation, mindfulness, and yoga are additional strategies for reducing anxiety. These techniques were included in only three studies [14, 33, 34] as effective modules for anxiety reduction in mobile applications. In the current research, these methods were also employed as reduction strategies, with each section incorporating multiple relaxation sessions and exercises. Furthermore, the effectiveness of these strategies in alleviating anxiety among healthcare providers has been demonstrated in other studies [54, 55]. Mindfulness, which is a fundamental aspect of ACT, reduces anxiety through daily practice. This finding is consistent with the research conducted by Vøllestad et al., which identified mindfulness and ACT as significant and effective approaches for anxiety reduction [56] and that in ACT, individuals are taught to concentrate on the present moment utilizing techniques such as meditation and yoga. This method assists individuals in releasing concerns related to the past or future, enhancing their focus on present experiences [57]. The analysis of the qualitative section revealed that these exercises were very effective in reducing anxiety; however, due to time constraints, the participants did not have the opportunity to attend training classes. Following these exercises through an application was deemed an effective and beneficial approach Breathing exercises, specifically diaphragmatic breathing, are another anxiety control strategy that was utilized in one study [40] as a reduction technique within the mobile application. This method helps users relax by focusing on their inhalation and exhalation. Additionally, results from other studies indicated that breathing exercises significantly contribute to the management and reduction of anxiety [58, 59]. Therapeutic breathing exercises were identified as valuable suggestions in the qualitative section. Because the interviewees were in a clinical setting, they recognized the positive aspects of this exercise and expressed enthusiasm for practicing it.

One effective way to reduce anxiety is training for preparedness in stressful situations. In this study, one of the health program modules provided training to healthcare providers, which helped reduce their anxiety through self-management [33]. Ebrahimi et al. [60] conducted an interventional study to examine the impact of educational interventions on the anxiety of nurses working in hospitals affiliated with Arak University of Medical Sciences. Their results indicated that teaching skills and techniques for mental relaxation, along with conducting continuous exercises, significantly alleviated hospital staff anxiety. In the current study, a section was dedicated to training for preparedness in emergencies and disasters. This section includes protocols for educational preparedness in emergencies and disasters, designed for all healthcare providers across various occupational categories, facilitated by disaster health specialists. Given that the interviewees were familiar with the educational protocols, they recommended that these protocols be included as a reminders section.

The results of another study indicated that nature exploration interventions through virtual reality effectively reduce anxiety and stress [35]. Supporting this finding, other studies have highlighted the positive impact of nature on alleviating anxiety [61]. Consequently, one of the modules in the present study was dedicated to facilitating nature tours, allowing users to learn about nature tour opportunities in their city and take action to register for them. The results of the qualitative section indicated that most individuals sought recreation, particularly nature excursions, to reduce their anxiety and made an effort to spend time in nature during holidays. They also responded positively to the nature exploration section of the application, which they consider one of the best ways to alleviate anxiety.

In mobile-based applications designed for managing and controlling diseases, there is typically a section titled “My Medications,” which enables users to manage their medications in terms of dosage and timing of consumption. The reminder feature of the medication system helps individuals with busy schedules or forgetfulness take their medications on time, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of the treatment [33, 62]. In this study, a similar section titled “My Medications” was designated to help users manage and receive reminders about their drug intake. The interviewees acknowledged that, due to their work commitments and personal lives, they sometimes forget to take their medications. They considered this section, given its capabilities, a useful feature.

Research has revealed that interpersonal communication can serve as a source of comfort, and can influencing levels of stress and anxiety [63, 64]. However, none of the studies included in this review addressed this specific topic. In this research, we created a section titled “Chat Room,” which allows healthcare providers to discuss and exchange ideas. This section was designed in the application based on the opinions and suggestions of the interviewees.

M-Health tools, due to their constant availability, can effectively reduce anxiety when providing appropriate content. The study by Planas and Yuguero [28] supports this finding, indicating that health-oriented technologies have a significant potential to enhance mental health, particularly regarding depression and anxiety. In this context, previous studies [29, 30] have investigated the effectiveness of anxiety management applications for healthcare providers and have demonstrated that the use of these technologies is effective in alleviating anxiety. The findings obtained from the qualitative study indicated that the presence of a comprehensive application with useful sections could be effective given the users’ busy schedules. Since the application was the result of a systematic review and qualitative analysis, it can be concluded that the designed application serves as an effective mHealth tool.

Limitation of the study

The study did not conduct quantitative and qualitative analyses of the demographic information of the interviewees. Future research should explore the correlation between job position, gender, and anxiety levels in more detail. One of the limitations of this study is that we were unable to assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the application. Therefore, it is recommended that future research evaluates the impact of his approach. Another limitation is the limited number of studies on anxiety reduction identified during the literature review. As a result, we also included studies that focused on anxiety-related outcomes, such as stress. Another limitation of this study is that the developed application is in Persian, which restricts its use by non-Persian speakers and consequently limits the results and effectiveness of the application. It is recommended that future research develop an English version of this application.

Implications of study

In our systematic review, we identified studies that assessed the impact of mobile phone self-care applications on reducing anxiety among healthcare workers. Based on the findings of this review and the qualitative study, a comprehensive self-care application was developed. Researchers interested in investigating healthcare workers anxiety can use the framework established in this study. This application can be utilized by officials and policymakers in healthcare organizations across various health-related fields. The application provides healthcare providers with practical tools to manage anxiety, potentially improving their mental health and the quality of patient care. The use of ACT, mindfulness, and other strategies offers a holistic approach to anxiety management. This study highlights the need for continuous support and accessible tools for healthcare providers to manage work-related anxiety.

Consequently, ensuring their security and privacy is key to building trust and enhancing service quality. The implementation of security technologies, including blockchain for its decentralized characteristics and post-quantum methods for their resilience against quantum attacks, can mitigate the risks associated with data sharing, usage, and manipulation, thereby addressing this issue effectively.

It is important to recognize that the capabilities of multiple technologies can be utilized simultaneously, rather than relying on a single one alone. An example of this situation involves the collection of user data through various means, including software test results, conversation history with both a doctor and a chatbot, medication records, and data obtained from wearable sensors. Subsequently, by employing machine learning algorithms derived from artificial intelligence, early detection of events such as panic attacks is achieved, with notifications sent to the physician via Internet of Things technology to facilitate the development of a treatment plan.

Conclusion

Given that the content of the application developed in this study is based on literature reviews, qualitative scientific methods, and interviews, it has the potential to positively impact the reduction of anxiety and its consequences for healthcare workers. Furthermore, It enhances understanding of anxiety and provides tools to measure and manage anxiety levels, self-efficacy, mindfulness, and perceived stress. This awareness will empower individuals to take proactive steps towards reducing their anxiety by utilizing the reduction strategies provided. Additionally, the methodology employed in this research can serve as a comprehensive model that integrates existing knowledge from the literature and expert opinions to designe similar applications in other health fields.

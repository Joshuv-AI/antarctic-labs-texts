Exploring mediating effects of B cell and monocyte in the causal relationship between diacylglycerol and trigeminal neuralgia
Authors: Hao-Dong Jiang, Guo-Qi Cao, Lin-Qi Su, Lei Yu, Jun-Jun Li, Xiao-Bing Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Changes in plasma lipidome often occur simultaneously with alterations in the immune system. The potential causal relationships between serum lipids changes and trigeminal neuralgia (TN), as well as the possibility of immune cells mediating these relationships, have not yet been understood. We conducted 2-step and 2-sample Mendelian randomization analyses using single nucleotide polymorphism data from genome-wide association studies to examine the causal relationships between genetically determined lipids levels and TN, including the intermediary impacts of immune cells. Mendelian randomization analyses revealed causal relationships between 14 lipids levels and TN, as well as between 22 immune cell phenotypes and TN. Statistically significant causal relationships were observed between 2 types of lipids (diacylglycerol (18:1_18:1), diacylglycerol (18:1_18:3)) and immune cells, including B cells (CD20 on IgD+, CD24 on IgD+ CD24+) and monocyte (CCR2 on CD14− CD16+ monocyte). The causal relationships were mediated by immune cell phenotypes to different degrees, ranging from 3.45% to 7.19%. Our study identified multiple causal relationships between various lipids levels, immune cell phenotypes and TN. We also found that the causal relationships between diacylglycerol and TN can be mediated by B cells and monocytes with specific phenotypes. These findings will contribute to our understanding of the underlying pathogenesis of TN and provide new therapeutic targets.

1. Introduction

Trigeminal neuralgia (TN) is a type of neuropathic pain that affects the area where the trigeminal nerve is distributed. Currently, it is considered to be one of the most severe facial pain syndromes.[1,2] The pain is characterized as paroxysmal, sharp knife-like pain. Patients with TN frequently experience facial trigger points that are activated by routine activities such as cleaning the face, brushing teeth, and eating. TN typically follows a prolonged course, with both pain intensity and seizure frequency increasing over time. The severe, often unbearable pain can significantly disrupt daily life and may increase the risk of neuropsychiatric disorders, such as depression.[3–5] The main strategies for treating TN involve the use of medications and surgical procedures. Carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine are the first-line medications for managing TN.[6] In cases where medication are ineffective or cause severe complications, surgical alternatives like microvascular decompression (MVD), radiofrequency ablation (RFT), and balloon compression (PBC) are considered. All surgical procedures carry a risk of recurrence and potential complications, including cerebrospinal fluid leakage, intracranial infection, hemorrhage, hearing loss, and facial numbness, and so on.[7–10] The specific etiology and pathogenesis of TN remain unknown. The discussion on the pathogenesis of TN is beneficial for the identification of novel therapeutic targets, improving treatment effectiveness, and reducing adverse effects.[11,12]

With the deeplearning of the study of lipid metabolism, many diseases are closely related to the abnormality of lipids metabolism. Lipids are crucial in diseases initiation, progression, and therapy. Plasma lipidome has the potential to reflect the pathophysiological, metabolic and endocrine changes of various systems of the body. Extensive research has been conducted to find lipidome biomarkers in the fields of oncology, infectious illnesses, metabolic disorders, and cardiovascular diseases.[13–17] Moreover, as key components of the central nervous system, lipids are linked to various neuropathological conditions.[18–21] Animal studies confirm neuropathic pain induced by nerve damage correlates with plasma lipid alterations. However, there is a lack of research on human neuropathic pain, such as TN. The potential causal relationships between plasma lipidome changes and neuropathic pain remains unknown.[22] Furthermore, researches indicate lipid metabolism alterations are related to immune responses, and immune cells play an important role in the occurrence and development of neuropathic pain. Consequently, it is necessary to further explore the causal relationship between plasma lipidome alterations and TN, including the potential mediating role of immune cells.[23–25]

Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis uses genetic variation to figure out causality between exposure and outcome. Due to the unpredictable nature of genetic variation in population distribution, the causal inference method that relies on genetic variation can successfully overcome the interference of confounding factors, including unknown ones, as well as reverse causality. As a result, it achieves a similar effect to that of randomized controlled clinical trials. Alternatively, when it is difficult to precisely gather exposure information for both cases and controls, or when the expense of getting such information is prohibitively high, the use of the Mendelian randomized research method[26,27] is also appropriate. Several Mendelian randomized studies have also confirmed that the levels of modifiable risk factors, such as years of schooling, anxiety disorders, depression, autoimmune, body mass index and adipose tissue-derived adiponectin levels have causal relationships with TN.[28,29] Consequently, this study aims to: investigate the causal relationships between gene-determined lipids levels and TN and to confirm the extent to which immune cells mediates the relationships of liposome on TN.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Research design

In this study, we performed 2-step, 2-sample MR analysis. First, we performed bidirectional 2-sample MR analysis to evaluate the causal relationships between lipids and TN, and selected lipids that had causal relationships with TN. Meanwhile, there is no causal relationships between TN and candidate lipids during reverse MR analysis. Secondly, immune cell phenotypes showing causal relationships to TN were identified. Using lipids identified in the first step as the exposure and immune cell phenotypes in the second step as the outcome, we screened for causative liposome-immune cell phenotypes combinations. We calculated β values (total β, β1, and β2) to assess the mediating effect of immune cells. The research workflow diagram is shown in Figure 1. 2.2. Data source

Gene-determined lipids levels were obtained from an original genome-wide association study (GWAS), featuring data on 179 lipids across 13 categories.[30] Immunophenotypes data, encompassing 731 phenotypes (Ebi-a-GCST0001391 to Ebi-a-GCST0002121), were sourced from the GWAS catalog (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/).[31] TN data, including 1777 cases and 3,60,538 controls, were obtained from the Finngen database (finngen_R10_G6_TRINEU, https://www.finngen.fi/en).

2.3. Selection of genetic variants

Candidate single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) for lipids and immune cell phenotypes were selected based on a P-value threshold < 1e−05. Ideally, SNPs associated with TN would be selected with a P-value threshold < 5e−08. However, due to only 1 SNP meeting this criterion, we adjusted the threshold to < 5e−06.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Five commonly used MR analysis methods are used to analyze the data, including: “inverse variance weighted (IVW),” “MR-Egger,” “weighted median,” “simple mode,” “seighted mode.” IVW was used as the primary analysis method, while the others providing additional verification. P < .05 was used as the statistical threshold for judging causality. The odds ratio (OR) determined whether lipids or immune cell phenotypes acted as protective or pathogenic factors for TN. For the results to be considered statistically significant, the P-value from the IVW analysis must be <.05. Additionally, the OR values should be consistently either >1 or <1 across all 5 MR methods. Sensitivity analyses were conducted for each causal analysis combination, including tests for pleiotropy, heterogeneity, and analyses such as funnel plot and leave-one-out. The MR-Egger intercept test represents the results of pleiotropy test. If the P-value of pleiotropy test is <.05, even if the results of primary analysis are statistically significant, the selected lipids or immune cell phenotypes need to be excluded. Cochran’s Q test and MR-PRESSO analysis were used to test the heterogeneity. Funnel plot and leave-one analysis were used to analyze whether there were outliers and their influence on the overall results from an intuitive point of view. Analyses were performed using R version 4.3.2, with the “TwoSampleMR,” “MRInstruments,” and “ieugwasr” packages for MR analysis.

3. Results

3.1. Causal relationships between lipids and TN

Using candidate SNPs and TN data, our bidirectional MR analysis identified 14 out of 179 lipid species as causally associated with TN. The information of 14 lipids can be shown in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q317. Four of these lipids are risk factors with OR values > 1 using all 5 methods. These lipids are phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylcholine (18:0_22:5), phosphatidylcholine (O-16:1_20:4), and phosphatidylcholine (16:0_22:5). While not all 4 alternative methods reached statistical significance, all IVW analyses yielded P-values < .05. Ten lipids were identified as protective factors against TN, specifically: phosphatidylinositol (18:0_20:3); triacylglycerol (51:3); diacylglycerol (18:1_18:1); triacylglycerol (51:2); diacylglycerol (18:1_18:3); phosphatidylcholine (18:2_18:2); diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1); phosphatidylcholine (18:1_18:1); phosphatidylcholine (18:0_18:2); diacylglycerol (16:0_18:2). P-value and OR value of each lipid obtained by IVW method were shown in Figure 2, and the data obtained by all 5 methods were shown in Figure S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q316. The forest plot and scatter plot were shown in Figures S2 and S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q316. Cochran’s Q test, MR-Egger intercept analysis (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q317), and MR-PRESSO analyses (Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q318) confirmed the absence of heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy. Furthermore, the leave-one-out analysis and funnel plot did not identify significant outliers among the SNPs (Figs. S4 and S5, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q316). Additionally, reverse MR analysis, with TN as the exposure and lipids as outcomes, yielded negative results (Table S4, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q319).

4. Discussion

Despite their crucial roles in cellular signaling, structure, and energy management, the research of lipidome has not received sufficient study attention. Lipidome studies have the potential to provide biomarkers for early disease onset and progression, offering new insights into personalized treatment approaches. Lipid alterations have the potential to be used as diagnostic indicators for neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric conditions, including as Alzheimer’s disease, autism, schizophrenia, and depression.[18–20,32–34] However, reports on the causal relationships between lipidome changes and related diseases, including neuropathic pain, are scarce. TN is one of the common neuropathic pains. The study of the causal relationships between lipodome and TN can provide more potential diagnostic markers for TN and a new path for the study of its pathogenesis and treatment.

In this study, we focused on whether genetically determined lipids levels affect the occurrence of TN. Through rigorous selection of instrumental variables, primary analysis, and sensitivity analysis, we identified 14 lipids causally related to TN. Diglycerides and triglycerides were identified as the main lipids offering protection effect against TN. The lipids that can promote the occurrence of TN is phosphatidylethanolamine, also known as brain phospholipid. Phosphatidylcholine variants were demonstrated to have either a protective or promotive effect on TN. To investigate if the 14 TN-associated lipids exert their effects through the immune cells, a 2-step MR analysis was conducted. Firstly, we identified 22 immune cell phenotypes, 12 immune cell phenotypes can promote the occurrence of TN, and 10 immune cell phenotypes have protective effects on TN. We investigated the causal connections between the 14 lipids and 22 immune cell phenotypes, and identified 4 groups that were causally connected. This study is the first MR study to explore the causal relationships between lipids and TN, including the mediating effects of immune cell phenotypes. Four of these groups were diglycerides combined with B cells or monocytes.

Our study identified 2 diglyceride combinations that exhibit protective effects against TN. Diglyceride is the precursor of 2-arachidonic acylglycerol, which is an important ligand of the endogenous cannabinoid system. The endogenous cannabinoid system is an important regulatory system in the human body, which participates in a large number of physiological processes, including emotional regulation, pain management, cognitive function, neurogenesis and neuroprotection, inflammation and immune function.[35–37] 2-Arachidonic glycerol binds to cannabinoid receptors CB1R and CB2R, playing a significant role in pain management and relief. CB1R exists in many tissues and predominantly in the nervous system, while CB2R receptors are mainly located in microglia and immune cells. The activation of CB2R can activate the immune system and play a regulatory role in a variety of chronic diseases.[38–41] Our findings suggest that the protective effects of the 2 diglycerides against TN are mediated partially through B cells and monocytes. CB2R receptors on B cells have been shown to mediate B cell maturation within tumor environments, crucially regulating tumor immunity.[42] The activation of CB2R receptor can also inhibit the secretion of cathepsin B by monocytes and macrophages after HIV infection, thus reducing neurotoxicity and alleviating HIV-related cognitive impairment. The inhibition of FAAH, an enzyme that degrades endocannabinoids, can alters microglia phenotype and offer a neuroprotection role.[43–45] The activation of CB2R receptor can also down-regulates the activation of brain macrophages/ microglia induced by stroke, thus provide the neuroprotective effect.[46] Therefore, the endogenous cannabinoid system may be a possible pathway of diglyceride involved in the prevention of TN. and thus provides a new potential target for the treatment of TN by utilizing drugs that can regulate the endogenous cannabinoid system or altering the levels of serum diglyceride.

We further confirmed the causal relationships between diglycerides and B cells/monocytes, as well as the causal relationships between B cells (CD20 on IgD+, CD24 on IgD+ CD24+)/monocytes (CCR2 on CD14− CD16+ monocyte) and TN. Diglyceride has a negative regulatory effect on CD20, CD24 and CCR2, and the level of 3 surface markers in immune cells can promote the occurrence of TN. B cells highly expressing CD20 and CD24 are known to infiltrate in autoimmune neurological disorders, including multiple sclerosis and neuromyelitis optica. Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, like rituximab, are used to treat multiple sclerosis and neuromyelitis optica.[47–50] The frequent co-occurrence of TN with multiple sclerosis also suggests that neuroimmunity plays a role in the occurrence of TN.[51,52] Many studies have confirmed that the chemokine family contributes to the occurrence and development of neuropathic pain through chemokine receptors.[53] Preclinical model of TN also confirmed that chemokine CCL7 mediates trigeminal ganglion pathological pain through CCR2/CCR3-ERK pathway, and CCL2-CCR2 signal transduction pathway in trigeminal nucleus is involved in the maintenance of trigeminal pain.[54–56] There is no research has focused on the effect of CCR2 levels expressed on immune cells, especially on peripheral monocytes, on TN. Our MR analysis confirmed the causal relationships between the level of CCR2 in CD14− CD16+ monocyte, which is a specific monocyte subset, and TN. Thus, therapies targeting specific immune cell surface markers, such as multiple antibody drugs, may offer new treatments for TN. Additionally, this study uniquely links lipids to TN via immune cells, establishing a continuous causal chain that underscores the intermediary role of immune cells in this relationship.

In summary, our study used a 2-step MR analysis to investigate the causal relationships between the lipidome and TN, and the mediating role of immune cell phenotypes, opening new avenues for understanding the mechanisms of the disease occurrence. These candidate lipids may serve as biomarkers for forecasting the occurrence of TN and assessing treatment efficacy. Therapeutics targeting lipids and potential pathways may provide a new sights on the therapy of TN. At the same time, stratified analysis of patient groups based on candidate lipids may provide a basis for finding the most appropriate treatment and provide patients with more accurate individualized treatment, which needs to be supported by more clinical trial results in the future. Moreover, antagonists against diglycerides or CD20, CD24, and CCR2 also provide new possibilities for the treatment of TN.

However, there were still some limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, this study predominantly focuses on a European population. The applicability of our findings to other ethnic groups requires caution and further validation in diverse cohorts. Secondly, the TN data used in this study included a small number of cases, only 1777 cases, future studies with larger datasets are needed to confirm the reliability of our conclusions. Thirdly, the identified mediating effects of immune cells are modest, with the highest effect size being only 7.19%. This suggests that there may have additional mediators in the causal pathway between lipids and TN. Consequently, further research needs to explore other factors as potential mediators, including lipids that have no causal relationships with immune cells but have relationships with TN.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, our study identified causal relationships between 2 lipids levels (diacylglycerol) and TN, with a small proportion of the effect mediated by 2 kinds of immune cells (B cell, monocyte). The major effects of lipids on TN are remain unknown, which need further research. Moreover, the discovery of causal relationship between lipids, immune cells and TN will be helpful in the research of diagnosis, treatment, and mechanism of TN. Treatments targeting lipids and immune cells may reduce the occurrence of TN.

Bishop Score vs. Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVS) for Predicting Successful Labor Induction by Prostaglandins in a Tertiary Healthcare Center
Authors: Aaliya Kazi, Neha M. Mahajan, Rashmi Kumari, Anshuli Trivedi, Kiranmai Pandi, CM Chaitra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:The induction of labor (IOL) is a common intervention in obstetrics, and its success is assessed by the Bishop score routinely in all patients prior to IOL.Objectives:To assess Bishop score vs. transvaginal ultrasound (TVS) for predicting successful labor induction by prostaglandins.Materials and Methods:The study included patients with ≥37 weeks of pregnancy with nonrandom opportunity sampling. The cervix was assessed by TVS and compared with the Bishop score. The labor was induced with the tablet misoprostol or dinoprostone gel.Result:The labor induction was successful in 80% of patients. The TVS cervical score had better sensitivity and specificity than the Bishop score.Conclusion:TVS is an objective method for assessing the cervix before induction and can be used as an adjunct to the Bishop score.

INTRODUCTION

The induction of labor (IOL) is one of the most common interventions in obstetrics, which implies stimulation of contractions before the spontaneous onset of labor. The condition of the cervix is important for successful labor induction.[1] The assessment of the cervix by Bishop score is subjective, biased by interobserver variations, and studies have shown poor predictive value.[2]

The cervical characteristics can also be assessed by ultrasound, and transvaginal sonography (TVS) has been evaluated as an alternative to the score. The sonographic cervical-length (CL) measurement by TVS has been shown consistently to be an efficient and cost-effective strategy.[3]

This study assessed Bishop score vs. TVS for predicting successful labor induction by prostaglandins.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This was a prospective observational study conducted in Northern Railway Central Hospital, New Delhi, a tertiary health care center, from December 2019 to August 2020.

Informed consent was taken from all the eligible patients. The study included pregnant women ≥37 weeks of gestation, with vertex presentation, a single live fetus and intact membranes. All eligible subjects were counselled regarding TVS for cervical assessment by transvaginal ultrasound using a Toshiba Famio 8 Machine with a TVS probe (6 MHz) in the center. The cervical scoring system, as proposed by Neha Bajpayee et al. in 2015,[4] was used to assess the TVS score [Table 1].

After the TVS, the Bishop score was calculated by digital examination of the cervix to assess for dilatation, effacement, consistency, position, and station of the presenting part. The labor induction was considered successful following the onset of regular uterine contractions and cervical dilatation of 5 cms or more within 24 h of induction. The data were analyzed using appropriate statistical tests.

RESULT

A total of 53 patients who underwent labor induction during the study period were observed. The TVS score and the Bishop score were analyzed and compared in these patients. Among the 53 patients, 40 delivered vaginally with no instrumentation, whereas 13 had caesarean section. DISCUSSION

The mean gestational age at the time of induction corresponds with the studies conducted by Gomez Laencina et al.[5] and Panchampree et al.[6] In the study, most common indications for IOL were post-datism, followed by gestational diabetes mellitus.

In the current study, the main outcome was taken as successful induction of active labor within 24 h of induction. This is comparable to studies of Ransiri PADL et al.[7]

The various studies plotted ROC curves and calculated the area under the curve (AUC) for both cervical length and Bishop score. On comparing the cutoff points for the Bishop score of the current study, it has the same cutoff of 4 as in studies by Yang et al.[8] Neha Bajpai et al.[4] found similar results in their study, TVS score has more AUC than the Bishop score.

CONCLUSION

Hence, the current study concludes that preinduction assessment score helps in better timing of IOL, which in turn reduces the chances of failure of induction and operative interference. Although the Bishop score is a time-tested, cost-effective method to assess the cervix digitally to predict successful induction.

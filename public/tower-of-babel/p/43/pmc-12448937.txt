Family history of sudden cardiac death as a risk marker for ventricular arrhythmias in laminopathies
Authors: Gauthier Giordano, Julie Proukhnitzky, Frédéric Fer, Adrien Bloch, Marine Thuillot, Carole Maupain, Isabelle Magnin-Poull, Isabelle Jonveaux, Aurélien Palmyre, Agathe Bertinotti, Nicolas Mansencal, Christian de Chillou, Pascale Richard, Estelle Gandjbakhch, Philippe Charron
Published: 2025

Introduction

Laminopathies are responsible for 5–10% of dilated cardiomyopathies (DCM)1,2 and up to 46% of these patients experience severe ventricular tachyarrhythmias (VTA) leading to sudden cardiac death (SCD).3,4 Among genetic causes of DCM, the LMNA gene was identified as the most frequently associated with VTA.5 A prognostic model estimating the absolute 5-year risk of VTA in patients with laminopathies was developed in 2019.6 Predictors of VTA included in this model were LVEF, male sex, nonsustained ventricular tachycardias (NSVT), non-missense variant in LMNA gene, and the presence of first- or higher-degree atrioventricular (AV) block. An external validation of the risk calculator noted a low occurrence of VTA in patients with intermediate risk (5-year risk 7–25%) leading to a risk of excessive ICD implantation.7 Over-implantation raises clinical and ethical issues in this young population with a significant number of ICD-related complications.8,9

The 2022 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Guidelines recommend prophylactic ICD implantation for patients with a ≥10% estimated 5-year risk and a ‘cardiac phenotype’ (LVEF < 50%, AV block, or NSVT).10 Individualized decision-making remains challenging in intermediate-risk cases. We hypothesized that a family history of SCD could be an additional risk factor useful to refine SCD stratification and guide prophylactic ICD implantation.

Methods

We retrospectively analysed LMNA mutation carriers seen at three French academic hospitals between 1990 and 2024. Inclusion required a pathogenic or likely pathogenic LMNA variant.

Exclusion criteria matched those of the original risk calculator study:6 prior VTA, disease onset before age 16, or additional pathogenic variants in other cardiomyopathy genes. All events and baseline data were adjudicated.

VTA was defined as SCD, haemodynamically unstable VT, or appropriate ICD therapy. Risk scores were calculated using the 2019 LMNA-VTA model.6 ESC 2022 criteria (risk ≥ 10% + cardiac phenotype) were assessed for 5-year VTA prediction using sensitivity, specificity, and the C-index. Independent predictors were identified by multivariable Cox regression (significance level 0.05). Family history was defined as SCD in a relative before age 60, and the prognostic impact was analysed through ROC curves (using DeLong’s test), net reclassification index (NRI), and Kaplan–Meier analysis.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Of 165 patients, 138 were included in the final analysis (median follow-up 79 months; males; 47.8%; mean age: 41.5 years, probands: 34.8%, non-missense mutation in 32.6%). At baseline, mean LVEF was 54% (SD 13%), first-degree AV block was present in 28.3%, higher-degree AV block in 23.2%, and NSVT in 31.8%. Family history of SCD before the age of 60 was found in 48 patients (34.7%) when considering first-degree relatives and in 84 (61.3%) when considering relatives from the first to the third degree.

Baseline predictors of ventricular arrhythmias

During follow-up, 30 patients (21.7%, 3.3%/year) experienced VTA (4 SCDs, 10 haemodynamically unstable VT, and 16 appropriate ICD therapies), 17 within the first 5 years. Fourteen patients without VTA were censored due to death (n = 6) or heart transplantation (n = 8).

After multivariable analyses, baseline characteristics independently associated with VTA in the 5 following years were a lower LVEF, AV block and a first-degree family history of SCD before the age of 60 (Figure 1A). Family history of sudden cardiac death and ESC 2022 Guidelines

Among 57 patients with intermediate 5-year risk (7–20%), 5 (8.8%) experienced VTA. Of these, 4/21 (19%) had a first-degree family history of SCD, compared to only 1/36 (2.8%) without such history. Kaplan–Meier analysis suggested significance for the prognostic value of family history in this subgroup (log-rank test: P = 0.04) (Figure 1B and C). Family history had no discriminative value in low- or high-risk groups. However, in intermediate-risk patients, integrating first-degree family history improved risk reclassification and predictive accuracy. An alternative strategy designed to predict VTA, targeting high-risk patients (risk > 20%) or intermediate-risk (7–20%) with both cardiac phenotype and SCD family history yielded higher sensitivity (88% vs. 82%), specificity (64% vs. 51%), overall NRI of 0.18 [0.02–0.34]—including an NRI of 0.06 for events and 0.12 for non-events—and a higher C-index (0.90 vs. 0.78) and AUC (0.75 vs. 0.62, P < 0.001) as compared to the ESC 2022 guidelines algorithm for VTA prediction.

Discussion

This study is the first to report an association between a first-degree family history of SCD before age 60 and the occurrence of severe ventricular arrhythmias in LMNA mutation carriers, independent of other known risk factors.

In intermediate-risk patients, family history had clear clinical relevance: those without a family history had a very low event rate, while those with affected first-degree relatives had VTA rates comparable to high-risk individuals. This additional marker did not improve stratification in low- or high-risk groups, where the existing calculator remains adequate. However, in borderline cases—especially around the 10% risk threshold— assessing family history might provide additional guidance in decision-making process.

Limitations

This retrospective design carries inherent limitations. The main challenge was the assessment of NSVT, as episodes detected by 24-hour Holter monitoring do not have the same prognostic significance as those identified through long-term monitoring. However, this limitation does not undermine the main conclusions of the study. Seven patients were loss to follow-up and excluded. About one-third of the cohort was previously included to develop the 2019 LMNA-VTA risk calculator. As such, this study does not qualify as an external validation of the 2019 calculator. Non-sudden cardiac death and heart transplant may be competing events regarding SCD evaluation. All patients were followed in tertiary centres, which may introduce selection bias, although such centres often care for entire families and reflect the disease spectrum. Finally, the composite endpoint, although widely used, groups clinically distinct heterogeneous events.

Conclusion

This study shows an independent association between a family history of SCD and the occurrence of SCD in LMNA mutation carriers, with particular relevance for intermediate-risk patients. This parameter could enhance decision-making regarding prophylactic ICD implantation. These findings warrant external validation and confirmation in larger prospective cohorts before considering a modification of existing ICD implantation recommendation.

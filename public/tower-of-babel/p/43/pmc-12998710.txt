Long-Term Outcomes of Surgical Resection for Thymic Epithelial Tumour With Intrathoracic Dissemination
Authors: Eun Chae Kim, Ji Hyeon Park, Bubse Na, Kwon Joong Na, Samina Park, Hyun Joo Lee, In Kyu Park, Young Tae Kim, Chang Hyun Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe treatment of metastatic thymic epithelial tumours (TET) remains challenging with no established protocol, particularly regarding the feasibility of complete surgical resection. This study aimed to elucidate the long-term outcomes and identify risk factors influencing overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) following surgical intervention.MethodsBetween July 2000 and November 2021, 149 patients underwent surgery for metastatic TET with intrathoracic metastases at a single centre. Of these patients, 121 met the inclusion criteria. Among them, 93 had thymoma including World Health Organization (WHO) types A, AB, and B1-B3, and 28 had thymic carcinoma. While 73 patients had pleural or pericardial seeding, 48 had lymph node or pulmonary metastasis. Synchronous metastasis was present in 72 patients, whereas 49 had metachronous metastasis.ResultsThe 5- and 10-year OS rates for the entire cohort were 76.4% and 55.1%, respectively. Multivariable analysis identified WHO type as a significant factor affecting OS (HR, 4.260; 95% CI, 2.139-8.482; P < .001). The 5- and 10-year PFS rates were 32.4% and 20.1%, respectively. In multivariable analysis, myasthenia gravis (MG) (HR, 0.362; 95% CI, 0.185-0.851; P = .018), WHO cell type (HR, 3.071; 95% CI, 1.857-5.079; P < .001), and number of metastases (HR, 2.396; 95% CI, 1.273-4.512; P = .007) emerged as risk factors for PFS.ConclusionsSurgical resection for metastatic TET with intrathoracic dissemination demonstrated favourable OS; however, high progression rates necessitated repeated treatments. Factors including the number of metastases, WHO cell type, and the presence of MG were identified as significant determinants influencing PFS.Keywords: thymoma, thymic carcinoma, myasthenia gravis

INTRODUCTION

Thymic epithelial tumours (TET) are rare neoplasms primarily found in the anterior mediastinum. However, within the spectrum of tumours occurring in this region, TETs are the most prevalent, with incidence rates ranging from 0.13 to 0.32 per 100 000 population for thymoma.1–4 The latest World Health Organization (WHO) classification of thymic tumours divided TETs into thymoma and thymic carcinoma.5 Thymomas including types A, AB, and B1 showed a more favourable prognosis than types B2 and B3, which still demonstrated better outcomes than thymic carcinomas.6 Thymomas typically manifest slow progression, even in the presence of metastasis. Interestingly, in metastatic TETs, pleural metastasis occurs more frequently than metastasis to distant organs.

Owing to its slow progression and limited metastasis to distant organs, local treatments have been investigated for metastasis, particularly in stage IVa. Although chemotherapy and radiotherapy are treatment options for metastatic TETs, surgery may also be considered for selected patients. Previous studies have suggested that surgery could improve overall survival (OS) compared with chemotherapy alone for thymomas with pleural metastasis; consequently, a multimodality approach is currently considered for stage IVa thymomas.7–9 However, complete resection is challenging, and the suitability of surgery for thymic carcinomas and stage IVb TETs remains uncertain.

Thus, this study aimed to examine the long-term outcomes of surgical resection in patients with stage IV TETs, including all histological subtypes and identify risk factors influencing long-term survival, thus aiding in the development of future guidelines.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study protocol

The study protocol was reviewed by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of our institute, and it was approved as a minimal-risk retrospective study that did not require individual consent (IRB approval No. 2404-072-1528; July 15, 2024). The investigation adhered to the principles of ethics outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki as revised in 2013.

Between July 2000 and November 2021, 149 patients underwent surgery at our institute for pleural, pericardial, pulmonary or lymphogenous metastasis of thymic malignant tumours with curative intents. To achieve complete resection, intrathoracic organs, including the great vessels and trachea, were intentionally resected and reconstructed when necessary and feasible. If the surgery aimed to relieve symptoms such as chest wall pain without achieving complete resection of tumours, it was defined as palliative surgery. Exclusions included 28 patients who underwent palliative surgery, or were diagnosed with malignancies other than TET, or had M0 status without pathological evidence of metastasis. Patients’ stage was classified according to the ninth edition of the Tumour, Node, Metastasis (TNM) classification system for TETs, proposed by the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer in January 2025.10 Finally, the study enrolled 121 patients, which encompassed 73 with pleural or pericardial seeding (stage IVa) and 48 with pulmonary or lymph node metastasis (stage IVb) (Figure S1). For consistent classification, TNM staging (IVa or IVb) was applied according to the anatomical location of the metastasis, regardless of the timing of its detection. Among these, 72 patients underwent surgery for synchronous metastasis during the initial operation, while 49 underwent surgery for metachronous metastasis detected during follow-up after the initial operation. For survival analysis, the index operation was defined as the initial surgery for metastasectomy whether it was concomitant to primary tumour resection at the same time or not.

Tumour response to therapy was evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST).11 Pathological classification and staging followed the TNM staging system and the WHO histological classification.12 Macroscopically complete resection (MCR) was defined as the gross excision of all metastatic lesions. The Clavien-Dindo classification was used to evaluate the severity of postoperative complications.

TET-related death was defined as death caused by tumour progression leading to mortality or a fatal complication of therapy targeting TETs. Other specified causes included the progression of other cancers without any evidence of aggravated TETs, pandemic infection and other lethal diseases.

Statistical analysis

This study was conducted to determine the prognostic effect of various clinical factors in patients with stage IV TETs, focusing on the prognosis according to the stages and cell types. Accordingly, a prior comparison between subgroups—including age, myasthenia gravis (MG), WHO cell type, number of metastases, and TNM stage—was planned to evaluate the outcomes of surgical resection for stage IV TETs. Risk factor analyses for OS or progression-free survival (PFS) between the subgroups were conducted using Cox proportional hazards models. To address potential confounding, multivariable analysis included variables with a P-value < .05 in univariable analysis, along with clinically relevant factors such as gender, age, MG, neoadjuvant therapy, WHO cell type, complete resection, number of metastases, TNM stage, timing of metastasectomy and adjuvant therapy. A backward stepwise elimination method was applied, retaining variables with P < .05 in the final model. All analyses were exploratory in nature. No prespecified plan was employed to adjust for multiple comparisons, and 95% CIs were not adjusted accordingly. Therefore, inferences drawn from these analyses should be interpreted with caution and may not be reproducible.

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA, 2022) and R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Data are expressed as mean (SD) for normally distributed data variables, whereas medians with IQR were reported for non-normally distributed data. Survival curves for OS and PFS were analysed using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival between groups was compared using the log-rank test. A P-value <.05 was considered significant. Potential collinearity between MG and WHO cell type was assessed by Ridge regression and variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis in Python (version 3.12.4). The Ridge regression coefficient between MG and WHO cell type was −0.12, suggesting minimal dependency between these variables and the VIF value was 1.11, indicating low collinearity.

All demographic and clinical characteristics data were complete, without missing values. Missing follow-up data were addressed through censoring; although one patient was lost at 118.6 months, data were still sufficient for long-term analysis.

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

The median duration from initial surgery to metachronous metastasis was 38.5 (IQR, 21.1-69.9) months. Detailed preoperative data are presented in the Table S1. Nineteen patients in stage IVb group were pathologically confirmed to have lymph node metastasis, with a mean number of 3.5 ± 3.4. Among these patients, 13 had thymic carcinomas and 6 had thymomas. Of the entire cohort, 22 patients had MG as a comorbidity and 18 received preoperative treatment including immunosuppressant therapies such as steroid (n = 6), tacrolimus (n = 1), and immunomodulating interventions such as plasmapheresis (n = 1), and intravenous immunoglobulin (n = 1).

Surgical procedures

All procedures aimed to achieve MCR, utilizing various surgical approaches including 25 (20.7%) cases of video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery. Most patients (n = 91, 75.2%) underwent concomitant lung resection. To achieve a tumour-free margin, en-bloc lung resection was performed in 40 patients, and distant lung resection to remove pulmonary or pleural metastasis was performed in 32, and 19 patients underwent both en-bloc and distant lung resections (Table S2). Concomitantly resected structures included various structures such as the pericardium, phrenic or recurrent laryngeal nerve, and diaphragm. The details regarding the extent of thymectomy for the primary tumour and the resections for metastatic lesions are summarized in Table S3. In the metachronous group, resections for metastases were performed separately from the initial thymectomy, whereas in the synchronous group, concomitant resections for metastases were performed simultaneously.

To achieve curative aims, in some feasible cases, the critical intrathoracic organs including the trachea (n = 2), aorta (n = 1), pulmonary artery (n = 1), and right atrium (n = 1) were resected and reconstructed. Of these patients, 3 had thymomas and 2 had thymic carcinomas. Except for one patient with a microscopic residual tumour at the tracheal margin, R0 resection was achieved. Postoperatively, 2 patients after tracheal resection experienced wound complications requiring surgical intervention, whereas the others recovered uneventfully.

Early postoperative outcomes and follow-up data

The final pathologic report revealed thymoma in 84 patients (69.4%) and thymic carcinoma in 37 (31.4%). Among 116 patients in whom MCR was achieved, microscopic complete resection (R0 resection) was successful in 99 (81.8%) (Table S4). There was no operative mortality, and postoperative complications developed in 30 patients (24.8%). The median postoperative hospital stay was 7 (4-10) days. As for adjuvant therapy, chemotherapy was typically used (n = 55, 45.5%) while radiotherapy was added if there were concerns about residual disease (n = 23, 19.0%). During follow-up, disease progression was observed in 79 patients (65.3%). The pleura was the most common site for tumour dissemination (n = 54, 44.6%), followed by the lung (n = 18, 14.9%) (Table 1).

Early Postoperative Outcomes and Follow-Up

Primary endpoint: OS

The median follow-up duration was 43.3 (IQR, 28.7-102.3) months. The 5- and 10-year OS rates for the whole cohort were 76.4% and 55.1%, respectively (Figure 1A). Significant differences in OS between subgroups were observed based on the stage and pathology (Figure 2). Patients with stage IVa and thymoma showed significantly higher 5- and 10-year OS rates than those with stage IVb and thymic carcinoma, respectively.

Secondary endpoint: PFS

The 5- and 10-year PFS rates of the whole cohort were 32.4% and 20.1%, respectively (Figure 1B). The maximal number of interventions to treat disease progression in a single patient reached up to 7 times, with the most common modality being redo-metastasectomy (n = 104), followed by chemotherapy (n = 24) and radiotherapy (n = 8) including stereotactic ablative radiotherapy (n = 5) (Figure S2). The average number of therapies performed for progression was 1.4 ± 1.2. When comparing PFS between subgroups, TNM stage, number of metastatic nodules, age, presence of MG, and WHO classification demonstrated statistically significant differences in PFS (Figure 3A-E).

Multivariable risk factor analysis for PFS revealed MG, neoadjuvant treatment, WHO cell type, and number of metastatic nodules (≤10 vs >10) as significant factors affecting PFS in stage IV TETs (Table 3). The number of metastatic nodules showed differences in PFS between subgroups divided by a cutoff of 10, not only within the whole cohort but also in each group of stage IVa (P = .049) and stage IVb (P = .009), respectively.

OS and PFS in patients with metastasis TETs without surgery

To evaluate the effect of surgery on the metastasis of TET, clinical outcomes of the entire cohort who underwent metastasectomy (group O, n = 121) were compared with those of patients who did not undergo surgery during the same period (group N, n = 28). Group N included patients with stage IV thymoma (n = 6) and thymic carcinoma (n = 22), and the reasons for avoiding surgery were significant comorbidities or uncontrollable extrathoracic metastases. The OS and PFS of group N were significantly less favourable than those of group O (Figure 4).

DISCUSSION

This study investigated the long-term survival and progression rates of patients with stage IV TETs who underwent surgical treatment. The 10-year OS and PFS rates for these patients were 55.1% and 20.1%, respectively. WHO cell type was identified as a significant risk factor associated with OS. Additionally, the presence of MG, neoadjuvant therapy, WHO cell type, and metastatic nodules count >10 were significant risk factors for PFS. The prognostic impact of having more than 10 metastatic nodules has been demonstrated in previous studies,13,14 and our findings are consistent with these results, supporting the validity of this cutoff value.

The discordance between OS and PFS may indicate the need for additional interventions. Many patients in our cohort underwent multiple therapies, including repeated surgeries, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and combined approaches with neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapies (Figure S2). In our institution, the decision to administer neoadjuvant therapy was made by a multidisciplinary team based on tumour burden, invasiveness, resectability and the patient’s general condition. Adjuvant therapy was considered in patients with aggressive tumour biology or extensive metastases, where the risk of recurrence was deemed high. These treatments likely contributed to prolonged OS despite progression. Additionally, the indolent nature of TETs can lead to slow disease progression, extending patient survival periods even with recurrent disease.

Surgical treatment is generally recommended for stage IV TETs if the lesions are deemed resectable.7–9 In our institution, surgical resection was considered when patients, especially those with >10 nodules, showed partial remission after neoadjuvant therapy, making complete resection of the primary tumour feasible and the burden of metastasis manageable on computed tomography. Retrospective studies comparing surgical resection with chemotherapy have shown favourable OS for surgery.7,14–16 Still, no randomized controlled studies have compared surgical treatment to other modalities for stage IV TETs. Retrospective studies have reported 10-year OS rates of 51%-82%14–16 and disease-free survival rates of 19%-29%,15,16 similar to our findings, still with a weak evidence level emphasizing further studies.

Neoadjuvant treatment is a commonly employed strategy in advanced TETs, known to increase the complete resection rate and improve disease control.17,18 Retrospective studies have suggested that neoadjuvant chemotherapy could enhance OS in stage IV disease.19–21 However, these studies have included a relatively small number of stage IV cases. In the present study, chemotherapy was the preferred neoadjuvant treatment, whereas concurrent chemoradiation was generally administered if resectability of the primary tumour was uncertain. If the lesion was deemed to be completely resectable, upfront surgery was performed. Consequently, in multivariable analysis, the neoadjuvant therapy group demonstrated a higher progression rate than the upfront surgery group. We believe that this outcome reflects differences in patient selection rather than the oncologic role of neoadjuvant therapy. Therefore, further studies may be needed.

Moreover, the process of determining the type and number of procedures performed for each patient with progression must be considered. In our institution, treatment decisions for disease progression were made through multidisciplinary discussions, focusing on the feasibility of complete resection and associated risks. The decision for additional treatment was dependent on the progression pattern—shorter intervals and more progressions indicated palliative care, while longer intervals with fewer progressions indicated a better chance for cure through repeated interventions. When surgery was too challenging, alternatives such as chemotherapy were considered. Patients with indolent progression were often managed with follow-up alone.

Surgery for stage IVb disease is another issue. Although surgical resection is generally recommended for pleural metastasis (stage IVa), its appropriateness for lymphogenous or distant metastasis (stage IVb) is unclear owing to poor long-term survival outcomes reported in previous studies.7,21 In the present study, surgical treatment was performed on 48 patients with stage IVb, finding a 5-year OS rate of 58.2% which was higher than expected. Thus, if lesions are resectable, surgery can be considered for stage IVb disease.

The candidacy of stage IV thymic carcinoma for surgical resection is a subject of debate due to its aggressive nature compared to thymoma.22–24 Lymphogenous and distant metastases are more common in thymic carcinomas and previous studies have reported poor outcomes in patients with stage IV thymic carcinomas.15,22,25 In the present study, the WHO cell type, specifically thymic carcinoma, was identified as a significant risk factor for PFS. Some patients with thymic carcinoma demonstrated rapid progression following surgery, whereas others exhibited indolent progression-like thymoma. The 5-year OS rate for stage IV thymic carcinoma was 51.0%. These findings support considering surgical resection if the lesions are resectable; however, further studies are necessary.

This study has several limitations. The retrospective, single-centre study design is subject to inherent selection bias because patient inclusion is dependent on referral patterns and institutional practices. Consequently, the identified risk factors should be interpreted with caution and validated in larger, prospective studies. The selection bias, particularly in surgical candidacy, might have favoured patients with better prognostic profiles. Second, although our cohort of 121 patients is relatively large for a rare tumour type such as thymic epithelial tumour, it remains small for robust multivariable analysis. The limited sample size, relative to the number of covariates in the multivariable analyses, may have reduced statistical power and increased the overfitting risk, which may affect the reliability and generalizability of the identified risk factors. Despite adjusting for known confounders, unmeasured confounding factors could have affected the results. Third, patients with both synchronous and metachronous metastasis were included, potentially affecting long-term outcomes caused by differences in tumour biology. Although metachronous metastasis might indicate a less severe metastatic burden, no significant difference in OS or PFS was found between the groups. Finally, given the distinct biological behaviour of thymic carcinomas from thymomas, a combined analysis may obscure different clinical long-term outcomes.

CONCLUSION

This study reports the long-term survival outcomes of patients who underwent surgical resection for stage IV TET. Despite a significant progression after surgery, the long-term survival gain was considerable given the advanced stage of the disease. Surgical resection should be considered as a treatment option in patients with stage IV TETs, particularly when the lesions are deemed surgically resectable.

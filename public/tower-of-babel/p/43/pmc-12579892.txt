Suppressed switch to Bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide compared with dolutegravir/lamivudine: Real‐world evidence from the OPERA cohort
Authors: Gerald Pierone, Laurence Brunet, Jennifer S Fusco, Michael G Sension, Megan S Dunbar, Joshua Gruber, Douglas T Dieterich, Gregory P Fusco
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo compare the virologic effectiveness and discontinuation of the commonly prescribed fixed‐dose combination 3‐drug and 2‐drug regimens, bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide (B/F/TAF) and dolutegravir/lamivudine (DTG/3TC), in virologically suppressed individuals in routine clinical care in the US.MethodsFrom the OPERA cohort, ART‐experienced, virologically suppressed (viral load <200 copies/mL) adults with HIV who switched to B/F/TAF or DTG/3TC (01AUG2020‐30JUN2022) were followed through 31DEC2022, death, loss to follow‐up or discontinuation. Cox proportional hazard models with stabilized inverse probability of treatment weights were used to assess the association between regimen and time to confirmed virologic failure (cVF200; 2 viral loads ≥200 copies/mL or 1 viral load ≥200 copies/mL + discontinuation) or time to discontinuation.ResultsA total of 3512 B/F/TAF users were followed for a median of 16 months (IQR: 11, 22); 2327 DTG/3TC users were followed for a median of 15 months (IQR: 10, 21). cVF200 was observed among 2% of B/F/TAF and 3% of DTG/FTC users, for an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.84 (95% CI: 0.59, 1.18) for B/F/TAF compared with DTG/3TC. Regimen discontinuation was observed among 17% of B/F/TAF and 19% of DTG/3TC users, for an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.73, 0.94) for B/F/TAF compared with DTG/3TC. Treatment‐related reasons for discontinuation represented 6% of B/F/TAF and 9% of DTG/3TC discontinuations.ConclusionsIn this large US cohort, both B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC were well tolerated and effective treatment options among virologically suppressed individuals in routine clinical care, although DTG/3TC tended to be discontinued faster than B/F/TAF.Keywords: bictegravir, cohort, dolutegravir/lamivudine, regimen discontinuation, virologic failure

INTRODUCTION

People with HIV face a high burden of comorbidities and polypharmacy, which can complicate HIV treatment due to concerns for drug–drug interactions and lower treatment adherence [1, 2]. Single tablet regimens may be preferred to improve adherence due to their ease of use [3]. The single tablet 3‐drug regimen (3DR) bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide (B/F/TAF) is the most commonly prescribed antiretroviral therapy (ART) regimen in the US. It was approved in the US in February 2018 [4] and is included in the list of recommended initial regimens in the United States Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) HIV treatment guidelines [3]. Clinical trials have demonstrated the efficacy of B/F/TAF to suppress HIV viral load to undetectable levels [5, 6, 7, 8]. B/F/TAF has also been shown to be well tolerated and to have a high barrier to resistance [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].

Dolutegravir/lamivudine (DTG/3TC) is the most prescribed single tablet two‐drug regimen (2DR) available in the US. It was the second 2DR to be approved in the US for ART‐naïve (April 2019) [11] and ART‐experienced individuals on a stable ART regimen with a viral load (VL) <50 copies/mL, no treatment failure history, and no resistance to DTG or 3TC (August 2020) [12]. DTG/3TC is included in the list of recommended initial regimens in the DHHS HIV treatment guidelines for those with a viral load ≤500 000 copies/mL, and after resistance testing [3]. Importantly, DTG/3TC should not be used in the presence of HBV co‐infection, as the use of 3TC in the absence of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), or TAF, leads to high risks of HBV resistance [13, 14]. Clinical trials have established the efficacy, safety and tolerability of DTG/3TC in controlled settings [15, 16, 17, 18, 19].

A clinical trial and a few observational studies from Italy, Spain and China have compared treatment outcomes between individuals switching to B/F/TAF or DTG/3TC and showed the effectiveness of both regimens [20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26]. However, clinical practice and population characteristics in these countries differ from the US.

The real‐world assessment and comparison of ART regimens is essential to evaluate their performance among individuals who truly use them under routine care. Indeed, an assessment of channelling, comparative virologic effectiveness and regimen discontinuation of the most commonly prescribed fixed dose combination 3DR (B/F/TAF) and 2DR (DTG/3TC) in routine clinical care in the US may provide additional insight into the long‐term management of HIV infection.

METHODS

Study Population

The study population was derived from the Observational Pharmaco‐Epidemiology Research and Analysis (OPERA) cohort, a database of electronic health records (EHR) from 96 clinics across 22 states and 1 US territory. The OPERA database complies with all HIPAA, HITECH and HITRUST requirements, which expand upon the ethical principles detailed in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki. In line with HIPAA regulations, results for aggregate data including 1 to 5 individuals were not reported. The OPERA database received annual institutional review board (IRB) approval by Advarra IRB, including a waiver of informed consent and authorization for use of protected health information.

People with HIV in OPERA were included if they were aged ≥18 years, ART‐experienced and virologically suppressed (i.e., last viral load <200 copies/mL) at the time of switch to either B/F/TAF or DTG/3TC between 01 August 2020 and 30 June 2022. The first exposure to one of the regimens of interest was included; treatment groups were mutually exclusive. The study population was restricted to those with ≥1 OPERA visit prior to the start of the regimen of interest and ≥1 viral load measured during follow‐up. Each individual was followed from the start of the regimen of interest until the first of (a) regimen discontinuation (i.e. addition or removal of any antiretroviral [ARV], or treatment gap of >45 days), (b) loss to follow‐up, defined as 12 months after the last clinical contact (i.e., telephone contact, visit, lab test or consultation), (c) death, or (d) study end (i.e., 31 December 2022).

Study Outcomes

Confirmed virologic failure (cVF200) was defined as either two consecutive viral loads ≥200 copies/mL or regimen discontinuation consecutive to a viral load ≥200 copies/mL. The date of the second qualifying event was used as the date of cVF200.

Regimen discontinuation was defined as discontinuation or modification of any regimen component, such as adding or removing any ARV, or a gap in ART of >45 days. Reasons for discontinuation were identified through diagnoses, lab results or notes in the charts, and classified as either treatment‐related or not. Treatment‐related reasons included virologic non‐suppression (as noted in chart or last viral load ≥200 copies/mL within 30 days prior to discontinuation), presence of an adverse diagnosis or side effect (as noted in the chart or new mental health, liver, renal, or bone comorbidity diagnosed within 21 days prior to discontinuation) or presence of a laboratory abnormality (any alanine transaminase [ALT], aspartate aminotransferase [AST], alkaline phosphatase [ALP] or bilirubin >3 times the upper limit of normal [ULN], or eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73m2 at the last reading within 21 days prior to discontinuation). Other reasons for discontinuation may or may not be related to the treatment itself, but the information in the chart was insufficient to make a direct connection. These included a switch to long‐acting ART, pregnancy, access issues (as noted in chart: cost/formulary/availability), non‐adherence (as noted in the chart), treatment gap >45 days, patient choice (as noted in the chart), provider choice (as noted in the chart) or any other reason (as noted in the chart). More than one reason for discontinuation could be attributed to the same discontinuation event, if applicable.

Statistical Analyses

Incidence rates of virologic failure and discontinuation were estimated for the B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC groups using univariate Poisson regression. The association between regimen and time to cVF200 or time to discontinuation was assessed with Cox proportional hazard models. The assumption of proportionality was assessed graphically. Stabilized inverse probability of treatment weights (IPTW) were used to control for race, payer type, baseline CD4 cell count and baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR; CKD‐EPI 2009 equation). The initial set of covariates considered was selected based on expert knowledge or observed imbalances across exposure groups. Logistic regression was used to obtain the propensity score for switching to either B/F/TAF or DTG/3TC. The final set of variables included in the propensity score model was determined by the distribution of demographic and clinical characteristics in the B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC groups, as well as the performance of the propensity scores generated. Overlap of propensity scores between B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC was assessed using a visual analysis of the density distribution of the propensity scores [27]. The distribution of weights was evaluated for the presence of large weights. Model specification was adjusted to obtain a mean weight of 1 and ensure covariate balance. Standardized mean differences in covariates were estimated before and after applying the IPTW to assess balance, using a threshold of ≤10% to indicate acceptable balance [28]. All analyses were conducted among individuals with complete data on included covariates.

Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the impact of the cVF definition. First, a stricter definition of failure was used: two consecutive viral loads ≥50 copies/mL, or discontinuation consecutive to a viral load ≥50 copies/mL (cVF50). Second, only the first virologic failure criterion was used: two consecutive viral loads ≥200 copies/mL (cVFVL). In this analysis, a single viral load ≥200 copies/mL followed by discontinuation was not considered a cVF event.

RESULTS

Study Population

Between 01 August 2020 and 30 June 2022, 3713 virologically suppressed individuals switched to B/F/TAF and were followed for a median of 16 months (IQR: 11, 22) after switch. In the same period, 2327 individuals switched to DTG/3TC and were followed for a median of 15 months (IQR: 10, 21). Confirmed Virologic Failure

Using a viral load threshold of 200 copies/mL, 2% of people on B/F/TAF and 3% of those on DTG/FTC experienced cVF200 (unweighted and weighted populations). Of those, 29% of B/F/TAF failures discontinued after a single viral load ≥200 copies/mL compared with 44% of those on DTG/3TC. The median time to cVF200 was 10 months (IQR: 5, 14) in the B/F/TAF group and 12 months (IQR: 4, 16) in the DTG/3TC group. Kaplan–Meier curves are available in supporting information figure S1. The incidence rate of cVF200 was 1.7 per 100 person‐years on B/F/TAF and 2.1 per 100 person‐years on DTG/3TC (Figure 2). The adjusted hazard ratio was 0.84 (95% CI: 0.59, 1.18) comparing B/F/TAF to DTG/3TC (Figure 2).

In the sensitivity analysis using a threshold of 50 copies/mL, cVF50 was more frequent, occurring in 9% of individuals on B/F/TAF and 8% of individuals on DTG/FTC (unweighted and weighted populations). Among those who experienced cVF50, 19% of those on B/F/TAF discontinued after a single viral load ≥50 copies/mL compared with 33% of those on DTG/3TC. The median time to cVF50 was 9 months (IQR: 6, 13) in the B/F/TAF group and 8 months (IQR: 5, 14) in the DTG/3TC group. The incidence rate of cVF50 was 6.7 per 100 person‐years (95% CI: 6.0, 7.4) on B/F/TAF and 6.4 per 100 person‐years (95% CI: 5.5, 7.4) on DTG/3TC. The hazard ratio for cVF50 was 1.04 (95% CI: 0.86, 1.26) for B/F/TAF compared with DTG/3TC.

In the sensitivity analysis defining failure as two consecutive viral loads ≥200 copies/mL, cVFVL occurred in 2% of people on B/F/TAF, and 1% of those on DTG/FTC (unweighted and weighted populations). The median time to cVFVL was 10 months (IQR: 5, 13) in the B/F/TAF group and 12 months (IQR: 5, 16) in the DTG/3TC group. The incidence rate of cVFVL was 1.2 per 100 person‐years (95% CI: 1.0, 1.6) on B/F/TAF and 1.1 per 100 person‐years (95% CI: 0.8, 1.6) on DTG/3TC. The hazard ratio for cVFVL was 1.08 (95% CI: 0.69, 1.69) for B/F/TAF compared with DTG/3TC.

Regimen Discontinuation

Over the course of follow‐up, 17% of B/F/TAF users discontinued the regimen, compared with 19% of DTG/3TC users (unweighted and weighted populations). There was minimal overlap between discontinuation and confirmed virologic failure outcomes, with only 7% of those who discontinued having also met the criteria for cVF200. The median time to discontinuation was 12 months (IQR: 6, 17) in the B/F/TAF group and 10 months (IQR: 4, 17) in the DTG/3TC group. Kaplan–Meier curves are available in supporting information figure S2. The incidence rate of discontinuation was 12.4 per 100 person‐years for B/F/TAF and 14.8 per 100 person‐years for DTG/3TC (Figure 2). Those on B/F/TAF were statistically less likely to discontinue their regimen than those on DTG/3TC, with a hazard ratio of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.73, 0.94; Figure 2). Treatment‐related discontinuations represented 6% of B/F/TAF and 9% of DTG/3TC discontinuations. The difference observed stemmed primarily from a higher proportion of virologic non‐suppression among DTG/3TC discontinuers; laboratory abnormalities were rare in both groups. Most discontinuations were related to other reasons, such as a switch to cabotegravir + rilpivirine long‐acting injections (B/F/TAF: 10%, DTG/3TC: 15%). A large proportion of discontinuers experienced a treatment gap >45 days (B/F/TAF: 55%, DTG/3TC: 37%). No reason for discontinuation could be identified among 13% of B/F/TAF and 15% of DTG/3TC discontinuers (Table 4).

CONCLUSIONS

In this large cohort study of 5839 virologically suppressed individuals who switched to B/F/TAF or DTG/3TC in routine clinical care in the US, both regimens were virologically effective, although DTG/3TC tended to be discontinued faster than B/F/TAF.

Indeed, virologic failure was infrequent and no statistically significant difference in effectiveness was observed between B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC. These two regimens were compared head‐to‐head in one clinical trial of people with a viral load <50 copies/mL switching from a regimen containing either cobicistat, efavirenz, TDF or consisted of >1 pill/day [26]. At 48 weeks, a switch to DTG/3TC was non‐inferior to a switch to B/F/TAF, with undetectability maintained in 93% and 90%, respectively. Only one virologic failure (viral load ≥50 copies/mL with consecutive viral load ≥200 copies/mL) was observed in the B/F/TAF group. A few observational studies have compared DTG/3TC and B/F/TAF [21, 22, 23, 25]. A Spanish cohort study reported that among 253 ART‐experienced individuals, 2% of B/F/TAF and 4% of DTG/3TC users had a viral load ≥200 copies/mL at 48 weeks after the switch, with no statistically significant difference between the groups. Of note, 78% of B/F/TAF and 86% of DTG/3TC users had a viral load <50 copies/mL at switch [22]. This study appears to be an extension of a previously published study with similar conclusions [21]. In a single‐centre study including 690 suppressed individuals switching to DTG/3TC or B/F/TAF in Spain, maintenance of suppression was high, at 96% in both groups. Similarly, virologic failure defined as two consecutive viral loads >200 copies/mL was rare, occurring in 1% in both groups [23]. In a retrospective analysis of 454 people in China, 94% of those switching to B/F/TAF and 96% of those switching to DTG/3TC had a viral load <50 copies/mL at 48 weeks; among those with a viral load <50 copies/mL at the switch, 96% in both groups maintained a viral load <50 copies/mL at 48 weeks [25].

In this OPERA study, regimen discontinuation occurred statistically faster with DTG/3TC than with B/F/TAF. Another OPERA study reported discontinuation rates to be approximately 1.8 to 2.1 times higher with DTG/3TC than with B/F/TAF, but 1.4 to 1.9 times lower than with DTG 3DR in ART‐naïve and ART‐experienced individuals [29, 30]. European studies found no difference in discontinuation between B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC. Indeed, an Italian cohort study of 476 virologically suppressed individuals switching to B/F/TAF or DTG/3TC found no difference in the probability of discontinuation at 24 weeks after switch [20]. Several Spanish observational studies reported comparable rates or hazards of B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC discontinuation [21, 22, 23, 24]. The proportion of discontinuation in this study was comparable to other reports (B/F/TAF: 17% vs. 5%–24%; DTG/3TC: 19% vs. 2%–24%) [20, 22, 23, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36].

This study found fewer treatment‐related discontinuations with B/F/TAF than DTG/3TC, although these represented <10% of all discontinuations, suggesting that both regimens were safe and well tolerated. DTG/3TC discontinuers were slightly more likely to have a viral load ≥200 copies/mL, although this did not translate into a higher risk of confirmed virologic failure. Treatment‐related discontinuations occurred less frequently in OPERA than in other studies, where discontinuation due to adverse events, intolerance or toxicity was reported in 14 to 69% of B/F/TAF discontinuers (1–5% of all B/F/TAF users) and 16% to 80% of DTG/3TC discontinuers (0%–4% of all DTG/3TC users) [20, 21, 22, 23, 32, 34, 35, 37]. However, in a single‐centre retrospective cohort study from China, B/F/TAF and DTG/3TC discontinuation was rare and unrelated to treatment effectiveness or adverse reactions [25]. Interestingly, those who discontinued the DTG/3TC in OPERA were more likely to switch to cabotegravir + rilpivirine long‐acting. This could be due, in part, to a likely correlation between oral and injectable 2DRs in terms of suitability and comfort level. The higher proportion of treatment gaps among B/F/TAF users could not be investigated, although it is consistent with higher proportions of B/F/TAF users being lost to follow‐up compared with DTG/3TC users in Spain [23]. One hypothesis to explain this is the possibility that individuals with adherence challenges may be channelled towards B/F/TAF and away from DTG/3TC due to a perception that 3DRs may have a higher barrier to resistance and thus be more forgiving than 2‐drug regimens [38, 39].

Findings from this study suggest the presence of channelling bias, with preferential use of B/F/TAF in some groups. Indeed, B/F/TAF was more likely to be prescribed to those who were of Black race, on Medicaid/Ryan White/ADAP, with previous virologic failure, lower CD4 counts and better kidney function. While some of these factors could have a direct role in the decision to prescribe B/F/TAF over DTG/3TC, others might be proxies for factors that could not be captured in the EHR, such as ART adherence, lifestyle, general health, social support or structural vulnerabilities. However, balance was achieved with inverse probability of treatment weighting. Channelling may also explain the higher proportion of treatment gaps among B/F/TAF users, as individuals with adherence challenges may be channelled towards B/F/TAF and away from DTG/3TC. Of note, this is consistent with higher proportions of B/F/TAF users being lost to follow‐up compared with DTG/3TC users in Spain [23]. Along with the channelling observed, the faster pace of discontinuation and the higher proportion of treatment‐related discontinuations such as loss of virologic suppression with DTG/3TC compared with B/F/TAF all suggest that providers use more caution when prescribing DTG/3TC than B/F/TAF.

This study has several strengths. The OPERA cohort is a large, diverse cohort representative of HIV care in the US, which includes clinics ranging from small rural practices to large metropolitan healthcare centres across the US. At the time of this study, it included EHR data from over 155 thousand people with HIV, representing approximately 14% of people with HIV in the US [40]. This large database of routine outpatient clinical care data provided access to information on ART prescriptions, laboratory testing and diagnoses in a real‐world setting. From the OPERA cohort, a large sample size of 6040 virologically suppressed individuals switching to one of the regimens of interest was derived, providing the power to detect small differences between groups. The design and statistical methods employed for this study helped ensure the validity of the results. The presence of channelling bias was formally assessed, and IPTW was employed to control for potential confounding. All covariates included in the propensity score exhibited an imbalance across treatment groups and may be predictors of the outcome, either directly or by proxy [41, 42]. Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of findings to the definition of virologic failure and confirmed the conclusions drawn from the main analysis.

Nonetheless, this study was limited by the uncertainty around reasons for discontinuation. Indeed, such reasons are often not documented in the EHR. Efforts were made to identify potential reasons by investigating diagnoses, lab results and other clinical notes, although none could be identified in 13 to 15% of cases. In addition, this study did not extend beyond discontinuation, and resistance profiles after virologic failure were not evaluated because resistance data, including the M184V mutation, were not available in the EHR. Individuals without any viral loads measured during follow‐up were excluded from the study due to the impossibility of assessing their virologic outcomes. It is possible that some experienced early discontinuation, thus preventing the measurement of follow‐up viral loads. However, their exclusion from the study prevented the assessment of their reasons for discontinuation and could have introduced some degree of selection bias in the modelling analysis if factors associated with early discontinuation were also associated with regimen selection. While statistical modelling employed a complete case analysis approach, only 5% of the study population was excluded due to missing covariates, and the risk of introducing selection bias was thus acceptably low. IPTW successfully balanced all covariates included in the propensity score (i.e., Black race, payer, CD4 cell count and eGFR) and one variable not included (i.e., failure of prior regimen). However, residual confounding is possible for variables that were not included in the model. While BMI was balanced prior to IPTW, the weighted standardized mean difference was marginally higher than the threshold for imbalance, and further statistical adjustment was not warranted. It has been suggested that real‐world comparisons of 2DRs and 3DRs could be biased because people who have been virologically suppressed for a long period are more likely to be considered a candidate for a switch to a 2DR than people with a recent history of viraemia, and thus represent a population with a lower risk of virologic failure than people switching to a 3DR [43]. However, in this study, the duration of the prior regimen was nearly identical in both groups. While the frequency of confirmed virologic failure was slightly higher in the B/F/TAF group, it remained a rare occurrence in both groups, reducing the potential impact of this possible immortal time selection bias. Moreover, if the aforementioned bias is present, it would have biased the results toward the null, suggesting that the point estimate for confirmed virologic failure which favours B/F/TAF could be an underestimation of the true effect size.

In conclusion, this large study of real‐world data in the US confirms that while the likelihood of discontinuation was statistically higher with DTG/3TC than B/F/TAF, both regimens are well‐tolerated and effective treatment options for virologically suppressed individuals in routine clinical care.

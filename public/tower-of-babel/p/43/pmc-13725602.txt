Watermelon genotype‐independent transformation via direct organogenesis from the cotyledon base
Authors: Jing Zhang, Yu‐Xuan Li, Rui Sun, Ding‐Ding Zuo, Zhong‐Hou Zhu, Xue‐Jie Zhu, Da‐Long Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInducing adventitious buds from the base of watermelon cotyledon through direct organogenesis resulted in a watermelon genetic transformation system that overcomes regeneration barriers and genotype limitations.Keywords: cotyledon base, direct organogenesis, genotype‐independent transformation, watermelon

As a critical tool for plant functional genomics, genetic transformation plays a pivotal role in advancing crop improvement and generating novel germplasm resources (Feng et al., 2021). Watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) is an important horticultural crop grown worldwide. Although researchers have explored various methods to enhance transformation efficiency, including selection of explants and diverse transformation techniques, the transformation of watermelon remains limited to a few genotypes (Tian et al., 2017). Conventional watermelon transformation methods often rely on callus induction from explants via dedifferentiation, followed by adventitious bud regeneration through redifferentiation (Tian et al., 2017; Li et al., 2025). Wild germplasms, such as “YL” and “TC”, are often used in these methods, whereas only a limited number of commercial cultivars or inbred lines have been successfully transformed (Tian et al., 2017; Feng et al., 2021). Although expression of ClGRF4 and ClGIF1 improves transformation efficiency in hard to transform watermelon genotypes, some studies suggest that constitutive expression of these GRF/GIF genes may cause abnormalities (Feng et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024). Therefore, a novel transformation strategy that overcomes the regeneration barrier and breaks genotypic constraints would greatly advance watermelon molecular breeding.

Procambium is a primary meristem that differentiates into primary vascular tissue. Procambial cells show stem cell characteristics and could be induced to participate in adventitious bud regeneration under tissue culture conditions (de Almeida et al., 2012; Xu and Huang, 2014; Xin et al., 2022). Vascular tissue acts as an essential structure for substance transport in plants. We considered that abundant vascular tissues harboring procambial cells are near the cotyledon–hypocotyl junction in newly germinated watermelon seeds. Accordingly, we created paraffin sections and used safranin‐fast green double staining, which successfully confirmed this distribution pattern (Figure 1A, B). Subsequently, an incision was made at the cotyledon base to expose the procambium cells, from which abundant adventitious buds were successfully induced (Figures 1C, S1). This strategy induces the formation of adventitious buds via direct organogenesis, bypassing the traditional callus (dedifferentiation–redifferentiation) pathway, thereby effectively overcoming regeneration barriers.

The novel strategy comprises three main stages: explant preparation, Agrobacterium‐mediated transformation, and subsequent culture (Figure 1D). Following surface sterilization, the dehulled seeds were cultured on basal medium (BM) in darkness for 3 d (Table S1). After germination, the hypocotyl of watermelon seed is removed to fully expose the procambium cells at the cotyledon base. Then, the distal end of the cotyledon relative to the hypocotyl is removed to help the explants better absorb nutrients from the medium, facilitating adventitious bud formation at the cotyledon base (Figure 1A–C).

Subsequently, the prepared explants were used for Agrobacterium‐mediated transformation. To more obviously evaluate the transformation efficiency of different parameter combinations, the explants were transformed with a RUBY‐expressing vector. Agrobacterium was cultured to an OD600 of 0.6–0.8, and then resuspended in liquid MS medium for explant infection (Table S1). The optimal combination was determined 3 d after co‐cultivation by the RUBY coloration rate (colored explants/total explants) (Figure 1C). Studies show that a 20‐min immersion enhances Agrobacterium‐mediated transformation (Zhang et al., 2023). The 14‐min vacuum infiltration from traditional watermelon transformation methods was retained (Wang et al., 2023). On this basis, the sonication time (15, 30, and 50 s at 45 kHz) was further optimized. By comparing the effects of different sonication times on the transformation efficiency, the optimal protocol comprised a 20‐min shaken immersion, followed by 30‐s sonication at 45 kHz and a 14‐min syringe vacuum infiltration (Figure 1D; Table S2).

A DsRed vector was used to screen for positive transgenic plants, in order to verify the feasibility of the transformation strategy. After co‐cultivation on Co‐culture medium (CM), Agrobacterium‐treated explants were transferred to bud induction medium (BIM) to induce adventitious buds (Table S1). After numerous adventitious buds emerged from the cotyledon base, these explants were transferred to bud elongation medium (BEM) to promote rapid bud growth (Figure 1C, D; Table S1). Buds showing DsRed fluorescence at this stage were excised and transferred to new BEM to continue growth. Following isolation, a single bud was cultured to develop into a sturdy plantlet, and then transferred to root induction medium (RIM) for rooting (Figure 1C, D; Table S1). In addition to DsRed, RUBY was also used to demonstrate the watermelon transformation effects. Successful generation of transgenic plants was achieved with both vectors, fully demonstrating the feasibility of this transformation strategy (Figure 1E, F).

Furthermore, to evaluate the efficiency of this strategy, experiments were conducted in “X77”, a watermelon cultivated genotype that was previously recalcitrant to transformation. Based on three independent biological replicates, the transformation efficiency of DsRed in “X77” was 14.3% ± 0.39%. “X77” and “YH” (wild germplasms) showed RUBY transformation efficiencies of 11.4% and 15.1%, respectively, yielding a mean of 13.25%. Additionally, fluorescence observation and genomic PCR collectively confirmed that DsRed is stably present in the T1 generation obtained from self‐pollinated T0 transgenic plants, demonstrating that the transgenic plants produced by this strategy can be stably inherited via sexual reproduction (Figures 1E, S2). In traditional watermelon genetic transformation, this process from callus induction to adventitious bud formation requires about 3–4 weeks in wild genotypes, whereas in cultivated genotypes, it takes even longer or often fails entirely. During the validation of “X77” using this transformation strategy, most explants produced adventitious buds in about 10 d (Figure 1C). Therefore, compared with conventional methods, this strategy not only enables the regeneration of “X77” but also achieves it in a shorter time (Figures S1, S3).

To further assess whether this transformation strategy successfully overcomes genotype restrictions, a CRISPR/Cas9 vector targeting the phytoene desaturase gene (ClPDS) was used for validation across diverse watermelon genotypes (Tian et al., 2017; Figure 1G). As shown in Figure 1H, albino‐positive plants were successfully obtained in multiple watermelon genotypes, including the cultivated genotypes “X77”, “HJ6”, “HJ18”, “W03C”, and the wild germplasms “YL”, “YH”. Hi‐Tom sequencing of albino “X77” plants revealed nucleotide deletions in both sgRNA target sites (Figure 1G). The percentage of albino‐positive plants was approximately 10.8%, 9.5%, 8.4%, 8.7%, 12.1%, and 12.7% for these six genotypes, respectively, with a mean value of 10.4%. While the efficiency varies among genotypes, this successful multi‐cultivar application obviously revealed the feasibility of this novel transformation strategy for cultivated genotypes.

This work presents a novel transformation strategy for watermelon, which induces direct organogenesis from the cotyledon base to bypass the traditional dedifferentiation and redifferentiation stages, thereby considerably overcoming the regeneration barrier, breaking genotype constraints, and shortening the adventitious bud regeneration time than traditional methods. This key advance unlocks the potential for functional gene studies and precise trait improvement in watermelon.

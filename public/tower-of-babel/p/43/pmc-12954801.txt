Real-world effectiveness, safety, and quality of life of biosynthetic semaglutide in Pakistani adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus: a study protocol
Authors: Muhammad Daoud Butt, Arshad Hussain, Siew Chin Ong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionType 2 diabetes (T2D) is a critical public health crisis in Pakistan, which currently holds the highest age-standardised prevalence globally. While semaglutide significantly improves glycemic control and weight, high costs limit access to the innovator molecule in low – and middle-income countries. Biosynthetic semaglutide provides an affordable local alternative, yet real-world evidence regarding its clinical performance and impact on quality of life (QoL) is currently lacking.Methods and analysisThe BIOSURE study is a 30-week, multicenter, prospective, non-interventional, single-arm observational protocol evaluating biosynthetic semaglutide in 268 adults with T2D across Pakistan. The primary objective is to quantify the absolute change in glycated hemoglobine (HbA1c) from baseline to 30 weeks. Secondary objectives include assessing changes in body weight, waist circumference, blood pressure, lipid profiles, and safety. Patient-centered outcomes will be measured via the Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire, Morisky Medication Adherence Scale, and SF-36v2 health survey. Statistical analysis will utilise mixed-effects modelling and multiple imputation for missing data.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been obtained from the USM Human Research Ethics Committee and local IRBs. The study is registered with the ANZCTR (ACTRN12625000610437). Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and policy forums to provide a clinical and assistive framework for scalable diabetes care in resource-constrained settings.Trial registration: Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry identifier: ACTRN12625000610437.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D) remains one of the most pressing global health challenges, with an estimated 589 million adults affected worldwide as of 2024 a number projected to rise to 853 million by 2050 (Aschner et al., 2021; Atlas, 2025). The disease contributes to over 3.4 million deaths annually, and global health spending on diabetes has now surpassed USD 1 trillion, underscoring its immense clinical and economic burden (Butt et al., 2024). Alarmingly, 43% of individuals with diabetes remain undiagnosed, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where healthcare infrastructure and screening programs are often under-resourced (Kengne & Ramachandran, 2024).

Pakistan ranks first globally in age-standardised diabetes prevalence (31.4%), facing a dual crisis of 9 million undiagnosed cases and a rising obesity epidemic, which acts as a primary driver of insulin resistance (Bhutta et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2022). Compounding this issue is the rising prevalence of obesity, a major driver of insulin resistance and T2D. According to the World Obesity Atlas, 2025, p. 43% of adults globally are overweight and 16% are obese, with Pakistan experiencing a sharp increase in obesity-related comorbidities, particularly among urban populations (Sun, 2025).

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) have revolutionised the management of T2D and obesity (Zheng et al., 2024). Importantly, patient-reported outcomes have become central to evaluating the real-world value of GLP-1 RAs. Improvements in body weight, energy levels, physical mobility, and treatment satisfaction translate into perceived gains in daily functioning and psychological well-being, underscoring the need for routine assessment of HRQoL in clinical practice settings (Ahmann et al., 2018; Ahrén et al., 2017; Aroda et al., 2019; Davies et al., 2021; Pantanetti et al., 2024; Pantanetti et al., 2025 Sorli et al., 2017; Webb et al., 2022;).

Subcutaneous semaglutide shows consistent, clinically meaningful weight loss and metabolic improvements in everyday care. In an Italian prospective cohort of 90 adults with T2D initiating weekly semaglutide, mean body weight fell by −4.69 kg at 6 months and −5.38 kg at 12 months; fat mass decreased by −2.1 kg at 6 months, with significant gains in BMI, waist circumference, HbA1c, blood pressure, and lipid profile, alongside higher treatment satisfaction (DTSQ) and improved SF-36v2 mental health (MCS) scores (Pantanetti et al., 2024). Extending these observations, a 48-week Spanish ambispective study in 117 obese adults with T2D documented median losses of −9.3 kg in body weight, −7.5 kg in fat mass, and −1.8 kg in visceral fat at 6 months, with skeletal muscle mass largely preserved at 12 months changes linked to improvements in metabolic control and risk markers relevant to renal and cardiovascular outcomes, and presumable HRQoL benefits (Caballero Mateos et al., 2024; Caballero-Mateos et al., 2025)

Evidence for oral semaglutide is similarly robust. A prospective Italian study reported HbA1c −1.24% and fasting plasma glucose −31 mg/dL after 6 months, significant improvements in body composition and cardiovascular risk factors, and increased DTSQ and SF-36v2 scores, underscoring combined clinical and patient-experience benefits with 14 mg/day oral therapy (Pantanetti et al., 2025). Longer-term public-health-oriented data showed sustained HbA1c reduction to 18 months and marked improvements in treatment satisfaction and perceived flexibility (T0 vs T3, p < 0.001). Additional real-world cohorts reaffirm durability: a 12-month study of 950 adults on oral semaglutide found HbA1c −0.84% (p < 0.001), 53% achieving HbA1c ≤ 7%, mean weight −2.28 kg, and significant reductions in LDL and diastolic blood pressure (p < 0.001). In mixed-formulation comparisons, a 175-patient Italian series reported 6-month reductions of −10.4 ± 8.1 kg (injectable) vs −6.7 ± 5.3 kg (oral); at 12 months, losses were −9.3 ± 7.5 kg (injectable) vs −10.7 ± 6.5 kg (oral), with HbA1c drops of up to −1.9% at 6 months in injectable users and −1.5% in oral users demonstrating broad glycometabolic benefit and weight maintenance to 24 months (−15.9 ± 11.4 kg in injectable users)(Balcázar-Valencia et al., 2024; Caballero-Mateos et al., 2025; Pantanetti et al., 2024; Pantanetti et al., 2025). Other pragmatic cohorts comparing oral and subcutaneous formulations suggest similar adjusted efficacy, but higher persistence with weekly injections at 12 months (92.4% vs 72.3%), and two-year analyses showing greater proportions of ≥10% weight loss with subcutaneous therapy, while ≥5% responders were comparable across routes. Shorter-term body-composition studies (24–26 weeks) consistently report preferential fat-mass loss with relative preservation of lean mass, whether semaglutide is given orally or subcutaneously – reinforcing the need to couple pharmacotherapy with resistance exercise and nutrition support to optimise skeletal muscle and physical function (Balcázar-Valencia et al., 2024; Del Prete et al., 2025; Rudofsky, Amadid et al., 2025 Ruseva et al., 2024; Yale et al., 2024;).

Beyond weight and glycaemia, emerging syntheses suggest HRQoL benefits across domains (e.g. SF-36v2, treatment satisfaction), with patient-reported improvements paralleling clinical change particularly where visceral fat, blood pressure, and lipids improve in tandem. In obesity-focused settings, systematic reviews and umbrella analyses corroborate double-digit per cent weight loss, modest reductions in SBP/DBP, favourable lipids, and QoL gains in SF-36v2 metrics, while highlighting typical gastrointestinal adverse events and the importance of lean-mass preservation strategies (Ruseva et al., 2024).

Safety requires deliberate discussion in light of recent signals regarding suicidal ideation and self-harm. A WHO global database disproportionality analysis reported higher odds of suicidal ideation with semaglutide versus other medications (including analyses stratified by antidepressant/benzodiazepine co-use) and urged urgent clarification. A large UK CPRD active-comparator cohort found crude incidence of suicidality at 3.9 vs 1.8 per 1,000 person-years for GLP-1 RAs vs DPP-4 inhibitors (HR 2.08, 95% CI 1.83–2.36), though design choices and residual confounding merit scrutiny (Shapiro et al., 2025). Conversely, a Spanish propensity-weighted population cohort comparing GLP-1 RAs vs SGLT-2 inhibitors observed no statistically significant increase (per-protocol HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.35–3.14) (Yu & Rej, 2024). Taken together, these mixed signals justify prospective neuropsychiatric monitoring and explicit documentation of patient-reported mental health as part of pharmacovigilance in routine care.

Despite its clinical success, access to innovator semaglutide remains limited in LMICs due to high costs, patent protections, and supply chain constraints (Pearson et al., 2025). In Pakistan, the monthly cost of branded semaglutide often exceeds the average per capita healthcare expenditure, which stands at USD 79 (Levi et al., 2022). This economic barrier has led to suboptimal treatment uptake, especially among low-income and rural populations.

From a manufacturing perspective, the method of production plays a critical role in accessibility and cost-effectiveness (Bharathi et al., 2024; Ntais et al., 2024). The innovator molecule is typically produced using recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology, where a gene of interest is inserted into a host cell commonly yeast or bacteria to express the therapeutic protein (Jayakrishnan et al., 2024; Kollabathula et al., 2025; Xu & Kuipers, 2025). From an access and manufacturing standpoint, production modality materially influences cost, scalability, and regulatory control. Solid-Phase Peptide Synthesis (SPPS) widely described in patents and technical literature constructs semaglutide’s 31-amino-acid backbone with high reproducibility and enables site-specific lipidation at Lys26 to extend half-life; convergent synthetic strategies and purification (e.g. HPLC) are now standard for high-purity API (Giri et al., 2025; Parisis et al., 2025). In Pakistan, local biosimilar/biosynthetic semaglutide initiatives have recently emerged to address shortages and affordability constraints part of a broader policy push to improve access and reduce counterfeit risk through strengthened regulatory oversight and domestic manufacturing capacity (Altabas et al., 2025).

While the innovator molecule has been extensively studied, no published data currently exists on the clinical performance, safety, or patient-reported outcomes of biosynthetic semaglutide (Rudofsky, Amadid et al., 2025). Furthermore, regulatory clarity remains evolving: although the FDA has resolved the shortage of status of branded semaglutide, compounded or biosynthetic versions may still be used under specific conditions, particularly in regions where affordability and access remain critical.

Against this backdrop of high unmet need, credible real-world effectiveness, and evolving manufacturing and safety considerations, our multicenter, non-interventional, single-arm observational study will evaluate the real-world effectiveness, safety, and HRQoL impact of biosynthetic semaglutide in adults with T2D across Pakistan. The primary endpoint is the absolute change in HbA1c from baseline to 30 weeks; secondary endpoints include changes in body weight, waist circumference, blood pressure, and lipid parameters, along with treatment retention, adverse events (including hypoglycaemia and neuropsychiatric signals), and medication adherence. We will capture patient-centered outcomes using DTSQs/DTSQc, MMAS-8, and SF-36v2, enabling a comprehensive appraisal of treatment satisfaction and QoL in routine practice. By generating robust local evidence, this study aims to inform clinical decision-making, guide policy on affordability and access, and support broader adoption of biosynthetic semaglutide in LMICs where cost, supply, and real-world performance are paramount.

Methodology

Study design and setting

This is a 30-week, prospective, multicenter, non-interventional, single-arm observational study designed to evaluate the real-world effectiveness, safety, and patient-reported outcomes of biosynthetic semaglutide in adults with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2D). The study will be conducted across outpatient diabetes clinics in Pakistan, encompassing both urban and semi-urban settings to ensure representative sampling. The protocol is developed in accordance with the SPIRIT 2025 guidelines (Chan et al., 2013, 2025) and adheres to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and ICH-GCP standards. The study is prospectively registered with the Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry (ACTRN12625000610437).

The primary objective of this multicenter, non-interventional observational study is to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of biosynthetic semaglutide in improving glycemic control among adults with Type 2 diabetes in Pakistan. Treatment administration

Biosynthetic semaglutide will be prescribed and administered in accordance with routine clinical practice. Available formulations include 0.25, 0.5, and 1.0 mg doses, administered subcutaneously once weekly. Dose titration and maintenance will be determined by the treating physician based on individual glycemic response and tolerability. Participants will receive standardised training on injection techniques and may self-administer or receive caregiver assistance.

Study visits and follow-up

Participants will be evaluated at three predefined time points: baseline (week 0), interim follow-up (week 12–18), and end-of-study (EOS) visit (week 30) as represented in Figure 1. Data will be captured using standardised electronic case report forms (eCRFs) and validated patient-reported outcome instruments. Clinical assessments will include anthropometric parameters, vital signs, laboratory investigations, and medication adherence. Study endpoints

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is the change in HbA1c levels (absolute percentage) from baseline to EOS.

Secondary endpoints

Secondary endpoints include the proportion of patients achieving HbA1c targets of <8.0%, <7.5%, and <7.0%, as well as a reduction in HbA1c by ≥1.0%-point. Additional secondary outcomes include the proportion of patients achieving ≥3% and ≥5% weight loss, changes in body weight, waist circumference, lipid profile, and blood pressure. Medication adherence, Treatment retention rate and incidence of adverse events will also be evaluated.

Exploratory endpoints

Exploratory endpoints include the weekly biosynthetic semaglutide dose at EOS, the proportion of patients who did not require additional antidiabetic medications, and physician-assessed clinical success. Changes in patient-reported outcomes will be assessed using the Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire (DTSQs and DTSQc) and the SF-36v2 health survey.

Sample size estimation

Assuming a clinically significant HbA1c reduction of 0.5% with a standard deviation of 1.0, a 95% confidence level, and 80% statistical power, the calculated sample size is 126 participants. After accounting for a 10% dropout rate and ensuring feasibility across multiple centres, the final sample size has been increased to 268 participants.

Data management and confidentiality

All study data will be entered in real-time into a secure, password-protected electronic data capture system. Unique patient identifiers will be used to maintain confidentiality. Access to the data will be restricted to authorised study personnel. Quarterly audits will be conducted, with 10% of the data randomly verified for accuracy and completeness.

Reporting guideline and checklist

This study protocol has been developed and reported in accordance with the SPIRIT 2025 (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials) guideline. A completed SPIRIT 2025 checklist is provided to ensure transparency and comprehensive reporting of all required protocol elements. The checklist maps each SPIRIT item to the corresponding section of the manuscript to facilitate reader verification and methodological clarity.

Statistical analysis

Analysis populations

All statistical analyses will be conducted using R (version 4.3.1). The full analysis set (FAS) will include all participants who initiated biosynthetic semaglutide and completed both baseline and end-of-study assessments. A per-protocol set (PPS) will be defined by excluding participants with major protocol deviations or incomplete data. Safety analyses will be performed on all participants who received at least one dose of biosynthetic semaglutide, regardless of follow-up status.

Descriptive and inferential statistics

Continuous variables such as HbA1c, body weight, waist circumference, lipid parameters, and blood pressure will be summarised using means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges, depending on the distribution of data. Normality will be assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For the primary endpoint change in HbA1c from baseline to week 30 – a paired t-test will be applied for normally distributed data, while the Wilcoxon signed-rank test will be used for non-parametric distributions. Secondary continuous outcomes will be analysed using repeated measures ANOVA or linear mixed-effects models to account for intra-individual variability over time.

Categorical variables, including the proportion of participants achieving predefined HbA1c targets (<8.0%, <7.5%, and <7.0%) and weight loss thresholds (≥3% and ≥5%), will be analysed using Chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Treatment retention, hypoglycaemia incidence, and the need for additional antihyperglycemic agents will be evaluated descriptively, with logistic regression models employed to explore predictors of treatment success, discontinuation, or dose escalation.

Patient-reported outcomes

Changes in patient-reported outcomes will be assessed using validated instruments including the Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire (DTSQs and DTSQc), Medication Adherence (MMAS-8) and the SF-36v2 health survey. Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) will be used to compare post-treatment scores while adjusting for baseline values. These analyses will provide insight into treatment satisfaction and health-related quality of life across the study period.

Handling of missing data

Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation techniques under the assumption of missing at random (MAR). Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to evaluate the robustness of findings, including comparisons with complete case analyses and last observation carried forward (LOCF) methods where appropriate.

Interim analysis and multiplicity adjustment

An interim analysis will be conducted once 50% of participants have completed the end-of-study visit. This analysis will focus on safety outcomes and preliminary effectiveness. To control for type I error inflation, adjusted significance thresholds will be applied using the O’Brien-Fleming alpha spending function. All interim and final analyses will be pre-specified in a detailed statistical analysis plan to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

Subgroup and covariate analysis

Exploratory subgroup analyses will be performed to assess treatment effects across demographic and clinical strata, including age, baseline HbA1c, BMI categories, and clinic location (urban vs rural). Multivariable regression models will be constructed to adjust for potential confounders and identify independent predictors of glycemic response, weight loss, and treatment satisfaction.

Conceptual framework

This protocol presents a real-world, multicenter observational study designed to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and patient-centered outcomes of biosynthetic semaglutide in adults with T2D in Pakistan. The study design is conceptually aligned with the Semaglutide Real-world Evidence (SURE) program, which comprises nine observational studies conducted across ten countries. These studies have consistently demonstrated that once-weekly biosynthetic semaglutide yields clinically meaningful reductions in HbA1c and body weight, alongside a favourable safety profile and high levels of treatment satisfaction.

The SURE program was developed to complement findings from the SUSTAIN randomised controlled trials (RCTs), which established semaglutide’s efficacy under tightly controlled conditions (Rudofsky, Amadid et al., 2025; Rudofsky, Menzen et al., 2025 Yale et al., 2024;). However, RCTs often exclude patients with multimorbidity, polypharmacy, or socioeconomic vulnerabilities, thereby limiting their external validity. In contrast, the SURE studies and this biosynthetic semaglutide investigation embrace the complexity of real-world clinical practice, capturing outcomes in more representative and heterogeneous populations (Holmes et al., 2021; Yale et al., 2024).

This study is particularly significant as it represents the first formal evaluation of biosynthetic semaglutide in a lower-middle-income country (LMIC) setting. While the originator molecule has demonstrated robust efficacy across global trials, its high cost and limited availability pose substantial barriers in resource-constrained environments (Drucker, 2025; Lee et al., 2025; Son et al., 2025). Biosynthetic alternatives, if proven effective and safe, may offer a scalable and economically viable solution for improving diabetes care in LMICs such as Pakistan. By assessing both clinical and patient-reported outcomes, this study aims to generate a holistic understanding of biosynthetic semaglutide’s role in routine practice.

The inclusion of validated instruments such as the Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire (DTSQs and DTSQc), MMAS-8 and the SF-36-v2 health survey ensures that the study captures not only metabolic improvements but also enhancements in health-related quality of life and treatment satisfaction (Ahmed et al., 2025; Kitsunai et al., 2026; Pylov et al., 2025 Saleem et al., 2012). These dimensions are critical in chronic disease management, where psychological and behavioural factors significantly influence medication adherence and long-term outcomes.

Moreover, the study’s endpoints including HbA1c reduction, weight loss, treatment retention, and physician-assessed clinical success mirror those used in the SURE trials, enabling meaningful cross-study comparisons (Rudofsky, Menzen et al., 2025 Yale et al., 2024). Exploratory analyses will further elucidate dose optimisation patterns, the need for adjunctive antidiabetic therapies, and subgroup-specific responses, all of which are essential for tailoring individualised treatment strategies (Holmes et al., 2021).

Safety monitoring will be rigorous, with adverse events systematically tracked throughout the study duration. Prior evidence from the SURE program indicates that semaglutide is generally well tolerated, with gastrointestinal symptoms being the most frequently reported adverse events and a low incidence of hypoglycaemia. This study will assess whether biosynthetic semaglutide maintains a comparable safety profile within the Pakistani population, thereby contributing to the global pharmacovigilance landscape (Ahmann et al., 2018).

This study addresses a critical evidence gap by evaluating a biosynthetic alternative to semaglutide in a real-world, multicentre LMIC context. Should biosynthetic semaglutide demonstrate comparable efficacy, safety, and patient satisfaction to its originator, it could represent a paradigm shift in diabetes management across South Asia and other low-resource regions. The findings may inform national treatment guidelines, reimbursement frameworks, and formulary decisions, ultimately enhancing access to effective diabetes therapies for underserved populations.

Data security and information governance

All data will be handled in accordance with the principles of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Malaysian Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) 2010, ensuring confidentiality, integrity, and secure storage of participant information.

Quantitative data

Participating clinical sites will be responsible for collecting and pseudonymising quantitative data. No personally identifiable information will be shared with the central research team. Geographic mapping, such as postal code-based socioeconomic indicators, will be conducted by designated local health entities with support from sponsor institutions.

Qualitative data

Patients will be invited to participate in interviews through their treating physicians or clinic staff, and fully informed consent will be obtained prior to participation. Healthcare professionals, commissioners, and providers will be contacted through institutional affiliations, and written informed consent will be secured before interviews. Interview recordings will be stored securely on encrypted servers at study centre, and only anonymised transcripts will be shared with collaborators outside the host institution.

Dissemination plan

Findings from the study will be disseminated through a 12-month interim report and a final study report to the sponsor and relevant stakeholders. Peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations will be subject to prior approval from Universiti Sains Malaysia and Alliance Healthcare Pvt Ltd. Additionally, policy briefs and stakeholder workshops will be conducted to inform diabetes care strategies in low- and middle-income countries.

Data sharing statement

De-identified participant data collected during the study will be made available upon reasonable request to the corresponding author following publication of the primary results. Access will be granted for academic and non-commercial research purposes, subject to ethics committee approval and compliance with data protection regulations.

Deep learning based optimal fish species identification to maximize production in fish ponds
Authors: Rajeshwarrao Arabelli, T Bernatin, Venkataramana Veeramsetty, C H Hussaian Basha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAquaculture is one of the most important activity in India that decides economic growth of the country. Currently, India ranked second in the world in terms of total fish production. In fish farming identifying the best fish species can grow in the fish pond is required that leads increase in production. In this based Deep Neural Networks (DNN) one of the deep learning models is used to identify the best fish species based on fish ponds water parameters like pH, temperature and turbidity. A dataset with 196 samples is developed by collecting real time data through pH sensor, temperature sensor and turbidity sensor in various ponds located at Warangal, Telangana State, India. The proposed DNN model is validated by comparing with other machine learning models like decision tree, random forest, SVC, KNN and Naive Bayes classifiers. Based on the simulation results it is observed that the proposed DNN model is performing better the other models with 100% accuracy in training and testing.Keywords: Deep Neural Networks (DNN), Fish Species, Aquaculture, Fish Ponds, Deep LearningSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

In India, aquaculture processes are of two types, such as freshwater and brackish water aquaculture. Freshwater aquaculture (FWA) means the breading of fresh water to fish such as katla, karp, and rohu. This type of aquaculture practices in states like Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan. In the case of brackish water aquaculture, the breading of fish involves seawater such as gray mullet and mud cabs. This type of aquaculture is practiced in states like West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, and Goa. Aquaculture is growing very fast in India, with an annual growth rate of more than 7%. India is in the second position in the world after China in fish production. In India, aquaculture is based on 3–6 species of fish, while in China it is 10 or more1.

The physical, chemical, and biological properties of water are largely responsible for optimal fish production2. In order to increase the fish production, we need to identify the best suitable fish that can grow well in fish ponds based on parameters like pH value, temperature, and turbidity. India ranks 3rd in fisheries by contributing 1.07% to national GDP and 5.30% to agriculture GDP3. Conventional aquatic species recognition techniques are expensive, time-consuming, expert-dependent, and inappropriate for applications on a massive scale. Deep learning-based approaches are required to identify fish species by avoiding the above limitations4.

A reduced version of AlexNet, a deep convolutional neural network, is developed to identify the fish species in the pond in5. The developed model’s performance is observed with other deep learning models like VGGNET and ALEXNET. An hybrid model with a combination of optical flow and Gaussian mixture models with a YOLO deep neural network is proposed in6 for fish species identification using datasets like LifeCLEF 2015 and dataset collected by The University of Western Australia (UWA). Identification of fish species eggs Using cytochrome c oxidase subunit is proposed in7.

Classification and regression tree approaches are developed to identify the best fish species in8. In this work a total of 62 fish species and 13 water quality parameters are considered. In4 a deep neural network-based smart system is developed to automatically identify the four fish species such as carp, grass carp, bighead carp and silver carp. The classification of fish species based on the use of a support vector machine based on color and texture characteristics is discussed in9. Automatic fish species detection underwater using with improved R-FCN with 99.94% accuracy is proposed in10. Similarly, using a CNN model with 5-fold cross validation having 100% accuracy is proposed in11.

Methodology

This sections presents details about data collection and data pre-processing techniques applied. And, also discuss about DNN models and its performance metrics.

Data collection

In order to train and test DNN model, a data consists of pH value, temperature, dissolved oxygen and turbidity is collected from Waddepally Lake, Warangal District, Telangana State, India. In this lake, local people grow fishes like karpio, katla, shrimp and silver-cap. In order to collect data of various water parameters corresponding sensors like pH, temperature, oxygen and turbidity are used. These sensor send the collected data to the cloud for future purpose like training and testing of DNN model. MATLAB provides Thingspeak13 cloud platform https://thingspeak.mathworks.com/ is used to store the collected data. Using these sensors, total 196 samples are collected with features like pH, temperature and turbidity. These samples are collected from fish pond where fish species like karpio, katla, shrimp and silver-cap are produced.

The selection of pH, temperature, and turbidity as the input features was based on both domain-specific literature and practical considerations related to dataset size and ecological relevance. These three parameters are widely acknowledged in aquatic and fishery sciences as critical determinants of fish habitat suitability and species distribution. pH influences fish physiology, enzyme function, and habitat preference. Studies such as14 and15 have identified pH as a key water quality parameter impacting fish diversity and health. Temperature is a primary driver of metabolic rates, breeding cycles, and migration patterns. It is frequently cited in species classification and habitat modeling16. Turbidity affects light penetration, feeding behavior, and predator avoidance. It serves as an important ecological signal in studies involving aquatic habitat selection17. Due to the limited dataset size (182 samples), we deliberately limited the number of input features to avoid overfitting and ensure model generalization. The chosen features align with real-world sensor availability in aquaculture monitoring systems, increasing the applicability of the model.

Deep Neural Network (DNN) model

Deep Neural Network (DNN)22,23 is developed by mimicking the functionality and architecture of human brain to build high complex non-linear relation between input and output variables for complex real time problems24. The proposed optimal DNN model has 3 neurons in input layer as there are 3 input features like pH, temperature and turbidity. The proposed optimal DNN model has 4 neurons in output layer as there are 4 fish species considered in this work like karpio, katla, shrimp and silver-cap. After fine tuning, DNN model with 3 hidden layers and 18 hidden neurons with ReLu activation function25 is considered as optimal DNN model architecture based on accuracy while predicting best fish species to grow in fish ponds based on water quality parameters. The chosen DNN architecture was lightweight, with a limited number of hidden layers and neurons. This design choice was made to prevent over-parameterization while still benefiting from the representational power of neural networks. In summary, our choice of a deep learning model was carefully balanced with appropriate architectural constraints by tuning hyperparameters. Given the complexity of the classification task and the encouraging performance observed during evaluation, DNNs proved to be a justified and effective approach, even under data-limited conditions.

The selection of comparison algorithms like Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVC, KNN and Naive Bayes was made to ensure coverage of diverse machine learning paradigms, each representing a different approach to classification as shown below.

Advanced ML models (e.g., XGBoost, LSTMs) and data augmentation methods (e.g., SMOTE, GANs) will be explored to enhance classification accuracy. These directions will ensure the scalability, reliability, and usability of fish species recommendation systems in real-world aquaculture. Conclusions

Fish farming, or aquaculture, has more impact on GDP of Indian economy, so needs some technology interventions to get more fish production that leads increase in GDP of country. In this paper, a deep neural network model is developed to identify the best fish species among Karpio, Katla, Shrimp and Silvercap to produce in a fish pond based on water parameters like pH, temperature, and turbidity. Data like pH, temeperature and turbidity is collected from Waddepally lake, Warangal, Telangana state, India. pH sensor, temperature sensor and turbidity sensors are used along with Raspberry Pi to collect data samples. After fine tuning, a DNN model with three hidden layers with 18 neurons in each layer with training and testing accuracy of 100% is considered as an optimal model to deploy in a real time application. In order to observe overfitting issues, perform a few tasks like K-fold validation. The performance of the DNN model is validated by comparing it with other models like dection tree, random forest, SVC. KNN and Naive Bayes.

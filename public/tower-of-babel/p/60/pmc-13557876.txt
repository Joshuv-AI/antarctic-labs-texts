Optimizing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy cycles based on radiologic response improves efficacy and survival in non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Jingyu Chen, Yichen Dong, Juemin Yu, Jialiang Wen, Haojie Si, Huikang Xie, Xinjian Li, Minglei Yang, Junqiang Fan, Yunlang She, Wei Li, Xiwen Sun, Junqi Wu, Long Xu, Chang Chen, Deping Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe optimal number of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy cycles in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains unclear. This study evaluates whether determining the number of treatment cycles based on interim radiologic response after the initial two cycles impacts therapeutic efficacy and survival.MethodsWe retrospectively identified 395 patients with stage IIB–IIIB NSCLC who received 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy between September 2020 and April 2024. Propensity score matching (PSM) was conducted between 2-cycle and 3–4-cycle groups, yielding 155 matched pairs. Patients were further stratified into partial response (PR) and stable disease (SD) subgroups based on their radiologic response after the first 2 cycles. Overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with postoperative complications.ResultsIn the overall matched cohort, receiving 3–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy was significantly associated with improved OS (P=0.01) and RFS (P=0.03), increasing 24-month RFS rates from 72.5% to 78.2%. Such improvements were observed without an increase in the risk of postoperative complications. Subgroup analysis revealed 3–4 cycles significantly prolonged RFS in patients with SD after 2 cycles (P=0.045), improving 24-month RFS from 60.9% to 72.8%. In this subgroup, continued treatment also significantly reduced residual viable tumor (RVT) (20.0% vs. 57.5%, P=0.04) and showed a trend toward an improved major pathological response (MPR) rate (43.8% vs. 31.8%). Conversely, no additional benefits were observed in patients who achieved an early PR.ConclusionsFor patients with radiologic SD after 2 cycles, continuing treatment to 3–4 cycles was associated with further reduction in RVT and prolonged RFS without increasing postoperative complications. By contrast, minimal benefit was observed with continued therapy in patients who achieved PR.

Highlight box

Key findings

• In the overall matched cohort, receiving 3–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy is significantly associated with improved overall survival and recurrence-free survival (RFS) compared to 2 cycles.

• The survival benefit of extended treatment is primarily driven by the stable disease (SD) subgroup, which exhibits significantly reduced residual viable tumor and prolonged RFS.

• Patients achieving a partial response (PR) after the initial 2 cycles derive relatively limited additional pathological or survival benefit from further neoadjuvant treatment.

• Extending neoadjuvant treatment to 3–4 cycles does not increase the risk of postoperative complications or compromise surgical safety.

What is known and what is new?

• Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy has become a standard of care for resectable non-small cell lung cancer, but the optimal number of treatment cycles (typically 2–4) remains controversial.

• This multi-center study demonstrates that the benefit of extending neoadjuvant cycles is response-dependent; interim radiologic assessment after 2 cycles can effectively identify patients who are most likely to derive benefit from additional therapy.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings support a response-adapted neoadjuvant strategy. Patients with SD after 2 cycles may benefit from additional treatment cycles, whereas those achieving an early PR appear to derive only limited additional benefit. Treatment duration should therefore be individualized to balance efficacy and surgical timing.

• Clinicians may consider interim radiologic assessment after 2 cycles as a decision-making tool; patients with SD are associated with improved efficacy after completing 3–4 cycles, whereas PR may warrant immediate surgery.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) plus chemotherapy have become the first-line treatment for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) without targetable driver mutations. For early-stage NSCLC, neoadjuvant immunotherapy has significantly improved pathological response and brought survival benefits (1). In pivotal phase III trials such as CheckMate 816, neoadjuvant immunotherapy increased the rate of pathological complete response (pCR) from 2.2% to 24.0% and extended the median event-free survival (EFS) from 20.8 to 31.6 months compared to chemotherapy.

Despite these promising results, the number of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy cycles varied across these clinical trials (Table S1). According to current expert consensus, 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy are recommended, with interim radiologic assessments performed every 2 cycles to guide subsequent treatment decisions (2,3). However, the optimal number of treatment cycles remains controversial. The phase II neoSCORE trial (4) compared 2 vs. 3 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in resectable NSCLC. Although the differences were not statistically significant, the 3-cycle regimen showed better pathological response [major pathological response (MPR): 41.4% vs. 26.9%, pCR: 24.1% vs. 19.2%], with comparable 12-month overall survival (OS) (92.3% vs. 86.2%). A retrospective study reported a higher radiologic response in patients who received more than 2 cycles compared with those who received only 2 (49% vs. 36%, P=0.007) (5). In contrast, another study found no significant difference in MPR between patients receiving 3–4 cycles and those receiving 2 cycles (57.4% vs. 57.3%, P=0.53) (6). These findings suggest that extending the number of neoadjuvant treatment cycles may not necessarily lead to improved outcomes in all patients.

However, it remains controversial which patient subgroups might benefit from extended neoadjuvant treatment cycles. Moreover, prolonged neoadjuvant treatment may delay surgical resection and potentially compromise the optimal timing of surgery. A prior study reported that due to disease progression and adverse effects, 16% to 22% of patients do not undergo resection of NSCLC as planned (7), suggesting that certain patient subgroups may not be suitable candidates for extended treatment cycles.

Postoperative pulmonary complications are important contributors to perioperative morbidity and mortality (8), and their incidence may be associated with the number of treatment cycles. A previous study reported a significantly higher incidence of postoperative complications in patients receiving only 2 cycles compared to those receiving 3–4 (28.0% vs. 12.9%, P=0.004) (6). However, current evidence remains limited.

Thus, this study aimed to evaluate the optimal duration of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and whether radiologic response during treatment could identify patients who may benefit from extended treatment cycles. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0522/rc).

Methods

Patients inclusion and study design

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital (IRB No. K25-569; May 26, 2025), and an informed-consent waiver was granted due to the inability to contact some patients because of loss to follow-up or other reasons. All participating hospitals were informed of and agreed to the study. Patients with clinical stage IIB-IIIB NSCLC [according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual, Eighth Edition (9)] who received 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy from four hospitals (Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital; Ningbo Hospital of Ningbo University; The Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine; and Ningbo Number 2 Hospital) between September 2020 and April 2024 were enrolled (Figure S1). Patients with known sensitizing driver gene mutations and progressive disease (PD) after the first 2 cycles were excluded. Treatment decisions were made by a multidisciplinary team comprising surgeons, medical oncologists, pathologists, and radiologists. The application of continued neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy was based on a comprehensive assessment of radiologic response after 2 treatment cycles, treatment tolerance, surgical resectability, and patient-specific factors including Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) status, comorbidities, and preferences.

Our study involved a direct comparison of OS and recurrence-free survival (RFS) between patients receiving 2 cycles and those receiving 3–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. To determine whether interim radiologic analysis could inform subsequent treatment strategies, patients were stratified into partial response (PR) and stable disease (SD) subgroups based on assessments conducted after the initial 2 cycles. Within these subgroups, we evaluated the impact of extending treatment (3–4 vs. 2 cycles) on residual viable tumor (RVT), OS, and RFS to identify the specific population most likely to benefit from a longer duration of therapy. Furthermore, we investigated the association between the number of neoadjuvant cycles and the incidence and severity of postoperative complications to ensure that extending treatment did not compromise surgical safety.

Radiologic assessment

Radiologic tumor response was assessed at baseline, after 2 cycles of neoadjuvant therapy, and prior to surgical resection. All imaging was reviewed by experienced radiologists, and radiologic response was evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 (10). PR was defined as at least a 30% decrease in the sum of the diameters of target lesions from baseline. SD was defined as a decrease of less than 30% or an increase of less than 20% in the sum of diameters.

Pathological evaluation

All resected tumor specimens underwent standardized histopathological examination to assess pathological response. MPR was defined as ≤10% RVT in the resected primary tumor and negative lymph nodes. pCR was defined as 0% RVT in both primary tumor and all sampled lymph nodes. Programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) expression was categorized by a tumor proportion score (TPS) of <1%, 1% to <50%, or ≥50%, with unavailable results recorded as missing data. Following propensity score matching (PSM), PD-L1 expression was well-balanced between the 2-cycle and 3–4-cycle groups.

Follow-up

The follow-up was completed in September 2025, with a median follow-up period of 26.9 months [95% confidence interval (CI): 24.0–30.5]. OS was defined as the time from the date of surgery to death from any cause. RFS was defined as the time from the date of surgery to recurrence or death, whichever occurred first.

Postoperative complications

Postoperative complications were systematically evaluated and graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification (CDC) system (11). Grade I complications included minor events requiring no specific clinical intervention, whereas grade II or higher included complications that were potentially life-threatening or required pharmacologic treatment, surgical intervention, or prolonged hospitalization.

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages), and continuous variables as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). To reduce potential selection bias and balance baseline covariates between 2-cycle and 3–4-cycle groups, PSM (12) was performed using 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching, with a caliper of 0.1 on the logit of the propensity score. The propensity score was estimated using a logistic regression model including the following baseline covariates: age, gender, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cardiac disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), smoking history, clinical tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, PD-L1 expression, radiologic response after 2 cycles, and pathological type. After matching, a total of 155 matched pairs were identified.

For comparative analysis, categorical variables were compared using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, and continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or Student’s t-test. OS and RFS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Predictors of postoperative complications were analysed using multivariable logistic regression. All statistical tests were two-sided, with P<0.05 considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.1).

Results

Patient characteristics

We retrospectively included 395 patients diagnosed with NSCLC who received 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy followed by surgical resection. Baseline characteristics of the overall cohort are summarized in Table 1. Among these patients, 177 (44.8%) received 2 cycles, 130 (32.9%) received 3 cycles, and 88 (22.3%) received 4 cycles. The median age was 64 years (IQR: 58–68 years). Most patients were male (n=355, 89.9%), and 253 (64.1%) were former smokers. The proportion of stage IIIB disease was 39.5%, 40.8%, and 51.1% in the 2-, 3-, and 4-cycle groups, respectively. PD-L1 positivity was observed in 37.8%, 44.6%, and 44.3%, and squamous histology in 66.1%, 64.6%, and 64.8%, respectively. After 2 cycles, 230 patients (58.2%) achieved PR; 58.3% of PR cases and 41.7% of SD cases received additional treatment cycles.

Efficacy and survival stratified by treatment cycle

Among the 310 patients who completed 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, 166 (53.5%) achieved MPR, including 94 (30.3%) with a pCR (Figure 1). There were no significant differences in MPR rates between groups (52.9% for 2 cycles vs. 54.2% for 3–4 cycles, P>0.99). However, significantly improved OS (P=0.01) and RFS (P=0.03) were observed in the 3–4 cycle group (Figure S2). Additionally, the 24-month OS and RFS rates differed between the groups (86.0% and 72.5% in the 2-cycle group vs. 93.3% and 78.2% in the 3–4 cycle group, respectively).

To further investigate the potential beneficiaries of 3–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, patients were stratified into PR and SD subgroups based on their radiologic response after the first 2 cycles. In the SD subgroup, the MPR rate was 31.8% for those receiving only 2 cycles but increased to 43.8% for those receiving 3–4 cycles, although this trend did not reach statistical significance (P=0.22). Notably, undergoing 3–4 cycles of neoadjuvant therapy led to a significantly greater reduction in RVT within the SD subgroup: the median RVT was 57.5% (IQR: 5.0–80.0%) in the 2-cycle group compared to 20.0% (IQR: 0–70.0%) in the 3–4-cycle group (P=0.04; Figure 2A). Furthermore, a cycle-dependent downward trend in RVT was observed within the SD subgroup as the number of treatment cycles increased from 2 to 4 (P=0.02; Figure S3). In contrast, no further reduction in RVT was observed in the PR subgroup (P=0.81; Figure 2B).

Patients with SD also exhibited significantly improved RFS when receiving 3–4 cycles (P=0.045; Figure 3A) and a trend toward better OS (P=0.06; Figure 3B) compared to those receiving only 2 cycles. Exploratory analyses within the SD subgroup

Given the heterogeneity of radiologic responses within the RECIST-defined SD category, we further explored whether different degrees of tumor shrinkage were associated with the benefit of extended neoadjuvant therapy. Candidate radiologic response cutoffs from 25% to 5% tumor shrinkage were assessed in 5% decrements.

Using a 10% cutoff, patients with 10–30% tumor shrinkage showed a trend toward improved outcomes with 3–4 cycles compared with 2 cycles (OS, P=0.07; RFS, P=0.051; Figure S4A,S4B), whereas those with <10% shrinkage did not show significant differences between treatment-cycle groups (OS, P=0.67; RFS, P=0.46; Figure S4C,S4D). Similar findings were observed using a 5% cutoff: patients with 5–30% shrinkage showed a trend toward improved OS (P=0.052; Figure S5A) and significantly improved RFS (P=0.048; Figure S5B), whereas those with <5% shrinkage did not show significant differences (OS, P=0.95; RFS, P=0.53; Figure S5C,S5D). Given the limited sample size in these subgroups, these findings should be considered exploratory but suggest that the benefit of extended therapy may be less evident in SD patients with very limited tumor shrinkage.

Nodal downstaging

The SD subgroup showed a trend toward nodal downstaging compared to the PR subgroup (SD: 54.8% with 2 cycles vs. 66.7% with 3–4 cycles, P=0.25; PR: 82.4% with 2 cycles vs. 81.8% with 3–4 cycles, P>0.99) (Table 2). However, 3 (5.0%) patients in the SD subgroup underwent an upstaging of lymph nodes after receiving 3–4 cycles of treatment prior to surgery, which was not observed in other subgroups. Among these 3 patients, one showed a negative radiologic response of the primary tumor (<0% size reduction) after the initial 2 cycles; two had baseline stage IIB disease and one had stage IIIA disease, while PD-L1 expression was 0–50% in two patients and >50% in one patient.

Surgical procedure and postoperative complications

Following 2–4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, two patients (0.6%) underwent wedge resection, 274 (88.4%) underwent lobectomy, 18 (5.8%) underwent extended lobectomy, and 16 (5.2%) underwent pneumonectomy. Postoperative complications occurred in 35 (22.6%), 22 (24.4%), and 17 (26.2%) patients in the 2-, 3-, and 4-cycle groups, respectively. Of the 74 patients who developed postoperative complications, 16 (21.6%) experienced a severity of grade III or higher according to CDC (Table 3). Furthermore, multivariable logistic regression indicated that the number of neoadjuvant cycles was not significantly associated with the risk of postoperative complications [odds ratio (OR) =0.90, 95% CI: 0.47–1.71, P=0.75; Figure S6].

Discussion

Recent studies have explored the correlation between neoadjuvant cycles and treatment efficacy. In the phase II neoSCORE trial (4), 34.5% of all enrolled patients achieved MPR, with rates of 26.9% in the 2-cycle arm and 41.4% in the 3-cycle arm, although this difference was not statistically significant.

Similarly, the MPR rate in SD cases showed an increase (2 cycles vs. 3–4 cycles: 31.8% vs. 43.8%, P=0.22) in our study, without statistical significance. This may be explained by the limited number of patients in the SD subgroup after PSM, with 66 patients in the 2-cycle group and 64 patients in the 3–4-cycle group. In addition, the conventional binary definition of MPR may not fully capture the extent of pathological response. Although MPR is defined as 10% or less RVT, recent evidence suggests that this single cutoff may not fully reflect treatment efficacy in neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (13).

To assess treatment response more accurately, we evaluated RVT as a continuous variable. Notably, extending neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy to 3–4 cycles significantly reduced RVT in patients exhibiting SD after the initial 2 cycles [median (IQR): 20.0% (0–70.0%) vs. 57.5% (5.0–80.0%); P=0.04]. In contrast, no such reduction was observed in patients with a PR (median: 3% vs. 1%, P=0.81). Regarding long-term survival, the SD subgroup also exhibited significantly prolonged RFS after receiving 3–4 cycles compared to only 2 cycles. These findings suggest that, while patients achieving an early PR derive limited additional benefit from extending therapy beyond 2 cycles, those with SD may benefit from completing a total of 3–4 cycles before surgery. Given previous reports that prolonged neoadjuvant treatment may increase the risk of missing the optimal window for surgical resection, treatment duration should be individualized rather than extended for all patients (14).

However, radiologic responses within the SD subgroup were heterogeneous (15). While some patients approached the threshold for a PR, others exhibited minimal tumor shrinkage or even subtle radiologic enlargement. Consistent with this heterogeneity, our exploratory cutoff analyses suggested that the benefit of extended therapy may be less evident in SD patients with very limited tumor shrinkage, although this observation requires further validation given the limited sample size after subgroup stratification. Within our cohort, 6 SD patients actually demonstrated a negative radiologic response (<0% size reduction) after the initial 2 cycles. The clinical outcomes for this subset were poor: one patient (radiologic response of −11%) developed nodal upstaging after continued treatment, and ultimately, 50% (3/6) of these patients experienced disease recurrence or death during follow-up. Given previous evidence suggesting that ineffective neoadjuvant therapy may risk surgical resectability due to disease progression (7), applying a uniform 3–4 cycle strategy across the entire SD cohort may not be appropriate. For the high-risk subset exhibiting subtle or negative radiologic responses, prompt surgical intervention or transitioning to alternative therapeutic regimens may present a more reasonable response. Further evidence is warranted to validate these findings and to establish a radiologic cut-off to guide such clinical decisions.

Severe immune-related adverse events (irAEs) can result in dose reduction or discontinuation of immunotherapy (16). In the neoSCORE trial (4), the incidence of grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) was 31.0% in the 2-cycle arm and 29.0% in the 3-cycle arm, consistent with prior findings. Although adverse events have been reported among randomized controlled trials (RCTs) (17,18), direct comparisons among treatment cycles are still lacking.

To our knowledge, no RCTs to date have compared pathological efficacy and survival between cycles, stratified by radiologic response. Although stratification by tumor response provided clinically relevant information in our study, dynamic changes in regional lymph node status during treatment may provide additional information for optimizing neoadjuvant treatment strategy (19). Notably, nodal upstaging was observed in 3 SD patients who received extended treatment, further suggesting that nodal status should be considered when optimizing neoadjuvant treatment-cycle selection. Further incorporation of dynamic lymph node assessment and molecular biomarkers, such as PD-L1 expression and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), may provide a more comprehensive framework for individualized treatment-cycle selection in future studies (20).

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small in subgroup analyses after PSM, which may have reduced the statistical power to detect differences within specific subgroups. Second, our analysis did not incorporate an integrated evaluation of primary tumor radiologic response, dynamic regional lymph node status, and molecular biomarkers during treatment. These limitations underscore the need for validation in larger cohorts and for further studies to better define the balance between clinical benefit and potential risks.

Conclusions

Extension of neoadjuvant immunotherapy was associated with improved survival without increasing the incidence of surgical complications. Notably, among patients with SD after the first 2 cycles, extending therapy to 3–4 cycles significantly reduced RVT and prolonged RFS. In contrast, patients achieving PR after 2 cycles derived no significant benefit from further treatment.

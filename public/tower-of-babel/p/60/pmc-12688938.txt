Intraoperative CRISPR editing: the feasibility of gene surgery inside the operating room
Authors: Muhammad Khizar, Muhammad Zaib, Qaima Ali, Raghabendra Kumar Mahato
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Intraoperative CRISPR editing is the application of gene-editing technology directly during surgery; it is a new era of molecular precision in operative medicine. By integrating CRISPR/Cas9 systems into surgical workflows, clinicians can correct pathogenic mutations or disable tumor-promoting genes in real time. This approach builds upon recent in vivo trials demonstrating safe, localized gene editing, such as subretinal CRISPR delivery in the US and CRISPR-modified T-cell therapies in China. Advances in viral and nanoparticle delivery, intraoperative electroporation, and rapid genetic diagnostics have made gene surgery technically plausible. Nevertheless, challenges remain in targeting efficiency, immunogenicity, and ethical oversight. With appropriate safety validation, standardized operative protocols, and transparency frameworks, intraoperative CRISPR could revolutionize surgical care by transforming the operating room into a site of direct genomic correction.

Dear Editor,

Intraoperative CRISPR editing represents the next frontier in precision surgery, merging molecular gene correction with operative technique. The recent regulatory approvals of CRISPR-based therapies for sickle cell disease and β-thalassemia in the UK, US, and EU confirm clinical acceptance of genome editing and signal a shift toward localized, in vivo applications[1]. Delivering CRISPR/Cas9 systems directly to pathological tissue during surgery offers a unique opportunity to correct genetic defects or inhibit oncogenic mutations at their anatomical source.

Early clinical translation underscores this potential. In the US, subretinal injection of EDIT-101 for Leber congenital amaurosis demonstrated that intraoperative, localized CRISPR delivery can restore light sensitivity with an acceptable safety profile[2]. In China, PD-1-edited T-cell infusions for non-small-cell lung cancer validated the feasibility and safety of CRISPR-based interventions in oncology[3]. Meanwhile, European programs in Germany and the UK are developing regulatory and technical frameworks to integrate real-time gene editing into neuro-oncology and reconstructive procedures[4]. These examples collectively affirm that intraoperative CRISPR editing is no longer theoretical; it is a near-term clinical reality.

The main technical barrier remains efficient intraoperative delivery. Novel electroporation systems, AAV and lipid nanoparticle vectors, and imaging-guided catheters are being optimized to ensure precise, transient expression and minimize off-target edits[5]. Integrating rapid molecular diagnostics will allow surgeons to confirm target sequences intraoperatively, while anesthesiologists and pathologists manage real-time genomic monitoring. Rigorous interdisciplinary collaboration will be critical to ensure patient safety and ethical oversight. Our work is in line with the TITAN Guidelines on the need for transparency in AI use in health care[6].

In conclusion, intraoperative CRISPR editing redefines surgical precision, transforming the scalpel into a genomic instrument. With validated delivery systems, ethical transparency, and standardized multidisciplinary frameworks, gene surgery inside the operating room could become a defining advancement of 21st century medicine.

Fluoride stimulates the MAPK pathway to regulate endoplasmic reticulum stress and heat shock proteins to induce duodenal toxicity in chickens
Authors: Yanan Li, Tianwen Ma, Xiaoping Lv, Zongsheng Qiu, Shuxin Li, Jingjing Qi, Chengwei Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFluoride is one of the essential trace elements for body. However, excessive fluoride poses a major threat to human and animal health. Fluorosis may cause pathological damage of the duodenum, but the underlying mechanism needs to be further studied. This study was to investigate the effects of long-term exposure to sodium fluoride (0, 500, 1,000, 2,000 mg/kg) on the duodenum of chickens. The results showed that after NaF exposure, intestinal epithelial cells were disarranged, necrotic or even exfoliated, goblet cells and mucus secretion were increased, and inflammatory response was induced in duodenal tissue. Oxidative stress, endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERs), and heat shock proteins (HSPs) are an adaptive response, however long-term, excessive changes are detrimental. Fluorosis activates ERs through IRE1, PERK and ATF6 pathways, increases the expression of HSP60, HSP70 and HSP90, and causes apoptosis and oxidative damage in duodenal tissue. In addition, fluorosis can activate the MAPK signaling pathway. This article can provide a reference for exploring the potential duodenal toxicity of sodium fluoride.Key words: fluoride, MAPK, endoplasmic reticulum stress, heat shock protein, duodenal toxicity

INTRODUCTION

Fluorine is widely present in nature and continuously forms in soil and aquifer sediments, mainly in the form of fluorite (CaF2), cryolite (Na3AlF6) and fluorapatite (Ca5(PO4)3F) (Meenakshi and Maheshwari, 2006). Therefore, humans often unknowingly consume excessive fluoride through water and various food sources, which may pose a health threat, especially for young children and male populations (Nelima et al., 2023). Fluoride mainly accumulates in the body through blood flow, and the concentration reaches its peak within 1 h after ingestion and is not limited by the total amount of fluoride (Jha et al., 2011). Any form of fluorosis is a serious problem, not only causing toxic effects, but also causing cell damage and affecting the normal physiological activities of the body, seriously affecting the development and function of a variety of tissues and organs (Wei et al., 2019; Solanki et al., 2022). A large number of data have proved that fluorosis can aggravate apoptosis by inducing excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and then lead to liver, kidney, intestine, lymph and other organ lesions (Deng et al., 2016; Khandare et al., 2017; Han et al., 2021; Guo et al., 2024). In addition, fluorosis can not only directly affect human health, but also indirectly through edible plants and animals. Poultry is one of the main sources of human food. As mineral feed additives, bone meal and calcium phosphate are not sufficiently defluorinated in the process of use, resulting in poultry fluorosis, which seriously affects poultry production performance and causes serious economic losses (Dai et al., 2019). It has been reported that high fluorine can induce apoptosis of cecal tonsil lymphocytes in broilers. There are also data suggesting that fluorine can disrupt the integrity of the intestinal epithelium by mediating ras homologous gene families and calcium signaling to interfere with cytoskeletal and tight junction proteins (Li et al., 2023). Therefore, it seems a reasonable hypothesis that HF exposure may induce apoptosis of broiler intestinal tissue cells leading to enterotoxicity.

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) exists in all eukaryotic cells and can be divided into 2 types: rough endoplasmic reticulum filled with ribosomes and smooth endoplasmic reticulum rich in lipid synthase. The rough ER is mainly involved in protein synthesis, folding, processing and modification. Rough ER is essential for the biological stress response caused by an increase in unfolded or misfolded proteins. ER stress (ERs) occurs when protein homeostasis is out of balance. The endoplasmic reticulum responds to ERs by activating the unfolded protein response, which contribute to maintain cell survival and normal function (Walter and Ron, 2011). Under ERs, ER resident chaper-binding immunoglobulin protein (Bip) dissociation leads to activation of PERK, IRE1, and ATF6 to restore cell homeostasis (Ben Othmene et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022). If ERs is not alleviated, the body will not be able to return to the normal physiological state and will directly trigger programmed death and induce apoptosis through the activation of C/EBP homologous proteins (CHOP) (Lu et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2023). It is worth noting that ERs in intestinal epithelial cells can cause a series of adverse cellular reactions, such as oxidative stress (OS), inflammatory response and apoptosis, which are involved in the pathogenesis of intestinal barrier dysfunction (Chaudhari et al., 2014; Holczer et al., 2015; Vancamelbeke and Vermeire, 2017). Although ERs can induce enterotoxicity, little attention has been paid to fluoride and the duodenum.

When animals are subjected to different stresses (heat stress or other stresses such as ERs), heat shock proteins (HSPs) are widely induced to play a protective role by participating in protein folding, unfolding, and remodeling (Nawaz et al., 2021; Balakrishnan, et al., 2023). HSP60 is a mitochondrial molecular protein and is closely related to OS damage (Kumar et al., 2022; Min et al., 2022). HSP70 is one of the most critical HSPs, which alleviates OS-induced protein damage by inhibiting oxidative protein aggregation or promoting its degradation, and has a special relationship with intestinal tissue damage (Wan et al., 2024). HSP90 is strongly associated with programmed cell death, and significantly elevated levels indicate disease (Peng et al., 2022). Although HSPs can provide protection, they can also lead to apoptosis. However, the extent to which these reactions play a role in fluoride-induced enterotoxicity in chickens is unclear.

OS and inflammation may be involved in endoplasmic reticulum processes as markers of the body's response to injury. When animals were under OS, several antioxidant enzymes such as gut superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) and total antioxidant capacity were decreased, while tissue damage indexes such as malondialdehyde (MDA) were increased (Zhuang et al., 2019; Malyar et al., 2021). Meanwhile, ERs can trigger the NF-κB pathway and increase the expression of inflammatory factors, leading to inflammation (Zhu et al., 2022). When inflammation occurs, the transcription of pro-inflammatory mediators such as inducible nitric oxide synthase, interleukin (IL)-6 and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) increases (Hu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2023), further aggravating mucosal damage (Liu et al., 2020). TNF-α is closely related to the classical apoptosis signaling pathway, and directly activates caspase3 through caspase 8 to regulate exogenous apoptosis pathways (Gao et al., 2022). In addition, there are data showing that HSPs not only participate in OS response, but also inhibit the activation of TNF-α-induced mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway and the transcriptional activation of NF-κB (Lyu et al., 2020; Saraswati et al., 2023). However, it is unclear whether fluoride-induced enterotoxicity in chickens is related to inflammation and MAPK pathways.

In this study, we established an experimental model of sodium fluoride (NaF) exposure in chickens to verify the toxic effects of subchronic NaF treatment on the duodenum. The molecular mechanism of fluorosis was explored from different angles, and whether HSPs, MAPK pathway and apoptosis were involved.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animals and Experimental Design

One hundred and twenty-one-day-old Ross 308 broilers (60 females, 60 males, Harbin Wei-Wei Co., LTD., China) were selected as experimental animals. After one week of rearing under appropriate temperature, humidity and light conditions, the chickens were randomly divided into 4 groups, namely control (CON) group, low NaF (LF) group, medium NaF (MF) group and high NaF (HF) group. The chickens were fed regular full-price feed and water with no dietary restrictions. The dietary NaF exposure model was established by adding different concentrations of NaF to the diets of C, LF, MF and HF groups to make the concentration of fluoride ion reach 0, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 mg/kg. The trial period was 42 d. At the end of the experiment, the chickens were killed after being anesthetized with ether, and duodenal tissues were collected and preserved for subsequent experiments. This study was approved by the Animal Care and Welfare Committee of Northeast Agricultural University.

Hematoxylin-Eosin Staining

Fresh duodenal tissue was fixed with paraformaldehyde. After dehydration, transparency, embedding and sectioning, H&E staining was performed. Finally, observations were made with a light microscope and photographs were taken.

Transmission Electron Microscopy Observation

After glutaraldehyde (2.5%) fixation, duodenal tissues were dehydrated, embedded, sectioned, and stained. Finally, the ultrastructure of the duodenum was photographed by TEM (GEM-1200 ES, Japan).

OS Detection

The duodenal tissue was subjected to sample pretreatment according to the instructions. The supernatants were collected and their contents were determined using commercial CAT, SOD, MDA, and GSH assay kits (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai), respectively.

Reverse Transcription and Real-Time PCR

After total RNA was extracted from duodenal tissue using the Trizol method (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA), the concentration and purity were determined. Then, the RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using a reverse transcription kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China). RT-PCR was performed on an ABI-7,500 instrument (Applied Biosystems, Norwalk, CT) using SYBR Premix RT-PCR (Takara, Dalian, China). Inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, TNF-β, IL-6, IL-8 and IL-2 were detected. Primer sequences were designed using Primer 5.0 software and are shown in Supplementary Table 1. Finally, the relative mRNA expression was analyzed by 2−ΔΔCT using β-actin as the reference gene.

Western Blot

Total protein was extracted from duodenal tissues using RIPA (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) supplemented with 1% PMSF. Protein concentrations were quantitatively measured using the BCA protein assay kit (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). Equal amounts of protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and then transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% skim milk (w/v), primary antibodies were incubated (4°C). Then, after washing with PBST containing 0.05% Tween 20, secondary antibodies were incubated. Finally, the protein bands were detected using ECL solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), and the density was calculated using ImageJ software. The primary antibodies involved in this study are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

Statistical Analysis

Experiment data are presented as mean ± SD (data from at least 3 independent experiments). GraphPad Prism software (San Diego, CA) was used for data analysis. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Tukey's multiple post hoc test (p < 0.05) were used to assess the significance of the data. *, p < 0.05; **, p < 0.01; ***, p < 0.001.

RESULTS

Histopathological and Ultrastructural Changes of the Duodenum Induced by Fluorosis

HE staining and TEM were used to observe the histopathological and ultrastructural changes of the duodenum in each group to explore the toxic effect of fluorosis on the chicken. In the CON group, the intestinal villi were intact and unchanged (Figure 1A). After NaF exposure, intestinal epithelial cells were not arranged neatly, some cells were necrotic and shed (red arrows), and goblet cells as well as mucus secretion increased (blue arrows) (Figure 1A). Analysis of the ultrastructural changes in duodenal tissue by TEM revealed no abnormal ultrastructural changes in the CON group (Figure 1B). After NaF exposure, mitochondrial swelling appeared vacuolated, cristae were broken or even disappeared (red letter M), and endoplasmic reticulum was disarranged (blue letter ER) (Figure 1B).

Effects of different NaF doses on the chicken duodenum's histopathology (A) and ultrastructure (B). (A) The histopathological changes of the duodenum were observed by H&E staining. In the control group, the tissue structure was standard, and the duodenum damage was more evident with the increased NaF exposure. Intestinal epithelial cells are not arranged neatly, some cells are necrotic and shed (red arrow), goblet cells and mucus secretion are increased (blue arrow). (B) The ultrastructural changes of the duodenum were observed by TEM (scale bar 2.0 µm, 2000 ×). Mitochondrial swelling with vacuolization, cristae fragmentation or even disappearance (red letter M), and disordered ER arrangement (blue letter ER). Yellow letter N, nucleus.

Duodenal Inflammatory Reaction Induced by Fluorosis

It has been observed by H&E staining that fluorosis can lead to pathological changes in duodenal tissue. Therefore, the changes of inflammatory factors should be further detected to explore the changes of inflammatory response in duodenal tissue caused by fluorosis. Compared with the control group, the expression of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6 and IL-8 was significantly increased after NaF exposure (Figure 2). Among them, the degree of increase was the most obvious after HF exposure. These findings suggest that fluorosis stimulates an inflammatory response in the chicken duodenum.

Effects of NaF on Inflammatory factors in chicken duodenum. The mRNA levels of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6 and IL-8 were detected by RT-PCR. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were compared to the CON group.

OS Induced by Fluorosis

Since fluoride is an oxide, we explored whether it could cause oxidative stress in duodenal tissue. It was found that the levels of antioxidant indicators such as CAT, GSH and SOD were significantly decreased, while the level of MDA, an indicator of oxidative damage, was significantly increased after NaF exposure compared with the CON group (Figure 3). These results indicate that fluorosis can decrease the antioxidant capacity of duodenal tissue and induce oxidative damage.

Effects of NaF on OS in chicken duodenum. The levels of CAT, GSH, MDA and SOD were detected by commercial kits. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were compared to the CON group.

Cell Apoptosis Induced by Fluorosis

Significant mitochondrial damage was observed by TEM. At the same time, mitochondrial damage is one of the factors leading to apoptosis. Therefore, it is reasonable to explore whether fluorosis can cause apoptosis in duodenal tissue. It was found that the level of anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2 was significantly decreased, while the levels of pro-apoptotic proteins BAX, Cleaved Caspase3 and Cytochrome C (Cyt-C) were significantly increased after NaF exposure compared with the CON group (Figure 4). We further explored whether the apoptosis was endogenous or exogenous. It was found that the levels of Fas, Caspase8 and Caspase9 were significantly increased after NaF exposure compared with the CON group (Figure 4). These results indicate that fluorosis can cause both endogenous and exogenous apoptosis in duodenal tissue.

Effects of NaF on apoptosis in chicken duodenum. The protein levels of BAX, BCL-2, Fas, Cyt-C, Cleaved Caspase3, Caspase8 and Caspase9 were detected by western blot. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were compared to the CON group.

Changes of Duodenal ERs and Molecular Chaperones HSPs After Fluorosis

ERs and HSPs are adaptive changes, however, excessive changes can cause harmful effects. Changes in ER were observed in transmission electron microscopy. Therefore, the changes in ERs and HSPs after fluorosis were explored. Most of the ERs-related proteins and HSPs were significantly changed in the MF and HF groups. Next, the pathway changes related to ERs were further explored. The results showed that HF significantly increased the expression of IRE1, p-PERK and ATF6 compared with CON (Figure 5). At the same time, ATF4, CHOP, GRP78 and Caspase12 were also significantly increased (Figure 5). This suggests that fluorosis can lead to ERs through IRE1, ATF6, and PERK pathways. In addition, HF could also increase the expression of HSP60, HSP70 and HSP90 (Figure 6), indicating that fluorosis can cause a duodenal stress response.

Effects of NaF on ERs in chicken duodenum. The protein levels of ATF4, ATF6, GRP78, IRE1, p-PERK, PERK, CHOP and Caspase12 were detected by western blot. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were compared to the CON group.

Effects of NaF on HSPs in chicken duodenum. The protein levels of HSP60, HSP70 and HSP90 were detected by western blot. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were compared to the CON group.

Changes of MAPK Pathway After Fluorosis

How does fluoride, as an exogenous stimulus, cause pathological changes in the duodenum? MAPK signaling is very important for cell survival and contains 3 pathways such as JNK, p38 and ERK. It was found that the ratio of p-JNK/JNK, p-p38/p38 and p-ERK/ERK increased significantly after NaF exposure compared with the CON group (Figure 7). This indicated that fluorosis could activate MAPK pathway.

Effects of NaF on MAPK pathway in chicken duodenum. The protein levels of p-JNK, JNK, p-p38, p38, p-ERK and ERK were detected by western blot. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001 were compared to the CON group.

DISCUSSION

Fluorine is widely present in organisms and is particularly important for odontogenesis and osteogenesis (Iwatsuki and Matsuoka, 2016; Lee, et al., 2017). However, the health of humans and animals is now threatened by fluoride pollution in the environment (Fu, et al., 2022; Hu, et al., 2023). Excess fluoride is a major public health problem with multiple negative health effects. A large body of evidence shows that fluorosis affects not only hard tissues such as teeth and bones, but also non-skeletal organs such as the heart (Xie et al., 2020) and intestine (Ommati et al., 2024). Studies have shown that long-term intake of fluoride can lead to significant changes in the cecal intestinal microbiota and induce intestinal barrier dysfunction (Liu et al., 2019). Meanwhile, it has also been reported that mice with long-term exposure to NaF (100 mg/L) have severe damage to tight junction structures in the small intestine (especially duodenum) and colon (Fu et al., 2022). Therefore, the choice of duodenum as the affected organ of fluorosis is reasonable. The present study shows that fluorosis can lead to inflammation in duodenal tissue, which is related to fluorine-mediated OS. Moreover, the activation of Caspase 3 and the increase of death receptors Fas and Cyt-C were further suggested to be the mechanism of fluorine-induced cell apoptosis in duodenal tissue.

Fluorine is oxidative and can easily cross the cell membrane through simple diffusion to attack oxygen, resulting in excessive ROS and oxidative damage (Song et al., 2017). Excessive fluoride, which leads to ROS accumulation and down-regulates SOD and CAT activities, has been reported (Jothiramajayam et al., 2014; Samanta et al., 2016). Our data show that fluorosis can down-regulate GSH, SOD, and CAT levels and up-regulate MDA levels in the duodenum, which is consistent with previous studies (Ju et al., 2021). This suggests that HF in the present study may cause OS. OS can cause mitochondrial damage. Studies have shown that OS can lead to mitochondrial structural disorder, decreased membrane potential and dysfunction in chicken hepatocytes (Yang et al., 2019). Consistent with these findings, we found that high doses of fluoride could cause mitochondrial enlargement, fragmentation or even disappearance of mitochondrial crista in duodenal epithelial cells. It is well established that mitochondrial structural disruption can lead to the release of Cyt-C from the mitochondria into the cytoplasm, inducing a caspase cascade and subsequently leading to apoptosis (Li et al., 2022). The results of this study showed that high-dose fluoride increased the expression of pro-apoptotic proteins Cyt-C, BAX and cleaved Caspase3, and decreased the expression of anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2. Apoptosis can be divided into extrinsic pathway mediated by death receptors and endogenous pathway mediated by cell stress damage to mitochondria. The results of this assay showed that HF increased the expression of Fas, caspase8 and 9. These results suggest that fluorosis may promote the endogenous and exogenous apoptosis of duodenal cells by OS or promoting the expression of Fas, but the specific mechanism is still unclear and needs further study.

ERs and inflammation are both adaptive systems necessary for function and survival, but are detrimental if prolonged. Studies have shown that one of the molecular mechanisms of fluorosis is to promote ERs, affecting the heart, spleen and bone tissue (Deng et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2019; Hou et al., 2024). HF can cause intestinal epithelial cell irregularity, hemorrhage, ultrastructural disorder, mitochondrial swelling, cristae disappearance, vacuolization, and ER dilation. In severe or long-term ERs, the expression level of CHOP is significantly increased, which induces cell apoptosis (Hetz et al., 2020). Long-term exposure to high NaF concentrations stimulated PERK, IRE1, and ATF6 pathways in the ER of chicken duodenum. At the same time, tissue injury will increase the expression of HSPs, resulting in stress injury. In turn, stress also promotes the expression of HSPs, leading to tissue inflammation and programmed cell death. Studies have shown that HSP60 can cause kidney damage in obesity-related kidney disease, and induce apoptotic cell death and inflammation (Yildirim and Tatar, 2022). The present results showed that HF resulted in increased levels of HSP60, HSP70, and HSP90 in duodenal tissues. There are data suggesting that the over-expression of HSPs trigger inflammatory responses, such as HSP70 activating TNF-α through the NF-κB pathway (Zhang et al., 2015) and HSPs promoting IL-1β expression through the activation of inflammasomes (Martine and Rebe, 2019). TNF-α induces pathological ERs in intestinal epithelial cells, destroys the integrity of epithelial cells, and promotes inflammation. Our study shows that HF increases the levels of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, and IL-8 in the duodenum of chickens, resulting in an inflammatory response (Xiao et al., 2023).

MAPK is an important transmitter of signals from the cell surface to the nucleus, which can be stimulated by different extracellular signals, such as cytokines, hormones and stress. It has been reported that fluoride can activate the oxidative stress-mediated MAPK signaling pathway by phosphorylating JNK and ERK (Karube et al., 2009). At the same time, MAPK signaling is essential for the regulation of cell growth and apoptosis. Among them, JNK can induce apoptosis in response to ROS accumulation. ERK phosphorylation can also induce apoptosis of rat neurons by up-regulating the levels of pro-apoptotic proteins (Guo et al., 2020). Our findings further confirmed that fluorosis can activate p38, JNK and ERK signaling pathways, which may be an important mechanism of duodenal tissue injury caused by fluorosis. These results indicate that fluoride, as an extracellular stimulus, activates the phosphorylation of MAPK and initiates a downstream cascade of reactions that eventually induces duodenal tissue injury.

CONCLUSIONS

Taken together, our findings suggest that fluorosis can reduce antioxidant capacity leading to OS and mitochondrial damage, promote ERs and up-regulate HSPs, thereby promoting apoptosis in duodenal cells. At the same time, one of the important mechanisms of fluorosis causing duodenal tissue damage may be the stimulation of MAPK signaling pathway. Thus, this study provides new insights into the potential risks of subchronic NaF exposure to the chicken duodenum and provides a preliminary explanation of the molecular mechanisms underlying the enterotoxicity of NaF.

Mapping research trends in immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer: a parallel dual-database bibliometric study
Authors: Boxiang Zhang, Lucy Yue Lau, Boyang Li, Zehao Hong, Zeliang Zhao, Kuan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundImmune cell metabolism has emerged as a critical regulator of tumor progression and therapeutic response. In breast cancer, metabolic reprogramming of immune cells within the tumor microenvironment profoundly influences antitumor immunity, immune evasion, and resistance to immunotherapy. Metabolic pathways such as glycolysis, lactate metabolism, lipid metabolism, and ferroptosis have increasingly been implicated in immune dysfunction. However, a systematic overview of research trends in this rapidly evolving field remains lacking.MethodsA dual-database bibliometric analysis was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus to comprehensively characterize global research on immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer. Parallel analyses with cross-database consistency checks were applied to enhance robustness. Visualization tools, including CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and Bibliometrix, were used to evaluate publication trends, collaborative networks, thematic evolution, and citation dynamics.ResultsParallel analyses of two independent datasets were performed, including 618 publications from the Web of Science Core Collection and 862 publications from Scopus (1,480 records in total). The analysis revealed a clear transition from early investigations of general immune dysregulation to mechanistic studies focusing on immunometabolic pathways. Keywords such as “tumor microenvironment,” “T cell exhaustion,” “macrophage polarization,” “lactate metabolism,” and “ferroptosis” exhibited prominent citation bursts and stable clustering patterns, indicating their emergence as core research frontiers. China and the United States were identified as leading contributors, and consistent patterns across databases reinforced the credibility of these findings.ConclusionThis study delineates the evolving knowledge landscape of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer. By highlighting convergent bibliometric signals, our findings identify ferroptosis and lactate-related metabolic programs as priority directions for future mechanistic investigations and combination immunotherapy strategies, providing a data-driven framework for hypothesis generation in breast cancer immunometabolism.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04915-3.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most common malignancy and a leading cause of cancer-related death among women worldwide [1]. Despite advances in surgery, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and endocrine therapy, patients with aggressive subtypes such as triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) still face limited treatment options and poor long-term survival [2]. The advent of cancer immunotherapy, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has provided new therapeutic opportunities [3]. However, in breast cancer the clinical benefits of ICIs remain modest and heterogeneous compared with other cancers such as melanoma or non-small cell lung cancer, largely due to the highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) that compromises the activity of tumor-infiltrating immune cells [4]. Recent studies suggest that such immune dysfunction is not only driven by checkpoint signaling but also shaped by metabolic constraints in the TME, including nutrient competition, hypoxia, and lactate accumulation, which collectively impair T cell, natural killer (NK) cell, and macrophage function [5, 6]. This phenomenon, termed immune cell metabolic reprogramming, has become a critical concept linking metabolism and immunity [7]. In breast cancer, clarifying how immune metabolism governs immune cell behavior is increasingly recognized as fundamental for developing more effective and personalized immunotherapeutic strategies.

Immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer has emerged as a rapidly evolving research area driven largely by mechanistic and preclinical investigations. Accumulating evidence indicates that metabolic constraints within the tumor microenvironment, including glucose deprivation, hypoxia, and lactate accumulation, impair the effector function of tumor-infiltrating immune cells, particularly CD8⁺ T cells and macrophages [8–11]. Beyond glycolysis, lipid metabolism has gained increasing attention, with studies demonstrating that fatty acid oxidation and glutamine metabolism contribute to immune cell differentiation and, in certain contexts, to immunosuppressive phenotypes of tumor-associated macrophages [12–14]. Recent advances in spatial metabolomics and transcriptomic profiling have further revealed that intratumoral metabolic heterogeneity shapes immune cell localization, polarization, and functional states [15, 16]. Ferroptosis has also emerged as a prominent immunometabolic axis in breast cancer, with growing evidence suggesting that ferroptotic signaling in tumor cells can modulate antigen presentation and immune activation, while excessive oxidative stress may conversely compromise immune cell viability [17–19]. In parallel, preclinical studies increasingly explore combinatorial strategies that integrate metabolic modulation with immunotherapy, aiming to enhance immune infiltration, sustain effector function, and overcome therapeutic resistance [20–22]. Collectively, these studies reflect a clear shift from descriptive observations toward translational exploration of immunometabolic vulnerabilities. Nevertheless, despite substantial mechanistic progress, integrative synthesis across immune cell subtypes and systematic mapping of research priorities at the field level remain limited, underscoring the need for a comprehensive bibliometric analysis to clarify knowledge structure, thematic evolution, and emerging translational directions in breast cancer immunometabolism [23, 24].

Despite significant progress in mechanistic studies, a comprehensive synthesis of the research landscape and thematic evolution of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer is still lacking [25]. Bibliometric analysis provides an effective approach to capture research trends, identify knowledge gaps, and visualize interdisciplinary linkages. Widely used tools such as CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and bibliometrix enable the analysis of co-authorship networks, keyword clusters, and citation patterns across large-scale datasets. However, existing bibliometric studies in this domain remain limited in scope—typically focusing on either tumor metabolism or immune cell function—and are mostly based on a single source, most often the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). This reliance creates the risk of database bias, as differences in indexing, coverage, and metadata standards may distort results. By contrast, a dual-database strategy integrating WoSCC and Scopus broadens coverage, mitigates structural bias, and improves the representativeness, stability, and comparability of findings across the two platforms. Therefore, there is a pressing need for an integrated bibliometric framework that combines dual-database validation, multi-tool visualization, and domain-specific interpretation to systematically delineate the knowledge structure and emerging research frontiers in breast cancer immunometabolism.

Unlike previous bibliometric studies that typically relied on a single database and focused broadly on tumor metabolism or cancer immunology, our study introduces several distinctive innovations. First, we integrate both WoSCC and Scopus, enabling cross-database consistency assessment and minimizing structural bias. Second, we employ complementary bibliometric tools (CiteSpace, VOSviewer, Bibliometrix, and Scimago Graphica) to conduct multidimensional mapping of publication trends, collaboration networks, thematic evolution, and citation impact. Third, this work is the first to specifically and systematically analyze immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer, providing a targeted overview of this emerging interdisciplinary field. These innovations highlight the originality of our approach and strengthen its contribution to both methodology and domain-specific insights. To address these gaps, the present study aims to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer using a dual-database approach. By integrating data from both the WoSCC and Scopus, we ensured broader coverage, reduced selection bias, and improved the robustness of trend identification. A suite of visualization tools—including CiteSpace, VOSviewer, bibliometrix, and Scimago Graphica—was employed to map global research productivity, collaborative networks, thematic evolution, and citation impact. To our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically examine this topic using a dual-database, multi-platform bibliometric strategy with cross-database consistency checks. By focusing specifically on immune cell metabolism within breast cancer, the study provides novel insights into how immunometabolic research has evolved from basic discovery to translational and clinical applications. In addition, it identifies emerging therapeutic targets, highlights collaborative patterns, and delineates knowledge clusters that may guide future priorities. Ultimately, this work seeks to offer researchers, clinicians, and policymakers a structured understanding of the intellectual landscape of breast cancer immunometabolism, thereby supporting evidence-based decision-making, promoting interdisciplinary collaboration, and informing the development of precision immunotherapeutic strategies targeting immune metabolic pathways.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategy

This study was conducted based on a dual-database design involving the WoSCC and Scopus to ensure comprehensiveness and methodological rigor in the bibliometric analysis. Given the differences in journal coverage, subject classification systems, citation structures, and metadata standards across databases, reliance on a single source may introduce bias, particularly in rapidly evolving interdisciplinary fields [26]. Therefore, a dual-database strategy was employed to enhance the representativeness, completeness, and generalizability of the results through cross-database comparison. Specifically, WoSCC is recognized for its rigorous citation tracking system and long-term coverage of high-impact journals in the biomedical sciences, making it suitable for tracing knowledge evolution and foundational research trends. In contrast, Scopus provides broader journal coverage, faster indexing updates, and improved representation of emerging disciplines, non-English literature, and regional journals [27]. The complementary strengths of these two databases enable an expanded analytical scope and depth, thereby improving the systematicity and global representativeness of the findings. Cross-database comparison also helps identify records that may be missed by a single database and allows assessment of database-specific differences in keyword indexing, temporal distribution, author affiliations, and source journals. This approach has been widely adopted in bibliometric studies across diverse disciplines and is particularly suitable for investigating emerging interdisciplinary topics such as immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer. Importantly, the bibliometric dataset used in this study was constructed exclusively from records that were officially indexed and directly exportable from WoSCC and/or Scopus. To maximize recall while maintaining thematic relevance, a compound Boolean search strategy was developed around the core concept of “immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer.” To ensure conceptual consistency across databases while accounting for differences in search syntax, a unified Boolean search framework was designed around the core concept of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer. This framework was implemented using database-specific field tags, with “TS” applied in the WoSCC and “TITLE-ABS-KEY” applied in Scopus. The search strategy combined three components: breast cancer, immune cell types, and metabolic reprogramming-related processes. The exact search strings as executed in WoSCC and Scopus, together with the export date and interface information, are provided in Supplementary Methods S1. The search covered publications published between January 1, 2005 and June 25, 2025, which defined the final inclusion window for this bibliometric analysis. Using this strategy, 650 records were initially retrieved from WoSCC and 937 records from Scopus. Records were first screened according to the predefined time window, yielding 645 WoSCC records and 921 Scopus records. Language screening subsequently excluded non-English publications (WoSCC: 2; Scopus: 6), resulting in 643 WoSCC records and 915 Scopus records. Within each database, duplicate records were identified and removed using a DOI-based matching strategy. Specifically, records with identical Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) were considered duplicates, and only one unique entry was retained. Using this approach, three duplicate records were detected and removed within WoSCC, and five duplicate records were removed within Scopus. For records lacking DOIs, no additional cross-record merging was performed to avoid introducing subjective bias; such records were retained as independent entries and subsequently evaluated during manual screening. Document-type filtering was subsequently applied to remove meeting abstracts, editorial materials, proceedings papers, and other non-eligible publication types, excluding 25 records from WoSCC and 53 records from Scopus. Following these steps, 618 WoSCC publications and 862 Scopus publications were retained for subsequent analyses. All subsequent bibliometric analyses were conducted separately within the WoSCC and Scopus datasets based on these retained records, and cross-database consistency was assessed by comparing the agreement of major findings across the two sources. Accordingly, the final corpus included 618 WoSCC publications and 862 Scopus publications (1,480 records in total), reflecting the combined total after database-specific cleaning rather than a single merged dataset subjected to cross-database deduplication. Data cleaning and inclusion criteria

Bibliographic records exported from WoSCC and Scopus contained complete metadata, including title, author(s), source journal, abstract, institutional affiliation, publication year, document type, citation count, and reference list. Data were exported in .txt, .ris, and .csv formats to ensure compatibility with multiple bibliometric analysis platforms. Strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied independently within each database to ensure thematic relevance and methodological consistency. Records were included if they met all of the following criteria: (1) the study explicitly focused on breast cancer, as indicated in the title, abstract, or keywords; (2) immune cells were a primary subject of investigation, including but not limited to T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, NK cells, and regulatory T cells; and (3) the study addressed metabolic reprogramming or immunometabolic processes, such as glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), lipid metabolism, fatty acid oxidation, glutamine metabolism, or mitochondrial metabolism. Studies were excluded if they: (1) examined breast cancer metabolism without involvement of immune cells; (2) focused on immune cell metabolism without relevance to breast cancer; (3) were pan-cancer analyses lacking breast cancer-specific results; (4) were unrelated to metabolism or immunology; or (5) belonged to non-eligible publication types, including meeting abstracts, editorial materials, proceedings papers, or other non-peer-reviewed documents. As summarized in Fig. 1, the screening process proceeded in a stepwise manner. Following time-window screening (WoSCC: 650 to 645; Scopus: 937 to 921) and language filtering (WoSCC: −2; Scopus: −6), document-type filtering excluded 25 WoSCC records (meeting abstracts: 6; editorial materials: 4; proceedings papers: 3; others: 12) and 53 Scopus records (meeting abstracts: 23; editorial materials: 12; proceedings papers: 12; others: 6), resulting in 618 WoSCC records and 862 Scopus records retained for analysis. To ensure methodological rigor and reproducibility, all retained records underwent a two-step manual screening process. First, titles and abstracts were screened to exclude clearly irrelevant publications. Second, two investigators independently reviewed all remaining records in parallel. Minor discrepancies—primarily related to borderline thematic relevance—were resolved through discussion, and unresolved cases were adjudicated by a third senior reviewer. Given the highly standardized inclusion criteria and the limited number of conflicts, formal inter-rater agreement statistics were not calculated; however, all disagreements were fully resolved through consensus. Collectively, these procedures ensured that the final analytical corpus accurately reflected the research landscape of immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer and provided a robust and reproducible foundation for all downstream bibliometric analyses.

Bibliometric and statistical analyses

Multiple mainstream bibliometric and visualization tools were used to construct a multidimensional knowledge framework: CiteSpace (Version 6.4.R1) was employed for keyword burst detection, reference co-citation clustering, and the construction of collaboration networks among authors, institutions, and countries. Key parameters included time slicing from 2005 to 2025 (1-year interval), selection of the top 50 most cited items per slice, and pruning using the Pathfinder algorithm. VOSviewer (Version 1.6.20) was used to visualize high-frequency keyword co-occurrence networks, co-authorship maps, international collaboration networks, and journal co-citation clusters. A minimum occurrence threshold of ≥ 10 was applied for keywords and authors to construct the networks. This threshold is widely adopted in bibliometric analyses involving medium-to-large datasets and is considered effective for balancing the identification of core thematic structures with the suppression of noise introduced by low-frequency terms. In the context of the present dataset comprising 1,480 records, a ≥ 10 threshold enabled retention of the most influential and recurrent terms while maintaining network interpretability and visual clarity. The robustness of this threshold selection was further evaluated through sensitivity analyses using alternative keyword occurrence cutoffs, as detailed below. Across all tested settings, the number of keyword clusters remained constant (n = 10), and the core thematic composition was preserved, with variations limited to network density and visual complexity. Accordingly, ≥ 10 was selected as the primary threshold for visualization, and the corresponding sensitivity analyses are provided in the Supplementary Materials (Supplementary Figure S1). Bibliometrix R-package (Version 4.2.1) was used to analyze annual publication trends, thematic evolution, author timelines, and institutional productivity. Outputs included Sankey diagrams and trending topic maps. Scimago Graphica (Version 1.0.51) was used to refine and finalize all figures and graphical outputs for publication-quality visualization. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.1). The majority of analyses in this study were descriptive in nature, including annual publication counts, citation distributions, H-index values, and geographic or institutional breakdowns, which were used to characterize the bibliometric landscape of immune cell metabolic reprogramming research in breast cancer. For analyses involving temporal trends, linear regression models were applied to assess changes in annual publication output over time. In addition, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to examine the association between publication volume and citation frequency where applicable. These inferential analyses were limited to specific trend or association assessments and were not applied to network visualizations or clustering results. To ensure robustness, sensitivity analyses were performed using alternative keyword occurrence thresholds (≥ 5, ≥ 8, ≥12, and ≥ 15). Across all threshold settings, the number of keyword clusters remained constant (n = 10), and the core thematic structure of the clusters was preserved. Variations were limited to network density and visual complexity resulting from the inclusion or exclusion of low-frequency keywords, while the overall cluster composition and major research hotspots remained stable. The corresponding sensitivity analysis visualizations are provided in the Supplementary Materials (Supplementary Figure S1).

Results

Publication trends and disciplinary distribution across WoS and scopus

We analyzed publication trends on immunometabolic reprogramming in breast cancer using data from WoS and Scopus (2005–2025). As shown in Fig. 2A, both databases demonstrated a steady increase in annual publications, with polynomial curve fitting showing high consistency (WoS: R² = 0.99; Scopus: R² = 0.94). The polynomial fitting was applied solely for descriptive visualization of long-term publication trajectories and was not intended for predictive modeling or inference of future publication growth. Citation analysis (Fig. 2B) revealed higher average citations per year in earlier publications (2007–2012), while recent articles showed lower values due to limited time for citation accumulation. Figure 2C shows the subject category distribution. Immunology was prominently represented in both databases, along with oncology, biochemistry, and pharmacology. Scopus included a broader range of interdisciplinary fields, whereas WoS was more concentrated in core biomedical categories. These differences were considered in downstream analyses to ensure reliability and comparability across datasets.

Country contributions and international collaboration analysis

Figure 3A illustrates the global collaboration network, where China and the USA occupy central positions, reflecting their dominant roles and frequent bilateral cooperation in immunometabolic reprogramming research in breast cancer. This strong partnership highlights shared priorities in immunotherapy, tumor metabolism, and translational applications. Figure 3B further shows that several European countries—including Germany, Italy, and the UK—exhibit frequent multilateral collaboration, indicating their active involvement in cross-border partnerships. Figure 3C compares international linkages at the institutional level, again confirming the prominence of China and the USA, while also showing Europe’s strong interconnectedness in collaborative clusters. Figure 3D ranks country-level productivity, with China and the USA consistently emerging as the leading contributors in both WoS and Scopus, followed by Germany, Italy, and the UK. These findings underscore the global distribution of research capacity across both major and emerging scientific regions. Cross-database comparison revealed consistent patterns in country rankings and collaboration intensity. However, Scopus captured a higher proportion of multi-country publications (MCP), suggesting greater sensitivity in detecting global collaborations. For instance, China’s MCP proportion was 7.9% in WoS compared with 12.1% in Scopus, while both the UK and Iran showed 50% MCP in Scopus (Table 1). Despite numerical differences, the overall convergence of major findings across the two databases supports the robustness of these results. Taken together, the interdisciplinary nature of immunometabolism—spanning molecular immunology, metabolic signaling, and cancer biology—makes international collaboration indispensable. Such cooperation enhances resource sharing, methodological complementarity, and mechanistic insight, thereby playing a critical role in advancing translational applications and addressing the clinical heterogeneity of breast cancer.

Core literature and journal analysis

Highly cited global references (Fig. 5A; Table 2) were predominantly published in top-tier journals such as Cancer Cell, Nature Reviews Cancer, and Cell Metabolism, addressing key topics including tumor immunity, metabolic regulation, and the immune microenvironment. For example, the most cited paper by Kroemer et al. (2008) received 1832 citations, while others like Angeli et al. (2019) and Dong et al. (2013) contributed substantially to foundational knowledge on ferroptosis and cancer metabolism. Locally cited documents (Fig. 5B) were mainly published in open-access journals such as Frontiers in Immunology, Cancers, and Frontiers in Oncology, reflecting their accessibility and alignment with emerging research needs. H-index results (Fig. 5C; Table 3) confirmed Frontiers in Immunology as the highest-impact local source in both WoS and Scopus, with Cancers and Frontiers in Oncology also ranking highly in citation-based metrics such as G-index and M-index. Bradford’s Law analysis (Fig. 5D) revealed a consistent set of core journals across both databases, primarily covering immunology, oncology, and metabolism. These sources collectively represent the primary publication platforms in the breast cancer immunometabolism field, facilitating knowledge dissemination across disciplinary boundaries (Table 4).

Keyword clustering and research hotspot analysis

The keyword co-occurrence networks (Fig. 6A) from WoS and Scopus show highly consistent structures centered around terms such as “breast cancer,” “tumor microenvironment,” and “metabolic reprogramming,” indicating stable and shared research priorities in the field. The strong interconnectivity of these terms reflects the close integration of immune and metabolic processes in cancer progression and treatment. Trend analysis (Fig. 6B) reveals that keywords like “immunotherapy,” “ferroptosis,” and “metabolic regulation” have gained prominence in recent years, marking a shift from basic mechanistic studies to translational and therapeutic applications. Cluster analysis identified ten major thematic modules (Fig. 6C), including “targeted therapy,” “lipid metabolism,” “macrophage polarization,” “T cells,” and “metabolic reprogramming” (Fig. 6D). Many clusters focus on the metabolic control of immune cell function, underscoring the interdisciplinary nature of immunometabolism in breast cancer and its potential for therapeutic innovation.

Discussion

This study presents a dual-database bibliometric analysis of breast cancer immunometabolism, integrating records from the WoSCC and Scopus to enhance coverage and cross-database consistency. This approach mitigates limitations of single-database analyses, including incomplete indexing and metadata bias, and enables robust mapping of the field’s intellectual structure and developmental trajectory. Using complementary tools, we characterized publication trends, collaboration networks, keyword co-occurrence, and citation dynamics. The strong concordance of results across databases indicates a coherent and rapidly evolving research landscape. Notably, the clustering and temporal evolution of high-frequency keywords—such as “T cell exhaustion,” “macrophage polarization,” “lactate metabolism,” and “ferroptosis”—highlight a clear shift toward metabolically regulated immune dysfunction within the tumor microenvironment. Overall, this bibliometric mapping clarifies thematic evolution and identifies key research nodes that inform future investigation.

Building on our bibliometric mapping, we interpreted how immune cell metabolic reprogramming has been prioritized in recent breast cancer research and why specific mechanistic directions have gained sustained attention. Consistent with the evolving tumor microenvironment paradigm, metabolic constraints within the tumor niche shape immune dysfunction, therapeutic responsiveness, and disease progression, thereby providing a plausible biological substrate for the observed bibliometric patterns [28]. Notably, keyword frequency and temporal trend analyses consistently highlighted aerobic glycolysis and lactate metabolism as prominent hotspots, aligning with the growing emphasis on optimizing CD8 + T-cell phenotypes in solid tumors and with experimental evidence that lactate can reshape CD8 + T-cell states and antitumor immunity [29]. In parallel, lipid metabolism and macrophage polarization emerged as stable, high-centrality clusters, reflecting the established role of tumor-associated macrophages in tumor progression and the increasing focus on lactate-driven metabolic reprogramming of tumor-associated macrophages as a tractable therapeutic axis [30, 31]. Importantly, ferroptosis showed one of the most rapidly intensifying signals across co-occurrence and burst analyses, mirroring a broader surge of interest in ferroptosis biology and its immunological implications, including emerging concepts of ferroptosis regulation in both cancer and immune cells [32–34]. In addition to these core mechanistic axes, the field is increasingly integrating quantitative stratification frameworks and multi-omics approaches, as exemplified by studies using multi-omics signatures or molecular subtyping strategies to link immune landscapes with prognosis or therapy response across cancers [35, 36]. This trend is also supported by rapid methodological advances in spatial transcriptomics and the combined single-cell-spatial profiling of breast cancer dissemination, which provide the technical basis for resolving spatially structured immune-metabolic interactions that underlie the thematic clusters identified here [37, 38]. In parallel, recent integrative transcriptomic and single-cell studies in breast cancer have begun to translate immune regulatory signals into clinically informative biomarkers, exemplified by reports identifying immune modulators with prognostic or immunotherapy-predictive value, thereby reinforcing the biological plausibility of the immunometabolic themes highlighted by our bibliometric analysis [39, 40]. Finally, translational convergence is evident in the growing interest in metabolic vulnerabilities of CD8 + T cells, pharmacologic opportunities such as monocarboxylate transporter inhibition, and the need to consider multimodality regimens, including high-LET radiotherapy-immunotherapy combinations and their breast cancer-specific cardiotoxicity implications [41, 42]. While some evidence streams (including nutritional genetic studies and adjacent immunotherapy-complication bibliometrics) primarily serve as contextual examples of data-driven causal inference and mapping strategies rather than direct breast cancer immunometabolism mechanisms, they reinforce the broader methodological shift toward integrative, reproducible evidence synthesis [43]. Collectively, these convergent lines of evidence support our bibliometric conclusion that glycolysis/lactate stress, lipid-macrophage metabolic programs, and ferroptosis-related immune modulation represent the most influential and rapidly evolving thematic axes, with increasing emphasis on multi-omics and spatially resolved approaches to translate these insights into precision immunotherapy in breast cancer.

Compared with previous bibliometric studies in cancer immunometabolism, the present study provides a clearer temporal perspective on the evolution of research attention within this interdisciplinary field. Figure 8 presents a chronological timeline of representative highly cited publications identified through year-stratified co-citation analysis, illustrating changes in scholarly focus and citation convergence across different periods. Early highly cited studies, such as Daruwalla J (2006) and Kroemer G (2008), primarily reflect broad academic interest in metabolic regulation in oncology and its immunological context, providing a general research background for subsequent investigations [44, 45]. As the field developed, publications including Tennant DA (2010), Dong CF (2013), Lochner M (2015), and Ohta A (2016) appeared frequently in co-citation networks, corresponding to an increasing concentration of research on immune cell metabolism, tumor microenvironmental regulation, and metabolic checkpoints [46–49]. In more recent years, highly cited works exemplified by Angeli JPF (2019), Wang ZH (2021), Martin-Perez M (2022), and Zhao Y (2024) have clustered around themes related to ferroptosis-associated pathways, metabolic adaptation, and precision or single-cell-oriented immunometabolic research [50–53]. By situating highly cited publications within a unified temporal framework, Fig. 8 complements the quantitative bibliometric analyses and provides an intuitive overview of how research priorities in breast cancer immunometabolism have shifted across different stages of the field’s development.

Importantly, it should be explicitly noted that bibliometric analyses primarily capture patterns of research attention, thematic association, and scholarly activity, rather than direct biological causality, therapeutic efficacy, or clinical readiness. Accordingly, the hotspots, clusters, and citation bursts identified in this study should be interpreted as indicators of emerging or sustained investigative interest, rather than as evidence of validated mechanistic importance or proven clinical benefit. While these bibliometric signals provide a valuable framework for prioritizing research directions, they are intended to inform hypothesis generation and strategic planning, not to substitute for experimental validation or clinical trials. This clarification is essential to ensure that bibliometric insights are contextualized appropriately within the broader landscape of translational and clinical research. Within this clearly defined interpretative framework, the bibliometrically identified bursts and high-centrality themes highlighted in our analysis may serve as data-driven hypotheses for future translational investigation in breast cancer immunometabolism.

Based on the consistency of signals observed across dual databases, temporal analyses, and co-occurrence networks, several research priorities emerge as particularly actionable: First, the strong and recurrent co-occurrence of “lactate metabolism,” “aerobic glycolysis,” and immune dysfunction suggests a high-priority hypothesis that targeting lactate transport or metabolism (e.g., monocarboxylate transporters) may enhance immune checkpoint blockade efficacy, especially in metabolically hostile tumor microenvironments. Second, the stable prominence of lipid metabolism and macrophage polarization across clustering and density analyses highlights tumor-associated macrophage metabolic reprogramming as a promising axis for therapeutic intervention, warranting focused experimental and translational exploration. Third, the rapidly intensifying citation bursts related to ferroptosis indicate growing investigative interest in ferroptosis-immune crosstalk, supporting its prioritization as a candidate mechanism to be evaluated in combination immunotherapy strategies. Collectively, these priorities do not represent validated therapeutic conclusions, but rather bibliometrically supported hypotheses that may guide the rational design of future mechanistic studies and translational trials.

Looking ahead, a central challenge in breast cancer immunometabolism is translating mechanistic insights into strategies that effectively modulate immune function within the tumor microenvironment. Our bibliometric analysis consistently associates key metabolic signaling pathways with immune regulatory checkpoints, underscoring their roles in shaping immune cell fate and effector activity. Although the present analysis focuses on breast cancer, emerging evidence from other solid tumors suggests that immunometabolic reprogramming represents a broadly conserved mechanism underlying immune resistance, providing a conceptual framework that may inform cross-contextual interpretation of immunometabolic vulnerabilities [54]. Importantly, breast cancer subtype heterogeneity—including ER-positive, HER2-enriched, and triple-negative disease—emerged as a critical contextual factor, indicating subtype-specific patterns of immunometabolic reprogramming. To address this complexity, recent research increasingly adopts systems-level approaches integrating single-cell transcriptomics, spatial metabolomics, and metabolic profiling to construct spatially resolved immune-metabolic landscapes. Anchored in bibliometric evidence, these directions highlight promising avenues for aligning metabolic intervention with immune reactivation and for advancing precision immunotherapy in breast cancer.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although a dual-database strategy integrating the WoSCC and Scopus was adopted to enhance coverage and enable cross-database comparison, bibliometric analyses remain inherently dependent on database indexing policies, journal selection criteria, and metadata quality. Consequently, relevant studies indexed outside these databases, subject to delayed indexing, or published in non-core journals may not have been captured, despite the complementary strengths of the dual-database design. Second, the analysis relied primarily on author-provided and database-indexed keywords for co-occurrence, clustering, and burst detection. While this approach is widely used and effective for identifying dominant themes and evolving research attention, it may not fully capture semantic nuances, emerging terminology, or mechanistic concepts that are not explicitly reflected in keywords. Accordingly, the resulting thematic structures represent patterns of scholarly focus rather than exhaustive representations of biological complexity. Third, the immune cell scope was intentionally restricted to cell types most consistently represented in the existing immunometabolism literature on breast cancer, including T cells, CD8⁺ T cells, regulatory T cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, and natural killer cells. Although other immune populations such as B cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells, and neutrophils are increasingly recognized as important components of the tumor microenvironment, they were not systematically included due to their relatively fragmented representation in keyword metadata and the risk of introducing noise into network-based analyses. This deliberate scope restriction was adopted to ensure network interpretability and analytical robustness and does not imply a lack of biological relevance of these cell types. Fourth, bibliometric indicators—including publication volume, citation frequency, and network connectivity—reflect scholarly attention and association rather than direct scientific importance, mechanistic validity, or clinical efficacy. Thus, the identified hotspots and trends are intended for landscape mapping and hypothesis generation, not for establishing causal biological relationships. Finally, consistent with standard bibliometric practice, this study was limited to English-language, peer-reviewed journal articles and excluded grey literature. In addition, studies published after the final retrieval date (June 25, 2025) were not included. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides a comprehensive and methodologically robust bibliometric overview of global research on immune cell metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer based on parallel analyses of the WoSCC and Scopus databases. By integrating multi-tool visualization and cross-database consistency assessment, we delineate the temporal evolution, geographic and institutional contributions, and thematic architecture of this rapidly expanding field. The results consistently highlight key immunometabolic interactions involving T cell dysfunction, macrophage polarization, glycolytic and lipid metabolic pathways, and emerging regulated cell death programs as prominent research themes. Importantly, these bibliometric signals reflect patterns of scholarly attention rather than validated biological causality. Collectively, our findings offer a data-driven framework to contextualize current knowledge and inform future research directions in breast cancer immunometabolism, emphasizing interdisciplinary studies and potential therapeutic avenues.

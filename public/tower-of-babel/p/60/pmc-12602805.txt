Extracellular vesicle-enclosed microRNAs serve as non-invasive biomarkers in the major upper and lower gastrointestinal tract tumors: a bibliometric analysis (2010–2025)
Authors: Guanlan Zhang, Jincheng Li, Jingwen Ling, Guoqiang Chen, Beibei Huang, Kunhua Xiong, Rongquan He, Diyuan Qin, Gang Chen, Siyan Chen, Mingyu Lai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGastrointestinal tract (GIT) tumors account for a large global cancer burden, partly due to the lack of efficient non-invasive early detection biomarkers. For microRNAs (miRNAs) can stably circulate in body fluids, research on body fluid miRNAs in GIT tumors is significant for non-invasive and precise medicine. This study aims to summarize the previous research and propose the future directions of body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors.MethodsPublications were retrieved and gathered from the Web of Science Core Collection database. Bibliometric analyses including year, area, journals, authors, institutions, keyword and reference analyses were conducted through CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and bibliometrix package.ResultsTotally 695 articles and 80 reviews were screened and included. The quantity of publications in this area was increasing, especially in China, Japan and the United States. The most productive journal was PLoS One, and the most prolific author was Ajay Goel from Baylor University Medical Center. The keyword analysis showed that although research on aberrant miRNAs expression status had been continuously published in previous years, the keywords “liquid biopsy”, “extracellular vesicles”, “machine learning”, and “resistance” frequently occurred in recent years. For citation burst references, articles associated with exosomal miRNAs had been highlighted lately.ConclusionIt was concluded that the prospective topic trends represented further exploration of the body fluid miRNAs encapsulated in extracellular vesicles and liquid biopsy technology, which will likely advance early screening, risk stratification, and personalized treatment.

Introduction

Originated from the gastrointestinal tract (GIT), GIT tumors mainly include esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, and colorectal cancer. According to worldwide cancer statistics in 2022 [1], colorectal cancer ranked the third in the incidence of 36 common cancers, with a 9.6% proportion of new cases and a 9.3% proportion of new deaths. Stomach cancer was ranked fifth in the incidence of 36 common cancers, while the esophageal cancer was ranked 11th. Despite the improvement of diagnostic techniques and therapeutic strategies, GIT tumors still accounted for a large proportion of global cancer burden [2], with high incidence and mortality. One of the factors contributing to the GIT tumors burden is lacking the early screening methods in population. Most GIT tumors are asymptomatic in early stages and are therefore often diagnosed late [3]. Currently, the screening for GIT tumors depends on endoscopic and pathological techniques, which are invasive and expensive, leading to the difficulty to be widely applied in population [4]. Thus, tumor diagnosis or screening using body fluids is prospective in the future.

Currently, several body fluid biomarkers have been applied in the diagnosis of GIT tumors such as carcinoembryonic antigen, carbohydrate antigen (CA)19-9, CA12-5, and CA72-4, but the sensitivity and the specificity are limited [3, 5]. MicroRNA (miRNA) is a type of conservative and single-strand RNAs, which can stably circulate in body fluids and have potential to become novel biomarkers [6, 7]. Numerous miRNAs have been studied to involve in the proliferation, invasion and migration of GIT tumors, which also can become diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers [5, 8]. Research on body fluid miRNAs is prospective for the non-invasive medicine of GIT tumors. With technological innovation driving, the research hotspot is various between different periods. In the era of precision medicine, research associated with miRNAs biomarkers detection in blood, serum, plasma, salvia, feces are emerging for GIT tumors [9, 10]. Hence, a systematic summarization is needed to comprehend the previous research situation and predict the prospective research direction.

Originally proposed by A. Pritchard in 1969, bibliometrics is a research method that investigates the distributed structures, quantitative relations, and changing rules of document information. It explores the current status, characteristics, and laws of science and technology through mathematical and statistical analyses [11]. Different from the traditional systematic reviews, bibliometric method offers quantitative view based on numerous literature data. It can reveal and visualize the dynamic evolution of specific research field, including publication number, contributive author, and research hotspots. Thus, bibliometric analysis has been applied to quickly obtain the knowledge structures of scientific research, which also help excavate novel perspective missed by traditional systematic reviews [12]. In this study, the authors gathered publication information from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database and conducted a bibliometric analysis covering the year, area, journals, authors, institutions, keyword and reference through CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and the bibliometrix package. The aims of our research were to summarize the previous research, uncover the research trends and propose the future directions of body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors, including esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, small intestinal cancer, and colorectal cancer.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

This research followed the biomedical literature guideline [13]. Researches related to body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors field were collected from the WoSCC database (https://www.webofscience.com). The records before March 16, 2025 were searched with the following strategies: Topic = (“miR*” OR “microR*”) AND (“circulating” OR “circulatory” OR “cell-free” OR “secretory” OR “extracellular” OR “fluid-derived” OR “body fluid” OR “plasma” OR “serum” OR “urinary” OR “urine” OR “salivary” OR “gastric fluid” OR “gastric juice” OR “gastric acid” OR “bile” OR “ascitic fluid” OR “ascites” OR “fecal” OR “stool”) AND (“digestive tract” OR “gastrointestinal” OR “gastrointestinal tract” OR “gastroenteric” OR “alimentary tract” OR “alimentary canal” OR “esophag*” OR “gastric” OR “stomach” OR “duoden*” OR “jejunum” OR “ileu*” OR “colon” OR “intestin*” OR “colorectal” OR “rectal” OR “enteral” OR “enteric canal”) AND (“cancer” OR “cancers” OR “carcinoma” OR “carcinomas” OR “tumor” OR “tumors” OR “tumour” OR “tumours” OR “neoplasm” OR “neoplasms” OR “malignancy” OR “malignancies”).

Data screening and exclusion criteria

Publication types of meeting abstract, retracted publication, early access, letter, editorial material, book chapters, proceeding paper, retraction, data paper, news item, publication with expression of concern were eliminated. Additionally, the publications about specific gastrointestinal tumors (hepatocellular carcinoma, pancreatic cancer, gallbladder cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, anal cancer), not homo sapiens and specimen not from body fluids were also excluded.

Data evaluation and validation

The evaluation of literatures was conducted by manual review, based on the data screening workflow. The manual review was carried by two authors (G.Z. and J.L.). A cross-validation of the included literatures in this study was performed via adopting the method from another publication [14]. The research outputs of the top 10 contributive authors related to body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors were searched in PubMed database (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and screened according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria in this study. The publication numbers of the top 10 contributive authors in both the results of this study and the manual review of PubMed database were analyzed by Pearson correlation test. A significant strong correlation was noted between the two groups (Pearson correlation coefficient = 0.705, p = 0.023), illustrating that the data quality included in this study was relatively high.

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometric tools

Common software used in bibliometric analysis includes CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and the bibliometrix package. CiteSpace, based on knowledge domain visualization theory and created by Chao-Mei Chen, provides a platform for researchers to excavate the current status and future directions in a certain field [15]. Bibliometric analysis publications using CiteSpace were discovered in environmentology [16], geology [17], medicine [18], and others, among which bibliometric research on medicine is of principal importance and on the rise. The VOSviewer, released in 2010, and the bibliometrix package launched in 2017, are two user-friendly software platforms for domain visualization [19–21].

Bibliometric procedures

Plain text files were exported in the “Full Record and Cited References” format for subsequent analysis. Annual publications and regional distribution statistics, author cooperation networks, institutional co-operation networks, burst keywords, and burst citations were analyzed using CiteSpace 6.1.R6 Advanced version. The analyses of journals, trend topics, and thematic maps were performed utilizing the bibliometrix package. Keyword analysis, citation analysis, and co-citation analysis were carried out using VOSviewer1.6.18. Excel, Pajek1.0, and Tableau software were used to visualize the data. Keywords with the same meaning were merged.

Results

Publications overview

A total of 775 publications were included (Fig. 1), consisting of 695 articles (89.7%) and 80 reviews (10.3%). From 2010 to 2020, publications related to body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors steadily increased every year (Fig. 2), with annual publications peaking in 2020 (92 papers). Although the number of papers declined after 2020, the enthusiasm for body fluid miRNAs research continues unabated. Geographically, all the publications sourced in this study were produced from 52 countries (Fig. 3), of which the top three countries in the number of published articles were China (465), Japan (85), United States (80). The journals with the top 10 published articles in the field of body fluid miRNAs research in the major upper and lower GIT tumors are shown in Table 1. PLoS One was the most productive journal (26), followed by Oncotarget (25), Cancer Biomarkers (23), and Oncology Letters (23). The journal British Journal of Cancer had the highest impact factor (6.4 in 2023 and a JIF quartile of Q1), publishing 14 articles in the field of body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors.

Keyword co-occurrence analysis

A keyword co-occurrence analysis was conducted via VOSviewer1.6.18, and all keywords were selected. The results showed that a total of 55 keywords appeared no fewer than 20 times (Fig. 6). The top eight keywords with the most frequent occurrences were biomarker (438), microrna (432), colorectal cancer (282), expression (255), serum (201), diagnosis (189), plasma (169), gastric cancer (183). The results of keyword co-occurrence network analysis showed that most current studies on body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors were concerned with biomarkers. The most studied tumors were colorectal cancer and gastric cancer, and the most studied body fluids were serum and plasma. Four clusters were extracted from Fig. 6B: (1) Cluster 1: miRNAs were significant blood biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis of the upper and lower GIT tumors, especially the miR-21. (Keywords included “biomarker”, “diagnosis”, “identification”, “plasma”, “serum mir-21”, “prognostic biomarker”). (2) Cluster 2: Research of serum miRNAs expression in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma was highlighted, indicating that serum miRNAs were capital for the identification or prognosis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. (Keywords included “serum”, “esophageal squamous cell carcinoma”, “signature”, “survival”). (3) Cluster 3: Body fluid miRNAs had a potential for the application of liquid biopsy and chemotherapy. (Keywords included “microrna”, “exosome”, “extracellular vesicles”, “liquid biopsy”, “chemotherapy”, “chemoresistance”). (4) Cluster 4: miRNAs within body fluid miRNAs played important roles in tumor growth, proliferation, metastasis and apoptosis. (Keywords included “growth”, “proliferation”, “progression”, “migration”, “metastasis”, “apoptosis”). Different colors were used to mark the average publication year (APY) of the keywords (Fig. 6A). The blue node indicates that the APY of the keyword was earlier, while the red node indicates that it was later. For instance, the keywords including “exosome”, “extracellular vesicles”, “liquid biopsy” were concerned with current years.

Hot keyword tendency analysis

Keywords that emerged frequently reflected hotspots in a research field. A trend topic plot based on author keywords was analyzed using the bibliometrix package (Fig. 7A), and the top 20 burst keywords which reflected popular topics at a certain stage were identified through CiteSpace 6.1.R6 Advanced version (Fig. 7B). For the research content of miRNAs, before 2020, the hot topics were associated with plasma, qrt-pcr, circulating microrna, serum, prognosis, diagnosis, biomarker; and the burst keywords included real time pcr (5.22, 2010–2013), expression profile (44.37, 2010–2017), serum (4.12, 2010–2013), marker (8.34, 2012–2015), signature (7.56, 2012–2015), circulating microrna (3.32, 2012–2013), clinical significance (3.84, 2017–2018), indicating that the clinical value of aberrant miRNAs expression were hotspot in this period. Nevertheless, the hot topics in current years were associated with liquid biopsy, chemoresistance, exosomal mirna, extracellular vesicle, machine learning, gastrointestinal cancer; and the burst keywords included extracellular vesicle (6.16, 2021–2025) and liquid biopsy (4.69, 2023–2025). On one hand, it indicated that body fluid miRNAs have important impact on tumor therapy. On the other hand, with the deepening and maturity of miRNA research, the studies of body fluid miRNAs had not only been limited to free miRNAs, but had expanded to the exploration of extracellular vesicles (EVs) or exosomal mirnas as novel liquid biopsy biomarkers. Additionally, researches on the application of artificial intelligence algorithms (such as machine learning) to the development of clinical miRNA biomarkers were beginning to emerge and potentially become one of future directions.

Thematic map

A thematic map based on author keywords was drawn up to predict the development tendency of body fluid miRNAs in GIT tumors. The map was separated into four quadrants: Motor Themes, Niche Themes, Emerging or Declining Themes, and Basic Themes. The topics in Motor Themes were considered important and well developed, while those in Niche Themes were adequately developed but were not significant in the present field of study. The topics in the Emerging or Declining Themes were marginal, meaning that they might emerge and then disappear soon. The topics in Basic Themes were regarded as significant in a certain field but had not been fully investigated. As shown in Fig. 8, the keywords inside the upper right quadrant were “colorectal adenoma”, “diagnostic value”, “survival”, “invasion” and “migration”, suggesting that the impacts of miRNAs were well developed in the field of colorectal cancer research. The keywords inside the upper left quadrant included “drug resistance”, “chemosensitivity”, “lncrna”, “circrna”, “immunotherapy”, indicating that tumor therapy associated with miRNAs had been well researched but were not the mainstream direction for the body fluid miRNAs research field of the major upper and lower GIT tumors. The keywords “radiotherapy” and “chemotherapy” were found in the lower left quadrant, belonging to the edge themes, which may be an upsurge or a decline in the future. The keywords in the Basic Themes area (“colorectal cancer”, “proliferation”, “lymph node metastasis”, “exosomal mirna”, “machine learning”, “small rna sequencing”) were related to studies on the biomarker and biological behavior of body fluid miRNAs, which were not comprehensively researched and had a great potential for development.

Discussion

General summary

This study utilized a bibliometric method to identify the features of annual publications, regions, journals, authors, institutions, keywords, thematic topics, and cited and co-cited references in 775 documents. The analysis encompassed the past developments, current status, and prospective directions of body fluid miRNAs research in the major upper and lower GIT tumors field. In the past decade, research on body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors had been growing, with a peak from 2010 to 2020. China was the leading region in publication volume worldwide, and the most productive institutions including Nanjing Medical University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Capital Medical University are all from China. China is a country with a numerous population, where the new cases of GIT tumors account over 50% of global cases [22] and therefore non-invasive methods of early-stage GIT tumors are urgent for the population screening. Moreover, support from research institutions and national science foundations is abundant [23]. These factors result in the leading publications for China. Interestingly, it was found in this study that the authors from United States had high citations but fewer papers than China. This phenomenon may be caused by the academic evaluation system and cultural differences: the United States places greater emphasis on originality and high quality, while some institutions in China still have an evaluation system that prioritizes quantity. In terms of authors, Ajay Goel (Baylor University Medical Center) stood out in the research field, the main research direction of whom was the significance of body fluid miRNAs in liquid biopsy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and colorectal cancer. In particular, the 2010 publication “A Transcriptomic Liquid Biopsy Assay for Predicting Resistance to Neoadjuvant Therapy in Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma” received 277 citations, it proposed that a combination of serum miRNAs and mRNAs was potential for liquid biopsy to predict the neoadjuvant therapy response in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [24]. The paper has generated a significant impact in the field, and body fluid miRNAs for liquid biopsy is a hotspot.

Future perspectives

The results of the keywords analysis and the dynamic topic changes showed that previous research had mainly been concerned on the clinical significance of miRNAs expression. Aberrant blood miRNAs are promising to become diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers for the major upper and lower GIT tumors [25–29]. Particularly, serum miR-21 not only has a high positive prediction rate of approximately 90%, but also can identify the stage I and stage IV gastric cancer [30]. Additionally, circulating miRNAs also play oncogenic or tumor-suppressor roles by combining with targeted mRNA, and potentially become therapeutic targets [31]. According to the results of the burst keywords and trend topic analyses, the topics of body fluid miRNAs associated with EVs, liquid biopsy, and machine learning are currently most focused. The shift toward EVs indicates a paradigm change from free-circulating miRNAs to the more stable and specific vesicle-protected signatures, which are attributed to the development of biotechnology.

EVs are a category of membranous vesicles secreted by approximately all cells and exist in body fluids, which encapsulate many biological molecules including proteins and nucleic acids from various cells [3]. EVs such as exosomes released from malignant diseases can reflect the disease information via some signaling molecules [32, 33]. Recently, miRNAs enveloped in EVs have been found to play crucial roles in malignancies, including GIT tumors. The expression characteristics of EVs associated miRNAs can reflect the progression and therapy response of disease [34, 35], making them becoming promising biomarkers. For instance, combination of six EVs associated miRNAs in saliva including miR-4505, miR-1268a, miR-6126, miR-4701-3p, miR-1972, and miR-4274 received a high discrimination efficacy for early-stage esophageal cell squamous carcinoma [36]. Overexpression of exosomal miR-519a-3p promotes liver metastasis and indicates a worse outcome for gastric cancer [37]. Suppression of EVs associated miR-21-5p and miR-200a is probable an innovative method for anti-PD-L1 treatment in colorectal cancer [38]. In mechanism, EVs associated miRNAs are the messengers for cell-to-cell communication. The miRNAs inside vesicles can carry messages from malignant cells to distant other cells through autocrine or paracrine pathways, establish the tumor microenvironment and consequently promote tumor progression [39]. EVs are considered efficient and stable carriers for miRNAs in body fluids. With the protection of vesicle membranes, the miRNAs secreted by donor cells can more accurately display the changes of the primary disease, which are served as prospective biomarkers of GIT tumors for liquid biopsy in the era of precision medicine. Additionally, EVs associated miRNAs involve in regulating the malignant behavior of tumor cells through transferring genetic information, which mediates the interaction between tumor cells and other non-cancerous cells. Similar to the functions of miRNA, EVs associated miRNAs can involve in tumor proliferation, angiogenesis, tumor metastasis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and promote the tumor immune escape by carrying the immunosuppressive molecules [40, 41]. However, the mechanisms of either EVs associated miRNAs or miRNAs are still not comprehensively elucidated, with the first proposal of nuclear activating miRNA (NamiRNA) by Wen-Qiang Yu (Fudan University, Shanghai, China) in 2017 [42]. Different from the classical miRNA function (negative regulation mechanism), NamiRNA can bind to enhancers and promote downstream gene expression [43]. For instance, it was evidenced that miR-339 can bind to the G protein-coupled estrogen receptor 1 (GPER1) enhancer and improve the expression level of the tumor-suppressor gene GPER1 in breast cancer [44]. The discovery of NamiRNA has supplemented miRNA biological functions, but no research has currently reported that EVs associated miRNAs may play the role as NamiRNA. Therefore, in-depth study of EVs associated miRNAs benefits the comprehensively understanding more mechanisms of tumor occurrence, metastasis and invasion, and providing more ideas and research basis of clinical anti-tumor therapy. Nevertheless, research on EVs is still confronted with some challenges. Limited to the technologies, the complex composition and functions of EVs are difficult to accurately classify [45], and thus relevant technological advancement is also a development direction in the future.

Liquid biopsy is a new detection technique developed in recent years. It is favorable for obtaining disease information through examining circulating tumor cells, circulating free DNA, circulating tumor DNA, nucleic acids, proteins as well as exosomes in body fluids [46]. Compared to tissue biopsy, liquid biopsy is non-invasive, convenient, and repeated. Particularly, for the specimen is conveniently to be collected, liquid biopsy technique can be utilized to monitor the real-time changes of tumors and assist clinical decision-making, whereas the consequence of traditional tissue biopsy is loco-regional [47]. In view of its advantages, liquid biopsy is a developing and ideal technology in the future [48]. With the demand of detection technologies advancement, sophisticated computation capacities are simultaneously improved. Owing to the superior nonlinear fitting ability for complex network, machine learning is increasingly used to identify biomarker signatures in various diseases including cancer [49]. The implication of machine learning in the liquid biopsy of GIT tumors performed a remarkable diagnostic accuracy [50]. For instance, a machine learning signature consisted of 10 miRNAs exhibited an area under the curve of 96.8% to identify early-stage gastric cancer [51]. The combination of machine learning and liquid biopsy will promote the non-invasive medicine development, and the exploitation of liquid biopsy technology will benefit the early-stage screening, risk stratification and individualized therapy for patients.

This study conducted a comprehensive analysis of body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors. However, certain limitations exist. Only WoSCC database has been retrieved for collecting literatures in this study, neglecting databases like PubMed or Scopus database. Despite the combination of multiple databases helps the study more comprehensive in theory, it also leads to data lost during the process of data conversion and merging, for the fact that the formats for storing literature in each database are different. The data exported from PubMed and Scopus databases have limitation for in-depth analysis in Citespace, VOSviewer and “Bibliometrix” R package. Additionally, other limitations in this study also include temporal bias, citation inflation, the impact of self-citations, potential language bias, and the fact that citation counts can lag behind recent impactful work.

Conclusion

In this study, the authors utilized a bibliometric analysis method to summarize the previous and current research status of body fluid miRNAs in the major upper and lower GIT tumors and to visualize the results. The analysis highlighted that previous research has been mainly concerned with abnormal expression and biological functions of free body fluid miRNAs. In the future, the research hotspots possibly focus on body fluid miRNAs enveloped in EVs and liquid biopsy technology, promoting the non-invasive and precise medicine. Continued integration of multi-omics, EV profiling, and AI-based diagnostics will likely transform early GIT cancer detection in the coming decade.

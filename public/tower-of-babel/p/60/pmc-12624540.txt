Predict SARS-CoV-2 genome interactions based on RNA language models
Authors: Weiwei Shen, Yixue Li, Liucun Zhu, Tao Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
HighlightsScientific questions: Accurately predicting interactions between severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) ribonucleic acid (RNA) fragments remains a significant challenge, and there is currently insufficient understanding of how to effectively integrate viral RNA in situ conformational sequencing (vRIC-Seq) data with computational models during viral infection and replication.Evidence before this study: Previous studies employed various machine learning methods to analyze RNA interactions, but lacked integration of vRIC-Seq data with advanced deep learning models like deepRAM transfer learning and RNAErnie.New findings: During analysis, our integrative approach combining vRIC-Seq data with traditional classifiers, deepRAM, and RNAErnie models demonstrated high accuracy in predicting viral RNA fragment interactions. Different machine learning methods consistently contributed to robust performance, highlighting the complementary strengths of these models.Significance of the study: This study provides a novel computational framework to better understand viral RNA behavior, which could guide future virology research and therapeutic development.

Introduction

The appearance of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) has precipitated an unprecedented global health crisis, resulting in millions of infections and significant mortality rates, which have strained healthcare systems worldwide. This urgent situation has spurred extensive research into the molecular biology and pathogenesis of these viruses. Recent advancements in genomics and computational biology have positioned machine learning techniques as pivotal tools for investigating viral variation and its biological implications. Notably, machine learning has made significant contributions to various aspects of SARS-CoV-2, including prevention, diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment [1].

Intragenomic ribonucleic acid (RNA)-RNA interactions (RRIs) within the viral genome serve as a critical structural basis for SARS-CoV-2 replication and pathogenesis, centrally characterized by the precise regulation of the viral life cycle through dynamic conformational reprogramming [2]. Such dynamic interaction networks further mediate the temporal regulation of the viral life cycle: positive-strand RNA viruses utilize conserved stem-loop structures and pseudoknots within the genome as molecular switches to toggle between replication and transcriptional modes via conformational reprogramming. This mechanism, demonstrated in dengue virus, involves the regulation of NS5 polymerase activity through intramolecular RRIs [3]. At the molecular mechanistic level, viral RNA adopts a specific topological framework through long-range intramolecular interactions, such as pseudoknot structures formed between the 5′ untranslated region (5′ UTR) and 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR). This architecture directly regulates the ribosomal frameshifting process: the pseudoknot structure located at the 3′ terminus of the genome engages in precise interactions with the small ribosomal subunit, generating tension on the messenger (mRNA) during translocation, thereby efficiently driving programmed ribosomal frameshifting. This process is critically sensitive for viral polyprotein synthesis, where even a single nucleotide mutation can completely abrogate viral replication [4]. Within the classical framework of genetic information transfer, transfer RNA (tRNA) and mRNA achieve precise codon decoding through complementary base-pairing interactions, while ribosomal RNA (rRNA) catalyzes peptide bond formation via its intrinsic catalytic activity. These two mechanisms operate in concert to accomplish the ultimate biological transformation of genetic information into functional proteins [5]. Recent studies suggest such RNA interactions may also hijack the host translational machinery to suppress innate immune responses while optimizing viral protein production [6], highlighting their potential as targets for antiviral drug development.

Furthermore, interactions between genomic and subgenomic RNAs regulate the expression of essential viral functional proteins. Transcriptional regulatory sequences mediate template switching of the replication-transcription complex via complementary base pairing, thus balancing viral replication and subgenomic RNA production. This equilibrium directly determines structural protein expression levels [7]. As these RNA-driven interactions not only govern core viral processes but may also influence host cell responses, understanding them is crucial for developing effective antiviral therapies [8].

To characterize the three-dimensional RNA structure of native SARS-CoV-2 virus particles, researchers have developed a new method called viral RNA in situ conformational sequencing (vRIC-seq). This method combines high-throughput sequencing with structure probing to produce nucleotide-resolution maps of RNA conformations in intact viruses. By preserving the native viral structure, vRIC-seq allows detailed analysis of RRIs and structural elements affecting viral replication, overcoming the limitations of previous studies of RNA dynamics in real viral environments. The core methodology of vRIC-seq involves using concanavalin A (ConA) magnetic beads that specifically bind to viral surface glycoproteins to capture the virions. Following this, cross-linking, nuclease treatment, and adapter ligation steps effectively elucidate the spatial interactions between viral RNA molecules. The application of vRIC-seq reveals the characteristic structure of the SARS-CoV-2 RNA genome, and in this study, we utilized the sequencing results obtained from vRIC-seq technology [9].

Currently, various methods exist to study RRIs, broadly categorized into experimental techniques and bioinformatic computational approaches. Experimental methods include RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) [10], crosslinking immunoprecipitation (CLIP) [11], fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) [12], RNA-Seq [13], etc., bioinformatic computational approaches such as sequence alignment, comparing the conservation of different RNA sequences to predict potential RRIs [14], machine learning algorithms to analyze RNA sequences, structures and their interactions patterns to identify specific interactions features as well as analyzing the RRIs networks using network biology approaches [15].

Recent advances in machine learning, fueled by the exponential growth of RNA sequencing data and computational resources, are revolutionizing the study of RRIs [16]. Unlike traditional statistical methods, modern machine learning approaches are able to extract hierarchical features from complex biological datasets, achieving unprecedented accuracy in interaction prediction [17]. This evolution will not only enhance our understanding of biological systems, but may also change the course of disease diagnosis and treatment [18]. The strength of this approach resides in its efficiency and accuracy, empowering researchers to rapidly identify potential therapeutic targets, thereby informing subsequent experimental validations [19].

In this study, three machine learning framework systems are used to resolve the RRIs of SARS-CoV-2: (1) One-hot model based on base coding [20]; (2) the Word2Vec approach for vector characterization [21]; (3) neural network models for migration learning using the deepRAM model [22]; and (4) the RNAErnie model of the Transformer architecture for fine-tuning of downstream tasks [23]. By comparatively analyzing these technological pathways, the aim is to discover the key interaction determinants that regulate viral pathogenicity. The models developed will not only deepen the understanding of viral replication mechanisms, but also provide a computational framework for predicting the functional impact of new mutant strains and guide the development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Data preparation

The complete genome sequence of SARS-CoV-2 (Wuhan-Hu-1 isolate, GenBank: NC_045512.2) was obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) GenBank database. For reproducibility, the raw FASTA file and associated processing scripts are also available in the GitHub repository RIC2Structure (https://github.com/caochch/RIC2Structure).

Raw RNA structure profiling data (vRIC-Seq) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository under accession number GSE155733 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) [9].

Data filtering and the generation of positive and negative samples

Hi-C data format mainly consists of a multidimensional matrix, which usually includes data points and their corresponding interaction strengths. In data extraction, the values in the matrix represent the interaction strengths between specific genomic locations, and these interaction strength values can reflect the spatial relationship of chromatin structure and provide data support for further research. In Hi-C data analysis, Juicebox is a widely used software tool [24]. In the data processing stage, we first used Juicebox software to visualize the vRIC-seq data in Hi-C format for quality control to ensure the integrity and reliability of the matrix structure at single-base resolution. Extract two reads on the same RNA fragment and the interaction strengths between them, and retain data with interaction strengths greater than 9 to ensure that there is an interaction between the loci. We have comprehensively considered the distribution of existing literature and actual data. Quantitative threshold is important for distinguishing true positives [25]. This threshold can effectively screen interactions with significant biological significance, avoiding the inclusion of noise and weak associations.

Based on the obtained site information and interaction strengths, the RNA sequences were sequentially processed in detail. During sequence processing, we employed targeted algorithms to ensure that the extracted sequence fragments correctly reflected their contextual information. Specifically, for each target site, we conditioned the sequence according to its position relative to the length of the sequence to construct two RNA sequence fragments representing the sequence around the site of interest. The process includes consideration of boundary conditions to avoid extracting invalid data beyond the length of the sequence to obtain the desired positive samples. Negative samples were generated by randomly selecting pairs of sites that excluded identical sites and did not overlap with positive samples.

Construction of predictive models and feature extraction process for three models

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive evaluation of traditional classification algorithms, neural network architectures, and multi-head attention mechanism approaches. For the representation of nucleotide bases in the traditional classification and neural network methods, we primarily employed One-hot encoding and word2vec embedding techniques.

One-hot encoding is a widely adopted discrete feature representation commonly utilized in machine learning and natural language processing. The fundamental principle of this model is to map each category to a unique binary vector, facilitating efficient processing by computers [26]. In this representation, features with N distinct categories are represented as N-dimensional vectors, wherein exactly one dimension is assigned a value of 1 (indicating the specific category), while all other dimensions are set to 0.

The core principle of One-hot encoding is to map each category (in this case, nucleotides) to a unique binary vector. For RNA sequences, the nucleotides adenine (A), uracil (U), guanine (G), and cytosine (C) are represented as four-dimensional binary vectors:(1)A→[1,0,0,0]U→[0,1,0,0]G→[0,0,1,0]C→[0,0,0,1]

This representation ensures that each nucleotide is uniquely identified by a binary vector, where only one dimension is set to 1 (indicating a specific nucleotide) and all other dimensions are set to 0. This binary encoding allows computers to process and manipulate data efficiently.

While the One-hot encoding model provides a simple and interpretable representation, it lacks the ability to capture contextual relationships between nucleotides. To address this limitation, we employ the Word2Vec model. The Word2Vec model uses Word2Vecto extract sequence features (subsequences of length k) for each k-mer.Word2Vec is a tool for RNA sequence representation learning [27], inspired by word embedding techniques in natural language processing such as Word2Vec [28], which is mainly used to convert words (or phrases) into fixed-length Word2Vec is mainly used to convert words (or phrases) into fixed-length vector representations that capture relationships and similarities between words. The word vectors used to compute word vectors in Word2Vec are categorized into the Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) model [29] and the Skip-gram model [30].

We use the Skip-gram model, the core idea of which is to use a word to predict its contextual relationships within a given context window. The Word2Vec model treats the nucleotides in an RNA sequence as ‘words’ and converts them into vector representations. It transforms RNA sequences into fixed-length vector representations, enabling us to utilize these representations effectively in downstream tasks. In particular, sequence features are extracted from each k-mer (a subsequence with length k) using the Word2Vec approach [31]. A k-mer is a fragment of nucleotide sequence of length k that is widely used in the analysis and processing of RNA sequences. k-mer division methods decompose an RNA sequence into all possible substrings of length k. A systematic evaluation of the deepRAM model, an end-to-end deep learning framework, demonstrates a significant enhancement in the accuracy of deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA)/RNA sequence binding site prediction through the integration of a hybrid architecture comprising convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Experimental results indicate that such hybrid architectures achieve a median area under the curve (AUC) of 0.951 when trained on sufficiently large datasets (training samples exceeding 10,000) [22]. For the analysis of local site interactions within the novel coronavirus genome, we adapted and applied the deepRAM framework accordingly. As shown in Fig. 3, building upon the original deepRAM model, we developed an interactive dual-channel modeling framework that overcomes the single-sequence input limitation inherent in deepRAM. Specifically, we proposed a novel dual-branch neural network architecture that enables simultaneous processing of two biological sequences, thereby facilitating the capture of their interactive features. To integrate heterogeneous biological molecule information, a feature fusion mechanism was implemented to enable collaborative representation learning. The model retained the optional use of One-hot encoding and Word2vec embeddings as employed in the original deepRAM framework. Notably, the One-hot encoding scheme was augmented by introducing a fuzzy base handling mechanism alongside a sequence padding strategy. Additionally, the Word2vec approach was enhanced through dynamic k-mer embedding techniques, accompanied by the construction of a shared embedding layer to facilitate knowledge transfer across sequences. Traditional classifiers typically rely on handcrafted features and shallow machine learning models, which often struggle to capture complex sequence patterns in RNA data. In contrast, deep learning-based approaches such as DeepRAM leverage neural networks to automatically extract hierarchical features, significantly improving prediction accuracy. Building upon these advancements, RNAErinie further enhances performance by incorporating a transformer-based architecture, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies and contextual relationships within RNA sequences.

RNAErnie introduces a three-stage masking strategy in the pre-training process. This approach allows the model to better capture complex features in RNA sequences. The RNAErnie model's architecture draws upon the Transformer paradigm used in natural language processing, utilizing its sophisticated context-processing capabilities to identify complex structural and functional relationships in RNA sequences. The RNAErnie model consists of two main core components: the input embedding layer and the self-attention mechanism. In the input embedding layer, RNA sequences are first converted into dense vector representations, which usually combine the semantic information of nucleotides and the positional information of sequences. Subsequently, through the self-attention mechanism, the model can efficiently capture long-range dependencies by focusing on the relationship between each nucleotide and other nucleotides while processing the sequence. Such a mechanism is particularly suitable for RNA sequences, as their function often depends on long-range sequence element interactions. We use the pre-trained RNAErnie model, fine-tuning it for feature extraction and classification of RRIs data by means of a hybrid neural network consisting of a CNN [34], which explores short, localized features (e.g., nucleotide pairs or small structural patterns), and a long short-term memory (LSTM) [35], as shown in Fig. 4. The former is used to explore short local features (e.g., nucleotide pairs or small structural patterns), while the latter considers long-distance relationships between different nucleotides in the whole RNA sequence [36].Fig. 4Feature extraction for RNAErnie models. Model details and evaluation

Among the traditional classifier methods, we used three different classifiers, random forest (RF), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), and support vector machine (SVM). This methodological approach emphasizes the structured combination of feature extraction and labeling processes, thus facilitating a robust framework for machine learning applications aimed at RNA sequence analysis. KNN takes advantage of proximity to capture local features and is suitable for discovering potential interoperations; RF relies on diversified decision trees to improve the ability to distinguish negative samples; SVM searches for segmentation boundaries through interval maximization and adapts to complex features. KNN takes advantage of proximity to capture local features and is suitable for discovering potential interoperations; RF relies on diversified decision trees to improve the ability to distinguish negative samples; SVM searches for segmentation boundaries through interval maximization and adapts to complex features.

In the transfer learning process of deepRAM, we employed three distinct layer combination schemes: CNN [37], bidirectional RNN [38], and a hybrid CNN-RNN architecture [39]. The convolutional layers utilize optimizable filters to scan biological sequences, thereby autonomously identifying local conserved motifs such as hairpin loops and stem regions. Meanwhile, the bidirectional LSTM layers capture long-range dependencies, effectively modeling the spatial proximity effects between distal sites during RNA molecular folding. Deep features extracted from the two sites are integrated within a fusion layer to facilitate interaction modeling, and the resultant representations are subsequently decoded by a fully connected layer to estimate their interaction probability.

In the RNAErnie model, after the feature extraction is completed, the generated feature vector is passed to the classifier part. The classifier consists of a series of fully connected layers, the first layer maps from 64 input features to 16 output features, through a ReLU activation function, which introduces nonlinearities, and then through a dropout layer to prevent overfitting, and the last layer maps the features to 2 output features, which corresponds to the classification of negative and positive classes.

In order to evaluate the effectiveness of the model, a five-fold cross-validation methodology was employed, wherein specific random seeds were established to maintain consistency across the various methods that were tested and trained. This approach ensures that the assessments of model performance are robust and comparable, thereby enhancing the reliability of the results obtained from the experimentation process [40].

We choose the precision, recall, F1-score, accuracy and AUC values as the evaluation metrics for the models. In addition, we provide the confusion matrix to visually illustrate the classification performance of the models across different classes [41].

The accuracy reflects the overall classification correctness of the model we use for positive and negative sample balance scenarios. Calculated by averaging across different subsets in 50 % discount cross-validation, but needs to be combined with other metrics due to possible category imbalance in RNA data. Precision measures the proportion of models predicted to have interactions that also have interactions, and may be prominent in highly spaced classifiers such as SVMs. Recall reflects the ability of real positive classes to be correctly recognized, and RF may prevail here due to integrated learning properties. The F1-score is the reconciled average of precision and recall, which comprehensively evaluates the classification results. Locally sensitive algorithms such as KNN may have large fluctuations in the F1 value in complex distributed data. AUC evaluates the overall model performance by calculating the area of the curve of the true positive rate (TPR) versus the false positive rate (FPR) by threshold sliding, which is applicable to the evaluation of the characterization capability of deep models such as RNAErnie.

Discussion

The findings from this investigation reveal that the RNAErnie deep learning model outperforms both the traditional One-hot encoding model and the Word2Vec model in predicting RRIs. Each of these models has distinct advantages and limitations, which are discussed in detail below.

Comparison of models: Traditional classifier methods, deepRAM, and RNAErnie

Traditional One-hot encoding, while straightforward, suffers from high dimensionality and sparsity, which obscures subtle but biologically critical interaction motifs and leads to suboptimal performance in discriminative models. For instance, in our SARS-CoV-2 interaction prediction task, an SVM trained on One-hot encoded sequences achieved only a modest AUC of 0.8767, reflecting its limited capacity to capture the nuanced patterns underlying RNA duplex formation. Word2Vec addresses some of these limitations by learning dense, low-dimensional embeddings of k-mers, improving the AUC of KNN and SVM to 0.9344 and 0.9216, respectively, through better representation of local sequence contexts. However, its static embeddings risk averaging out rare k-mers involved in key interactions, and its fixed window size inherently restricts modeling of long-range viral RNA structures.

The deepRAM model overcomes these constraints by synergizing neural architecture design with feature embedding strategies. The neural network architecture employed by deepRAM, particularly its integrated CNN-RNN framework, demonstrates remarkable capability in modeling long-range sequential dependencies. This architecture shows considerable promise in effectively identifying functional interactions among distally spaced RNA elements within viral genomes, thereby providing critical insights into the sophisticated regulatory mechanisms governed by viral RNA secondary and tertiary structures [22]. When using One-hot encoding, its RNN layer achieves an AUC of 0.9369 by modeling sequential dependencies, while the CNN-RNN hybrid (AUC: 0.9392) further refines performance through joint learning of localized motifs and their spatial arrangements. Notably, combining deepRAM with Word2Vec embeddings yields even greater gains, as evidenced by the CNN variant’s AUC of 0.9581 and balanced precision-recall metrics (0.9148 precision, 0.8832 recall), demonstrating superior robustness in distinguishing true interaction sites from noise. This improvement highlights the advantage of integrating learned embeddings with hierarchical feature extraction, where convolutional operations efficiently capture complementary k-mer interactions while avoiding the computational overhead of recurrent layers (CNN-RNN AUC: 0.9456 vs. RNN AUC: 0.9448). Together, these results position deepRAM as a powerful framework for SARS-CoV-2 RNA interaction prediction, capable of addressing both the local and global sequence determinants that govern viral RNA-RNA binding.

RNAErnie surpasses traditional models with a precision of 0.8910 and an AUC of 0.9550, thanks to its hierarchical feature learning and self-attention mechanism that captures long-range nucleotide interactions missed by One-hot and Word2Vec. During its pre-training phase, the RNAErnie model systematically integrates RNA motifs by employing a multi-level masking strategy, thereby learning the biological priors inherent in contiguous subsequences and motifs. This approach enables the model to accurately identify key structural elements within tertiary RNA structures (e.g., stem-loop structures and junction regions), consequently elucidating the pivotal role these motifs play in mediating RRIs and driving the assembly of complex ribonucleoprotein complexes[23]. This improves recall (0.8874) and reduces false negatives. The fully connected layer, ReLU activation, and dropout enhance feature discrimination and prevent overfitting, resulting in a stable F1 score of 0.8872. Its superior AUC highlights better distinction between positive and negative classes compared to threshold-based classifiers like SVM (Table 5).

Implications and future directions

In current RNA sequence modeling, there is a clear trade-off between performance and complexity among traditional classifier methods, deepRAM models, and RNAErnie models. Traditional classifier methods are suitable for basic tasks, but deepRAM models and RNAErnie models demonstrate significant performance improvements when predicting complex RNA interactions. Future research could explore hybrid methods that combine the advantages of these models to further improve prediction performance.

With the continued abundance of biological data, especially the increasing amount of RNA virus-related data, it is particularly important to construct more complex RNA virus macro models. Such macro models are capable of not only capturing the nuances of RNA sequences, but also learning the complex interactions between viruses and their hosts. Utilizing deep learning and natural language processing techniques, researchers can design multilevel representations that integrate environmental variables such as temperature changes, mutability, and host specificity to improve the predictive accuracy of the models.

In addition, combining advanced techniques such as graph neural networks and self-attentive mechanisms helps capture long-range dependencies and complex structural information in the temporal dynamics of RNA interactions [42]. These techniques can enhance the ability of the model to migrate and learn across different RNA viruses, enabling it to achieve better performance with emerging viruses and variants.

In practice, The developed comprehensive model for RNA viruses exhibits broad applicability across multiple scientific and clinical domains, including vaccine design, drug discovery, and prevention and control of virus transmission [43]. For example, by analyzing a large number of RNA sequences, key structural domains and functional regions can be identified, thus providing theoretical support for target development of new drugs.

In summary, with the deepening of research and the expansion of datasets, the construction of large models of complex RNA viruses in the future will significantly improve our understanding of RNA biological functions and viral interactions, providing an important foundation for public health and biomedical research.

Conclusion

The research highlights the promising capabilities of machine learning algorithms in predictive analytics, particularly deepRAM and RNAErnie, in accurately predicting SARS-CoV-2 RRIs, offering a significant improvement over traditional method. By leveraging advanced sequence features and deep learning frameworks, these models provide robust and generalizable predictions, with potential applications in understanding viral mechanisms and designing therapeutic strategies. The investigation underscores persistent limitations in machine learning applications, particularly regarding data integrity, transparency of predictive models, and processing efficiency. Ongoing methodological advancements must prioritize algorithmic optimization and the integration of heterogeneous biological datasets to enhance clinical utility, and validating models in clinical settings to advance the field of RNA interaction studies and its applications in disease research.

Janus kinase inhibitor audit
Authors: Jenni Beck, Samantha Banford
Published: 2023

Introduction

Janus kinases (JAK) are intracellular tyrosine kinases that are activated by various receptors, becoming more popular for use in rheumatological conditions. The JAK/STAT pathway mediates transcriptional regulation of genes for cell proliferation, differentiation and activation as well as metabolic homeostatic regulation. These genes are critical for immune function and homeostasis. JAK inhibitors interrupt the JAK/STAT pathway. FDA and EMA guidance states that JAK inhibitors should be reserved as second-line treatment and in caution with patients at risk of VTE/cardiovascular events. We assessed our database of 145 patients on JAK inhibitors under the care of rheumatology for risk factors, compliance and appropriateness of treatment.

Materials and methods

145 patients' details were taken from a centralised database. Data were retrospectively collected from electronic patient care records, including previous biologics used, cardiovascular risk factors, compliance with blood monitoring and mean age at initiation of JAK inhibitor. Data were compared with a 2021 audit from the same database. Guidance was for monthly bloods for 3 months then 3 monthly, and compliance with this was also assessed via the electronic lab centre.

Results and discussion

13 patients were active smokers. 21 patients had a cerebrovascular accident / thrombotic event prior to initiation of JAK inhibitor. Four patients had a myocardial infarction or CVA during treatment. 85 patients were older than 50 years with two or more risk factors. 49 patients were 65 years or older with two or more risk factors. There were two deaths while on treatment – one due to bronchopneumonia, one due to congestive cardiac failure. 58 patients were not undergoing the advised blood monitoring due to either non-attendance or being lost to follow-up. Compared with the 2021 audit, with the introduction of a JAK blood clinic and results being emailed to teams – compliance with risk factor monitoring increased from 40% to 61%. The mean age of patients receiving JAK inhibitors had reduced from 76.5 years to 63.6 years from 2021 to 2022.

Conclusion

JAK inhibitors are high-risk medications and should be used with caution in patients with cardiovascular or thrombotic risk factors. Close monitoring of lipids/blood pressure needs to be ensured. This audit loop showed that, with the introduction of weekly blood monitoring email lists and a blood test protocol with a centralised database, compliance with risk factor monitoring did increase; however, it is still not at the level desired of 100%. Following this, it has been communicated to the patients who are no longer receiving communication and they have been resent appointments. We have introduced a patient education letter that is automatically sent out to patients if they do not attend two lipid and blood pressure monitoring appointments to educate them on the associated risks of the treatment. All teams will now be contacted if individual patients do not attend follow-up, so the appropriateness of a JAK inhibitor for these patients can be re-reviewed. JAK blood results are now emailed to a named doctor on each team to ensure that there is a designated doctor with responsibility to monitor the results.

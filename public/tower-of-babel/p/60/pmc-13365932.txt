IFPHOR0039 Beyond the Brush: Addressing Oral Health Inequalities Through Early Years Interventions
Authors: Alisha Maria Benedict
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOral health inequalities remain a significant public health challenge in the UK, disproportionately affecting children from socioeconomically disadvantaged communities. This aligns with national priorities such as the CORE20PLUS5 approach, which emphasises targeted action on prevention and early intervention in high-risk groups. Early years settings provide a key opportunity to establish positive oral health behaviours and reduce long-term inequalities.ApproachDrawing on frontline experience as an Oral Health Promoter, this work reflects the delivery of a supervised toothbrushing programme within early years settings serving high-risk populations. The programme combined daily supervised brushing with staff training, parental engagement, and consistent reinforcement of oral health messages. Behaviour change principles were embedded to support habit formation and sustainability.Insights from PracticeImproved engagement was observed among children, particularly where staff demonstrated confidence and consistency in delivery. However, variations in staff capacity, competing priorities within early years settings, and differing levels of parental involvement influenced programme effectiveness. Wider social determinants, including deprivation and limited access to dental services, remained key barriers to sustained impact.DiscussionThese experiences highlight that while supervised toothbrushing programmes are an important preventive intervention, their success is shaped by workforce engagement, organisational support, and the broader social context. Aligning delivery with frameworks such as CORE20PLUS5 can support more targeted and equitable approaches.ConclusionReducing oral health inequalities requires a holistic, community-based approach that goes beyond programme delivery. Strengthening workforce capability, embedding behaviour change strategies, and addressing wider determinants are essential to achieving sustainable improvements in oral health outcomes.

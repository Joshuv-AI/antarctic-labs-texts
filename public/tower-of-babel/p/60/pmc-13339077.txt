Risk of fracture following androgen receptor pathway inhibitors in men with advanced prostate cancer
Authors: Grace Lu-Yao, Nikita Nikita, Scott W Keith, Krupa Gandhi, Amy L Shaver, Swapnil Sharma, Christina Steinbock-Malfer, Hushan Yang, Christopher Yang, Kevin K Zarrabi, Stephen J Freedland, William K Kelly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis population-based study aimed to quantify fracture risk after androgen receptor pathway inhibitors in advanced prostate cancer patients by preexisting health conditions.MethodsPatients were identified from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results–Medicare files who received abiraterone acetate with prednisone or enzalutamide between January 1, 2013, and December 31, 2020. Health and fracture history were based on claims 1 year before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor with follow-up through December 31, 2020. The main outcome of the study was the cumulative fracture risk after first date of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor. Fine and Gray subdistribution hazard model was used to obtain adjusted relative risks with confounding factors.ResultsThis study included 10 463 patients (6037 treated with abiraterone acetate with prednisone; 4426 treated with enzalutamide). The 3-year fracture risk after androgen receptor pathway inhibitor was high, exceeding 25% among those without a prior fracture. Among 1445 men with a fracture the year before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor, 3-year fracture risk exceeded 50% and remained high (above 44%) despite using bone health agents. A recent history of fracture was associated with a 2.84-fold fracture risk (adjusted hazard ratio [HR] = 2.84, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 2.58 to 3.12). Preexisting osteoporosis and a comorbidity score of 2 or higher were associated with 15% (adjusted HR = 1.15, 95% CI = 1.03 to 1.29) and 11% (adjusted HR = 1.11, 95% CI = 1.00 to 1.24) higher fracture risks. Bone health agent use was associated with a 23% lower fracture risk (adjusted HR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.70 to 0.83).ConclusionsFracture risk after androgen receptor pathway inhibitor was high, exceeding 44% within 3 years in those with prior fractures despite bone health agents, suggesting limited benefit in patients with poor bone quality. Early identification and intervention for patients at high risk of fractures are critical.

Introduction

Abiraterone acetate with prednisone and enzalutamide are the most commonly prescribed androgen receptor pathway inhibitors for advanced prostate cancer.1,2 However, pivotal randomized clinical trials (RCTs) of abiraterone acetate with prednisone and enzalutamide often exclude men with clinically significant comorbidities;3-9 although the strict eligibility criteria for RCTs may stem from safety concerns, they limit the generalizability of the findings.10,11

Androgen deprivation therapy for advanced prostate cancer is associated with a substantial increase in fracture risk. A population-based study reported 19% fracture risk among men with advanced prostate cancer surviving 5 years after androgen deprivation therapy initiation.12 The Systemic Therapy in Advancing or Metastatic Prostate Cancer: Evaluation of Drug Efficacy (STAMPEDE) trial reported that among men with advanced prostate cancer defined by distant metastasis, 26% of the patients were hospitalized for fractures.13 Current clinical guidelines recommend combining androgen receptor pathway inhibitors with androgen deprivation therapy based on RCT results.14,15 However, most of these trials have limited follow-up, and, as androgen receptor pathway inhibitors are being used earlier in the disease course, the potential for long-term side effects emerges as a major safety concern. Unfortunately, knowledge about the long-term risks of bone fractures from androgen receptor pathway inhibitors is limited. Given the crucial role androgens play in bone metabolism,16-18 combining androgen receptor pathway inhibitors with androgen deprivation therapy might futher compromise bone health and thus increase the fracture risk, though this topic is underexamined.

The general association between androgen deprivation therapy and fracture risk has been well established;19,20 however, the long-term fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitor therapy and the contribution of preexisting conditions such as osteoporosis, fracture history, and other comorbidities remain unclear.21 This population-based study aimed to fill these knowledge gaps to facilitate individualized treatment decisions.

Methods

This study received approval from the Thomas Jefferson University institutional review board.

Data sources

This study used the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) registries linked with the Medicare database with hospital, physician, and outpatient claims.22 SEER has a case ascertainment rate of 98% and covers approximately 48% of the US population as of 2020.23,24 Medicare is the primary health insurance provider for US patients aged 65 years or older.25

Study participants

The study cohort included patients treated with abiraterone acetate with prednisone or enzalutamide between January 1, 2013, and December 31, 2020, for advanced prostate cancer, excluding those who were diagnosed at death or lack of fee-for-service Medicare 12 months before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor.

Drug exposure

Abiraterone acetate with prednisone was identified from Medicare Part D as having a prescription for abiraterone acetate, or Zytiga, and enzalutamide was identified as having a prescription for enzalutamide, or Xtandi. The abiraterone acetate with prednisone group excluded patients treated with enzalutamide. Similarly, the enzalutamide group excluded patients treated with abiraterone acetate with prednisone.

Study endpoint

The primary endpoint was the time from the first date of abiraterone acetate with prednisone or enzalutamide to the first fracture after treatment. Fractures were categorized by type: pathological, osteoporotic, and by site (vertebral, hip and/or pelvis, long bone, and other fractures) (Table S1). Pathological fractures were defined as fractures coded as “pathologic” in diseased bone (eg, due to metastatic or primary bone lesions or other bone pathology), whereas osteoporotic fractures were defined using osteoporosis-related fracture codes without concurrent pathologic fracture codes. We further categorized fractures by anatomic site (vertebral, hip and/or pelvis, long bone, and other). Fracture diagnoses were ascertained from inpatient, outpatient, and provider Medicare claims.

Descriptive variables

Demographic variables and use of cancer therapies were derived from the SEER-Medicare linked file. Charlson comorbidity index and prior fracture or osteoporosis was based on documented diagnosis codes within 1 year before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor initiation. Use of bone health agents was defined as having documented prescription 6 months before or within 3 months of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor initiation. We categorized bone health agents into 3 groups: denosumab monotherapy, zoledronic acid monotherapy, or both.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were compared by type of treatment using χ2 tests (see Table 1). Fine and Gray subdistribution hazard models, evaluating the associations between preexisting conditions and fracture risk, while accounting for competing risks, were adjusting for age, race, marital status, comorbidity status, socioeconomic status indicators, geographic regions, treatment year, and chemotherapy use (see Table 2). Two-way interactions were tested between treatment type (abiraterone acetate with prednisone, enzalutamide) and race, as well as prior fracture and use of bone health agents. A hierarchical backward selection method was used for these interaction terms, starting with a full model, then paring away the term with the largest P value, refitting after removing that term, and repeating until all remaining interactions had a P value less than .05. Because real-world data and our data show no significant differences in the fracture risks following denosumab or zoledronic acid,26 we combined bone health agents into 1 group. All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS v9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

The study cohort included patients treated with abiraterone acetate with prednisone (n = 6037) or enzalutamide (n = 4426) between January 1, 2013, and December 31, 2020, for advanced prostate cancer. Most patients in this cohort had metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer because abiraterone acetate with prednisone and enzalutamide were approved for metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer in 2018 and 2019. Of the cohort, 14% had a fracture within 1 year before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor, and 45.7% were prescribed bone health agents around the time of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor initiation. The distributions by fracture site are 17.2% vertebral fractures, 20.7% skull or rib fractures, 20.8% long bone fractures, 8.2% pelvic and/or hip fractures, and 33.1% unknown (Table S2). Among identified fractures, 20.9% are pathological fractures (Table S3). More than 95% of pathological fractures have unspecified fracture site.

Cumulative fracture risk

Figure 1 shows the overall cumulative fracture risk by prior fracture. Among those with a prior fracture, the unadjusted fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitor exceeded 50% within 3 years. Figure 2 presents the cumulative fracture risk by bone health agent use. Among patients with a prior fracture, the unadjusted fracture risk among those with bone health agents remained high (44.6%) (Figure 2, A). Among patients without prior fracture, the unadjusted fracture risk among those with bone health agents reached 20% within 3 years (Figure 2, B).

Multivariate analyses

After adjusting for potential confounding variables, the fracture risk between the 2 androgen receptor pathway inhibitor treatments was similar (enzalutamide vs abiraterone acetate with prednisone adjusted hazard ratio [HR] = 0.93, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 0.85 to 1.00); therefore, we combined both treatment groups in quantifying the relative risk of fracture related to prior fracture and preexisting conditions. A higher Charlson comorbidity index score (2 or higher) is associated with 11% higher risk of fracture. Prior fracture and preexisting osteoporosis is associated with 184% (adjusted HR = 2.84, 95% CI = 2.58 to 3.12) and 15% (adjusted HR = 1.15, 95% CI = 1.03 to 1.29) higher fracture risk (Table 2). Bone health agent use around the time of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor initiation was associated with a 23% lower fracture risk (adjusted HR = 0.77, 95% CI = 0.70 to 0.83). Both denosumab (adjusted HR = 0.76, 95% CI = 0.69 to 0.83) and zoledronic acid (adjusted HR = 0.68, 95% CI = 0.57 to 0.81) are associated with a lower risk of fracture.

Sensitivity analysis

We conducted a sensitivity analysis to examine the timing of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (within 6 months or beyond 6 months of advanced prostate cancer diagnosis) and found similar results (Figure S1). Furthermore, we used Z19.1 and Z19.2 International Classification of Diseases codes 1 year before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor treatment to identify disease status—metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer and metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. The risk of fracture among men with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer was 33% lower compared with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (HR = 0.67, 95% CI = 0.48 to 0.93). A recent fracture history was associated with 5.48-fold risk of fracture (95% CI = 2.60 to 11.53) among those with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer and 2.12-fold risk (95% CI = 1.36 to 3.30) of fracture among those with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.

We also quantified the cumulative risk of fracture, excluding pathological or vertebral fracture (Figure S2): 33.0% and 18.9% 3 years after androgen receptor pathway inhibitor among those with and without prior fracture. When the analysis excluded pathological fracture or vertebral fracture, bone health agent use was associated with a 24% reduction in fracture risk (adjusted HR = 0.76, 95% CI = 0.69 to 0.84).

Discussion

This study provides critical real-world data on fracture risks in advanced prostate cancer patients undergoing androgen receptor pathway inhibitor therapy, addressing an important literature gap not addressed by RCTs, particularly among patients with preexisting conditions such as osteoporosis, prior fractures, or significant comorbidities. As expected, this study confirms that preexisting osteoporosis and higher comorbidity burden are associated with an elevated relative risk of fracture. Importantly, this study reveals that prior fractures are common in patients treated with androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (1 in 7), which nearly triple the fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (Table 2). Similar patterns were observed in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer and metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. The 3-year fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitor among men with a prior fracture was high (above 52%) and remained very high (above 44%) even with bone health agent treatment. These findings suggest that the potential impact of bone health agents might be limited in men with compromised baseline bone health. Given that most of the observed fractures are likely to have a substantial impact on quality of life, early identification and intervention for men who are at an increased risk of fractures are critical.

Results from a meta-analysis of 6545 patients in RCTs show that androgen receptor pathway inhibitor treatment arms have approximately twofold fracture risk compared with androgen deprivation therapy arms.27 Applying this risk multiplier to the 3-year fracture risk among androgen deprivation therapy users with advanced prostate cancer (14.7%) in a population-based study,12 the estimated fracture risk in androgen receptor pathway inhibitor users will be approximately 29%, similar to what we found in this study. Figure 3 shows that the fracture risk observed in this real-world study far exceeds those in clinical trials.27,28 This real-world study found that the fracture risk following abiraterone acetate with prednisone among those without prior fracture (30%) was approximately 15 times those reported in the meta-analysis (2.2%) by Jones et al.27 Additionally, this study showed that 1 in 7 men receiving androgen receptor pathway inhibitor had a fracture the year before androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (Table 1); among these men, the risk of fracture following abiraterone acetate with prednisone (54%) was approximately 27 times that reported in the meta-analysis by Jones et al.27 Similarly, the fracture risk following enzalutamide in this study (Figure 3) was far more than those reported in the meta-analysis of RCTs.27,28

The much higher 3-year fracture risk in our real-world cohort compared with the Jones et al.27 meta-analysis RCT patients likely resulted from several contributing factors. In addition to being older, our patients differ from RCT participants in functional status, recent fracture history, osteoporosis status, and comorbidity status. Our data reflect an unselected, comorbid population at intrinsically higher baseline risk of falls and fractures. Fractures in RCTs are captured as adverse events over heterogeneous and often shorter follow-up. The lack of data on the timing of fracture also precludes estimation of standardized 3-year cumulative incidence. Moreover, trial settings often provide more systematic bone-health management than routine practice. These differences in case mix, outcome ascertainment, follow-up, and supportive care together likely account for the large gap in absolute fracture rates, beyond the modest age effect observed within our cohort. These limitations demonstrate the importance of real-world data in filling in knowledge gaps, especially for older patients with coexisting medical conditions.

In this real-world study, we found that Black men had a 42% lower adjusted risk of fracture compared with White men. The findings are consistent with the report that African American men have higher baseline bone mineral density and fewer prevalent vertebral fractures compared with White men. 29

The mechanisms underpinning increased fracture risk with androgen receptor pathway inhibitors primarily involve androgen deprivation–induced deterioration in bone integrity.30,31 Testosterone and related androgens serve essential functions in bone remodeling by supporting osteoblast activity, inhibiting osteoclast overactivity, and maintaining overall bone homeostasis.30,31 When androgen receptor pathway inhibitors are used alongside androgen deprivation therapy, the already low circulating androgen levels are further reduced, which exacerbates bone-density loss and weakens the microarchitecture of the skeleton, making fractures more likely. Furthermore, androgen deprivation leads to decreased muscle mass, diminished muscle strength, and poorer neuromuscular coordination, all of which increase the likelihood of falls, a major contributor to fractures.32-34 Enzalutamide pharmacodynamics further potentiate fall risk given drug penetration through the blood-brain-barrier and off-target effects including dizziness and falls.35 These effects may be particularly pronounced in older men who already have comorbidities that increase their susceptibility to such events. These findings underscore that fracture history, osteoporosis, and comorbidities play a crucial role in predicting new fracture events.

Starting bone health agents before or around the time of initiating androgen receptor pathway inhibitors is associated with a 23% reduction in fracture risk. When the analysis excluded pathological fracture or vertebral fracture, the result remained similar (adjusted HR = 0.76).

This finding is consistent with the findings from the Prostate Cancer Consortium in Europe’s PEACE III trial and previous studies.36-40 Further investigation is needed to examine the optimal timing of bone health agents and other bone health interventions, especially for those with prior fractures and considering androgen receptor pathway inhibitors.

Half of the fractures identified in this study likely have a negative impact on functional status and quality of life: 20.7% skull or rib fractures, 20.8% long bone fractures, 8.2% pelvic and/or hip fractures. Given the substantially elevated fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitor among those with prior fractures or osteoporosis, it is critical to proactively assess bone health prior to initiating treatment early in their disease course. Initiation of bone health evaluation and intervention prior to or concurrently with androgen receptor pathway inhibitor treatment is essential to prevent fractures because delayed interventions offer minimal benefit.41-44 The use of androgen receptor pathway inhibitors in early disease settings is increasing, leading to greater drug utilization and exposing more patients to toxicity, as androgen receptor pathway inhibitors are now recommended by guidelines for patients with very high-risk localized disease and in the biochemical recurrence settings.45,46 Comprehensive patient care is essential and requires a multidisciplinary approach to optimize bone health outcomes and reduce fracture risk, enhancing overall quality of life and survival.44 Additionally, patient education that emphasizes adherence to bone health regimens and regular monitoring of bone mineral density is vital.42,44 To support these efforts, it is recommended that clinical guidelines incorporate structured assessment pathways, including standardized fracture risk calculators such as FRAX, into routine oncology care for the timely identification and management of high-risk patients.47

The greatest strength of this study is the extensive, rich, population-based database with a broad representation of patients with preexisting conditions in diverse clinical settings. Consequently, the findings are likely to be generalized to most patients who are considering treatment with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor. The unique contribution of this study is to provide long-term risk estimates by type of androgen receptor pathway inhibitor, preexisting health conditions, use of bone health agents, and race, all factors with high generalizability in the general advanced prostate cancer patient population. Another major strength was its use of longitudinal data at the individual level, with over 3 years of follow-up data in many cases and a competing risks model to examine the cumulative fracture risks over time, which would be challenging to derive from RCTs.

This study had several limitations. As a retrospective study based on secondary data, it likely had some misclassifications of fracture, comorbidities, and confounders. The primary endpoint—fracture—was derived from Medicare claims diagnosis codes, and we could not distinguish digital fractures from clinically symptomatic fractures. In addition, only a minority of fractures carried specific codes for osteoporosis or pathologic fracture, and most fractures were coded using generic fracture diagnoses without an explicit underlying etiology. Given that claims-based algorithms consistently show that osteoporosis and osteoporotic fractures are undercoded, it is likely that many fractures categorized as “unclassified” in our study represent osteoporotic events. Although incidental fractures may contribute to the fractures identified in our study, the available literature suggests that the main concern is underascertainment of such fractures rather than inflation of fracture counts. Furthermore, we have defined the sites of fracture and cumulative risk of nonpathological, nonvertebral fracture (Figure S2) to address these concerns. More than half of the reported fractures are of clinical significance. The misclassification of study variables was likely nonsystematic and unrelated to underlying fracture risk; as such, the results are likely underestimating the strength of some associations between preexisting conditions and fracture risks. Additionally, our study does not have data on the reasons for bone health agent use, use of dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry, performance status, and other potential confounding factors such as vitamin D status, bone mineral density, and testosterone levels to explain the mechanisms underlying observed racial difference.

Currently, dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry is rarely done in real-world settings.48,49 It is important to note that the paradigm of treatment has changed during the study period. It was uncommon to use the combination of androgen receptor pathway inhibitors with androgen deprivation therapy in men newly diagnosed with metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. Although some patients still receive androgen deprivation therapy then androgen receptor pathway inhibitor sequentially, the current standard for newly diagnosed metastatic advanced prostate cancer is androgen deprivation therapy plus androgen receptor pathway inhibitor. With treatment intensification, the risks shown in this study likely represent the lower end of the risks expected with the new treatment paradigm. Lastly, this study included patients receiving androgen receptor pathway inhibitor within a few months of advanced prostate cancer diagnosis to over 20 years beyond advanced prostate cancer diagnosis. To address whether the results would be different among those with de novo advanced prostate cancer vs those with a progressing tumor, we conducted a sensitivity analysis, which showed that the cumulative fracture risk was consistent among patients treated with androgen receptor pathway inhibitor within 6 months or beyond 6 months of advanced prostate cancer diagnosis (Figure S1).

Future research is warranted to investigate the precise mechanisms underpinning bone loss and fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitor therapy. Prospective studies investigating the interaction between multimodal interventionssuch as combining androgen receptor pathway inhibitors with radioligand therapy, optimal timing of bone health agents, fall-prevention strategies, physical rehabilitation programs, and patient educational interventions, may clarify optimal strategies for fracture prevention.

The fracture risk following androgen receptor pathway inhibitors is high, especially among those with prior fractures. Notably, despite bone health agent use, fracture risk remained alarmingly elevated, highlighting the urgent need for early identification of high-risk patients and bone-protective interventions. These findings firmly call for integrating proactive fracture-risk assessment and bone health management strategies into routine clinical practice to enhance patient outcomes and quality of life. By identifying high-risk patients early, administering bone protection interventions before or alongside androgen receptor pathway inhibitor treatment, and employing a multidisciplinary care model, clinicians can potentially mitigate this serious complication and improve overall patient outcomes and quality of life.

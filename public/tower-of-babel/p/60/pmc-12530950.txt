Vascular Reconstruction Device for Coil Embolization of Wide-Neck Type Cerebral Aneurysm: Findings from a Post-Marketing Surveillance Study in Japan
Authors: Yoichiro Kawamura, Atsuko Honda, Akio Hyodo, Shigeru Miyachi, Yuji Matsumaru, Waro Taki, Yasushi Ito, Naoya Kuwayama, Hirotoshi Imamura, Takashi Izumi, Chiaki Sakai, Nobuyuki Sakai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveEndovascular treatment of wide-neck cerebral aneurysms with conventional coiling alone remains challenging. Stent-assisted coiling using a vascular reconstruction device offers an alternative strategy. This study evaluated the safety and effectiveness of the Enterprise Vascular Reconstruction Device (Cerenovus, Irvine, CA, USA) in patients with wide-neck cerebral aneurysms.MethodsPost-marketing surveillance records were collected for all Enterprise stent usage using case report forms at 30 days and at 1, 2, and 3 years post-procedure. A total of 738 cases, including 411 on-label cases, were analyzed across 83 hospitals.ResultsStent deployment was successful in 98.8% of cases, and the overall procedure success rate was 93.9% immediately post-procedure. The aneurysm occlusion rate was 92.90%, with 73.7% classified as Raymond–Roy class I/II immediately post-procedure. Among the 411 on-label cases, 17 patients (4.1%) demonstrated worsening of the modified Rankin Scale score, but no deaths were attributable to the utilization of the Enterprise device. The postoperative ischemic stroke complication rate was low (4.5%) but increased significantly in patients with a proximal parent artery diameter <2.5 mm (18.6%) and in those aged ≥65 years (8%). Multivariable logistic regression revealed that age ≥65 years, diabetes, hypertension, and a past medical history of ischemic stroke were significant risk factors for postoperative ischemic stroke.ConclusionVascular reconstruction device implantation was effective and safe for treating wide-neck cerebral aneurysms.

Introduction

Endovascular treatment has become a cornerstone in the management of cerebral aneurysms, with wide-neck cerebral aneurysms posing particular technical challenges without adjunctive techniques.1–3) In the current landscape, approaches such as intrasaccular flow disruption, flow diversion, or stent-assisted coiling are frequently employed, contingent upon the location and morphology of aneurysms. Among these, stent-assisted coiling with vascular reconstruction devices (VRDs) has demonstrated favorable outcomes by enabling higher packing density and reducing parent artery occlusion rates compared with conventional coiling.4–9)

The introduction of self-expanding stents, beginning with the Neuroform device (Stryker Neurovascular, Fremont, CA, USA) in the United States for the treatment of wide-neck intracranial aneurysms (parent artery diameter ≥2.0 and ≤4.5 mm), marked an important advance.10,11) The subsequent approval of the Enterprise VRD (Cerenovus, Irvine, CA, USA) by the Food and Drug Administration further expanded treatment options for wide-neck aneurysms.12)

In Japan, evaluation of the Enterprise VRD serves as a valuable benchmark for treatment outcomes. This study aimed to assess the effectiveness and safety of the Enterprise stent-assisted coiling as an innovative approach for managing wide-neck aneurysms. The study focuses on the treatment outcomes using the self-expanding nitinol stent in conjunction with coiling for wide-neck aneurysms.

Materials and Methods

Study design and patients

The study was conducted prospectively, including all cases following the insurance coverage approval on July 1, 2010. Patient registration was planned to continue until either 4 years after study initiation or the enrollment of at least 300 on-label cases, each followed for 3 years. Case enrollment concluded on July 31, 2022 when the target was reached, and follow-up was extended for 3 years thereafter.

Eligibility criteria followed the standards established by the Japan Stroke Society, the Japan Neurosurgical Society, and the Japan Society for Neuroendovascular Therapy. Participating institutions were required to meet facility and operator requirements and adhere to associated prerequisites. All patients who underwent Enterprise VRD (Cerenovus) implantation between July 2010 and July 2011 were included. Oral informed consent was obtained, and explanations provided to each patient were recorded and documented as on-label by the physician’s signature on the consent form. As the study was mandated by regulatory authorities, ethics committee approval was obtained only at sites where stipulated by institutional policy.

In Japan, on-label indications for the use of the Enterprise VRD are: unruptured aneurysms (>7 mm in maximum diameter) with a wide neck (neck width ≥4 mm or dome-to-neck ratio <2 mm) and a parent artery diameter of 2.5–4.0 mm. All other cases, including ruptured aneurysms, were considered off-label. The device was used during the interventional coil procedure to prevent coil protrusion and/or dropout into the parent artery.

Device implantation

The Enterprise VRD was positioned across the aneurysm neck to provide scaffolding, thereby creating an artificial neck that stabilized coil placement, prevented coil migration, and ensured proper coil embolization.

Effectiveness outcome

The effectiveness endpoints included: (1) aneurysm occlusion rate—defined as the proportion of aneurysm volume occupied by coils, based on imaging performed pre-procedure and at 1, 2, and 3 years post-procedure. Occlusion status was classified using the Raymond–Roy (RR) scale, and the mean occlusion percentage was calculated. (2) Deployment success—evaluated post-procedure and defined as proper placement of the VRD across the aneurysm neck with ≥5 mm coverage of the parent artery on both sides, while maintaining ≥50% luminal patency. (3) Procedure success—evaluated post-procedure and defined as deployment success combined with stable coil placement that does not prolapse into the parent artery or result in device- or procedure-related serious adverse events (AEs). (4) Coil mass stability—defined as preservation of coil position within the aneurysm and maintenance of ≥50% intra-VRD patency, evaluated at 1, 2, and 3 years post-procedure. (5) Neurological evaluation—assessed using modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores recorded pre-procedure, within 1 week post-procedure, and at 30 days, 1 year, 2 years, and 3 years.

Safety outcome

The safety outcomes included: (1) serious AEs, including stroke or intra-stent thrombosis; (2) aneurysm recurrence, such as regrowth or re-rupture; and (3) antiplatelet therapy rates and duration of use.

All AEs were recorded at 30 days and at 1, 2, and 3 years post-procedure, with information collected on causal relationships, severities, interventions, and outcomes. In particular, postsurgical stroke, VRD migration, and thrombosis were the main focus.

Statistical analysis

Sample size was calculated assuming a binomial distribution, with a target of ≥300 on-label cases to enable detection of AEs with an incidence of 1% at a 95% confidence interval. Multiple comparisons were performed using the Cochran–Armitage trend test or Fisher’s exact test, with a significance threshold of p <0.05. Analyses were conducted using SAS software (version 9.2; SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Multivariate logistic regression was performed with the occurrence of stroke events judged to be device-related as the dependent variable, and the following covariates were entered as independent variables: aneurysm neck width, maximum aneurysm size, parent artery diameter, rupture status (ruptured/unruptured), dissecting aneurysm, other lesion morphology, Raymond classification immediately post-procedure, immediate post-procedural aneurysm occlusion rate, past medical history (ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke), comorbidities (hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes mellitus, renal disease), age, and sex. Variable selection was performed using a stepwise procedure with entry and removal criteria set at α = 0.2.

Results

Patient disposition and characteristics

A total of 738 patients were enrolled from 83 institutions in Japan (Table 1). The mean age was 62.1 years, and most patients were female (74.5%). Of these, 411 patients (58.2%) underwent Enterprise VRD (Cerenovus) treatment under on-label indications and 295 (41.8%) under off-label indications. Thirty-two patients in whom the VRD was used for non-aneurysmal indications were excluded, leaving 706 patients for analysis (Fig. 1).

Patient disposition.

Of the 706 included cases, 626 aneurysms (88.7%) were unruptured and 80 (11.3%) were ruptured, including 16 (2.26%) in the acute phase. The most frequent aneurysm sites were the internal carotid artery system, particularly the paraclinoid segment (228 cases, 32.3%) and cavernous segment (85 cases, 12.0%). Posterior circulation aneurysms accounted for 233 cases (33%).

The VRD sizes included 14 mm (17 cases), 22 mm (288 cases), 28 mm (334 cases), and 37 mm (67 cases). The predominant coiling strategy was the jailing technique (538 cases, 76.2%), followed by the trans-cell technique (75 cases, 10.6%), and a combined approach (53 cases, 7.5%).

Effectiveness

The effectiveness endpoints are summarized in Table 2. Deployment success was achieved in 696 cases (98.6%), with similar rates for on-label (406/411, 98.8%) and off-label (290/295, 98.3%) groups. Post-procedure, immediate coil mass retention, where feasible, was achieved in 688 of 705 cases (97.6%), including 404/411 cases (98.3%) in the on-label group and 284/294 cases (96.6%) in the off-label group. At follow-up imaging 1 year postoperatively (348 cases, including 215 in the on-label use group), 2 years postoperatively (99 cases, including 60 in the on-label use group), and 3 years postoperatively (48 cases, including 34 in the on-label use group), coil mass retention and device patency were maintained in all cases.

Multiple device deployments were required in 44 cases (6.2%), due to inappropriate positioning (15 cases), coil unraveling (12 cases), Y-stenting (9 cases), rectification effects (5 cases), sizing errors (4 cases), and inappropriate positioning for various reasons (5 cases).

Immediately after coiling, 172 cases (24.4%) achieved complete occlusion, while 510 cases (72.2%) showed 80%–100% occlusion. Mean occlusion rates exceeded 90% in both groups (on-label: 92.90% ± 12.99%; off-label: 92.25% ± 14.67%). The post-procedural RR class is summarized in Table 2. Immediately post-procedure, the total percentage of RR class I/II was 72.7% overall (73.7% in the on-label group).

Safety

In the analysis of overall survival prognosis (Table 3), outcomes were assessed in the on-label group to exclude the confounding effect of subarachnoid hemorrhage. The proportion of patients with an mRS score between 0 and 2 was 96.8% (398/411) pre-procedure and 92.7% (381/411) immediately post-procedure. Seventeen patients (4.1%) experienced worsening of mRS scores, but no deaths were attributable to Enterprise VRD use.

Complications associated with VRD included ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and intra-stent thrombosis. The incidence of ischemic stroke was significantly higher in patients aged ≥65 years (27/307, 8.0%) compared with those <65 years (15/401, 3.7%; p = 0.016). Ischemic stroke incidence was also examined by aneurysm size and condition. In aneurysms with a diameter <7 mm, ischemic stroke occurred in 3/73 cases, with no intra-stent thrombosis. In aneurysms with a diameter >7 mm, ischemic stroke occurred in 30/648 cases, including 4 cases of intra-stent thrombosis. The incidence of VRD thrombosis was further stratified by parent artery diameter (<2.5 vs. >4.0 mm), with lower rates observed in off-label cases where the parent artery was <2.5 mm in 30-day case report forms (CRFs).

Multivariable logistic regression was conducted to assess the association between postoperative ischemic stroke and factors, including aneurysm morphology, immediate postoperative occlusion status, past medical history, comorbidities, age, and sex (Table 4). Older age (≥65 years), diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and a past medical history of ischemic stroke were significantly associated with postoperative ischemic stroke. In contrast, aneurysm morphology, rupture status, and immediate postoperative occlusion rate were not significantly associated.

VRD migration occurred in 4 of 738 cases (including 1 off-label) and was not linked to severe AEs or clinical symptoms. Ischemic stroke remained the most frequent posttreatment complication, occurring in 33 patients within 1 month post-procedure. Of these, 43.8% occurred at the VRD placement site, while 59.4% involved remote territories, including contralateral regions. Hemorrhagic stroke occurred in 11 patients on the day of the procedure and in 22 patients within the 1st month postoperatively.

Aneurysm regrowth was observed in 6 patients within the 1st year and in 11 patients by 3 years. One rupture occurred under on-label use. Retreatment was required in 1 case within the 1st year, 10 cases between years 1 and 2 (6 on-label cases), and 11–12 cases between years 2 and 3 (9 on-label). In total, 6 ruptures were reported during the 3-year follow-up period.

Antiplatelet regimens varied according to patient history and complications, with a trend toward multiple drug administration observed immediately before the procedure (Supplemental Table 1). Preoperatively, multidrug therapy was common in unruptured cerebral aneurysms. Among patients who developed ischemic stroke within 1 month post-procedure, the antiplatelet regimen at the time of treatment included dual therapy with aspirin + clopidogrel (22/388, 5.67%), aspirin + cilostazol (4/23, 17.39%), and clopidogrel + cilostazol (2/38, 5.26%); triple therapy with aspirin, clopidogrel, and cilostazol (4/159, 2.52%); and 1 case occurred under an alternative regimen.

In the postoperative period, triple therapy use decreased after 7 days, while dual therapy rates remained stable. Beyond 1 year, multidrug use declined and single-drug regimens increased. By 2 years, >30% of patients had discontinued antiplatelet therapy, and by 3 years, >40% had discontinued.

Discussion

In this post-marketing surveillance study in Japan, the Enterprise VRD (Cerenovus) demonstrated effectiveness and safety for wide-neck cerebral aneurysms, as shown by improvements in the primary effectiveness endpoints and the lack of new safety concerns. High rates of stent deployment and procedural success were also confirmed in the 30-day CRFs. The VRD appeared to act as a restraint, allowing the coil mass to spread within the aneurysm and preventing protrusion into the arterial trunk, thereby ensuring adequate coverage of residual spaces during embolization. The maintenance of coil mass percentage was high at 30-day CRFs and was sustained for several years.

The immediate complete or near-complete occlusion rate in our cohort was 72.7% (73.7% in on-label cases), which compares favorably with a systematic review and meta-analysis that reported 57.4%.2) At 1 year, our near-complete occlusion rate (75.1%) was consistent with the 74.5% reported in the same review.2) Long-term neurological outcomes were also favorable, with an mRS score of 0–2 rate in 95.8% of cases at 1-year CRFs. To exclude the confounding effect of subarachnoid hemorrhage, survival prognosis was assessed only in on-label cases, yielding a mortality rate of 0.49%, which was lower than previously reported.13–15)

No significant difference in the incidence of ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and VRD thrombosis was observed according to ruptured aneurysm status or maximum aneurysm diameter in all CRFs. However, proximal parent artery diameter was associated with stroke risk: arteries <2.5 mm showed a higher incidence of ischemic stroke within 30 days post-procedure. Overall, the safety profile of the VRD implant was comparable to that reported in prior reviews2) and studies of alternative stents, such as the low-profile visualized intraluminal support junior (LVIS Jr.; Terumo Neuro, Tustin, CA, USA; with 3% morbidity and 1% mortality),16) Leo Baby (Balt, Montmorency, France; with 3.8% morbidity and 0% mortality),17) and LVIS stent (Terumo Neuro; with 1.4% morbidity and 0% mortality).18) Although the LVIS Jr. stent achieved high occlusion rates, it was associated with increased stent thrombosis, stroke, and migration.19) Our results support the Enterprise VRD as a safe and effective option.

Modern aneurysm treatment approaches now include intrasaccular flow disruption, flow diversion, or stent-assisted coiling.20–24) While our study did not observe unfavorable outcomes, each approach offers unique advantages.20) The availability of multiple modalities allows tailoring to patient-specific anatomy, such as branching vessels and aneurysm neck angles. Future studies should further clarify optimal patient selection for each technique.

The Enterprise VRD’s design also contributes to its safety. Its self-expanding nickel–titanium alloy structure provides close wall apposition and coil support while mitigating the risk of vessel wall injury compared with balloon-expandable stents.

To date, no comprehensive study has evaluated over 700 unruptured wide-neck aneurysms treated with Enterprise stents. Although this study did not directly compare VRD outcomes with alternative devices such as flow diverters, intrasaccular devices, or PulseRider (Cerenovus), it offers important insights into real-world practice and may guide future comparative research.

Limitation

This study was conducted several years ago, which limits its novelty. Nevertheless, the Enterprise VRD remains clinically relevant, with applications extending to off-label use. Although the study was designed with strict inclusion criteria targeting 300 on-label cases, it also provided valuable insight into real-world usage patterns, where off-label use was frequent.

Additionally, aneurysm classification in this dataset was limited. Aneurysms were categorized only as saccular or fusiform at the time of data collection, without distinction between terminal (bifurcation) and side-wall types, precluding more detailed subgroup analyses.

Conclusion

The Enterprise VRD (Cerenovus) demonstrated robust effectiveness and safety in Japanese patients with wide-neck cerebral aneurysms. Importantly, the incidence of ischemic stroke was associated with parent artery diameter, suggesting that vessel caliber should be considered in risk assessment. Overall, VRD implantation represents a viable treatment option for unruptured, wide-neck cerebral aneurysms.

Multicenter experience of the Monopoint reperfusion system in acute large vessel occlusion stroke thrombectomy
Authors: Manisha Koneru, Jane Khalife, Marco Colasurdo, Ryan Priest, Matthew K McIntyre, Fabio Settecase, Joey English, Warren Kim, Blaise W Baxter, JaeHyun Kim, Mohamad Abdalkader, Piers Klein, Thanh N Nguyen, Matthew D Alexander, Oded Goren, Philipp Hendrix, Tyler Bielinski, Peter J Pema, Rami Z Morsi, Tareq Kass-Hout, Pratit Patel, Ajith J Thomas, Tudor G Jovin, Hamza A Shaikh, Daniel A Tonetti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe Monopoint reperfusion system (Monopoint; Route 92 Medical, San Mateo, California, USA) is a large bore (0.088 or 0.070 inch inner diameter) aspiration thrombectomy platform designed to minimize ledge effect and improve neurovascular navigation and embolectomy. We aimed to describe a multicenter, real world experience of the safety and performance of the Monopoint system in first line aspiration thrombectomy for large vessel occlusions (LVOs), outside of the recently completed SUMMIT MAX (A Randomized, Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Effectiveness of the Route 92 Medical Reperfusion System) clinical trial.MethodsAdults with acute anterior circulation LVO stroke between January 2019 and December 2024 consecutively treated with first line aspiration thrombectomy using the Monopoint at 10 centers were retrospectively reviewed. The primary outcome was first pass effect (FPE, modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) 2C/3 on first pass) and modified FPE (mFPE, mTICI 2B/2C/3 on first pass). The primary safety outcome was the rate of intraprocedural complications attributed to the Monopoint system.ResultsIn 193 included patients, median age was 67 years (IQR 67–78), and 46.6% (90/193) were women. Successful delivery of the aspiration catheter to the clot site occurred in 96.2% (185/193) of patients. FPE was achieved in 57.5% (111/193) and mFPE was achieved in 68.4% (132/193) of patients. Of 10 (5.2%) total complications, most were vasospasm treated with intra-arterial verapamil (8/193, 4.1%); major complications included one dissection (1/193, 0.5%) and one perforation (1/193, 0.5%).ConclusionThis multicenter study of the Monopoint reperfusion system for LVO thrombectomy outside of the SUMMIT MAX trial demonstrated a high FPE rate and a low rate of major complications.

Introduction

Innovations in neurovascular devices have improved the safety and efficacy of aspiration endovascular thrombectomy (EVT), which has become the standard of care for acute, anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke.1 First line aspiration EVT approaches involving the delivery of aspiration catheters (ACs) of progressively increasing inner diameter have demonstrated favorable procedural efficacy and thus have been increasingly used.24 Traditional EVT techniques use intermediate and microcatheters introduced through a guide catheter over a microwire to advance the AC to the target site.5 However, the 'ledge effect' between large bore ACs and traditional microcatheters may impede intravascular navigation, particularly when attempting to advance constructs through tortuous vasculature or beyond arterial branch origins.6

The Monopoint reperfusion system (Monopoint; Route 92 Medical, San Mateo, California, USA) is a novel neurovascular access platform synergistically designed to efficiently deliver large bore ACs intracranially, while maximizing aspiration force through a single point of aspiration,7 consisting of the Base Camp Sheath, Tenzing delivery catheter, and HiPoint 88 and 70 large bore ACs. The Monopoint system minimizes ledge effect between devices, thus potentially improving navigation across difficult vasculature.8 Moreover, this system does not generally require transgressing the clot with a device, unlike other EVT techniques necessitating partial or full clot crossing with either a microcatheter or microwire, potentially reducing the likelihood of clot disruption, fragmentation, and subsequent distal embolization.911

The Monopoint system was evaluated in the recently completed SUMMIT MAX (NCT05018650; A Randomized, Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Effectiveness of the Route 92 Medical Reperfusion System) randomized clinical trial which compared the safety and efficacy of the Monopoint system with a predicate aspiration system.7 In this multicenter study, we aimed to further characterize the safety and performance of the Monopoint reperfusion system for first line aspiration EVT to treat patients with acute LVO stroke outside of the SUMMIT MAX trial.

Methods

De-identified data may be made available on reasonable request to the corresponding author. This retrospective study was conducted with institutional review board approval at all participating sites, with waiver of informed consent. Analysis was reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.

Patient cohort

Prospectively collected institutional registry data of adult patients with acute stroke were retrospectively reviewed between January 2019 and December 2024 across 10 health systems in the US. Consecutive patients were included if they underwent EVT for an acute, anterior circulation intracranial LVO (defined as occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA), M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery, or proximal M2 segment of the middle cerebral artery) using the Monopoint system as the first line aspiration technique. Patients were treated by fellowship trained neurointerventionalists with at least 2 years of experience. Patients with any pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS), any admission National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), and presenting within any timeframe between symptomatic stroke onset and puncture were included to capture heterogeneity in the EVT treated stroke population, including patients who may have not met criteria for the SUMMIT MAX trial. Patients were excluded if the Monopoint system was not used on the first EVT pass, or if they were enrolled in SUMMIT MAX.

Monopoint thrombectomy technique

The Monopoint reperfusion system consists of the 8 F BaseCamp Sheath, the Tenzing delivery catheter, and the HiPoint catheter extender. The HiPoint is a catheter extender with a catheter shaft distally, connected to a pushing wire proximally (figure 1A–C). Two HiPoint and Tenzing sizes are available: the Tenzing 7 delivery catheter is paired with the HiPoint 70 catheter extender (distal inner diameter 0.070 inches), while the Tenzing 8 delivery catheter is paired with the HiPoint 88 aspiration catheter (distal inner diameter 0.088 inches)12 (figure 1A–B). When the HiPoint 88 (±inner HiPoint 70) is telescoped into BaseCamp, a single aspiration lumen is created, with aspiration on the BaseCamp being transmitted to the distal tip of the HiPoint catheter(s) (figure 1C). Furthermore, each catheter is controlled from a single point outside the patient’s body using their corresponding pusher wires.13

After obtaining vascular access with the BaseCamp Sheath navigated over its paired Nav Cath inner catheter into the ICA bulb, the HiPoint 88 aspiration catheter or HiPoint 70 aspiration catheter is coaxially introduced over the Tenzing 8 or Tenzing 7 delivery catheter. A 0.016 inch or smaller diameter guide microwire may be introduced through the Tenzing delivery catheter if needed, such as to aid navigation to prevent selection of non-target arterial branch origin variants (eg, ophthalmic artery or posterior communicating artery).14 15 As the Tenzing/HiPoint construct is advanced through the cerebral vasculature, the Tenzing tip is positioned and pinned distal to the HiPoint aspiration catheter. At the occlusion site, the distal radiopaque marker of the Tenzing may be advanced just beyond the angiographic limit of contrast without needing to cross the occlusion (figure 2).16 17 The HiPoint aspiration catheter is then advanced over the Tenzing delivery catheter to the angiographic limit of contrast, and the Tenzing is withdrawn. Withdrawal of the Tenzing usually causes the HiPoint aspiration catheter to advance passively toward and often over the face of the clot due to the vacuum created by removal of the Tenzing.9 18 Vacuum aspiration is applied through the Tuohy–Borst port attached to the BaseCamp. If the operator is unable to advance a HiPoint 88 to the occlusion, the HiPoint 88 can also be used as a guide catheter extender. In this setting, after Tenzing 8 removal, a HiPoint 70, loaded with Tenzing 7, is loaded into the BaseCamp and HiPoint 88 (figure 1C). The HiPoint 70 aspiration catheter is then advanced over the Tenzing 7 delivery catheter to the angiographic limit of contrast, and the Tenzing 7 is withdrawn. Vacuum aspiration is applied through the side port of the Tuohy–Borst attached to the BaseCamp.

Anteroposterior fluoroscopy during M1 thrombectomy shows the proximal and distal radiopaque markers of the Tenzing delivery catheter approaching the angiographic limit of contrast at the site of the M1 thrombus.

Based on assessment of angiographic reperfusion at the occlusion site, additional passes were performed, either with the Monopoint system or with different EVT devices. Termination of the procedure was at the discretion of the treating physician.

Data collection

Demographic data, including age, sex, race, comorbidities, previous antiplatelet or anticoagulant use, tobacco use, clinical characteristics of the ischemic stroke event, pre-stroke mRS, admission NIHSS, laterality, occlusion site, time from last known well to puncture time, Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) from non-contrast CT, and thrombolytic administration were collected. These baseline variables were used to determine whether included patients would have been eligible for SUMMIT MAX based on corresponding parameters specified in the trial’s inclusion criteria. Key SUMMIT MAX inclusion criteria were: (1) last known well to puncture time <8 hour; (2) admission NIHSS ≥6; (3) pre-stroke mRS ≤2; and (4) ICA or M1 occlusion.

Procedural characteristics were collected, including access site, anesthesia type, base catheter position, size of Tenzing/HiPoint construct used, number of passes, successful delivery of HiPoint with Tenzing, rate of Tenzing crossing the thrombus, rate of technically successful reperfusion (modified Treatment in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) 2B/2C/3), and puncture to reperfusion time. The use of additional devices was also collated, such as using a guiding microwire with Tenzing or needing to convert to another system. Post-EVT clinical data included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (defined as Heidelberg Bleeding Class 2 or higher) within 48 hours, discharge NIHSS, discharge and 90 day mRS scores, and all cause mortality by 90 day follow-up.19 20

Outcomes and statistical analysis

The primary outcomes were the first pass effect (FPE) with the Monopoint reperfusion system, defined as achieving mTICI 2C/3 reperfusion on the first aspiration pass, and modified FPE (mFPE), defined as mTICI 2B/2C/3 reperfusion on the first aspiration pass.21 The primary safety outcome was the rate of intraprocedural complications possibly attributable to the Monopoint reperfusion system, including vasospasm, dissection, vessel perforation, and access site complications. Continuous variables were summarized as median (IQR) if non-normal and average with SD if normally distributed. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies. Missing data were minimal and not imputed. All statistical analyses were performed using JMP V.18.0 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA).

Results

A total of 193 patients were included in this study. Median age was 67 years (IQR 60–78), and 46.6% of patients were women (90/193, table 1). The most common comorbidity was hypertension (65.8%; 127/193, table 1). Median admission NIHSS was 18 (IQR 13–21) (table 2). Most occlusions were in the M1 segment (71.0%; 137/193, table 2). General anesthesia was used in 142 (73.6%) patients. A total of 86 (44.6%) patients would have been ineligible for SUMMIT MAX. Among these ineligible patients, the most common reason for trial ineligibility was presenting in the late time window (68.6%; 59/86), or having an unknown time of last known well (16.3%; 14/86, table 2). It is possible that patients who otherwise met the SUMMIT MAX trial criteria were not included in the trial due to lack (or denial) of consent, or unavailability of the clinical research staff (eg, after hours); these data were not retrospectively available for the present analysis.

Procedural characteristics and performance

Primary aspiration through the Tenzing 8 and HiPoint 88 combination was used in 179/193 (92.7%) patients. Successful HiPoint delivery to the target occlusion was achieved in 96.2% (185/193). A leading microwire was used in 42.5% of cases (82/193, table 3). FPE (2C/3) was achieved in 57.5% (111/193), and mFPE (2B/2C/3) was achieved in 68.4% (132/193, table 4) of patients. FPE was similar across trial eligible and trial ineligible subgroups (eligible: 65/107, 60.7% vs ineligible: 46/86, 53.5%); mFPE was also similar across trial eligible and trial ineligible subgroups (eligible: 74/107, 69.2% vs ineligible: 58/86, 67.4%).

The median number of passes needed for reperfusion was 1 (IQR 1–3) and 57.0% (110/193) of patients underwent single pass EVT. Final successful (mTICI 2B/2C/3) reperfusion was achieved in 97.4% (188/193, table 4) of patients. Final excellent (mTICI 2C/3) reperfusion was achieved in 78.2% (151/193, table 4) of patients. Adjunctive devices or catheters were used after the initial Monopoint pass(es) for further embolectomy in 27.5% (53/193) of cases (table 3).

Complications

A total of 10 (5.2%) complications occurred during EVT attributable to the Monopoint system; the most common complication was vasospasm treated successfully with intra-arterial verapamil (8; 80% of complications; 4.1% of all patients). One dissection was attributed to the system (1; 0.5%). One vessel perforation (1; 0.5%) in a middle cerebral artery branch was treated using Onyx liquid embolic agent (Medtronic, Minneapolis, Minnesota, USA) with resolution of the extravasation. Access site complications occurred in 1.6% (3/193), inclusive of: a femoral artery occlusion requiring thrombectomy, a femoral artery injury requiring surgical repair, and a small groin hematoma that resolved without further intervention (table 4).

Clinical outcomes

Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage of any type occurred in 3.1% (6/193) of patients; symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage occurred in 0.7% (1/139) of cases that exclusively used the Monopoint system for thrombectomy. Median discharge NIHSS was 6 (IQR 2–12). Median improvement in NIHSS by discharge was 10 (IQR 5–15), and median per cent improvement in NIHSS by discharge was 68.5% (IQR 28.9–85.7%) (table 5). Good functional recovery (90 day mRS 0–2) was achieved in 44.1%; median 90 day mRS was 3 (IQR 1–5) (table 5).

Discussion

This study reports the largest multicenter experience with the Monopoint reperfusion system for first line aspiration EVT in acute anterior circulation LVOs treated outside of the SUMMIT MAX trial. The Monopoint system had high rates of successful HiPoint delivery (96.2%), FPE (57.5%), and mFPE (68.4%). Additionally, the rate of intraprocedural complications with the Monopoint system was low (5.2%), with most complications being vasospasm, resolved with intra-arterial verapamil administration (8/10; 80% of complications). Expanding on the initial 33 patient experience with the Monopoint system, our real world analysis demonstrated favorable safety and performance profiles for the Monopoint/Tenzing reperfusion system.18

The rates of FPE and mFPE with the Monopoint system were higher than reported rates for 6 F aspiration devices, traditionally referred to as large bore.22 23 Recent analyses in large patient cohorts for other EVT platforms for first pass aspiration in LVOs generally reported rates between 20% and 50% in FPE and between 40% and 70% for mFPE.921 2429 In comparison, FPE and mFPE rates for the Monopoint system reported in this study indicated positive performance metrics within the context of the current landscape of available EVT devices and techniques. Consequently, the data presented here suggest that in a real world patient cohort, the Monopoint system has promising potential as a first line aspiration thrombectomy system.

The sites included in this analysis were sites who were early adopters of the Monopoint reperfusion system, and the data collected here includes the initial clinical experience at each site. Sites were encouraged to submit their consecutive series of patients for analysis. As such, any learning curve associated with the adoption of the Monopoint system, which differs from many commercially available aspiration platforms, is likely to be reflected in the data presented here.

This case series was inclusive of a mix of patients who may not have been eligible for the SUMMIT MAX trial, or who declined enrollment.7 Approximately one-third of patients in this study presented in time windows exceeding the threshold permissible within SUMMIT MAX. Hence this study captured data for Monopoint performance in an expanded, more heterogeneous stroke population, inclusive of the crucial subpopulation of patients who may benefit from EVT presenting in the late time window.30 Therefore, this data set likely contains a greater cohort of patients who were slow progressors than the SUMMIT MAX trial.31 Consequently, Monopoint performance metrics, as characterized in this analysis, may be generalizable to an expanded range of patients with acute stroke.

Limitations

There are limitations to acknowledge with this study. The study was limited by factors inherent to its retrospective design, including confounding, selection bias, and study size. Future studies in large, heterogeneous stroke populations in a diverse range of practice settings are required to validate the generalizability of these results. Most of the sites included in this cohort were early adopters of the Monopoint system; additional real world data, particularly from centers that were not early adopters of the Monopoint system, are needed to further externally validate this study’s findings. Sites were encouraged to submit consecutive cases, however, by the retrospective nature, this cannot be verified. Reperfusion and safety outcomes were not adjudicated by an unblinded operator and consequently may be subject to observer bias; future studies with blinded, core laboratory adjudication of reperfusion success are needed to validate these results. Future prospective studies, including the results of the SUMMIT MAX trial, are needed to continue to evaluate the performance of the Monopoint system, particularly in additional stroke subpopulations with indications for EVT.

Conclusions

Using real world, multi-institutional data, this study characterized the safety and performance of the Monopoint reperfusion system for first line aspiration EVT for acute anterior circulation LVOs. High rates of FPE and mFPE (57.5% and 68.4%, respectively), and relatively low rates of Monopoint related complications suggest favorable safety and technical performance.

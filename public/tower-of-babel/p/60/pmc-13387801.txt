Clinical Characteristics of Early-Onset Severe Hemolytic Disease of the Fetus and Newborn and Treatment Efficacy of Serial Intrauterine Transfusions: A 10-Year Single-Center Experience in China
Authors: Quanrui Liu, Jingya Zhao, Jingyu Liu, Danlun Li, Zhiming He, Yanmin Luo, Yi Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:To summarize the clinical characteristics of early-onset severe hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN) in a Chinese cohort and identify factors influencing intrauterine transfusion (IUT) frequency and pregnancy outcomes.Methods:This retrospective cohort study included cases managed between 2013 and 2022. Factors affecting IUT efficacy were analyzed with regard to subsequent IUT risk and adverse pregnancy outcomes. Survival analysis for recurrent events was performed to assess subsequent IUT risk.Results:Of the 97 cases included, 38 were identified as early-onset severe HDFN. Compared with non-early-onset severe HDFN, early-onset severe HDFN was characterized by higher anti-D titer (1:1024 vs. 1:512, respectively, P = 0.006), multiple alloantibodies (50.00% vs. 20.34%, respectively, P = 0.002), and an earlier gestational age at initial IUT (22.4 ± 3.2 vs. 29.0 ± 3.5 weeks, respectively, P < 0.001). In addition, early-onset severe HDFN correlated with increased IUT frequency (hazard ratio (HR): 2.01, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.26–3.20, P = 0.003). Factors prolonging IUT intervals included transfused volume (HR: 0.99, 95% CI: 0.97–0.99; P < 0.001), hematocrit after IUT (HR: 0.96, 95% CI: 0.95–0.98; P < 0.001), and immunotherapy (HR: 0.59, 95% CI: 0.38–0.93; P = 0.024).Conclusion:The characteristics of early-onset severe HDFN in Chinese patients are different from those observed in western cohorts. These patients frequently present with higher anti-D titers and a more complex spectrum of multiple alloantibodies. The occurrence of early-onset severe HDFN is associated with an increased frequency of IUT and lower live birth rates. Therefore, exploring effective interventions to delay the first IUT is critical to optimizing the timing and efficacy of intrauterine management for these high-risk cases.

Introduction

Hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN) is an immune-mediated disorder arising from blood group incompatibility between the mother and fetus. Maternal antibodies cross the placenta and target fetal red blood cells expressing the corresponding antigens, resulting in hemolysis and fetal anemia.1 HDFN affects approximately 1/300 to 1/600 live births in Caucasian populations.2345 Untreated progressive HDFN can lead to fetal anemia, fetal hydrops, and intrauterine death (IUD). Furthermore, it can cause neonatal complications such as anemia, cerebral palsy, and death.5,6

Early-onset severe HDFN represents a critical clinical phenotype, characterized by severe anemia occurring early in gestation (frequently before 20 weeks), which is strongly associated with adverse fetal and neonatal prognosis.789 Thus far, most studies on severe HDFN and early-onset severe HDFN have been carried out in developed countries (mainly Caucasian populations),1011121314 and data concerning the Chinese population remain limited. Although recent studies have described the general clinical characteristics of HDFN in the Chinese population,15 there is a notable paucity of data specifically focusing on early-onset severe cases.

Intrauterine transfusion (IUT), first introduced by Liley in 1963, has evolved into the cornerstone of prenatal management for severe HDFN and is effective for cases of nearly any etiology.16,17 However, severe cases often require serial IUTs, increasing the demand for blood products. This presents a unique challenge in China due to the relative scarcity of Rh-negative blood resources. The gestational week at initial IUT, pre- and post-IUT hemoglobin (Hb) levels, and higher transfusion volume have been associated with IUT frequency.18 Nevertheless, adverse pregnancy outcomes (e.g., IUD) may occur after IUT. The gestational age (GA) at initial IUT, the interval between the first IUT and the gestational week at delivery, fetal Hb levels before the first IUT, and fetal hydrops are associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes.19 At present, there is a gap in our understanding regarding the potential association of other factors with transfusion frequency and adverse pregnancy outcomes after transfusion, as well as factors that exert a protective effect.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to summarize the clinical characteristics of early-onset severe HDFN in a Chinese cohort, analyze factors influencing IUT frequency and pregnancy outcomes, and explore the efficacy of serial IUT combined with immunotherapy for the treatment of early-onset severe HDFN.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective, single-center cohort study was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Guangzhou, China). As a major tertiary referral center for fetal anemia and IUT, our institution serves patients from Guangdong and surrounding provinces. We systematically reviewed the medical records of all pregnant women who underwent antibody screening at our center between January 2013 and December 2022. Of the 1990 women screened, alloantibodies were detected in 822 patients. The subsequent selection flow for these 822 patients is detailed in Fig. 1.

Flow chart showing derivation of study participants. HDFN: Hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn; IUT: Intrauterine blood transfusion; MCA-PSV: Middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity.

Participants

The inclusion criteria were: (1) pregnant women with alloantibodies confirmed by serological screening; (2) fetuses or neonates diagnosed with HDFN based on laboratory evidence of hemolysis (e.g., anemia, hyperbilirubinemia, positive Coombs test); and (3) delivery at our center between January 2013 and December 2022. Exclusion criteria were: (1) incomplete medical records or missing key outcome data; (2) fetal chromosomal abnormalities or major structural malformations; and (3) fetal anemia caused by other etiologies (e.g., parvovirus B19 infection, thalassemia).

Pregnant women were screened for alloantibodies based on the following characteristics: (1) Rh-negative blood type; (2) history of blood transfusion; (3) previous pregnancy with fetal anemia, fetal hydrops, and IUD; and (4) ultrasonographic sign of fetal anemia and hydrops in the current pregnancy. Fetal blood sampling was performed if the middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity ≥ 1.5 multiple of the median, fetal hydrops, the titer of maternal alloantibodies was markedly increased (i.e., more than fourfold increase) in a short period of time. IUT was performed if the fetal Hb levels were more than two standard deviations below the mean or the hematocrit (HCT) levels were < 0.3.20 Over 95% of IUT procedures were performed via the free segment of the umbilical vein at our center.

Severe HDFN was diagnosed if the fetus was affected by maternal–fetal alloimmunization and the fetal blood count showed fetal HCT levels < 0.3.212223 The diagnosis of early-onset severe HDFN was based on the following criteria: an increase in the titer of alloantibodies (anti-D > 1:128, etc.) during the current pregnancy affected by maternal–fetal alloimmunization and previous pregnancies with one of the following: (1) fetal anemia and fetal hydrops with middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity ≥ 1.5 multiple of the median which presented at 24 weeks of gestation or earlier; and (2) IUD and neonatal death (NND) associated with alloimmunization.12,14,24 Based on literature,252627 for early-onset severe HDFN, plasmapheresis was performed (before 20 weeks of gestation) and oral prednisone was administered (10 mg, twice daily, initiated from 25 weeks of gestation) after obtaining consent from the patient. The two treatments were defined as immunotherapy in this study. In addition, IUT was performed following the detection of fetal anemia.

Data collection

We collected data from the medical records of the fetal medicine center. General maternal information included age, method of conception, transfusion history, type of alloantibody, and antibody titer before any treatment. Multiple alloantibody positivity was defined as the detection of two or more alloantibodies. Obstetric history included gravidity, parity, and history of HDFN (defined as previous pregnancy with fetal loss, fetal hydrops, IUT history, NND, neonatal anemia, and neonatal transfusion related to maternal–fetal alloimmunization).18 Ultrasound manifestations included the presence of fetal hydrops and whether fetal hydrops was relieved after IUT. Transfusion information included the number of IUTs, details of each IUT (GA, pre- and post-procedural fetal Hb and HCT levels, transfusion volume, and the interval between blood collection and use). Adverse pregnancy outcomes included fetal loss (miscarriage, abortion, IUD), preterm birth before 34 weeks of gestation, and NND.18,19 Neonatal data collected included GA, birth weight, sex, mode of delivery, Apgar scores, the need of transfusion or phototherapy or intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), number of days in hospital, and neonatal complications (iron overload, ABO forward-reverse typing discrepancies, thrombocytopenia, necrotizing enterocolitis, etc.).

The primary outcome was the interval time between consecutive IUT procedures. The secondary outcomes included: (1) clinical parameters (GA at the first IUT, GA at delivery, and the total number of IUT procedures per patient); (2) composite adverse pregnancy outcomes (defined as the occurrence of fetal loss (intrauterine fetal death), NND, or preterm birth before 34 weeks of gestation); and (3) neonatal morbidities, including thrombocytopenia, necrotizing enterocolitis, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, and retinopathy of prematurity.

Statistical analysis

Due to the rarity of early-onset severe HDFN, no a priori sample size calculation was performed. To maximize statistical power, we included all eligible cases available at our center over a 10-year period (2013–2022). Descriptive statistics were used to characterize the cohort, followed by univariate and bivariate analyses to compare group differences and assess associations between variables. For quantitative data, normally distributed variables are reported as the mean ± standard deviation. For those with skewed distribution, data are presented as the median and interquartile range.

Categorical variables are presented as counts and proportions. Given the distribution, group comparisons were performed using appropriate statistical tests. Means of normally distributed continuous variables were compared between two groups using independent sample t-tests. For categorical variables, chi-squared tests were used to assess associations. For non-normally distributed data, the Kruskal–Wallis test was used. Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables with small sample sizes (< 5). Statistical significance was determined at P-values < 0.05.

Analysis of factors associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes

Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify factors associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes. Candidate variables included maternal age, gravidity, parity, mode of conception, type of alloantibody, early-onset severe HDFN, presence of fetal hydrops, history of HDFN, fetal sex, GA at initial IUT, fetal Hb levels before first IUT, volume of first IUT, number of IUTs, total transfused volume, and time from first IUT to delivery. Odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used to estimate effects.

Analysis of factors influencing intervals between IUTs

A number of potential factors influencing the intervals between IUTs were selected for analysis. These factors included maternal age, gravidity, history of HDFN, early-onset severe HDFN, type of alloantibody, fetal hydrops, GA at first IUT, transfused volume of IUT, fetal HCT levels after IUT, interval between blood collection to use, combined immunotherapy, and fetal sex. The variance inflation factor was used to assess the presence of multicollinearity between candidate variables. Variables were considered free of multicollinearity if their variance inflation factor was < 4. Given the dependent nature of IUT events and the variable risk of subsequent IUTs, the Prentice–Williams–Peterson gap time model was employed to analyze the effect of covariates on the instantaneous hazard rate.28 This model specifically accounted for the intra-subject correlations by stratifying analysis according to the order of IUT events, thereby allowing for the estimation of transition-specific effects across repeated procedures. Repeated measures survival analysis was used to identify the factors influencing the intervals between IUTs. The analysis was initiated at the time of initial IUT and completed at delivery or IUD. Kaplan–Meier curves were plotted for binary variables with statistically significant effects, and the log-rank test was used to calculate P-values.

Statistical analyses were conducted using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and Stata Statistical Software Version 17.0 (STATA, College Station, TX, USA).

Ethical approval

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Approval No. 2024-084), and the research was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the responsible committee on human experimentation and with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964 and its later amendments. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for written informed consent was waived by the committee. Intrauterine transfusion and immunotherapy were administered as part of the clinical treatment protocol, with all necessary clinical consents duly obtained. The retrospective collection of data in this case is considered to be exempt from further informed consent requirements, as all data presented have been meticulously anonymized to protect the privacy of the patients involved.

Results

Baseline characteristics of severe HDFN and early-onset severe HDFN

Among 822 pregnant women with detected alloantibodies, 97 diagnosed with severe HDFN were enrolled in this study. These patients underwent a cumulative total of 339 IUT procedures (Fig. 1). The mean maternal age was 31.34 ± 4.26 years. Of those cases, 78.35% (76/97) and 16.49% (16/97) had a history of HDFN and blood transfusion, respectively. The types of alloantibodies included Rh alloimmunization (89.69% (87/97)), MNs alloimmunization (5.15% (5/97)), anti-CD36 alloimmunization (3.09% (3/97)), and ABO alloimmunization (2.06% (2/97)). Patients with severe HDFN were characterized by high titers of anti-D alloantibodies (≥ 1:256: 50.52% (49/97)) and multiple alloantibodies (31.96% (31/97)). The mean GA at initial IUT was 26.3 ± 4.6 weeks. The median number of IUTs was three (interquartile range (IQR): 2–5, with 67.01% (65/97) of cases required three or more IUTs. The rate of fetal live birth was 90.72% (88/97) and the majority of fetuses were delivered at 34+0–36+6 gestational weeks (67.05% (59/88)). The median birth weight was 2.65 kg (IQR: 2.40–2.92) (Table 1).

Compared with non-early-onset severe HDFN, early-onset severe HDFN was associated with significantly higher anti-D titers (1:1024 vs. 1:512, P = 0.006), multiple alloantibodies (50.00% (19/38) vs. 20.34% (12/59), P = 0.002), increased incidence of fetal hydrops (31.58% (12/38) vs. 10.17% (6/59), P = 0.008), and adverse pregnancy outcomes (13.56% (8/59) vs. 42.11% (16/38), P = 0.001). The alloantibody types of early-onset severe HDFN were Rh alloimmunization (89.47% (34/38)), MNs alloimmunization (7.89% (3/38)), and anti-CD36 alloimmunization (2.63% (1/38)), while no cases of ABO alloimmunization were identified in this group. With regard to treatment, cases of early-onset severe HDFN exhibited several distinct features compared with those with non-early-onset severe HDFN. These included earlier GA at initial IUT (22.4 ± 3.2 vs. 29.0 ± 3.5, P < 0.001) and high frequency of IUT (4.5 (3.0–6.0) vs. 3.0 (2.0–4.0), P < 0.001). Initial IUT was required before 20 weeks in 23.68% (9/38) of early-onset severe HDFN cases, with the earliest performed at 16+2 weeks. Moreover, early-onset severe HDFN was linked to lower live birth rate (78.95% (30/38) vs. 98.31% (58/59), P = 0.001) and neonatal birth weight (2.48 ± 0.47 vs. 2.67 ± 0.38, P = 0.039) after IUT, as detailed in Table 1.

Neonatal complications were frequently observed in this cohort, primarily reflecting the severe clinical course of HDFN, including anemia, ABO forward-reverse blood typing discrepancies, iron overload, thrombocytopenia, and others. Notably, no cases of bronchopulmonary dysplasia or retinopathy of prematurity were observed in either group. The median number of days in hospital was 14 (IQR: 11–18). Furthermore, 100% (88/88) of neonates required phototherapy and IVIG, 95.45% (84/88) required additional blood transfusion, and 17.05% (15/88) required blood exchange therapy. Neonates in the early-onset severe HDFN group were more likely to have ABO forward-reverse blood typing discrepancies (43.33% (13/30) vs. 18.97% (11/58), P = 0.010) and thrombocytopenia (26.67% (8/30) vs. 8.62% (5/58), P = 0.020) than those in the non-early-onset severe HDFN group. Additionally, newborns with early-onset severe HDFN spent more days in hospital (15.00 (IQR: 11.00–23.00) vs. 13.50 (IQR: 10.00–15.25), P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A127).

Analysis of factors associated with the interval between serial IUTs and adverse pregnancy outcomes

A total of 339 IUTs were performed in 97 patients with severe HDFN. Of note, 17 patients who underwent only a single IUT were excluded. Consequently, 80 patients (comprising 322 IUTs) were included in the survival analysis. Covariance diagnostics were performed on the variables included in the analysis and the results are detailed in Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A127. Table 2 displays the results of the adjusted multivariate Prentice–Williams–Peterson gap time model for intensity risk survival analysis. Early-onset severe HDFN (hazard ratio (HR): 2.01, 95% CI: 1.26–3.20; P = 0.003) and fetal hydrops (HR: 1.51, 95% CI: 1.01–2.27; P = 0.045) were significantly associated with increased IUT frequency. Late GA at initial IUT (HR: 0.67, 95% CI: 0.62–0.72; P < 0.001), large transfusion volume (HR: 0.99, 95% CI: 0.97–0.99; P < 0.001), high fetal HCT after IUT (HR: 0.96, 95% CI: 0.95–0.98; P < 0.001), and combination treatment (HR: 0.59, 95% CI: 0.38–0.93; P = 0.024) were associated with longer IUT intervals. Maternal age, gravidity, Rh alloimmunization, fetal sex, and history of HDFN were not significantly associated with the interval between IUTs. Kaplan–Meier curves were plotted for statistically significant dichotomous variables. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 1 and Fig. 2, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A127, early-onset severe HDFN and fetal hydrops groups were significantly associated with shorter IUT intervals (HR: 2.01, 95% CI: 1.26–3.20; P = 0.003; and HR: 1.51, 95% CI: 1.01–2.27, P = 0.045, respectively). Supplementary Fig. 3, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A127 illustrates that combination treatment was effective in prolonging the IUT interval (HR: 0.59, 95% CI: 0.38–0.93; P = 0.024).

Analysis of treatment efficacy for early-onset severe HDFN

The above results indicated that earlier GA at initial IUT was associated with increased IUT frequency and adverse pregnancy outcome. Therefore, we proceeded to investigate the efficacy of combination immunotherapy for treating early-onset severe HDFN. In 38 cases of early-onset severe HDFN, the efficacy of IUT combined with immunotherapy (n = 13) was compared with that of IUT only (n = 25). The differences in clinical characteristics and treatment outcomes between the two groups are shown in Table 4. The IUT combined with immunotherapy group of pregnant women exhibited higher antibody titers at the time before any treatment compared with the IUT-only group (1:5120 vs. 1:1024, P = 0.040). Despite this higher initial disease burden, the combination of immunotherapy and IUT was associated with a numerical delay of approximately 7 days in the timing of the first IUT. Furthermore, the combination group demonstrated several favorable clinical trends, including higher fetal HCT levels prior to the first IUT, lower initial and cumulative transfusion volumes, a reduced incidence of hydrops fetalis, and an increased live birth rate. However, likely due to the limited sample size, these observed differences did not reach statistical significance.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study represents the most comprehensive analysis to date specifically focusing on the clinical characteristics and management outcomes of early-onset severe HDFN in a Chinese population. Considering that most existing literature originates from studies involving western cohorts, our results highlight distinct population-specific differences. Although this was a single-center retrospective study, our center is one of the largest fetal centers in China, managing patients from multiple provinces. This enhances the representativeness of our findings.

The present investigation revealed the high incidence of early-onset severe HDFN among patients with severe HDFN and its characteristic clinical presentation, including high anti-D titer, multiple alloantibodies, early GA at initial IUT, and high incidence of adverse pregnancy outcomes. These observations align with established literature regarding the nature of early-onset severe HDFN.12,14,29 Notably, this study also demonstrated that early-onset severe HDFN was associated with shorter intervals between IUTs. In addition, larger transfusion volume, higher fetal HCT levels after IUT and combination treatment were associated with decreased IUT frequency. Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified that earlier GA at the initial IUT was the sole independent predictor of adverse pregnancy outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of meticulous prenatal monitoring and the implementation of strategies to prolong the pregnancy to a more advanced gestational age before the first intervention, thereby optimizing the timing and clinical efficacy of the procedure.

A distinctive finding of our research is the divergent alloantibody spectrum in Chinese patients compared with western cohorts. Prospective studies from the Netherlands have identified anti-K, anti-c, and anti-E as the primary pathogenic alloantibodies alongside anti-D.4 In contrast, anti-K was entirely absent in our study. The alloantibodies causing severe HDFN in our cohort were predominantly Rh (anti-D, -c, -E), MNs, and ABO alloantibodies, as well as a specific platelet alloantibody (anti-CD36). The latter has been reported almost exclusively in Asian populations.303132 Furthermore, the prevalence of multiple alloantibodies in our study was 31.96%, significantly higher than that previously reported in the literature.4,33 We also observed a marked disparity in anti-D titers. While previous studies reported titers > 1:512 in 19.6% of cases (with a maximum of 1:2048),19 25.77% of our patients exceeded this threshold, with titers reaching as high as 1:8192. The discrepancy may stem from a lack of awareness of such diseases among Chinese clinicians and the difficulty in obtaining anti-D immunoglobulin. Ultimately, early-onset severe HDFN, primarily driven by Rh isoimmunization,12,24,34 was characterized by early GA at initial IUT, lower fetal HB levels before initial IUT, low volume of initial IUT, and a large number of IUTs compared with non-early-onset severe HDFN. The significantly lower live birth rate in the former group (78.95% (30/38) vs. 98.31% (58/59), P = 0.001) emphasizes the urgent need for more effective management strategies for these high-risk patients.

In our study, a significantly higher incidence of thrombocytopenia was observed in the early-onset severe HDFN group compared with the non-early-onset severe HDFN group. This finding aligns with the pathophysiology of severe HDFN, and the underlying mechanism is likely multifactorial. First, the compensatory acceleration of erythropoiesis in anemic fetuses suppresses bone marrow megakaryopoiesis, a phenomenon known as the “crowding out” effect.31 Second, repeated high-volume IUTs can induce a dilutional effect, further reducing platelet counts.34,35 Although the difference was statistically significant, the thrombocytopenia in our cohort was generally transient and effectively managed with supportive care, resulting in no adverse long-term sequelae.

Snelgrove et al.18 used survival analysis to investigate factors influencing transfusion interval. They found that fetal hydrops was associated with shorter IUT intervals, while higher pre- and post-IUT fetal Hb levels were linked to a reduced number of IUTs.18 Our evidence revealed that early-onset severe HDFN, fetal hydrops, and earlier GA at first IUT were associated with a short interval between IUTs. Factors associated with long IUT intervals were larger transfusion volume, higher fetal HCT levels after IUT, and combination treatment. As fetal anemia progresses, anemia cardiac decompensation occurs and fetal hydrops develops. These fetuses have difficulty tolerating the acute increase in afterload during IUT. Therefore, strict control over the volume of IUT is necessary, which also increases the likelihood of requiring repeat IUT and shortens the intervals between IUTs.18 Early-onset severe HDFN and early gestational week at first IUT indicate that fetal anemia is severe and progresses rapidly; consequently, the likelihood of repeat IUT is high. Longer transfusion intervals with immunotherapy may be related to the fact that immunotherapy reduces the production of maternal alloantibodies and inhibits the hemolysis process. This study indicated that GA at initial IUT was the only risk factor associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes. In contrast, the time between initial IUT and delivery, fetal Hb levels before initial IUT, and fetal hydrops were not associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes. These findings were inconsistent with those reported in previous literature.18 This discrepancy may be attributed to the high volume of procedures and the implementation of standardized management protocols at our specialized tertiary referral center. Consistent clinical expertise and refined procedural techniques in high-volume settings have been shown to mitigate the impact of known risk factors, such as fetal hydrops, thereby potentially explaining the differences between our findings and those of multicenter or lower-volume studies. Since this study was conducted in a single center, the impact of differences in the procedures and experience of the operators were minimal. Limited by the number of cases, this study only compared the influence of etiology on transfusion intervals between Rh alloimmunization and non-Rh alloimmunization. We anticipate an increase in case numbers in future prospective studies to clarify the impact of different etiologies on transfusion intervals.

Patients with early-onset severe HDFN are expected to have early onset of fetal anemia (even before 20 weeks of gestation). During this early period, IUT is technically challenging and carries a significantly higher risk of fetal loss compared to procedures performed after 20 weeks.8 Consequently, there is a critical need for alternative or adjunctive strategies to delay the onset of severe fetal anemia and postpone initial IUT. Recent studies have explored various immunotherapeutic approaches (e.g., nipocalimab, IVIG) to achieve this goal.7121424252631 In this context, our study investigates the clinical efficacy of conventional immunotherapies—specifically, the combination of plasmapheresis and oral prednisone—in delaying the need for the first IUT. Plasmapheresis works by removing alloantibodies from the maternal circulation, reducing the binding of alloantibodies to the corresponding antigen-expressing fetal red blood cells. This delays the onset and progression of HDFN. Glucocorticoids (such as prednisone) suppress the immune response in several ways, interfering with the function of numerous immune cells and affecting the production and action of alloantibodies.1,36 In this study, regarding early-onset severe HDFN, we observed a numerical trend toward a 7-day delay in the gestational age at the first IUT in the combined immunotherapy group compared to the serial IUTs-only group, although this difference did not reach statistical significance. However, this result should be interpreted in the context of the baseline characteristics. As noted (Table 4), the immunotherapy group presented with significantly higher antibody titers than the control group, indicating a more severe baseline disease status. Typically, higher antibody titers are strongly associated with earlier onset of fetal anemia and an earlier need for intervention. The fact that these high-risk patients achieved a comparable—or numerically later—gestational age at the first IUT compared to the lower-risk control group suggests that immunotherapy may have effectively mitigated the rapid progression of hemolysis.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective single-center study, selection bias (confounding by indication) was unavoidable, as patients with more aggressive disease were more likely to receive immunotherapy. Notably, the immunotherapy group had significantly higher baseline antibody titers, indicating a more severe disease status that may have obscured the statistical significance of the treatment efficacy. Second, the sample size was restricted by the rarity of the condition, which may have resulted in insufficient statistical power (Type II error) to confirm the observed numerical delay in the first IUT. Finally, due to inconsistent data recording, we were unable to analyze certain factors, such as reticulocyte counts. These limitations suggest that the present findings require further validation in large-scale prospective trials.

Conclusion

The characteristics of early-onset severe HDFN in Chinese patients are different from those observed in western cohorts. These patients frequently present with higher anti-D titers and a more complex spectrum of multiple alloantibodies. The occurrence of early-onset severe HDFN is associated with an increased frequency of IUT and lower live birth rates. Therefore, exploring effective interventions to delay the first IUT is critical to optimizing the timing and efficacy of intrauterine management for these high-risk cases.

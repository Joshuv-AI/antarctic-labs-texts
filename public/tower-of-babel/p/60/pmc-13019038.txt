Effectiveness of intravenous recombinant plasminogen activator treatment in Chinese patients with acute ischaemic stroke aged over 80 years: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jiansheng Yang, Yu-hui Huang, Wansi Zhong, Xiaoxian Gong, Yi Chen, Zhicai Chen, Haidi Jin, Shenqiang Yan, Liyan Huang, Chang-zheng Yuan, Min Lou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRisks and benefits of intravenous recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) remain unclear among elderly patients with acute ischaemic stroke (AIS). This study investigated 1-year clinical outcomes of intravenous rt-PA treatment in Chinese patients aged >80 years with AIS.MethodsThis retrospective multicentre study included patients with AIS aged >80 years from the Computer-based Online Database of Acute Stroke Patients for Stroke Management Quality Evaluation stroke registry platform between January 2017 and March 2020 who arrived at the hospital within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. Patients who received intravenous rt-PA were propensity score-matched (1:1) by baseline characteristics with those who did not receive reperfusion therapy. The primary outcome was modified Rankin scale (mRS) score 0–1 at 1 year; secondary outcomes were any intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) and all-cause mortality during hospitalisation, mRS 0–2, mRS score distribution and all-cause mortality at 1 year.ResultsThe analysis included 1560 propensity score-matched elderly patients (intravenous rt-PA, n=780; non-reperfusion, n=780). At 1 year, the intravenous rt-PA group had a higher proportion of patients with mRS 0–1 (27.7% vs 23.8%; OR 1.87, 95% CI 1.35 to 2.59, p<0.001), mRS 0–2 (37.3% vs 33.7%; OR 2.02, 95% CI 1.48 to 2.75, p<0.001) and an overall shift towards better outcomes than placebo (mRS mean±SD score: 3.5±2.4 vs 3.7±2.3; OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.64 to 0.93, p=0.007). No significant differences were observed in any ICH and all-cause mortality during hospitalisation and at 1 year.ConclusionsThis study provided real-world evidence for a positive benefit-risk profile of intravenous rt-PA in Chinese patients with AIS aged >80 years.Trial registration numberNCT05401149.

Introduction

The prevalence of acute ischaemic stroke (AIS), the most common subtype of stroke, is higher among elderly patients compared with younger patients.1 Based on a cross-sectional study in China in 2020, the age-standardised prevalence of AIS among those aged ≥80 years was 5.4%; this was 0.4%–4.3% in those aged 40–69 years.1 According to data from the Hospital Quality Monitoring System in China, around 17% of patients with AIS who were admitted to the hospital were aged ≥80 years.2 With a rapidly ageing population, the prevalence of AIS among elderly patients in China is likely to increase over time.3 Despite this increasing need, there is no locally approved medication for the management of AIS in elderly Chinese patients.

While the benefits of intravenous recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) in patients with AIS aged ≤80 years within 4.5 hours of symptom onset are well-recognised,4 its use in those aged >80 years is less established. Previous studies have suggested that elderly patients with AIS may also benefit from intravenous rt-PA.59 In a pooled analysis of seven randomised trials of intravenous rt-PA versus placebo, intravenous rt-PA treatment within 4.5 hours of symptom onset was associated with a higher proportion of good stroke outcome (modified Rankin scale (mRS) score 0–1; intravenous rt-PA: 19.1% vs placebo: 13.1%, p=0.0109) and similar 90-day mortality (intravenous rt-PA: 29.5% vs placebo: 30.2%, p=0.8382) in patients aged >80 years.5 Based on these results, the American Heart Association/American Stroke Association and Chinese guidelines currently recommend the use of intravenous rt-PA within 4.5 hours of symptom onset in patients with AIS aged >80 years.1012 However, the use of intravenous rt-PA remains restricted to patients aged ≤80 years according to the local drug label due to limited treatment data, including long-term outcomes of intravenous rt-PA in elderly Chinese patients.13

This study aimed to evaluate the 1-year clinical outcomes of elderly Chinese patients with AIS treated with intravenous rt-PA in a real-world setting (Clinicaltrials.gov identifier: NCT05401149).

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective study analysed data from a prospectively designed multicentre stroke registry in China, the Computer-based Online Database of Acute Stroke Patients for Stroke Management Quality Evaluation (CASE-II).14 Patients with AIS aged >80 years who arrived at the hospital within 4.5 hours of symptom onset and were registered on CASE-II between January 2017 and March 2020 were included. Key exclusion criteria were contraindication (except age) to intravenous rt-PA treatment; missing key data (ie, age, gender, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), time of symptom onset, time of hospital arrival/admission, received intravenous thrombolysis or not, time of intravenous rt-PA treatment); received thrombolysis agents other than rt-PA; received endovascular treatment; or received intravenous rt-PA after 4.5 hours of symptom onset. Eligible patients were divided into the intravenous rt-PA group (ie, received intravenous rt-PA within 4.5 hours of symptom onset) or the non-reperfusion therapy group (ie, did not receive any reperfusion treatment).

Data collection and management

The CASE-II database was designed to examine the current status of stroke care in China in order to develop strategies to improve stroke care and includes de-identified patient demographics, clinical, laboratory and imaging data. All patients’ data were entered by local trained registrars using standardised protocols, and the overall process of case registration, monitoring of the data quality, and inquiry and correction of erroneous data was managed and supervised by a steering committee. The study evaluated clinical outcomes at 3 months and 1 year. mRS scores at 1 year were assessed by certified external clinical evaluators during a standardised telephone interview. All the telephone interviews were recorded and traceable.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was favourable clinical outcomes (mRS 0–1) at 1 year (defined as 12±1 months). The secondary outcomes were any intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) and all-cause mortality during hospitalisation, functional independence (mRS 0–2), distribution of mRS scores and all-cause mortality at 1 year. Further outcomes were the distribution of mRS scores, favourable clinical outcomes and all-cause mortality at 3 months.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated assuming 1-year favourable outcomes in 28.0% of intravenous rt-PA treated elderly patients, based on the same relative risk of 1.46 per previous randomised controlled trials,5 and 19.2% for 1-year favourable outcomes in the non-reperfusion therapy group. In order to achieve a power of 90% at a two-sided, 5% significance level to detect a treatment difference of 8.8%, further considering a treatment ratio of 1:1 and a 15% loss to visit, a sample size of 1146 patients was required.

Statistical analyses

Descriptive data regarding patient demographics, lifestyle-related factors and clinical characteristics were summarised. To account for potential confounding, the two cohorts were matched by baseline characteristics using propensity score matching. The intravenous rt-PA and non-reperfusion therapy groups were propensity score-matched 1:1 based on age, sex, baseline NIHSS, medical insurance status, smoking status, hospital level, comorbidities (eg, diabetes, coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, prior stroke/transient ischaemic attack and hypertension), co-medications (eg, antiplatelet, lipid lowering and oral anticoagulation medications) and time from symptom onset to hospital admission. Nearest neighbour matching (a maximum calliper width of 0.2 of the SD of the logit of the propensity score) was used.15 Missing data for prior mRS scores were imputed with the median. Patients with missing data for mRS score at 1 year were excluded from the analysis. Propensity score matching and multivariable analysis were used to address potential sources of bias: baseline characteristics that were not sufficiently balanced (absolute standardised difference (ASD) ≥0.1) after propensity score matching were included in an appropriate multivariable model to adjust for the differences.

For the primary outcome, a comparison of favourable clinical outcomes (mRS 0–1) at 1 year between the propensity score-matched cohorts was calculated using a χ2 test; conditional logistic regression was used to calculate the OR between the two matched cohorts, with adjustment for prior mRS scores (≤1 or not), baseline NIHSS score and time from symptom onset to hospital admission. For the secondary outcomes, descriptive summaries were conducted on the propensity score-matched cohorts separately. For categorical variables, including any ICH and mortality during hospitalisation and functional independence (mRS 0–2) at 1 year, the percentage and 95% CI were calculated; a conditional logistic regression model was used to calculate the ORs (adjusted for the same covariates as the primary outcome). Kaplan-Meier estimation was used to analyse time to death up to 1 year and Cox regression was used to estimate the HR for 1-year all-cause mortality between the propensity score-matched cohorts; the HR was also adjusted for the same covariates as the primary outcome. For mRS distribution, an ordinal logistic regression model was used to calculate the OR between the matched cohorts. Descriptive analyses were performed for the distribution of mRS scores, favourable clinical outcomes and all-cause mortality at 3 months.

To further assess the robustness of our findings, several sensitivity analyses were conducted. First, we compared the primary and secondary outcomes between patients who received the standard intravenous rt-PA dosage and those in the non-reperfusion therapy group. Second, we rematched the two groups using a stricter calliper width of 0.1 SD of the logit of the propensity score to ensure better balance of baseline characteristics. Third, we performed multiple imputation for missing data on 1-year mRS scores and re-evaluated the primary and secondary outcomes in the propensity score-matched intravenous rt-PA and non-reperfusion therapy groups. Finally, we conducted another analysis with multiple imputation for missing data on prior mRS scores and assessed the primary and secondary outcomes in the matched groups. These analyses were designed to address potential biases and ensure the stability of our results. A post-hoc analysis was conducted due to the imbalance in the proportion of patients lost to follow-up between the intravenous rt-PA and non-reperfusion therapy groups. As patients with missing date of death were excluded from the secondary outcome analysis, this may introduce bias in the Cox regression analysis. The post-hoc analysis used a conditional logistic regression model evaluating 1-year mortality (yes or no) as the outcome and intravenous rt-PA treatment as the exposure. This allowed the inclusion of patients with missing date of death in the model.

Results

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics

A total of 98 330 consecutive AIS patients in the registry from January 2017 to March 2020 were identified, including 4245 patients aged >80 years who were admitted to the hospital within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. Of these, 319 patients were excluded due to meeting the exclusion criteria, and 868 due to missing data on mRS at 1 year. Of the 3058 patients with AIS included, 1847 patients received intravenous rt-PA treatment and 1211 patients did not receive reperfusion treatment (figure 1). Baseline characteristics of the whole study population are summarised in online supplemental table 1. After propensity score matching, a total of 1560 patients were included for analysis, of whom 780 patients treated with intravenous rt-PA were matched against 780 patients who did not receive reperfusion treatment (figure 1).

Primary outcome: favourable clinical outcomes at 1 year

A total of 216 (27.7%) patients in the intravenous rt-PA group and 186 (23.8%) patients in the non-reperfusion therapy group had favourable clinical outcomes (mRS 0–1) at 1 year. Secondary outcomes: any ICH and all-cause mortality during hospitalisation and functional independence, distribution of mRS scores and all-cause mortality at 1 year

The intravenous rt-PA group had a higher proportion of patients with functional independence (mRS 0–‍2) at 1 year versus the non-reperfusion therapy group (37.3% vs 33.7%; adjusted OR 2.02, 95% CI 1.48 to 2.75, p<0.001) (table 2). The distribution of mRS scores at 1 year shifted towards better performance in the intravenous rt-PA versus non-reperfusion therapy group (3.5±2.4 vs 3.7±2.3; adjusted OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.64 to 0.93, p=0.007) (figure 2).

Further outcomes: distribution of mRS scores, favourable clinical outcomes and all-cause mortality at 3 months in the intravenous rt-PA group

At 3 months, the proportion of patients with favourable clinical outcomes (mRS 0–1) was 27.8% and all-cause mortality was 32.4% (online supplemental table 2).

Sensitivity analysis

A sensitivity analysis was conducted on 2677 patients after excluding the 381 patients who received a non-standard dose of intravenous rt-PA, of whom 1376 were propensity score-matched 1:1 (688 patients per treatment group) (figure 1). Baseline characteristics between the two groups are summarised in online supplemental table 3 and were mostly balanced between the two groups. Any differences in baseline characteristics (ASD≥0.1) were adjusted for in the outcomes analysis.

Consistent with the study outcomes reported in the main analyses, the intravenous rt-PA group had a higher proportion of patients with favourable clinical outcomes and functional independence at 1 year, and lower mRS scores compared with the non-reperfusion therapy group (online supplemental table 4 and figure 1). There was no statistically significant difference in any ICH during hospitalisation, all-cause mortality during hospitalisation and all-cause mortality at 1 year between the two groups (online supplemental table 4 and figure 2).

In addition, we rematched the two groups using a stricter calliper width of 0.1 SD of the logit of the propensity score to ensure better balance of baseline characteristics. The results showed that the baseline characteristics between the two groups were well-balanced, and the associations for primary and secondary outcomes were similar to those in the main analysis (online supplemental table 5).

We also performed multiple imputation for missing data on 1-year mRS scores and re-evaluated the primary and secondary outcomes in the propensity score-matched intravenous rt-PA and non-reperfusion therapy groups. Furthermore, we conducted another analysis with multiple imputation for missing data on prior mRS scores and assessed the primary and secondary outcomes in the matched groups. Both analyses yielded results similar to those of the main analysis (online supplemental tables 6 and 7).

Post-hoc analysis

An additional 120 patients were confirmed dead at 1-year follow-up, but without the date of death and were excluded from the secondary outcome analysis (14 patients in the intravenous rt-PA group, 79 in the non-reperfusion group). To further explore the association between intravenous rt-PA treatment and 1-year mortality, we conducted a post-hoc analysis that included the patients with missing date of death. After including patients with missing date of death in the regression model (n=780 in each group) and controlling for baseline characteristics, the intravenous rt-PA group had a significantly lower risk of 1-year all-cause mortality versus the non-reperfusion therapy group (1-year crude mortality rate 39.5% vs 38.8%; adjusted OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.99, p=0.045).

Discussion

In this retrospective, multicentre, cohort study, intravenous rt-PA treatment was associated with increased odds of favourable clinical outcomes and functional independence and a shift towards better performance at 1 year in patients with AIS aged >80 years. Furthermore, this study found no statistically significant differences in ICH during hospitalisation, all-cause mortality during hospitalisation or 1-year all-cause mortality between the intravenous rt-PA and non-reperfusion therapy groups.

In our propensity score-matched cohorts, patients with AIS aged >80 years who were treated with intravenous rt-PA were statistically significantly more likely to have favourable clinical outcomes (mRS 0–1) at 1 year than those who received no reperfusion (27.7% vs 23.8%, absolute difference +3.9%). These findings align with previous studies that reported improved functional outcomes at 6 months5 7 8 and at discharge6 among elderly patients with AIS aged >80 years. Furthermore, the subgroup analysis from the third International Stroke Trial (IST-3) showed that intravenous rt-PA treatment was associated with a higher proportion of patients with functional independence (Oxford Handicap Scale (OHS) score 0–2) and a shift towards better performance (lower OHS scores) at 6 months among patients with AIS aged >80 years (OR 1.27, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.47, p=0.001).8 Longer-term (18 months) functional benefits of intravenous rt-PA have also been demonstrated in the IST-3 trial,16 which aligns with the findings of our study. Despite these benefits, the absolute improvement in favourable outcomes (mRS 0–1) reported in our study was modest (+3.9%), and approximately half of the intravenous rt-PA-treated elderly patients exhibited poor functional outcomes (mRS>3) at 1 year. This observation is consistent with a recent study that consecutively enrolled patients with AIS aged >80 years, which reported that 50% of patients had poor functional outcomes (mRS>3) at 1 year.17 Advanced age, comorbidities and greater stroke severity are likely contributing factors to these suboptimal outcomes, regardless of treatment allocation. These findings highlight the limitations of current reperfusion therapies and the urgent need for more comprehensive ischaemic stroke management strategies for elderly patients with AIS.

ICH remains a significant safety concern, as previous studies have consistently reported an increased risk of ICH following intravenous rt-PA administration, especially in older populations.8 Importantly, our study showed that there was no statistically significant difference in any ICH during hospitalisation between patients with AIS aged >80 years who received intravenous rt-PA and those who did not, which occurred in 13.7% and 10.8% of patients, respectively (p=0.054). This finding is consistent with previous studies,18 including those involving Asian patients with AIS aged >80 years.19 20 As ICH has been considered a potential safety concern for elderly patients receiving the standard intravenous rt-PA dosage,21 it is worth noting that the results were consistent in the sensitivity analysis even after excluding patients who received a lower intravenous rt-PA dosage. Nevertheless, the near-significant trend towards a higher ICH rate in the intravenous rt-PA group warrants caution and highlights the importance of individualised risk assessments and close monitoring for elderly or high-risk patients when considering intravenous rt-PA therapy.

There have also been concerns about increased mortality rates with intravenous rt-PA treatment among elderly patients with AIS.22 23 In the present study, we showed that among elderly patients with AIS, there was no statistically significant difference in all-cause mortality during hospitalisation and at 1 year between the intravenous rt-PA and non-reperfusion therapy groups. This is consistent with previous studies showing that intravenous rt-PA treatment was not associated with increased in-hospital mortality62426 or mortality at 6 months.8 The 1-year all-cause mortality rate reported in the present study (36.2%) is consistent with that reported in a recent real-world cohort study in the USA of patients with AIS aged ≥65 years with a median age of 80 years (January 2006 to December 2009: 36.9%; January 2010 to December 2014: 35.2%).27 Similarly, among Asian patients with AIS aged >80 years, intravenous rt-PA treatment was not associated with increased mortality risks up to 3 months.19 20

There were patients who were not included in the analysis for all-cause mortality at 1 year due to missing date of death, which could have introduced bias. To further explore the association between intravenous rt-PA treatment and 1-year all-cause mortality, we performed a post-hoc analysis to include patients with missing date of death. In the post-hoc analysis, the intravenous rt-PA group had a significantly lower risk of 1-year all-cause mortality versus the non-reperfusion therapy group. Previously, the randomised controlled IST-3 study showed that intravenous rt-PA treatment was associated with improved survival in the longer term (up to 3 years) in patients aged >80 years who received intravenous rt-PA compared with those who did not receive intravenous rt-PA, although this difference did not reach statistical significance (mortality at 3 years: 59.5% vs 64.7% in intravenous rt-PA treated and untreated groups, respectively).28 Further longer-term studies are required to better understand the impact of intravenous rt-PA treatment on mortality in patients with AIS aged >80 years.

Strengths and limitations of the study

There are several strengths to this study. Patients in this study were selected from CASE-II, which is a large prospectively designed database that included 98 330 patients with AIS. Quality control measures were taken to improve data integrity and accuracy, while reducing possible information bias. Furthermore, 1-year follow-up data were available for both the intravenous rt-PA and the non-reperfusion therapy group, and propensity score matching and multivariate analysis were used to ensure baseline characteristics were sufficiently balanced.

There are also several study limitations. Three-month follow-up data were not available for the non-reperfusion therapy group. Patients with missing date of death were unbalanced between the treatment groups and were excluded in the analysis for all-cause mortality at 1 year, although this was followed up with a post-hoc analysis to include all patients with confirmed death at 1 year. In addition, the retrospective study design inherently carries the risk of unmeasured confounding factors. Important lifestyle variables, such as education level, dietary habits, alcohol consumption and physical activity levels, as well as certain clinical assessments, such as the precise timing of ICH during hospitalisation, were not systematically recorded in the CASE-II registry. The lack of access to these potential confounding factors prevented the use of Kaplan-Meier analysis for time to ICH and may have influenced the observed outcomes. Due to the nature of observational studies, residual confounding bias may still exist despite the propensity score matching and adjustments for imbalanced covariates. Potential bias due to loss to follow-up may also exist. Given these limitations, our findings should be interpreted with caution. We recommend conducting further prospective studies to validate this study’s results.

Conclusion

This study provided real-world evidence demonstrating that intravenous rt-PA administered within 4.5 hours of symptom onset improved 1-year functional outcomes without increasing any ICH during hospitalisation or all-cause mortality in Chinese patients with AIS aged >80 years.

Mitochondrial DNA Copy Number, a Damage-Associated Molecular Pattern Molecule, Can Predict Pancreatic Necrosis and Is Correlated with the Severity of Acute Pancreatitis
Authors: Ghada M. Ezzat, Soha M. Nageb, Mostafa Abd-Allah Haredi, Muhammad Abbas El-Masry
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is a damage-associated molecular pattern molecule that can trigger an immune-inflammatory response during pancreatic necrosis (PN).AimTo evaluate the role of mtDNA in the detection of PN and severe acute pancreatitis (SAP).MethodsThe present study included 40 AP patients and 30 controls. AP patients were grouped into mild AP (MAP, n = 15), moderately severe AP (MSAP, n = 17), and SAP (n = 8). Also, the SAP + MSAP group, n = 25, was compared to MAP. AP patients were divided into NAP (n = 7) and non-necrotizing AP (n = 33). The mtDNA copy number, IL-6, and STAT3 expression levels were measured using quantitative real-time PCR.ResultsThe mtDNA, IL-6, and STAT3 levels were significantly higher in AP patients than in controls and in the SAP + MSAP than in the MAP. However, the SAP had non-significantly higher levels of mtDNA, STAT3, and IL-6 levels than the MSAP and statistically significant mtDNA, STAT3, and IL-6 when compared to the MAP. mtDNA, IL-6, and STAT3 showed significantly higher levels in NAP compared with non-necrotizing AP. mtDNA was positively correlated with STAT3, IL-6, CRP, APACHE, and CT severity index (CTSI) and negatively correlated with albumin. In the receiver operating curve (ROC), mtDNA was the most significant independent predictor of PN and MAP vs. SAP + MSAP. IL-6 and mtDNA + CRP had higher diagnostic abilities for SIRS and high CTSI.ConclusionsmtDNA could enhance the prediction of NAP; however, its diagnostic ability of SAP needs further study.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10620-023-08049-2.

Introduction

The presence of pancreatic necrosis indicates a severe case of acute pancreatitis (SAP) and is associated with high mortality [1]. Currently, a contrast-enhanced CT (CECT) scan is the “gold standard” for the diagnosis and classification of acute pancreatitis. However, in the case of pancreatic necrosis, CECT has the pitfalls of delaying non-enhancement for at least 3 days after its onset, having hazardous effects in high-risk patients, and having a high radiation dose [2]. Several scoring systems, like Ranson and APACHE11, are complicated and time-consuming.

Mitochondrial dysfunction is claimed to be related to the progression of pancreatic cell necrosis [3]. Mitochondrial permeability transition pore dysfunction by toxins triggers a biochemical and cellular cascade that starts with cytoplasmic calcium overload and ends with failure of ATP production [3, 4]. Consequently, initiation of necrosis through stimulation of phosphoglycerate mutase 5 and inhibition of autophagy leads to activation of trypsinogen [5]. A shortage of ATP production by mitochondria triggers signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) activation [6]. The release of damage-associated molecular pattern molecules connects the local pancreatic cell necrosis with inflammatory pathways as the signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) [6].

Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), as a strict intracellular constituent, is considered a damage-associated molecular pattern molecule (DAMP), once exposed to the immune system, it can trigger an inflammatory cascade [3]. The methylation of the CpG regions in mtDNA makes mtDNA mimic that of microbial DNA, and this is why mitochondrial DNA is more immunogenic than that of nuclear DNA [4]. Extracellular mtDNA binds toll-like receptors (TLR9) [5] on the surface of neutrophils and macrophages and induces the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemotaxis, and phagocytic activation through a MyD88-dependent signaling cascade triggering MAPK and NF-κβ signaling, or through interferon regulatory factor 7 (IRF7) to enhance type 1 interferon response [6].

Interleukin 6 (IL-6) is a glycoprotein of 26 kDa that is secreted by numerous immune cells such as monocytes, fibroblasts, phagocytes, and T-cells [7]. It signals through the Janus-Kinase signal transducer and activator of transcription proteins signaling transduction pathway (JAK-STAT), which is a group of cytoplasmic and intramembranous proteins that interact together in cascade reactions to induce transcription of certain proteins. They control a variety of processes, including immunity, cell division, and cell death [12]. During cytokine stimulation, STAT3 translocates to the nucleus upon tyrosine phosphorylation [13]. Interestingly, a regulatory role of STAT3 in the electron transport chain and mitochondrial function has been reported by previous studies [14, 15].

Due to the high mortality that is associated with NAP, its early management relies on a delicate diagnosis of pancreatic cell necrosis. Moreover, understanding the mechanism of pancreatic cell necrosis will help reduce its mortality. The aim of this study is to clarify the relationship between plasma levels of mtDNA, IL-6, and STAT3 during SAP and to evaluate their roles in the prediction of pancreatic necrosis.

Methods

Study Design

A case–control study included 40 AP patients and 30 healthy adult controls, who were matched in age and gender and recruited for the study from September 2019 to September 2020. The Institutional Review Board of the Faculty of Medicine at Assiut University approved the study protocol. The IRB approval number was 17200285. Written informed consent from all participants was obtained before their enrollment in the present study. In the study, forty consecutive patients with acute pancreatitis were admitted within 48 h of the onset of symptoms.

The clinical diagnosis of AP was based on two of the three following features: characteristic abdominal pain, serum amylase and/or lipase levels that were three times higher than the upper limit of normal, and characteristic findings of AP on the CECT scan. Patients were classified into the mild AP group (MAP) (n = 15) and the severe AP group (n = 25) which included 17 moderately severe AP patients and 8 severe AP patients (MSAP + SAP) group according to the revised Atlanta classification system (2012). A Marshall score of ≥ 2 was used to determine organ failure (respiratory, circulatory, and renal). Also, scoring systems including APACHE II, Bedside Index of Severity in Acute Pancreatitis (BISAP), and the systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) were calculated upon admission. All included patients underwent computerized tomography (CT) 48 h after the onset of symptoms. The Balthazar score was used to classify CECT findings and determine the CT severity index.

Patient demographics, including age, sex, and hospital stay, were recorded. Vital signs, a full examination, and 24-h urine output were analyzed. Laboratory tests included renal and liver function tests, hematocrit levels, white blood cells with differential counts, electrolytes, blood pH, and CRP. All the tests were done on the first day of admission, with follow-up within 48 h.

Collection of Samples

Blood samples were collected after patients' admission. A 5-ml blood sample was collected and stored at – 80 °C in EDTA tubes until DNA and RNA extraction. DNA extraction was done using a thermos scientific DNA extraction kit, Gene JET Genomic DNA Purification Kit catalogue number (K0721 USA). The DNA concentration and purity were detected by Nanodrop.

Measurement of the mtDNA Copy Number

The mtDNA copy number evaluation was based on the ratio of mtDNA to nuclear DNA. The mtDNA and nuclear DNA were quantified based on the mitochondrial gene, cytochrome-B (CYT-B), and the single-copy nuclear pyruvate kinase (PK) gene [8]. The relative amounts of mtDNA and nuclear DNA were measured using quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR). The primers for the CYTB gene were forward (5′-CAC GAT TCT TTA CCT TTC ACT TCA TC-3′) and reverse (5′-TGA TCC CGT TTC GTG CAA G-3′). The primers for the PK gene were forward (5′-AGC CCA AAT GGC CTT GAA G-3′) and reverse (5′-AGA GAC AGA ATG CCA GTG AGC TT-3′). Genomic DNA (20 ng) was used as a template per 10 μl reaction with SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Applied Biosystem) in the qPCR 7500 HT system. The qPCR conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 10 min (pre-denaturation) and 35 cycles of two steps, i.e., 94 °C for 30 s (denaturation) and 58 °C for 30 s (annealing) and extension at 72 °C for 45 s. The relative mtDNA copy number was calculated using the equation: 2−ΔΔCT (ΔCt = Ct mtDNA CYTB – Ct PK).

RNA Extraction and Quantitative Real-Time PCR of STAT3 and IL-6

The RNA was extracted from the whole blood using the TRIzol™ Reagent (Invitrogen, catalogue number 15596026, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions, which included two chloroform precipitations and two 70% ethanol washes. Total RNA concentration was measured with a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (SPECTROstar ® Nano (Microplate and cuvette spectrophotometer, BMG LABTECH, Germany). RNA with a purity equal to 1.9 to 2 was utilized for cDNA synthesis with a reverse transcription kit (high-capacity cDNA synthesis kit (Applied Biosystems™, Cat. No. 4368814, USA)). qRT-PCR was performed with a 20 µl reaction that included 10 µl syber green (Sybr green master mix Bioline My Taq™ Red Mix (2X) kit, USA, Cat. No. BIO-2504 (5 µl cDNA, 3 µl double distilled water, and 1 µl of both forward and reverse). The PCR primers for STAT3 were as follows: forward (5-GCT TTT GTC AGC GAT GGA GT-3) and reverse (5-ATT TGT TGA CGG GTC TGA AGT T-3) [9]. IL-6 PCR primer sequences (5-AGACAGCCACTCACCTCTTCAG-3) and (5-TTCTGCCAGTGCCTTTGCTG-3) [10].

Forward: (5 ′GCACCGTCAAGGCTGAGAAC 3′); reverse: (5 ′ATGGTGGTGAAGACGCCAGT′ 3′) [11]. The cycling conditions for IL-6 and STAT3 were 95 °C for 10 min, 94 °C for 30 s, 58 °C for 30 s, 72 °C for 45 s, and 72 °C for 10 min. Statistical Evaluation

The data was analysed by SPSS version 21 software (Chicago, USA). The data was presented as means, standard error (SEM), or number (%). The student T test compared two groups, while the one-way ANOVA test compared the means of three groups. The significance between categorical variables was determined by chi-square (× 2). The correlation between variables was done by the Spearman correlation test. Univariable and multivariable binary logistic regression detected the predictors of severe acute pancreatitis and pancreatic necrosis. Finally, the receiver operating curve (ROC) was utilised for determining the sensitivity and specificity of the biomarkers. Differences were considered significant when p values were less than 0.05.

Binary Logistic Regression Analysis for the Prediction of Pancreatic Necrosis and the Severity of Acute Pancreatitis

To validate our results, we analysed the predictive values of mtDNA, IL-6, and STAT3 levels in detecting severe AP (MAP vs. MSAP + SAP) by binary logistic regression. The univariate logistic regression analysis showed that increasing levels of mtDNA were associated with an increased severity of acute pancreatitis. ROC Curve Analysis

We analysed the optimal cut-off values of mtDNA, IL-6, and STAT3 calculated by the ROC analysis according to MAP vs. (MSAP + SAP) and presented them in Fig. 4a. The areas under the curve (AUC) of mtDNA, IL-6, and STAT3 were 0.91, 0.86, and 0.64, respectively. STAT3 was an insignificant factor in the diagnosis of MSAP + SAP. The optimal cut-off values were mtDNA (110) and IL-6 (18.52). The highest sensitivity was in favour of mtDNA (86.4%), followed by IL-6 (76.2%). The specificity of mtDNA was 93%; for IL-6, it was 92.4%. The p-values were all < 0.001, and the 95% CI was (0.95–1) (0.64–0.94) (0.79–0.97). The CRP has a lower AUC, sensitivity, and specificity than mtDNA and IL-6. The AUC for mtDNA was 0.789 (95% CI 0.67–0.94) for the prediction of a high CTSI. However, the AUC of IL-6 was higher than that of mtDNA and STAT3, but mtDNA showed higher sensitivity (Fig. 4b). Discussion

Numerous studies confirmed the need for sensitive and specific biomarkers for the early diagnosis of SAP [8]. Severe cases of AP are associated with pancreatic necrosis and systemic inflammation that pose a serious threat to the live of patients as it leads to a high rate of mortality [2]. In this study, we tried to show the relationships between mtDNA and the STAT3/IL-6 pathway with the severity of AP and to evaluate their ability to predict pancreatic necrosis.

During acute pancreatitis, autodigestion by activated pancreatic enzymes increases the level of cellular necrosis and death. Necrotic cells release their constituents, mtDNA, as one of the restricted intracellular constituents, is postulated to be increased in the plasma during cell necrosis and induces a strong immunogenic reaction. In our study, we measured the copy number level of mtDNA and correlated it with different inflammatory biomarkers and severity scores.

Our results revealed a higher statistically significant mtDNA copy number in AP patients compared to the controls. mtDNA, as one of the damage-associated molecular pattern molecules (DAMPs), is closely related to pancreatitis as it completes the link between inflammation and cell death [9]. Also, the MSAP and SAP groups showed higher levels of mtDNA than the control and MAP groups. This is with the Wu et al. study that observed a higher levels of mtDNA in MAP than the control group [10]. This means that the levels of mtDNA can be used as a marker of disease severity but as there was a clear relationship between mtDNA and pancreatic necrosis, SIRS, and CTSI. mtDNA was associated with the severity of various diseases as cancer [11], sepsis [12] and cardiovascular diseases [13].

Scozzi et al. reported that mtDNA CYTb in Corona virus patients exhibited higher levels in severe hospitalized patients [14]. The SAP group showed a non- significantly higher levels of mtDNA than MSAP group as both groups are vulnerable to organ damage (data are not shown) [15]. In univariable and multivariable analyses, mtDNA can significantly predict severe cases of AP (MSAP + SAP) and pancreatic necrosis. Moreover, ROC curve revealed good sensitivity and specificity of mtDNA copy number as an early independent predictor for increased severity of acute pancreatitis and detection of pancreatic necrosis.

Signal transducer and activator of transcription-3 (STAT3) is concerned with proinflammatory cytokine release during inflammatory conditions such as IL-8 and IL-6 [16]. Upon activation of neutrophils, they form neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) that are composed of their DNA and histones [17]. In the acinar pancreas, NETs stimulate trypsinogen release and STAT3 [18]. JAK-STAT3 plays a role in the emergence of acute pancreatitis by stimulating the secretion of cytokines such as IL-6 [19].

In our study, we found a statistically significant difference between the control group and APs subgroups at the STAT3 levels. According to the STAT3 levels, it was significantly higher in the MSAP + SAP patients than those observed in the MAP patients.STAT3 has been associated with severity of AP [20]. Furthermore, patients with pancreatic necrosis or SIRS, high CTSI index exhibited higher STAT3 expression levels than AP patients with no previous complications. Its relationships to organ failure associated AP were reported. Li et al. study that pointed out the role of JAK-STAT3 in liver injury resulting from AP and indicated that the inhibition of this pathway helped in reduction of the severity of liver injury [21]. A previous study also showed the contribution of STAT3 in acute kidney injury associated with AP [22].

Shigekawa et al. tested ceruline-induced AP in two groups of mice, one with conditional STAT3 knockout and the other with active STAT3.They found that pancreatic recovery after AP was significantly delayed in STAT3 knockout mice compared to the other group [23]. The present study observed an association between higher STAT3 levels and SIRS, CT necrosis, and CTSI. Blocking the STAT3 pathway could inhibit the inflammation in cerulein-induced AP. Also, our study showed positive moderate correlations between STAT3 relative expression levels, IL-6, and mtDNA. However, univariable binary logistic regression analysis revealed that the STAT3 expression level was not a predictor for the severity or necrosis of AP. STAT3 correlated with mtDNA positively [18].

Interleukin-6 (IL-6) secretion is stimulated during inflammation and plays a crucial role in the inflammation cycle. It has gain great interest in predicting the course of different diseases. IL-6 can predict disease severity since its circulating levels peak 24–48 h before those of CRP [24]. We measured serum IL-6 in patients with AP, and we found a highly statistically significant difference between the control group and study subgroups as regards IL-6. The expression of IL-6 was significantly higher in the (SAP + MSAP) patients than that observed in the MAP patients. Also, there was a significant positive correlation between levels of IL-6 and necrosis presented in the CECT scan. Our results consistent with a previous study that pointed to the role of serum IL-6 in the prediction of SAP as well as the multiple organ failures associated with SAP and NAP [25].

Ćeranić et.al. reported increasing IL-6 value 48 h after admission was associated with an increase in the likelihood of exhibiting a severe course of AP [26]. Jain et al. documented that serum IL-6 increased the positive predictive value of persistent SIRS and predicted SAP [27]. In ST-elevation myocardial infarction, Ritschel et al. concluded the correlation between peak troponin level and IL-6 and the association with myocardial necrosis [28].

In conclusion, mtDNA plasma level is a significant predictive biomarker for both severity of AP and pancreatic necrosis. Increasing the plasma levels of mtDNA can predict a poor prognosis for SAP and pancreatic necrosis. Although IL-6 and STAT3 show associations with clinical severity scores of AP (CTSI, CT necrosis), their prediction ability is less than that of mtDNA in differentiating both the severity of acute pancreatitis and pancreatic necrosis. Large studies are needed for confirmation of our results.

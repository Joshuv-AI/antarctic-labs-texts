Advances in antibody-drug conjugates in cancer: latest updates from the 2026 AACR annual meeting
Authors: Yan-Ruide Li, Yichen Zhu, Yuning Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are rapidly evolving from conventional cytotoxic delivery systems into multifunctional, immune-integrated therapeutic platforms. Highlights from the 2026 American Association for Cancer Research (AACR) Annual Meeting demonstrate significant advances in ADC design, including dual- and multi-payload constructs, multispecific targeting strategies, and immunostimulatory payloads. These innovations aim to overcome key limitations such as tumor heterogeneity, resistance, and systemic toxicity. Novel approaches targeting the tumor microenvironment, including depletion of regulatory T cells and tumor-associated macrophages, further expand the therapeutic scope of ADCs beyond direct tumor cell killing. In parallel, integration with emerging modalities such as engineered CD16-enhanced natural killer T (NKT) cells underscores the potential for synergy between ADCs and cellular immunotherapies. Advances in AI-guided target discovery and antibody engineering are also enhancing tumor selectivity and internalization. Collectively, these developments highlight a paradigm shift toward precision, multi-mechanistic ADCs with the potential to improve clinical outcomes across diverse cancer types.

To the editor,

The 2026 AACR Annual Meeting highlighted a new wave of innovation in ADCs, underscoring their rapid evolution from conventional cytotoxic delivery systems into multifunctional, immune-integrated therapeutic platforms. Across multiple preclinical and early clinical studies, emerging ADC technologies demonstrated improved precision, enhanced efficacy, and the ability to overcome resistance mechanisms that have historically limited this class of therapeutics.

Expanding ADC functionality: from single payloads to multifunctional platforms

A defining theme was the transformation of ADCs beyond mono-payload cytotoxic agents into integrated, multifunctional therapeutic systems (Table 1). Early-generation ADCs largely relied on a single cytotoxic payload, often limited by tumor heterogeneity and the emergence of resistance. In contrast, next-generation strategies such as dual-payload constructs and multi-payload conjugates demonstrated the ability to co-deliver agents with orthogonal mechanisms of action, including microtubule inhibitors and topoisomerase I inhibitors [1–3]. These designs enable simultaneous disruption of multiple survival pathways within tumor cells, thereby enhancing cytotoxic potency and reducing the likelihood of resistance. Importantly, improvements in linker chemistry, such as more stable non-maleimide conjugation strategies, have minimized premature payload release and improved therapeutic indices.

Emerging ADC platforms and design innovations

Equally notable was the emergence of immunostimulatory ADCs, which extend functionality beyond direct tumor cell killing. Phosphonate-based ADCs were shown to selectively activate Vγ9Vδ2 T cells following tumor-specific delivery, leading to cytokine release and targeted tumor lysis [4]. Similarly, CCR8-targeting ADCs incorporating amanitin payloads enabled the direct and efficient depletion of intratumoral regulatory T cells, independent of Fc-mediated effector functions, resulting in an increased CD8⁺/Treg ratio and a more favorable immune microenvironment [5]. These approaches highlight a paradigm shift toward ADCs that actively modulate anti-tumor immunity.

Advances in molecular and computational engineering further enhanced ADC performance. AI-guided multi-omics platforms enabled the identification of tumor-specific epitopes, including those defined by aberrant post-translational modifications such as hypoglycosylation, thereby improving tumor selectivity [6, 7]. In parallel, innovative antibody formats, including biparatopic and trispecific constructs, demonstrated enhanced binding avidity, internalization, and target engagement by simultaneously recognizing multiple epitopes or antigens. Additionally, alternative scaffolds such as VHH-Fc-based ADCs provided improved tissue penetration, faster tumor uptake, and more homogeneous drug-to-antibody ratios [8].

Targeting the tumor microenvironment and integrating with immune therapies

Beyond direct tumor cell targeting, studies underscored the expanding role for ADCs in actively modulating the tumor microenvironment (TME), a critical determinant of therapeutic response and resistance. Several approaches demonstrated the ability to selectively deplete immunosuppressive cellular populations that sustain tumor progression (Table 2). For instance, CD64-directed ADCs employing highly potent payloads such as PNU-159,682 effectively eliminated tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and leukemic monocytes, including non-proliferative subsets that are typically resistant to conventional chemotherapies [9]. Similarly, CCR8-targeting ADCs incorporating amanitin payloads achieved precise depletion of intratumoral regulatory T cells (Tregs), leading to a marked increase in the CD8⁺/Treg ratio and restoration of anti-tumor immune activity [5]. Collectively, these strategies highlight the capacity of ADCs to reprogram the TME, rather than solely targeting malignant cells.

Mechanistic strategies and tumor microenvironment effects

Importantly, AACR 2026 also showcased the growing convergence between ADCs and cellular immunotherapy platforms. Engineered CD16-enhanced stem cell-derived NKT cells demonstrated potent antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) when combined with tumor-targeting antibodies, and exhibited enhanced persistence, metabolic fitness, and tumor-infiltrating capacity [10]. Notably, these cells were capable of remodeling the TME by depleting suppressive myeloid populations such as TAMs and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), thereby amplifying downstream immune responses. This approach addresses a key limitation of antibody-based therapies, which is their reliance on endogenous effector cells, and offers a scalable, off-the-shelf strategy to enhance ADC and monoclonal antibody efficacy [10].

The integration of multi-specific ADCs further exemplifies this therapeutic convergence. Trispecific constructs targeting PD-L1, B7-H3, and VEGF were designed to simultaneously deliver cytotoxic payloads while blocking immune checkpoints and inhibiting angiogenesis [3]. These agents demonstrated enhanced tumor binding, improved internalization, and superior antitumor efficacy compared to monospecific counterparts, while maintaining a favorable safety profile by sparing non-target immune cells. Such designs effectively embed combination therapy within a single molecular entity, maximizing therapeutic synergy.

In summary, findings from the 2026 AACR illustrate a paradigm shift in ADC development toward multifunctional, microenvironment-aware, and immune-integrated therapeutics. Continued clinical translation will require careful optimization to balance efficacy and safety as molecular complexity increases.

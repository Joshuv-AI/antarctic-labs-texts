Sonographic Evaluation of Thyroid Nodules with Elastography and Cytopathological Correlation
Authors: P. B. Abinaya, Lavanya Dharmalingam, Siva Kaliyamoorthy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Thyroid nodules are commonly detected with high-resolution ultrasonography. While most are benign, a significant minority are malignant, necessitating accurate risk stratification. Conventional ultrasound is sensitive but has limited specificity due to overlapping features.Aim:To assess the diagnostic accuracy of ultrasound elastography in thyroid nodules by correlating elastography findings with cytopathology.Materials and Methods:This analytical cross-sectional study included 100 patients (20–60 years) with thyroid nodules evaluated using B-mode ultrasound and TI-RADS classification. Shear wave elastography was performed to assess tissue stiffness. Nodules with suspicious features underwent ultrasound-guided FNAC, reported using the Bethesda system. Statistical analysis was done using SPSS v26.0, with significance set at P < 0.05.Results:The mean age was 39.16 ± 12.24 years, with female predominance. Most nodules were hypoechoic, and 22% were TI-RADS 5. Elastography identified 28% of nodules as stiff. Cytopathology showed 56% benign and 44% malignant lesions. A weak but statistically significant negative correlation was observed between elastography findings and cytopathology (P = 0.05).Conclusion:The combined use of ultrasonography, shear wave elastography, and cytopathology enhances thyroid nodule characterization. Elastography is a valuable adjunct to conventional ultrasound and FNAC, supporting improved risk stratification and clinical decision-making.

INTRODUCTION

Thyroid nodules are frequently encountered in clinical practice, with rising detection due to widespread use of high-resolution ultrasonography. Radiologically, a thyroid nodule is a discrete focal lesion distinct from the surrounding parenchyma and includes a wide pathological spectrum ranging from benign lesions to malignant neoplasms.[1] Although most nodules are benign, malignancy occurs in approximately 5–15% of cases, making accurate risk stratification essential.[2] Ultrasonography is the first-line imaging modality owing to its accessibility, safety, and high spatial resolution, allowing detailed assessment of nodule characteristics. However, significant overlap between benign and malignant sonographic features limits its specificity.[3] Standardized systems such as TI-RADS have improved risk assessment, yet diagnostic uncertainty persists.[4]

Ultrasound elastography has emerged as a valuable adjunct for evaluating tissue stiffness, with malignant nodules typically being firmer than benign ones. Both strain and shear wave elastography are noninvasive and reproducible and may help reduce unnecessary biopsies.[5] Fine-needle aspiration cytology remains the diagnostic gold standard but is invasive and may yield indeterminate results.[6] Correlating elastography with cytopathology may enhance diagnostic accuracy and optimize biopsy selection. This study evaluates thyroid nodules using ultrasonography and elastography and correlates imaging findings with cytological outcomes to assess the role of elastography in improved nodule characterization.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This analytical cross-sectional, hospital-based study was conducted over six months (July–December 2025) in the Departments of Radiology, Pathology, and General Surgery at Aarupadai Veedu Medical College and Hospital, Puducherry, after obtaining Institutional Human Ethics Committee approval (IHEC No: AV/IHEC/2024/088) and written informed consent. A total of 100 patients aged 20–60 years with thyroid nodules detected on B-mode ultrasonography were included by purposive sampling. Patients with diffuse thyroid disease, thyroiditis, or refusal of consent were excluded. The sample size was calculated according to Zhao et al. assuming an expected ultrasound sensitivity of 0.89, absolute precision of 6, 5% level of significance, and 90% study power.[7] All participants underwent ultrasound evaluation using a MINDRAY DC-80 scanner with a 6.2–12 MHz transducer. Nodules were assessed for standard sonographic features and categorized using TI-RADS. Shear wave elastography was performed in the same session to assess tissue stiffness. Nodules with suspicious findings underwent ultrasound-guided FNAC, and results were reported using the Bethesda system. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Descriptive statistics were calculated, Chi-square test assessed associations, and Pearson’s correlation coefficient evaluated correlation between elastography and cytopathology, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

In the present study, participants ranged in age from 20 to 59 years, with a mean age of 39.16 ± 12.24 years. The majority of patients belonged to the 20–30 years age group (32%), followed by 31–40 years and 51–60 years (26% each). This finding is consistent with previous studies by Moon et al. in 2008 and Frates et al. in 2005, which reported a higher prevalence of thyroid nodules in middle-aged adults, likely due to increased health surveillance and hormonal influences during these age groups.[89]

Gender-wise distribution in the present study showed a mild female predominance (54%) compared to males (46%), which aligns with recent studies documenting a higher incidence of thyroid disorders in women, attributed to hormonal and autoimmune factors. Studies by Durante et al. in 2018 and Trimboli et al. in 2021 have similarly reported a higher prevalence of thyroid nodules among females.[1011]

In the present study, TI-RADS categorization showed a relatively even distribution across categories, with TR 2 at 30% and TR 5 at 22%. This aligns with Li et al. in 2021 reporting that overlap of sonographic features across TI-RADS categories can reduce predictive accuracy, particularly in indeterminate nodules.[12] In contrast, Kim et al. in 2023 showed a strong correlation between higher TI-RADS categories (TR 4–5) and increased malignancy risk, emphasizing the utility of TI-RADS in risk stratification when sample sizes are sufficient.[13]

However, a statistically significant association was observed between shear wave elastography interpretation and cytopathological interpretation. Stiff nodules were more frequently associated with papillary carcinoma, while intermediate nodules correlated more with benign colloid nodules and follicular neoplasms. This weak but significant inverse correlation highlights the complementary role of elastography in refining cytological assessment, consistent with studies by Bhatia et al. in 2012, who emphasized the added value of elastography in conjunction with FNAC.[14]

CONCLUSION

This study demonstrates that a multimodal diagnostic approach integrating ultrasonography, shear wave elastography, and cytopathological assessment enhances the evaluation of thyroid nodules, although no single parameter independently predicted malignancy with consistent statistical significance. Shear wave elastography showed a statistically significant, though weak, correlation with cytopathological interpretation, with stiffer nodules more frequently associated with papillary carcinoma and softer or intermediate nodules more frequently associated with benign or follicular-pattern lesions. These findings support the use of elastography as a useful adjunct rather than a replacement for conventional ultrasound and FNAC. Cytology using the Bethesda system revealed diagnostic heterogeneity, reflecting known limitations of FNAC, particularly in follicular-patterned lesions. Final histopathology emerged as the most definitive diagnostic modality with a significant association with outcomes, reaffirming its gold-standard status.

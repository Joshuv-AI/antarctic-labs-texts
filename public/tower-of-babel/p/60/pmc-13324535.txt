Baseline assessment of sedimentary heavy metals and ecological risks in the high-siltation Gorai River, Bangladesh
Authors: Sanjida Sultana Santa, Md Kamal Hossain, Mohammad Moniruzzaman, Arnob Ghosh, Rukaiya Hossain, M Belal Hossain, Md Harunor Rashid Khan, Takaomi Arai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Gorai River, the largest distributary of the Ganges in Bangladesh, experiences high sedimentation and increasing anthropogenic pressure, raising concerns about heavy metal accumulation. This study examined the spatial distribution, pollution status, source apportionment, and ecological risks of 10 heavy metals in surface sediments collected from 22 sampling points along the Gorai River using a grab sampling method. The mean concentrations (mg/kg) followed the order: Mn (367.0) > Cr (23.97) > Ni (21.97) > Cu (18.66) > Co (9.55) > Pb (7.65) > As (5.41) > Se (0.187) > Cd (0.088) > Hg (0.0146), with Mn, Cu, and Ni exceeding the Toxicity Reference Values. Pollution indices including contamination factor (CF), enrichment factor (EF), and the pollutant load index indicated minimal pollution (CF < 6, EF < 5, PLI < 1). Nevertheless, EF for Se reached 6.93, and Cd showed elevated potential ecological risks (Er > 40). The overall Risk Indices indicated low pollution risks. Non-carcinogenic health risks were negligible, though children showed relatively higher sensitivity to As and Cr exposure. Ni represented relatively higher carcinogenic risks for children. The source apportionment analyses collectively indicated that most heavy metals (As, Pb, Cd, Co, Ni, Cr, Cu, Se) suggest mixed geogenic and anthropogenic origins. In contrast, Hg showed a distinct source signature, indicating localized contamination. These findings provide important baseline data for sediment quality assessment, management and long-term monitoring of the Gorai River system.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-48853-0.Keywords: Gorai river, Sediment pollution, Heavy metal, Ecological risk assessment, Source apportionment, Human health riskSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences, Risk factors

Introduction

Heavy metal pollution has become a significant global issue owing to its toxicity, bioaccumulative characteristics, persistence, and non-degradable nature in aquatic ecosystems1–3. Rivers, particularly in developing countries such as Bangladesh, are under increasing pressure due to the growing level of pollutants; while, many river systems lack extensive and continuous monitoring of the environment4,5. Sediments in aquatic environments act both as reservoirs and sources of pollutants, which demonstrate the history of contamination of the environment6,7. As a result, the sediments have become essential matrices in evaluation of the extent and distribution of heavy metal contamination. Metal behavior in sediments is complex and dynamic and influenced by processes such as dissolution, sorption, desorption, and precipitation8,9.

The contamination of sediments with heavy metals may be dangerous to human health both directly and indirectly. The high concentrations of heavy metals found in a water body can lead to significant uptake by aquatic organisms, particularly fish, resulting in bioaccumulation within tissues and organs10. Furthermore, seafood, including seaweed, can absorb heavy metals from sediments, increasing the likelihood of heavy metals to infiltrate the food web and impact human health11,12. Moreover, river water is usually used in various domestic, and recreational activities, which may increase human exposure to contaminated sediments and water. Consequently, such exposure may pose adverse effects on human health13. The extent of these negative impacts depends on toxicity, persistence, difficulty in degradation, bioaccumulation capacity of the metals in the body, and biomagnification pathways in the food chain5,14.

Bangladesh lies within three major river systems of South Asia, namely Ganges-Padma, the Brahmaputra, Jamuna, and the Meghna. Rivers have long played a significant role in shaping the country’s ecological and socio-economic landscape. However, these river systems are currently facing increasing pressure from both natural and artificial influences. Gorai River is one of the largest distributaries of the Ganges and a key source of water in the south-west of the country15. It typically receives 15% of the Ganges’ annual flow and ultimately releases into the Bay of Bengal via the Madhumati and Baleswar Rivers16. The Passur and Sibsa Rivers have become the main source of the drainage route of Gorai water to the Bay. It also provides fresh water to the area through Bhairab and Mathabhanga Rivers. Dry-season discharges of the Gorai have been reduced significantly, reducing its capacity to transport sediments and resulting in continuous siltation of the channel and river mouth. Reduced drainage is among the main factors leading to the fine sediments accumulation in the Gorai system17. Despite its hydrological and ecological importance, the Gorai River has received relatively limited scientific attention compared with other major rivers in Bangladesh. This river plays an important role in maintaining the salinity levels at the coast, agriculture, aquaculture, and domestic water supply in numerous districts. Yet, its ecological health is increasingly threatened by rapid land-use changes, uncontrolled waste discharge, and ongoing sedimentation processes. The Gorai river represents a significant gap in research as despite its importance, the contamination of heavy metal in sediment has not been well studied.

Although several studies have investigated heavy metal contamination in other rivers of Bangladesh and reported concerning pollution levels18,19, the comprehensive assessment of heavy-metal contamination in the Gorai River remains scarce. Therefore, establishing baseline data on the heavy metals concentrations is essential for understanding contamination levels and supporting future monitoring programs. This will assist the environmental managers and policymakers to assess the degree of contamination, and identify problematic metals, evaluate ecological risks, and assess potential human health implications. In this context, the present study was conducted in the Gorai River located in the Southwestern Bangladesh, which is faced with significant challenges related to water availability and quality. The aims of the study were to (i) evaluate the spatial distributions of the selected heavy metals in sediments; (ii) find out the degree of the contamination and associated ecological risks using established pollution indices; (iii) identify potential sources of heavy metals using multivariate statistical analyses; and (iv) assess potential human health risks associated with sediment-bound heavy metals. This investigation will provide baseline information for future monitoring and management of heavy metal contamination in the Gorai River system.

Materials and methods

Study area description

The area of the basin of the Gorai River measures 15,160 km², and it lies between latitudes 21° 30’ N and 24° 0’ N, and longitudes 89° 0’ E and 90° 0’ E, has its origin in the Ganges at Talbaria, north of the Kushtia town, 19 km below Hardinge Bridge, and the northwestern part of Bangladesh at Bardia Point15,17. The Padma supplies freshwater to the Gorai, which transfers it to the Madhumati River, hence connects with the Nabaganga River. Gorai River is ecologically critical as it has contributed to the freshwater supply of the Sundarbans, aquatic biodiversity and productive floodplain farming, salinity control and hydrology in the region, and livelihoods of the neighboring population. Most of the rivers in this area are characterized by insignificant flows at the dry season due to the Farakka effect20. The kilns of bricks are right next to the riverbank and many metal-processing industries are close to the river. These facilities are likely contributors of particulate emissions, effluents, and metal-laden waste to the adjacent aquatic environment. The riverbank is bordered by agricultural fields, where fertilizer application, soil runoff can enhance the input of contaminants. The riverside is also adjacent to a public park, where recreational activities may exacerbate the influx of pollution. Together, these land-use features indicate a complex mixture of industrial, agricultural, and domestic influences on the river system. An electronic pump station for the irrigation project (GK project) is located in Bheramara, 12 km upstream of the Gorai Offtake on the Ganges River. Water from the river is pumped into the primary irrigation canal.

Sample collection, preparation, and analysis

The Gorai River is a distributary of the Padma River. This river is considered as a high-silt river due to an imbalance between water discharge and sediment load. Sampling was initiated from the upstream off-take point where the Gorai branches from the Padma and continued sequentially toward the downstream reaches. The surface sediment samples were collected in October 2024 from the 22 selected sampling points (0 to 20 cm depth) of the Gorai River. Sampling sites were numbered sequentially from upstream to downstream. Site 1 represents the most upstream location and Site 22 represents the most downstream section. (Fig. 1). Three replicate sediment samples were collected using a portable Ekman grab sampler from each location and amalgamated into a composite sample for each sediment sample. Samples were stored in fresh zipper poly bags individually and labeled appropriately. Sediments were dried, crushed well, and subsequently sieved using a 1 mm mesh strainer. The processed samples were stored in airtight plastic bottles at 4 °C until analysis21. Two grams of each sample was initially treated with 10 mL of 65% HNO₃; for blank samples, only 10 mL HNO₃ was utilized. The samples were digested on a hot plate with gradual heating starting at 40 °C until the solution had become clear22. 2 mL of H₂O₂ and 10 mL of HNO₃ were incorporated after cooling, and subsequently heated between 150 and 220 °C until a paste-like consistency was achieved23. After digestion, samples were filtered using Whatman No. 44 filter paper and diluted to 50 mL using distilled water, and stored to undergo subsequent instrumental analysis24,25. Duplicate samples were analyzed alongside the sediment samples to assess potential contamination and analytical precision26. Grain size analysis of the sediments was performed following the hydrometer method as described by Day27. Textural classes were determined by Marshall’s Triangular Co-ordinates as described by the USDA28. The organic carbon content of sediments was determined according to Walkley and Black’s (1934) wet oxidation method, as outlined by Jackson29. The organic matter of sediment was determined by multiplying the content of organic carbon with a factor of 1.724 (the Van Bemmelen factor) on the assumption that the organic matter of average sediment contains 58% organic carbon.

Map showing the Gorai River of Bangladesh with sampling point (Created using Arcmap 10.8, https://www.esri.com).

Metal levels were determined in triplicate through Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS) (NexION, Perkin-Elmer, USA) under optimized conditions to get high sensitivity and low levels of detection. The ICP-MS was routinely evaluated using a NexION Setup solution, which verified that the multi-element calibration curves exhibited linearity (R² > 0.996). Spike recovery (%) ranged between 90 and 110% confirming the precision of the instrumental analysis. Instrument precision was verified using triplicates and the RSD values ranged between 5 and 7%. The limits of detection (LOD) and limits of quantification (LOQ) were calculated as three and ten times the standard deviation of the procedural blanks, respectively. The recovery percentage (95–103%) was assessed using a standard reference material (CRM320 sediment, N = 3) for all metals. Table S1 summarizes the instrument settings along with LOD, LOQ, recovery percentage (%) for each metal.

Statistical analyses

Mathematical analysis and plotting were done using Microsoft excel 2019, Python 3.13, and IBM SPSS 26. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was also performed in IBM SPSS 26 to evaluate site-specific differences in heavy metal concentrations. Additionally, ArcMap 10.8 was employed for cartography and spatial analyses. To reduce interpolation errors, a river border was defined by digitizing a satellite picture (obtained on 04 June 2024 from the USGS Explorer) precisely depicting the course and width of the Gorai River. The correlation analysis conducted by Pearson was done using IBM SPSS 26 to determine the correlations between the variables being evaluated. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and clustering were utilized in Origin2024 to identify underlying patterns, grouping elements with shared sources or geochemical characteristics, while ensuring heterogeneity among clusters30. Additionally, the Positive Matrix Factorization (PMF) was developed to evaluate the fundamental sources of contaminants, optimizing the goal function by incorporating the uncertainties linked to each data using EPA PMF (version 5.0) 19. Various indices were used to assess the overall effects of metals in the study site that can be categorized into four namely: enrichment indices, pollution indices, health risk indices and ecological risk indices31(Table S2).

Results and discussion

Heavy metal concentration in sediment from Gorai River

The concentration of 10 heavy metals in sediments is presented by their mean, maximum, and minimum. The ANOVA results showed a very low p value (< 0.01), indicating statistically significant differences among the mean concentrations of heavy metals in the studied sites (Table S3). The increased As content at G-4 and G-5 is likely attributable to both natural and human-induced factors. As is prevalent in the Earth’s crust, polluting groundwater is a significant origin of high As levels49. There are two brick kilns near this site. Brick production often utilize materials capable of releasing As into the ecosystem, and the burning of fuel in this process can disseminate As in the adjacent region16. The highest value of Se was recorded towards the downstream of the Gorai River. Increased Pb concentrations in G-4 and G-5 were likely attributable to brick kilns beside the river, potentially augmenting Pb levels in river sediment. Brick kilns around the river emit Pb through combustion of low-quality coal and other fuels, with atmospheric deposition settling into river sediments50. This metal is capable of traveling long distances before eventually settling on land or in aquatic environments51. The concentration of Co and Ni increased as the river flowed downstream. Cd and Ni both had surpassed that of the Karnafuli River and approaching the tolerance level, while remaining beneath the shale value (Table 1).

The heightened Cd concentration in the Gorai River was probably from industrial effluents and agricultural runoff, that frequently include trace quantities of Cd. Considering the river’s geomorphic setting and surrounding land-use dynamics, the observed concentrations are plausibly influenced by effluents from nearby industrial activities and nutrient inputs from fertilizer use in adjacent agricultural zones. Cr concentrations varied from 6.37 mg/kg (G-22) to 38.13 mg/kg (G-18), which were lower than those recorded for the Payra River (44.59 mg/kg), but higher than those of the Rupsa, Passur and Halda Rivers (Table 1). Cu demonstrated a mean concentration of 18.66 ± 9.86 mg/kg, which is inferior to the concentration recorded for the Karnafuli River but superior to that of the Halda, Meghna Rivers (Table 1). This result exceeded the TRV, indicating a possible ecological concern. The average Mn content (367 ± 110.4 mg/kg) exceeded that of most comparable river systems except Payra River (Table 1). Its enrichment is likely attributable to natural weathering of Mn-rich rocks in the catchment that is enhanced by anthropogenic sources of influx which include but are not confined to, brick kilns and metal factories19. The Hg content was low among all heavy metals, where the average value was 0.0146 ± 0.0087 mg/kg. The identified values surpassed the minimal concentration documented in the Halda River (Table 1).

The measured heavy metal concentrations in Gorai River sediments indicate a blend of natural lithogenic sources and anthropogenic contributions. A comparative analysis with regional and global rivers highlights the moderate pollution level of the Gorai River, with specific cases of heightened concentrations, especially for Mn, Cu, and As. The grain size analysis of the sediment was sandy silt. The mean organic matter in sediments varied from 0.63 to 3.5%, showing an increase from upstream to downstream reaches of the river (Table S4).

Assessment of heavy metal pollution

The CF of the majority of metals exhibited a low to moderate level of contamination at the sampling stations. Among all metals, Se showed the highest CF values indicating that the contamination is relatively higher than the other metals. PLI represents the overall cumulative contamination of all metals at a location whereas EF evaluates the enrichment of a specific metal compared to its background value. PLI values were below 1, signifying the absence of major overall pollution, despite localized enrichment of certain metals (e.g., Se and Cd)53. The cumulative effect of multiple metals remains relatively low. (Fig. 3).

The EF values of the Gorai River vary between 0.169 (for Hg at G-21) to 6.93 (for Se at G12). Figure 4a illustrates the EF values of heavy metals in the sediments of the Gorai River. Throughout all instances, the enrichment factor (EF) values of As and Se were markedly elevated compared to other metals, falling within the range of 2 ≤ EF < 554, signifying moderate enrichment. In this research, relatively higher EF values for Se (2.86–6.93) indicate moderate to significant enrichment at several stations, suggesting potential anthropogenic contributions despite the generally low PLI values. In contrast, most other metals exhibited deficiency to minimal enrichment (EF < 2), implying that their presence is largely associated with natural geological sources.

In the majority of stations, Igeo levels exhibited negative readings, indicating minimal to no pollution for metals55. Se exhibited moderate pollution in terms of Igeo Index. Station G-20 exhibited marginally elevated Igeo values, especially for Cd, suggesting it neared the limit for moderate pollution; nonetheless, it ultimately stayed within the range of uncontaminated levels (Fig. 4b).

The Er findings indicated that Hg and Cd exhibited greater ecological concerns than other metals. Hg indicated high ecological risks (Er) at Stations G-4 (27.95), G-5(21.75), but Cd posed moderate ecological risk at Station G-20 with a figure of 59.98. Notwithstanding these elevated levels, the overall ecological risk persisted at a relatively low level. RI representing the aggregate of the PERI values for a site, provided a comprehensive evaluation of ecological risk56. All examined stations belonged to this group, with RI values varying from 27.81 at Site G-22 to 113.77 at Site G-20. This signified that the aggregate pollution risk across all sites was minimal except G-20 which shows moderate risk (Table 2).

Ecological risk factors (Er) and potential ecological risk indices (RI) of heavy metals in sediments of the Gorai River.

The concentrations of heavy metals As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb and Hg were below the ERL and ERM thresholds established by Sediment Quality Guidelines (SQGs), suggesting that adverse biological effects are improbable as a result of these metals in the river silt. The concentration of Ni is situated between the ERL and ERM thresholds, suggesting that biological effects may occur in the benthic environment, but are not expected to be frequent. This indicates that Ni may represent a potential ecological concern in certain sections of the river, although the overall sediment quality remains within acceptable limits. The sediment site ratings determined by SQGs, which considered the ERL/ERM thresholds46, indicated that 16 sites [G2, G3, G4, G5, G7, G8, G9, G10, G11, G15, G16, G17, G18, G19, G20, G21] were categorized as possessing intermediate sediment quality [> ERL limit] due to Ni concentration, whereas the other sites were categorized as exhibiting good sediment quality. The one value of Cu at point G21 (53 mg/kg) was between the ERL and ERM threshold (Table S2). Overall, the combined interpretation of multiple pollution indices (CF, EF, Igeo, PLI, Er, and RI) indicates that the Gorai River sediments are generally characterized by low to moderate contamination levels, although localized enrichment of Se, Cd, and Hg highlights the presence of potential contamination hotspots within the study area.

Health risk assessment

Non-carcinogenic health risk evaluations did not indicate significant risks for both adults and children57. The HI values for As (2.32 × 10− 1), and Cr (1.16 × 10− 1) in Children were close to the threshold limit. Carcinogenic risk assessment showed that CR and TCR values for all elements were along with the acceptable limit of 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁴, suggesting a low probability of cancer development from exposure58. Nonetheless, higher TCR values for children, specifically for As (8.97 × 10⁻⁶), Cr (2.22 × 10⁻⁶), and Ni (4.66 × 10⁻⁵), indicate the need for enhanced preventive measures (Table 3).

Source apportionment of the heavy metals

A correlation matrix is employed to evaluate the relationship among the analyzed heavy metals. The correlation matrix can identify a common metal source in the study area31. This correlation analysis of heavy metal concentrations in Gorai River sediment indicates strong interrelationships among the majority of the analyzed elements (Table S5). Strong to very strong positive correlations were identified between Pb and Mn (r = 0.99), Co and Ni (r = 0.994), As and Pb (r = 0.984), and Cd and Mn (r = 0.922), indicating that these metals likely derive from similar sources or share comparable geochemical behavior, including industrial effluents particularly from the metal processing, textile dyeing mills, urban runoff from densely populated area, or agricultural runoff from fertilized farmland. Significant correlations were identified between As and Mn (r = 0.982), Cd and Cu (r = 0.91), and Ni and Cr (r = 0.872), suggesting probable co-mobility and co-deposition processes within the sedimentary environment. The findings underscore the simultaneous presence and possible shared origin of multiple heavy metals, offering essential insights into pollution causes and the necessity for focused environmental management measures in the Gorai River basin19.

PCA was utilized to determine metal origins which shows eigenvalues, variances, and cumulative percentages (Table S6). PC1 explained 78.03% of the total variance and had positive loadings for similar magnitude with As (0.35), Pb (0.35), Cd (0.33), Co (0.34), Mn (0.35), and Ni (0.35), alongside moderately significant contributions from Cr (0.31), Se (0.31), and Cu (0.30) (Fig. 5). This grouping suggests a predominant geogenic influence associated with lithogenic inputs from weathering of Himalayan source rocks rich in ferromanganese minerals which explains the elevated and co-varying Mn, Co, and Ni concentrations as the primary contributing pathway59. The clustering of multiple metals also implies that these elements may be transported together by natural processes such as surface runoff, sediment resuspension, and mineral dissolution. Despite this, the slight enrichment of Pb, Cd, and As indicates additional anthropogenic contributions, likely from agricultural runoff of fertilizers containing Cd and As, pesticides with Pb and As residues and diffuse inputs from urban or industrial discharges. This grouping of metals suggests that the anthropogenic activities are superimposed over geogenic inputs60,61. PC2 explaining 11.86% of the variance, is characterized by a strong loading of Hg (0.89). The distinct separation of Hg from the other metals indicates a localized and predominantly anthropogenic source. Potential contributors include mercury-containing waste from medical and electronic equipment, small-scale artisanal gold extraction where Hg is used for amalgamation, and effluents from industrial processes such as chlor-alkali production62. Unlike the other metals, Hg has a high affinity for organic matter and fine-grained sediments, which may enhance its retention and spatial separation in the aquatic system63.

Cluster analysis (CA) using Ward’s linkage technique and Euclidean distance was used to assess heavy metal relationships64. The dendrogram shows three clusters, revealing these metals’ likely origins and geochemical properties (Fig. S1). Cluster 1 contains As, Pb, Mn, Co, and Ni, suggesting a mix of geogenic and anthropogenic origins such as industrial effluents and agricultural runoff65. Their proximity suggests similar environmental processes or sediment matrix co-association. Cluster 2, which includes Cr, Cd, and Cu, is often found in metal finishing, electroplating, and dye manufacture, suggesting a similar source, maybe industrial or urban wastewater discharges. Cluster 3 contains Se and Hg, with Hg having a distinct branch at the lowest similarity level, indicating a point-source anthropogenic input such as medical waste, batteries, or artisanal mining.

Figure 6 displays the distribution of ten metals in the Gorai River sediments66. 220 observations (10 metals × 22 sites) were evaluated providing a sufficient dataset for dependable PMF outcomes. This assessment established the number of factors at 2–5 and conducted 20 runs. According to the PMF processing results, when the factor number was set to 3, the Q value was minimized and stabilized, with most residuals falling between − 3 and 3, and the signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio of all HMs surpassing 9 (except Se, Cd, Hg). Factor 1 exhibited the highest overall contribution, accounting for approximately 48.2% of the total heavy metal mass. This factor was strongly associated with Cu, Cd, and Pb, with loadings of 61.5%, 48.9%, and 33.4%, respectively. These are associated with multiple anthropogenic activities such as mining, metal industries, pesticides, municipal waste. It is important to note that while PCA grouped Cd and Pb within PC1 under a geogenic-dominant component, PMF Factor 1 identifies these same metals as predominantly anthropogenically sourced. This reflects the dual geogenic-anthropogenic nature of Cd and Pb in the study area, consistent with the CA Cluster 1 findings and the enrichment factor analysis. The PMF result suggests that although these metals partially co-vary with lithogenic elements, their mass contributions are disproportionately driven by anthropogenic inputs. Factor 2 accounted for 29.5% and was predominantly influenced by Hg and Se, contributing 69.60% and 40.40% respectively. Previous literature demonstrate that coal burning in the brick kilns may be a major source of Hg emissions, which then deposits into the soil via atmospheric deposition67,68. Factor 3 contributed for 22.30% and was mainly driven by Co and Ni, contributing 44.2% and 43.4%, respectively. Co and Ni are associated with industrial activities and agricultural runoff.

Conclusions

The present study provides baseline information for future environmental monitoring and management by presenting one of the first comprehensive evaluations of the heavy metal contamination of the Gorai River. The findings indicate that majority of heavy metals in the sediments occur at comparatively low levels, and pollution indices (CF, PLI, and Igeo) generally classify the river sediments as low to moderately polluted. Nonetheless, the EF values of Se showed moderate to significant enrichment indicating possible anthropogenic influence. Ecological assessment revealed localized hotspots of Hg and Cd specially at G-20, indicating site-specific contamination despite generally low overall pollution levels. SQG analysis revealed that the Ni levels were above the ERL threshold at 16 of the 22 sampling points, indicating potential biological effects on benthic organisms in certain parts of the river. Carcinogenic values were generally within acceptable limits; despite this, elevated risks for As, Cr, and Ni in children warrant precautionary attention and further monitoring. Source analyses revealed indicative contamination trends, highlighting the need for integrated management of industrial discharges, agricultural runoff, and municipal waste to protect the ecological health and long-term viability of the Gorai River ecosystem. Considering the ecological and economic importance of the Gorai River within the GK irrigation project, long-term monitoring programs and pollution control strategies are recommended to safeguard sediment quality and aquatic health.

Limitations

A systematic transect-based sampling design was not implemented due to logistical constraints. Future investigations will be planned with denser sampling networks and transect-based approaches to more accurately capture spatial heterogeneity. Bioavailable fractions, biodiversity endpoints, or food web transfer dynamics were not assessed despite the ecological significance of the Gorai River’s linkage to the Sundarbans, due to the lack of laboratory facilities. Source identification was primarily based on multivariate statistical techniques such as PCA and CA. Advanced approaches such as isotopic tracers or conditional probability analysis were not applied due to analytical and resource constraints.

A collagenous extracellular matrix regulates germline gene expression in the sea star embryo
Authors: Gerardo Reyes, Nathalie Oulhen, Gary Wessel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The sea star Patiria miniata uses cell–cell signaling mechanisms in the inductive process of germline formation and here we test if these cells require the extracellular matrix (ECM) in this process. The ECM is a network of proteins and long-chain sugar polymers necessary for structural support, cell signaling, and migration. Collagen is a fundamental component of the ECM network that provides tensile strength and structural integrity, in addition to interacting with other proteins such as laminin and integrins. Here we used β-aminopropionitrile (BAPN), a well-known inhibitor of lysyl oxidase (LOX; an enzyme important for cross-linking mature collagen), a morpholino antisense oligonucleotide to LOX2A, and Col003, an inhibitor of collagen processing to test for a role of the collagen-based ECM with cells that form the germ line. A DEG-RNA-seq approach was used to broadly test for genes responsive to the ECM at different times in development. Candidates resulting from this analysis were subjected to qPCR analysis, which showed that some ECM responsive genes markedly increase gene expression, including eIF5A1, LvN1.2, ATF3, and the newly described BAPN-Associated Target with Malformed Archenteron (BATMAN), identified here. Furthermore, genes involved in germline formation in this embryo differentially respond to the ECM, including Nanos3, Vasa, PIWI, and the signaling factors BMP2/4, Nodal, and Wnt8. Our results show that disruption of the ECM dysregulates germline gene expression and normal gut morphogenesis during specific development stages. This paradigm adds to a growing list of mechanisms in how primordial germ cells use the ECM in an inductive mode.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20746-8.

Introduction

The extracellular matrix (ECM) plays an essential role in maintaining and supporting different biological mechanisms that include structural support, cell communication, tissue repair, regeneration, as well as tissue homeostasis1–3 One of the major, and conserved ECM components is collagen, a triple helix protein containing glycine and proline-rich repeats (Gly-X–Y)4,5. Collagens are synthesized as procollagens containing long extensions at the N-terminal and C-terminal ends that retain the intracellular solubility of the growing polypeptides. Collagen monomers assume a triple helix assembly within the rough endoplasmic (ER), and the N- and C-terminal pro-peptides of procollagen are cleaved off outside the cell by procollagen N-proteinases and procollagen C-proteinases, respectively, forming tropocollagen units. These tropocollagen units then self-assemble into fibrils, which are stabilized by the action of lysyl oxidases (LOX). LOX-mediated cross-linking of collagen molecules imparts the characteristic tensile strength to the fibrils6,7 and tissues that lack LOX activity (e.g., aortic wall) have compromised ECM, often leading to fibril formation diseases such as aortic aneurysms and Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome phenotypes8–11.

The collagenous catch connective tissue in echinoderms, also known as mutable collagenous tissue (MCT), exhibits unusual collagen functions. In sea cucumbers, the collagenous extracellular matrix (ECM) plays a crucial role in the transition between the soft, standard, and stiff states of the catch connective tissue12. The extracellular matrix in sea urchins is essential for gastrulation13, brittle stars require ECM deposition for arm regeneration3, and the sea star Patiria miniata, requires ECM processing for development of the anterior coelomic pouches14. However, the extent of the ECM’s impact, especially in gene expression and formation of the germ line, has not been tested.

Different from a sea urchin, which uses an acquired mechanism of germline formation15,16, the closely related sea star uses an inductive mechanism to form the germ line. The inductive mechanism is used by mammals and relies on cell-to-cell communication to guide the cell to become the germline17.Several studies show how cell–cell signaling is used in this process in echinoderms18,19and here, we test the hypothesis that the ECM functions in the inductive mechanism of germ cell formation.

The primordial germ cells (PGCs) of the sea star Patiria miniata begin developing as a bump-like structure on the left side of the gut approximately three days after fertilization. This structure becomes more pronounced by the early larva stage, around day four after fertilization. Many germline factors that were initially expressed broadly in the embryo become restricted to the posterior enterocoel (PE), the structure that contains the PGCs18–20. One of the germline factors is Vasa, an ATP-dependent DEAD-box helicase, which has been shown to be selectively retained in the PGCs by the larval stage16,19–21. Nanos3, an RNA-binding translational regulator, is also enriched in the posterior enterocoel at the larval stage through Nodal signaling20. Piwi (involved in the piRNA pathway) is another conserved germline factor enriched in the PE at the larva stage in the sea star19.

Echinoderms exhibit a complex ECM with a vast diversity of collagen genes22. One approach to studying collagen function involves systematically knocking down each collagen gene; vertebrates have over 25 distinct collagen genes, possibly as many in sea stars22. However, this method is very limited and can be misleading since each test would require gene-specific analysis of compensatory activity in collagen synthesis upon gene knockouts. Instead, here we employ pharmacological agents to investigate the broader impacts of collagen disruption in the developing sea star embryo and knockdowns of LOX activity to test the hypothesis.

β-aminopropionitrile (BAPN) is a well-known pharmacological agent used to study induced aortic diseases in mice, including aortic aneurysms and dissection (a condition where the inner layer of the aorta tears, allowing blood to rush through it)23,24. BAPN has also been shown to affect cross-linking in bone collagen in a dose-dependent manner in rabbits and to increase hemorrhage incidence in turkeys25,26. BAPN works by inhibiting the activity of lysyl oxidase (LOX) enzymes, with collagen and elastin cross-linking27. We also incorporate a second, non-overlapping inhibitor of collagen processing (Col003;28) with the reasoning that both inhibitors may have side effects, but that the functional overlap in phenotype may instruct our conclusions of collagen function. Our results suggest that the ECM regulates many gene activities at distinct times during early development, including germline-related genes important for forming the sea star’s primordial germ cells (PGCs).

Material and methods

Animals

Adult Patiria miniata animals were collected by Peter Halmay (San Diego Fishermen’s Working Group) or Josh Ross (South Coast Bio-Marine) off the Long Beach coast of California. Embryos were cultured as described previously20,29. All embryos were cultured at 16 °C by standard methods30.

Pharmacological treatments and sample collection

β-aminopropionitrile monofumarate salt (BAPN; SIGMA), prepared as a 100 mM stock solution in deionized water (dH2O), was added to 10 mL of filtered seawater (FSW) embryo culture water at final concentrations of 0.8 mM and 1.2 mM. Col003; ≥ 98% (HPLC; SIGMA), was prepared as a 0.5 M stock solution in Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO), added to 10 mL of FSW embryo cultured at 5uM final concentration. Controls of dH2O or DMSO for BAPN or Col003, respectively, were added to the proper control culture. BAPN and Col003 were added at different time windows, 1hpf-Day1, Day1-Day2, and Day2-Day3; in some cases, we incubated the embryo cultures in the drug for up to 3 days. Embryos were collected at the end of the time window for morphological analysis, RNA extraction, and sequencing. Similarly, a different embryo batch was prepared and collected as described above, for RT-qPCR analysis.

RNA sequencing

Embryos were denatured in Trizol (Invitrogen) samples collected at various time points, with approximately 200–250 embryos used per sample. RNA was isolated from the Trizol solution (according to manufacturer’s recommendations) and poly A + RNA was sequenced from paired ends (Novogene).

RT-qPCR analysis

To assess gene expression changes following BAPN treatment, quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed on separate biological batches of treated and untreated P. miniata embryos. While experimental conditions mirrored those of the BAPN RNA-seq samples (designated as E#), the embryos used for qPCR were not part of the sequencing cohort.

All qPCR analyses were performed as follows. RNA was isolated using the RNeasy Micro kit (Qiagen). First-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using Maxima reverse transcriptase (Life Technologies). RT-qPCR was performed using the Quant Studio 3 instrument. All primers are listed in Supplementary Table S3. Experiments were run in technical and biological triplicates, normalized to ubiquitin. Student’s t-test (two-tailed, two-sample equal variance t-test) was used to determine p-value significance (P-value < 0.05 is annotated as “*” while a P-value < 0.005 is annotated as “**”).

In situ hybridization

Whole-mount in situ RNA hybridization was performed using a digoxigenin-labeled RNA probe, as previously described31. Complementary DNA (cDNA) from a combination of day 2 and day 3 RNA was used as a template for polymerase chain reactions (PCRs), using specific primers of each gene of interest and either adding an SP6 RNA polymerase binding sequence in the 5′ ends of reverse primers or cloning the PCR product into the pGEMT easy plasmid20. Genes were amplified using primers shown in Supplementary Table S2. Amplification products were transcribed with SP6 RNA polymerase to yield an antisense RNA probe with the DIG RNA Labeling Kit (SP6/T7; Roche Applied Science, Indianapolis, IN). Embryos were fixed and hybridized, and the signals were detected by color or by fluorescence32.

Gene manipulation

Custom morpholino anti-sense oligos (MASOs), which block mRNA translation, were synthesized by Gene-Tools. The sea star LOX2A MASO sequence used was 5’-CATTTTCTTGATGACTCGCAAG-3’. A control MASO against the sea urchin FoxY was injected with the sequence 5’-TGCACACCAGGACAACGATGTTTCC-3’. To prevent aggregation, MASOs were warmed for 5 min at 37 °C before injection. They were then injected into sea star immature oocytes at a concentration of 1 mM with 10,000 MW fluorescent dextran without glycerol. RT-qPCR was subsequently used to test the continuous effect of these MASOs on gene expression throughout development.

Results

The extracellular matrix regulates sea star embryonic development

To test the effects of collagen integrity on sea star embryos, we incubated them with 0.8 mM BAPN at various time points: From 1 h-post-fertilization (hpf) to hatched blastula (HB), from hatched blastula to mid-gastrula (MG), from mid-gastrula to late gastrula (LG)/early larvae, and from late gastrula to larvae (L). Only the HB-MG and MG-LG were used for RNA sequencing. In addition, we incubated blastulae for two days in BAPN to test the effects of continuous exposure on development (Fig. 1). During the 1hpf-HB treatment window, the developing embryos failed to form a normal blastocoel; they appeared to be delayed in development or contained cells that were still dividing but poorly linked in forming the blastocoel, and from this phenotype we decided to exclude them from further analysis. Chronic treatment (more than 3 days) of BAPN on developing embryos resulted in lethality. Pulse treatments of BAPN revealed windows particularly sensitive to the ECM in development, especially in endoderm development at gastrulation. During the D1-D2 (the nomenclature here is D = day, and numbers refer to which day) time window, the endodermal gut did not develop properly; it was more elongated and curved than the controls. Embryos incubated in BAPN for an extra day (D1-D3) appeared more deformed than those recovering from BAPN on D2 to D3. In addition, the gut was more elongated than at D2. At day 3, the control embryos developed coelomic pouches (CP), and the posterior enterocele (PE, the germline precursor) became visible; however, these structures were missing in the two-day BAPN-treated embryos (D1 to D3). The gut defects were partially rescued in embryos placed in seawater lacking BAPN (treated from D1 to D2 and washed from D2 to D3). However, compared to control embryos, they still had features of earlier embryos. Although rescued embryos temporally should have reached the larval stage, like their sibling day 4 control embryos, the rescued embryos instead resembled day 3, late gastrula-stage embryos.

Embryos treated with BAPN from day 2 to day 3 showed a substantial defect of the most anterior gut region containing the coelomic mesoderm. The BAPN treatment resulted in a lack of coelomic pouch formation and a failure to form the mouth compared to controls. Furthermore, embryos treated with BAPN from day 3 to day 4, showed PE formation even though its full restriction to the left side was delayed or abnormal in 20% of the embryos (Fig. 1).

Gene enrichment analysis shows compensatory activity for the ECM.

A gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis on samples treated with BAPN from day 1 to day 2 (E3) compared to control (E4) shows how different processes are affected by BAPN (Fig. 3). On the y-axis, we highlighted molecular function (MF) in blue, cellular components (CC) in green, and biological processes (BP) in violet. Some of the most upregulated cellular components are extracellular-related processes, i.e., extracellular matrix and extracellular region, suggesting embryos treated with BAPN might have activated compensatory gene expression for ECM-homeostasis. BAPN affects collagen maturation by inhibiting LOX activity, and the DEG dataset also showed that LOX2A (LOC119735518), the sea star LOXL2 homolog, was upregulated across the stages analyzed (Supplemental Data). RNA in situ hybridization for this LOX2A reveals that its expression is enriched in the posterior enterocoel (PE) and at the tips of the coelomic pouches in the sea star larva (Fig. S4C). Lysyl oxidase like 2 (LOXL2) catalyzes the cross-linking of collagen fibers, which is essential for collagen stability and strength33. In addition, genes involved in protein synthesis, such as tRNA aminoacylation and amino acid activation, are upregulated, which could be related to a compensatory increase in the translation of the genes involved in the ECM structure and regulation. On the other hand, the GO enrichment analysis of downregulated processes reveals that genes involved in protein remodeling or tissue remodeling, such as endopeptidase complex and peptidase complex, are downregulated in embryos treated with BAPN compared to the controls at D1-D2 (Fig. 3B). The downregulation of these processes could indicate a relationship between the increased ECM expression (extracellular matrix and extracellular region) due to the downregulated crosslinking effects of BAPN on collagen. RT-qPCR analysis supports the RNA-sequencing findings.

We then tested, by qPCR, some of the most upregulated genes and an extreme downregulated gene from the DEG list (Supplementary Table S1). All the transcripts tested by qPCR support the RNA sequencing dataset (Fig. 4A). In addition, many of these BAPN upregulated transcripts were barely/or not detectable in the control embryos by RNA in situ hybridization but were highly expressed in the BAPN-treated embryos (Fig. 4B). These transcripts included eukaryotic translation initiation factor 5A-1 like (elF5A1), a gene necessary for the proliferation of eukaryotic cells34,35, cyclic AMP-dependent transcription factor ATF-3-like (ATF3), which is involved in metabolic homeostasis and regulating cell cycle and apoptosis36,37, development-specific protein LvN1.2-like (LvN1.2), this gene in sea urchins is specific to the endoderm of the embryo in the hindgut-midgut regions and might be involved in gut specification, which we will refer to it as PmLvN1.238,39. We also found an uncharacterized gene, LOC119745626, one of our embryos’ most responsive transcripts to the BAPN treatments. Due to the nature of its spatial expression after BAPN treatment, we name the gene BAPN Associated Target with a Malformed Archenteron (BATMAN). The ECM affects germline gene expression.

Sea stars have a germline formed by induction, which requires cell-to-cell communication to guide the fate and localization of these cells19,20. We tested if inhibition of collagen maturity through BAPN would affect germline genes, such as Nanos3 and Vasa. The abundance of these transcripts did not change significantly during the HB-MG (or day 1 to day 2) window (Fig. 5A), but increased during the MG-LG (or day 2 to day 3) window (Fig. 5B), at a time when the germ line fate appears to be initiated. We also examined the spatial distribution of Vasa mRNA using fluorescence in situ hybridization. After BAPN treatments, Vasa expression was no longer enriched in the typical areas or cells. Instead, it appeared diffused throughout the embryo’s gut (Fig. 5C, D). Additionally, we tested signaling pathways involved in germline formation and differentiation19,20. When treated with BAPN, Nodal mRNA was downregulated early on during early gastrulation, from day 1 to day 2, while upregulated in late gastrula, from day 2 to day 3 (Fig. 5A, B). Nodal is an essential signaling component in forming the asymmetric PE, the site of germline formation in this embryo. Wnt8, a known signaling ligand for germ cells in various animals, was upregulated in both time windows, from day 1 to day 2 and from day 2 to day 3. Although our BAPN RNA sequencing did not detect germline genes such as Nanos3, Vasa, or Piwi as differentially expressed genes (DEGs), this may be due to their low abundance and limited presence in a small number of cells. Since one of the most striking phenotypes observed in the BAPN-treated embryos was the deformation of the gut, we tested for the expression of gut markers such as FoxA, a gene involved in gut development42. During the HB-MG treatment window, FoxA remains unchanged (Fig. 5A); however, at the MG-LG treatment window, FoxA is upregulated (Fig. 5B). Moreover, based on our RNA-seq results, some processes that could be linked to gut function are downregulated, for example, heme binding and iron ion binding (Fig. 3B). If the gut is losing its identity or ability to function correctly, a compensatory activity may be to increase the expression of genes like FoxA to maintain gut identity. Additionally, alkaline phosphatase activity, characteristic of the sea star embryo gut, is reduced in BAPN-treated embryos. This finding further supports the notion that the identity, or at least the function of the gut, was compromised when the development of the ECM was disrupted (Fig. S1).

Perturbation of the ECM with a distinct small molecule inhibitor

While BAPN is a common reagent used to study LOX and collagen function with their associated diseases24,43, more recently developed pharmacological reagents are employed to inhibit different steps in collagen biosynthesis, creating distinct collagen phenotypes. We selected Col003, which has been shown to specifically inhibit collagen biosynthesis. Col003 is a small molecule that competitively inhibits Hsp47, a chaperone found in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) that is essential for the unique folding of collagen and its secretion outside the cell. Its use thereby eventually depletes collagen from the ECM28. Thus, embryos in BAPN or Col003 are exposed to distinct molecular environments and may respond with distinct transcriptional activities.

Col003 effects were tested in the same time window experiments as was used for testing BAPN. In normal development, Nanos3 is specific to the germline of the sea star embryo, and it is present in the PE of the larvae19. In the treatment regime, Nanos3 mRNA was downregulated during the 1hpf-HB time window but then upregulated at the later ones, HB-MG and MG-LG (Fig. S2, Fig. 6). Vasa expression increased drastically during the 1hpf-HB time window, but it was downregulated at the HB-MG time window, and no significant change was observed in the MG-LG time window (Fig. S2, Fig. 6). Piwi’s expression follows the same pattern as that of Vasa, being upregulated during the 1hpf-HB time window, downregulated during the HB-MG time window, and unchanged in the MG-LG time window (Fig. S2, Fig. 6). Wnt is an important signaling pathway involved in germline formation and stemness44,45. Here, we observed that Wnt3 was downregulated at every single point of treatment, but not Wnt8, which did not change during the 1hpf-HB time window but was downregulated during the HB-MG time window and upregulated in the MG-LG time window (Fig. S2, Fig. 6). Nodal and BMP2/4, members of the TGF-β superfamily, were downregulated in every time window, except for Nodal during the 1hpf-HB, where it was upregulated (Fig. S2, Fig. 6). Additionally, the phenotypes observed in the Col003-treated embryos showed both differences and similarities to those treated with BAPN. During the HB to MG time window, the Col003-treated embryos showed a very underdeveloped gut, whereas in BAPN-treated embryos, this gut was often elongated (Fig. 1 and Fig. 6C). In the MG to LG time window, the coelomic pouches were underdeveloped in both BAPN and Col003-treated embryos. In contrast, only the Col003-treated embryos showed a loss of cells at the top of their archenteron (Fig. 6C). C) Vasa mRNA in situ hybridization on treated embryos at different time points showed disruption of Vasa spatial expression and restriction to the left side of the embryo’s gut at MG-LG and LG-L time points. Col003 final concentration was 5uM. P-value < 0.05 is * while P-value < 0.005 is **.

In addition, Vasa mRNA in situ hybridization revealed a disruption in the normal spatial expression of Vasa at the various time points tested (Fig. 6C). During the HB-MG time window, Vasa mRNA was found at the remains of the top of the archenteron but expression at the base of the gut, as seen in control embryos was unclear. During the MG-LG window, Col003-treated embryos showed Vasa expression throughout the middle of the gut instead of being restricted to the left side, as in the controls. However, the top of the archenteron displayed somewhat normal Vasa expression. Lastly, at the LG-L time point, Vasa expression was present in the remains of the coelomic pouches but appeared less specific, possibly because the coelomic pouches were mostly developed before the drug was added, and Vasa expression was not fully disrupted. Also, the Vasa-expressing primordial germ cells did not fully migrate to the left side of the embryo’s gut, unlike in the controls (Fig. 6C).

We also tested the genes we used to validate our BAPN RNA-seq results in our Col003 treatments. Every transcript tested (except elF5A1 at the MG-LG time point) followed the pattern observed in the qPCR and RNA seq of BAPN-treated embryos, suggesting that Col003 and BAPN have similar effects on gene expression. However, Col003 might be more specific, as the changes were not as dramatic (in terms of expression fold changes and phenotypes) as they were with BAPN (Fig. S3).

Discussion

We used two different inhibitors and a MASO knockdown of LOX2A (Supplemental Figures 4, 5) to disrupt the collagenous ECM and found that they have complementary results. This suggests that the ECM is critical in early embryogenesis, influencing both morphogenesis and specific gene activity. Although we do not have antibodies to the collagens in the sea star nor most of the genes identified in the RNA-seq study, we conclude that the embryonic cells are in constant feedback with their ECM since disrupting it at different time points results in morphological and transcriptional changes. Further analysis of how different the ECM structure is after BAPN, Col003, or LOX2A MASO would shed light on how these embryonic cells are affected. For example, BAPN treatment in this embryo greatly disrupts laminin immunolabeling in the anterior region of the gut, revealing the nature of the interactive matrix of the ECM14. Our DEG lists show an upregulation of many laminins and integrins transcripts, both essential ECM components. The upregulation of these ECM components may be linked to a disorganized ECM environment characterized by an unusually high expression of non-collagen ECM components (laminin and integrin) due to collagen disruption.

Were these BAPN-treated embryos simply developmental delayed, they could be an interesting and impactful phenotype to explore, but that is not what is occurring here. By morphogenesis metrics alone we conclude that the embryo is not “simply” delayed; 1) the gut in HB-MG treated embryos is nearly twice the length of the control embryos. A gut delayed in development would be shorter, and it is not. This is a very interesting phenotype of accelerated elongation, not a delayed elongation. 2) The gut of the MG-LG treated embryos is the same length as the control, but it has no evidence of coelomic pouches developing from it. These morphogenetic phenotypes alone speak to the lack of overall developmental delay, and instead to differential sensitivity to the extracellular matrix as a mechanism of imparting information of the cells in development.

Our RNA sequencing analysis showed that different GO terms are enriched in the BAPN-treated embryos. BAPN is a disruptor of collagen maturation by inhibiting LOX cross-linking activity. However, this might not be a complete disruption since some collagens may be less dependent on LOX activity; a small, but significant amount of mature collagen might still form, or remain from an earlier deposition in our BAPN-treated embryos. Additionally, the RNA-seq results showed an upregulation of biological processes, such as tRNA aminoacylation, which is generally involved in translation and plays a key role in forming the extracellular matrix and remodeling of the ECM. The increase in such processes suggests that the embryonic cells detected a deficiency of mature collagen and respond to it by increasing protein synthesis for the genes involved in ECM remodeling. Downregulated biological processes, such as DNA replication and DNA replication initiation, suggest that cellular division may also be a process regulated by the ECM and may lead to a decrease in the cell cycle frequency. Embryos placed in fresh AFSW slowly returned to normal healthy embryos; however, our RNA sequencing showed they were delayed in their biological development. The integrity of the ECM in a developing embryo is crucial, as it provides structural tensile strength and facilitates cell-to-cell interactions with its environment via integrins (cell surface receptors)46,47. Disruption of the collagenous ECM is probably not only affecting its tensile strength but also how cells interact with each other and their environment, potentially impacting the homeostasis of various cellular processes such as proliferation, pluripotency, and cell identity.

Our pharmacological approach suggests that ECM participates in the formation of all germ layers during the sea star’s early embryonic development. The ectoderm was affected and often resulted in smaller-sized embryos. The mesoderm and endoderm were the most notorious defects, having no coelomic pouches formed and an elongated gut, respectively. Moreover, ECM also regulates germline-related genes. Nanos3 is upregulated when it typically decreases and becomes restricted to PE. It is possible that the delay in Nanos3 mRNA degradation is a consequence of a delay in the PE restriction of the sea star embryo. Nodal is important for the asymmetrical establishment of the PGCs20. Interestingly, the downregulation of Nodal transcript did not increase Vasa expression, which we would have expected, and on the contrary, Vasa observed a downregulation in our D1-D2 Col003 treatments. Perhaps Nodal downregulation leads to a loss of cell identity for the PGCs and not just failure to be restricted. More experiments testing PGCs’ identity are needed to test this hypothesis. Vasa transcript seems to be increasing in the Col003 in the 1hpf-D1 time window (Fig. S2); however, we believe this is due to the inability of the embryo to properly form a blastula and to begin degrading and restricting Vasa mRNA20,48. Interestingly, BAPN and Col003 affect Vasa transcript expression at different development stages (Fig. 5, Fig. 6). This is possible because while they both inhibit collagen, they do so at different steps in its synthesis. Col003 inhibits collagen triple helix formation by targeting Hsp47 in the ER thus inhibiting collagen secretion globally. BAPN blocks collagen cross-linking via lysyl oxidase activity once tropocollagen is formed in the ECM. If tropocollagen is still being synthesized or released, some form of collagen fibrils may still be made. However, due to the decrease in LOX activity to cross-link them (due to BAPN effects), these fibrils will not be sufficiently stable to maintain a well-established collagenous ECM. In contrast, Col003 treatments likely suppress collagen fibril formation significantly as it acts upstream in the synthesis pathway. Further experiments are needed to compare collagen presence and structure in BAPN versus Col003 treatments.

LOX is a key player in collagen formation, and without it, collagen cross-linking is significantly impaired8. While our drug treatments to disrupt collagen formation showed effects on germline genes and other diverse processes (Figs. 3, 5, and 6), the LOX2A MASO approach shows little to no change in the transcriptional expression of germline genes (Fig. S4B), previously analyzed genes, or gut gene markers (Fig. S5). One possible explanation for these results is that another LOX in the sea star genome compensates for the absence of LOX2A. Further analysis of other potential LOX genes in the sea star revealed a second LOX2A (LOC119738367), which we will refer to as LOX2Ab, that was missing from Echinobase (www.echinobase.org)49, but present in NCBI. Future studies will involve knocking down this new putative LOX2Ab alone or in combination with LOX2A to determine its effects on embryo development, germline gene expression, and whether there is a compensatory or dosage-dependent effect between them.

The complexity of the sea star ECM is significant and many components can have crucial roles in the development of the embryo and the establishment of important cell lineages like the PGCs. For example, the tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases (TIMPs) maintains and organizes germline stem niche in the Drosophila ovary50 and there is also evidence that the ECM can control cell migration based on the stiffness of the substrate51–53. In addition, the ECM is responsible for guiding PGC migration, and pluripotency in birds, worms, and mice54–57. Many collagens and collagen-associated genes are present in the sea urchin; searching for “collagen” in Echinobase reveals a long list of them, and the sea star is probably not an exception, although the genome of this animal is less annotated. In humans, many collagens are tissue-specific, while others are more widespread4. A similar pattern might exist in the sea star embryo; however, further analysis is needed to explore these possibilities. In addition to collagen, other ECM proteins could be involved in germline formation. For example, through our analysis, we observed that a heparan sulfate proteoglycan (HSPG) gene was downregulated and explicitly localized to the gut and PE of the sea star embryo (Fig. S6). Heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs) play crucial roles in cell adhesion, proliferation, cell signaling, and extracellular matrix (ECM) organization58,59. It would be insightful to investigate their function in the animal’s PGCs, the PE, after knockdown. The growing body of literature on the role of the extracellular matrix (ECM) in various processes in other echinoderms suggests that ECM is intricately linked to many biological functions in these animals. Overall, the findings presented here demonstrate that the ECM, particularly collagen synthesis and integrity, is crucial for development and is required for normal activity of germline cells in the sea star embryo.

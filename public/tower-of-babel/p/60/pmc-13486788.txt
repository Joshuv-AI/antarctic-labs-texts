Associations between Red Blood Cell Folate Forms and Demographic, Physiologic, and Lifestyle Factors in the United States Population: Cross-Sectional Data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey August 2021–August 2023
Authors: Zia Fazili, Christine M Pfeiffer, Maya R Sternberg, Phuong Ngac, Ming Zhang, Krista S Crider, Jacqueline Leachman, Anne Williams, Jaime J Gahche, Neelima Paladugula, Kelly Hong, Christina Fischer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRed blood cell (RBC) total folate (TFOL) is an indicator of long-term folate status. Data on RBC folate forms are lacking. They may inform associations with health-related indicators.ObjectivesWe aimed to describe associations between RBC folate forms and different factors in the United States population.MethodsWe used data for 5 biologically active RBC folate forms [5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-methylTHF), unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA), tetrahydrofolate (THF), 5-formyltetrahydrofolate (5-formylTHF), and 5,10-methenyltetrahydrofolate (5,10-methenylTHF); sum of which is TFOL] and 1 oxidation product of 5-methylTHF (MeFox) in persons aged ≥6 y participating in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2021–2023 cycle. We excluded pregnant or lactating females. We assessed bivariate associations between folate biomarkers [concentrations for TFOL, 5-methylTHF, THF, and MeFox; prevalences ≥limit of detection (LOD) for UMFA, 5-formylTHF, and 5,10-methenylTHF] and demographic, physiologic, and lifestyle variables in persons ≥20 y, accounting for the complex sample design. We estimated the impact of covariates on biomarker concentrations or odds ratios after covariate adjustment.ResultsIn persons ≥6 y (n=7126), geometric mean concentrations (nmol/L) were 913 (TFOL), 835 (5-methylTHF), 26.3 (THF), and 161 (MeFox); prevalences ≥LOD (%) were 43.6 (UMFA), 40.6 (5-formylTHF), and 73.0 (5,10-methenylTHF); and contributions to RBC TFOL were 92.7% (5-methylTHF), 5.02% (THF), and 1.61% (5,10-methenylTHF), varying by race/Hispanic origin. In persons ≥20 y, fasting status was not associated with biomarker concentrations or the prevalence ≥LOD. Biomarkers were significantly associated before and after covariate adjustment with age (TFOL; 5-methylTHF; THF; 5,10-methenylTHF), sex (MeFox), race/Hispanic origin (TFOL; 5-methylTHF; THF; MeFox), body mass index (TFOL; 5-methylTHF), tobacco use (TFOL; THF; MeFox), and folic acid supplement use (TFOL; 5-methylTHF; THF; MeFox; 5-formylTHF; 5,10-methenylTHF). Covariates explained ≤21% of the variation of 5-methylTHF, THF, or MeFox concentrations.ConclusionsFolic acid supplement use was the strongest predictor of RBC folate biomarker concentrations and detection. Differences in the methyl-to-nonmethyl makeup of RBC TFOL by race/Hispanic origin suggest potential biological and/or genetic influences.Keywords: 5-methyltetrahydrofolate; 5,10-methenyltetrahydrofolate; 5-formyltetrahydrofolate; tetrahydrofolate; unmetabolized folic acid; MeFox; HPLC-MS/MS; demographic; physiologic; lifestyle

Introduction

Red blood cell (RBC) folate is an indicator of tissue stores and long-term status because of the 120-d lifespan of RBCs [1]. Folate in mature RBCs originates from uptake during erythropoiesis, where erythroblasts rely on the reduced folate carrier, a transporter that moves mostly reduced folates across the plasma membrane via facilitated diffusion [2]. Reduced forms are then polyglutamated for retention. Mature RBCs are terminally differentiated and lack most membrane receptors and transporters that mediate folate uptake; thus, they reflect folate status at the time of formation in the bone marrow and release the entire folate content during apoptosis. Folate deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia, and insufficiency increases the risk of neural tube birth defects (NTD) and is therefore of public health concern [1,3].

Because of the complexities in measuring RBC folate [4], national nutrition surveys often use serum or plasma folate as a status indicator [5]. However, the interpretation of plasma folate is not only influenced by recent folate intake [1] but also limited relative to health outcomes because the relation between RBC and plasma folate is modified by BMI and genotype and substantially by low plasma vitamin B12 [6]. NHANES has been monitoring the folate status of the United States population through measurement of serum and RBC folate since the 1970s [7].

After the introduction of mandatory folic acid fortification of enriched cereal grain products in 1998 [8], NTD rates in the United States have decreased by 19% to 32% [9]. Although clinical folate deficiency, as represented by megaloblastic anemia, has nearly disappeared during the postfortification era [10], 19.5% of nonpregnant women of reproductive age (WRA; 12–49 y) were still characterized as folate insufficient [11]. Approximately 2 of 3 WRA in the United States have pre-existing modifiable risk factors for birth defects (e.g., obesity, low RBC folate concentrations, smoking exposure, low food security, or diabetes), and the percentage has increased from 65.3% (2007–2010) to 69.5% (2015–March 2020) [11]. Although the effects of these risk factors may differ, the strong relationship between folate status and NTDs warrants a closer look at RBC folate concentrations. RBC folate forms may provide information on physiologic shifts in folate metabolism.

The conventional assessment of RBC folate in NHANES is through the measurement of whole blood folate [WB-FOL; using a radio protein-binding assay before 2007 and a microbiologic assay (MBA) since 2007], serum folate [S-FOL; using a radio protein-binding assay before 2007, a microbiologic assay from 2007 to 2010, and HPLC-MS/MS since 2011 for measurement of serum folate forms that are summed to total folate (TFOL)], and hematocrit (Hct) [10,[12], [13], [14]]. RBC TFOL is then calculated by correcting the WB-FOL concentration for the S-FOL contribution and normalizing to the proportion of packed RBCs as measured by Hct. Although individual folate forms can be measured by HPLC-MS/MS in whole blood, the conventional approach does not allow an accurate estimation of the quantity of folate forms in erythrocytes because different folate forms are found in the 2 matrices, leading to potentially negative results when subtracting the contribution of serum folate forms.

To allow the correct assessment of individual RBC folate forms, washed erythrocytes were used for the first time in the NHANES August 2021–August 2023 cycle to directly measure RBC folate forms by HPLC-MS/MS [15]. This required the measurement of hemoglobin in the same RBC lysate aliquot to correct for residual moisture in the packed RBCs and the use of the mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC) to calculate RBC folate form concentrations, which can be summed to RBC TFOL, the biomarker that offers clinical interpretability.

The goal of this article is to describe the distributions and correlations of RBC folate forms and TFOL in this nationally representative sample of the United States population aged 6 y and older and to assess associations between the folate biomarkers and demographic, physiologic, and lifestyle variables. We also compared the directly measured RBC TFOL by HPLC-MS/MS with the indirectly calculated RBC TFOL by MBA and assessed the difference in the prevalence of folate deficiency and insufficiency by the 2 approaches.

Methods

Participants and survey design

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's (CDC) National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) conducts NHANES to collect cross-sectional data on the health and nutrition status of the civilian, noninstitutionalized United States population in 2-y cycles [16]. Home-based interviews are combined with health examinations, as well as the collection of biological specimens at a mobile examination center (MEC). The survey uses a stratified, multistage, probability sample designed to represent the United States population. Interview and examination response rates for each survey cycle are publicly available [17]. The data used in this analysis were collected from August 2021 to August 2023. The interview, examination, and phlebotomy response rates for participants ≥6 y covered in this analysis were 34.0%, 25.6%, and 19.6%, respectively. Participants ≥18 y consented, parents provided consent for those <18 y, and youth 7 to 17 y assented. The NCHS Ethics Review Board reviewed and approved the NHANES protocol [18].

Biomarker measurements

EDTA blood samples collected at the MEC were triple-washed with saline, then diluted with saline (∼1+1 part) and then with 1% ascorbic acid (1/11) to generate RBC lysates. Lysate samples were stored at –20°C for ∼1 wk before shipping on dry ice to CDC, where they were stored at –80°C until analysis.

CDC’s Nutritional Biomarkers Laboratory at the Division of Laboratory Sciences, National Center for Environmental Health used HPLC-MS/MS to measure 5 biologically active folate forms and 1 oxidation product of 5-methylTHF in RBC lysate samples [19,20]: 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-methylTHF), tetrahydrofolate (THF), 5-formyltetrahydrofolate (5-formylTHF), 5,10-methenyltetrahydrofolate (5,10-methenylTHF), unmetabolized folic acid (UMFA), and pyrazino-s-triazine derivative of 4α-hydroxy-5-methylTHF (MeFox). Assay performance was monitored using Westgard-type quality control multirules [21]. Long-term quality control coefficients of variation for RBC folate forms by HPLC-MS/MS were <3% for 5-methylTHF and ≤8.5% for the other folate forms (Supplemental Table 1).

To normalize for residual moisture in the packed RBCs, hemoglobin was measured in the same RBC lysate aliquot by the sodium lauryl sulfate method [22,23]. The RBC folate form concentrations (nmol/L) were calculated as RBC lysate folate/hemoglobin × MCHC. For individual RBC folate forms with lysate concentrations below the limit of detection (LOD), an imputed value equal to the LOD divided by the √2 was used before calculating the RBC folate concentration. The RBC TFOL concentration (measured by HPLC-MS/MS) was calculated as the sum of the 5 biologically active folate forms (not including MeFox). As a rule, RBC TFOL was calculated only when results were available for all folate forms; results <LOD were retained for the calculation. In this analysis, all samples had valid results for all folate forms.

For the conventional assessment of RBC TFOL, the MEC generated whole blood lysate samples (EDTA blood diluted 1/11 with 1% ascorbic acid) for measurement of WB-FOL by MBA [24] and serum samples for measurement of serum folate forms by HPLC-MS/MS and calculation of TFOL as the sum of biologically active folate forms [25,26]. RBC TFOL (measured by MBA) was calculated as [WB-FOL – S-FOL × (1 – Hct)]/Hct.

Study variables

We used 6 RBC folate forms (5-methylTHF, THF, 5-formylTHF, 5,10-methenylTHF, UMFA, and MeFox) and RBC TFOL as outcome variables and demographic, physiologic, and lifestyle variables as covariates. Demographic variables (available in the publicly released NHANES demographic files) included: 5 age groups (6–11, 12–19, 20–39, 40–59, and ≥60 y); sex (male and female); and 4 race and Hispanic origin groups [Hispanic, non-Hispanic Asian (NHA), non-Hispanic Black (NHB), and non-Hispanic White (NHW); other race and Hispanic origin groups were included in overall estimates but not presented separately per NCHS analytic guidelines] [27].

We included physiologic and lifestyle variables for which data were available in NHANES and that have been examined in a previous analysis [28]. Physiologic variables (available in the publicly released NHANES examination or laboratory files) included: inflammation as determined by C-reactive protein (CRP; measured as high-sensitivity CRP; <5 mg/L no inflammation and ≥5 mg/L inflammation [29]); kidney function as determined by estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR; 0 to <60 chronic kidney disease (moderate or severe decrease in eGFR, including kidney failure), 60 to <90 mild decrease in eGFR, and ≥90 mL/(min × 1.73 m2) normal eGFR], which was calculated using the chronic kidney disease epidemiology collaboration creatinine equation and accounts for age and sex [30]; and BMI category (<18.5 underweight, 18.5 to <25 normal, 25 to <30 overweight, and ≥30 kg/m2obesity) [31].

Lifestyle variables (available in publicly released NHANES interview files) included: alcohol intake based on self-reported drinking in the past 12 mo (1 drink defined as 12 oz beer, 5 oz wine, or 1.5 oz liquor equaling ≈14 g ethanol; no drink, 1 to <2, and ≥2 drinks per drinking day); tobacco use (during the past 5 d including the exam day; yes/no); folic acid dietary supplement use (self-reported average daily use over the past 30 d; yes/no); and fasting status (yes ≥8 h and no <8 h).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS for Windows software version 9.4 (SAS Institute) and SAS callable SUDAAN software version 11 (RTI) to account for the complex survey design. RBC folate forms were measured in participants ≥6 y. We excluded pregnant and lactating females (n = 64) from our analytical sample using the NHANES-provided pregnancy and lactation information. Our analytical sample consisted of persons with complete data for all RBC folate forms, including MeFox: n = 7126 participants ≥6 y for the age variable and n = 5640 participants ≥20 y for all other variables (as data for children were not available for some physiologic and lifestyle variables). The sample set used for the method comparison (HPLC-MS/MS compared with MBA) was further restricted to participants with nonmissing RBC TFOL by both assays (n = 7101; 25 participants were excluded). We used the NHANES August 2021–August 2023 phlebotomy weights throughout this analysis to account for differences in non-response patterns identified in the phlebotomy component of the MEC [32].

We calculated selected percentiles for each RBC folate form grouped by demographic variables and suppressed percentiles that fell within the portion of the distribution based on RBC lysate results <LOD. We estimated the mean percent contribution of 5-methylTHF, THF, and 5,10-methenylTHF to RBC TFOL by demographic variables in participants aged ≥6 y by first calculating the percent contribution for each participant. We also calculated the mean concentrations (nmol/L) of 5-methylTHF, THF, and 5,10-methenylTHF to RBC TFOL by weighted decile of RBC TFOL for all participants ≥6 y and stratified by folic acid supplement use. The decile categories for RBC TFOL were 1st (<562); 2nd (562–<657); 3rd (657–<737); 4th (737–<811); 5th (811–<890); 6th (890–<987); 7th (987–<1092); 8th (1092–<1250); 9th (1250–<1528); and 10th (≥1528).

We used weighted Spearman coefficients to assess correlations among RBC folate biomarkers, as well as pairwise correlations between serum folate biomarkers and RBC folate biomarkers, all measured by HPLC-MS/MS (we only used biomarkers with a detection frequency >50%). Survey-weighted Spearman correlations were estimated by regressing ranked, standardized values of 1 variable on the other and accounting for the sampling design. P values were derived from design-based t tests of the slope coefficient and, to be conservative, defined as the larger of the 2 regression-based P values (i.e., X compared with Y or Y compared with X).

To assess bivariate associations between folate biomarkers measured by HPLC-MS/MS and influential variables, we applied 2 approaches based on the detection rate (percentage of samples with RBC lysate concentrations ≥LOD). For RBC folate forms with (nearly) complete detection rates [100% (5-methylTHF and MeFox) and 98.8% (THF)], we calculated geometric mean concentrations [95% confidence interval (CI)] by variable categories. For RBC folate forms with lower detection rates [74% (5,10-methenylTHF], 45% (UMFA), and 42% (5-formylTHF)], we calculated the prevalence of results ≥LOD (95% CI) by variable categories using the Clopper–Pearson method [33], with the Korn and Graubard adjustment applied to account for the complex survey design [34]. Estimates were evaluated for reliability and reportability according to the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions [35]. We did not calculate a nonmethyl sum parameter of the 3 minor folate forms THF, 5,10-methenylTHF, and 5-formylTHF as in our previous report for serum folate forms [36] because the behavior of the sum parameter would be mostly determined by THF (nearly complete detection), and findings would be difficult to interpret because of the lower detection rates of the other 2 forms. Furthermore, no estimated LOD value can be derived for nonmethyl folate because the calculation of an RBC concentration from the measured RBC lysate concentration involves the use of each participant’s Hb and MCHC concentration (an estimated LOD value can be generated for serum). We grouped biomarker estimates by demographic, physiologic, and lifestyle variables and assessed unadjusted differences for each variable using a Wald F test.

To assess covariate-adjusted associations between the folate biomarkers measured by HPLC-MS/MS and the covariates among adults ≥20 y (because some variables were not available for younger age groups), we again applied 2 approaches based on the detection rate. For the 3 folate forms with >98% detectable concentrations (5-methylTHF, THF, and MeFox), we used multiple linear regression after log-transforming the dependent variable to estimate adjusted percent differences on biomarker concentrations relative to the reference category or per unit increase for continuous variables. For the other 3 folate forms with higher proportions of results <LOD (UMFA, 5-formylTHF, and 5,10-methenylTHF), we used multiple logistic regression to estimate the impact of covariates on odds ratios (ORs) of biomarker concentrations ≥LOD after covariate adjustment. We assessed the significance of the variable after covariate adjustment using a Wald F test. All statistical comparisons were evaluated at a 2-sided significance level of α = 0.05. No Bonferroni correction was applied because our analyses were exploratory and descriptive rather than intended to test a limited set of prespecified hypotheses, and findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating [37].

To assess the method comparison for RBC TFOL between HPLC-MS/MS and MBA, we calculated geometric means for persons ≥6 y and for WRA, as well as by demographic characteristics. To assess differences in the prevalence of folate deficiency (for persons ≥6 y and WRA) and insufficiency (for WRA) by the 2 methods, we used the MBA-specific RBC folate cutoffs of <305 and <748 nmol/L, respectively [38]. The insufficiency cutoff corresponds to the WHO-specified cutoff of 906 nmol/L [39] but is lower because the CDC folate MBA is calibrated with 5-methylTHF instead of folic acid. We also performed an unweighted method comparison between HPLC-MS/MS and MBA to assess agreement between the 2 methods rather than produce population-based estimates. To assess method differences across the distribution of concentrations, we included a scatter plot and Bland-Altman plot for persons ≥6 y, and frequency distribution curves for persons ≥6 y and for WRA.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

Among United States persons aged ≥6 y in the weighted NHANES August 2021–August 2023 sample, 8% were children (6–11 y), 11.5% were adolescents (12–19 y), and 80.5% were adults (≥20 y); of the adults, half were female and ∼60% were NHW; ∼60% were fasting for ≥8 h, ∼20% had elevated CRP, ∼35% had a mild decrease in kidney function or chronic kidney disease, ∼73% were overweight or had obesity, 86% reported some alcohol intake in the past year, ∼17% reported tobacco use, and about one-third reported using folic acid-containing dietary supplements during the last 30 d (Table 1).

Relative contributions of RBC folate forms to RBC TFOL by HPLC-MS/MS

In persons ≥6 y, 5-methylTHF (92.7%) was the biggest contributor to RBC TFOL, whereas THF (5.02%) and 5,10-methenylTHF (1.61%) contributed small amounts (Supplemental Table 9). The relative contribution of folate forms to TFOL was similar across age groups and sex categories, except for 5,10-methenylTHF, which showed a lower contribution in children 6–11 y (1.34%) compared with other age groups (∼1.6%); all pairwise comparisons were significant (P ≤ 0.05). We observed significant differences (P < 0.0001) in the contribution of folate forms to TFOL by race and Hispanic origin: NHB persons had a lower contribution of THF (2.64%) and 5,10-methenylTHF (1.0%) and thus a higher contribution of 5-methylTHF (95.6%) compared with the other groups (∼5%–6%, ∼1.6%–2%, and ∼91%–93%, respectively); all pairwise comparisons were significant (P ≤ 0.05). Hispanic persons had the highest contribution of THF (5.98%) and 5,10-methenylTHF (1.95%), but the difference was only statistically significant compared with NHB persons (P < 0.0001).

When we categorized the concentrations of folate biomarkers by decile of RBC TFOL, we noted a mostly linear increase in 5-methylTHF, whereas THF and 5,10-methenylTHF concentrations remained relatively constant during the lower deciles and showed a large increase in the 10th decile (Figure 1 and Supplemental Table 10). The relative contribution of these folate forms to RBC TFOL by decile showed only minor fluctuations for 5-methylTHF around 90%, whereas both THF (6.80%–4.81%) and 5,10-methenylTHF (2.35%–1.52%) showed a small decrease with increasing decile (Supplemental Table 10).

Correlations among folate biomarkers measured by HPLC-MS/MS

Spearman correlations were significant for all RBC folate biomarker pairings in persons ≥6 y (Table 3). Major folate biomarkers measured by HPLC-MS/MS: concentrations by demographic characteristics

In persons ≥6 y, geometric mean (95% CI) concentrations (nmol/L) for RBC TFOL by HPLC-MS/MS and the 3 RBC folate forms with ≥98.8% detection rates were: 913 (891–935) TFOL, 835 (814–856) 5-methylTHF, 26.3 (25.0–27.8) THF, and 161 (157–165) MeFox (Supplemental Table 11). We observed significant (P ≤ 0.0003) differences by age (highest concentrations in older adults), sex (higher concentrations in females), and race and Hispanic origin (highest concentrations in NHW persons), except for THF, which showed no sex differences. A slight U-shaped pattern was observed, with adolescents aged 12–19 y having the lowest concentrations. When we stratified by folic acid supplement use, the slight U-shaped age pattern was accentuated in supplement users, whereas nonusers had mostly similar biomarker concentrations (Figure 2 and Supplemental Table 11). Sex differences were also accentuated in supplement users, whereas race and Hispanic origin means were more comparable between supplement users and nonusers. Compared with NHB persons, NHW persons had 22% and 17% higher geometric mean concentrations of 5-methylTHF and MeFox, respectively, but an 80% higher concentration of THF. This was equally true for supplement users (18% and 12% compared with 80%) and nonusers (15% and 12% compared with 69%).

Major folate biomarkers measured by HPLC-MS/MS: concentrations by physiologic and lifestyle characteristics

In bivariate analyses in persons ≥20 y, we observed no association between fasting status and RBC TFOL, 5-methylTHF, THF, or MeFox concentrations (Table 4). For 3 covariates, kidney function, tobacco use, and folic acid supplement use, we observed significant (P ≤ 0.0002) associations with all 4 RBC biomarkers. Biomarker concentrations increased with decreasing kidney function by ∼20% to 30% when comparing persons with chronic kidney disease to those with normal kidney function. Biomarker concentrations were higher in persons reporting no tobacco use (∼12%) and much higher in persons reporting folic acid supplement use (∼50%). For the remaining 3 covariates, inflammation, BMI, and alcohol intake, we observed significant (P ≤ 0.0379) associations with some of the RBC biomarkers. TFOL was slightly higher in persons with inflammation (∼5%). TFOL, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox increased with increasing BMI by ∼10% when comparing persons who have obesity to normal weight persons. TFOL and MeFox were highest among persons with moderate alcohol intake and lower among both nondrinkers and those consuming ≥2 alcoholic drinks/d. In contrast, THF was highest among persons reporting no drinks in the past 12 mo.

Major folate biomarkers measured by HPLC-MS/MS: multiple linear regression modeling to estimate adjusted percent differences in RBC folate biomarkers

After controlling for covariates (age, sex, race and Hispanic origin, inflammation, kidney function, BMI, alcohol intake, tobacco use, and folic acid supplement use) in persons ≥20 y, many of the significant bivariate associations were no longer significant (Table 6). The variables that remained significantly associated were: age with 3 RBC folate biomarkers (P ≤ 0.0043), sex with 1 (P = 0.0069), race and Hispanic origin with 4 (P ≤ 0.007), kidney function with 1 (P = 0.0393), BMI with 3 (P ≤ 0.0442), tobacco use with 3 (P ≤ 0.0252), and folic acid supplement use with all 4 (P < 0.0001) (Table 7). After holding all other variables in the model constant, for every 10-y increase in age, biomarker concentrations increased by <8% for TFOL, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox. Females had 3.41% higher MeFox concentrations than males. Although race and Hispanic origin differences were attenuated, NHB persons still had notably lower biomarker concentrations compared with NHW persons: 19.7% TFOL, 15.9% 5-methylTHF, 40.4% THF, and 12.6% MeFox. For every 25% increase in eGFR, TFOL concentrations decreased by 1.75%. For every 5% increase in BMI, TFOL, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox concentrations increased by <1%. Persons reporting tobacco use had ∼5% lower TFOL and MeFox and 12.9% lower THF concentrations. Among all variables, folic acid supplement use emerged as the strongest predictor of higher RBC folate biomarker concentrations, in the order of ∼40%. Together, all variables explained <30% of biomarker variability (R2): 28.2% TFOL, 21.2% 5-methylTHF, 9.4% THF, and 18.2% MeFox.

Comparison of RBC TFOL measured by HPLC-MS/MS compared with MBA

The overall RBC TFOL by HPLC-MS/MS in persons ≥6 y was highly correlated (Lin’s r = 0.91; Figure 4A) with the RBC TFOL by MBA, but the geometric mean concentration was 14% lower (912 compared with 1060 nmol/L). The 95% limits of agreement ranged from 0.67 to 1.10 of the log concentrations, suggesting that for 95% of samples, the HPLC-MS/MS measurements were ∼33% lower to ∼10% higher than MBA measurements (Figure 4B). This systematic difference was relatively constant across demographic subgroups (Supplemental Table 13). A slight left shift and tightening of the HPLC-MS/MS distribution was noted compared with the MBA distribution, both for persons ≥6 y (Figure 5A) and for WRA (Figure 5B). The prevalence (95% CI) of folate deficiency in persons ≥6 y was 0.28% (0.139%, 0.501%) by HPLC-MS/MS compared with 0.21% by MBA (0.09%, 0.416%), whereas in WRA it was 0.34% (0.048%, 1.14%) compared with 0.10% (0.009%, 0.403%), respectively. The prevalence of folate insufficiency in WRA was 38.4% (34.4%, 42.5%) by HPLC-MS/MS compared with 21.7% (18.9%, 24.7%) by MBA.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first report describing RBC folate forms and their association with demographic, physiologic, and lifestyle variables in a large, nationally representative sample of the United States population aged 6 y and older. Across demographic characteristics, 5-methylTHF (∼93%) was the predominant contributor to RBC TFOL. THF (5.02%) and 5,10-methenylTHF (1.61%) contributed much less but showed notable race and Hispanic origin variations in concentration (i.e., THF) and detection frequency (i.e., 5,10-methenylTHF). UMFA and 5-formylTHF were detected in less than half the population at very low concentrations.

Folic acid supplement use was the strongest predictor of RBC folate biomarker concentrations and detection, and it was significantly associated with most biomarkers in crude and covariate-adjusted analyses. Sternberg et al. [40] demonstrated that folic acid supplement use explained 16.3% of the variability of RBC TFOL (measured by protein-binding assay in adults aged ≥20 y in NHANES 2003–2006) compared with dietary folate intake only explaining 1.85% of the variability, which was also the reason why we chose to include supplement use and not dietary intake as a variable in our analysis. In serum, folic acid supplement use and total folic acid intake mostly explained UMFA concentrations >1 nmol/L in fasting adults in NHANES 2007–2008 [41]. In this analysis, UMFA was the only RBC folate form not associated with folic acid supplement use in bivariate or multivariate analysis. This should not come as a surprise because we measured UMFA in triple-washed RBCs after removing the serum with its UMFA content. Only reduced folates are expected in mature RBCs [2]. If small amounts of folic acid enter the developing erythroblast, they would be reduced to THF and methylated intracellularly. Although folic acid has a very high affinity for membrane-associated folate-binding proteins, the expression of which is largely restricted to cells important for embryonic development (e.g., placenta and neural tubes) and folate resorption (kidney), it has very poor affinity for the reduced folate carrier transporter present in most tissues, including erythroblasts [1,[42], [43], [44]]. The very low UMFA concentrations in less than half of the triple-washed RBC samples may reflect leftover trace amounts from serum. The difference in the interpretation of serum UMFA compared with RBC UMFA emphasizes that folate forms are different indicators depending on the matrix and their biological location.

Folic acid supplement use was associated with higher odds of detecting 5-formylTHF (OR: 1.59) and 5,10-methenylTHF (OR: 2.02) and with ∼40% higher THF, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox concentrations, consistent with higher concentrations or detectability for most folate forms during a state of high folate intake. A small folic acid supplementation study in pregnant and non-pregnant Canadian females measured RBC folate forms by HPLC-MS/MS and found 5-methylTHF and THF in most females regardless of MTHFR genotype; most females consuming folic acid supplements also had detectable concentrations of 5,10-methenylTHF [45].

The current analysis showed that folic acid supplement use appeared to explain much of the age and sex differences in the United States population; namely, a slightly U-shaped age pattern and higher concentrations in females compared with males, patterns also seen previously for serum folate forms [14,36]. Age has been shown to be a confounder of the relationship between RBC TFOL and folic acid supplement use, whereas sex, race, and Hispanic origin were not [40].

The race and Hispanic origin differences observed in this analysis varied by the RBC folate form, as was the case for serum folate forms [14,36]. For example, 5-methylTHF and MeFox showed the same patterns in serum and RBCs: 5-methylTHF, highest concentrations for NHW, lowest for NHB, and intermediate for Hispanic and NHA persons; MeFox, highest concentrations for NHW, lower and similar concentrations for Hispanic, NHA, and NHB persons. Components of nonmethyl folate (THF, 5-formylTHF, and 5,10-methenylTHF; with THF being the largest contributor), however, showed different patterns in serum and RBCs. For example, nonmethyl folate did not vary across race and Hispanic origin groups in serum, whereas in RBCs, THF concentrations were much lower in NHB persons compared with NHW persons (before and after controlling for folic acid supplement use); other major folate forms also had consistently lower concentrations in NHB persons. The observation with THF together with our finding that 5,10-methenylTHF in RBCs had the lowest detection frequency in NHB compared with other groups (before and after controlling for folic acid supplement use) may be a phenotypic expression of methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) 677 C>T polymorphism, which leads to an accumulation of nonmethyl folate in RBCs [[46], [47], [48]] and may explain some of the observed differences in RBC folate forms by race and Hispanic origin. African American persons are known to have a low frequency (<2%), whereas Latin American (∼20%) and European persons (∼10%) have a higher frequency of the T/T genotype [49]. The smaller differences we observed for the major folate forms between Hispanic and NHW persons than would be expected from genotype frequency data could be due to other factors such as dietary patterns, differences in consumption of fortified foods, and environmental influences on folate biomarkers. The strong correlation of RBC THF with RBC 5,10-methenylTHF (r = 0.75) in this analysis indicates that these 2 folate forms appear in concert, which would be expected in individuals with the T/T genotype.

RBC MeFox and RBC 5-methylTHF concentrations were also highly correlated (r = 0.93) in contrast to the moderate correlation reported earlier between serum MeFox and serum 5-methylTHF concentrations (r = 0.66) [14]. RBC MeFox and RBC 5-methylTHF also showed the same significant crude associations with the covariates studied and similar but not identical adjusted associations: age was associated with 5-methylTHF but not MeFox, whereas sex and tobacco use were associated with MeFox but not 5-methylTHF; BMI was associated with both biomarkers. This suggests that the concentration of the oxidation form MeFox in RBCs was related to the concentration of 5-methylTHF in RBCs. Although this does not provide information about the site of MeFox formation (in vivo compared with in vitro), it deviates from our earlier findings in serum, where we concluded that serum MeFox may be an indicator of negative health factors [14,36,50]. In RBCs, MeFox seems to be a surrogate indicator of 5-methylTHF and thus related to folate status. With MeFox, we are again reminded that folate forms can be different indicators depending on the matrix and their biological location.

The above notion is also supported by the weak to moderate strength in pairwise correlations for the same folate form in serum compared with RBCs; for example, we observed only weak correlations for THF (r = 0.14) and MeFox (r = 0.31) between the 2 matrices and a moderate correlation for 5-methylTHF (r = 0.67). The moderate correlation of serum TFOL with RBC TFOL (r = 0.68) is consistent with data from different populations [6,40,[51], [52], [53]].

Of the remaining physiologic (inflammation, kidney function, and BMI) and lifestyle (alcohol intake and tobacco use) variables studied, all had significant crude associations with most RBC folate biomarker concentrations or detection frequencies except for inflammation, which was only associated with TFOL. However, after covariate adjustment, only BMI (TFOL and 5-methylTHF) and tobacco use (TFOL, THF, and MeFox) remained significantly associated with >1 RBC folate biomarker. The positive association of RBC TFOL observed with BMI (∼1% higher concentrations with every 5% increase in BMI) has been noted with earlier NHANES data [28,54]. The negative association observed with tobacco use (∼5%–13% lower biomarker concentrations in persons reporting tobacco use) has been reported previously and was attributed to smoking-induced oxidative stress, changes in diet, and/or changes in metabolism [[55], [56], [57]].

In a previous analysis of serum folate forms [36], multiple factors remained significantly associated with ≥3 serum folate biomarkers after covariate adjustment: kidney function (UMFA, nonmethyl folate, and MeFox), BMI (TFOL, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox), smoking status (TFOL, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox), and alcohol intake (TFOL, 5-methylTHF, UMFA, and MeFox). Given that folate is incorporated into RBCs during their formation in the bone marrow and does not change throughout the lifespan of the RBC [2,58], it may not be surprising that fewer factors are associated with RBC folate forms compared with serum folate forms after covariate adjustment.

In a sensitivity analysis, we compared our findings to a model including dietary folate intake in addition to folic acid supplement use. Results were similar in terms of magnitude, direction, and statistical significance of associations between covariates and RBC folate forms for age, race and Hispanic origin, tobacco use, and folic acid supplement use. Dietary folate intake did not confound primary variables, and we observed <5%–10% change among estimates across all covariates. Dietary folate intake was statistically significant for TFOL, 5-methylTHF, and MeFox, the 3 biomarkers that directly or indirectly represent folate status.

The comparison of the directly measured RBC TFOL by HPLC-MS/MS compared with the conventional approach of estimating RBC TFOL by MBA from WB-FOL, S-FOL, and Hct yielded a nearly identical method difference (–14%) in this large sample set of over 7000 NHANES participants as an earlier report (–13.5%; 95% CI: –15.2%, –11.8%) from a convenience set of 60 blood donor samples [19]. Although this highlights the notable reproducibility and robustness of the laboratory method over time, it also reinforces that there is an unexplained systematic difference between these 2 methods. A 15% difference may seem modest in the context of large, documented method differences across clinical platforms, particularly for RBC folate [5]. Indeed, the difference in deficiency prevalence (<1% by both methods) at the tail of the distribution was negligible; however, the difference in insufficiency prevalence (38.4% by HPLC-MS/MS compared with 21.7% by MBA) was not. This shows that even modest method differences can notably influence the interpretation of laboratory data in terms of public health impact. It is worth noting, however, that the folate cutoff values have been established with MBA methods, and it is possible that assay-specific cutoff values are needed for correct interpretation.

The major strengths of this study are the following: 1) the use of a nationally representative survey of the United States population aged 6 y and older; 2) the availability of demographic, physiologic, and lifestyle data for the same study participants to allow investigation of crude and covariate-adjusted associations; and 3) the high-quality laboratory data. Limitations are that we did not investigate associations with various chronic conditions and diseases, as that was beyond the scope of this analysis. We also did not develop tailored models for each folate biomarker but instead used a predefined group of covariates jointly to assess how they relate to the biomarkers. Nonresponse bias assessments for August 2021–August 2023 showed that despite the decline in response rates and changes in sampling methods, no major sources of bias were found that were not addressed with weighting adjustments [32].

In conclusion, to our knowledge, this is the first report on associations of RBC folate forms with demographic, physiological, and lifestyle factors in a large, nationally representative population. In the United States population, folic acid supplement use was the strongest predictor of RBC folate biomarker concentrations and detection. Folate forms can be different indicators depending on the matrix (RBC compared with serum) and their biological location. Given that mandatory folic acid fortification has been in place for nearly 3 decades and that the United States population has a high prevalence of folic acid supplement use, it remains to be seen whether our findings can be replicated in other populations that have lower folate exposure and status.

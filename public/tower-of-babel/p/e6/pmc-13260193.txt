Comparative profiling of virulence determinants, capsular serotypes, and antimicrobial resistance in hypervirulent and classical Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates from liver abscess patients
Authors: Jie Liu, Jing Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to compare the virulence and resistance gene profiles, capsular serotypes, and molecular epidemiology of hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKP) and classical Klebsiella pneumoniae (cKP) strains isolated from patients with liver abscesses in China, and to evaluate their phenotypic virulence using in vitro and in vivo models.MethodsA total of 83 non-repetitive K. pneumoniae strains were collected from liver abscess patients between January 2023 and December 2024. Strains positive for the string test and harboring both rmpA and iutA genes were classified as hvKP (n = 47), while the others were designated as cKP (n = 36). PCR was used to detect virulence genes (rmpA, iutA, aerobactin, magA, wcaG, kfu, fimH, mrkD, allS, iroB, ybtS, entB, peg-344, uge), capsular serotypes (K1, K2, K5, K20, K54, K57), and resistance genes (KPC, NDM, TEM, CTX-M-1). Multilocus sequence typing (MLST) was performed to determine sequence types. Virulence was further assessed through serum resistance assays, biofilm formation tests, and a Galleria mellonella infection model.ResultsThe hvKP group showed significantly higher carriage rates of nine virulence genes (rmpA, iutA, aerobactin, magA, wcaG, kfu, allS, peg-344, iroB) compared to cKP (all P < 0.05), while fimH and mrkD were universally present. K1 was the predominant serotype in hvKP (63.8%), and magA and allS were exclusively detected in K1 strains. Serotypes K1 and K20 were significantly more frequent in hvKP than in cKP. MLST revealed that hvKP isolates belonged to eight STs, mainly ST23 and ST65, with all K1 strains corresponding to ST23. In contrast, cKP displayed greater ST diversity (17 STs). Resistance genes KPC, NDM, and CTX-M-1 were less prevalent in hvKP, and KPC was detected exclusively in cKP (16.7% vs. 0%, P < 0.05). Serum resistance was significantly stronger in hvKP than in cKP (P < 0.05), whereas biofilm formation did not differ significantly between groups. In the G. mellonella model, hvKP caused significantly higher mortality at inocula of 105, 106, and 107 CFU/mL (P < 0.05).ConclusionHvKP isolates from liver abscesses in this region are characterized by a distinct molecular profile, including a high prevalence of K1/ST23 clones and key virulence genes. Although hvKP remains largely susceptible to carbapenems, the detection of CTX-M-1 in one hvKP strain signals potential convergence of virulence and resistance. These findings support the use of combined molecular and phenotypic assays for the timely identification of hvKP and underscore the need for ongoing surveillance to monitor the evolution of high-risk clones.

Introduction

A liver abscess is a purulent pathological lesion within the liver parenchyma caused by infection with bacteria, fungi, or parasitic microorganisms. The most common causative pathogens of bacterial liver abscesses include Escherichia coli, Klebsiella species, Streptococcus species, Staphylococcus species, and anaerobic bacteria. Currently, Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) has emerged as the predominant pathogen responsible for liver abscesses in China (Doshi et al. 2022).

KP is a Gram-negative bacterium widely distributed in aquatic and soil environments, frequently isolated from human mucosal surfaces, fecally contaminated hands, sewage-contaminated water sources, and various surfaces within hospital settings. It commonly colonizes the respiratory and intestinal tracts and can cause both nosocomial and community-acquired infections.

In 1986, a distinctive form of KP causing liver abscesses accompanied by invasive multi-site infections, with unique phenotypic and genotypic characteristics, was first reported in Taiwan. In 2012, Pomakova et al. designated this variant as hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKP), while the previously recognized form was termed classical Klebsiella pneumoniae (cKP) (Pomakova et al. 2012). HvKP typically harbors large virulence plasmids such as pLVPK, which carry key virulence determinants (Seng et al. 2010; Quéro et al. 2019). In contrast, cKP is commonly associated with healthcare-associated infections, particularly in immunocompromised individuals.

East and Southeast Asia represent major endemic regions for hvKP infections, with a substantial proportion of reported cases originating from Taiwan, South Korea, Iran, and mainland China (Lam et al. 2018; Turton et al. 2007; Karlsson et al. 2019). In China, the liver remains the most frequent site of hvKP invasion, underscoring the clinical and epidemiological significance of this pathogen in the region.

Despite growing recognition of hvKP, systematic comparisons of virulence gene profiles, antimicrobial resistance patterns, capsular serotypes, and molecular epidemiology between hvKP and cKP in liver abscess isolates remain limited in the Chinese context. Furthermore, the relationship between genotypic features and phenotypic virulence in experimental models requires further elucidation.

The Galleria mellonella (greater wax moth) larval model has emerged as a valuable in vivo system for assessing bacterial virulence due to its several advantages over mammalian models, including lower cost, ease of handling, and the absence of ethical constraints associated with vertebrate animal experiments. The larval immune system shares structural and functional similarities with the innate immune system of mammals, including cellular (hemocytes) and humoral (antimicrobial peptides) defense mechanisms. Compared to in vitro cell culture models, the G. mellonella model provides a whole-organism context that captures complex host–pathogen interactions, including tissue invasion, immune evasion, and systemic infection dynamics. This model has been extensively validated for virulence assessment of K. pneumoniae and other bacterial pathogens (Li et al. 2020a; Russo and Macdonald 2020).

This study aims to investigate the distribution of virulence genes, resistance determinants, capsular serotypes, and sequence types in hvKP and cKP isolates recovered from patients with liver abscesses. Additionally, we assess and compare their virulence potential using in vitro and in vivo models, including serum resistance assays, biofilm formation tests, and a Galleria mellonella infection model. The findings are expected to enhance the understanding of hvKP pathogenicity, support early clinical identification, and inform targeted prevention and treatment strategies for KP liver abscesses.

Materials and methods

Bacterial isolates

A total of 83 non-duplicate KP isolates were obtained from patients diagnosed with liver abscesses between January 2023 and December 2024. The isolates were recovered from either blood cultures or percutaneous drainage specimens and were identified using standard microbiological methods (Doshi et al. 2022). Strains were classified as hvKP if they tested positive in the string test and carried both the rmpA and iutA virulence genes. The remaining strains were classified as cKP (Pomakova et al. 2012; Russo et al. 2018).

Phenotypic string test

The hypermucoviscous phenotype was assessed using the string test. A single colony was picked from overnight cultures grown on blood agar and gently stretched with an inoculating loop. Strains forming a viscous string longer than 5 mm were considered positive for hypermucoviscosity (Pomakova et al. 2012; Luo et al. 2014).

DNA extraction

Bacterial genomic DNA was extracted using the boiling lysis method. Briefly, three to five colonies were suspended in 300 µL of sterile distilled water, boiled for 10 min, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was collected as template DNA for polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays (Seng et al. 2010).

PCR amplification of virulence, capsular, and resistance genes

Primers targeting 14 virulence genes (rmpA, iutA, aerobactin, magA, wcaG, kfu, fimH, mrkD, allS, iroB, ybtS, entB, peg-344, uge), 6 capsular serotypes (K1, K2, K5, K20, K54, K57), and 4 resistance genes (KPC, NDM, TEM, CTX-M-1) were designed based on previously published sequences (Tables 1 and 2). Additionally, seven housekeeping genes (rpoB, gapA, mdh, pgi, phoE, infB, tonB) were amplified for multilocus sequence typing (MLST) (Table 3) (Chen and Chen 2021). PCR reactions were performed in a final volume of 25 µL using standard cycling conditions. Amplified products were visualized by electrophoresis on a 1.2% agarose gel stained with ethidium bromide (Quéro et al. 2019).

Multilocus sequence typing (MLST)

MLST was performed according to the Pasteur Institute scheme. The seven housekeeping genes were amplified and sequenced, and sequence types (STs) were assigned via the K. pneumoniae MLST database (https://bigsdb.pasteur.fr/klebsiella/) (Lam et al. 2018).

Serum resistance assay

Bacterial resistance to human serum was evaluated as previously described (Choby et al. 2020; Russo et al. 2015). Briefly, overnight cultures of KP were adjusted to 1 × 106 CFU/mL in fresh LB broth and mixed with pooled human serum from healthy volunteers. Aliquots were incubated at 37 °C for 0, 60, 120, and 180 min. Viable bacterial counts were determined by plating serial dilutions on Mueller–Hinton agar. Serum resistance was classified into six grades, and strains were grouped as highly sensitive, moderately sensitive, or serum-resistant based on survival rates relative to the initial inoculum (Gao et al. 2020).

Biofilm formation assay

Biofilm formation was assessed using a microtiter plate crystal violet staining method. Overnight cultures were diluted to 1 × 106 CFU/mL in fresh LB broth, and 200 µL was added to wells of a 96-well plate. After 24 h of static incubation at 37 °C, planktonic cells were removed, and adherent biofilms were stained with 1% crystal violet, dissolved in absolute ethanol, and quantified by measuring optical density at 570 nm (OD570). A cutoff value was defined as three standard deviations above the mean OD of negative control wells (Chen et al. 2020a).

Galleria mellonella infection model

Galleria mellonella larvae were used to evaluate in vivo virulence (Li et al. 2020b). Healthy larvae weighing 250–350 mg were injected with 10 µL of bacterial suspensions at concentrations ranging from 104 to 107 CFU/mL. Sterile PBS was used as a negative control. Larvae were incubated at 35 °C in the dark, and survival was monitored every 24 h for 72 h. Larvae showing no movement upon gentle stimulation were considered dead (Russo and Macdonald 2020).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 27.0. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves for the G. mellonella model were plotted using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

This study provides a comparative analysis of virulence factors, genotypic antimicrobial resistance profiles, and molecular characteristics of hvKP and cKP isolates obtained from patients with liver abscesses in China. Our findings align with and extend existing knowledge regarding the distinct epidemiological and pathogenic features of hvKP.

Consistent with prior studies, hvKP isolates demonstrated significantly higher carriage rates of key virulence genes, including rmpA, aerobactin, iutA, magA, wcaG, kfu, allS, peg-344, and iroB, compared to cKP (Russo et al. 2018, 2015; Bialek-Davenet et al. 2013; Yan et al. 2023; Tan et al. 2019; Lin et al. 2014; Wei et al. 2016). Notably, magA and allS were exclusively detected in K1 serotype isolates, reinforcing their roles as serotype-specific markers and potential drivers of hypervirulence (Lin et al. 2014; Wei et al. 2016). The 100% prevalence of fimH and mrkD across all isolates highlights their essential function in adhesion and biofilm formation, independent of virulence phenotype (Fierer et al. 2011).

K1 remained the dominant serotype in hvKP (63.8%), with a strong association with ST23, a lineage frequently linked to invasive infections across Asia (Turton et al. 2007; Al Ismail et al. 2024; Kain et al. 2024a). This finding underscores the regional persistence of high-risk clones, supporting the need for targeted surveillance. The broader ST distribution observed in cKP suggests greater genomic diversity, though the presence of ST11 and ST15 in KPC-positive isolates reflects potential transmission of carbapenem resistance within certain high-risk lineages (Yu et al. 2006).

Several sequence types identified in this study correspond to internationally recognized high-risk clones. ST23, which accounted for 63.8% of hvKP isolates and was exclusively associated with K1 serotype, represents a well-established hypervirulent clone with global dissemination (Donà et al. 2025; Kain et al. 2024b). ST11, detected in three cKP isolates (all KPC-positive), is a prominent high-risk clone in China frequently associated with carbapenem resistance (Haghighifar et al. 2021). ST15, identified in one KPC-positive cKP isolate, has been increasingly reported as a vehicle for both ESBL and carbapenemase genes. The convergence of hypervirulence and multidrug resistance in such high-risk clones poses an emerging threat requiring enhanced surveillance.

Based on our integrated molecular and phenotypic analysis, K. pneumoniae strains associated with liver abscess in our region exhibit distinct profiles depending on their virulence status. The characteristic hvKP profile includes: (1) positive string test and hypermucoviscous phenotype; (2) carriage of key virulence genes including rmpA, iutA, aerobactin, peg-344, and iroB; (3) predominance of K1 serotype (63.8%) with strong linkage to ST23; (4) enhanced serum resistance; (5) high lethality in G. mellonella model; and (6) relative susceptibility to carbapenems, though with emerging ESBL positivity. In contrast, cKP isolates demonstrate greater genomic diversity, lower virulence gene carriage, variable serum susceptibility, and higher prevalence of resistance genes including KPC. These characteristic profiles may aid in early recognition and appropriate management of KP liver abscesses.

The significantly lower prevalence of carbapenemase and ESBL genes in hvKP aligns with the traditional view that hypervirulence and multidrug resistance are seldom concurrent. It is important to note that K. pneumoniae exhibits intrinsic resistance to ampicillin due to the constitutive production of chromosomal SHV-1 β-lactamase; therefore, the absence of acquired resistance genes does not imply susceptibility to aminopenicillins. In this study, the detection of resistance genes such as KPC, NDM, and CTX-M-1 should be interpreted as indicators of acquired resistance mechanisms, while intrinsic resistance to ampicillin is presumed in all isolates regardless of genotype. However, the detection of CTX-M-1 in one hvKP isolate signals an emerging trend of convergence between virulence and resistance, which poses a serious public health threat (Zhang et al. 2016, 2021; Feng et al. 2018; Liu and Guo 2019). It is important to note that our PCR-based detection targeted the CTX-M-1 gene as a representative of the CTX-M group 1, which encompasses multiple variants including the globally dominant CTX-M-15. While our assay confirms the presence of a group 1 CTX-M gene, it does not differentiate specific variants such as CTX-M-15. Therefore, the detected CTX-M-1 should be interpreted as an indicator of CTX-M group 1 carriage, with the potential presence of CTX-M-15 or other group 1 variants requiring confirmation by sequencing in future studies. In our setting, KPC was exclusively detected in cKP, predominantly in ST11 and ST15 isolates, reinforcing the role of certain STs in the dissemination of carbapenem resistance (Yu et al. 2006).

HvKP exhibited superior serum resistance compared to cKP, a phenotype likely facilitated by its enhanced capsule production and complement evasion mechanisms (Chen et al. 2020a; Wang et al. 2021). The lack of significant difference in biofilm formation between the two groups suggests that while fimbrial genes are ubiquitous, additional regulatory or environmental factors may modulate biofilm-associated virulence (Haghighifar et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2020b; Hasani et al. 2020; Shon and Russo 2012; El-Mahdy et al. 2018).

In the Galleria mellonella infection model, hvKP induced significantly higher mortality at inocula ≥ 105 CFU/mL, corroborating its enhanced pathogenic potential in vivo (Russo and Macdonald 2020; Li et al. 2020b). This model offers a reliable and ethically acceptable alternative to mammalian systems for preliminary virulence assessment.

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting our findings. The single-center design and moderate sample size may limit the generalizability of the results. Additionally, this study relies primarily on genotypic resistance profiling without corresponding phenotypic AST. While molecular detection of resistance genes provides valuable epidemiological insights and indicates resistance potential, it does not confirm functional resistance expression or predict clinical susceptibility breakpoints. Furthermore, our resistance gene analysis was restricted to a selected panel of targets (KPC, NDM, TEM, and CTX-M-1 as a representative of CTX-M group 1). A more comprehensive genotypic screening, including specific identification of globally prevalent variants such as CTX-M-15 and additional carbapenemase genes (e.g., OXA-48-like, VIM, IMP), would provide a more complete resistance profile. Therefore, our resistance-related findings should be interpreted with these methodological limitations in mind. Future multicenter studies with longitudinal sampling should integrate broad-spectrum molecular genotyping with phenotypic AST according to CLSI or EUCAST guidelines to validate our observations, provide clinically actionable data, and monitor the evolution and spread of virulent-resistant hybrids. Clinically, the persistence of ST23-K1 hvKP highlights the importance of rapid molecular diagnostics to guide early intervention and infection control measures.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates clear distinctions between hvKP and cKP in terms of virulence gene carriage, capsular serotype distribution, and molecular epidemiology. HvKP isolates were predominantly associated with the K1 serotype and ST23, and exhibited enhanced serum resistance and in vivo lethality. Although hvKP currently shows a lower prevalence of carbapenemase genes, the emergence of ESBL-positive hvKP strains underscores the ongoing risk of convergence between hypervirulence and antimicrobial resistance. These findings reinforce the importance of integrated molecular surveillance, virulence phenotyping, and antimicrobial stewardship in managing KP infections, particularly in endemic regions. Further research is warranted to elucidate the genetic mechanisms driving the evolution of high-risk clones and to develop targeted strategies for prevention and treatment.

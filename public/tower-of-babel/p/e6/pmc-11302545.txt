Who is (not) vaccinated? A proposal for a comprehensive immunization information system
Authors: Giacomo Pietro Vigezzi, Elena Maggioni, Fabrizio Bert, Corrado de Vito, Roberta Siliquini, Anna Odone
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe role of immunization in public health is crucial, offering widespread protection against infectious diseases and underpinning societal well-being. However, achieving optimal vaccination coverage is impeded by vaccine hesitancy, a significant challenge that necessitates comprehensive strategies to understand and mitigate its effects. We propose the integration of Population Health Management principles with Immunization Information Systems (IISs) to address vaccine hesitancy more effectively. Our approach leverages systematic health determinants analysis to identify at-risk populations and tailor interventions, thereby promoting vaccination coverage and public health responses. We call for the development of an enhanced version of the Italian National Vaccination Registry, which aims to facilitate real-time tracking of individuals’ vaccination status while improving data accuracy and interoperability among healthcare systems. This registry is designed to overcome current barriers by ensuring robust data protection, addressing cultural and organizational challenges, and integrating behavioral insights to foster informed public health campaigns. Our proposal aligns with the Italian National Vaccination Prevention Plan 2023–2025 and emphasizes proactive, evidence-based strategies to increase vaccination uptake and contrast the spread of vaccine-preventable diseases. The ultimate goal is to establish a data-driven, ethically sound framework that enhances public health outcomes and addresses the complexities of vaccine hesitancy within the Italian context and beyond.KEYWORDS: Immunization information system, proactive medicine, immunization programs, vaccination hesitancy, ethical issues

Introduction

The significance of vaccination within the realm of public health cannot be overstated. Indeed, vaccinations have reduced the prevalence and impact of infectious diseases, underpinning global health security and societal well-being.1 Establishing and maintaining high vaccination coverage is crucial for achieving herd immunity, which protects those who are unvaccinated or cannot be vaccinated due to medical reasons.2 These objectives are threatened by vaccine hesitancy, defined as a delay in acceptance or refusal of vaccines despite availability, endangering the success of vaccination programs.3,4 Therefore, a complex legislative and organizational architecture is required, spanning from the formulation and enforcement of vaccination policies, to the orchestration of comprehensive public health campaigns.5 The WHO’s Global Vaccine Action Plan,6 the International Health Regulations7 and the recent proposal for a Pandemic Treaty8,9 underscores the legal basis for countries to work together for global health security, with a special focus on vaccine-preventable diseases.

In parallel, the emergence of population health management (PHM) as a theoretical and practical framework offers innovative avenues for emphasizing the systematic analysis of health determinants across populations to improve health outcomes,10,11 thus enabling health authorities to identify at-risk populations, tailor interventions, and allocate resources more effectively.12 This approach aligns with the principle of proactive prevention, meant to recognize social determinants of health for evidence-based programs targeted to health disparities.13

The application of PHM principles to vaccine hesitancy could address one of the most pressing challenges in public health. By identifying specific populations and subgroups with heightened levels of hesitancy, PHM facilitates the design and implementation of targeted communication and intervention strategies, grounded in an understanding of local beliefs, cultural norms, and individual concerns.14

The post-pandemic context set the scene for a complex interplay of challenges and opportunities concerning vaccination coverages and the management of vaccine hesitancy.15 As a paradigmatic example, established by a decree from the Ministry of Health in 2018, the Italian National Vaccine Registry should aim to accurately assess vaccination rates across the nation, essential for the implementation of current vaccination programs and providing data to national and international health authorities. It collects detailed information on vaccinated individuals, those due for vaccination, and those exempt under specific health risks. Additionally, it records vaccination doses, schedules, and any adverse effects. Nevertheless, the registry’s national and regional implementation is still incomplete, reflecting challenges in achieving uniform data collection and reporting standards. Moreover, despite the 2017 mandatory vaccination law,16 the national rates still persist under 95% for some of the mandatory and recommended vaccines.17 At the regional level, the National Health Service (NHS) faces specific challenges in implementing common measures to enforce the vaccination mandates and navigate the complexities of informed dissent, due to diverse socioeconomic landscapes, varying levels of access to healthcare services, and differences in public attitudes.18,19

There is a call for an enhanced version of current national and regional immunization information systems (IISs) that can more effectively manage vaccine hesitancy, with a focus on improving interoperability, data accuracy, and the integration of behavioral insights into vaccination registries.20,21 According to the strategic objectives of the latest Italian National Vaccination Prevention Plan 2022–2025,22 the rationale behind developing an integrated registry including data both on vaccinated and unvaccinated individuals is anchored in the pressing public health imperative to identify and comprehend which segments of the population are under-vaccinated. The proposed registry would not represent an intrusive oversight mechanism but rather a proactive game-changer tool to enhance public health responses through precise, data-driven insights into mandatory and recommended vaccination gaps, especially in the event of epidemiological outbreaks.

The primary objective of this proposal focuses on refining aspects such as interoperability, data accuracy, and user-friendliness of the existing IISs, leveraging on digital tools.23 Enhancements will facilitate real-time tracking of vaccination statuses and trends, improving the registry’s interoperability among different healthcare systems and regions. The accessibility of the registry needs improvement, making it easier for healthcare providers to input and access data.24,25 Another objective of this renewed registry is to enable the identification of demographic and socioeconomic factors associated with vaccine hesitancy and to discern patterns among different population groups, depending on the root causes of hesitancy, whether they be cultural, informational, or access-related.14,26 The ultimate aim is to inform, design and implement public health interventions specifically tailored to increase vaccination uptake.27,28 Implementing the aforementioned objectives requires a multi-disciplinary approach, incorporating expertise from public health professionals, data scientists, policymakers and community leaders.

Materials and methods

Available literature overview

Prior to formulating our proposal, we comprehensively searched the available literature. We conducted a systematic review of the current state-of-the-art of registries of non-vaccinated individuals. The search strategies adopted (Supplementary Table 1), the PRISMA flow diagram (Supplementary Figure 1) and the adoption of PRISMA guidelines29 for conduction underscore the rigorous methodology employed. The search yielded no substantial results, indicating a scarcity of focused research in this area and emphasizing the need for more targeted research efforts to understand and improve the available tools and suggested mechanisms for tracking and engaging non-vaccinated populations.

Ethical considerations

The development and implementation of a registry including non-vaccinated individuals necessitates a rigorous examination of ethical considerations, particularly concerning privacy, data protection, and the autonomy of individuals. Ensuring the ethical integrity of the registry is paramount, as it involves the collection, storage, and potentially, the sharing of sensitive health information.

In the European Union, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) serves as the cornerstone for data protection, including health data. The GDPR stipulates specific conditions for the processing of personal health data, highlighting the importance of ensuring that data is processed for legitimate purposes directly related to health care and public health protection.30 Moreover, the GDPR introduces the concept of data minimization, where only the necessary amount of data should be collected and processed for the specific purpose at hand. Additionally, Article 9 of the GDPR places strict conditions on the processing of special categories of personal data, such as health data, generally prohibiting its processing unless specific exemptions apply. These exemptions include cases where data processing is necessary for public health purposes, under the condition that it is based on EU or member state law and provides suitable and specific measures to safeguard the individual’s rights and freedoms.31

First, in compliance with GDPR, the registry must adhere to the highest standards of privacy and data protection, complying with national and international regulations. It is essential to implement robust encryption and anonymization techniques to protect the data from unauthorized access and ensure that the information is used solely for public health purposes. The principle of data minimization should be applied, collecting only the information necessary for the registry’s objectives.32

Secondly, the registry must ensure the confidentiality of the data collected, restricting access to authorized personnel only. Measures should be in place to prevent data breaches and ensure that individuals’ health information is not disclosed without reason. Policies and procedures for data access and sharing should be transparent and governed by strict ethical standards.33

At last, the use of health data for proactive medicine, including the management of a comprehensive registry for vaccinated and non-vaccinated individuals, requires a deep understanding of the legal frameworks around the collection, storage, and utilization of such data. Proactive medicine aims to anticipate health issues and intervene before they manifest, necessitating access to comprehensive health data. However, this approach must be balanced with stringent data protection laws to ensure individual privacy and security.

Considering Italian national laws, specific regulations address the processing of health data for public health purposes, outlining additional safeguards and conditions under which health data can be used to support proactive health interventions.34 Italy’s legislation on vaccination, particularly Law No. 119/2017, mandates the vaccination for children against 10 infectious diseases for school admission.35 The law also includes provisions for the collection and processing of vaccination data to ensure compliance with established requirements, suggesting a legal framework that could potentially accommodate the establishment of a comprehensive IIS for public health purposes.36 However, any initiative to establish such a registry would need to meticulously ensure compliance with GDPR requirements regarding the processing of health data. This includes ensuring that the data collected is strictly necessary for the stated public health purpose and providing clear mechanisms for identifying an appropriate legal basis that justifies the processing without consent under specific conditions, such as the case of threat to collective health, as endorsed by Article 32 of the Italian Constitution. Moreover, the ethical considerations and potential societal impacts of creating a registry of vaccinated and non-vaccinated individuals must be carefully weighed, including concerns related to privacy, stigmatization, and the potential for coercion. Engaging with stakeholders, legal experts, ethicists, public health professionals, and the public, would be essential to deal with these complexities and ensure that the registry’s development aligns with both legal requirements and societal values. Transparent communication, robust data protection measures, and clear benefits to individuals and communities are essential components of this approach, as was recently testified by the lawsuits which followed the use of personal health data for targeted anti-SARS-CoV-2 vaccination administration in Friuli Venezia Giulia.37

Data collection

The methodology for data collection in establishing a comprehensive registry on vaccinations requires meticulous planning to ensure the accuracy, privacy, and utility of the data collected. The strategy encompasses various data sources, types of data, and methodologies for data aggregation and anonymization, in line with best practices and ethical standards.

With reference to the type of data needed, detailed records of vaccinations administered (i.e., type of vaccine, number of doses, dates of administration) should be included. Moreover, the registry should comprehend information on why individuals are unvaccinated or under-vaccinated, including medical exemptions, access issues, or personal beliefs, as well as demographic variables (i.e., age, gender, geographic location, and socioeconomic status) to analyze vaccination coverage disparities and target interventions effectively. Medical records from any healthcare provider could serve as a primary source for integration of data. These records offer detailed patient histories, including any medical conditions. Centralized health databases at the national and regional levels are invaluable for tracking population vaccination coverage.

In order to ensure data aggregation, statistical software and methodologies to compile data from various sources into a unified database are to be adopted. This process involves standardizing data formats, verifying data accuracy, and resolving discrepancies. Implementing techniques such as pseudonymisation or encryption will guarantee individuals’ privacy.38

Stakeholder engagement

Effective stakeholder engagement is essential for the success of establishing such a registry. The engagement strategy should be widely inclusive of healthcare professionals, community leaders, public health officials, and, importantly, non-vaccinated individuals and their families, as well as culturally sensitive and respectful of individual rights and community values.39

First, collaboration with healthcare professionals can help identify barriers to vaccination, inform effective communication strategies, and facilitate the accurate collection of vaccination data. Methods of engagement can include surveys, workshops, and participation in advisory committees.

Secondly, community leaders and representatives of non-governmental organizations play a vital role in shaping public opinions about vaccination. Engaging with them can enhance the registry’s acceptance and effectiveness by ensuring that interventions are culturally sensitive and community-specific.40

Moreover, the coordination with public health authorities at local, regional, and national levels ensures that the registry aligns with broader public health objectives and strategies. This collaboration can also provide access to resources, data, and platforms necessary for the registry’s implementation and interoperability.

Finally, and most difficulty, debating with non-vaccinated groups and their families is crucial to understanding their concerns and fostering trust in the registry and vaccination campaigns. Direct engagement through surveys, interviews, and participation in community dialogs offers valuable insights into personal beliefs and barriers to vaccination. This approach not only aids in tailoring interventions but also respects and values the autonomy and perspective of those directly impacted by the registry.39,41

The stakeholder engagement process should be ongoing, with mechanisms for feedback, collaboration, and strategy adjustment as needed.

Results and discussion

Data analysis

The analytical framework for assessing the data collected by the registry will employ a variety of statistical methods to uncover trends, patterns, and associations related to non-vaccination or under-vaccination. The initial analysis will involve descriptive statistics to summarize the demographic and socio-economic characteristics, highlighting the overall distribution of non-vaccination across different population segments. Time series analysis will identify trends in vaccination hesitancy over time, especially in relation to public health campaigns, policy changes, or outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases. Further analyses will be conducted to help identify significant associations between non-vaccination and factors such as geographic location, socioeconomic status, educational background, and access to healthcare services. Multivariate logistic regression analysis will be used to control for confounding variables and to determine the independent predictors of hesitancy. Cluster analysis will be employed to segment the non-vaccinated population into distinct groups based on similar characteristics. Geospatial analysis techniques will be used to map vaccination rates and identify geographical patterns and “hot spots” where public health efforts can be concentrated.

Intervention development

The insights gained from the data analysis phase will be instrumental in informing the development of targeted interventions, tailored to specific association factors, ensuring that they resonate with the intended audiences. Data revealing misconceptions or lack of knowledge will lead to the design of educational campaigns, focused on disseminating accurate, evidence-based information about the safety and efficacy of vaccines, debunking common myths, and highlighting the risks of vaccine-preventable diseases. A variety of media channels, including social media, will be employed, adapting linguistic registers to cultural sensitivities to enhance receptivity.42,43 To foster trust in healthcare providers, the development of community-based initiatives will be essential. These initiatives could involve training community health workers to conduct outreach vaccination education or establishing partnerships with local leaders and organizations to co-create culturally appropriate and community-endorsed interventions.44,45

Challenges and opportunities

The implementation of a registry of non-vaccinated individuals is not without its challenges and barriers. These obstacles span organizational, cultural, and regulatory domains, each requiring careful consideration and strategic planning to overcome.

One of the primary organizational barriers includes the integration of disparate health information systems across healthcare providers, schools, and public health agencies. Achieving interoperability among these systems is essential for the accurate and efficient collection of vaccination data but often encounters technical and logistical hurdles.46 Organizational challenges can be addressed through the adoption of standardized data protocols and technologies that enhance system interoperability. Engaging with technology partners and leveraging advancements in health information technology can facilitate this process.

Furthermore, navigating the complex landscape of data protection and privacy laws is non-negotiable, necessitating the development of robust data governance frameworks that ensure the ethical handling of personal health information.32

Cultural resistance to vaccination, fueled by misinformation, skepticism, and mistrust of healthcare authorities, poses a significant challenge. Overcoming these barriers requires culturally sensitive engagement strategies that respect community norms and values while effectively communicating the benefits of vaccination.14,47

In conclusion, the proposal for establishing a comprehensive registry of vaccinated and non-vaccinated individuals presents a transformative opportunity to enhance public health through targeted engagement and data-driven strategies. This initiative, rooted in the principles of PHM and informed by a detailed analysis of current challenges in vaccine coverage and hesitancy, aims to leverage existing infrastructure while addressing critical gaps in data collection, stakeholder engagement, and intervention development. By facing the ethical, organizational, and regulatory landscapes with care and precision, this proposal sets forth a roadmap for improving vaccination uptake and thereby, the health and well-being of populations. The challenges and barriers identified, while significant, are not insurmountable and provide valuable insights into the complexities of implementing such an initiative within the existing legal and societal framework. As this proposal moves from concept to implementation, it holds the promise of not only addressing the immediate concerns related to vaccine-preventable diseases but also setting a precedent for how data and technology can be harnessed to meet public health challenges. Ultimately, the success of this registry will depend on collaborative efforts across sectors, a commitment to ethical principles, and the continuous evaluation and adaptation of strategies to meet the evolving needs of public health.48

Biography

Anna Odone (MD MSc MPH PhD) is Professor and Director of the School of Public Health at the University of Pavia. She is a member of the Italian Supreme Health Council (CSS) and the Ministry of Health 2021–2025 National Immunization Prevention Plan Working Group. Medical doctor by training, Prof. Odone received an MSc in Epidemiology from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine (London, UK) and an MPH in Health Policy and Management as a Fulbright Scholar from the Harvard School of Public Health (Boston, US). She holds a PhD in Medical Sciences from the University of Parma. Passionate about the value of public health, she mentors public health researchers, residents, and PhD students. Her research is in infectious diseases control and health determinants, with a focus on immunization programs and policies, mental health, and behavioral risk factors.

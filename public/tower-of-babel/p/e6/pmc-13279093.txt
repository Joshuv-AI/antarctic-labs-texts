Left Atrial Volume Index and Left Ventricular Mass Index Determine the Benefits of Spironolactone in Patients With Heart Failure With Preserved Ejection Fraction
Authors: Hongbin Zang, Chenhong Jia, Yilong Pan, Xiaodong Li, Qiongyu Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIt is unclear whether patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction respond to spironolactone based on cardiac structure or function.MethodsA post hoc analysis was performed among participants with left ventricular (LV) ejection fraction ≥50% and evidence of abnormal LV diastolic function or filling pressures in TOPCAT (Treatment of Preserved Cardiac Function Heart Failure with an Aldosterone Antagonist Trial). Log‐rank tests and Cox regression models were used for comparison between groups.ResultsTotally, 757 subjects were qualified and stratified by parameters on cardiac structure or function. In patients with increased left atrial volume index, spironolactone reduced cardiovascular mortality (adjusted hazard ratio [HR], 0.48 [95% CI, 0.24–0.98]; P=0.042, log‐rank; interaction P=0.045). Meanwhile, in patients with increased LV mass index, spironolactone decreased heart failure hospitalization (adjusted HR, 0.51 [95% CI, 0.32–0.80]; P=0.009, log‐rank; interaction P=0.041) and the composite outcome of cardiovascular mortality, heart failure hospitalization, and aborted sudden death (adjusted HR, 0.63 [95% CI, 0.43–0.93]; P=0.045, log‐rank; interaction P=0.029). Then, in participants with increased left atrial volume index and LV mass index, who matched at 1:1 ration by propensity score matching, spironolactone reduced cardiovascular mortality (adjusted HR, 0.31 [95% CI, 0.11–0.92]; P=0.039, log‐rank), heart failure hospitalization (adjusted HR, 0.26 [95% CI, 0.10–0.68]; P=0.005, log‐rank), and the composite outcome (adjusted HR, 0.33 [95% CI, 0.15–0.72]; P=0.006, log‐rank).ConclusionsSpironolactone reduced cardiovascular mortality, heart failure hospitalization, or the composite outcome of cardiovascular mortality, heart failure hospitalization, and aborted sudden death in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction with increased left atrial volume index or LV mass index.

Currently, a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of ≥50% is used to define heart failure with preserved EF (HFpEF). Patients with HFpEF constitute approximately half of all cases of HF. 1 Among them, older individuals, women, and people with obesity make up a larger percentage. The statistics indicate that the 1‐year mortality for patients with HFpEF ranges from 20% to 30%, 2 , 3 whereas the 5‐year mortality gets up to 55%. 4 Several factors have been identified to influence the prognosis of HFpEF, including anemia, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and frequent hospital readmissions for HF. 1

Up to now, limited treatments have been confirmed to enhance the prognosis of patients with HFpEF. In the TOPCAT (Treatment of Preserved Cardiac Function Heart Failure with an Aldosterone Antagonist Trial), which was the largest clinical trial of aldosterone receptor antagonists in HFpEF, spironolactone did not alter the composite primary outcome but only reduced the incidence of readmission for HF. 5 Subsequent post hoc analyses suggested that the benefits of spironolactone treatment were partially dependent on sex, body mass index, and concomitant diseases. 6 , 7 , 8 However, these studies overlooked the echocardiographic parameters, which could be used to diagnose HFpEF. These parameters included the average E/e′ ratio, global longitudinal systolic strain, left atrial volume index (LAVi), LV mass index (LVMi), LV wall thickness (LVWT), and tricuspid regurgitation (TR) velocity. 9 It is known that HFpEF is a heterogeneous disease, and the aforementioned echocardiographic parameters can also be used to classify HFpEF. Therefore, we hypothesize that these echocardiographic parameters may be capable of identifying subgroups of patients with HFpEF who can benefit from spironolactone therapy.

Differences in outcomes were detected across the Americas and Russia/Georgia in TOPCAT. Only in the Americas, spironolactone reduced the primary outcome, cardiovascular death, and HF hospitalization. 10 Studies attributed the regional variation to the enrollment criteria. Compared with those in the Americas, a higher proportion of patients in Russia/Georgia were enrolled for prior HF hospitalization, resulting in the heterogeneity of patient profiles. Patients from the Americas suffered from more risk factors for HFpEF, which, as we mentioned, could also indicate the benefits of spironolactone. 7 , 8 , 11 Interestingly, in post hoc analyses, when NPs (natriuretic peptides) or early diastolic mitral annular systolic velocity were incorporated as an essential criterion, the benefits of spironolactone became significant. 12 , 13 These discrepancies raised concerns about suboptimal trial criteria in TOPCAT. Thus, in this article, we decided to reinforce that point via identifying patients with HFpEF according to echocardiographic and laboratory evidence.

There were still challenges in identifying patients with HFpEF. Several diagnostic algorithms were recommended, such as the heavy, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, pulmonary, elder, filling pressure score algorithm, 14 the Heart Failure Association pretest probability echocardiography, functional testing, final diagnosis (HFA‐PEFF) score algorithm, 9 , 15 and the American Society of Echocardiography guideline for diastolic dysfunction. 16 The HFA‐PEFF score included a series of echocardiographic and laboratory indices. The major and minor criteria for these indices were designed to address diagnostic specificity and sensitivity, respectively. The other 2 algorithms prioritized the E/e′ ratio and TR velocity as the primary metrics. However, the 2 parameters had limited records in TOPCAT. Considering the incomplete echo data, we employed the criteria for indices in the HFA‐PEFF score but not the whole scoring system to screen participants in TOPCAT, aiming to ensure a sufficient sample size and identify participants with objective evidence for HFpEF. Moreover, the cutoff values for the echocardiographic parameters used in the stratification analysis were consistent with the criteria specified in the HFA‐PEFF score.

Current guidelines define HFpEF as LVEF ≥50%, whereas the cutoff value is 45% in TOPCAT and the previous post hoc analyses. 5 , 15 , 17 Although spironolactone has been recommended for selected patients with HFpEF, particularly those with LVEF at the lower end of the LVEF spectrum, 17 its therapeutic benefits in patients with currently defined HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) remain unclear. Meanwhile, it is not clear which echocardiographic parameters can predict the benefits of spironolactone in those patients. Therefore, we conducted a post hoc analysis of TOPCAT to clarify the role of spironolactone in currently defined patients with HFpEF and identify specific patients with HFpEF who might benefit from spironolactone therapy based on echocardiographic parameters.

Methods

Data Extraction

TOPCAT was a multicenter, randomized controlled trial that evaluated the effectiveness of spironolactone in symptomatic patients with HF with an LVEF ≥45%. The design and results were published. An echocardiographic subgroup was enrolled. The baseline characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, and outcome events were recorded in the Biologic Specimen and Data Repository Information Coordinating Center. Following the authorized proposal, data of our interest were obtained. This study was approved by the institutional review board at Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University. The authors declare that all supporting data are encompassed within this article (and its online supplemental materials). The patient‐level data from the TOPCAT study are accessible through the Biologic Specimen and Data Repository Information Coordinating Center.

Screening Criteria

Guidelines have updated the definition of HFpEF. Consequently, we screened the participants in the echocardiographic subgroup using the following criteria: (1) symptoms and signs of HF; (2) LVEF ≥50%; and (3) evidence for cardiac structural or functional abnormalities, including average E/e′ ≥9, global longitudinal systolic strain <16%, LAVi >29 mL/m2 in subjects with sinus rhythm or >34 mL/m2 in subjects with atrial fibrillation, LVMi ≥115 g/m2 in men or ≥95 g/m2 in women, LVWT ≥12 mm, relative wall thickness >0.42, TR velocity >2.8 m/s, BNP (B‐type NP) >35 pg/mL or NT‐proBNP (N‐terminal proBNP) >125 pg/mL in subjects with sinus rhythm, and BNP >105 pg/mL or NT‐proBNP >365 pg/mL in subjects with atrial fibrillation. The criteria for the echocardiographic parameters and NPs were based on the HFA‐PEFF score. For each index, a minor criterion, if available, was used to balance diagnostic rigor with pragmatic adaptation to TOPCAT's data, and ensure a sufficient sample size. 9

Stratification Factors

According to the number of patients with corresponding echocardiographic parameters, we decided to undertake stratification analysis based on average E/e′, global longitudinal systolic strain, LAVi, LVMi, LVWT, and TR velocity.

Outcome Measures

We retrieved all outcome events for each subject during the follow‐up period from TOPCAT. In accordance with the original study, the primary outcome was defined as a composite of cardiovascular mortality, HF hospitalization, and aborted sudden death. Other outcomes included all‐cause mortality and all‐cause hospitalization.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD. Binary or categorical variables were presented as frequency (percentage). Student's t tests were used to compare continuous variables, and χ 2 tests were used to compare binary or categorical variables. Kaplan–Meier curves were plotted for time‐to‐event outcomes. Log‐rank tests were used to examine differences in outcomes between groups. If Kaplan–Meier curves were crossed over, inverse probability weighting would be performed to adjust log‐rank tests. 18 Cox proportional‐hazards models were used to obtain the crude and adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs. The adjusted covariates included age, sex, body mass index, diabetes, hypertension, and serum creatinine (≥1.5 mg/dL), 6 , 7 , 8 , 19 , 20 which had been shown to have an impact on the effectiveness of spironolactone in patients with HFpEF by previous studies. A threshold of 1.5 mg/dL was established for serum creatinine because it was associated with a high rate of rehospitalizations and had an independent unfavorable effect on 6‐month mortality in patients with HF. 21 Furthermore, these covariates had P values <0.1 in the univariate Cox regression with the primary outcome or its components as dependent variables. The proportional hazards assumption of the Cox models was assessed using the Schoenfeld residuals tests. Time‐dependent variables would be modeled using time‐varying functions. The heterogeneity of the effectiveness of spironolactone across the strata was assessed using interaction P value in Cox proportional‐hazards models. Propensity scores were established using a multivariate logistic regression model, and the measured confounders were consistent with the adjusted covariates mentioned earlier. A 1:1 nearest‐neighbor matching without replacement was conducted. The caliper was 0.005. Standardized mean differences and kernel density curves were used to evaluate the balance of covariates after propensity score matching (PSM), with standardized mean difference >0.2 indicating significant baseline differences between groups. Overlap was assessed by kernel density curves with a ratio of the overlapping area to the total area under the curves. The robust Cox proportional‐hazards models, which could prevent overfitting or bias in the estimation effects, were employed to obtain the adjusted HRs after PSM. The sensitivity analysis for survival analysis was performed using the Fine–Gray models, 22 and the sensitivity analysis for PSM was performed using the Rosenbaum bounds tests. 23 Only 1 case had missing data. Given the extremely low proportion, the missing data in numerical variables were replaced with the mean, whereas those in categorical variables were imputed with the mode. A P value <0.05 was considered significant. Several packages in R studio were used for data analysis. Log‐rank tests, Schoenfeld residual tests, and robust Cox regression were analyzed using the survival package. Inverse probability of treatment weighting was analyzed using the Research in Informatic and Statistic for Cohort Analyses package. Standardized mean differences were calculated using the cobalt package. Fine–Gray competing risk analysis was conducted using the cmprsk package. Rosenbaum bounds sensitivity analysis was conducted using the rbounds package. Other statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 25.0.

Results

The flow diagram was shown in Figure 1. A total of 757 subjects were qualified. The mean follow‐up interval was 3.2 years in each group. In the spironolactone group, 26 participants withdrew consent, 7 participants were lost to follow‐up, and 1 participant discontinued the study for other reasons. Meanwhile, in the placebo group, 18 participants withdrew consent, 11 participants were lost to follow‐up, and 1 participant discontinued the study for other reasons. For those who discontinued the study, vital status was obtained as of the last potential study visit. Subsequently, the statistical analysis was performed.

On the primary outcome, spironolactone showed different effects between the strata by LVMi. In the increased LVMi stratum, 104 participants experienced at least 1 primary‐outcome event: 45 (26.9%) in the spironolactone group, and 59 (36.2%) in the placebo group. The adjusted HR was 0.63 (95% CI, 0.43–0.93; P=0.045, log‐rank; Figure 2; Table 4). In the normal LVMi stratum, 93 participants experienced at least 1 primary‐outcome event: 52 (24.9%) in the spironolactone group and 41 (20.3%) in the placebo group. The adjusted HR was 1.18 (95% CI, 0.78–1.79, P=0.297, log‐rank; Figure 2; Table 4). The interaction test for the primary outcome was statistically significant (interaction P=0.029, Table 4).

On cardiovascular mortality, spironolactone demonstrated distinct effects between the strata by LAVi. Sensitivity Analysis

Sensitivity analysis for the survival results was performed using Fine–Gray competing risk models with noncardiovascular mortality as competing events. The results confirmed the robustness of the benefits of spironolactone in subjects with increased LAVi or LVMi, which were identified by traditional Cox proportional‐hazards models (Table S7). In addition, sensitivity analysis for PSM was conducted using Rosenbaum bounds tests. For the primary outcome, cardiovascular mortality, and HF hospitalization, the γ values were >2.0, indicating that the results were robust enough against hidden bias (Figure S2). 23

DISCUSSION

The use of spironolactone in patients with HFpEF remains controversial. Previous small‐scale randomized controlled trials 24 , 25 , 26 and cohort studies 27 suggested potential benefits of spironolactone therapy for patients with HFpEF. However, in the TOPCAT trial, spironolactone did not alter the composite primary outcomes, only reducing the rate of HF hospitalization. 5 Then, the American Heart Association guideline recommended spironolactone for selected patients with HFpEF to reduce HF hospitalization. 17 Our findings expanded the scope of the selected patients. In this post hoc analysis, we found that LAVi and LVMi indicated the benefits of spironolactone in patients with HFpEF. Specifically, spironolactone reduced the incidences of cardiovascular mortality in those with increased LAVi, as well as the composite primary outcome and HF hospitalization in those with increased LVMi. Furthermore, the aforementioned benefits were also observed in patients with increased LAVi and LVMi. Previous evidence indicated that the benefits of spironolactone vary across the LVEF spectrum. 28 However, we demonstrated no heterogeneity of spironolactone across the LVEF spectrum in patients with HFpEF with increased LAVi or LVMi. Additionally, we also confirmed that the benefits of spironolactone were maintained in patients with HFpEF with increased LAVi or LVMi and LVEF ≥55%.

It was noteworthy that LAVi delineated cardiovascular mortality but not other clinical outcomes in the overall patients with HFpEF. The possible reasons were as follows: First, cardiovascular mortality accounted for only a relatively low proportion of the total events. The benefits of spironolactone in patients with increased LVMi were mainly due to the reduction in HF hospitalization. In contrast, in patients with increased LAVi, the benefits were mainly due to the reduction in cardiovascular mortality. However, the rates of cardiovascular mortality, aborted sudden death, and HF hospitalization were 11.0%, 0.1%, and 18.6%, respectively, indicating that HF hospitalization, rather than cardiovascular mortality, accounted for a major proportion of the total events. Therefore, LVMi delineated both HF hospitalization and the primary outcome, whereas LAVi delineated only cardiovascular mortality. Second, although LAVi failed to delineate HF hospitalization and the primary outcome, a trend of reduction by spironolactone was detected, suggesting that the limited sample size might account for the statistical insignificance. However, with the available data, this hypothesis could not be confirmed. Finally, the reduction in HF hospitalization and the primary outcome by spironolactone in patients with increased LAVi and LVEF ≥55% implied that the delineating role of LAVi on these outcomes might be LVEF‐dependent. Because the P values for interaction across the LVEF spectrum failed to reach the statistical thresholds, these benefits should be interpreted with caution.

Studies have suggested that serum aldosterone levels are associated with the development and prognosis of HFpEF. 29 Aldosterone induces myocardial remodeling and decline cardiac compliance by promoting myocytes hypertrophy, chronic inflammation, and extracellular matrix dysfunction. 30 , 31 These effects suggest the underlying effectiveness of blocking aldosterone pathway in HFpEF. However, HFpEF is a heterogeneous disease. 32 The pathophysiological variations among phenotypes may account for the failure of clinical trials to establish a successful therapy for HFpEF.

In current guidelines, a series of echocardiographic parameters, including average E/e′ ratio, global longitudinal systolic strain, LAVi, LVMi, LVWT, and TR velocity, have been recommended to evaluate LV diastolic function or LV filling pressure of patients with HFpEF. 9 , 15 , 33 When stratified by these parameters, to our surprise, we identified spironolactone treatment specially benefited patients with HFpEF with increased LAVi or LVMi. LAVi represents LA remodeling and LV filling pressure. 16 The upper limit of LAVi for healthy subjects is 29 to 34 mL/m2, whereas patients with atrial fibrillation always have an enlarged left atrium. 34 , 35 Guidelines recommend separate cutoff values for LAVi in sinus rhythm versus atrial fibrillation. Currently, the value is 34 mL/m2 for patients with atrial fibrillation. 9 LVMi, which is calculated by dividing LV mass by body surface area, is commonly used to assess LV remodeling. 36 Compared with LVWT, LVMi better depicts the degree of LV hypertrophy.

The reasons why patients with HFpEF with higher LAVi or LVMi levels benefit from spironolactone remain unknown. A possible explanation is that these benefits could be attributed to varying degrees of aldosterone elevation. Studies have shown that elevated aldosterone levels are associated with the enlargement of the left atrium and left ventricle, 37 indicating higher levels and more dominant pathogenic roles of aldosterone in patients with increased LAVi or LVMi. The enlarged left atrium and ventricle can interact with each other. LV hypertrophy leads to the enlargement of the left atrium, which in turn exacerbates the abnormality in LV diastolic function or filling pressure, subsequently worsening LV hypertrophy. 38 Additionally, LAVi and LVMi may represent different subtypes of HFpEF, with distinct mechanisms and prognostic outcomes. Evidence suggests that increased LAVi or LVMi are predictive of adverse outcomes in patients with HFpEF. 39 The benefits of spironolactone treatment may be apparent only at higher event rates.

As of now, only SGLT2 (sodium/glucose cotransporter 2) inhibitors have demonstrated an improvement in outcomes for patients with HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%). 40 , 41 , 42 Sacubitril/valsartan has been endorsed by the US Food and Drug Administration for use in patients with HF with an LVEF of <55%. 43 Our findings suggest that patients with HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%) with increased LAVi or LVMi should be treated with spironolactone, offering additional options to enhance the prognosis of patients with HFpEF.

This article had several limitations. First, incomplete echocardiographic data, caused by the missing parameters for some patients, affected the analyses and results, and even resulted in only 94 patients remaining after PSM. To maximize the influence, we implemented a series of measures. For example, we used appropriate criteria to balance diagnostic rigor with pragmatic adaptation to TOPCAT's data. Moreover, the analysis of each echocardiographic parameter was conducted among all individuals who reported the parameter to ensure a sufficient sample size and the credibility of the results. Second, the sample size was insufficient to conduct stratification analysis with multiple factors. This restricted our attempts to delineate outcomes using multiple echocardiographic parameters. Third, although confounding variables that might affect the benefits of spironolactone treatment in patients with HFpEF were adjusted for, the results of this post hoc analysis were less credible than those from randomized controlled trials. Therefore, further high‐quality randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm our findings.

Conclusions

Before our study, spironolactone had been endorsed for the treatment of HFpEF to reduce HF hospitalization, although its recommendation level was only 2B. We further confirmed that LAVi and LVMi could determine the benefits of spironolactone, which reduced cardiovascular mortality, HF hospitalization, or the composite primary outcome in patients with HFpEF with increased LAVi or LVMi.

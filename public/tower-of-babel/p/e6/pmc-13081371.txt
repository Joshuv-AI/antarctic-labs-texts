68Ga/177Lu-labeled nectin4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide: a novel nectin-4-targeted radioligands for theranostic of urothelial carcinoma
Authors: Li Sun, Bin Zhu, Ke Zuo, Liyan Bai, Jialiang Lei, Ye Li, Xinyu Liu, Xiaoyu Pan, Juan Li, Aiguo Wu, Yijun Shen, Xiaoping Xu, Shaoli Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nectin-4 is markedly overexpressed in urothelial carcinoma (UC) and represents a promising theranostic target for both radiotherapy and positron emission tomography (PET) imaging. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved Nectin-4-targeted drug, enfortumab vedotin, has demonstrated substantial efficacy in the treatment of UC. Developing a non-invasive quantitative detection technology based on positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) imaging is crucial for accurately measuring Nectin-4 expression levels, facilitating precise patient selection, treatment stratification, and efficacy monitoring in UC. In the present study, a series of 68Ga/177Lu-labelled covalent bicyclic peptide (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs) targeted Nectin-4 were constructed for PET/CT imaging and targeted radiotherapy based on a SuFEx-bio-orthogonal strategy. In vitro experiments demonstrated that 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs exhibited high radiochemical purity, stability, and strong Nectin-4 affinity. MicroPET/CT imaging confirmed that covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs exhibited strong tumor enrichment, low off-target binding, and excellent tumor/muscle ratios (> 4.90 at 60 min), thus validating Nectin-4 as an effective imaging target. In terms of clinical translation, all the 68Ga-FZ-NRs localized in a highly precise manner to lesions in UC patients. PET/CT-positive areas matched 18F-Fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positioning and regions of high Nectin-4 expression in biopsied tissues. In terms of radiotherapy, covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs exhibited a significantly higher tumor uptake (an average increase of ~ 4-fold at 24 h) and retention (~ 4-fold longer, up to 48 h) than non-covalent 177Lu-FZ-NR-1, thus enhancing therapeutic effects. In treatment experiments, covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs strongly inhibited tumor growth without obvious toxicity. In summary, our novel Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands provide new molecular imaging tools for the non-invasive assessment of Nectin-4 and establish a foundation for UC-targeted radiotherapy.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12951-026-04253-0.

Introduction

Over recent years, targeted antibody-drug conjugates directed against Nectin-4 [1–5], such as enfortumab vedotin (EV) [6–7] and EV-301 [8], have made significant progress in the treatment of advanced urothelial carcinoma (UC), notably extending patient survival (with a median overall survival increase of 3.9 months in the EV-301 trial) [9]. However, approximately 30% of metastatic patients experience treatment failure due to the loss of Nectin-4 expression [10]; a process that exhibits significant heterogeneity among molecular subtypes [11]. This highlights the critical need for the systematic evaluation of receptor expression prior to treatment, along with subsequent dynamic monitoring, to identify patients who are most likely to benefit from therapy. Furthermore, antibody-based drugs often suffer from insufficient tumor penetration and face challenges related to payload release and stability [12]. Therefore, developing new diagnostic and therapeutic strategies targeting Nectin-4-positive tumors is essential.

Biopsy remains the clinical gold standard for guiding the diagnosis and treatment of cancer [13]. However, traditional biopsies face challenges in meeting the demand for the dynamic monitoring of Nectin-4 due to their invasive nature and potential operational risks, especially in sensitive areas such as the brain [14]. Consequently, non-invasive molecular imaging techniques based on positron emission tomography/ computed tomography‌ (PET/CT) are being considered as a potential solution [15]. Despite the development of Nectin-4-targeted radioligands [16–17], such as 89Zr-DFO-Enfortumab [18], the long half-life of these compounds in the blood leads to imaging delays of three to five days, thus compromising the tumor-to-background ratio. To enhance the efficiency of imaging, bicyclic peptide (BT8009) [19] derivatives, such as 68Ga-N188 [20–21], provide significant advantages due to their rapid renal clearance and minimal retention in normal tissues. In our previous work [22], 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 could effectively detect Nectin-4 positive lesions with high accumulation in triple-negative breast cancer patients, as validated by 18F-Fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) imaging and immunohistochemistry. However, 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 showed rapid clearance from tumors, which restricts its theranostic potential.

Targeted radioligand therapy (TRT) addresses the limitations of traditional targeted therapies [18, 23–24], such as EV-based treatments, by utilizing radiation-induced physical damage.This approach not only has the potential to overcome drug resistance to some extent, but it also has the potential to enhance the safety of the overall treatment while effectively addressing micro-metastasis [25]. However, the development of therapies labeled with 177Lu3+ that rely on existing bicyclic peptides remains limited by inadequate tumor retention time [26] and low uptake efficiency. Over recent years, certain strategies, such as the incorporation of polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains (e.g., 68Ga-DN68 [27], 68Ga-NOTA-PEG12-BP [28] and lipophilic groups [29], have led to a marked enhancement in the efficiency of radiotracer uptake by tumors. For example, the incorporation of hydrophobic groups into radiolabeled therapeutic agents has been shown to enhance retention time by increasing interactions with serum proteins [30]; however, this strategy may also result in elevated radiation exposure to normal tissues [31]. Furthermore, antibody-TRT pre-targeting strategies combine the benefits of both macromolecules and small molecules [32]; however, this process requires a two-step administration process, and clinical efficacy of this strategy has yet to be validated. Furthermore, these modification strategies can increase non-specific absorption in the liver and gastrointestinal tract [33], leading to radiation toxicity in these organs, thus presenting new challenges for treatment safety. Therefore, developing and optimizing novel targeted radioligands with improved pharmacokinetic profiles, enhanced tumor uptake rates, and extended retention times is essential if we are to increase treatment efficacy while minimizing toxicity. To address these challenges, we propose a bio-orthogonal strategy utilizing a ‘latent warhead’ [25, 34]. This approach employs sulfur(VI)–fluorine exchange reactions (SuFEx) to introduce aromatic sulfonylfluoride (SF) and aromatic fluorosulfonate (FS) groups into targeted radioligands [25, 35–36]. This enables irreversible covalent bonding with specific amino acid residues–such as lysine, tyrosine, and histidine–within target proteins [37–38]. By combining non-covalent and covalent action mechanisms, this design maintains high affinity and enhances tumor retention via the proximity effect, effectively reducing off-target toxicity. This innovative method provides a promising opportunity to improve both the targeting and safety of radioligand therapy.

Here, building on the Nectin-4-targeting radiotracer previously developed by us (68Ga-FZ-NR-1), we developed covalent bicyclic peptide theranostic radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-7). The 68Ga-labeled radioligands were designed as PET imaging tracers for Nectin-4-positive UC in a first-in-human study, whereas the 177Lu-labeled radioligands were evaluated for anti-tumor activity in Nectin-4-positive tumor models.

Results

Synthesis and characterization of 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs

Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide precursors (FZ-NR-5, FZ-NR-6, and FZ-NR-7) were modified by a SuFEx-engineered molecular strategy and analyzed by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), revealing purities > 90%, with retention times of ~ 13 min (Supplementary Figure S1). Mass spectrometry further confirmed the structures of the precursors (Fig. 1A and Supplementary Figure S2). Surface plasmon resonance analysis demonstrated that FZ-NR-5, FZ-NR-6, and FZ-NR-7 exhibited excellent binding affinities for Nectin-4 protein, with dissociation equilibrium constants of 5.722 nM, 3.801 nM, and 3.234 nM, respectively (Supplementary Figure S3). Radioactive high-performance liquid chromatography (radio-HPLC) analysis of the purified bicyclic peptide Nectin-4-targeted covalent radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-7, Supplementary Figure S4A), using Sep-Pak C18 columns, revealed purities exceeding 95%, with retention times consistent with their respective precursors (Supplementary Figure S4B). Furthermore, when the covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 (177Lu-FZ-NR-5), 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 (177Lu-FZ-NR-6), and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 (177Lu-FZ-NR-7) were incubated in fetal bovine serum (FBS) and saline for 3 h (and 48 h), radio-HPLC detection showed that these radioligands maintained radiochemical purities > 95%, thus indicating that they possessed high stability (Supplementary Figure S5). Additionally, the LogP values for the radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NR-5 (-1.69 ± 0.10), 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 (-1.70 ± 0.23), and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 (-1.58 ± 0.36)) indicated clear hydrophilicity (Supplementary Figure S6).

Cell uptake of 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs

To evaluate the targeting ability of 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs towards Nectin-4, we selected HT1376 cells (which express high levels of Nectin-4) and RT112 cells (which express low levels of Nectin-4) for comparison [39]. Western blotting and semi-quantitative analysis of grayscale data confirmed that the expression levels of Nectin-4 in HT1376 cells was significantly higher than that in RT112 cells (Fig. 1B). To evaluate the specificity of the targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs), we measured the cellular uptake of each radioligand in both cell lines (1 × 10^5 cells/well, n = 6). Analysis revealed that HT1376 cells exhibited a higher uptake rate of 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs when compared to RT112 cells (Fig. 1C and D). Compared to the non-covalent 68Ga-FZ-NR-1, the uptake of Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-7) was significantly increased in cells with high Nectin-4 expression. Moreover, blocking experiments using an excess (400-fold) of the lead compound (Supplementary S3) revealed a significant reduction in uptake by HT1376 cells after 1 h of radioligand injection. After 24 h, the uptake of 177Lu-FZ-NRs in HT1376 cells remained > 2%. Data arising from cell uptake and blocking experiments provide strong evidence for the specific uptake of Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs) by HT1376 cells, further confirming the high expression of Nectin-4 on HT1376 cells.

Preclinical study of the 68Ga-FZ-NRs in UC by microPET/CT imaging and ex vivo biodistribution

The in vivo tumor-targeting capability of 68Ga-FZ-NRs was evaluated in a UC tumor model by microPET/CT imaging technology (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Figures S7-S11). In this experiment, 5.55 to 7.40 MBq of 68Ga-FZ-NR-1, 68Ga-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6, or 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 was injected into the HT1376 (high Nectin-4 expression) and RT112 (low Nectin-4 expression) tumor models (Balb/c-nu, n = 3/group). MicroPET/CT imaging analyses were conducted at 30-, 60-, 120-, and 180-mins after injection (Supplementary Figures S7-S11). Analysis showed that in the HT1376 tumor model (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Figures S8-S11), significant radioactive accumulation in the tumor region was detectable within 30 min after the injection of 68Ga-FZ-NRs; this concentration was significantly higher than that in the contralateral normal tissues. In contrast, although the RT112 tumor model also exhibited some radioactive accumulation at the same time point, the levels were lower than those observed in the HT1376 tumor model. Moreover, no significant radioactive uptake was observed at the tumor site in the blocking-group, further confirming the excellent targeting capability of 68Ga-FZ-NRs for tumors with Nectin-4 overexpression. Notably, we observed the significant uptake of 68Ga-FZ-NRs in the kidneys and bladder during imaging, thus suggesting that these tracers were primarily excreted via the urinary pathway, and indicating a rapid blood clearance rate (Supplementary Figure S12).

Quantitative analysis of the microPET/CT imaging results (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Figures S8-S11) revealed that in the HT1376 tumor model, the degree of radioactive accumulation at 60 min varied among the different radioligands: 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 exhibited 4.63 ± 0.15%ID/g ,68Ga-FZ-NR-5 exhibited 6.40 ± 0.45%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 exhibited 6.80 ± 0.70%ID/g, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 exhibited 6.07 ± 0.40%ID/g (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, the ratios of radioactive accumulation between the tumor and muscle (T/NT) tissue at 60 min were as follows: 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 (5.13 ± 0.57), 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 (4.63 ± 1.10), 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 (5.25 ± 0.64), and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 (4.64 ± 0.94). These results demonstrated that 68Ga-FZ-NRs possess favorable pharmacokinetic characteristics. The rapid clearance properties contribute to a high tumor-to-muscle contrast in the HT1376 tumor model at 60 min, with the ratio of T/NT increasing over time (Fig. 2C). Further analysis revealed that in the HT1376 tumor model, the covalent radioligands 68Ga-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7, developed using the SuFEx-engineered molecular strategy, exhibited slightly higher levels of tumor uptake compared to non-covalent 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 at 180 min post-injection, without a significant reduction in uptake (Fig. 2A and C and Supplementary Figures S8-S11). This indicates that these radioligands not only enhanced tumor uptake but also prolonged tumor retention time. Additionally, at 60 min, the uptake levels of the radioligands in the HT1376 tumor blocking group (3.37 ± 0.38%ID/g for 68Ga-FZ-NR-1, 3.70 ± 0.50%ID/g for 68Ga-FZ-NR-5, 3.53 ± 0.06%ID/g for 68Ga-FZ-NR-6, and 2.53 ± 0.32%ID/g for 68Ga-FZ-NR-7) were significantly lower than those in the experiment group (Fig. 2B). In the RT112 tumor model, which expressed low levels of Nectin-4 (Fig. 2D and Supplementary Figures S8-S11), the tumor uptake of the radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NR-1: 2.53 ± 0.76%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-5: 1.57 ± 0.31%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6: 3.76 ± 0.64%ID/g, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7: 3.93 ± 0.64%ID/g at 60 min) was markedly lower than that observed in the HT1376 tumor model; this trend was consistent with the immunohistochemical results for Nectin-4 (Fig. 2E). This further demonstrates that the expression level of Nectin-4 significantly influences the uptake of 68Ga-FZ-NRs in tumors.

Analysis of biodistribution data clearly demonstrated the radioactive uptake of 68Ga-FZ-NRs in tumors and various organs across different groups (Fig. 3, Supplementary Figures S13 and S14, and Tables S1-S8). In the HT1376 tumor model (n = 3/group), the biodistribution data showed that the uptake of Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7) at the tumor site was slightly higher than that of non-covalent 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 at 30-, 60-, 120-, and 180-minutes, with longer retention times in tumor tissue (Supplementary Figure S13 and Supplementary Tables S1-S4). At 60 min, the radioactive distribution in HT1376 tumors was as follows: 68Ga-FZ-NR-1: 4.57 ± 0.73%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-5: 6.41 ± 0.51%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6: 7.43 ± 0.60%ID/g, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7: 5.91 ± 0.49%ID/g (Supplementary Figure S13 and Supplementary Tables S1-S4). These values were moderately higher than those observed in the blocking group (68Ga-FZ-NR-1: 2.60 ± 0.63%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-5: 2.75 ± 0.60%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6: 3.39 ± 0.32%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-7: 2.05 ± 0.76%ID/g) (Supplementary Figure S13 and Supplementary Tables S1-S4) and the RT112 control group (68Ga-FZ-NR-1: 3.98 ± 0.27%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-5: 2.42 ± 0.25%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6: 2.38 ± 0.54%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-7: 2.93 ± 1.71%ID/g) (Supplementary Figure S14 and Tables S5-S8) at 60-min. Furthermore, the T/NT ratio in the HT1376 group at 60 min was slightly higher than that in both the RT112 group and the blocking group (Fig. 3A and Supplementary Figure S14C). The background levels of 68Ga-FZ-NRs in the bloodstream were lower (Fig. 3B), and the uptake in the HT1376 (Nectin-4 high expression) tumor model was greater than that observed in the RT112 (Nectin-4 low expression) tumor model (Fig. 3C) and the blocking group (Supplementary Figure S13). Notably, the higher uptake in the kidneys indicated that 68Ga-FZ-NRs were primarily metabolized through the renal pathway.

To evaluate the safety of 68Ga-FZ-NRs, a systematic toxicity assessment was conducted on mice receiving intravenous injections of radioligands (Fig. 3D and Supplementary Figure S15). Biochemical analysis indicated that critical blood parameters in the experimental group remained within normal ranges compared to the control group (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, histological examination of HE-stained sections of major organs did not reveal any abnormal changes (Supplementary Figure S15). These findings suggest that within the tested dose range, the Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NRs) exhibit good safety profiles.

Preclinical SPECT/CT imaging and the biodistribution of 177Lu-FZ-NRs in UC

Following our investigation of the enhanced tumor uptake of targeted covalent radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NRs), we next investigated the long-term pharmacokinetic properties of the targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7) for therapeutic applications (Balb/c-nu, n = 3/group). We focused on the uptake and metabolic processes of 177Lu-FZ-NRs in various tissues and organs in vivo, as well as microSPECT/CT imaging experiments and biodistribution analysis conducted in a HT1376 tumor model with high expression levels of Nectin-4 (Fig. 4, Supplementary Figures S16 and S17, and Tables S9-S12). In this experiment, tumor-bearing mice were administered with 177Lu-FZ-NRs via tail vein injection, and microSPECT/CT imaging was performed at 1-, 6-, 24-, 48-, 96-, and 168-hours post-injection, with image reconstruction and analysis conducted using MOLECUBES reconstruction and MOLECUBES acquisition software. Analysis indicated that 177Lu-FZ-NRs exhibited significant uptake in both the tumor and the kidneys (Figs. 4A, Supplementary Figures S16, and Supplementary Tables S9-S12). Over time, signal intensity in both the tumor and kidneys gradually decreased. Notably, we observed the pronounced accumulation of 177Lu-FZ-NRs in the bladder and kidneys, indicating that this radioligand was partially cleared via the renal excretion pathway. In the HT1376 tumor model (Fig. 4A), clear imaging of tumors was apparent after the 1-hour post-administration of 177Lu-FZ-NR-1, 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, or 177Lu-FZ-NR-7; quantitative analysis (Fig. 4B and Supplementary Tables S9-S12) revealed the following tumor uptake values: 177Lu-FZ-NR-1: 2.79 ± 0.51%ID/g, 177Lu-FZ-NR-5: 4.12 ± 0.44%ID/g, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6: 5.16 ± 0.57%ID/g, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7: 4.38 ± 0.53%ID/g. Background signal was negligible (Fig. 4A and Supplementary Figure S17), and over time, the tumor uptake of all radioligands remained at a high level, while signals from the background and other organs remained low. By 48 h post-administration (Supplementary Tables S9-S12), the tumor uptake of 177Lu-FZ-NR-1 had decreased significantly to 0.34 ± 0.24%ID/g, reflecting its shorter retention time in the tumor. In contrast, the tumor uptake values for the targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7) were 2.08 ± 0.53%ID/g, 1.07 ± 0.18%ID/g, and 1.42 ± 0.32%ID/g, respectively, all significantly superior to the non-covalent 177Lu-FZ-NR-1. Furthermore, over time, the retention times of 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 in tumors were notably longer than that of 177Lu-FZ-NR-1. In the HT1376 tumor model, 1 MBq of 177Lu-FZ-NR-1, 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, or 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 was injected via the tail veins of mice. Computational analyses indicated that the absorbed doses of each radioligand at the tumor site were as follows: 50.35 mGy for 177Lu-FZ-NR-1, 140.76 mGy for 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 139.35 mGy for 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 140.06 mGy for 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 (Fig. 4C). Collectively, these data demonstrate the enhanced uptake and retention times of the engineered targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7) in tumors through the introduction of covalent molecules, thus achieving enhanced efficacy and reduced toxicity, thereby demonstrating promising applications in the field of targeted radiotherapy for tumors.

Radiopharmaceutical therapy of bicyclic peptide-targeted covalent radioligands (177Lu-FZ-NRs) in HT1376 tumors

The treatment protocol for HT1376 tumors included regular monitoring of tumor volume and body weight over the 20-day treatment period for each group of mice (Balb/c-nu, n = 5/group) (Fig. 5 and Supplementary Figures S18-S20). High-dose (two doses, each approximately 26 MBq) and low-dose (two doses, each approximately 13 MBq) targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7) were delivered to tumors via tail vein injection, while the control group received saline (Fig. 5A). Analysis indicated significant growth of HT1376 tumors in the saline group over the 20-day treatment period (Fig. 5B and Supplementary Figures S18 and S19). After two treatment cycles with 26 MBq or 13 MBq of the targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands, the tumors in the experimental groups showed slow growth during the first few days. After 20 days of treatment, the tumor growth rates and volumes in the groups treated with 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 were significantly lower than those in the saline group (P < 0.0001). This indicates that 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 effectively inhibited tumor growth in mice bearing HT1376 tumors. However, no differences were observed among the groups treated with 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 (P > 0.05). Furthermore, no statistically significant difference was observed between the 26 MBq and 13 MBq dose within each group (P > 0.05). These results may stem from the small number of mice in the experiment or the limited sample size in the high-dose and low-dose subgroups. During treatment, the mice exhibited weight loss (< 15%), likely due to the treatment dose (Fig. 5C and Supplementary Figure S20). Furthermore, we investigated the potential side effects of the Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands. Following the completion of treatment, we examined hematoxylin and eosin-stained tissue sections from the heart, lungs, liver, spleen, and kidneys of tumor-bearing mice in each group; no significant pathological changes were detected, such as inflammation or necrosis (Supplementary Figure S21).The results suggest that the targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7) exhibit good tolerance in tumor-bearing mice, highlighting the biological safety of this treatment strategy.

PET/CT imaging of patients with UC

Preclinical trials comprehensively evaluated the targeting capability and safety of 68Ga-FZ-NRs, demonstrating that 68Ga-FZ-NRs efficiently identified and bound to Nectin-4 without significant toxic side effects. Based on these encouraging findings, we initiated a preliminary clinical study involving patients with UC. The aim of this study was to assess the diagnostic potential of the radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NRs) for UC. In static PET/CT imaging, performed ~ 60 min post-injection, 68Ga-FZ-NRs clearly identified lesions in patients with UC (Supplementary Tables S13 and S14). To date, 17 patients with UC have participated in this study (Fig. 6 and Supplementary Figure S22). No adverse events were reported by any of the subjects after 68Ga-FZ-NRs PET/CT examinations. Analysis indicated that 68Ga-FZ-NRs were primarily excreted via the urinary system, with relatively high uptake in the kidneys. Moreover, the physiological uptake of 68Ga-FZ-NRs in most normal organs and tissues, including the brain, lungs, gastrointestinal tract, liver, bile ducts, spleen, bones, and muscles, was low. This low physiological uptake characteristic significantly enhanced tumor-to-background ratios (Supplementary Table S14), thereby increasing the potential application of 68Ga-FZ-NRs in imaging various cancer types.

Patient 01 was diagnosed with UC; 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 PET/CT imaging clearly demonstrated a primary lesion in the bladder (SUVmax = 15.1) and metastatic lymph nodes adjacent to the left supraclavicular and right iliac vessels (SUVmax = 17.8 and 23.7, respectively). The presence of these lesions was also confirmed by 18F-FDG PET/CT, revealing metabolically active lesions in the bladder and metastatic lymph nodes (SUVmax = 22.2, 21.8, and 24.1), corroborating the findings of 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 imaging (Fig. 6A). Immunohistochemical analysis further confirmed the significant upregulation of Nectin-4 in the lesion of UC; this aligned with the radioligand’s targeting mechanism, thus validating the specificity of the 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 imaging results from Patients 06 and 07 (Supplementary Figure S22). For Patient 02 with UC, 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 PET/CT and 18F-FDG PET/CT both accurately detected the primary bladder lesion and bilateral iliac lymph node involvement. Specifically, the SUVmax for the primary bladder lesion was 12.4 and 12.6 in the two imaging modalities, respectively, while the SUVmax for the bilateral iliac lymph nodes was 11.4, 7.6 and 11.0, 7.0, respectively. These results indicate that both 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 and 18F-FDG exhibited comparable diagnostic efficacy for detecting lesions in this patient, providing reliable evidence for clinical decision-making (Fig. 6B). For Patient 03 with UC, the SUVmax for the urethral mucosal lesion detected by 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 was 8.0, this was higher than the value of 5.5 detected by 18F-FDG, thus indicating a greater uptake level. This suggests that 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 is advantageous for targeting lesions in the urethral mucosa when Nectin-4 is overexpressed (Fig. 6C). Patient 04 presented with UC and multiple bone metastases; 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 PET/CT imaging detected increased radioactive uptake in some bone metastatic lesions, with an SUVmax of 4.9; 18F-FDG PET/CT identified only one site with clearly increased uptake (SUVmax = 2.1) (Fig. 6D). This indicates that 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 may exert higher sensitivity for detecting bone metastatic lesions in UC, thus providing a more comprehensive assessment of Nectin-4 expressing lesions, thereby providing more detailed information for clinical diagnosis. These cases demonstrate the potential of 68Ga-FZ-NRs for high-specificity Nectin-4 PET/CT imaging. More details of 68Ga-FZ-NRs and 18F-FDG uptake in 17 UC patients are presented in Supplementary Table S13 and Table S14.

For Patient 02 with UC, both 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 PET/CT and 18F-FDG PET/CT accurately identified the primary bladder tumor and lesions in the bilateral iliac vascular lymph node (Fig. 6B). The SUVmax values for both imaging modalities were closely aligned (12.4 vs. 12.6 for the bladder primary and 11.4,7.6 vs. 11.0, 7.0 for the lymph nodes), thus indicating similar diagnostic efficacy between the two radioligands. For patient 02, following four cycles of targeted therapy with Vedolizumab and immune therapy with Tislelizumab, no uptake was observed in the bilateral iliac para-arterial lymph nodes for 68Ga-FZ-NR-7; although uptake was observed in the posterior bladder wall with SUVmax = 4.3 for 68Ga-FZ-NR-7. This analysis demonstrated potential differences between 68Ga-FZ-NRs (68Ga-FZ-NR-5 and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7) and 18F-FDG radiotracers in monitoring a patient’s response to treatment (Fig. 7A). Notably, for Patient 05, who was concurrently diagnosed with UC and prostate cancer, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 PET/CT effectively detected systemic multiple bone metastatic lesions expressing Nectin-4. This technique may provide complementary information alongside 18F-PSMA-3Q PET/CT (which identified PSMA-overexpressing lesions), thereby offering more comprehensive and precise diagnostic insight, especially in complex cases, thus highlighting the diagnostic advantages of utilizing multiple tracers (Fig. 7B).

Discussion

Over recent years, Nectin-4-targeted radioligands have achieved significant progress [16]. In particular, antibody-based Nectin-4-targeted radioligands [40], such as 99mTc-HYNIC-mAbNectin−4 [41], 124I-EV [42], 89Zr-DFO-N4MU01 [18], and 89Zr-AGS-22M6 [43] have shown great potential for tumor diagnosis in preclinical studies. However, their large molecular weight, limits tumor penetration, prolongs clearance, reduces signal intensity, hinders early imaging, and poses several clinical challenges [12]. In contrast, novel bicyclic peptide-based radioligand, such as 68Ga-N188 [21], 68Ga-NOTA-PEG12-BP [28], 68Ga-DN68 [27], and 18F-AlF-N231 [44], especially 68Ga-N188, and our preliminary work on 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 [22], have demonstrated higher sensitivity and specificity in vivo studies. However, Nectin-4-targeted peptide radioligands face a major barrier in tumor therapy due to insufficient tumor retention. To solve the problem, we utilized a molecular engineering strategy based on the second-generation ‘click chemistry’ SuFEx reaction [35] and developed efficient Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-7) in an attempt to achieve accurate theragnostic options for UC. This strategy may overcome current limitations and improve cancer therapy outcomes.

The Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide precursors developed in this study, namely FZ-NR-5 (5.722 nM), FZ-NR-6 (3.801 nM), and FZ-NR-7 (3.234 nM), all exhibited high affinity for Nectin-4, rivaling the lead compound (1.048 nM) and non-covalent FZ-NR-1 (2.81 nM). These precursors bound specifically to cells that overexpress Nectin-4, as also indicated by western blotting results, and compared to FZ-NR-1, and aligning with subsequent microPET/CT and microSPECT/CT imaging results. Our novel Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-7) all showed good stability and hydrophilicity; furthermore, these characteristics were verified by in vivo studies. MicroPET/CT imaging confirmed their excellent selectivity and specificity. Compared to our previous of non-covalent 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 (tumor uptake: 4.63 ± 0.15%ID/g; T/NT: 5.13 ± 0.57%ID/g at 60 min), the Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands exhibited higher tumor uptake: 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 at 6.40 ± 0.45%ID/g, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 at 6.80 ± 0.70%ID/g, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 at 6.07 ± 0.40%ID/g, all at 60 min. Covalent radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NRs) accumulated to a greater extent in tumor sites than non-covalent radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NR-1), a trend that was consistent with the results arising from microSPECT/CT imaging. In addition, microPET/CT imaging indicates that 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 is slightly superior in uptake compared with 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7. These data are also consistent with those arising from cell uptake experiments, thus indicating that 68Ga-FZ-NRs possess significant tumor-targeting capabilities, and that the application of ‘covalently bonded warheads’ is a key factor for enhancing uptake by tumors. Biodistribution data also confirmed the significant targeting of 68Ga-FZ-NRs to Nectin-4. The retention time of covalent radioligands in tumors was significantly prolonged, with tumor uptake exceeding 5.80%ID/g for covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs; this compared to 3.41 ± 0.67%ID/g for the non-covalent 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 at 3 hours. RT112 tumor model and blocking experiments demonstrated markedly reduced tumor uptake, confirming the specificity of covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs for Nectin-4. Biodistribution data indicate a slightly higher systemic exposure of the covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs compared with the non-covalent 68Ga-FZ-NR-1 in the blood of mice. Safety assessments demonstrate that the covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs are well tolerated and do not cause harm to normal organs in mice. MicroPET/CT imaging indicated that our novel Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands are predominantly cleared rapidly by the kidneys; these data are consistent with biodistribution data. This low non-target-organ distribution is crucial if we are to accurately measure Nectin-4 expression in tumors in future clinical studies.

All covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands-68Ga-FZ-NRs exhibit robust selectivity for Nectin-4, yielding high targeting efficiency. Based on the in vivo preclinical analysis of covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs, a decisive preference among 68Ga-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7 for clinical development could not be established, suggesting that further optimization is required prior to clinical translation. After our safety evaluation of Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NRs), we conducted a clinical study to assess the potential and safety of Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga-FZ-NR-7) in UC patients, particularly for Nectin-4-targeted PET/CT imaging. Our study included 17 UC patients who underwent PET/CT imaging approximately 60 min post-tracer injection. Analysis demonstrated that 68Ga-FZ-NRs exhibited rapid blood and non-specific organ clearance, primarily by renal excretion and possess favorable pharmacokinetic properties. No adverse events or abnormal vital signs were reported from any of our subjects following injection. Immunohistochemistry confirmed the overexpression of Nectin-4 at tumor sites. Preliminary clinical findings indicated that Nectin-4-targeted covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga-FZ-NRs) identify UC lesions in an accurate manner. When we performed PET/CT imaging, we found that the tumor lesions identified by 68Ga-FZ-NRs were consistent with those detected by 18F-FDG. For instance, patient 01 had UC and exhibited SUVmax values of 17.8, 23.7 and 15.1, respectively, for 68Ga-FZ-N-1 and 21.8, 24.1 and 22.2 for 18F-FDG. Patient 02 also had UC and exhibited SUVmax values of 11.4,12.4 and 7.6 for 68Ga-FZ-NR-5 and 11.0, 12.6 and 7.0 for 18F-FDG. After four cycles of targeted therapy with Vedolizumab and immune therapy with Tislelizumab for patient 02, no uptake was observed in the bilateral iliac para-arterial lymph nodes for 68Ga-FZ-NR-7, although uptake was observed in the posterior bladder wall with an SUVmax of 4.3 for 68Ga-FZ-NR-7. Patient 03 also had UC and exhibited an SUVmax value of 8.0 for 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 and 5.5 for 18F-FDG. For patient 04 with UC, the SUVmax values was 4.9 for 68Ga-FZ-NR-7; there was no significant uptake according to 18F-FDG, except for one lesion (SUVmax = 2.1) for 18F-FDG. This suggests that Nectin-4 was overexpressed in lesions, as corroborated by immunohistochemistry. Furthermore, patient 05 was pathologically diagnosed with prostate cancer and UC; 68Ga-FZ-NR-6 was able to detect lesions that 18F-PSMA-3Q (Prostate specific membrane antigen, PSMA-targeted) failed to identify, forming a complementary relationship between the two radioligands. These clinical data suggest that these radioligands can detect lesions associated with UC, as confirmed by 18F-FDG, and are also capable of identifying UC lesions that remain undetected by 18F-FDG. Furthermore, the uptake of 68Ga-FZ-NRs by lesions was correlated with Nectin-4 expression levels, thereby providing strong support for the application of these radioligands in the diagnosis and treatment of UC. We also demonstrated that our 68Ga-FZ-NR radioligands exhibit significant targeting capability for primary bladder tumors, lymph nodes, and bone metastases in patients with UC. 68Ga-FZ-NRs exhibited higher levels of uptake in the urethral mucosa and bone metastases when compared to 18F-FDG, indicating greater diagnostic sensitivity. These radioligands provide a new imaging tool for the precise diagnosis of UC. Although the feasibility of PET/CT imaging has been preliminarily confirmed in this study, further research and validation are now required for the clinical application of 68Ga-FZ-NRs PET/CT. This technology has the potential to enable precise quantitative analysis of Nectin-4 expression, which could offer crucial information for diagnosis, treatment planning, and disease monitoring.

By applying microSPECT/CT, the covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs exhibited longer retention times and slightly higher uptake in tumors overexpressed Nectin-4 than the non-covalent 177Lu-FZ-NR-1, thus aligning with prior microPET/CT results. However, comparative analysis at 48 h post-injection showed that covalent 177Lu-FZ-NR-5 (2.08 ± 0.53%ID/g) exhibited modestly higher tumor retention than 177Lu-FZ-NR-6 (1.07 ± 0.18%ID/g) and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 (1.42 ± 0.32%ID/g). Analysis of in vivo microPET/CT, microSPECT/CT, and biodistribution data for covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs did not identify a clearly superior candidate. Targeted radiotherapy experiments further confirmed the efficacy of covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs in a HT1376 tumor model. Administered in two doses of 26 MBq or 13 MBq, the covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs significantly inhibited tumor growth, demonstrating effective therapeutic effects. No meaningful differences were observed in tumor growth inhibition among covalent radioligands 177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7, indicating comparable anti-tumor activities across the three constructs. The success of our study in using an SuFEx-engineered ‘latent warhead’ molecular engineering strategy effectively extended the ligand-receptor binding time window. This enhances binding stability to target receptors and reduces the likelihood of ligand dissociation. The introduction of covalent molecular ‘latent warheads’ offers new potential for cancer therapy and enhances the properties of radioligands.

While we achieved promising results in the integrated diagnosis and treatment of UC, several limitations remain to be addressed to further enhance research in this field. First, clinical data indicates that the kidneys exhibit high uptake of these radioligands, which may restrict the development of subsequent Nectin-4-targeted therapeutic radioligands. In addition, potential off-target toxicity associated with the covalent strategy-specifically non-specific covalent binding to other proteins in normal tissues-warrants concern. To address this challenges, future research should investigate new ligand-design strategies to reduce kidney uptake and optimize in vivo drug distribution. Secondly, despite our initial imaging study demonstrating the potential of covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs, more mouse tumor models and a larger clinical sample-size is now needed to fully investigate the correlation between Nectin-4 expression and tumor development. Future research should aim to recruit more patients to investigate this correlation and validate the clinical value of Nectin-4 as a tumor biomarker; this may provide more effective tools for the precise diagnosis and personalized treatment of UC.

Conclusion

Covalent functional group modification was first introduced into bicyclic peptide molecules to evaluate a series of covalent 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs in vitro and in vivo. Preclinical studies demonstrated that these covalent radioligands effectively detected tumors overexpressing Nectin-4 with excellent PET/CT imaging performance. In initial clinical study, the covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs successfully identified and visualized tumors expressing Nectin-4 in UC patients. The covalent 177Lu-FZ-NRs exhibited initial therapeutic effects and effectively inhibited tumor growth. Covalent 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs provide a non-invasive method for assessing Nectin-4 and a foundation for Nectin-4-targeted therapy for UC. Our findings highlight the significance of molecular engineering in modifying bicyclic peptides to advance the development of radioligands, thus providing strong support for further translational research that could pave the way for Nectin-4-targeted therapies alongside early cancer diagnosis and treatment.

Materials and methods

Detailed specifications for experimental reagents, analytical method parameters for instruments, and standard operating procedures are provided in the Supporting Information.

Synthesis and radiolabeling

DOTA-Sar10FS-Nectin-4 (FZ-NR-5), DOTA-Sar10SF-Nectin-4 (FZ-NR-6), and DOTA-Sar10mFS-Nectin-4 (FZ-NR-7) precursors were provided by Chinese Peptide (Shanghai, China). Radiolabeling of FZ-NRs with 68Ga/177Lu and purification were done as reported previously [22]. A comprehensive process for the synthesis and purification of Nectin-4-targeted radioligands was described in Supplementary S2.

Cell uptake

The cell uptake of covalent bicyclic peptide radioligands (68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-5, 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-6, and 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NR-7) in HT1376 cells (high Nectin-4 expression) and RT112 cells (low Nectin-4 expression) was determined using the procedure described in Supplementary S7.

In vivo imaging and biodistribution studies

HT1376- and RT112-tumor-bearing mice were administered covalent 68Ga/177Lu-FZ-NRs via tail-vein injection for microPET/CT (Supplementary S15) and microSPECT/CT imaging (Supplementary S16). Inveon microPET/CT scans were performed at 30, 60, 120, and 180 min post-injection (Supplementary S10). MicroSPECT/CT imaging (MOLECUBES) was conducted at additional post-injection time points: 1, 6, 24, 48, 96, and 168 h (Supplementary S10). Biodistribution studies were performed by sacrificing mice at 30, 60, 120, and 180 min post-injection for 68Ga-FZ-NRs and at 1, 6, 24, 48, 96, and 168 h post-injection for 177Lu-FZ-NRs, with radioactivity measured in all major organs and blood (Supplementary S11). All procedures were approved by the animal ethics committee of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (Approval No.: FUSCC-IACUC-S2025-0441).

Therapeutic evaluation of 177Lu-FZ-NRs

5-6-week-old BALB/c-nu mice were injected subcutaneously with HT1376 cells (1 × 10^6 cells/mouse) in the right upper abdomen. Two weeks later, the tumor-bearing mice were randomly assigned to experimental groups. The experiment included a total of seven groups (n = 5/group), namely, 177Lu-FZ-NR-5 (2 doses of 26 MBq/dose), 177Lu-FZ-NR-5 (2 doses of 13 MBq/dose), 177Lu-FZ-NR-6 (2 doses of 26 MBq/dose), 177Lu-FZ-NR-6 (2 doses of 13 MBq/dose), 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 (2 doses of 26 MBq/dose), and 177Lu-FZ-NR-7 (2 doses of 13 MBq/dose), and saline control. All treated mice received 2 treatment doses via the tailvein on days 0 and 7. Tumor volume was calculated using the ellipsoid formula (V = width2 × length × 0.5, with units in mm3), and dynamic changes in tumor volume were monitored throughout the 20-day duration of the experiment until its conclusion. The body weight of each mouse was recorded during the experimental period.

Patients

Covalent 68Ga-FZ-NRs were used in the first-in-human PET/CT imaging trial in patients with UC at the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center. The research received approval from the ethics committee of the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (Approval No.:2407299-29) and strictly adhered to the ethical guidelines set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent prior to participation. The study included patients with pathologically confirmed UC. Exclusion criteria consisted of severe liver or kidney dysfunction, women who were pregnant or breastfeeding, and individuals with a history of Nectin-4 targeted therapy. Patients initially underwent PET/CT imaging with 68Ga-FZ-NRs, followed by 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging within one week. Radioactive uptake in normal organs and tumor was assessed by measuring SUVmax to determine the capacity of the radioligand to detect Nectin-4. Ultimately, we included a total of 17 patients with UC. Detailed information about the patients, along with individualized treatment data, is available in Supplementary Table S13 and Supplementary Table S14. PET/CT scans were performed using a Biograph mCT Flow scanner (Siemens Medical Solutions). Specific protocols for image acquisition and reconstruction are detailed in the Supplementary S14.

Statistical analysis

All experimental results are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Data comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and t-tests. Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

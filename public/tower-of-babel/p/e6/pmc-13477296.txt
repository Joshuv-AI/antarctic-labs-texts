Aorto‐Ostial Right Coronary Artery Chronic Total Occlusion Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: Technical Outcomes and Predictors of Failure From the ERCTO Registry
Authors: Ioannis Skalidis, Luca Esposito, Thomas Hovasse, Pietro Laforgia, Andrei G Florescu, Stephane Champagne, Gerald S Werner, Roberto Garbo, Alfredo R Galassi, Roberto Diletti, Nicolas Boudou, Gabriele L Gasparini, Omer Goktekin, Pierfrancesco Agostoni, Carlo di Mario, Michael Behnes, Giuseppe Vadalà, Claudiu Ungureanu, Georgios Sianos, Kambis Mashayekhi, Alexandre Avran, the ERCTO Investigators, in Collaboration with the MLCTO Academy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAorto‐ostial chronic total occlusions (CTOs) of the right coronary artery (RCA) represent a particularly complex subset of CTO percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI), yet dedicated data remain limited. We aimed to evaluate technical outcomes of aorto‐ostial versus non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTO PCI and to identify predictors of failure in RCA aorto‐ostial lesions.MethodsPatients undergoing RCA CTO PCI between 2016 and 2024 and enrolled in the ERCTO (European Registry of Chronic Total Occlusion) registry were included in the present analysis. The primary end point was technical success. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify independent predictors of technical failure.ResultsA total of 26 374 patients undergoing RCA CTO PCI were included in the analysis, of whom 2850 had aorto‐ostial and 23 524 non–aorto‐ostial lesions. Technical success was lower in aorto‐ostial compared with non–aorto‐ostial lesions (79.6% versus 89.7%; P<0.001). Aorto‐ostial location was independently associated with technical failure (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 1.85; P<0.001). In multivariable analysis restricted to aorto‐ostial CTOs, stumpless proximal cap morphology (adjusted OR, 2.44, P<0.001), moderate‐to‐severe tortuosity (adjusted OR, 2.13 P<0.001), severe calcification (adjusted OR, 1.92, P<0.001), and peripheral artery disease (adjusted OR, 1.57 P<0.001) were independently associated with technical failure. In‐hospital major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events rates were low and did not differ significantly between groups.ConclusionsAorto‐ostial RCA CTO PCI has a significantly lower technical success despite extensive use of advanced techniques. Technical failure is driven primarily by lesion‐specific anatomical features, underscoring the need for dedicated strategies and refined risk stratification tailored to aorto‐ostial anatomy.Keywords: coronary disease, CTO, PCI, RCASubject Categories: Percutaneous Coronary Intervention

Clinical Perspective

What Is New?

This study provides the largest dedicated evaluation of aorto‐ostial right coronary artery chronic total occlusions to date, demonstrating that these lesions account for 10.8% of all right coronary artery chronic total occlusions and are associated with significantly lower technical success compared with non–aorto‐ostial right coronary artery chronic total occlusions (79.6% versus 89.7%; P<0.001), despite more frequent use of advanced procedural techniques.

What Are the Clinical Implications?

These findings underscore the importance of careful preprocedural planning, early consideration of retrograde strategies, and referral to experienced centers for aorto‐ostial right coronary artery chronic total occlusions and highlight the need for dedicated risk stratification tools tailored to the unique anatomical challenges of this lesion subset.

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for chronic total occlusions (CTOs) remains one of the most technically demanding procedures in interventional cardiology. Nevertheless, due to the refinement of dedicated techniques and devices, the adoption of standardized algorithms, and increased operator experience, success rates have improved substantially, reaching >85% to 90% in expert hands and high‐volume centers. 1 , 2 , 3

Despite these advances, CTO PCI outcomes remain strongly influenced by lesion and procedural complexity. 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 Among the most challenging scenarios are aorto‐ostial CTOs, which are most commonly located in the right coronary artery (RCA). 8 Indeed, aorto‐ostial CTOs present distinct anatomical and technical challenges, including ambiguous or stumpless proximal cap flush with the aortic wall, poor coaxial guide catheter engagement, and limited antegrade crossing options—often necessitating a retrograde approach. 9

However, data specifically focusing on this lesion subset remain limited and aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs are poorly captured by widely used scores such as the Japanese Chronic Total Occlusion Score (J‐CTO) and PROGRESS‐CTO (Prospective Global Registry for the Study of Chronic Total Occlusion Intervention) scores, which were not specifically designed to account for the anatomical constraints of aorto‐ostial CTOs. 10 , 11 The aim of this study was to evaluate technical outcomes, procedural strategies, and predictors of technical failure in patients undergoing PCI for aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs, and to compare these outcomes with non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTO lesions.

METHODS

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Population

This study is a retrospective observational analysis of the ERCTO (European Registry of Chronic Total Occlusion), a multicenter database supported by the European CTO Club. The registry enrolls consecutive patients undergoing CTO PCI performed by experienced operators across high‐volume centers in Europe and Turkey. For the present analysis all patients undergoing RCA CTO PCI between January 1, 2016, and December 31, 2024, were included. In cases with multiple CTOs, only the RCA occlusion was considered. Procedural strategy and device selection were left at the discretion of the operator. For the present analysis, patients were required to have complete information on the primary end point (technical success), resulting in exclusion of procedures with missing angiographic outcome adjudication. In‐hospital clinical outcomes, including major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) and procedural events, were evaluated exclusively in procedures with available follow‐up data within the registry.

Ethical Approval

This study is a retrospective analysis of the ERCTO registry, which was approved by the institutional review boards or ethics committees of all participating centers in accordance with national regulations. As this was a retrospective registry study, written informed consent was waived per institutional protocols. The registry was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and international data protection standards. The authors attest they are in compliance with human studies committees and animal welfare regulations of the authors' institutions and Food and Drug Administration guidelines, including patient consent where appropriate.

Definitions

CTO was defined as a native coronary occlusion with thrombolysis in myocardial infarction 0 flow and a presumed or documented duration exceeding 3 months. Aorto‐ostial RCA CTO was defined as an occlusion originating within the proximal 5 mm of the RCA ostium, including flush occlusions without a proximal stump; lesions arising distal to this segment were classified as non–aorto‐ostial. Angiographic and lesion characteristics were assessed according to the ERCTO data dictionary. Calcifications, assessed semiquantitatively using angiography and consistent with previous studies, were graded as mild when limited to spotty radiopacities, moderate when radiopacities involved a single arterial wall during the cardiac cycle, and severe when radiopacities were visible without cardiac motion, typically involving both arterial walls. Tortuosity, occlusion length, stump morphology, distal vessel quality, collateral grade, and collateral filling pattern were systematically recorded. Bifurcation involvement was defined by the presence of a side branch at least 2 mm in diameter within 5 mm of either the proximal or distal cap. 12 Crossing strategies were assigned based on the method ultimately resulting in distal true lumen entry and were adjudicated according to the Chronic Total Occlusion Academic Research Consortium classification. 13 , 14

Technical success was defined as <30% residual stenosis with thrombolysis in myocardial infarction 3 flow in the target vessel at procedure completion. MACCE were defined as a composite of all‐cause death, myocardial infarction, stroke, urgent target‐vessel revascularization with PCI or coronary artery bypass grafting, or cardiac tamponade requiring pericardiocentesis or surgical intervention. Myocardial infarction was defined according to the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction. MACCE events were captured if they occurred periprocedurally or during the index hospitalization, when follow‐up data were available.

Outcomes

The primary end point of the study was technical success. The primary analytical objective was to identify independent predictors of technical failure within the subgroup of aorto‐ostial occlusions. Secondary end points included the incidence of in‐hospital MACCE and individual adverse events.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as mean±SD or median with interquartile range, depending on distribution, and were compared using the Student t‐test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages based on available‐case denominators and were compared using the χ 2 test or Fisher's exact test when appropriate.

Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify independent predictors of technical failure. All clinical and angiographical relevant variables, consistent with contemporary CTO literature and found to be significant in the univariable analysis (P<0.10) were included in the multivariable model. Two prespecified multivariable logistic regression models were constructed. The first model was applied to the overall cohort with RCA CTO and included aorto‐ostial location alongside clinical and angiographic variables significant in univariable analysis (P<0.10). The second model was restricted to the aorto‐ostial subgroup and included a separate set of covariates selected based on univariable significance within this subset. Formal interaction testing between lesion location and individual predictors was not performed, as the subgroup‐specific model was designed to identify factors independently associated with failure within aorto‐ostial CTOs. Anatomical predictors were dichotomized according to established thresholds: stumpless versus any stump, moderate‐to‐severe versus straight‐to‐slight tortuosity, and moderate–severe versus none–mild calcification. Categorical predictors with >2 levels were otherwise incorporated using indicator variables. Secondary analyses compared procedural success and MACCE between aorto‐ostial and non–aorto‐ostial lesions and evaluated the distribution of final crossing strategies as a procedural characteristic. No adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied to secondary analyses, given their exploratory nature and the prespecified hierarchy of end points. A 2‐sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R version 4.3.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

Study Population

A total of 49 228 CTO PCI procedures were included in the ERCTO registry between January 2016 and December 2024. After the exclusion of 22 647 patients without an RCA CTO, 26 581 patients who underwent RCA CTO PCI were identified. Among these, 207 procedures lacked essential variables required for adjudication of technical success and were excluded from the primary end point analysis, yielding a final analytic cohort of 26 374 patients. Of these, 2850 (10.8%) had an aorto‐ostial RCA CTO and 23 524 (89.2%) had non–aorto‐ostial lesions (Figure 1).

Primary Outcome

Technical success was achieved in 23 436 of 26 374 procedures (88.8%). Success rates were significantly lower in aorto‐ostial compared with non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs (79.6% versus 89.7%; P<0.001) (Figure 2). Within a multivariable model applied to the overall cohort, aorto‐ostial location was independently associated with technical failure (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 1.85 [95% CI 1.59–2.17]; P<0.001), after adjustment for clinical and angiographic characteristics (Figure S1).

Within the prespecified multivariable model restricted to aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs (Figure 3), stumpless proximal cap morphology (adjusted OR, 2.44 [95% CI, 1.83–3.26; P<0.001), moderate‐to‐severe tortuosity (adjusted OR, 2.13 [95% CI, 1.48–3.06]]; P<0.001), and moderate‐to‐severe calcification (adjusted OR, 1.92 [95% CI, 1.37–2.69]; P<0.001) were independently associated with technical failure. Among clinical characteristics, peripheral artery disease emerged as an independent associate of failure (adjusted OR, 1.57 [95% CI, 1.11–2.22]; P=0.011), whereas prior coronary artery bypass grafting showed only a borderline, nonsignificant association (adjusted OR, 1.42 [95% CI, 0.99–2.03]; P=0.057).

Secondary Outcome

In‐hospital clinical outcomes were available for 25 199 patients, corresponding to 2230 aorto‐ostial and 22 969 non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTO procedures. The overall incidence of MACCE was numerically higher in aorto‐ostial versus non–aorto‐ostial lesions (2.3% versus 1.8%; P=0.076; Table 4, Figure 1), although this difference did not reach statistical significance. Mortality rates were similar between the groups (0.5% versus 0.4%; P=0.426), as was the incidence of myocardial infarction (1.0% versus 0.7%; P=0.121). Stroke was rare, occurring in 0% of aorto‐ostial cases and 0.1% of non–aorto‐ostial cases (P=0.227). Rates of urgent revascularization (0.5% versus 0.4%; P=0.653) and the need for urgent pericardiocentesis (0.7% versus 0.5%; P=0.228) did not differ significantly between the groups.

DISCUSSION

The present analysis provides a comprehensive, contemporary evaluation of aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs within a large, multicenter European registry (Figure 4). The main findings of the present analysis can be summarized as follows. First, aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs accounted for 10.8% of all RCA CTOs in the ERCTO registry, underscoring their nonnegligible prevalence in contemporary practice. Second, aorto‐ostial lesions were characterized by markedly greater anatomical complexity, reflected by higher J‐CTO and European Chronic Total Occlusion Score scores, a higher prevalence of stumpless proximal caps, and more severe calcification and tortuosity. Third, despite more frequent use of advanced techniques—including retrograde approaches, advanced calcium modification devices, and intravascular imaging—technical success remained significantly lower in aorto‐ostial compared with non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs, and procedures were associated with longer duration, higher radiation exposure, and greater contrast use. Fourth, multivariable analysis demonstrated that technical failure in aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs was driven predominantly by lesion‐specific anatomical factors—stumpless proximal cap morphology, severe calcification, and moderate‐to‐severe tortuosity—as well as by peripheral artery disease as the only independent clinical predictor. Finally, although overall in‐hospital adverse event rates were low, aorto‐ostial RCA CTO PCI was not associated with a statistically significant excess of in‐hospital MACCE compared with non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs.

This large multicenter registry analysis confirms that aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs constitute a distinct and particularly complex lesion subset, with significantly lower technical compared with non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs. These findings underscore the unique anatomical and procedural challenges of aorto‐ostial CTOs and provide the most comprehensive assessment to date of this high‐risk lesion subset.

Aorto‐ostial occlusions accounted for about 1 in 10 RCA CTOs in our series, which is higher than previously reported in a prior study focusing on aorto‐ostial CTOs. 9 This discrepancy may be partly explained by differences in study populations: while our investigation was restricted to RCA CTOs, the previous study also included aorto‐ostial left main CTOs, which are considerably less common. Their inclusion likely diluted the overall prevalence, thus accounting for the observed epidemiological differences.

Patients with aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs exhibited a higher overall disease burden, including higher rates of prior coronary revascularization and peripheral artery disease, consistent with more diffuse and aggressive atherosclerosis. From an anatomical standpoint, aorto‐ostial lesions displayed multiple hostile features (higher J‐CTO scores, frequent stumpless proximal caps, and more severe calcification and tortuosity) findings that align with previous reports identifying ostial location as a marker of increased CTO complexity. 8 , 9 , 15 , 16

The high adoption of retrograde strategies highlights the technical challenge, often requiring a septal crossing, externalization, or snaring strategies in a higher proportion than typically reported in contemporary CTO PCI series. 2

These anatomical challenges translated into distinct procedural characteristics. Aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs were approached more frequently with retrograde strategies, including externalization and snaring techniques, reflecting the limited feasibility of straightforward antegrade wire escalation in the absence of a clearly defined proximal cap. 2 Similarly, the more frequent use of advanced calcium modification devices and intravascular ultrasound highlights the need for aggressive lesion preparation and optimization in this setting. These observations are consistent with prior ERCTO analyses demonstrating that the need for calcium modification identifies a particularly complex CTO phenotype that nevertheless can be treated safely in experienced centers. 7 Despite the more frequent adoption of advanced techniques, together with longer procedural time and higher radiation exposure, technical success rates remained significantly lower in aorto‐ostial compared with non–aorto‐ostial CTOs.

From a practical standpoint, guide catheter selection and coaxial engagement are particularly critical in aorto‐ostial CTOs: the absence of a defined proximal stump limits coaxial alignment, and aggressive catheter manipulation carries a risk of iatrogenic dissection. The use of Amplatz Left or specifically shaped catheters with side holes, guide catheter extensions, and anchoring techniques may help overcome these challenges. When antegrade wire escalation is not feasible, early consideration of retrograde approaches is warranted, as reflected by the high proportion of retrograde strategies, externalization, and snaring observed in our cohort.

Despite lower success rates, the overall technical success of 79.6% in this anatomically complex group compares favorably with prior smaller studies and it reflects the feasibility of these challenging cases in experienced centers. 8 , 17 Moreover, although procedures were longer and more resource‐intensive, aorto‐ostial RCA CTO PCI was not associated with a statistically significant increase in in‐hospital MACCE, providing reassurance regarding procedural safety in appropriately selected. Notably, predictors of technical failure in aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs were driven predominantly by lesion‐specific anatomical factors. Stumpless proximal cap morphology was independently associated with failure, reflecting the difficulty of establishing guide catheter stability and achieving initial wire entry when the occlusion is flush with the aortic wall. Moderate‐to‐severe tortuosity and calcification, which represent well‐established markers of CTO complexity, 10 remained robust independent associates of failure in this anatomical context. Among clinical variables, peripheral artery disease was the only independent associate of failure, likely serving as a surrogate for diffuse systemic atherosclerosis and adverse vascular biology rather than lesion‐specific technical constraints.

Collectively, these data reinforce that aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs remain among the most technically demanding scenarios in contemporary CTO PCI. They highlight the importance of careful patient selection, referral to experienced centers, and the routine use of advanced techniques and imaging in this setting. Future studies should aim to validate dedicated risk models incorporating aorto‐ostial–specific anatomical features and to assess whether long‐term clinical outcomes differ from those observed after non–aorto‐ostial CTO PCI.

LIMITATIONS

This study has several limitations. First, it is a retrospective analysis of a multicenter registry, and residual confounding and selection bias cannot be excluded. Second, procedural strategies and device use were left to the operators' discretion and may have varied across centers and over time, potentially influencing outcomes. Third, although the ERCTO Registry captures core data, some baseline variables had missingness, which we addressed using multiple imputation; nevertheless, residual bias cannot be excluded. Fourth, only in‐hospital outcomes were systematically collected; therefore, longer‐term follow‐up regarding restenosis, reocclusion, or clinical benefit is unavailable. Fifth, as all participating operators were highly experienced CTO operators at high‐volume centers, the generalizability of these findings to lower‐volume settings may be limited. Sixth, the registry does not fully capture the sequence of wiring techniques or the intraprocedural decision‐making, which may have further influenced technical success or failure. Moreover, selection bias may disproportionately affect the aorto‐ostial subgroup, as operators may have been less likely to attempt PCI in cases with particularly hostile anatomy (such as the absence of both an antegrade target and viable retrograde options) potentially leading to an underestimation of the true difficulty associated with this lesion subset. Finally, although intravascular ultrasound guidance was used more frequently in aorto‐ostial lesions, its overall penetration remained modest, likely reflecting heterogeneity in institutional practices, reimbursement frameworks, thus limiting the ability to assess the true impact of intravascular imaging on outcomes in this specific anatomical setting.

CONCLUSIONS

Aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs constitute a frequent and anatomically complex subset of CTO interventions and are associated with significantly lower technical success compared with non–aorto‐ostial RCA CTOs, despite greater use of advanced procedural strategies. Technical failure was primarily associated with lesion‐specific anatomical features, particularly stumpless proximal cap morphology, severe calcification, and moderate‐to‐severe tortuosity, with peripheral artery disease as the only clinical variable independently associated with failure. These findings underscore the need for dedicated technical approaches and refined risk stratification tools tailored to the unique challenges of aorto‐ostial CTOs.

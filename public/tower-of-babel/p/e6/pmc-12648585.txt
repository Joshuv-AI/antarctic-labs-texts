Long term dataset of Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a Eu standard solution determined via multi-collector inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry during 2017–2024
Authors: Seung-Gu Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article provides an update to a previously published data article and associated data set regarding Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a Eu chemical solution, determined over the period 2017–2024. The Eu isotope ratios were measured via MC-ICP-MS using various Sm isotope pairs —147Sm-149Sm, 147Sm-154Sm, 149Sm-154Sm and 150Sm-154Sm—based on the exponential law over the eight-year period. Measurements were conducted under both wet plasma conditions (using spray chamber) and dry plasma conditions (using Aridus II system). The mean δ¹⁵³Eu values for all Sm isotope pairs are close to 0 ‰, and their 2SD intervals include zero, indicating no systematic bias from the choice of Sm pair or the sample-introduction system. While the precision of δ¹⁵³Eu values for NIST 3117a varies slightly among different Sm isotope pairs, it remains generally consistent. These results demonstrate that Sm-based internal normalization yields robust and reproducible Eu isotope ratios by MC-ICP-MS. The dataset can serve as reference for studying Eu isotope fractionation in geological rocks and meteorites. 36 • No measurable bias in the Eu isotopic composition of the NIST 3117a standard solution is observed with respect to the selection of Sm isotope pairs for normalization or the sample introduction technique.Keywords: NIST3117a, Eu isotope fractionation, MC-ICP-MS, Sm internal standard

Background

Among the rare earth elements (REEs), europium (Eu) exists in both divalent (+II) and trivalent (+III) oxidation states, whereas most REEs occur only in the trivalent state (+III) in nature. The presence of divalent oxidation state of Eu can cause elemental fractionation during magma differentiation [6]. Europium has only two isotopes, 151Eu (47.81 %) and 153Eu (52.19 %). However, since Eu has only two isotopes, it is difficult to precisely determine its isotope ratio. Accurate determination of Eu isotope ratios in geological materials requires internal isotope pair for normalization [[7], [8], [9]]. Since the first report of an experimental method for measuring Eu isotope ratio in geological materials using MC-ICP-MS [1], several research groups [[10], [11], [12]] have proposed methods for measuring Eu isotope ratios in geological samples, commonly using NIST3117a as a standard solution. However, other studies have not reported differences in Eu isotopic ratios depending on the sample introduction method or the kinds of Sm isotope pair for normalization. In this context, this article reports Eu isotope ratio measurements of NIST3117a standard solution conducted between 2017 and 2024. Nicol et al. [10] reported a Eu purification protocol using La resin and measured Eu isotope ratio via MC-ICP-MS with Sm internal standard method. Wu et al. [11,12] used neodymium (Nd) as the internal standard for Eu isotope ratio determination. Nicol et al. [10] also reported Eu isotope ratios of the NIST3117a solution measured under dry plasma condition via MC-ICP-MS.

Operating conditions for the Neptune Plus MC-ICP-MS.

Firstly, an in-house Sm solution as a dopant was prepared from ultrapure Sm2O3 produced by Alfa Aesar (99.998 %purity, Lot no. P4362) [1]. In addition, NIST3117a standard solution (Lot. No. 120,705) that is a homogeneous and concentrate form (10,000 μg/mL) was used as an in- house standard solution for determining Eu isotope ratios from geochemical reference materials. In order to obtain similar intensity of Eu and Sm isotopes, the concentration ratio of Eu and Sm was 1 to 2. Sample dilution for Eu isotope measurement via MC-ICP-MS was performed with 2 % HNO3 which was prepared from 60 % ultrapure HNO3 (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany) and DIW (Milli-Q system, Millipore, Milford, USA).

The signal intensity of 151Eu in a 1μg/mL NIST3117a solution was measured to be 80–120 mV/ppb under wet plasma condition using spray chamber, and 600–1000 mV/ppb under dry plasma conditions utilizing Aridus II desolvation system. Under wet plasma condition, the concentrations of Eu (from NIST 3117a) and Sm (from Alfa Aesar) in mixed solution were 50 μg/mL and 125 μg/mL, respectively. Under dry plasma conditions, the concentrations were 20 μg/mL for Eu and 50 μg/mL for Sm. The solution used for dry plasma condition was prepared by diluting a stock solution containing 50 ng/mL Eu and 125 ng/mL Sm. The experimental designs for these data are described in detail by Lee and Tanaka [1,2].

Eu isotopic measurements were conducted using a Neptune Plus MC-ICP-MS (Thermo Fisher Scientific Ltd.) equipped with nine Faraday cups, operated under wet and dry plasma conditions at the Korea Institute of Geoscience and Mineral Resources (KIGAM), Daejeon, Korea. The 147Sm(L4), 149Sm(L3), 150Sm(L2), 151Eu(L1), 152Sm(C), 153Eu(H1), 154Sm(H2), 155Gd(H3), and 157Gd(H4) isotopes were monitored simultaneously using nine Faraday cups for Sm normalization (Table 1). Sample introduction was achieved using a PFA-100 microflow nebulizer with aspiration rate of approximately 100 μL/min. Data acquisition consisted of one block of 50 cycles, with an integration time of 4.194 s per cycle. Sample aspiration rates were 80–100 μL/min. Ultrapure 2 % HNO3 blank solutions under wet plasma conditions were analyzed before and after each sample measurement. After each measurement of each sample (including blank solution), the sample introduction lines were washed by 3 % HNO3 for 250 s to prevent memory effect. Measurements were carried out in low-resolution mode. For wet plasma conditions (spray chamber), Ni normal-sampling and Pt X-skimmer cones were used. For dry plasma conditions (using Aridus II desolvation system from CETAC Technologies™), Jet-sampling and Pt X-skimmer cones were employed. The gain on each Faraday cup was monitored daily to ensure accurate efficiency normalization. Eu isotopic ratios were calculated using normalization based on multiple Sm isotope pairs, including 147Sm-149Sm, 147Sm-152Sm, 147Sm-154Sm, 149Sm-154Sm, and 150Sm-154Sm (Fig. 1, Fig. 2).

Method validation

The dataset are provided as two Figures (Fig. 1, Fig. 2) and Excel file available in the Mendeley Data repository (https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/b4zzwjv6pz/1). The Excel file contains two worksheets, each corresponding to Table 1 and Table 2 in the Mendeley Data repository, respectively. Table 1 in the Mendeley Data repository reports Eu isotope ratios of NIST3117a determined via MC-ICP-MS using spray chamber under wet plasma conditions whereas Table 2 shows those using Aridus II under dry plasma conditions.

Eu isotope ratio data measured via MC-ICP-MS under wet plasma condition

Fig. 1. presents the Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a standard solution determined via Neptune MC-ICP-MS under wet plasma conditions with spray chamber, employing five different Sm isotope pairs for mass bias correction: 147Sm/154Sm = 0.6626506, 147Sm/149Sm = 1.0868, 147Sm/152Sm = 0.562992, 149Sm/154Sm = 0.609726 and 150Sm/154Sm = 0.325396 [7]). The data span the period from December 23, 2017, to July 29, 2024. The yellow band in the figure indicates the mean repeatability of Eu isotope ratio for the NIST3117a Eu standard solution, within an uncertainty of ± 0.1 ‰ (2SD) over 2017–2024. The notation “n” refers to the number of experimental days, i.e., the number of days on which the Eu isotope ratios were measured. The Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a under wet plasma in this work is consistent with the value reported by Nicol et al. [10], 0.0003 ± 0.0040 ‰ (2SD).

Eu isotope ratio data measured via MC-ICP-MS under dry plasma condition

Fig. 2 presents the Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a standard solution determined by Neptune MC-ICP-MS under dry plasma condition with an Aridus II desolvation system. Measurements were performed from January 27, 2017 to June 14, 2024, using five different Sm isotope pairs for internal normalization: 147Sm/154Sm = 0.6626506, 147Sm/149Sm = 1.0868, 147Sm/152Sm = 0.562992, 149Sm/154Sm = 0.609726 and 150Sm/154Sm = 0.325396 [7]) from Jan. 27, 2017 to Jun. 14, 2024. The yellow band in Fig. 2 indicates the mean repeatability of the Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a Eu standard solution within an uncertainty of ± 0.1 ‰ (2SD) during 2021–2024. The symbol “n” denotes the number of experimental days on which Eu isotope ratio was determined. The Eu isotope ratio of NIST3117a under dry plasma in this work also is consistent with the value reported by Nicol et al. [10], 0.0003 ± 0.0040 ‰ (2SD).

Impact of ICU Length of Stay and Illness Severity on Rehabilitation Progress and Functional Recovery: A Secondary Analysis of the Japanese Rehabilitation and Risk Factors on the Post-Intensive Care Syndrome (J-RELIFE) Prospective Registry
Authors: Yorihide Yanagita, Yuki Iida, Shinichi Watanabe, Tomoyuki Morisawa, Yusuke Kawai, Ryo Kozu, Shigeaki Inoue, The Japanese Rehabilitation and Risk Factors on the Post-Intensive Care Syndrome (J-RELIFE) Study Group Collaborators
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IMPORTANCE:Although survival rates in ICUs have improved, ICU length of stay is increasing, particularly among critically ill patients requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation. These patients often face challenges in early rehabilitation. Despite the importance of early mobilization, its implementation is often hindered by the severity of patient conditions. The details of rehabilitation interventions, particularly the element of intervention time, have not been fully examined.OBJECTIVES:This study evaluated the time, content, and effects of rehabilitation during ICU stays.DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS:This study is a secondary analysis of data from the Japanese Rehabilitation and Risk Factors on the Post-Intensive Care Syndrome (J-RELIFE) prospective multicenter registry, which enrolled critically ill patients across 22 institutions in Japan. From this registry, we identified 423 patients who underwent mechanical ventilation in the ICU for greater than 48 hours. Patients were categorized into three groups based on ICU stay length: short-, medium-, and long-term (199, 157, and 67 patients, respectively).MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES:The primary variable was rehabilitation intervention time in the ICU, analyzed relative to ICU stay length. Secondary measures included the association between rehabilitation time and the highest Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, as well as functional outcomes at ICU and hospital discharge.RESULTS:The long-term group had significantly greater rehabilitation time (p < 0.05). A significant correlation was observed between rehabilitation time and highest SOFA score (r = 0.354; p < 0.001). In the long-term ICU group, even with high illness severity and delayed mobilization, patients’ activities of daily living were restored by discharge.CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE:Our registry-based analysis shows that severely ill ICU patients require extended rehabilitation interventions. This highlights the need for staffing and implementation systems that can ensure sufficient rehabilitation time for severely ill patients.

KEYPOINTS

Question: This study aimed to evaluate the timing, content, and effects of rehabilitation in ICU patients requiring mechanical ventilation for more than 48 hours.

Findings: In this prospective cohort study of 423 patients across 22 Japanese ICUs, longer ICU stays were associated with significantly longer rehabilitation times and higher illness severity. Despite delayed mobilization, patients in the long-term ICU group recovered their activities of daily living by discharge.

Meaning: Severely ill ICU patients benefit from extended rehabilitation, highlighting the need for adequate staffing and systems to support rehab in prolonged ICU stays.

Recent advancements in the understanding of critical illnesses, medical technology, and therapeutic interventions have significantly enhanced survival rates of patients in ICUs (1–4). Nevertheless, extended ICU admissions have become increasingly prevalent and are often correlated with the severity of the illness and the need for mechanical ventilation (5). Patients experiencing prolonged ICU stays are at an elevated risk of developing post-intensive care syndrome (PICS), characterized by physical, cognitive, and psychologic impairments, with prevalence rates reaching up to 63.5% in Japan (6–8).

Early mobilization and rehabilitation are essential in preventing or alleviating PICS and improving both short- and long-term patient outcomes (9–12). Despite its proven benefits, the implementation of early rehabilitation is frequently challenging in patients with severe illnesses requiring advanced life support such as extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) or continuous renal replacement therapy (13).

Continuous rehabilitation is considered essential throughout an ICU stay; however, the optimal approach to patient interaction, particularly with respect to the amount of rehabilitation time provided, remains inadequately defined in the literature.

To address this issue, we conducted a study to evaluate the timing, therapeutic activities, and effects of rehabilitation during the ICU stay. We hypothesized that an extended ICU stay and increased illness severity would delay mobilization and prolong rehabilitation time. However, we anticipated that sustained rehabilitation would facilitate the recovery of activities of daily living (ADL) by the time of discharge. This study, by categorizing patients according to the length of their ICU stay, aimed to ascertain the effect of long-term rehabilitation on clinical outcomes across different patient populations.

METHODS

Participants

This study is a secondary analysis of data from the Japanese Rehabilitation and Risk Factors on the PICS (J-RELIFE) study, a prospective multicenter registry of critically ill patients across Japan. Twenty-two institutions, including members of the Japanese Society of Intensive Care Medicine, where rehabilitation by physical therapists is routinely provided in the ICU, were included in the registry. The recruitment period for the study was between October 1, 2021, and December 31, 2023. The study population included all patients admitted to the ICU at designated centers who were undergoing rehabilitation interventions. Inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) use of a mechanical ventilator for greater than or equal to 48 hours and 2) age of 20 years old or older at the time of consent for participation in the study. The exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) discharge from the ICU within 48 hours; 2) neurologic disorders; 3) difficulties in effective communication in Japanese, such as those experienced by foreign nationals or individuals with cognitive impairments that hinder cooperation with rehabilitation efforts; 4) inability to walk even with the assistance of mobility aids prior to admission; and 5) end-of-life care not directed toward medical treatment. Ethical approval for the J-RELIFE study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Fujita Health University (HM21-077), the Ethics Committees of the 22 participating institutions, and the Japanese Intensive Care Research Group. For more details, please refer to the Supplemental Digital Content (https://links.lww.com/CCX/B562). The study was registered with the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trial Registry (UMIN 000047941). The study protocol was in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all the study participants or their family members before participation.

Measurements

Data were collected at three time points—ICU admission, ICU discharge, and hospital discharge—and longitudinally during the ICU stay to quantify rehabilitation exposure. At ICU admission, the following baseline variables were extracted from medical and ICU records: age, sex, body mass index, presence of sepsis, prevalence of septic shock, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), pre-admission Barthel index (BI), Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score, and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score.

During the ICU stay, we recorded the use of ECMO, blood purification therapy, endotoxin adsorption therapy, occurrence of hyperglycemia, corticosteroid administration, duration of mechanical ventilation, and length of ICU stay. SOFA scores were calculated according to standard methodology (14), and the highest SOFA during the ICU stay was used as an indicator of cumulative organ dysfunction (15).

Rehabilitation exposure in the ICU was documented in terms of timing and duration of interventions. Consistent with prior studies (16, 17), a supervising physiotherapist measured the duration of each rehabilitation session with a stopwatch, from the beginning to the conclusion of the session—including preparation and rest periods—to the nearest minute. Functional milestones were also evaluated, with the day of ICU admission defined as day 1, and the interval (in d) to each milestone was recorded. These milestones included initiation of rehabilitation, sitting on the edge of the bed, standing, sitting in a wheelchair, walking, and achieving independence.

Functional outcomes were assessed at both ICU discharge and hospital discharge, including BI, Medical Research Council (MRC) sum score, and grip strength. Finally, we collected data on the length of general ward stay, timing of rehabilitation interventions during the general ward stay, total length of hospital stay, and patient outcome at discharge.

Rehabilitation Protocol

The rehabilitation protocol is a standardized practice implemented across various centers, and its safety has been validated previously (18). The protocol involved passive joint range of motion exercises for the patient’s extremities while in the supine position, gradually progressing to active exercises performed by the patient. Respiratory physiotherapy was administered to enhance pulmonary function and prevent pneumonia and atelectasis. As the patient’s overall health stabilized, progression toward activities, such as bed weaning and mobility milestones, including getting up, sitting, standing, and walking, was facilitated. Intervention methods, decisions regarding continuation or discontinuation of rehabilitation, and various types of ICU care were based on the respective standards of each institution in accordance with the Pain, Anxiety, Agitation/Sedation, Delirium, Immobility, and Sleep Disruption (PADIS) guidelines (19) and a previous study (20).

Statistical Analysis

Data were presented as medians (interquartile range), numbers, and percentages. For each variable, comparisons were conducted among the short-term (ICU stay, 1–7 d), medium-term (ICU stay, 8–14 d), and long-term (ICU stay, ≥ 15 d) ICU stay groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for continuous variables, followed by Tukey post hoc test for multiple comparisons among groups. The chi-square test was used for categorical variables. Although continuous data were summarized using medians and interquartile ranges due to non-normal distribution, ANOVA was used based on the assumption of approximate normality and equal variances across groups. The primary outcome, which was the daily rehabilitation time in the ICU, was calculated as the mean value for each group on each ICU day and is depicted in a histogram.

Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to examine factors associated with the total duration of rehabilitation in the ICU (in min). This served as the dependent variable. Two models were constructed: full and reduced. The full model included the following independent variables: age, sex, primary diagnosis at ICU admission, presence or absence of sepsis, CCI, highest SOFA score, presence of blood purification therapy, time from ICU admission to rehabilitation initiation, time to first sitting on the edge of the bed, and duration of mechanical ventilation. The reduced model included a subset of clinically relevant variables: age, sex, highest SOFA score, time to rehabilitation initiation, time to first sitting on the edge of the bed, and duration of mechanical ventilation. Additionally, the daily rehabilitation intervention time in the ICU was analyzed in relation to the highest SOFA score using the Pearson correlation coefficient. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY), with the significance level set at 5%. Although an a priori sample size calculation was not performed, a sample size of 423 patients provided adequate power to detect moderate correlations (r = 0.3) with 80% power at a 5% significance level.

RESULTS

During the study period, 22 hospitals participated in patient enrollment. A patient flow diagram is shown in Figure 1. A total of 423 patients met the inclusion criteria and were included in the analysis. The participants were classified according to the duration of their ICU stay as follows: 199, 157, and 67 in the short-, medium-, and long-term groups, respectively. Significant differences were identified between the groups according to age, APACHE II score, and SOFA score (Table 1). Additionally, significant differences were observed in the rates of blood purification, hyperglycemia, and steroid therapy during the ICU stay.

DISCUSSION

The principal findings of our study were as follows: 1) patients with prolonged ICU stay required extended periods of rehabilitation; 2) patients with severe illnesses in the ICU tended to require longer rehabilitation durations; and 3) despite disease severity and delayed mobilization progression in the long-term ICU group, their ADL were restored by the time of discharge. These results support our hypothesis that prolonged ICU stay and greater illness severity delay mobilization, but do not preclude functional recovery with continued rehabilitation.

Conditions such as sepsis, renal failure, and unstable hemodynamic status significantly contribute to extended ICU stay (21). Furthermore, the emergence of new cascading illnesses, such as persistent critical illnesses, results in prolonged ICU stays, which are often reported to be more severe than the initial reasons for ICU admission (22). These extended stays are associated with poor long-term outcomes, particularly in older or frail patients, and are correlated with higher mortality rates in the ICU and post-discharge (21, 23). Consequently, various pathologic conditions contribute to the prolongation of ICU stay, and many of these factors may also affect the timing of rehabilitation initiation and the nature of the interventions. Specifically, for severely ill patients, the stabilization of hemodynamics and respiratory status requires time, thereby delaying rehabilitation progress. Indeed, delayed progression of mobilization has been observed in patients with prolonged ICU stay. In this study, we found that patients with an extended ICU stay required a significant amount of rehabilitation time, and we further identified a significant correlation between the duration of rehabilitation intervention in the ICU and disease severity. Although various studies have previously examined rehabilitation intervention time within the ICU as an outcome measure (24–28), none have emphasized its association with disease severity. Our study outcomes substantiate that the rehabilitation process for critically ill patients requires substantial time.

Barriers to early rehabilitation have been extensively reported, including factors such as the patient’s condition and insufficient staff for exercise (29–31). Notably, statistics indicate that only 8% of patients dependent on intubated ventilators engage in activities outside their beds (13). Patients with more severe illnesses typically depend on medical devices such as ventilators and ECMO. Their mobilization requires comprehensive management and coordination among staff. Therefore, the percentage of patients who underwent blood purification in the long-term group was significantly higher than that in the short- and medium-term groups. The mobilization of critically ill patients reliant on multiple medical devices necessitates not only dedicated patient care but also substantial time and workforce resources for the appropriate management of these medical devices. Although the safety of early exercise and mobilizing patients out of bed while on mechanical ventilation in the ICU has been established (32, 33), it is crucial to acknowledge that time and a well-coordinated team are essential prerequisites for safely conducting these rehabilitative efforts.

It is imperative to consider the comprehensive health status of the patients to ensure the safety of the rehabilitation process. Meticulous patient monitoring is vital for preventing activity-related adverse events. The intervention methods and decisions regarding the continuation or cessation of rehabilitation and various types of ICU care were aligned with the PADIS guidelines (19) and previous studies (20). Stepwise mobilization was guided by established mobility protocols. If mobilization could not proceed to the next step for any reason, efforts were made to facilitate progression, either immediately or after a brief interval. This adaptive approach may have contributed to the extended duration of rehabilitation interventions observed in this study.

Despite the severity of illness and delayed progression of motor function in the long-term ICU stay group, ADL recovered at the time of hospital discharge. This finding underscores the necessity for individualized rehabilitation strategies based on ICU length of stay and disease severity. Notably, even in severely ill patients with delayed mobilization, continued rehabilitation after ICU discharge may facilitate the recovery of functional independence. Previous studies have also reported that patients who received high-intensity rehabilitation during their ICU stay had significantly lower ADL dependency at discharge than those with lower rehabilitation exposure (15). These findings suggest that extending the duration and intensity of rehabilitation in severely ill patients may enhance recovery of ADL.

Conversely, studies focusing on patients with acute respiratory failure have reported that intensive physiotherapy programs do not yield superior long-term physical outcomes compared with standard care (12, 34). These contrasting results may reflect differences in disease characteristics and timing of interventions, warranting further investigation. In the present study, both groups showed an increase in BI from the ICU to hospital discharge, indicating that continued rehabilitation after ICU discharge may contribute to improvements in ADL. However, it is important to note that the inpatient care environment and rehabilitation services provided after ICU discharge can vary significantly across different facilities. Therefore, further studies are needed to clarify the interaction between rehabilitation interventions during ICU stay and after ICU discharge as well as to determine the optimal rehabilitation volumes according to disease severity. Establishing benchmarks for daily rehabilitation tailored to disease-specific needs may contribute to developing more effective rehabilitation protocols (35).

Limitations of this study include significant confounding factors due to variations across multiple institutional systems. Second, implementation of a standardized rehabilitation protocol across multiple institutions proved impractical. Finally, the criteria for daily rehabilitation endpoints were inadequately defined and relied instead on patient tolerance and subjective assessment by a physiotherapist. Future studies should prioritize the establishment of well-defined criteria for rehabilitation endpoints. Nonetheless, the inclusion of patients from diverse institutional backgrounds enhanced the capacity of this study to reflect prevailing conditions in Japanese ICUs. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first prospective study to elucidate the relationship between illness severity in ICU patients and the corresponding rehabilitation intervention time.

CONCLUSIONS

A prospective multicenter registry study identified a correlation between illness severity and duration of rehabilitation intervention in patients receiving treatment in an ICU. Our findings emphasize that patients with severe illness in the ICU require extended rehabilitation interventions. This underscores the critical need for staffing and implementing systems that can accommodate the rehabilitation time required for patients with severe conditions.

Identification of a novel signature for prognostic stratification and integrative analyses in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Haoran Li, Peijun Cao, Jialong Li, Peiyu Wang, Ying Yi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecently, research has revealed that the Golgi apparatus is involved in the development process of cancer; however, the specific effect of Golgi apparatus-related genes (GAGs) in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains unclear. This study aims to construct a more concise and practical risk model in LUAD using GAG.MethodsThe gene expression profiles of patients with LUAD were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases, and GAGs were downloaded from the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) database. Univariate Cox and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) analyses were performed to identify the prognostic GAG signature. Kaplan-Meier and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to validate the predictive effect of the prognostic signatures. The correlation between the risk model and the immune landscape was examined using CIBERSORT and TIDE analyses. Also, the genes in the signature were assessed by single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq).ResultsA prognostic signature comprising 5 GAG genes (GNPNAT1, RGS20, CAV3, NTSR1, and FURIN) was established after LASSO and multi-Cox analyses. Both the Kaplan-Meier analysis and the ROC curves supported the strong predictive utility of the risk model. Specifically, the former yielded significant stratification in all three validation datasets (P=1.2001e−05, P=0.006, and P=0.04), while the latter provided further evidence of its predictive precision through the area under the curve. In addition, we found that the low-risk group responded better to immunotherapy than the high-risk group (P<0.0001). scRNA-seq analysis revealed the distribution patterns of the 5 GAG genes in cells. Finally, we assessed the situation of tumor mutation burden (TMB) and performed functional analysis based on the risk model of GAGs.ConclusionsThe risk model based on GAGs can effectively stratify the prognosis of patients and predict immunotherapy responses in LUAD.

Introduction

Lung cancer (LC) remains one of the most lethal malignancies, and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) comprises roughly 85% of LC (1,2). Of these, lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) constitutes the majority, representing more than 50% of NSCLC subtypes. Despite advances in LUAD management—such as immunotherapy, targeted therapies, and radiation—the 5-year survival rate remains unacceptably poor (3). Consequently, developing robust predictive models for early prognosis evaluation is critically needed.

The Golgi apparatus, also referred to as the Golgi complex, is a membrane-bound organelle found in eukaryotic cells, where it plays a key role in processing and sorting proteins and lipids (4). During cell migration and division, the Golgi undergoes dynamic changes, and its transient disassembly and redistribution are essential for proper cell division. One of the most prominent pathological hallmarks of the Golgi is its fragmented state. The Golgi experiences irreversible fragmentation, implying that its structural breakdown may contribute to pathology (5). In contrast, under normal physiological conditions, the organelle maintains its characteristic stacked morphology, with its functional dynamics under precise control. Consequently, disruptions in Golgi structure have been proposed as potential drivers or aggravators of disease (6). Moreover, numerous genes encoding proteins associated with Golgi apparatus-related functions have been identified as recurrently mutated in cancers. For example, the production of phosphatidylinositol-4-phosphate (PI4P) by the Golgi-dedicated enzyme phosphatidylinositol-4-kinase IIIβ (PI4KIIIβ) boosts secretory activity and accelerates LUAD growth by stimulating GOLPH3-mediated vesicle trafficking from the Golgi apparatus (7). The GOLPH3/CKAP4 signaling axis is instrumental in facilitating the release of exosomal WNT3A, thereby reinforcing cancer stem-like properties and metastatic spread in NSCLC (8).

In this study, we established a Golgi apparatus-related gene (GAG) 5-gene risk model capable of effectively prognosticating patient outcomes. The model was constructed using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-multivariate analysis and externally validated with the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Based on the risk stratification, we further analyzed somatic mutation patterns, tumor mutation burden (TMB), immune cell infiltration and responses to immunotherapeutic agents. Additionally, we conducted single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analyses to investigate the expression patterns of risk model genes by re-assessing GEO datasets through systematic re-annotation. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0943/rc).

Methods

Data preparation

RNA-seq data and associated clinical parameters for 524 LUAD and 58 normal samples were extracted from TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Expression values were subsequently normalized using the R package limma. The GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) provided two independent validation sets, GSE37745 and GSE68465, which included 196 and 462 samples, respectively. Clinical metadata for these cohorts were also acquired from GEO. Additionally, a total of 1,686 GAGs were sourced from the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp). The overall design of the study is summarized in Figure 1A. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Screening the GAGs in TCGA-LUAD DEGs. (A) The workflow of this study; (B) the volcano plot and (C) heatmap plot of DEGs in TCGA-LUAD; (D) Venn diagram showing the number of DEGs associated with GAGs. DEGs, differentially expressed genes; GAGs, Golgi apparatus-related genes; GEO, Gene Expression Omnibus; GO, Gene Ontology; KEGG, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes; KM, Kaplan-Meier; LASSO, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator; LUAD, lung adenocarcinoma; ROC, receiver operating characteristic; TCGA, The Cancer Genome Atlas; TMB, tumor mutation burden; t-SNE, t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding.

Construction and validation of the prognostic GAGs signature

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the TCGA cohort were identified using the “DESeq2” R package. To identify GAGs with prognostic relevance, a univariate Cox regression analysis for overall survival (OS) was conducted, retaining genes with P<0.05. Subsequently, a LASSO regression model was applied via the “glmnet” package in R to select features and determine the optimal λ value for constructing a prognostic signature. Significant genes were further incorporated into a multivariate Cox regression to establish a risk prediction model. The model’s performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. To evaluate its robustness, risk scores were computed within the GEO datasets (GSE37745 and GSE68465), and ROC analysis along with survival studies were performed using the “survival” package in R.

Gene expression and survival analysis

Candidate gene expression patterns were examined using The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org/). Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated based on the GEPIA platform (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/).

Immune infiltration and immunotherapy analysis

The CIBERSORT algorithm, implemented via an R package, was used to estimate the abundance of 22 types of tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs) in LUAD samples from the TCGA dataset. Differences in immune cell infiltration between high-risk and low-risk groups were compared using the Wilcoxon test. Immune scores were further computed with the ESTIMATE algorithm using the R package “estimate” (v4.5.1). Reference immune cell signatures were acquired from TISIDB (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/). Immunophenoscore (IPS) data for the TCGA cohort were obtained from The Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA, https://tcia.at/home) and visualized in R.

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were conducted using R packages “clusterProfiler”, “org.Hs.eg.db”, “enrichplot”, and “ggplot2” (R v4.5.1). These analyses were performed on DEGs, including those identified between high- and low-risk groups. Terms with a P value <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Mutation profiling

Somatic mutation data for LUAD were retrieved from the cBioPortal database (https://www.cbioportal.org/). The R package “maftools” (v4.5.1) was employed to visualize the mutation spectrum via waterfall plots and to compare profiles between high- and low-risk subgroups. TMB was also calculated for each sample.

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

The LUAD scRNA-seq dataset GSE164983 was obtained from the GEO database. Processing and analysis were performed using the “Seurat” R package. Cells expressing fewer than 200 or more than 5,000 genes were excluded. Normalization was carried out with the NormalizeData function. Dimensionality reduction was conducted through principal component analysis (PCA) on highly variable genes, followed by construction of a shared nearest-neighbor graph (FindNeighbors) and cluster identification (FindClusters, resolution = 1.5). Results were visualized using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE). Cell clusters were annotated based on canonical marker genes. Expression levels and risk scores of GAGs were further evaluated at single-cell resolution.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (v4.5.1). Categorical variables were compared via nonparametric tests. Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups according to GAGs-based risk scores. OS between groups was compared using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Screening of GAGs among DEGs in LUAD

In the present study, the concise workflow was charted in Figure 1A. We downloaded the transcriptome RNA data of LUAD from the TCGA database and then screened the DEGs between normal tissue and LUAD tissue. A total of 5,389 DEGs were detected, including 2,004 that were downregulated and 3,385 that were upregulated (Figure 1B). The top 20 downregulated DEGs and upregulated DEGs were shown in Figure 1C. Then, there were 382 common genes between the DEGs and the GAGs in LUAD (Figure 1D).

Construction of GAG signature for LUAD stratification

These genes above were all chosen for the univariate Cox regression analysis, and we found that 109 genes were significantly associated with OS in LUAD (Table S1). Then, a LASSO regression analysis was applied to find the potential prognostic genes (Figures 2A,2B). The 8-gene signature involving ERO1A, GNPNAT1, BCAN, RGS20, CAV3, NTSR1, IGFBP1 and FURIN was identified by the optimal value of λ. Next, these 8 genes were further chosen to multi-variate Cox regression analysis, and only five genes (GNPNAT1, RGS20, CAV3, NTSR1 and FURIN) were retained to construct the risk model (Figure 2C,2D). The risk scores were subsequently computed, and the median value was adopted as the threshold to stratify patients into low- and high-risk cohorts (Figure 2C). The distributions of the risk scores and their association with OS in LUAD are illustrated in Figure 2E,2F. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that patients in the high-risk group had a significantly poorer OS compared with those in the low-risk group (P<0.0001; Figure 2G). The ROC curves assessed the prognostic value for OS, producing areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.730 (1-year), 0.680 (3-year), and 0.671 (5-year) (Figure 2H). In addition, survival analysis with GEPIA and protein expression profiling via The Human Protein Atlas were also conducted (Figure S1).

Validation of the GAGs signature in GEO datasets

External validation of the TCGA-based model was performed using the GSE37745 and GSE68465 datasets, which were sourced from the GEO platform. First of all, we carried out normalization processing on the GEO data (Figure 3A,3B). Then, we categorized the GSE37745 and GSE68465 samples into low- and high-risk groups using the same risk scoring system derived from TCGA. Kaplan-Meier analyses demonstrated that high-risk patients had inferior OS in both validation cohorts (P=0.04 and 0.006; Figure 3C,3D). To verify the model’s stability, we generated ROC curves for each dataset. For GSE37745, the AUCs at 1, 3, and 5 years were 0.639, 0.608, and 0.617 (Figure 3E), and for GSE68465, they were 0.599, 0.605, and 0.585, respectively (Figure 3F).

ROC curves and AUC for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival in GSE37745 (E) and GSE68465 (F). Mutation patterns and TMB in various risk groups

Firstly, the TCGA LUAD cohort was divided into high- or low-risk groups based on the risk scores above. Then, the “mafTools” R package was further used to investigate the distribution pattern of the top 15 somatic mutations in various risk groups. TP53 and TTN were identified as the two most frequently mutated genes in both groups (Figure 4A,4B). In addition, EGFR mutation was detected in the low-risk group, and KRAS mutation was detected in the high-risk group. Besides, TMB quantification analyses demonstrated that the high-risk group had a higher TMB value (Figure 4C-4E). In summary, the high-risk group had a higher TMB value and a higher mutation frequency.

Mutation pattern and TMB in various risk groups. The mutation pattern in the high-risk group (A) or low-risk group (B). (C-E) Comparisons of TMB in various risk groups. TMB, tumor mutation burden.

Comprehensive analysis of immune cells and immunological responses between high- and low-risk groups

Here, we analyzed the correlation between the infiltration level of immune cells and risk score, based on CIBERSORT and TIDE databases. In the analysis of 22 immune cells, we found that macrophage M0 and macrophage M1 had a significantly higher infiltration proportion in the high-risk group, whereas resting memory CD4+ T cells had a higher infiltration in the low-risk group (Figure 5A,5B). Besides, the correlation analysis between immune score and risk score reveled that a higher immune score was usually accompanied by a lower risk score (P<0.0001, Figure 5C). Then, the K-M plot showed that LUAD in the high-risk group has a poor response to immunotherapy (Figure 5D) and a shorter survival time (P<0.0001, Figure 5E). Next, we analyzed the IPS and the responses to CTLA-4 or PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors based on the TCIA database. In the low-risk group, a greater proportion of patients with LUAD showed a better immunological response, whether it was for PD-1 or CTLA-4 checkpoint inhibitors (Figure 5F-5I).

Identification and functional enrichment analysis of GAGs

Firstly, gene set variation analysis (GSVA) was performed in various risk groups. The top signaling pathways that differed in high- and low-risk groups were shown (Figure 6A). KRAS signaling, myogenesis, Hedgehog signaling, oxidative phosphorylation and so on were significantly associated with cancer development. Then, GO analysis, including biological process (BP), cellular component (CC) and molecular function (MF), was also assessed (Figure 6B). The top GO analysis of BP was cilium movement, microtubule-based movement and cilium movement involved in cell motility. The KEGG enrichment results showed that the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, cornified envelope formation, neuroactive ligand signaling, and cell cycle were the enriched pathways for GAGs (Figure 6C).

GAGs scores in scRNA-seq analysis

First, we filtered ineligible cells and yielded 21,542 core cells for subsequent analysis. In the PCA, we selected 10 principal components (PCs) with a P value <0.05 for subsequent analysis. Then, the core cells were classified into 19 independent cell clusters using the t-SNE algorithm (Figure 6D). The different clusters were annotated by finding marker genes in the CellMarker database and references, resulting in 12 cell types, namely mast cells, fibroblasts, epithelial cells, natural killer (NK) cells, T cells, regulatory T (Treg) cells, B cells, macrophage, NKT cells, plasma cells, CD37+ NK cells and myeloid cells (Figure 6E). The expression of important marker genes for each cluster was shown (Figure 6D). After annotation, we detected the expression of GAGs at the single-cell level (Figure S2). Besides, the GAGs were primarily located in immune cells and fibroblasts, not epithelial or plasma cells (Figure 6F,6G).

Discussion

Patients with LUAD frequently experience unfavorable clinical outcomes and diminished long-term survival, primarily because the disease is often diagnosed at an advanced stage (9). The increasing utilization of low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) has enhanced early detection rates (10); however, a robust predictive tool to accurately assess the prognosis of LUAD remains insufficient. Recent therapeutic innovations, particularly immunotherapy and targeted agents, have contributed to improved survival in advanced-stage cases (11,12). Despite these advances, complete tumor resection through surgery continues to be the sole potentially curative intervention. Consequently, there is a pressing need to develop streamlined and dependable risk stratification models for LUAD.

The dynamics of the Golgi apparatus are tightly regulated, and their disruption contributes to the pathogenesis of multiple diseases, including cancer. Notably, Golgi-associated genes are frequently mutated in malignancies, and these genetic aberrations correlate with increased metastatic propensity and reduced patient survival rates (13). For example, PITPNC1 binds PI4P on the Golgi membrane and mediates RAB1B localization, thereby promoting malignancy-associated secretion in multiple cancers (14). In addition, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition initiates a PAQR11-mediated Golgi compaction process that drives metastasis in LUAD models (15). Thus, the dysfunction of the Golgi apparatus was involved in the development of cancers and it is vital to explore the relationship between the prognosis of LUAD and GAGs.

Herein, the gene expression data from the TCGA and GEO databases were used to construct and validate a 5-gene signature for a prognostic risk model after LASSO-multivariate analysis. The risk model, comprising GNPNAT1, RGS20, CAV3, NTSR1, and FURIN, was effective in the present study. However, a previous study reported a 14-Golgi apparatus-related gene signature was established to predict the prognosis of LUAD (16), and the AUC was 0.72 at 1 year. In contrast to their model, our refined algorithm attained comparable predictive performance (AUC =0.73 for 1-year OS) with merely 5 genes, significantly improving clinical utility. Consequently, our re-analysis represents a deliberate and meaningful optimization of the earlier work, culminating in a risk-stratification framework that is substantially more conducive to practical clinical application. Beyond the analyses of immune infiltration, immunotherapy response, and TMB, we further incorporated single-cell spatial mapping, rendering our model both more streamlined and biologically informative for clinical translation. In our study, TP53 was identified as the most frequently mutated gene in both groups. This observation aligns with the established paradigm that TP53 mutations are not merely passenger alterations but rather clonal driver events that fundamentally reshape tumor biology, including the tumor microenvironment (TME) and, consequently, immunotherapy responsiveness. A cellular and spatial atlas of TP53-associated tissue remodeling in LUAD further revealed that TP53-mutant tumors exhibit enriched angiostatic and immune checkpoint interactions, including CD274-PDCD1 and PVR-TIGIT, which likely engender a more favorable response to checkpoint blockade therapy (17).

GNPNAT1, a dimeric Golgi matrix and endomembrane-associated protein, functions as the rate-limiting enzymatic component in the second stage of hexosamine biosynthesis (18). Moreover, GNPNAT1 has been shown to enhance the growth, proliferation, migratory capacity, and invasiveness of LUAD cells, facilitating metastasis through the stabilization of Snai2 (18,19). In addition, GNPNAT1 expression was linked to B cells, CD4+ T cells, and dendritic cells in LUAD (20). Therefore, GNPNAT1 might be a potential prognostic biomarker and novel target for treatment in LUAD. RGS20 has been implicated in the development of various cancers, including penile cancer, bladder cancer, oral squamous cell carcinoma, triple-negative breast cancer, renal cancer and NSCLC (21). In particular, RGS20 enhances NSCLC proliferation via PKA-Hippo inhibition-mediated autophagy, supporting its utility as both a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target (21).

CAV3, traditionally characterized as a muscle-specific structural protein of the caveolin family, exhibits significant expression in mammary gland myoepithelial cells. Strikingly, virgin Cav-3-deficient mice exhibited profound resistance to mammary tumorigenesis (22). Alifano et al. reported that NTSR1 expression was strongly associated with worse 5-year OS rate and relapse-free survival in surgically resected stage I LUAD (23). Moreover, NTSR1 expression is higher in tumors than in healthy tissue and is correlated with the most advanced pathological grade and pTNM stages (24). Additionally, dysregulated NTSR1 expression commonly occurs in digestive system tumors [e.g., gastric cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)], with evidence suggesting its involvement in regulating HCC responsiveness to targeted therapies such as sorafenib and erlotinib (24,25). Simultaneously, Wu et al. showed that the neurotensin-NTSR1 axis is often dysregulated in epithelial cancers and antibody-mediated neurotensin blockade alters NSCLC phenotypic plasticity and enhances cisplatin efficacy through modulation of tumor cell heterogeneity (26). A previous study showed that genetic ablation of Furin in mammary epithelium significantly attenuates primary tumor progression and pulmonary metastasis in an oncogene-driven triple-negative breast cancer murine model (27). Moreover, Furin serves as a significant prognostic indicator in Middle Eastern papillary thyroid carcinoma and critically mediates BRAF-dependent MAPK/ERK pathway activation and subsequent tumorigenesis (28).

Targeted disruption of immune checkpoint pathways (particularly PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4) by immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has ushered in a new era in NSCLC management, mechanistically overcoming immunosuppressive constraints in the TME to reestablish effective antitumor immunity (29). Herein, we found that patients in the low-risk group had a better therapeutic response to ICIs due to the various distributions of immune cells. On the other hand, the high-risk groups exhibited higher TMB, partly resulting in a worse response to ICIs. By providing both single-cell resolution and genome-wide transcriptional coverage, scRNA-seq analysis enabled unbiased deconvolution of cellular diversity, establishing itself as an indispensable tool for dissecting the complex immune landscape within tumor ecosystems (30). In the present study, we also performed a re-annotation of GEO single-cell data and analyzed the risk gene scores.

In this study, we performed a comprehensive analysis of the GAGs risk model, but there are still limitations. Our results are based on bioinformatic analysis without experimental validation, and the functions of these 5 genes in LUAD need to be further studied.

Conclusions

In summary, our study provided novel insights into the association between GAGs and LUAD. Through systematic evaluation of GAGs expression patterns and their prognostic significance, we developed a GAGs-based molecular signature capable of stratifying LUAD patients into distinct risk groups with robust predictive accuracy. Furthermore, we conducted multi-dimensional characterization of the risk model, incorporating analyses of TMB, immune cell infiltration, immunotherapy response potential, functional pathway enrichment, and scRNA-seq-derived cellular signatures.

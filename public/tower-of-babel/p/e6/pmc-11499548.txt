Cost-Utility Analysis of Adjuvant Olaparib for Germline BRCA1/2-Mutated, High-Risk HER2-Negative Early Breast Cancer in Spain
Authors: Sergio Cedillo, Almudena González-Domínguez, Yoana Ivanova-Markova, Rafael López López, Sara López-Tarruella Cobo, José Alberto Peña Pedrosa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveHere we estimate the cost-effectiveness of olaparib in the Spanish National Health Service (SNHS) as adjuvant treatment of early germline mutations in the BRCA1/2 genes (gBRCAm) HER2-negative (HER2neg) breast cancer (BC) with high risk of recurrence.MethodsA semi-Markov model was adapted to the Spanish healthcare setting, using the perspective of the SNHS, and a lifetime horizon. Two scenarios were compared: receiving olaparib versus standard of care (SoC) treatment. The model comprised five health states and included the clinical results of the OlympiA trial, along with the direct healthcare costs associated with the use of early BC and subsequent treatment resources (€2023). A discount rate of 3% was applied for future cost and quality-of-life outcomes. A probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) was carried out.ResultsThe introduction of olaparib as adjuvant treatment for patients with early gBRCAm HER2neg BC with high risk of recurrence could involve an incremental cost of €44,273 and €50,164, with an improvement of 1.14 and 1.28 quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) for hormone receptor-positive (HR+) and triple-negative (TN) patients, respectively. Therefore, adjuvant olaparib could be cost-effective for early gBRCAm HER2neg BC, with an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of €38,839/QALY and €39,084/QALY for HR+ and TN patients, respectively. The results from the PSA showed that 75.7% and 82.2% of the simulations fell below the €60,000/QALY threshold.ConclusionsOlaparib as adjuvant treatment could be cost-effective in gBRCAm patients with early HER2neg BC in Spain.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s41669-024-00518-2.

Key Points for Decision-Makers

Our results suggest that olaparib costs more in the early-stage setting (invasive disease–free survival), but it is associated with lower costs in nonmetastatic breast cancer (BC), early metastatic BC, and late metastatic BC health states.Olaparib treatment could increase both the life years gained and the quality-adjusted life years in patients with high-risk HER2-negative germline BRCA 1/2 mutated BC in Spain.The adjuvant treatment, olaparib, for high-risk HER2-negative germline BRCA1/2-mutated BC could be cost-effective for Spain.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common type of cancer and the one with the highest death rate among women in Spain, with 35,001 new cases estimated for 2023 and 6614 deaths in 2021 [1]. More than 90% of BC are diagnosed in early stages [2]. It is estimated that 80–85% express low levels of the HER2 (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2) protein [3], denominated HER2neg. They may be hormone receptor (HR)-positive (HR+ or luminal BC) or HR-negative [HR- or triple-negative (TN)] tumors. Inheritance of germline mutations in the BRCA1/2 genes (gBRCAm) is one of the major risk factors in the development of breast and ovarian cancers [4, 5]. However, the prevalence of gBRCAm varies in different molecular subtypes of BC, with an approximate prevalence of 5% in HER2neg/HR+ [6] and 15% in TN [4].

gBRCAm BC has a high histologic grade, which leads to a higher incidence of metastasis to the central nervous system [7] or the appearance of a second BC or another neoplasm such as ovarian cancer [8]. Additionally, there is evidence that patients with gBRCAm tumors have a higher risk of recurrence compared with those with a BC but without such mutations [9–11]. More well-designed studies on this same line are awaited.

Historically, therapeutic alternatives available for patients with gBRCAm HER2neg BC tumors have been the same as for patients without germline mutations. In the HER2neg BC adjuvant setting, new therapies for TN and HR+ tumors have been recently approved and included in the latest Spanish Clinical Guidelines; however, their evidence in patients with gBRCAm is still limited. Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors (PARPi) represent an important new class of oral personalized agents targeting cancers with defective DNA-damage repair. Olaparib is the first PARPi authorized by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for the adjuvant treatment of adult patients with gBRCAm, HER2neg, high-risk, early BC who have previously received neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy [12].

OlympiA is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase III trial that assessed the efficacy and safety of olaparib as adjuvant treatment for a maximum of 12 months, in patients with early-stage HER2neg BC with the presence of gBRCAm and who are at a high risk of recurrence after chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant or adjuvant setting [13]. The study results showed that olaparib was associated with a statistically significant and clinically meaningful 41.9% risk reduction of invasive disease recurrence or death in patients with high-risk early gBRCAm HER2neg BC [13]. Recently, a statistically significant improvement in overall survival (OS) was demonstrated with a 32% risk reduction after a median follow-up of 3.5 years [14]. As a result, the new indication for olaparib was authorized by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in March 2022 and the EMA in August 2022. It was recommended by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guideline as a treatment specifically targeted for early-stage, high-risk gBRCAm HER2neg BC [15], and it obtained the highest score for adjuvant therapies in the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Magnitude of Clinical Benefit Scale (MCBS) [16].

Patients with this type of tumor present an important need for new targeted therapies capable of reducing the risk of recurrence and improving OS. Thus, the purpose of this study was to estimate the cost-effectiveness (CE) of olaparib from the perspective of the Spanish National Health System (SNHS) in the adjuvant treatment of early gBRCAm HER2neg BC with high risk of recurrence.

Materials and methods

A semi-Markov model with a 1-month cycle developed for the UK in Microsoft Excel [17] was adapted to the SNHS; this method was chosen because of its ability to explicitly model the relationship between each health state. Based on the OlympiA randomized clinical trial (RCT) [13], the model compares two scenarios: HR+ and TN patients receiving olaparib for 1 year as adjuvant treatment versus standard of care (SoC) in 2022 (“watch and wait,” adding endocrine therapy in HR+ patients), in both arms of the model. The modeling of each receptor subgroup was considered as a separate analysis to provide greater flexibility in capturing the different patterns of long-term disease recurrence in patients with HER2−/HR+ and TN BC [18].

The adaptation was conducted from the SNHS perspective, and the time horizon (57 years) was patient lifetime. A 3% annual discount rate was applied to the costs as well as to the health outcomes as mentioned by the current Spanish recommendations [19]. Outcomes measured were survival, quality-adjusted life years (QALY), and costs associated with each scenario, estimating the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) and the incremental cost-utility ratio (ICUR). According to the Spanish guidelines, a cost-effectiveness threshold between €25,000 and €60,000/QALY was considered [20, 21].

We reported our findings according to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS) checklist, which is provided in the supplementary material (Table S1).

Model

Simulated patients were 43.2 years old with a mean weight of 68.7 kg and body surface of 1.71 m2 based on the baseline characteristics of OlympiA RCT [13]. Experts confirmed that these characteristics are similar to those of Spanish patients.

The model included five health states (Fig. 1): invasive disease-free survival (IDFS), nonmetastatic breast cancer (nmBC), early metastatic breast cancer (emBC), late metastatic breast cancer (lmBC), and death. The simulation initiated with patients in the IDFS state who had received surgery and neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy and who may progress to nmBC, metastatic BC (early or late), or death from any cause. As the progression of the disease is an irreversible process, patients in the nmBC, emBC, and lmBC state could not return to the IDFS state. Patients who enter the nmBC state could remain in this state until they experience distant metastasis or die. Finally, patients with TN who experience distant metastatic recurrence during the first 2 years, after completing local treatment, enter the emBC state [22]. In the case of HR+, according to the Spanish clinical practice, patients enter the emBC state when recurrence occurs within the first 5 years after completing local treatment and, in the lmBC state, when it occurs after the first 5 years [23]. From the metastatic states, patients progress to the death state. The transitions between the different health states in both the olaparib arm and SoC were modeled after the survival curves observed in the OlympiA RCT [13], except from the lmBC health status where, due to a lack of sufficiently mature data from OlympiA, the OlympiAD (the only RCT with similar characteristics to the study population in the OlympiA RCT where survival in this health status was studied) study was used [24, 25].Fig. 1Structure of the semi-Markov model. Data Sources

A targeted literature review was performed to obtain data on patients with early HER2neg and gBRCA1/2m BC. Clinical practice guidelines, Medline/PubMed, Embase, and MEDES databases were consulted. The search was focused on the loss of QALY due to adverse events, utilities during the different health states, treatments given in early BC and during recurrent phases, first- and second-line treatments’ distribution, and medical consultation and tests. Spanish and European (if national data are not available) studies were prioritized. All data used in this study were discussed and validated, during an online meeting, by a Spanish expert group composed of two medical oncologists specialised in BC and a hospital pharmacist during an online meeting. In those cases where there was a disparity of opinions, a discussion was held among the experts until a consensus was reached.

Efficacy

All clinical data were extracted from the OlympiA RCT [13] and OlympiaAD RCT [24, 25]. Following the recommendations on multistate modeling described by the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) and by Putter et al. [26, 27], statistical distributions were used to simulate long-term patient survival, which were fitted to the patient-level data from the OlympiA RCT [13]. The best-fitting distribution (exponential, Weibull, log-normal, log-logistic, Gompertz, and generalized gamma), using the Akaike information criterion [28], was determined. The IDFS (Fig. S1), survival curve for nmBC (Fig. S2), and OS curves for the lmBC (Fig. S4) were simulated through a log-normal distribution, while the OS for nmBC and emBC (Figs. S3 and S4) were simulated using an exponential. IDFS was modeled using data from the intention-to-treat (ITT) population in both the HR+ and TN patient subgroups as no differences were observed between the ITT population and the hormone receptor status in the OlympiA RCT, and neither was there heterogeneity in treatment effect across subgroups [13]. Furthermore, the simulations were performed using patient-level data from the second interim analysis of the OlympiA RCT [13].

The mortality rate in patients who occupied the IDFS state was estimated using the mortality rate of the general Spanish population [29] adjusted by age and sex using the OlympiA data and applying the gBRCAm excess mortality risk [13]. Excess mortality risk was modeled using a mortality hazard ratio of 1.46, based on excess mortality from other diseases in women younger than 50 years old and who are gBRCAm carriers [30]. In addition, according to the local expert validation and in line with previous studies, any patient who remained disease-free at 5 years for TN and 20 years for HR+ was considered a long-term survivor and was not expected to experience recurrence of disease. These patients remained in the disease-free state until death from other causes [18, 31, 32].

Resource Use

The model included the following healthcare resources: treatments (surgery, radiotherapy, and pharmacological treatment) corresponding to each category of patients included in the model (nmBC, early and late mBC), adverse events, resources used for monitoring patients, medical tests, consultations, and palliative care. Only high-frequency severe adverse events (grades 3 and 4) were considered (Table S2). Finally, information regarding medical tests and consultations were obtained from Spanish clinical practice guidelines (Table S3) and validated by a group of experts to represent the real-life current patient management in Spain.

Costs

All costs were expressed in 2023 euros and were updated using the consumer price index (CPI) for medicine [33]. Drugs costs were obtained from the Spanish database Botplus (Table S4), and the unit costs of administration, visits, and medical tests reflected the median value of the unit costs for Spanish Autonomous Communities.

List prices were extracted for each drug and discounted by the Royal Decree Law (RDL) 8/2010 [34]. The monthly cost of olaparib for the treatment of patients with early BC after (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy was estimated considering a daily dose of 600 mg for 30.4 days [35]. Distribution in first- and second-line treatments for metastatic and nmBC was considered as well to reflect the current clinical practice in Spain (Table S5).

Unit costs of administration, oncology visits, and medical tests reflected the median value of the unit costs for all Autonomous Communities in Spain (Table S3). Administration costs were also calculated, using the administration cost per minute in day hospital (€0.57) and the duration of administration of each intravenous drug, adding an additional half hour for preparation and observation of the patient (Table S6). The percentage of patients that need surgery or radiotherapy is presented in Table S7. Surgery and radiotherapy costs, collected from the literature, were €4674.21 and €7817.14, respectively [36]. Patients treated with olaparib require a monthly blood test that costs €4.49. Visits and medical tests costs are summarized in Table S3. Table S2 presents the adverse events frequency, their duration, and associated costs. No costs were attributed to lymphocytopenia as no source indicating this cost was found. Palliative care cost per patient (€3697.39) was obtained from a previously published study in Spain [37].

Utilities

Health state utilities values can be seen in Table S8. Utility related to the IDFS state was estimated from the EORTC QLQ-C30 of the OlympiA RCT to EQ-5D, by mapping using a published algorithm in patients with BC [38]. The utilities for nmBC and mBC (early and late) were obtained from previous studies based on the EQ-5D score [13, 38–41]. Disutility associated with adverse events were also considered (Table S8) [42–45]. In the absence of published studies quantifying the loss of quality of life due to these adverse events, no disutility was associated with injection site reactions, irregular menstruation, and lymphocytopenia.

Sensitivity Analysis

To demonstrate the robustness of the model, both deterministic sensitivity analysis (DSA) and probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) were conducted. In addition, a scenario analysis was also performed.

The DSA included the most sensitive parameters [age TN patients (standard error [SE] 0.258); age HR+ patients (SE 0.545); weight (SE 0.384); resource use and adverse events costs (±10%); frequency of adverse events (±10%) and utilities: invasive disease-free survival (SE 0.002), nonmetastatic breast cancer (SE 0.086); early metastatic breast cancer (0.072) and late metastatic breast cancer (0.054)], and the results were represented by a tornado graph. PSA included 1000 simulations, whether this number of simulations was sufficient was checked by means of a convergence analysis (Fig. S5). Parameters used for the PSA are presented in Table S9. The PSA results were shown using the cost-effectiveness planes and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEAC).

Finally, the scenario analyses included the discount rate (according to the Spanish recommendations, varied to 0% and 5% [46]), the time horizon (30, 40, and 50 years), the time in which it is considered a late recurrence (TN: 1 and 3 years and HR+: 4 and 6 years), the time in which it is considered a long-term survivor (varies by ±20%; TN: 4 and 6 years; HR+: 16 and 24 years), utilities (different methods for estimating utility by health status: Longworth mapping [47], Paracha et al., 2016 (0.857) [39] and Lidgren et al., 2007 (0.779) [48]), and different statistical distributions were used to simulate long-term patient survival (exponential, Weibull, log-normal, log-logistic, Gompertz, and generalized gamma).

Results

Invasive Disease-Free and Overall Survival

Treating HR+ patients with olaparib and endocrine therapy could be associated with a longer IDFS compared with those in the SoC “watch and wait and endocrine therapy” arm with a 1.65-year increase in IDFS (16.06 years versus 14.42 years). In TN, it could also be associated with longer IDFS in the olaparib arm, resulting in a 1.84-year increase in IDFS (19.11 years versus 17.27 years) compared with the “watch-and-wait” arm, which represent SoC in these patients.

Additionally, the model could predict an improvement in OS in both HR+ and TN groups treated with olaparib. The OS in this simulation was 17.03 years for HR+ patients and 19.63 years for TN patients treated with olaparib, compared with 15.69 and 18.08 years for the SoC arms (Table 1). Life years gained (LYG) could range from 0.92 to 2.93 for HR+ patients and from 1.1 to 2.34 for TN patients, varying the efficacy data from the OlympiA RCT across the 95% confidence interval (CI). Quality-Adjusted Life Years

Olaparib treatment could improve patients’ QALYs by 1.14 in the HR+ group (13.70 versus 12.56) and by 1.28 in the TN group (15.75 versus 14.47), which would represent a relative improvement of 9.1% and 8.9%, respectively (Table 1). Considering the 95% CI for efficacy from OlympiA RCT, QALY could range from 0.75 to 2.46 in the HR+ group and from 0.89 to 1.95 in the TN group.

Costs

Costs results are presented in Table 1. Treatment of HR+ patients with olaparib could lead to higher total costs (€109,003.61) when compared with the SoC group (€64,730.96), mainly due to the cost of olaparib. However, the use of olaparib could involve important cost reductions for HR+ patients occupying the emBC states (€9515.54) as it allows savings in expenses associated with drugs administration costs, surgery, radiotherapy, and disease management. Similar results were observed for TN patients. Treating this group with olaparib could increase the total cost by €50,164.29 compared with the SoC group but reduce the costs associated with drug administration costs, surgery, radiotherapy, and disease management for patients found in the metastatic phases (€1711,87). Olaparib could also generate savings in the terminal phase as costs were reduced by €226.76 for the HR+ BC group and €267.14 for the TN group.

Incremental Cost-Effectiveness

The results of our study showed that treatment with olaparib in HR+ could imply an incremental cost of €44,273 and an increase of 1.34 LYG. Therefore, the ICER is €32,952 per LYG and could vary from €9521 to €57,208, according to the 95% CI of the OlympiA RCT efficacy. Furthermore, this treatment could improve QALYs by 1.14 compared with SoC, resulting in an ICUR of €38,839/QALY (Table 1). ICUR could be as low as €11,344/QALY and as high as €70,507/QALY when varying the OlympiA RCT efficacy across the 95% CI.

The ICER for TN patients could be €32,351/LYG, with an incremental cost of €50,164 and an increase of 1.55 LYG in the treatment arm and could vary from €20,948 to €46,575, according to the 95% CI of the OlympiA RCT efficacy. Likewise, olaparib treatment could improve QALYs by 1.28 compared with SoC and represent an ICUR of €39,084/QALY (Table 1). ICUR could be as low as €25,164/QALY and as high as €57,298/QALY when varying the OlympiA RCT efficacy across the 95% CI.

The results of the DSA are shown in Fig. S6. All DSA were demonstrated consistency with the base case analysis. For both the HR+ and TN analyses, the largest difference compared with the base case ICER is close to €1000/QALY (utilities parameters), demonstrating the robustness of the results.

Discussion

This is the first study to estimate the CE of olaparib in the adjuvant treatment of early gBRCAm HER2neg BC with high risk of recurrence from the Spanish healthcare system perspective. Results showed that treating with olaparib could be cost-effective (with willingness-to-pay thresholds between €25,000 and €60,000) and prevents recurrence among patients with early gBRCAm HER2neg BC with high risk of recurrence, improving their OS and QALYs compared with patients treated with SoC alone.

Treating patients with olaparib generates higher pharmacological costs than patients treated in the current SoC: watch and wait with or without endocrine therapy, mainly due to the drug acquisition cost. However, by reducing the risk of disease recurrence, olaparib has the potential to generate important savings that are later associated with treating patients in the metastatic phase (drug administration costs, surgery, radiotherapy, and disease management). This should be an important consideration for the Spanish healthcare system.

Olaparib is also cost effective in early BC in other countries. In the USA, the resulting ICUR was approximately $111,000 per QALY gained, with a willingness-to-pay threshold of $150,000 per QALY [49]. In Sweden, the resulting ICUR was SEK385,183 per QALY gained for olaparib versus watch and wait, falling below the threshold (SEK1,000,000) [50].

These results are also in line with other adjuvant and neoadjuvant treatments for early BC [51–53]. In Italy, abemaciclib in combination with adjuvant endocrine therapy has been shown to be cost-effective versus endocrine therapy alone, with an ICUR of €22,651 per QALY gained [51]. Similar results have been observed with this treatment in the UK, with a ratio of £17,810 per QALY [53]. Compared with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, pembrolizumab in combination with chemotherapy as a neoadjuvant treatment and continued as a single-agent adjuvant treatment after surgery is considered a cost-effective option for TN in the Switzerland ($27,285 per QALY) [52].

Methodological Limitations

Our study has some limitations. Firstly, due to the lack of real-world Spanish patients’ data, our simulation is based on the OlympiA RCT. Secondly, in the absence of data for the Spanish population, utilities were assumed for the UK population. However, as sensitivity analyses have shown, although the variation in utilities was the parameter that most influences the results, it did not significantly impact the ratios. Third, our study only includes severe adverse events registered in the OlympiA RCT and discards those of grade 1 and 2, which might underestimate the total burden of the adverse events in terms of costs and impact on health status.

Furthermore, our study might underestimate the benefits of olaparib as indirect costs were not considered for this study. As gBRCAm is often associated with young working-age women, adjuvant treatment with olaparib could maximize their possibilities to remain active and working.

Despite its limitations, the main contribution of this study is the economic evaluation of olaparib for the adjuvant treatment of patients with early gBRCAm HER2neg BC with high risk of recurrence in Spain.

Conclusions

Our results support the recommendation of the most up-to-date clinical guidelines [15] and the ESMO-MCBS score [16] about the use of olaparib as the only treatment for early-stage, high-risk gBRCAm HER2neg BC with an ICUR of €38,839/QALY and €39,084/QALY, in the HR+ and TN subgroup, respectively.

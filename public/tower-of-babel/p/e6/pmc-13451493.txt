Tuning the structural and physical properties of Hf5Si3 intermetallic compound under pressure: insights for next-generation high-temperature technology
Authors: Md. Emon Hassan, Md. Riad Khan, Zihad Hossain, Md. Khairul Alam, Mohammad Abdur Rashid, Md. Lokman Ali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The growing demand for multifunction compound that can withstand extreme environment has stimulated interest in refractory intermetallic silicide. In this study, a detailed investigation of the structural, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties of the transition metal silicide Hf5Si3 under pressure is carried out using density functional theory (DFT) within the framework of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA-PBEsol), as implemented in WIEN2k. Volume optimization along with negative formation energy and cohesive energy are examined that indicate structurally and thermodynamically stability of intermetallic compound Hf5Si3, respectively. Mechanical property analysis demonstrates high elastic moduli, mechanical anisotropy, covalent bonding and brittleness nature, with Poisson’s ratio (ν < 0.26), Pugh’s ratio (B/G < 1.75) and negative Cauchy pressure. Study of melting temperature shows high melting point (2230 ± 300K) at 15 GPa reflect thermal stability under extreme temperature. Debye temperature and elastic wave velocities increase with pressure reflects enhanced interatomic bonding strength, signifying a progressively stiffer and less compressible lattice. Optically Hf5Si3 exhibit strong absorption, high refractive index and large optical conductivity with optical anisotropy, highlighting potential for photonic and plasmonic study. Electronic band structure corresponding electron density of state confirm metallic nature of Hf5Si3. Finally, the charge density map analysis confirms the covalent bonding with a substantial metallic contribution of Hf5Si3. However, the comprehensive findings highlight the multifunctional nature of Hf5Si3 and its potential for diverse applications in extreme-environment structure, and optoelectronic.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-52144-z.

Introduction

Achieving future performance goals for gas turbine engines requires improvements in propulsive efficiency and high-temperature thermal stability1. Silicon-based ceramics with non-oxide structured, including silicon carbide SiC, silicon nitride (Si3N4), and SiC, matrix composites exhibit significant potential for gas turbine engines application due to their superior mechanical strength, creep resistance, and thermal stability at elevated temperatures2,3. Remarkably, silicon’s well-matched thermal expansion coefficient (CTE) (2.6–3.5 × 10⁻6 °C⁻1)4 relative to matched with SiC (4.5 × 10⁻6 °C⁻1)4, as well as owing to its low elastic modulus and excellent bonding capability, makes it commonly utilized as an interfacial layer5–7. Nonetheless, Si-based ceramic materials and their composites tend to undergo hydrothermal reactions in the engine operating environments, leading to the formation of volatile Si(OH)4 species, which contributes to performance degradation over time8,9. Environmental-barrier-coatings (EBCs) are essential for safeguarding silicon-based ceramics and their composites against high temperatures resistance, chemical corrosive environments, and high-velocity gas flow10. In high-temperature oxidation atmospheres containing water vapour, silicon dioxide (SiO₂), undergoes hydrolysis to form volatile ortho-silicic acid vapour Si(OH)4, which leads to accelerated material degradation(coating’s failure)11,12.

In the field of modern physics and materials science, the investigation into compounds that exhibit both structural robustness and high-temperature stability has brought hafnium silicide (Hf5Si3) into the spotlight. This transition metal silicide has been broadly investigated for their suitability to construct high temperature thermally stable structural materials. It has not only high melting point more than 2100 °C2 but also excellent thermal conductivity, and remarkable resistance to oxidation and corrosion13–15. These properties make Hf5Si3 a promising candidate for applications in aerospace and power-generation industries, including turbine engine components, thermal protection systems and environmental barrier coatings, where materials must withstand extreme temperatures and harsh environments16–18.

In the previous work, experimentally investigate the existing of Hf5Si3 system, where high-purity alloys are examined by Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Electron Microprobe Analysis (EMPA), and Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD), confirming the formation of Hf5Si3 phase in Hf-Si alloys shows Mn5Si3-type (hP16, P63/mmc)19. Additionally, investigate the mechanical and thermal characteristics of the Hf-Si system and Hf-Si phase diagram2,15. Furthermore, a revised version of the Hf-Si binary phase diagram was constructed, in this study reveal that the Hf5Si3 phase as a stable component of the system20.

However, despite earlier investigations, a comprehensive first-principles study addressing the pressure-dependent evolution of both structural and physical properties of Hf5Si3 remains lacking. In particular, a unified investigation that systematically examines its intrinsic behaviour alongside its response to applied hydrostatic pressure-covering structural stability, mechanical properties, electronic structure, and optical characteristics has not been reported to date.

In this work, we address this gap by performing a detailed first-principles study of Hf5Si3 under hydrostatic pressure up to 15 GPa. The aim is to elucidate the pressure-induced modifications in its structural, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties, and to establish clear correlations between bonding characteristics, electronic structure evolution, and anisotropic behaviour. This comprehensive analysis provides deeper physical insight into the material’s response under extreme conditions and highlights its potential for high-temperature and advanced functional applications.

Computational method

All first-principles calculations in the present study were performed using the WIEN2k21 package, which is based on the full-potential linearized augmented plane-wave (FP-LAPW)22 algorithm and operates within the framework of density functional theory (DFT). The exchangecorrelation effects were treated using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof revised functional for solids (PBEsol)23 within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). For the self-consistent field (SCF) calculations and structural optimization, the muffin-tin radii (RMT) were set to 2.45 a.u. for Hf atoms (Hf1 and Hf2) and 2.08 a.u. for Si atoms. The plane-wave cutoff parameter (RKmax) was fixed at 8. The maximum angular momentum inside the atomic spheres (Lmax) was set to 10, while the charge density Fourier expansion parameter (Gmax) was taken as 12. Brillouin zone integrations were performed using a uniform k-point mesh generated according to the Monkhorst–Pack24 scheme. A (6 × 6 × 8) k-point grid was employed during structural optimization to ensure accurate sampling of the Brillouin zone and reliable convergence of total energy and electronic properties. The convergence thresholds were carefully set to ensure accuracy with an energy convergence criterion of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times {10}^{-5}$$\end{document} Ry, a charge convergence of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times {10}^{-4}$$\end{document} e, a force convergence of 0.1 mRy and a q-limit of 0.01. The denser K-point mesh of 3000 points in the irreducible Brillouin zone was used to calculate optical characteristics, band structure and density of states (DOS) for highly accurate findings. The mechanical properties were obtained using stress-strain analysis implemented by IRelast package25 through WIEN2k simulation. In order to attain stable convergence across varying applied pressures, a mixing factor of 0.1 was implemented for pressures at 0 GPa and 5 GPa, whereas a reduced mixing factor of 0.05 was utilized for pressures at 10 GPa and 15 GPa. The XCrySDen26 package was utilized for the visualization and analysis of structural data.

Mechanical properties and stability of Hf5Si3

Elastic constants of materials are very crucial parameters in solid state physics due to serving a direct relationship between microscopic interatomic interaction and macroscopic mechanical characteristics. To gather knowledge about the structural stability, ductility, brittleness, stiffness, mechanical anisotropy of a compound and propagation of elastics waves along with normal mode oscillations, elastic constants study are indispensable. Moreover, understanding the elastic constants is very essential for study the acoustic and thermodynamics property of the relevant materials.

Cauchy pressure provides deep insight into the understanding atomic bonding nature and serves as a reliable indicator about ductile and brittle behaviour of materials32. For hexagonal system, two important Cauchy pressure relation are C13-C44 and C12-C6633 under different pressure, as illustrated in Fig. 5b. The positive outcome of C13-C44 and C12-C66 indicate the material dominated by the metallics bonding, on the other hand the negative outcome of Cauchy relation suggests the covalent and angular bonding of the material. From the Fig. 5b, it is clearly noticed the Cauchy pressure for both relation is negative value at 0 GPa pressure and shift more negative value with increasing pressure, reflecting the covalent bonding nature of Hf5Si3 compound. The Cauchy relations is also used to differentiate the material ductile and brittle response and zero value indicate the ductile and brittle transition line (critical value), where positive value corresponds the ductile behaviour and negative value corresponds the brittle behaviour. In our calculated result shows the negative value of C13-C44 and C12-C66, suggesting the material shows brittle behaviour. As the pressure increase, this of Cauchy relation value becomes more negative implies that Hf5Si3 shows more brittle response (more stiffness) under increasing of pressure.

Mechanical anisotropy

Crystal often exhibits anisotropic nature, displaying varying physical and chemical response along different crystallographic directions. In addition, the lattice distortions and microcracks in a material are generally associated with the elastic anisotropy. So, study of elastic anisotropy is very crucial.

External pressure is a powerful tool to tune the structural, electronic, and mechanical behaviour of crystalline materials37,38. By reducing interatomic distances and modifying bond geometries, pressure can significantly alter the balance between ionic and covalent interactions, leading to significant changes in elastic properties and stability39. Interstitial silicides like Hf5Si3 are important intermetallic compounds in high-stress environments due to their strong covalent-metallic bonding, high melting points, and good stiffness.

With increasing pressure, however, the surfaces of B and E become elongated along the [001] axis compared to the [100] and [010] directions, reflecting enhanced resistance to compression and uniaxial deformation in that direction38. Similar trends have been reported for Bi2Se337 and Bi2Te339, where bond shortening under compression strengthens covalent interactions along specific crystallographic orientations, leading to anisotropic stiffening. The shear modulus surfaces show increasing deformation from spherical to ellipsoidal with pressure. This indicates that resistance to shear is highly direction-dependent at higher pressures42. The pressure-induced anisotropy in G arises from unequal compression of Hf-Hf metallic bonds and Hf-Si covalent bonds, which change the sliding resistance between atomic planes and thus becomes direction-dependent, amplifying anisotropy. The absorption spectrum provides important insight about the electronic band diagram, forbidden gap (band gap) energy, and defect states in the material.

Conclusion

In this study, first-principles calculations reveal that Hf5Si3 is a thermodynamically, mechanically and thermal stable metallic compound whose structural, mechanical, electronic, and optical properties are significantly enhanced under hydrostatic pressure up to 15 GPa. Pressure strengthens bonding interactions, leading to increased stiffness and mechanical robustness, while maintaining a predominantly covalent and brittle character. The increase in Debye temperature and elastic wave velocities indicates enhanced vibrational stability, while the high melting temperature (2230 ± 300K) and hardness further confirm its rigidity, all of which improve with increasing pressure. The material exhibits notable optical anisotropy and pressure-dependent dielectric response, reflecting strong coupling between electronic structure and optical behaviour. These combined features highlight Hf5Si3 as a promising candidate for high-temperature and extreme-environment applications, including aerospace components, thermal barrier coatings, and advanced optoelectronic and plasmonic systems. The pronounced optical anisotropy and expected collective electronic response (plasma frequency,\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega}_{p}=12 eV$$\end{document}) in the ultraviolet range further suggest potential applications in shielding functionalities within harsh conditions, where conventional materials can suffer from oxidation and mechanical instability.

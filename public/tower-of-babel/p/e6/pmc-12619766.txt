A novel ubiquitination-based molecular signature predicts prognosis in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Yubing Li, Shuangping Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL) exhibits significant biological heterogeneity, leading to diverse clinical outcomes. Ubiquitination, a post-translational modification process, plays a crucial regulatory role in tumor development and progression. The prognostic profile of ubiquitinated genes in DLBCL needs to be explored. We analyzed three datasets (GSE181063, GSE56315, and GSE10846) to identify ubiquitination-related survival-associated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in DLBCL. We constructed a ubiquitination-based prognostic signature and calculated risk scores. Using R software, we investigated relationships between the identified DEGs, high- and low-risk groups, immune microenvironment, drug sensitivity, and single-cell composition. Three key ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs were identified: Cell Division Cycle 34 (CDC34), Fizzy-related protein homolog 1 (FZR1), and OTU Deubiquitinase with Linear Linkage Specificity (OTULIN). Elevated expression of CDC34 and FZR1, coupled with low expression of OTULIN, correlated with poor prognosis in DLBCL. These genes correlation with endocytosis-related mechanisms, T-cell, and drugs sensitive. Significant differences in immune scores and concentration for Boehringer Ingelheim compound 2536 and Osimertinib were observed between high- and low-risk groups. This study establishes a novel ubiquitination-based prognostic signature for DLBCL, offering insights into potential therapeutic targets and strategies for personalized treatment approaches.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06668-5.Keywords: Diffuse large B-Cell lymphoma, Ubiquitination, Molecular signature, Prognosis

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most prevalent form of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, accounting for approximately 30–35% of all cases [1]. This aggressive B-cell malignancy is characterized by the rapid proliferation of abnormally large B lymphocytes [2]. DLBCL primarily affects older adults, with a median age at diagnosis of 64 years and a slightly higher incidence in males [3]. Based on genetic and molecular characteristics, DLBCL can be further classified into several subtypes, including the germinal center B-cell (GCB) and activated B-cell (ABC) subtypes [4].

The standard first-line treatment for DLBCL typically involves a combination of chemotherapy, immunotherapy (e.g., rituximab), and, in some cases, radiation therapy [5]. For patients who relapse or fail to respond to initial therapy, second-line treatments may include targeted therapies [6]. Notably, there are significant differences in clinical prognosis between DLBCL subtypes, with GCB subtypes generally exhibiting better outcomes than ABC subtypes [7–9]. This disparity in overall survival may be attributed to the distinct genetic and molecular characteristics of these subtypes [10].

Ubiquitination, a post-translational modification process involving the covalent attachment of ubiquitin to target proteins, plays a crucial role in various cellular processes, including cell cycle control, DNA repair, and protein quality control [11, 12]. Recent studies have highlighted the importance of ubiquitination in cancer progression and treatment resistance [13–17]. Aberrant ubiquitination and degradation of tumor suppressor proteins can promote cancer growth and metastasis [18]. Moreover, the ubiquitin-proteasome system has emerged as a potential therapeutic target for DLBCL. For instance, Zhang et al. demonstrated that miR-17∼92 is involved in NF-κB activation by targeting ubiquitin-editing proteins to mediate RIP1 complex polyubiquitination [19]. Similarly, Wang et al. found that SNHG20 enhances DLBCL cell proliferation and apoptosis resistance through USP14-mediated deubiquitination of β-catenin [20].

Bioinformatics analysis has become an invaluable tool in cancer research, facilitating the discovery of novel biomarkers and signaling pathways involved in cancer progression and treatment response [21–23]. However, bioinformatics studies focusing on ubiquitination in DLBCL are relatively scarce. While some research has touched upon related topics, such as the LINC00963 regulation of the miR-320a/XBP1 axis in DLBCL [24] and the identification of FAT4 mutations as a positive prognostic biomarker [25], comprehensive analyses of ubiquitination-related mechanisms in DLBCL using bioinformatics approaches remain limited. Although Raman spectroscopy has been used to analyze plasma from patients with DLBCL, this technique does not directly provide insights into the ubiquitination pathway [26].

To address this gap in knowledge, our study aims to analyze the prognostic value and functional mechanisms of survival-related and ubiquitination-related differential genes in DLBCL using advanced bioinformatics methods. We employ Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator COX Regression (LASSO COX) regression and cluster analysis to identify the most promising candidate genes. Furthermore, we integrate enrichment analysis, prognostic feature construction, immune microenvironment assessment, drug sensitivity analysis, and single-cell sequencing techniques to elucidate the role of survival-related ubiquitination genes in DLBCL and explore their therapeutic potential. This comprehensive approach will provide new insights into the role of ubiquitination in DLBCL pathogenesis and treatment, potentially uncovering novel therapeutic strategies targeting the ubiquitination pathway in this aggressive lymphoma.

Materials and methods

Data collection

DLBCL gene expression profiles (GSE181063, GSE56315, and GSE10846) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database, comprising 1,800 DLBCL samples (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE10846 served as the training set, GSE181063 as the validation set for subsequent analyses, its use is primarily in independent testing and external validation and GSE56315 for differential gene screening. Single-cell data were acquired from the heiDATA database (https://heidata.uni-heidelberg.de/dataset.xhtml? persistentId=doi:10.11588/data/VRJUNV).

Validation of ubiquitination-related genes and differential gene screening

Samples from the GSE56315 dataset were categorized into tumor and normal tissue groups. DEGs were identified using the limma package (criteria: Fold Change > 2, FDR < 0.05). The optimal cut-off value for each DEG was determined using the surve_cutpoint function from the “survminer” package in R, stratifying samples into high and low expression groups. Ubiquitination-related genes associated with survival were selected through univariate Cox regression.

LASSO Cox survival analysis

The LASSO analysis was performed using the “glmnet” package to identify the most valuable genes for predicting DLBCL patient survival. Ten-fold cross-validation was employed to determine the optimal penalty parameter. Kaplan-Meier curves were generated and log-rank tests conducted using the “survminer” package.

Cluster analysis

To explore the functional and prognostic significance of ubiquitination-related genes in DLBCL, consensus clustering was performed using the “ConsensusClusterPlus” package in R. Parameters included 1,000 repetitions and k = 10. The analysis incorporated three selected ubiquitination-related genes. Kaplan-Meier curves were plotted to confirm the prognostic value of the cluster classifications.

Construction and validation of prognostic signature

A prognostic signature based on ubiquitination-related genes was constructed using multivariate Cox regression coefficients. Risk scores were calculated using the formula: β1 * Exp1 + β2 * Exp2 + βi * Expi, where β represents the coefficient from multivariate Cox regression and Exp denotes gene expression level. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk subgroups based on the median risk score. Kaplan-Meier curves were plotted and log-rank tests performed.

Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis

Genes with correlation coefficients |R| >0.45 were considered closely associated with the risk score. GO functional and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted using clusterProfiler. Thresholds were set at FDR < 0.2 and P < 0.05.

Assessment of immune microenvironment composition

The CIBERSORT package in R was utilized to analyze immune cell infiltration in the GSE10846 dataset, calculating the relative abundance of infiltrating immune cells. Differences in immune cell infiltration between high-risk and low-risk DLBCL tissues were examined using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Spearman correlations between ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs and infiltrating immune cells were analyzed. Tumor purity was visualized using ggplot2 and pivot table packages in R.

Drug sensitivity analysis

The oncoPredict package in R was employed to calculate the sensitivity of 198 drugs in DLBCL. Drugs with significant differences in half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) between high-risk and low-risk groups were identified and subjected to correlation analysis.

Single-cell analysis

Single-cell analysis was performed using the Seurat package in R. Cells expressing fewer than 200 or more than 6,000 genes were filtered out, and the remaining expressed genes were log-normalized. Dimensionality reduction was achieved using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) with 10 principal components and a resolution of 0.5. Cells were clustered into discrete subgroups using the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions with dimensions of 10 and a resolution of 0.5. Cell types were annotated using the SingleR package and manual annotation, resulting in seven major cell types. Finally, the distribution of specific genes across DLBCL cell types was determined.

Statistical analysis

All data analyses and visualizations were performed using R software. Differences between two groups were compared using the Wilcoxon test, while comparisons among three or more groups employed the Kruskal-Wallis test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

DEG screening

The study workflow is illustrated in Fig. 1. A total of 20,544 differentially expressed genes were identified in the GSE56315 dataset. Among these, 1,394 genes were found to be associated with survival in the GSE10846 and GSE181063 datasets, with 1,350 being both differentially expressed and survival-related. Of the differentially expressed genes, 83 were related to ubiquitination. The intersection of ubiquitination-related and survival-associated differentially expressed genes yielded only 7 candidates (Fig. 2A and B). As shown in Fig. 2C and D, the optimal lambda value was determined to be 4. At this L1 norm, only 3 genes exhibited non-zero coefficients. Consequently, in GSE10846 dataset, LASSO Cox survival analysis identified 3 genes (CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN) from the 7 ubiquitination-survival-related DEGs that were significantly associated with survival outcomes in DLBCL patients. Based on GSE181063 dataset, these 3 genes showed significant differential expression between healthy individuals and DLBCL patients (Supplementary Fig. 1). In DLBCL patients, lower expression of CDC34 and FZR1 correlated with improved survival outcomes, while higher expression of OTULIN was associated with poorer survival. These trends were consistently observed in both the GSE10846 and GSE181063 datasets (Fig. 3A and B).

Cluster analysis of ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs

To further explore the functional significance and prognostic value of the identified ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs (CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN) in DLBCL, we performed consensus clustering analysis (GSE10846 dataset). The results indicated that as the number of clusters (k) increased, the consensus matrix became more stratified, with k = 2 showing the most distinct separation (Fig. 4A and D). UMAP analysis revealed that while some patient overlap persisted, a general bifurcation into two clusters was evident at k = 2. However, increasing k led to substantial overlap in both t-SNE1 and t-SNE2 dimensions (Fig. 4E and H). Furthermore, consensus clustering analysis demonstrated that at k = 2, approximately 40% of object pairs exhibited a consistency index less than or equal to 0.2. The clustering algorithm produced the most consistent solution at k = 2. Although the area under the CDF curve continued to decrease with increasing k, the rate of decrease diminished. Overall, k = 2 emerged as the optimal number of clusters, yielding the most consistent clustering results (Fig. 5A and B). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of the two patient groups identified by consensus clustering revealed significantly poorer prognosis for patients in Group 1 compared to those in Group 2 (Fig. 5C). Notably, the expression levels of CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN differed significantly between these two patient clusters (Fig. 5D).

Construction and validation of a prognostic signature based on ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs

Based on GSE10846 dataset, multivariate regression analysis revealed that several clinicopathological factors were associated with patient prognosis, with the risk score demonstrating the highest hazard ratio (Fig. 6A). The risk score was calculated using the following formula: 3.4392439 × CDC34 + 0.1977902 × FZR1–4.3104360 × OTULIN. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated significantly worse outcomes for patients in the high-risk group compared to those in the low-risk group (Fig. 6B). Risk scores varied significantly across different clinical subgroups, particularly with respect to molecular subtype and chemotherapy regimen (Fig. 6C). Cox regression analysis of the three ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs (CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN) consistently showed hazard ratios greater than 1 for CDC34 and FZR1 in both the GSE10846 and GSE181063 datasets (Supplementary Fig. 2). Furthermore, the stratification into high-risk and low-risk groups was validated in the GSE181063 dataset, where the high-risk group exhibited significantly worse survival outcomes compared to the low-risk group (Supplementary Fig. 3). To more fully assess the performance and predictive ability of the prognostic signature, we constructed a nomogram and conducted receiver operating characteristic curve analysis in GSE10846 and GSE181063. The results indicated that when the total points value was between 140 and 160, the probability of patients’ overall survival being less than 5 years, 3 years, and 1 year was less than 50%, 45%, and 30%, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 4). Both datasets showed that the area under the curve values for 3-year survival were greater than 0.6 (Supplementary Figs. 5, 6).

Functional and pathway enrichment analysis of ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs

GO enrichment analysis of the ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs closely linked to the prognostic risk score revealed a predominant association with the proteasome-mediated ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolic process (GSE10846 dataset) (Fig. 7A and B). This finding further corroborates the established relationship between CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN and ubiquitination in DLBCL. KEGG pathway analysis highlighted enrichment in endocytosis-related processes (Fig. 7C and D). These results suggest that the identified ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs may modulate ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation through endocytic pathways.

Evaluation of the immune microenvironment in relation to ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs

Based on GSE10846 dataset, significant differences in the proportions of eosinophils, gamma delta T cells, activated memory Cluster of Differentiation 4 (CD4+) T cells, Cluster of Differentiation 8 (CD8+) T cells, and M2 macrophages were observed between high-risk and low-risk patient groups (Fig. 8A). While tumor purity showed negative correlations with both stromal and immune scores in the tumor microenvironment, only the immune score exhibited a significant difference between high-risk and low-risk groups (Fig. 8B). Correlation analysis between CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN expression and infiltrating immune cell populations revealed strong negative correlations between CDC34 and FZR1 with gamma delta T cells. All three genes showed positive correlations with tumor purity and negative correlations with ESTIMATE score and stromal score (Fig. 8C and D).

Drug sensitivity analysis in relation to ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs

Based on GSE10846 dataset, among 198 drugs evaluated for DLBCL treatment, six compounds (BI-2536, BMS-536924, BMS-754807, Daporinad, Dasatinib, and Osimertinib) demonstrated significant differences in IC50 values between high-risk and low-risk groups. Notably, BI-2536 exhibited lower IC50 values in the low-risk group, while Osimertinib showed higher IC50 values in this group. Correlation analysis revealed positive associations between CDC34 and FZR1 expression and BI-2536 sensitivity, and negative associations with Osimertinib sensitivity (Fig. 9).

Single-cell analysis of ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs

Based on GSE10846 dataset, single-cell RNA sequencing analysis of DLBCL patient samples identified six distinct cell types: regulatory T cells, transit-amplifying cells, central memory CD4 + alpha-beta T cells, blood vessel endothelial cells, plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and monocytes (Fig. 10A). OTULIN expression was detected across multiple cell types, with the highest levels observed in transit-amplifying cells. CDC34 and FZR1 exhibited lower overall expression levels compared to OTULIN, with predominant expression in transit-amplifying cells (Fig. 10B and E).

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the prognostic significance of ubiquitination-related survival-associated DEGs in DLBCL. Our consensus cluster, Kaplan-Meier survival, and Cox regression analysis revealed that patients with poor prognosis exhibited higher expression of CDC34 and FZR1 genes, and lower expression of the OTULIN gene. GO analysis substantiated the involvement of CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN in the ubiquitination process.

CDC34, a ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme, plays a crucial role in the ubiquitination of various proteins and is essential for the G1 to S phase transition of the cell cycle [27–30]. Research has confirmed that CDC34 is associated with anti-tumor mechanisms, tumor progression, and prognosis [31–33]. Although CDC34 has been implicated in tumor progression, its role as a therapeutic target in DLBCL remains unconfirmed. Furthermore, the functional implications of ubiquitination in cancer biology are not yet fully understood. FZR1 is a critical regulator of the cell cycle, playing an essential role in meiosis and early embryonic development in both male and female mammals [34–36]. FZR1 is also involved in the progression and treatment of various tumors [37–39]. OTULIN is a linear deubiquitinase that specifically cleaves linear ubiquitin chains, playing a crucial role in various physiological processes [40–42]. OTULIN has been implicated in tumor resistance and the development of leukemia [43–45]. Therefore, further research on the ubiquitination process and the role of CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN in lymphoma, particularly DLBCL, holds significant promise. In ABC-DLBCL, ubiquitination enzyme may enhance the stability of critical oncoproteins, thereby promoting tumor cell survival [46]. In contrast, the ubiquitination regulatory network in GCB-DLBCL appears to predominantly sustain normal B-cell differentiation programs [47]. Therefore, comparative analysis of ubiquitination regulation across ABC/GCB subtypes will not only elucidate the molecular basis underlying their prognostic disparities but may also inform the development of novel targeted therapeutic strategies. Through multivariate Cox regression analysis, this study revealed that the risk score had a hazard ratio much greater than 1 (P < 0.01), while the Molecular_subtype had a hazard ratio less than 1. This suggests that the risk score exerted a greater influence on survival events compared to the Molecular_subtype. Furthermore, the significant differences observed in risk scores among distinct Molecular_subtype further underscored the prognostic utility of the three DEGs. MYC translocations constitute a critical prognostic determinant in DLBCL. Pharmacological inhibition of ubiquitination enzyme facilitates MYC proteasomal degradation, suggesting that targeting MYC-associated ubiquitination/deubiquitination processes represents a potential therapeutic strategy [48, 49]. Collectively, the interplay between MYC translocations and ubiquitination processes significantly influences DLBCL outcomes, and mechanistic investigation of this crosstalk may improve prognostic stratification and treatment approaches. Beyond established biological risk factors, approximately 25–30% of DLBCL patients exhibit concurrent MYC and BCL2 expression (dual-expression lymphoma) [50]. Furthermore, Kamran et al. demonstrated that ubiquitin-specific protease 37 (USP37) stabilizes MYC protein through deubiquitination [51]. While both double-hit status and aberrant ubiquitination are associated with poor outcomes in DLBCL, their precise relationship remains to be fully elucidated. The direct comparison between CDC34, FZR1, OTULIN and established biological risk factors in DLBCL remains undefined. Compared with these established biological risk factors, FZR1 demonstrates potential advantages in providing more direct cell cycle regulatory information, CDC34 exhibits mechanistic strength through modulating MYC protein stability, while OTULIN shows unique functional value in coordinating cell death execution and inflammatory regulation. Therefore, integrating ubiquitination-related genes into established biological risk factor frameworks may enhance prognostic stratification in DLBCL. This approach also provides novel mechanistic insights into ubiquitination-mediated oncogenesis. However, the detailed molecular data required for MYC translocations and MYC and DLBCL double expresser status (including FISH data for translocations, or reliable IHC/molecular data for double expressers) were not consistently available or reliably compiled for the entire cohort within the public GEO datasets used for our primary analysis. Therefore, this study could not incorporate these specific markers into the multivariate prognostic model alongside our risk score.

Ubiquitination is increasingly highlighted for its role in enhancing cancer resistance [52, 53]. Nan Zhou et al. demonstrated that inactivation of the ubiquitin ligase KLHL6 allows mutated NOTCH2 to evade ubiquitin-dependent degradation, thereby activating oncogenic RAS signaling and promoting chemoresistance in DLBCL [54]. Similarly, Dan Yang et al. found that CENPF overexpression leads to immune dysregulation in DLBCL cells by mediating protein deubiquitination in various immune signaling pathways, potentially facilitating tumor escape [55]. Moreover, PCLAF overexpression has been shown to mediate DLBCL resistance to Pevonedistat by hindering its ubiquitination and promoting DNA damage repair [56].

In the context of potential therapeutic interventions, we explored the relevance of two drugs, BI.2536 and Osimertinib, in DLBCL treatment. BI.2536, a PLK1 inhibitor, has shown promise in preclinical studies of DLBCL [57]. Our analysis revealed positive regulation of CDC34 and FZR1 by BI.2536, suggesting a potential mechanism for its antitumor effects. Conversely, Osimertinib, an EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor primarily used in non-small cell lung cancer, has been found to selectively induce CD34 + leukemia stem/progenitor apoptosis in EGFR-negative acute myeloid leukemia and chronic myeloid leukemia [58]. While its role in DLBCL remains to be fully elucidated, our findings suggest potential off-target effects that could be therapeutically exploited. Notably, we observed lower IC50 values for Osimertinib in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group, with the high-risk group exhibiting worse survival outcomes and prognosis. The negative correlation between Osimertinib and the gene abundance of CDC34 and FZR1 reaffirms that high expression of these genes leads to reduced sensitivity to Osimertinib, potentially contributing to adverse outcomes and prognosis.

Our study also identified a potential link between DLBCL ubiquitination-related genes and endocytosis, an important mechanism for cellular uptake of external substances that plays a key role in DLBCL therapy [59–61]. Ubiquitination serves as a crucial trigger signal for endocytosis, with E3 ubiquitin ligases typically mediating this process [62, 63]. Recent studies have found that Osimertinib involvement is associated with endocytosis [64]. Through KEGG pathway analysis of gene expression data, we hypothesized that the ubiquitination process involving CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN is related to endocytosis. This finding suggests that the ubiquitination process may influence DLBCL pathogenesis and treatment response by modulating endocytosis.

Interestingly, our analysis of tumor purity revealed an inverse correlation with stromal and immune scores, with a significant difference in immune scores between high-risk and low-risk patients. Given the poor survival outcomes in the high-risk group and the identification of CDC34 and FZR1 as high-risk genes in DLBCL, we investigated the relationship between ubiquitination-associated survival-related DEGs and the immune microenvironment. Our findings revealed strong correlations between T cell subsets and CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN. These observations are consistent with previous studies highlighting the roles of these genes in immune cell function and development [65–72]. Furthermore, single-cell sequencing data revealed the presence of CDC34, FZR1, and OTULIN in central memory CD4-positive α-β T cells, suggesting complex interactions between ubiquitination-related genes and the immune microenvironment in DLBCL.

In conclusion, our results provide novel insights into the role of ubiquitination-related genes in DLBCL prognosis and their intricate relationship with the tumor immune microenvironment. The identification of CDC34, FZR1, and OTU-LIN as potential prognostic markers and therapeutic targets opens new avenues for personalized medicine in DLBCL management. Nevertheless, it is important to acknowledge that our investigation primarily relied on data analysis from existing online repositories. To bolster our findings, additional experimental validation and prospective clinical studies are essential. Future studies should focus on validating these findings in larger cohorts and exploring the functional mechanisms underlying the observed associations. Additionally, investigating the potential to target these genes or their associated pathways, possibly in combination with immunomodulatory therapies, could provide valuable insights for improving DLBCL treatment strategies. Finally, collecting consistently available established biological risk factors, such as MYC translocations and MYC and DLBCL double expresser status, for multivariate analysis would further emphasize the value of the ubiquitination-based prognostic signature in predicting prognosis.

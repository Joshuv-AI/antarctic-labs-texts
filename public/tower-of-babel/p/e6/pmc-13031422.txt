A multi-method integrated weighting framework for biosafety risk assessment of infectious substances
Authors: Fengze Wu, Chen Li, Yong Bian, Jin Li, Jie Tian, Zhian Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Biosafety risk assessment of infectious substances in cross-border movement is essential for preventing biosecurity threats and supporting regulatory decision-making. However, existing approaches often rely either on subjective expert judgment or purely objective data, making it difficult to effectively integrate heterogeneous information sources. This study proposes a multi-method integrated weighting framework for cross-border biosafety risk assessment by combining the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and an integrated TOPSIS–GRA evaluation model.In the proposed framework, FAHP and PCA are used to derive subjective and objective indicator weights, respectively. The discrepancies between different weighting results are explicitly characterized, and a constrained deviation-minimization optimization model is constructed to reconcile heterogeneous weighting information and obtain a combined weight vector under normalization constraints. The integrated TOPSIS–GRA model is then applied to quantify biosafety risks by jointly considering the distance from ideal solutions and grey relational similarity. A scenario-based case study on cross-border infectious substances demonstrates that the proposed framework improves the consistency, robustness, and interpretability of biosafety risk evaluation. The results indicate that the proposed approach provides a structured and reproducible tool for multi-criteria decision-making under uncertainty and offers practical support for biosafety management.

Introduction

Biosafety constitutes a critical component of national security, as the regulation of cross-border infectious substances directly affects both public health and international trade. Recently, highly pathogenic viruses such as the mpox virus and the Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever virus have continued to spread globally. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), as of June 2025, more than 127,000 confirmed cases and 283 deaths due to mpox have been reported worldwide1. Widespread outbreaks have strained healthcare systems, restricted population mobility, and significantly disrupted public health infrastructure and global economic activities.

China’s customs supervision system has similarly faced growing biosafety pressures. The China CDC’s Risk Assessment Report (June 2025) identified 11 infectious diseases posing importation risks, including seven classified as high-risk2. Notably, incidents at ports such as Ningbo and Guangzhou3 revealed smuggled biological materials and contaminated cargo, highlighting weaknesses in interception and control. These events demonstrate that inadequate assessment and management of biosafety risks at entry–exit points can have profound implications for national health security and trade stability.

Infectious substances encompass a wide spectrum of biological agents that differ markedly in their pathogenicity, transmission routes, infectious dose, host range, and environmental stability. The heterogeneity across these dimensions means that their risk profiles cannot be characterized by a single attribute but instead require a systematic, multi-criteria analytical approach. To support harmonized global biosafety management, international authorities such as the World Health Organization (WHO), the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), and the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) have established standardized classification schemes. These schemes categorize infectious agents according to their disease severity, likelihood of laboratory and community exposure, and the corresponding Biosafety Level (BSL) required for handling. This hierarchical structure provides a foundational qualitative framework that guides laboratory containment, transport conditions, and operational protocols.

Importantly, these qualitative classifications are not merely taxonomic; they directly inform the construction of quantitative risk assessment systems. Aligning indicator development with internationally recognized criteria ensures that essential risk dimensions—such as exposure pathways, containment requirements, and pathogen-specific hazards—are systematically incorporated. These classifications also shape expert judgments in multi-criteria decision-making processes such as the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP), helping to anchor subjective evaluations to established biosafety principles. Consequently, integrating such standards enhances both the scientific rigor and operational relevance of cross-border biosafety risk assessment models.

In this study, biosafety risk in the cross-border movement of infectious substances is conceptualized in a process-oriented manner. Rather than being attributed solely to the intrinsic hazard of a pathogen, biosafety risk is understood as emerging from the interaction between pathogen-related properties and failures in a series of operational processes, including specimen collection and preparation, packaging and internal transport, cross-border logistics and customs clearance, use and storage at the receiving institution, and emergency response to abnormal events. The literature on biosafety and laboratory risk assessment shows that weaknesses at any of these stages—such as inadequate containment, improper temperature control, poor documentation, or delayed incident reporting—can increase the likelihood of unintended exposure, release, or onward transmission and thereby amplify overall biosafety risk. Accordingly, the primary dimensions of the indicator system developed in this paper—product risk, biosafety control capacity of the using entity, logistics-chain conditions, packaging characteristics, and emergency management—are designed to capture the key process links through which infectious substances may pose biosafety threats along their cross-border trajectory.

Although global research has made notable progress in biosafety management4–9 existing studies remain primarily focused on policy frameworks and qualitative analyses. Quantitative methods that systematically integrate expert knowledge with objective data remain limited. While the United States and the European Union have established comprehensive multi-level pathogen surveillance systems10, domestic research11–13 still relies heavily on expert-based judgment and single-method evaluation approaches14,15, constraining both scientific rigor and generalizability16–19.

In parallel with these developments in biosafety governance, recent advances in multi-criteria decision-making (MCDA) have further enriched the methodological toolbox for handling complex, multi-attribute risk problems. The fuzzy normalization-based multi-attributive border approximation area comparison (FN-MABAC) method and its stochastic variant STFN-MABAC extend the classical MABAC model by combining fuzzy normalization with border approximation area concepts in order to better align MCDA rankings with reference preference orders and reduce ranking reversals, as demonstrated in e-commerce evaluation studies20. Complementary research on preference reidentification has compared stochastic identification procedures based on weights, fuzzy normalization, and reference objects (SITW, STFN, SITCOM), showing that different paradigms are better suited to monotonic, single-extremum, or more complex multi-extremum preference structures and highlighting the limitations of purely weight-based models in non-trivial decision problems21. In addition, a large-scale systematic review of subjective weight determination methods has mapped the landscape of expert-based weighting techniques, emphasizing the central role of expert knowledge in contemporary MCDM and the need for more robust, consistent, and hybrid weighting frameworks22. Together, these studies underscore the importance of transparent weighting schemes, robust normalization procedures, and explicit treatment of uncertainty and preference reidentification in modern decision models, and they provide a broader methodological context for the multi-method integrated weighting framework developed in this paper.

Despite these advances in both biosafety management and multi-criteria decision-making methodology, several important research gaps remain in the context of biosafety risk assessment for the cross-border movement of infectious substances. First, existing studies predominantly rely either on subjective approaches, such as the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), expert scoring, and Delphi methods, or on purely objective statistical techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and entropy-based weighting. Few studies provide a systematic and transparent mechanism for integrating these heterogeneous sources of weighting information within a unified analytical framework.Second, although differences between subjective and objective weighting results are widely acknowledged in practice, they are often treated implicitly or resolved in an ad hoc manner, without explicitly modeling or quantifying the deviations between alternative weighting schemes. As a result, the resulting risk evaluations may suffer from instability or limited interpretability. Third, many existing biosafety risk assessment models adopt single-method MCDA techniques, with insufficient attention to integrated evaluation models that can simultaneously account for distance to ideal solutions and relational structures among indicators. These limitations highlight the need for a unified analytical framework that emphasizes transparent integrated weighting, systematic information reconciliation, and robust multi-method evaluation for cross-border biosafety risk assessment.

To address these methodological limitations, this study introduces a multi-method integrated weighting framework as an integrative mechanism within a multi-criteria biosafety risk assessment system. The framework emphasizes the reconciliation of heterogeneous weighting information arising from subjective expert judgment and objective data-driven analysis during indicator construction. Previous studies23–28 have demonstrated that coordinating subjective and objective weighting schemes through structured combination or optimization-based approaches can enhance the robustness, consistency, and interpretability of multi-criteria decision-making outcomes. Building on these insights, the present study employs a constrained deviation-minimization-based weighting mechanism to integrate expert knowledge and statistical evidence, enabling a transparent and mathematically interpretable assignment of indicator weights for biosafety risk evaluation.

The primary contributions of this study are threefold. First, a structured three-level indicator system for assessing the biosafety risks of imported and exported infectious substances is established. The indicator system is refined through a combination of expert consultation and data-driven screening methods, including Principal Component Analysis and cluster analysis, ensuring consistency with international biosafety standards and enhancing the transparency and rationality of indicator selection.Second, a deviation-minimization-based combined weighting model is developed to integrate subjective and objective weighting information. Subjective weights derived from FAHP, which capture expert uncertainty through triangular fuzzy numbers, and objective weights obtained via PCA are reconciled through a deviation-minimization mechanism defined under normalization constraints. The resulting combined weight vector provides a balanced representation of expert judgment and statistical structure, improving stability and interpretability compared with single-weighting approaches.Third, an integrated evaluation model based on the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) and Grey Relational Analysis (GRA) is constructed. By jointly considering closeness to ideal solutions and grey relational similarity using the coordinated combined weights, the proposed model enhances discrimination among alternatives and provides a robust and reproducible basis for ranking biosafety risks.

Building upon these contributions, this study constructs a multi-method integrated weighting framework for biosafety risk assessment of infectious substances in cross-border movement. The framework integrates three core methodological components: (i) a comprehensive indicator-design process combining expert judgment with data-driven techniques for indicator screening; (ii) a coordinated combined-weighting model that reconciles FAHP-derived subjective weights with PCA-derived objective weights29; and (iii) a composite TOPSIS-GRA evaluation model30 for ranking biosafety risks based on the coordinated weights. Together, these components form a transparent and reproducible system for identifying, comparing, and prioritizing biosafety risks at international entry-exit points.

Compared with existing approaches that rely solely on subjective expert judgment or purely statistical methods, the proposed multi-method integrated weighting framework offers several advantages. By explicitly reconciling heterogeneous weighting information, the framework reduces bias associated with single-source weighting while retaining essential domain knowledge and data-driven structure. The coordinated weighting mechanism enhances the stability and interpretability of assessment results, while the integrated TOPSIS–GRA evaluation model improves robustness and discrimination under uncertainty. These features collectively demonstrate the methodological soundness and practical value of the proposed framework for complex biosafety risk assessment scenarios, providing effective support for evidence-based risk governance in cross-border contexts.

Methodology: a multi-method integrated weighting framework

Framework for cross-border biosafety risk assessment

Port biosafety risk assessment for infectious substances in cross-border movement involves multiple heterogeneous considerations, including pathogen-related hazards, biosafety control capacity, logistics-chain conditions, packaging characteristics, and emergency management capability. These considerations are characterized by differences in data structure, information sources, and evaluation perspectives, making it necessary to establish a structured analytical framework for their systematic integration.

In this study, a multi-method biosafety risk assessment framework is constructed to organize heterogeneous information inputs and to support transparent indicator screening, weight determination, and comprehensive risk evaluation. Rather than modeling strategic interactions among stakeholders, the framework focuses on the integration and propagation of information derived from expert judgment and data-driven analysis throughout the assessment process.

The overall framework of the proposed biosafety risk assessment system is illustrated in Fig. 1. The framework consists of three main stages: (i) construction and refinement of a hierarchical indicator system through literature review, clustering analysis, and principal component analysis; (ii) integration of subjective and objective indicator weights using a deviation-based optimization mechanism; and (iii) comprehensive biosafety risk evaluation based on a combined TOPSIS–GRA model. These indicators covered five primary dimensions: product risk, biosafety control capability of the using entity, logistics-chain conditions, packaging characteristics, and emergency management capacity.

Subsequent hierarchical clustering grouped these indicators into 16 secondary categories, which were then consolidated into five primary dimensions consistent with the conceptual structure of the biosafety risk framework.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was employed to further examine the suitability of the 60 selected indicators and to extract latent factors. Sixteen principal components with eigenvalues greater than 1 were retained, cumulatively explaining 86.3% of the total variance, indicating that the selected indicators effectively captured the core functional characteristics of the system.

To make the indicator reduction process transparent, explicit elimination criteria were applied after PCA. Indicators were removed if they met one or more of the following conditions: (i) very low variance across samples, suggesting limited discriminatory power; (ii) weak conceptual relevance to biosafety risk in the cross-border movement of infectious substances, as assessed by domain experts; (iii) high redundancy with other indicators, reflected in strong correlations and overlapping regulatory implications; or (iv) insufficient explanatory power in the factor structure, manifested as consistently low factor loadings and negligible contributions to the retained components. Applying these criteria led to the removal of a small number of indicators and resulted in a refined set of 57 indicators, which further stabilized and simplified the multi-level framework.

Building on this refined indicator set, a deviation-minimization-based integration mechanism was introduced to reconcile discrepancies between expert judgment and the statistical structure revealed by PCA. Seven experts in customs regulation and biosafety management provided pairwise comparisons to generate FAHP-based subjective weights, all satisfying consistency requirements (CR < 0.1). In parallel, variance contributions of the retained principal components were aggregated to construct an objective weight vector.

Rather than treating these two weight vectors independently, a deviation-minimization objective function was constructed to penalize inconsistencies between subjective and objective weighting information. Solving this deviation-minimization problem yielded a combined weighting scheme that integrates expert knowledge with data-driven evidence under explicit optimization criteria. According to the criteria of “average combined weight exceeding a predefined selection threshold” and “defuzzified expert score ≥ 7.0,” three indicators that were initially down-weighted by PCA but consistently regarded as critical by experts were re-incorporated into the system. The formal weighting procedure is detailed in the subsequent subsection Weighting of Indicators.

To assess robustness, a stability and sensitivity analysis was conducted. The sample was repeatedly partitioned into random subsamples, and the clustering and PCA procedures were re-estimated. Across these subsamples, the composition of the main indicator groups and dominant principal components remained broadly consistent. Additional robustness checks were performed by varying the number of retained principal components (within an 80–90% cumulative variance range) and testing alternative clustering cut-off distances and linkage criteria. The core functional dimensions of the indicator system remained stable under these variations.

Based on the integrated indicator-selection process—including clustering analysis, PCA-based screening, and coordinated reconciliation of expert and data-driven information—a comprehensive evaluation system consisting of 5 primary indicators, 16 secondary indicators, and 60 tertiary indicators was ultimately established. Construction of the integrated TOPSIS–GRA risk assessment model

Single-method approaches to cross-border infectious-substance biosafety risk assessment exhibit inherent limitations. While the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) enables multi-indicator ranking based on distances from ideal solutions, its performance may be sensitive to small sample sizes or incomplete information. Grey Relational Analysis (GRA), in contrast, is effective in handling uncertainty and partial information but may provide limited discrimination when used independently. To address these limitations, the comprehensive indicator weights obtained through the deviation-based integration procedure are integrated into a combined TOPSIS–GRA evaluation framework, allowing the complementary strengths of both methods to be jointly utilized.

Discussion and conclusions

This study develops a multi-method framework for assessing biosafety risks associated with the cross-border movement of infectious substances, in which heterogeneous weighting information is integrated through a deviation-minimization mechanism. By integrating indicator system construction, combined weighting, and composite evaluation, the proposed framework provides structured methodological support for risk-based biosafety governance at entry–exit ports. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and cluster analysis were employed to support indicator screening and dimensional refinement, while a deviation-minimization logic was introduced to mathematically balance expert judgment with objective data characteristics during the indicator optimization process. Through this procedure, 60 tertiary indicators were retained, reflecting both professional expertise and statistical evidence. The empirical application indicates that institutional biosafety capacity—particularly laboratory capability, procedural standardization, and emergency preparedness—plays a dominant role in mitigating cross-border biosafety risks.Subjective and objective weights were calculated using the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP) and PCA, respectively. To reconcile the epistemic uncertainty inherent in expert judgment with the structural information captured by data-driven analysis, a deviation-based weight integration model was constructed. This formulation yields a theoretically interpretable compromise solution that reduces the potential bias associated with single-method weighting. Moreover, the associated multi-indicator profiles help explain the underlying sources of risk in an operationally meaningful manner, thereby supporting differentiated and risk-based regulatory decision-making.

From a methodological perspective, the proposed multi-method TOPSIS-GRA framework with coordinated weighting complements recent advances in multi-criteria decision-making (MCDA), including fuzzy normalization-based and preference reidentification approaches. While fuzzy normalization-based border approximation and reidentification methods emphasize the transformation of uncertain evaluations and the reconstruction of reference rankings, the present framework focuses on the coordinated integration of subjective (FAHP-based) and objective (PCA-based) weights, as well as the joint interpretation of distance-based and correlation-based evaluation results. This provides an alternative and flexible approach for combining heterogeneous information sources in MCDA, particularly in contexts where expert knowledge must be reconciled with limited quantitative data.

From a managerial perspective, the proposed multi-method framework with coordinated weighting offers several practical implications for biosafety governance at entry-exit ports. The integrated weighting mechanism facilitates the identification of key risk drivers and supports the allocation of inspection manpower, laboratory capacity, and monitoring equipment. The TOPSIS-GRA evaluation outputs provide a transparent basis for prioritizing high-risk shipments and port profiles, enabling risk-based supervision without undermining operational efficiency. In addition, the combined use of expert judgment and objective data characteristics strengthens early warning capabilities and supports differentiated regulatory interventions.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the subjective weighting component relies on expert judgments, and any bias or inconsistency in these judgments may influence the FAHP-based weights and propagate to the final evaluation results. Second, correlations among indicators may affect the stability of the combined weights, particularly when subjective and objective methods respond differently to multicollinearity. Third, the performance of the integrated TOPSIS–GRA model depends on the normalization procedure applied to the raw data, as inappropriate normalization may distort distance and relational measures. Finally, the empirical analysis is based on a limited number of representative port profiles constructed under data-access constraints and should be interpreted as a scenario-based case study rather than a large-sample validation.

Future research could focus on obtaining anonymized operational datasets in collaboration with customs and port authorities to enable large-sample validation and robustness testing. In addition, direct empirical comparisons between the proposed coordination-based framework and fuzzy normalization-based MCDA methods would help clarify their respective strengths and applicability ranges. Extensions incorporating fuzzy or interval data and dynamic risk monitoring mechanisms may further enhance the framework’s practical value.

In conclusion, this study proposes a multi-method framework for biosafety risk assessment of infectious substances in cross-border movement, in which heterogeneous weighting information is integrated through a deviation-minimization mechanism. The results indicate that the framework provides transparent, discriminative, and operationally meaningful support for risk-based biosafety governance at international entry–exit points, while offering a flexible foundation for future methodological refinement and empirical extension.

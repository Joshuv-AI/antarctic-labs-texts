Tracheobronchial Tuberculosis During Treatment for Bronchial Asthma
Authors: Hiromitsu Sakai, Hirokazu Tokuyasu, Shuichi Matsuoka, Katsunori Arai, Chika Esumi, Akira Yamasaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTracheobronchial tuberculosis is a disease that requires careful attention when treating patients with chronic cough, such as those with bronchial asthma.

A 52‐year‐old woman with a history of asthma, diagnosed approximately 20 years ago based on typical symptoms and objective tests of lung function, visited our department due to a persistent wet cough lasting 4 months after receiving intensive asthma treatment at a local clinic. Chest computed tomography revealed multiple irregularly shaped nodules and tiny centrilobular nodules, suggesting pulmonary mycobacterial infection (Figure 1). Although the interferon‐gamma release assay was positive, the acid‐fast bacillus test of the sputum was negative. Bronchoscopy revealed abundant white plaques extending from the trachea to the left main bronchus and the entrance of the left lingular segment (Figure 2). A smear of bronchial washes was positive for acid‐fast bacilli, and polymerase chain reaction and culture results confirmed the presence of Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Histopathological examination of the endobronchial biopsy specimen revealed a caseating epithelioid granuloma. The patient was diagnosed with tracheobronchial and pulmonary tuberculosis and transferred to another hospital for treatment. Tracheobronchial tuberculosis occurs in approximately 10%–40% of patients with active pulmonary tuberculosis [1]. Severe coughing in tracheobronchial tuberculosis can lead to the expulsion of large amounts of bacteria, making it easy to infect others. Tracheobronchial tuberculosis requires careful attention when treating patients with chronic coughing.

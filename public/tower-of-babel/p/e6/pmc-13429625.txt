WHiAR-Net: an interpretable multi-scale forecasting framework via Wavelet-Hilbert feature engineering
Authors: Kai-Cheng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate forecasting of non-stationary time series remains challenging due to complex volatility. We introduce WHiAR-Net, an interpretable framework integrating wavelet theory with Hilbert spectral analysis to separate long-term trends from transient fluctuations. By structurally embedding operator error bounds, this transparent architecture offers a principled alternative to conventional black-box models. Systematic experiments on electricity and environmental datasets demonstrate that our method achieves highly competitive accuracy, outperforming modern deep learning baselines, with promising future applications in smart energy grids and climate monitoring.

Introduction

The forecasting of non-stationary time series constitutes a fundamental challenge in modern data science, with critical applications ranging from energy grid management to environmental monitoring. While recent advancements in deep learning, particularly Transformer-based architectures1,2 and their wavelet-enhanced variants3, alongside hybrid deep frameworks for energy and environmental forecasting4,5, have achieved remarkable empirical accuracy, they often operate as “black boxes”6. These models typically rely on massive parameterization to approximate complex dynamics, lacking the explicit theoretical guarantees required for high-stakes decision-making. The absence of a rigorous mathematical framework limits their interpretability and raises concerns regarding their stability in out-of-distribution scenarios. Interestingly, recent studies indicate that inherently simpler linear models can sometimes match or even exceed the effectiveness of these complex attention mechanisms7, highlighting the need for architectures that balance performance with interpretability.

Previous hybrid architectures have successfully combined statistical and neural models to separately process linear trends and non-linear residuals8. However, our approach goes beyond sequential hybridization by proposing WHiAR-Net, a theoretically grounded framework that bridges the dichotomy between classical analysis and modern data-driven forecasting. Our approach diverges from standard hybrid models that utilize wavelets merely as a static preprocessing step9. Instead, we embed the mathematical structures of multiresolution analysis (MRA) and Hilbert spectral analysis directly into the learning architecture. Rather than inventing a new integral transform, our core contribution lies in the structural embedding of explicit operator error bounds within a forecasting neural architecture. By leveraging an explicit error decomposition and transparent mapping properties, we systematically separate signal regularity from transient volatility.

First, regarding the trend component, we formalize the approximation power of the discrete wavelet transform. Unlike general neural networks that approximate functions via activation non-linearities without specific convergence rates10, we invoke the classical theory of Daubechies11 to establish Theorem 2. We prove that for any trend belonging to the Hölder space \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C^\alpha$$\end{document}, the Daubechies wavelet basis with sufficient vanishing moments provides an optimal sparse representation, guaranteeing an error decay of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(2^{-J\alpha })$$\end{document}. This allows us to replace the heavy attention mechanisms found in Transformers with a parsimonious, mathematically justified autoregressive (AR) structure on the scaling coefficients.

Second, to capture non-stationary volatility, we integrate the Hilbert transform. While the Hilbert-Huang Transform (HHT) has been widely applied for instantaneous frequency analysis12,13, its empirical mode decomposition (EMD) often lacks explicit analytic bounds for spectral leakage. In contrast, our framework (Theorem 3) explicitly evaluates the Bedrosian spectral separation condition14. This ensures that the Hilbert operator acts as an isometry on the high-frequency detail coefficients, enabling an analytically bounded extraction of instantaneous amplitude envelopes while systematically mitigating the mode-mixing artifacts common in heuristic algorithmic approaches.

Furthermore, we provide a deterministic guarantee for the system’s reliability. Unlike purely statistical error estimations, Proposition 4 derives a explicit upper bound for the total prediction error using operator norm analysis. This bound explicitly quantifies the contributions from wavelet approximation, spectral leakage (Bedrosian residual), and AR stability, offering a level of transparency difficult to attain in standard deep learning paradigms. Our extensive experiments on the ETT electricity benchmarks and environmental datasets (PM2.5, Jena Climate) demonstrate that WHiAR-Net achieves highly competitive performance on par with recent state-of-the-art Transformer architectures. Importantly, this empirical precision is achieved while preserving linear computational complexity, presenting a compelling theoretical and computational alternative for time series forecasting.

The paper is organized as follows: Section “Results” presents the main theoretical results and experimental validation; Section “Discussion” discusses the implications of the spectral components; and Section “Methods” details the methodology and mathematical proofs.

Results

In this section, we substantiate the theoretical framework established in the previous section with extensive empirical evidence. Generalization on environmental data

Finally, we examined the generalization capability of WHiAR-Net across distinct physical domains using the Beijing PM2.5 (air quality) and Jena Climate (temperature) datasets. These datasets represent two distinct temporal dynamics: highly non-stationary (PM2.5) and strongly seasonal (temperature).

As depicted in Fig. 3, the model exhibits robust adaptability compared to the observed ground truth. In the PM2.5 case (Fig. 3a), where the signal features rapid and irregular fluctuations, the Hilbert volatility channel facilitates tracking the instantaneous amplitude of pollution spikes. Conversely, for the Jena temperature data (Fig. 3b), the Daubechies wavelet basis effectively compresses the smooth diurnal cycles into sparse coefficients, enabling the AR component to perform stable extrapolation.

To quantitatively substantiate these visual results, we evaluated the forecasting errors (RMSE and MAE) on both datasets, as detailed in Table S1 in the Supplementary Information. The quantitative metrics confirm that WHiAR-Net maintains highly competitive accuracy across both stochastic and periodic regimes, outperforming traditional statistical baselines. Discussion

The empirical success of WHiAR-Net across synthetic, industrial, and environmental datasets is not serendipitous but theoretically grounded in the frame-theoretic properties established in our theoretical analysis. By bridging the gap between harmonic analysis and data-driven forecasting, this work addresses the interpretability deficit inherent in modern deep learning approaches. Specifically, WHiAR-Net distinguishes itself from conventional black-box models through the structural embedding of explicit operator error bounds. The architecture leverages an explicit error decomposition and transparent mapping properties to ensure that every operation possesses a clear functional-analytic meaning.

Bridging theory and practice

The highly competitive performance on the ETTh1 dataset (Section "Forecasting performance on electricity benchmarks") validates the mapping properties discussed in Theorem 2. While standard Transformers (e.g., Informer, Autoformer) attempt to learn global dependencies via attention mechanisms, they often suffer from spectral bias, struggling to efficiently capture high-frequency transients. In contrast, WHiAR-Net exploits the vanishing moments of Daubechies wavelets to systematically suppress polynomial trends, establishing an approximation error that decays deterministically as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(2^{-J\alpha })$$\end{document}. This explains why our model achieves accuracy on par with recent state-of-the-art architectures (such as PatchTST) across extended horizons up to h=192: the wavelet transform provides a sparse, optimal representation for the regularity-dominated trend, allowing the AR component to operate safely within its stability region.

The role of the Bedrosian identity

The generalization results on PM2.5 data (Section "Generalization on environmental data") highlight the critical function of the Hilbert branch. The ablation study (Section "Ablation study and component analysis") revealed that removing this branch causes a substantial performance degradation in non-stationary regimes. This confirms Proposition 4: when the signal violates global stationarity, the Bedrosian residual bounded by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Vert r_{Bedrosian}\Vert _{L^2}$$\end{document} becomes a critical factor. Rather than assuming perfect spectral separation in practice, our mathematical framework mathematically confines this spectral leakage into a deterministic bound. As empirically demonstrated by the robust quantitative results on the highly stochastic PM2.5 dataset (Table S1 in the Supplementary Information), WHiAR-Net effectively mitigates this volatility envelope, a feature that pure time-domain models often struggle to extract explicitly.

Parsimony vs. Complexity

From a computational perspective, WHiAR-Net offers a compelling theoretical alternative. Deep forecasting models typically scale quadratically \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(L^2)$$\end{document} or log-linearly O(L łog L) with sequence length. Conversely, the discrete wavelet transform and the AR process operate with linear complexity O(N). Our results demonstrate that this mathematical parsimony maintains highly competitive forecasting capacity; rather, it acts as a structural regularization mechanism, preventing the overfitting often observed in heavily parameterized neural networks.

Limitations and future directions

We acknowledge that the theoretical guarantees of our framework govern the error bounds (Proposition 4), while the perfect spectral separation condition itself is rarely strictly satisfied in real-world time series. Even when spectral assumptions are stretched, our framework isolates the penalty; however, if the spectra of the trend and volatility components heavily overlap, the residual \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Vert r_{Bedrosian}\Vert _{L^2}$$\end{document} inflates the approximation error. Future work will explore the integration of adaptive basis pursuit or EMD to relax this spectral constraint. Additionally, extending the scalar WHiAR-Net to high-dimensional vector-valued time series using Clifford analysis remains a promising avenue for multivariate forecasting.

In conclusion, WHiAR-Net demonstrates that an operator-theoretic perspective–specifically utilizing wavelets and Hilbert transforms–is not merely a preprocessing heuristic but can constitute the core learnable architecture of forecasting models, offering a mathematically transparent alternative to black-box deep learning.

Definition 2

Mathematical proofs

In this subsection, we present the rigorous mathematical derivations that underpin the WHiAR-Net framework. Specifically, we establish the well-posedness of the variational problems, verify the spectral conservation laws, and derive the sharp error bounds that govern the system’s predictive reliability.

Proof of Lemma 1

We proceed by demonstrating the well-posedness of the optimization problem (1) and deriving the geometric constraint on the decomposition level J. The argument relies on the support properties of the discrete wavelet transform operators and the boundedness of the entropy functional.

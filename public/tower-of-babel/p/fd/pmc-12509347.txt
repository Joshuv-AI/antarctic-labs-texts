LncRNA-ANRIL/miR-34a-mediated polarization of rheumatoid arthritis macrophages to regulate the adhesion, morphology, and survival of fibroblasts
Authors: Zhouzhou Xu, Chunhuai Wang, Long Qian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis work focused on exploring the regulatory effect of long non-coding RNA (lncRNA)-ANRIL/miR-34a on rheumatoid arthritis (RA) fibroblasts (FLS), aiming to provide experimental support for clinical treatment of RA. Methods: The pcDNA, pcDNA-ANRIL, antagomiRNA, antagomiR-34a, pcDNA-ANRIL, agomiRNA, pcDNA-ANRIL, and agomiR-34a were transfected into MH7A cells using liposomes. M1 macrophages and MH7A cells were co-cultured in Transwell, and the proliferation of MH7A cells at different time points was observed using crystal violet staining (CVS). The effect of M1 macrophages on the proliferation of MH7A cells was detected by MTS method. The expressions of ANRIL and miR-34a in MH7A cells were detected by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The apoptosis rate was detected by flow cytometry (FCM). The protein expressions of Caspase-3, Bcl-2, and Bax were detected by Western blotting (WB). ResultsCompared with the HFLS group, the ANRIL in the MH7A group was increased (P < 0.05). After overexpression of ANRIL, the apoptosis rate (AR) of MH7A cells was markedly decreased, the expressions of Caspase-3 and Bax decreased, and the expressions of Bcl-2 increased (all P < 0.05). MH7A cell proliferation was significantly inhibited in M1 macrophage co-cultures. Following miR-34a inhibition, MH7A cells exhibited an anti-apoptotic effect comparable to that observed with ANRIL overexpression. ConclusionM1 macrophages in RA joint synovial tissue could inhibit the proliferation of MH7A cells. LncRNA-ANRIL was highly expressed in RA synovial cells (SCs) and could inhibit the apoptosis of RA SCs, which was correlated with targeting miR-34a, inhibiting apoptosis in anti-RA SCs.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) mainly erodes joints throughout the body [1], often causes joint damage, disability, and various complications, which directly affect the quality of life of patients. According to data, the global prevalence of RA is about 1.5%, and it mostly occurs in elderly patients [2, 3]. With the intensification of aging, the number of RA patients in my country is as high as 4.8 million [4, 5]. However, the mechanism of RA occurrence is not yet clear, and it is generally believed to be related to factors such as gender, familial inheritance, alcoholism, and smoking [6]. The main pathological features of RA include abnormal proliferation of synovial cells (SCs) and aggregation and migration of inflammatory cells [7]. Normal joint synovium includes synovial macrophages and synovial fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS). These two types of cells can produce pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and proteases, which degrade the connective tissue in the synovium and destroy the articular cartilage [8]. Infiltration of inflammatory factors and migration of inflammatory leukocytes, especially with the production of TNF-α and IL-1β, synovial FLS develops towards a highly migratory and highly destructive type, enhancing osteoclast function [9]. The long chain noncoding RNA (LncRNA) has regulatory functions in all diseases of the body, including RA [10–12]. Antisense noncoding RNA in the INK4 Locus (ANRIL) is located at a genetic susceptibility locus in the chromosome 9P21 region. ANRIL is an antisense LncRNA at the inhibitor of cell cycle kinase 4b (INK4b) site, with a size of approximately 3.8 kB. It plays a crucial role in various diseases, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [13], chronic airway inflammatory diseases [14], osteoarthritis [15], and osteoporosis [16]. The exact role of LncRNA-ANRIL in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) remains to be further elucidated.

MicroRNA (miRNA) primarily regulates gene expression by binding to target mRNA through complementary base pairing, thereby inhibiting mRNA translation or promoting its degradation. Current studies have shown that miRNAs play an important role in tendon injury and osteoarthritis [17, 18]. Studies have reported that the miR-34a in the synovial tissue of RA patients is lower than the normal level [19]. Therefore, up-regulation of miR-34a can inhibit the activation, migration, and invasion of RA synovial FLS [20]. However, the mechanisms underlying synovial macrophage-FLS interactions and the regulatory role of the LncRNA-ANRIL/miR-34a axis remain unclear. Elucidating these processes is critical for understanding RA pathogenesis and constitutes a key focus of this study. Therefore, this work took RA FLSMH7A as the research object, aiming to explore the regulation of LncRNA-ANRIL/miR-34a on RA FLSMH7A, and provide an experimental basis for clinical treatment of RA.

Materials and methods

Instruments and materials

Normal joint synovial membrane FLS (HFLS) and RA FLS (MH7A) were from American Culture Collection. Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 medium and fetal bovine serum (FBS) were purchased from Gibco, USA; phorbol ester (PMA) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS) were purchased from Sigma in the United States. LipofectamineTM3000 was purchased from Invitrogen, USA; real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) kit was purchased from TaKaRa, Japan. All primers in this work and the design and synthesis of antagomiRNA, antagomiR-34a, agomiRNA and agomiR-34a were completed by Guangzhou Ribo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate and propidium iodide (An-nexin V-FITC/PI) apoptosis detection kit, purchased from Beijing Biolab Technology Co., Ltd. Rabbit anti-Caspase-3 polyclonal antibody, rabbit anti-Bax polyclonal antibody, rabbit anti-Bcl-2 polyclonal antibody, and horseradish peroxidase-labeled goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody were from Miltenyi Biological Co., Ltd., Germany. Dual-luciferase reporter gene kit was from Shanghai Yisheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

CostarTranswell cell culture chamber was purchased from Corning Company, USA. 450 microplate reader was purchased from American Bio-Rad Company. FACS Calibur flow cytometer (FCT) was purchased from American Becton Dickinson Company.

Cell culture

HFLS and MH7A cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. After inverting the culture flask, cells were incubated for 4–6 h. Upon reaching 70–80% confluence (approximately 3/4 of the culture surface), cells were detached using 0.25% trypsin for 1–1.5 min. Cells between passages 3–6 were collected and prepared as single-cell suspensions for subsequent experiments.

Cell grouping and transfection

The normal cultured HFLS and MH7A cells were marked as the HFLS group and the MH7A group, respectively. The Lipofectamine TM3000TM, pcDNA group (transfected pcDNA), pcDNA-ANRIL group (transfected with pcDNA-ANRIL), antagomiRNA group (transfected with antagomiRNA), antagomiR-34a group (transfected with antagomiR-34a), pcDNA-ANRIL + agomiRNA group (respectively transfected with pcD-NA-ANRIL and agomiRNA), and pcDNA-ANRIL + agomiR-34a group (respectively transfected with pcDNA-ANRIL and agomiR-34a) were transfected into MH7A cells. After transfection for 10 h, the new medium was replaced, and the culture was continued for 48 h. The transfection efficiency was detected by RT-qPCR.

In vitro induction of human M1 macrophages

THP-1 in the logarithmic growth phase (LGP) was inoculated into 6-well plates in 106 wells, 100 ng/mL PMA was added, and placed in a constant temperature incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C for two days. When the cells were completely adherent, washed 3 times with PBS, added 2 mL of 20% FBS RPMI-1640 medium, and left in the incubator for one day. After washing three times with phosphate buffer, RPMI-1640 medium containing 2 mL of 1 µg/mL lipopolysaccharide and 20 ng/mL IFN-γ was added, and M1 macrophages were induced after 24 h. In an inverted microscope, the morphological changes of M1 macrophages before and after induction were observed and compared.

Establishment of transwell co-culture system of M1 macrophages and MH7A cells

To recapitulate the physiological microenvironment of MH7A cells, we established a Transwell co-culture system with M1-polarized macrophages. Fifth-passage MH7A cells in logarithmic growth phase (LGP) were seeded into Transwell inserts at a density of 1.5 × 105 cells per well. Following cell adhesion (typically 4–6 h), the inserts were transferred to 6-well plates containing pre-differentiated M1 macrophages (1.5 × 105 cells/well) and co-cultured for 48 h to comprise the experimental group. Parallel control groups included: [1] MH7A cells cultured alone in Transwell chambers, and [2] M1 macrophages cultured without MH7A cells.

Observation of cell morphology by CVS

The Transwell chamber was carefully removed and the culture medium aspirated for subsequent analysis. MH7A cells were gently rinsed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, 1×) using slow, lateral motions to prevent cell detachment. Cells were fixed with 95% ethanol for 15 min at room temperature. After ethanol removal, cells were stained with crystal violet solution (CVS) for 15 min. Excess stain was removed by thorough rinsing with double-distilled water (ddH2O). Following complete air-drying at room temperature, the chamber was transferred to a 6-well plate. Cell morphology was examined using an inverted phase-contrast microscope (200× magnification).

Detection of cell proliferation by MTS method

MH7A cells in log phase were taken and seeded in 3 × 103 wells in a 96-well plate, 50 µL of M1 cell culture supernatant was added to each well of the experimental group, and RPMI-1640 medium (containing 20% FBS) was added to 100 µL. For other control groups, only RPMI-1640 culture medium (without FBS) was added and supplemented to 100 µL. It was placed in an incubator, cultured for 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h, and the absorbance (A) value at 490 nm was detected by MTS method. Six replicate wells were set, and the experiment was repeated three times to draw the growth curve.

RT-qPCR detection of ANRIL and miR-34a expression

The cells to be tested were collected to extract the total RNA, and cDNA was synthesized based on the instructions of the reverse transcription kit as a template. Expression of ANRIL and miR-34a in the template was detected by RT-qPCR. Using GAPDH and U6 as internal controls, the relative expression levels of ANRIL and miR-34a were calculated by the 2−ΔΔCtmethod. Amplification conditions were 93 °C, 10 min; 96℃, 15 s; 60℃, 30 s; 45 cycles, and 72℃, 5 min. Table 1 showed the primer information.

Detection of Caspase-3, bax, and Bcl-2 proteins by WB

The cells were lysed with RIPA lysis buffer on ice for 30 min to extract the total cell protein. The protein content was determined by the Bradford method, the protein was denatured using a boiling water bath method, centrifuged, and the supernatant was separated. SDS-PAGE was used for electrophoresis. Proteins (60 µg) from supernatants were electrophoresed and immobilized on agarose gels. Using a membrane transfer machine, it could transfer the corresponding protein on the agarose gel to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane, which was blocked with 2.5% non-fat dry milk and incubated for around 60 s. After the membrane was blocked, the membrane was transferred to the primary antibody solution for 2 h (dilution ratio, rabbit anti-Caspase-3 polyclonal antibody, 1:2000. Rabbit anti-Bax polyclonal antibody, 1:1000; rabbit anti-Bcl-2 polyclonal antibody, 1:1000), incubated at 4 °C for 12 h. The membrane was washed, transferred to the diluted horseradish peroxidase-labeled secondary antibody solution for 1 h (dilution ratio, 1:1000), and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. The membrane was washed and exposed to electrochemiluminescence (ECL). Gel imaging system was used for scanning and analysis, β-actin was undertaken as the internal reference, and the gray values of the target protein and the internal reference were undertaken as the relative expression of Caspase-3, Bax, and Bcl-2 proteins. Three independent experiments were performed in triplicate.

Detection of TNF-α and IL-12 in cell culture supernatant by ELISA

The concentrations of TNF-α and IL-12 were detected based on the instructions of the ELISA kit, and the value of sample A was read by a microplate reader at 450 nm. Three independent experiments were performed in triplicate.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

pMIR-LUC-ANRIL-WT and pMIR-LUC-ANRIL-MUT were transfected into MH7A cells with agomiRNA, agomiR-34a, antagomi-RNA, and antagomiR-34a, respectively. The cells were seeded in 24-well plates and were collected after transfection for 48 h. The bioinformatics online prediction website LncBase Experimental v.2 predicted whether there is a complementary binding site between LncRNA ANRIL and miR-34a. Chemical synthesis of ANRIL-MUT sequences without binding sites and A. Finally, a dual-luciferase reporter gene kit was employed to detect the fluorescence activity (FA) of cells.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 19.0. The normally distributed measurement data were expressed as ‾x ± s, and the enumeration data was expressed as percentage (%). The independent samples t test was to compare whether there were differences between the two groups, the one-way analysis of variance was to analyze whether there were differences between multiple groups, and the SNK-q was to analyze the multiple differences between groups. The difference was statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Discussion

In RA, due to the excessive proliferation of synovial tissue, the neovascular pannus directly invades the articular cartilage and damages the bone tissue around the joint, resulting in excessive synovial tissue hyperplasia [21–24]. The structure and function of skeletal muscle tissue can be repaired through the involvement of various cell types and innate immune responses [25].

Therefore, the first step in the treatment of RA is to inhibit the proliferation of FLS. Small interfering RNA (siRNA) can specifically silence the expression of target genes by designing specific siRNA sequences, providing an effective tool for gene function research and disease therapy [26, 27]. Studies showed that siRNAs can be used to identify molecular targets and evaluate the efficacy of specific drugs, holding potential value in the treatment of RA [28]. Recent studies indicated that long non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs), as evolutionarily conserved endogenous long-chain non-coding RNAs, can bind and inhibit their complementary miRNAs, regulating gene expression at the transcriptional or post-transcriptional level, playing a critical role in the pathological process of RA [29, 30]. Some data show that LncRNA is not only involved in the regulation of inflammation in RA, but also closely related to the proliferation, apoptosis, and migration of FLS in synovial tissue [31, 32]. The results of Zhang et al. (2021) [33] showed that LncRNA promoted the apoptosis and migration of FLS in synovial tissue by inhibiting the expression of miR-4701-5p. Wang et al. (2020) [34] found that LncRNA zinc finger protein antisense 1 promoted the proliferation and migration of FLS in synovial tissue by inhibiting miR-27a in RA. This work suggested that in contrast to the HFLS group, LncRNA-ANRIL in the MH7A group was increased, and the miR-34a in the MH7A group was decreased (both P < 0.05). Li et al. (2019) [35] found that, compared to normal synovial cells, the expression of ANRIL was significantly increased in osteoarthritis synovial cells. After transfection with siRNA, the expression of ANRIL was reduced in osteoarthritis synovial cells, inhibiting cell proliferation. Moreover, ANRIL could regulate the proliferation and apoptosis of osteoarthritis synovial cells through the miR-122-5p/DUSP4 axis. This finding is consistent with the results of the present study, both suggesting that the expression level of LncRNA-ANRIL may be associated with musculoskeletal diseases. Compared to the FLS of osteoarthritis patients, the expression level of miR-34a was lower in the FLS of RA patients. miR-34a is expected to be a promising therapeutic target for RA, as it inhibits FLS proliferation and reduces the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and MMPs [36]. Further in-depth exploration of the relationship between the two found that miR-34a is the target gene of ANRIL, and LncRNA-ANRIL has a negative regulatory function on miR-34a, indicating that LncRNA-ANRIL may correlated with RA by targeting miR-34a.

Although more and more studies have confirmed that lncRNAs can regulate gene expression and involve in the integration of various diseases, some new studies have shown that the functions of lncRNAs in RA are not clear. Compared to the normal control population, the expression level of ANRIL in peripheral blood mononuclear cells of RA patients is significantly decreased [37]. Cao et al. (2021) [38] measured ANRIL expression in serum of RA and normal volunteers by RT-qPCR and found that ANRIL was highly expressed in RA patients, and it was positively correlated with the expression levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP-9) in patient SCs, indicating the potential value of ANRIL as an independent factor for RA. Piao et al. (2021) [39] found that ANRIL can mediate the Wnt-β-catenin signaling pathway to regulate repair of articular cartilage. This work found that overexpression of ANRIL could inhibit the cell apoptosis, the Caspase-3 and Bax proteins decreased, and the Bcl-2 increased. The results of Shang et al. (2021) [40] remain consistent. To further explore the specific mechanism of ANRIL in RA synovial tissue apoptosis, this work confirmed the existence of a complementary relationship between ANRIL and miR-34a using a bioinformatics prediction system and dual luciferase reporter experiments, which directly affects the regulation of apoptosis by ANRIL in RA.It has been recognized at home and abroad that miRNAs participate in various regulatory roles in body diseases. Among them, miR-34a is closely associated with cancer, RA, and other diseases, but its specific mechanism needs to be further explored [41–43]. The results of Lu et al. (2021) [44] showed that miR-34a was down-regulated in RA synovial tissue FLS, and it can be used as a downstream target gene of LncRNA taurine up-regulated gene to participate in the apoptosis, migration, and invasion of synovial tissue FLS.Wei et al. (2022) [45] induced arthritis in rats using complete Freund’s adjuvant and analyzed the impact of lncRNA on the progression of adjuvant-induced arthritis. The study found that miR-34a-5p was downregulated in the synovium and fibroblast-like synoviocytes of adjuvant-induced arthritis rats. The results indicated that X chromosome inactivation-specific transcripts regulate the function of fibroblast-like synoviocytes in adjuvant-induced arthritis and promote the progression of rheumatoid arthritis through miR-34a-5p. This work found that inhibiting the expression of miR-34a, the miR-34a in the antagomiR-34a group decreased, and the apoptosis rate was reduced. The Caspase-3 and Bax decreased, and the Bcl-2 increased. Further investigation revealed that overexpression of miR-34a could reverse the inhibition of ANRIL on FLS apoptosis.

Macrophages play a pivotal role in promoting proper wound healing and facilitating the resolution of inflammation in response to pathogen challenges or tissue injury. Cytokines secreted by immune cells include both pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory factors, which influence the healing process and macrophage polarization [46]. In RA synovial tissue, the active proliferation of FLS is a key factor leading to synovial hyperplasia, and the active proliferation of FLS is closely related to the inflammatory environment in which it grows. M1 and M2 macrophages infiltrate the synovial tissue of RA, and the inflammatory factors secreted by them are directly involved in the occurrence and development of RA [47]. The progression of early osteoarthritis is driven by an interrelated process involving pro-catabolic and pro-inflammatory mediators [48]. This work investigated the effect of M1 macrophages on FLS (MH7A). M1 macrophages mainly secrete TNF-α, IL-12, IL-23, etc., and participate in the body’s anti-tumor function. However, there is still a lack of research on the correlation between M1 macrophages and the proliferative activity of MH7A. Therefore, a co-culture system of M1 macrophages and MH7A cells was constructed to observe and analyze the effect of M1 macrophages on the proliferation of MH7A cells. M1 macrophages and MH7A cells were co-cultured in a Transwell co-culture chamber, and the absorbance was measured at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h. Compared with the independent culture of MH7A cells, the in vitro proliferation ability of co-cultured MH7A cells was reduced (P < 0.05). The proliferation ability of MH7A cells was further stimulated with M1 macrophages culture supernatant, which was similar to the results of co-culture [49]. This indicates that M1 macrophages can inhibit the proliferation of MH7A cells greatly.

According to tumor research results, M1 can inhibit the proliferation of cancer cells; M2 can promote the proliferation and migration of cancer cells in lung cancer, gastric cancer, and colon cancer [50]. In the joint cavity of RA, M1, and M2 coexist, jointly maintaining the inflammatory microenvironment in the joint cavity. This work showed that compared with the independent culture of MH7A, the concentration of TNF-α and IL-12 in the co-culture of M1 macrophages and MH7A cells increased (P < 0.05). It means that the inhibitory effect of M1 macrophages on MH7A cells may be achieved through the regulation of TNF-α and IL-12. In tumor-related studies, it was pointed out that TNF-α can promote the expression of the apoptosis gene Bax, while inhibiting the anti-apoptotic gene Bcl-2 to inhibit the proliferation and apoptosis of tumor cells. IL-12 promotes anti-tumor proliferation by enhancing the activity of NK cells and increasing the expression of IFN-γ. In RA, the specific mechanism of how TNF-α and IL-12 inhibit the proliferation of MH7A cells needs to be further explored.

The limitation of this work was that all experimental results were carried out in vitro cell experiments and were not further explored in small animals. Furthermore, it had not been studied whether M2 macrophages play a role in promoting cell proliferation in MH7A cells and needed to further explore. Future studies will validate the current in vitro findings through in vivo and clinical investigations to further elucidate the mechanistic role of the lncRNA-ANRIL/miR-34a axis in RA. This will involve establishing collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) or monosodium urate (MSU) crystal-induced synovitis models to simulate the RA synovial microenvironment, thereby evaluating the functional mechanisms of this molecular axis in RA pathogenesis. Additionally, the future work will investigate the regulatory effects of M2 macrophages and other inflammatory pathways to comprehensively characterize the role of the ANRIL/miR-34a axis in RA progression.

Taken together, the results of this work indicated that M1 macrophages in the synovial tissue of RA joints greatly inhibit the proliferation of MH7A cells. LncRNA-ANRIL was highly expressed in RA SCs and has the function of inhibiting the apoptosis of RA SCs. The mechanism of this action was related to targeting miR-34a, which inhibited the apoptosis of RA SCs. It provided a new direction for the precise targeted therapy of RA in the future.

Conclusion

This work took RA FLSMH7A as the research object, and investigated the regulation of LncRNA-ANRIL/miR-34a on RA FLSMH7A. This work suggested that M1 macrophages in the synovial tissue of RA joints inhibited the proliferation of MH7A cells. LncRNA-ANRIL was highly expressed in RA SCs, and had the function of inhibiting the apoptosis of RA SCs. The limitation of this work was that all experimental results were carried out in vitro cell experiments and were not further explored in small animals. Therefore, it was necessary to further study the mechanism of action of ANRIL in the RA mouse model in the later stage to provide more convincing animal experimental support for the treatment of LncRNA-ANRIL/miR-34a in RA.

A lightweight deep learning model with attention mechanisms for hypertensive retinopathy classification
Authors: Ruoyu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hypertensive Retinopathy is a common complication of hypertension, requiring timely diagnosis to prevent vision loss. Traditional diagnosis relies on manual examination of fundus images, which is time-consuming and subjective. To address this, we propose MA-DNet, a lightweight deep learning model combining DenseNet with channel and spatial attention mechanisms. Our approach leverages feature enhancement and data balancing techniques to improve classification accuracy. Evaluated on the OIA-ODIR dataset, MA-DNet achieves 95.8 % accuracy, outperforming existing methods. This study demonstrates the potential of attention mechanisms in enhancing HR classification for clinical decision support.

Introduction

Hypertensive retinopathy (HR), a prevalent complication of hypertension, progressively damages retinal vasculature and severely threatens patients' visual health and quality of life [1]. Early and precise identification is critical for timely intervention to mitigate disease progression. Current clinical diagnosis relies predominantly on manual fundus image assessment by ophthalmologists, which is an approach constrained by subjectivity, inefficiency, and diagnostic inconsistency [2]. These limitations exacerbate missed and misdiagnosis rates in resource-limited settings or large-scale screenings, underscoring an urgent need for automated, objective HR classification systems to address healthcare disparities and support early intervention initiatives.

Research in automated HR diagnosis has evolved through sequential methodological advances [[2], [3], [4]]. Initial efforts focused on retinal vascular morphology, exemplified by Neubauer et al. [5] arteriovenous diameter ratio (AVR) quantification via vessel segmentation. Subsequent innovations included artificial neural network for color-texture feature classification by Choi et al. [6] and vascular radial tracing algorithm for enhanced morphological precision by Badawi et al. [7]. Sajid et al. [8] further developed a lightweight HR-related eye disease diagnosis system by integrating a pretrained model and dense blocks. However, predominant reliance on vascular morphometric parameters such as diameter and curvature metrics overlooks critical hypertensive retinopathy manifestations including cotton-wool spots and hemorrhages, while dependence on hand-engineered feature extraction fails to comprehensively characterize complex pathological patterns.

Deep learning breakthroughs in diabetic retinopathy (DR) diagnosis offer promising paradigms for HR [9,10]. Lightweight DenseNet-based models [11] and fusion architectures [12] demonstrate significant potential. Notably, persistent challenges in hypertensive retinopathy diagnosis include three critical constraints: public datasets such as OIA-ODIR are limited by severe sample scarcity of approximately 3.4 percent, significant image heterogeneity, and class imbalance [13]; existing models demonstrate insufficient capability for synergistic representation of multifocal lesions including exudates, hemorrhages, and cotton-wool spots, particularly regarding discrimination between advanced stages such as severe versus proliferative retinopathy [14]; and current methodologies disproportionately focus on vascular morphometrics while neglecting critical lesions and inadequately adapt diabetic retinopathy models to hypertension-specific pathologies such as cotton-wool spots.

To address these limitations, we introduce MA-DNet, a lightweight dual-attention network engineered for hierarchical feature refinement in hypertensive retinopathy diagnosis. The methodology integrates Gaussian-weighted contrast enhancement and geometric augmentation to mitigate data imbalance; employs a DenseNet121 backbone enhanced with SENet modules for channel-wise feature recalibration prior to transition layers, coupled with terminal convolutional block attention modules for spatial-channel optimization; and utilizes stochastic gradient descent optimization using an initial learning rate of 0.001 with cross-entropy loss. Achieving 95.8 percent classification accuracy with only 7.33 million parameters, the model reduces computational complexity by 37 percent and severe misclassification rates by 12.6 percentage points relative to conventional approaches. This framework establishes a resource-efficient diagnostic solution for underserved primary care settings, directly countering clinical inequities in ophthalmic resource distribution.

Experimental data comprised the OIA-ODIR dataset and other public repositories as shown in Fig. 1. This dataset consists of heterogeneous fundus images sourced from multiple medical institutions, encompassing retinal pathologies including diabetic retinopathy (DR), glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration (AMD), and hypertensive retinopathy. Pathological regions in fundus images often exhibit minimal pixel-intensity differentiation from surrounding tissues, leading to model confusion and reduced diagnostic accuracy. Additionally, imaging artifacts including variable illumination, capture angle deviations, and sensor noise further obscure subtle lesions. These factors necessitate feature enhancement to amplify discriminative pathological signatures. Here we employ Gaussian blurring followed by adaptive weighting to augment lesion contrast. As a linear low-pass filter, Gaussian convolution suppresses normally distributed noise through neighborhood pixel weighting governed by 1D and 2D Gaussian kernel formulation as followed:(1)G(x)=12πe−x22σ2(2)G(x)=12πe−x2+y22σ2Where, σ is standard deviation, x2 is center pixel, y2 as neighborhood pixels.

Following Gaussian denoising, we apply contrast enhancement using the OpenCV addWeighted function:(3)Xenhanced=α·Xoriginal+β·XGaussianwhere α = 1.5 controls the weight of original features, β = −0.4 suppresses Gaussian-blurred background interference, and γ = 0 denotes no bias term (validated empirically).

HR classification model design and experimental analysis

Hypertensive retinopathy (HR) classification constitutes a fine-grained recognition task demanding precise extraction of subtle pathological features. DenseNet was adopted as the backbone network due to its dense connectivity resolving gradient vanishing in deep architectures while enabling hierarchical feature reuse essential for microvascular pathology discrimination.

DenseNet backbone network

DenseNet, inspired by ResNet, ingeniously employs dense connections to address the performance degradation issue in deep convolutional networks as their depth increases. The overall structure is illustrated in Fig. 8. The DenseNet model primarily consists of Dense Blocks and Transition Layers. The Dense Blocks handle feature extraction, while the Transition Layers perform downsampling and model compression. The Dense Block module utilizes shortcut connections to address the degradation problem caused by increasing model depth. Within this module, each layer's input incorporates the outputs of all preceding layers, while also transmitting its own output to every subsequent layer. This structure is depicted in Fig. 9. Although resembling traditional shortcut connections, it differs significantly: features are first combined via concatenation before being fed into convolutional layers. Consequently, the output at layer l consists of feature maps from all preceding convolutional blocks. Similarly, layer l's output propagates to all subsequent layers, creating l(l+1)/2 network pathways. Notably, Dense Block employs feature concatenation, whereas traditional shortcuts use element-wise addition.Fig. 9Shortcut connections.Fig. 9

Shortcut connections.

Let the input to the convolutional network be denoted as x, and the depth of the network be l layers. The function Hl(.) represents a nonlinear transformation at layer l, comprising sequential operations such as convolution, pooling, and normalization. Between layers l and l−1, multiple convolutional layers may exist, and xl denotes the output of layer l. The output of a traditional layer l is given by:(5)xl=Hl(xl−1)In ResNet, residual connections are employed to address the degradation problem in deep networks by combining the input and output of preceding layers. The output of the l-th layer is formulated as:(6)xl=Hl(xl−1)+xl−1In DenseNet, the utilization of Dense Blocks enhances information flow between convolutional layers by propagating features to all subsequent layers. This mechanism is formally expressed as(7)xl=Hl([x0,x1,⋯,xl−1])Where, [x0,x1, …,xl-1] denotes concatenating the feature maps from layer 0 to layer l−1 along the channel dimension.

Compared to other models, the most prominent feature of DenseNet lies in its exhaustive utilization of output from all preceding feature layers. Consequently, the configuration of convolutional filters per layer differs fundamentally from conventional architectures. Within the Dense Block module, every layer maintains an identical number of convolutional filters, known as the growth rate k. Each layer aggregates outputs from all previous layers, thereby capturing holistic network information and effectively mitigating degradation issues inherent to deep architectures.(2)Transition layer module

(2)Transition layer module

Transition layer module

In the Dense Block module, each convolutional layer generates feature maps with k channels, where the input to layer l comprises concatenated outputs from all preceding layers, resulting in k0+k(l−1) input channels, where k0 denotes the initial channel count. As network depth increases, the exponentially growing input dimensionality significantly escalates computational complexity. To mitigate this, the Transition Layer module employs a BN-ReLU-1 × 1 convolutional layer prior to the BN-ReLU-3 × 3 convolution, strategically compressing channels from l × k to k. This design reduces model computations while maintaining representational capacity. Additionally, a 2 × 2 average pooling layer performs spatial downsampling, further optimizing computational efficiency by diminishing feature resolution prior to subsequent processing stages.

The DenseNet architecture alternately employs Dense Blocks and Transition Layers, concluding with a global average pooling operation that flattens the output, followed by a fully connected layer for final classification. Building upon this framework, the MA-DNet model in this study adopts DenseNet-121—a computationally efficient variant of DenseNet—as its feature extraction backbone, leveraging its distinctive feature reuse capability to address Hypertensive Retinopathy (HR) classification. Specifically, after the input layer, the architecture sequentially connects four Dense Blocks with layer counts 6, 12, 24, and 16 respectively interlaced with three Transition Layers, terminating in an averaging pooling layer and classification fully connected layer.(3)Feature refinement module

(3)Feature refinement module

Feature refinement module

The MA-DNet model proposed in this work employs DenseNet-121 as its feature extraction backbone, integrating both SENet and CBAM modules to enhance feature refinement capabilities, minimize critical feature loss, and optimize classification performance. As illustrated in Fig. 10, SENet modules are inserted after each DenseBlock but before Transition Layers to recalibrate channel-wise features prior to dimensionality reduction. This strategic placement mitigates information degradation during compression. By leveraging global feature squeezing and a gating mechanism, SENet dynamically weights channel-wise features, prioritizing salient markers while mitigating overfitting and expanding receptive fields without compromising efficiency. Complementarily, the CBAM module combines channel attention similar to SENet's channel recalibration and spatial attention mechanisms, collectively capturing cross-dimensional feature importance. As illustrated in Fig. 11, CBAM first applies channel attention via parallel max/average pooling and shared MLP weighting, followed by spatial attention that fuses pooled features through convolutional layers to generate position-sensitive masks. This dual-path design recalibrates features along both channel and spatial axes, with channel attention emphasizing diagnostically relevant patterns, and spatial attention suppressing irrelevant backgrounds.Fig. 10SENet location.Fig. 10Fig. 11CBAM location.Fig. 11

In MA-DNet, CBAM is strategically inserted after the fourth DenseBlock and before global average pooling. Features extracted by the backbone undergo sequential channel-spatial recalibration through CBAM, followed by element-wise summation to fuse attentional cues into consolidated representations. Despite introducing negligible parameter overhead, this integrated attention framework significantly enriches feature expressiveness between late-stage DenseBlocks and the classification head, reducing information dissipation and improving hypertension retinopathy grading accuracy by 4.7–6.2 % against non-attention baselines.(4)The optimization of hyperparameters

The optimization of hyperparameters

Manually configured parameters that govern model performance and training dynamics—is essential for developing optimal models. Key hyperparameters include the learning rate, training epochs, batch size, loss function, and optimizer, each directly or indirectly influencing training efficacy and speed. The learning rate critically determines convergence stability: excessively high rates impede convergence, while excessively low rates decelerate it, thereby affecting the model's ability to approach global minima. Training epochs define data iteration cycles, and batch size specifies sample quantity per training step; both require empirical calibration. Loss functions quantify disparities between predicted and true labels, with problem-specific selection dictated by task requirements and computational characteristics. Optimizers (e.g., Adam, SGD, Momentum) guide parametric updates to accelerate convergence and enhance efficiency during large-scale training. Conclusion

This study establishes that the MA-DNet framework significantly advances hypertensive retinopathy classification through hierarchical attention mechanisms, achieving 95.8 % diagnostic accuracy on the OIA-ODIR dataset. The integration of SENet-mediated channel recalibration prior to transition layers and terminal CBAM-based spatial-channel optimization enables precise localization of critical lesions including microaneurysms and cotton-wool spots, reducing severe misclassification by 12.6 percentage points. Gaussian-weighted contrast enhancement combined with geometric augmentation effectively mitigates data imbalance, increasing hypertensive retinopathy sample representation from 3.4 % to 52.9 % while preserving pathological authenticity.

These innovations yield a computationally frugal solution with only 7.33 million parameters, decreasing model complexity by 37 % relative to conventional approaches. The framework demonstrates particular efficacy in resource-constrained settings by enabling accurate screening without specialized hardware, directly addressing healthcare disparities in ophthalmologic access.

While MA-DNet achieved high specificity by 96.3 %, two false negatives occurred in early-stage HR cases with subtle microaneurysms. Future work will incorporate longitudinal analysis to improve early detection. We validated deployability on low-cost devices (Raspberry Pi 4), achieving 2.3s inference time per image – suitable for resource-limited clinics.

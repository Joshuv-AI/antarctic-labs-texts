Perspectives of Femoroacetabular Impingement Syndrome: A Bibliometric Analysis
Authors: Alexander David Puyol, Charlotte Ganderton, Matthew King, Oren Tirosh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFemoroacetabular impingement syndrome (FAIS) is a risk factor for future hip osteoarthritis and is prevalent in both athletic and non-athletic populations. This bibliometric review aimed to understand the history of FAIS and provide detailed information for future FAIS research. The Clarivate Analytics Web of Science Core Collection was used to search and obtain all studies between 2020 and 2025 using bibliometrix (ver. 3.0.4) and R package software. Between 2020 and 2025, 1,486 articles were published on FAIS. There was an overall 41.3% downward trend in published research during this period. Furthermore, recent key terms include “physical impairments” and “muscle strength”. Institutions and surgical authors across North America, Europe, and Australia published the highest volume of research papers. Across keywords, femoroacetabular impingement and osteoarthritis had the strongest relationship. FAIS research continues to be led by surgical authors within surgical journals. While surgical key terms have been historically prevalent within the existing literature, research aligning with more conservative management has started to emerge. Despite an increase in physiotherapy researchers, inter-disciplinary collaboration remains limited and concentrated in western countries.Keywords: Hip, Femoroacetabular impingement, Rehabilitation

INTRODUCTION

Femoroacetabular impingement syndrome (FAIS) is a common cause of hip, groin, thigh and lower back pain in both athletic and non-athletic populations1,2). It involves the presence of an extraphyseal bony growth at the anterolateral femoral head-neck junction, or Cam morphology, that causes premature contact between the proximal femur and the acetabulum during hip flexion and internal rotation type-movements3-6). There is a much higher risk (odds ratio=3.67) of developing end-stage hip osteoarthritis (OA) for individuals diagnosed with FAIS which may lead to total hip replacement surgery (THR)7,8). Although the condition was first described in 1936, a marked increase in research on the diagnosis and clinical care was not seen until 2001, when Ganz et al.9) highlighted the connection between Cam morphology and future hip OA1,9).

Over the past two decades, a plethora of research into FAIS has been published10). Study designs have included prospective cohort studies, randomised control trials and systematic reviews, and have explored the pathoanatomical cause, associated impairments and possible treatments relating to FAIS11-15). Through the investigation of geographical hotspots, leading institutions, authors and keywords, a bibliometric analysis examines the history and future direction of FAIS16-19). Previous bibliometric analysis on FAIS research between 2000 and 2019 showed a progressive increase from 2000 in the number of yearly FAIS publications, with a sharp increase between 2009 and 2013 and a peak in 201817). Though, since the completion of this search in 2019, FAIS research has evolved from a predominantly surgical focus20) to include conservative rehabilitation interventions for FAIS management10,21,22). Therefore, by investigating research trends between 2020 and 2025, this bibliometric study intends to build on prior bibliometric analysis17) and analyze these changes from prior study findings17) to inform future research directions.

STUDY DESIGN

1. Search Strategy & Data Collection

Bibliometric analysis was performed as per previously published methods17). This method was utilized to provide researchers with a more accurate, reliable and consistent search strategy for future bibliometric studies. The key words (topic search=(((“femoroacetabular impingement”) or (“femoracetabular impingement”) or (“Femoro-Acetabular Impingement”) or (“Femoro Acetabular Impingement”))) were input and searched in the Clarivate Analytics Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) on 30 January, 2025. The results were refined by setting the document type to “articles”. The Web of Science index type was set as “Science Citation Index Expanded” (SCI-EXPANDED) and “Social Sciences Citation Index” (SSCI). All peer-reviewed articles between 2020 and 2025 in any language from WosCC were included. Review articles, editorial materials, proceedings papers, meeting abstracts, letters, book chapters, early access articles, corrected and retracted publications were excluded. The search was run to pertain to articles published between 2020 and 2025.

These articles were all examined for citation and bibliographical information, abstract, keywords, and references. Citations were normalized to account for the number of years since publication.

2. Data Analysis

R package software (ver. 2024.09.1+394) was first used to analyze for duplicates. R-package software and bibliometrix (ver. 3.0.4)23) were then used to analyze: (i) the total number of overall articles and publication growth of FAIS research, (ii) the number of FAIS articles published each year, (iii) journals with the highest number of publications and citations, (iv) the most cited documents, authors and their associated country, region and institution, (v) a cluster analysis of professional collaborations between authors, and (vi) the highest trending FAIS topics, keywords and evolution of word frequency over time. Data was also exported from bibliometrix23) to VOSviewer (ver. 1.6.20)24), to analyze and separate keyword clusters into distinct groups. Specific keywords were represented by nodes and connected by links which represented a co-occurrence between keywords. The minimum cluster size was set to 20.

Data pertaining to the years 2020-2025 were analyzed to establish the key trends in research output over the past five years. The categorical and numerical data were extracted from bibliometrix, along with their respective graphical forms, to address this study’s primary aim.

RESULTS

Between 2020 and 2025, 1,486 distinct articles were published. Subsequent to the 302 papers published in 2020, 354 articles were published in the 2021 calendar year. This was the highest number of articles produced globally over the past two decades. However, this peak was followed by a reduction in FAIS research output between 2022 and 2025. Comparatively, 307, 240, and 295 articles were published in the years 2022, 2023, and, 2024, respectively (Fig. 1). An overall 41.3% decline in published articles was seen between 2020 and 2025.

1. Journal Analysis

Between 2020 and 2025, 189 journals published 1,486 articles. The top three publishing journals during that five year period included The American Journal of Sports Medicine (n=196, 13.2%), Arthroscopy: The Journal of Arthroscopic and Related Surgery (n=186, 12.5%) and Orthopaedic Journal of Sports Medicine (n=123, 8.3%) (Fig. 2). The journals with the most citations, or the number of times that a journal’s published papers were referenced by other manuscripts within the dataset between 2020 and 2025, were The American Journal of Sports Medicine (n=7,070), Arthroscopy: The Journal of Arthroscopic and Related Surgery (n=6,776) and Clinical Orthopaedics and Related Research (n=5,015). The journals with the highest local impact (normalized local citation score [NLCS]), which considers each journal’s individualized influence within the dataset (normalized to publication year), were The American Journal of Sports Medicine (NLCS: 23) and Arthroscopy: The Journal of Arthroscopic and Related Surgery (NLCS: 20) over the past five years.

Trends in the publication quantity per journal between 2020 and 2025.

2. Most Cited Documents

The most cited paper since 2020, titled “Defining the clinically meaningful outcomes for arthroscopic treatment of femoroacetabular impingement syndrome at minimum 5-year follow-up” by Nwachukwu et al.15) was published in The American Journal of Sports Medicine with 110 citations and carries an average of 18.33 citations per year since publication. This was followed by “Consensus recommendations on the classification, definition and diagnostic criteria of hip-related pain in young and middle-aged active adults from the International Hip-related Pain Research Network, Zurich 2018” by Reiman et al.2) in the British Journal of Sports Medicine with 91 citations; and “The incidence of hip arthroscopy in patients with femoroacetabular impingement syndrome and labral pathology increased by 85% between 2011 and 2018 in the United States” by Zusmanovich et al.25) published in Arthroscopy: The Journal of Arthroscopic and Related Surgery with 76 citations.

When normalized to account for the number of years since publication, Zusmanovich et al.25) had the highest total normalized global citations (NC: 14.78). This was followed by “Development of a novel system for measuring femoroacetabular contact forces in hip arthroscopy” by Gonçalves et al.26) and “Hip arthroscopy patients with lower back pain show delayed clinical improvement and inferior time-dependent survivorship: a propensity matched study at mid-term follow-up” by Kazi et al.27), both carrying a total normalized global citations score of 10.50.

3. Contribution by Authors

During the five-year period studied, 4,574 authors contributed to the 1,486 articles published between 2020 and 2025. Representing 7.5% of the overall published FAIS studies since 2020, S.J. Nho (Rush University Medical Center) had the highest output with 111 published articles. This was followed by B.G. Domb (University of Chicago) with 105 articles (7.1%), and D.R. Maldonado (The University of Texas) with 89 published papers (6.0%). Of the published authors, Nho had the highest impact measure (h-index: 16), followed by Domb (h-index: 15), Lall (h-index: 14), and Maldonado (h-index: 14). Domb was the most cited author with 506 citations, followed by Nho (480 citations) and Maldonado (422 citations).

4. Country, Region, and Institution

Between 2020 and 2025, a total of 56 countries produced FAIS articles (Fig. 3). The USA had the highest number of scientific publications (n=2,349), followed by Australia (n=361) and Switzerland (n=348). The USA was also the country with the highest number of corresponding authors, followed by China and Switzerland. These countries produced 629, 104, and 56 papers, respectively, between 2020 and 2025. The USA had the highest number of citations (n=5,100), followed by Canada (n=531) and Switzerland (n=519).

Worldwide country scientific production.

The highest number of cross-country collaborations all involved the USA. For example, the USA and Switzerland published 31 papers in collaboration, followed by the USA and Canada (n=28) and the USA and Australia (n=26). The highest non-US country collaboration was between Australia and the UK (n=25). Finally, outside of the US, Rush University published the highest number of articles (n=174, 11.7%), followed by University of Bern (n=143, 9.6%) and University of Bern Hospital (n=103, 6.9%).

DISCUSSION

1. Overview of Study Findings

This study highlights research trends in FAIS over the last five years. The most significant finding was an overall 41.3% decline in FAIS research output between 2020 and 2025. The second significant finding, most notably since 2021, was that keywords traditionally associated with conservative management for FAIS have begun to trend in the literature. The American Journal of Sports Medicine published the highest number of articles. This coincided with the highest number of citations and impact factor across all journals. The USA published the highest number of articles, and had the highest number of international collaborations and corresponding authors. The author collaboration network showed that, although there was some inter-disciplinary collaboration between physiotherapists and orthopedic surgeons, most of the collaborations over the past five years were between orthopedic surgeons, medical doctors, and researchers alone. Although this highlights that the research field is changing to include more non-surgical and medical-based healthcare professionals, authors mostly collaborate within their own professional circles. A more holistic FAIS literature base may be supported by increases in cross-professional collaborations, which may include both surgical and non-surgical treatment and management approaches.

2. Yearly Article Production

The results within this bibliometric analysis hold similarities and differences to Tang et al.’s study17), exploration of trends within the FAIS literature between 2000 and 2019. The most notable difference was the decrease in article production since 2022. Tang et al.17) reported a yearly increase in publications between 2000 and 2019. However, this analysis found a 41.3% decline in publications since 2020 despite the publication of a 2016 consensus statement expressing the need for more research in FAIS1,5). The output of FAIS research is important to help to ensure that healthcare clinicians continue to receive evidence-based guidance to improve the management and treatment of their patients. The possibility exists that the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, which occurred between 2000 and 2022, contributed to the decline in FAIS research output between 2022 and 202528,29). The impact of COVID-19 may be condition-specific, given that some bibliometric analyses show similar trends in other areas of musculoskeletal health30) whilst others do not show a decline in publication output during this period31).

3. Keyword Topic Trends

The second significant difference was the change in trending keywords. Tang et al.17) explored periods of citation bursts amongst keywords, finding predominantly surgical and structural terms. These included “acetabular labrum” (between 2002 and 2011), “surgical hip dislocation” (between 2007 and 2012), and “labral repair” (between 2018 and 2019)17). Conversely, the keyword topic trends used in this study found that the terms “physical impairments” and “muscle strength”, have begun trending over the past five years. This is of key significance to the field of research as conservative treatment is included in FAIS management in consensus statements1). Furthermore, recently published randomized control trials and systematic reviews have examined the role of non-surgical, physiotherapy-led management to treat patients with FAIS10,21,22). Future research should continue to include studies that explore non-surgical approaches to FAIS management. This added diversification, combined with historically and current surgical-related research trends, may ultimately provide a greater benefit to the research and to patients diagnosed with FAIS.

4. Leading Authors and Journals

This bibliometric analysis found that surgical authors and journals continue to lead the FAIS literature body. These findings, similar to that of Tang et al.17), found that The American Journal of Sports Medicine continues to be ranked in the top three in terms of publications and impact factor. The highest published authors in this study included Nho, Domb, and Maldonado. This result is similar to that of previous years which show Domb, Philippon, and Nho, to be the leading authors between 2000 and 201917). These authors are all orthopedic surgeons who have been leaders in the FAIS field for the past two and half decades.

5. Collaboration Network

Although this study found that there is an emerging trend of physiotherapy authors potentially examining conservative modalities, there continues to be an inter-disciplinary segregation amongst researchers. Surgeons are crucial in delivering healthcare outcomes and their research holds strong merit within the FAIS literature. Even so, the involvement of a wider multi-disciplinary network of health professionals who explore more holistic forms of FAIS management could help to strengthen the current body of evidence.

6. Country Collaboration

Country collaboration data shows a Western dominance in research. This study found that, when compared to Tang et al.17), North America, Western Europe, and Australia continue to have the highest number of authors and publications. However, there remains a lower diversification across geographical locations. In contrast, knee OA, frozen shoulder, and ankle arthroscopy research examine a broader geographical landscape which includes Asia, South America, and the Middle East32-34).

7. Keyword Cluster Analysis

Cluster analysis shows the importance of the keyword relationship between “femoroacetabular impingement” and “OA”. This is most likely due to FAIS leading to increased risk of developing hip joint OA in the future7,8). This has subsequently led to a plethora of publications exploring this relationship8,35,36). Despite this being a fundamental part of FAIS research, the prevalence of hip OA and the number of THR surgeries have increased significantly over the past 30 years37). The new trend in the keywords “physical impairments” and “muscle strength” may be evidence of an emerging shift in the research to help address this problem.

8. Current Research Gaps and Recommendations for Future Research

This bibliometric analysis explored the trends in the FAIS literature over the past five years and made comparisons to previous published analyses over the past two decades17). Significant research gaps which have been highlighted by this analysis allow for future research recommendations. Firstly, as longitudinal studies explore how non-structural and modifiable (i.e., strength, hip range of motion and balance) risk factors contribute to the development of FAIS, this may help healthcare professionals understand the role of physical impairments resulting from this condition. This recommendation aligns with current shifts towards more conservative approaches and has been prioritized for future research in recent consensus statements5). Understanding these risks would help plan and guide future high-quality randomized control studies to explore conservative FAIS treatments. Future research may greatly benefit from more diversity geographically and among inter-disciplinary research teams who utilize both surgical and conservative research approaches. With the inclusion of both surgical and non-surgical patient cohorts in research, outcomes for people living with this condition could be maximized.

9. Limitations

This study’s limitations need to be acknowledged despite the results found within this bibliometric analysis. Firstly, the search strategy used only one database to align with methodology demonstrated in a single, prior bibliometric analysis17). As such, FAIS research papers not published in Web of Science may have been excluded. The inclusion of additional databases may have increased the number of sourced articles related to FAIS, thus altering the findings. Secondly, it is acknowledged that the found manuscripts do not carry a methodological appraisal even though this is not within the scope of the bibliometric analysis. Therefore, the quality of evidence within this bibliometric analysis is unclear. Other limitations include potential delays between manuscript publication and indexing on the Web of Science database. This, in combination with using only English language key search terms, may have reduced the number of articles included within the review, particularly those published in a different language. Finally, due to the quantitative data analysis, the positive impact that this research may have had on patients, policy and healthcare pathways is unclear. When interpreting the findings of this analysis, this lack of clarity should be considered by researchers and healthcare professionals.

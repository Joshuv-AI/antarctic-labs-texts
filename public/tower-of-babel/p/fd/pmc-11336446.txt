Study on the fractional creep model of roadway filling paste under the coupling effect of pore water pressure and stress
Authors: Hongmiao Lv
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Filling paste in deep roadways is significantly affected by groundwater, especially high pore water pressure, which increases the complexity and variability of the filling paste's mechanical properties. To explore the creep characteristics and long-term stability of filling paste under varying pore water pressure, the MTS815.03 test system is used to conduct creep tests under different pore water pressures and stress. Then the creep deformation of the filling paste under the complex pressure field of static pore water pressures is analyzed. Finally, through the one-to-one correspondence between the numerical simulation of the creep model and the characteristic points of the creep curve, a method for determining the creep parameters under the complex pressure field of static pore water pressures is proposed. Results show that the creep test curves of filling paste under different pore water pressures and stress are in good agreement with the model curves. This shows that the creep constitutive model in this research can better reflect the whole process of creep deformation of filling paste. The result also verifies the rationality of the proposed method to determine creep model parameters. The newly proposed creep model can effectively compensate for the traditional Nishihara model, which is inability to reflect the acceleration of creep and can more accurately describe the creep characteristics of the primary and steady-state creep stages.

Introduction

Mineral resources are important basic resources in national construction and provide sufficient impetus for the development of the national economy [1]. However, with the continuous exploitation of mineral resources and the gradual expansion of the mining scale, large numbers of goaves and tailings ponds have also been formed. These areas can lead to safety production accidents, such as goaf collapses and tailings dam breaks [[2], [3], [4]]. These hazards pose significant risks to the safety of mine production and to the lives and property of surrounding communities. Filling is a mining method that reduces surface subsidence rate, improves the stope stress environment, and reduces tailings discharge [5,6]. Given its ability to effectively solve the above problems, it has achieved rapid development and wide application in China. The primary development direction of filling technology is in paste-filling mining, which is currently the preferred scheme in mining design. The cemented filling body in the stope is not damaged at the beginning. However, during the mining process, the deformation of the filling body gradually develops over time due to stress redistribution or the deterioration of geological conditions [7,8]. Therefor, the above problems lead to the instability of the filling material which in turn can cause safety issues. Various engineering practices show that the time effect plays a crucial role in the failure and instability process of the filling material. In order to ensure the stability of the filling mining structure, it is necessary to consider the design and analysis of the stability of the filling body. That is, it is necessary to ensure the stability of the filling body in the process of advancing the coal mining face and the long-term stability after the completion of coal mining. At the same time, creep deformation runs through the entire filling mining process [[9], [10], [11]]. The filling body produces short-term creep deformation as coal mining work gradually advances as well as due to the constant load of the overlying roof that the filling material will undergo long-term creep deformation after mining is completed [12,13]. When the creep deformation is too large, it causes instability in the filling body, which in turn affects the safety of the roof. Therefore, the creep behavior of the backfill must be considered in backfill design.

In recent years, both domestic and foreign research have achieved fruitful results in the study of creep constitutive model and the creep deformation mechanism of roadway filling paste. Sun et al. [14] used the by step loading method to study the creep deformation mechanism of cemented backfill under dynamic disturbance. They found that dynamic disturbances have a significant impact during the creep process of the filler material.In particular, an increase in the disturbance impact energy shortened the time for the filling body to reach ultimate failure. Wang et al. [15] carried out uniaxial creep tests on cemented coal gangue backfill under acid mine drainage corrosion. Experimental results show that the creep process of coal gangue backfill immersed in acid mine drainage was similar to that of untreated backfill. Therefore, it was determined that the drainage from acidic mines has a minor impact on the long-term strength of the backfill material.Guo et al. [16] studied the axial creep characteristics of early-age backfill under graded loading. Hou et al. [17] studied the creep characteristics of gangue aggregate cemented backfill under true triaxial conditions and found that the gangue aggregate cemented backfill has obvious rheological properties. The creep model under the coupling of stress and seepage was established based on the test data. The above research has greatly enriched creep model research in the geotechnical field. However, few models can characterize the creep characteristics of paste backfill under seepage-creep coupling. Therefore, constructing the corresponding seepage-creep damage model is of great research significance.

In this study, the mechanism of creep deformation of filling paste under seepage-stress coupling is discussed by combining laboratory tests and theoretical research. The seepage-creep model of filling paste is constructed. The total strain is separated to determine the mutual influence between creep parameters and pore water pressure and stress.It analyzed the variation law of creep parameters under the coupled effects of seepage-stress coupling fields. By incorporating the above relationships into the seepage creep model, we obtain a modified creep model. Through comparison of experimental and model curves, the correctness and rationality of the new model are validated. Finally, the creep properties of filling paste under seepage-creep coupling are revealed.

Isochronous stress–strain curve

The isochronous stress–strain curve is a curve describing the strain of the material under stress in material mechanics. The curve represents the relationship between stress and strain under conditions of equal time (maintaining the same stress application duration). At the beginning of stress application, the law of strain and stress curve is basically linear proportional. At this time, when the external force is removed, the filling paste can be completely restored to its initial state, and it will not deform permanently. As the stress increases, the stress begins to increase continuously with the increase of strain. At this stage, the relationship between stress and strain of the filling paste presents a nonlinear law [21]. At this time, the filling paste undergoes reversible plastic deformation and irreversible plastic deformation. At the same time, the isochronous stress-strain curves begin to diverge at a certain critical stress point.According to the literature [22], The divergence point marks the transition of the material's behavior from linear to nonlinear performance.The stress value at this point can be considered the long-term strength value of the material. The abscissa is generally the applied external stress load value, and the ordinate is the strain value corresponding to the stress at a certain moment. A fractional-order creep model that can describe the law of accelerated deformation is established. Based on the one-to-one correspondence between the model and each stage of the creep curve, the instantaneous strain, primary creep, steady-state creep, and accelerated creep deformation are separated individually. The creep parameters under different conditions are calculated by one-to-one correspondence. By substituting the relationships between the creep parameters and the condition parameters into the traditional model, the modified creep constitutive model is obtained. The following section describes the establishment of a modified elastic model, modified viscoelastic model, modified viscoplastic model, and modified fractional order dashpot.

Conclusions

The modified creep model aligns with the creep curve variations when the pore water pressure is 0, 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 MPa. The analysis of the creep test curves and model curves for the paste under different pore water pressures and stresses demonstrates that the modified creep constitutive model can more accurately reflect the deformation mechanisms throughout the entire creep process of the paste.

The applicability of the modified creep constitutive model is effectively validated using conventional triaxial compression creep test data of the paste under different stresses and pore water pressures. Results show that this model not only accurately captures the creep characteristics of the primary and steady-state stages but also addresses the limitations of traditional creep models, which often struggle to describe accelerated creep.

The creep model can be employed to forecast the deformation trends of filling paste in deep roadways over time and offer a theoretical foundation for surrounding rock support and lining construction. At the same time, combined with engineering examples, the numerical simulation analysis of the deformation of filling paste under different stress and pore water pressure can be carried out to provide suggestions for the rationality and safety of the construction process.

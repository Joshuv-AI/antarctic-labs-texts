A first-in-human Phase 1/1b randomized trial evaluating the safety and immunogenicity of a respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) virus-like particle subunit vaccine (IVX-121) in healthy young and older adults
Authors: Ilse De Coster, Isabel Leroux-Roels, Kanchanamala Withanage, Leen Suykens, Valentino D’Onofrio, Geert Leroux-Roels, Pierre van Damme, Max Ciarlet, Elizabeth M. Adams, Wasima Rida, Judy Wen, Margaret MacDonald, Jennifer Price, Niranjan Kanesa-thasan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTRespiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of lower respiratory tract disease in all age groups, with substantial burden in older adults. Here, we report the safety and immunogenicity of IVX-121, an investigational virus-like particle vaccine against RSV. This Phase 1/1b trial (EudraCT number: 2020-003633-38) randomized 90 healthy young (18–45 y of age; Phase 1) and 130 older adults (60–75 y of age; Phase 1b) to receive a single intramuscular injection of IVX-121 at low (25 µg), medium (75 µg), or high (250 µg) dose, with/without the adjuvant aluminum hydroxide (Al[OH]3), or placebo. Outcomes were assessed through Day 180. Older adult participants who had received IVX-121 or placebo could continue into an extension study in which safety was assessed for an additional 6 months before receiving IVX-121 (75 µg, unadjuvanted, open-label) on Day 365; safety and immunogenicity were assessed up to Day 546. Adverse reactions were generally mild, and there were no vaccine-related serious adverse events. All IVX-121 dose levels and formulations boosted preexisting RSV neutralizing antibody (nAb) responses. Although not statistically powered to compare treatment groups, there was no clear dose response and the adjuvant Al(OH)3 did not appear to improve immunogenicity. For both age groups, IVX-121 induced nAbs against RSV A and B, which remained above baseline levels for at least 6 months. A second dose of IVX-121 in older adults elicited a modest increase in nAbs against RSV A but not RSV B. Overall, IVX-121 had a favorable safety profile and induced immune responses against RSV in young and older adults.

Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a single-stranded, negative sense ribonucleic acid (RNA) virus belonging to the Orthopneumovirus genus of the family Pneumoviridae, with two major subtypes, RSV A and RSV B.1 RSV is one of the most common causes of lower respiratory tract disease (LRTD) in infants and young children and causes a substantial burden in adults, particularly those who are older or have comorbidities.2–5 In the US alone, RSV causes approximately 110,000–180,000 hospitalizations annually in adults ≥50 y of age.6 Furthermore, hospitalized adults with RSV typically have worse outcomes and longer hospital stays than those hospitalized for other respiratory viruses, such as influenza.7,8

The primary goal of vaccination against RSV is to boost neutralizing antibodies (nAbs), which remain the best available correlates of protection.9 Current RSV vaccines provide the strongest protection against severe outcomes rather than asymptomatic or mild infections.10–12 RSV infection elicits nAbs that mainly target the prefusion conformation of the viral fusion protein (pre-F).13 There are currently three RSV vaccines licensed by the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, which contain or encode stabilized RSV pre-F antigen.14–20 The approved indications of these vaccines include adults ≥60 y of age, and younger adults at increased risk for LRTD.14–19 There remains a need for effective RSV interventions that are accessible to a broader population to help reduce the burden of RSV-related diseases.

Virus-like particles (VLPs) are self-assembling protein structures that resemble viruses but lack genetic material. They form the basis of several licensed vaccines, including those against hepatitis B, human papillomavirus, and chikungunya, all of which have shown high efficacy and durable immunogenicity.21–26 The VLP platform provides several additional features that enhance immunogenicity compared to conventional vaccines.21,27 VLPs are believed to enhance immune responses by mimicking the size and geometry of viruses, thereby facilitating antigen presentation and T-helper cell responses. Licensed VLP vaccines have shown greater durability compared to mRNA vaccines.28 IVX-121 is a VLP vaccine multivalently displaying a stabilized DS-Cav1 RSV A pre-F protein on the exterior surface of a two-component protein nanoparticle (I53-50).29–31 The nanoparticle has an icosahedral symmetry constructed from trimeric (I53-50A) and pentameric (I53-50B) components. Each IVX-121 VLP displays 20 copies of the stabilized RSV pre-F trimer; the high-density antigen display facilitates B cell activation, enhancing the immune response.

Here, we present the results of a first-in-human Phase 1/1b trial of the RSV vaccine IVX-121. At the time of trial initiation in 2021, no licensed RSV vaccines were available. The trial objectives were to evaluate the safety and immunogenicity of IVX-121 in healthy younger (18–45 y) and older adults (60–75 y); determine the optimal formulation of the vaccine; and assess the safety and immunogenicity of revaccination with IVX-121 in older adults.

Methods

Trial design

This randomized, placebo-controlled, observer-blind Phase 1/1b trial was conducted at three sites in Belgium. The trial consisted of three parts (Figure 1). Part 1 (August 26, 2021, to May 12, 2022) was a Phase 1, first-in-human assessment of IVX-121 in healthy adults 18–45 y of age. Part 2 (November 17, 2021, to August 10, 2022) was a Phase 1b evaluation in healthy older adults 60–75 y of age. Part 3 (June 7, 2022, to August 10, 2023) was a Phase 1b open-label extension evaluating longer-term safety and immunogenicity, together with revaccination of older adults who participated in Part 2. In Parts 1 and 2, participants were randomized equally to one of the seven vaccination groups to receive a single dose of IVX-121 25 µg, 75 µg, or 250 µg, with/without the adjuvant 500 µg aluminum hydroxide (Al[OH]3; Alhydrogel, Croda, Frederikssund, Denmark), or placebo. Cell-mediated immunity (CMI) assessments were performed at the two sites with capabilities for cell collection, in a subset of randomly selected participants comprising approximately 20% of the total study participants. The Phase 1b extension (Part 3) was conducted only at these two sites. Figure 1.Study design.

Study design.

The trial was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of Good Clinical Practice, according to the ICH Harmonised Guidelines. All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment. The protocol and its amendments were approved by an independent ethics committee (Ethics Committee Research UZ/KU Leuven) prior to commencement of the trial. The trial was registered at EudraCT (2020-003633-38).

Participants

Eligible participants were healthy adults, 18–45 y of age for Part 1 and 60–75 y of age for Part 2, with body mass index of 17–35 kg/m2 at screening. Key exclusion criteria were: prior receipt of an investigational RSV vaccine; high-risk comorbidities for RSV disease; frailty; acute illness at time of vaccination; or progressive, unstable, or uncontrolled clinical conditions. Full details of eligibility criteria are provided in the Supplementary Methods. Participants who completed Part 2 could continue to Part 3 if they met the eligibility criteria for continued enrollment.

Randomization and masking procedures are detailed in the Supplementary Methods.

Vaccine

A 0.5 mL dose of IVX-121 or placebo was administered via intramuscular injection (deltoid, non-dominant arm). The vaccine was formulated at 250 µg/0.5 mL with aqueous diluent or adsorbed to 500 µg Al(OH)3 as an adjuvant. The lower dosage levels (25 µg and 75 µg) were prepared by dilution just prior to administration using either aqueous or Al(OH)3 diluent. Specific instructions for extemporaneous preparation of the vaccine were provided in the pharmacy manual. Placebo was aqueous diluent.

Procedures

In Parts 1 and 2, participants were randomized 6:6:1 to receive IVX-121 with/without 500 µg Al(OH)3 or placebo with the dose escalating from 25 µg to 75 µg to 250 µg. Dose escalation followed a sentinel approach (see Supplementary Methods).

Participants who received IVX-121 or placebo in Part 2 at the two sites performing cellular collections could continue into the open-label extension (Part 3), which included 6 months of additional safety monitoring after Day 180 before administration of a single dose of unadjuvanted IVX-121 at 75 µg on Day 365. This dose level was selected after analyses of interim data from Parts 1 and 2. The sentinel dosing approach is described in the Supplementary Methods. Following vaccination on Day 365, participants were followed up until Day 393 (28 d post-vaccination) for collection of initial safety and immunogenicity data and then until Day 546 for longer-term data.

Outcomes and assessments

The primary safety outcomes were serious adverse events (SAEs), medically attended adverse events (MAAEs), adverse events (AEs) leading to trial withdrawal, solicited local and systemic adverse reactions (ARs), and unsolicited AEs. Solicited ARs were collected for 7 d post-vaccination (Days 0–6 in Parts 1 and 2; Days 365–372 in Part 3). Unsolicited AEs were collected up to 28 d post-vaccination (Days 0–28 in Parts 1 and 2; Days 365–393 in Part 3). SAEs, MAAEs, and AEs leading to withdrawal were collected from Day 0 to trial end. Severity grading for ARs and AEs is detailed in the Supplementary Methods. Secondary safety outcomes included the incidence of moderate-to-severe lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI; AE of special interest [AESI]; defined in the Supplementary Methods) up to trial end and clinical laboratory parameters up to 28 d post-vaccination (Day 28 in Parts 1 and 2; Day 393 in Part 3). The occurrence of LRTIs was monitored via a collection of nasopharyngeal swabs from participants with symptoms of acute respiratory infection for virologic analysis by multiplex real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). Additional safety assessments included physical examinations, vital signs, and clinical laboratory measurements. Home-health visits could be conducted in lieu of clinic visits for safety assessments, if permissible by local regulations and/or institutional standard operating procedures.

The primary immunogenicity outcome was geometric mean fold rises (GMFRs) from baseline to 28 d post-vaccination (Day 0 to 28 in Parts 1 and 2; Day 365 to 393 in Part 3) for RSV A- and RSV B-specific nAbs. Secondary immunogenicity outcomes, evaluated through 180 d post-vaccination, were RSV A- and RSV B-specific nAb levels, seroresponse rates (SRR) defined as the percentage of participants with ≥4-fold rise in nAbs versus pre-vaccination baseline, pre-F-specific immunoglobulin G (IgG) levels, and geometric mean ratio (GMR) of fold rise in pre-F IgG to fold rise in RSV A-specific nAbs. Exploratory objectives included: VLP core (I53-50)-specific IgG levels at Days 0, 28, and 180 in Parts 1 and 2, and at Days 365, 372, and 546 in Part 3; and CMI. An additional post hoc analysis was performed to assess the association between VLP core-specific IgG levels and RSV A nAb responses after revaccination.

Blood samples were collected for humoral immunogenicity assessments on Days 0, 7, 28, and 180 (Parts 1 and 2) and Days 365, 372, 393, and 546 (Part 3). Neutralization assays were used to determine RSV A- and RSV B-specific nAb levels. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays were used to assess RSV pre-F-specific IgG and VLP core-specific IgG levels. Details on serological assessments are included in the Supplementary Methods.

For participants randomly selected for CMI evaluation in Parts 1 and 2, these assessments were performed on blood samples taken on Days 0 and 7. In Part 3, these assessments were performed on blood samples taken on Days 365 and 372. RSV pre-F-specific memory B-cell frequencies and T-cell responses were measured by qualified enzyme-linked immune absorbent spot (ELISpot) and FluoroSpot assays, respectively (see Supplementary Methods).

Statistical analyses

The trial was not powered to identify statistically significant differences between groups and the results were analyzed descriptively. However, the sample size was adequate to provide reasonable estimates of immune responses to guide dose and formulation selection for future studies.

Safety data were presented as the number and percentage of participants with a given AR/AE by trial arm and age group for the safety set (all participants who received IVX-121 or placebo). Participants were analyzed according to the study product received.

For Parts 1 and 2, immunogenicity data were summarized by geometric mean concentration (GMC) and GMFR for each treatment arm, trial visit, and age group for the per-protocol set (PPS; all randomized participants who received IVX-121 or placebo and who had no major protocol deviations between Days 0–28). A complete case analysis in which participants with missing data were excluded was performed. No missing data were imputed. For participants with documented RSV infection, immune responses measured after RSV infection were excluded from analysis through Day 180. Two-sided 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for GMCs and GMFRs were back-transformed from the CIs for the mean of the log2-transformed response or fold rise assuming a Student t-distribution.

For the open-label extension (Part 3), immunogenicity data were summarized by the GMC and GMFR at Days 365 (baseline), 372, 393, and 546. Analysis was limited to the per-protocol set (PPS2; all participants who received IVX-121 on Day 365 and who had no major protocol deviations between Days 365–393). Participants with a documented RSV infection prior to Day 365 were not excluded from PPS2. However, data from participants with an RSV infection after Day 365 were excluded from the analysis.

When computing the GMC, antibody concentrations below the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) were replaced with ½ LLOQ. When computing GMFR, the following if/then scenarios were used: if the baseline concentration was ≥LLOQ and the post-vaccination concentration was <LLOQ, then the post-vaccination concentration was replaced with ½ LLOQ; if the baseline concentration was <LLOQ and the post-vaccination concentration was ≥LLOQ, then the baseline concentration was replaced with the LLOQ; if both baseline and post-vaccination values were <LLOQ, then both values were converted in the same way. If a concentration was above the upper limit of quantification (ULOQ), it was replaced with the ULOQ when calculating the GMC or GMFR.

SRR was calculated for RSV A and RSV B nAbs, and for RSV pre-F-specific IgG.

All data analyses were conducted with the SAS® software version 9.4 or higher.

In Part 1, 145 participants (18–45 y of age) were screened and 90 were randomized, received study vaccination, and were included in the safety set (Figure 2). The PPS included 89/90 (98.9%) participants (one participant who received a COVID-19 booster vaccination prior to the Day 28 visit was excluded). A total of 89 (98.9%) participants completed the trial and one (1.1%) participant discontinued (consent withdrawn). Part 3 (follow-up and revaccination in adults 60–75 y of age)

Between Days 180 and 365 (prior to Part 3 vaccination), among the 108 participants there were four with SAEs (all in the prior IVX-121 group, none considered vaccine related) and one with an AESI (moderate LRTI on Day 310, assessed as unrelated to study vaccine); there were no vaccine-related MAAEs (Table S1). The SAEs were acute myocardial infarction, retinal detachment, coronary artery disease and aortic stenosis, and supraspinatus muscle rupture.

Among the 90 participants who received IVX-121 on Day 365, 20/75 (26.7%) participants in the prior IVX-121 group and 3/15 (20.0%) participants in the prior placebo group reported a solicited AR within 7 d post-vaccination (Figures 3 and 4).

A total of 37/75 (49.3%) vaccinated participants in the prior IVX-121 group and 5/15 (33.3%) of participants in the prior placebo group reported an unsolicited AE within 28 d post-vaccination (Tables 2 and S1). Two AEs were considered vaccine related in two (2.7%) participants in the prior IVX-121 group. There were no vaccine-related SAEs, MAAEs, and no AESIs were reported from Day 365 up to the end of the trial.

RSV infections

Participants were evaluated for RSV infection as part of an acute respiratory illness visit (no subtyping was performed on RSV clinical isolates). No participants in Part 1 tested positive for RSV during follow-up. Four participants in Part 2 and Part 3 pre-revaccination tested positive on Days 95, 143, 312, and 318 post-vaccination. All received IVX-121 ± Al(OH)3 at the low or medium dosage level. One participant in Part 3 tested positive for RSV 16 d post-revaccination. This individual had been previously vaccinated with 75 μg IVX-121 plus Al(OH)3. Illness was considered mild in all cases except one, which was moderate (AESI unrelated to vaccine in Part 3).

Immunogenicity

Part 1 (adults 18–45 y of age)

RSV A- and RSV B-specific nAbs increased 3- to 10- and 2- to 4-fold from baseline at Day 28, respectively, while GMFRs of 1 were observed with placebo (Figure 5). RSV A-specific nAbs increased more with unadjuvanted IVX-121 than with adjuvanted IVX-121. At Day 180, GMFRs for RSV A nAbs were higher for unadjuvanted vaccine than for adjuvanted IVX-121 across all doses. All GMFRs and lower bounds of their 95% confidence intervals at Day 180 were greater than 1.0 with IVX-121, suggesting some persistence of RSV A-specific nAbs. A similar pattern was observed for RSV B nAbs with GMFRs greater in the unadjuvanted groups and persistence through Day 180, at least for the unadjuvanted groups. RSV A and RSV B SRRs at Day 28 were higher in the unadjuvanted groups than in the adjuvanted groups. IVX-121 induced a 3- to 7-fold increase in RSV pre-F-specific IgG levels from baseline to Day 28 (Figure 6). These levels persisted through Day 180, as evidenced by GMFRs remaining between 3- to 6-fold over baseline. GMFRs for RSV pre-F-specific IgG tended to be higher with unadjuvanted compared with adjuvanted formulations at all trial visits.

To assess the relationship of the rise in RSV pre-F-specific IgG levels relative to the rise in RSV A-specific nAbs, the GMR of the fold rise in IgG concentration to the fold rise in nAbs was calculated. Discussion

In this Phase 1/1b trial, the experimental RSV vaccine IVX-121 demonstrated favorable safety and immunogenicity profiles in both younger and older adults. No emergent safety concerns were identified, and vaccination with IVX-121 led to substantial increases of nAbs against both RSV A and RSV B, which persisted above baseline levels for at least 6 months.

Across the three dosage levels and formulations (with or without Al[OH]3) IVX-121 was well tolerated in both age groups, with no AEs leading to study discontinuation, no vaccine-related SAEs, and no deaths during the trial. A larger proportion of older participants (but not younger participants) reported ARs in the IVX-121 with Al(OH)3 group. Following open-label IVX-121 vaccination of older adults in Part 3, the overall safety profile (solicited ARs, unsolicited AEs, and MAAEs) in the prior IVX-121 group was similar to that in the prior placebo group.

The nAb responses following vaccination with IVX-121 persisted through the 6-month follow-up period, irrespective of dosage level in both age groups. GMFRs of nAbs against RSV A were lower with the adjuvant than without, at each dose level; also, at Day 28, they were greater in the younger cohort than in the older cohort. In the placebo group for Parts 1 and 2, nAbs for RSV A and RSV B remained unchanged from baseline levels throughout the 6-month follow-up period, suggesting a low level of RSV circulation in the community between August 2021 and August 2022, consistent with surveillance data.32 Pre-F-specific IgG responses were also sustained through 6 months post-vaccination in Parts 1 and 2, irrespective of dose or use of adjuvant. VLP core-specific IgG responses following vaccination were low overall (most near the LLOQ). Overall, no clear IVX-121 dose-level effect and no benefit of the addition of the adjuvant Al(OH)3 were observed in either age group.

Based on the interim safety and immunogenicity results from Parts 1 and 2, the unadjuvanted IVX-121 75 µg formulation was selected for the Part 3 open-label vaccination. Among older adults who continued into Part 3, nAb concentrations for RSV A at Day 365 baseline were approximately 3-fold higher in participants who received IVX-121 versus placebo in Part 2, demonstrating the persistence of nAb up to 1 y post-vaccination. A Phase 3 trial of the FDA-approved RSV pre-F protein-based vaccine, Arexvy, in adults ≥60 y of age also found that humoral immune responses remained above pre-vaccination levels for at least 1 y.33

Similar nAb response levels were observed after administration of 75 µg of IVX-121 to either prior Part 2 placebo or IVX-121 recipients who received the same dose and formulation in Part 2. In general, the revaccination of Part 2 IVX-121 recipients demonstrated a modest booster effect against RSV A, with a 2-fold rise in nAbs following 28 d post-revaccination. However, an increase in RSV B nAbs was not observed, as GMCs remained at approximately the same pre-revaccination level. This may have been due to undetected natural exposure to RSV B virus eliciting significant RSV B nAbs prior to the revaccination time point. Indeed, there was a rise in RSV B nAb concentrations (versus a decline in RSV A nAbs) immediately prior to re-vaccination in the participants who took part in both Parts 2 and 3 (Figure S5), suggesting RSV B exposure in both prior IVX-121 and prior placebo recipients. Prior IVX-121 recipients had higher nAb concentrations pre-revaccination compared to their baseline at Day 0. Furthermore, revaccination occurred between November 2022 and February 2023, with 60% of revaccinations in January 2023, directly after the peak incidence of RSV in Belgium for the 2022–2023 season.34 The 2022–2023 season had a substantially higher incidence of cases than the 2021–2022 season.34

In addition to RSV-specific nAb responses, T cell responses are also crucial for protection against RSV.35 Compared to younger adults, RSV-specific T cell responses tend to be lower in older adults, which may contribute to increased disease severity.36 While only evaluated in a small subset of participants, our results suggest that IVX-121 stimulated the production of RSV-specific IgG from B cells and induced IFN-γ-secreting-specific T cell responses to RSV. However, larger scale investigations are needed to comprehensively assess the CMI responses induced by IVX-121.

One of the limitations of this trial was the small sample size and lack of power for statistical comparisons among treatment groups. Furthermore, as this was a Phase 1 trial, the relatively healthy trial population limits the generalizability of the results. However, this first-in-human trial provided the initial clinical data to guide development and future studies in other and larger populations.

When this Phase 1/1b trial of IVX-121 was initiated in 2021, no licensed vaccines against RSV were available. Since then, three RSV vaccines have been licensed, including two recombinant pre-F protein vaccines14,19 and an mRNA vaccine encoding RSV pre-F.15 The safety, immunogenicity, and efficacy of these vaccines have been extensively reviewed based on data from clinical trials, observational studies, and post-marketing surveillance, demonstrating meaningful clinical protection, sustained immune responses, and acceptable safety profiles.20 VLP vaccines may offer several potential advantages over recombinant protein and mRNA platforms, including strong immunogenicity, durability of protection, and improved stability facilitating storage and transport.21,27 Another advantage is the ability to use multiple VLPs in combination vaccines. IVX-121 has been further evaluated as a component of the RSV and hMPV combination VLP vaccine, IVX-A12, in Phase 137 and Phase 2 trials.38,39 In the Phase 1 trial, IVX-A12 was administered to older adults (60–75 y of age) with a fixed RSV VLP dose of 75 μg combined with varying hMPV VLP dose levels, with or without the oil-in-water adjuvant MF59®. MF59® was selected for the IVX-A12 trial following the findings from the IVX-121 study reported here, where Al(OH)3 did not appear to enhance immunogenicity. In the Phase 1 trial of IVX-A12, the RSV component-induced RSV A and RSV B nAb responses.37

In summary, the novel RSV VLP vaccine, IVX-121, was well tolerated and elicited immune responses against both RSV A and RSV B in young and older adults. These findings support further clinical evaluation of IVX-121 for prevention of RSV-associated LRTD.

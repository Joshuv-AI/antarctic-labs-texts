Central Message

Our program has a 20-year history of performing robotic-assisted coronary artery bypass grafting, transitioning from a low to high volume program.

Minimally invasive direct coronary artery bypass combined with robotic assistance (RA-MIDCAB) or totally endoscopic coronary artery bypass procedures allows patients to realize the benefits of minimally invasive surgery.1 Despite enthusiasm for minimally invasive approaches, RA-MIDCAB has still achieved limited adoption.2 Our program has a 20-year experience performing robotic-assisted coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and has transitioned to a high-volume program with >100 procedures performed annually with excellent outcomes (Table 1). Case Description and Discussion

A 70-year male patient with multiple cardiac risk factors and recent chest discomfort underwent a workup leading to cardiac catheterization showing a 100% occluded left anterior descending artery (LAD) and no other significant coronary artery disease. He was referred for coronary revascularization and deemed to be a good candidate for robotic-assisted left internal thoracic artery (LITA) to LAD coronary artery bypass grafting. Through description of procedure steps, we illustrate lessons learned over 20 years. Informed consent was waived. The study protocol (#19-1230-3) was approved by the Trinity Health New York institutional review board January 13, 2020.

Prerequisites

We believe that experience with off-pump and multiarterial grafting is an essential prerequisite for performing RA-MIDCAB. This is arguably difficult for many programs given that the adoption of off-pump CABG remains below 20% of all CABG procedures. Given this, it is reasonable to conclude that the initial experience should be concentrated in 1 or 2 surgeons to maximize proficiency.

LITA Harvest

The importance of the LITA conduit cannot be overstated and it is incumbent on the surgeon to conclude the LITA harvest with a functional conduit. We minimize manipulation of the LITA and dissect away from the vessel to minimize risk of injury. Typically the dissection is performed with micro bipolar forceps (left arm) and spatula tip cautery (right arm) with the goal to dissect a skeletonized LITA to maximize length. The energy level is set to 2 and adjusted as necessary. The fascia is scored lateral to the LITA and along the entire length, which allows gravity and carbon dioxide to assist dissection. The distal dissection is aided by allowing the LITA to fall off the chest wall. In patients with a less favorable body habitus there may be conflicts between the robotic arms. This can be mitigated by performing the distal and proximal dissection 1 handed and ensuring that the patient side surgeon recognizes and mitigates conflicts. It is important to perform careful hemostasis because bleeding is magnified and the surgeon doesn't have the luxury of suction or sponges. We routinely open the pericardium to visualize the LAD, assess LITA length, and plan minithoracotomy incision location.

Minithoracotomy

Incision location is based on LAD position approximated on chest radiograph and location at the conclusion of the LITA harvest. The anastomosis is easier if the LAD target is superior to incision. The interspace incision can be adjusted if LAD position isn't optimal. We rarely have to remove a rib and soft tissue retractor surgeon can be used to enhance visualization. We routinely perform a cryo nerve block and use a local anesthesia nerve block to facilitate postoperative pain control.

Anastomosis

A standard off-pump stabilizer can be used if the LAD position is superior to incision; however, as we have progressed to smaller incisions with experience we have converted primarily to using an endoscopic stabilizer. The advantage of the endoscopic stabilizer is that the target vessel can be moved into the incision. An important consideration is that the anastomosis can be performed from either the right or left side of the operating table and suturing performed in either a right to left direction or left to right direction depending on the relationship of the LITA to the LAD and surgeon preference. Given the limited working space needle angle flexibility when performing the anastomosis represents an important consideration. We routinely only snare the LAD proximally and use a carbon dioxide mister blower to keep the field blood free. We will place an intracoronary shunt for ST segment elevation >2 mm and hemodynamic instability. We routinely measure flow.

Conclusions

A high-volume robotic-assisted coronary artery bypass program can be achieved by adhering to basic principles of rigorous patient selection to optimize outcomes, concentration of experience in surgeons with off-pump experience, operative team engagement, flexibility in anastomotic techniques, and patience.

Protocol for in vivo and in vitro activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in mice using monosodium urate
Authors: Didi Liu, Yi Huang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryAlthough intraperitoneal injection of monosodium urate (MSU) is an effective model for studying peritonitis, its establishment remains challenging. Here, we present a protocol for using MSU to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome in bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) and to induce peritonitis in mice. We describe steps for isolating and culturing BMDMs, preparing MSU crystals, and activating the NLRP3 inflammasome using western blot and ELISA. We then detail procedures for inducing peritonitis and testing for relevant indicators using flow cytometry and ELISA.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Huang et al. (2023).1

Before you begin

Here, we describe the specific steps involved in monosodium urate (MSU) activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs), as well as the induction of MSU-induced peritonitis in mice. Both BMDMs and MSU should be prepared before you begin.

Authority of the institution

The accumulation of MSU in vivo leads to the occurrence of gout. Due to the promotion of uric acid excretion and inhibition of MSU crystal formation and deposition by estrogen, gout occurs less frequently in premenopausal women, while the incidence is higher in men.2,3 Male C57BL/6J mice were used in this protocol and maintained at 22°C–25°C under a 12-h light/dark cycle, and animal manipulations were preapproved by the Ethics Committee of Laboratory Animal Management and Animal Welfare, Jiangnan University. Ethical approval was obtained for the use of animals and tissues before this protocol was attempted.

Preparation of MSU crystal

Timing: 3 days

Timing: 3 days

Purchased MSU is difficult to enter cells due to its large size, and appropriately sized MSU crystals need to be prepared first. This section describes the preparation of MSU crystals.4.Dissolution of sodium uric acid salt.a.weigh 1.68 g sodium uric acid salt with an analytical balance and prepare 0.01 M NaOH solution with ultrapure water.b.Dissolve sodium uric acid salt in 500 mL of 0.01 M NaOH solution heated to 70°C in advance.Note: Adjusting the pH to 7.1–7.2 using additional NaOH sufficiently dissolved uric acid.5.Filter the solution with a 0.22 μm filter and shake it evenly with a magnetic stirrup at a slow speed at room temperature.Note: Stirring until crystal formation, this step generally takes 24–48 h.6.Transfer the solution to a 50 mL centrifuge tube.7.Discard the supernatant and centrifuge at 600 × g for 15 min.8.Wash the crystal twice with 10 mL absolute ethanol.9.Autoclave and dry overnight in 65°C oven.10.Weigh the crystals and resuspend to 50 μg/μL with sterile PBS.Note: Weigh the weight of the 50 mL centrifuge tube before transferring the liquid in step 6, the mass of the crystals is obtained by subtracting the mass of the centrifuge tube in step 6 from the mass weighed in step 10.11.Observe the MSU crystal size under the microscope. Step-by-step method details

MSU activates the NLRP3 inflammasome

Timing: 2 days

Timing: 2 days

NLRP3 inflammasome activation requires two phases: the priming phase and the activation phase. In the priming phase, the inflammatory stimulant LPS activates the NF-κB signaling pathway, which in turn promotes the expression of NLRP3 and pro-IL-1β. In the activation phase, PAMPs and DAMPs induced the assembly of NLRP3 inflammatory vesicles, caspase-1-mediated secretion of IL-1β and IL-18, and pyroptosis. Therefore, LPS priming is an essential step in the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome. MSU is the commonly used DAMPs for classical NLRP3 inflammasome activation This section describes in detail the activation of NLRP3 by MSU and the subsequent detection of relevant indicators.1.Seed Limitations

Activation of the inflammasome can be induced by danger signals and microbial invasion and is an essential component of innate immunity.4 The previously discovered inflammasomes mainly include NLRP1 inflammasome, NLRC4 inflammasome, AIM2 inflammasome, Pyrin inflammasome and NLRP3 inflammasome.5 However, in this protocol, MSU can only target the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome. In addition, numerous NLRP3 activators have been identified, such as ATP, Nigericin, Alum using different methods than MSU, but the assays used in this protocol are still applicable.6,7,8

The accumulation of MSU in human may cause various diseases, and MSU-induced peritonitis is also a widely recognized model of peritonitis. However, this protocol only detected the recruitment of neutrophils and the increase of IL-1β in peritoneal lavage fluid, and more data may be needed to prove the key role of NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

MSU-induced peritonitis has been used in many studies of NLRP3 inflammasome, but whether this model is dependent on the NLRP3 inflammasome remains controversial. Previous studies have found that neutrophil recruitment and IL-1β release were significantly reduced in NLRP3 deficient mice compared with WT mice with MSU-induced peritonitis.9,10,11 Moreover, NLRP3 inhibitors Tranilast, OLT1177 and β-hydroxybutyric acid have good therapeutic effects on MSU-induced inflammation in vivo.12,13,14 These all suggest the dependence of MSU-induced peritonitis on the NLRP3 inflammasome. However, Other studies found that NLRP3 deficient mice had limited relief from MSU-induced inflammation, and IL-1 deficiency may be the main reason for the decrease of MSU-induced inflammatory cell recruitment and secretion of inflammatory factors.15,16 Therefore, the mechanism of MSU inducing inflammation in vivo still needs to be further explored.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The differentiation efficiency of BMDMs was not detected after isolation and culture (related to before you begin).

Potential solution

The spindle-shaped adherent BMDMs could be observed under the microscope after 5–7 days of culture. The high efficiency of 50 ng/mL M-CSF in inducing BMDMs differentiation was verified by the establishment of laboratory system. In general, the induction efficiency can be determined by collecting adherent cells for flow sorting techniques.

Problem 2

Failure to observe the characteristics of MSU-induced NLRP3 activated cells under the microscope (related to Step 4).

Potential solution

The addition of MSU crystals to BMDMs resulted in the covering of the cell surface, making it difficult to observe cellular morphology. Therefore, this is the primary reason why characteristic features of NLRP3 activation cells were not observed under the microscope. It is suggested to perform western blot and ELISA after MSU induction to determine the induction time and concentration of MSU.

Problem 3

No GSDMD-N in western blot (related to expected outcomes).

Potential solution

In addition to caspase-1 cleavage and IL-1β maturation release, pyroptosis caused by GSDMD cleavage is another important marker of NLRP3 inflammasome activation. This experimental protocol primarily focuses on the release of inflammatory cytokines and does not include the detection of GSDMD-N. Relevant data on GSDMD-N can be supplemented based on the protocol.

Problem 4

Caspase-1 was not detected in peritonitis (related to expected outcomes).

Potential solution

Caspase-1 is the classic marker of NLRP3 activation, and the detection of caspase-1 level is necessary to demonstrate the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in peritonitis. We used a simple method to measure the level of IL-1β in ascites, which partly reflects the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome. Later, if necessary, macrophages could be collected and sorted, and western blot was performed to detect the level of caspase-1 in macrophages to reflect the activation of NLRP3 induced by MSU in vivo.

Problem 5

MSU failed to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome (related to expected outcomes).

Potential solution

In the process of preparing MSU, ultrasonic vibration and decomposition must be carried out to allow MSU to enter cells and activate the inflammasome. Additionally, due to the different laboratory environments, it is recommended to perform gradient exploration after the preparation of MSU is completed.

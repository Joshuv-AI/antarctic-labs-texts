NODG-09 Management of hemangioblastomas about 8 cases in Abidjan
Authors: Mèhomè Wilfried Dossou, Raïssa Abibatou Yasmina Diaby, Joël Emmanuel N’guessan Brou, Wilfried Innocent Mukando Meuga, Jean Marcel Djiby Okamon, Gabriel Gildas Nognegengi, Melaine Landry Konan, Konan Médard Kakou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHemangioblastomas are relatively rare and benign neoplasms. When it occurs sporadically, it is not uncommon to think at first of other diagnoses that provoke debates between pathologists and surgeons. The objective is to identify diagnostic elements that allow them to be differentiated from other pathologies. It was a retrospective study taking into account patients operated between June 2021 and March 2025 and whose anatomopathological diagnosis was hemangioblastoma. The average age was 33.75 years and the sex ratio 3/5. The reason for consultation was related to intracranial hypertension syndrome and statokinetic cerebrovascular syndrome. Ataxia and balance disorder were present in 6/8 patients. The lesions objectified were kysto-fleshy (2/5), tissue in (2/5), or cystic (1/5) with blurred boundaries rising after injection. These lesions were in hyposignal T1 and hypersignal T1 Gado and T2. They were located at the vermix (3/8) or cerebellar (5/8). No associated injury found. The diagnostic hypothesis was pilocytic astrocytoma (3/8), medulloblastoma (3/8) and hemangioblastoma (2/8). The course was marked by parietal suppuration in 1 patient. 4/8 patients were presented to the neuro-oncology staff and did not require additional treatment. The discovery of a cyst with a wall tumor nodule in the cerebellar hemispheres suggests mainly pilocytic astrocytoma but other tumors hemangioblastomas may present in a similar form. There are no discriminatory imaging features and only histology can make the correct diagnosis.

Utilization of Ontology to Develop Artificial Intelligence Systems in the Healthcare Industry
Authors: Elahe Parsanasab, Alihasan Ahmadipour, Esmaeil Mehraeen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesOntologies play a crucial role in healthcare systems due to the diversity of concepts, roles, users, and diagnostic and therapeutic methods. They facilitate the development of knowledge bases and the sharing and representation of information. With the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into healthcare, ontologies can serve as complementary tools to enhance the quality of services.MethodsThis review study examines existing research on the application of ontologies in AI systems within the healthcare industry. By analyzing their applications, benefits, challenges, and limitations, the study seeks to provide a deeper understanding of their impact on advancing AI technologies and improving healthcare processes. In addition, the study offers recommendations for strengthening the development and use of ontologies in intelligent healthcare systems.ResultsThe findings of this review indicate that ontologies enhance the accuracy of results and support medical decision-making by enabling the semantic exchange of diverse and heterogeneous data. They are essential for the development of decision support systems and for fostering intelligent interactions between patients and healthcare systems. Furthermore, ontologies contribute to healthcare decision-making by semantically analyzing the connections between diseases, geographic regions, and environmental factors.ConclusionsThe use of ontologies in healthcare improves data analysis, patient diagnosis, treatment, and decision-making. Ontologies enhance data inference and interoperability in AI systems through data modeling, concept relationship extraction, knowledge enrichment, and information sharing. Given the vast scope of the healthcare domain, the diversity of specialties and data, and the absence of a dedicated ontology development methodology specific to this field, there is a clear need for a tailored and robust methodology.Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Delivery of Health Care, Health Information Systems, Health Care Sector, Biological Ontologies

Introduction

In healthcare information systems, ontology has become an essential tool [1]. Providing knowledge in a formalized representation facilitates efficient data analysis and strengthens healthcare services [1]. In the healthcare industry, ontology-based approaches support the organization, management, and retrieval of information [2]. By establishing a uniform nomenclature, they enable the integration of multiple data sources and formats, thereby promoting interoperability [3]. This technology also ensures effective representation and communication of data among system components. For medical professionals, however, the process of creating ontologies remains time-consuming and prone to errors despite its potential [4]. Nevertheless, ontology-based systems may improve healthcare services across a wide range of areas, from diagnosis and treatment to specialized domains such as cognitive rehabilitation.

When artificial intelligence (AI) is applied in healthcare, ontologies serve as complementary tools to improve the quality of services. The integration of ontology and AI holds great promise for advancing healthcare, ranging from more accurate diagnostic capabilities to fully personalized treatment approaches. Within AI systems, ontology provides a framework for the structured representation, exchange, and reuse of healthcare knowledge [5].

Given the rapid expansion of AI in healthcare and the pivotal role of ontology in this field, this review study was undertaken to examine the applications of ontology in AI systems within the healthcare industry. By analyzing their applications, benefits, challenges, and limitations, this research seeks to deepen understanding of the impact of ontologies in advancing AI technologies and enhancing healthcare processes. Furthermore, the study aims to identify research gaps and provide recommendations for strengthening the development and utilization of ontologies in intelligent healthcare systems.

Concept of Ontology

The definition and use of the philosophical term ontology have evolved within computer science. Aristotle originally employed it to explore the nature of existence. In computer science, ontology refers to a machine-readable representation of a domain’s consensus view [6]. Ontologies serve as sophisticated knowledge organization systems that provide rich semantic links between concepts [7]. By establishing semantic relationships among concepts, ontologies deliver meaning and are widely applied in diverse areas of computer science and technology [8,9]. Ontology encompasses multiple aspects, including its nature, applications, development tools, languages, and platforms. Each of these facets involves distinct and often technically complex methodologies. Within each domain, specific methods and tools are employed, tailored to the domain’s requirements and characteristics. In the healthcare industry, ontologies function as vital tools with significant roles. By offering a structured framework for organizing and integrating complex data, they enhance the efficiency of healthcare systems. This study further examines the various applications of ontologies in healthcare and their influence on the advancement of AI-driven health systems.

Ontology Development and Maintenance Methodologies

The construction, implementation, and updating of ontologies rely on systematic development and maintenance methodologies. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges associated with these tasks. For healthcare decision support systems, recent studies emphasize the importance of considering issues such as stakeholder engagement, project management practices, feasibility assessments, and support for reuse within collaborative ontology engineering approaches [12].

A variety of methodologies for developing and maintaining ontologies have been proposed, some of which are listed in Table 1. The healthcare sector has adopted several of these approaches, including collaborative ontology engineering methods [13] and frameworks emphasizing knowledge representation and inference rules [13], to manage medical data and design effective decision support systems. One proposed four-step methodology combines grounded theory with participatory design to create ontology-based health information systems, focusing on knowledge collection from users, ontology design, system implementation, and evaluation [14]. To ensure interoperability and reusability, methodologies for developing healthcare ontologies should incorporate health information standards [15].

Nevertheless, the main challenge remains the absence of a methodology specifically tailored to the healthcare domain. This field is characterized by its diversity of medical specialties, inherent complexity, and distinct cultural contexts. As a result, there is a need for strategies capable of effectively addressing these unique features when designing and refining ontologies for healthcare [16–34].

Development of Ontology in Healthcare

In the healthcare industry, ontologies enhance interoperability, interpretability, and explainability for pandemic intelligence and global health surveillance [35]. They also enable the explicit representation of domain knowledge in virtual reality–based healthcare instruction [36]. Ontology-based systems have become essential tools for improving decision-making and healthcare delivery. These systems formally represent healthcare knowledge, thereby facilitating better information management, organization, and understanding [37]. By supporting efficient data analysis, strengthening patient diagnosis and treatment, and assisting policymakers in decision-making, ontologies contribute to advancing healthcare [1,37]. In the development of health ontologies, concepts are modeled according to the components presented in Figure 3 [37]. Each of these components contains sub-sections, each with its own ontology. A central feature of ontology tools is their ability to integrate these components, a capability also supported by the Protégé platform.

Components of concept modeling in the health domain.

Numerous studies have investigated the practical implementation of ontology in healthcare systems. The authors of [38] conducted a study to develop a centralized semantic knowledge base for India’s public health system. Their innovative approach integrated data on diseases, locations, and environmental factors through ontology. The researchers designed and implemented algorithms to extract data from diverse sources, such as hospitals and satellite data, and mapped this information to three domain-specific ontologies. This framework enabled semantic data analysis and querying. The findings demonstrated that ontologies can substantially improve healthcare decision-making by clarifying the relationships among diseases, locations, and the environment, thereby providing a knowledge-based foundation for public health policy development.

Dhiman et al. [39] focused on creating an ontology-based framework to enhance disease diagnosis through semantic analysis of health data. Their research addressed the challenges of working with large volumes of structured and unstructured medical data. They introduced the Ontology-based Healthcare Framework and a Health Ontology, both designed to support semantic indexing of electronic health records. The results indicated that the proposed framework enables accurate semantic inferences regarding patients’ medical conditions and effectively supports physicians in diagnostic decision-making.

Shahzad et al. [40] explored the integration of innovative healthcare services through ontological modeling and integration. They addressed the challenges caused by fragmented development of Internet of Things-based healthcare services and highlighted the need for a unified platform to support semantic data exchange. The authors proposed an ontology-driven framework that defines healthcare services through ontologies and integrates them based on semantic similarities, differences, and dependencies. Their findings, validated through a two-stage evaluation that included model consistency assessment and semantic relation validation, showed that the proposed approach improves coordination among healthcare services and supports the development of efficient decision support systems.

A study of Djitog and Kah [41] sought to develop a novel ontology for simulating healthcare systems by modeling their main components and the relationships among them within an integrated structure. This ontology was formally expressed using the Entity Structure Language and mapped to the web ontology language (OWL), allowing visualization and querying in platforms such as Protégé. The study resulted in a framework that documents and formalizes the healthcare system simulation domain, offering capabilities such as unified model representation across platforms, reusability, querying, and browsing of models.

Ontologies can also support the integration, deployment, and operation of healthcare applications. Goumopoulos and Igoumenakis [42] demonstrated this in a case study on cognitive rehabilitation using serious games. Similarly, Okikiola et al. [43] conducted a systematic review of studies published between 2009 and 2018, examining the methods and techniques used to apply ontologies in healthcare. Their findings revealed that knowledge modeling with tools such as Protégé and standards like OWL enhances disease classification, service quality, and access to reliable data. This work underscored the importance of ontology in advancing healthcare services and strengthening interactions between patients and providers.

Goyer et al. [44] developed an integrated ontological analysis of healthcare systems to identify relationships among patients, healthcare professionals, and provider organizations. Their ontology model aimed to enhance interoperability within learning health systems. Adhikary et al. [45] proposed an ontology-based healthcare hierarchy and explored its application in chatbot development. Their framework involves embedding the healthcare ontology hierarchy into chatbots by establishing connections among individuals and real-world entities. Saranya et al. [46] proposed an automated ontological framework for personal health services using semantic web technologies to improve diagnostic accuracy. Similarly, Sondes et al. [47], designed an ontology-based healthcare monitoring system for the Internet of Things, which facilitates semantic interoperability between users and devices.

The study by Hooda and Rani [48] aimed to propose an ontology-based healthcare framework that models the disease diagnosis process based on dynamically sensed clinical data. The proposed system manages data heterogeneity and enhances semantic knowledge exchange, enabling semantic interoperability and integration. The findings demonstrated that using inference rules based on semantic knowledge facilitates accurate disease classification and diagnosis. Furthermore, by leveraging semantic querying, the proposed model can deliver efficient and effective outcomes in health monitoring and treatment.

Overall, ontologies are potent tools for knowledge modeling and structuring healthcare data, enabling semantic interoperability, information integration, and service quality enhancement. They facilitate knowledge exchange among patients, healthcare providers, and various systems. With the expanding application of AI in healthcare, ontologies can significantly improve data quality and the accuracy of AI models. The following section will discuss the applications of this tool in AI-based healthcare systems.

Applications of Ontology in AI Systems

AI is a multidisciplinary field dedicated to developing intelligent machines that replicate human cognitive abilities and problem-solving skills [49]. Figure 4 illustrates the fundamental concepts of AI [50]. These subfields encompass multiple domains, each of which plays a crucial role in designing and implementing intelligent systems.

Fundamental concepts of artificial intelligence (AI).

Ontologies now play a vital role in establishing coherent semantic frameworks within AI systems. Their use has grown considerably in fields such as systems engineering and healthcare. Ontologies facilitate cross-domain interoperability, reasoning, and knowledge representation [51]. By supporting knowledge refinement, commonsense reasoning, and reference modeling, ontologies contribute to the development of explainable AI [52]. They provide reusable and sharable knowledge with rich, machine-interpretable semantics that support a shared understanding [53]. Ontologies also address challenges in data querying and enable semantic reasoning in AI systems, as they can represent concepts and relationships in ways comparable to human cognitive functions [51,53].

Extensive research has explored the application of ontologies in AI systems within healthcare. Some notable examples are highlighted below.

Organizations adopting LLMs face challenges such as hallucinations, where models produce incorrect outputs due to incomplete or outdated training data, restricted knowledge limited to training inputs, and lack of response traceability. Integrating LLMs with structured knowledge sources such as ontologies can help mitigate these issues, improving accuracy and interpretability [54].

Knowledge bases provide reliable and authoritative information for AI applications by organizing data in a structured manner. Ontologies supply the conceptual framework to contextualize and interpret this knowledge across domains. Ontology-driven AI enables logical inference based on content and relationships, allowing systems to deliver personalized and targeted outcomes by linking customer data with the most relevant products or services [55].

A study by Aguna et al. [56] sought to define the fundamental components of a care ontology to guide the design of AI systems in the care domain. They identified four core elements: care as the knowledge object, nursing as the cognitive subject, inquiry as the process of knowing, and care models as the outcome. The study followed a deductive approach in two phases (knowledge extraction and expert validation) and ultimately proposed a conceptual framework for embedding care knowledge into intelligent systems.

Arbabi et al. [57] proposed a machine learning model known as the Neural Concept Recognizer, which leverages the hierarchical structure of biomedical ontologies to identify medical concepts in unstructured texts and recognize previously unseen synonyms. The model employs a convolutional neural network to encode input phrases and rank medical concepts based on similarity. Evaluations conducted on datasets such as PubMed abstracts and clinical reports showed a significant improvement in F1-scores compared with rule-based approaches. This study demonstrated that incorporating ontological structures can markedly improve concept recognition accuracy without requiring large-scale labeled datasets.

Singla’s study [58] examined the role of ontologies in strengthening information systems within healthcare. The author emphasized that ontologies improve the scalability of information management by providing a standardized framework for representing medical concepts. The results indicated that the use of ontologies enables faster access to patient data and enhances both the efficiency and accuracy of decision-support systems. Overall, the study underscores the significant contributions of ontologies to improving the precision, speed, and effectiveness of AI-based healthcare systems.

Martinho et al.’s study [59] focused on the development of an ontology for food intake management in patients with obesity, diabetes, and those receiving tube feeding. The main objective was to create a standardized vocabulary for describing food and nutritional information, allowing integration across diverse healthcare systems and enabling personalized dietary recommendations for each patient. The findings suggested that embedding ontologies into AI-based systems improves their accuracy and capabilities, particularly in managing chronic diseases requiring continuous monitoring. In addition, the approach enhanced the performance of recommendation and expert systems in processing knowledge.

According to Silva et al. [60], ontologies and knowledge graphs facilitate data integration, analysis, and the application of AI techniques in cancer research. Similarly, Vaz et al. [61] presented the development of an anonymous hospital data management ontology designed for use in AI-driven healthcare applications.

Overall, ontologies are fundamental to AI in healthcare, as they enhance interoperability, diagnostic precision, personalized treatment, and semantic reasoning. They also support medical research, chronic disease management, and applications such as digital twins, decision-support systems, and secure anonymized data storage.

Discussion

A review of studies shows that ontologies in healthcare possess multiple capabilities that can substantially enhance the performance of health systems and services. These capabilities include improving healthcare decision-making by clarifying relationships among diseases, locations, and environmental factors, thereby providing a foundation for public health policy development. Ontologies also enhance diagnostic accuracy by enabling semantic analysis of medical data, which supports the diagnostic process and assists healthcare professionals. In addition, they facilitate the integration of healthcare services, particularly in systems with varying clinical exchange standards, by fostering improved coordination among services. Another important application is the modeling and simulation of healthcare systems, which allows for efficient indexing and searching of models across different software environments.

With the advancement of AI systems in healthcare, ontologies have contributed to strengthening their capabilities. A key benefit is increased diagnostic accuracy and improved medical decision-making through precise analysis of medical data. Ontologies enable the integration and semantic exchange of diverse and heterogeneous data from multiple sources, such as electronic health records, diagnostic tests, and environmental information. This integration allows AI systems to generate more accurate and reliable results. Ontologies are also fundamental to the development of knowledge-based decision support systems, facilitating clinical decision-making by enhancing semantic inference. Furthermore, they support intelligent interactions by improving communication between patients and healthcare systems.

The primary advantages of ontologies include improved diagnostic accuracy, enhanced data integration, better coordination of healthcare services, and more effective semantic data exchange. To realize these benefits, it is essential to clearly identify and capture the relevant concepts and relationships within each domain. Equally important is adherence to principles of data quality, as proper data management and logical structuring are crucial. When healthcare AI models are trained with high-quality, well-structured data, they are more likely to deliver accurate diagnostic and predictive outcomes.

Another significant advantage of ontology-based studies is their applicability across multiple sectors and specialties within healthcare. This versatility makes ontologies practical tools for integration and deployment across the full spectrum of medicine and healthcare.

Challenges and Recommendations

A key challenge in developing ontologies for healthcare is the improper use of methodologies and the failure to follow the prescribed steps of each approach. In some cases, unsuitable methodologies have created obstacles during development. Therefore, future studies should focus on designing a dedicated, coherent methodology tailored to the healthcare domain. Such a methodology should align the processes of creating, maintaining, and updating ontologies with the characteristics of complex, large-scale healthcare data. This would improve the accuracy, efficiency, and applicability of ontologies in both clinical and research decision-making.

The abundance of unstructured data—such as medical and descriptive reports—poses another challenge. Enhancing semantic inference capabilities in this area would greatly assist in extracting concepts and their relationships, offering valuable input for AI models, including natural language processing systems.

The vast and diverse activities within healthcare require the creation of integrated frameworks for data fusion through ontologies. Such frameworks would combine different ontologies to manage data coherently and efficiently.

In addition, ensuring the privacy and security of sensitive medical data is critical in ontology-based systems. Secure and reliable ontology methods should be implemented when collecting and processing data to safeguard patient confidentiality and maintain data integrity.

Limitations

One of these studies’ main limitations is the failure to identify, collect, and integrate data concepts and their relationships from credible clinical sources. This shortcoming diminishes the efficiency of AI models. To address this, future ontology development should rely on comprehensive and authoritative clinical sources for concept extraction, supported by expert input from domain specialists.

Another limitation is the insufficient evaluation of ontology models. Some studies either lack evaluation altogether or employ inadequate validation methods. Moreover, the shortage of professionals who possess expertise in both healthcare and ontology design represents a critical constraint. Expanding training and interdisciplinary collaboration could help overcome this limitation.

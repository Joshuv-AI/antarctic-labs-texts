Uncertainty-aware feature-weighted ensemble framework for heart disease prediction
Authors: Xinling Wang, Yangrui Fan, Minglian Yu, Fujiang Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper considers the challenge of clinical reliability and generalization in heart disease diagnosis, where traditional ensembles often overlook feature heterogeneity and lack uncertainty quantification. We propose an uncertainty-aware feature-weighted ensemble (UAFE) framework designed for robust cardiovascular prediction. UAFE integrates three core components: (1) Feature Stratification to train specialized learners on importance-based subgroups; (2) Dynamic uncertainty weighting to adaptively adjust model contributions based on prediction disagreement; and (3) Neighborhood refinement to rectify decisions for high-uncertainty samples using local geometric priors. Comprehensive experiments on a multi-center dataset demonstrate that UAFE outperforms state-of-the-art baselines with an accuracy of 0.8660. Furthermore, leave-one-center-out (LOCO) validation across four international clinical sites shows that UAFE maintains superior stability (mean accuracy 0.8332) against institutional distribution shifts, establishing its effectiveness for deployment in unseen clinical environments.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of global mortality, accounting for approximately 19 million deaths in 2021 and dominating non-communicable disease mortality1. Given the multifactorial etiology and non-linear progression of heart diseases, there is an urgent clinical imperative for diagnostic frameworks capable of achieving early warning and precision intervention2,3. While traditional diagnosis relies on invasive procedures or subjective clinical experience, automated and non-invasive systems have emerged as pivotal tools for processing complex physiological data and alleviating healthcare burdens in real-world medical scenarios. However, the high-stakes nature of clinical decision-making imposes rigorous requirements on the robustness of diagnostic systems. Consequently, identifying and addressing the inherent uncertainty of medical data while maintaining high predictive accuracy has become a critical bottleneck to be resolved in the field of intelligent healthcare.

Currently, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as an indispensable tool in modern cardiology4. By integrating diverse biomedical data, including clinical demographics and physiological markers, existing research trajectories mainly fall into two categories: the first focuses on leveraging deep learning to uncover latent non-linear patterns that are often imperceptible to traditional statistical methods5–7, while the second emphasizes enhancing predictive benchmarks through heuristic feature selection and stacked ensemble learning8,9. Although these data-driven approaches demonstrate significant potential for precision medicine, their clinical reliability remains constrained by two fundamental limitations. First, most frameworks adhere to a “homogeneous learning assumption,” failing to differentiate the heterogeneous diagnostic contributions of distinct medical attributes, which leads to critical pathological signals being diluted by background noise. Second, these models predominantly generate deterministic outputs without quantifying epistemic uncertainty. This “over-confident” decision-making mechanism hinders the system’s ability to identify its own cognitive blind spots, significantly increasing the risk of confident misdiagnoses in high-stakes clinical scenarios.

To address these limitations, this study proposes the Uncertainty-Aware Feature-Weighted Ensemble (UAFE) framework, specifically designed to enhance the reliability of heart disease prediction on tabular clinical data. Unlike black-box deep learning models that often lack transparency, UAFE integrates feature sensitivity with risk-aware decision-making. Related work

Traditional machine learning approaches

Early research on heart disease prediction established automated diagnostic baselines using classical supervised algorithms, including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Naïve Bayes (NB), and k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)2,3,10,11. While these studies utilized the stability of ensemble trees and the high-dimensional mapping capabilities of SVM, their performance relied heavily on intensive data preprocessing. For instance, Chang et al.2 and Reddy et al.3 demonstrated that integrating effective normalization with feature selection allows single classifiers to identify key risk factors effectively. However, these traditional models often exhibit limited generalization to unseen patient cohorts and struggle to capture the complex, non-linear dependencies–such as the interplay between metabolic markers and symptomatic indicators–inherent in clinical data. To address these constraints, recent studies have explored hybrid learning and dimensionality reduction to mitigate noise and combine algorithmic strengths8,12,13. Despite these refinements, a fundamental limitation persists: existing frameworks predominantly treat input features with uniform importance, failing to account for the heterogeneous diagnostic value of distinct medical attributes.

Ensemble learning and fusion strategies

To overcome the generalization deficits and high variance associated with single classifiers, ensemble learning has emerged as a dominant strategy in cardiovascular research. By aggregating predictions from diverse base learners, these approaches construct a more robust decision boundary through voting-based consensus9,14 and hierarchical stacking architectures15,16. For instance, Kumar and Thakur9 utilized stacked generalization to exploit the complementary strengths of heterogeneous algorithms, while Khan et al.15 developed specialized ensemble networks to enhance predictive reliability. Furthermore, hybrid frameworks have integrated deep learning-based feature extraction with conventional classifiers to bridge representative power with algorithmic stability5,17. However, a critical limitation of these existing frameworks is their heavy reliance on static fusion. In these paradigms, the influence assigned to each sub-model is fixed during training and remains invariant across all test samples18. This rigidity prevents the system from adaptively responding to the specific complexity of “hard” or ambiguous cases near the decision boundary. Consequently, there is an urgent need for a dynamic fusion strategy capable of adjusting model contributions based on instance-level confidence, which remains an under-explored area in current diagnostic systems. While recent advances have explored dynamic aggregation through model-agnostic confidence for multimodal data19 or deep neural network uncertainty for adversarial robustness20, a lightweight and robust dynamic weighting mechanism specifically tailored for heterogeneous clinical tabular data remains a critical gap that our work aims to fill.

Deep learning in cardiology

Driven by growing computational power, deep learning architectures such as Autoencoders, Transformers, and LSTM networks have increasingly been applied to capture complex, high-level representations directly from physiological signals7,21,22. These models excel at uncovering latent non-linear patterns that standard statistical approaches might overlook. For instance, Alghamdi et al.23 utilized a hybrid Autoencoder-DenseNet framework to compress input noise into latent representations for enhanced classification, while Yi et al.24 integrated Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) with Transformers to refine hyperparameter tuning for tabular medical data. However, despite their state-of-the-art performance in large-scale applications, deep models face significant hurdles in typical clinical settings characterized by small-to-medium scale tabular datasets.25 These “data-hungry” architectures are prone to overfitting when sample sizes are limited, often memorizing training noise rather than learning generalizable patterns. More critically, deep networks often function as “black boxes” lacking intrinsic uncertainty quantification. Their tendency to generate high-confidence predictions even when incorrect poses a serious liability in high-stakes medical decision-making. Consequently, well-designed ensemble tree models often provide a superior trade-off between predictive reliability and data efficiency compared to resource-intensive deep architectures.

Feature optimization and heterogeneity

Feature engineering is critical in medical informatics to distinguish informative biomarkers from redundant clinical variables. Various optimization techniques, such as the biologically inspired IVY algorithm6 and heuristic search methods5,8,26,27, have proven effective in navigating vast feature spaces to enhance classification accuracy and reduce computational overhead. However, most current strategies predominantly focus on binary feature selection rather than feature stratification. By discarding “weak” features entirely, these approaches overlook complementary information essential for specific patient subgroups28. Furthermore, feeding all selected attributes into a monolithic model ignores the inherent heterogeneity of medical data, where demographic, symptomatic, and biochemical measurements possess distinct statistical distributions requiring tailored modeling paradigms.

Notwithstanding these methodological advancements, translating high predictive performance into safe clinical practice remains hindered by several unresolved research gaps. First, the prevalent “uniform modeling” approach allows dominant features to overshadow subtle but clinically relevant signals. Second, existing ensemble methods rely on static fusion mechanisms that fail to adapt to individual patient profiles, leading to suboptimal performance on ambiguous cases. Most critically, the lack of uncertainty quantification and error correction mechanisms–such as the integration of local neighborhood information–forces clinicians to rely on deterministic outputs without a measure of confidence. To bridge these gaps, our UAFE framework integrates stratified modeling with uncertainty-aware dynamic weighting, ensuring a robust and risk-sensitive diagnostic process that respects the diverse nature of clinical features.Fig. 1Overall workflow of the proposed UAFE framework.

Method

The proposed Uncertainty-Aware Feature-Weighted Ensemble (UAFE) framework aims to enhance the reliability of heart disease prediction by explicitly modeling epistemic uncertainty and exploiting local geometric structures. Unlike static ensemble methods, UAFE dynamically adapts its inference strategy based on sample-level ambiguity, integrating feature stratification, variance-based weighting, and neighborhood-consistent refinement. The overall framework is shown in Fig. 1.

Specifically, Fig. 1 illustrates the logical progression from raw data to the final diagnosis. The top panel depicts the hierarchical processing stage, where features are ranked and partitioned into groups to train specialized heterogeneous learners. The bottom panel shows the dynamic inference stage: for low-uncertainty samples, a standard weighted consensus is output; whereas for high-uncertainty samples, the system triggers dynamic weight adjustment and local neighborhood refinement to rectify potential decision boundary errors.

Uncertainty-aware dynamic weighting

To quantify prediction reliability, we define the epistemic uncertainty U(x) as the inter-model disagreement, calculated via the standard deviation of the ensemble outputs:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} U(x) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K-1} \sum _{k=1}^K (p_k(x) - \bar{p}(x))^2}, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\bar{p}(x)$$\end{document} is the mean probability. An adaptive threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau$$\end{document} is set at the 60th percentile of the training set uncertainty distribution, serving as a boundary between confident and ambiguous samples.

We propose a dynamic weighting mechanism that shifts from performance-priors to variance-smoothing. The weight for model k, denoted as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_k(x)$$\end{document}, is defined as:2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} w_k(x) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} w_k^{acc}, & \text {if } U(x) \le \tau , \\ \alpha \cdot w_k^{acc} + (1-\alpha ) \cdot \frac{1}{K}, & \text {if } U(x) > \tau , \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_k^{acc}$$\end{document} represents the static validation accuracy of model k, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} controls the smoothing intensity. This mechanism preserves high-performance discrimination in low-uncertainty regions while enforcing a conservative, uniform consensus in high-uncertainty regions to mitigate overfitting. The initial ensemble prediction is given by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_{\text {ens}}(x) = \sum _{k} \hat{w}_k(x) p_k(x)$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{w}_k$$\end{document} are normalized weights.

Neighborhood-Consistent Refinement

Global models often struggle with decision boundaries in data-sparse regions. To rectify potential misclassifications for high-uncertainty samples (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$U(x) > \tau$$\end{document}), we introduce a local refinement module using K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN). For a query x, the local risk is estimated by:3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} p_{\text {knn}}(x) = \frac{1}{K_{nn}} \sum _{x_j \in \mathcal {N}(x)} y_j, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {N}(x)$$\end{document} denotes the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_{nn}=6$$\end{document} nearest neighbors in the feature space.

The final prediction \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_{\text {final}}(x)$$\end{document} integrates the global consensus with local empirical evidence:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} p_{\text {final}}(x) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} p_{\text {ens}}(x), & \text {if } U(x) \le \tau , \\ \beta \cdot p_{\text {ens}}(x) + (1-\beta ) \cdot p_{\text {knn}}(x), & \text {if } U(x) > \tau . \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta =0.8$$\end{document}. This dual-branch inference ensures that confident samples are processed efficiently by the global ensemble, while ambiguous samples undergo geometric correction, thereby improving robustness against boundary noise.

Furthermore, while the state-of-the-art tabular deep learning model TabNet31 provides a competitive recall, it suffers from a significantly lower accuracy (0.8191) and higher performance variance (Std = 0.0532), suggesting that complex neural architectures may be prone to overfitting on small-to-medium medical datasets.

The tree-based models (LightGBM32, XGBoost33, Random Forest34) show stronger results. Conclusion and future work

This study introduces the Uncertainty-Aware Feature-Weighted Ensemble (UAFE) framework, which enhances cardiovascular diagnostic reliability by integrating feature stratification with dynamic uncertainty modeling. Our main findings demonstrate that: (1) Adaptive Feature Stratification effectively preserves subtle clinical signals by training specialized learners on heterogeneous attribute subgroups; (2) Dynamic Uncertainty Weighting mitigates the risk of overconfident misclassifications by adaptively adjusting model contributions based on inter-model disagreement; and (3) Neighborhood-Based Refinement provides a robust safety net for high-uncertainty samples, ensuring stable decision boundaries. Experimental validation on a multi-center dataset confirms that UAFE achieves state-of-the-art performance, while the Leave-One-Center-Out (LOCO) analysis establishes its superior generalization against institutional distribution shifts. These findings suggest that transitioning from static deterministic modeling to a risk-aware paradigm significantly bridges the gap between algorithmic performance and clinical trust.

Despite its promising results, this study is grounded in several key assumptions and faces certain limitations. First, the UAFE framework assumes that inter-model disagreement, as quantified by variance, is a reliable proxy for the epistemic uncertainty of clinical samples; while effective, this may not capture all facets of predictive risk. Second, the current validation is conducted on a single-center, relatively small-scale tabular dataset, which may limit the generalizability of the findings across diverse patient populations. Third, our framework primarily focuses on structured clinical attributes, excluding unstructured multimodal inputs such as ECG signals or diagnostic imaging, which could provide additional diagnostic evidence. Finally, while the dual-branch inference strategy enhances robustness, it introduces a slight increase in computational overhead compared to single-baseline models, necessitating further efficiency optimization for real-time clinical deployment.

For practical applications, the framework’s uncertainty scores should be utilized to flag ambiguous cases for mandatory human clinician review, thereby ensuring a collaborative human-AI diagnostic process. Our projections for future research serve as a direct roadmap to address the identified constraints. This includes: (1) conducting external validation on multi-center, large-scale cohorts to verify cross-institutional generalizability; (2) extending the framework to multimodal data fusion—incorporating ECG waveforms and medical imaging—to provide a more holistic diagnostic view.

Electronic and Magnetic Properties of Ordered Double Transition-Metal MXenes: Ti2MC2T2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W; T = F, O, OH, and Cl)
Authors: Taoufik Sakhraoui, František Karlický
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Here, we use a GFN-xTB (Geometries, Frequencies, and Noncovalent interactions Tight Binding) Hamiltonian of the density functional tight binding (DFTB) method to study the structural, electronic, and magnetic properties of Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W) and Ti2MC2T2 (T = –F, –O, −OH, and –Cl). By investigating the magnetic ordering, we found that the bare, F, −OH, and Cl-terminated Ti2MC2 MXenes prefer an antiferromagnetic (AFM) alignment and show a metallic character. However, the magnetism is suppressed by O termination. This result suggests that magnetism can be enhanced in the presence of F-, OH-, and Cl-surface functional groups. We also investigated the Ti and M vacancy defects. We remark that the M-defect maintains the AFM ordering, whereas a magnetic transition from AFM to ferromagnetic (FM) ordering is shown by the creation of a Ti vacancy. Among the studied double transition-metal MXenes, the Ti-defected systems show the highest magnetic moments, and the magnetism is mainly due to the Ti atoms. Our DFTB investigations allow for efficient exploration of the magnetism in double metal MXenes and the effect of functionalization on the electronic and magnetic properties of the studied materials. The obtained electronic properties suggest that the Ti2MC2T2 monolayers are metals, the same behavior as compared to the majority of monotransition-metal MXenes. These findings can serve as a guide for comprehending the electronic and magnetic properties of double metal MXenes.

Introduction

Transition-metal carbides, nitrides, and carbonitrides, MXene two-dimensional (2D) materials, are currently the subject of intense experimental − and theoretical research − for their use in multiple applications, including biomedical, electronics, catalysis, and spintronics applications.

It is noteworthy that during the synthesis of MXenes, the formation of terminations is unavoidable due to the complex reaction process. In general, three-dimensional (3D) MAX phases as origin of MXene 2D materials have a crystallographic structure that contains a stacking of transition-metal (M) carbide or nitride (X) that has the M6X octahedrons interleaved with a plane of pure (A) atoms. By use of a proper etching method, the A element can be removed to form the MXene. The M atoms in the formed material are exposed and lose electrons and, therefore, show high reducibility, which makes the MXene reactive to the etchants and the solvent. Thus, for the conservation of charge, the negatively charged groups in the environment form bonds with the M atoms. As a result, terminations are formed. In most cases, the terminal groups are randomly arranged and they possess variable composition. , The widely studied MXene, Ti3C2, is produced by hydrofluoric acid (HF) etching Al out of Ti3AlC2 and has diverse composition and nonstoichiometry of terminations, including Ti2.94C2F2O0.55(OH)0.65 and Ti3C2(OH)0.12F0.8O0.54 as reported in refs and , respectively. However, for the sake of simplicity, the theoretical investigations consider single terminations that are uniformly distributed over the surface, such as Ti3C2F2, Ti3C2O2, and Ti3C2(OH)2. , In addition, single termination is surely also targeted in the experiment (Ti3C2O2). ,

Recently, ordered double transition-metal MXene, M2M′X2, was successfully synthesized. ,,, Double transition-metal MXene, whose formula unit is M2M′X2, where M and M′ represent two different transition metals and X is C or N atoms, consists of an inner transition-metal layer (M′) sandwiched between two layers of C or N and two layers of outer transition metal (M). The list of transition-metal elements of the ordered double MXene phases is provided in the recent ref . It was shown that varying the middle metal layer of the M3X2 composition by other metal atoms is a way to tune the properties of MXenes.

Moreover, studies focused on the functionalized double metal ordered MXene with a composition of M2M′C2T2 (T is the terminal group) show tunable electronic and magnetic properties using first-principles calculations. Functionalized MXenes based on Mo and Cr were already experimentally synthesized with high hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) activity. , A number of double transition metal MXenes have been experimentally synthesized ,,, and theoretically predicted, ,, such as (Mo2/3Y1/3)2CT x , Mo2VC2, , TiNbC, Mo2NbC2, , WCrC, Cr2MC2T x (M = Ti, V, and Nb; T = O, F, and OH), ,, (Ti0.5,Nb0.5)2C, (V0.5,Cr0.5)3C, (Nb0.8M0.2)4C3T x (M = Ti, Zr), Mo2MC2T x (M = Zr, Sc, Nb, Ti, V, Ta, Hf), ,,− and MCr2CT x (T = H, O, F, and OH; and M = Sc, Y, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Mo, and W). The density functional theory (DFT) method was utilized to predict the existence of the ordered double metal MXenes. Tunable properties are found based on the control of the composition of the double metal MXenes. , It was also shown that MXenes with uniform surface terminations have been realized. ,,, ,,, Recently, FM and AFM alignments in the ordered double metal MXenes have been predicted. ,, Some of them have already been synthesized, ,,, i.e., Mo2TiC2T2 and Cr2TiC2T2. Mo2TiC2(F/OH)2 and Cr2TiC2(F/OH)2 show AFM alignment; however, Cr2Ti2C3O x is FM. The first experimental evidence of a magnetic transition in the ordered double transition-metal carbide MXene material, Cr2TiC2T x , was in 2020 by the work of K. Hantanasirisakul et al., which offers the opportunity to investigate magnetism within this extensive group of 2D materials.

On the other hand, point defects are unavoidable during the synthesis of materials. Understanding their electronic band structures and magnetic properties may help in the broadening of MXene applications.

In the present work, we considered a single transition-metal defect in the studied MXenes. The structural stability, electronic structure, and magnetic properties of M2M′C2 (M = Ti; M′ = Cr, Mo, and W) were investigated using the xTB Hamiltonian of the DFTB method. Our results showed that the studied MXenes are AFM metals. In addition, functionalization by either F or Cl atoms maintains the AFM alignment; however, the terminal group of the O group suppresses the magnetism. This work is expected to provide insight into the design and preparation of novel double metal MXenes.

Theoretical Details

All calculations were carried out using the xTB Hamiltonian implemented in the DFTB+ code, , based on the DFTB method. − The spin-polarized correction was applied to the systems considered. The convergence criteria for the total energy and atomic force of each atom were set at 1 × 10–5 au per unit cell and 1 × 10–4 au, respectively. A vacuum spacing of 20 Å along the normal direction to the Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W) was used to avoid interactions caused by the periodic boundary condition. The Brillouin zone was sampled using 12 × 12 × 1 Γ-centered k-point Monkhorst–Pack grids for the calculation of structural and electronic properties of Ti2MC2 and Ti2MC2T2 (T = F, O, and Cl). Then, a Γ-point-only setup was used for the 5 × 5 × 1 supercells used to study the defective structures. We note that the xTB Hamiltonian was recently successfully applied on monometal MXenes and the spin constants used in this study were taken from the work of Neugebauer et al.

We also conducted molecular dynamics (MD) simulations for 20 ps in an NVT ensemble with a time step of 1.0 fs to assess the thermal stability of Ti2CrC2, Ti2MoC2, and Ti2WC2 double metal MXenes. The Nosé–Hoover thermostat was applied to control the temperature.

Defected Double Metal MXene

Here, we performed xTB-DFTB calculations to investigate the effects of different types of metal defects on the electronic behavior and magnetic properties of Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W). Vacancy defects are expected to occur on the top and middle metal sublayers. Figure shows the optimized structures of the considered single defects in 5 × 5 × 1 supercells, including a Ti vacancy (VTi) and an M vacancy (VM).

Models of different types of defects considered in Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W), including Ti vacancy and M vacancy. Blue, green, and chocolate colors represent Ti, M, and C atoms, respectively.

We calculated the formation energies (E f) of the considered vacancies in Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W) MXenes to better understand the energetic costs of the formation of defects as E f = −E pure + E vacancy + E atom, where E pure and E vacancy are the total energies of pure and defective MXene, respectively, and E atom is the total energies of Ti, Cr, Mo, and W atoms removed to create the vacancy. E atom was calculated using the energy per atom of one unit cell of the bulk metal in its most commonly occurring form, i.e., a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure for the Cr, Mo, and W metals and a hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure for Ti atom.

The results of the defect formation energies and magnetic moments are listed in Table . Since a defect formation energy is positive, it costs (consumes) energy to make a defect. Our results indicate that Ti vacancy creation is more energy-consuming than that of metal (Cr, Mo, and W) defects. The results show that defect formation in the outer sublayer is energetically more difficult than within the inner sublayers. To highlight the effect of vacancy defects on the electronic and magnetic properties of Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, W) MXenes, we explore their electronic properties. Figure shows the density of states of the defective structures under investigation. Our calculated results of the total DOS of the defected Ti2MC2 MXene indicate that all the considered defective MXenes maintain a metallic behavior. The symmetric distribution of DOS between the spin-up and spin-down states observed for Cr-, Mo-, and W-vacancy-deficient systems confirms the formation of magnetic AFM ground states. In Figure S3, we provide a schematic distribution of the local magnetic moments of the Ti atoms. We remark that magnetism is located mainly at the Ti sites. Functionalization of Double Metal MXene

It was already shown that the electronic and magnetic properties of MXenes are strongly influenced by the composition of the surface terminal groups. DFT calculations show that Ti3C2T x (T = −OH and −F) MXene sheets are metals. , However, it was found that the oxygen termination can open the band gap and transform them into semiconductors, depending on the type and position of the terminations. Here, bare Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, and W) MXene material consists of a 2D hexagonal unit cell, where M sheets are sandwiched between the Ti and C sheets (that is, Ti–C–M–C–Ti), as represented in ref . The two Ti, M, and the two C atoms are placed in (2/3, 1/3, 0.5 + u), (1/3, 2/3, 0.5 – u), (0, 0, 0.5), (1/3, 2/3, 0.5 + v), and (2/3, 1/3, 0.5 – v) positions, respectively. However, it has been shown that the etching of A layers from the bulk MAX phase leads to the termination of the MXene surfaces by terminal groups, such as O, − F, ,− Cl, − Br, ,, I, S, Se, OH, ,− BrI, and ClBrI.

In this study, we consider functionalized MXene with surfaces fully terminated by O, F, OH, and Cl. To form Ti2(Cr/Mo/W)­C2T2, T being a surface terminal group (i.e., F, OH, O or Cl), three models were considered including top C, top Ti, and top M sites as represented in Figure . It was shown that the M-top site is the most energetically favorable, ,, and therefore, it will be used in the rest of the study.

Our reported results show that the lattice parameters in MXene terminated with F, OH, and Cl are higher than that of bare Ti2MC2. However, the O termination keeps almost the same value. In addition, the Ti–C and Ti–M bond lengths in F- and Cl-terminated MXene are shorter than those of Ti2MC2. The opposite behavior was found with O termination.

First, we note that the functionalization of Ti2CrC2, Ti2MoC2, and Ti2WC2 by F, OH, and Cl, respectively, maintains the alignment of the AFM. However, an AFM-to-NM transition was found for O functionalization. This feature was also shown for the Cr2TiC2 double metal MXene. We found that the local magnetic moments of Ti atoms in the functionalized Ti2MC2 from ±1.69, ±1.93, and ±1.95 μB for Ti2CrC2, Ti2MoC2, and Ti2WC2, respectively, decreased to ±0.88, ±1.42, and ±1.38 μB after F functionalization and also decreased to ±1.03, ±1.56, and ±1.52 μB by the OH-terminal group. However, local magnetic moments in Ti are slightly increased by Cl functionalization except for Ti2CrC2Cl2. The terminal groups show small values of the magnetic moment ranging from 0.02 to 0.17 μB. Moreover, C moments present slight variation by F termination, it decreases from ±0.21, ±0.26, and ±0.27 μB in bare Ti2CrC2, Ti2MoC2, and Ti2WC2 materials, respectively, to ±0.14, ±0.22, and ±0.22 μB. The carbon atoms in Ti2CrC2(OH)2, Ti2MoC2(OH)2, and Ti2WC2(OH)2 possess local magnetic moments of ±0.18, ±0.25, and ±0.26 μB, respectively. Whereas for the Cl termination, the local magnetic moments in C atoms are ±0.21, ±0.34, and ±0.34 μB for Ti2CrC2Cl2, Ti2MoC2Cl2, and Ti2WC2Cl2, respectively.

We note that no magnetic moment was induced in M (M = Cr, Mo, and W) atoms in Ti2MC2F2, Ti2MC2(OH)2, and Ti2MC2Cl2 compounds. Here, we consider the surface termination of –F, OH, –O, and –Cl, and our results show that ordered Ti2MC2T2 MXenes maintain metallic behavior. This feature is in contradiction with band gap engineering through the functionalization shown in some MXenes, ,,, although low DOS values are observed at the Fermi level in the O-terminated Ti2MC2.

Conclusions

Under the framework of the DFTB method, we systematically explored the structure and electronic and magnetic properties of Ti2MC2 (M = Cr, Mo, W) double metal MXenes. Our calculations show that the structure is dynamically stable. We also studied the effect of vacancy defects creation on the electronic structure and magnetic properties. Density of states calculations indicate that there are minor changes in the electronic states, and the defected systems maintained a metallic behavior. Our results of the magnetic properties show magnetic characteristics with AFM and FM alignment for M and Ti defects, respectively. Moreover, we found that the magnetic moments are dominated by contributions from Ti atoms in bare, defected, and F-, OH-, and Cl-terminated Ti2MC2 MXenes. F, OH, and Cl terminal groups modify the local magnetic moments and maintain the AFM behavior. However, O termination transforms the AFM MXene to NM.

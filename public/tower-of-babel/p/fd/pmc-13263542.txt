The Prognostic Value of Serum Inflammatory Markers in Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma Patients Treated With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors: A Multicenter Study
Authors: Yang Xu, Jingyi Xie, Danmeng Zhang, Liru Zhu, Xiaojun Chen, Qingqing Zhang, Haoyu Lv, Kaihua Chen, Ling Li, Song Qu, Xishan Chen, Wenqi Liu, Xiaodong Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundAlthough immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have revolutionized the treatment of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), reliable predictive biomarkers need to be further explored. This study aimed to investigate the predictive ability of inflammatory markers for poor prognosis in NPC patients treated with ICIs.MethodsIn this multicenter retrospective study, a total of NPC patients treated with at least two cycles of ICIs from May 2019 to December 2022 at three independent medical centers in China were included. In this study, we collected the multiple hematological parameters from patients prior to treatment to calculate serum inflammatory markers, and we performed the multivariate COX analysis to identify independent prognostic factors for constructing columnar plots. Then the ROC survival curves, calibration curves, and clinical curve analysis were used to assess the predictive performance of the model.ResultsA total of 170 patients were included in the training group with a median follow‐up of 26 months. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses showed that Hb, Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI), and the number of metastases were independent prognostic factors for overall survival (OS). A nomogram was constructed based on these three factors to predict the prognosis of NPC patients after ICI treatment. The nomogram achieved a C‐index of 0.733 in the training cohort and 0.743 in the validation cohort, indicating good discriminative ability. Time‐dependent ROC curves demonstrated favorable predictive accuracy, with area under the curve (AUC) values for 1‐, 2‐, and 3‐year OS of 0.701, 0.756, and 0.760 in the training set, and 0.773, 0.784, and 0.777 in the validation set. The model was further validated using calibration plots and decision curve analysis (DCA), and patients were categorized into high‐ and low‐risk groups, with the validation cohort also showing consistent survival differentiation.ConclusionThe combination of serum inflammatory markers and clinicopathological factors may serve as a practical prognostic tool to predict NPC patients who may benefit from ICIs.Keywords: immune checkpoint inhibitors, immunity, inflammation, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, nomogram, nutrition, prognosis

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is an epithelial malignancy situated in the nasopharynx and has a strong correlation with infection by the Epstein–Barr virus (EBV). This type of cancer is frequently observed in regions such as southern China, North Africa, and Southeast Asia [1]. Due to the insidious and aggressive nature of nasopharyngeal cancer, more than 70% of patients are in locally advanced stages or with distant metastases when first diagnosed [2, 3, 4]. About 20%–30% of patients with advanced nasopharyngeal cancer are still not completely cured after comprehensive radiotherapy, mainly due to recurrence or metastasis [5]. Standard treatment options for patients suffering from advanced nasopharyngeal cancer, including radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and surgical interventions, tend to yield minimal results. These conventional approaches not only fail to provide substantial benefits for this particular patient population but are also frequently accompanied by significant adverse side effects [6].

At present, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have become an encouraging treatment choice for individuals diagnosed with NPC [7]. the drugs karelizumab and teraplizumab showed positive efficacy and safety profiles for treating recurrent or metastatic Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (RM‐NPC) [8, 9]. Consequently, the NCCN guidelines established in 2022, along with the clinical guidelines from the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO), advocate for the use of cisplatin/gemcitabine (GP) combined with a PD‐1 inhibitor (such as pembrolizumab, navulizumab, karelizumab, or teraplima) as the primary treatment option for patients suffering from RM‐NPC. Pembrolizumab and nivolumab have received approval from both the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the Chinese National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), while camrelizumab and toripalimab have been approved by the NMPA, with toripalimab also receiving approval from the FDA [10, 11, 12, 13].

Nonetheless, while ICIs can extend the lifespan of individuals with recurrent/metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (RM‐NPC), the objective response rate remains approximately 20%, indicating a relatively low effectiveness [14]. The effectiveness of PD‐1 inhibitors shows significant variability among patients, and at present, there are no reliable predictive biomarkers available to determine which patients might derive benefit from anti‐PD‐1 treatment [15]. Furthermore, the application of biomarkers derived from pathological testing faces challenges due to the low incidence of surgeries and biopsies in NPC. Hence, investigating non‐invasive biomarkers for NPC patients receiving ICIs is crucial. Of the various potential markers, peripheral blood parameters from tumor patients show the most promise, including the neutrophil‐to‐lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet‐to‐lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI). These indices reflect the intricate equilibrium between inflammatory processes and antitumor immune activity within the organism.

Numerous studies have consistently shown that the inflammatory response is crucial in the development of tumors. In recent times, various indicators representing the body's inflammatory status, including NLR, PLR, and PNI, have been associated with the prognosis of different malignant tumors [16, 17, 18, 19]. Notably, NLR, PLR, and PNI have demonstrated encouraging outcomes in forecasting the prognosis of melanoma, non‐small cell lung carcinoma, and various other cancers treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors [20, 21, 22]. Nonetheless, the connection between these metrics and survival outcomes in patients with nasopharyngeal cancer who receive ICIs has not been extensively explored or validated. As a result, we undertook this study to examine the prognostic importance of serum inflammatory biomarkers in nasopharyngeal cancer patients undergoing treatment with ICIs.

Materials and Methods

Patient Chosen and Treatment

From May 2019 to December 2022, 244 patients receiving ICIs treatment at three medical institutions (the Affiliated Tumor Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, the Second Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University) were included in the study, with the following inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) diagnosed with nasopharyngeal carcinoma by histology; (3) received at least two cycles of ICIs treatment or combined chemotherapy and radiotherapy; (4) had no infection, trauma, or use of immunomodulatory drugs within 3 months prior to the initiation of immunotherapy. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with other types of cancer within the past 5 years; (2) lack of complete clinical pathological data, hematological data, and follow‐up data. The training set consisted of 170 patients from the Affiliated Tumor Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, while the validation set comprised 74 patients from the Second Affiliated Hospital and the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University. Follow‐up continued until October 2024.

This research utilized the eighth edition of the staging system established by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC). The investigation adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee at the Affiliated Tumor Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (KY2024555). All procedures were carried out following the applicable guidelines and regulations.

Data Collection

The clinical and pathological characteristics of the patients were extracted from the electronic inpatient medical record system. In addition, treatment and follow‐up information were gathered, either through the electronic medical records or via telephone follow‐up with the patients. The clinical records encompass a variety of important data, including the patient's gender, age, Body Mass Index (BMI), and their smoking and drinking habits. Furthermore, details such as family medical history, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score, clinical stage of the disease, and the number of distant organ metastases were meticulously recorded. In terms of hematological parameters, key indicators such as hemoglobin levels, white blood cell counts, lymphocyte counts, monocyte counts, neutrophil counts, platelet counts, and serum albumin levels were also documented to provide a comprehensive overview of each patient's status. The formulas that calculate the serum inflammatory markers are as follows: NLR = Neutrophil count/Lymphocyte count, PLR = Platelet count/Lymphocyte count, LMR = Lymphocyte count/Monocyte count, PNI = albumin +5 × lymphocyte count.

Overall survival (OS) was characterized as the duration from the initial administration of ICIs to mortality from any reason or to the most recent follow‐up, serving as the primary endpoint of our research. Progression‐free survival (PFS), which was the secondary endpoint, was defined as the start of immunotherapy or the first documentation of tumor progression or death from any cause, whichever took place first.

Statistical Analysis

In the descriptive analysis, continuous variables exhibiting normal and non‐normal distributions were represented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and median [interquartile range (IQR)], respectively. For variables with a normal distribution, Student's t‐test was employed, whereas the Mann–Whitney rank sum test was utilized for statistical comparisons between groups for non‐normally distributed variables. Additionally, chi‐square or Fisher's exact tests were performed to evaluate categorical variables in order to analyze the baseline characteristics of the patients. The optimal cutoff value was determined by examining all potential unique splits of the OS, with the value for separation maximization identified in the KM analysis through the minimal bias adjustment of the log‐rank p value [23].

Cox survival analysis was employed for the univariate analysis, while the multivariate analysis utilized the forward stepwise Cox regression model. A nomogram model for immunotherapy in patients with NPC was developed based on the factors that were screened. For the process of verifying and evaluating the model, internal validation was carried out using the bootstrap resampling method with 1000 iterations, and a calibration plot was created to assess how well the predicted results aligned with the actual outcomes, thereby demonstrating the model's accuracy. The clinical applicability of this model was assessed using Decision Curve Analysis (DCA). The model's prediction accuracy and discriminability were evaluated through the consistency index (C‐index) and time‐receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. An area under the curve (AUC) greater than 0.7 signifies strong predictive power and discrimination. Statistical analyses and visual representations were conducted using R version 4.3.1, GraphPad version 10.0, and SPSS version 26.0. A two‐tailed p value of < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Patient Characteristics

In this study, there were 244 patients in total; among them, 174 patients were in the training group, and 74 patients were in the validation group. Their average age was 46.5 years. Regarding the TNM stage at diagnosis, 44 patients (18.0%) were at TNM stage III, 200 patients (82.0%) were at TNM stage IV, and 136 patients (55.7%) had occurred distant metastasis. In terms of treatment, 18 (7.4%) patients received only monotherapy for ICIs, 163 (66.8%) patients received ICIs and chemotherapy, 3 (1.2%) patients received ICIs and radiotherapy, and 60 (24.6%) received ICIs combined with synchronous chemotherapy. As of the last follow‐up on October 30, 2024, the median follow‐up time for patients was 27 months (interquartile range: 20–33 months). Among the patients treated with ICIs, 107 cases (43.8%) had disease progression, and regrettably, 66 cases (27.0%) died. The median progression‐free survival (PFS) of the entire cohort was 22 months (interquartile range: 11–29 months). The detailed information of other baseline clinical variables is listed in Table 1.

Association Between Biomarkers and Clinical Outcomes

In the training cohort, patients' overall survival was significantly shortened when the levels of inflammatory marker exceeded the preset threshold, patients with high baseline Hb were associated with longer OS (not reaching median OS vs. 45 months, p = 0.00051, Figure 1B), patients with high baseline LMR were associated with longer OS (not reaching median OS vs. 41 months, p = 0.013, Figure 1C), patients with low baseline NLR were associated with longer OS (not reaching median OS vs. 45 months, p = 0.048, Figure 1D), patients with low baseline PLR were associated with longer OS (not reaching median OS vs. 45 months, p = 0.04, Figure 1E), patients with high baseline PNI were associated with longer OS (not reaching median OS vs. 39 months, p < 0.0001, Figure 1F). Similarly, when these critical values of Hb, LMR, NLR, and PNI were used, PFS also showed significant differences (Figure 2). In the validation queue, higher LARs, lower PLRs, and higher PNI levels showed longer OS (Figure S1). A similar pattern was observed in PFS of PLR and PNI (Figure S2).

We conducted univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses to figure out the variables affecting OS. Univariate analysis showed that TNM stage (p = 0.002), the number of metastatic foci (p < 0.001), Hb (p = 0.004), PLR (p = 0.03), PNI (p < 0.001), and LMR (p = 0.016) were important prognostic factors. After incorporating these indicators into the multivariate Cox regression model, the results were as follows: TNM stage (HR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.18–2.42, p = 0.529); number of metastatic lesions (HR = 2.14, 95% CI: 1.08–4.21, p = 0.028); Hb (HR = 0.36, 95% CI: 0.18–0.70, p = 0.003); PLR (HR = 1.39, 95% CI: 0.66–2.97, p = 0.388); PNI (HR = 0.43, 95% CI: 0.24–0.79, p = 0.007); LMR (HR = 0.74, 95% CI: 0.38–1.46, p = 0.388). These results indicate that the number of metastatic lesions, Hb, and PNI are independent predictors of poor prognosis in these patients. Further details are provided in Table 2.

Construction and Evaluation of Nomogram

We developed prognostic nomograms based on independent factors which were identified in multivariable COX regression analysis, including the number of metastases, PNI, and Hb levels (Figure 3). The C‐index value of the nomogram was 0.733 in the training set and 0.743 in the validation set. Then, according to its prediction ability, we drew the AUC curves that vary with time. The AUC values of 1–3 years of the training set OS were 0.701, 0.756, and 0.760, respectively, and the AUC values of 1–3 years of the verification set OS were 0.773, 0.784, and 0.777, respectively (Figure 4). The nomogram was verified using Bootstrap analysis and calibration curves which indicated that the prediction accuracy of the nomogram was extremely high (Figure 5). Furthermore, we constructed the clinical Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) graph to enhance the assessment of the nomogram's predictive accuracy. The DCA graph also illustrated the strong predictive capacity of the nomogram (Figure 6).

Discussion

In recent years, there have been significant advancements in the immunotherapy of NPC, with ICIs playing a crucial role in treating patients with this condition. Nonetheless, the high expense associated with immunotherapy drugs, coupled with immune‐related side effects, creates an urgent necessity to discover highly predictive biomarkers. These biomarkers could facilitate the clinical application of ICIs by forecasting both their benefits and the risk of severe toxicity. Non‐invasive biomarkers, in particular, might hold greater promise for patients suffering from NPC [24, 25]. Therefore, our study aims to explore whether there are inflammatory markers with such capabilities in this case. To our knowledge, this might be the first multicenter study to investigate the efficacy and prognosis of NPC patients receiving ICIs treatment. By comparing various inflammatory and nutritional indicators, our goal is to determine the best prognostic factors for NPC patients receiving ICIs treatment. Our findings indicate that low hemoglobin levels and a low Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI) are negative prognostic indicators for patients with NPC undergoing immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Additionally, these hematological assessments and biomarkers are essential, readily accessible, and could act as dependable measures to assist healthcare professionals in recognizing patients who are likely to reap significant advantages from targeted treatment approaches.

Inflammatory responses play an important role in tumorigenesis, progression, and prognosis [26]. More and more evidence indicates that systemic inflammatory biomarkers from blood are effective predictive indicators for the prognosis of ICIs in various cancers. In a 2022 investigation regarding gastric cancer, Sun et al. examined 146 patients undergoing ICIs and discovered that the PNI held a significant correlation with patient prognosis and served as an independent prognostic indicator for individuals treated with ICIs [27]. A comparable conclusion was drawn in a separate study focusing on lung cancer [28]. Ongoing research is also delving into the biomarkers associated with NPC immunotherapy. In their analysis, Guo et al. evaluated 131 patients diagnosed with De Novo Metastatic NPC and identified that the baseline NRI, PNI, Systemic Immune Inflammation Index (SII), uric acid, and post‐treatment EBV DNA exhibited strong predictive value for prognosis [29]. Our research results further confirmed the prognostic correlation of PNI and Hb before treatment, among which the lower values of these biomarkers were associated with adverse outcomes after ICI treatment. Furthermore, unlike the study by Xu et al., which was limited to recurrent/metastatic NPC with a small sample size, our study included all NPC patients receiving ICIs and analyzed a larger multicenter cohort, offering broader applicability. The prognostic value of PNI in various solid tumors has gradually attracted attention in recent years [30]. Numerous research efforts have validated that PNI serves as an all‐encompassing marker indicating the nutritional status and inflammation level within the body, showing a significant correlation with the survival outcomes of cancer patients undergoing treatment with ICIs [31, 32, 33]. This study found that PNI ⩽ 42.8 was an independent adverse prognostic factor for ICI treatment in patients with NPC, providing an important reference for clinical screening of potential beneficiary populations. The mechanism may be related to the interaction between nutritional status and systemic inflammation: Low PNI not only reflects the immunosuppressive microenvironment caused by malnutrition but may also promote tumor immune escape through chronic inflammation [30, 34]. Anemia is usually an indicator of invasive tumor biology. In the context of nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), anemia may arise from tumor‐related mechanisms such as bone marrow infiltration, chronic inflammation (inflammatory anemia), or the systemic effects of advanced disease. These underlying processes not only reflect a more aggressive tumor phenotype but may also contribute to an immunosuppressive microenvironment that influences response to immune checkpoint inhibitors [22, 35, 36]. Therefore, anemia can serve as a biomarker for identifying patients who may benefit from ICI therapy. Our research further confirmed these associations.

To evaluate the contribution of inflammatory markers to the prognosis of NPC patients receiving ICIs, we developed a nomogram based on the number of metastases, Hb, and PNI, and the nomogram showed good predictive ability. Additionally, the calibration curve alongside the DCA decision curve demonstrated the strong predictive capability of the nomogram developed from inflammatory markers. To assess this nomogram's performance and its applicability, we performed rigorous validation using independent external cohorts. The findings derived from t‐ROC analysis, C index, and calibration curve revealed that the nomogram, which incorporates inflammatory markers, is capable of precise prognosis stratification and exhibits consistent generalization across diverse clinical settings.

However, our research has some limitations. Although we increased the cohort size and evaluated some inflammatory markers, the retrospective study design should be verified by prospective studies and a larger sample size. To minimize selection bias, we included every NPC patient who received ICIs treatment at our three institutions from 2019 to 2022, reflecting real‐life applications. Additionally, our analysis did not incorporate immune‐related adverse events (irAEs), a crucial aspect when assessing the safety of ICI therapy. This exclusion hinders our ability to associate hematological inflammatory markers with irAEs, which could offer valuable insights. Moreover, EBV DNA titer, a well‐established prognostic biomarker in NPC, was not included in the analysis due to inconsistent testing standards and incomplete data across participating centers, which represents another limitation of this study. The findings of this study require further validation in future research, particularly in prospective studies that involve larger populations. Furthermore, it is important to note that a portion of the patients in this cohort underwent combination therapy, making the influence of confounding factors unavoidable.

Conclusion

Integrating serum inflammatory markers with clinicopathological factors may yield a useful and readily available prognostic instrument capable of forecasting treatment outcomes and aiding in treatment decision‐making for patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma undergoing immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.

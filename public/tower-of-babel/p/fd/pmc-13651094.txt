Differences in systemic inflammation and prethrombotic biomarkers between AECOPD patients with and without pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Xiao Bo Wang, Zhu Qi Gao, Ying Yu, Yang Fei Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe relationship between inflammatory cytokines and prothrombotic markers with PH during the acute phase of acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD) remains unclear. This study aims to compare the profiles of peripheral blood biomarkers in AECOPD patients with and without Pulmonary Hypertension(PH), and to evaluate their diagnostic significance.MethodsWe conducted a case-control study enrolling 148 patients admitted to the Department of Respiratory Medicine at Fuyang First People’s Hospital in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, China, between January 2023 and April 2025. The patients were categorized into three groups: a control group (n = 49), an AECOPD group (n = 59), and an AECOPD with PH group (n = 40). The clinical characteristics and the relationships between inflammatory cytokines and prothrombotic markers were compared among the three groups. Logistic regression analysis was employed to identify inflammatory biomarkers and prothrombotic markers with independent predictive value. The predictive accuracy of these risk factors was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.ResultsCompared with healthy volunteers, AECOPD patients showed elevated levels of PIV, SII, IL-8, CRP, NLR, and PLR (p < 0.0001) based on quartile comparisons. Levels of these inflammatory markers were significantly elevated in both AECOPD and AECOPD + PH groups compared to controls (p < 0.0001). However, no significant differences were found between the AECOPD and AECOPD + PH groups. Among the prethrombotic markers, D-dimer and TAT were elevated in AECOPD patients with PH. ROC curve analysis identified PIV, SII, IL-8, CRP, NLR, and PLR as significant predictors of AECOPD. Among the inflammatory markers, SII exhibited the highest area under the curve (AUC) of 0.834 (95% CI: 0.748–0.905). The combination of SII and IL-8 yielded the highest AUC among inflammatory markers, at 0.926 (95% CI: 0.876–0.967). Furthermore, the combination of D‑dimer and TAT resulted in a higher AUC than either marker alone, with an AUC of 0.919 (95% CI: 0.865–0.966) for identifying PH in AECOPD patients.ConclusionAECOPD patients with PH exhibit a distinct biomarker pattern characterized by a plateau in systemic inflammation alongside progressive coagulation activation. We speculate that this may be attributed to a regionalized redistribution of the inflammatory response or functional exhaustion of the immune system. The combination of inflammatory markers (SII and IL-8) and prothrombotic markers (D-dimer and TAT) provides excellent discriminative ability for diagnosing AECOPD and identifying concomitant PH, respectively. These findings support the use of combined biomarker panels for individualized risk assessment and early detection of PH in AECOPD patients.Clinical trial registration numberNot suitable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12950-026-00508-7.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is the third leading cause of death worldwide [1] and the fifth leading cause of death in China [2]. It is characterized by high morbidity and mortality rates [3, 4]. An acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) refers to an acute worsening of the condition [5], marked by the deterioration of one or more symptoms such as increased cough, worsened dyspnea, increased sputum production, and changes in sputum consistency [3]. Importantly, AECOPD is a major cause of hospitalization and mortality among COPD patients, with in-hospital, 3-month, 6-month, and 2-year mortality rates of 6.7%,18%,26%,and 31% [6–8], respectively. Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a common and serious complication of COPD [6] and is closely associated with poor prognosis [9]. Studies have shown that the mortality rate among AECOPD patients with PH is significantly higher than that among those without PH, with a 28-day mortality rate ranging from 15.73% to 20.83% in patients with PH [9], while the mortality rate in the non-PH group is significantly lower. Furthermore, higher grades of PH are associated with increased mortality [10].

The coexistence of COPD and PH exists within a state of heightened systemic inflammation [11, 12], which promotes the formation of venous thrombosis. This aberration in coagulation function is particularly pronounced during episodes of AECOPD [13]. A hypercoagulable state is also termed a prothrombotic state (PTS) [14, 15]. It represents a pathological process involving an imbalance of the haemostatic, coagulation, and anticoagulation systems caused by multiple factors [16]. The resultant alterations within the haematological system predispose individuals to thrombus formation [17].

Research indicates that abnormal coagulation status and thrombosis can accelerate the progression of COPD and hasten the development of pulmonary hypertension [18]. Thrombosis increases pulmonary vascular resistance, consequently leading to a remarkably high probability (up to 92.6%) of cor pulmonale in AECOPD patients. Studies have reported that the combination of inflammatory indices such as neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet to lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and systemic immune/inflammatory index (SII) [19] provides stronger predictive power and clinical evidence than any single marker alone. Significant correlations have been observed between NLR, PLR, SII and traditional markers like C-reactive protein (CRP)and procalcitonin (PCT) in AECOPD patients, with elevations of these markers being highly significant [20]. In both AECOPD and AECOPD-PH, the concurrent elevation of NLR/PLR/SII with CRP/PCT often indicates exacerbated systemic inflammation and immune dysregulation [21]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that combined use of these markers (e.g., high NLR + high CRP, high SII + high PCT) predicts disease severity, treatment response, complications (such as respiratory failure), length of hospital stay, readmission rate, and mortality more accurately than individual parameters. These biomarker combinations may serve as independent risk indicators for both AECOPD and AECOPD-PH patients.

Under physiological conditions, severe inflammation activates the coagulation system and suppresses the fibrinolytic system [22], while simultaneously causing injury to vascular endothelial cells.D-dimer, TAT (thrombin-antithrombin complex), PIC (plasmin-α2-plasmin inhibitor complex), and t-PAIC (tissue plasminogen activator-inhibitor complex) [23] serve as standard biomarkers in AECOPD for assessing VTE [24] risk, disease severity, and prognosis. They are also key indicators of chronic hypercoagulability and microthrombosis prognosis in patients with stable COPD and PH [25].

It remains unclear whether the presence of PH in AECOPD [26] constitutes a straightforward amplification of systemic inflammation and hypercoagulability, or represents a distinct pathophysiological phenotype with a divergent biomarker signature.Therefore, this study was designed to conduct a cross-sectional comparison across three participant groups to delineate the differences in inflammatory and prothrombotic biomarkers and to evaluate their potential diagnostic value for AECOPD complicated by PH.

Materials and methods

Study design

This case-control study was conducted at the First People’s Hospital of Fuyang District, Hangzhou, China. Consecutive patients admitted to the Department of Respiratory Medicine between January 2023 and April 2025 were enrolled as the case group, while age- and sex-matched healthy controls were concurrently recruited during the same period. All participants provided written informed consent prior to study inclusion. The study protocol received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the First People’s Hospital of Fuyang District in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Furthermore, studies comparing spirometric diagnostic criteria (GOLD, NICE, and LLN) have demonstrated that the concordance between these methods progressively declines with age, with a notable decrease in agreement after age 55, supporting a narrowed lower age boundary to reduce diagnostic heterogeneity [29]. Data collection, variables, and blood sampling

Clinical and demographic data collection

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were systematically obtained from electronic medical records and supplemented by standardized patient interviews upon admission. The collected variables included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, history of hypertension, and the length of hospital stay (also presented as “Hospitalization days” in tables).

Definition of key variables

Smoking history

Smoking status was meticulously classified and quantified due to its central role in COPD. Assessment followed a structured protocol:

Status classification

Patients were categorized as current smokers (active smoking or cessation within the past 6 months), former smokers (cessation > 6 months prior), or never smokers (lifetime exposure of < 100 cigarettes).

Quantification of exposure

For current and former smokers, the average number of cigarettes smoked per day and the total duration of smoking in years were recorded. Cumulative tobacco exposure was calculated and expressed as pack-years using the standard formula: Pack-years = (cigarettes per day / 20) × years smoked.

History of hypertension

This was defined as a binary variable (yes/no) based on any of the following: (1) a pre-existing diagnosis documented in the medical record, (2) current use of antihypertensive medication, or (3) repeated systolic/diastolic blood pressure measurements ≥ 140/90 mmHg during the current hospitalization.

Timing of blood sample collection

To accurately capture biomarker profiles during the acute exacerbation phase, peripheral venous blood samples were collected in EDTA and sodium citrate tubes within 24 h of hospital admission, prior to the administration of systemic corticosteroids or anticoagulant therapy.

Laboratory assays

Processed plasma and serum samples were analyzed for the following parameters using standardized automated platforms and commercial assay kits:

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.1. For continuous data with non‑normal distribution, group comparisons were conducted using the Kruskal–Wallis test and the Mann–Whitney U test, with results expressed as H and z statistics, respectively. The chi-squared test (χ² statistic) was used for preliminary screening of independent variables and for analysis of categorical data. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic value of each parameter in patients with AECOPD. Binary logistic regression analysis was applied to estimate the combined diagnostic probability of peripheral blood inflammatory markers; the corresponding ROC curves were constructed, and the area under the curve (AUC) was employed to compare the diagnostic performance of different modalities.

Result

Baseline characteristics of study population

A total of 148 participants were enrolled, including 49 healthy controls, 59 patients with AECOPD alone, and 40 patients with AECOPD complicated by PH. The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Significant differences were observed across groups in age (H = 34.68, p < 0.001) and BMI (H = 6.69, p = 0.035). Post-hoc analysis revealed that both AECOPD and AECOPD + PH patients were significantly older than controls (both p < 0.001), while no significant age difference was detected between the two patient groups (p = 0.102). BMI was significantly lower in the AECOPD + PH group compared with controls (p = 0.011), but did not differ significantly between AECOPD and AECOPD + PH groups (p = 0.282).Sex distribution, smoking status, and history of hypertension were comparable among the three groups (all p > 0.05). Notably, the proportion of current smokers was higher in the AECOPD + PH group (85.0%) than in the AECOPD (64.4%) and control (61.2%) groups, although this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.144).As expected, patients with AECOPD + PH had a significantly longer length of hospital stay than those with AECOPD alone (mean ± SD: 13.15 ± 6.74 vs. 6.48 ± 4.51 days; H = 496.5, p < 0.001).

These findings confirm that AECOPD patients with concomitant PH represent a more severe clinical phenotype characterized by advanced age, lower BMI, and prolonged hospitalization, despite comparable smoking and comorbidity profiles.

Inflammatory cytokine correlation analysis

Inflammatory cytokines serve as core biomarkers for assessing the severity of AECOPD, guiding treatment decisions, and predicting clinical outcomes. Therefore, relevant inflammatory markers including PIV, SII, IL-8, CRP, NLR, and PLR were measured. Based on comparisons using boxplot analyses according to quartiles, the study found that PIV, SII, IL-8, CRP, NLR, and PLR were all significantly up-regulated in both AECOD patients and those with AECOPD complicated by pulmonary hypertension compared to the control group. However, no statistically significant differences were observed in SII, IL-8, CRP, NLR, or PLR between AECOPD patients and those with AECOPD combined with pulmonary hypertension (Table S1; Fig. 1).

In patients with AECOPD complicated by PH, vascular endothelial injury—driven by hypoxia and inflammation—leads to activation of the coagulation system. Predictive performance of biomarkers for AECOPD and AECOPD with PH

To identify independent predictors and develop clinically applicable tools, multivariable logistic regression models were constructed with adjustment for age, BMI, gender, hypertension, and smoking status. Figure 3A and supplement Table 2 illustrates the adjusted associations of candidate biomarkers with the presence of AECOPD and AECOPD + PH relative to healthy controls. Compared with controls, both AECOPD and AECOPD + PH groups demonstrated significantly elevated odds ratios for IL-8, SII, NLR, PLR, and log (CRP + 0.1) (all p < 0.05) Among them, the increases in IL-8 and SII were more significant. In contrast, D-dimer and TAT exhibited stronger and more selective associations with the AECOPD + PH group.

Based on the final regression coefficients, two prediction nomograms were constructed. Figure 3B shows the nomogram for estimating the individual probability of AECOPD. Higher levels of IL‑8 and SII, older age, lower BMI, male sex, presence of hypertension, and current smoking contributed more points, leading to a higher predicted risk of AECOPD. Figure 3C presents the nomogram for estimating the probability of PH among AECOPD patients. Elevated D-dimer and TAT, older age, lower BMI, male sex, absence of hypertension, and current smoking were associated with increased total points and thus a greater predicted probability of AECOPD + PH.

ROC curve analyses were performed to evaluate the discriminative ability of individual biomarkers and their combinations (Table S3). For distinguishing AECOPD patients from healthy controls (Fig. 3D), IL-8 alone yielded an AUC of 0.739 (95% CI: 0.642–0.832), SII alone an AUC of 0.834 (95% CI: 0.748–0.905). After full adjustment for covariates, the combined model achieved the highest AUC of 0.926 (95% CI: 0.876–0.967).For distinguishing AECOPD patients with PH from healthy controls (Fig. 3E), D-dimer alone exhibited an AUC of 0.827 (95% CI: 0.735–0.918), TAT alone an AUC of 0.772 (95% CI: 0.667–0.855).The covariate-adjusted combined model demonstrated superior discriminative performance, with an AUC of 0.919 (95% CI: 0.865–0.966).

Collectively, these results demonstrate that systemic inflammatory markers (IL-8, SII) are robust predictors of AECOPD, whereas prothrombotic markers (D-dimer, TAT) are more specifically associated with the presence of PH in the setting of AECOPD. The constructed nomograms and combined ROC models provide quantitative, individualized risk assessment tools that may facilitate early identification of high-risk patients.

Discussion

This study delineates a divergent pathophysiological trajectory in AECOPD patients with concomitant PH: systemic inflammation reaches an elevated plateau, whereas coagulation activation intensifies progressively.This divergent trajectory is supported by three core findings.

First, circulating inflammatory markers (PIV, SII, IL-8, CRP, NLR, PLR) were uniformly elevated in both AECOPD and AECOPD + PH groups versus controls, yet no further escalation accompanied the development of PH.Despite marked elevations relative to healthy controls, the absence of a further inflammatory surge in AECOPD-PH patients contrasts partially with prior reports [32] observed higher NLR, PLR, and SII in AECOPD-PH versus AECOPD alone; however, multivariable adjustment abrogated these associations, it may suggest residual confounding by disease severity or comorbidities.The discrepancy may stem from differences in cohort characteristics our patients were older, had longer disease duration, and higher smoking prevalence factors that promote chronic low-grade inflammation with blunted acute responsiveness, a phenotype compatible with immune exhaustion [33]. Moreover, inflammatory trajectories are biomarker-specific; progressive elevation of CA125 [34] in AECOPD-PH highlights heterogeneity across mediators.Furthermore, the prognostic value of these inflammatory markers for other AECOPD-related complications has been documented in several studies [35] demonstrated that CRP predicts 90-day readmission more robustly than sputum inflammatory markers [36] identified NLR, PLR, and SII as independent predictors of frequent exacerbations [21] recently showed that the same biomarkers—NLR, PLR, SII—independently predict symptomatic venous thromboembolism in hospitalized AECOPD patients. Collectively, these studies affirm that systemic inflammation drives multiple adverse outcomes in AECOPD. Our findings suggest that PH may represent an exception to the paradigm of inflammation-driven AECOPD complications: despite its established association with inflammatory processes, the development of PH in our cohort was not accompanied by a parallel systemic inflammatory surge.We hypothesize that this phenomenon may reflect an inflammatory plateau or systemic immune exhaustion, and suggest that AECOPD-PH could represent a distinct endotype diverging from other inflammatory-driven comorbidities.

Second, in marked contrast to the static inflammatory milieu, D-dimer and TAT exhibited a stepwise increase from AECOPD alone to AECOPD + PH. Multivariable analysis confirmed both as independent predictors of PH (D-dimer: aOR = 2.17 per µg/mL; TAT: aOR = 1.50 per µg/L). This progressive coagulopathy is consistent with the established role of coagulation markers in AECOPD. incorporated D-dimer into a predictive model for symptomatic VTE, underscoring its thrombotic risk association. The present findings suggest that PH may confer an additional prothrombotic risk in patients with AECOPD-an observation that, to our knowledge, has not been previously reported in the literature. The coexistence of an inflammatory plateau and progressive coagulopathy may point to a distinct biomarker phenomenon, potentially offering a novel rationale for anticoagulation in selected patients with AECOPD-PH.

Third, ROC analyses quantified diagnostic utility. For AECOPD detection, SII (AUC = 0.834) and IL-8 (AUC = 0.739) individually, and their covariate-adjusted combination (AUC = 0.926), exhibited excellent performance. For PH identification among AECOPD patients, the adjusted D-dimer + TAT model achieved an AUC of 0.919, substantially outperforming single markers.

We speculate that the observed inflammatory plateau may reflect either localized pulmonary vascular inflammation, which may evade accurate detection in peripheral blood, or systemic immune exhaustion, a concept that remains speculative and requires further investigation in this context.The concept of immune exhaustion, originally described in the context of chronic infection and malignancy, has been characterized by diminished effector cytokine production and attenuated responsiveness to persistent antigenic stimulation. Whether a analogous phenomenon occurs in the setting of recurrent AECOPD and contributes to blunted systemic inflammatory responses in patients with PH remains speculative and warrants further investigation. Conversely, hemodynamic alterations secondary to PH may promote sustained activation of the coagulation cascade. This dissociation between quiescent systemic inflammation and progressive hypercoagulability may represent a distinct pathophysiological phenotype of AECOPD‑PH.

Our study is subject to several limitations. First, due to its cross-sectional, case-control design, causality between observed biomarker profiles and disease status cannot be inferred; longitudinal studies are required to delineate temporal dynamics. Second, the single-center, retrospective nature and relatively modest sample size constrain generalizability. Although a post-hoc power analysis confirmed adequate statistical power (> 90%) for detecting our primary findings (e.g.differences in prothrombotic markers), the study may have been underpowered to identify more subtle distinctions, such as in systemic inflammatory markers between AECOPD and AECOPD + PH groups, necessitating cautious interpretation of these null results. Finally, the diagnosis of pulmonary hypertension relied solely on echocardiography rather than the gold-standard right heart catheterization, which may introduce classification bias. These limitations underscore the need for larger, prospective, multi-center studies incorporating invasive hemodynamic confirmation to validate and extend our observations.

Therefore, the primary contribution of this study is not the isolated reporting of inflammation or hypercoagulability, but the integrated observation of their dissimilar evolution in AECOPD-PH. The pattern—an early plateau in systemic inflammation concurrent with a rising trajectory of coagulation activation—provides a new pathophysiological framework. It challenges the assumption of a simple additive effect and substantiates our proposed hypotheses that either a compartmentalized pulmonary vascular inflammatory focus or a state of systemic immune exhaustion may characterize the progression to PH in AECOPD. This dissociation between inflammatory and coagulation biomarker dynamics underscores AECOPD-PH as a distinct endotype with potential implications for targeted management strategies.

Role of cystatin C in predicting the risk of hypermethotrexemia and kidney injury after high-dose methotrexate chemotherapy in childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia
Authors: Wenting Pei, Xiao Zhang, Li-an Du, Zhijuan Liu, Li Song, Muyuan Ji, Xiao Mou, Luping Ding, Fu Li, Xiaoling Li, Xiaomei Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study analyzed the correlation among serum cystatin C (CysC) levels, hypermethotrexemia, and kidney damage following high-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX) chemotherapy in children with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) to aid early prediction, diagnosis, and treatment. Clinical data from 103 children diagnosed with ALL were collected, totaling 412 HD-MTX chemotherapy sessions. The association between 20-h serum CysC levels, 44-h MTX blood concentrations, and 44-h serum creatinine levels was analyzed. Forty-four patients in the low-risk group received 3 g/m2 of HD-MTX, while 59 patients in the intermediate-risk group received 5 g/m2 of HD-MTX. Some patients experienced a 20% dose reduction due to high 44-h MTX concentrations in previous cycles; however, no dose increases were observed. In the high MTX concentration group, 31.4% (27/86) of patients had elevated 20-h serum CysC levels, significantly higher than the 2.8% (9/324) in the low-concentration group (P < 0.001). However, baseline serum CysC levels did not differ significantly (P = 0.377). Among the 36 cycles with elevated 20-h serum CysC levels, 19.4% (7/36) of patients progressed to elevated serum creatinine levels at 44 h. In contrast, only one patient in the normal serum CysC group showed such progression (P < 0.001). Furthermore, the 20-h serum CysC levels positively correlated with 44-h MTX concentrations and serum creatinine levels. Our findings indicate that 20-h serum CysC levels can predict hyperammonemia and kidney injury.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-026-06938-w.

Introduction

Cystatin C (CysC) is a low-molecular-weight protein produced consistently by nucleated cells and completely filtered by the glomeruli. Therefore, changes in its levels can reflect early damage to kidney function [1]. Methotrexate (MTX) is a classic antifolate anticancer drug that primarily inhibits tumor cell growth by blocking the dihydrofolate reductase activity, thereby impairing tumor cell synthesis. High-dose MTX (HD-MTX) chemotherapy is a key treatment modality for pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and effectively improves patient survival [2, 3]. However, kidney failure is one of the most severe adverse reactions during HD-MTX therapy [4]. As MTX is primarily excreted unchanged via the kidneys, studies investigating the correlation between serum CysC—an early indicator of kidney function—MTX concentration and creatinine levels are of particular importance. Furthermore, studies determining whether delayed MTX excretion leads to acute kidney injury (AKI) are also necessary. Currently, few such studies have been conducted in children. Hence, this study aimed to provide evidence for early prediction of kidney injury as well as for the identification of delayed MTX excretion.

Methods

Subjects

A retrospective analysis was conducted on pediatric patients with ALL who were admitted to our department (Department of Pediatric Hematology/Oncology, Children’s Hospital affiliated to Shandong University, Jinan, China) between November 2020 and December 2023. We collected serum CysC and creatinine levels, as well as MTX concentrations, before and after HD-MTX use, and analyzed their correlations. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) diagnosis of pediatric ALL based on bone marrow classification integrating morphology, immunology, cytogenetics, and molecular biology (MICM); (2) age within the pediatric definition, typically 0–18 years; (3) complete and accessible clinical data, including data on MTX concentration and kidney function monitoring at 20 and 44 h post-chemotherapy, as well as baseline information, such as age, sex, and pre- and post-chemotherapy test results; and (4) having received the Chinese Children’s Cancer Group-Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia-2020 (CCCG-ALL-2020) Protocol, including HD-MTX chemotherapy during consolidation therapy (Supplementary Table 1). Regarding treatment regimens, HD-MTX was administered at 3 g/m2 for the low-risk group and 5 g/m2 for the intermediate-risk group. Notably, a 20% dose reduction was implemented in some patients owing to elevated MTX concentrations 44 h after previous chemotherapy. The standard HD-MTX administration protocol was followed: 1/10 of the total dose was rapidly infused (completed within 30 min), and the remaining dose was continuously infused over 23.5 h. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) presence of other severe conditions (such as congenital heart, liver, or kidney disease) that could affect chemotherapy efficacy or increase study complexity and (2) having received other experimental treatments or chemotherapy regimens before study initiation that could influence the study outcomes. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Jinan Children’s Hospital (SDFE-IRB/P-2024035), and guardians and children (aged ≥ 8 years old) signed an informed consent form. No patients opted out of this retrospective study, given that it involved only the analysis of routine clinical data and posed no additional interventions or risks.

Data collection and definition

The information listed below was collected from all patients included in this study. Basic information about the pediatric patients (sex and age); specific regimen for HD-MTX chemotherapy (dose, interruption, etc.); and laboratory test data: (1) MTX concentration monitoring and (2) kidney function indicators: serum CysC and creatinine levels measured 20 and 44 h after chemotherapy. MTX concentration test results were collected at 20 and 44 h after chemotherapy. Based on the concentration data, patients were divided into a high MTX concentration group (44-h MTX concentration ≥ 1 µmol/L) and a low concentration group (44-h MTX concentration < 1 µmol/L). AKI was defined according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) clinical practice guidelines [5], as an increase in serum creatinine by ≥ 0.3 mg/dL (≥ 26.5 µmol/L) within 48 h, or an increase in serum creatinine to ≥ 1.5 times baseline, or urine volume < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h. Because of the limited available data due to the retrospective study design, we assessed AKI based on serum creatinine measured at 44 h post-chemotherapy. Patients were classified as having AKI if their 44-hour serum creatinine represented an increase from the baseline value, exceeding the following age-specific thresholds: >40 µmol/L for children under 2 years of age, > 60 µmol/L for children aged 2–8 years, and > 80 µmol/L for children and adolescents aged 9–18 years. Elevated serum CysC level was defined as a level above the baseline.

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically processed and analyzed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences 27.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.10. Descriptive statistics were conducted on the basic information, chemotherapy information, and laboratory test data for pediatric patients, including mean, standard deviation, median, and quartiles, to examine the distribution of the data. Independent samples t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests were used to compare serum CysC levels between the high and low MTX concentration groups before and 20 h after chemotherapy. The appropriate test method was selected based on the normality of the data and homogeneity of variances. Simple linear regression was used to analyze the correlation between serum CysC levels at 20 h and MTX concentrations at 44 h, and between CysC levels at 20 h and serum creatinine levels at 44 h. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to analyze the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of the 20-h CysC levels in predicting high MTX blood levels and AKI. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess predictive performance. The optimal predictive threshold for 20-h CysC levels was determined using the ROC curve by selecting the cutoff value with the maximum Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1). The ROC curves were compared using the DeLong test to assess whether differences in predictive efficacy occurred among the 20-h CysC level, 20-h MTX concentration, and combined prediction.

Results

Basic data

This study included 103 pediatric patients with ALL who received HD-MTX chemotherapy, totaling 412 chemotherapy cycles. Data for the 20-h serum CysC and creatinine levels were missing for two cycles. The study population included 58 males and 45 females, with a median age of 4 years and 4 months (range: 11 months to 13 years) at diagnosis. Risk stratification identified 44 cases in the low-risk group and 59 in the intermediate-risk group. All patients received chemotherapy in accordance with the CCCG-ALL-2020 protocol, with dose reductions implemented in 86 cycles; no dose increases were required. ROC curves of 20-h CysC levels, 20-h MTX concentrations, and their combination in predicting the occurrence of hypermethotrexemia

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the ROC curve analysis of 20-h CysC levels, 20-h MTX concentrations, and their combined index revealed that the AUCs for the three were 0.729 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.663–0.795, P < 0.0001), 0.748 (95% CI: 0.695–0.802, P < 0.0001), and 0.823 (95% CI: 0.774–0.872, P < 0.0001), respectively. A DeLong test comparison revealed that the predictive efficacy of 20-h CysC levels for hypermethotrexate blood levels was comparable to that of the clinically established 20-h MTX steady-state concentration (P = 0.668). However, the combined use of both markers significantly outperformed either marker alone in predictive efficacy (P < 0.0001). Further analysis determined the optimal diagnostic threshold for 20-h CysC to be 0.955 µmol/L (Youden’s index). Discussion

ALL is one of the most common malignant tumors in childhood, and HD-MTX is a key treatment regimen for pediatric ALL. While HD-MTX effectively eliminates tumor cells, it poses risks of hypermethotrexemia and kidney injury [6–8]. Hypermethotrexemia is a serious complication that can lead to central nervous system toxicity, liver toxicity, AKI, and potentially life-threatening conditions, significantly impacting the child’s prognosis and quality of life [9–12]. Furthermore, the effects of HD-MTX on the kidney may extend beyond the acute treatment phase. A recent study by Mahmoud et al. [6] highlighted that the prevalence of kidney dysfunction, including tubular injury, is high among childhood cancer survivors, emphasizing the importance of early detection and long-term monitoring of kidney health in this population. Predicting high levels of MTX in the blood is essential because it enables timely intervention measures. In their prospective biomarker study involving 20 pediatric patients with ALL, Ylinen et al. [13] demonstrated that CysC exhibits a transient and mild elevation after high-dose MTX administration, representing a more sensitive indicator of early changes than serum creatinine. In a separate prospective study of 80 adult patients with lymphoma receiving high-dose MTX, pharmacokinetic analysis showed that the CysC-based estimated glomerular filtration rate equation correlated most closely with MTX clearance, surpassing all creatinine-based equations [14]. Therefore, this study was aimed at investigating the potential of serum CysC as an early biomarker for predicting kidney impairment and MTX accumulation.

All 103 patients assessed in this study were treated according to the CCCG-ALL 2020 protocol, which includes four HD-MTX chemotherapy sessions during consolidation therapy. MTX concentrations were monitored 20 h post-chemotherapy to define steady-state concentrations and guide subsequent adjustments in hydration and alkalization rates. If MTX concentrations exceeded 150% of the steady-state level, chemotherapy was terminated early. Previous studies have shown that the optimal alternative marker for MTX (HD-MTX)-related toxicity is delayed MTX clearance, defined as a serum MTX concentration ≥ 1 µmol/L at 48 h [15–17]. Therefore, this study defined the high MTX concentration group as those with MTX concentrations ≥ 1 µmol/L at 42 h.

Some centers currently struggle to effectively monitor MTX concentrations, and frequent blood draws may reduce patient compliance. Therefore, there is a need for simpler and more convenient monitoring methods to predict the occurrence of high MTX levels and AKI at an early stage.

Barreto et al. [18] demonstrated through two cases that serum CysC is more effective than creatinine in assessing glomerular filtration rate (GFR) for predicting MTX clearance in adult HD-MTX recipients. CysC is a cysteine protease inhibitor whose elevated levels are closely associated with damage to the glomerular filtration membrane. It is unaffected by inflammation, infection, muscle mass, sex, age, and most medications, making its serum concentration an ideal indicator of GFR [1, 19]. These characteristics make CysC a sensitive indicator for monitoring changes in kidney function, particularly in the early stages of kidney injury [1]. During HD-MTX chemotherapy, the drug itself or its metabolites may cause kidney damage, leading to impaired kidney function [20, 21]. Given that kidney clearance accounts for at least 90% of total MTX excretion, including the parent drug and its metabolites [22], changes in CysC levels can reflect early kidney damage and predict MTX clearance.

In this study, we investigated the predictive value of 20-h CysC for delayed MTX clearance and hypermethotrexemia. The results indicated that 31.4% (27/86) of patients in the high MTX concentration group had elevated 20-h CysC levels, which was significantly higher than the 2.8% (9/324) observed in the low-concentration group. Baseline serum CysC levels showed no statistically significant difference between the two groups. Correlation analysis indicated that 20-h CysC levels showed a significant positive correlation with MTX blood concentrations. In terms of predictive performance, the AUC for 20-h CysC was 0.729, indicating a good predictive value. When 20-h CysC levels exceed 0.955 µmol/L, intensifying hydration and alkalization therapy is recommended to prevent or mitigate hyperammonemia. Notably, the predictive efficacy of 20-h CysC levels was comparable to that of the conventional monitoring indicator (20-h MTX concentration). However, combining both indicators significantly improved predictive efficacy (AUC = 0.823), demonstrating greater clinical application value.

CysC has shown high sensitivity and specificity across various kidney diseases, often detecting changes in kidney function earlier than traditional indicators [23, 24]. Additionally, some studies have linked CysC to chemotherapy-induced kidney damage, with kidney toxicity being one of the most common adverse reactions of HD-MTX. In a large-scale retrospective study conducted by Skärby et al. [25] (enrolling 264 children with ALL), only 0.02% (28/1164) of MTX treatment courses resulted in elevated serum creatinine levels, and CysC was not detected. In contrast, Krawczuk-Rybak et al. [26] observed transient elevations in plasma CysC levels in 21 children with ALL following MTX infusion but no abnormalities in other kidney function markers. Additionally, Krawczeski et al. [27] reported that CysC levels > 1.16 mg/L at 12 h post-surgery in pediatric cardiac surgery patients could predict AKI. In the present study, data from 410 HD-MTX chemotherapy cycles were monitored. Among the 36 cycles with elevated 20-h serum CysC levels, 19.4% (7/36) met the criteria for AKI based on elevated 44-h serum creatinine. In contrast, only one of the patients in the normal serum CysC group (1/374) developed AKI, demonstrating a statistically significant between-group difference. Further analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between 20-h CysC levels and the magnitude of 44-h creatinine elevation. ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC for 20-h CysC in predicting AKI reached 0.976, demonstrating excellent predictive performance. A 20-h CysC level ≥ 1.015 µmol/L may serve as a reliable early warning indicator for AKI.

Some limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, owing to its retrospective design, we were unable to adhere strictly to the KDIGO criteria for AKI diagnosis, which include assessment of serum creatinine changes within a 48-hour window or urine output monitoring. Instead, we used a pragmatic definition based on 44-hour serum creatinine values exceeding age-specific thresholds with an increase from baseline. This may have led to under- or overestimation of AKI incidence. Second, the single-center design limits the generalizability of our findings. Third, whereas we found a significant association between 20-h CysC levels and subsequent AKI, the optimal cutoff values identified (0.955 µmol/L for hypermethotrexemia and 1.015 µmol/L for AKI) require external validation in larger, multicenter prospective cohorts. Future studies should incorporate earlier and more frequent biomarker sampling, as well as standardized AKI definitions, to confirm and expand upon our findings.

In summary, the 20-h serum CysC level, as a novel marker of early kidney injury, significantly correlates with 44-h MTX concentrations and serum creatinine during HD-MTX chemotherapy, enabling early prediction of hypermethotrexemia and AKI. This finding provides a reliable alternative monitoring indicator for medical centers unable to routinely monitor MTX blood concentrations. Moreover, it has important practical significance for guiding clinical adjustments to hydration and alkalization regimens to prevent severe complications in children.

Comment on “Spectrum and Outcome of Acute Kidney Injury in Nonsurgical Cardiac Intensive Care Unit Patients: A Prospective Observational Study”
Authors: Shivali Sandal, Surender Himral, Jai B Sharma, Kunal Mahajan
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the article, “Spectrum and Outcome of Acute Kidney Injury in Nonsurgical Cardiac Intensive Care Unit Patients: A Prospective Observational Study” by Saini and Bhattacharjee.1 The authors have provided valuable data on acute kidney injury (AKI) within the nonsurgical cardiac intensive care unit (ICU) setting in India, illuminating key clinical outcomes and prognostic factors.

However, we would like to highlight several important methodological considerations that affect the interpretability and generalizability of the findings.

First and foremost, the study lacks a control group of comparable cardiac ICU patients without AKI. The absence of such a comparator significantly limits the ability to attribute adverse outcomes—such as progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD), mortality, or subsequent cardiovascular events—specifically to AKI itself. A control group would allow for more precise quantification of the excess risks conferred by AKI, as opposed to risks inherent in the critically ill cardiac population. Moreover, considering the heterogeneous nature of cardiac ICU patients in terms of comorbidities and illness severity, having a well-matched control group would enable adjustment for confounders and allow more accurate causal inference. Without this context, the significance of the reported outcomes (for example, a 27.8% progression to CKD at 3 months) cannot be meaningfully interpreted or translated into clinical practice.

Additionally, the single-center design and relatively short follow-up period of 3 months further restrict the external validity of the results. Patient demographics, comorbidity profiles, and management strategies may vary greatly across different centers and regions in India, limiting the applicability of the study's conclusions to wider settings.

We commend the authors for their valuable contribution and respectfully suggest that future studies incorporate appropriate control groups, extended follow-up, and a multicenter design. Such enhancements will be critical to more robustly determine the true impact of AKI in the cardiac ICU and to inform evidence-based care strategies.

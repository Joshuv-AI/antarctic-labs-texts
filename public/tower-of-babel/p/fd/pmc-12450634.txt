High-resolution visualization of the heart surface using multi-detector computed tomography: A case study of coronary artery bypass grafting
Authors: Yuichiro Yokoyama, Takeshi Emmoto, Akira Kurata, Hiroshi Higashino, Yumiko Abe, Hiroyuki Fujieda, Mitsunori Abe
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMulti-detector computed tomography is commonly used as a preoperative screening tool for coronary artery bypass grafting. Electrocardiographically gated cardiac computed tomography facilitates accurate identification and assessment of the target coronary artery segment. While cardiac computed tomography can visualize the morphology of coronary arteries, the surgeon’s view of the heart surface may differ considerably because the arteries can be obscured by the myocardium (myocardial bridge), pericardial fat, or parallel-running coronary branches. To address this, we developed a dedicated computed tomography imaging method that incorporates tissue classification based on cardiac anatomy. We present this imaging method in the case of a 77-year-old female patient diagnosed with angina pectoris who underwent coronary artery bypass grafting. This method may help cardiac surgeons navigate cardiac anatomy more accurately, enhance confidence during surgical exploration of the target vessel, and improve communication with imaging professionals.Keywords: Cardiac imaging techniques, Multi-detector computed tomography, Coronary artery bypass grafting

Introduction

Coronary arteries are often not visible on the surface of the heart. The surgeon’s view of the heart surface can vary considerably because the coronary arteries can be obscured by the myocardium, pericardial fat, or parallel-running coronary branches. This information is difficult to obtain reliably from coronary angiography.

Although volume rendering (VR) images that show only the coronary arteries are widely used, images that include the surrounding fat and myocardial tissues are rarely created. We believe that realistic visualization of the heart surface would be useful during surgery, as most cardiac surgeons have encountered difficulty locating target vessels intraoperatively.

Herein, we report a case of successful complete coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) in a 77-year-old female in which real cardiac surface images were created to avoid errors in targeting the coronary artery. We developed realistic VR cardiac surface images using a tissue segmentation technique with computed tomography (CT).

Case report

A 77-year-old female experienced worsening chest discomfort on exertion. She had been followed up at our clinic for the past 7 years after being diagnosed with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy. A severe systolic murmur was auscultated at the left fourth intercostal space. Although her laboratory data showed high triglyceride levels (471 mg/dL) and high brain natriuretic peptide levels (265 pg/mL), no other specific abnormalities were found. She was diagnosed with severe left ventricular outflow obstruction, aortic valve stenosis, and left anterior descending artery (LAD) obstruction, and surgery was scheduled based on these findings. Electrocardiographically gated cardiac CT images with standard VR (Fig. 1A) showed the entire coronary anatomy and an occluded lesion in the LAD. Multi-planar reformatted images revealed that the proximal LAD (blue circle, Fig. 1A) was a myocardial bridge covered by myocardium and deep pericardial fat (Fig. 1B), while the distal segment (orange circle, Fig. 1A) ran through superficial pericardial fat (Fig. 1C). A curved-planar image indicated that CABG was preferable to percutaneous coronary intervention, as the obstruction, caused by partially calcified plaque, was near the bifurcation of the LAD and left circumflex artery (arrow, Fig. 1D).

The cardiovascular tissue within the pericardium (green area, Fig. 2A) was extracted from the original axial CT images within a defined volume of interest. The following images were generated: an unadjusted VR image (Fig. 2B), a standard VR image adjusted for blood and cardiovascular structure (Fig. 2C), and a VR image with segmented coronary arteries (red, Fig. 2D) and coronary veins (blue, Fig. 2E). The low-density region was identified by subtracting the cardiovascular tissue VR image (Fig. 2E) from the unadjusted VR image (Fig. 2B) and was defined as pericardial fat (yellow, (Fig. 2F). The CT surgeon’s view image (Fig. 3A) showed pericardial fat covering the entire heart except for portions of the great cardiac vein and the winding diagonal branch. The actual intraoperative view (Fig. 3B) confirmed this finding. The LAD is not visible from the surface of the heart (arrow, Figs. 3A and B).

(A) All tissue segments were generated on the VR image. This image closely resembles the actual heart surface. (B) Actual surgeon’s view. The LAD is not visible from the surface of the heart (arrow).

A cardiac anatomy-based CT image showed that the target LAD, covered by superficial pericardial fat, ran parallel to the great cardiac vein and the proximal diagonal branch (Fig. 4A). The cardiac surgeon successfully explored the target vessel without misinterpretation and exposed the target lesion for CABG by dissecting the myocardial layers and pericardial fat (Fig. 4B). The patient’s postoperative course was uneventful, and she was discharged on postoperative day 12.

Discussion

Coronary angiography is the classical method for visualizing the coronary artery lumen, and findings such as stenosis or milking effects are well recognized with this modality. However, the information it provides is limited to the vessel lumen. In comparison, CT provides additional information on plaque, calcification, and coronary artery morphology; however, less attention has been paid to the surrounding tissues, such as myocardial and pericardial fat. These tissues are typically removed during image processing to isolate the coronary artery morphology. During actual cardiac surgery, coronary arteries are often obscured by these tissues, and only limited segments are visible from the heart surface. The anastomosis site is typically identified based on coronary artery course as seen on angiography; however, the target vessel may be misidentified if it is embedded in fat or myocardium, or if it runs parallel to the artery. Most surgeons have encountered challenges with anastomosis site misidentification at least once. The anatomical relationship between the myocardium and coronary arteries, known as myocardial bridging, can cause ischemia, occasionally requiring treatment [1,2]. CT is considered a valuable modality for detecting myocardial bridging [[2], [3], [4], [5], [6]], particularly in patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy who are at an increased risk for sudden cardiac death [3,7]. The relationship between coronary arteries and surrounding fat has also been studied and is associated with the risk and prognosis of coronary artery stenosis [[8], [9], [10]]. Although the relationship between coronary arteries and surrounding tissues has been studied, no reports have described CT-based reconstruction of coronary artery visibility on the heart surface. Realistic cardiac images may enable recognition of the cardiac surface anatomy before surgical exposure by opening the patient’s chest. This information may be useful in CABG and could facilitate more accurate identification of the target vessel. The actual depth at which the vessel can be seen remains unclear, necessitating improved accuracy by providing feedback on findings during surgery and accumulating case studies. Our method generates a realistic heart image that may be beneficial not only for CABG but also for other types of cardiac surgeries.

Identification of mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes UQCR10 and NDUFA6 in pediatric tetralogy of fallot: an exploratory bioinformatics study
Authors: Yuwei Qin, Tong Lang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is one of the most common cyanotic congenital heart diseases in infants and young children. Its molecular basis remains incompletely understood. This study aimed to identify mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes associated with pediatric TOF using public heart tissue transcriptomic datasets from the GEO database.MethodsDatasets GSE146218 and GSE217772 were downloaded and merged, followed by batch-effect correction. Differential expression analysis was performed to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Functional enrichment analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis were used to prioritize candidate genes. The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) was used as an exploratory literature-based tool to summarize gene–disease associations.ResultsA total of 960 DEGs were identified. Functional enrichment analyses showed that these genes were mainly enriched in mitochondrial energy metabolism-related pathways, including oxidative phosphorylation and the mitochondrial respiratory chain. WGCNA and PPI network analyses further prioritized UQCR10 and NDUFA6 as candidate genes, and both genes showed increased expression in TOF heart tissue samples. CTD analysis suggested literature-based associations between these genes and cardiovascular or developmental disease-related terms.ConclusionThis exploratory bioinformatics study identified UQCR10 and NDUFA6 as mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes upregulated in pediatric TOF heart tissue. These findings suggest that mitochondrial respiratory chain-related transcriptional alterations may be involved in TOF-associated myocardial remodeling or stress responses. Further experimental and clinical validation is required to confirm their biological relevance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04398-x.

Introduction

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is one of the most common cyanotic congenital heart diseases (CHDs) in infants and young children. It is characterized by four typical anatomical features: right ventricular outflow tract obstruction, ventricular septal defect, overriding aorta, and right ventricular hypertrophy [1–3]. TOF may present with cyanosis and hypoxic spells during infancy, and most patients require surgical correction early in life [3]. Although advances in surgical repair and perioperative care have markedly improved survival, long-term complications such as right ventricular remodeling, pulmonary regurgitation, ventricular dysfunction, and arrhythmias remain important clinical concerns during follow-up [4].

The etiology of TOF is complex and incompletely understood. Current evidence suggests that genetic susceptibility, de novo or inherited variants, chromosomal abnormalities, and environmental factors may jointly contribute to abnormal cardiac development [5, 6]. Disturbances in cardiac neural crest cells, second heart field development, and outflow tract septation are considered important embryological mechanisms underlying conotruncal defects, including TOF [7]. Therefore, exploring TOF-related molecular alterations may provide useful clues for understanding disease-associated myocardial remodeling and for identifying candidate genes that warrant further experimental validation.

With the popularization of high-throughput sequencing technologies and the rapid development of bioinformatics, deep mining of public gene expression databases, such as the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), has become a useful approach for identifying candidate genes and pathways in complex diseases [8]. This approach enables integrated analysis of publicly available transcriptomic datasets, although findings derived from such analyses still require careful interpretation and independent validation.

Notably, the infant heart is in a dynamic stage of growth and metabolic maturation. During perinatal and postnatal development, cardiac energy metabolism undergoes substantial remodeling, and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation plays a central role in meeting the high energy demand of cardiomyocytes [9]. UQCR10 and NDUFA6 encode components related to mitochondrial respiratory chain complex III and complex I, respectively [10, 11]. However, the relationship between UQCR10, NDUFA6, and TOF-related myocardial transcriptional alterations remains unclear.

Therefore, this study used an exploratory bioinformatics approach to identify differentially expressed genes between TOF heart tissue samples and control heart tissue samples. Functional enrichment analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis were performed to prioritize mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes. Based on this workflow, UQCR10 and NDUFA6 were selected for further interpretation as preliminary candidate genes associated with TOF-related transcriptional alterations. This study does not aim to establish causality, diagnostic value, or prognostic significance, but rather to provide candidate molecular clues for future experimental and clinical validation.

Methods

Tetralogy of fallot datasets

This study focused on pediatric Tetralogy of Fallot. Dataset profiles GSE146218 and GSE217772 were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. GSE146218 includes 5 TOF and 4 normal tissue samples, and GSE217772 includes 5 TOF and 5 normal tissue samples. These tissue samples were used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with TOF. Detailed and consistent anatomical sampling information was not available across all included samples. In addition, the available clinical metadata were limited, and detailed information regarding patient age, sex, severity or duration of hypoxia, perioperative status, treatment history, and long-term clinical outcomes was not consistently available. Therefore, these variables could not be included as covariates in the present analysis.

Batch effect removal

For the integration of multiple datasets, the R package inSilicoMerging was first used to merge the expression matrices, resulting in a combined matrix. Batch effects were then corrected using the removeBatchEffect function from the limma package, and the batch-corrected matrix was used for subsequent analyses. To improve methodological transparency, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to visualize the sample distribution of the batch-corrected merged matrix.

Screening of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

Differential expression analysis was performed using the limma package based on the merged batch-corrected expression matrix from GSE146218 and GSE217772. Raw P values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to control the false discovery rate (FDR). Fold change (FC) was calculated based on the expression difference between TOF and control samples. Genes with P < 0.05 and |log₂FC| > 1 were considered differentially expressed genes (DEGs), indicating that both upregulated and downregulated genes were included. A volcano plot was generated to visualize the DEGs.

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed to characterize the biological functions and pathways associated with the DEGs. GO annotations from the R package org.Hs.eg.db and KEGG pathway annotations were used as background references. Enrichment analysis was performed using the R package clusterProfiler, with the minimum and maximum gene set sizes set to 5 and 5000, respectively. A P value of < 0.05 and an FDR of < 0.25 were considered statistically significant. In addition, Metascape was used as a complementary tool for enrichment visualization and cross-checking of functional clusters, rather than as an independent validation analysis.

GSEA analysis

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed to evaluate pathway-level trends across the whole expression profile, complementing the DEG-based GO and KEGG analyses. Samples were divided into TOF and control groups according to disease status. The c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt gene set was downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database. The minimum and maximum gene set sizes were set to 5 and 5000, respectively, and 1000 permutations were performed. A P value of < 0.05 and an FDR of < 0.25 were considered statistically significant.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

First, using the pediatric heart tissue gene expression profile, the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) for each gene was calculated, and the top 50% of genes with the smallest MAD were removed. The goodSamplesGenes method from the R package WGCNA was used to remove outlier genes and samples. Further, WGCNA was used to build a scale-free co-expression network. Specifically, after selecting a power of 6, the adjacency matrix was transformed into a Topological Overlap Matrix (TOM), and the corresponding dissimilarity (1-TOM) was calculated. To classify genes with similar expression profiles into gene modules, average linkage hierarchical clustering was performed on the gene dendrogram based on the TOM-based dissimilarity measure, with a minimum module size of 30 and deepSplit set to 3. To further analyze the modules, differences between module eigengenes were calculated, a cut line was selected for the module dendrogram, and some modules were merged. Additionally, modules with a distance less than 0.25 were merged. Notably, the grey module is considered a collection of genes that cannot be assigned to any module. Given the limited sample size of the merged dataset, WGCNA was used as an exploratory network-based approach to prioritize TOF-associated gene modules rather than to establish definitive co-expression structures. The results of WGCNA were interpreted together with differential expression and PPI analyses to reduce overreliance on any single method.

Construction and analysis of protein-protein interaction (PPI) network

The list of pediatric TOF differentially expressed genes was input into the STRING database to construct a PPI network for candidate gene prioritization, with a minimum interaction confidence score of > 0.4. Cytoscape software was used to visualize and analyze the PPI network. The Molecular Complex Detection (MCODE) plugin was used to identify the most relevant network module. In addition, the top ten genes were ranked using three Cytoscape topological algorithms, including Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC), Maximum Neighborhood Component (MNC), and Density of Maximum Neighborhood Component (DMNC). The intersection of genes identified by these algorithms was used to obtain the final candidate gene list.

Gene expression heatmap

The R package heatmap was used to create a heatmap of the expression levels of the core genes found by the three algorithms in the PPI network in GSE146218 and GSE217772, visualizing the expression differences of the core genes between TOF and normal tissue samples.

CTD analysis

To explore literature-based gene–disease associations, the candidate genes were input into the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD). CTD analysis was used as an exploratory tool to summarize reported disease-related associations of the candidate genes. The CTD results, including inference scores and reference counts, were visualized using reproducible script-based graphics rather than manual spreadsheet-based plotting.

Results

Differential expression gene analysis

To assess the overall distribution of samples after dataset integration and batch-effect correction, PCA was performed using the batch-corrected merged expression matrix. The first two principal components explained 33.2% and 18.3% of the total variance, respectively. The PCA plot showed that the samples were not completely separated according to dataset source after correction, although residual sample heterogeneity remained (Supplementary Figure S1). These results support the use of the corrected merged matrix for subsequent exploratory analyses, while also indicating that platform- and sample-related heterogeneity should be considered when interpreting the findings.

In this study, based on the set cutoffs, differential expression genes were identified from the debiased merged matrix of GSE146218 and GSE217772, resulting in a total of 960 DEGs in infant TOF heart tissue (Fig. 1).

These results suggested that mitochondrial and metabolic processes were prominent among the DEGs.

GSEA analysis

GSEA was performed to evaluate pathway-level trends across the whole expression profile. Consistent with the DEG-based enrichment results, GSEA indicated enrichment of pathways related to oxidative phosphorylation, cellular respiration, organelle inner membrane, and mitochondrial function (Figs. 2E-H). These findings provided complementary pathway-level support for the involvement of mitochondrial energy metabolism-related transcriptional alterations in TOF heart tissue.

Metascape analysis was used as a complementary visualization and functional clustering tool. The enriched terms included cellular respiration, small molecule catabolic process, and monocarboxylic acid metabolic process (Fig. 3A). Enrichment networks colored by cluster ID and P value were generated to visualize the relationships among enriched functional terms (Figs. 3B and C and 4). These analyses consistently indicated enrichment of broad mitochondrial and metabolic processes, but should not be interpreted as independent mechanistic validation.

Based on the cutoff criterion of |MM| > 0.9, approximately 1,460 highly connected genes in the selected TOF-associated module were identified as candidate module genes. Given the limited sample size, these genes were not considered validated hub genes, but were used for subsequent intersection with DEGs and PPI-based prioritization. Construction and analysis of protein-protein interaction (PPI) network

The overlapping genes obtained from DEGs and WGCNA-derived candidate module genes were imported into the STRING database to construct a PPI network, which was then visualized and analyzed using Cytoscape (Fig. 7A). A key network module was identified using the Molecular Complex Detection (MCODE) plugin (Fig. 7B). Three topological algorithms, including MCC, MNC, and DMNC, were used to rank central genes in the PPI network (Figs. 8A-C). The intersection of the top-ranked genes identified by these algorithms yielded five candidate genes: UQCR10, NDUFB1, NDUFB3, NDUFAB1, and NDUFA6 (Fig. 8D).

CTD analysis

CTD analysis was used as an exploratory literature-based tool to summarize reported disease-related associations of UQCR10 and NDUFA6. The results suggested that both genes were linked to disease terms related to developmental disabilities, endocarditis, heart failure, heart diseases, necrosis, and inflammation, among others (Figs. 9B, C). In the CTD visualizations, the inference score reflected the strength of the gene–disease association inferred by the database, while the reference count indicated the number of supporting references. These findings provide only literature-based contextual information and should not be interpreted as independent biological validation.

Discussion

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is a severe cyanotic congenital heart disease in infants and young children. Although surgical repair has substantially improved survival, postoperative myocardial remodeling, right ventricular dysfunction, pulmonary regurgitation, arrhythmia, and other long-term complications remain important clinical concerns during follow-up [12–14]. In the present exploratory bioinformatics study, UQCR10 and NDUFA6 were prioritized as mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes that were upregulated in pediatric TOF heart tissue samples. Rather than indicating established causal or prognostic biomarkers, these findings suggest that mitochondrial respiratory chain-related transcriptional alterations may be involved in TOF-associated myocardial stress responses.

UQCR10 is associated with the ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase complex, also known as mitochondrial respiratory chain complex III, whereas NDUFA6 is a subunit of mitochondrial complex I [10, 15]. The mitochondrial respiratory chain is composed of multiple enzymatic complexes embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane and is responsible for coupling electron transfer to proton gradient formation and ATP production [16]. Complex I participates in electron transfer from NADH to ubiquinone and contributes to proton translocation, whereas Complex III transfers electrons from ubiquinol to cytochrome c as a central segment of the respiratory chain [17, 18]. Given the high energy demand of cardiomyocytes, even subtle changes in mitochondrial respiratory chain activity may be associated with myocardial metabolic stress, altered redox balance, and adaptive or maladaptive remodeling.

Previous studies have shown that cardiac energy metabolism changes substantially during perinatal and postnatal maturation, with increasing reliance on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation to meet the energy requirements of the developing and maturing heart [19]. In addition, metabolic remodeling is closely involved in myocardial stress and heart failure, and mitochondrial dysfunction is increasingly recognized as a key component of pathological cardiac remodeling [20]. In this context, the increased expression of UQCR10 and NDUFA6 observed in TOF samples may reflect altered mitochondrial energy demand, hypoxia-related myocardial stress, or compensatory transcriptional remodeling. However, because the present study was based on postnatal heart tissue transcriptomic data, it cannot determine whether these genes are involved in embryonic cardiac malformation or represent secondary responses after structural defects have already occurred. Accordingly, we interpret the upregulation of UQCR10 and NDUFA6 primarily as TOF-associated myocardial transcriptional alterations that may occur after established structural defects, rather than as direct evidence that these genes initiate embryonic cardiac malformation.

The enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation, mitochondrial respiratory chain, and cellular respiration-related pathways further supports the involvement of mitochondrial metabolic remodeling in TOF heart tissue. These enrichment results are biologically plausible because hypoxemia, altered loading conditions, and myocardial stress in TOF may increase the demand for metabolic adaptation. Nevertheless, pathway enrichment should be interpreted cautiously. First, the enrichment of broad mitochondrial and metabolic pathways does not prove a specific disease-driving mechanism. Second, the enrichment of the non-alcoholic fatty liver disease pathway should not be interpreted as a direct mechanistic link between TOF and liver disease. This KEGG term contains multiple genes involved in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, oxidative stress, and metabolic regulation; therefore, its enrichment may reflect shared mitochondrial metabolic components rather than a TOF-specific hepatic mechanism. The upregulation of mitochondrial respiratory chain-related genes may represent an adaptive response to hypoxemia and hemodynamic overload, but it may also be associated with maladaptive myocardial stress under certain conditions. The current transcriptomic data do not allow discrimination between these possibilities. Therefore, UQCR10 and NDUFA6 should be considered preliminary candidate genes for future validation rather than confirmed drivers of TOF pathogenesis.

Although this study used an integrated bioinformatics workflow based on public transcriptomic datasets, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the sample size was relatively small, and no formal power analysis was performed because this study was based on all available eligible samples from public GEO datasets. The limited sample size may increase the risk of false-positive findings and reduce the stability of downstream analyses, particularly WGCNA-based module detection and hub gene prioritization. Therefore, WGCNA, GSEA, and multi-algorithm PPI analyses in this study should be regarded as complementary exploratory tools for candidate gene prioritization rather than as independent evidence of stable network architecture or causal biological mechanisms. In addition, the merged dataset included samples from different GEO datasets. Although batch-effect correction was performed and PCA visualization was added to assess the distribution of the corrected merged matrix, residual heterogeneity may remain because of differences in platform type, tissue source, sample characteristics, and data processing procedures. Therefore, the results should be interpreted as exploratory findings and require validation in larger, more homogeneous cohorts.

Second, the available public datasets provided limited clinical and anatomical annotations, including tissue sampling site, patient age, severity and duration of hypoxia, perioperative status, and long-term clinical outcomes. As a result, the present analysis could not determine whether the observed transcriptional changes were directly related to TOF-specific mechanisms or reflected secondary responses to hypoxemia, hemodynamic overload, or myocardial stress. Third, this study was based on transcriptomic association analysis and did not include experimental validation. Therefore, the current results cannot establish whether UQCR10 and NDUFA6 have a causal role in TOF pathogenesis or merely reflect secondary transcriptional responses to hypoxemia, hemodynamic overload, or myocardial stress. Functional experiments, such as gene knockdown or overexpression in cardiomyocyte models, induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, or appropriate animal models, are needed to clarify whether these genes actively regulate mitochondrial energy metabolism, oxidative stress, or cardiac developmental processes. In addition, validation using independent clinical specimens, quantitative PCR, Western blotting, immunohistochemistry, and clinical correlation analyses would be necessary to further confirm the expression patterns and potential biological relevance of UQCR10 and NDUFA6 in TOF.

Future studies should therefore focus on validating these preliminary candidate genes in independent datasets and experimental systems. In particular, larger clinical cohorts with detailed anatomical and clinical information are needed to determine whether UQCR10 and NDUFA6 are associated with specific myocardial regions, hypoxic burden, ventricular remodeling, or postoperative outcomes. Moreover, mechanistic studies evaluating mitochondrial respiratory function, ATP production, reactive oxygen species generation, and related metabolic pathways may help clarify whether the upregulation of these genes represents an adaptive compensatory response or a maladaptive feature of myocardial stress in TOF.

In summary, this exploratory bioinformatics study identified UQCR10 and NDUFA6 as mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes that were upregulated in pediatric TOF heart tissue. These findings suggest that mitochondrial respiratory chain remodeling may be involved in TOF-associated myocardial transcriptional alterations. However, because of the limited sample size, residual dataset heterogeneity, lack of detailed clinical annotation, and absence of experimental validation, these genes should be interpreted as preliminary candidates rather than confirmed causal, diagnostic, or prognostic biomarkers. Further experimental and clinical validation is required before their biological or translational significance can be established.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this exploratory bioinformatics study identified UQCR10 and NDUFA6 as mitochondrial energy metabolism-related candidate genes that were upregulated in pediatric TOF heart tissue. These findings suggest that mitochondrial respiratory chain-related transcriptional alterations may be involved in TOF-associated myocardial remodeling or stress responses. However, due to the limited sample size, residual dataset heterogeneity, incomplete clinical annotation, and lack of experimental validation, these genes should be interpreted as preliminary candidates rather than confirmed causal, diagnostic, or prognostic biomarkers. Further validation using independent clinical samples and experimental models is required to clarify their biological relevance in TOF.

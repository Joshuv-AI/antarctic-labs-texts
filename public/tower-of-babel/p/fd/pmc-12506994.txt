Screening Using Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration 2021 Equation Glomerular Filtration Rate Underestimates in Living Kidney Donors Compared With Measurement of Glomerular Filtration Rate
Authors: Artur Quintiliano, Tera B. Schreiner, Oscar A. Garcia Valencia, Suryanarayanan Balakrishnan, Naim Issa, Carrie A. Schinstock, Samy M. Riad, Aleksandra Kukla, Timothy S. Larson, Andrew J. Bentall
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background.Living kidney donation (KD) evaluation particularly focuses on glomerular filtration rate (GFR). Assessing GFR in living kidney donors can be done by estimation (eGFR) or measurement (mGFR). This study aims to evaluate the impact of eGFR equations, focusing on the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) 2021 equation for donor eligibility screening and the reclassification of donors excluded by eGFR criteria to mGFR assessments.Methods.Retrospective, single-center observational study of 2512 kidney donors evaluated between 2011 and 2023, at the Mayo Clinic. We performed analyses and comparisons of 4 eGFR formulae and 2 mGFR methods in terms of performance, eligibility criteria according to Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes guidelines/age-adjusted criteria, eligibility reclassification, and subgroup analyses.Results.The CKD-EPI 2021 resulted in a higher percentage of patients being eligible for KD (81.4%) and achieved the highest P10 (32%–34%) and P30 (87%–88%) across all mGFR methods. When patients were deemed eligible for donation by any eGFR formula, 95% retained this status on reassessment with mGFR. Conversely, when an eGFR formula classified a patient as ineligible, this was revised to be eligible in about 90% of cases on reassessment with mGFR, with a higher reclassification rate observed when the IoCl was used instead of CrCl.Conclusions.The CKD-EPI 2021 performs best for screening potential KD. If a KD is deemed ineligible by CKD-EPI 2021, there is a high likelihood that they will be reclassified as eligible when assessed with mGFR. This study strongly supports using mGFR in donor evaluations, especially when the eGFR does not meet the criteria to ensure equity of access to being a living donor.

INTRODUCTION

Living kidney donation (KD) is considered a safe procedure, with minimal short-term risks for healthy donors, and it provides significant advantages to kidney transplant recipients by improving their quality of life and is associated with increased kidney transplant survival.1,2 The long-term risks of developing end-stage renal disease (ESRD) remain low in KDs.3-5 The potential increased risk of ESRD in KD can be influenced by several factors, including ethnicity, sex, and genetic relationship to the recipient.6 To ensure a thorough evaluation of a living donor candidate, a systematic approach is essential, with a strong focus on risk factors, particularly glomerular filtration rate (GFR). Accurate assessment of kidney function is crucial for risk stratification in living donors, aiming to reduce the long-term risk of ESRD in KD.7

To assess a potential living kidney donor, the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) Guidelines recommend initially estimating GFR using serum creatinine (sCr) and then confirming it by measured GFR (mGFR) or by a repeat eGFR (creatinine or cystatin based).2 According to the KDIGO guidelines, a GFR of >90 mL/min/1.73 m² should be considered an acceptable level of kidney function for donation, whereas a GFR of <60 mL/min/1.73 m² should disqualify a candidate from donation.2 There are several limitations in using creatinine to estimate GFR in screening potential kidney donors,8-10 as well as performance issues in estimating GFR using sCr calculations compared with mGFR.11 There may be significant variations between centers for eGFR/mGFR criteria; indeed, these may be more restrictive than laid out by the KDIGO guidelines. The widely adopted Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) 2021, race-free formula is now used, but it has not been widely validated in living kidney donors.8,12 The combined creatinine–cystatin C equation has been suggested as an alternative to improve estimation accuracy; however, cystatin C is not routinely performed in all centers, including ours. Although direct measurement of GFR remains the gold standard, its availability is limited in many institutions, where cystatin C–based equations may play an important complementary role. A previous publication demonstrated the difference in mGFR and eGFR before the induction of the CKD-EPI 2021 equation.11

In addition, several guidelines suggest using an age-adapted threshold of GFR for living KD,13,14 aiming to ensure safer and more appropriate donor selection across different age groups.15,16 This study has 2 main objectives: first, to evaluate whether donors excluded by eGFR formulae, in particular the CKD-EPI 2021 equation, would be reclassified as eligible using mGFR evaluation. Second, to evaluate the impact of age-adjusted GFR on donor selection as an alternative to KDIGO criteria.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

We conducted a retrospective, single-center observational study of all potential kidney donors evaluated between January 2011 and December 2023 at the Mayo Clinic in Rochester. Data from all potential donors who underwent a standard workup, which includes simultaneous eGFR and mGFR measurements, were included. This was irrespective of subsequent eligibility for KD, unless there was an absence of research consent. Standard workup includes eGFR and mGFR, measured by both creatinine clearance and iothalamate clearance (IoCl) at the same visit. The study was approved by the local Institutional Review Board (No.: 24-002652—assessing donor kidney function using eGFR and mGFR). The clinical and research activities being reported are consistent with the Principles of the Declaration of Istanbul. Measurements included sCr, creatinine clearance (CrCl), and IoCl, with the latter 2 classified as mGFR. Individuals with a GFR greater than the fifth percentile adjusted for age were considered as meeting the donation criteria. The fifth percentile values of GFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) by age are as follows (respectively): at age 20, 91; age 25, 88; age 30, 86; age 35, 83; age 40, 81; age 45, 78; age 50, 76; age 55, 73; age 60, 71; age 65, 70; age 70, 70; and age 75, 70 (Table S1, SDC, https://links.lww.com/TXD/A799).15

eGFR Calculators

The eGFR was calculated using 4 methods: CKD-EPI 2009,17 European Kidney Function Consortium (EKFC),18 Full Age Spectrum (FAS),19 and CKD-EPI 20218 (Supplemental Methods S1, SDC, https://links.lww.com/TXD/A799). sCr was measured using a Roche Cobas chemistry analyzer (c701 or c501; Roche Diagnostics, IN), using enzymatic methods standardized to international reference materials in the Mayo Clinic Renal Laboratory.20 Cystatin C is not routinely measured in donor evaluations at Mayo Clinic. Race was categorized into 2 groups, Black or non-Black, to accommodate prior race-adjusted eGFR calculators as defined in the medical chart and summarized in Table 1.

Measurement of GFR

GFR was measured using both CrCl and IoCl, each corrected for body surface area, and performed during all in-person evaluations. For CrCl, potential donors were informed about the procedure and instructed to maintain a normal diet without any restrictions on protein intake, as well as to avoid medications that may affect kidney function. All urine produced during 24 h was collected in a clean, dry container and kept refrigerated at a temperature between 2° and 8 °C during this period to prevent creatinine degradation. A blood sample was drawn immediately after the urine collection was completed to measure sCr levels. The 24-h CrCl was calculated as: (uCr × urine volume)/sCr, where uCr is the urine creatinine concentration (mg/dL), urine volume is the total urine collected (mL) divided by 1440 min, and sCr is the concentration of sCr (mg/dL). Efforts were made to minimize the impact of 2 major errors that can limit the accuracy of CrCl: errors in urine collection and increased creatinine secretion.

For IoCl, on the day of the procedure, participants were instructed to fast for at least 4 h (2 h for patients with diabetes), drink 1000 mL of water in the hour preceding the test, and refrain from urinating for 45 min before arrival. Following a baseline urine collection and bladder ultrasound to ensure complete emptying, a subcutaneous injection of iothalamate was administered in the arm opposite to that used for blood sampling. A 60-min equilibration period followed, during which the patient remained hydrated. Afterward, urine and blood samples were collected at designated intervals. Bladder emptying was monitored by ultrasound, and catheterization was performed when necessary to ensure complete urine collection. All specimens were labeled with precise time stamps and processed according to institutional guidelines. GFR was calculated using standard clearance equations based on urinary and plasma iothalamate concentrations.21

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R (R Core Team, 2024, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Data are presented as means ± SDs for parametric data or as medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) for nonparametric data. Descriptive statistics were used to address the primary endpoint. The data analysis plan involved assessing the differences between estimated and mGFR using accuracy metrics (within 10% [P10] and 30% [P30]), and precision metrics (root mean square error [RMSE]) for each set of values. The threshold criteria for donation at the time of assessment were compared, with a focus on the acceptable criteria for being a donor based on eGFR and mGFR.

RESULTS

Demographic Characteristics of Potential Donors

In this study of 2512 individuals, the majority were women (n = 1536; 61.1%) and predominantly non-Black individuals (n = 2454; 97.7%). The age of the cohort averaged 47.0 ± 12.7 y (mean ± SD), ranging from 18 to 80 y. The median sCr level was 0.9 mg/dL (IQR, 0.8–1.01). Comparing the eGFR values (mL/min/1.73 m²) within this cohort, the CKD-EPI 2021 formula yielded the highest eGFR values (90 ± 15.3). The other equations produced mean eGFRs as follows: CKD-EPI 2009 at 86.6 ± 15.8, FAS at 83.6 ± 15.5, and EKFC at 82.6 ± 14.1. Both mGFR methods showed higher values, with mean GFRs of 101.6 ± 23.5 for CrCl and 102.5 ± 19.2 for IoCl. Detailed demographic characteristics of the cohort are presented in Table 1.

Eligibility of Donors by GFR Assessment

Table 2 demonstrates variation in age-adjusted criteria acceptable eGFR values across different formulae. More patients met the acceptable age-adjusted eligibility criteria using the CKD-EPI 2021 formula, with 2046 (81.4%) qualifying, followed by the CKD-EPI 2009 equation (1804; 71.8%), FAS (1649; 65.6%), and EKFC (1601; 63.7%). For mGFR, the CrCl method identified 2276 individuals (90.6%) as eligible, whereas IoCl had the highest eligibility rate, with 2385 individuals (94.9%). Among the eGFR equations, 455 individuals (18.1%) were not categorized as eligible by any method, whereas all formulae concurrently identified 1545 individuals (61.5%) as eligible. Regarding mGFR, 2187 potential donors were simultaneously eligible according to both methods, whereas 39 donors (2%) were ineligible by both methods. Among these individuals who were ineligible by both methods (n = 39), 5 (12.8%) were eligible according to all eGFR equations, 27 (69.3%) were also ineligible by all eGFR equations, and 12 (30.8%) were eligible by at least 1 eGFR equation, with CKD-EPI 2021 always being among the qualifying estimations (Table 3). More potential donors were eligible per KDIGO eGFR criteria (90 mL/min/1.73 m2) using the CKD-EPI 2021 formula (46.8%). Most potential donors would have been automatically excluded by an eGFR of <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 in the FAS formula (4.9%) and the fewest in the CKD-EPI 2021 formula (1.4%). Given the significant differences in eligibility rates among the 4 different eGFR formulae, we compared the performance of the different eGFR formulae with both mGFR measurements. The CKD-EPI 2021 equation demonstrated the best overall performance, yielding the lowest RMSE—24.8 for CrCl and 21.9 for IoCl. It also achieved the highest proportion of eGFR values within 30% of mGFR, at 87% (86%, 88%) for CrCl and 88% (87%, 89%) for IoCl. Additionally, the equation showed a moderate Pearson correlation of 0.43 and 0.49, respectively (Tables 4 and 5).

Meeting GFR Criteria

The KDIGO guidelines specify GFR thresholds of >90 mL/min/1.73 m² and <60 mL/min/1.73 m² as absolute values, which we classified as positive and negative, respectively. We also describe the GFR range of 60–90 mL/min/1.73 m2 as intermediate. The eGFR formulae were assessed using these threshold criteria and compared with mGFR and age-adjusted GFR criteria (Table S3, SDC, https://links.lww.com/TXD/A799). Thus, the analysis was divided into 3 groups: those meeting both criteria (KDIGO positive/age-adjusted GFR positive), those in the KDIGO intermediate range who either met or did not meet the age-adjusted eGFR, and those who did not meet either of the criteria (KDIGO negative/age-adjusted GFR negative). Using the CrCl method, 69.9% of the potential donors (n = 1757) evaluated met both the KDIGO and the age-adjusted criteria. Additionally, 20.6% (n = 518) were within the KDIGO intermediate range but still met the age-adjusted criterion, whereas 8.1% (n = 204) fell within the KDIGO intermediate range but did not meet the age-adjusted criterion. A small proportion (1.3%; n = 33) failed to meet either the KDIGO or age-adjusted criteria. Using the IoCl method, 73.7% (n = 1877) met both the KDIGO and the age-adjusted criteria and 20.2% (n = 508) were in the KDIGO intermediate range but still met the age-adjusted criterion. In contrast, 4.4% (n = 111) were in the KDIGO intermediate range but did not meet the age-adjusted criterion, and very few (0.6%; n = 16) did not meet either the KDIGO or age-adjusted criteria. Among the eGFR formulae, CKD-EPI 2021 was the 1 that resulted in the highest number of eligible patients according to both criteria (n = 1174; 46.8%) and the lowest number of simultaneously excluded patients (n = 38; 1.5%; Figure 1; Tables S3.1 and S3.2, SDC, https://links.lww.com/TXD/A799).

Reclassification of Potential Donors with CKD-EPI 2021

Given the high number of potential donors who met the criteria by mGFR, we compared the distribution of those who did not meet the age-adjusted criteria using the eGFR formulae. Figure 2 shows all the different eGFR formulae with age-adjusted GFR criteria and highlights changes in eligibility categories when comparing mGFR to eGFR formulae (numerically shown in Table 4). Using the CKD-EPI 2021 formula, 2046 individuals (81.5%) were initially classified as eligible for donation, whereas 466 (18.6%) were classified as ineligible. After evaluating with CrCl, 1895 (n = 1895/2046; 92.6%) individuals remained eligible, whereas 151 transitioned to being ineligible. When assessed using IoCl, 1974 (n = 1974/2046; 96.5%) remained eligible and 72 were reclassified as ineligible, meaning that in a number of cases, IoCl either confirmed or overruled concerns raised by CrCl. However, those using CrCl 380 of 466 (81.5%) were reclassified as eligible, and those using IoCl 394 of 466 (84.5%) were classified as eligible. Among the individuals initially categorized as ineligible by CKD-EPI 2021, 86 (18.5%) remained ineligible when evaluated with CrCl. For those initially categorized as ineligible by IoCl, 55 (11.8%) remained ineligible, whereas 411 transitioned to eligible status.

DISCUSSION

Precise assessment of kidney function is crucial in evaluating potential live kidney donors. Traditionally, this is done using CrCl and various estimating GFR formulae not developed in healthy people. This study demonstrates that using mGFR allows a greater number of individuals to meet criteria for living KD compared with using eGFR calculators alone. We showed that the CKD-EPI 2021 formula performed the best; however, reclassification from ineligible to eligible occurred in 380 potential donors (81.5%) using CrCl and 411 potential donors (88.2%) using IoCl. Subgroup analysis showed no significant differences in performance based on sex and age, ensuring consistent results. However, in a small population of Black race donors, there was a higher rate of exclusion by eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 compared with mGFR.

This article supports prior work by Gaillard et al,11 who demonstrated the differences in eGFR performance in living donor evaluation using eGFR calculations. We extended the data to the now universally adopted race coefficient-free CKD-EPI 2021 formula. A recent comparison between this equation in a Japanese population of potential donors showed reduced underestimation but higher overestimation of eGFR compared with mGFR.22 In our study of 2512 potential donors, we report a 17% underestimation of GFR by CKD-EPI 2021 compared with mGFR. The mean difference between eGFR by CKD-EPI 2021 and mGFR was 12 mL/min for CrCl and 13 mL/min for IoCl. In potential donors of Black race, our finding further suggests that the CKD-EPI 2021 equation underestimates mGFR in Black individuals,23 potentially excluding donors if eGFR is used as an exclusion criterion. The median difference between eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 in Black potential donors was 15 mL/min/1.73 m2 (IQR, 24) for CrCl and a median GFR of 16 mL/min/1.73 m2 (IQR, 33) for IoCl. Therefore, confirming or exclusively using mGFR for Black individuals being considered for KD may help avoid inaccuracies in donor eligibility.

The CKD-EPI 2021 equation demonstrated the best performance among the eGFR formulae. However, it would exclude approximately 17% of individuals who were eligible based on direct mGFR. Furthermore, when patients were deemed eligible for donation by any of the eGFR formulae, this status remained consistent when assessed by mGFR in about 95% of cases. Conversely, when any eGFR formulae designated an individual as ineligible, this was reclassified as eligibility in approximately 85% of cases when a mGFR method was applied. A higher reclassification rate was observed when IoCl was used instead of CrCl. The CKD-EPI 2021 formula likely performed better because it incorporates updated data and improved statistical methods, which can provide more accurate estimates of kidney function across diverse populations, accounting for variations in age, sex, and race.8,24,25 Although the CKD-EPI 2021 is the most widely adopted eGFR formula currently, it may still exclude some potential donors who might be eligible based on mGFR criteria. This may be particularly important if the donor center evaluation has higher GFR criteria and thus another mGFR may be indicated, depending on local availability.

It is also important to consider the age of the donor when evaluating kidney function. Age-adjusted GFR values are crucial because kidney function naturally declines with age. Using a GFR threshold that is greater than or equal to the lower fifth percentile for the donor’s age group ensures that older donors are not unfairly excluded on the basis of criteria designed for younger individuals.15 This approach helps maintain a fair and inclusive evaluation process, recognizing that a lower GFR might still be normal and acceptable for older donors.

This study presents real-world data but is limited by the specific potential donor population that was evaluated, leading to several limitations. We did not have retrospective data on cystatin C, which is not routinely performed. Although combining eGFR with both creatinine and cystatin C is advocated, it is important to note that most transplant centers worldwide do not routinely assess cystatin C. We included both CrCl and IoCl for comparison, as CrCl is used in many transplant centers for donor evaluation in the United States and gives insight into simultaneous assessment results. We also acknowledge the small number of Black potential donors in this single-center study. We did not include other variables or other medical issues that may make potential donors ineligible, as we focused on potential donor GFR evaluations. However, we believe that these limitations are mitigated by the large cohort size and by using 4 eGFR equations in combination with an additional mGFR measurement. Our performance values are similar to those recently published, although those studies focused on transplant recipients of both kidney and other solid organs.26 When using CrCl and eGFR equations, caution is needed as CrCl overestimates true GFR, whereas the CKD-EPI 2021 equation may underestimate it. Thus, mGFR remains the best and safest method for evaluating potential donors, ensuring eligible individuals are not excluded on the basis of eGFR alone as shown in our study.

This study demonstrates that screening using the eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 criteria, which is supportive of candidacy for being a donor if within the criteria, may exclude potential donors if used as the only tool. Thus, the combined use of eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 and mGFR improves the reliability of renal function testing in potential donors. Our study demonstrated agreement in 1973 potential donors (78.5%) in eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 and CrCl, thus 539 potential donors (21.5%) would require an additional test to determine whether they meet GFR criteria. Different centers have access to different methodologies of testing, including iothalamate, iohexol, and nuclear medicine.27,28 In our data, if eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 and CrCl were not within the criteria, iothalamate was within the criteria for 59 potential donors (2.3%). In conclusion, the eGFR CKD-EPI 2021 formula performs best among recent eGFR calculations for screening potential living donors, but it would exclude 17% of patients who might be eligible based on using age-adjusted mGFR criteria. If deemed ineligible by eGFR CKD-EPI 2021, there is an 81%–88% chance of reclassification to eligible status by mGFR. This study highlights the importance of comprehensive evaluation methods and potential barriers in screening using eGFR before completing full evaluations. The impact of age-adjusted GFR thresholds, as determined by direct GFR measurement, also highlights the impact of age on eGFR formulae to accurately determine donor eligibility. The role of this study strongly supports mGFR for potential donors where eGFR may be lower than transplant center threshold to allow a more accurate assessment of GFR criteria as part of their living donor evaluation and increase confidence in allowing donors to be eligible for donation.

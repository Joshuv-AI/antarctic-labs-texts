ADGL-05 INTRAOPERATIVE 5-ALA FLUORESCENCE-GUIDED RESECTION OF INTRACRANIAL TUMOURS.THE GREATER ACCRA REGIONAL HOSPITAL EXPERIENCE
Authors: Jonathan Oppong, Samuel Kaba Akoriyea, Jennifer Yenuman Berrick, Mariela Infante Paez, Meshack Agyapong-Manu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction5-aminolevulinic acid hydrochloride (5-ALA) is an imaging agent that makes brain tumors fluoresce under microscope and guides neurosurgeons when resecting intracranial tumors. Nevertheless, not all central nervous system tumors have been well studied for the potential benefit of 5-ALA. As such, it is unknown what kind of tumors would benefit from fluorescence-guided surgery employing 5-ALA.AimTo evaluate the impact of 5-aminolevulinic acid-assisted fluorescence-guided resection on surgical radicality in intracranial malignancies.ResultsWe examined 59 patients operated in our center from December 2021 to June 2023, 27 men and 32 women. Of the patients of which 5-ALA was used 23 had meningiomas, 16 metastasis and the remaining 20 having high grade glioma. In our research, near gross total resection was approximately 81%, subtotal 12%, and partial resection 7%. Three patients had tumors located at eloquent areas and the remaining in non-eloquent area, which also contributed to a safer resection. 5-ALA-is associated with a significant increase in the rate of gross total resection, reaching more than 90% in some series. The use of 5-ALA in eloquent areas is attainable and safer when combined with intraoperative electrophysiological functional brain monitoring to accurately find and protect eloquent zones.Conclusions and relevanceNear gross total resection is achieve with decreased postoperative neurological deficit rates when 5-ALA is used. It is a valuable surgical imaging drug for resection of intracranial tumors and does not carry any known significant complications.

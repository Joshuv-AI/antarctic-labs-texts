Comparison of KN012, a denosumab biosimilar, versus reference denosumab in Chinese postmenopausal women with osteoporosis: efficacy, safety, and population pharmacokinetics in a 12-month phase III study
Authors: Xiufen Zhu, Jia Liu, Yanhong Mao, Junlong Li, Feng Shao, Hua Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the efficacy, safety, bone turnover markers, population pharmacokinetics, and immunogenicity profiles of KN012 compared to US reference denosumab (US-denosumab) in Chinese postmenopausal osteoporotic patients.MethodsThis 12-month, double-blind, randomized, active-controlled Phase III study enrolled 279 participants who were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 60 mg of KN012 or US-denosumab subcutaneously at months 0 and 6. The primary endpoint was the mean percent change in lumbar spine bone mineral density (BMD) from baseline at Month 12. Efficacy equivalence was concluded if the associated 95% confidence intervals (CI) for least squares (LS) mean group differences fell within ±1.75%. The Secondary endpoints included percent changes in BMD at the hip, femoral neck, and trochanter. Safety, bone turnover markers, population pharmacokinetics, and immunogenicity were assessed throughout the study period.ResultsThe primary efficacy analysis indicated that the LS mean difference between the KN012 group and the US-denosumab group was 0.22% (95% CI, −0.78, 1.23) in the full analysis set (FAS) and 0.11% (95% CI,-0.92, 1.14) in the per-protocol set (PPS). Both confidence intervals fell entirely within the predefined equivalence margin (±1.75%), confirming clinical equivalence between KN012 and US-denosumab. Secondary efficacy endpoints, safety, bone turnover markers, population pharmacokinetics, and immunogenicity results were comparable between the two groups up to Month 12.ConclusionsKN012 demonstrates comparable efficacy, safety, bone turnover markers, population pharmacokinetics, and immunogenicity to US-denosumab in Chinese postmenopausal women with osteoporosis at high risk of fracture.Keywords: Biosimilar, Denosumab, Osteoporosis, KN012, Population pharmacokinetcs

Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is among the most common skeletal disorders, characterized by low bone mass, deterioration of bone microarchitecture, increased bone fragility, and elevated fracture risk (World Health Organization, 1998). Although OP can occur at any age, it is particularly prevalent in postmenopausal women and older men (Tariq et al., 2024). With global population aging, societal impacts are set to increase (Subarajan et al., 2024).

Postmenopausal osteoporosis (PMO) is the most prevalent subtype of OP. In China, recent data indicate that the prevalence of OP among postmenopausal women is as high as 34.73%, increasing with age (He et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023a). Evidence-based clinical guidelines strongly recommend pharmacologic therapy for patients at high fracture risk (Charatcharoenwitthaya et al., 2023; Watts et al., 2021). Denosumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting RANKL, inhibits RANK–RANKL signaling, reduces osteoclast activity and bone resorption, and thereby increases bone mass and strength (Moen and Keam, 2011; Langdahl, 2021). Based on the main study of denosumab (FREEDOM Trial), the drug significantly reduced the risk of new vertebral and nonvertebral fractures and improved BMD at the lumbar spine and total hip (Bone et al., 2017; Cummings et al., 2009). It has been approved across multiple countries and regions for treatment of OP in patients at high fracture risk (Amgen Europe BV, 2020; Amgen Inc., 2024). Over more than a decade of clinical use, denosumab has demonstrated robust efficacy and a favorable safety profile (Bone et al., 2017). Nevertheless, treatment cost remains a major barrier to access, and biosimilars offer a promising pathway to reduce costs and improve availability (Busse and Lüftner, 2019; Schulze-Koops and Skapenko, 2017; Kvien et al., 2022).

In recent years, China and other countries/regions have actively advanced the research and development of denosumab biosimilars (Gu et al., 2022; Jeka et al., 2024; Reginster et al., 2024; Chung et al., 2025). KN012 (Fiyang Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Jilin, China) is a biosimilar of denosumab and belongs to the IgG2 monoclonal antibody class. Its primary structure closely resembles that of the reference US-denosumab (Prolia®, Amgen Manufacturing Limited, Thousand Oaks, CA, USA). Additionally, it exhibits a high degree of similarity to the reference drug in terms of post-translational modifications, biochemical properties, and biological functions (data not published). A Phase I study (Chinadrugtrials.org identifier: CTR20181231) involving healthy Chinese participants included a total of 44 male (43.1%) and 58 female (53.9%) subjects. This study showed a high degree of similarity between KN012 and US-denosumab in pharmacokinetics, safety, and immunogenicity. Specifically, the geometric mean ratios (GMRs) for Cmax, AUC0-t, and AUC0-∞were 98.74%, 102.54%, and 102.18%, respectively—with their 90%CI all falling within the predefined equivalence range of 80–125% (Zhang et al., n.d.).

Building on these findings, we conducted a double-blind, randomized, active-controlled Phase III study—the first head-to-head comparison of KN012 versus US-denosumab in Chinese postmenopausal women with OP. The primary objective was to establish equivalence for the mean percent change from baseline in lumbar spine BMD at Month 12. Secondary objectives included assessment of additional efficacy endpoints, safety, bone turnover markers, population pharmacokinetics, and immunogenicity, aiming to provide high-quality evidence for the clinical value and accessibility of a denosumab biosimilar in the Chinese PMO population.

Methods

Study design

This double-blind, randomized, active-controlled Phase III study was conducted at 25 centers in China (Chinadrugtrials.org identifier: CTR20201555). Eligible participants were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either 60 mg of KN012 or US-denosumab subcutaneously at baseline (month 0) and month 6, with follow-up visits scheduled at months 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12. All participants received daily supplementation with calcium carbonate D3 (600 mg) and vitamin D (800 IU) from the screening period until the end of the study.

The study employed block randomization with competitive enrollment across centers. Eligible patients were randomized 1:1 to KN012 or US-denosumab using SAS software (version 9.4 or higher) integrated with a central randomization system (DAS-IWRS). Blinded personnel, including patients, investigators, study nurses, clinical research coordinators (CRCs), blinded clinical research associates (CRAs), and other data-processing staff, were responsible for screening, data recording, evaluation, analysis, and review, with the exception of drug management. Unblinded personnel managed drug administration and injections.

This study was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study materials were approved by independent ethics committees/institutional review boards at each site. All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

Participants

The participants enrolled in this trial were Chinese postmenopausal women aged 50–80 years. All participants were required to have a T-score between −2.5 and − 4.0 at either the total hip or lumbar spine, measured during screening. Additionally, all participants had at least one of the following risk factors: a) history of prior fragility fractures; b) parental history of hip fractures; c) elevated bone turnover at screening, defined as serum CTX (s-CTX) > 1.0 standard deviation (SD) above the mean for healthy premenopausal women; d) low body mass index (BMI ≤ 19 kg/m2); e) advanced age (≥ 65 years); and f) current smoking.

Major exclusion criteria included: past or current diseases that might affect drug safety and efficacy, such as various metabolic bone diseases (e.g., osteomalacia or osteogenesis imperfecta), hyperparathyroidism or hypoparathyroidism, malignant tumors, abnormal blood calcium, and oral/dental diseases (e.g., evidence of previous or existing osteomyelitis or osteonecrosis of the jaw; acute dental or mandibular diseases requiring oral surgery; planned invasive dental procedures; unhealed dental or oral surgeries, etc.). Additionally, patients who had received bisphosphonates (cumulative use ≥3 years) and other bone metabolism drugs (such as calcitonin) were excluded. Full eligibility criteria are detailed in the Supplementary Methods.

Endpoints and assessments

Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) scans were performed using Hologic or GE Lunar DXA scanners to determine T scores and BMD equivalents at baseline and at months 6 and 12. For each individual subject, all measurements were obtained using the same DXA scanner throughout the study, and the same reference database for T-score conversion was consistently applied at all time points. All DXA scan data from this study were independently assessed for trial efficacy endpoints by a blinded independent imaging review committee. The primary endpoint was the mean percent change in lumbar spine BMD, measured by DXA at Month 12. Secondary endpoints included the mean percent change in lumbar spine BMD from baseline to Month 6; the mean percent changes in total hip, femoral neck, and femoral trochanter BMD from baseline to Months 6 and 12; and the median percent changes in the serum bone turnover markers carboxy-terminal cross-linking telopeptide of type I collagen (s-CTX) and procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (PINP) measured using blood samples from fasted subjects at baseline and months 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12.

Clinical efficacy and safety assessments included vital signs, physical examinations, electrocardiograms, and laboratory tests. Laboratory safety monitoring covered hematology, urinalysis, blood biochemistry, electrolytes, and coagulation function. Adverse events (AEs) and medication use were meticulously recorded and analyzed. Immunogenicity was evaluated by determining the incidence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) and neutralizing antibodies (NAbs) at Month 12, for which blood samples were collected at Months 0, 6, and 12 before administration.

Blood samples were collected at various time points throughout the 12-month period for population pharmacokinetics analysis. Intensive sampling was conducted on 24 participants (12 per group), whereas sparse sampling was performed on the remaining participants, with 3 mL of blood drawn at each time point. PK analyses were conducted by Junke Zhengyuan (Beijing) Pharmaceutical Research Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Serum concentrations of KN012 and US-denosumab were determined using an Electrochemical Luminescence method (ECLA) on the Meso-Scale Discovery (MSD) platform. Detailed information regarding sampling time points and windows is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

To demonstrate the equivalence of KN012 and US-denosumab with respect to the mean percent change in lumbar spine BMD from baseline at Month 12, approximately 280 patients (140 per treatment group) were planned to be randomized. The sample size calculation assumed a common standard deviation of 4% and accounted for a 20% dropout rate, based on a two one-sided 2.5% significance level and an equivalence margin of −1.75% to 1.75%. This equivalence margin was derived by retaining 50% of the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval (3.50%) from a meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials of US-denosumab in Asian populations (Center for Drug Evaluation, National Medical Products Administration, 2020).

The primary efficacy endpoint was analyzed in the FAS and PPS. The statistical analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint employed analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), with treatment as a fixed effect and baseline lumbar spine BMD T-score as covariates. Equivalence was determined if the 95%CI for the between-group difference was entirely within an equivalence margin of ±1.75%. For the secondary efficacy endpoint, the ANCOVA method, similar to that used for the primary efficacy endpoint, was adopted for analysis, and the baseline values of the corresponding variables were included in the model as covariates for adjustment analysis.

The safety analysis was based on the Safety Set (SS). All AEs were classified according to the coding of the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) and graded in accordance with the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0. The immunogenicity analysis was based on the Immunogenicity Set (IGS). For immunogenicity, the number of patients with positive ADAs and NAbs, positive rates, and 95% CI were calculated for each group at each immunogenicity measurement time point.

Population pharmacokinetic modeling was developed using nonlinear mixed-effects modeling software NONMEM (version 7.4, ICON Development Solutions, USA) and its tools Wings for NONMEM and Perl Speaks NONMEM (version 3.2.4, Uppsala University, Sweden). R software (version 4.0.2) was used for modeling, plotting, and constructing virtual populations.

All statistical analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) to organize and analyze the dataset.

Results

Subject allocation and demographic characteristics

Between July 31st, 2020, and January 26th, 2022, a total of 280 patients with PMO at high risk of fracture were planned for enrollment across 25 centers in China. Patients were recruited through multiple channels, including outpatient clinics and ethics committee-approved advertisements, with the study protocol and recruitment materials reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of each participating center. All patients provided informed consent prior to any study-related procedures. Of the 501 screened subjects, 221 failed the screening process (Fig. 1). Reasons for screening failure included failure to meet inclusion/exclusion criteria (146 subjects), voluntary withdrawal (42 subjects), and other reasons (33 subjects). Out of the 280 patients randomized, 262 (93.6%) completed the main study period (131 in each group).

Flowchart of subjects' disposition.

The baseline demographic characteristics were generally similar between the two groups. Baseline T-scores and bone turnover marker levels were also comparable between treatment groups (Table 1). In the KN012 group, 50 subjects (35.7%) had a history of prior fractures, compared to 36 subjects (25.9%) in the US-denosumab group. The most common type of fracture was vertebral compression fracture (13 cases in the KN012 group and 9 cases in the US-denosumab group). Additionally, 32 subjects (22.9%) in the KN012 group and 36 subjects (25.9%) in the US-denosumab group had a family history of hip fractures.

Efficacy

For the primary endpoint in the FAS and the PPS, The LS mean difference in the percent change from baseline in lumbar spine BMD at Month 12 between the KN012 group and the US-denosumab group was 0.22 (95% CI, −0.78, 1.23) in the FAS and 0.11 (95% CI, −0.92, 1.14) in the PPS. The 95% CI of these differences were completely within the pre-defined equivalence range ± 1.75%, indicating that KN012 is clinically equivalent to US-denosumab (Table 2).

Safety

The incidence of TEAEs of Grade > 3 in the KN012 group was 7.1% (10/140), compared to 15.1% (21/139) in the US-denosumab group. Notably, the incidence in the KN012 group was lower than that in the US-denosumab group. Given that the incidences of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) and serious adverse reactions (SARs) were generally consistent between the two groups, the difference in the incidence of TEAEs of Grade > 3 may be attributed to other factors, such as the subjects' underlying diseases and comorbidities.

Regarding ADRs, the incidence rates were 44.3% (62/140) in the KN012 group and 36.7% (51/139) in the US-denosumab group, with no statistically significant difference observed. All ADRs reported were grade 1–2; no grade ≥ 3 ADRs occurred in either group. ADRs with an incidence >5% (KN012 vs. US-denosumab) included hypocalcemia (9.3% vs. 10.8%), hypophosphatemia (9.3% vs. 7.9%), and urinary tract infection (4.3% vs. 5.0%), with comparable rates between the two groups.

The incidence rates of Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) in the KN012 group and the US-denosumab group were 8.6% (12/140) and 13.7% (19/139), respectively. The incidence rates of SAEs were similar between the two groups. Common SAEs (incidence >1%, KN012 vs. US-denosumab) included vertebrobasilar insufficiency (1.4% vs. 0%) and vertigo (0% vs. 1.4%), all of which were judged to be unrelated to the study drug. The remaining SAEs, with an incidence of less than 1%, occurred in individual subjects.

During the trial, only one participant in the US-denosumab group experienced a single SAR: grade 2 abnormal liver function. In phase III clinical trials of denosumab, the incidence of SAE “abnormal liver function” was 0.03%, while that of SAE “elevated liver enzymes” was 0% (A Study to Evaluate Denosumab in the Treatment of Postmenopausal Osteoporosis, 2022). Consistent with these data, the incidence of serious adverse reactions/events related to liver function abnormalities is extremely low for this class of drugs. Furthermore, none of the serious adverse reactions listed in the denosumab package insert were observed in this study, including hypocalcemia, serious infections, cutaneous adverse reactions, osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical subtrochanteric and femoral shaft fractures, and multiple vertebral fractures following denosumab discontinuation (Bone et al., 2017). These events require continuous monitoring and vigilance during long-term follow-up of medication use.

Immunogenicity analysis results

Immunogenicity analysis was conducted in the IGS, with blood samples collected at baseline (Month 0), Month 6, and Month 12 to assess ADAs and Nabs. Prior to treatment initiation (Month 0), 1 participant (0.7%) in the KN012 group and 2 participants (1.5%) in the US-denosumab group tested positive for ADAs. By Month 6, these 3 ADA-positive participants had converted to ADA-negative status. However, new ADA seroconversion was observed in both groups: 2 cases emerged in the KN012 group (positivity rate: 1.5%) and 6 cases in the US-denosumab group (positivity rate: 4.4%). By Month 12, further changes in ADA status were noted: the 2 newly identified ADA-positive participants in the KN012 group and 3 of the 6 new ADA-positive cases in the US-denosumab group (from Month 6) had reverted to ADA-negative. At this final time point, no ADA-positive participants were detected in the KN012 group (0%), whereas 3 participants (2.3%) in the US-denosumab group remained ADA-positive. Notably, throughout the entire study period, all participants in both groups tested negative for Nabs, indicating no neutralizing antibody response to either treatment.

Population pharmacokinetics modeling

The population pharmacokinetics analysis was conducted on 277 subjects, comprising 139 participants in the KN012 group (with 922 plasma concentration samples collected) and 138 participants in the US-denosumab group (with 925 plasma concentration samples collected). Baseline characteristics of the two groups were well-balanced: the KN012 group had a mean age of 64.8 ± 6.55 years and a mean weight of 55.5 ± 7.47 kg, whereas the US-denosumab group had a mean age of 65.3 ± 6.42 years and a mean weight of 56.3 ± 7.89 kg (see Supplementary Table 3 for detailed demographics).

The primary objectives of this analysis were to characterize the population pharmacokinetics (PK) profiles of KN012 and US-denosumab, and to utilize a population PK approach to assess the biosimilarity of KN012 in comparison to the reference product US-denosumab.

From the population PK dataset, we successfully developed population PK models for both KN012 and US-denosumab. For postmenopausal women with OP at high risk of fractures, the population PK characteristics of both agents were best described by a one-compartment model incorporating first-order absorption and parallel linear-nonlinear elimination pathways. Body weight was identified as a key covariate influencing the PK exposure of both KN012 and US-denosumab. These models underwent comprehensive validation using goodness-of-fit (GOF) analyses, visual predictive checks (VPC), and bootstrap resampling methods, which collectively confirmed their robustness and excellent predictive capacity (detailed model validation data, including Figs. 1–4 and Tables 4–5, are available in the Supplementary Materials).

The PK parameters (Cmax, AUC0-6mon, and AUCinf) were natural log-transformed and analyzed using an ANOVA model to compare the least-square means between the KN012 and US-denosumab groups. The GMR and its 90% CI were obtained by back-transformation. Bioequivalence was concluded if the 90% CI fell entirely within the 80–125% range (FDA, 2003; CFDA, 2018). As shown in Table 4, the GMR for all PK parameters and their corresponding 90% CIs were entirely contained within the prespecified equivalence interval of 0.80–1.25. Furthermore, incorporating the clinically recommended 60 mg dose for PMO patients (Eastell et al., 2019), this study simulated the typical pharmacokinetic characteristics of a hypothetical standard subject with a body weight of 60 kg after administration of 60 mg. The results indicated that the ratios of Cmax, AUC0-6mon, and AUCinf between the two agents also fell within the range of 0.80–1.25, further confirming that they have similar pharmacokinetic characteristics (see Supplementary Table 6 for detailed simulation results).

Discussion

This randomized, double-blind, active-controlled Phase III trial evaluated the clinical equivalence of KN012 and US-denosumab in Chinese postmenopausal women at high fracture risk. Equivalence was achieved for the primary endpoint—the mean percent change from baseline in lumbar spine BMD at Month 12—with closely matched effects between groups. Secondary endpoints, safety outcomes, immunogenicity, and population pharmacokinetics were likewise comparable, collectively supporting therapeutic equivalence in this population.

The primary endpoint showed mean lumbar spine BMD increases of 5.8% with KN012 versus 5.5% with US-denosumab at Month 12, values that are consistent with the pivotal FREEDOM Phase 3 results for reference denosumab (Cummings et al., 2009). Concordant gains at the total hip, femoral neck, and trochanter further reinforce equivalence in antiresorptive efficacy at clinically relevant sites. Dynamic changes in bone turnover markers mirrored the expected pharmacodynamic profile of denosumab. S-CTX, reflecting bone resorption, declined rapidly to post-dose nadirs, rebounded modestly before the subsequent injection, and declined again after the second dose, with a small rise observed by six months. P1NP, a marker of bone formation, exhibited a parallel pattern. These trajectories align with prior denosumab studies (Paul et al., 2025; Sánchez et al., 2016; Brown et al., 2009) and indicate that KN012 reproduces the characteristic suppression-and-recovery cycles of bone turnover under a 6-month dosing interval.

Overall safety was comparable between KN012 and US-denosumab. The incidence of musculoskeletal and connective-tissue adverse ADRs—including arthralgia, back pain, musculoskeletal discomfort, and limb pain—was 7.1% with KN012 and 6.5% with US-denosumab, both lower than rates reported in the denosumab label for PMO (Amgen Inc., 2024). This difference is plausibly explained by the shorter observation period in the present study relative to the 3–5-year data underpinning the label, as well as potential contributions from baseline characteristics, subjective symptom reporting, sample size, and ADR adjudication criteria. Longer-term follow-up will better contextualize these rates and capture less frequent events.

During the trial, TEAEs leading to study withdrawal were infrequent and balanced (1.4% with KN012; 2.2% with US-denosumab). Events included isolated cases of vertebral compression fracture and malignancy in each arm, and one case of drug-induced liver injury with US-denosumab. The drug-induced liver injury associated with the SAE may be unrelated to the study drug US-denosumab. At the D181 visit, prior to the second dose, the subject presented with elevated liver enzymes: alanine aminotransferase (ALT) 375 U/L, aspartate aminotransferase (AST) 217 U/L, gamma-glutamyl transferase (γ-GGT) 271 U/L, and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) 420 U/L, and the dose was withheld. The subject was hospitalized for further evaluation. Tests for viral hepatitis, thyroid function, tumor markers, and other etiologies were unremarkable. Following hepatoprotective therapy, liver function improved: ALT 29 U/L (reference range: 7–40 U/L), AST 28 U/L (reference range: 13–15 U/L), γ-GGT 43 U/L (reference range: 7–45 U/L), and LDH 269 U/L (reference range: 120–250 U/L), and the SAE resolved. Comprehensive assessment suggested a possible association with prior use of Qinghou Liyan Granules, and causality with the study drug was considered unlikely. No deaths occurred, and there were no TEAEs or ADRs necessitating dose reduction or temporary suspension. The low withdrawal and interruption rates suggest that both agents are conducive to sustained therapy in clinical practice.

Immunogenicity was low with both products. ADA positivity was rare for KN012 and US-denosumab at baseline (0.7% vs. 1.5%), Month 6 (1.5% vs. 4.4%), and Month 12 (0% vs. 2.3%), with most ADA-positive results reverting on retesting; no NAbs were detected. These findings are consistent with the denosumab label (<1% ADA over five years; no NAbs) (Amgen Inc., 2024) and indicate a negligible immunogenicity risk for KN012. Furthermore, no clinically meaningful differences in efficacy, pharmacokinetics, or safety outcomes were observed between ADA-positive and ADA-negative subjects in either treatment group. These results are in line with the established safety profile of denosumab (Amgen Inc., 2024) and support that the immunogenicity of KN012 is comparable and does not pose a clinical risk to treatment response.

Population pharmacokinetics modeling is integral to biosimilar development, enabling robust assessments of exposure equivalence and covariate effects (EMA—NAMs Regulatory Acceptance of New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) to Reduce Animal Use Testing, 2025; Hasegawa et al., 2014). In this study, Population pharmacokinetics analyses characterized the PK profiles of KN012 and US-denosumab in PMO, confirmed exposure equivalence, and explored dosing-related behavior. Results align with published reference-product literature (Sánchez-Vidaurre et al., 2025; Sutjandra et al., 2011): denosumab clearance increases with body weight, and elimination approaches linearity at doses ≥60 mg while exhibiting nonlinear target-mediated disposition below 60 mg. Simulations at the approved 60 mg subcutaneous regimen matched clinical observations and support the standard dosing recommendation (European Medicines Agency, 2010). Together, these data substantiate that KN012 achieves comparable systemic exposure under the clinically utilized regimen.

Two prior Phase III trials of denosumab biosimilars in China employed placebo-controlled designs (Gu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023b). By contrast, the present study is the first head-to-head, active-controlled Phase III comparison with the reference product (US-denosumab) in the Chinese PMO population. This design addresses the inferential limitations of placebo controls for equivalence testing and provides more direct, robust evidence of clinical equivalence across efficacy, safety, bone turnover markers, immunogenicity, and Population pharmacokinetics domains.

Conclusion

This 12-month, randomized, double-blind, active-controlled Phase III trial established the clinical equivalence of KN012 to the reference US-denosumab in Chinese postmenopausal women at high risk of fracture. Comparable efficacy in terms of bone mineral density gains, along with similar safety and pharmacokinetic profiles, was observed between the two treatments. The study employed a parallel-group design without crossover. These findings collectively support KN012 as a well-tolerated and effective biosimilar alternative in this patient population.

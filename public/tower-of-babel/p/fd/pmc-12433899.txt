Current and Future Projection of Scrub Typhus Risk Related to Land Use Change in China
Authors: Ling Han, Zhaobin Sun, Guwei Zhang, Yunfei Zhang, Hongyu Ren, Zhongqiu Teng, Jianguo Xu, Tian Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe widespread concern surrounding the enhanced spillover risk of infectious diseases due to dramatic global land use changes has sparked significant discussion. However, the specific implications of these changes on scrub typhus, a vector‐borne infectious disease facing increasing incidence and substantial expansion, remain unclear. Here, we constructed a comprehensive landscape fragmentation index (LFI), which reflects the interaction between human activities and natural habitats. Then we utilized a generalized additive model (GAM) to estimate the comprehensive and segmented impacts of LFI on scrub typhus incidence in China, grouping the results by year, land use type and fragmentation level. Additionally, we projected changes in such impacts under four shared socioeconomic pathways (SSPs), including SSP1‐2.6, SSP2‐4.5, SSP3‐7.0, and SSP5‐8.5. Key results were: (a) The LFI exhibited a nonlinear positive correlation with scrub typhus incidence. Each 0.1 increase in the LFI was associated with a relative risk of 1.20 (95% CI:1.19–1.21) for scrub typhus. Notably, at higher fragmentation levels, scrub typhus incidence tended to decrease. (b) Forest fragmentation had the most significant impact on scrub typhus, followed by cropland fragmentation, whereas construction land fragmentation was negatively associated. (c) The future areas of elevated scrub typhus risk varied among the SSPs, but they were mainly concentrated at the interface between urban expansion and natural habitats. Our results indicate that human interference with the natural ecosystem is a critical factor for the incidence of scrub typhus. These findings are conducive to promoting ecological protection and the prevention and control of scrub typhus.Keywords: infectious disease, land use change, landscape fragmentation, projection, vector‐borne disease

Introduction

In recent years, intensified human development activities have led to drastic changes in global land use, resulting in an increased spillover risk of infectious disease (Pei et al., 2025; Rogalski et al., 2017). The land use change has contributed to the outbreaks of Ebola virus in Africa (Rulli et al., 2017), Zika virus in the United States (Ali et al., 2017), and Nipah virus in Malaysia (Vonesch et al., 2016). However, quantitative studies assessing the impact of land use change on infectious diseases are limited, and the future variation in this impact remains uncertain.

Scrub typhus is an ancient zoonotic disease caused by infection with Orientia tsutsugamushi (Ot), with rodents as the main reservoir host and chigger larvae as the transmission vector (Wei et al., 2017). Scrub typhus is clinically characterized by the appearance of specific eschars at the site of chigger bites, high fever, headache, rash, and lymphadenopathy, and is often complicated with pneumonia, meningitis, or disseminated intravascular coagulation (Han et al., 2023). In severe cases, it can lead to multiple organ failure and death. Scrub typhus is widely prevalent in the circum‐Asia‐Pacific triangle region (also known as the “scrub typhus triangle”), with major epidemic areas including China, Japan, Korea, Thailand, and Australia (G. Xu et al., 2017). In recent years, the global scrub typhus cases have been increasing, and the range has gradually expanded. Scrub typhus cases have also been reported in areas outside the “scrub typhus triangle,” such as the Middle East, Africa, South America, and Europe (G. Xu et al., 2017). In China, the incidence of scrub typhus was 0.13/100,000 population during 1952–1989 but has remarkably increased to 1.06/100,000 population during 2006–2022, with a total of 249,873 cases during 2006–2022 (Han et al., 2024). Meanwhile, the geographic distribution of scrub typhus has expanded to the northern and western regions (Han et al., 2024). About 200 million people were estimated to live in areas of potential scrub typhus infection in China (Zheng et al., 2019).

As the transmission vector of scrub typhus, chiggers are mainly distributed in habitats such as forests, farmland, grasslands, and shrubbery (Elliott et al., 2019). Contact between humans and chiggers is a necessary step for the occurrence of scrub typhus, which depends on the overlap between human activity environment and chigger habitat. Therefore, the core of studying the impact of land use change on the occurrence of scrub typhus lies in characterizing the interaction between human activity environments and chigger habitat. Human activities have been reported as the main driver for land use changes in modern society (X. Zhang et al., 2022), and their direct consequence is landscape fragmentation, which denotes that the landscape transforms from a single, homogeneous, and continuous integrity into a complicated, heterogeneous, and discrete patch (Zou et al., 2022). Therefore, landscape fragmentation can reflect the interaction and extent of human activities with the natural environment, thereby serving as an appropriate indicator to study the impact of land use change on scrub typhus.

In this study, we aimed to: (a) evaluate the impact of land use change on scrub typhus risk in China by constructing a comprehensive landscape fragmentation index (LFI). (b) Group the results by year, land use type and fragmentation level. (c) Project changes in this impact under four shared socioeconomic pathways (SSPs), including SSP1‐2.6, SSP2‐4.5, SSP3‐7.0, and SSP5‐8.5. We find that as landscape fragmentation intensifies, scrub typhus risk intially increases and then decreases. Forest fragmentation and cropland fragmentation are positively related to scrub typhus incidence, while the association between the fragmentation of construction land and scrub typhus is negative. Moreover, we project that the areas of increased scrub typhus risk will be mainly distributed at the interface between urban expansion and natural systems.

Materials and Methods

Historical Data on Scrub Typhus, Meteorological, Population, and Socioeconomic Factors

Data on scrub typhus cases in mainland China were collected from the National Notifiable Infectious Disease Reporting Information System (NNIDRIS). We identified the longitude and latitude of scrub typhus cases according to the administrative division code of their residences. We extracted the scrub typhus case data for 2010, 2013, 2015, 2018, and 2020 according to the onset year to match with the land use remote sensing monitoring data. Clinically diagnosed and laboratory‐confirmed cases were included in our study.

Land use remote sensing monitoring data were derived from the Resource and Environmental Science Data Platform, established by the Institute of Geographic Sciences and Nature Resource Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences. We obtained the China's National Land Use and Cover Change data set (CNLUCC) (X. Xu et al., 2018), which is constructed through artificial visual interpretation by using the US land satellite Landsat remote sensing image as the main information source. The land use type was classified as cropland, forest, grassland, construction land, water, and unused land. We obtained land use remote sensing monitoring data for the years 2010, 2013, 2015, 2018, and 2020.

Considering that meteorological and socioeconomic factors can also influence the incidence of scrub typhus (Han et al., 2023; Tsai & Yeh, 2013; Xin et al., 2020), we adjusted for the effect of meteorological factors (temperature and precipitation), population density, and Gross Domestic Product (GDP) when estimating the effect of LFI on scrub typhus incidence. Data on temperature and precipitation were obtained from the National Tibetan Plateau Data Center (TPDC), including the 1‐km monthly mean temperature data set for China (Peng, 2019) and the 1‐km monthly mean precipitation data set for China (Peng, 2020). Then we calculated the annual monthly mean temperature and mean precipitation for 2010, 2013, 2015, 2018, and 2020 using the ArcGIS 10.2 software. Population density data for the same period were obtained from WorldPOP, and GDP data were obtained from the Resource and Environmental Science Data Platform (Xu, 2017), both at a 1 km resolution.

Land Use Projection Data

Land use projection data was obtained from the future global land use and land cover (LULC) data set at a 1 km resolution (T. Zhang et al., 2023). This data set takes into account future climate and socioeconomic changes as well as the impact of simulated results of the former year on temporally adjacent periods, including the historical land use data in 2020 and the future land use data under four SSPS, including SSP1‐2.6, SSP2‐4.5, SSP3‐7.0, and SSP5‐8.5 scenarios in 2030, 2050, 2070, and 2100. The SSPs were adopted by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change in the Sixth Assessment Report, and replaced the Representative Concentration Pathways scenarios, aiming to quantitatively describe the relationship between climate change and socioeconomic development pathways. SSP1‐2.6 represents the combination of a sustainable development pathway and a low radiative forcing scenario, with the radiative forcing stabilized at approximately 2.6 W/m2 by 2100. SSP2‐4.5 represents the combination of a moderate development pathway and a medium radiative forcing scenario. SSP3‐7.0 represents the combination of a regional competitive pathway and a higher radiative forcing scenario, and SSP5‐8.5 represents a pathway that is dominated by traditional fossil fuels and a high emissions scenario (L. X. Zhang et al., 2019). The land use type was classified as cropland, forest, grassland, urban, barren, and water, which was overall consistent with the classification method in the CNLUCC.

Construction of Landscape Fragmentation Index (LFI)

Landscape pattern indices have been widely used to estimate land use landscape fragmentation (Z. M. Zhang & Gao, 2016). The use of multiple types of landscape pattern indices can avoid the limitations and one‐sidedness of individual landscape pattern indices which can comprehensively reflect the landscape fragmentation level. We selected nine landscape pattern indices to construct the LFI, including the Number of Patches (NP), Mean Patch Area (MPA), Patch Density (PD), Edge Density (ED), Landscape Shape Index (LSI), Contagion (CONTAG), Land Division Index (DIVISION), Aggregation Index (AI), and Shannon's Diversity Index (SHDI). Specifically, the NP, MPA, and PD manifest the degree of landscape fragmentation; the LSI reflects the complexity of patch shape; the ED denotes the edge characteristic; the CONTAG, DIVISION, and AI reflect the patch connectivity; and the SHDI denote the patch diversity and heterogeneity. Additionally, NP, PD, ED, LSI, DIVISION, and SHDI are positively correlated with the degree of landscape fragmentation, while the MPA, CONTAG, and AI are negatively correlated with landscape fragmentation level. The moving window method has been widely used to establish the LFI, and previous studies have indicated that the moving window with a size of 2 km × 2 km can better reflect the landscape fragmentation pattern (Gong et al., 2015). Therefore, we adopted the moving window method to establish a rectangular window of 2 km × 2 km. With the window as the basic analysis unit, the calculated landscape pattern index values were assigned to the central grid of the window. The spatial distribution pattern of the landscape pattern index was calculated by moving the window from the upper left grid of the image. These landscape pattern indices were calculated using Fragstats 4.2 software (McGarigal et al., 2012).

Then we utilized spatial principal component analysis to construct the LFI. This method can transform multivariate data from different spaces into a few comprehensive indicators through the rotation of spatial coordinate axes. Due to inconsistencies in units and dimensions among various landscape pattern indices, we first standardized the original data to a range between 0 and 1 to ensure comparability. Then we calculated the principal components and their contribution degrees using the spatial principal component analysis method. Principal components with a cumulative contribution degree of ≥95% were selected. Based on this criterion, the first and second principal components were finally selected for each year. We constructed the LFI for the current period (2010, 2013, 2015, 2018, and 2020) and the future period (2030, 2050, 2070, and 2100) under the SSP1‐2.6, SSP2‐4.5, SSP3‐7.0, and SSP5‐8.5 scenarios. Considering the bias between the projected and current data, we calculated the grid‐specific correction factor by comparing the LFI for 2020 from the future data set and the current data set. Then we corrected the projected LFI in each grid using the correction factor.

Impact Estimation of Landscape Fragmentation on Scrub Typhus Incidence

We used a semi‐parametric generalized additive model (GAM) to assess the impact of landscape fragmentation on scrub typhus incidence. Compared to the total population, the daily number of scrub typhus cases is a low‐probability event, and its actual distribution approximately follows a Poisson distribution. Therefore, a Poisson regression model was incorporated into the GAM to fit the relationship between the LFI and the number of scrub typhus cases in nonlinear and linear additive forms. The incidence of scrub typhus is intricately linked to residents engaging in outdoor activities, particularly in natural ecosystems such as farmland, forests, and grasslands. Therefore, the occurrence of scrub typhus is closely related to the land use characteristics of the areas where infection occurs. However, the NNIDRIS only contains information on the residence of cases, but lacks information on the actual locations where infection occurred. Therefore, our analysis of the land use characteristics impacting scrub typhus is constrained to relying solely on the residential data of the reported cases. Our previous analyses indicated that the vast majority of scrub typhus cases are farmers, and they have a history of epidemiological exposure to agricultural activities prior to the onset of illness (Han et al., 2024). Generally speaking, farmland is usually located in proximity to residential areas to facilitate farming operations and easy access. Therefore, in this study, we assumed that the incidence of scrub typhus was related to the land use characteristics within 10 km around the residence. Accordingly, the country was divided into 96,521 grids with a grid size of 10 km × 10 km. Impact Projection of Landscape Fragmentation on Scrub Typhus Risk

We first analyzed changes in land use area and LFI under different SSPs. Then, based on the characteristics of the exposure‐response relationship between LFI and scrub typhus incidence during the current period, we classified the impact of LFI on scrub typhus incidence into three levels: no risk, high risk, and moderate risk. Then we analyzed changes in the landscape fragmentation‐related scrub typhus risk under different SSPs in each grid. A change in a grid's scrub typhus risk level from no risk to high/moderate risk or from moderate risk to high risk was defined as an increased risk; a change from high risk to moderate/no risk or from moderate risk to no risk was defined as a decreased risk.

Results

Distribution of LFI and Scrub Typhus Cases

From 2010 to 2020, the area of construction land increased dramatically, with a growth rate of 21.0% in 2020 compared to 2010. The areas of cropland, forest, grassland, and barren land showed downward trends, while the water area increased slightly (Table S1 in Supporting Information S1). Nationally, the landscape fragmentation level can be roughly divided by the Heihe‐Tengchong Line, with relatively higher fragmentation in the southeastern region and lower fragmentation in the northwestern region (Figure 1a). Changes in the LFI also followed this division. Compared to 2010, the LFI increased in most parts of the southeastern region by 2020 (Figure 1c).

During the 5 years, a total of 86,775 scrub typhus cases were reported, with cases increasing rapidly from 4,085 in 2010 to 27,549 in 2020. The growth was mainly observed in the southern region (Figure 1d). The majority of scrub typhus cases were distributed in the southeastern region, and the geographical distribution expanded significantly northward and westward (Figure 1b).

The majority of scrub typhus cases (95%) were distributed in fragmented forest, cropland, and construction land, accounting for 53%, 37%, and 5%, respectively. Therefore, in subsequent subgroup analyses, we analyzed the impact of fragmented forest, cropland, and construction land on scrub typhus incidence.

Association Between LFI and Scrub Typhus Incidence

Overall, a nonlinear positive association was observed between LFI and scrub typhus incidence (Figure 2a). The RR of scrub typhus incidence was 1.20 (95% CI:1.19–1.21) associated with a 0.1 increase in LFI (Table 1). Specifically, when the LFI was below 0.1, the negative RR indicated that lower landscape fragmentation was not favorable for the occurrence of scrub typhus. When the LFI exceed 0.1, scrub typhus incidence increased with higher LFI. However, scrub typhus incidence declined when the LFI was above 0.6 (Figure 2a). Therefore, we classified the impact of landscape fragmentation on scrub typhus incidence into three levels: no risk (LFI ≤ 0.1), high risk (0.1 < LFI < 0.6), and moderate risk (LFI ≥ 0.6). Based on this classification, we analyzed changes in landscape fragmentation‐related scrub typhus risk under different SSPs for each grid. Results grouped by year also suggested a positive association between LFI and scrub typhus incidence, with a more notable decline at high fragmentation levels in 2015 and later (Figure 2b). Results by land use type suggested that forest and cropland fragmentation were positively related to scrub typhus incidence, with forest fragmentation having a greater effect than cropland fragmentation. The RR was 1.37 (95% CI:1.36–1.39) for forest fragmentation and 1.04 (95% CI:1.03–1.05) for cropland fragmentation. However, fragmentation of construction land was negatively associated with scrub typhus incidence (Figure 2c and Table 1). Classifying the landscape fragmentation levels in a moving way further verified the trends between LFI and scrub typhus incidence obtained from the overall results and provided estimates at different fragmentation levels (Figure 2d and Table 1). Sensitivity analyses confirmed that the nonlinear positive association between LFI and scrub typhus incidence remained constant, with the greatest effect observed in fragmented forests (Figures S1–S4 in Supporting Information S1).

Projected Changes in Land Use Area and LFI

Overall, cropland area is projected to gradually decrease, while urban area is expected to expand steadily. Changes in forest and grassland areas vary among scenarios, whereas barren and water areas are not expected to change significantly (Figure S5 and Table S2 in Supporting Information S1). Specifically, under SSP1‐2.6, the decline in cropland area is the greatest among the four scenarios, estimated at 25% by 2100 compared to 2030, while both urban and forest areas increase. Under SSP2‐4.5, urban expansion surpasses that of SSP1‐2.6, accompanied by reductions in forest and grassland areas. In contrast, under SSP3‐7.0, the reduction in cropland area and expansion of urban areas are smallest among the scenarios. Under SSP5‐8.5, urban expansion will peak among all scenarios, with a 259% increase by 2100 compared to 2030, accompanied by reductions in forest, cropland, and grassland areas.

We analyzed differences in LFI from SSP1‐2.6 to the other three scenarios to examine the effects of SSPs on landscape fragmentation (Figure 3). The results indicate that, in 2030, landscape fragmentation in most regions will be lower under other scenarios compared to SSP1‐2.6, with a notable exception in northeastern China, where fragmentation increases significantly. By 2050, the landscape fragmentation under SSP5‐8.5 will increase widely, while it will decrease under SSP2‐4.5 and SSP3‐7.0, a trend that reverses by 2070. By 2100, increased landscape fragmentation under SSP3‐7.0 will be particularly notable. Under the same scenario, the spatial distribution differences in landscape fragmentation change over time. Specifically, in eastern Inner Mongolia, southeastern Yunnan, and eastern Gansu, landscape fragmentation increases significantly by 2100 compared to 2070 under SSP2‐4.5 (Figure S6 in Supporting Information S1).

Projected Changes in Landscape Fragmentation‐Related Scrub Typhus Risk

We compared scrub typhus risk associated with landscape fragmentation between years under different SSPs to identify regions with changing risk (Figure 4; Table S3 in Supporting Information S1). Overall, regions with increased risk are mainly distributed in the northern and western parts of China. Under SSP1‐2.6, scrub typhus risk is projected to increase across an area of 1,850 km2 by 2050, mainly in Heilongjiang Province, with scattered areas in Jilin, Shandong, Henan, Tibet, and Xinjiang. Notably, the risk in Heilongjiang is projected to persist and potentially intensify in subsequent years. Under SSP2‐4.5, the expansion of scrub typhus risk zones by 2100 will be the most extensive among the four scenarios, covering 3,810 km2. This increase is primarily observed in Heilongjiang, Jilin, eastern Inner Mongolia, and eastern Sichuan. Under SSP3‐7.0, despite having the smallest changes in scrub typhus risk area among the scenarios, a significant rise in risk over eastern Sichuan by 2070 is noteworthy. Under SSP5‐8.5, the increased risk area is relatively widespread, particularly in Qinghai by 2100, making a distinction from other scenarios. By comparing changes in land use type and scrub typhus risk distribution, we found that the areas of elevated scrub typhus risk are mainly located at the interface between urban expansion and the natural environment.

Discussion

Our study finds that landscape fragmentation exhibits a nonlinear positive relationship with scrub typhus incidence in China, and this effect varies by year, land use type and fragmentation level. We project that future areas of increased scrub typhus risk related to landscape fragmentation will be mainly distributed at the interface between urban expansion and natural habitats.

A few studies have analyzed the association between land use characteristics and scrub typhus risk. For instance, the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), which serves as an indicator of vegetation cover intensity, was reported to be positively associated with the presence of scrub typhus in southern China (Zheng et al., 2019). In Taiwan, the proportion of land containing mosaics of cropland and vegetation may increase scrub typhus incidence (Wardrop et al., 2013). In southern India, residents living in houses surrounded by bushes or shrubs had a significantly higher risk of contracting scrub typhus compared to the control group, with an odds ratio of 1.95 (95% CI:1.08–3.53) (Varghese et al., 2016). These findings contribute to understanding the impact of land use on scrub typhus, though they primarily focus on the effects of isolated or specific land use types.

Our study systematically investigates the impact of landscape fragmentation on scrub typhus incidence, providing novel insights and methodologies for examining how land use changes affect scrub typhus dynamics. We find that landscape fragmentation levels can be approximately divided by the Heihe‐Tengchong Line, with higher fragmentation levels in the southeastern regions. The Heihe‐Tengchong Line represents a population and socioeconomic boundary in China, with the southeastern region generally exhibiting higher urbanization levels and denser populations. From 2010 to 2020, the significant increase in construction area indicates an acceleration of urbanization. Cities continue to expand into surrounding areas to obtain more land for housing and infrastructure, leading to the division of originally contiguous land into smaller patches. Natural landscapes such as farmland, forestland, and grassland continue to shrink, having been converted into urban construction land, thus being divided into small patches and forming fragmented landscapes. These changes suggest that intensifying human activities have being the main drivers for land use change and landscape fragmentation in China in recent decades.

Our results indicate an overall positive association between landscape fragmentation and scrub typhus incidence. Landscape fragmentation refers to the breaking up of continuous landscapes into smaller, isolated patches, which has profound impacts on the ecology and epidemiology of vector‐borne diseases. First, fragmented habitats may increase the risk of disease transmission by enhancing opportunities for vector‐human encounters. For example, a high rate of deforestation has occurred in China's important forest resource areas due to economic growth and land demand (Sarathchandra et al., 2018). Deforestation reduces the availability of natural habitats for vectors and their animal hosts, forcing them to seek alternative habitats closer to human settlements (Bloomfield et al., 2020). Urbanization and the shrinkage and subdivision of farmland can further fragment the landscape, creating more edge habitats that are conducive to vector‐host interactions. Second, edge habitats resulting from landscape fragmentation tend to have relatively high species diversity and abundance (Galle et al., 2018), thereby increasing opportunities for disease transmission. Third, fragmented habitats can create barriers to host migration, leading to genetic isolation and reduced genetic diversity in vector and host populations (Perrin et al., 2021), which may weaken their resistance to pathogens and increase the transmission potential of pathogens.

However, we also find that the association between LFI and scrub typhus incidence is nonlinear, with risk decreasing at high fragmentation levels. This result may be related to the reason that the heavily fragmented habitats can reduce the habitat suitability for vectors and limit their movement range when seeking hosts (Oliver & Morecroft, 2014), which is unfavorable for their growth and reproduction, thereby decreasing the risk of disease transmission. Fragmentation also has nonlinear effects on pathogen persistence in host‐pathogen systems, with the most fragmented landscapes not necessarily being the most conducive to infectious disease outbreaks or pathogen persistence (White et al., 2018). Our results indicate that the association between landscape fragmentation and infectious disease is complex, and more investigations are needed to reveal the underlying mechanisms.

The results grouped by land use type suggest that the impacts of landscape fragmentation on scrub typhus incidence vary among land use types. Forest fragmentation can significantly increase scrub typhus risk, followed by cropland fragmentation, while the fragmentation of construction land is negatively associated with scrub typhus incidence. These results can be explained by the fact that the abundance and species richness of chigger mites are significantly affected by habitat type, which is the highest in forest borders, followed by plantations, and the lowest in human dwellings (Alkathiry et al., 2022). The Ot‐infected rodents have also been reported to be more likely found in forest‐covered habitats (Chaisiri et al., 2017). Additionally, the composition and richness of chigger mites and rodent hosts are negatively associated with increasing levels of human disturbance and urbanization (Chaisiri et al., 2019; Linsuwanon et al., 2021). These findings suggest that it is necessary to fully consider the impact of land use planning on landscape fragmentation and its consequent effect on scrub typhus. Particularly, it is important to protect the integrity of forest ecosystems through scientific land use planning. Additionally, we find that the downward trend in scrub typhus incidence at high landscape fragmentation levels is more remarkable after 2015, which may be related to the highly fragmented landscapes resulting from rapid urbanization.

We observe significant changes in future land use under the comprehensive influence of climate and socioeconomic changes. Under SSP1‐2.6, the farmland area will decrease, while urban and forest areas will increase. This scenario represents an environmentally friendly and sustainable development pathway, which involves adjustments and optimizations in land use to better balance economic development and ecological protection. Policies to return farmland to forests may be implemented to prevent soil erosion. In contrast, the SSP5‐8.5 denotes a scenario of high economic growth, high energy demand, and high greenhouse gas emissions. A large amount of farmland and forest will be converted into industrial, commercial, and residential land to meet the sharply increasing demand for land. Moreover, high greenhouse gas emissions will lead to substantial warming and consequent increase in evapotranspiration, resulting in severe drought and land degradation in some areas, thus reducing the areas of farmland and forest.

At present, although the epidemic areas of scrub typhus are mainly distributed in southern China, we are witnessing a remarkably increasing incidence rate and geographical expansion of scrub typhus in recent years, which has been reported to be associated with climate and land use changes (Yao et al., 2019). The future spatiotemporal dynamics of scrub typhus under the influence of climate change have been projected in previous studies (Ding et al., 2022), but the future impact of land use change on the risk of scrub typhus remains to be investigated. We project that the future areas of elevated scrub typhus risk related to landscape fragmentation are mainly located at the interface between urban expansion and the natural environment, particularly in the northern and western regions. Urbanization plays an important role in global land use change (Dadashpoor et al., 2019). Although the urban area is projected to expand in China under different SSPs, the change trends vary among regions, with more remarkable urban expansion in the northern and western regions (Figure S2 in Supporting Information S1). Urban construction invades cropland, forest and grassland, resulting in an intensification of landscape fragmentation, thus increasing the risk of scrub typhus at the interface between urban areas and natural systems, rather than inside the cities. Additionally, some areas of elevated scrub typhus risk can be attributed to the dispersed cropland which was transformed from forest, grassland, and barren land, such as the increased risk in Qinghai by 2100 under SSP5‐8.5. The results emphasize the importance of formulating reasonable land use planning, setting rational boundaries for urban expansion, and promoting land consolidation during the process of urbanization. Moreover, scrub typhus is often misdiagnosed or missed in clinical practice owing to the lack of typical clinical symptoms, particularly in the northern and western regions where the incidence rate is currently low, due to a significant knowledge gap (Han et al., 2024). Areas at urban and natural interfaces also often experience a lag in economic development in China, resulting in relatively underdeveloped healthcare service capabilities. Facing the increasing risk of scrub typhus brought by land fragmentation in the future, in addition to scientific land planning, the urban fringe areas in the northern and western regions also need to strengthen their awareness of scrub typhus and improve detection capabilities in advance.

The limitations of our study should also be acknowledged. First, the scrub typhus data were collected from a passive surveillance system, therefore, underreporting may be inevitable to some extent and could influence the data quality. Second, data on rodents and chigger mites were unavailable due to the lack of field surveys, so we were unable to analyze the impact of land use change on scrub typhus through the chain of “landscape fragmentation‐host and vector‐scrub typhus incidence” to provide richer information and more rigorous evidence. Third, in this study, we estimated the impact of landscape fragmentation by adjusting for the confounding effect of meteorological factors. It remains unclear whether land use and meteorological conditions exert varying impacts on scrub typhus across different regions, and furthermore, whether these two factors have a combined effect on the incidence of scrub typhus. It is meaningful to explore such a topic in future research for developing more effective prevention and control strategies for scrub typhus.

Conclusions

Our results revealed a nonlinear positive association between LFI and scrub typhus incidence in China. The RR of scrub typhus incidence was 1.20 (95% CI:1.19–1.21) associated with a 0.1 increase in LFI. However, at higher fragmentation levels, the scrub typhus incidence tended to decrease.

Our results revealed a nonlinear positive association between LFI and scrub typhus incidence in China. The RR of scrub typhus incidence was 1.20 (95% CI:1.19–1.21) associated with a 0.1 increase in LFI. However, at higher fragmentation levels, the scrub typhus incidence tended to decrease.

The impacts of LFI on scrub typhus incidence varied among different land use types. Forest fragmentation had the greatest impact, with an RR of 1.37 (95% CI:1.36–1.39). The cropland fragmentation was also positively related to scrub typhus incidence, with an RR of 1.04 (95% CI:1.03–1.05). However, the construction land fragmentation was negatively associated with scrub typhus incidence.

The future areas of increased scrub typhus risk are varied among the SSPs. Under SSP2‐4.5, the area with an increased risk of scrub typhus is projected to reach 3,810 km2 by 2100 compared to 2070, which is the most extensive among four SSPs.

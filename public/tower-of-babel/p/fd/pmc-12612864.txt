Experimental Study on Coal Seam Fracturing Based on Hydrodynamic Horizontal Slitting-CO 2 Blasting Collaboration
Authors: Hao Chen, Yiping Zhang, Qinzhi Liu, Lianhua Wu, Chuan Li, Tianle Li, Maoxin Liu, Jinchun Hu, Youwen Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
With the deepening of coal mining depth, the geological conditions of coal seams become more and more complicated, and the effect of single gas permeability enhancement technology in practical engineering applications is not ideal. To address the limitations of the single coal seam permeability enhancement technology, this paper takes the No.9 coal seam of a coal mine in Guizhou as the research object and puts forward a gas–liquid two-phase comprehensive action coal seam permeability enhancement technology. The hydraulic horizontal slitting technology is used to create a free surface for pressure relief and then the CO2 fracturing and permeability technology is used to synergize the pressure relief and permeability of the coal body. By analyzing the crack expansion of similar models through simulation experiments and examining the characteristics of displacement field distribution on the model surface using digital image correlation techniques, COMSOL Multiphysics was utilized to analyze coal body displacement and validate the simulation experiments. The results show that the delamination cracks generated between the roof slab and the coal seam inhibit the radial crack extension of the coal seam. The y-axis displacement values above the slit show regular changes, gradually decreasing from the center to both sides, while the displacement values below the slit remain essentially unchanged. The unpressurized free surface created by the hydraulic horizontal slit left the coal body above unsupported, resulting in the formation of a large number of horizontally expanding cracks as well as radially expanding cracks. CO2 blasting further perturbed the internal coal body, inducing crack expansion and generating numerous new radial cracks. Together, these processes formed a complex crack network. The hydraulic horizontal slit-CO2 blasting gas–liquid two-phase integrated action permeability enhancement technology provides a novel approach for the efficient mining of deep coal seams.

Introduction

Coal is the “ballast” of China’s energy security and plays an important role in guaranteeing the bottom line. As China’s coal mining gradually advances to the deep, the tectonic complexity, high gas pressure, and low permeability faced by deep mines are becoming more and more serious. − The difficulty of preventing and controlling disasters represented by coal and gas protrusion is gradually increasing, which seriously restricts the safe and efficient mining of coal in China. , Therefore, improving the permeability of coal seams and increasing the efficiency of gas extraction is a hot issue that needs to be solved urgently in the coal mining industry. −

In order to improve the permeability of coal seams, scholars at home and abroad have carried out a lot of research and put forward many technical methods. These methods primarily include hydraulic fracturing, hydraulic slitting, high-pressure pulse techniques, and so on. − They mainly start from changing the external pressure of the coal seam to provide channels for the desorption and flow of gas, and all these technical measures have played a greater role in promoting the improvement of coal seam permeability. − Shi Cheng et al. investigated the mechanism of hydraulic slitting to cause stress release and thus improve the gas extraction rate. Through tests, they demonstrated that after hydraulic slitting, the amount of gas extracted and the pure amount were 4.41 and 4.20 times higher than that of the traditional drilling technique, and the permeability of the coal seam was effectively improved. Zuo Sun et al. used FLAC3D to establish a numerical model of high-pressure water jet cutting coal and studied the effect of different slit parameters on the coal seam decompression. The results showed that the optimal slit parameters are a slit width of 0.2 m and a slit groove spacing of 3 m. This study provides valuable theoretical guidance for hydraulic slit cutting technology. ZhaolongGe et al. proposed a self-pressurized water jet generation method based on the water jet theory and the hydraulic pressurization principle. They conducted jet pressure acquisition, morphology capture, and granite erosion tests. The experimental results show that the error between the calculated pressure and the theoretical design value is less than 3%, which confirms the feasibility of the method and the good erosion effect of the water jets on rocks. Theoretical studies and field tests show that the use of high-pressure water jet cutting to form a ring-shaped slit inside the coal seam can effectively improve the stress state of the drilled coal body, increase the permeability of the coal seam, and improve the efficiency of gas extraction. − All of the above technologies have certain applications. However, most of the current permeability enhancement techniques are centered on the hole to expand the fissure in all directions, ignoring the self-gravitational stress of the coal body itself, and fail to achieve the effect of a large area of collapsed cracks. − Additionally, China’s coal seam gas endowment geological conditions are very complex, coal mine underground roadway layout in a variety of forms, the existing single coal seam permeability technologies face numerous challenges in practical applications, and the effectiveness of coal seam permeability improvement remains suboptimal . −

In recent years, liquid CO2 phase change fracturing technology has been gradually applied to coal seam permeability and blasting due to its advantages of safe and fast blasting, high power, no shock damage, and easy operation. It has become a new type of coal seam permeability technology. − Shu Ma et al. conducted blasting tests on coal rock model specimens under different liquid CO2 phase change fracturing pressures, and the results showed that the number of cracks produced by the coal rock specimens increased significantly with the increase in blasting pressure, gradually evolving into a complex fissure network, and the fracturing effect on the coal body was good. Bo Fang utilized the gas tracer method to carry out carbon dioxide blasting tests on the working face and found that the radius of collision impact caused by liquid carbon dioxide phase change fracturing was 2 m. After blasting, the amount of gas extracted from the coal seam increased by 4 to 8 times, proving that liquid carbon dioxide phase change fracturing technology was not restricted by the geological conditions of the coal seam and had high-efficiency characteristics. Fake Ren et al. synergized hydraulic fracturing technology and liquid carbon dioxide phase change fracturing technology to develop a multifunctional fracturing pipe capable of realizing joint gas–liquid fracturing. They conducted related fracturing tests on samples, which proved that the synergy of the two can shorten the duration of the fracturing stage, reduce the breakdown pressure, and change the direction of fracture extension, which is of great significance for improving the permeability of coal seams. After adopting the liquid CO2 blasting and permeability technology, the effective radius of gas drainage of the borehole and the permeability of the high-gas coal seam have been increased, and the gas drainage rate has been significantly improved. −

Based on this, this study proposes a gas–liquid two-phase integrated action coal seam permeability enhancement technology. Through the combination of CO2 fracturing technology and hydraulic horizontal slit technology, it circumvents the limitations of the traditional method of unloading pressure and increasing the permeability of the coal seam centered on the hole. It creates a free surface for unloading pressure by using the hydraulic horizontal slit technology, which changes the displacement characteristics of the coal body and realizes the effect of replacing the roadway with the slit. CO2 blasting is then utilized to provide dynamic loads to damage the surrounding coal body, further inducing crack expansion. The combination of these two technologies can control the direction of crack expansion, form a complex crack network, increase the range of influence on coal seam permeability enhancement, and improve the effectiveness of coal seam gas extraction.

Sample Preparation and Determination of Mechanical Parameters

The core specimens were extracted from stratigraphic unit 9 of Longfeng Colliery, a representative mining site situated in the karst-dominated region of southwestern China. This unit is located in the middle and at the bottom of the upper section of the Longtan Formation, with a burial depth of 426 m, an average seam thickness of 2.7 m, and a relative gas influx of 46.06 m3/t. 9# coal seam protruding danger comprehensive index K is ≥ 19, and the results of grade appraisal in previous years classify it as a high gas mine. The mine has a history of coal and gas outburst accidents and is categorized as a coal and gas outburst mine. The geological histogram of the mine is shown in Figure .

Through precision machining protocols involving diamond-tipped cutting tools and surface grinding systems, all specimens were standardized to meet the geometric specifications. Hydraulic Horizontal Slit-CO2 Blasting Unloading Fracturing Similarity Simulation Experiments

The hydraulic horizontal slitting-CO2 blasting similarity simulation experiment was carried out with the help of a self-designed similarity simulation experiment platform with the dimensions of l = 180 cm in length, d = 30 cm in width, and h = 185 cm in height. The dimensions of the constructed experimental model were 135 cm × 30 cm × 100 cm. Employing the principle of similarity, the established scaling relationships demonstrated a geometric proportionality of 1:10, with volumetric correspondence at 1:1.6 and power similarity scaling governed by a 1:16 coefficient. The ground stress was simulated by the working pressure provided by a mechanical loading system consisting of an ultrahigh-pressure electric pump, a hydraulic line, a hydraulic jack, and a three-way valve, which provided a longitudinal loading stress of 0.67 MPa. The experimental method is shown in Figure . The model was laid using a mixture of sand, lime, and gypsum mixed with water according to ratios obtained based on mechanical experiments of similar materials. The model was demolded 1 week after molding, and after the model was maintained for 28 days, a layer of white matte paint was applied to its surface. After air-drying, black scattering spots were randomly created for the subsequent 3D-DIC experiment. The experimental setup is shown in Figure .

Schematic diagram of the hydraulic horizontal slit-CO2 blasting fracturing unloading experiment.

Similar simulation experiment platform.

Based on the principle of protective layer mining, when the height of the coal seam is 27 cm and the height of the designed seam groove is 1 cm, the maximum deformation expansion rate is calculated to be 0.037, which is greater than 0.003. This can make the coal seam unpressurized, thereby achieving the effect of permeability enhancement.

A 30 cm protective coal pillar was reserved on the left and right sides of the coal body, and a slit groove with a length of 75 cm, width of 30 cm, and height of 1 cm was cut horizontally about 8 cm above the bottom of the seam to create a pressure-relieving free surface based on the interior of the seam. To reduce drilling workload and production costs while enhancing mining efficiency, this experiment employed a set of blasting holes arranged horizontally at 20 cm intervals within the upper-middle section of the coal seam, positioned 14.5 cm above the floor. This approach was based on the principle of similar proportions.

The effectiveness of CO2 phase-change fracturing technology in enhancing the permeability of coal seams stems from its synergistic physicochemical coupling mechanism rather than a single physical process. The critical point of liquid CO2 occurs at 31.1 °C and 7.38 MPa. Under the instantaneous high-pressure stimulus generated by a remote detonator, the chemical heating agent ignites and heats the fracturing pipe. The liquid CO2 rapidly transitions to a supercritical state and undergoes a violent phase change, expanding in volume by approximately 600 times or more. This expansion caused the constant-stress shear plate to rupture. Subsequently, high-energy CO2 gas is ejected from the release port, generating a powerful shockwave that disturbs the coal body and creates fractures. After the primary blasting fragmentation process is completed, high-pressure CO2 gas infiltrates both newly formed and existing fractures, promoting further expansion and the interconnection of the cracks. Simultaneously, a portion of the CO2 gas dissolves in the inherent moisture of the coal matrix to form carbonic acid, lowering the pH of the coal seam’s fracture water and creating a localized acidic environment. This dissolves the mineral fill within the fractures, thereby significantly enhancing the permeability of the coal seam.

During this period, the digital scattering system was utilized to complete the entire displacement change monitoring data collection, and the endoscope was used to observe the changes in coal damage fissures inside the fracturing holes. Experimental Results and Analysis

Model Surface Crack Evolution Law

The test crack analysis is mainly based on the coal seam and its top and bottom muddy siltstone, tracing the model surface cracks to characterize it. The damage and crack pattern of the model after the cut and after the explosion are shown in Figure . Figure a shows the damage pattern of the model after cutting. Cracks appeared on the surface of the model specimen after cutting, and the cracks were distributed symmetrically on the left and right. The overall cracks showed a “trapezoidal” shape, and the overall shape of the cracks was similar to the shape of the rock layer collapsing after the mining of the protective layer. The cracks near the cut are through and perpendicular to each other. The cracks on both sides above the coal seam are oriented directly above the model, and one transverse delamination fracture fissure is produced between the top plate and the coal seam, while there are only two longitudinal cracks in the top plate siltstone. It can be concluded that the horizontal space is formed inside the coal body after hydraulic slit, the original equilibrium state of the coal rock body is broken, and under the action of the gravity of the overlying coal rock body itself, the structure is changed, and a certain amount of deformation and displacement is produced, resulting in cracks in the coal body.

Model specimen damage: (a) After slitting. (b) After explosion.

The crack pattern of the coal body after liquid CO2 phase change fracturing is shown in Figure b. Under the action of the gas shock wave and explosive gas, the cracks of the coal body were further expanded, and at the same time, the coal body was damaged and started to sprout new cracks. The cracks expanded radially from the center of the fracturing holes to the surroundings, and horizontal development was more significant. Horizontally, under the effect of liquid CO2 phase change fracturing, the cracks between the fracturing holes penetrated through appearance of transverse laminar cracking and peeling and extend to the left and right ends of the model. This was accompanied by a gradual weakening of the energy, with a consequent decrease in crack width, and eventually stops expanding above the ends of the cut. Vertically, one crack developed vertically upward from the cut below each of the 2 fracturing holes, with the right crack connecting with the transverse crack near the fracturing holes and the left crack stopping its development below the delamination fissure; three cracks intersected below the left fracturing hole. Under the action of gravity and stress waves, the surface of the model spalled off. During the process of explosion, the coal seam and the roof produced delamination fissures between the coal seam and the roof plate. The coal seam lost the vertical stress effect of the overlying rock layer and was subjected only to lateral pressure in the horizontal direction the radial crack expansion from the fracturing holes was suppressed. Additionally , the damage area and the compensation space had a guiding effect on the crack expansion, which resulted in fewer cracks forming above the coal seam.

Characteristics of Crack Distribution Inside the Fracturing Holes

After the explosion, an endoscope was used to observe the distribution of cracks inside the fracturing holes. The inside of the fracturing holes is shown in Figure . After the detonation, the blast wave first acted on the inner wall of the holes, and the shock wave propagated around the holes, gradually weakening into a stress wave. When the tensile stress exceeds the cohesive force of the coal body, the stress wave causes damage to the coal rock body, forming damage zones and producing microcracks. Subsequently, the explosive gas in the confined space undergoes rapid expansion, forming a high-pressure jet. The blast wave generated by the damage region guides the flow of the high-pressure jet, and under the explosive gas wedge effect, the cracks rapidly expand and penetrate, ultimately forming macroscopic cracks within the coal body. It can be clearly observed at a distance of 15 cm from the hole that the damage is more obvious on the left and right sides of the hole, producing a crushed area with a small amount of coal chips falling. The crack extension directions of the left and right fracturing holes are 30° and −30°, respectively, which are consistent with the crack direction on the model surface.

Numerical Simulation Methods

COMSOL Multiphysics Modeling

Hydrodynamic horizontal seam cutting technology utilizes high-pressure water jets to cut coal seams, involving fluid-mechanical coupling, CO2 phase change fracturing involves the impact of high-pressure gas generated by the instantaneous gasification of liquid CO2 on coal rock and permeability change, involving thermal-force coupling, and fluid-pore medium coupling, While COMSOL Multiphysics can better simulate the coupling of multiphysics fields such as fluid flow, solid mechanics, heat transfer, and damage evolution, it can construct a complete water-force-heat-damage (THMD) coupling model.

Numerical simulation of the hydraulic horizontal slit-CO2 blasting synergistic action to fracture the coal body process is based on Longfeng coal mine in Guizhou. The CO2 blast fracturing process changes with time, so a transient model was chosen to simulate the process in order to solve it. A specific fracturing function is loaded at the boundary of the fracturing hole, which is fitted based on the specific law of the shock wave generated by the high-pressure gas decaying with time. The relationship between the propagation distance of the shock wave and the time is also taken into account in order to simulate the actual fracturing process, and to ensure that the model can accurately reflect the influence of CO2 blasting on the fracturing effect of the rock at different points in time. This is expressed by the expression of the function as follows: p1(t)=p1e(−γt/t0)sin(4πt/(t+t0))

where: p 1Peak pressure of fracturing, MPa;

tTime, s

t 0fracturing process time, 20 ms;

γdecay rate takes the value of 0.28 according to the fracturing decay rate.

In order to reduce the interference of the stress waves generated by the blasting process on the blasting simulation results and to reduce the impact of boundary reflections on the simulation results, low-reflection boundary conditions are set at the perimeter boundaries of the model, which makes the simulation closer to the actual situation. Meanwhile, in order to improve the computational efficiency and accuracy of the model, the grid size in the model was optimally adjusted, especially in the area near the fracturing holes, a finer mesh division was used, and this treatment aimed to improve the model’s ability to capture the details of the blasting area. Furthermore, the pressure equation obtained from fitting the liquid carbon dioxide blasting to fracture the coal body is applied as an energy equation at the fracturing hole for the numerical simulation of blasting.

In the solution process of the model, the triangular face mesh in the finite element method is used to construct the solution domain through the sectional sweep method. In order to improve the operability of the model and the accuracy of the simulation, special emphasis is placed on the refinement of local grid cells in the model and the chamfering of sharp corners in the model before simulation calculations are carried out, so as to avoid the appearance of inverted cells near the boundaries that are beyond the range of the solution domain, which may have an impact on the accuracy and convergence of the model solution. A total of 5115 cells are generated in the coal body region, as shown in Figure .

Displacement Results and Analysis

Numerical simulation was carried out based on the similar model test; the whole simulation process was divided into three sequences: hydraulic slit at the lower position of the coal body, then phase change fracturing at the right hole, and then finally the left hole fracturing, and the final stabilized y-direction displacement change of the different processes is shown in Figure .

Schematic diagram after displacement stabilization for different conditions. (a) Schematic diagram of displacement in the y-direction after hydraulic slitting. (b) Schematic of y-direction displacement after fracturing in the right hole. (c) Schematic of y-direction displacement after fracturing in the left hole.

After the hydraulic horizontal cutting seam stabilized, the upper part of the unloading free face coal body had an obvious downward displacement with a maximum displacement of 7.29 cm, and the lower part of the unloading free face had an upward displacement with a displacement value of 0.59 cm due to the stress concentration on the two sides of the unloading free face and the extrusion of the lower part of the unloading free face coal body. After the right-hole fracturing was stabilized, Coal body displacement above the unloading free face further increased to 8.29 cm, with significantly higher displacement values directly below the fracture holes and generally around the right fracture hole compared to the hydraulic horizontal cutoff, and the maximum was 1 cm larger than that of the hydraulic horizontal cut, with an increase of 13.71% in the displacement value. After the left-hole fracturing was stabilized, the overall displacement was larger than that observed just after the right-hole fracturing, with an increase of 0.84%, the difference between the two displacements was not a large value, and the displacement value was larger than that after the hydraulic horizontal cut alone, with an increase of 1.07 cm and a total increase of 14.68%. The overall y-direction displacement of the coal body after hydraulic horizontal slitting shows a downward displacement; the displacement after right-hole fracturing further increases based on the basis of the displacement after hydraulic horizontal slitting; and the displacement value after left-hole fracturing is even larger, building upon the previous two steps. This illustrates that the effect of hydraulic horizontal slitting on the coal body is significant, the displacement value is relatively larger after combined CO2 phase change fracturing, and the synergistic effect of the two technological methods on the displacement of the coal body is significant.

Crack Fractal Dimension Analysis

The model will be damaged after cuts and explosions, and the distribution of the surface crack field has significant self-similarity, so the crack fractal dimension can be utilized to quantitatively respond to the degree of crack development in the model. Box dimension is one of the methods commonly used to characterize cracks, which is based on the principle of taking a square box with side lengths r k to cover the cracks, calculating the number of its boxes N k , and, as the box side length converges to 0, the fractal dimension D can be obtained, which is calculated by the following formula: D=limrk→0⁡lgNk(rk)lgrk

First, the scattered spots in the coal and rock layers were processed and then imported into MATLAB software for picture graying, binarization, and other methods to calculate the box dimension of the cracks on the surface of the model, and finally, the box dimension curve was fitted. The fractal dimension of cracks in cut and blasted coal seams are all larger than that of the top plate; the fractal dimension of coal seams ranges from 1.138 to 1.237, while the fractal dimension of the roof muddy siltstone ranges from 0.875 to 0.974. As can be seen from Figure , under the synergistic effect of hydraulic horizontal slitting and CO2 phase change fracturing, the degree of crack development in the coal seam is more pronounced than that in the overlying rock layer. After the detonation of the right fracture hole, there is a further enhancement of the crack development of both the top plate and the coal seam, whereas the left fracture hole has little effect on the crack extension due to the large amount of energy that leaks out of the fracture holes and fissures during detonation.

Potential Engineering Applications and Discussions

Hydraulic horizontal slotting combined with CO2-assisted fracturing demonstrates significant advantages in depressurizing and enhancing the permeability for high-gas, low-permeability coal seams. This technology overcomes the limitations of traditional fracturing methods, which are centered on drilling holes and have a restricted fracturing range. It establishes a novel, environmentally friendly, and highly stable approach for large-scale coal body fracturing, offering significant engineering value for enhancing coalbed methane extraction efficiency.

This study systematically verified the positive effect of the synergistic action between hydraulic fracturing and CO2 blasting on coal seam pressure relief and permeability enhancement through similarity simulation and numerical simulation experiments, demonstrating that this technology possesses excellent engineering application potential. The main process of this technology is “drillinghorizontal slitliquid CO2 blasting synergistic decompression.” The specific process is as follows:1)Before the construction, connect the components of high-pressure water jet horizontal cutting device in sequence, construct to the predetermined position through drilling holes, and drill fracturing holes according to the parameters.2)The cutting parameters are determined according to the parameters of the coal seam and the drilling holes, and the high-pressure water jet horizontal cutting device is activated to carry out the cutting operation, and the horizontal space on both sides of the drilling holes increases continuously as the working face advances. After a period of time, the horizontal space generated by the slit and the coal body’s own stress work together to produce cracks, fissures and even collapse of the upper coal rock body.3)in the blaster with a high-pressure pump filled with liquid CO2, in the blasting holes one by one inserted into the gun tube, and connect the gun tube and low-pressure initiator wiring between the blasting tube end of the detonation head connected to the detonation current, the activation of the activator within the low-pressure fuse triggered by a rapid reaction, so that the liquid CO2 inside the tube is rapidly converted to gaseous, the coal body to produce perturbation, and finally achieve a wide range of coal decompression permeability of the effect of increased.

From an economic perspective, hydraulic horizontal slotting technology enables “slotting instead of coal mine tunnel”, completely eliminating the construction costs of bottom drainage drifts while significantly reducing the number of drill holes required. It is projected to reduce the number of boreholes by approximately 75% and lower drilling costs by 50%, significantly saving on construction investment. Additionally, CO2 is widely available, with a material cost of approximately 2–4 yuan per kilogram, making it inexpensive and further reducing project costs. The CO2 phase-change fracturing process is an endothermic reaction that produces no open flames or intense shock waves, significantly reducing the risk of gas explosions. It is suitable for hazardous mining environments, such as high-gas coal seams.

The combined application of these two methods in gas control represents a promising approach, facilitating the advancement of coal seam pressure reduction and permeability enhancement technology under challenging conditions, such as deep strata, high geological stress, high gas content, and low-permeability coal seams. The theoretical basis provided by this technology enhances the feasibility of large-scale pressure relief and permeability enhancement at coal mine sites. However, as specialized equipment for on-site applications is currently unavailable, further device development and field testing are required.

Conclusions

In this paper, we propose a method of hydraulic horizontal slit-CO2 blasting synergistic fracturing of coal seams , establish a similar model, use a high-speed camera and digital image correlation technology (3D-DIC) to monitor crack extension on the model surface, and analyze the characteristics of displacement field distribution on the model surface with numerical simulation; the fracturing effectiveness of coal seams was further validated using fractal dimension analysis. The main research results are as follows:(1)There is a potential connection between the modeled displacement concentration area and the fracture development, and the displacement concentration phenomenon often exists in the fracture development area. At the upper end of the slit, the y-axis displacement value gradually decreases from the middle of the two fracturing holes to both sides, and the displacement value at the lower end of the slit does not change much.(2)The overall fracture is “trapezoidal” diffraction, the hydraulic horizontal cut is mainly effective in fracturing around the cut, on both sides of the coal seam and between the roof and the coal seam, and the CO2 phase change fracturing has an obvious effect on the crack initiation of internal cracks in the coal seam.(3)The delamination fissures between the coal seam and the roof plate will weaken the vertical stress on the coal seam and inhibit the emergence and expansion of radial cracks, while the fracturing holes and the decompression free surface have a guiding effect on the crack expansion, which results in the cracks of the coal body mainly appearing on both sides of the fracturing holes and below after blasting. The fractal dimension of the cracks on the surface of the coal seam increased from 1.138 to 1.237 after the slit, an increase of 8.70%.(4)The interaction between the slit technology and the explosion technology not only provides the necessary spatial conditions for the overall displacement of the overlying coal and rock seams, but also significantly promotes the formation, expansion and permeability of cracks in the coal body. This synergistic effect makes the internal structure of the coal seam change and the number of cracks increase significantly, which in turn dramatically improves the permeability of the coal seam.

Neffy: A latest needle-free epinephrine
Authors: Konica Chittoria, Arun Mukesh, Ankur Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Anaphylaxis is a rapid-onset, potentially fatal allergic reaction requiring prompt administration of epinephrine. Until recently, epinephrine autoinjectors have dominated emergency treatment, but challenges, such as needle phobia, improper use, and poor carriage rates, reduce their real-world efficacy. Neffy (ARS-1; epinephrine nasal spray) introduces a needle-free, intranasal route for epinephrine delivery. This review examines the clinical evidence, pharmacological profile, indications, and safety data for Neffy and discusses implications for clinical practice.

Introduction

Anaphylaxis is a severe, systemic hypersensitivity reaction that demands fast intervention to prevent morbidity and mortality. Epinephrine, administered intramuscularly via an autoinjector, is considered the first-line therapy.[1] Despite its proven efficacy, studies indicate underutilization of epinephrine autoinjectors (EAIs) due to needle-related anxieties, device availability, and improper technique.[23] There is a pressing need for home-use, user-friendly, needle-free alternatives to improve treatment timeliness and accessibility. In this context, Neffy, an intranasal epinephrine formulation, was developed and has garnered US FDA approval for emergency use in anaphylaxis.[45]

Overview of Neffy (Epinephrine Nasal Spray)

Neffy comprises three main components: epinephrine (the active agent), dodecyl maltoside (Intravail A3, an absorption enhancer), and a unit-dose nasal spray device.[513] Intravail is an alkyl saccharide, also called DDM, used for increasing bioavailability and absorption of drugs, such as epinephrine. This agent helps lower the dose of the active ingredient, thereby producing equivalent efficacy and reducing the risk of overdosing.

Neffy is US FDA approved in 2024 for the emergency treatment of type I allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, in adults and children weighing at least 30 kg.[14] Each Neffy device contains a single, ready-to-use 2 mg intranasal dose; a second dose may be administered after 5 minutes if symptoms persist [Figure 1].[5]

Mechanism of Action

Neffy, the intranasal epinephrine spray, is absorbed through the nasal capillaries into the systemic circulation. The special characteristic of epinephrine is differential activation of receptors based on its route of administration. When administered via the intranasal route, the skeletal muscle received only 15%–20% of the total dose.[17] The β1 receptor has a relatively higher affinity than other adrenergic receptors. It activates alpha- and beta-adrenergic receptors, resulting in peripheral vasoconstriction, bronchodilation, and the inhibition of mediator release from mast cells.[15]

Indications and Dosage

It is indicated for the treatment of anaphylaxis in adults and children ≥30 kg with a dose of 2 mg (one spray, intranasal). If there is an inadequate response, it may be repeated after 5 minutes in the opposite nostril.

Practical Considerations and Future Directions

Neffy expands emergency anaphylaxis management options, especially for patients with needle phobia, poor dexterity, or historical reluctance to use self-injectors. Widespread education and access programs are required for optimal uptake. Post-marketing surveillance and registry data are needed to address long-term safety and effectiveness, especially in pediatric and high-risk populations.

Conclusion

Neffy offers a promising needle-free epinephrine delivery system, with clinical studies suggesting comparable efficacy and safety to standard EAI products. Its rapid absorption and pharmacodynamic effects, along with increased patient acceptability and usability, may mark a paradigm shift in the management of anaphylaxis. However, further research is required to substantiate clinical outcomes in real-world emergencies and to assess long-term population benefits.

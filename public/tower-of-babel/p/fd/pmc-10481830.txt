Uptake of human papillomavirus vaccination and its associated factors among adolescents in Gambella town, Southwest, Ethiopia: a community-based cross-sectional study
Authors: Eyob Getachew Woldehawaryat, Alehegn Bishaw Geremew, Desale Bihonegn Asmamaw
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the uptake of human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination and its associated factors among adolescents in Gambella town, Southwest Ethiopia.DesignA community-based cross-sectional study.SettingGambella town, Southwest Ethiopia.ParticipantsA total of 882 adolescents were included between May and July 2022.OutcomeUptake of HPV vaccination.MethodsA cluster sampling technique was used to select study participants. An interviewer-administered and pretested questionnaire was used to collect the data. The collected data were entered into Epi-data V.4.6 and exported to STATA V.16 for cleaning, coding and analysis. A binary logistic regression model was used to test the association between independent and dependent variables. Variables with a p<0.05 in the multivariable logistic regression were considered statistically significant factors for the uptake of HPV vaccination.ResultsA total of 825 adolescents participated in this study. Among participants, 48% (95% CI 44.6% to 51.4%) have been vaccinated for HPV. Ever heard of the HPV vaccine (AOR 2.5, 95% CI 1.23 to 5.08), good knowledge of HPV infection (AOR 3.62, 95% CI 2.17 to 6.02), good knowledge of the HPV vaccine (AOR 5.54, 95% CI 3.28 to 9.36) and attitude towards the HPV vaccine (AOR 2.74, 95% CI 1.88 to 3.98) were significantly associated HPV vaccination.ConclusionIn the current study, the uptake of HPV vaccination was found to be low. The finding highlights the importance of promoting community health education about HPV infection and vaccines and providing behaviour change education, which plays a substantial role in promoting the uptake of HPV vaccination.Keywords: COVID-19, antenatal, anaemia, epidemiology

Introduction

Cervical cancer is the fourth most commonly diagnosed cancer and death in women globally, with an estimated 604 000 women diagnosed with cervical cancer in 2020, 342 000 dying from it.1 Cervical cancer was predicted to have a global number of new cases of 13.1 per 100 000 women, which varied by country, with the lowest in Egypt and the highest in Swaziland (from less than 2–75 per 100 000 women).2 In sub-Saharan Africa, the incidence and death of cervical cancer were 34.8 and 22.5 per 100 000 women annually, respectively.3

According to WHO estimate, cervical cancer affects 42.7 cases per 100 000 women in East Africa and kills 35 per 100 000 women.4 Cervical cancer is the second most frequent malignancy in Ethiopia, 20.9 million women are at risk of acquiring cervical cancer, with an estimated 4648 new cases and 3235 deaths every year.5 In Ethiopia, the incidence, fatality and proportion of cervical cancer were 17.3%, 16.5% and 18.20%, respectively.6

Cervical cancer is one of the preventable forms of cancer through the prevention of human papillomavirus (HPV) infection.2 This can be achieved by the highly effective and cost-effective prevention strategy using the HPV vaccine, which prevents the most common strains of high-risk and low-risk HPV infection.7 8 As of 2020, more than half of the WHO member countries including Ethiopia have introduced HPV vaccination programmes to meet the 2030 Sustainable Development Goal elimination target of 90%.9 10 After up to 8 years of follow-up after vaccination, studies reported 83% reduction in HPV 16 and 18 infections among adolescents aged 13–19 years. HPV vaccination protects over 70% of invasive cervical cancer in women around the world.10 11

According to world statistics of HPV vaccine coverage 33.6% of females aged 10–20 years who obtained the complete course of vaccine were from industrialised nations, whereas only 2.7% of females from poor countries received the vaccine.12 Ethiopia has also recently introduced the HPV vaccine with help of the Global Alliance for Vaccine and Immunisation in 2018 at ages 13 and 14 years.4 National guideline recommend two doses of HPV vaccine 6-month interval of first dose and the vaccine is presently mainly provided through a school-based strategy.13 Ethiopia has immunised over 2 million girls against HPV since 2018.14 Place of residence, hearing about the HPV vaccine, availability of awareness creation, favourable attitude, mother education, knowledge about HPV vaccination and receiving childhood immunisation are some of the identified factors that can affect HPV vaccination.15–17 Maternal education and wealth index were also the strongest predictors of adolescents taking the HPV vaccine.18 Lack of parental awareness can result in vaccine refusal and as a result, adolescent girls have expressed their reluctance to vaccinate without parental consent. Parents of adolescent girls need to be aware of HPV, how it is transmitted and the efficacy of the HPV vaccine in preventing cervical cancer.19 Moreover, attending school was one of the strongest predictors of the HPV vaccine. If adolescents do not attend school, they may have poor awareness and attitudes towards the vaccine, which may result in vaccine refusal.19

Although few studies have been conducted in Ethiopia, almost all of them were conducted in institution-based settings in which those adolescents considerably have better knowledge about healthy practices. In the Gambella region, polygamy, levirate marriage, STIs (Sexually Transmited Diseases) and HIV/AIDS are very common; moreover, in this region, the vaccination programme is available via school, health institutions and community. However, the populations are lack of aware on the availability of the vaccine and location.20 Therefore, these conditions are creating opportunities for high HPV infection rates. Additionally, in Ethiopia particularly in developing region like Gambella, the vaccine is newly introduced and studies done to determine the vaccine uptake level and its associated factors are very limited. Therefore, this study might be used to identify barriers and factors affecting the uptake of the HPV vaccine which might be used as basic information for further interventions, moreover, this study aimed at providing important information for policy-makers and programme implementers at national as well as regional levels, and the information derived from the study considered it would benefit the adolescent by providing directions or location on service provision. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the uptake of papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination and its associated factors among adolescents.

Methods

Study setting, design and period

A community-based cross-sectional study design was conducted from May to July 2022 in Gambella town, Southwest Ethiopia. Gambella town is the capital city of the Gambella region, which is located 768 km from the capital city of Ethiopia, Addis Ababa. There are five kebeles in Gambella town with an estimated total population of 65 396 in 2022, of which total female adolescents aged 14–19 years old were estimated at around 3040. There are two health centres, one general hospital and one primary hospital, and seven private clinics in Gambella town. HPV vaccination started in December 2018 and the vaccine has been delivered through school based, health facilities and community based.21

Study participants

All adolescents aged 14–19 years who were living in selected kebeles in Gambella town during the data collection period in Gambella town, south-west Ethiopia were included in this study. All adolescents’ aged 14–19 years who lived in selected kebeles in Gambella town who were critically ill and unable to respond were excluded from this study.

Sample size determination and sampling procedures

The sample size was calculated using a single population proportion formula, considering the following statistical assumptions: margin of error 4% (0.04), Z-value 1.96 corresponding to 95% confidence level, 10% non-response, design effect of 1.5 and the magnitude of HPV uptake was 66.5%, which gave a final sample size of 882.22 To select study participants, a cluster sampling technique was used, about 825 participants selected out of the total on 3040 female adolescents in Gambella. There are two clusters in each kebeles (10 clusters). From each kebele, we selected one cluster using a simple random sampling technique.

Measurements

In the current study, adolescents who had received at least one dose HPV vaccine were considered as uptake of HPV vaccine.16 Good knowledge of HPV infection is explained as having good knowledge if adolescents score four points and above from eight questions.16 Good knowledge of HPV vaccination is explained as having good knowledge if adolescents score three points and above from six questions.16 Furthermore, a positive attitude towards HPV vaccination was determined by using 11 attitude-related questions with a five-point Likert scale and participants with scores above the mean were classified as having a ‘positive attitude’ towards HPV vaccinations.22

A 28 data items on household selected assets, access to water and toilet used to generate wealth status of the household and the household wealth index status was done by using principal component analysis. All continuous and categorical variables were classified between ‘0’ and ‘1’ according to the quantity for the continuous variables. All statistical assumptions of factor analysis were done such as Keiser-Meyer-Olkin and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. Any variable with less than a 0.5 communality value was removed from the analysis and the analysis was done repeatedly until all variables met the inclusion criteria for factor analysis. Next, all eligible factor scores were computed using a regression-based method to generate one variable, wealth index. The final scores were ranked in five quantiles as first, second, third, fourth and fifth. Finally, ranks were coded as richest, rich, middle, poorer and poorest, respectively.23 24

Data collection tools and quality control

Data were collected using an interviewer-administered structured questionnaire. The questionnaires were originally developed in English and then translated into Amharic. Later on translated back to English to ensure its consistency. The data were collected by interviewing the adolescents face to face after getting parents and the participants informed verbal consent using pretested structured questionnaires. The data were collected by four female diploma nurses and one female degree-holder nurse supervisor. One day of training was given on techniques of data collection before the actual data collection. The questionnaire was pretested 5% (44) of the study participants with the same sociodemographic characteristics in Etang town and necessary modifications were made according to the pretest results.

The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient in the pilot study was α=0.77, α=0.81 and α=0.79 for knowledge of HPV infection, knowledge of HPV vaccination and attitude, respectively. This indicated that the tools for measuring knowledge of HPV infection, knowledge of HPV vaccination and attitude were highly reliable. With regard to validity, the instrument used was the same one used in the previous study done in local areas.

Data processing and analysis

The collected data were entered into EPI-data V.4.6 software and exported to STATA V.16 for cleaning, coding and further analysis. Descriptive statistics were done using frequencies, percentages, mean and SD and presented using text, tables and figures. Normality assumption was checked using kurtosis and skewness.

Model fitness was checked using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness of fit test (Prob>χ2=0.38). The multicolinearity test done to see the correlation between independent variables using the variance inflation factor (VIF) and minimum of VIF 1.05, maximum of VIF 3.02 and mean of VIF 1.80 was found. This indicated that no multicolinearity between each independent variable.

The binary logistic regression analysis was used. Variables with a p value of 0.25 in the bivariable logistic regression were considered in the multivariable model. Variables with a p≤0.05 were considered as statistically associated with the outcome variable. AOR (Adjusted Odds Ratio) with its corresponding 95% CI was used to examine the strength and direction of the association.

Patient and public involvement statement

Adolescents were included in this study by providing valuable information. Nevertheless, they have never been involved in the study design, protocol, data collection tools, and reporting and disseminating the finding.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics of the respondents

A total of 825 study participants from 3040 female adolescents aged 14–19 years, with a response rate of 93.5%, were included in this study. The mean age of participants was 16.64 (±1.49 years). Regarding the education of the respondents, 377 (45.70%) had primary education, and 311 (37.7%) were orthodox followers. Nearly one-third (30.3%) of respondent mothers had no formal education, and 334 (41.8%) mothers’ occupations were housewife (table 1).

Uptake of HPV vaccination among adolescents

Over all magnitude of respondents who have received at least one dose of HPV vaccine was 48% (95% CI 44.6% to 51.4). Of which 286 (34.67%) of them received one dose and 110 (13.33%) received a second dose of HPV vaccine. The main reason mentioned by the participants for not taking HPV vaccine was they did not know where to get 145 (33.8%) and worried about the vaccine side effects of 89 (20.74%) (figure 1).

Factors associated with uptake of HPV vaccination

In the multivariable logistic regression analysis, the results showed that knowledge of HPV infection, knowledge of the HPV vaccine, ever heard of the HPV vaccine and attitude towards the HPV vaccine were significantly associated with the uptake of HPV vaccination at a p<0.05 with 95%CI. Accordingly, the odds of taking the HPV vaccine among adolescents who heard about the HPV vaccine were 2.5 times higher compared with adolescents who didn’t hear about the HPV vaccine (AOR 2.5, 95% CI 1.23 to 5.08). The odds of HPV vaccination among adolescents who had good knowledge of HPV infection were 3.6 times higher compared with adolescents who had poor knowledge of HPV infection (AOR 3.62, 95% CI 2.17 to 6.02). Similarly, the odds of HPV vaccine uptake were 5.5 times higher among adolescents who had good knowledge of the HPV vaccine than those who had poor knowledge of the HPV vaccine (AOR 5.54, 95% CI 3.28 to 9.36). Likewise, the odds of HPV vaccine uptake were 2.74 times higher among adolescents who had a positive attitude towards the HPV vaccine than those who had a negative attitude towards the HPV vaccine (AOR 2.74, 95% CI 1.88, 3.98) (table 3).

Discussion

This study was conducted to assess the uptake of the HPV vaccine and associated factors among adolescents in Gambella town, southwest Ethiopia. The current study showed that nearly half of the respondents received at least one dose of the HPV vaccine, which is consistent with the previous study done in Malaysia (51.4%).25 However, our finding is lower than several studies conducted in different countries, such as Debre Birhan, Ethiopia, 66.5%,17 South Africa (75%),26 Brazil (83.5%).27 This discrepancy could be due to the difference in sociodemographic characteristics such as education, the majority of study participants of mothers in Brazil and South Africa had secondary education and above whereas in the current study, more than half of the study participants of mothers had no secondary education and above.27 Study population, study areas and time of launching the HPV vaccine variations may result in this discrepancy. For example, the current study is done in Gambella town, which is located in the developing regions of the Ethiopia, where adolescents are inaccessible to healthcare services such as youth friendly services.28 29 Besides, there are also sociocultural barriers on the utilisation of healthcare services particularly adolescent healthcare services in the developing community compared with the other parts of Ethiopia. The current study was also community-based whereas the previous studies were institutional-based studies. In addition, studies done in Brazil and South Africa were developed nations, which means all of the populations aware of the HPV vaccine.

In addition, the findings of this study are lower than the previous studies conducted in North Shoa, Amhara region, Ethiopia (66.5%).22 The possible explanation might be the difference in the study setting, in which the former studies were based at the facility that recruited adolescents who came to the school. These populations are usually considered to have better health literacy and opportunities to obtain information about vaccines.30 However, the uptake of HPV vaccination in this study is higher than study conducted in Ethiopia (44.4%),31 Uganda (22%),32 China (33.5%).33 The variation might be due to the difference in the study area and the time gap. For instance, the current study was conducted only in urban areas, whereas the previous studies included rural areas. Regarding the time gap, the HPV vaccination programme has been expanded and population awareness has improved over time. In addition, the current study was conducted as a small-scale survey as compared with the previous study done in Uganda, which was the DHS, which is a nationally representative survey and included both rural and urban areas.32

This finding revealed that adolescents who had good knowledge about HPV infection were positively associated with HPV vaccination uptake. This study is supported by the study conducted in Brazil.26 The possible justification is that someone is aware of the disease severity, mode of transmission and prevention of HPV infection and helps to encourage people to receive the HPV vaccine.

This study also indicates that having ever heard about the HPV vaccine is a more statistically significant association with the uptake of HPV vaccination. This finding is consistent with the previous studies done in Ethiopia,31 Malaysia22 and the USA.34 The possible explanation might be that someone hearing about the HPV vaccine helps to enhance the vaccine’s benefits, enables a good attitude towards the vaccine and encourages adolescents to take the vaccine.31

Likewise, adolescents who had good knowledge of HPV vaccines had higher odds of HPV vaccination uptake. This is consistent with studies done in Ethiopia.35 This study is also in line with the previous studies in the USA,36 Brazil26 and Nigeria.32 This could be justified by the fact that adolescents would be more likely to receive HPV vaccination if they were aware of the benefits of the HPV vaccine.36

Similarly, adolescents who had a positive attitude towards HPV vaccination had a higher uptake of HPV vaccination. The finding is congruent with previous evidence from Brazil,26 the USA,37 Nigeria38 and Uganda.39 This might be because the uptake of HPV vaccination among adolescents is largely driven by their attitudes and beliefs regarding vaccination effectiveness, safety perception benefit, and ease of access as proximal factors.16

In this study, attending school, parental education and wealth index were not significantly associated with the uptake of HPV vaccination. This might suggest that attending school, parental education and wealth index have no effect on the uptake of HPV vaccination, or it may appear that HPV vaccination uptake does not vary by these variables. The non-association needs further investigation. However, a study in Gondar, Ethiopia23 revealed that HPV vaccination uptake was associated with the wealth index. The study conducted in Arba Minch town, Southern Ethiopia15 found that parental education was significantly associated with HPV vaccination uptake. This finding is similar to that of Debre Markos town, Ethiopia13 and Brazil.27

Limitation of the study

Adolescents may not recall when the vaccines were administered and how long it took between doses since vaccination data were collected retrospectively. Whenever possible, the vaccination card was checked to reduce recall bias. The cross-sectional nature of the survey does not show the causal relationship between outcome and independent variables.

Conclusion

In the current study, the uptake of HPV vaccination was found to be low. Ever heard about the HPV vaccine, knowledge of HPV infection, knowledge of the HPV vaccine and attitude towards the HPV vaccine were significantly associated with uptake of the HPV vaccine. The finding highlights the importance of promoting community health education about HPV infection and vaccines and providing behaviour change education, which plays a substantial role in promoting the uptake of HPV vaccination. Moreover, the concerned bodies should provide educational efforts to improve community awareness of HPV vaccines, focusing on access to vaccine locations.

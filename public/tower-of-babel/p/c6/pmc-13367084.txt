Partial root-zone drying enhances antioxidant signaling and physiological resilience in psyllium (Plantago ovata) under arid conditions
Authors: Rashid Iqbal, Salman Ahmad, Khaqan Suleiman, Ghulam Murtaza, Sajid Ullah, Wiwiek Harsonowati, Dilfuza Jabborova, Sidra Ahmad, Allah Ditta, Mohamed Taha Yassin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDrought-induced reduction in crop yield is becoming a serious threat worldwide, especially in arid to semiarid regions. The introduction of medicinal plants into cropping patterns in arid regions is a suitable alternative to conventional crops because of their high demand and market value. Keeping in view the situation, an experiment was planned under natural arid conditions to screen out the best psyllium varieties in less time for cultivation in arid regions. The experiment included three psyllium varieties (V1 = Gujrat isabgol-2, V2 = Vallabh isabgol and V3 = Jawahar isabgol-4) and three irrigation regimes (control = CK, partial rootzone drying = PRD and deficit irrigation = DI) and was carried out in a completely randomized design with eight replications. The results of our experiment revealed that although cell stress indicators (electrolyte leakage, relative cell injury, methylglyoxal, malondialdehyde, and hydrogen peroxide contents) were less and photosynthesis, water relationships, and membrane stability were greater in the control irrigation, the activities of enzymatic and nonenzymatic antioxidants were greater in the PRD and DI regimes. Among the water deficit treatments, more water potential, proline contents, ascorbic acid contents, membrane stability index, and yield were recorded in the PRD than in the DI. Among the varieties, V3 performed the best under water deficit conditions, whereas variety V2 presented the lowest values. It was concluded that under less water availability, the combination of the PRD with the variety V3 optimized physiological and yield outcomes by sustained higher relative water contents (77.10%), mesophyll conductance (0.67 mol CO2 m−2 s−1 bar−1), photosynthesis (15.10 µmol of CO2 m−2s−1), and membrane stability index (78.80%). Consequently, maximized water use efficiency (0.21 g L−1) and antioxidant activities, leading to higher yield (0.76 g plant−1). Future research should focus on integrating PRD with other water management techniques like mulching, osmoprotectants spray to enhance its efficacy under water deficit conditions.Keywords: Antioxidants, drought, plant water relationships, membrane stability index, water use efficiency

Introduction

The use of medicinal plants is multipurpose. They can be used as herbs, spices and medicines. 1 Medicinal plants have been used in medicines for the last 5000 y in the Indian, Egyptian, Romanian, and Persian systems. 2 Over the past decade, medicinal plants have gained worldwide attention because of the trend of using organic healthcare treatment rather than the allopathy. This is due to minimum side effects of organic products, despite of their regular use. 3 Therefore, the medicinal plant industry has become a billion-dollar industry worldwide, and its scope is increasing daily. The WHO also confirms that the demand and use of medicinal plants are increasing in developing countries, where almost 80% of the population uses them as sources of food and medicine. Moreover, approximately 40% of modern pharmaceuticals are derived from traditionally grown medicinal plants. According to international trade center (ITC) data from 2024, the global export value of medicinal plants has markedly increased from $2.04 billion to $4.18 billion (97.8% increase) between 2010 and 2013. 4 Therefore, increasing the production of medicinal plants not only fulfils increasing demand but also results in greater economic returns than traditional crops.

Among a number of abiotic stresses, drought is the most challenging and damaging. 5-7 The adverse effects of drought start from seed sowing until the plants mature. Drought disturbs germination by improper imbibition, subsequently lowering stem and leaf growth by reducing cell turgor and leaf relative water contents, while inducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production that drives cell membrane damage and electrolyte leakage. 8 During the reproductive stage, drought stress causes pollen sterility, results in fewer fruiting bodies, disturbs assimilate partitioning, decreases seed weight, yield, and quality of produce. 9

Recent climate change has increased the frequency and intensity of global droughts, threatening food security and agro-based economies of countries. 10 These drought-based threats are more significant in arid to semiarid areas, which already face shortages of water for crop production. 11 Farmers in these regions barely subsist due to limited resource availability, which results in lower crop yields and living standards. 12 Therefore, the cultivation of drought-tolerant medicinal plants in these areas is a suitable alternative to less profitable traditional growing crops, helping subsistence farmers in these regions to improve their living standards. 13

The selection and cultivation of stress-tolerant varieties in challenging environments is one of the best options. These varieties perform better in terms of yield than sensitive varieties do. However, the development and selection of a stress-tolerant variety via conventional methods is a time-consuming process and requires years of research and experimentation. 14 Another drawback is that most conventional breeding techniques rely on morphological and yield traits. Given the rapid pace of climate change, methods that provide rapid and authentic results are needed. The selection of varieties on a physiological basis is a more reliable, authentic and time-saving approach. 15 Many scientists have reported that screening/selecting varieties on physiological and biochemical bases in a lab/wire house is an effective alternative to conventional selection methods. Varieties performed under laboratory/wire house conditions also perform better under field conditions. 16-19

Psyllium is an important medicinal plant. It is native to the Indo–Pak region, where it has been growing for centuries in desert belts, as well as in irrigated areas. It is easily grown on lighter sandy soils with less fertility. It also has proven drought tolerance abilities because of its small height, small leaves, and deep root system; hence, it easily grows in water-scarce areas. 20 It is used in natural medicine, the pharmaceutical industry, veterinary medicine, the food industry, and waste treatment. 21 The introduction of psyllium to the cropping pattern of desert/arid agriculture may provide a better economic alternative because of its worldwide demand and successful growth in drought-prone areas.

The management of irrigation is a prerequisite for successful crop production, especially in water-scarce areas. 22 Various techniques, such as osmoprotectants and micro-irrigation (drip irrigation), are being adopted to minimize the detrimental effects of drought, their high cost and site-specific requirements limit widespread adoption by resource-poor farmers. 23 , 24 Therefore, in the present study, we used simple and economic irrigation techniques that do not require specific protocols.

One technique is deficit irrigation, in which relatively less water than normal irrigation is applied to the entire plant's root zone at every irrigation. 25 The other is the advanced form of water deficit, known as partial rootzone drying (PRD). In PRD, less water was applied to a specific portion of the root zone in an alternate way. One portion of the roots was watered, and at the same time, the remaining portion of the roots remained dry. For the next irrigation, the wet portion was allowed to dry, and the dry portion was watered. This process continues in a cyclic way. 26 The theory behind these techniques involves the production of root and leaf-derived abscisic acid (ABA), which causes partial stomatal closure to minimize water loss and further activating the plant's defense system by activating enzymatic and nonenzymatic antioxidants. In practice, it improves water use efficiency, soil moisture content, and overall plant growth. 19 The major drawback of DI is that a specific portion of roots always remains dry, and long-term dryness may inhibit normal root function. 27 In the PRD, alternating wetting and drying of the root zone maintains normal root functions and maintains a balance between water transport and ABA production. Both techniques have proven effective in a number of crop plants. 28

To the best of our knowledge, it is the first study to investigate the physiological and biochemical-based selection of psyllium varieties under PRD and DI conditions. Most of the work has focused on screening psyllium varieties on the basis of morphological characteristics 29 that rely mostly on growth and yield attributes, with limited attention given to cell stress indicators, membrane stability, and antioxidant mechanisms. Therefore, there is a research gap in physicochemical-based screening of psyllium varieties under water-scarce conditions. Since crop responses to PRD are highly crop- and genotype-dependent, findings from other crops cannot be directly extrapolated to P. ovata. The study therefore provides new information regarding varietal variability in physiological efficiency, osmotic adjustment, and antioxidant defense mechanisms. Given the increasing scarcity of irrigation water in arid and semi-arid regions, identifying PRD-responsive P. ovata varieties has direct practical implications for sustainable cultivation and water-saving agriculture. The study therefore contributes applied novelty in addition to physiological insights. The primary objective of this study is to compare the effectiveness of PRD and DI techniques on the growth, physiological signaling, water relations, and yield of different psyllium varieties. By analyzing these physiological and biochemical responses under water deficit conditions, this research aims to identify the best-suited psyllium variety and optimize its water use efficiency. In PRD, the wet portion effectively supplies water to the plant, while dry portion moderately produced ABA which moves towards leaves and minimize water loss by partial stomatal closure. While in DI a specific portion of roots always receive very little water and hence produces a stronger stress signal for longer periods. That's why we hypothesized that PRD is superior over DI. Screening of varieties based on physiological and biochemical markers is more reliable than morphological traits, as these markers directly reflect internal stress response mechanisms. The success of this study will support scientists in advising drought-tolerant psyllium varieties, help farmers in decision-making regarding the selection of varieties and adoption of alternative watering techniques under low water availability. By optimizing yield and income for farmers in arid regions, this research directly aligns with the United Nations sustainable development goals, specifically SDG 1 (no poverty) and SDG 13 (climate action).

Materials and methods

The purpose of this study was to select a drought-tolerant psyllium variety on the basis of physiological and biochemical indicators under natural arid conditions. The experimental site has semiarid to arid environmental conditions characterized by long summers, high temperatures, sporadic rains, sandy soils, and a lack of canal irrigation water in most areas. Disinfected seeds of three different psyllium varieties (V1 = Gujrat isabgol-2, V2 = Vallabh isabgol and V3 = Jawahar isabgol-4) were obtained from the Arid Zone Research Institute (AZRI) Bahawalpur. Gujrat isabgol-2 is an old psyllium variety characterized by erect plant type, medium green leaves, a medium height, and moderate stress tolerance. Variety Vallabh is a relatively new psyllium variety. It has erected plants, dark green compact leaves, early maturity, and moderate stress tolerance. The variety Jawahar isabgol-4 is an early-maturing variety of psyllium with semierect plants and dark green narrow leaves. These varieties are mostly grown in psyllium growing areas. Under the scenarios of climate change, increasing temperature and drought stress, we screened these varieties on a physiological and biochemical basis under natural arid conditions rather than on the basis of morphological characteristics alone.

The study was conducted in a wire house from mid-October to the end of March. Wire house was situated in the research area of the Department of Agronomy, Faculty of Agriculture and Environment, Islamia University of Bahawalpur. The plants were grown under natural semiarid conditions. To simulate field conditions under natural arid conditions, plants in wire-house were exposed to ambient temperature, relative humidity, and solar radiation except for rainfall. Because our experiment focused on drought stress, we covered wire house with a waterproof sheet only during rainfall to minimize impact of precipitation on our irrigation treatments. The experiment was conducted in accordance with a completely randomized design with a factorial arrangement in the pots. The factors included three different varieties (V1 = Gujrat isabgol 2, V2 = Vallabh isabgol and V3 = Jawahar isabgol 4) and three irrigation methods, namely, control irrigation (CK), deficit irrigation (DI) and partial rootzone drying (PRD) irrigation. A total of seventy-two (72) pots were used, with eight replications and each repetition included nine pots. For the PRD treatment, special pots (length × width × depth 1 × 1 × 1 feet) with a plastic separator were used. In these pots, tap roots moved along the separator, and lateral roots were equally divided on both sides of the separator to induce alternate wetting and drying. Seeds were placed in a uniform geometry (plant × plant distance = 10 cm and row × row distance = 10 cm). To ensure proper roots and seedling establishment, plants were irrigated equally for 15 d after emergence from the soil. After that, irrigation treatments were started. In tap-rooted plants, the tap root is not divided into equal splits. Instead, the lateral roots were divided into splits. Lateral roots absorb water and provide water to the taproot, while the taproot functions as a transport pathway. Alternate wetting and drying in the PRD occurs in the lateral root zone, which directly experience alternate wetting and drying. In the PRD treatment, 40% field capacity (FC) was maintained by applying water to one side of the root zone in an alternate manner. In the DI treatment, 40% FC was maintained by applying water to the whole root zone. For the control irrigation, the soil moisture was maintained at 80% field capacity. Water was applied every 4 to 5 d in the pots to maintain the desired FC until the plants reached maturity. Time domain reflectometery (TDR) sensors (Campbell Scientific CS 640) were used to monitor the field capacity for irrigation scheduling.

Local field soil was used for the experiments. Before the pots were filled, the soil was sieved with a 2 mm mesh sieve to remove impurities. After that, the soil was placed between layers of polythene plastic sheets and allowed to dry under sunlight for one week. After that, the pots were filled with soil for sowing of seeds. Photosynthetic performance and gas exchange

Using a portable infrared gas analyzer (IR-400, Yokogawa, Japan), gas exchange characteristics and photosynthesis were determined in completely expanded leaves from 09:00 to 11:00 h under ambient light conditions (1200 µmol/m2/s PAR) to reduce diurnal variation. Oxidative stress biomarkers

Hydrogen peroxide (H 2 O 2 )

The concentration of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) was determined via the method described by Velikova et al. 37 The leaf sample (0.5 g) was ground with 5 mL of 0.1% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid (TCA), and the suspension was centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min to extract the supernatant. Subsequently, 0.5 mL of supernatant was mixed with 0.5 mL of 10 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0) and 1 mL of 1 M potassium iodide. The absorbance was recorded at 390 nm on a UV-visible spectrophotometer. The H2O2 concentration was calculated using an extinction coefficient of 0.28 µM−1 cm−1.

Malondialdehyde (MDA)

The MDA content was measured via the procedure outlined by Heath and Packer. 38 Leaf samples (0.3 g) were combined with 5 mL of 0.1 trichloroacetic acid (TCA), homogenized, and centrifuged. One milliliter of the supernatant was subjected to 20% trichloric acid and 0.5% thiobarbituric acid (TBA). The resulting mixture was heated at 95 °C for 30 min and centrifuged. The absorbance at 532 nm was determined, and the difference between this value and the absorbance at 600 nm was calculated to obtain the resultant absorbance Which was then transformed into MDA using an extinction coefficient of 155 mM−1 cm−1.

Methylglyoxal (MG)

In accordance with the methodology of Wild et al. 39 the methylglyoxal content in leaf tissues was determined via homogenization with 5% perchloric acid, neutralization with potassium carbonate, and centrifugation. The supernatant was then reacted with sodium dihydrogen phosphate and N-acetyl-L-cysteine, and its absorbance was measured at 288 nm. The MG content was determined via a standard curve prepared from known concentrations of methylglyoxal.

Dehydroascorbate (DHA)

In accordance with Law et al. 40 the quantity of DHA was measured by determining the total ascorbate (ascorbic acid + DHA) content by reducing the iron III ions (Fe3+ to Fe2+) with 2,2'-pyridyl, and the quantity of DHA was then calculated by finding the difference between total ascorbate and ascorbate measured after the addition of dithiothreitol treatment, using a UV-visible spectrophotometer at 265 nm.

Abscisic acid (ABA) content

ABA extraction and determination were performed as described by Verslues and Bray. 41 To extract ABA from freeze-dried leaf tissues (0.1 g), the tissue was homogenized in a mixture of 80% (v/v) methanol and 100 ng of the internal standard [2H6]-ABA combined with 1% acetic acid. ABA was measured via a high-performance liquid chromatographic (HPLC) system with a UV detector (260 nm) running a C18 reverse-phase column (250 × 4.6 mm, 5-m particle size) with a 60:39:1 (v/v/v) proportion of methanol, water, and acetic acid as the mobile phase at a rate of 1.0 mL/min.

Osmoprotectants and compatible solvents

Proline content

Proline was extracted and quantified according to Bates et al. 42 Leaf tissue (0.5 g) was homogenized in sulfosalicylic acid (3%) and centrifuged, and the resulting mixture was reacted with acid ninhydrin and glacial acetic acid. The mixture was heated at 100 °C for 1 hour and then cooled and extracted with toluene. The absorbance was measured on a UV-visible spectrophotometer at 520 nm, and the concentration of proline was measured via a standard curve.

Total soluble sugars

Soluble sugars were estimated via the anthrone method. 43 Ethanol (80%) was used to extract leaf tissue (0.2 g), after which the mixture was centrifuged, and the supernatant was evaporated. The residue was dissolved in distilled water, and anthrone reagent (0.2% in concentrated H2SO4) was added. The mixture was subsequently heated at 100 °C for 10 min and cooled. A glucose standard curve was used to measure the absorbance of the solution at 620 nm.

Antioxidant defense system

Ascorbic acid (AsA)

Kampfenkel, Van Montagu, and Inze 44 explained the technique for determining the degree of ascorbate depletion. A frozen leaf sample was thawed to extract the leaf cell sap. Then, the leaf material aqueous extract was mixed with a solution containing 6% trichloroacetic acid (TCA), 2,2′-dipyridyl, and ferric chloride (FeCl3) as a coloring solution and incubated at 37 °C. The absorbance was recorded on a UV-visible spectrophotometer at 525 nm.

Glutathione peroxidase (GPX)

A modified procedure defined by Flohé and Günzler 45 was used to determine GPX activity. The reaction was performed in Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.6) with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, reduced glutathione (glutathione), hydrogen peroxide, and an enzyme extract. The reaction time was determined by the addition of TCA to the reaction mixture, after which the level of leftover glutathione was measured via the Ellman reagent (DTNB). GPX activity was measured as μmol of glutathione oxidized/min/mg protein.

Glutathione S-Transferase (GST)

GST activity was quantified according to the procedure described by Habig et al. 46 Potassium phosphate buffer at pH 6.5, CDNB (1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene), and reduced glutathione (GSH) were used to perform the GST assay with the crude enzyme extract. The absorbance was measured and observed to increase at 340 nm within three minutes. The absorbance value was obtained to determine the GST activity via the extinction coefficient (9.6 mM−1 cm−1) used in the test.

The activities of catalase (CAT), ascorbate peroxidase (APX), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and peroxidase (POD) were recorded following the methods of Vanacker et al. (2000), Nakano and Asada (1981), Beyer and Fridovich (1987), and Pandolfini et al. (1992), respectively.

Statistical analysis

Recorded data regarding various parameters were analyzed statistically by using STATISTIX (10.0) software following two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) technique, 47 considering the main effects of variety, irrigation, and variety × irrigation. Mean values were the average of eight replications. Least significant difference (LSD) test was used at 5% (p ≤ 0.05) probability level to identify the source of variation. Treatment means with different alphabets showed significant differences at the 5% probability level (p < 0.05). Graphs, multivariate analysis (radar charts, principal component analysis) and correlation analysis were performed using Origin 9.1. Multivariate analysis was used to show the relationship between different physiological and biochemical traits of psyllium varieties under different irrigation regimes.

Growth and water relations

The growth and water relations-related parameters were significantly affected by both variety and irrigation regime (Table 4). Among varieties, larger leaves were found in variety V2, while all other parameters (plant height, leaf relative water contents, water potential, osmotic potential, and turgor potential) were higher in V3 and least values were recorded in V2 under all irrigation regimes. While minimum plant height (9.90 cm), leaf relative water contents (66.88%), water potential (−0.96 MPa), osmotic potential (−0.89 MPa), and turgor potential (0.07 MPa) were recorded in V2DI. Multivariate analysis (radar chart) also showed the same trend.

Oxidative damage and membrane stability

Oxidative stress biomarkers and membrane stability differ significantly among varieties (Tables 6 and 7). Discussion

Growth indicators

Plant height and leaf area are important plant growth parameters, especially under drought stress. 48 A variety with a relatively medium leaf area under drought retains more water, maintains balanced stomatal oscillation and performs better photosynthesis than one with a larger leaf area. 49 Compared to the DI treatment, the PRD treatment resulted in greater plant height and leaf area. This is mainly due to more balance in the water status (RWCs, water potential and osmotic potential) of plants than in the DI treatment, which enhances cell turgidity, more growth and hence greater leaf growth and plant height. 50 Similar results were reported by Iqbal et al. 28 and Ahmad et al. 6 Varieties also showed significant differences in height and leaf area due to variation in their genetic makeup, whereas variety V3 presented moderate plant height and leaf area than the other tested varieties, which may function as a drought tolerance trait. This result is in accordance with Ahmadikhah and Marufinia 51 and Anantha et al. 52 who reported that varieties with moderate plant height and leaf area, exhibit greater drought tolerance than short-statured varieties. This resilience is primarily due to a balance among stomal conductance, transpiration, mesophyll conductance and photosynthesis.

Water relations and abscisic acid

Among various drought indices, the leaf relative water content (RWC) is the most important one. 53 Similarly, leaf water potential, osmotic potential, and turgor potential are important determinants of the drought tolerance of varieties, with higher values reflecting greater resilience to drought stress. 54 These parameters indicate the amount of water and solutes available in plant tissue. A variety with relatively high-water relation parameters is better suited for growth under low water availability. In the current study, variety V3 showed greater values of these parameters than did the other tested varieties because of greater membrane stability, stronger antioxidant defense system, less relative cell injury, and moderate stomatal oscillation which maintains the plant’s internal water balance. 54 Among the water deficit irrigation treatments, the PRD-treated plants exhibited greater values because of relatively less ABA production, better control of stomatal regulation, and sufficient water uptake by the wet portion of the roots to sustain the internal water balance. While in the DI treatment, all the root systems experienced severe drought stress and hence lower internal water contents. Multivariate analysis also shows that water relations are negatively correlated with ABA contents. Our results are in accordance with those of Ahmad et al. 6 and Iqbal et al. 28 However, accumulation of osmoprotectants in reduced water treatments may influence tissue rehydration capacity. This alters the process by artificially inflating the turgid weight of rehydrated leaf tissues, subsequently leading to an underestimation of relative water contents. 55

The main concept of water deficit regimes (PRD and DI) is the production of abscisic acid (ABA) in roots and leaves, causes partial stomatal closure to minimize transpirational water loss. 6 In addition to reducing transpiration, ABA also acts as a stress-stimulating hormone to activate the stress response mechanism of plants (both enzymatic and nonenzymatic). In our experiment, the PRD treatment resulted in a greater balance in ABA production than DI treatment because in the PRD treatment, only a partially dry portion produced ABA, whereas the wet portion of roots efficiently provided water to plants to maintain the internal water balance along with stable physiological and biochemical processes. Among the tested varieties, the V3 variety exhibited a balance in ABA production and internal water conservation which resulted in a better stress response via the production of more antioxidants, less relative cell injury, greater membrane stability, ultimately leading to improved growth and yield. Similar results were reported by Zhang et al. 56 and Gowtham et al. 57

Gas exchange and photosynthesis

Stomatal conductance is necessary for gaseous exchange and control of transpirational water loss, especially under water deficit conditions. Similarly, mesophyll conductance controls the supply of CO2 to chloroplasts for fixation in photosynthesis. Therefore, mesophyll conductance under drought stress is also crucial. 58 Greater stomatal closure minimizes transpirational water loss and mesophyll conductance which significantly decreases photosynthesis. 59 , 60 Under reduced water treatments, the stomatal and mesophyll conductance of V3 was more balanced due to more internal water status, moderate ABA synthesis, and less production of bio-stress markers than that of V1 and V2. This balance further improves photosynthesis and hence shows better genetic makeup of V3 for drought tolerance. Similar findings were reported by Izadi-Darbandi and Mehdikkhani 61 and Tomeo and Rosenthal 62 that drought-tolerant genotypes maintain a balance in stomatal conductance, transpiration and mesophyll conductance for better photosynthesis, yield and water use efficiency. Compared with the PRD treatment, the DI treatment minimized stomatal conductance by excessively producing leaf ABA. 63 In DI treatment, prolonged drought further restricts the intercellular CO2 supply due to the greater thickness of the cell wall and less surface area available for CO2 fixation compared to the PRD treatment. 64 This greater stomatal closure limits transpirational water loss but significantly decreases mesophyll conductance and photosynthesis, ultimately reducing economic yield under the DI treatment. Multivariate analysis also shows that gas exchange traits are positively correlated with photosynthesis and yield. Similar results were reported by Ouyang et al. 65

Chlorophyll is the main site of carbon fixation for photosynthesis. Damage to chlorophyll directly influences photosynthesis. In the DI treatment, reduced stomatal conductance limits CO2 availability to chlorophyll compared to the PRD and control treatments. Further, increased relative cell injury due to increased ROS production and suppressed antioxidant activity results in decreased membrane stability index and directly damage the leaf chlorophyll. 66 Similar results were reported by Wakeel et al. 67 Among varieties, V2 presented the lowest chlorophyll content under less water availability due to low internal water maintenance and increased production of ROS, which directly damage the chlorophyllase enzyme (chlorophyll-forming enzyme) and chlorophyll cells. Our results are also supported by the findings of Khan et al. 68

The growth and yield of crops mainly depend on photosynthesis, which is the main contributor to final yield. 69 In our experiment, variety V3 showed maximum photosynthesis under the control and water deficit regimes because it maintained relatively high stomatal conductance, mesophyll conductance, chlorophyll contents, and relative water contents, showed its superior capacity to perform better under limited water availability. Among the irrigation regimes, the PRD treatment stimulated a robust antioxidant defense system, which resulted in less ROS production, less damage to cell membrane and chlorophyll contents, and enhanced stomatal and mesophyll conductance, thereby driving higher photosynthetic rates. 6 Similar results were reported by Iqbal et al. 28

Oxidative damage and membrane stability

Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and Methylglyoxal (MG) are important molecule in plants that plays a vital role in cell signaling. At low concentrations, they act as important signaling molecule whereas at relatively high concentrations, they act as toxic metabolic byproduct, causing damage to cellular proteins and amino acids, producing ROS, and inhibiting photosynthesis. 70 Under drought stress, the concentration of H2O2 increases due to increased production of excited electrons, which results in more production of reactive oxygen species (ROS). 71 Under drought, MG is produced mainly through disruption of glycolytic flux and increased production of triose phosphates. ROS-induced damage to the lipid components of the cell membrane is determined by the malondialdehyde (MDA) content. 72 A relatively high MDA concentration indicates increased damage to the cell membrane. Drought stress results an increase of electrolyte leakage due to damage to the cell membrane. Varieties with lower H2O2, MDA, and electrolyte leakage are considered ideal for growth under stressful conditions. 73 In the current study, variety V3 showed the lowest value of these stress indicators under both control and water deficit conditions. This resilience was mediated by a greater membrane stability index, enhanced osmotic adjustment, moderate ABA synthesis, and higher production of antioxidants than the other tested varieties. Similar findings were reported by Zayed et al. 74 and Maghsoudi et al. 75 that drought-tolerant varieties showed relatively high MSI and relatively low MG contents and electrolyte leakage. Multivariate and correlation analysis also revealed less bio-stress markers and oxidative stress in variety V3 and hence more membrane stability than other tested varieties. Among the deficit irrigation regimes, PRD-treated plants resulted in less concentration of these stress indicators. This reduction was primarily due to greater internal water balance, more stomatal oscillation, photosynthesis, and stronger protective mechanisms against ROS. 76 Although DI also produces a stress signal response but due to constant drought conditions in specific portions of roots, the DI stress response is weaker than that of PRD. 77 Similar results were reported by Sonawane and Shrivastava, 78 Majiath et al. (2022), and Zhang et al. 79 that long-term stress conditions in DI-treated plants resulted in more ROS production, less MSI, more cell membrane damage and higher bio-stress markers contents.

The membrane stability index (MSI) is an important parameter used to measure the drought tolerance ability of genotypes. 80 MSI simply describes the strength (stability) of the cell membrane under stress. In our study, variety V3presented the maximum value of MSI due to its improved genetic characteristics for drought tolerance, which results in a greater balance in water relations, better ROS scavenging system and less electrolyte leakage. Similar results were reported by Mokhtari et al. 81 and Urmi et al. 82 The greater MSI in PRD irrigation is due to greater production of AsA than DI, which protects membrane lipids and proteins from the damaging effects of the ROS. 83 Similarly, nonenzymatic (proline, sugars) and enzymatic antioxidant activities were greater in the PRD than in the DI and control irrigation treatments. These results are in accordance with Deng et al. 84 who reported greater antioxidant activities in PRD than in DI.

Osmoprotetants

osmoprotectants play an important role in the osmotic adjustment of plants, especially under water deficit conditions. 85 This osmotic adjustment helps maintain cell turgor and minimize the harmful impacts of drought induced production of ROS. Proline acts as a first line of defense against stress in plants. Under drought, the proline content of psyllium significantly increases. 86 Sugars act as readily available forms of energy, especially when photosynthesis is limited due to drought stress. 87 Similarly Ascorbic acid and dehydro-ascorbic acid (DHA) also act as a potential antioxidant in plants under drought. They maintain cellular redox homeostasis by scavenging harmful reactive oxygen species; 88 protects chlorophyll from degradation and maintain a balance in photosynthesis under stressful conditions. 89 In our experiment variety V3 showed greater osmoprotectants contents because of its better drought tolerance mechanism (more MSIs, RWCs, osmotic potential and antioxidant activities) under less water availability than the other tested varieties. Similar results were reported by Mokhtari et al. 81 and Raza et al. 19 as they reported that drought-tolerant genotypes produce more proline than drought-sensitive genotypes. The increased synthesis of osmoprotectants under PRD irrigation (except ascorbic acid) is due to the production of a specific stress response (through hormonal signaling, especially ABA) that triggers greater production of proline and soluble sugars. 79 , 90 Less production of AsA in PRD than control irrigation treatment might be associated with production of ABA, which triggers the antioxidant defense system to consume and deplete vitamin C (ascorbic acid). 91 , 92 Our results are in accordance with those of Jovanovic and Stikic. 93 Under DI, a portion of roots continuously experiences drought, which results in the loss of root cells sensitivity, may causing fluctuations in signaling and hence less osmolytes synthesis. 63 Similar results were reported by Zou et al. 94 and Shen et al. 95

Antioxidant enzymes

Drought stress mainly decreases growth and yield by producing excessive ROS, which are lethal to plants at relatively high concentrations (Yang et al., 2021). These ROS damage important organelles in the cell membrane and cause lipid peroxidation, electrolyte leakage, and ultimately poor yield and WUE. 89 These ROS are detoxified by a number of enzymatic and nonenzymatic antioxidants in plants. Varieties that produce greater amounts of antioxidants under stressful conditions are considered more drought-tolerant. 96 In our experiment, variety V3 exhibited greater antioxidant activity, yield, and WUE, which makes it more drought-tolerant than the other tested varieties. Among the water deficit regimes, the PRD treatment produced greater concentrations of enzymatic and nonenzymatic antioxidants than did the DI treatment (except for CAT and GR activities). The PRD produces a specific stress response in an alternate way in which the dry portion maintains the balance of hormones (ABA) to trigger the defense system, whereas the wet portion provides sufficient water to sustain metabolic processes. While DI treatment disrupts metabolism by generating long-term drought stress. 76 Our results are in accordance with those of Ahmad et al. 26

Yield and water use efficiency (WUE)

A variety that results in greater economic yield under less water availability is best suited to water-scarce areas. 97 Similarly, water use efficiency (WUE) is also the main parameter for varietal selection in arid regions. 26 , 98 In our experiment, variety V3 performed best in terms of yield and water use efficiency under less water availability. This optimal performance was achieved by maintaining internal water homeostasis, producing moderate ABA, more antioxidants, membrane stability, and better scavenging of ROS, which altogether resulted in greater 1000-seed weight, seed yield, and WUE. Correlation analysis (Figure 11) also showed that water relations, gaseous exchange, and photosynthesis are positively correlated with yield and water use efficiency. Our results are supported by the findings of Belay et al. 99 and Raza et al. 19 who reported that drought-tolerant varieties maintain a normal pace of physio-biochemical activity and produce relatively high yields under low water availability. Among the water deficit regimens, the PRD treatment imposed less severe stress due to alternates wetting and drying of the root zone, which maintains a balance in water uptake, internal water balance, ABA production, and the activation of the antioxidant defense system. This enables plants to tolerate drought stress with a smaller reduction in important metabolic processes, and results in higher yield and water use efficiency than DI treatment. 76 , 100 In the DI treatment, continuous water deficit imposed severe, prolonged drought stress, which was the main reason for the lower growth, imbalance in water relations, disrupted metabolic processes, and ultimately reduced both yield and WUE. Similar findings were reported by Iqbal et al. 28 Multivariate (radar chart and PCA) and correlation analysis also show that H₂O₂, MDA, and methylglyoxal were negatively correlated with photosynthesis, water relations, and yield. Among water deficit treatments, these indicators were markedly higher in DI than PRD, explaining the lower yield observed in DI.

Conclusion

A reduction in crop yield due to drought is becoming a major challenge for crop production, particularly under arid conditions. Research is limited to morphological and yield-based screening of psyllium varieties under arid conditions. The current study involves physiological and biochemical screening of psyllium varieties with relatively little water. The results of the experiments revealed that the drought-tolerant variety performed better in terms of growth, physiology, stress tolerance response, and water use than did the varieties. Among the water deficit techniques, PRD induced drought tolerance in the tested varieties by maintaining a balance in terms of water content, increasing the activities of enzymatic and nonenzymatic antioxidants, and improving membrane stability compared with those of the DI treatment. It was concluded that under low water availability, the combination of the PRD with the V3 variety gave the best results in terms of drought stress tolerance based on physiochemical indicators, yield, and water use efficiency. Future research should focus on integration PRD with other water management techniques, such as mulching, biochar, and osmolytes, which may provide synergistic benefits. This approach can improve both crop yield and resource efficiency in water-scarce areas.

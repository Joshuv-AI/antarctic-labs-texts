Gestational age-specific MRI reference values for fetal renal morphology and ADC
Authors: Yangmei Pu, Shuzhen Ma, Qiyan Wang, Ran Li, Xuejing Zou, Yuchen Liu, Min Kang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFetal renal developmental abnormalities are among the most common congenital malformations and are associated with adverse perinatal outcomes. Although fetal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) can provide complementary morphological and quantitative information, gestational age (GA)-specific reference values integrating fetal renal morphology and diffusion parameters remain limited. This study aimed to establish MRI-based reference values for normal fetal renal morphology and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) across gestation.MethodsIn this retrospective single-center observational study, 313 singleton pregnancies (GA 23–38 weeks) with normal postnatal urinary outcomes underwent 1.5T fetal MRI, including T2-weighted and diffusion-weighted imaging sequences. Morphological parameters [anteroposterior, transverse and longitudinal diameters, and mean renal parenchymal thickness (MRPT) and functional parameters T2 relative signal intensity (RSI-T2) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC)] were measured for both kidneys. Reproducibility was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC). Associations between MRI parameters and GA were evaluated using correlation and linear regression analyses.ResultsAll measured parameters showed good to excellent inter-observer reproducibility. Renal diameters and MRPT increased significantly with GA (r = 0.72–0.89; all P < 0.0001). In contrast, renal ADC values showed moderate negative correlations with GA (right kidney: r =-0.47; left kidney: r =-0.52; both P < 0.0001). The regression equations for ADC (×10−3 mm²/s) were ADC_right = 3.23 − 0.049 × GA and ADC_left = 3.46 − 0.058 × GA.ConclusionsThis study provides GA-stratified reference ranges for key morphological and functional MRI parameters of normal fetal kidneys in the second and third trimesters. These quantitative benchmarks may provide a reference framework for fetal renal MRI assessment and for future studies of fetal renal abnormalities.Trial registrationNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02512-y.Keywords: Fetal kidney, Magnetic resonance imaging, Diffusion-weighted imaging, Apparent diffusion coefficient, Gestational age, Renal development, Reference values, Prenatal diagnosis

Background

Fetal renal developmental abnormalities represent one of the most common categories of congenital malformations, accounting for approximately 15%-20% of all fetal anomalies [1, 2]. Severe bilateral renal abnormalities in particular are strongly associated with oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia, and increased perinatal mortality, thereby contributing to adverse pregnancy outcomes and lifelong health implications [2, 3]. Accurate prenatal evaluation of fetal renal morphology and development is therefore essential for assessing abnormality severity, guiding perinatal management, and supporting clinical decision-making [2, 4].

Ultrasound remains the first-line modality for prenatal screening owing to its accessibility, low cost, and non-invasiveness [1, 5, 6]. However, fetal renal assessment may be affected by maternal body habitus, fetal position, amniotic fluid volume, and operator-dependent variability [1, 7]. Fetal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provides superior soft-tissue contrast and multiparametric assessment, and has increasingly been used as a complementary modality in prenatal diagnosis [5, 6, 8, 9]. Among MRI-derived parameters, the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) from diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) quantitatively reflects water diffusion within renal tissue and may provide indirect information regarding renal microstructure and maturation [10, 11]. Preliminary MRI studies have explored fetal renal size, diffusion parameters, and signal intensity characteristics across gestation [12–14].

Although these preliminary studies support the potential value of fetal renal MRI, comprehensive gestational age-specific reference values integrating renal morphology, parenchymal thickness, ADC, and relative T2 signal intensity remain limited. To address this need, the present study aimed to establish multiparametric MRI reference ranges for normal fetal kidneys during the second and third trimesters, incorporating both morphological parameters and quantitative MRI parameters. By analyzing their correlations with gestational age, this study sought to provide reproducible quantitative benchmarks for assessing fetal renal growth and maturation.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective observational study was conducted at Sichuan Provincial Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital and was approved by the institutional Ethics Committee (Approval No. 20240419-076). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to MRI examination. Pregnant women who underwent fetal MRI between June 2018 and June 2023 were screened for eligibility.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) singleton pregnancy; (2) gestational age between 23 and 38 weeks confirmed by first-trimester ultrasonography; (3) MRI images of sufficient diagnostic quality to allow quantitative analysis; (4) normal fetal renal morphology and signal characteristics confirmed independently by two radiologists with ≥ 10 years of fetal MRI experience; and (5) a normal neonatal urinary system confirmed through postnatal follow-up for 12–18 months based on hospital medical records and pediatric ultrasound examinations when available. Absence of renal or urinary tract abnormalities during follow-up was considered indicative of normal fetal kidneys.

The exclusion criteria included: (1) maternal systemic diseases (e.g., gestational diabetes, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, autoimmune or connective tissue disorders); (2) fetal anomalies involving other organ systems or chromosomal abnormalities confirmed by prenatal or postnatal genetic testing; (3) severe MRI artifacts resulting from fetal motion or technical limitations that precluded reliable measurement; and (4) incomplete clinical data or loss to postpartum follow-up.

MRI acquisition protocol

All examinations were performed using a standardized fetal abdominal MRI protocol focused on evaluation of the fetal abdomen. Imaging was conducted on a 1.5-T MR system (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA) using a phased-array body coil. The protocol included multiplanar T2-weighted single-shot fast spin-echo sequences (SSFSE), balanced steady-state free-precession sequences (FIESTA), axial T1-weighted imaging, and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI). T2-weighted images were acquired in axial and coronal planes relative to the fetal abdomen to optimize visualization of renal morphology. DWI was performed using a single-shot echo-planar imaging sequence with b values of 0 and 600 s/mm². This two-b-value protocol was selected to balance diffusion weighting, signal-to-noise ratio, and acquisition time in fetal MRI. Because fetal imaging is particularly susceptible to motion-related degradation, especially at 1.5T, b = 600 s/mm² was used as a practical compromise for fetal renal ADC measurement. Apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps were automatically generated on the vendor workstation using a monoexponential model without additional post-processing. Imaging parameters were adjusted as necessary to accommodate fetal position and maternal comfort while maintaining consistent acquisition settings throughout the study period. Image processing and quantitative measurement

Image analysis was performed on an AW 4.7 workstation. Two radiologists with 8 and 10 years of experience in fetal MRI, respectively, independently reviewed all datasets while blinded to gestational age and clinical information. Prior to the study, both radiologists underwent standardized training on ROI placement to ensure methodological consistency.

Image processing and quantitative measurements included both functional and morphological parameters. For functional parameters, ADC values were obtained by placing three circular regions of interest (ROIs; 15–20 mm²) on coronal DWI images at the upper pole, middle, and lower pole of each kidney, and then copying the ROIs to the corresponding ADC maps to extract ADC values, while avoiding the renal sinus, collecting system, and image artifacts. The mean ADC value was calculated from these three measurements. ROIs affected by motion artifacts or image distortion were excluded from analysis. Images with substantial motion artifacts or distortion affecting the renal parenchyma were excluded after visual inspection.

Relative T2 signal intensity (RSI-T2) was calculated as the ratio of renal parenchymal signal intensity to hepatic parenchymal signal intensity on axial T2-weighted images obtained at comparable anatomical levels (RSI=SI_renal/SI_liver). For morphological parameters, measurements were obtained from T2-weighted images as follows: anteroposterior diameter (AD) was measured on axial images at the level of the renal hilum; transverse diameter (TD) and longitudinal diameter (LD) were measured on coronal images at the maximal renal extent; and mean renal parenchymal thickness (MRPT) was calculated as the average of measurements obtained at the upper, middle, and lower poles. Each parameter was measured three times per kidney, and the mean value was used for statistical analysis. Representative ROI placement and measurement strategy are illustrated in Fig. 1.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), with percentile estimation conducted in R (version 4.5.2). Data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test and visual inspection. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Gestational age–specific reference values were generated by calculating the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 95th percentiles for renal parameters separately for the left and right kidneys and for pooled mean values. Because gestational age was recorded as completed weeks in the retrospective clinical records, reference values were summarized using clinically interpretable week-based intervals. The 35–38-week group was retained as a combined late-gestation stratum to preserve sample size. Pooled reference values are presented in Tables 2 and 3, and side-specific centiles are provided in the Supplementary Material. Associations between renal MRI parameters and gestational age (GA) were examined using Pearson correlation analysis, and linear regression models were constructed to characterize GA-related trends. Model assumptions were evaluated visually. Sex-related differences were assessed using linear regression models including GA as a continuous predictor. For each outcome (mean renal ADC, morphological parameters, and relative T2 signal intensity), both a sex main effect (adjusted for GA) and a GA×sex interaction term were tested (sex coded as male = 0, female = 1). To account for multiple comparisons, P-values from correlation and sex-effect analyses were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure. A two-tailed P < 0.05 (after correction when applicable) was considered statistically significant. Inter-observer reproducibility was evaluated in 30 randomly selected fetuses balanced across trimesters. Two radiologists independently performed ROI-based measurements using the predefined protocol while blinded to each other’s results. As the ROIs were standardized circular sampling regions rather than whole-organ segmentation masks, agreement was quantified using intraclass correlation coefficients for continuous quantitative measurements. ICC(2,1), based on a two-way random-effects model with absolute agreement, was reported with 95% confidence intervals. The overall study workflow is summarized in Fig. 2.

Results

Study population and selection

Between June 2018 and June 2023, a total of 445 fetal MRI examinations were retrospectively screened. Of these, 132 cases were excluded due to incomplete diffusion-weighted imaging sequences (n = 31), substantial motion artifacts precluding reliable ROI placement (n = 55), postnatal diagnosis of renal or urinary tract abnormalities (n = 17), or incomplete clinical follow-up (n = 29). After applying these exclusion criteria, 313 fetuses were included in the final analysis.

MRI examinations were performed following referral from obstetric ultrasound. Referral indications primarily included suspected central nervous system anomalies, thoracic abnormalities, and other non-renal fetal conditions identified on prenatal imaging. None of the included fetuses demonstrated structural renal abnormalities on either prenatal MRI or postnatal follow-up. The final cohort comprised 168 male (53.7%) and 145 female (46.3%) fetuses. Gestational age ranged from 23 to 38 weeks, with the majority of examinations performed between 31 and 33 weeks of gestation. Maternal age ranged from 20 to 44 years (median, 28.7 years).

Establishment of gestational age–specific reference values

Gestational age-stratified reference values were calculated for all renal morphological and functional parameters. In the primary analyses, data from the left and right kidneys were pooled. Because relatively few examinations were available at ≥ 37 weeks, the final 35–38-week stratum was retained for descriptive reference estimation. This interval included late-preterm and early-term fetuses, with no fetuses at 39 completed weeks or later included. Morphological measures (LD, TD, and MRPT) are presented as mean±standard deviation together with P5, P25, P50, P75, and P95 values (Table 2). Functional parameters, including renal ADC and RSI-T2, are summarized in the same manner using pooled data (Table 3). To provide side-specific reference information, complete percentile distributions (P5, P25, P50, P75, and P95) for each kidney-including AD as well as LD, TD, MRPT, ADC, and RSI-T2-are reported separately in the Supplementary Material.

Correlation between renal parameters and gestational age

Pearson correlation analysis demonstrated a significant negative association between renal ADC values and gestational age (GA) for both kidneys (right: r = − 0.46; left: r = − 0.52; both FDR-adjusted P < 0.001). In contrast, relative T2 signal intensity (RSI-T2) showed weak but statistically significant positive correlations with GA (right: r = 0.15; left: r = 0.20; both FDR-adjusted P < 0.001) (Fig. 3).

To assess potential non-linearity, generalized additive models (GAMs) were fitted for ADC and RSI-T2. GAM analysis suggested only minimal deviation from linearity for ADC and no substantial non-linearity for RSI-T2. Therefore, linear regression models were retained for clarity and clinical interpretability (Supplementary Figures S1–S2).

Regression analysis

Linear regression models were constructed to characterize the association between renal parameters and gestational age (GA). Significant linear relationships were observed for both ADC and mean renal parenchymal thickness (MRPT) in the right and left kidneys (all FDR-adjusted P < 0.001).For the right kidney, ADC (×10⁻³ mm²/s) decreased with GA according to the equation:

ADC = 3.23 − 0.049 × GA (R² = 0.218),whereas MRPT (cm) increased with GA: MRPT = 0.026 × GA − 0.037 (R² = 0.518).For the left kidney, the regression equations were: ADC = 3.46 − 0.058 × GA (R² = 0.273),MRPT = 0.025 × GA − 0.027 (R² = 0.548).These findings indicate a progressive decline in renal ADC values and an increase in parenchymal thickness with advancing gestational age.

Sex effects and measurement reliability

Fetal sex was available for all 313 included fetuses (168 male, 145 female). In GA-adjusted linear regression models, no significant main effect of sex was observed for mean renal ADC (β = −0.049, SE = 0.040, p = 0.228; FDR-adjusted q = 0.613), and the GA × Sex interaction term was not significant (β = −0.00019, p = 0.985), indicating comparable developmental trajectories between male and female fetuses. Similarly, no significant sex-related differences were identified for morphological parameters or relative T2 signal intensity after FDR correction (all q > 0.05).

Measurement reproducibility was assessed in a randomly selected subset of 30 fetuses. Inter-observer agreement was excellent for morphological parameters, with ICC(2,1) values of 0.946 (95% CI: 0.891–0.974) for longitudinal diameter, 0.936 (95% CI: 0.870–0.969) for transverse diameter, and 0.931 (95% CI: 0.862–0.967) for mean renal parenchymal thickness. For functional parameters, inter-observer agreement was good to excellent, with ICC(2,1) values of 0.898 (95% CI: 0.799–0.950) for ADC and 0.868 (95% CI: 0.743–0.935) for relative T2 signal intensity ratio.

Discussion

In this study, we established gestational age–specific MRI reference ranges for fetal renal morphology and diffusion parameters during the second and third trimesters. Renal morphological measures (LD, TD, AD, and MRPT) demonstrated strong positive associations with gestational age, whereas mean renal ADC values showed a moderate but statistically significant decline. Together, these findings are consistent with coordinated macroscopic growth and microstructural maturation of the fetal kidney across mid-to-late gestation.

Increasing renal parenchymal thickness across gestation

Mean renal parenchymal thickness (MRPT) increased progressively between 23 and 38 weeks and was strongly correlated with gestational age. This pattern is consistent with established models of renal development. Following mid-gestation nephrogenesis, renal growth predominantly reflects hypertrophic expansion of existing nephrons, increasing tubular length, vascular development, and interstitial tissue maturation. These microstructural changes plausibly contribute to the observed macroscopic thickening of the renal parenchyma [15].

While our data do not directly assess histological correlates, the strong and consistent association between MRPT and gestational age supports its utility as a morphologic marker of fetal renal growth. Deviations from gestational age–specific reference values may warrant further evaluation in suspected renal abnormalities; however, validation in pathological cohorts is required before clinical application can be fully established [16, 17].

Decreasing ADC values with advancing gestation

We observed a progressive decline in mean renal ADC values with increasing gestational age. Similar decreasing trends have been reported in prior fetal MRI studies. For example, Savelli et al. [14] reported a regression model of ADC = 1.45 -0.0104×GA, whereas Manganaro et al. [13]described a comparable negative association (ADC = 1.69 -0.0169×GA). Although absolute ADC values differed between studies, likely reflecting variations in imaging protocols and acquisition parameters, both studies demonstrated the same overall pattern of decreasing renal ADC with advancing gestation.

Several mechanisms may plausibly contribute to this pattern. Increasing cellular density, tubular elongation, and interstitial complexity may reduce extracellular diffusion space. Additionally, progressive maturation of tubular transport function may alter intrarenal water compartmentalization. Although fetal renal perfusion increases with gestation, its proportional contribution to overall fetal cardiac output remains relatively limited, suggesting that structural factors likely play a dominant role in shaping diffusion characteristics.

Importantly, these interpretations remain inferential, as direct histopathological validation was beyond the scope of this study. The stronger negative correlation observed in the left kidney compared with the right warrants further investigation but should be interpreted cautiously [18].

Relative T2 signal intensity changes

The correlation between RSI-T2 and gestational age was relatively modest but statistically significant. RSI-T2 reflects relative parenchymal signal characteristics rather than diffusion properties and may therefore provide complementary information regarding renal tissue maturation. Changes in relative T2 signal intensity may be related to evolving renal water content and microstructural organization during fetal development. Although the clinical implications of RSI-T2 remain limited at present, this parameter may contribute to multiparametric MRI assessment of fetal renal maturation when interpreted together with diffusion and morphological measurements. However, the clinical utility of RSI-T2 requires further validation in studies including pathological renal conditions [18].

Morphology–function complementarity

The parallel but distinct trajectories of morphological growth and diffusion changes suggest that structural enlargement and microstructural maturation may not occur in a strictly linear or identical fashion. Rather than implying diagnostic superiority, our findings indicate that morphological and diffusion parameters provide complementary information regarding fetal renal development [19].

Whether combined multiparametric assessment improves early detection of specific renal pathologies remains speculative and requires prospective evaluation in abnormal cohorts. At present, the primary contribution of this study is the establishment of normative reference ranges against which future pathological studies may be compared [16, 17].

Study limitations

Several limitations merit consideration. First, although the overall cohort was relatively large, the number of fetuses at ≥ 37 weeks was limited. Therefore, the 35–38-week group was retained as a combined late-gestation stratum, including late-preterm and early-term fetuses, which may reduce the precision of category-specific estimates in late gestation. In addition, gestational age was summarized by completed weeks in this retrospective study; future prospective studies with day-level gestational age data may provide more refined reference curves. Second, this was a single-center study performed on a single-vendor 1.5-T MRI system, potentially limiting generalizability to other scanners, field strengths, and populations. Third, despite high inter-observer reproducibility, renal ADC measurements remain susceptible to ROI placement, fetal motion, and sequence-specific technical parameters; thus, residual variability cannot be entirely eliminated even with standardized acquisition and measurement procedures. Fourth, although parenchymal volume may provide a more comprehensive characterization of renal development, thickness-based MRPT was selected for its feasibility and reproducibility in routine fetal MRI; future studies should directly compare thickness- and volume-based approaches. Future multicenter, prospective investigations across diverse MRI platforms are warranted to validate these reference ranges and assess their clinical utility in pathological conditions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study establishes gestational age–specific MRI reference ranges for fetal renal morphological and diffusion parameters during the second and third trimesters. Renal size and parenchymal thickness increase progressively with gestation, whereas mean renal ADC values demonstrate a moderate decline. These findings provide normative imaging benchmarks for fetal renal development and may serve as a reference framework for future studies investigating pathological conditions. Further validation in multicenter and disease-specific cohorts is required before clinical implementation.

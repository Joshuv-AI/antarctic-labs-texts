Reprogrammable metamaterial robot with embodied versatile computation and mechanical intelligence
Authors: Wu Zhou, Yi-Ze Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Due to the flexible and robust features, recent advances emerging from computing intelligence in mechanical systems have led to many innovative techniques from signal processing to robotics. However, conventional designs of mechanical computing are typically manifested as deterministic functions with single-task layouts, which show limitations in versatility. This work proposes a versatile mechanical computing architecture, which can integrate both analog and logic operations into a unified robotic system. Inspired by Fourier optics, elastic wave metamaterials are introduced to spatial analog computing with robot crawling, enabling flexible spatiotemporal modulations and self-regulating locomotion. Using serial/parallel strategies, the metamaterial allows nested and cascaded designs from combinational analogs to the extended binary logics. In particular, by encoding analog signals as mechanical qubit-like states, the system can realize higher dimensional logic mappings through reprogrammable matrix operations. Beyond single-task layouts, nonlinear harmonics are also introduced to realize the parallel computing of multiple tasks or their transformation to specific tasks. The versatile strategies are shown to support the parallelism, integration and transformation from analogs to logics, which wish to offer an effective approach for incorporating mechanical intelligence in robotics.

Introduction

Metamaterials are known as new kinds of composite materials and structures, which are designed artificially with unusual physical and mechanical properties. Since the emergence of band gap theory, elastic wave metamaterials have received extensive attention due to their special wave manipulation abilities1–4. The design inspirations of elastic wave metamaterials are diverse and range from phononic crystals, cellular materials to biomimetic structures, etc. Such versatility provides elastic wave metamaterials with rich mechanical properties, e.g., acoustic cloaking, negative refraction and topological insulation properties5–8. Recently, wave-based mechanical computing devices have attracted increasing attention due to their special signal processing and storage capabilities9–13. Mechanical computing can operate without electrons and its ability to emulate digital calculation has attracted growing interests in intelligent material and structure fields14,15. In such framework, computations are directly performed by position, deformation or vibration signals with high interference resistance and environment adaptability16,17. In particular, the inherent reprogrammable and embodied features provide possibilities for integrating computing autonomy and mechanical intelligence into smart robotic materials18,19.

Recent studies in mechanical/optical computing have led to many novel computational metamaterials from logic, analog to qubit computing20–35. As the basic units of logic operations, logic gates have been realized through multiple mechanical implementations, such as buckled beams, origami and waveguides20–22. Investigations about mechanical logics have developed many effective strategies. For instance, structural properties of metamaterials such as chirality and multi-stability have been employed to achieve logic encoding, signal processing and cascaded computing designs23–25. However, traditional mechanical logic processors often struggle with accuracy or efficiency when processing large-scale dynamic tasks. In contrast, wave-based analog computing26–30 enables continuous signal processing in both time and space domains, which shows advantages for complex dynamic computing. Since the proposals of Green’s function method26, spatial analog computing have achieved from convolution and calculus operations to equation solver and quantum search functions27–30. Compared to digital logics, the special amplitude and phase modulations offer spatial analog computing more flexible information storage and processing capabilities. In particular, the similarities between mechanical and optical waves in signal superposition and interference also provide platforms for the mechanical analog of qubit-based optical operations31–35.

In recent years, increasing attention has been paid to the versatile designs of computational systems, which highlights their abilities to process various tasks in a unified framework. Conventional designs of mechanical computing are mainly manifested as deterministic functions with single-task configurations, which show limitation in versatility. In order to address these challenges, increasing efforts have focused on the parallel strategy that can perform multitasking computing36–38. Relevant attempts range from spatial analogs to digital logics, such as reprogrammable sequential memory36, cascaded logics in multi-stable beams37, as well as parallel analogs with nonlinear harmonics38. Although many studies about versatile computing have been reported, the effective method that can integral analog, logic and qubit-like matrix computing into a unified framework is still sporadic. On the other hand, mechanical metamaterials have also inspired many interesting robotic designs from worm-like crawler, origami robot to metamaterial swimmer39–42. Previous studies about mechanical computing are mainly focused on the static and table-top demonstrations with prospects in smart and wearable devices. The inspiration of integrating mechanical intelligence and computational autonomy into robotic systems also deserves further explorations.

In this work, a versatile mechanical computing architecture is proposed, which can integrate mechanical intelligence into robot designs with both analog and logic functions. Inspired by Fourier optics, elastic wave metamaterials are introduced to spatial analog computing with robot crawling, which enables flexible spatiotemporal modulations and self-regulating locomotion (e.g., calculus, convolution and solving equations). Using the spatial serial/parallel strategies, the metamaterial robot allows the combinations of several analog functions from nested and cascaded designs (e.g., addition/subtraction and multiplication) to their extended binary digital logics (e.g., AND, OR and XOR gates). In particular, nonlinear harmonics are introduced to realize parallel computing of multiple tasks or their transformation to specific tasks. On the other hand, due to the similarity between mechanical and optical waves, the mechanical analog computing can also be applied to emulate the qubit matrix operation achieved in optical systems. By encoding analog signal pairs as combinations of mechanical qubit-like states |0〉 and |1〉, multiple linear and invertible matrix transformations can be achieved. The associated computing results can perform the logical mappings such as Hadamard, Pauli X and CNOT gates. Furthermore, analytical derivations and experiments are performed to verify the theory with reprogrammable versatile performances.

Results

Configuration and design principle

The metamaterial robot in Fig. 1a employs a periodic network of hexagonal blocks with two translational components u1 and u2 in the 2D plane. Each hexagonal block is composed of a tunable mass m(x, y) and a supporting base to behave as the robot foot. Adjacent moving blocks are connected by two curved metal sheets, which can be considered as leaf springs with shear (ks), linear (kl) and nonlinear (kn) tension stiffness. In addition, a linear vibration motor array is installed at the front edge of the robot to provide the spatial profile f(y) of incident waves. Inspired by previous studies about mechanical/optical computing26–30, this work introduces the framework of elastic wave metamaterials to regulate the robot crawling with spatiotemporal modulations. As shown in Fig. 1b, the metamaterial robot is divided into 3 sub-arrays with 2 Fourier transform arrays (ℱ1, ℱ2) and 1 transfer function layer H. Based on Fourier optics, the spatial output g(y) associated with the incident profile f(y) can be determined by the integral relation g(y) = ℱ{H(ky)ℱ[f(y)]} with ky being the spatial frequency. Since the spatial frequency ky is in the same domain of y, the transfer function with target operators can be expressed as H(ky) = H(y), in which y replaces the role of ky. During the calculations, the metamaterial robot can be driven to move or steer in response to the spatial output g(y). In order to minimize the interference from incident signals, the robot feet near the input layers are designed with symmetric friction but the others remain asymmetric. Then, the metamaterial robot in Fig. 1b can switch between different locomotion modes according to the output field distribution from g1 to g3 regions.

Based on the architecture above, Fig. 1d–f provide multiple mechanical computing strategies from reprogrammable analog computing to their extended logic and matrix operations. Firstly, with the ℱ1-H-ℱ2 layout in Fig. 1b, the metamaterial can integrate the robot crawling with multiple computing functions by proper designs of transfer layers H, e.g., integral, differential, translation operations and their related motion modes in Fig. 1d. Secondly, in addition to single-task computation, Fig. 1e introduces nonlinear sub- ωsub and super- ωsuper harmonics into mechanical computing and combines them with harmonic band gaps. By tuning incident signals ω0 within different harmonic band gaps, the metamaterial allows parallel computing of multiple tasks or single task with specific frequency. Thirdly, the metamaterial robot can be also extended to digital logics by labeling the presence and absence of analog signals as binary ‘1’ and ‘0’, e.g., AND, OR and XOR operations in Fig. 1f. In particular, by encoding the analog signal pairs {f1, f2}T and {g1, g2}T as the combination of qubit-like states |0〉 = {1, 0}T and |1〉 = {0, 1}T, the metamaterial can be designed to perform mechanical matrix operations. The associated results enable logical mappings such as Hadamard, Pauli X and CNOT gates43,44. At last, using the spatial serial and parallel strategies, Fig. 1g further extends the metamaterial of several transfer functions H1…N or inputs f = (f1, f2) to their combinational multiplication H = H1H2…HN and addition/subtraction operations g = f1 ± f2.

Among these functions, the proposed versatile platform allows parallel computing in frequency (Case II), bitwise (Case III) and spatial (Case IV) domains with multitasking capabilities. The following discussion is focused on the computational functions achieved by the metamaterial robot. Details about the robot design and computing principle are demonstrated in Movies S1 and S2.

Cooperative and transformable computing

During the derivations above, we have discussed the principle of mechanical computing and its application in wave modulation and analog operation. The reprogrammable functions from convolution and calculus to their combinations with robot crawling suggest the potential in complex working conditions. However, due to the single-task layout, the robot structure is required to be one-for-one matched with the computational function, which shows limitations in multitasking. In order to improve the computing performance, the following work introduces nonlinear sub- ωsub and super- ωsuper harmonics into the robot to achieve the cooperation and transformation of multiple computing modes.

As shown in Figs. 1e and 3, the principle for implementing mechanical computing with nonlinear harmonic waves is illustrated. Based on the versatile ℱ1-H-ℱ2 layout in Fig. 1b, the previous GRIN lens ℱ2 is replaced by a Fresnel lens ℱ2* and a periodic array of filtering layers (PLs) in Fig. 3a, b. The Fresnel lens ℱ2* employs a piecewise mass distribution as m*(y) = mGRIN(y)–n*Tm* (n* ∈ ℤ), which satisfies the constraint 0 <m*(y) <m0 and period Tm* = m0λ0/Δℱ *. Compared to conventional GRINs, the Fresnel lens ℱ2* can be designed thinner in Fig. 3b to achieve the same focusing function of ℱ2 in Fig. 3c. After the Fresnel lens, the periodic filtering layers PLs are introduced to regulate the nonlinear harmonic components by their corresponding band gaps. Mechanical matrix operations

In addition to spatial analog computing, the elastic wave system can be also applied to perform matrix operations on encoded mechanical signals. In the following discussion, we employ the versatile computational framework to realize multiple matrix operations which are commonly used in information processing. Different from spatial analog computing with single input f, the matrix operations are achieved by several encoded mechanical signals {f1, f2, …, fN}T with a N-dimensional transformation matrix M(N). For example, we can encode the two-channel analog inputs {f1, f2}T and outputs {g1, g2}T as vectors in a classical 2D state space. Then, the metamaterial can achieve the linear transformation {g1, g2}T = M(2){f1, f2}T by a 2D matrix M(2). Such transformation can be employed to realize multiple fundamental 2×2 operations such as Hadamard or Pauli-X type matrices in linear algebra. Accordingly, this framework can be also extended to higher-dimensional state spaces by increasing the number of wave channels, e.g., the 4×4 controlled-NOT (CNOT) matrix. It is worth mentioning that many high-dimensional linear and invertible transformations can be decomposed into sequences of 2×2 matrix operations33. As a result, a robust and reprogrammable M(2) transformation device is essential to achieve more complex and scalable higher- dimensional operations.

As shown in Fig. 5a, the metamaterial robot employs a similar multilayer ℱ1-H-ℱ2 layout to spatial analog computing. A mass barrier array mb » m0 is placed along the axial centerline y = Y/2 which divides the input f into a signal pair {f1, f2}T. For the harmonic signals with identical frequency, the signal pair {f1, f2}T defines a 2D complex state vector {A1*exp(iφ1), A2*exp(iφ2)}T with A1*2 + A2*2 = 1. This vector is characterized by the normalized amplitudes and phases A1*, A2*, φ1 and φ2 of {f1, f2}T. We consider the state vector as a linear combination {α, β}T = α{1, 0}T + β{0, 1}T, in which the basis states {1, 0}T and {0, 1}T are defined by single-mode excitations (A1* = 1, A2* = 0) and (A1* = 0, A2* = 1). In order to better describe the two-channel signals, we adopt the symbolic notation from acoustic phi-bit computing32–34, which labels classical basis states {1, 0}T and {0, 1}T as mechanical qubit-like states |0〉 and |1〉. Then, a general input state is written as {α, β}T = α | 0〉 + β | 1〉 with α = A1*exp(iφ1) and β = A2*exp(iφ2). It is worth mentioning that this notation is a purely mathematical convention without relevance to quantum computing. With the normalization A1*2 + A2*2 = 1, the mechanical state can be fully characterized by the amplitude ratio A1/A2 and the relative phase Δφ = φ2 − φ1, Then, it can be visualized on a Bloch sphere by the mapping of two polar coordinates θ = 2arccos(A1*) and ϕ = Δφ. Robotic crawling with embodied versatile computations

Beyond conventional single or serial strategies, the combinational, cooperative and transformable features enable the parallelism of the proposed functions in spatial, frequency and bitwise domains. Following the concept of mechanical intelligence in Fig. 1d, this work introduces computational functions into robot designs and combines them with robot crawling. During the calculation, the robot can be driven to move or guided to steer in response to the modulated wave propagation. Discussion

In this work, a versatile mechanical computing architecture is developed, which can integrate mechanical intelligence into robot locomotion with both analog and logic functions. Inspired by Fourier optics, this work introduces elastic wave metamaterials to analog and logic computing and incorporates them into robot designs. The embodied computational functions allow flexible spatiotemporal modulations during robot crawling with multifunctional and self-regulating locomotion.

Based on the versatile computational architecture, a variety of fundamental (e.g., differential, integral, convolution and translation) as well as combinational (e.g., nesting computing and solving equations) analog operations are demonstrated. Using the spatial parallel or serial strategies, the metamaterial robot allows the nested and cascaded designs of several inputs and transfer functions to implement their addition, subtraction and multiplication. On the other hand, the metamaterial robot can be extended to digital logics by labeling the presence/absence of analog signals as binary ‘1/0’ (e.g., AND, OR, and XOR operations). In particular, by encoding analog signals as the combinations of mechanical qubit-like states |0〉 and |1〉, multiple linear and invertible matrix transformations and the associated logical mappings can be achieved, (e.g., Hadamard, Pauli X and CNOT gates). Beyond the single-task computations above, nonlinear super- ωsuper and sub- ωsub harmonics are introduced to realize the cooperative and transformable computing. By tuning the incident signal ω0 within different harmonic stop and pass bands, the mixed harmonics can be directly output as multi-task results or filtered as single-task results (e.g., g(y)|ω0, dg(y)/dy|ωsuper and ∫g(y)dy|ωsub). In particular, it can transform different harmonic inputs into the same frequency while maintaining their computational results. Such a property provides possibilities for the subsequent operations requiring identical frequency condition, e.g., spatial addition/subtraction operations. Furthermore, analytical derivations and experiments are also performed to support the theory with reprogrammable versatile performances.

It is worth noting that this work engages in revealing a general principle from nonlinear elastic wave metamaterials to robot crawling with different computational functions. Such a principle is not only limited to our present work but also applicable to other combinational schemes with more complex functionalities. From analog to logic and matrix operations, the proposed strategies can support the parallel, transformable and integrated computing in spatial, frequency and bitwise domains. This work wishes to provide some promising strategies for the applications of elastic wave metamaterials into mechanical computing and intelligent robotics.

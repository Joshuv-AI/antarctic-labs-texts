Severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is associated with significant morbidity and mortality, yet many patients are deemed inoperable due to advanced right ventricular dysfunction or comorbidities.1

Percutaneous caval valve implantation (CAVI) has emerged as a palliative transcatheter option to reduce venous congestion and improve symptoms in this high-risk population who is not fit for tricuspid edge-to-edge repair due to large gaps.2,3

However, recurrent regurgitation after prior CAVI remains a procedural challenge and never described before, particularly when used for palliative treatment.

We report an innovative IVC Tric valve downsizing and valve-in-valve CAVI technique using non-premounted stent to create a suitable landing zone for a balloon-expandable valve in a patient with recurrent severe TR.

Case Summary

A 78-year-old man with ischemic cardiomyopathy (LVEF 30%), atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, and prior mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair presented with recurrent right-sided heart failure due to torrential TR. Because of prohibitive surgical risk, he had previously undergone transcatheter bicaval valve implantation (TricValve system: 29 mm superior vena cava, 35 mm inferior vena cava), which initially reduced TR to mild and maintained stability for 2 years. He re-presented with progressive right-heart failure and imaging consistent with severe transvalvular regurgitation of the IVC prosthesis from leaflet thickening and restricted motion (Videos 1 and 2). Cardiac computed tomography (CT) demonstrated an oval-shaped IVC valve with maximal dimensions of 37 to 38 mm, with a geographic miss involving the posterior aspect of the valve on CT imaging. There were no degenerative changes noted on the leaflet or calcification. The cardiac CT showed no leaflet motion restriction, subclinical thrombosis, or migration. The cause of the regurgitation assumed to be due to the oval shape of the IVC valve and overexpansion with time over 2 years with mild structural degeneration (leaflet thickening) (Figures 1 and 2). Given recurrent decompensation despite medical therapy, a percutaneous reintervention was planned using IVC downsizing with a non-premounted 39-mm Andra XXL stent, followed by valve-in-valve implantation of a 30-mm balloon-expandable valve, which successfully abolished regurgitation on postprocedural transesophageal echocardiography (TEE).

Procedural Steps

1.Preprocedural Planning

Preprocedural Planning

Multidetector computed tomography and 3-dimensional reconstruction were used to evaluate the prior IVC valve position, assess the caval diameter, and plan the intervention. The average IVC diameter at the intended landing site measured 34 mm. This exceeded the maximum balloon-expandable size available in our country at the time (30 mm).

To achieve secure anchoring and avoid migration, we planned IVC downsizing with a balloon-expandable Andra XXL stent, followed by valve-in-valve implantation within the existing IVC prosthesis.

2.Venous access and baseline assessment

Venous access and baseline assessment

Under local anesthesia, fluoroscopy, and TEE guidance, right femoral venous access was obtained. A large-bore access 24-F Gore Dryseal was used after implantation of 2 proglide suture based for closure. A pigtail catheter was positioned in the IVC for angiographic reference, confirming the relationship between hepatic veins and the prior IVC valve (Video 3).

3.Stent deployment for IVC downsizing

Stent deployment for IVC downsizing

A non-premounted 39-mm (Andra XXL) stent was delivered via the femoral sheath and deployed within the pre-existing IVC stent frame to reduce the effective diameter of the landing zone. The anticipated stent length after full expansion is 22 to 24 mm. The exact distance from the atrium to Hepatic inflow was 26 to 27 mm. Slow balloon inflation achieved symmetric expansion and coaxial alignment with the prior frame, creating a a smaller rigid diameter, suitable for new valve implantation (Video 4).

4.Valve-in-valve implantation

Valve-in-valve implantation

A 30-mm balloon-expandable transcatheter valve was advanced and precisely positioned within the downsized IVC segment. The valve was intentionally positioned slightly above the hepatic venous inflow to maintain unobstructed drainage (Video 5).

5.Postdeployment assessment

Postdeployment assessment

Immediate TEE and angiography confirmed excellent valve positioning, full expansion, and complete abolition of regurgitant flow. No migration or paravalvular leak was observed, and hepatic venous flow was preserved (Video 6).

Potential Pitfalls

Several technical challenges may arise during valve-in-valve caval implantation following prior CAVI. Accurate preprocedural imaging and sizing are critical; underestimating the IVC diameter may result in valve malapposition or migration, whereas oversizing can cause caval wall injury or hepatic vein compromise.

Landing-zone positioning must preserve unobstructed hepatic venous inflow—deployment too caudal risks hepatic congestion, while too cranial may lead to suboptimal anchoring. During stent expansion within the previous CAVI frame, asymmetric deformation or frame misalignment may occur; slow, incremental balloon inflation under fluoroscopy and TEE guidance ensures coaxial alignment (Video 7).

Finally, the triple-metal configuration (existing stent, downsizing stent, and transcatheter valve) increases thrombogenic risk, warranting meticulous anticoagulation and structured imaging follow-up to confirm valve stability and long-term patency.

Conclusions

This case demonstrates the feasibility and safety of performing valve-in-valve caval implantation using a non-premounted stent for IVC downsizing to accommodate a balloon-expandable transcatheter valve in a patient with prior CAVI. The technique provides an effective solution for managing recurrent or residual TR in patients with large-diameter or distorted caval anatomy, where direct valve implantation is not possible.

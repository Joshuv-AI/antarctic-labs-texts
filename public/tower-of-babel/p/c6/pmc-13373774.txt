Characteristics and Risk Factors Associated With Hospitalization and Severe Disease in Patients Aged ≥ 60 Years With Human Metapneumovirus Infection Over Three Consecutive Seasons (2021–2024) in Valladolid, Spain. The VALLAVIRUS Study
Authors: Iván Sanz‐Muñoz, Silvia Galindo‐Carretero, Marina Toquero‐Asensio, Alejandro Martín‐Toribio, Raquel Iglesia‐Aparicio, Celia López‐Gonzalo, Diana Rodríguez‐González, Laura Tamayo‐Pérez, Javier Sánchez‐Martínez, Carla Rodríguez‐Crespo, Alberto Caballero‐García, Marta Domínguez‐Gil, Silvia Rojo‐Rello, José María Eiros
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundHuman metapneumovirus (hMPV) is a respiratory pathogen often underestimated in the elderly. Although studies suggest it causes around 10% of respiratory hospitalizations in the United States, national and local data remain scarce. This study aimed to describe patients hospitalized with hMPV and identify risk factors for hospitalization and severe disease.MethodsA retrospective study included adults aged ≥ 60 years diagnosed with hMPV infection in two hospitals in Valladolid, Spain, during three consecutive seasons (2021–2024). Data on demographics, comorbidities, clinical presentation, laboratory results and severity parameters were collected. Severe disease was defined as hospital stay ≥ 11 days, ICU admission, need for mechanical ventilation, readmission within 90 days or in‐hospital death.ResultsAmong 703 hMPV‐confirmed cases, 608 required hospitalization, following a clear seasonal epidemic pattern (November–March). The hospitalization rate was 123.2 per 100,000 > 60 years old inhabitants (95% CI: 113.6–133.4), representing 20.5% of respiratory virus detections. Mean hospital stay was 10.5 days; 6.4% of the patients were admitted to ICU, 3.3% required mechanical ventilation, 6.9% died, and 14.0% were readmitted within 90 days. Advanced age (≥ 75 years) increased hospitalization risk threefold to fivefold compared to people between 60 and 74 years old. Co‐infections, particularly bacterial, and living in nursing homes were also associated with a 1.5–2.7 fold higher risk of severe disease and hospitalization.ConclusionshMPV infections pose a significant health burden among the elderly. The findings underscore the need for greater awareness, surveillance, and development of preventive strategies, including vaccination, against hMPV in older adults.

Introduction

Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) is a single‐stranded, negative‐stranded RNA virus belonging to the Pneumoviridae family, along with the only other representative of this phylogenetic group capable of infecting humans, the respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) [1, 2]. This virus, first identified in 2001 by B.G. Van den Hoogen et al. [3], causes epidemics in temperate countries between November and May, depending on the local climate [4, 5, 6, 7]. In countries such as Spain, for example, the epidemic circulation of hMPV usually occurs shortly after the circulation of influenza, and over the last 10 years, as with other epidemic respiratory viruses, the intensity of hMPV circulation has varied considerably between seasons [6].

Despite recent interest in hMPV, evidence suggests that it has been circulating since at least the 1950s, as various serological studies conducted with old sera have detected antibodies against hMPV [8, 9, 10, 11]. It is now known that hMPV is the third leading cause of community‐acquired pneumonia in children [12] and the fourth in adults [13]. However, far less is known about the burden of disease, hospitalization, severity and mortality associated with this virus, especially in older adults. According to some studies, hMPV is responsible for about 10% of hospitalizations in the United States [14]. A systematic review of 46 studies conducted between 2005 and 2023 found that hMPV causes around 473,000 hospitalizations (95% CI, 396,000–777,000) worldwide each season [15]. In turn, this same study highlighted a significant gap of knowledge regarding the impact of this virus, due to the limited number of national and local studies available.

Like other epidemic respiratory viruses, such as influenza or RSV, hMPV places a substantial burden on hospital admissions among patients aged ≥ 60 years. Studies conducted in the United States and France report hospital stays of 5–7 days [16, 17]. The HARTI study, conducted across 12 countries [18], found hospital stays closer to 5 days. These three studies also indicate that, overall, 8% of patients hospitalized with laboratory‐confirmed hMPV require ICU admission [18]. However, these studies also showed differences when analyzed at the regional level. For instance, in the United States [16], data show that 16.5% of patients required ICU admission, similar to the rates observed in France [17]. In terms of mortality, some studies have estimated that hMPV causes around 14,000 deaths in the United States each year [18, 19, 20], while studies conducted in other countries show mortality rates ranging from 4% to 9.3% [17, 21].

Some recent studies on hMPV have also focused on risk factors and complications in older adults. The study by P. Loubet et al., published in 2021 [17], reported that more than 84% of hMPV hospitalizations in France occurred in people aged ≥ 65 years and were most frequent among those with chronic heart disease. The same study found that 62% of hospitalized individuals experienced at least one complication during infection, the most common being pneumonia (36%) and acute heart failure (25%).

Respiratory viruses and infections represent a major public health issue, causing a significant burden in older adults [22]. Besides, the co‐circulation of viruses makes it difficult to understand their burden [23]. One of the main issues in the coming decades is the aging of the population [24], so vaccination may play an important role in sustaining healthy aging, in line with the WHO strategy [25, 26]. No vaccine development for hMPV has progressed significantly in recent years, and no vaccine is currently licensed for clinical use. However, multiple platforms are under investigation [27].

Given the available evidence on hMPV, the objective of the VALLAVIRUS study is to analyze, over three consecutive seasons (2021–2022, 2022–2023, and 2023–2024), the characteristics and risk factors associated with hospitalization and severe disease in patients ≥ 60 years hospitalized with laboratory‐confirmed hMPV infection.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This retrospective observational study, named “VALLAVIRUS”, was conducted to assess the burden of hospitalizations, patients' characteristics, seasonality, and risk factors in patients aged ≥ 60 years with laboratory‐confirmed hMPV infection in two hospitals in the province of Valladolid, Spain. The study period comprised three consecutive seasons, from October 1, 2021 to May 31, 2024 (seasons 2021–2022, 2022–2023, and 2023–2024). The primary objective of this study was to describe the characteristics of patients hospitalized with hMPV infection and to identify the risk factors associated with hospitalization and severe disease.

Study Settings

The study was conducted in two hospitals in the province of Valladolid, Spain. The two participating hospitals were “Hospital Clínico Universitario de Valladolid” and “Hospital Universitario Río Hortega”. The total population aged ≥ 60 years served by both hospitals was 161,641 inhabitants [28].

Study Population

This study included all patients aged ≥ 60 years with an acute respiratory infection (ARI) fulfilling the WHO definition [29] (sudden onset of at least one of the following symptoms: cough, sore throat, dyspnea, and runny nose, together with a clinical judgment of infection), and with an onset of the first symptom/sign ≤ 7 days before the medical visit, who were attended at the emergency rooms (ERs) or hospitalized in either of the two participating hospitals included in the study. From this population, we selected patients with a positive molecular diagnostic result for any respiratory virus, and then, we specifically analyzed those cases with laboratory‐confirmed hMPV infection (Figure 1). No sample size estimation was performed, since all patients aged ≥ 60 years hospitalized with an hMPV infection were included in the study, and the retrospective nature of the study did not allow for prior sample size calculation.

Methodology and Data Collection

Data Sources

Demographic and clinical data were obtained from patients' medical records and recorded in a RedCap database for analysis. Microbiological data were collected from the MICROB and MODULAB systems of the participating hospitals.

Molecular Diagnostic of RSV Infections

During the two seasons analyzed, samples from both ER and hospitalized patients were tested using various molecular methods, such as Luminex NxTAG Respiratory Pathogens (Luminex, USA), bioMérieux FilmArray RP (bioMérieux, France), GenXpert Influenza, RSV rapid diagnostic (Cepheid, USA), and Seegene AllPlex Respiratory Panel (Seegene, South Korea). These panels identify the following respiratory pathogens: RSV A and B; influenza A, B, H1 and H3; human CoV‐OC43, NL63, HKU1, and 229E; SARS‐CoV‐2; parainfluenza 1–4; hMPV; human bocavirus; adenovirus; rhinovirus/enterovirus; Chlamydophila pneumoniae; Mycoplasma pneumonia; Legionella pneumophila; Bordetella pertussis; and Bordetella parapertussis .

Study Variables

Data were collected on demographic variables (age, sex, date of diagnosis, and vaccination status), clinical variables (comorbidities and high‐risk conditions), signs and symptoms at admission, laboratory data at admission, and severity parameters. Severity was defined as the presence of any of the following: length of stay (LoS) at hospital ≥ 11 days; admission to the ICU; need for mechanical ventilation; hospital readmission within 90 days after discharge; and in‐hospital death. To determine the cut‐off for the number of the LoS days indicating severity, we have used the criterion established in other similar publications [30, 31, 32], in which this value has been defined as the 75th percentile of the median LoS for the entire group. In our study, the 75th percentile of the median LoS (7 days; IQR: 5–11) was 11 days, which enabled us to establish that a LoS equal to or greater than this period is considered a severe case.

Statistical Analysis

For the descriptive part of the study, demographics, clinical, and microbiological characteristics and outcomes were described in ER and hospitalized severe and nonsevere patients. Significance was tested using two‐sided chi‐square or Fisher's exact tests (categorical variables) and Student t or Mann–Whitney U tests (continuous variables), with p‐value < 0.1 considered statistically significant.

To calculate the hMPV hospitalization rate overall and by age, the total population aged ≥ 60 years in the Valladolid health area was used as a denominator. Then, the age‐specific hospitalization rates were calculated using the total number of people living in this area for each specific age group.

After that, a series of multivariable regression models were used to identify risk factors for hospitalization and for severe disease. Odds ratios (ORs) and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. We analyzed the data using both a stepwise and a LASSO approach. However, there were no substantial differences among them, so we finally selected the stepwise method. Then, the stepwise approach with both entry and exit level p‐values < 0.05 was applied in multivariable logistic regression models to identify potential confounders and to determine risk factors associated with disease outcomes. Statistically significant covariates were selected by the stepwise approach and then were combined with clinically significant covariates (such as age) into the final analyses. All data were processed and analyzed using SPSS v.23 and R software 4.3.

Ethical Approval

The study was approved by the Valladolid East Ethics Committee under the code PI‐25‐710‐H. This study adheres to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki for research involving human subjects.

Results

Patient Characteristics and Clinical Profile

Information was collected from a total of 26,115 patients with ARI requiring either hospitalization or ER attendance in any of the two hospitals included in the study. From them, a total of 7575 patients had a laboratory‐confirmed infection of any respiratory virus during the three seasons analyzed. Of these, a total of 4607 (60.8%) were attended at ER, and 2968 (39.2%) were hospitalized. A total of 703 patients had a laboratory‐confirmed hMPV infection (9.3%). Of these, 608 were hospitalized (86.5% of all hMPV cases), including 339 nonsevere patients (48.2%) and 269 (38.3%) severe patients. On the other hand, 95 patients were included who were seen only at ERs (13.5%). Patients hospitalized with hMPV accounted for 20.5% of all patients hospitalized with laboratory‐confirmed respiratory virus infections.

First, we analyzed the demographic and clinical characteristics by comparing hospitalized patients (severe and non‐severe) with patients treated in the ER (Table 1). The mean age of hospitalized patients (80.8 years; SD, 9.5) was significantly higher than that of ER patients (74.8 years; SD, 9.8) (p‐value < 0.001). Compared with patients treated in the ER, hospitalized patients were much more likely to live in nursing homes (23.0% vs. 6.3%) (p‐value < 0.001). In terms of comorbidities and risk factors, home oxygen use, acute heart failure, atrial fibrillation, anticoagulant use, and chronic renal failure were significantly more frequent among hospitalized patients than among ER patients (p < 0.1). In contrast, asthma and chronic bronchitis were more frequent in patients managed at ERs (p < 0.1). Co‐infections were also more frequent among hospitalized patients, especially those involving bacteria (p < 0.001). In terms of symptoms and laboratory parameters, hospitalized patients had significantly more frequent expectoration, dyspnea, wheezing, tachypnea, anemia, leukocytosis, neutrophilia, and desaturation (p < 0.01). In contrast, patients treated in the ER more often exhibited loss of appetite, odynophagia, and preserved vesicular murmur (p < 0.001).

Regarding severity parameters, a total of 175 (28.8%) hospitalized patients had a hospital stay of ≥ 11 days. Overall, severe and nonsevere hospitalized patients had a mean LoS of 10.5 days (SD, 14.3) and a median LoS of 7 days (IQR: 5–11), while severe patients had a mean LoS of 16.5 days (SD, 19.8). A total of 39 hospitalized patients (6.4%) had to be admitted to the ICU, with a mean ICU stay of 14.6 days (SD, 22.6). A total of 20 hospitalized patients (3.3%) required mechanical ventilation, 42 (6.9%) died, and 85 (14.0%) required readmission within 90 days.

Epidemiology and Seasonality of hMPV Infections Requiring Hospitalization

The prevalence of hMPV hospitalizations showed a clear epidemic pattern, starting in November, peaking in January–February and lasting until July across all three seasons studied (Figure 2). The hospitalization rate over the whole study period was 123.2 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants (95% CI; 113.6–133.4). Extrapolating this rate to Spain, a country with a total population of 12,567,543 inhabitants aged ≥ 60 years in 2022 [28], this corresponds to an estimated 11,704 hMPV‐related hospitalizations per year in this age group.

The orange (hMPV) and blue (non‐hMPV) dashed lines represent the 95% confidence intervals for the hMPV and non‐hMPV hospitalization rate, respectively.

For non‐hMPV viruses, the hospitalization rate during the whole study period was 478.1 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants (95% CI; 462.0–495.0). The highest monthly hospitalization rate was reached in January 2023 (2022–2023 season; 55.4 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants; 95% CI, 44.6–68.0). Then, a second peak was reached in February and March 2024 (2023–2024 season; 28.0 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants; 95% CI, 20.6–37.3). Although a clear epidemic pattern was observed, additional interseason hospitalization peaks were also corroborated during the study, in April 2022 (13.0 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants; 95% CI, 8.0–19.9) and in September 2024 (13.7 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants; 95% CI, 8.7–20.6).

Then, we analyzed the hospitalization rates by age group. The highest prevalence of hMPV hospitalizations was observed in patients aged ≥ 80 years (367.4 hospitalizations per 100.000 inhabitants; 95% CI, 322.2–417.2). Furthermore, this age group exhibited the highest prevalence of non‐hMPV hospitalizations (1369.3 hospitalizations per 100.000 inhabitants; 95% CI, 1280.7‐1462.5) (Figure 3). The peak of hMPV‐related hospitalizations in this age group was observed in February 2023 (171.2 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants), slightly exceeding the prevalence observed for non‐hMPV viruses (161.9 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants).

hMPV Risk Factors for Hospitalization and Severe Disease

In this section, we analyze the demographic and clinical factors related to hospitalization, severe disease, and other severity outcomes. First, we examined the factors related to hospitalization due to hMPV (Figure 4) (Table S1). People aged 75–84 years (OR = 4.7; 95% CI, 1.9–11.5) and ≥ 85 years (OR = 5.1; 95% CI, 1.6–15.9) have a higher risk of hospitalization than people aged 60–74 years (p < 0.05). On the other hand, co‐infections (OR = 2.9; 95% CI, 1.5–5.4), especially bacterial ones, anemia (OR = 3.4; 95% CI, 1.9–6.3), leukocytosis (OR = 2.4; 95% CI, 1.2–4.8), and desaturation (OR = 4.7; 95% CI, 2.8–7.9) at screening increase the risk of hospitalization.

Forest plot showing factors associated with hMPV hospitalization (ER patients were used as a comparator).

When analyzing the factors related to severe hospitalization, we considered those patients who had experienced at least one of the following five outcomes: hospitalization ≥ 11 days, ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, death, or readmission within 90 days. Multivariate analysis showed that residence in nursing homes (institutionalized) (OR = 1.6; 95% CI, 1.03–2.5), co‐infections (OR = 2.1; 95% CI, 1.5–2.9), and anemia (OR = 2.0; 95% CI, 1.4–2.9) are factors that predispose to severe infection (p‐value < 0.05) (Figure 5) (Table S2).

Forest plot showing factors associated with severe hMPV hospitalization (nonsevere hospitalizations were used as the comparator).

Finally, we performed a new multivariate analysis to check whether there were differences between the five different severity outcomes analyzed (Figure 6) (Tables S3–S7). This analysis showed that patients aged ≥ 85 years are less likely to experience a prolonged hospital stay of ≥ 11 days (OR = 0.5; 95% CI, 0.3–0.8), while co‐infections (OR = 3.4; 95% CI, 2.3–5.1), anemia (OR = 2.3; 95% CI, 1.6–3.5), and thrombocytopenia (OR = 1.7; 95% CI, 1.1–2.8) predispose to longer LoS (p‐value < 0.05). Similarly, patients aged 75–84 years (OR = 0.3; 95% CI, 0.2–0.8) and ≥ 85 years (OR = 0.2; 95% CI, 0.1–0.5) are less likely to be admitted to the ICU compared with those aged 60–74 years. In contrast, co‐infected patients were more frequently admitted to the ICU (OR = 2.6; 95% CI, 1.2–5.3). Regarding mortality, patients aged ≥ 85 years (OR = 4.6; 95% CI, 1.1–19.6) and those with chronic kidney disease (OR = 2.4; 95% CI, 1.2–4.6) had a higher risk of in‐hospital death after hMPV infection. Only patients with chronic lung disease showed an increased risk of readmission within 90 days (OR = 2.0; 95% CI, 1.2–3.3). No factors related to the use of mechanical ventilation were found.

Discussion

In our study, conducted over three consecutive seasons in Valladolid, Spain, after the COVID‐19 pandemic, we analyzed a total of 26,115 patients aged ≥ 60 years with ARI who required medical attention in either ER or hospital settings. Of these, 29% had a positive diagnosis for a respiratory virus, and within this group, 9.3% tested positive for hMPV. These data highlight the significant disease burden of hMPV in the elderly. Notably, 86.5% of patients aged ≥ 60 years with hMPV required hospitalization, demonstrating either the severity of the clinical processes caused by this virus in the older population or that diagnostic testing in the ER is only performed in patients who are more seriously ill. In either case, the proportion of hMPV in our study, compared to other respiratory viruses, is twice as high (20.5%) as the rates reported in other studies conducted in the United States, which show that hMPV accounts for between 8.5% and 9.8% of viruses detected in patients hospitalized with ARIs [14, 33]. A systematic review also showed that medically attended hMPV cases represent between 1.1% and 8.3% of infections [21], depending largely on the country reporting the data. Several factors may explain these differences. First of all, the increase in the number of requests for laboratory diagnoses following the COVID‐19 pandemic may have increased the representativeness of hMPV in hospitalized patients, bringing it closer to the actual figure. Furthermore, some studies, such as the one performed by A.S. Meulen et al. [21], included patients aged ≥ 18 years; therefore, the data are not directly comparable to ours, as we only analyzed patients aged ≥ 60 years.

We found that advanced age and the presence of comorbidities are clear predisposing factors for hospitalization due to hMPV, as are co‐infections, especially bacterial ones. In fact, our study population had a considerable burden of comorbidities, especially chronic respiratory, cardiovascular, and neurological conditions, which were particularly prevalent in patients hospitalized due to hMPV. Some studies have previously described that people hospitalized for hMPV more frequently suffer from underlying diseases [21, 34, 35], which is linked to a poorer response to infection and a higher risk of severe disease. In fact, according to some of these studies [35], more than 89% of patients diagnosed with hMPV who require medical attention have at least one comorbidity, which is also reflected in our data.

The seasonality of infections clearly reflects an epidemic pattern in hMPV circulation during the winter months, with an increase in hospitalizations in November–December, a peak between January and March, and widespread circulation until June–July. Most of the available studies also describe circulation centered on the winter months, very similar to what we observed in our study, and partially overlapping with RSV circulation [36, 37], resulting in a period of co‐circulation of both viruses that may extend beyond 3 months. This pattern seems to be repeated in various temperate countries, such as the United States, Canada, England, Germany, France, and Italy, with slight variations between the two viruses [4, 7, 38]. The hospitalization rate observed in our study was 123.2 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants, which was slightly lower than that described in a study conducted in the United States, which showed a rate of 231 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants (95% CI, 41–421) [15]. However, a meta‐analysis conducted in 2025 showed hospitalization rates for hMPV in older adults ranging from 41 to 593 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants across different countries [21], highlighting the great variability depending on the location. Our data revealed that, roughly, in Spain, there are over 11,700 hMPV‐related hospitalizations per season in individuals aged ≥ 60 years and that these hospitalizations are especially high in those aged ≥ 70 years.

Our study showed that 6.4% of patients aged ≥ 60 years hospitalized for hMPV infection required ICU admission, which is very similar to the findings of a systematic review conducted in 2025 (6.6%) [21]. These findings contrast with other studies conducted in the United States, which report ICU admission rates of up to 13.2% [33]. On the other hand, we observed that 3.3% of our patients required mechanical ventilation. To our knowledge, there are no data on mechanical ventilation beyond the study by Walsh et al. conducted in the United States, which reported a rate of 12.0% [33], and our own study. Furthermore, our data showed a mortality rate of 6.9%, which is very similar to that reported in various studies conducted in the United States (7%–8.7%) [19, 33] and slightly higher than rates reported in Canada (3.43%) [39] and France (4%) [40]. The aforementioned systematic review shows that, in high‐risk patients with hMPV, 6.6% required ICU admission, 9.3% died, and 7.8% required mechanical ventilation [21]. In addition, our data also showed that readmission at 90 days is 14.0%, much lower than that reported in the HARTI study (33%), which analyzed data from 12 different countries, although these refer to patients ≥ 18 years of age and during two seasons prior to the COVID‐19 pandemic [18]. Finally, the mean length of hospital stay was 10.5 days, which was similar but slightly longer than that reported in other studies, which ranged from 3 to 9 days [21, 35].

Our study confirms that advanced age is a significant risk factor for hospitalization due to hMPV. In fact, being over 75 years of age can increase the risk of hospitalization by threefold to fivefold compared with individuals aged 60–74 years. This is clearly seen when evaluating hospitalization rates in our study, as the highest rates were observed among patients aged ≥ 85 years. In fact, some studies show that the proportion of patients requiring hospitalization due to hMPV is up to 15% higher in those aged ≥ 75 years compared with the 65–74 age group [41].

Co‐infections are a clear predisposing factor for hospitalization and severe disease, increasing this risk by twofold to 2.8‐fold compared with patients in whom hMPV was detected as a mono‐infection. In this regard, it should be noted that 26.6% of hospitalized patients in our study had co‐infections involving at least one bacterium, which is very similar to other studies such as HARTI, which showed that bacterial co‐infections were present in 29% of hospitalized patients [7]. Other studies, such as that conducted by Q Philippot et al., showed similar data with co‐infection rates of 30% [35]. Co‐infections, especially bacterial ones, are a well‐established factor associated with worse disease progression. Several studies have shown that bacterial pathogens, such as Streptococcus pneumoniae and Staphylococcus aureus , are frequently linked to viral respiratory infections, such as influenza, increasing mortality by up to 3.36‐fold compared with non–co‐infected patients, particularly in the presence of comorbidities [42, 43]. Co‐infections were also a clear risk factor for prolonged hospital stays (≥ 11 days) and ICU admission, as co‐infected patients had a twofold to threefold higher risk of these outcomes. Notably, 28.8% of hospitalized patients in our cohort spent ≥ 11 days in hospital, which represents a substantial burden for this patient population.

Additionally, residing in institutional settings, such as nursing homes, is a risk factor that is also reflected in our findings. According to our data, living in this type of institution can increase the risk of hospitalization due to hMPV by 2.7‐fold and the risk of severe disease by up to 1.5‐fold. In this regard, it should be remembered that institutionalized patients are often very old and often have multiple comorbidities. Therefore, the likelihood of severe outcomes is undoubtedly higher. Furthermore, the closed nature of these institutions greatly facilitates the spread of the virus [44, 45]. Several studies have shown that living in nursing homes appears to be a risk factor for hospitalization and severe disease not only for hMPV but also for other viruses such as influenza and RSV [41, 46].

It is also important to note that the risk of being admitted to an ICU or requiring mechanical ventilation is lower in people aged > 75 years compared with younger patients in this study, despite the fact that mortality is higher, particularly in those aged > 85 years. This paradoxical effect is likely due to limitations in treatment for older people or, indeed, a preference for palliative care, as documented in studies such as that by C Brandberg et al. [47]. Indeed, in the latter study, the authors report that people aged over 80 receive, on average, 10% less invasive mechanical ventilation than those aged 65–79, and even when it is administered, the duration is, on average, almost three times shorter.

There were several limitations to this study. First, the retrospective nature of the study and the reliance on medical records may have resulted in incomplete data, as the information was not originally collected for the purpose of this study. On the other hand, some cases may have been missed among patients who were hospitalized but not tested in either hospital, which could be a bias that does not allow for a fully accurate representation of the burden of hMPV in this location. This limitation may be even more relevant in patients treated in the ER, where testing was likely performed primarily in those with more severe clinical presentations, even if they were not finally hospitalized. Another important limitation is the use of the stepwise method as the methodological approach for multivariate analysis, rather than other methods that do not overestimate the statistical significance of the variables analyzed to such an extent. However, as the LASSO method was carried out in parallel, which showed a very similar set of predictors and comparable effect estimates to the stepwise model, this suggests that the main findings are robust to the variable‐selection strategy. Also, Hosmer and Lemeshow goodness of fit (GOF) was performed for both methods (stepwise and LASSO), getting similar data for both of them. On the other hand, the greatest strength of this study is that the number of patients analyzed is quite high and over a substantial period of time (three seasons), which may provide a fairly representative overview of the reality of hMPV in individuals aged ≥ 60 years.

Conclusions

In conclusion, hMPV is associated with a high hospitalization rate in individuals aged ≥ 60 years, especially in the elderly and those with different comorbidities such as chronic cardiorespiratory diseases. Hospitalizations due to hMPV follow a classic seasonal pattern in the form of an epidemic, beginning in November–December and peaking in January–March. The hospitalization rate during the study period was 123.2 hospitalizations per 100,000 inhabitants (95% CI; 113.6–133.4). Patients hospitalized with hMPV had a mean hospital of stay of 10.5 days, 6.4% required ICU admission, 3.3% required mechanical ventilation, and 6.9% died after infection. Additionally, 14.0% required hospital readmission within 90 days. Advanced age emerged as one of the main risk factors for hospitalization due to hMPV, increasing the risk by threefold to fivefold in patients aged ≥ 75 years. Co‐infections, especially bacterial ones, are a clear factor that increases the risk of hospitalization and severe disease. Likewise, living in nursing homes increased the risk of hospitalization and severe disease by 1.5‐ to 2.7‐fold. Overall, the findings of this study emphasize the clinical relevance and disease burden of hMPV infections in the elderly and highlight the need for future vaccination plans and strategies against this respiratory virus.

Favezelimab plus pembrolizumab for anti–PD-1–refractory classic Hodgkin lymphoma: an open-label phase 1/2 study
Authors: John Timmerman, David Lavie, Nathalie A. Johnson, Abraham Avigdor, Peter Borchmann, Charalambos Andreadis, Ali Bazargan, Gareth P. Gregory, Colm Keane, Inna Tzoran, Vladan Vucinic, Pier Luigi Zinzani, Rachel Marceau West, Pallavi Pillai, Patricia Marinello, Alex F. Herrera
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•Favezelimab plus pembrolizumab showed sustained antitumor activity in participants with anti–PD-1–refractory cHL.•Favezelimab plus pembrolizumab had a manageable safety profile with no new safety signals observed.

Introduction

Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors are a standard-of-care option for relapsed or refractory (R/R) classic Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL).1,2 They are associated with high response rates, but relapse still occurs, and the optimal treatment approach after progression on anti–PD-1 therapy remains unclear.2,3

T cells play a critical role in cHL. Although enriched in the cHL tumor microenvironment, the T cells present are ineffectual because they tend to differentiate into T helper 2 or regulatory T cells, exhibit T-cell exhaustion, and have limited ability to recognize Reed-Sternberg cells.4 Lymphocyte activation gene 3 (LAG-3) is an immune checkpoint receptor, with a crucial role in regulating the adaptive immune response, that is widely expressed in the cHL tumor microenvironment, including on T cells.5, 6, 7, 8 The expression of LAG-3 on T cells leads to downregulation of the activity of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and enhances the immunosuppressive function of regulatory T cells, leading to impaired antitumor response.9 PD-1 also functions to regulate the adaptive immune response by inhibiting T-cell function via a different signaling pathway than LAG-3.9 Results of preclinical studies have shown that PD-1 and LAG-3 are coexpressed on tumor-infiltrating CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and that dual blockade of these receptors leads to decreased tumor growth and enhanced antitumor activity.10

Consequently, dual inhibition of PD-1 and LAG-3 is being evaluated clinically as a strategy for the treatment of cancer. This approach has been used successfully in advanced melanoma, with the anti–LAG-3 antibody relatlimab plus nivolumab improving progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared with nivolumab alone in the RELATIVITY-047 trial.11, 12, 13 The results led to the approval of a fixed-dose combination of relatlimab plus nivolumab for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic melanoma.14

The multicohort phase 1/2 MK-4280-003 study was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of favezelimab, an anti–LAG-3 monoclonal antibody, plus pembrolizumab in participants with R/R hematologic malignancies. We present results from the safety lead-in phase in participants with R/R hematologic malignancies and from the efficacy expansion phase in participants with anti–PD-1–refractory cHL (cohort 2).

Methods

Study design and treatment

The open-label, multicohort, phase 1/2 MK-4280-003 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03598608) was designed to evaluate favezelimab plus pembrolizumab in hematologic malignancies. The study included 2 phases: a safety lead-in phase designed to determine the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) of favezelimab when used in combination with pembrolizumab in participants with R/R hematologic malignancies; and an efficacy expansion phase that evaluated favezelimab plus pembrolizumab in participants with anti–PD-1–naive or anti–programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1)–naive R/R cHL (cohort 1), anti–PD-(L)1–refractory cHL (cohort 2), R/R diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL; cohort 3), and R/R indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma (cohort 4).

In the safety lead-in, participants received favezelimab at a starting dose of 200 mg, escalating to 800 mg per a modified toxicity probability interval design, plus pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. The safety lead-in phase concluded after 14 participants were treated at the selected dose level. The efficacy expansion phase was initiated after the RP2D was established. Participants who were enrolled in the efficacy expansion phase received favezelimab at the RP2D plus pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. Study treatment continued for up to 35 cycles (∼2 years) or until progressive disease, unacceptable toxicity, or other discontinuation criteria were met.

Participants

Participants eligible for the safety lead-in phase were aged ≥18 years; had histologically confirmed cHL, DLBCL, or indolent B-cell lymphoma; had measurable disease, defined as at least 1 lesion that could be accurately measured in 2 dimensions (>15 mm in longest diameter or >10 mm in the short axis); and had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status score of 0 or 1. Participants eligible for cohort 2 had R/R cHL that did not respond to, or progressed after, previous autologous stem cell transplantation (SCT), had not achieved complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) to salvage chemotherapy, or were ineligible for autologous SCT, and had to have progressive disease after at least 2 doses of anti–PD-1–based therapy and within 12 weeks of the last dose of anti–PD-1–based therapy. Participants who previously received anti–LAG-3 or chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy, had clinically active central nervous system involvement, or had clinically significant heart disease were excluded.

End points and assessments

The primary objectives were to determine the safety and RP2D of favezelimab plus pembrolizumab. Safety end points were the rate of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), adverse events (AEs), and AEs leading to treatment discontinuation. The secondary end point was objective response rate (ORR; defined as the proportion of participants who experienced CR or PR) per International Working Group 2007 criteria15 by investigator review. Exploratory end points included duration of response (DOR; defined as the time from first documented evidence of CR or PR until progressive disease or death from any cause, whichever occurred first) and PFS (defined as the time from the first dose of study treatment to the first documented progressive disease or death from any cause, whichever occurred first) per International Working Group 2007 criteria15 by investigator review and OS (defined as the time from the first dose of study treatment to death from any cause).

Participants were monitored for DLTs for 21 days from the first study dose. DLTs included grade 4 nonhematologic toxicity; grade 4 hematologic toxicity lasting >7 days; grade 4 platelet count decrease of any duration; grade 3 platelet count decrease if associated with bleeding; any grade 3 nonhematologic toxicity, excluding nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea, which was not considered a DLT unless it lasted >3 days despite optimal supportive care; any clinically significant grade 3 or 4 nonhematologic abnormal laboratory findings; grade 3 or 4 febrile neutropenia; any treatment-related AE that resulted in discontinuation of treatment; any grade 5 toxicity; and any treatment-related AE that caused more than a 2-week delay in initiation of the second cycle of treatment. AEs were monitored throughout the study and for 30 days after treatment end (90 days for serious AEs). Severity of AEs was graded per the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.0. Treatment-related AEs were determined by the investigator to be related to study treatment. Immune-mediated AEs and infusion reactions were based on a list of preferred terms intended to capture known risks of pembrolizumab and were considered regardless of attribution to study treatment by the investigator. Tumor imaging by computed tomography was performed at baseline and every 12 weeks thereafter, or more frequently if clinically indicated. Tumor imaging by positron emission tomography was performed at baseline, week 12, and week 24, and to confirm CR. Survival was assessed every 12 weeks after confirmed progressive disease or start of new anticancer therapy.

Statistical analysis

Safety was evaluated in all participants who received at least 1 dose of study treatment. DLT was evaluated in participants in the safety population who were observed for 21 days after the first dose of study treatment or experienced DLT before 21 days after the first dose of study treatment. Efficacy was evaluated in all participants who received at least 1 dose of study treatment and had a baseline assessment of measurable disease by investigator review. Planned enrollment for the efficacy expansion was ∼30 participants. An interim analysis of safety and efficacy was conducted after 12 participants were enrolled and the last participants completed the first response assessment or discontinued treatment. Safety and tolerability were summarized descriptively. For ORR, point estimates and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were derived using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate DOR, PFS, and OS. A post hoc analysis of ORR was conducted for participants who received anti–PD-1 or non–anti–PD-1 as their most recent previous therapy.

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and was approved by the appropriate institutional review boards and regulatory agencies. All participants provided written informed consent.

Results

Safety lead-in

The safety lead-in phase initially enrolled 6 participants who received favezelimab 200 mg plus pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. One DLT of grade 4 autoimmune hepatitis was observed. This patient was treated with steroids, and the autoimmune hepatitis resolved 7 days later; the steroids were tapered after resolution, and no further recurrence was reported. Based on these findings, the dose was increased to favezelimab 800 mg plus pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. Fifteen additional participants were treated with favezelimab 800 mg plus pembrolizumab 200 mg. No additional DLTs were observed. The RP2D was established as favezelimab 800 mg plus pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks.

Efficacy expansion: cohort 2

Between 20 January 2019 and 30 November 2021, 34 participants were enrolled in cohort 2 and received treatment. As of the data cutoff (22 February 2024), 8 participants had completed 35 cycles of treatment, and 26 participants had discontinued treatment because of progressive disease (n = 14), treatment-emergent AEs (n = 7), clinical progression (n = 2), nonstudy anticancer therapy (n = 1), physician decision (n = 1), or withdrawal by the participant (n = 1). The median time from first dose to the database cutoff date was 47.0 months (range, 26.7-61.1). Seventeen participants (50%) had received anti–PD-1 therapy as their most recent therapy; the median time from last dose of anti–PD-1 therapy to first dose of study treatment for these participants was 1.8 months (range, 0.7-14.7). Discussion

The results from the safety lead-in part of the phase 1/2 MK-4280-003 study showed that favezelimab plus pembrolizumab had manageable safety in participants with R/R hematologic malignancies and established the RP2D of the combination as favezelimab 800 mg plus pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. Results from the efficacy expansion portion of the study further showed the safety of the combination and that favezelimab plus pembrolizumab had antitumor activity in participants with heavily pretreated anti–PD-1–refractory cHL. These findings are of particular interest given PD-1 inhibitors are being evaluated earlier in the cHL treatment paradigm. Recent results from the phase 3 SWOG S1826 study showed that nivolumab plus AVD (doxorubicin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine) improved PFS compared with brentuximab vedotin plus AVD in participants with newly diagnosed advanced cHL, likely resulting in anti–PD-1 therapies being incorporated into first-line regimens.16 The safety and efficacy of treatment after anti–PD-1 therapy is, therefore, of substantial relevance to the field of oncology.

In this analysis, the safety profile of the combination of favezelimab and pembrolizumab was consistent with the safety profiles in studies of this combination in other indications, as well as in studies evaluating relatlimab combined with nivolumab. No new safety signals were observed, and no participant died of treatment-related AEs. In the first-in-human study of favezelimab plus pembrolizumab in participants with previously treated, advanced, microsatellite-stable colorectal cancer, treatment-related AEs occurred in 65.2% of participants, grade 3 or 4 treatment-related AEs occurred in 20.2%, and immune-mediated AEs occurred in 36.0% (including infusion-related reactions in 6.7%)17; these results are similar to those observed in this analysis. In RELATIVITY-047, which was designed to evaluate relatlimab combined with nivolumab in participants with melanoma, treatment-related AEs occurred in 83.7% of participants who received the combination, and grade 3 or 4 treatment-related AEs occurred in 21.1%; 15.2% of participants discontinued treatment because of treatment-related AEs, similar to the rate observed in this analysis (18%).12 The safety profile of favezelimab combined with pembrolizumab was also generally comparable with that observed with pembrolizumab monotherapy in participants with R/R cHL.18, 19, 20 The incidence of treatment-related AEs in this analysis (any grade, 82%; grade 3 or 4, 18%) was only marginally higher than that observed with pembrolizumab monotherapy in participants with R/R cHL in the KEYNOTE-013 (any grade, 71%; grade 3, 19%), KEYNOTE-087 (any grade, 72.9%; grade 3 or 4, 12.9%), and KEYNOTE-204 (any grade, 74%; grade 3, 16%; grade 4, 3%; grade 5, 1%) studies, despite the addition of favezelimab.18, 19, 20 The most common treatment-related AEs in this analysis were hypothyroidism (18%), nausea (18%), and fatigue (15%), which are AEs commonly observed with pembrolizumab.18, 19, 20 The incidence of any-grade immune-mediated AEs and infusion reactions in this analysis was similar to those observed with pembrolizumab monotherapy in R/R cHL (50% vs 45% in KEYNOTE-013; 34% in KEYNOTE-087; and 33% in KEYNOTE-204), as was the incidence of grade 3 or 4 immune-mediated AEs and infusions reactions (9% vs 10% in KEYNOTE-013; 2.9% in KEYNOTE-087; and 7% in KEYNOTE-204).19, 20, 21

Limited prospective data are available regarding the efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors in patients with anti–PD-1–refractory cHL, and there is no agreed-upon standard of care in this setting. In both the KEYNOTE-087 and CheckMate 205 studies, participants with cHL who initially achieved response and then experienced disease relapse after treatment with pembrolizumab or nivolumab, respectively, were eligible to receive a second course of anti–PD-1 therapy.19,22 Among 19 evaluable participants in KEYNOTE-087 who received a second course of pembrolizumab, the ORR was 73.7%, with 7 participants achieving CR and 7 achieving PR,19 although it should be noted that the definition of anti–PD-1–refractory used in this analysis was more stringent than the eligibility for a second course of pembrolizumab in KEYNOTE-087. In CheckMate 205, 3 participants were retreated with nivolumab; 2 achieved CR and 1 had PR.22 In a phase 2 study that was undertaken to evaluate combination therapy with the DNA-demethylating agent decitabine plus the PD-1 inhibitor camrelizumab, a high response rate and long-term benefits were observed in participants with R/R cHL who experienced relapse after PD-1 blockade.23 Results of a phase 1 trial undertaken to evaluate combination therapy of pembrolizumab plus the histone deacetylase inhibitor vorinostat showed manageable safety and a high ORR rate in participants with R/R cHL, including participants with anti–PD-1–refractory disease.24 Results of 2 retrospective studies also showed efficacy with PD-1 blockade in anti–PD-1–refractory cHL, with activity observed with use of nivolumab or pembrolizumab after avelumab and with use of nivolumab plus brentuximab vedotin after nivolumab.25,26

Dual inhibition of LAG-3 and PD-1 is being investigated in several tumor types, but investigation has progressed furthest in melanoma. Based on the results of the phase 2/3 RELATIVITY-047 trial, combination therapy with relatlimab plus nivolumab is recommended as a treatment option for participants with unresectable or metastatic melanoma.11, 12, 13,27 Relatlimab combined with nivolumab has also been evaluated in participants with advanced melanoma that progressed with anti–PD-(L)1 therapy in the phase 1/2a RELATIVITY-020 trial; results showed that the combination had manageable safety and durable clinical activity.28 However, as with MK-4280-003, RELATIVITY-020 was limited by the single-arm design, raising the question of the contribution of the LAG-3 inhibitor to the efficacy of the combination. In a post hoc analysis that included participants from this study, the relative efficacy of favezelimab and the efficacy of favezelimab combined with pembrolizumab was evaluated by comparing it with participants with cHL who received pembrolizumab monotherapy after disease progression in KEYNOTE-087.29 The results showed that favezelimab combined with pembrolizumab was associated with a higher ORR (37% vs 2%) and deeper response than pembrolizumab alone, indicating that favezelimab contributed substantially to the efficacy observed.29 The combination of relatlimab plus nivolumab is also being evaluated in pediatric and young adult participants with Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (RELATIVITY-069; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05255601), in gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (RELATIVITY-060),30 and in microsatellite instability–high or mismatch repair–deficient metastatic colorectal cancer (CheckMate 142).31 Tebotelimab, a PD-1– and LAG-3–targeting bispecific molecule, is being evaluated in advanced neoplasms, including hematologic malignancies, and promising results are being reported for participants with DLBCL.32 Combination treatment with ieramilimab (anti–LAG-3) and spartalizumab (anti–PD-1) has shown antitumor activity across different solid tumor indications in a phase 1/2 trial and a phase 2 trial.33,34 The phase 2 KEYFORM-008 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05508867) was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a coformulation of favezelimab and pembrolizumab in a larger population of participants with anti–PD-1–refractory cHL; publication of the results is pending.

The primary limitations of this study were the small participant population and lack of a comparator treatment arm. These limitations are typical of early phase clinical trials but still limit the conclusions that can be drawn from the study. Furthermore, although patient samples were collected for correlative studies, LAG-3 analyses were limited by small sample size, and no clear trends were identified. Overall, the results of this analysis showed that favezelimab combined with pembrolizumab had manageable safety and antitumor activity in participants with heavily pretreated anti–PD-1–refractory cHL.

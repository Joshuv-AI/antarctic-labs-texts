The genetic structure and selective sweep analysis of meat pigeon breeds raised in Hainan using whole genome resequencing technology
Authors: Zixin Xu, Tieshan Xu, Shun Guo, Dingping Bai, Li Zhang, Yaya Sun, Wenpeng Xia, Yuting Liu, Mengru Xue, Lihong Gu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the genetic relationships and domestication status of Hainan meat pigeon populations, whole-genome sequencing was performed on 70 individuals across seven breeds: Shiqi (SQ), Silver King (SK), Shenwang (SW), Dabao (DB), Tianxiang No.1 (TX), Hybrid King (HK), and White Carneau (WC), with each breed represented by 10 individuals (5 males and 5 females). From 824 Gb of high-quality sequencing data, we identified 13,347,452 high-confidence single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). Population structure analysis revealed divergent genetic clustering: WC, DB, TX, and HK individuals demonstrated extensive genomic admixture, while SK, SQ, and SW breeds formed distinct clusters with minimal interbreed genetic exchange. Selective sweep analysis highlighted genes under strong positive selection (TAFA2, FNDC3B, TFAP2B, PDCL3, EPC1 and LHX2). Functional annotation linked TAFA2 to accelerated growth in SK pigeons through muscle development modulation. The genes FNDC3B and TFAP2B were associated with fat deposition capacity in SQ pigeons. Concurrently, PDCL3, NEK6, LHX2, and EPC1 were implicated in SW pigeons' large body size and superior production traits. This study provides the first genomic compendium for Hainan meat pigeons, delineating their genetic diversity, adaptive evolution, and domestication patterns. The identified selection signatures establish a foundation for precision breeding strategies targeting growth, reproduction, and climate resilience in commercial pigeon production.Keywords: Pigeon, SNP, Population structure, Selective sweep

Introduction

Pigeons are widely distributed throughout the world, and many cultures have a tradition of domesticating or eating pigeon meat(Lan et al., 2025). Globally, domesticated pigeons are primarily categorized by their primary use: homing pigeons, utility pigeons (raised for meat), and fancy (ornamental) pigeons(Liu and Li, 2021). China plays a dominant role in the global meat pigeon industry. Currently, China's squab output represented over 80 % of the world's total production(Jiang et al., 2019). The United States and Canada are also major producers, with the United States producing approximately 2.5 million squabs annually; In addition, the largest producers in Europe are the United Kingdom, France, and Italy, and pigeon farming is also popular in Denmark, Germany, Spain, and Hungary (Kokoszyński et al., 2020). Recognizing its scale, the Chinese Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs formally classified pigeons as the nation's fourth major poultry species (after chickens, ducks, and geese) in 2020. By 2023, China's breeding stock had grown to nearly 50 million pairs (49.94 million), with annual squab slaughter reaching 716 million (Yang et al., 2023). Within China, regions offering optimal conditions, such as Hainan Province with its favorable warm climate (average annual temperatures of 22-27°C / 72-81°F), have seen particularly rapid development in the pigeon breeding sector.(Luo et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2019).

Third-generation DNA markers, based on single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and insertions/deletions (InDels), have been widely used in the assessment of genetic diversity and germplasm identification in both animals and plants, due to developments in sequencing technologies(Koshiishi and Wada, 2024; Saadatabadi et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2019). The computational comparison and whole-genome resequencing is very effective in comparing the genetic differences between various targets(Xiong et al., 2023). Shao et al. identified many positively selected genes related to the central nervous system, particularly those related to spatial learning and memory, such as LRP8, by comparing the genomes of homing pigeon populations with those of other breeds to reveal the genetic basis of homing pigeons' long-distance navigation abilities(Shao et al., 2020). Hou et al. compared the genomes of commercial meat pigeons, European pigeons, Silver King pigeons, and high-flying ornamental pigeons, combining selection signals with the pectoral muscle transcriptome to reveal multiple genes related to muscle development and function, such as SLC16A10(Hou et al., 2021). Although numerous breeds of meat pigeons have been introduced or bred in recent years, the examination of genetic diversity in regional populations of meat pigeons in Hainan Province, however, has not been documented. To elucidate the genetic relationship of meat pigeon populations in Hainan and its direction of selection and breeding, we selected seven meat pigeon breeds for whole-genome resequencing and investigating genetic diversity and population structure. The results will provide valuable data support for the conservation and utilization of genetic resources in meat pigeon breeds raised in Hainan province.

Materials and methods

Animals

This study incorporated 70 pigeons representing seven commercial breeds (10 individuals per breed with 1:1 sex ratio), systematically sourced from specialized breeding institutions. The Dabao (DB), Shiqi (SQ), and Hybrid King (HK) breeds were procured from Dove Delicacies Agricultural Science and Technology Co., Ltd (Hainan, China). Similarly, the White Carneau (WC), Tianxiang No.1 (TX), and Silver King (SK) breeds were obtained from Haikou Laihe Li Pigeon Professional Cooperative (Hainan, China). The Shenwang (SW) breeding cohort was acquired from Hainan Tianyuan Dafeng Pigeon Agricultural Science and Technology Co., Ltd (Hainan, China).

In addition, 23-day-old male pigeons of the SK, SQ, SW, TX and DB breeds (n = 20) were collected. The TX and DB breeds were used to represent the HZ group. Measure the body dimensions and weight according to Performance terminology and measurements for poultry: NY/T 823—2020(Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People's Republic of China, 2020). Samples of the lateral thigh skeletal muscle were taken for RT-qPCR (Reverse Transcription–quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction) validation of the candidate genes.

Blood sample collection and DNA extraction

About 2.5ml of blood was drawn from the brachial vein and preserved by adding ACD anticoagulant before being placed in a freezer set to -20°C. The manufacturer's instructions were followed to extract DNA using a blood DNA extraction kit from Tiangen Biotech (Beijing, China) Co., Ltd. Following agarose gel electrophoresis, the isolated DNA was quantified using Qubit® 2.0 analysis.

Library construction and DNA sequencing

GenoBaits® DNA Library Prep Kit (Biovision, USA) was used to create a resequencing library from 2 µg of genomic DNA. qPCR analysis and the Qubit® 2.0 Fluorometer were used to assess the quality of the DNA. Finally, sequencing was carried out by BGI (Beijing Genomics Institute, China) on the MGI-T7 sequencing platform utilizing the PE150 sequencing mode.

Quality control and SNP calling

The raw sequencing data underwent a comprehensive and stringent quality control and processing pipeline. Initially, the reads were filtered using fastp v0.20.0(Chen et al., 2018) with strict parameters. These parameters included discarding reads with more than 10 % N bases (-n 10), setting a Phred quality threshold of 20 (-q 20), and retaining reads with no more than 40 % unqualified bases (-u 40). This step effectively removed low-quality sequences, resulting in high-confidence clean reads. Subsequently, the processed reads were mapped to the Columba livia reference genome (GCA_036013475.2, NCBI Assembly) using BWA-MEM v0.7.18(Li, 2013) with default parameters. For variant calling, GATK4 v4.1.6.0(Poplin et al., 2017) HaplotypeCaller was employed for joint SNP/InDel calling. This was followed by a multistage filtering process. In the primary filtering stage, bcftools v1.13(Li, 2011) was used to retain variants that met the following criteria: Quality-by-Depth (QD) ≥3.0, Genotype Quality (GQ) ≥20, Fisher Strand Bias (FS) ≤60, Mapping Quality (MQ) ≥40, MQRankSum ≥-12.5, and ReadPosRankSum ≥-8.0. The secondary filtering was performed using PLINK v1.90, applying the following thresholds: a genotype call rate of more than 90 %, a minor allele frequency (MAF) of at least 0.05, and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium P < 1 × 10−6. The phased quality controls at each processing step ensured the reliability of the data.

Population structure analysis

Genome-wide linkage disequilibrium (LD) analysis was performed using PopLDdecay v3.41(Zhang et al., 2019), with pairwise SNP r² values calculated to quantify allelic associations. LD decay patterns were visualized through internal R scripts, plotting mean r² values against physical distances (0-300 kb). To minimize LD-induced bias, SNP pruning was conducted via PLINK v1.90(Purcell et al., 2007) using the parameter set "–indep-pairwise 50 10 0.2" (window size: 50 SNPs; step size: 10 SNPs; r² threshold: 0.2). Population structure was interrogated through three complementary approaches: A Neighbor-Joining tree constructed in MEGA11(Tamura et al., 2021) using Reynolds genetic distances with 1,000 bootstrap replicates; Principal component analysis (PCA) of pruned SNPs, calculated by PLINK; Genome-wide identity-by-state (IBS) matrix calculation implemented in PLINK. Phylogenetic tree visualization was enhanced using iTOL v6.8(Letunic and Bork, 2024) with interactive annotation capabilities.

Selective sweep analysis

Prior to selective sweep analysis, rigorous genomic data filtration was implemented: (1) Sex chromosomal regions were excluded, retaining only autosomal SNPs; (2) Outliers were removed from all 70 individuals. Genomic differentiation was quantified through joint computation of population differentiation (Fst) and nucleotide diversity ratio (θπ ratio) using vcftools v0.1.16(Danecek et al., 2011), employing 40-kb sliding windows with 10-kb steps. Statistical normalization included Z-score transformation of Fst values and log2 transformation of θπ ratio to mitigate distributional skewness. Candidate domestication regions (CDRs) were identified through dual-threshold filtering: comparative group CDRs were defined as genomic regions simultaneously ranking in the top 1 % of Fst values and bottom 1 % of log2(θπ ratio) values across an intergroup comparison. Conversely, reference group CDRs required concurrent top 1 % rankings in both metrics. The CDRs obtained from the three comparisons were combined to obtain the total CDRs for a population. KEGG pathway analysis with P < 0.01 significance thresholds of CDR-associated genes was performed using Metascape(Zhou et al., 2019).

Result

SNPs calling statistics

The whole genome resequencing generated 824 Gb of high-quality data with an average sequencing depth of 10.19× per individual, identifying 24,550,685 raw SNPs and 3,199,016 indels. After primary filtering, 22,960,791 SNPs and 2,840,120 indels were retained, with subsequent rigorous filtering yielding 13,347,452 high-confidence SNPs (Fig. 2A&B). Analysis of substitution patterns revealed a transition/transversion (TS/TV) ratio of 2.3 (9,298,373 TS vs. 4,049,079 TV), aligning with vertebrate genomic polymorphism trends. Functional annotation classified 9,086,251 SNPs in coding regions and 4,261,201 SNPs in non-coding regulatory elements (Table S1).

Types and distribution of SNPs. (A) Variant types of SNPs after secondary filtering. (B) Heatmap of the distribution of SNPs across the genome after secondary filtering.

Population structure analysis

Population structure analysis (Fig. 3A-B) revealed distinct genetic clustering patterns: individuals from the SK, SQ, and SW groups formed independent monophyletic clusters, whereas those from DB, TX, WC, and HK exhibited significant admixture. Based on these findings, we consolidated the admixed populations (DB, TX, WC, HK) into a hybrid group (HZ) and excluded genetic outliers for downstream selective sweep analyses. Comparative evaluation of LD decay rates across groups (Fig. 3C) demonstrated that SW maintained the highest LD persistence (r² = 0.1102 at 250 kb), followed by SK (0.1040), SQ (0.1019), and HZ (0.0446), suggesting reduced genetic diversity in the purebred SW group whereas the admixed HZ group displayed elevated diversity due to interbreeding-driven genetic introgression. IBS distance analysis (Fig. 3D) further indicated high genomic similarity among all individuals, with pairwise IBS distances exceeding 0.7 across the cohort.

Selective sweep analyses revealed distinct candidate domestication regions (CDRs) across groups. The SK group exhibited 6.45 Mb of CDRs encompassing 101 genes, with GSK3B, PDE3B, PRKAA2, and ATP6V1H significantly enriched in insulin signaling (P = 4 × 10−3) and mTOR pathways (P = 6.3 × 10−3). A prominent selection signal was identified at the TAFA2 locus (Chr1:176,963,588-177,158,165 bp), showing consistent signals across all SK comparisons (Fig. 4E), supported by elevated Fst values (up to 0.39) and log₂ (θπ ratio) < -1.78 (Figs. 4B-D). The SQ group displayed 6.84 Mb of CDRs containing 124 genes, including ADCY2, CACNA1C, and RYR2 enriched in oxytocin signaling pathway (P = 8 × 10−4), insulin secretion (P = 1.6 × 10−3) and circadian entrainment (P = 2.5 × 10−3), potentially reflecting adaptations to tropical photoperiod cycles. Among the candidate genes identified in each comparison, three genes consistently appeared: SLC12A7, TFAP2B, and FNDC3B. In the SW group, 6.48 Mb CDRs harbored 124 genes, with CRHR2, AFDN, MAPK10, and RYR2 linked to cAMP signaling (P = 7.9 × 10−3), while the genes PDCL3, NEK6, LHX2, EPC1, and C11orf24 were consistently identified as candidate genes in all three comparisons of the SW group. In the HZ group, the total CDRs amounted to 6.15 Mb, spanning 122 genes. Notably, six genes (ADCY1, EDNRB, KDR, PHKA2, RYR3, and BEST2) were significantly enriched in the Calcium signaling pathway (P = 4 × 10−4) and Salivary secretion (P = 1.6 × 10−3).

Discussion

Population structure

Population genetic structure analysis provides critical insights into understanding evolutionary potential(Hansen, 2006). Our analyses revealed distinct clustering patterns: individuals from the DB, TX, WC, and HK groups exhibited significant genetic admixture, forming a cohesive cluster designated as the HZ category. This extensive intergroup genetic homogeneity suggests high population mobility and active gene flow among these groups. In contrast, the SK, SQ, and SW groups constituted a separate phylogenetic branch, with individuals clustering according to species-specific divisions. Notably, these three groups demonstrated substantial genetic divergence from the HZ cluster, consistent with previous findings by Hou et al classified SK, SQ, and SW within a unified phylogenetic lineage(Hou et al., 2022). The observed genetic architecture implies limited historical gene exchange between the HZ cluster and the SK-SQ-SW lineage, potentially reflecting differential evolutionary trajectories or ecological adaptations. On the other hand, unlike chickens, there is no standardized grandparent-parent breeding system for meat pigeons. Furthermore, self-breeding and self-raising lead to problems such as genetic resource degradation and breed mixing(Sun, 2023). LD analysis demonstrated significantly reduced r² values in the HZ group compared to other groups at matched genomic distances, indicative of elevated genetic diversity and ongoing inter-subgroup gene flow within the HZ group. Complementing these findings, IBS distance matrix analysis revealed a conserved genomic architecture across all individuals, with minimum IBS distances exceeding 0.7 – reflecting ∼70 % allele sharing across loci, consistent with high intra-group genetic homogeneity despite subgroup differentiation.

Selection signal

Selective sweep analysis serve as a critical methodology for investigating directional selection pressures in both natural and artificial selection processes(Panigrahi et al., 2023). A comparison with the study by Hou et al. (Hou, et al., 2022) revealed that when the Silver King pigeon was used as a control, four (6 %) of the selected genes in the Shiqi pigeon were consistent with those in the study by Hou et al., while no consistent genes were found in the selected genes of the Shen King pigeon. As mentioned earlier, commercial pigeon breeding has not yet established a large-scale standard system and remains somewhat chaotic. We think this is the main reason for the differences, on the other hand, different test procedures and selection thresholds should not be overlooked.

Silver King Pigeon Selection Signal. The SK pigeon, an American-developed meat pigeon breed originating from California in 1949, exhibits exceptional commercial traits including accelerated squab growth, high feed conversion efficiency, and superior meat quality characterized by tenderness(Tang et al., 2018). Ni et al. studied the weights of pigeons at 28 days of age and found that YW pigeons grew faster(Mu et al., 2022). In Table S4, we also found that 23-day-old YW pigeons had the highest body weight. Our analysis revealed pronounced selection signals in the SK group at the gene TAFA2, demonstrating significantly elevated FST (largest) values in its coding regions compared to other breeds. TAFA protein is widely expressed, with the highest expression in brain tissue, where it acts as a neurotrophic factor. Functionally, TAFA2 regulates appetite control and metabolic homeostasis (Okada et al., 2019), with genetic variants in this gene strongly correlated with enhanced insulin sensitivity (Walford et al., 2016). Recent studies demonstrate that TAFA2 enhances migration, recruitment, and proliferation of human mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) without affecting their differentiation into osteoblasts or adipocytes(Jafari et al., 2019). Based on these findings, Takaya et al. hypothesize that during broiler muscle development, myoblast-secreted TAFA2 may recruit MSCs into muscle tissue(Takaya et al., 2021). In this study, qPCR analysis revealed the lowest TAFA2 expression in SK—a fast-growing pigeon breed (23 days old, growing period). This suggests that TAFA2 might be highly expressed during embryonic stages to recruit MSCs, while its expression is downregulated in growth phases to prevent excessive proliferation from interfering with differentiation. Further investigation is required to elucidate the precise role of this gene in skeletal muscle. Pathway enrichment analysis further identified coordinated activation of insulin and mTOR signaling cascades. Mechanistically, insulin stimulates muscle protein synthesis through PI3K/Akt-mediated activation of mTOR, facilitating cellular amino acid uptake (Ramasubbu and Devi Rajeswari, 2023). These pathways significantly enhance growth and production performance by modulating key aspects of metabolism and cell activity(Dibble and Cantley, 2015).

Shiqi Pigeon Selection Signal. The SQ pigeons, an indigenous breed officially recognized in China and native to Guangdong Province, demonstrates remarkable environmental adaptability and superior reproductive efficiency, particularly in fertilization success, incubation capacity, and squab rearing performance(Tang, et al., 2018). Ni et al. studied the reproductive performance indicators and mortality rates of different pigeon breeds and found that SQ pigeons have good egg-laying performance and strong disease resistance(Mu, et al., 2022). When other different groups were selected as controls, three genes were subject to positive selection, namely SLC12A7, TFAP2B, and FNDC3B. Among these, TFAP2B promotes the differentiation of melanocytes and may regulate melanin production in skeletal muscle(Dou et al., 2022); FNDC3B regulates the browning of white fat and fat production(Zhang et al., 2020). The qPCR results of this study showed that the FNDC3B gene expression level was highest in SQ among the four groups. Positive selection in the region where this gene is located may lead to increased expression levels, regulating metabolism and enhancing fat synthesis. Four KEGG pathways—calcium signaling, insulin secretion, oxytocin signaling, and purine metabolism—were significantly enriched and may be related to the meat and egg production performance of Shiqi pigeons. In addition, two KEGG pathways, Circadian entrainment and Phosphatidylinositol signaling, were significantly enriched, which may mediate the tropical adaptability and disease resistance of the Shiqi pigeon(Dakhiya and Green, 2024; Wang et al., 2022). What is more, we observed significant enrichment in three pathways: calcium signaling pathway, cAMP signaling pathway, and dopaminergic synapse, we speculated that this may be related to the flight capabilities of birds. The flight ability of birds is an outstanding product of the co-evolution of neural regulation and metabolic adaptation. The dopaminergic system, as the “command center” for motor commands, enhances energy mobilization and motivational drive through the cAMP pathway(Srinivasan et al., 2015). Calcium signaling, as the “execution terminal”, converts neural electrical activity into mechanical contraction(Tang and Bezprozvanny, 2004).

Shenwang Pigeon Selection Signal. The SW pigeons, developed through selective breeding of exhibition-type King pigeons originally imported from Australia in 1987 by Tian Xiang Da Pigeon Co. (Guangdong, China), exhibits distinctive physical characteristics including an exceptionally large body size and broad chest structure(Tang, et al., 2018). Ni et al. studied and compiled statistics on the body measurements and weights of different pigeon breeds and found that SW pigeons are larger in size, with female pigeons significantly heavier and wider in chest and pelvis than WC and YW pigeons(Ni et al., 2019). In Table S4, we also found that SW pigeons had the deepest and widest chests. Comparative genomic analyses identified shared CDRs containing five key genes - C5H11orf24, PDCL3, NEK6, LHX2, and EPC1 - that were all subject to selection pressure in SW compared to three other pigeon groups. The limb development-associated LHX2 gene contributes to skeletal formation patterns(Bach et al., 1999), while EPC1 interacts with HOPX-encoded proteins to potentiate skeletal muscle differentiation pathways(Davis et al., 2015). Of biological relevance, PDCL3(phosphoprotein-like protein 3) functions as a molecular chaperone regulating Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptor-2 (VEGFR-2) expression and angiogenesis processes critical for vascular development(Srinivasan, et al., 2015). In this study, qPCR results showed that the PDCL3 gene expression level in SW was the highest among the four groups. The selection of the PDCL3 gene in the SW population may have led to increased expression levels of this gene, thereby better regulating vascular formation. These molecular features collectively provide a genetic foundation for understanding SW's pronounced morphological characteristics and superior growth performance, with PDCL3-mediated vascular development and EPC1-driven myogenesis being particularly influential in establishing the breed's robust physique and enhanced productive capabilities.

Conclusion

This study employed whole-genome resequencing technology to investigate the genetic relationships and genomic characteristics of artificially selected meat pigeon breeds in Hainan. Population structure analysis revealed that individuals from four breeds (WC, DB, TX, and HK) exhibited mixed genetic clustering, while three breeds (SK, SQ, and SW) formed distinct independent clusters. Selective sweep analysis identified several functional genes under significant selection pressure, including TAFA2, FNDC3B, TFAP2B, PDCL3, EPC1 and LHX2. Among these, TAFA2 was proposed as a key candidate gene responsible for the rapid growth trait in the SK pigeons. The genes FNDC3B and TFAP2B were associated with fat deposition capacity in SQ pigeons. Meanwhile, the gene group containing PDCL3, NEK6, LHX2, and EPC1 was identified as a critical genetic basis for the large body size and superior production performance of the SW pigeons.

Hybrid control structure employing fuzzy-sliding mode systems for the guidance of coaxial octorotor UAV
Authors: Khaled Toudji, Lakhmissi Cherroun, Mohamed Nadour, Yousef A Alsabah, Abdelaziz Rabehi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, the design and simulation of a hybrid control structure using a fuzzy sliding mode controller (FSMC) and PID regulators for a six-DOF coaxial octorotor unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) are presented. The dynamic model of the coaxial octorotor is derived using the Newton–Euler formalism and decomposed into four control subsystems: altitude, pitch, roll, and yaw. PID controllers are used in the translational x and y position loops to generate desired pitch and roll references, while the FSMC regulates the four main attitude and altitude subsystems. The proposed FSMC integrates fuzzy logic with the sliding mode framework to eliminate the chattering phenomenon while maintaining robustness against model uncertainties and external disturbances. To establish a rigorous proof of concept, this study is focused on a high-fidelity simulation environment. MATLAB/Simulink simulations under these controlled conditions demonstrate the superior accuracy, stability, and disturbance rejection capability of the FSMC compared to conventional methods in both nominal and perturbed flight scenarios. It is important to note that the present work serves as a foundational theoretical and numerical validation; practical implementation factors such as actuator saturation, sensor noise, and real-time computational constraints, while critical for final deployment, are reserved for subsequent experimental and hardware-in-the-loop studies.Keywords: Modelling, Control, Octorotor, Sliding mode, Fuzzy logic, PID, Coupled systems, MIMOSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Multirotor unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have evolved from their initial hobbyist use to become essential instruments in numerous professional fields, such as search and rescue, precision agriculture, environmental monitoring, and infrastructure inspection1–6. Their defining operational features, including hovering, vertical take-off and landing (VTOL), and maneuvering in confined spaces, make them uniquely adaptable. Among various configurations, the coaxial octorotor stands out by offering enhanced payload capacity, inherent mechanical redundancy, and a compact footprint. Such versatility is achieved by placing pairs of counter-rotating rotors on the same axis at each arm’s end7,8, reducing the number of arms required compared to a conventional star-type octorotor and resulting in a more stable and agile structure9.

However, the control of coaxial octorotors presents significant challenges. Their dynamics are inherently nonlinear and underactuated and represent a complex multi-input multi-output (MIMO) system with strong coupling between axes10,11. These characteristics render simple linear controllers, such as PIDs, insufficient for high-performance applications, as they struggle with robustness under large parameter variations and external disturbances4. This limitation has motivated the widespread adoption of robust nonlinear control strategies.

Sliding Mode Control (SMC) has been a particularly prominent solution due to its compelling invariance properties once the system trajectory reaches the sliding surface, providing inherent robustness to matched uncertainties and disturbances10,12,13. Despite its strengths, the practical application of conventional SMC is plagued by the “chattering” phenomenon—high-frequency oscillations around the sliding surface, which can excite unmodelled dynamics and cause undue mechanical wear on actuators.

The need to mitigate chattering while preserving robustness has driven the development of hybrid and intelligent control approaches. A foundational strategy has been the integration of SMC with fuzzy logic. Early work by Zeghlache et al.7 demonstrated that using a Type-2 Fuzzy Logic System to generate the switching signal could smooth control actions and significantly reduce chattering. Mekki et al.14 and Zeghlache et al.3 extended this approach, developing adaptive fuzzy schemes and fault-tolerant controls for coaxial octorotors, validated through Lyapunov stability theory. The integration of fuzzy logic with sliding mode control remains an active and evolving area of research. Recent studies continue to highlight the strong performance of Fuzzy-SMC (FSMC) approaches across different UAV platforms15,16, demonstrating improved trajectory tracking and disturbance rejection for various UAV platforms.

The development of higher-order sliding modes17 and adaptive SMC techniques18 has provided sophisticated methods to attenuate chattering. Furthermore, the integration of SMC with other computational intelligence paradigms, such as neural networks, has shown promise for handling complex uncertainties19,20. Beyond SMC, other robust nonlinear strategies like backstepping control have been successfully applied to octorotors, as seen in21, and the use of meta-heuristic algorithms for auto-tuning controller parameters is another growing trend to optimize performance22,23. Recent studies have also explored control strategies for coaxial octorotors using learning-based methods, such as Neuro-LQR for swarm synchronization in uncertain environments24. In contrast, the FSMC-PID approach proposed here is fully model-based and does not rely on learning. It is tailored for single-agent trajectory tracking, with a focus on predictable robustness and low computational demand rather than adaptive capabilities. This distinction clearly situates our work within the domain of deterministic, analytically grounded control strategies for coaxial platforms.

Recent advancements continue to push the boundaries of UAV control. For instance, model-free adaptive control techniques have shown promise in handling complex dynamics without precise models25. Similarly, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is emerging as a powerful tool for end-to-end flight control, though it demands significant computational resources and training data26. Within the SMC domain, the Super-Twisting Algorithm (STA) and higher-order SMC variants have become benchmarks for chattering reduction and finite-time convergence, as discussed in works like25–37.

A key design decision in fuzzy control, however, lies in choosing between Type-1 and Type-2 fuzzy systems. Type-2 fuzzy logic, as investigated in recent works38–40, is particularly effective at handling uncertainty and measurement noise. The downside is that it comes with higher computational cost and more complex design and tuning processes. For this initial simulation-based proof of concept on a coaxial octorotor, we intentionally adopt a simpler Type-1 Mamdani fuzzy system. This choice offers a practical balance: it reduces chattering effectively while remaining computationally efficient and easier to design. It also provides a clear and solid baseline, which can be extended to more advanced Type-2 approaches in future work.

For example, a recent study41 introduced A unified framework combining robust sliding mode control, RBF neural networks, RRT* path planning, and PSO optimization. Although it delivers strong performance, it typically requires careful gain tuning and involves quite complex stability analysis. By comparison, the FSMC approach we propose is designed to be more straightforward and transparent, with lower computational demands, making it easier to implement in practice especially on coaxial platforms. While the aforementioned methods offer high performance, a persistent gap exists for solutions that balance sophisticated robustness with straightforward design and computational efficiency, particularly for mechanically complex platforms like the coaxial octorotor. This is the niche our work aims to fill.

Despite these advancements, there is a persistent need for control solutions that strike a balance between high performance, computational efficiency, and practical implementation, especially for complex platforms like the coaxial octorotor. Many advanced methods can introduce significant computational complexity or require intricate stability proofs that hinder real-world deployment.

This work presents the development, validation, and critical analysis of a novel hybrid FSMC-PID architecture explicitly designed for the complex dynamics of a coaxial octorotor UAV. While hybrid FSMC–PID schemes are established in the literature, our contribution is distinct in its structured, scope-aware, and platform-tailored approach, emphasizing simplicity and practical feasibility. Unlike common strategies that use fuzzy logic primarily for PID tuning or embed PID within sliding surfaces, our novelty lies in the specific functional allocation, implementation, and rigors validation of the FSMC. Beyond nominal robustness, maintaining stable flight in the presence of actuator or sensor faults is essential for real-world UAV operation especially in safety-critical missions like search and rescue or infrastructure inspection. Recent work in fault-tolerant control (FTC) has shown promising approaches for handling actuator faults and even rotor failures42,43. While the proposed FSMC inherently offers robustness against bounded disturbances which can be seen as a form of passive fault tolerance it does not explicitly address fault accommodation. Designing an active FTC scheme that fully exploits the mechanical redundancy of the coaxial octorotor (with its eight rotors) is therefore a natural next step. In this context, the current work serves as a solid and reliable baseline, paving the way for the future integration of more advanced, fault-tolerant control strategies.

Coaxial octorotor dynamic modelling

The dynamic model structure presented in this section is derived from the standard Newton-Euler formulation and is consistent with established models for coaxial octorotors found in the literature7,8,44. These references provide validation of the model structure through both simulation and experimental results. While the current study focuses on control design and simulation-based validation, full experimental validation of the model on a physical platform is an important direction for future research.

. At the rigid-body level, the governing equations of motion of the coaxial octorotor are formally similar to those of a conventional quadrotor, as they describe the same translational and rotational dynamics driven by collective thrust and control torques1,2,45. However, the coaxial octorotor configuration offers structural advantages in terms of compactness, symmetry, and improved stability characteristics, particularly when compared to classical star-shaped octorotor designs that require additional elevated arms to ensure sufficient rotor separation6,9,15,46.

In the present work, the dynamic model captures the dominant rigid-body dynamics relevant for control design, while higher-order physical effects such as aerodynamic drag, rotor–rotor interference specific to coaxial propulsion, and motor–propeller dynamics are not explicitly included in the state equations. Consistent with common practice in early-stage UAV control studies, these effects are instead treated as bounded uncertainties and external disturbances acting on the system30,32,34. This modelling choice allows the development and evaluation of the proposed control strategy within a tractable and analytically transparent framework, while remaining aligned with the control-oriented objectives of the study.

The proposed control structure

The proposed control architecture is designed according to a hierarchical (cascade) control framework, which is widely adopted in multi-rotor UAV systems to exploit the natural time-scale separation between translational and rotational dynamics4,5,32. The primary objective of this design is to combine the robustness of sliding mode control with effective chattering mitigation while maintaining computational efficiency and structural simplicity.

The controller is organized into two main layers. The outer loop regulates the translational motion along the x and y axes using conventional PID controllers. Since these translational dynamics are indirectly actuated through the vehicle’s attitude and evolve on a slower time scale, PID control is well suited for generating smooth roll and pitch reference commands without amplifying high-frequency disturbances4,33. This choice also facilitates straightforward tuning and preserves implementation feasibility.

The inner loop governs the attitude (roll, pitch, yaw) and altitude dynamics, which are directly actuated, strongly nonlinear, and highly sensitive to external disturbances and modeling uncertainties, particularly in coaxial multi-rotor configurations5,30–33. For these subsystems, a fuzzy sliding mode controller (FSMC) is employed to enhance robustness while alleviating the chattering phenomenon typically associated with classical sliding mode control. Specifically, the fuzzy logic component replaces the discontinuous sgn() function in the SMC switching term with a smooth inference mechanism, thereby reducing high-frequency control oscillations while retaining robustness against parameter variations and external perturbations15,18.

The overall octorotor dynamics are decomposed into four principal subsystems: altitude, roll, pitch, and yaw. Altitude and yaw are each regulated by dedicated FSMC loops, reflecting their direct actuation and sensitivity to disturbances. The roll and pitch dynamics are controlled within a cascade structure, where the outer position controllers (x, y) generate attitude references, and the corresponding inner FSMC loops ensure fast and robust tracking of these commands. This structured decomposition effectively manages the system’s inherent MIMO complexity while preserving a transparent and modular control design, consistent with established multi-rotor UAV control methodologies18,32. Fuzzy sliding mode controller

The altitude, pitch, roll, and yaw control subsystems are governed by a hybrid fuzzy sliding mode controller (FSMC), while the lateral position (X and Y) subsystems are stabilized using conventional PID controllers. The FSMC synthesizes sliding mode control with fuzzy logic, employing the latter to generate a smooth, continuous switching signal. This design replaces the standard discontinuous signum function, thereby mitigating the chattering phenomenon inherent in traditional SMC. However, the controller’s ultimate performance is fundamentally governed by the tuning of its core SMC parameters: the sliding surface coefficient, which dictates the convergence rate in the sliding phase, and the control gain, which determines robustness against disturbances. The selection of these parameters represents a critical trade-off, where higher values can accelerate response and strengthen robustness but at the cost of increased control activity and residual chattering. A significant limitation of the present study is that it does not examine how the performance of the designed FSMC, including its convergence speed, disturbance rejection, and the smoothness of the control signal, varies with respect to these key sliding mode tuning parameters.

Fuzzy logic controller.

Fuzzy logic controller.

The standard sliding mode control (SMC) approach employs a discontinuous switching term, typically sign(s), to force the system toward the sliding surface and reject disturbances. However, this term induces high-frequency chattering, which can lead to actuator saturation or mechanical wear, a critical concern in sensitive applications such as coaxial octorotor control. To mitigate chattering, this work replaces the discontinuous term with a single-input, single-output (SISO) Type-1 Mamdani fuzzy logic controller (FLC). The FLC generates the switching signal Usw based on the sliding surface variable Si, thereby providing a smooth control action. The fuzzy system’s membership functions chosen as five triangular MFs with 50% overlap were designed to balance chattering suppression and response speed, ensuring smooth output transitions while maintaining robust disturbance rejection. To obtain analytically justified initial gains for the proposed cascade control structure, a linearization-based tuning approach is employed. It is important to clarify that the tuning process combines analytical initial estimates with manual, simulation-based iterative refinement, essentially following a heuristic trial-and-error (grid-search) approach. No automated global optimization techniques such as genetic algorithms or particle swarm optimization were used. In this paper, the term “optimization” refers only to this manual tuning process, rather than to any formal or automated optimization method. It is important to clarify that the classical sliding mode robustness condition (where denotes the lumped disturbance bound) is derived for controllers based on the discontinuous sign function. This condition ensures that the control input can dominate worst-case disturbances and enforce ideal sliding, as established in classical sliding mode control theory50.

In the proposed FSMC framework, however, the discontinuous switching term is replaced by a smooth fuzzy inference output . This modification changes the nature of the control action: instead of abrupt switching, the controller applies a continuous, state-dependent correction. As discussed in nonlinear control literature35, such smoothing leads to practical stability (ultimate boundedness) rather than strict ideal sliding, while significantly reducing chattering.

Generalization

While this study focuses specifically on a coaxial octorotor UAV, the proposed FSMC–PID architecture is inherently modular and can be adapted to other multirotor configurations and scales. The controller’s generalizability stems from several key features:

Decoupled control structure: The hierarchical decomposition into attitude/altitude (inner loop) and position (outer loop) is standard across multirotor UAVs. The FSMC can be directly applied to the inner loops of quadrotors, hexarotors, or other octorotor configurations without structural modification.

For radically different configurations (e.g., tilt-rotor VTOLs), the control allocation layer would require modification, though the core FSMC logic remains applicable. In summary, the proposed controller is structurally generalizable to a wide range of multirotor UAVs, with scaling and re-tuning being straightforward engineering tasks rather than fundamental redesign challenges.

When deploying control algorithms on embedded flight controllers, efficiency matters just as much as robustness. Classical sliding mode control (SMC) is extremely lightweight—it only needs a simple sign function, about one floating-point operation per loop. Our proposed Fuzzy Sliding Mode Controller (FSMC) swaps that sign function for a Type-1 Mamdani fuzzy inference system. Altogether, each FSMC loop costs ~ 150–200 floating-point operations. With four loops plus two outer-loop PID controllers, the total stays under 1000 operations per cycle. Modern hardware like the Pixhawk 4 (STM32F765, Cortex-M7 at 216 MHz) can handle this effortlessly executing 1000 operations in microseconds. Even at UAV control frequencies of 200–500 Hz, the FSMC-PID architecture runs comfortably in real time, leaving plenty of headroom for communication, state estimation, and other onboard tasks. In short, this design isn’t just theoretically sound it’s practically feasible, delivering robust fuzzy-enhanced control without straining today’s flight controllers.

Robustness test

To evaluate controller robustness under realistic conditions, the simulation emulated a sustained wind disturbance. Specifically, a pitch angle additive disturbance of 0.2 radians (approximately 11.5°) was injected during the trajectory-tracking test to simulate a lateral wind gust and maintained for a 5-second duration. This magnitude and duration were selected to reflect critical operational conditions: studies such as Bianchi et al.54 report that pitch deviations in the range of 0.15 to 0.25 rad can critically degrade UAV tracking performance due to strong aerodynamic coupling. Furthermore, sustained pitch disturbances of this scale are established as a stringent benchmark in evaluations of multi-rotor ADRC performance55] and as a standard test case for assessing controller resilience56–60, as demonstrated in an LQR-based study using a 0.2 rad deviation27.

This test serves a dual purpose: it validates general disturbance rejection and implicitly tests the controller’s ability to handle the coupled, nonlinear disturbances representative of coaxial-specific effects like torque coupling and transient flow interference. The results of the robustness test are shown in Fig. 8, which illustrates the pitch-angle response of the coaxial octorotor when subjected to a robustness test involving an external disturbance. Conclusion

This work has presented the modelling, design, and evaluation of a Fuzzy Sliding Mode Controller (FSMC) for a six-degree-of-freedom coaxial octorotor UAV. The dynamic model, derived using the Newton–Euler formalism, was decomposed into four principal control subsystems: altitude, pitch, roll, and yaw, while translational motion in the x- and y-axes was regulated by outer-loop PID controllers. By integrating sliding mode robustness with the smooth approximating capability of fuzzy logic, the proposed FSMC effectively eliminates the chattering phenomenon inherent in conventional SMC.

The core contribution of this study is a rigorous theoretical and numerical validation within a high-fidelity simulation environment. MATLAB/Simulink results demonstrate that the FSMC provides accurate and stable trajectory tracking, achieving significantly lower root-mean-square (RMS) errors than conventional SMC. For example, in the altitude (Z) channel, the RMS error was reduced from 0.1722 m with SMC to 0.0012 m with FSMC, while the yaw error decreased from 0.0089 rad to 0.0000396 rad. The total control effort also decreased slightly, from 1.9119 × 105 to 1.8852 × 105, indicating improved energy efficiency alongside smoother control signals that are far more suitable for physical actuators.

Under a sustained pitch disturbance of 0.2 rad, the FSMC exhibited strong disturbance rejection, as quantified by the integral-of-time-absolute-error (ITAE = 2.7272 × 105) and integral-absolute-error (IAE = 51.09) indices. The controller rapidly restored the UAV to its reference trajectory without overshoot or instability, confirming its robust performance against dynamic perturbations.

The proposed FSMC has therefore met all control objectives within the present analytical scope, proving to be a reliable and effective conceptual solution for the guidance and stabilization of coaxial octorotors. It offers a promising balance of robustness, simplicity, and computational efficiency.

The present work provides a simulation-based proof of concept. However, several steps are still required to move toward real-world implementation, and these are planned as future work. A structured experimental roadmap is envisioned in three stages. First, Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing will be carried out by deploying the FSMC-PID algorithm on a Pixhawk 4 autopilot connected to a real-time simulation of the coaxial octorotor. This step will mainly verify real-time execution, computational load, and interface timing before any physical flight. Second, a Software-in-the-Loop (SITL) environment using Gazebo/ROS will be developed, including more realistic actuator models, sensor noise (IMU, GPS, barometer), and wind disturbances. Finally, progressive flight experiments will be conducted on a custom coaxial octorotor platform, starting with tethered hover tests, then controlled indoor trajectory tracking, and eventually outdoor GPS-based flights. In terms of modeling, the current study treats coaxial-specific effects such as rotor–rotor interaction, aerodynamic drag, and motor dynamics as lumped bounded disturbances. While this is sufficient for control design, it limits simulation fidelity. Future work will focus on developing a higher-fidelity model that explicitly includes coaxial wake interaction, state-dependent aerodynamic effects, and more realistic motor–propeller dynamics. In addition, future research will move beyond passive disturbance handling by integrating active disturbance observers or adaptive compensation within the FSMC framework. This would allow online estimation of unmodeled effects and reduce reliance on conservative switching gains, improving tracking accuracy.Further extensions will also explore more advanced fuzzy control structures, such as Interval Type-2 FSMC, to better handle uncertainty and noise. The design of membership functions and rule bases could also be systematically optimized using metaheuristic methods. Finally, the inherent redundancy of the coaxial octorotor opens the door to fault-tolerant control strategies. Future work will investigate extending the proposed approach to handle actuator faults or rotor failures, building on recent developments in multirotor fault-tolerant control.

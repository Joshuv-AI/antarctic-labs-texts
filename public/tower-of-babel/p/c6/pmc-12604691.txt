Full-thickness macular hole after uneventful phacoemulsification cataract surgery: Case report and review of literature
Authors: Ahmed Omer Kenawy, Mohammed Al-Abri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A 61-year-old healthy female patient underwent an uneventful left phacoemulsification procedure under local anesthesia for a left visually significant senile cataract. One month postoperatively, her decimal visual acuity improved from 0.06 preoperatively to 0.5. However, 3 months postoperatively, the best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) had decreased to 0.3. Fundus examination revealed the development of a full-thickness macular hole (FTMH). Diagnosis was confirmed by optical coherence tomography of the macula, which demonstrated FTMH. The patient subsequently underwent pars plana vitrectomy with internal limiting membrane peeling using an inverted flap technique, and injection of gas (C3F8 12%) as an endotamponade. Six months postoperatively, she showed a favorable anatomical closure with restoration of the macular contour and visual improvement to 0.63. Timely detection and management of FTMH following phacoemulsification is crucial for optimizing postoperative visual outcome, as this is a rare but serious complication of cataract surgery. The underlying pathophysiology of this condition remains unclear and is believed to be multifactorial. Prompt recognition and appropriate surgical intervention are essential to achieve the best possible visual outcomes in such cases.

Introduction

Cataract surgery has become an increasingly safe and effective means of restoring vision, owing to recent advancements in technology. The availability of state-of-the-art surgical tools and affordable high-quality intraocular lenses has significantly reduced the incidence of most complications. Although these improvements have been made, rare complications, such as full-thickness macular hole (FTMH), can still occur after phacoemulsification, especially when postoperative visual outcomes are unfavorable.[12]

The implicated pathophysiology of FTMH following uneventful cataract surgery is not completely understood and appears to be multifactorial.

In this paper, we present a case of spontaneous macular hole (MH) formation 3 months after uneventful phacoemulsification. Early detection and timely intervention helped to achieve good visual outcomes.

Case Report

A 61-year-old healthy female patient presented with left gradual decrease in vision over 4 months. She had a history of cataract surgery in the right eye in 2019. Ophthalmic examination revealed BCVA in the right eye of 0.67 (−0.50/−2.50 × 165) and severe visual impairment in the left eye with BCVA of 0.06 secondary to a visually significant senile cataract with unremarkable posterior segment examination. After obtaining informed consent, the patient underwent uneventful phacoemulsification with in-the-bag implantation of a foldable acrylic single-piece intraocular lens (SN60WF +19.0 D). The procedure was performed under local anesthesia using a sub-Tenon injection of 3 mL lidocaine hydrochloride 2%. A sub-Tenon gentamycin/dexamethasone mixture was injected at the conclusion of the surgery as a routine precaution. One month postoperatively, vision improved to 0.5, and an unremarkable anterior and posterior segment examination with flat macula was confirmed by optical coherence tomography (OCT) [Figure 1]. However, 3 months postoperatively and during her final check-up before discharge, her vision deteriorated to 0.3, and posterior segment examination showed a FTMH confirmed by OCT macula [Figure 2]. Conservative management with topical nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs was offered for 6 weeks, with no improvement. The patient then underwent left pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) with internal limiting membrane (ILM) peeling using an inverted flap technique with injection of gas (C3F8 12%) as an endotamponade. Two months postoperatively, OCT showed anatomical closure of the MH with residual subfoveal fluid [Figure 3a] that was resolved 6 months postoperatively [Figure 3b], and the corresponding objective BCVA improved to 0.63.

Discussion

MH is an extremely uncommon outcome of advanced cataract surgery.[1] Although the exact cause is unknown, it has been hypothesized that anteroposterior vitreous forces are the primary cause of acute posterior vitreous detachment (PVD) or traction around the fovea, leading to the formation of MH.[3] Other possible interacting mechanisms include tangential tractional forces involving the ILM and degenerative changes in the inner retinal layers.[4]

In addition, a pro-inflammatory fibrinolytic mechanism has been postulated to play a role in reopening of the MH.[5]

Following phacoemulsification, diagnosed MH can be classified into: preexisting MH may not have been visible before cataract extraction. Early-stage MHs (stages 1 and 2) progressed following cataract surgery. New-onset or de novo symptomatic MH.[6]

We believe that our patient belonged to the third category, as the OA study conducted by Patterson et al. revealed an acute FTMH that occurred during the early postoperative period following uncomplicated phacoemulsification surgery. Mechanical forces or vitreofoveal traction may have been factors that contributed to the development of this condition.[2]

A detailed ocular examination, including assessment of the macula and disc, is essential in the context of unexplained reduced postoperative visual outcomes after uneventful cataract surgery. OCT has become an indispensable diagnostic tool for ophthalmologists and serves as the gold standard for diagnosing and monitoring MHs.[7] Furthermore, the development of OCT-based MH indices, such as the macular hole area index(MAI),macular hole cystoid space area index (MCSAI) and the macular hole tissue area index(MTAI), has facilitated decision-making for surgical intervention and prognostication of post-operative MH closure.[8]

An initial conservative management trial was attempted. However, most MHs are surgical cases. The recommended treatment for FTMH remains surgical by PPV, ILM peeling, and the use of intraocular tamponade such as SF6 gas, C3F8 gas, or silicone oil.[9]

Our patient was managed surgically using 23G PPV, ILM peeling using an inverted flap technique with injection of gas (C3F8 12%) as an endo-tamponade. This resulted in favorable anatomical closure with restoration of macular contour and visual improvement.

The ILM peeling method was introduced by Eckardt et al. in 1997.[10] This technique is used to eliminate residual adherent vitreous cortex remnants, which can boost retinal compliance. In addition, by removing the ILM, this approach helps prevent the reopening of MHs by curbing the associated fibrocellular proliferation. It is reported that the use of ILM peeling can achieve a primary anatomical closure rate of 90%–95%.[9]

The inverted flap procedure was first devised to treat large MH and stimulate glial tissue growth by employing Müller cell fragments present in the inner limiting membrane. The ILM flap acts as a scaffold and protective barrier, promoting the proper positioning of photoreceptors and preventing fluid from entering the hole.[9] Although anatomical success is attainable, visual acuity outcomes may not always be satisfactory, and other factors, such as photoreceptor integrity and outer retinal layer thickness, can impact postoperative BCVA.[11]

Conclusion

Thorough pre- and post-operative assessments, including OCT, are crucial for the timely detection and management of FTMHs following phacoemulsification. Reducing intraoperative fluctuations in the anterior chamber may help prevent the occurrence and progression of MHs as well as other complications. In addition, further research is needed to better understand the underlying pathophysiology and risk factors associated with FTMH after cataract surgery.

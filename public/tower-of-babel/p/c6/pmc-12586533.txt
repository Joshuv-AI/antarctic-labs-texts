Construction and validation of a LASSO penalized logistic regression model predicting hypernatremia after pituitary adenoma surgery
Authors: Xiao Liu, Zhe Wang, Yanli Zhang, Xiaorong Luan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHypernatremia is a common postoperative complication after pituitary adenoma surgery. Despite its prevalence, there is a significant gap in research regarding the construction of predictive models for assessing risk. Therefore, it is essential to create a robust model to accurately predict the risk of postoperative hypernatremia. To explore the risk factors for developing hypernatremia after pituitary adenoma surgery and establish a prediction model. From April 2022 to June 2024, 269 patients were admitted to the Department of Neurosurgery at Qilu Hospital as part of this cohort study. Initially, the sample was split into a training set and a validation set using a ratio of 3:1 randomly. Then, a univariable analysis was conducted for each variable in the training set. Subsequently, the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression and stepwise regression were employed to identify the candidate variables for further examination. A multivariable logistic regression analysis was then performed to develop a risk prediction model. A nomogram was created to enhance the model’s utility, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow (HL) test was implemented to evaluate the model’s goodness of fit. The predictive efficiency of the model was assessed by calculating the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), while model discrimination was determined using the calibration plot and decision curve analysis (DCA). This study indicated that the overall incidence of hypernatremia after pituitary surgery is 8.55%. We collected data on 46 potential risk factors. After LASSO regression, we constructed three models: LR model (LASSO regression), FR model (forward stepwise regression), and BR model (backward stepwise regression). The multivariable analysis identified that LR model, including pituitary stalk involvement, surgery time, and output 1st day, is the most effective at predicting postoperative hypernatremia. The HL test of LR model indicated a p-value of 0.448, and the AUC value was 0.813. In the test set, the AUC was 0.894. The calibration plots and DCA plots demonstrate substantial clinical relevance. Additionally, we developed a nomogram for predicting hypernatremia after pituitary surgery based on this regression model. The prediction model serves as a clinically valuable tool for identifying patients at heightened risk of postoperative hypernatremia following pituitary surgery, facilitating timely implementation of prophylactic management protocols to optimize clinical outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-22512-2.Keywords: Hypernatremia, Pituitary adenoma, Postoperative, Risk, ModelSubject terms: Health care, Risk factors

Introduction

Pituitary adenomas represent the third most prevalent form of intracranial neoplasm, comprising 10–15% of all detectable intracranial adenomas1. Surgical resection via a trans-sphenoidal approach is typically employed as the therapeutic strategy2, with sodium perturbations being a common complication after surgery. Several factors have been reported to adversely affect the rate of sodium perturbation complications, including younger age, male gender, greater adenoma volume, and preoperative pituitary function3,4. However, the mechanisms involved in regulating water and sodium balance after pituitary adenoma surgery remain inadequately understood.

Hypernatremia after pituitary adenoma surgery is the major kind of sodium perturbation and ranges from 10 to 60%, leading to longer hospital stays, and contributing to higher healthcare costs5,6. Hypernatremia is prevalent in a substantial proportion of patients, particularly among those undergoing neurosurgical procedures. Postoperative hypernatremia is typically caused by the development of diabetes insipidus (DI), which tends to occur during the acute phase after pituitary surgery, manifesting as hypotonic polyuria7. However, predicting the incidence of hypernatremia after pituitary adenoma surgery is challenging due to the complex regulation of water and electrolyte homeostasis. To our knowledge, no previous study has created a model to predict the rate of postoperative hypernatremia. Therefore, there is a need to develop a predictive model for the quick identification of risk factors to allow for prompt intervention.

This study aimed to construct a predictive model utilizing LASSO-logistic regression to evaluate the risk of hypernatremia in patients undergoing surgical procedures for pituitary adenomas. The primary objective of this study is to identify patients who are at a high risk of hypernatremia after adenoma surgery. This approach enables timely interventions that can significantly lower healthcare costs and accelerate recovery.

Materials and methods

This study conducted a retrospective cohort analysis of patients who underwent pituitary adenoma surgery at the Department of Neurosurgery, Qilu Hospital of Shandong University, between April 2022 and June 2024.

Ethical approval, accordance, and consent

Approval: The study protocol received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (Approval No: KYLL-202408-046-1).

Accordance: All study procedures were conducted in strict accordance with the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Informed consent: Due to the retrospective design utilizing anonymized data from electronic medical records, the requirement for obtaining individual informed consent was formally waived by the approving ethics committee.

Patient selection and methodology

The research adhered to the Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) statement guidelines8. Eligible patients were identified by reviewing the hospital’s electronic medical record systems. During the study period, 5 patients were excluded due to partial absence of essential data, resulting in a final cohort of 264 patients (including 23 patients with hypernatremia) for subsequent statistical analysis.

Sample size calculation

Riley et al. suggested that sample size, as well as the number of predictor variables, should be carefully considered because a small sample size results in inaccurate forecasts and, as a result, inaccurate healthcare decisions for some people9. The sample size was calculated using the “pmsampsize” package10, which is based on the minimum sample size criteria proposed for developing new multivariate predictive models. The “pmsampsize” package can be used to calculate the minimum sample size for developing a continuity variable, binary variable, or temporal event outcome prediction model10. Upon completion of the dataset, it was methodically divided into training and validation sets utilizing a 3:1 ratio to optimize model performance.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The criteria for inclusion were as follows: (1) Patients over 16 years of age; (2) Patients who underwent pituitary adenoma surgery; (3) The excised lesion was confirmed through a postoperative pathological test.

The exclusion criteria included: (1) Patients with serum sodium disorders before surgery; (2) Patients with severe complications such as cerebral hernia, asphyxia, or death; (3) Patients whose symptoms were not followed up or whose crucial data were incomplete.

Data collection

The outcome variable of the model was hypernatremia, defined as any patient having serum natremia exceeding 145 mmol/L after surgery. Demographic information was collected from electronic records and included details such as age, gender, height, weight, medical history, and the history of other illnesses. Pituitary stalk involvement denotes a pathological state wherein a pituitary neoplasm encroaches upon or exerts compressive effects on the pituitary stalk during its expansion. Diagnostic evaluation predominantly relies on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), with key radiological features encompassing stalk thickening or displacement, abnormal enhancement patterns, or tumor envelopment exceeding 50% of the stalk’s circumference. Fascia lata excision was identified as a potential concomitant surgical procedure during pituitary adenoma resection, primarily employed for repairing substantial dural defects or cerebrospinal fluid leaks, which was extracted from operative records. Laboratory test results were obtained both before and after the surgery, which included measurements of HDL, LDL, cortisol, D-dimer, ACTH, sodium, and potassium levels. Additionally, the intake and output of fluids were monitored during the operation and daily after surgery.

Univariable analysis

We made a limited number of modifications to the predictor variables in order to facilitate the training and validation of our models. We appropriately assigned values to categorical variables. All variables underwent univariable analysis, and those with a p-value less than 0.05 were included in the multivariable logistic analysis to identify risk factors for postoperative hypernatremia in patients undergoing pituitary surgery. The t-test was used to calculate the standard deviations of continuous variables11. Categorical variables are presented as proportions and absolute numbers as determined by the Chi-square12 or Fisher’s exact tests13.

Construction of LASSO-logistic model

The dataset was randomly divided into training and test sets (3:1 ratio) using R’s caret package14. LASSO regression was then applied to the training set for feature selection, effectively addressing multicollinearity concerns in the analytical model15. LASSO regression can estimate model parameters and select variables simultaneously, providing a good explanation for the results16. LASSO regression algorithm corrects the coefficient of some variables that contribute less to the dependent variable, compresses the coefficient of some less important variables to 0, retains the coefficients of significant variables greater than zero, and effectively reduces the number of variables in further regression17. To mitigate the potential bias in LASSO regression where correlated variables may be randomly selected, a stepwise regression approach (forward and backward) was applied to refine variable selection based on clinical relevance18.

A multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted, utilizing the variables identified through LASSO regression, forward stepwise regression, and backward stepwise regression methodologies. The logistic regressions, which make no assumptions about the distribution of the explanatory data, classify data by using maximum likelihood functions to predict the probabilities of outcome variables19. Three distinct regression models were constructed to evaluate variable selection approaches:

(1) a logistic regression (LR) model incorporating covariates selected through LASSO regression; (2) a forward selection regression (FR) model built using variables identified via forward stepwise selection; and(3) a backward elimination regression (BR) model developed with variables retained after backward stepwise selection.

The accuracy of the LASSO-logistic model was assessed using the AUC value and HL tests. The AUC value is the proportion of all patient pairs whose predicted results are consistent with the actual results. It estimates the likelihood that the predicted outcome will match the actual observation20. We evaluated the models with the HL goodness of fit test to determine how well the expected probabilities matched the actual probabilities generated by the predictive model21. A p-value less than 0.05 indicates a significant difference between the model’s predicted values and the observed values, suggesting that the predictive model is ineffective. Conversely, a p-value greater than 0.05 is considered acceptable, indicating that the model’s estimates align well with the data and that the scoring model functions effectively. The level of discrimination was assessed using calibration plots.

In addition, we created a nomogram based on risk variables and independent prognostic variables to predict the risk of hypernatremia after pituitary surgery. The optimal cut-off value for postoperative hypernatremia was established using the “pROC” package22. The cut-off value, known as Youden’s index, indicates a test’s overall ability to distinguish between actual patients and non-patients. It is calculated by subtracting 1 from the sum of sensitivity and specificity23. A decision curve was plotted to inform clinicians about the range of threshold probabilities for which the prediction model would be of clinical value24. DCA offers insight into the clinical consequences of using the model by determining the relationship between a chosen predicted probability threshold and the relative value of false-positive and false-negative results to obtain a value of the net benefit of using the model at that threshold25.

Statistical analysis

In order to assess the differences between the training set and the validation set, we conducted a comparison of their AUC values and calibration plots. This study considered p < 0.05 (bilateral) statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 and R software (version 4.3.3, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Variable selection

To identify key risk factors, we performed LASSO regression on the training set. Given the high-dimensional nature of our data (many variables but relatively few cases), we employed ten-fold cross-validation to determine the optimal penalty term (λ), ensuring robust variable selection while mitigating overfitting. Variables demonstrating both non-zero coefficients and statistical significance (p < 0.05) in the LASSO regression analysis were selected for inclusion in the LR model (Fig. 2). To optimize variable selection, we implemented bidirectional stepwise regression analyses. The backward elimination approach produced the BR model containing three clinically relevant variables. In parallel, forward selection identified the FR model incorporating six significant variables. All VIF values were below the threshold of 5, confirming the absence of substantial multicollinearity among the included variables.

Multivariable logistic regression

Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of hypernatremia. After eliminating non-significant variables, three independent variables remained significantly associated with the outcome in the LR model: pituitary stalk involvement (OR = 9.357, 95% CI 1.685–57.774, p = 0.002), surgery time (OR = 2.164, 95% CI 0.979–4.88, p < 0.001), and output 1st day (OR = 1.001, 95% CI 1–1.002, p < 0.001). The BR model identified two significant independent variables: Fascia lata excision (OR = 2.972, 95% CI 0.971–8.998, p = 0.021) and Output 1st day (OR = 1.001, 95% CI 1.001–1.002, p = 0.007). In the FR model, six significant variables were included: VTE (OR = 10.695, 95% CI 0.616–187.225, p = 0.036), pituitary stalk involvement (OR = 8.739, 95% CI 1.149–76.149, p = 0.01), ACTH (OR = 3.099, 95% CI 0.848–12.843, p = 0.018), surgery time (OR = 2.1, 95% CI 0.939–4.878, p = 0.029), Output 1st day (OR = 1.001, 95% CI 1–1.002, p = 0.001), and TG (OR = 0.446, 95% CI 0.159–0.901, p = 0.031). ROC analysis revealed that the LR model had an AUC value of 0.813, the FR model had 0.872, and the BR model had 0.808, indicating that these models possessed good discriminant ability (Fig. 3). The HL tests yielded non-significant results (all p-values > 0.05), indicating adequate model calibration. The AUC values of the FR model in both the training set and the validation set were the highest, but its calibration plot showed a significant deviation from the center line, indicating poor stability of the model. The AUC values of the BR model in both the training set and the validation set were the lowest, suggesting poor predictive ability of the model. The AUC value of the LR model in both the training set and the test set was greater than 0.8, indicating high predictive ability. Meanwhile, the deviation from the center line in its calibration plot was the lowest, suggesting the best stability (Fig. 4). Therefore, we believe that the LR model is the optimal model in this study, which has both high predictive ability and good stability.

Diagnostic nomogram establishment

Based on the discussion above, we finally chose the LR model to construct a risk prediction nomogram model of postoperative hypernatremia (Fig. 5). Clinicians can use this nomogram to calculate points based on patients’ characteristics. The total points, representing the sum of points from all characteristics, are used to assess the predicted possibility of hypernatremia after pituitary surgery. A vertical line was drawn at the total score, intersecting with the risk line that indicates the predicted incidence of postoperative hypernatremia.

Discussion

We have successfully developed and temporarily validated a model that accurately predicts a patient’s risk of hypernatremia after pituitary adenoma surgery. This highlights the importance of addressing electrolyte disorders, as patients with hypernatremia require close monitoring. Otherwise, it can lead to extended hospital stays and prolonged postoperative recovery. A method to identify patients at particularly high risk for electrolyte disorders after surgery would be beneficial for planning the observation and treatment. The final model included surgery time, pituitary stalk involvement, and 1st-day postoperative output as key parameters for predicting the risk of hypernatremia after pituitary surgery.

Our findings partly aligned with previous studies. Wang et al. found that MRI grading can predict both the occurrence and severity of pituitary stalk involvement, which are correlated with levels of pituitary target hormone deficiencies, while pituitary stalk involvement serve as key imaging markers of pituitary structural and functional integrity26. Lin’s machine learning model identified the preoperative-to-postoperative differences in pituitary stalk deviation angle, measurable pituitary stalk length, and postoperative day 2 serum sodium levels as significant predictors of delayed hyponatremia27. This study incorporated preoperative MRI characteristics of the pituitary stalk, defining stalk involvement by the presence of stalk deviation, abnormal contrast enhancement, or > 50% tumor encirclement. We demonstrated a significant association between pituitary stalk involvement and postoperative hypernatremia. This association may stem from disruption of the stalk’s endocrine function or intraoperative mechanical stimulation. Identification of pituitary stalk involvement on preoperative MRI necessitates enhanced postoperative surveillance for the development of hypernatremia. The precise underlying mechanisms warrant further investigation.

Postoperative output volume is closely related to water and electrolyte loss, primarily due to urine output. DI often results in hypotonic urine, with daily volumes ranging from 3.5 to 16.8 L28. This condition leads to greater water loss compared to sodium loss. In our study, the hypernatremia group showed significantly higher output than the non-hypernatremia group each day after pituitary surgery. Both groups experienced increased postoperative output, particularly on the 2nd day after surgery, after which the output volume gradually decreased. This suggests that we should closely monitor changes in output and consider increasing water intake as needed.

Furthermore, we analyzed the differences between postoperative fluid intake and output. Our univariate analysis revealed a significant correlation between postoperative fluid intake and hypernatremia. However, in the multivariable logistic regression, the ‘intake’ variable was excluded due to its strong relationship with the ‘output’ variable. In this specific clinical context, we observed that an increase in fluid intake was associated with a corresponding increase in postoperative urine output, resulting in a stable discrepancy between fluid intake and output. This finding suggests that the strategies currently implemented may not address all relevant risk factors comprehensively or may not be initiated in a timely manner. Therefore, further investigation into optimal treatment and precise fluid intake is necessary through additional clinical trials.

Only a limited number of studies have examined the relationship between surgery time and the incidence of postoperative hypernatremia. Del et al. conducted an experiment on dogs and found that surgery time was significantly longer in those who developed hypernatremia29. Our study yielded results identical to Del’s findings. However, the underlying mechanism remains unclear, and an unknown intermediary factor may bridge the relationship between surgery time and hypernatremia.

Our study has several strengths. First, our prediction model effectively optimizes and personalizes patient care by utilizing clinical factors that are routinely known pre-operatively and easily collected in practice. This enhances the model’s potential for ease of use. Second, in our study, we employed a nomogram for the prediction of postoperative hypernatremia, enhancing the visualization and accuracy of clinical decisions.

This study has several limitations. First, regulating the water-sodium balance is complex, making it impossible to consider all risk factors. Some potential risk factors were excluded due to incomplete or missing data. Additionally, conducting a single-center study may have introduced bias in our results. A more robust statistical analysis was not feasible because of the low number of effective patients with hypernatremia in this cohort.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our LASSO-logistic regression model identified pituitary stalk involvement, operative duration, and postoperative day 1 drainage output as significant predictors of post-operative hypernatremia following pituitary adenoma resection. This model demonstrates robust predictive capability, enabling early identification of high-risk patients for prophylactic intervention. We recommend external validation in multicenter cohorts with larger sample sizes to assess generalizability and explore additional variables to further enhance predictive precision.

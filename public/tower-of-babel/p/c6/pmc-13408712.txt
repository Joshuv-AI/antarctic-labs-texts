Comparative Evaluation of the Efficacy of Biologically-based Regenerative Endodontic Procedures in the Healing of Periapically Involved Young, Immature Permanent Teeth With/Without Using Mineral Trioxide Aggregate
Authors: Shivani Gupta, Shalini Garg, Abhishek Dhindsa, Bhavna G Saraf, Sumit Garg, Pragati Poddar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEndodontic management of an immature nonvital tooth along with apical periodontitis poses many potential complications. A randomized clinical trial was conducted to assess the effectiveness of three different techniques: Gp I—revascularization with mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) (as additional coronal plug below final restoration); Gp II—apexification with MTA (MTA as a direct apical plug); and Gp III—revascularization without using MTA (no MTA below final restoration).Materials and methodsA total of 28 patients with a mean age of 9.44 ± 2.55 years finally completed the study of 12 months duration. Maturogenesis was assessed by developing tooth to next Nolla's stage, visual examination of radiographs, and quantitative assessment of digitalized radiographs in three parameters, that is, root lengthening, root thickening, and narrowing of the apical foramen. A decrease in PAI score assessed healing of periapical periodontitis/radiolucencies.ResultsAbout 100% clinical success was seen in all three groups, Gp I, Gp II, and Gp III, regarding the resolution of signs and symptoms of pain, tenderness, swelling, sinus, and mobility. On quantitative examination of digitalized radiographs, Gp I showed maximum maturogenesis. Maximum healing in minimum time was observed in Gp II <6 months' time interval.Clinical significanceThis study highlights the effectiveness of MTA apexification and MTA revascularization in managing nonvital immature permanent teeth with apical periodontitis, aiding clinicians in tailored treatment decisions for improved patient outcomes.ConclusionThere is no difference in efficacy between MTA apexification and MTA revascularization to treat nonvital immature permanent teeth.

Introduction

Apexification using biomimetic materials and procedure and cell-free regenerative therapy (CFRET) using blood clot are two established options for treating the immature nonvital tooth with apical periodontitis.1 The clinical method for apexification involves either creating an artificial barrier at the apex or inducing an apical closure. Tricalcium silicate cement is the preferred material because of its biocompatibility and sealing properties.2–6 Biologically based techniques intended to restore damaged materials, such as dentin and pulp-dentin complex cells, can be characterized as the new idea of regenerative endodontic procedures.7,8 Achieving a bacteria-tight seal coronally to prevent bacterial invasion into the pulp space before revascularization can occur is crucial to the procedure's effectiveness.2,10 The present study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of biologically based regenerative endodontic procedures in healing periapically involved young immature permanent teeth in children.

Materials and Methods

Children between the ages of 7–14 participated in the randomized clinical study at the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Maharishi Markandeshwar College of Dental Sciences and Research, Mullana (Ambala). The study's design, technique, and procedure were approved by the institutional ethical committee. This trial was registered with the Clinical Trial Registry in India (CTRI) under the reference number CTRI/2014/11/005197 (REF/2014/05/007018).

Based on the predicted timing of root completion, the children were screened for the presence of young permanent teeth that were severely carious and likely had immature root formation. Permanent teeth with immature apices, children free from any systemic illness and in good general health, with incompletely formed root, adequate bone, and clinically exposed teeth showing delayed response to pulp sensibility test were included, whereas teeth undergoing root resorption or with mature root apices or children with any major illness or any history of systemic disease were excluded.

A tooth was considered as a unit for the study. Detailed history and clinical assessment were recorded on a predesigned proforma. The chit method of randomization was then used to split these teeth into three groups. It was done by allotting sequential numbers to every selected tooth and through a random draw of lots.

The XCP extension (cone paralleling technique) was used to create periapical radiographs of the teeth under consideration for treatment in the study. Standardization of the radiographic exposures was done by using the Rinn XCP film holder. Exposures were made with dental X-ray equipment operating at 70 kVp and 8 mA with 0.3 seconds of exposure time for every preoperative and postoperative radiograph.

Treatment Protocol

Following the administration of local anesthesia, the tooth was isolated with a rubber dam, an access cavity was made, and each root canal orifice was gently irrigated with 10 mL of 2.5% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) using a 27-gauge open-ended needle positioned about 1–2 mm below the root canal orifice with minimal filing. Next, intracanal medication was placed in the pulp chamber and loosely packed into the coronal portion of the root canal orifice, the access cavity was sealed with fortified ZnO, and the patient was called back after 3 weeks.

Three weeks later, the teeth were isolated with a rubber dam, sedated with 2% lidocaine without a vasoconstrictor, and then reopened. After extensive irrigation with 2.5% NaOCl to remove the intracanal medication, the root canals were finally irrigated with 10 mL of sterile saline and dried with sterile absorbent paper points.

The above procedure was followed in teeth included in all the groups.

Further, in group I (revascularization with MTA)—apical bleeding was induced by gently irritating the tissue using a size 15 K file. Following the creation of a blood clot, MTA was softly fitted over the clot in the coronal canal space in accordance with the manufacturer's directions. The MTA was covered with a moist cotton pellet, and interim restorative material (fortified ZnOE and GIC) was used to temporarily restore the access cavity. After a day, an endodontic explorer was utilized to confirm that MTA had been set, and silver amalgam/composite was used for the final restoration (Figs 1 and 2).

In group II (apexification with MTA) – a diagnostic radiograph was used to assess the working length. In order to generate a 3–4 mm apical plug, MTA was prepared in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions and condensed to the apical end of the root using pluggers or paper points. The MTA was covered with a moist cotton pellet, and interim restorative material (fortified ZnO) was used to temporarily restore the access cavity. One day later, an endodontic explorer was used to ensure that MTA had been set and final obturation and restoration were done (Figs 1 and 2).

In group III (revascularization without MTA) – apical bleeding was induced by gentle irritation using size 15 K files. After blood clot formation, the coronal canal space was sealed directly with fortified ZnOE and GIC. The final restoration was done with silver amalgam/composite. Postoperatively, clinical assessment [pain, tenderness to percussion (TOP), swelling, sinus, mobility, and intact restoration] of all children was carried out at recall visits of 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. Radiographs were taken at 6 and 12 months follow-up (Fig. 2).

A digital camera (Canon PowerShot SX 1100 IS) was used to digitize the intraoral periapical (IOPA) radiographs of each site. After being converted to JPEG format, the digital photos were imported into Adobe Photoshop (CS3 Extended version). The radiographic pictures were overlaid with 0.5 mm square grid lines. Statistical Consideration

SPSS for Windows (version 23.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for all analyses. Since the data were normally distributed, a one-way ANOVA was used to compare the three groups. The post hoc test was used to compare intergroups. Fisher's exact test and the Pearson Chi-squared test were used to compare categorical data. An interexaminer correlation (r-value) was obtained using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC).

Results

A total of 42 children between the ages of 7 and 14 were recruited; however, 7 patients were later excluded because they did not return with the consent form or did not meet the inclusion criteria. 28 patients completed the total period of 12 months regarding regular follow-up (Fig. 3). The patients' average age was 9.44 ± 2.55 years. The intraclass correlation coefficient (r² = 0.875) showed high interexaminer repeatability.

Discussion

The management of young permanent immature nonvital teeth with apical periodontitis has been considered a great challenge.1 The treatment of such cases using calcium hydroxide apexification is well established.6–8 However, the long-term use of calcium hydroxide has several drawbacks.8 To address this limitation, the technique of forming a direct apical plug was introduced, enabling single-visit apexification using MTA to seal the apical portion of the root canal.9–11

A regenerative endodontic procedure, revascularization, is a biologically based alternative having added advantage over apexification by inducing apexogenesis or facilitating root maturation by regenerating/preserving vital tissue needed for root maturation.5

In the present study, statistically very highly significant and almost equal clinical success was seen in all three groups. Shah et al. in 2008,5 El-Meligy and Avery in 2006,11 Nagata et al. 2014,12 Alobaid et al. in 2014,13 Asgary et al. in 2016,14 and Wikström et al. in 202115 reported similar results regarding clinical efficacy.

The difference between the performance of all groups for maturogenesis (by visual examination of pre- and postoperative radiographs) was highly statistically significant (p-value 0.000). The least observed maturogenesis in group II (apexification with MTA) may be due to the cessation of growth after using MTA for apical plugging followed by sealing the whole root canal space with gutta-percha in this procedure. Revascularization (both with and without using MTA as a coronal plug) groups increased root length and thickness over the follow-up period. Ongoing root development can be explained by the presence of viable pulp cells, periodontal ligament stem cells, or apical papilla stem cells, which are capable of proliferating and differentiating into odontoblasts under the directional influence of an intact Hertwig's epithelial root sheath. Similar findings are associated with Bose et al. in 2009,16 Jeerupan et al. in 2012,17 Tawfik et al. in 2013,18 Nagy in 2014,19 Saoud et al. 2014,20 and Wang et al. 2015,21 Zhujiang and Kim 2016.22

The quantitative analysis of individual parameters measuring total maturogenesis in group I (revascularization with MTA) showed maximum root lengthening with an increase of 21.29%. Cehreli et al. in 201123 reported a 7.7% increase, Jeerupan et al. in 201217 showed a percentage increase of 14.9%, Tawfik et al. in 201318 showed a 17.8% increase, and Aly et al. in 201924 reported a 5.02% increase.

Group I (revascularization with MTA) showed more root thickening with a percentage increase of 27.41% and narrowing of the apical foramen by 46.53%. Comparable increases were shown by Cehreli et al. in 2011,23 Nagata et al. in 2014,12 and Nagy et al. in 2014.19 The findings of Chan et al. 201725 indicate that teeth exhibited the greatest increase in root thickness in the apical region, along with the most pronounced root elongation. Pereira et al. in 201926 reported increases in the average root length 14.28%, root width 8.12%, and decrease in apical diameter 48.37%.

Group II (apexification with MTA) showed no root thickening. Jeerupan et al. in 201217 and Tawfik et al. in 201318 reported this limitation of the apexification technique. The use of MTA in the root canal is known to provide an effective apical seal; however, it does not support further root development, as reported by Bucher et al. 201627 and Demiriz and Hazar 2017.28

In the present study, group II (apexification with MTA) showed maximum healing in minimum time within a 6-month interval. This effect may indicate that the periapical tissues tolerate MTA better than other materials.

An increase in the size of radiolucency may not have affected maturogenesis in teeth. The viability of the Hertwig's epithelial root sheath (HERS), apical papilla, and surrounding dental follicle or periodontal ligament in immature permanent teeth with necrotic pulps is likely influenced by the severity and duration of apical periodontitis or abscess. Similar findings are associated with Wang et al. 201029 and Yamauchi et al. 2011.30 No statistical difference was seen in maturogenesis when maxillary and mandibular teeth were compared. Therefore, the findings of this study may be applied to both mandibular and maxillary teeth.

Clinical Significance

This study's findings offer valuable insights for endodontic practice. Nonvital young immature permanent teeth with apical periodontitis can be successfully treated using MTA apexification and MTA revascularization procedures. By considering individual case characteristics, clinicians can optimize treatment selection, leading to improved patient outcomes and enhanced clinical practice.

Limitations

The present study had certain limitations. The sample size was small and study duration was short which may affect the results. Future research with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods is recommended to validate the findings and assess long term clinical effectiveness.

Conclusion

In the present study, the best resolution of periapical periodontitis in minimum time was shown by the apexification with MTA group, followed by revascularization with MTA group, and revascularization without MTA group, whereas maturogenesis was highly significant in revascularization with MTA compared to revascularization without MTA. The present study indicates that management of nonvital young immature permanent teeth with apical periodontitis/radiolucency requires careful, case-wise selection and individualized treatment planning.

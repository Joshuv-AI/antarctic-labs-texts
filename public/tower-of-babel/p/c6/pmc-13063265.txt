Incidence of acute exacerbation and its related factors among chronic obstructive pulmonary disease patients aged 40 years and older in China
Authors: Qi Luo, Shu Cong, Shige Qi, Jing Fan, Wenjing Wang, Xueting Wang, Liwen Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (AECOPD) represents a pivotal event in the clinical course of COPD and contributes substantially to disease progression and healthcare burden. This study examined the incidence and demographic distribution of AECOPD among Chinese COPD patients aged ≥40 years and evaluated factors associated with its occurrence, providing population-based evidence to inform community prevention, standardized treatment, rehabilitation, and health management strategies.MethodsData were derived from the Chinese Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Surveillance (2019–2020), a nationwide cross-sectional survey conducted in 125 surveillance counties (districts) across 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities in China. Using a multistage stratified cluster random sampling design, residents aged ≥40 years were enrolled. Participants completed standardized questionnaires, physical measurements, and pre- and post-bronchodilator spirometry administered by trained investigators. COPD was defined as a post-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV₁)/forced vital capacity (FVC) <70%. The incidence of AECOPD and corresponding hospitalization rates were estimated and compared across subgroups. Stepwise multivariable logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with AECOPD, with Model I including sociodemographic, behavioral, and environmental factors and Model II additionally incorporating disease-related characteristics.ResultsA total of 7455 COPD patients were included in the analysis. The overall incidence of AECOPD was 9.0% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 7.9–10.1%), and the hospitalization rate was 4.3% (95% CI: 3.5–5.0%). Higher incidence and hospitalization rates were observed among patients aged ≥65 years (10.8%, 5.7%) and those residing in southern region (10.3%, 5.0%). Elevated rates were also noted among individuals with body mass index (BMI) <18.5 kg/m² (16.1%, 10.0%), smoking history (9.1%, 4.3%), prior hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood (21.6%, 9.3%), exposure to biomass (10.8%, 5.3%), occupational exposure to harmful factors (10.5%, 4.9%), and a history of chronic respiratory diseases (28.2%, 14.9%). The highest rates were observed in patients with a modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) score ≥2 (40.7%, 23.0%). Rates were 22.4% and 11.5% among those with a COPD Assessment Test (CAT) score ≥10 and 33.0% and 19.5% among those with ≥3 comorbidities. By Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) grades, incidence and hospitalization rates were 4.6% and 1.7% (Stage I), 10.7% and 4.6% (Stage II), and 28.7% and 18.9% (Stages III–IV). Multivariable analyses identified residence in southern region, hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, mMRC score ≥2, CAT score ≥10, GOLD stage III–IV, and a higher number of comorbidities as independent risk factors for AECOPD.ConclusionAECOPD incidence and hospitalization rates remain substantial among community-dwelling COPD patients aged ≥40 years in China, with a significantly higher burden in southern region. Early-life severe respiratory disease, greater symptom severity, poorer lung function, and multiple comorbidities are strongly associated with increased AECOPD risk. Addressing regional disparities, strengthening comprehensive assessment and management, identifying high-risk individuals, and implementing targeted community-based interventions are essential to reduce AECOPD risk and mitigate the overall burden of COPD.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a major global cause of disability and mortality, particularly among older adults, and currently ranks as the third leading cause of death worldwide.1 The global burden of COPD is expected to continue increasing in the coming decades.2,3 In China, COPD is the most prevalent chronic respiratory disease and is associated with high prevalence, high mortality, and substantial societal and healthcare burden.4, 5, 6 Among individuals aged ≥40 years, the reported prevalence of COPD ranges from 13.6% to 13.7%.7,8 At the national level, the overall prevalence among adults aged ≥20 years is 8.6%, corresponding to an estimated 100 million individuals with COPD and accounting for approximately 25% of the global COPD burden.8

Acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) represents a pivotal event in the disease course. Exacerbations substantially increase the risk of hospitalization, contribute to irreversible decline in lung function and deterioration in quality of life,9 accelerate disease progression,10 and may result in death in severe cases.11,12 Accordingly, prevention of AECOPD is a central therapeutic objective during stable disease and a core component of long-term health management for patients with COPD in the community. In China, this priority is reflected in the National Essential Public Health Services Program, specifically the COPD Health Management Service Initiative. This nationally funded program provides free health services and standardized management for community-dwelling COPD patients, with prevention and management of AECOPD designated as key objectives. Within this context, robust population-based evidence on the incidence of AECOPD and its associated factors is of substantial public health and clinical importance. Such evidence supports targeted risk stratification and intervention, improves quality of life, and reduces hospitalization, disability, and mortality, while also enabling evaluation of the effectiveness of national COPD health policies and service delivery.

Definitions of AECOPD vary across epidemiological studies. The Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) defines AECOPD as an acute worsening of respiratory symptoms, including increased dyspnea, cough, sputum volume, and/or sputum purulence, which necessitates a change in medication or additional therapy.13 This definition has been widely adopted in major domestic and international epidemiological studies.14, 15, 16, 17 Despite this, data on the incidence of AECOPD in community settings remain limited, particularly in China. Existing evidence is largely derived from hospital-based or regional studies,18, 19, 20, 21 which may not accurately reflect the characteristics and disease burden of the broader community population. Using nationally representative data from the Chinese Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Surveillance conducted in 2019–2020, the present study examined the incidence and demographic distribution of AECOPD among community-dwelling COPD patients aged ≥40 years and explored factors associated with its occurrence. By providing comprehensive population-based estimates, this study aims to characterize the burden of AECOPD in the community, support targeted COPD health management strategies, and inform the development and optimization of healthcare policies and interventions for COPD patients in China.

Methods

Study population

Data were obtained from the Chinese Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Surveillance conducted during 2019–2020, a nationally representative cross-sectional survey. The surveillance employed a multistage stratified cluster random sampling design and was conducted in 125 surveillance counties (districts) across 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities in China. Data from Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, Macau Special Administrative Region, and Taiwan Province of China are not available. Eligible participants were residents who had lived in the selected counties or districts for at least 6 months during the 12 months preceding the survey. All participants completed standardized face-to-face interviews and underwent physical examinations, and pre- and post-bronchodilator spirometry. Detailed descriptions of the study design, sampling strategy, and quality control procedures have been reported previously.7,22 COPD was defined on the basis of post-bronchodilator spirometry, using a forced expiratory volume in one second to forced vital capacity ratio (FEV₁/FVC) of <0.7.13 Information on the occurrence of AECOPD and related hospitalizations within the previous year was collected through face-to-face interviews. Among 74,559 individuals assessed during the 2019–2020 surveillance period, 7457 participants met the diagnostic criteria for COPD. After exclusion of two individuals with missing data, a total of 7455 patients were included in the final analysis. The survey protocol was approved by the ethics review committee of the National Center for Chronic and Non-Communicable Disease Control and Prevention, Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (approval No. 201901). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Survey content

Data collection and management were conducted using an independently developed electronic data capture system. All interviews were administered face-to-face by trained healthcare professionals who completed standardized training and qualification assessments before participation. Collected data included sociodemographic characteristics (sex, age, ethnicity, education level, marital status, occupation, place of residence [urban/rural], and geographic region), personal medical history, and information on outpatient visits and hospitalizations related to worsening respiratory symptoms in the preceding year. Disease-related assessments included the COPD Assessment Test (CAT) and the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC). Information on smoking status, smoking exposure (pack-years), exposure to biomass, occupational exposure to harmful factors, and diagnoses of chronic respiratory diseases, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, diabetes, hypertension, and other chronic conditions was obtained based on diagnoses made by township-level or higher medical institutions. Physical measurements included height and weight. All eligible participants subsequently underwent spirometry using the same brand of spirometer, with FEV₁ and FVC recorded as the primary lung function parameters.

Definitions

The primary outcomes were: (1) the incidence of AECOPD within the preceding year, and (2) the corresponding hospitalization rate. AECOPD was defined in the questionnaire based on the GOLD guidelines,13 and confirmed by an affirmative response to the following survey question: “In the past year, did you experience an acute worsening of respiratory symptoms (e.g., aggravated cough, increased sputum production, shortness of breath, and/or wheezing) that disrupted daily activities or required medical attention?” Hospitalization for AECOPD was assessed using a two-step approach: participants were first asked whether they had been hospitalized for worsening respiratory symptoms during the past year (Yes or No), and those responding affirmatively were subsequently asked to report the number of hospitalizations within the previous 12 months.

The northern and southern region was defined using the Qinling Mountains–Huai River boundary. The Geographic regions were further categorized into North China, East China, Central China, South China, Southwest China, Northwest China, and Northeast China, based on provincial-level geographic and cultural similarities.23 A history of chronic respiratory disease was defined as a diagnosis made prior to the survey by township‑level or higher medical institutions and included asthma, chronic bronchitis, emphysema, pulmonary tuberculosis, bronchiectasis, and pulmonary interstitial fibrosis. Smoking exposure was quantified as pack-years, calculated as the number of cigarette packs smoked per day multiplied by the number of years smoked. Body mass index (BMI, kg/m2) was categorized as underweight (<18.5), normal weight (18.5 ≤ BMI < 24.0), overweight (24.0 ≤ BMI < 28.0), or obese (BMI ≥ 28.0). Definitions of other behavioral and environmental exposures, including smoking status, hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, occupational exposure to harmful factors, and biomass exposure, have been described previously.7,24, 25, 26 Comorbidities were defined based on diagnoses made by township‑level or higher medical institutions prior to the survey and included respiratory diseases, solid tumors, coronary heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, depression, and osteoporosis. Post-bronchodilator lung function indices were calculated using Chinese population‑specific reference equations,27 and airflow limitation severity was classified according to the GOLD criteria (GOLD I–IV).13

Quality control

During the study design phase, the questionnaire underwent iterative revision and optimization through expert consultation and pilot testing to ensure comprehensive coverage, clarity, and logical consistency. Data collection was conducted using an electronic information management system with built-in quality control functions designed to minimize entry errors and missing data. Standardized survey procedures were applied throughout the study. All investigators received uniform training and conducted surveys in accordance with a centralized protocol and quality assurance plan. The electronic questionnaire incorporated mandatory fields, predefined skip logic, and range checks to reduce invalid entries, missing responses, and logical inconsistencies. Audio recordings were used to monitor interview quality. A three-level quality control framework (site-level, provincial-level, and national-level) was implemented. Pulmonary function testing was subject to rigorous on-site quality control, and spirometry results were graded, with a quality pass rate exceeding 95%. Prior to statistical analysis, a unified data cleaning protocol was applied. Two independent teams conducted parallel data cleaning to ensure data accuracy and consistency.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Participant characteristics were summarized using frequencies and proportions. The Taylor series variance estimation method was applied to calculate sampling errors and 95% confidence intervals for AECOPD incidence and hospitalization rates across subgroups defined by sociodemographic characteristics, geographic region, exposure to risk factors, symptom burden, airflow limitation severity, and number of comorbidities. Differences between subgroups were assessed using the Rao–Scott chi‑square test to account for the complex survey design. Multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify factors independently associated with AECOPD using a two-step modeling strategy. Model I included age, sex, residence (urban or rural), marital status, educational level, northern and southern region, BMI, smoking exposure (pack-years), hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, exposure to biomass, and occupational exposure to harmful factors. Model II was additionally adjusted for disease-specific characteristics, including mMRC score, CAT score, GOLD grades, and number of comorbidities. Sensitivity analyses were performed using hospitalization for acute exacerbation within the past year as the outcome variable. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Incidence of acute exacerbation and hospitalization rate across participant characteristics

Among COPD patients aged ≥40 years, the overall incidence of AECOPD was 9.0% (95% CI: 7.9–10.1%), and the corresponding hospitalization rate was 4.3% (95% CI: 3.5–5.0%). Both incidence and hospitalization rates were significantly higher among individuals residing in southern region (10.3% and 5.0%, respectively) than among those living in northern region (7.2% and 3.2%; both P < 0.01). Similarly, patients aged ≥65 years had higher rates of AECOPD incidence and hospitalization (10.8% and 5.7%) compared with those aged 40–64 years (7.5% and 3.0%; both P < 0.001). Higher rates were also observed among participants without a spouse (11.5% and 6.6%) and among those with an educational level of primary school or lower (9.7% and 5.0%) (all P < 0.05). Incidence of acute exacerbation and hospitalization by disease characteristics

Marked differences in AECOPD incidence and hospitalization rates were observed across disease-related characteristics (P < 0.001). The highest rates were observed among patients with an mMRC score ≥2, with an AECOPD incidence of 40.7% and a hospitalization rate of 23.0%. Patients with a CAT score ≥10 also demonstrated substantially higher rates (22.4% and 11.5%) compared with those with a CAT score < 10 (3.3% and 1.1%, P < 0.001). Furthermore, both rates increased progressively with higher GOLD grades: GOLD I (4.6% and 1.7%), GOLD II (10.7% and 4.6%), and GOLD III–IV (28.7% and 18.9%) (both P < 0.001). Multivariable analysis of factors associated with AECOPD

Multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify factors associated with AECOPD. In Model I, the dependent variable was AECOPD, and independent variables included age, sex, residence (urban/rural), marital status, education level, region (southern/northern), BMI, smoking exposure (pack-years), hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, exposure to biomass, and exposure to occupational harmful factors. The results indicated that older age (≥65 years), without a spouse, living in southern region, BMI<18.5 kg/m2, hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, exposure to biomass and occupational exposure to harmful factors were significantly associated with an increased risk of AECOPD.

Model II further incorporated COPD-specific clinical characteristics. After adjustment, residence in southern region (OR = 1.57, 95% CI: 1.27–1.94), hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood (OR = 2.04, 95% CI: 1.33–3.14), mMRC score ≥2 (OR = 2.67, 95% CI: 2.01–3.54), and CAT score ≥10 (OR = 4.10, 95% CI: 3.28–5.11) remained independently associated with a higher risk of AECOPD. Increasing severity of airflow limitation and a greater number of comorbidities were also significantly associated with elevated risk. Sensitivity analyses using hospitalization for AECOPD within the past year as the outcome yielded consistent findings, demonstrating that residence in southern region, mMRC score ≥2, CAT score ≥10, greater airflow limitation severity, and higher comorbidity burden were associated with an increased risk of hospitalization for AECOPD (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

Reliable population-level estimates of the incidence and hospitalization rates of AECOPD, particularly from community-based studies, remain limited in China. Using data from a nationally representative survey spanning 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities, this cross-sectional analysis provides the nationwide assessment of AECOPD incidence and hospitalization rates among community-dwelling COPD patients aged ≥40 years. In addition, multivariable analyses were performed to identify factors associated with AECOPD across sociodemographic, behavioral, environmental, and disease-specific domains. Together, these findings establish an important evidence base for advancing precision in COPD health management, informing targeted AECOPD prevention strategies, and evaluating the effectiveness of related health policies and interventions.

The results demonstrate that both the incidence and hospitalization rates of AECOPD among community COPD patients aged ≥40 years in China remain substantial, at 9.0% and 4.3%, respectively. Given the estimated 100 million individuals with COPD nationwide, including approximately 77.2 million patients aged ≥40 years,7,8 the absolute annual number of AECOPD episodes and hospitalizations is considerable, imposing a significant social and economic burden. These findings underscore the considerable challenges associated with preventing and managing AECOPD in community settings. When compared with studies using similar definitions of AECOPD and comparable survey methodologies, the incidence and hospitalization rates observed in this study were slightly higher than those reported in the PLATINO study. The PLATINO study, a population-based investigation conducted in 2002 across five Latin American countries, reported an AECOPD incidence of 6.2% and a hospitalization rate of 2.0% among individuals aged ≥40 years.14 In contrast, a global survey conducted across 12 countries during 2012–2013 reported that 15% of COPD patients aged ≥40 years had been hospitalized for AECOPD in the preceding year. Within that survey, Japan reported an AECOPD incidence of 9.0% and a hospitalization rate of 5.0%,28 which are comparable to the present findings. In China, a 2022 analysis from the “Happy Breathing” project reported an AECOPD incidence of 26.9% among 1634 COPD patients identified from a high-risk, hospital-visiting population.21 Similarly, data from the multicenter prospective REAL study conducted between 2017 and 2019 showed that 40.2% of 2597 COPD patients attending tertiary hospitals experienced at least one acute exacerbation in the previous 12 months, compared with 59.4% of those treated in secondary hospitals.20 These marked differences across studies likely reflect variation in study populations, sampling frameworks, representativeness, definitions of AECOPD, and baseline disease severity.

Leveraging the breadth of nationally representative data, this study incorporated detailed sociodemographic, behavioral, environmental, and disease-specific clinical variables to examine potential determinants of AECOPD. The analysis demonstrated that patients aged ≥65 years, those without a spouse, living in southern region, individuals with BMI <18.5 kg/m2, history of hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, and those exposed to biomass or occupational harmful factors experienced a higher incidence of AECOPD. Older age was associated with increased AECOPD risk, potentially reflecting age-related declines in respiratory function, reduced pulmonary reserve, and heightened susceptibility to respiratory infections and respiratory failure.29 Previous studies have shown that recurrent lower respiratory infections in childhood are associated with impaired lung function and persistent respiratory symptoms in adulthood.30 Consistent with this evidence, the present study found that COPD patients with a history of hospitalization for severe pulmonary disease in childhood experienced a higher incidence of AECOPD later in life. These findings suggest that strategies for AECOPD prevention and control should adopt a life-course perspective. In addition to strengthening prevention and management of childhood respiratory infections, systematic assessment of early-life respiratory disease history among COPD patients may improve identification and management of individuals at high risk for AECOPD, thereby reducing the long-term risk of both COPD progression and acute exacerbations. Tobacco exposure remains a well-established contributor to the occurrence and progression of AECOPD.31,32 In China, more than 40% of smokers aged ≥40 years exhibit moderate or higher levels of nicotine dependence.33 Although the association between tobacco exposure and AECOPD incidence did not reach statistical significance in the present multivariable analysis, patients with a history of smoking and higher smoking intensity nonetheless exhibited higher rates of AECOPD. These findings indicate that the role of tobacco exposure in AECOPD risk remains clinically relevant and warrants continued attention in COPD management and prevention strategies.

This study identified clear geographic variation in the incidence of AECOPD, with a significantly higher incidence observed in southern region of China (10.3%) compared with northern regions (7.2%). This regional disparity is likely multifactorial and may reflect differences in influenza activity, climatic conditions, and winter heating practices between northern and southern region. Previous research has demonstrated distinct seasonal patterns of influenza epidemics across China, with northern region experiencing a primary peak during winter and spring, whereas southern region exhibits an additional smaller peak during summer. This pattern suggests more sustained or frequent influenza virus circulation in southern region.34 Moreover, higher population density and greater population mobility in southern region may further contribute to increased influenza transmission.35 Environmental factors may also play an important role. Studies have shown that cold and humid conditions can increase airway mucus secretion in patients with COPD, impair mucociliary clearance, increase susceptibility to respiratory viral infections, and trigger acute exacerbations.36 Higher ambient humidity levels and the relative absence of centralized winter heating in southern region may therefore contribute to the elevated incidence of AECOPD observed in these regions. Further research is warranted to elucidate the relative contributions of climatic, environmental, and social factors underlying regional differences in AECOPD prevalence across China.

Building on analysis of sociodemographic, behavioral, and environmental factors, this study further incorporated key indicators of COPD severity and progression, including mMRC score, CAT score, airflow limitation severity, and comorbidity burden. Several factors were independently associated with an increased risk of AECOPD, including residence in southern region, hospital admission for severe pulmonary disease in childhood, mMRC score ≥2, CAT score ≥10, severe or very severe airflow limitation (GOLD III–IV), and a higher number of comorbidities. Prior studies by Yamaya et al37 and Bouhuis et al38 similarly reported that higher symptom burden, as reflected by CAT and mMRC scores, and more severe airflow limitation were associated with increased AECOPD risk. In the present study, patients with an mMRC score ≥2 experienced a markedly high AECOPD incidence of 40.7% and a significantly elevated risk (OR = 2.67, 95% CI: 2.01–3.54) compared with those with mMRC scores <2. These findings are consistent with those reported by McGarvey et al39 in a community-based COPD cohort in the United Kingdom, in which mMRC scores of 2, 3, and 4 were associated with 1.36-, 2.00-, and 3.82-fold increases in future AECOPD risk, respectively. A clear dose–response relationship was also observed between airflow limitation severity and AECOPD incidence. Among COPD patients aged ≥40 years, incidence rates increased progressively from 4.6% in GOLD stage I to 10.7% in GOLD stage II and 28.7% in GOLD stages III–IV. This graded association between worsening lung function and AECOPD risk is consistent with previous evidence.40 Comorbid conditions are another major contributor to AECOPD, substantially increasing healthcare utilization, hospitalization, and mortality.41 Prior studies have reported that 32.1% of COPD patients have at least one comorbidity.17 Consistent with these findings,42 the present study demonstrated a progressive increase in AECOPD incidence with increasing comorbidity burden.

Taken together, these findings highlight two critical priorities for community-based COPD management. First, early identification of COPD and timely recognition of individuals at high risk for AECOPD are essential, particularly through targeted interventions addressing modifiable behavioral and environmental risk factors, especially among patients with early-stage or mild disease. Second, greater emphasis should be placed on comprehensive assessment of disease progression, including evaluation of respiratory symptoms, airflow limitation severity, and comorbidities. On the basis of these assessments, standardized treatment strategies and active rehabilitation programs should be implemented to slow disease progression, reduce the incidence of AECOPD, and improve patient quality of life.

This study represents the nationally representative, community-based investigation in China to estimate the incidence of AECOPD and identify its associated factors, providing the most up-to-date national estimates of AECOPD incidence and hospitalization rates. Rigorous quality control procedures were applied to both questionnaire administration and spirometry testing, ensuring high data quality, reliability, and representativeness. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. Information on respiratory symptom exacerbations during the preceding year was collected through face-to-face interviews, which may be subject to recall bias. In addition, because the survey was conducted in community settings, certain populations may have been underrepresented, including individuals with lower educational attainment, patients with mild disease who did not seek medical care, and those with severe medical conditions who were unable to participate in the survey. These factors may have led to an underestimation of AECOPD incidence. Despite these limitations, compared with hospital-based studies and regional surveys, this study provides a more representative depiction of COPD burden and AECOPD occurrence among community-dwelling patients across China.

In summary, the incidence and hospitalization rates of AECOPD remain high among COPD patients aged ≥40 years in China, presenting substantial challenges for community-level prevention and management. Multiple modifiable risk factors such as exposure to biomass are associated with an increased AECOPD risk. In addition, a history of severe pulmonary disease during childhood correlates with a higher incidence of AECOPD later in life. As COPD progresses, greater symptom severity, higher symptom burden, more advanced airflow limitation, and increasing comorbidity burden are also strongly associated with elevated risk. Effective community COPD management should therefore address regional disparities, emphasize early identification of high-risk individuals, target modifiable risk factors, and promote early and standardized intervention in patients with mild or early-stage disease. Concurrently, strengthening comprehensive assessment and integrated management strategies is essential to deliver precise health services, reduce AECOPD risk, improve quality of life, and ultimately alleviate the overall burden of COPD.

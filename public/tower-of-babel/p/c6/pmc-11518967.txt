Reply: Promoting the floating Bentall technique: Acknowledging historical context and advocating for wider adoption in cardiac surgery
Authors: Hugo Monteiro Neder Issa, Yuan Qiu, Munir Boodhwani
Published: 2024

Reply to the Editor:

We express our appreciation to Drs Kırali and Adademir for their insightful comments regarding our recently published article on the “floating Bentall” technique for aortic root enlargement.1,2 We fully acknowledge and respect the work of Dr Yakut on the “flange technique,” which shares many similarities with the technique we described.3 It is evident that both methods share the same concept and aim to address the critical issue of prosthesis-patient mismatch in patients with a small aortic annulus, an issue that continues to affect patients undergoing surgical aortic valve replacement. The Placement of Aortic Transcatheter Valves (PARTNER)-3 trial clearly demonstrated this issue as smaller prostheses were implanted in 57% of patients who underwent surgical aortic valve replacement (#19: 3%; #21: 17%; and #23: 37%).4

Although we were not specifically aware of their 2001 publication at the time of our submission, we were aware that variations of the “floating Bentall” have been described and used by others.5 The purpose of our work was first to highlight the salient features of this technique, specifically to accommodate a larger aortic valve prosthesis and second to provide a video demonstration of the technique in an extreme case of a very small aortic annulus.

We applaud the work of Yakut and colleagues in the description of a novel technique for the Bentall procedure and hope that our demonstration of a similar technique will ultimately promote greater adoption and facilitate its wider dissemination among the surgical community, ensuring that more patients can benefit from its application.

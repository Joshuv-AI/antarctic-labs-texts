Habitat analysis and other AI technologies for Ki-67 prediction in hepatocellular carcinoma: a multi-center study
Authors: Min Zhao, Chongfeng Duan, Xiaoming Zhou, Yuanxiang Gao, Xiaopeng Diao, Feng Li, Jun Zhang, Zhenbo Sun, Ruirui Zhao, Gang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo develop various artificial intelligence (AI) models including radiomics, deep transfer learning (DTL) and habitat analysis models, as well as a combined model, for the preoperative noninvasive prediction of Ki-67 expression in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).Materials and methodsA retrospective analysis was performed on 433 patients with pathologically confirmed HCC from two institutions. According to the postoperative immunohistochemical Ki-67 expression levels, patients were divided into a high Ki-67 expression group (n = 320) and a low Ki-67 expression group (n = 113). They were further split into a training set (n = 349) and a test set (n = 84) in chronological order. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify independent predictors of Ki-67 expression. Feature extraction was performed from radiomics, DTL, and habitat analysis, followed by subsequent model construction. Subsequently, the extracted multiple features were combined with clinical variables to establish a combined model, and a multivariable logistic regression model was used to construct a nomogram for this combined model. The performance of different models was compared using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), along with other diagnostic metrics including sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV). Statistical comparisons were conducted to evaluate the differences in performance between the models.ResultsIn the test set, the AUC values of the clinical, radiomics, DTL, habitat analysis and combined models were 0.741(95% CI: 0.616–0.866), 0.754(95% CI: 0.633–0.875), 0.805(95% CI: 0.700–0.911), 0.814(95% CI: 0.712–0.917) and 0.819(95% CI: 0.719–0.920), respectively. Among all the models, the combined model achieved the highest AUC value, followed by the habitat analysis model, but there was no statistical significance between the two. In addition, the combined model had the highest accuracy (94.3%) and sensitivity (95.2%) in the training set, while the habitat analysis model showed the highest accuracy (86.9%) and sensitivity (91.4%) in the test set.ConclusionRadiomics, DTL and habitat analysis models based on Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI are effective for preoperative noninvasive prediction of Ki-67 expression in HCC, and the combined model nomogram has potential clinical application value.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16192-y.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1], with treatment primarily involving surgical resection, liver transplantation, and transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) [2]. Despite the fact that surgical resection is an early radical treatment, the high recurrence rate remains the main cause of postoperative mortality in patients [3]. Ki-67 is a nuclear antigen that has been associated with treatment efficacy and prognosis of malignant tumors [4–6], and it is an independent predictor for assessing early recurrence and poor prognosis in HCC patients after surgery [7]. However, as Ki-67 detection currently relies on postoperative immunohistochemistry, its preoperative noninvasive prediction is valuable for assessing HCC recurrence risk and guiding treatment planning.

Radiomics can reflect the histological heterogeneity of tumors through high-throughput extraction of quantitative imaging features [8]. It has been shown that ultrasound, CT, or MR image features based on radiomics methods enable noninvasive prediction of Ki-67 expression [9–14]. However, the interpretation of radiomic features remains subjective.

Deep learning method, which can automatically extract higher-order image features to reduce manual dependency, has been successfully applied to tumor segmentation, diagnostic evaluation, and prognostic prediction [15–18], and provide a new way to predict Ki-67 expression noninvasively before surgery. This study used a deep transfer learning (DTL) method, pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned on task-specific datasets to enhance performance.

To visually analyze and quantitatively characterize tumor heterogeneity, habitat imaging has been developed, which segments tumors by multiple algorithms and divides the tumor and its microenvironment into subregions (i.e., habitats) with different biological characteristics [19–21]. However, habitat imaging methods have rarely been applied to liver assessment.

In addition, models combining multiple features can integrate multi-dimensional information, which is expected to further improve the prediction performance. There are no studies related to the establishment of deep learning model, habitat analysis model and combined model for preoperative prediction of Ki-67 metrics in HCC patients.

Gadolinium ethoxybenzyl diethylenetriamine pentaacetic acid (Gd-EOB-DTPA)-based MRI examination plays an important role in the diagnosis and prognostic evaluation of HCC [9, 22–24], and has more advantages than the traditional imaging methods such as ultrasound, CT, etc.Thus, This study aimed to construct the radiomics, DTL, and habitat analysis models based on Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI, and to establish a combined model integrating the clinical features with the above features. The performance of multiple models was compared for preoperative Ki-67 prediction in HCC, providing a novel approach for individualized treatment.

Materials and methods

This study included retrospective data from two medical centers. The institutional review board approved this retrospective study, and informed consent was waived due to the study’s retrospective nature.

Study population

Patients who underwent curative resection for HCC were identified retrospectively from two medical centers. Patients from Qingdao University Affiliated Hospital (October 2018 to March 2023) and Pingdu Central Hospital (March 2021 to April 2024) were included and categorized into the training set and the test set. Inclusion criteria were: (1) pathologically confirmed HCC and available Ki-67 testing results, (2) preoperative Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI within 3 weeks, and (3) no preoperative cancer-related treatment, including radiotherapy, chemotherapy, interventional embolization, or targeted therapies. Exclusion criteria were: (1) combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma, or metastatic liver tumors, (2) recurrent HCC after surgical resection or interventional therapy, and (3) poor-quality radiologic images. Pathologic characteristics

Immunohistochemical staining for Ki-67 protein was conducted, and its expression level was evaluated by quantifying the percentage of positively stained cells with brownish yellow nuclei. Then, based on previously established criteria [25–27], Ki-67 expression was classified as low (≤ 10%) or high (> 10%).

MRI protocols

All patients underwent Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI examinations using a unified GE Signa HDx 3.0T MRI scanning system with an 8-channel body coil, and there were no inter-institutional differences in scanner equipment. The scanning range was from the top of the diaphragm to the lower edge of the liver. All participating institutions strictly followed the same standardized scanning protocol, which included conventional sequences (fat-suppressed T2WI, DWI, dual-echo T1WI) and Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced LAVA sequence scans. For enhanced scans, contrast agent was injected via elbow vein at 0.1mL/kg and 1.0mL/s, with arterial, portal venous, equilibrium, and hepatobiliary phase images acquired at 15s, 45s, 180s, and 15 min post-injection, respectively. Eventually, each subject’s image dataset contained T2WI, DWI, arterial phase (AP), portal venous phase (PVP), and hepatobiliary phase (HBP) sequences. Image analysis and data measurement

MR image analysis was performed by two abdominal radiologists (9 and 6 years of abdomen diagnostic experience, respectively) who were blinded to all clinical and pathological information of the patients. In case of disagreement, a third reader (with 20 years of experience in HCC diagnosis) was consulted to reach a consensus. Two radiologists independently evaluated the following 14 MR imaging features of HCC, including maximum tumor diameter, number of nodules, tumor edge, shape, intratumoral fat, tumor pseudocapsule, halo sign, intratumoral necrosis, enhancement pattern in the arterial phase, vascular malformation, signal on hepatobiliary phase images, peritumoral hypointensity on hepatobiliary, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and satellite nodules. Delineation of the Region of Interest (ROI)

Using ITK-SNAP (version 4.02, https://www.itksnap.org/), 3D manual segmentation of the imaging data was performed. The intra-tumoral regions of HCC were manually delineated on T2WI, DWI, AP, PVP, and HBP images. Select the region of interest (ROI) with the largest possible measurement level and maintain consistency in the ROI level between sequences. Before feature extraction, crop the MRI images at 0.5% and 99.5% of the intensity of the entire three-dimensional data and use the z-score method for intensity normalization.

Extraction of radiomics features and DTL features and model construction

Using 3D Slicer software (version 4.11, https://www.slicer.org/) for image preprocessing and tumor segmentation. The images were preprocessed to ensure standardization across different datasets. To eliminate undesired low-frequency intensity inhomogeneity, the N4ITK MRI bias field correction technique was used. Radiomic features were extracted from each ROI using 3D Slicer’s Radiomics module, and pre-fusion of radiomics features from five sequences was conducted. The fused features were filtered by intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), Pearson correlation, and the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 5-fold cross-validation. A single machine learning model was then trained with the selected features to build an optimal predictive fusion model.

Following intensity normalization of the images, preprocessing for generating 2D slice images was performed. For the generation of 2D slice images, a cubic bounding box was positioned at the largest cross-section of the tumor to ensure complete enclosure of the entire tumor within the bounding box. On each MRI slice image, the bounding box containing the tumor was resampled to 224 × 224 pixels using bilinear interpolation to serve as input for the deep learning model. This study employed a classic pre-trained CNN model (ResNet-50), which was fine-tuned using the largest ROI from both the training set. The batch size and epochs were set to 32 and 30, respectively, for model fine-tuning. All images from the training set and test set were fed into the fine-tuned model separately to extract DTL features, where the test set images were only used as independent data for feature extraction and subsequent performance evaluation, without any involvement in model training or optimization. The prediction probabilities output by the five single-sequence deep learning models were integrated into a multivariate logistic regression analysis, and a stepwise regression method was employed to develop the final fusion model. Specifically, deep transfer learning was utilized for pre-training, followed by fine-tuning optimization performed exclusively on the five-sequence slice images within the training cohort, with no information from the test set used in this process.

Extraction of habitat analysis features and model construction

Image preprocessing was performed using pyradiomics (version 3.0.1). In addition to the default filters provided by the official version, additional filters including Laplacian, Gabor, High Pass Filter, and Contrast Stretching were applied to extract multi-level feature information for enhanced model training. The voxel spacing for image resampling was set to [3, 3, 3]. Feature extraction was performed using the pyradiomics module, which included localized (ROI) pixel intensity normalization of the original images. The voxel values were mapped to the range [0, 255] to mitigate intensity variations caused by different scanning devices, thereby improving model fitting performance. Data standardization was performed using the Quantile Transform method. Five distinct habitat segmentations were applied individually to each of the five single sequences, dividing each sequence into two regions (Region 1 and Region 2) (Fig. 3).

Combined model construction

Univariate logistic regression was performed for all clinical features against the grouping labels, and features with a significance test p-value of the coefficient less than 0.05 were selected. The selected features were then combined with radiomic features, deep learning features, and habitat analysis features to develop a multivariate logistic regression model. To identify key predictive variables and reduce model complexity, this study employed stepwise regression to optimize dimensionality reduction by eliminating non-significant features. A bidirectional stepwise regression was adopted, with variable selection guided by the principle of minimizing the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). Specifically, the method initiates with the full model and iteratively alternates between variable removal and inclusion, ultimately yielding a refined model optimized by the lowest AIC value, which represents the optimal combined model.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables are presented as mean and standard deviation (if normally distributed) or median and range (if non-normally distributed), while categorical data are presented as counts and proportions. Continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, while categorical variables were analyzed using χ² or Fisher’s exact test. Perform univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses to identify independent risk factors for predicting Ki-67 expression. Multivariate regression analysis will include univariate predictors with p < 0.05. The inter-observer and intra-observer reproducibility of the extracted features was evaluated using the ICC. Radiomic features, DTL features, and habitat analysis features demonstrating high stability (ICC ≥ 0.8) were retained for subsequent analysis. To evaluate the discriminative ability of parameters and models, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. The DeLong test was employed to compare the AUC values among different predictive models. Two-sided p-values < 0.05 were considered significant. Statistical analyses were performed with R (version 4.3.1; The R Foundation for Statistical Computing), Python (version 3.7; https://www.python.org/).

Results

Clinical data analysis

A total of 433 HCC patients (median age: 59 years, range: 17–82 years; 353 males (81.5%)) were included in this study. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed on clinical variables and MRI imaging features, identifying sex, age, HBV status, location, and MRI characteristics (including maximum tumor diameter, intratumoral fat, and vascular malformation) as independent risk factors for predicting Ki-67 expression (Table S1 and Supplementary Material S1). Subsequently, a logistic regression model was constructed based on these selected clinical features.

Radiomic feature selection and model construction

A total of 1,834 radiomics features were extracted from each sequence ROI, and the LASSO algorithm was further used to select the 12 most predictive features (Fig. 4).

Habitat analysis model construction

A total of 3290 features were initially extracted. Subsequent feature selection was performed for each individual sequence (Fig. 5). Based on model performance optimization, the following sequences and subregions were ultimately selected for the integrated habitat model: 15MIN (1), A (1), V (1), V (2), T2(1), DWI (1), and DWI (2), where (1) and (2) denote specific habitat subregions within each sequence. After integration, 113 radiomic features were derived from these models. Following LASSO reduction, 61 features were retained (Table S2).

Combined model development and evaluation

The predictive performance of the clinical model, radiomics model, DTL model, habitat analysis model, and combined model was compared. The AUC values with 95% CI, accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of the five models in both the training and test sets are presented in Table 3. In the training set, the AUC values for the clinical model, radiomics model, DTL model, habitat analysis model, and combined model were 0.751, 0.891, 0.944, 0.837, and 0.974, respectively. In the test set, the corresponding AUC values were 0.741, 0.754, 0.805, 0.814, and 0.819. Comparative analysis of ROC curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) across different models demonstrated that the combined model outperformed clinical, radiomics, and deep learning models in preoperatively predicting Ki-67 expression in HCC patients (Fig. 6). Although the combined model achieved the highest AUC value, the DeLong test revealed no statistically significant differences in AUC values among the five models (all p > 0.05, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant). In the training set, the combined model achieved the highest values in accuracy (94.3%), sensitivity (95.2%), and specificity (91.9%). In the test set, the habitat analysis model demonstrated the highest accuracy (86.9%) and sensitivity (91.4%), while the highest specificity (92.9%) was observed in both the radiomics and DTL models (Table 3). Overall, the combined model exhibited the best predictive performance, leading to the subsequent development of a nomogram (Fig. 6c) to simplify the interpretation of complex and multidimensional parameters [28].

Discussion

In this study, we first developed a deep learning model, a habitat analysis model, and an integrated multi-feature combined model based on Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI to preoperatively predict Ki-67 expression in HCC patients. We systematically evaluated the predictive performance of multiple models for Ki-67. Additionally, this study incorporated multi-center data with chronological grouping, which enhanced the model’s stability and reproducibility.

Studies by QIU et al. [29] and LIU et al. [30] demonstrated that the nomogram combining T1 mapping features and clinical factors can predict Ki-67 expression. LI et al. [31] employed multi-model DWI histogram analysis for Ki-67 prediction. Although these studies achieved promising results, the image information extracted from single sequences remains relatively limited and may not fully capture the comprehensive characteristics of tumors. YAN et al. [32] developed a random forest model using multi-sequence features to predict Ki-67 expression. Single-sequence AUC values ranged approximately between 0.70 and 0.75. After integrating the top 10 radiomic features from the single sequences, the AUC increased to 0.77, likely due to reduced redundancy and enhanced key features through multi-sequence fusion. Similarly, FAN et al. [10] demonstrated that the model integrating AP and HBP features achieved optimal predictive performance. Rather than directly comparing the efficacy of the five single sequences, this study applied dimensionality reduction to their features and derived 12 texture features. The AP sequence contributed more texture features, likely due to the complex enhancement patterns. LIAO et al. [33] demonstrated that incorporating peritumoral radiomic features improves model performance, suggesting that HCC progression is associated with both the tumor microenvironment and surrounding regions. ZHU et al. [34] extracted features from intratumoral and 3 mm peritumoral regions using super-resolution reconstructed CT images, confirming that combined features outperformed those from a single region. Our study focused solely on intratumoral features derived from multi-sequence Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI, future work could integrate T1 mapping and peritumoral regions to further optimize the model. Currently, the prediction of Ki-67 expression in HCC remains primarily relies on traditional radiomics method, which has certain limitations. Traditional radiomics method depends on manual feature extraction and typically analyze the entire tumor region, yet fail to adequately capture intratumoral spatial heterogeneity [35], thereby compromising the predictive accuracy of the model. Previous studies have demonstrated the feasibility of deep learning methods [36–38] and habitat imaging [39, 40] in predicting the biological behavior of HCC and its postoperative recurrence. The DTL model constructed in this study learns features directly from imaging data, avoiding the subjectivity of manual feature definition. Compared to radiomics model, it demonstrates superior predictive performance, indicating that large-scale pre-trained models possess stronger feature transfer capabilities, enabling them to maintain robust performance even with limited medical samples. However, tumor segmentation was performed manually using ITK-SNAP, and the DTL model relies on the maximum two-dimensional section rather than the complete three-dimensional tumor volume, which leads to potential loss of three-dimensional heterogeneity information and observer dependence. These limitations may result in the DTL model performing secondarily in the efficacy of all single models, as spatial information loss prevents the model from fully learning comprehensive tumor characteristics.

Further comparison revealed that the habitat imaging model outperformed the deep learning model, indicating that quantifying and visualizing distinct subregions within the tumor can provide more detailed information regarding HCC heterogeneity, thereby offering more valuable insights for the preoperative prediction of Ki-67 expression in HCC. The habitat analysis model exhibited excellent overall performance; although its AUC value was not the highest, it achieved the highest accuracy and sensitivity in the training set, which may be attributed to its ability to capture tumor spatial heterogeneity closely related to tumor biological characteristics, with strong stability and generalization ability.

Chen et al. [41] developed a combined model integrating radiomic features and deep learning features based on venous-phase CT images, while Liu et al. [42] constructed a combined model integrating habitat analysis features and deep learning features using CE-MRI images. Although these two studies focused on different disease domains, their results consistently demonstrated that the combined model outperformed any single-modality model. This indicates that integrating multiple feature types can further enhance model performance by leveraging complementary information, which is partially consistent with our findings. Since Gd-EOB-DTPA MRI provides both structural information of lesions and functional information from the hepatobiliary phase [23, 43], we developed a combined model integrating clinical features, radiomic features, deep learning features, and habitat analysis features based on Gd-EOB-DTPA MRI to predict HCC Ki-67 expression, which achieved optimal predictive performance. However, the AUC value was only marginally higher than that of the habitat analysis model alone. This limited improvement may be primarily attributed to the insufficient predictive power of the clinical and radiomics models when used independently. The combined model achieved the highest AUC of 0.974 in the training set and 0.819 in the test set, and its significant AUC decrease raises concerns about overfitting. Potential reasons include the inclusion of multi-dimensional features that may lead the model to capture non-generalizable noise in the training set, as well as inherent data distribution differences between the two sets. Future studies aim to further enhance model performance by expanding the sample size and optimizing feature selection methods.

This study has several limitations. First, although we attempted to reduce bias by using dual-center data and dividing the training and test sets chronologically, the retrospective design made selection bias unavoidable. Second, there is currently no standardized threshold for Ki-67 expression levels in HCC, and different criteria such as 5%, 14% or 25% have been used in established studies [30, 44, 45]. The cutoff value of 10% that we defined may be controversial. Third, the absence of external validation may lead to a certain degree of overfitting of the data. Additionally, tumor segmentation was performed manually using ITK-SNAP. In future studies, we will attempt to adopt ITK-SNAP-supported semi-automatic or automatic segmentation methods such as active contour methods to reduce observer dependence and improve segmentation reproducibility, and explore integrating three-dimensional tumor volume information to enhance model performance and clinical application value.

In conclusion, the habitat analysis model and deep learning model established in this study based on Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI provide new references for the preoperative prediction of Ki-67 expression in HCC. Furthermore, the combined model integrating multiple types of features further improves the predictive performance for Ki-67 expression. This non-invasive and effective method for predicting Ki-67 expression could assist clinicians in making better treatment-related decisions and may serve as a reference for guiding individualized prognostic management in HCC patients.

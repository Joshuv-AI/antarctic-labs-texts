Knowledge mapping and visualization of immune checkpoint inhibitor-related pneumonitis: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Yanyan Jiang, Mingling Xu, Shanlin Xu, Qian Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown significant effectiveness in cancer treatment, yet checkpoint inhibitor-related pneumonitis (CIP) remains a severe immune-related adverse event (irAE). This study conducts a bibliometric analysis to identify potential research directions for CIP associated with ICIs in cancer therapy.MethodsWe retrieved articles published from 1991 to 2024 in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. Bibliometric tools, including VOSviewer, R-bibliometrix, and CiteSpace, were employed to analyze publication trends, contributing countries, journals, authors, and keywords.ResultsA total of 673 publications were analyzed, with the United States, China, and Japan identified as the primary contributors. The Lancet Oncology was the leading journal in terms of article volume and impact factor. Jarushka Naidoo had the highest H-index and publication output among authors. Recent research has focused on enhancing ICI efficacy in lung cancer while minimizing safety risks associated with adverse events through various drug combinations and therapeutic modifications. Key areas of emphasis include expanding ICI applications beyond melanoma and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), managing severe CIPs and pre-existing pulmonary conditions, integrating radiotherapy and chemoradiotherapy with immunotherapy, and exploring corticosteroids and immunosuppressants.ConclusionAlthough the number of clinical trials on ICIs is increasing, effective strategies for preventing CIP and managing its symptoms are still lacking, and international collaboration in this research area is limited. This analysis offers a comprehensive overview of the current landscape, highlighting emerging research trends and key areas for future investigation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03769-5.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) that block inhibitory molecules regulating immune system pathways in the tumor microenvironment. This blockade promotes the systemic activation of immune cells and enhances anti-tumor T cell activity. As a result, ICIs have been integrated into cancer treatments [1], with numerous studies showing clinical benefits for a subset of patients with advanced-stage cancers [2]. ICIs specifically target cell surface receptors, such as cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4 (CTLA-4), programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), and programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) [2]. However, various immune-related adverse events (irAEs) have been reported in the literature, which may be associated with ICI therapies. Among these adverse events, checkpoint inhibitor-related pneumonitis (CIP) is the leading cause of death among irAEs linked to ICIs, accounting for 28.0% (23/82) of irAE-related fatalities [3]. The incidence of CIP varies by tumor type, with significantly higher rates observed in lung cancer and renal cell carcinoma. Although CTLA-4 inhibitors alone may pose a lower risk of pneumonitis, their combination with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors markedly increases the risk of CIP [4].

CIP is characterized by respiratory distress and/or other symptoms following ICI treatment, alongside the emergence of new inflammatory lesions on chest computed tomography (CT) scans. It is crucial to rule out infections, tumor progression, and alternative causes such as radiotherapy-induced lung injury or acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [5]. Common symptoms include dyspnea, reduced exercise tolerance, cough, fever, and chest pain. Due to the challenge of distinguishing CIP from infectious pneumonia, early bronchoscopy, including bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL), is recommended whenever feasible to aid in diagnosis and treatment [6]. Clinical symptoms, CT findings, and serological markers often lack specificity, complicating diagnosis in critically ill patients who cannot undergo bronchoscopy. Pathologically, CIP typically features lymphocytic infiltration, and in some cases, granulomatous inflammation. It remains unclear whether these infiltrates correspond to specific subgroups of anti-tumor T cells [7]. The severity of CIP is graded based on clinical and imaging criteria, ranging from asymptomatic (Grade 1) to life-threatening (Grade 4). The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines stress the need for rigorous monitoring, particularly in severe cases, regardless of the initial grade. Most cases of CIP respond well to corticosteroids; however, 15%-30% exhibit poor responsiveness. An evaluation for glucocorticoid-resistant CIP is recommended within 48–72 h, defined as a lack of improvement or progression despite adequate steroid dosing. For refractory cases unresponsive to steroids, it is essential to exclude alternative diagnoses. Available treatment options include intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), tocilizumab (IL-6 inhibitor), infliximab (TNFα inhibitor), mycophenolate mofetil, and cyclophosphamide, though these require further investigation [6, 8].

Bibliometric analysis is a widely used quantitative research method for mapping the structure, development, and trends of scientific fields. It is especially valuable for emerging disciplines or topics with relatively limited literature, as it can systematically reveal research hotspots, collaboration patterns, and knowledge gaps, even when the total number of publications is small [9, 10]. Previous studies have shown that bibliometric approaches can provide meaningful insights into new or rapidly growing areas, independent of the absolute volume of publications [11]. For instance, bibliometric studies with fewer than 100 included papers have successfully identified critical research directions and facilitated scholarly communication in niche fields [12]. Therefore, the number of included articles should not be viewed as a limitation, but rather as a reflection of the field’s current stage of development.

In recent years, interest in ICIs for cancer treatment has surged, especially regarding their clinical outcomes and safety. However, the current research landscape is still unclear. To address this uncertainty, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis is necessary to identify publication trends, geographic distribution of research efforts, leading institutions, prominent journals, key contributors, and significant thematic keywords. This study aims to clarify and reveal patterns of scientific collaboration, identify emerging trends, and forecast future research directions.

Methods

Literature source and search strategy

A comprehensive search was conducted to identify publications from 1991 to 2024 using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. The search employed the keywords “pneumonitis” and “immune checkpoint inhibitor,” along with their variations. The complete search strategy was as follows: (TS=((Pneumonitis* AND (Immune checkpoint inhibitor OR CTLA-4 OR PD-1 OR PD-L1)) OR Immune-related Pneumonitis* OR Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-related pneumonitis* OR ICI-P OR Immune checkpoint inhibitor-related chronic pneumonitis* OR Immune Blockade-Related Pneumonia)). To minimize fluctuations in the number of articles and citations, the database retrieval was performed on a single day, August 22, 2024. The inclusion criteria for this review were: (1) publications must be in English, and (2) the literature type classified as an article.

Data collection and statistical analysis

General information regarding the selected studies—including the author, publication year, title, journal, country of author affiliation, author institutions, keywords, annual publication count, total citations, and average citations—was extracted from the full text and imported into Microsoft Excel 16 for further identification and analysis. For statistical analysis, bibliometric tools were utilized, specifically VOSviewer 1.6.20, R-bibliometrix 4.3.3, and CiteSpace 6.3.R1. R-bibliometrix enabled the assessment of the national distribution of corresponding authors’ publications and the publication counts of various institutions [13]. VOSviewer generated visual maps that illustrated the intricate network of research interests and hotspots within the field. In this bibliometric analysis, VOSviewer also examined collaborations among countries, organizations, and authors, in addition to the co-occurrence of journal networks and keyword co-occurrence networks [14]. CiteSpace provided supplementary support for keyword burst analysis, facilitating the identification of the initiation and conclusion of changes in keyword prominence, as well as sudden keyword bursts [15] .

Results

Overview of publications

Literature searches in the database identified 1,083 studies in total. After excluding 358 studies that did not meet the specified criteria, 673 studies subjected for final analysis (Fig. 1). The literature included contributions from 6,247 authors across 222 journal sources spanning from 1991 to 2024. Figure 2 shows the increasing publication trend, with notable growth beginning in 2015, reflecting heightened academic interest and research focus in the field. These articles were cited a total of 12,110 times, yielding an average citation rate of 59.93 times per article. The research demonstrated high relevance to the topic, as indicated by the 1,095 keywords assigned by authors that aligned with the research themes. Notably, all selected studies were published collaboratively, with the exception of five studies authored by a single individual. On average, each study had 11.5 co-authors, and the level of international collaboration among authors was 21.4%.

The United States ranked first in Total Publications (TP) with 1,295 publications and Total Citations (TC) with 23,190 citations, while China and Japan ranked second in TP and TC, respectively (Table 1 & Supplementary Figure S1). The United Kingdom and Egypt recorded the highest Proportion of Multiple Country Publications (MCP_Ratio) at 66.7%, indicating a strong collaborative approach. Among the top 20 countries, Spain had the highest average citations per publication at 260.0, followed by the United Kingdom at 124.3. Analysis of journals

The top 20 journals with the highest impact are presented in Table 3. A majority of these journals were classified within the Q1 or Q2 categories. Analysis of keywords

The network visualization presented in Fig. 7 illustrates the co-occurrence of keywords within the selected literature. A total of 132 keywords were identified in the co-occurrence network, with the least frequently occurring keyword appearing five times. “nivolumab” (193), “docetaxel” (133), “pembrolizumab” (124), and “chemotherapy” (109) emerged as the top four keywords. The yellow keywords (e.g., “nsclc” and “radiotherapy”) surfaced in recent years, supplanting the green nodes (e.g., “nivolumab” and “chemotherapy”) and the purple nodes (e.g., “advanced melanoma” and “long-term safety”), highlighting the evolution of research focus over time. The visualization map of keyword bursts is presented in Fig. 8. Among the top 20 keywords identified from the extracted citation bursts, the three foundational keywords with the highest burst strength were “melanoma” (15.22), “ipilimumab” (7.17), and “radiotherapy” (6.02). In recent years, “risk factors”, “radiotherapy”, “chemoradiotherapy”, and “efficacy” have attracted the attention of researchers and continue to be subjects of intensive investigation.

Discussion

General summary

This study presents a bibliometric analysis of immune checkpoint inhibitors and their association with pneumonitis over the past three decades. It includes visualizations and tables that enhance understanding of the research landscape, highlighting hotspots, emerging trends, and the intellectual framework of the field. A significant increase in the number of published articles was noted, with overall publications steadily rising and peaking in 2020. The United States was the leading contributor, followed by China and Japan. The top three institutions in this research were the University of Texas System, Harvard University, and the MD Anderson Cancer Center, while Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center exhibited the highest level of collaboration. Among the authors, Jarushka Naidoo had the most publications, while F. Stephen Hodi stood out for both his high publication count and substantial collaborative contributions. The journal Lancet Oncology published the most articles, followed by the Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer. These findings indicate that further investigation in this area is needed.

International collaboration in ICI-related pneumonitis research

Although our analysis demonstrates that international collaboration remains limited in this field, there are signs of increasing multinational efforts. For example, the KEYNOTE-001 and KEYNOTE-024 studies [17, 18] were large, multicenter trials involving researchers from the United States, Europe, and Asia, and they included systematic evaluation of immune-related adverse events, including pneumonitis. More recently, the IMpower150 and IMpower133 trials [19, 20] also involved international research teams and addressed pulmonary toxicity in global patient cohorts. Furthermore, Naidoo et al. [21]conducted a multinational meta-analysis of ICI-related pneumonitis, pooling data from North America, Europe, and Asia. These examples illustrate the growing, but still insufficient, international research collaboration in this area. As the clinical use of ICIs expands worldwide, there is a compelling need for more prospective, globally coordinated studies to better understand and manage ICI-related pneumonitis across diverse populations [3, 17, 22].

Current research from High-impact authors

In the field of ICIs and cancer immunotherapy, the three authors who have made the most significant contributions are Jarushka Naidoo, F. Stephen Hodi, and Julie R. Brahmer. Professor Jarushka Naidoo is recognized as an expert in tumor immunology, with a research focus on immunotherapy across various malignancies. Recently, she has investigated the improved prognostic outcomes associated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and ICIs in early-stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), suggesting a potential shift in treatment strategies [23]. Julie R. Brahmer’s research and clinical practice are concentrated on the development of novel therapies for lung cancer and mesothelioma. She has co-authored numerous publications concerning drug development for thoracic malignancies and immunotherapy. Her most recent studies address the resistance of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and HER2 exon 20 to TKI-based therapies in NSCLC [24]. F. Stephen Hodi’s research primarily focuses on advancements in genetic therapies and immunotherapies for the treatment of malignant melanoma. His latest investigations have cantered on various ICI drugs utilized in the management of advanced melanoma [25]. The authors with the highest levels of co-authorship are Ou Yamaguchi and Takaaki Tokito from Japan. Ou Yamaguchi has engaged in extensive collaborations with colleagues at Saitama Medical University. His team’s recent research has concentrated on clinical issues related to adverse immune events associated with immune checkpoint inhibitors [26]. Takaaki Tokito’s team from Shizuoka Cancer Center has recently explored the characteristics of the tumor microenvironment in NSCLC patients treated with PD-1/L1 inhibitor monotherapy over time [27].

Research hotspots and frontiers

The citation burst intensity of keywords indicates that “melanoma” and “combination” exhibited the highest levels of citation intensity in the initial years, reflecting a concentrated research interest in melanoma and combination therapies before 2015. As immunotherapy advanced, related keywords such as “ipilimumab,” “PD-1,” and “antibody” gained prominence between 2016 and 2020, particularly in studies examining antibodies and PD-1 pathways. By 2020, terms such as “immune-related adverse event” and “inhibitor-related pneumonitis” also experienced an increase in citation intensity, signaling a growing scholarly interest in the adverse effects associated with immunotherapy. Following 2022, the integration of treatment modalities, including “radiotherapy” and “chemoradiotherapy,” emerged as a new research focus, emphasizing a trend toward the amalgamation of immunotherapy with traditional therapies. Collectively, these keywords illustrate the evolving landscape of ICIs and CIP, capturing the transition from foundational research to clinical applications and the management of adverse effects.

A re-examination of the top 20 keywords that experienced the most significant citation surges revealed key research hotspots and trends across various areas: Since the approval of ICI therapies in 2011, there has been a notable increase in the number of ICI-related pharmaceuticals entering clinical trials and receiving approvals for real-world cancer treatments. The range of cancers treated with ICI agents has expanded beyond melanoma and NSCLC to encompass urothelial carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, and gastric or esophagogastric junction cancer [17, 22, 28, 29]. Then, there has been a particular focus on CIP in NSCLC, with reported prevalence rates ranging from 2% to 38% in clinical trials and from 4.8% to 39.3% in real-world studies. These rates are significantly higher than the overall incidence of 3% to 5% observed across various tumor types. Patients face an increased risk of developing severe and complex CIP symptoms, particularly due to pre-existing pulmonary conditions associated with their tumors [30]. Effectively managing adverse events is crucial, especially for long-term survivors, emphasizing the need to balance treatment efficacy with safety in ICI therapies. A variety of ICI drugs and combinations—including monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies [31, 32] targeting CTLA-4, PD-1, and PD-L1—are being utilized in this patient population to enhance treatment efficacy while minimizing the risk of irAEs. Research is increasingly focused on identifying ICI therapies that can be employed across a broader range of malignancies to improve patient survival while reducing the incidence of CIP and other irAEs [33]. The introduction of specific chemotherapeutic agents, such as docetaxel, paclitaxel, and cisplatin, highlights advances in integrating traditional chemotherapy with ICIs, potentially improving response rates and addressing resistance. The European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) recommends adjusting drug doses and modifying treatment cycles in response to severe toxicities [34]. Common management strategies include escalating corticosteroid doses and utilizing immunosuppressants [33]; however, optimal corticosteroid regimens remain undefined [30]. Furthermore, the combination of ICI therapies with targeted therapies reflects an increasing interest in these integrated approaches, which aim to enhance therapeutic outcomes while effectively managing the risks associated with CIP and other irAEs. This indicates ongoing research into optimizing treatment protocols.

Limitations

Potential biases in our analysis arise from our reliance on citation counts, which may not accurately reflect the clinical impact of an article due to their dependence on publication date. While the WoSCC database includes a substantial number of high-quality scientific publications across diverse research topics, focusing solely on studies from this single database may overlook relevant research found elsewhere, potentially skewing the overall trends. Additionally, our bibliometric analysis was limited to studies published in English, which may reduce the generalizability of our findings and introduce a heightened risk of bias [35]. Furthermore, several additional limitations inherent to bibliometric approaches should be acknowledged. First, citation-based metrics can be influenced by self-citations and may not always capture the true scientific or clinical value of a publication. Second, delays in the accumulation of citations (citation lag) can underestimate the impact of more recent research. Third, author name disambiguation and institutional name variations may result in under- or over-estimation of productivity and collaboration networks. Fourth, the use of a single database (WoSCC) may introduce selection bias, as some relevant articles indexed in other databases such as Scopus or PubMed may have been missed. Fifth, bibliometric analysis primarily provides quantitative data and does not assess the methodological quality or scientific rigor of individual studies. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted with these limitations in mind, and future research could benefit from integrating multiple databases and qualitative assessment strategies to achieve a more comprehensive overview of the field [9, 11].

Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of ICIs and cancer immunotherapy-related complications to assess the literature landscape, track the topic’s evolution, and identify future research hotspots. Since 2010, when ICI drugs successfully completed Phase III clinical trials and received initial approval for cancer treatment, the field has attracted considerable attention. This interest has led to a significant increase in clinical studies exploring various ICI drugs as therapeutic options for cancer. Our analysis offers foundational insights into this research area, outlining the current state of scholarly inquiry and highlighting potential trends. Current research hotspots primarily focus on the efficacy and safety of different ICI drugs compared to alternative therapies for various cancer types. Additionally, the prevention and management of CIP in lung cancer patients may represent a crucial area for future investigation.

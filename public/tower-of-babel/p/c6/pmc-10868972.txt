Dermoscopy of Washboard Nails
Authors: Shivani Bansal, Parul Chojer
Published: 2023

Sir,

Habit tic deformity is a form of nail dystrophy caused by habitual external trauma to the nail matrix. Thumbnails are most commonly involved. However, the middle fingernail is most commonly affected when a single nail is involved. History of manipulation of proximal nail fold by adjacent fingernail is almost always present.[1] Changes are manifested as central depression and transverse parallel ridging over nails, resembles washboard, thus given the name, washboard nails. In severe cases, cuticles may disappear.[1] Diagnosis of this condition is mainly clinical. However, dermoscopy aids in delineating difficult to observe signs of repetitive traumas; microhaemorrhage, branching grooves, etc.[2] Median canaliform dystrophy of Heller; its principal differential diagnosis, is manifested as midline splitting of the nail with oblique ridging. The cuticle is retained in the condition.

Three patients presented to our skin department with roughened nails of bilateral thumbs for 3-4 years. All three patients had a history of nail biting and repeated manipulation of nails with index fingernails. The rest of the history was unremarkable. Clinically, central transverse ridging extending laterally was seen in all patients [Figure 1a and b]. Dermoscopic examination revealed loss of nail shine, periungual scales, transverse, longitudinal and branching furrows, pyramid lunula or macrolunula, splinter haemorrhages and onycholysis [Figure 2a-d]. The fungal infection was excluded using direct microscopic examination with potassium hydroxide preparation and fungal culture. Clinical and dermoscopic features confirmed the diagnosis of washboard nails or habit tic deformity. All three patients were advised tape application and peri-ungual scaling improved after 2 months of follow-up.

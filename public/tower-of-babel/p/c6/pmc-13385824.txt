Novel class of population mean estimators based on robust regression methods
Authors: Anoop Kumar, Renu Kumari, Abdullah Mohammed Alomair, Mohammed Ahmed Alomair
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn survey sampling, the accurate estimation of the population mean is often challenged by the presence of outliers in the data. Traditional estimators may become inefficient or biased under such conditions. This study proposes a novel class of estimators for the population mean under simple random sampling (SRS) by incorporating robust regression methods that are less sensitive to outliers. The proposed estimators are formulated using robust regression methods, such as Hample-M, Huber-M, Tukey-M, Huber-MM, least trimmed squares (LTS), and least median of squares (LMS) to improve resistance against atypical observations while preserving efficiency under ideal conditions. The theoretical properties such as bias and mean square error (MSE) of the proposed estimators are examined. Through extensive simulation study and empirical application to real survey data, the proposed estimators demonstrate superior performance over the existing robust regression based estimators in terms of minimum relative mean square error (RMSE) and maximum relative efficiency (RE). The findings suggest that the proposed estimators provide a viable alternative for practitioners dealing with the data contaminated with outliers in sample surveys.Keywords: Robust regression methods, Least median square, Least trimmed square, Huber-M, Huber-MM, Simple random samplingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In survey sampling, the accurate estimation of the population mean is a fundamental objective, especially when dealing with real-world data that may be contaminated with outliers. Traditional estimators, although efficient under ideal conditions, often exhibit sensitivity to such anomalies, leading to biased or inefficient results. To address these challenges, the use of robust statistical techniques has gained significant attention in recent years. In survey sampling, robust regression methods provide a powerful alternative by minimizing the influence of extreme values while preserving the essential structure of the data. These methods are designed to mitigate the effect of outliers in regression and location-scale problems, providing more reliable estimates when classical methods (like ordinary least squares) fail in the presence of outliers.

This paper introduces a novel class of population mean estimators under SRS based on different robust regression methods. These proposed estimators are designed to enhance reliability and efficiency of the mean estimation in the presence of outliers. By using the robustness properties of estimators like LMS, LTS, Huber-M, Hampel-M, Tukey-M, and Huber-MM, the new class aims to provide more stable and accurate estimates of the population mean. The theoretical properties of the proposed estimators are studied and compared with the existing estimators through both analytical derivations and simulation studies. Furthermore, the performance of these estimators is validated using real world data that demonstrate their practical utility in the presence of outliers.

Existing robust regression estimators

Robust regression methods have been well-established in the statistical domain to address the limitations of classical least square estimators, particularly in the presence of outliers and leverage points. Traditional estimators like ordinary least squares (OLS) are highly sensitive to such anomalies, often leading to biased or inefficient parameter estimates. To overcome these issues, some prominent authors have developed different robust regression methods, including Hample-M, Huber-M, Tukey-M, LMS, LTS, and Huber-MM. The review of estimators based on such robust regression methods is crucial for identifying their strengths, limitations, and suitability under outliers. It helps in understanding the development trends, comparing performance across methods, and guiding the selection or improvement of estimators for specific applications.

A key observation across Tables 3 and 4 is the inferior performance of the OLS method in comparison to robust regression methods. The estimators based on OLS consistently exhibit higher RMSE, which clearly indicates their sensitivity to outliers and contaminated observations. This behavior is expected, as OLS relies on minimization of squared error and is highly influenced by extreme values. In comparison, the estimators constructed using robust regression methods such as Huber-M, Hampel-M, Tukey-M, LTS, LMS, and Huber-MM demonstrate remarkable improvements over OLS. These methods effectively down-weight the influence of extreme observations, and result in reduced RMSE.

From Tables 3 and 4, it is clearly observed that the proposed estimators consistently yield the lowest RMSE values across OLS method and all robust methods, including Huber-M, Huber-MM, LTS, LMS, Hampel-M, and Tukey-M. Correspondingly, the RE values for the proposed estimators are uniformly equal to 1, indicating that they serve as the most efficient estimators among all competitors. In comparison, the existing estimators such as Zaman and Bulut16,17,9 exhibit comparatively higher RMSE values and lower RE, and reflecting reduced efficiency under heavy-tailed symmetric t-distribution and asymmetric gamma distribution.

Thus, the simulation results demonstrate that the proposed class of estimators achieves a significant reduction in estimation error and substantial efficiency gains compared to the existing estimators. This superior performance under both heavy-tailed and skewed distributions highlights the effectiveness of the proposed methodology in handling outliers, and making it a reliable choice for practical survey sampling applications.

Discussion of real data findings

Tables 6 and 7 present the empirical performance of various robust regression-based estimators in estimating the population mean using two real datasets. These datasets, which concern agricultural credit and apple production, and include significant outliers, serve to evaluate the estimators under real-world contamination scenarios.

Across Tables 6 and 7, the newly proposed class of estimators consistently exhibit the lowest RMSE values and the highest RE across OLS (non-robust) as well as all six robust regression methods (Hample-M, Huber-M, Tukey-M, LMS, LTS, and Huber-MM). This confirms their robustness and superior performance compared to the existing methods. Moreover, the existing and proposed estimators perform better under robust regression methods compared to the OLS method. This shows the importance of robust regression-based estimation methods in the presence of outliers.

In comparison to the proposed estimators, the estimators from Ref.[10],16,17,9 show higher RMSEs and significantly lower REs. Some estimators display extremely poor performance and indicate their vulnerability to outliers. Even the better-performing estimators such as or still lag behind the proposed estimators in both datasets.

Conclusion

This study introduced a novel and efficient class of population mean estimators under SRS by incorporating the robust regression methods to effectively address the challenges posed by outliers in survey data. Through rigorous theoretical derivation, simulation study, and application to real-world datasets, the proposed estimators demonstrated to consistently outperform existing robust regression-based estimators, namely those by Zaman and Bulut (2018),16,17,9. The new class of estimators exhibited the minimum RMSE and maximum RE under various robust regression methods such as Hample-M, Huber-M, Tukey-M, LMS, LTS, and Huber-MM. Their superiority holds across both t-distributed and gamma distributed populations, as well as in real datasets with significant contamination. These findings underscore the practical utility and theoretical robustness of the proposed estimators, making them a reliable alternative for population mean estimation in the presence of outliers.

In future, this work can be extended for the mean estimation in the presence of outliers using different sampling designs such as stratified, systematic, and two-stage sampling. The incorporation of multiple auxiliary variables29 may further improve estimation efficiency.

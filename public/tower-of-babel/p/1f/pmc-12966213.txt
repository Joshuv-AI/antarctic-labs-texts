NFE2L2 rs35652124C>T polymorphism predicts Grade 4 neutropenia in esophageal cancer patients treated with docetaxel, cisplatin, and fluorouracil chemotherapy: results from exploratory and validation cohorts
Authors: Hisanaga Nomura, Takaya Suzuki, Takashi Kojima, Kunihiko Itoh, Daiki Tsuji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeChemotherapy-induced neutropenia is a serious adverse event. Combination chemotherapy with docetaxel, cisplatin, and 5-fluorouracil (DCF) is an effective neoadjuvant therapy for esophageal cancer, but it is associated with a high incidence of Grade 4 neutropenia. In previous study, we identified associations between genetic variants and severe neutropenia. The aim of this study was to explore genetic factors related to pharmacokinetics and endogenous antioxidant defense mechanisms that may be associated with the development of Grade 4 neutropenia in esophageal cancer patients treated with DCF chemotherapy using two independent cohorts.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective pharmacogenetic analysis using DNA samples from the National Cancer Center Biobank in Japan. Two independent cohorts of esophageal cancer patients treated with the DCF chemotherapy were analyzed: an exploratory cohort (n = 157) and a validation cohort (n = 121). Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in genes related to docetaxel pharmacokinetics and the Keich-like ECH-associated protein 1-Nuclear factor erythroid2-related factor 2 (Keap1-Nrf2) antioxidant system were examined.ResultsA total 53 (33.8%) and 62 (51.2%) patients developed Grade 4 neutropenia in exploratory and validation cohort, respectively. Multivariate analysis revealed that age, Adenosine triphosphate-binding cassette (ABC) G2 rs2231137G > A and Nuclear factor, erythroid 2 like 2 (NFE2L2) rs35652124C > T were significant in exploratory cohort, while baseline absolute neutrophil count and NFE2L2 rs35652124C > T were significant in the validation cohort.ConclusionsNFE2L2 rs35652124C > T was identified as an independent predictive genetic factor for Grade 4 neutropenia in esophageal cancer patients treated with DCF chemotherapy.Keywords: Esophageal cancer, DCF chemotherapy, NFE2L2, Variants

Introduction

Esophageal cancer is a malignancy with a relatively poor prognosis, as evidenced by an annual incidence of 25,920 cases and a 5-year relative survival rate of 42%. This contrasts with the more favorable prognoses observed in other gastrointestinal cancers, such as gastric and colorectal cancers. A phase III trial demonstrated a significant improvement in overall survival with preoperative chemotherapy compared to postoperative chemotherapy in patients with clinical stage II/III esophageal cancer [1]. Furthermore, the docetaxel, cisplatin, and 5-fluorouracil (DCF) chemotherapy, a modification of the standard 5-fluorouracil and cisplatin (FP) chemotherapy, has shown superior efficacy in preoperative chemotherapy, resulting in a significant overall survival benefit [2]. While a phase II study in Japan reported a high response rate with the DCF chemotherapy, it also revealed a significant incidence of adverse events, including nausea, vomiting, renal dysfunction, alopecia, and hematologic toxicities [3]. Chemotherapy-induced neutropenia, a major dose-limiting toxicity, can progress to febrile neutropenia (FN) and potentially lead to fatal complications. Our previous research has focused on identifying genetic factors identifying genetic factors that underlie individual variability in treatment-related adverse events, with the goal of enabling personalized adverse events management. Since the early 2000s, pharmacogenomic studies have highlighted the clinical significance of variants in genes related to drug transport, metabolism, and adverse events [4–6]. Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are common genetic variations that can influence gene expression and potentially contribute to inter-individual differences in pharmacotherapy responses [7]. In a previous study, utilizing biobank samples from 158 esophageal cancer patients, we identified age, baseline absolute neutrophil count (ANC), Adenosine triphosphate-binding cassette (ABC) B1 rs1045642C > T, and ABCC2 rs12762549C > G as independent predictors for severe neutropenia following the first cycle of the DCF chemotherapy [8]. In our previous studies, we found possible associations between certain genetic factors and severe neutropenia caused by the DCF chemotherapy. Given that neutropenia is more pronounced with docetaxel than with cisplatin or 5-fluorouracil, we focused on genetic variants affecting docetaxel pharmacokinetics. Therefore, we should clearly demonstrate the robustness of this study.

Oxidative stress can lead cancer cells to apoptosis. However, some studies reported that enhanced intrinsic antioxidant capacity or somatic variants in the Keich-like ECH-associated protein 1-Nuclear factor erythroid2-related factor 2 (Keap1-Nrf2) pathway can allow cells to survive oxidative stress without undergoing cell death [9–11]. The Keap1-Nrf2 signaling pathway, which plays a pivotal role in protecting cells from deleterious environmental insults, including oxidative stress, has attracted attention. Keap1, a cytosolic protein, normally binds to Nrf2, sequestering it in the cytoplasm and inhibiting its nuclear translocation, thereby suppressing the activation of antioxidant and detoxification genes [11]. However, once it is exposed to oxidative stress or electrophilic substances, Keap1 undergoes conformational changes, and leads to the release of Nrf2. The emitted Nrf2 translocate to the nucleus, promoting the expression of antioxidant and detoxification enzymes. The translocated Nrf2 promotes the expression of cytoprotective enzymes, thereby protecting cells [12]. A wide range of anticancer drugs induce oxidative stress, which can impact therapeutic outcomes and cause systemic toxicity. The Keap1–Nrf2 pathway plays a central role in regulating cellular and oxidative stress induced by pharmacological agents. Nrf2, encoded by the Nuclear factor, erythroid 2 like 2 (NFE2L2) gene, activates the transcription of antioxidant and cytoprotective genes in response to stress [13]. Therefore, genetic variants in NFE2L2 may influence the activity of the Keap1–Nrf2 pathway and contribute to interindividual differences in cellular defense capacity during drug exposure [14]. Song et al. demonstrated in an in vitro assay comparing Nrf2 promoter activity between the rs35652124 T and C alleles that the T-allele group exhibited significantly lower transcriptional activity than the C-allele group [15]. Similarly, Hraoui et al. confirmed the induction of Nrf2 activation in response to enforced mitochondrial Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) generation [16]. Based on this rationale, we believed that analyzing NFE2L2 variants might influence the variability of adverse drug reactions. The choice of rs35652124 and rs6721961 among the candidate variants was based on two considerations. First, these SNPs show relatively little imbalance in allele frequencies in the Japanese population [17]. Second, among the many NFE2L2 SNPs that have been investigated, these two have been most frequently reported to be associated with various diseases, suggesting that they may have a relatively large impact on Nrf2 activity [18, 19]. We hypothesized that the variability in antitumor response and adverse events observed following chemotherapy may be attributable to differences in endogenous antioxidant defense mechanisms, which are influenced by genetic variants, leading to personalized responses to oxidative stress. The genetic factors affecting the overall antioxidant defense system, rather than those specific to individual components of the DCF chemotherapy, would have a greater impact on neutropenia. The aim of this study was to validate the robustness of previous findings by analyzing two independent cohorts.

Materials and methods

Study design

Using DNA samples from the National Cancer Center (NCC) Biobank Registry, we conducted a retrospective study to identify clinical and genetic predictors for Grade 4 neutropenia in patients with esophageal cancer who received DCF chemotherapy. The medical data of individual patients who were diagnosed with esophageal cancer and received neoadjuvant the DCF chemotherapy at the NCC Hospital East were collected. We established two cohorts based on the timing of DCF chemotherapy. The exploratory cohort included patients treated between July 2011 and November 2016, while the validation cohort included those treated between December 2016 and March 2021. All patients received the DCF chemotherapy according to standardized procedures, including uniform infusion volumes and administration. All patients received the DCF chemotherapy according to standardized procedures, including infusion volume and administration rates. The DCF chemotherapy consisted of a 1-h intravenous (i.v.) infusion of docetaxel at 70 mg/m2 and a 2-h infusion of cisplatin at 70 mg/m2 on day 1, followed by a continuous i.v. infusion of 5-fluorouracil at 750 mg/m2/day on days 1–5. This chemotherapy was repeated every 3 weeks. The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients who received the DCF without any dose reduction during the first cycle, and those who received prophylactic antibiotics but did not receive prophylactic granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF). Prophylactic antibiotics used were ciprofloxacin 200 mg 3 times a day or levofloxacin 500 mg once a day. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of the NCC, Japan (2015-088 and 2021-081). All patients provided their written informed consent to allow the use of their clinical information and DNA sample, available in the NCC Biobank Registry. All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were by the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki declaration and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards.

Assessment

ANC was recorded as a primary outcome measure. Hematological toxicities were assessed based on the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. We defined a treatment-related event as Grade 4 neutropenia, characterized by an ANC of less than 500 cells/mm3, observed during the three cycles of the DCF chemotherapy. Several baseline variables, including gender, age, body mass index (BMI), clinical stage, and baseline laboratory data that could potentially affect the onset of severe neutropenia were included for all patients in this study.

Pharmacogenetic analysis

The variants for the ABCB1, ABCC2, ABCG2, Glutathione S-transferases (GST), Solute carrier organic anion transporter family member (SLCO) 1B3, and Cytochrome P450 (CYP) 3A5 genes, related to docetaxel pharmacokinetics (i.e., key drug transporter and metabolism enzyme) were analyzed. We selected representative SNPs that have been suggested to be associated with docetaxel-induced neutropenia and had the criteria of minor allele frequency > 0.10 reported in the Japanese population. Furthermore, we conducted variants analysis of genes encoding antioxidant enzymes and genes encoding Keap1-Nrf2. The impact of variants in these genetic factors on their functional activity and expression levels has been extensively reported [20, 21]. Importantly, the allele frequency of these factors is known to display a low degree of bias within the Japanese population, suggesting a homogeneous distribution [17]. The following variants in five genes associated with docetaxel pharmacokinetics were analyzed: ABCB1 rs1128503C > T, rs2032582G > T/A, and rs1045642C > T; ABCC2 rs12762549C > G; ABCG2 rs2231137G > A and rs2231142C > A; GST M1/T1 null and P1 rs1695A > G; CYP3A5 rs776746G > A; and SLCO1B3 rs11045585A > G. And in three genes associated with antioxidant enzymes and Keap1-Nrf2: Superoxide Dismutase (SOD1) rs2070424A > G; SOD2 rs4880A > G; Catalase (CAT) rs79943316A > T; NFE2L2 rs35652124C > T, and rs6721961G > T were analyzed. All variants, excluding ABCG2 rs2231142C > A and GST M1/T1 null and P1 rs1695A > G, were determined by polymerase chain reaction-restriction fragment length variant (PCR–RFLP) analysis. The variants ABCG2 rs2231142C > A and SOD2 rs4880A > G was determined by allele-specific polymerase chain reaction, and genotyping of GST M1/T1 was performed using a multiplex polymerase chain reaction. In addition, for further confirmation, the representative samples were sequenced by direct sequencing using T100TM Thermal Cycler (BIO RAD).

Statistical analysis

Continuous data are summarized as median (range). Categorical data presented as frequencies (percentage) were compared using Fisher’s exact test. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare non-normally distributed continuous variables between groups. A univariate analysis was performed to identify genetic risk factors for severe neutropenia after DCF chemotherapy. A total of 15 genetic variants were genotyped, and their association of these genotypes with severe neutropenia were analyzed using both with dominant and recessive models. A multivariate logistic regression analysis was then performed to identify independent predictors of Grade 4 neutropenia. Patient background characteristics and genetic variants with a P value < 0.10 in the univariate analysis were included as independent variables, and the occurrence of Grade 4 neutropenia was used as the dependent variable. The results were reported as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and P values. Genotypic distributions of all variants were also assessed for their deviation from the Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium using the chi-squared test. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant; values equal to or above this threshold were regarded as indicating a trend. Because the univariate analyses were exploratory, P values are reported as nominal and no adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed. This statistical analysis was performed using EZR ver1.61 (Saitama Medical Center, Jichii Medical University, Saitama, Japan), a modified version of R commander designed to add statistical functions frequently used in biostatistics.

Results

Patient characteristics

Table 1 presents the patient characteristics in the exploratory and validation cohorts. The majority of patients were male in both cohorts with approximately 88% in each group. The median age (range) was 63 years (36–75) in the exploratory cohort and 66 years (47–76) in the validation cohort, indicating that patients in the validation cohort were older than those in the exploratory cohort. Stage III was the most frequently observed clinical stage in both cohorts. Table 2 shows the incidence of neutropenia and febrile neutropenia (FN). Grade 4 neutropenia occurred in 53 patients (33.8%) in the exploratory cohort and 62 patients (51.2%) in the validation cohort. Furthermore, FN was observed in 41 patients (26.1%) and 39 patients (32.2%) in the respective cohorts.

Association between Grade 4 neutropenia and genotypes by univariate analysis on exploratory cohort and validation cohort

To explore the potential of genetic factors as predictors for Grade 4 neutropenia, we analyzed genetic variants s related to docetaxel pharmacokinetics and antioxidant enzymes. The observed genotype distributions were in line with Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (P > 0.10 for all variants). The genotypes of patients were classified into two groups: (1) homozygous wild-type genotype and variant allele-carrying genotypes (dominant model) or (2) homozygous wild-type and heterozygous genotypes or homozygous variant genotype (recessive model). Tables 3 and 4 shows the results of the univariate analysis for dominant and recessive models in the exploratory and validation cohort, respectively, including the proportions of patients with Grade 4 neutropenia in each genotype group. Candidate risk factors for Grade 4 neutropenia

In the exploratory cohort, 8 factors—including 6 genetic variants—were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis: age, baseline ANC, ABCB1 rs1128503C > T, ABCG2 rs2231142G > A, GST P1 rs1695A > G, GST M1/T1 null, NFE2L2 rs35652124C > T, and CAT rs7943316A > T. In the validation cohort, two clinical and genetic factors were included: baseline ANC and ABCC2 rs12762549G > C, NFE2L2 rs35652124C > T, NFE2L2 rs6721961G > T. As a result, baseline ANC and NFE2L2 rs35652124C > T were identified as independent predictors of Grade 4 neutropenia in multivariate analysis across both cohorts (Table 5). For NFE2L2 rs35652124, which was identified as a risk factor in both cohorts, box plots of the lowest ANC were generated (Fig. 1). In the exploratory cohort, the mean neutrophil nadir was 796.9/mm3 in the non-TT group and 576.3/mm3 in the TT group (P = 0.018). In the validation cohort, the corresponding values were 599.1/mm3 and 385.5/mm3, respectively (P = 0.029). These results indicate a tendency toward lower neutrophil nadir values in the TT group in both cohorts.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the genetic variants as risk factors for Grade 4 neutropenia in esophageal cancer patients treated with preoperative the DCF chemotherapy by conducting a validation study in two cohorts. We demonstrated that genetic variants of the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway, an endogenous antioxidant defense mechanism, may serve as predictive maker for Grade 4 neutropenia. A study investigating chemotherapy-induced neutropenia and genetic variants was conducted in 2008 by Kiyotani et al., who demonstrated using Japanese biobank samples that variants in ABCC2 and SLCO1B3 are useful predictors of the risk of severe leukopenia/neutropenia [22]. Since then, many researchers have conducted similar studies on the association between docetaxel-induced neutropenia and genetic variants [23, 24]. Yan et al. conducted a meta-analysis and revealed that the ABCB1 rs1045642C > T is significantly associated with the risk of hematologic toxicity [25]. Our previous study also revealed that these genetic variants similarly influence the occurrence of neutropenia [8]. Conversely, a few other studies have suggested no association between these genetic variants and neutropenia. Sone K. et al. conducted a study in lung cancer patients and found that ABCB1 rs1045642C > T and ABCC2 rs12762549C > G were not associated with neutropenia. Similarly, Ji et al. also failed to confirm a significant association between ABCB1 rs1045642 and hematological toxicity [26]. These inconsistencies highlight the challenges in identifying reliable genetic predictors of neutropenia across different studies and cancer types. This may be due to various factors, including interethnic differences in genetic variants [27] and the influence of concomitant medications. This study was conducted using two cohorts under the same conditions to ensure more accurate results. As a result, the previous study, which used Grade ≥ 3 adverse events as the cutoff, identified ABCB1 rs1045642C > T and ABCC2 rs12762549C > G as potential associations; however, these findings were not statistically significant in the validation study. This study set Grade 4 neutropenia as the primary outcome due to its substantial clinical relevance. In the exploratory cohort, ABCG2 rs2231137G > A, which is implicated in the efflux of docetaxel, was identified. However, the association was not statistically significant in the validation cohort. In the exploratory cohort, we investigated factors influencing the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of docetaxel, cisplatin, and 5-fluorouracil. We initially hypothesized that docetaxel-related factors, due to its predominant impact on neutropenia, would be most influential. However, the involvement of the cell division-inhibiting effects of cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil may have contributed to the divergent findings between the exploratory and validation cohorts.

Therefore, in this study, we investigated the relationship between genetic variants in endogenous antioxidant defense mechanisms and Grade 4 neutropenia induced by cytotoxic anticancer agents. Iwasaki T. et al. conducted an analysis of genetic variants, and treatment outcomes, including patient prognosis, utilizing a comprehensive database comprising approximately 60,000 Japanese cancer patients (C-CAT database) [10]. As a result, they demonstrated that specific variants in the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway, a critical pathway regulating gene expression, are associated with poor patient prognosis and increased malignancy across various cancer types [10]. Fujiwara et al. reported that ototoxicity was genetically associated with the GST T1 null genotype and the G allele of NFE2L2 rs6721961, whereas the absence of ototoxicity was associated with the GST M1 null genotype [28]. To our knowledge, no clinical research has investigated the association between the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway and Grade 4 neutropenia induced by chemotherapy. It has been reported that cytotoxic anticancer agents induce DNA damage within the mitochondria, leading to disruption of complexes I and II on the mitochondrial inner membrane. This disruption causes leakage of superoxide from the electron transport chain, resulting in the generation of cell-lethal levels of reactive oxygen species [29]. A possible explanation is that excessive reactive oxygen species cause DNA damage through base oxidation, which activates cell cycle checkpoints, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, ultimately resulting in myelosuppression [30]. Furthermore, the activation of the Nrf2/Keap1 pathway in normal tissues is believed to contribute to toxicity reduction as well as drug metabolism and excretion. It has been reported that mitochondria-derived reactive oxygen species induce Nrf2 activation [31]. Additionally, there are reports suggesting effects of myelosuppression on blood cells [32]. The two cohorts in our study, conducted under identical eligibility criteria, demonstrate consistency with these previous findings, indicating a potentially strong association between the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway and Grade 4 neutropenia induced by chemotherapy.

In research on predictive factors for chemotherapy-induced adverse events, it is rare to conduct a subsequent validation in independent cohorts. Although the failure to replicate the findings of the exploratory cohort on pharmacokinetic factors in the validation cohort was contrary to expectations, the findings remain important. In combination chemotherapy, the complex interactions among different drugs influence the development of neutropenia, making it challenging to identify consistent predictive factors.

Therefore, by focusing on endogenous antioxidant defense mechanisms involved in the response to various cytotoxic anticancer drugs, the current study identified candidate predictive makers. The fact that these finding were validated in both exploratory and validation cohorts provides preliminary support for their relevance. We believe the present results as a preliminary step that may serve as a basis for future reverse translational research on Nrf2, rs35652124, and their potential roles in myelosuppression. The neutropenia associated with DCF chemotherapy for esophageal cancer represents a dose-limiting toxicity. In the neoadjuvant setting, careful management is crucial to facilitate completion of the planned treatment course. Universal administration of G-CSF preparations is not considered justified in terms of pharmaco-economics or in clinical practice. Accordingly, reliable prediction of neutropenia would be beneficial for optimizing clinical management. These results indicate the need for prospective studies in patients at increased risk of neutropenia to assess approaches such as prophylactic G-CSF administration while maintaining full-dose therapy.

Systemic oxidative stress at lethal levels can be induced not only in patients with esophageal cancer treated with DCF chemotherapy, but also by other anticancer agents.

Accordingly, this study may offer a basis for future investigations into the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway and its possible contribution to chemotherapy- related hematologic toxicity across different anticancer drugs. The findings presented here could help guide additional research exploring the involvement of the Keap1–Nrf2 pathway in hematological toxicities associated with various chemotherapy regimens.

Limitation

There are at least two limitations in this study. First, this study is a retrospective analysis using biobank samples from a single institution. Although this was a single-institution study, this can be seen as a strength due to the consistent institutional scale and standardized clinical practice. Second, survival outcomes such as overall survival (OS) or progression-free survival (PFS) were not included as endpoints, which represents another limitation of this study. In this study, we investigated interindividual variability in responses to chemotherapy-induced adverse events. As treatment outcomes such as OS and PFS were not targeted as endpoints at the start of the research, data on therapeutic efficacy were not collected [8, 33]. Subsequently, prior studies were published demonstrating that specific variants in the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway affect patient prognosis [10]. If treatment efficacy data had been collected, in this study, it might have enabled the identification of a potential link among the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway, treatment outcomes, and Grade 4 neutropenia.

Conclusion

To our knowledge, this study provides preliminary evidence that the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway may be involved in neutropenia induced by combination chemotherapy. Further investigations will be needed to clarify additional factors and to achieve a more comprehensive understanding of the association between the Keap1-Nrf2 pathway and chemotherapy-induced neutropenia.

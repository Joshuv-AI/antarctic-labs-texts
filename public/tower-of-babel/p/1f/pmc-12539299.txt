Comprehensive examination of demographic, psychological, cognitive, biochemical, and genetic profiles of methamphetamine addicts
Authors: Haider K. Hussain, Yolanda Loarce Tejada, Anna Barbaro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Methamphetamine (MA) addiction is a serious public health concern with wide-ranging neurobiological and behavioral effects. This study aimed to assess the demographic, psychological, cognitive, biochemical, and genetic profiles of individuals with methamphetamine dependence, focusing on neurotransmitter levels and the expression of addiction- and aggression-related genes. Sixty male methamphetamine users and thirty age-matched healthy controls were recruited. Participants underwent psychological assessments, cognitive testing, and biochemical evaluation of serotonin and dopamine levels using ELISA. Gene expression of SLC6A4 and COMT was quantified via real-time PCR. Significant alterations were observed in the methamphetamine group compared to controls, including reduced serotonin (17.1 ± 3.1 vs. 20.5 ± 3.2 ng/mL; p = 0.002) and dopamine levels (46.3 ± 7.2 vs. 52.4 ± 6.5 ng/mL; p = 0.015), as well as down-regulation of SLC6A4 (0.64-fold vs. 1.00; p = 0.001) and up-regulation of COMT (1.47-fold vs. 1.00; p = 0.028). These biochemical and genetic changes were correlated with increased aggression and cognitive impairments. The findings underscore the impact of prolonged MA use on neurochemical balance and gene expression, contributing to the development of aggressive behaviors and addictive patterns. Tailored treatment strategies that integrate genetic and psychological profiling, along with longitudinal monitoring, are essential to address the multifactorial nature of methamphetamine addiction and improve clinical outcomes.

Introduction

Drug addiction is a chronic condition characterized by compulsive drug-seeking and brain circuit changes related to reward and self-control control.19 Psychological profiles capture mental traits,8 while cognitive profiles involve processes like memory.22 Substance abuse impacts health, families, and economies globally.13 WHO and UNODC highlight the need for comprehensive solutions.25 Drug patterns vary: opioids, cocaine in Europe/America; methamphetamines in Asia; prescription drugs in the Middle East.10

Drug addiction has complex health impacts, including cardiovascular diseases, infectious diseases, anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairments.15 It also leads to social issues like stigmatization, unemployment, and economic burdens.16, 2 Diverse demographic and psychological profiles show high mental health issues, including post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and cognitive impairments.4, 6, 9 Neurotransmitter imbalances in serotonin and dopamine play a crucial role in addiction's biochemical mechanisms.5, 24

Methamphetamine is a potent stimulant affecting the central nervous system, causing increased energy and euphoria.26 Addiction involves compulsive use despite adverse effects.17 Genetic predisposition plays a significant role in substance use disorders.11 Methamphetamine causes cognitive impairments, mood disturbances, anxiety, and aggression.12, 18 High depression rates in addicts highlight the need for targeted treatments3 strategies.20

The solute carrier family (SLC) genes, particularly SLC6A4, and the catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) gene regulate neurotransmitter transport, influencing addiction risk7; (M. Tunbridge et al., 2012). Altered expression disrupts neurotransmitter balance, increasing vulnerability to addiction and psychological dysfunctions. Polymorphisms in the nucleotide sequence of SLC6A4, which affects serotonin reuptake, have been linked to neuropsychiatric disorders.27 COMT impacts dopamine metabolism, influencing reward and cognition.23. Targeted treatments are needed for drug addicts with aberrant SLC and COMT expression.21

Abdulaal et al.,1 found that adolescents with substance use disorders suffer notable cognitive dysfunctions. Cannabis affects problem-solving, inhalants impair attention, and alcohol impacts cognitive flexibility, with polysubstance use causing more harm than single-substance use.

The rationale of this study is to provide a comprehensive understanding of drug addiction by examining the complex interplay between demographic, psychological, and cognitive factors. It focuses on how socio-economic and environmental influences shape addiction profiles, and assesses the impact of methamphetamine on neurotransmitter levels and gene expression. By integrating these aspects, the study aims to offer insights into addiction’s biochemical and behavioral dimensions, facilitating the development of targeted interventions and improving treatment strategies.

The study aims to explore distinct demographic, psychological, and cognitive profiles among drug addicts, influenced by socio-economic and environmental factors. It details demographic aspects such as age, gender, socio-economic status, and family background, while assessing psychological conditions, emotional well-being, and behavioral patterns. The study also examines cognitive aspects like memory, attention, and executive functions. By analyzing the impact of methamphetamine on neurotransmitter levels (serotonin and dopamine) and gene expression (SLC and COMT), it provides biochemical and genetic insights into addiction's effects. This comprehensive approach seeks to develop targeted interventions and enhance addiction treatment strategies by addressing both behavioral and biochemical aspects of addiction.

This study provides critical insights into the biochemical and genetic alterations associated with methamphetamine addiction, particularly in relation to neurotransmitter levels and gene expression linked to aggression. By examining these changes, the research highlights the profound impact of methamphetamine on both brain function and behavior. These findings underscore the importance of developing targeted treatment strategies that address the genetic and psychological profiles of addicts, offering new avenues for improving addiction therapies and mitigating the severe health consequences associated with long-term methamphetamine use.

Materials and methods

This study included 60 newly diagnosed methamphetamine-addicted males, aged 17 to 48 (mean age 27.77 ± 8.03 years), from the Ibn Rushd Psychiatric Teaching Hospital in Baghdad (Dec 2022 − June 2023). A control group of 30 healthy adults, matched by age and gender, was included for genetic analysis. Ethical standards from the University of Alcalá, Iraq’s Ministry of Health, and Ibn Rushd Hospital were followed, ensuring consent and confidentiality. Participants with unrelated psychiatric disorders or other substance abuse were excluded.

Collection of data

Data were collected using a structured questionnaire of validated psychometric tools administered in Arabic. Psychological symptoms—including anxiety, depression, hallucinations, and psychotic features—were assessed using the DSM-5 Self-Rated Level 1 Cross-Cutting Symptom Measure—Adult Version, a tool designed to screen for multiple psychiatric domains and translated into Arabic with demonstrated reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.89 in our sample). Aggressive behaviour was evaluated using the Buss–Perry Aggression Questionnaire (BPAQ), a standardized instrument assessing physical aggression, verbal aggression, anger, and hostility (α = 0.88). Sleep-related disturbances were assessed using the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), a validated Arabic tool for evaluating sleep difficulties (α = 0.90). The demographic section included age, family size, education, occupation, marital status, and duration/frequency of methamphetamine use. All instruments had validated Arabic versions previously used in regional mental health studies and demonstrated high internal consistency in this population (Cronbach’s α range: 0.88–0.93). Trained clinical researchers conducted face-to-face interviews to ensure uniform data collection.

Sample collection

Blood samples were collected from 60 male patients and 30 male controls with informed consent. Approximately 5–10 mL of blood was drawn from each participant. The samples were centrifuged to separate the serum, which was then carefully preserved at −80 °C for ELISA tests. The remaining blood was processed for RNA and DNA extraction to analyze genetic and biochemical markers.

Elisa-based measurement techniques

Serotonin levels were measured using a Human Serotonin ELISA Kit from MyBioSource, USA, with a Sandwich-ELISA technique, involving antibody-coated microplates, HRP-conjugated antibodies, and colorimetric detection at 450 nm. Dopamine and methamphetamine levels were similarly measured using respective ELISA kits from MyBioSource, USA, applying Sandwich-ELISA methods for dopamine and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays for methamphetamine. Concentrations were determined by comparing sample absorbance values to standard curves, ensuring accuracy through strict protocol adherence. These methods allowed for precise quantification of neurotransmitter and drug levels in patients and controls.

RNA and DNA extraction

Molecular studies were conducted at AL-Nahrain University and AL-Warathyoon Laboratory. RNA was extracted using TRIzol® reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol and stored at −80 °C. RNA purity was confirmed using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., USA). cDNA synthesis followed using the WizScript RT FD mix kit (Wizbiosolutions Inc. Korea), with samples stored at −18 °C for further analysis.

Quantification of gene expression using real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Gene expression of SLC6A4 and COMT was quantified using Top Green qPCR SuperMix kit and qRT-PCR (Bio-Rad, USA), which measures amplification via the cycle threshold (Ct) value. Primers for SLC6A4, COMT, and GAPDH were stored at −18 °C and prepared for use. GAPDH served as the internal control to ensure accurate ΔCt value calculations. Statistical analysis

SPSS Statistics 27.0 was used for the study's statistical analysis. The Chi-square test evaluated age group distributions and associations. Descriptive statistics, t-tests, and ANOVA analyzed psychological effects, behavioral impacts, and neurotransmitter levels. Mann-Whitney U tests compared gene expression levels, while ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests assessed neurotransmitter levels by usage frequency. Regression and correlation analyses explored relationships between variables like starting age and neurotransmitter levels. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study provides a comprehensive examination of the demographic, psychological, cognitive, biochemical, and genetic profiles of methamphetamine addicts. Significant findings include variations in gene expression and neurotransmitter levels correlating with aggressive behavior and addiction severity. Specifically, the return to baseline SLC gene expression and sustained elevated COMT levels in long-term methamphetamine users indicate complex genetic adaptations. Our findings align with previous research on the epigenetic effects of chronic substance abuse, emphasizing the need for targeted interventions.

From the authors' perspective, this study highlights the urgent need for interventions tailored to the genetic and biochemical profiles of methamphetamine users, particularly in addressing aggression and cognitive decline. We believe that personalized treatment approaches, informed by an individual’s genetic and epigenetic predispositions, could significantly improve recovery outcomes. Our findings also suggest that genetic markers like COMT expression could serve as indicators of addiction severity and long-term behavioral risks, providing potential targets for pharmacological and behavioral interventions.

The study recommends developing targeted treatment programs that cater to the specific genetic and psychological profiles of methamphetamine users is recommended. Future research should further explore the role of epigenetic mechanisms, including DNA methylation and histone acetylation, in mediating methamphetamine-induced changes in gene expression and behavior. Longitudinal studies are needed to track these genetic changes over time and to understand their implications for addiction treatment and relapse prevention. Additionally, more research is required to investigate how environmental factors, such as stress and early life trauma, interact with genetic predispositions to influence addiction susceptibility and progression. By integrating genetic, epigenetic, and environmental factors, future research can pave the way for more effective and individualized treatment strategies for methamphetamine addiction. Comprehensive mental health services should be integrated into addiction treatment programs to address co-occurring psychological disorders. Additionally, public health policies should be implemented to tackle the socio-economic factors contributing to drug addiction, encompassing both prevention and education initiatives.

Evaluation of Salivary Biomarkers, Cortisol and Total Antioxidant Capacity, and their Possible Role in Treatment Outcome in Patients with Symptomatic Oral Lichen Planus: A Pilot Study
Authors: Admaja K. Nair, Leela S. Sreela, Thara Cherngod
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Oral lichen planus (OLP) is a chronic immune-mediated mucocutaneous disorder characterized by phases of remission and exacerbation, often requiring long-term clinical monitoring. Saliva, a biochemically stable fluid, is increasingly being utilized for the diagnosis and prognosis of oral diseases, including OLP. Moreover, collecting saliva is non-invasive. Among salivary biomarkers, cortisol and total antioxidant capacity (TAC) have emerged as potential indicators of psychoneuroimmunological and oxidative mechanisms, respectively. OLP is usually associated with elevated cortisol due to stress and reduced TAC due to impaired antioxidant defense.[12] The predictive value of salivary biomarkers in assessing treatment response is still debated. Also, antioxidant supplementation appears to improve the clinical outcomes in OLP,[3] but its direct impact on salivary TAC remains unclear.

We conducted a prospective pilot study at Government Dental College, Kottayam, Kerala (Ethical approval no. M26/2023/R500/ DCK, dated 21/09/2023), to evaluate whether salivary cortisol and TAC could serve as short-term prognostic markers in symptomatic OLP patients undergoing standard treatment. Twenty-one patients with symptomatic OLP were recruited after satisfying strict inclusion and exclusion criteria. Patients diagnosed with OLP based on the modified WHO 2003 criteria—confirmed clinically or histopathologically where required (with biopsy performed only in clinically inconclusive cases)—who were symptomatic at presentation (VAS > 0) were included, while patients receiving corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy for OLP or other systemic diseases, those with a history or presence of psychiatric illness, cases showing epithelial dysplasia on histopathology, and pregnant or breastfeeding women were excluded. Of the included patients, 16 had complete biomarker data at baseline and at 2 months. Clinical severity was assessed using the reticulation/keratosis, erythema, and ulceration (REU) scoring system,[4] while symptom intensity was measured using a visual analogue scale (VAS). Psychological status was recorded at baseline using validated scales for stress, anxiety, and depression. Patients received standard therapy,[5] including topical betamethasone 0.5 mg (dissolved in water, applied thrice daily) for erosive and atrophic OLP, topical vitamin A 25,000 IU for reticular OLP, systemic lycopene capsules (10,000 IU once daily), and when indicated, clotrimazole 10 mg troche used as a swish-and-spit topical antifungal. This combination aimed to reduce inflammation, improve mucosal healing, and counteract oxidative stress, with individualized modifications based on lesion severity and response.

Saliva samples were collected using the passive drool method, were processed, and stored at –80°C. Cortisol levels were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (Calbiotech, Inc. (CBI) Cortisol ELISA Kit), and TAC was assessed using the ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assay.[12]

The study population consisted predominantly of females over the age of 50 years, with varied clinical characteristics [Table 1]. Most participants presented with moderate pain and lesion severity, alongside elevated salivary cortisol and reduced TAC levels. Psychological assessments revealed generally mild-to-moderate stress, with minimal anxiety or depression. After 2 months of standard therapy, clinical parameters showed statistically significant improvement [Table 2]. However, changes in salivary cortisol and TAC were minimal and did not reach statistical significance. Only about one-third of participants achieved marked or complete clinical recovery, and no significant associations were observed between pre- and post-treatment biomarker levels and clinical outcomes [Table 3].

These findings demonstrate the clinical effectiveness of standard OLP therapy in reducing pain and lesion severity, but the prognostic utility of salivary cortisol and TAC in the short term is doubtful. The lack of significant changes in salivary levels may be attributed to the inherent variability in cortisol secretion, which is influenced by circadian rhythms and transient stressors, making single-point measurements less reliable. Likewise, TAC levels are subject to fluctuation based on individual dietary habits and systemic health conditions, potentially requiring longer follow-up duration to capture meaningful therapeutic responses.

The limitations of the study include a small sample size, short follow-up, lack of a healthy control group, and potential variability in treatment adherence. Subgroup analyses by clinical subtype of OLP (atrophic, erosive, reticular) were not attempted, although this would be highly relevant in future studies with increased sample size and long-term follow-up.

In conclusion, while standard treatment effectively improves clinical symptoms in OLP, this pilot study suggests that salivary cortisol and TAC are not reliable indicators of short-term treatment response. Larger, controlled longitudinal studies with repeated biomarker assessments are recommended to validate the role of these non-invasive markers in OLP management.

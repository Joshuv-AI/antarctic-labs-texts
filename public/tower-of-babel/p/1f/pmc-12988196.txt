A hybrid optimization and graph network for sustainable electric vehicle charging using a dual active bridge converter and renewable energy
Authors: Priya Narayanan, Prabaakaran Kandasamy, Nehru Kandasamy, Jaisiva Selvaraj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Hybrid Renewable Energy (HRE) in Electric Vehicle (EV) charging involves combining power from Photovoltaic (PV), Fuel Cell (FC), and batteries, as well as charging batteries for upcoming charging cycles. However, power conversion efficiency and grid stability are required for reliable, smooth charging of EVs. The problems at hand necessitate a hybrid approach to the economic assessment of the EV Charging System (EVCS) implemented with a Dual Active Bridge (DAB) converter and Hybrid Renewable Energy Sources (HRES). The proposed method integrates the Pelican Optimization Algorithm (POA) and Attributed Multi-order Graph Convolutional Network (AMOGCN); therefore, it is called the POA-AMOGCN technique. The objective is to reduce the Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) in order to enhance the economic performance of the EVCS combined with HRES. The POA optimizes power management by balancing power generation from RES and battery storage with the demand of EVCS while generating control signals for the DAB converter. The AMOGCN predicts the optimal control signal for the DAB converter to enhance resource distribution, facilitating the integration of EVs with RES. Accordingly, the proposed method is implemented in the MATLAB environment and its performance is assessed against several existing approaches, including the Gorilla Troop Optimizer (GTO), Hybrid Crow Search Algorithm-Particle Swarm Optimization (HCSA-PSO), Neural Network with Improved PSO (NN-IPSO), Forensic Investigation Algorithm-Archimedean Optimization Algorithm (FBIAOA), and conventional PSO. The proposed POA-AMOGCN approach achieves the lowest LCOE of $0.0549/kWh, demonstrating a substantial improvement in cost-effectiveness compared to other optimization methods.

Introduction

Background

The charging infrastructure for EVs is being rapidly transformed with integration of HRE sources1,2. This helps to reduce conventional power grids, lowers carbon emissions, and enhances energy sustainability3,4. The main problem arises due to fluctuating Renewable Energy (RE) production; thus, Energy Management (EM) frameworks are necessary for providing stable and continuous power supply to EV charging stations5,6. The DC converter plays a significant role in voltage regulation, increased power converging efficiency, and efficient energy transfer between RES, Energy Storage Systems (ESS), and EVs7,8. In addition, combining Battery Energy Storage (BSS) with pieces of training from RESs will make the safety of the system by lowering fluctuations in availability of energy, thus ensuring smooth delivery of energy supply. However, many challenges still remain in optimizing energy flow, power conversion, and overall system stability in EV charging networks integrated with HRE sources9,10. The rapid and intermittent EV charging demand overlaps with fluctuations generated by RE. This necessitates advanced EM strategies11,12. Price-Based Demand Response can be employed to balance energy supplies reducing operational costs and alleviating the peak load impact on the grid13. This is coupled with choosing the appropriate converter topologies to enable efficient transfer of energy while minimizing losses and enhancing overall system efficiency14. The addition of the multiple RES with EV charging infrastructure has added to the idea of system scalability, interoperability, and reliability while opening up further avenues for innovative research in adaptive and efficient EM frameworks15.

Challenges

Certain challenges emerge on account of EM, power conversion, and reliability of the system when combining HRE sources with EV charging infrastructure. Buildings intermittent RE systems like utilities from solar and wind imply variability in available power. Such a supply of stable and continuous energy for EV charging requires efficient ESS and controls that balance the supply with the demand. The variability of demand in EV charging also adds to the complexity in that the demand patterns are often unpredictable and might invoke peak loads that influence the energy distribution system. Topologies of DC converters must also be selected and optimized effectively so as to meet conversion efficiency, voltage regulation, and system stability. Following that, high-efficiency DC converters facilitate bi-directional energy flow between the renewable sources, ESS, and EVs with minimum loss and other compatible charging standards. Additionally, the scalability and interoperability of the EV charging stations integrated with HRE sources pose another major challenge. With the increasing adoption of EVs, charging infrastructure needs to be designed such that it also meets the increase of energy demand without compromising performance. The design should incorporate grid integration strategies with energy distribution criteria that must address issues concerning power quality, harmonics, and voltage stability to allow for seamless operation. Moreover, such stations require advanced monitoring and control systems for optimized energy flow, avoiding instances of overload or under-utilization of resources. Issues related to cyber security and data privacy can also not be overemphasized; for instance, unauthorized access to EM by entities that predispose cyber-attacks could paralyze the process of charging. This has brought about the necessity for adaptive EM frameworks and reliable power conversion techniques and communication protocols to make the charging infrastructure not only reliable but also sustainable.

Literature survey

Some research studies examine quite a number of other aspects and techniques for EV Charging via HRE Integration. These are discussed in the following review.

GTO algorithm has been introduced by A. Eid et al.16. The chosen fitness functions reduce overall voltage variation and power loss. By reducing the suggested fitness functions, BES units are designed to improve performance of distribution system and regulate power flow within them. The power generated by any BES was mostly determined by State of Charge (SoC) and system limitations. P. Ray et al.17 have recommended a HCSA-PSO was used to maximize the installation and running costs of the charging station. The efficacy of the proposed strategy was compared to the PSO, CSA and hybrid genetic pattern search algorithm. G. Anjinappa et al.18 have presented a NN-IPSO control algorithm was developed. It establishes an isolated converter topology for PHEVs to preserve battery charging functions through a lesser number of power electronic devices over the existing topology. For the PHEV, this isolated converter architecture was controlled by NN-IPSO, which interfaces with the battery. R. Ilango et al.19 have presented the combination of FBI and AOA; thereafter referred to as the FBIAOA technique. By strategically allocating parking lot for EVs, the suggested technique aims to reduce both real and reactive power loss while simultaneously lowering costs. The recommended method was applied to evaluate the reliability of the smart grid (SG), when the random parameters of the EV are added to the state matrix. K. El. Mezdi et al.20 have presented a new algorithm for EM. The algorithm considers many operational modes to achieve network stability, good energy quality, and optimal power supply among different sources and EV. To do so, the recommended fuzzy logical controllers, which are known for flexibility, easy to implement, and resistant to uncertainties, are used. The fuzzy logic control parameters are tuned using PSO to ensure maximum performance.

H. U. R. Habib et al.21 have presented a JO for the best design and operation of grid-connected residential, rural microgrid (MG) that was integrated with EVs and RE in light of DRPs. This study combines aspects of residential household energy efficiency under various DRPs and RER integration. Innovative hybrid control system strategies that improve the energy efficiency of electronic converters in power electronics have been introduced by S. Sodagudi et al.22. To enhance the energy efficiency of the control system, a hybrid energy storage electric converter has been employed to provide a synergetic battery reference adaptive controller set to supplement the control system. A plug-in hybrid EV with PV module was used in a performance assessment of the control approach that was represented that allows on-grid control of the electric power by discharging batteries during peak times and charging during off-peak times to reduce load at converter and enable EV charging faster. A hybrid approach to optimizing the size of HRE systems in the presence of EVs has been presented by T. Ramesh and K. Balachander23. The hybrid approach was developed by combining the deep attention dilated residual convolutional neural network (DADRCNN) with the light spectrum optimizer (LSO). It was often called the LSO-DADRCNN approach. Maximizing system dependability, reducing system costs, and guaranteeing an adequate power supply for EVs and other electrical loads were the main objectives of the suggested technology. The LSO approach was used to optimize the size of the RE system and it put into consideration factors like load demand, solar power, wind turbine power, and battery capacity. The DADRCNN approach was used to predict the future energy consumption of EVs. A methodology of the integration of RE sources (RES) and EV charging stations into radial distribution networks has been demonstrated by V. K. Paravasthu et al.24 using Hybrid Whale Optimization Algorithm (HWOA). The approach synergistically combines Genetic WOA (GWOA) and Thermal Exchange Optimization (TEO) to optimize the placement and sizing of RESs and EVCSs. The usage of the method was adapted to address the intricate optimization issues in a distribution network, such as the allocation of resources efficiently, the improvement of stability of the network, and the strategic coverage of EVCSs and was presented on IEEE 33- and 69-bus networks. R. T. Kumar and C. C. A. Rajan25 proposed a Hybrid Energy System (HES) integrating PV and wind sources with the grid for reliable EV charging. The key contribution is a high-gain bidirectional modified Zeta converter that enhances PV efficiency through voltage boosting. A Social Spider Optimization Algorithm (SSOA)-based fuzzy Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) controller optimizes energy extraction, while a Proportional Integral (PI)-controlled Doubly Fed Induction Generator (DFIG) ensures stable wind power operation and voltage regulation.

Research gap and motivation

Even though the EV charging, EM, and optimization have been improved, there are still gaps in research. Present researches mainly concentrate on cost optimization, power loss minimization, and energy efficiency, with algorithms such as GTO, HCSA-PSO, NN-IPSO, FBIAOA, and PSO, but they do not usually address the problem with a comprehensive method of integrating PBDR, EV charging, EM, and HRE systems. Other issues, including flexibility to changing RE generation, energy uncertainty, and validation in various circumstances, are yet to be resolved. Other problems such as computational complexity, slow convergence, vulnerability to local optima, and lack of scalability also impede the use of optimization techniques in large-scale EVCS. There is a lack of research on the integration of holistic RES to ensure the best EM, particularly in market-based pricing schemes. Also, the adaptive approaches to dealing with RE volatility, scalability, interoperability, and cyber security risks remain underreported. Absence of sophisticated fault management solutions, including multi-level fault management and predictive maintenance, also constrain resilience of EVCS operations with the help of hybrid RESs. These shortcomings underline the necessity of this study.

Novelty

This study’s novel hybrid technique, which combines the POA and the AMOGCN for the economic evaluation of an EVCS integrated with a DAB converter and HRES. The limitations to grid stability, power conversion efficiency and cost optimization are addressed by this unique method as a way to balance the generation, storage, and optimal control signal prediction to make resource allocation. The proposed POA-AMOGCN technique increases the economic feasibility and reliability of RE-powered EVCS; it shows the potential for a remarkable development in sustainable EM and optimization techniques.

Configuration of EVCS using DAB converter and HRE

Figure 1 shows the layout of EVCS with HRES based on a DAB converter. PV, FC and BES system are connected together with a shared DC bus, which acts as the central power coupling point where the energy enters and exits the system. The PV system is the main source of RE, which delivers power based on the sun availability and the FC includes a backup and dispatchable generation to supplement renewable intermittency. The battery acts as an energy buffer, which stores the extra RE when demand is low and provides the power when there are the highest EV charging requirements. The DAB converter plays a central role in the system by enabling bidirectional and galvanically isolated power transfer between the DC bus and the EV load. It consists of active H-bridge converters on the primary (P1-P4) side tied to DC bus and secondary (P5-P8) side tied to the EV battery and to the EV load via DC/DC interface and AC/DC interface. High-frequency transformer, series inductor makes it easy to match voltages, provide electrical isolation and controlled power transfer, as well as provides safe voltage control and smooth current profiles. The phase-shift control of the DAB converter is a method of power flow regulation, in which the amplitude and direction of transmitted power depend on the phase difference between primary and secondary bridge switching signals. The inductor smooths current variations and minimizes ripple, enabling stable and efficient power conversion. Control signals generated by the proposed framework dynamically adjust the DAB operating conditions based on EV charging demand and the availability of RES, thereby maintaining DC bus voltage stability and reliable energy delivery. The coordinated use of the PV, FC, and battery resources leads to the economic optimization, and the focus on the low-cost RE should be considered first. The battery charging and discharging time is defined to minimize the use of more costly generation and reduce energy losses and DAB converter working is controlled to improve the conversion efficiency. This combined power management approach has a direct effect of reducing the LCOE and guarantees efficient, uninterrupted, and cost-efficient EV charging operation.Fig. 1Structure of EVC using DAB converter and HRE.

Modelling of FC

Proposed POA-AMOGCN approach for EVCS

The proposed POA-AMOGCN technique for reducing the LCOE combines the POA and the AMOGCN to enhance the economic performance of an EVCS integrated with a DAB converter and HRES. The POA is able to optimize power management by adjusting the power that is generated by the RES and battery storage to meet the demand of EVCS and produce control signals to the DAB converter. At the same time, the AMOGCN effectively estimates optimal control variables of the DAB converter, which guarantees the optimal allocation of resources and an uninterrupted integration of EVs and RES. This mixed approach is aimed at increasing the cost-efficiency and dependability of EVCS.

Interaction of renewable variability, storage, and load demand in EVCS

In the EVCS with hybrid sources of renewable power, power demand and power availability is extremely time-varying. The PV source is inherently intermittent due to changing solar irradiance, while the FC provides controllable but cost-sensitive supplementary generation. At the same time, the requirement of EV charging depends on the battery SoC and user charging requirements, creating dynamic load behaviour at the DC bus. The ESS of battery-based energy storage has a vital compensatory role that absorbs the surplus of the RE during the low demand periods and provides the power during peak charging periods or during the lack of RE. Nevertheless, unsynchronized battery functioning can be more harmful to reduce losses and speed up degradation and decrease economic functioning. Thus, to ensure the stability of the system and cost-efficiency, coherent scheduling charging and discharging is necessary. A hybrid optimization-prediction framework is used to ensure that these coupled dynamics can be properly managed. The POA is an algorithm that is used to make optimal power management decisions by reducing the LCOE, taking system constraints into consideration based on variable renewable and load conditions. In parallel, the AMOGCN predicts optimal control signals for the DAB converter by capturing higher-order dependencies among renewable sources, storage behavior, and EV load demand. This integrated way of operation guarantees the consistent interaction between generation, storage, and consumption, the stable operation and the economically optimized EV charging.

Pelican optimization algorithm

POA is a nature-inspired optimization method that models hunting and cooperative foraging of pelicans31. Pelicans catch prey efficiently by utilizing dynamic soaring, coordinated diving, and adaptive movement, demonstrating a near-optimal balance between exploration and exploitation in their search strategies. Inspired by these behaviours, POA employs adaptive position updates and intelligent movement patterns to navigate complex optimization landscapes, ensuring efficient convergence toward optimal solutions. When charging EVs sustainably with the help of a DAB converter and HRES, POA is utilized to optimize power management through balancing between RE generation, battery storage, and EV charging needs and delivering the required control signals to the DAB converter. Using its cooperative search mechanism makes POA more stable in a grid, conversion of power more efficient, energy cost decreased, and economic performance of EVCS better. Attributed multi-order graph convolutional network

The AMOGCN is a superior deep learning architecture that optimizes the processing and analysis of graph-structured data through the use of multi-order feature propagation and feature representation as attributes on nodes32. Unlike traditional Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), AMOGCN captures higher-order dependencies within the network, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of complex relationships in dynamic systems. AMOGCN employs the benefits of multi-hop connection and attribute-enhanced learning to guarantee higher feature extraction and effective information propagation over the graph. AMOGCN is applied in the scenario of sustainable EV charging with a DAB converter and HRES to predict optimal control signals to be applied to the DAB converter to make the energy distribution effective and manage the loads. Its ability to analyse multi-source energy interactions enhances the stability of power flow, ensuring seamless integration of RESs with EVCS. The multi-order AMOGCN capability allows it to capture nonlinearities in the energy demand patterns so that they can increase the adaptability of the system and the accuracy of the decision-making process. In this form of the architecture, power forecasting can be done efficiently together with real-time control signal generation and classically optimized resource allocation that can significantly increase optimization capabilities in SG EV charging infrastructure.

Converter efficiency and loss characteristics

The efficiency profile of the DAB converter depends on operating conditions such as transferred power level, phase-shift angle, and switching frequency. The leakage inductance and the current flowing through the power semiconductor devices during high-power transfer raise the conduction losses. However, the DAB topology enables effective current shaping through phase-shift modulation, which limits excessive current stress and reduces conduction-related losses compared to hard-switched converter structures. The soft-switching nature of the DAB converter is crucial in reducing switching losses which appear when turning the power devices on or off. With suitable phase-shift conditions zero-voltage switching can occur with both the primary and secondary bridge switches which leads to minimized switching energy losses and minimized electromagnetic stress during high-frequency operation. This is particularly advantageous during medium-to-high power transfer, where switching losses typically dominate in conventional converters. In the proposed framework, the POA-AMOGCN strategy dynamically regulates the DAB phase-shift angle according to RE availability and EV charging demand. This coordinated control avoids unnecessary power circulation and extreme operating points, maintaining the converter within an efficient operating region. Thus, decreased switching transitions, minimal current stress, and loss components are all aspects that lead to increased power conversion efficiency, allowing the efficient and economical functioning of the EVCS.

Discussion

This performance analysis of DAB converter in HRES shows that it is capable of stabilizing EV charging processes. The dynamic nature of the power transfer processes is confirmed through the output current and voltage waveforms, which balance energy through the PV, FC, and Battery. The POA-AMOGCN method gives an optimal result in maintaining a stable DC Bus voltage of 410 V and a DC load power of 4100 W, leading to an uninterrupted energy supply. The EV battery charging profile demonstrates a very rapid voltage increase to 230 V within a second, it thereafter remains stable, whilst current transitions from − 30 to − 20 A, and suggesting controlled charging effort. The proposed POA-AMOGCN method achieves the minimum LCOE of $0.0549/kWh, indicating a very high degree of improvement in cost-effectiveness and energy efficiency. These results validate robustness and reliability of methodology towards the optimization of RE integration and the power conversion efficiency of sustainable EV charging.

Conclusion

The paper introduces a hybrid POA-AMOGCN model to ensure the economic feasibility of an EVCS together with a DAB converter and a HRE supply. The suggested strategy yields a lower levelized energy cost of 0.0549 $/kWh and overcomes the problems of power conversion efficiency, system stability, and the cost optimization. The smooth power management and energy delivery plan increases the reliability and sustainability of renewable-powered EV charging infrastructure. The comparative analysis with the traditional optimization strategies shows that the proposed framework can be effective to enhance cost efficiency, which proves its possible relevance to the practical EVCS. Future developments will be aimed at improving predicting power in conditions of highly variable renewables and supporting the suggested framework with real-life case studies.

Limitations of the proposed POA-AMOGCN framework

Despite the fact that the suggested POA-AMOGCN framework shows better economic and operational performance, some limitations can be acknowledged. The assessment is based on a fixed EVCS configuration, and component ratings, and system performance can be different when it comes to various charging capacities or renewable penetration rates. In addition, the prediction module may require retraining when applied to operating environments with significantly different renewable availability or EV charging demand patterns. The combined optimization and prediction structure also introduces higher computational complexity compared to conventional approaches, which may affect scalability when applied to large-scale EV charging networks.

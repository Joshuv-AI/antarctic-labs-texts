Reframing Mechanistic and Clinical Paradigms of Osimertinib-Associated Cardiotoxicity
Authors: Defeng Zhao, Danni Li
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the study by Tatebe et al,1 which reports a significant association between osimertinib therapy and hospitalization for heart failure. Although the investigators present robust epidemiologic findings, we believe that 3 considerations warrant attention.

First, the proposed mechanistic explanation for this cardiotoxicity requires further refinement. Although the investigators suggest HER2 inhibition as a potential contributor, emerging evidence indicates a distinct mitochondrial pathogenesis. Recent studies have demonstrated that osimertinib directly impairs cardiac bioenergetics by inhibiting adenosine triphosphate synthase and up-regulating PDK4, leading to mitochondrial calcium overload and subsequent necroptosis.2 This unique metabolic mechanism differentiates osimertinib from other kinase inhibitors and implies that conventional heart failure treatments may potentially be inadequate unless they specifically address the underlying mitochondrial dysfunction.

Second, the study’s reliance on claims data and heart failure hospitalization does not allow the detection of early, reversible myocardial dysfunction. The lack of cardiac imaging represents a limitation, as measures such as global longitudinal strain have been shown to be more sensitive for the detection of subclinical dysfunction than left ventricular ejection fraction.3 Routine assessment of global longitudinal strain could facilitate the timely recognition of mitochondrial injury during osimertinib therapy and help mitigate the underestimation of subclinical cardiotoxicity in affected patients.

Third, we propose that sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors may serve as a targeted prophylactic intervention. Unlike traditional neurohormonal antagonists, SGLT2 inhibitors improve mitochondrial energetics and mitigate cytosolic calcium accumulation, thereby directly counteracting the molecular pathways disrupted by osimertinib.4 Early clinical experience suggests that SGLT2 inhibitors can reverse osimertinib-induced cardiomyopathy and enable continued administration of life-prolonging anticancer therapy.5

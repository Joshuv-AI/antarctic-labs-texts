Posterior Bony Bankart Bridge and Reverse Remplissage in an Elite Contact Athlete
Authors: Ayham Jaber, Tyler J. Uppstrom, Michael Nocek, Colin P. Murphy, Taylor K. Calibo, Peter J. Millett
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Posterior or multidirectional shoulder instability are common among athletes subjected to repetitive posterior loading. Posterior glenohumeral dislocation can result in a posterior bony Bankart lesion, which presents a unique challenge in arthroscopic repair.Indications:Surgical repair is considered for patients with high physical demands and for those with instability refractory to nonoperative treatment, as observed in this 21-year-old professional hockey player. In select cases of elite contact athletes, even a modest reverse Hill-Sachs lesion (rHSL) and posterior bone loss may justify augmentation with a posterior bony Bankart bridge and reverse remplissage, as these techniques aim to minimize the risk of recurrence and optimize return to play.Technique Description:The patient is positioned in the beach-chair position. The rHSL is gently debrided. A straight crescent suture lasso is used to pierce the lateral subscapularis from anterior to posterior, and a 1.3-mm SutureTape is shuttled. Both suture limbs are retrieved and loaded into a 4.75-mm SwiveLock anchor. The anchor is placed in the rHSL, securing the tendon into the defect under individual limb tensioning. To repair the posterior bony Bankart lesion, a posteroinferior portal is created. The bony lesion is debrided and elevated. Two 1.8-mm knotless FiberTak anchors are placed to repair the posterior labrum inferiorly and superiorly to stabilize the bony fragment to the glenoid and aid in repair. A 2.4-mm SutureTak anchor is then placed at the glenoid neck medial to the lesion, and its sutures are inserted into a 2.9-mm PushLock anchor at the glenoid margin between 2 previously placed anchors, creating a “bony Bankart bridge” for compression. Capsular closure is performed with a polydioxanone suture, which is passed through the posterior capsule using a lasso from the posterior portal and tied down with Weston knots to close the deep capsule and maintain tension. When the anchor trajectory is challenging, curved guides may be substituted for straight guides. The posterior bony Bankart fragment is carefully exposed and debrided before fixation to ensure optimal healing.Results:Published outcomes of the posterior bony Bankart bridge demonstrate excellent stability with high return-to-sport rates. Similarly, while most literature focuses on anterior remplissage, the principles and outcomes of reverse remplissage are consistent with low recurrence rates in patients with rHSLs. Even small rHSLs and modest posterior bone loss may warrant augmentation in elite collision athletes, as recurrent instability can have career-threatening consequences. Further research should focus on long-term durability and comparative effectiveness of this combined strategy.Discussion/Conclusion:Appropriate portal placement and meticulous technique execution are integral to avoiding possible complications, including recurrent instability, loss of motion, and/or subscapularis weakness. Additionally, deep closure of the posterior capsule is critical to maintain capsular tension. While standard posterior labral and bony Bankart repairs generally achieve excellent outcomes, they are less reliable in elite collision athletes, where even modest posterior bone loss or an rHSL can predispose to failure. In this case, reverse remplissage neutralized the humeral defect and reinforced subscapularis restraint, while the bony Bankart bridge provided compression and anatomical restoration of the fragment. This combined approach offered superior protection against recurrence and progression of bone loss compared with soft-tissue repair alone.Patient Consent Disclosure Statement:The author(s) attests that consent has been obtained from any patient(s) appearing in this publication. Background

This video demonstrates our preferred technique for combined posterior bony Bankart repair using a suture anchor bridge construct with an adjunct reverse remplissage procedure, in an elite contact athlete.

Indications

The patient is a 21-year-old professional hockey player who suffered a direct hit to the left shoulder during a game, resulting in an instability event with posterior subluxation, with significant pain and subsequent posterior shoulder pain after the injury. The patient also had a history of a left anterior shoulder dislocation 1 year before presentation, which had been treated nonoperatively. Physical examination during the preoperative consultation demonstrated preserved range of motion, full rotator cuff strength, and a positive posterior loading shift test.

Preoperative radiographs demonstrated a posterior bony Bankart lesion best appreciated on the axillary view. Preoperative computed tomography scan redemonstrated the posterior bony Bankart lesion with a reverse Hill-Sachs lesion (rHSL), which was confirmed on preoperative magnetic resonance imaging with associated bony edema of the anterior humeral head just medial to the subscapularis insertion.

Based on the acute nature of the injury, the senior author (P.J.M.) decided that an attempted primary repair, rather than a bony augmentation procedure, was the preferred surgical option.

Technique Description

The patient is placed in the beach-chair position, and examination under anesthesia demonstrates a grade 3 posterior load and shift and a grade 2 anterior load and shift.

A standard posterior portal is established, followed by the anterosuperior portal with a 5.5 mm cannula. A diagnostic arthroscopy is completed with a 30° scope. In the presented case, an anterior labral tear extending from 6- to 10-o'clock positions was demonstrated without glenoid bone loss, and a posterior labral tear from 1-to 6-o'clock positions with a bony Bankart component. The rHSL and posterior aspect of the lateral subscapularis tendon were gently debrided with an arthroscopic shaver from the anterosuperior portal to create a bony bed for healing. Care is taken to avoid damage to the subscapularis tendon. Next, a straight crescent suture lasso is utilized from the anteroinferior portal to pierce the lateral margin of the subscapularis tendon from anterior to posterior. A Nitinol wire is used to shuttle a 1.3-mm SutureTape (Arthrex). The posterior limb of the SutureTape is retrieved through the anterosuperior portal such that both limbs are exiting the anterior-superior cannula. The SutureTape limbs are then loaded into a 4.75-mm SwiveLock anchor, and the appropriate size punch is placed into the reverse Hill-Sachs defect. The anchor is placed in standard fashion with each suture limb individually tensioned to reduce the subscapularis tendon into the defect. The sutures are cut, and the repair is assessed, demonstrating excellent reduction of the lateral upper border of the subscapularis tendon into the reverse Hill-Sachs defect. The anterior labrum is repaired using three 1.8-mm FiberTak all-suture anchors (Arthrex). This technique has been previously described. 1

The arthroscope is then moved to the anterosuperior portal using a switching stick to view the posterior bony Bankart lesion. Using an 18-gauge spinal needle for localization, a posteroinferior portal is made approximately 4 cm distal to the posterolateral corner of the acromion, and a 5.5-mm cannula is placed. Appropriate positioning of this portal is essential to ensure an appropriate trajectory for anchor placement and suture passage. Next, a 1.8-mm knotless FiberTak anchor is drilled into the articular margin of the posteroinferior glenoid at the 6 o'clock position, and the repair suture is retrieved to the anteroinferior portal. A straight crescent suture lasso is passed through the capsulolabral tissue, and the repair stitch is shuttled through the tissue and out the posteroinferior portal using the Nitinol wire. The repair stitch is then shuttled through the anchor using a No. 5 Ethibond suture for counter tension, which is tensioned appropriately to reduce the inferior aspect of the posterior bony Bankart lesion to the posterior aspect of the glenoid. This allows for the reduction of the inferior margin of the tear to stabilize the bony fragment and aid in repair.

After a 5.5-mm cannula is placed into the original posterior portal, a drill guide for a 2.4-mm SutureTak anchor (Arthrex) is then placed through the initial posterior portal and drilled medial to the bony Bankart lesion into the nonarticular surface of the glenoid. These sutures are maintained in the posterior portal. A 1.8-mm knotless FiberTak anchor is drilled into the articular margin of the posterior glenoid at the level of the bony lesion through the posteroinferior portal. A straight crescent suture lasso is then passed through the capsulolabral tissue, and the repair stitch is shuttled using the Nitinol wire. The repair stitch is then shuttled through the anchor and tensioned appropriately to reduce the posterior bony Bankart lesion to the posterior aspect of the glenoid.

An additional 1.8-mm knotless FiberTak anchors are placed as needed, depending on the tear pattern of the posterior labrum. In the presented case, 2 FiberTak anchors were placed at the superior aspect of the posterior bony Bankart lesion. The sutures from the 2.4-mm SutureTak anchor are then placed into a 2.9-mm PushLock anchor (Arthrex) at the articular margin of the glenoid defect between the 2 previously placed FiberTak anchors. This creates a bony Bankart bridge construct, providing compression across the bony Bankart lesion. A suture lasso is utilized from the posterior portal to shuttle a No. 0 polydioxanone suture around the capsule through the posterior portal. The sutures are tied onto the posterior aspect of the capsule to provide deep closure of the posterior arthroscopic portals. This step is critical to maintain posterior capsular tension in patients with posterior instability.

Potential complications of this procedure include recurrent posterior instability, loss of motion, or subscapularis weakness related to reverse remplissage. Recurrent instability can be mitigated by using an appropriate number of anchors to achieve adequate repair, which are typically placed approximately 8 to 10 mm apart or every hour of the clock face. Additionally, deep closure of the posterior capsule, where the posterior arthroscopic portals are placed, is a critical step to prevent undue postoperative posterior capsule laxity. Precise passage of the suture through the subscapularis tendon and placement of the reverse remplissage anchors are also critical steps, as they help avoid overtensioning or shortening of the subscapularis tendon. Only a small portion of the upper border of the subscapularis tendon is typically required for reverse remplissage. If a significant portion of the subscapularis tendon is required to fill the reverse Hill-Sachs defect, alternative options, such as a bone augmentation procedure, should be considered. Results

The postoperative rehabilitation protocol involves the use of a sling for 6 weeks. Passive range of motion begins immediately postoperatively, with limitations on external rotation to 30° and internal rotation to the abdomen for the first 4 weeks postoperatively. Full passive range of motion is allowed at 4 weeks postoperatively, and active range of motion begins at 5 to 6 weeks with a strengthening program typically starting at 7 to 8 weeks. Return to sport is generally achieved 4 to 5 months postoperatively after a progressive, therapist-driven program.

Discussion/Conclusion

Previous studies have demonstrated that the bony Bankart bridge repair is associated with reliable postoperative shoulder function and a return to preinjury sport level, with a mean follow-up of 8 years. 2 A recent systematic review also demonstrated improved postoperative outcome scores and lower occurrence rates after the modified McLaughlin procedure. 3

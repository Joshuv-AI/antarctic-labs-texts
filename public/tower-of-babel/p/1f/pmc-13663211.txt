Integration of single-cell sequencing and transcriptomic analysis to identify novel diagnostic biomarkers for pre-eclampsia
Authors: Zhengrui Huang, Jiachun Wei, Lu sun, Andong He, Xiufang Wang, Jian Wang, Wanchang Yin, Xinyao Lu, Jing Li, Ruiman Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPre-eclampsia is a significant obstetric complication. Immune cells within the maternal circulatory system hold substantial promises for the diagnosis and treatment of pre-eclampsia. However, research focusing on the maternal circulatory system remains inadequate.MethodsWe conducted differential analysis and weighted correlation network analysis on a dataset to identify blood biomarkers for early-onset pre-eclampsia. These biomarkers were subsequently validated through ELISA. Multiple bioinformatic analyses were subsequently performed to predict the biological functions associated with the selected genes and potential protein‒miRNA regulatory relationships. Additionally, by analysing single-cell transcriptomic data from blood samples, we predicted the genetic profiles of the selected genes, investigated changes in the proportion of blood immune cells and abnormalities in intercellular communication in the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia, and predicted potential protein-compound interactions.ResultsWe identified and validated the expression levels of F2R-like trypsin receptor 1 (F2RL1) and granzyme H (GZMH) in maternal blood samples obtained from women with and without pre-eclampsia. Gene set enrichment analysis revealed that F2RL1 participates in the regulation of classic signaling pathways, including the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, pattern recognition receptor signaling pathway, oxidative stress-induced endogenous apoptosis signaling pathway, and vesicle targeting process. GZMH primarily contributes to immune processes associated with natural killer cells. Analysis of single-cell sequencing datasets revealed significant alterations in T cells and haematopoietic stem cell growth factor (HSC-G-CSF) within the maternal blood system during the onset of pre-eclampsia. Furthermore, receptor–ligand interaction analysis and protein–compound interaction analysis highlighted the critical role of aspirin in alleviating pre-eclampsia pathology, while also revealing the potential therapeutic value of tyrosine kinase inhibitors.ConclusionThis study identified the pre-eclampsia biomarkers F2RL1 and GZMH, which showed good diagnostic value. This research provides new insights for the prediction of pre-eclampsia and deepens our understanding of changes in the maternal blood immune system during pre-eclampsia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-09361-1.Keywords: Pre-eclampsia, Single-cell sequencing, Biomarkers, WGCNA, Immune environment

Introduction

Pre-eclampsia is a pregnancy-related hypertensive disorder characterized primarily by hypertension and proteinuria that occurs after 20 weeks of gestation. Based on the onset of symptoms before or after 34 weeks of gestation, pre-eclampsia can be classified into early-onset pre-eclampsia and late-onset pre-eclampsia [1, 2]. Early-onset pre-eclampsia, which occurs earlier in gestation, is of particular concern due to its more severe clinical manifestations. Currently, in addition to placental delivery, effective treatment methods for established pre-eclampsia are lacking, mainly due to limitations such as the lack of specific intervention targets, the complexity of the pregnancy environment, and the heterogeneity of the disease [3].

Therefore, the current widely recognized strategy for the diagnosis and treatment of pre-eclampsia involves detection during early pregnancy (approximately 13 weeks of gestation) and timely preventive therapy to alleviate maternal disease symptoms following the onset of pre-eclampsia. The maternal circulatory system serves as a medium for the transport of various biomarkers and harmful substances. Therefore, it is necessary to employ bioinformatics to discover blood biomarkers with high detectability and investigate the potential mechanisms of these biomarkers in disease progression [4, 5].

Single-cell transcriptomics of blood can identify the cell types expressing specific genes, study the impact of genes on cell differentiation and development, and predict interactions and communication between cells [6]. To date, several single-cell transcriptomic studies have investigated the influence of the placenta and decidua on the onset of pre-eclampsia, as well as the underlying mechanisms of placental development [7, 8]. However, few studies have combined blood transcriptomics and single-cell transcriptomics of blood to explore the potential pathogenic mechanisms of early-onset pre-eclampsia.

Based on the aforementioned strategies for the diagnosis and treatment of pre-eclampsia and the current research status on pre-eclampsia, we first conducted a comprehensive analysis of the early-onset pre-eclampsia datasets, GSE44711 and GSE48424. As a result, we identified GZMH and F2RL1 as promising biomarkers with high detectability, and these markers were further validated. Using single-cell transcriptomic analysis of blood, we predicted the cellular localization of the selected genes and further investigated the changes in immune cells and their potential intercellular relationships during the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia. Protein-drug target prediction was employed to explore potential therapeutic approaches, providing new insights for the prediction and treatment of pre-eclampsia.

The blood single-cell transcriptomic dataset GSE192693 comprises 4 samples from the control group and 6 samples from the pre-eclampsia group. All the aforementioned transcriptomic samples were obtained in the third trimester (information for the samples in this dataset is provided in Table 3.

The batch effect was removed using the “sva” R package, and the expression values were normalized using the Combat method [11].

Differential analysis

Differential analysis was performed using the “limma” R package. The thresholds for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were set at an absolute value of log2-fold change > 1 and P < 0.01. A log2FC less than − 1 was defined as a downregulated gene, and a log2FC greater than − 1 was defined as an upregulated gene. A volcano plot was used to display the differential analysis results.

Gene ontology analysis

To investigate the effect of biological changes in the circulation system of mothers, the differential gene sets from GSE48424 were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) analysis by using the R package “clusterProfiler”. Then, the R packages “digest” and “GOplot” were used to calculate the z score of each enrichment term. A circle plot was used to present the results of the GO analysis.

Weighted correlation network analysis

Weighed correlation network analysis (WGCNA) was utilized to analyse the blood dataset GSE48424 to explore genes that exhibited a strong correlation with pre-eclampsia. First, the top 5000 genes ranked by gene expression levels in the matrix were identified for analysis. The “WGCNA” R package was used for further analysis. According to the principle of a scale-free network, a soft threshold of 0.89 was selected to construct the gene coexpression network. Subsequently, the merged gene modules with high similarity were identified. Pearson correlation analysis was used to identify gene modules that are highly correlated with pre-eclampsia. The genes within the top three modules ranked by correlation were extracted and intersected with the DEGs obtained from the aforementioned differential analysis. The resulting selected genes were considered potential blood biomarkers highly correlated with pre-eclampsia.

Gene set enrichment analysis

To predict the potential mechanisms related to the selected genes, we conducted gene enrichment analysis using gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) software (version 5.7). The selected genes were subjected to gene enrichment analysis based on the Gene Ontology (GO, ‘c5.go.bp.v7.2.symbols.gmt’) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG, ‘c2.cp.kegg.v7.2.symbols.gmt’) databases in GSEA software. The genes in the gene sets were ranked based on their expression values using Pearson correlation coefficients. The enrichment threshold was set at a NOM P value < 0.05.

Single-cell analysis and intercellular communication analysis

Batch correction of samples was performed using the “harmony” R package, and dimensionality reduction was carried out using the “tsne” method with a resolution value set at 1.5. The “doubletfinder” R package was utilized to remove doublets from the data. Subsequently, the “Single R” and “celldex” packages were used to identify cell types. T cells were identified using “CD8A” and “CD27”. Monocytes were identified using “CD14” and “CD163”. B cells were identified using “GZMB”. Platelets were identified using “CD9”. CMPs were identified using “CD34”. NK cells were identified using “CD38” and “CD39”. The proportions of various immune cells in the plasma of normal pregnant women and pre-eclampsia patients were statistically analysed.

The “CellChat” R package was utilized to perform intercellular communication analysis, and the CellChatDB.human dataset was used as a reference. The cellular receptor‒ligand interactions were calculated using a 20% trimmed mean to assess intercellular communication.

Preparation of plasma samples

The inclusion criteria were as follows: singleton pregnancy, normal blood pressure, and no medical history or medication use prior to pregnancy. The diagnosis of pre-eclampsia and severe symptoms was based on the American College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists (ACOG) report. The baseline information of the pregnant women in the early pregnancy group is shown in Table 4, and the baseline information of the pregnant women in the late pregnancy group is shown in Table 5. Two patients were removed from the normal group and three from the pre-eclampsia group due to refusal to participate in the trial or loss to follow-up. Patients with undetectable protein levels were excluded. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinan University (Approval Number: KY-2021-092) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All study participants provided written informed consent.

Blood samples were collected into EDTA-Vacutainer tubes (Improve Medical, 101 680 967) and placed on ice. The samples were then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C and stored as aliquots at -80 °C.

Sample size estimation was conducted using PASS (power analysis and sample size) software in conjunction with the latest research on the incidence of pre-eclampsia [12]. This study included plasma samples from 83 pregnant women in the third trimester, including 43 in the normal group and 40 in the pre-eclampsia group. Additionally, plasma samples from 115 pregnant women in the first trimester were included; there were 62 in the normal group and 53 in the pre-eclampsia group.

ELISA

Plasma concentrations of biomarkers were measured using commercially available F2RL1 (ml060077, Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) and GZMH (ml025497, Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The detection limit ranged from 0.1 to 8 ng/ml.

The specific experimental steps are shown below. First, a series of known concentration standard samples were prepared for generating a standard curve to calculate the concentration of unknown samples. The plasma sample was added, and 10 µl of sample was added to each reaction well. The analyte solution was then added to the wells of a microplate to allow it to adhere to the plate. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h, followed by washing with wash buffer to remove unbound substances. Next, the enzyme-labelled secondary antibody was added. If an indirect ELISA is being performed, the enzyme-labelled secondary antibody is added to bind with the coated antigen or antibody. Subsequently, the microplate was washed again to remove unbound secondary antibody. An appropriate substrate solution was then added to generate a signal detectable by the enzyme. A stop solution was added to halt the enzyme reaction. The absorbance (OD) of each well was measured using a microplate reader, and the concentration of the target analyte in the samples was calculated based on the standard curve.

Gene-miRNA interaction analysis

NetworkAnalyst (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) is an online analysis platform that offers a wide range of genetic analyses, including gene-drug analyses. Gene-miRNA interaction analysis was conducted using data from the TarBase database [13].

Protein-chemical interactions analysis

Similar to the method described above, protein-chemical interaction analysis was conducted using the NetworkAnalyst analysis platform (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) on the results obtained from single-cell sequencing. This analysis relies on data sourced from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD, https://ctdbase.org/).

Statistical analysis

Biostatistical data were analysed using the empirical Bayesian-adjusted t test. Since the sample size exceeded 30 and did not follow a normal distribution, a nonparametric rank-sum test was employed to analyse the experimental data. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, or ***P < 0.001 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

Verification of the diagnostic efficacy of the analytical results

ELISA was performed to validate the expression levels of F2RL1 and GZMH in maternal peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) during the third trimester (n = 83). An exploratory analysis was also conducted to evaluate the expression of these selected genes in early pregnancy plasma (n = 115) and their potential predictive value for pre-eclampsia. In the third trimester, F2RL1 expression was significantly decreased and GZMH expression was significantly increased in the pre-eclampsia group compared with the control group (Fig. 4A). These differences were analyzed using the nonparametric rank-sum test (Mann-Whitney U test) and reached statistical significance (*P < 0.05 or **P < 0.01), consistent with the findings from the GSE48424 dataset. In early pregnancy plasma, ELISA showed a significant increase in F2RL1 expression and a significant decrease in GZMH expression in the pre-eclampsia group compared with the control group (Fig. 4B), as determined by the nonparametric rank-sum test (Mann-Whitney U test). *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001 were considered statistically significant.

Elisa validation of the expression levels of selected genes in maternal blood. A Expression levels of F2RL1 and GZMH in maternal plasma during late pregnancy. B Expression levels of F2RL1 and GZMH in maternal plasma during early pregnancy. C Binary logistic regression indicated good predictive performance of F2RL1 and GZMH for early-onset pre-eclampsia. D ROC curves demonstrating the diagnostic performance of F2RL1, GZMH, and a combined model based on the two markers for pre-eclampsia, suggesting that the combined model has the best diagnostic performance for pre-eclampsia

The diagnostic performance of F2RL1 and GZMH in predicting pre-eclampsia was evaluated using binary logistic regression. The analysis results indicated that both F2RL1 (95% confidence interval: 1.0106–1.2966) and GZMH (95% confidence interval: 0.6492–0.8587) have diagnostic value for pre-eclampsia (Fig. 4C). The diagnostic performance of the prediction model was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, and the goodness of fit and complexity of the fitted model were evaluated using the Akaike information criterion (AIC). When F2RL1 alone (ROC = 0.72, AIC = 146.09) or GZMH alone (ROC = 0.739, AIC = 127.27) were used as predictive factors for pre-eclampsia, the predictive performance was inferior to that of the diagnostic model constructed using both F2RL1 and GZMH (ROC = 0.757, AIC = 124.7). Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to evaluate the differences in diagnostic performance among the three models and revealed that there was no statistically significant difference between the diagnostic models using F2RL1 or GZMH alone. However, the fitted model using F2RL1 + GZMH showed statistically significant differences in its predictive performance for pre-eclampsia compared to the diagnostic model constructed using a single factor. The equation fitted based on the regression coefficients was log P = 0.4958 + 0.1306*F2RL1-0.2793*GZMH (Fig. 4D).

Results of gene set enrichment analysis of selected genes

To gain further insight into the biological processes associated with the selected genes, GSEA was conducted. The analysis revealed that F2RL1 is involved in the cellular response to mechanical stimuli, negative regulation of the oxidative stress-induced intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway, regulation of the pattern recognition receptor signaling pathway, regulation of the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, and vesicle targeting (Supplementary Fig. 2A). Additionally, GZMH is involved in natural killer cell-mediated immunity, the positive regulation of natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity, the positive regulation of natural killer cell immunity, the regulation of lymphocyte chemotaxis, and the regulation of natural killer cell-mediated immunity (Supplementary Fig. 2B).

Gene-miRNA interactions

The analysis results from NetworkAnalyst suggested that 21 miRNAs were potentially correlated with F2RL1, and 2 miRNAs were potentially correlated with GZMH. Among these miRNAs, hsa-mir-23b-3p was correlated with both selected genes simultaneously (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Localization of selected genes in PBMCs immune cells

After applying the “harmony” R package to correct the data, each sample exhibited high consistency (Supplementary Fig. 2). With a resolution threshold of 1.5, the cells were clustered into 29 cell clusters. Cell annotation using the “single R” R package revealed that the PBMCs’ samples mainly consisted of T cells, NK cells, monocytes, B cells, haematopoietic stem cells-granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (HSC G-CSF), platelets, and common myeloid progenitors (CMPs) (Fig. 5A). Differential analysis indicated a significant increase in the number of T cells and a significant decrease in the level of HSC G-CSF in the circulatory system of pre-eclampsia patients (Fig. 5B, C). The use of various immune cell-specific biomarkers for labelling has demonstrated the reliability of the aforementioned clustering. Importantly, single-cell transcriptomic analysis of PBMCs revealed that GZMH primarily maps to NK cells, and F2RL1 primarily maps to platelets and NK cells (Fig. 5D).

Potential regulatory relationships between PBMCs’ cell types in pre-eclampsia patients

To further investigate the changes in immune cells in the maternal circulatory system during the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia and the potential regulatory mechanisms underlying these changes, additional analyses were conducted using the “CellChat” R package on the single-cell dataset. Based on the previously mentioned results of the differential analysis of immune cell proportions, our focus was directed towards T cells and HSC G-CSF.

The analysis results indicated that in normal pregnancy, common myeloid progenitor (CMP) cells exhibited the strongest regulatory effect on HSC G-CSF, followed by the regulatory effects of HSC G-CSF on T cells. CMP, NK cells, and platelets had the greatest regulatory effects on T cells. The primary targets regulated by T cells were NK cells, monocytes, and B cells. During the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia, some of these physiological phenomena undergo alterations. The regulatory effects of CMP on HSC G-CSF were significantly decreased, although T cells remained the primary target of HSC G-CSF regulation. The regulatory effect of T cells on NK cells, monocytes, and B cells was greater in pre-eclampsia pregnant patients than in normal pregnant patients (Fig. 5E, F).

The receptor‒ligand interactions between cells were explored, and the overall analysis results are presented in Fig. 6A. Analysis of the receptor‒ligand interactions on the input side revealed that during the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia, the relative regulation intensity of macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF), LCK Proto-Oncogene, Src family tyrosine kinase (LCK), and B and T lymphocyte-associated (BTLA), which originate from multiple cell types, decreased. However, the relative regulation intensity of selectin P ligand (SELPLG), CD22, adhesion G protein-coupled receptor E5 (ADGRE5), and midkine (MK) significantly increased. In the pre-eclampsia group, CD99, GALECTIN, CD22, ADGRE5, and MK from multiple cell sources exhibited significantly increased regulatory effects on HSC G-CSF (Fig. 6B).

Analysis of the receptor‒ligand interactions on the output side suggested that in pre-eclampsia, the secretion of C-type lectin (CLEC), CD45, integrin beta 2 (ITGB2), lectin L (SELL), and F2R-like trypsin receptor 1 (PARs, F2RL1) by T cells significantly increased, and the secretion of MIF, CD99, ANNEXIN, interleukin 16 (IL16), LCK, and fms-related receptor tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) significantly decreased. HSC G-CSF increased the secretion of CD45, ITGB2, and SELL but decreased the secretion of major histocompatibility complex II (MHC-II) (Fig. 6C). GO analysis of the differentially expressed receptors and ligands revealed that the altered receptor‒ligand interactions related to T cells were mainly involved in the regulation of host‒virus interactions, immunity, adaptive immunity, innate immunity, cell adhesion, and the inflammatory response (Fig. 6D). The altered receptor‒ligand interactions related to HSC G-CSF mainly participate in the regulation of host‒virus interactions, immunity, adaptive immunity, and innate immunity (Fig. 6E).

Discussion

Current research on the mechanisms and omics of pre-eclampsia predominantly focuses on decidua and placental tissues [14, 15]. In this study, we used a different approach by analyzing PBMCs’ RNA-seq data to investigate biomarkers strongly associated with pre-eclampsia. We subsequently experimentally validated these biomarkers and conducted preliminary assessments of their predictive value. Additionally, we utilized GSEA to predict their potential biological functions, and single-cell RNA-seq was employed to predict the expression of selected genes in maternal circulating immune cells. Furthermore, we conducted analyses of cell communication and ligand‒receptor interactions to explore potential abnormalities in cell communication processes within the maternal circulation during the onset of pre-eclampsia. These findings provide novel insights into the treatment of pre-eclampsia.

The results of Gene Ontology (GO) analysis of differentially expressed genes indicated the significant involvement of NK cells in the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia. As innate lymphocytes, NK cells constitute approximately 5%-20% of leukocytes in the circulatory system. The dynamic equilibrium of signals received by killer activating receptors (KARs) and killer inhibitory receptors (KIRs) governs NK cell functionality. Upon activation, NK cells release lysosomes containing perforin and granzyme, leading to target cell lysis and death. Concurrently, proinflammatory cytokines produced by NK cells serve as potent activators of neighbouring immune cells. Clinical studies have provided evidence of an increased proportion of NK cells in the circulatory system of pregnant women with pre-eclampsia, mirroring observations in animal models [16]. Presently, various therapeutic approaches targeting neutrophil function inhibition, such as neutrophil extracellular trap inhibition via DNase I infusion, have garnered considerable interest in cancer, cardiovascular, and other research domains. Our findings emphasize the crucial role of NK cells in the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia, corroborating existing knowledge. Additionally, the results of GSEA in our study suggest the extensive involvement of GZMH in NK cell-related immune processes. Furthermore, the results of single-cell analysis indicated the relatively specific expression of GZMH in NK cells. GZMH belongs to an enzyme family of proteins and serves as a crucial regulatory molecule in cytotoxic lymphocytes (CTLs) and natural killer cells. Research indicates that cytokines and immune regulatory molecules regulate the expression of GZMH [17]. Upon immune system activation triggered by infections, tumours, or other diseases, the expression levels of GZMH may increase, thereby increasing the cytotoxicity of immune cells to aberrant cells. Moreover, GZMH participates in modulating the production and release of cytokines, thereby influencing the magnitude and direction of immune responses. Nevertheless, limited research has explored the potential role of GZMH in the pathogenesis and diagnosis of pre-eclampsia [18]. Our study suggested that GZMH may be an effective target for NK cell inhibition therapy, which is worthy of further experimental verification.

F2RL1, also known as protease-activated receptor 2 (PAR2), encodes a member of the G protein-coupled receptor 1 protein family [19–21]. Previous research has demonstrated that activation of this receptor stimulates smooth muscle relaxation, vasodilation, increased blood flow, and decreased blood pressure. Additionally, this protein plays a significant role in inflammation [22, 23] and innate and adaptive immunity, often by acting as a promoter of inflammatory responses. Previous studies on F2RL1 in pregnancy or pre-eclampsia have focused primarily on placental tissue, as evidenced by research by Al-Ani B et al. and Hirai C et al. [24, 25]. The activation of F2RL1 has been suggested to stimulate the secretion of sFLT-1 by endothelial cells (Al-Ani B et al., 2013). Furthermore, Al-Ani B et al. proposed that resveratrol could inhibit this pathological process. The findings of Wang et al. confirmed these results and suggested that the upregulation of neutrophil F2RL1 expression might be a pregnancy-specific change rather than one specific to pre-eclampsia [25]. In contrast to previous studies, our research focused on the maternal PBMC. Our results indicate that F2RL1 is predominantly localized in NK cells and platelets in the PBMC. Furthermore, GSEA suggested that F2RL1 is primarily involved in regulating the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway and the intrinsic apoptosis signaling pathway induced by oxidative stress. Notably, our study revealed that F2RL1 is involved in vesicle targeting regulation. Vesicle targeting is a highly regulated and precise process that is crucial for various cellular functions [26–29]. During the pathogenesis of pre-eclampsia, disruptions in the secretory functions of syncytiotrophoblasts and various immune cells have garnered widespread attention [30–32].

Additionally, Gene Ontology Biological Process enrichment analysis revealed distinct functional specialization among the three modules. The violet module was strongly enriched in mitochondrial translation and gene expression, suggesting a primary role in supporting mitochondrial protein synthesis and cellular energy metabolism. In contrast, the saddle brown module showed significant enrichment in NK cell activation and NK cell-mediated immunity, indicating its involvement in innate immune responses. The dark green module was enriched in pyrimidine deoxyribonucleotide catabolic process along with multiple T cell-related pathways, including T cell receptor signaling, lymphocyte activation, and cytokine production, implying a coordinated function in nucleotide metabolism and adaptive immunity. Collectively, these findings demonstrate clear modular organization of the transcriptome, separating mitochondrial metabolic functions from innate and adaptive immune processes, which may reflect underlying biological mechanisms such as metabolic reprogramming or immune cell activation in the studied context [33–35].

Several studies have aimed to construct diagnostic models for pre-eclampsia using omics data. However, most of these studies have relied on sequencing data from placental tissue samples, which may limit the detectability and reproducibility of their findings due to the focus on placental rather than PBMCs [36–40]. In this study, ELISA validation in the third trimester and exploratory experiments in the first trimester showed significantly increased F2RL1 and decreased GZMH levels in pre-eclampsia. Although both markers can promote inflammation in certain contexts, the elevated F2RL1 aligns with its role in early inflammatory responses and tissue repair at the maternal-fetal interface, whereas the reduced GZMH is consistent with recent single-cell studies showing downregulation of cytotoxic genes in T/NK cell subsets in PE, likely reflecting an early defect in immune surveillance and tolerance that contributes to later placental dysfunction. Moderate variability of F2RL1 and GZMH was observed in controls, which is attributable to their inherent biological heterogeneity. F2RL1 sensitivity to hormonal and low-grade inflammatory fluctuations, and GZMH variation due to individual differences in NK cell subpopulation and activation status. Despite this physiological fluctuation, the group differences remained statistically significant (P < 0.01), and ROC analysis demonstrated good discriminatory power for both markers individually and in combination. Binary logistic regression further supported their promising diagnostic efficacy, although larger validation cohorts are still needed.

Our analysis of the proportion of PMBCs in the haematopoietic system revealed a significant increase in the number of T cells and a decrease in HSC G-CSF levels in pre-eclampsia patients. Existing research indicates a marked increase in the number and activity of Th1 cells (producing proinflammatory cytokines such as interferon-gamma) and Th17 cells (secreting proinflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-17) in the peripheral blood of pre-eclampsia patients, and regulatory T cells (Treg cells) are suppressed [41–43]. This study aimed to analyse the impact and potential therapeutic value of collective changes in T-cell populations in pre-eclampsia patients. Our findings suggest that imbalances in the numbers of different T-cell types during pre-eclampsia development are partly influenced by decreased relative regulatory effects of various cell factors, such as MIF, LCK, and BTLA, on T cells and significantly increased relative regulatory effects of factors such as SELPLG, CD22, ADGRE5, and MK. Analysis of T-cell secretion activities during pre-eclampsia pathogenesis revealed a significant increase in the secretion levels of CLEC, CD45, ITGB2, SELL, and PARs, and the secretion levels of factors such as MIF, CD99, ANNEXIN, IL16, LCK, and FLT3 were significantly decreased. Aberrant secretion by T cells regulates various immune modulation activities, including innate immunity, adaptive immunity, and immune responses. Therefore, we further explored potential approaches to block these abnormal regulatory processes. Targeted drugs are broadly categorized into four categories. The first category includes acetylsalicylic acid (C9H8O4) and salicylic acid (C9H8O3), with acetylsalicylic acid, commonly known as aspirin, widely used in alleviating pre-eclampsia pathology [44]. The second category comprises drugs affecting the nervous system, with antidepressant drugs primarily used as secondary interventions for treating depression during pregnancy. The third category involves citric acid. Although there is no direct research linking citric acid to pre-eclampsia, our analysis suggested that a high dietary intake of citric acid may have preventive and therapeutic effects on pre-eclampsia. The fourth category encompasses tyrosine kinase inhibitors associated with FLT3. Both FLT1 and FLT3, members of the receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) family, play essential roles in angiogenesis and vascular function regulation. FLT1 has been extensively studied in the pathogenesis and diagnosis of pre-eclampsia. However, research on FLT3 has focused primarily on tumours, particularly diseases related to the haematopoietic system [45–47]. Currently, there is limited research on the role of FLT3 in pre-eclampsia pathogenesis. Nonetheless, tyrosine kinase inhibitors may represent potential targets for pre-eclampsia treatment, although further research on their efficacy and safety for pre-eclampsia treatment is needed.

Although GO analysis of the differentially expressed genes strongly implicated NK cell-related pathways in pre-eclampsia pathogenesis, we observed no significant difference in NK cell proportions between the control and PE groups. This discrepancy is not unexpected, as functional alterations in immune cells often occur independently of changes in their relative abundance [48, 49]. In the PE group, NK cells displayed notable changes in cell–cell communication strength and interaction patterns with other immune populations, indicating functional dysregulation despite stable proportions [50]. These transcriptional and interaction-level changes likely account for the enrichment of NK cell-associated terms in the GO analysis. This pattern aligns with previous studies showing that NK cell effector functions, rather than their circulating numbers, are frequently perturbed in pre-eclampsia, contributing to immune imbalance and impaired placentation [51].

This study has certain limitations. There is still insufficient research on the single-cell transcriptomics of blood samples from pre-eclampsia patients, resulting in a lack of available datasets. In this study, quantitative analysis of the results and preliminary exploration of gene diagnostic performance were conducted using clinical samples only, without in-depth mechanistic research. Therefore, further experimental validation is needed to verify the results of single-cell transcriptomics analysis and predictions of protein-drug targets.

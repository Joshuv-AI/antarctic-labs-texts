PRHOCARER study: protocol for a multicentre, convergent, mixed-methods trial assessing the effectiveness of pulmonary rehabilitation programmes and the needs of informal carers of people with chronic respiratory diseases
Authors: Manon Robic, Jean-Marie Grosbois, Michael Racodon, Olivier Le Rouzic, Philippe Ramon, Nicolas Masson, Claudine Fabre, Sarah Gephine
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAs the population ages, informal carers play an increasingly essential role in supporting patients with chronic respiratory diseases (CRDs), often at the expense of their own health. While the psychological effects are well documented, less is currently known about the physical toll of caregiving. Some trials have shown promising outcomes from pulmonary rehabilitation (PR) interventions targeting both patients and informal carers, but study designs have been limited. This trial aims to better understand informal carers’ experience by assessing their physical, psychological and social well-being at home, before and after the individual they care for participates in either a home-based or centre-based PR programme.Methods and analysesThis is a multicentre, convergent parallel mixed-methods study using a before-and-after intervention design. 54 informal carers of patients with CRDs will be recruited from two PR services—27 from a home- and 27 from a centre-based programme. Assessments of the physical, psychological and social well-being of the informal carers will be conducted at home. Semistructured interviews, conducted within 2-weeks post-PR, will explore informal carers’ expectations, experiences and support needs. The home-based PR programme will involve weekly 90-min sessions for 8 weeks, supervised at home by a care manager. Informal carers will have the opportunity to participate in PR sessions in accordance with their needs, availability and patient preferences. At the centre-based PR site, patients with CRDs will be enrolled in a 4-week inpatient PR programme or in a 5- to 10-week outpatient PR programme, consisting of 2 or 4 supervised sessions per week (for a total of 20 supervised sessions). Informal carers’ attendance will be tracked at both sites.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval was obtained from the University Sud Mediterranean 3 (24.04596.000457;2024/10/07). The trial results will be presented at scientific conferences, submitted to peer-reviewed journals and shared with decision-makers.Trial registration numberNCT06832709.Keywords: Pulmonary Disease, Chronic Obstructive; Pulmonary Rehabilitation; COPD Pathology; Exercise

Introduction

An informal carer is defined as an individual who provides unpaid care to a person with one or more disabilities, facilitating daily living activities and supporting medical care and symptom management.1 As the population ages, the primary setting for chronic care has shifted from hospital to home, with many informal carers now assuming the role of healthcare professionals in the delivery of care.2 In 2021, almost 9 million adults in France reported providing regular assistance to a relative experiencing a loss of independence.3 Consequently, identification and support of informal carers have evolved into a national priority, as demonstrated by the publication of new legislation aiming to promote the recognition of informal carers’ rights.4 However, this initiative might not specifically address the needs expressed by informal carers of patients with chronic respiratory diseases (CRDs), and further scientific data are required to support these claims with decision makers and payers.

Approximately 60%–80% of patients with CRDs report requiring daily support from others, mainly their spouse or children, providing physical, psychological, social, financial and emotional support.5 They represent invaluable unpaid help for society, providing, for example, an average of 9.5 hours of caregiving tasks (bathing, dressing, cooking, housework and shopping) per day to patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).6 In addition to providing physical assistance, they also manage medications, appointments and symptoms, and offer psychological and emotional support.7 8 By promoting medication adherence and healthier behaviours, such as increased physical activity, their involvement may help reduce exacerbations and hospitalisations.9,12

The impact of the caring role on informal carers’ own health has been extensively documented in CRDs.5 13 14 Informal carers of patients with COPD or interstitial lung disease (ILD) have reported anxiety and depression, fatigue/exhaustion, cognitive disorders, decreased social connection and quality of life, powerlessness, sleep disorders, burden, guilt and frustration.513,15 Alongside the patient, informal carers also experience reduced levels of physical activity11; however, their exercise capacity has never been assessed. This important dimension is often overlooked, yet evaluating informal carers’ physical health is crucial to identifying potential impairments and providing personalised support to help them maintain their caring role. Given that informal carers often prioritise the patient’s needs over their own16 and face mobility challenges related to caregiving,17 in-home assessments may offer an accessible solution. The 6-min stepper test (6MST) is a valid tool for assessing exercise capacity at home18 and may help identify physical impairments in informal carers. The needs and unmet needs of informal carers of patients with COPD or ILD have been widely reported in quantitative and qualitative studies.519,21 Informal carers reported needing support with physical and emotional care; information and education about the disease, its future course, treatment options and available resources and services; and support in managing the patient’s anxiety, panic, dyspnoea and exacerbation, as well as their own feelings and worries.20 22

Pulmonary rehabilitation (PR), a cornerstone of treatment for patients with CRDs, focuses on physical training, education and self-management support.23,27 Benefits of both centre- and home-based PR programmes have been well documented in patients with COPD or ILD.23,28 Although 70%–80% of patients enrolled in PR report receiving informal carer support at home,8 29 30 programme designs fail to adequately engage, educate or support informal carers.31 32 Healthcare professionals have a key role in identifying the informal carers’ needs and offering personalised support. PR has the potential to address both of these key aspects. A few studies have shown promising results in improving coping strategies, anxiety, fatigue and burden, among informal carers who participated in educational and self-management sessions during PR.3033,35 However, these interventions are scarce, have a narrow scope and have not comprehensively assessed the overall health of informal carers, including exercise capacity. Furthermore, this study compares two PR modalities: a home-based programme, where informal carers can be more actively involved, and a centre-based programme, where their participation is typically minimal. This design allows us to explore whether improvements in informal carers’ health mirror the benefits experienced by patients or whether more direct involvement in PR provides additional gains for informal carers themselves.

Aims and objectives

Primary aim

To evaluate the effect of a home-based versus centre-based PR programme on the physical, psychological and social health of informal carers of patients with CRDs.

Primary hypothesis

We hypothesise that informal carers participating more actively in the home-based PR programme will experience greater improvements in physical and psychological health compared with those in the centre-based programme. In contrast, we expect that informal carers in the centre-based group will mainly benefit in terms of social well-being, as the patient’s temporary absence may provide them with opportunities to re-engage in social or personal activities.

Secondary objectives

To describe the baseline overall health of informal carers, including their exercise and functional capacity, caring burden, anxiety and depressive symptoms, fatigue, quality of life and social well-being. We hypothesise that these outcomes will be adversely impacted compared with normative data from healthy adults, but will not differ significantly between the two PR models at baseline.

Methods and analysis

Study design and place

PRHOCARER is a multicentre, convergent parallel mixed-methods study using a before-and-after intervention design, involving both informal carers and patients with CRDs. Sites will be two PR services in the north of France: FormAction Santé, Pérenchies, providing the home-based PR programme (site investigators SG and J-MG) and Clinique de la Mitterie, Lille, France, providing the centre-based PR programme (site investigators MRa, NM and PR).

Study participants and period

Adult informal carers of patients with COPD or ILD will be recruited between July 2025 and July 2027. As the trial focuses on informal carers, no data will be collected from patients who do not report having an informal carer. The number of these patients will be documented solely for inclusion in the participant flow chart. Informal carers may still be included in the study even if the patient they care for chooses not to participate; this will not affect the patient’s access to care in either PR programme. Participants’ recruitment and informed consent

Patients with CRDs and their informal carers will be consecutively recruited from home- and centre-based PR facilities at the time of the patient’s admission to the programme. Site investigators, who are members of the PR team, will introduce the study and provide a leaflet. If the informal carer is not present, the leaflet will invite them to contact the main assessor directly. All informal carers’ assessments will be conducted by the main assessor (MRo, PhD student), who is independent of the PR clinical teams and not involved in delivering either the home- or centre-based programme. All informal carers will be required to sign a written informed consent form at the time of enrolment and prior to the initial home assessment. Written informed consent from patients with CRDs will be recorded at each site in accordance with their standard practice.

Sample size

The primary outcome of the study will be the change in the exercise capacity of informal carers following PR programmes, measured at home using the 6MST. This test was selected because, to our knowledge, it is the only validated and safe method to assess exercise capacity in a home setting.18 However, the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) has so far only been established in patients with COPD. Therefore, based on this literature, we assumed a mean difference of 40 steps as the MCID for this study.36 Using a paired-samples t-test, a power of 90%, an SD of 70 steps and an alpha at 5%, the sample size was calculated as 23 informal carers. As the integration of the informal carers will be different between the home- and centre-based PR programmes, each site will include 23 informal carers for a total of 46 participants. To account for an anticipated 15% dropout rate, the target recruitment is set at 54 informal carers (27 per group). However, recruitment will continue until 46 participants have completed the postintervention assessment.

Interventions

Each patient with a CRD will be referred to either the home-based (FormAction Santé, Pérenchies, France) or the centre-based PR programme (Clinique de la Mitterie, Lomme, France) by their respective respiratory physicians following traditional practice. In this real-life study, allocation to home- or centre-based PR will not be randomised. Instead, the respiratory physician will present both options, allowing the patient to choose their preferred setting. Informal carers may also participate in the decision-making process.

Home-based PR

Patients with CRDs will be enrolled in an 8-week personalised, home-based PR programme, including a weekly 90-min face-to-face supervised session. Physical training, education and self-management strategies will be implemented during these sessions.30 Educational, motivational and self-management plans and physical training will be designed and implemented through a collaborative process among the PR team, the patient and his/her informal carer. Exercise equipment, such as bicycles, steppers, mini bikes, elastic bands and dumbbells, will be loaned to participants during the 8-week programme. Cardiorespiratory training will be performed in 10-min bouts (shorter if needed), aiming for a total of 30–45 min per day, in one or several sessions. Exercise intensity will be adjusted to maintain a Borg dyspnœa score of 3–4 (0–10 scale). Training will be complemented by upper and lower limb strengthening exercises using dumbbells, elastic bands and/or body weight, with intensity progressively increased based on dyspnoea or fatigue. Both patients and informal carers will be encouraged to increase daily life physical activities (eg, gardening, housekeeping and shopping) to promote long-term activity integration. The transdisciplinary team is composed of a respiratory physician, two physiotherapists, three nurses, six kinesiologists, a dietitian, an art therapist, a psychotherapist and a sociomedical beautician. Apart from the weekly visit of the team member who supervises the sessions, participants will be expected to perform, on their own, personalised physical training (at least five sessions a week) and self-management plans for the rest of the week. Informal carers will be offered the opportunity to participate in educational and exercise sessions, according to their needs, availability and the patient’s preferences. Participation will always occur alongside the patient, but informal carers will be encouraged to express their own needs so that appropriate information and support can be provided. Each participation will be documented in a diary. Informal carers attending the weekly PR team visits will be able to share their experiences and challenges in supporting patients’ daily activities, as well as their own feelings and emotions. Those who express interest will be invited to take part in training sessions with the patient and may be offered cognitive behavioural therapy, counselling or motivational support. If any uncertainty arises regarding medical contraindications to physical activity, the assessor (MRo) will contact the carer’s family physician for confirmation.

Centre-based PR

Patients with CRDs will be enrolled in either a 4-week inpatient or a 5- to 10-week outpatient PR programme, depending on the plan negotiated with their respiratory physician. Enrolment in the inpatient programme will not indicate that the patient is experiencing an acute exacerbation. In line with recommendations,37 at least 3–4 weeks will elapse after hospitalisation for an exacerbation before inclusion. In both modalities, each patient will complete a total of 20 supervised sessions: 5 sessions per week for inpatients and 2–4 sessions per week for outpatients. Physical training and education will be offered by a multidisciplinary team including 4 respiratory physicians, 3 nurses, 2 dietitians, 1 physiotherapist, 4 kinesiologists, 1 occupational therapist and 1 social worker. A variety of physical activities will be offered, including outdoor walking, gymnastics (focusing on muscle strengthening, flexibility, balance and coordination), occupational therapy, balneotherapy and bicycling. Physical training and educational sessions will be delivered in groups of 8–10 participants. In line with standard practice, patients will be informed that their informal carer is welcome to attend the educational component. However, in practice, this opportunity is seldom used by informal carers.

Informal carer’s assessments

Quantitative data collection

All assessments will be conducted at the informal carer’s home at the beginning and end of the PR programme (within the first 2 weeks of the programmes and 2 weeks after it ends, respectively) by the same assessor (MRo), regardless of whether the patient is enrolled in the home- or centre-based programme (figure 1 and table 1). The assessor will not be blinded to the allocation group, as she will receive a prescription from either the home- or centre-based PR medical investigator. Once the informal carer provides consent to participate in the study, anthropometric data, including body weight and height, will be measured, and body mass index calculated. Comorbidities and medications will be recorded, and the Charlson score will be calculated.38 Oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate (HR) and blood pressure will be measured at rest prior to any physical test. Pulmonary function of informal carers will be assessed using a portable spirometer (COPD-6 Tester, Colson, France). With the informal carer’s consent, a summary of the data collected at baseline and at study completion will be forwarded to their primary care physician.

Attendance

The number of educational sessions attended by the informal carers, in both home- and centre-based PR programmes, and content of these sessions will be recorded in a diary. In the home-based PR programme, the number of supervised physical training sessions performed by the patient-informal carer dyad will also be recorded. Informal carers may participate in physical training only if they have no cardiovascular contraindications or unstable cardiovascular conditions; otherwise, a medical clearance from their family physician will be required. In both groups, informal carers will be encouraged to increase their level of physical activity (according to their needs and capacities) by performing activities either with the patient or independently. They will then be asked to record these physical activities in a diary.

Exercise capacity, functional capacity and muscle strength

The 6MST is a valid test for assessing exercise capacity in patients with COPD.18 It will be performed at home according to the original protocol.39 The test consists of performing the maximum number of steps on a stepper (Athletic GoSport) over a 6-min period. A 2-min familiarisation period will be followed by a rest period of more than 3 min in a seated position, after which the test will begin. Standardised instructions and encouragement will be given to participants.39 At rest, during the test and 5 min of recovery, SpO2 and HR will be monitored. Dyspnoea and leg fatigue will be assessed with the modified Borg scale (0–10) at rest and at the end of the test. The main outcome will be the total number of steps automatically recorded by the stepper. The MCID is considered to be a change of 40 steps in patients with COPD.36

The short physical performance battery test (SPPB) is a valid test for assessing functional capacity and risk of frailty in older adults.40 41 It is a multicomponent test comprising standing balance tests (feet side-by-side, semitandem and tandem; each stance is maintained for 10 s), a 4-m gait speed test (performed at habitual pace; the faster time of two trials will be reported in m/s) and a 5-sit-to-stand test (one trial as fast as possible reported in seconds). The sum of the three components (each subscore ranges from 0 to 4) determines the final SPPB score, with a possible range from 0 (functional impairment) to 12 (maximal functional capacity). The MCID of the SPPB is considered to be a change of 1 point in patients with COPD,42 and a score≤8 points has been selected for screening for physical frailty in older adults.41

Handgrip strength will be measured in both upper limbs with an adjustable-handled dynamometer (electronic hand dynamometer EH-101, Camry, Hong Kong, China). Three successive trials will be performed in a sitting position with the elbow flexed at 90°.43 For each hand, the highest value will be recorded. In older adults, grip strength is a predictor of mortality, length of hospital stay and sarcopenia.44 45

Burden, anxiety and depressive symptoms, fatigue and social well-being

Informal carers’ burden will be self-assessed using the Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI) (revised version; 22 items, with a total score ranging from 0 to 88; lower scores indicate less burden).46 The ZBI measures subjective burden in terms of the degree (from ‘never=0’ to ‘almost always=4’) to which the informal carer experiences physical, psychological, emotional, social and financial problems as a result of their caregiving role. A ZBI score>24 is considered indicative of a high burden.47

Anxiety and depressive symptoms and general fatigue of the informal carers will be self-assessed with the Hospital Anxiety and Depression (HAD) scale (14 items: 7 each for anxiety and depression, with minimum and maximum subscores of 0 and 21; lower scores are better; an anxiety or depressive symptoms score≥11 indicates a probable clinical diagnosis of anxiety or depression48) and the Fatigue Assessment Scale (FAS) (10 items: 5 reflecting physical fatigue and 5 reflecting mental fatigue with a test score ranging from 10 to 50; lower is better; a score≥22 suggests abnormal fatigue49). In patients with CRDs, the MCID of the HAD and FAS is considered to be a change of 1.5 units50 and 4 points,51 respectively.

Perceived health status will be assessed with a visual analogue scale for overall health status, derived from the EuroQol-5Dimensions-3L questionnaire, ranging from 0 (the worst health imaginable) to 100 (the best health imaginable).52

Socioeconomic deprivation will be assessed using the self-administered Evaluation of Deprivation and Inequalities in Health Centres (EPICES) questionnaire.53 EPICES is a multidimensional questionnaire evaluating social deprivation on a quantitative and continuous scale ranging from 0 (no deprivation) to 100 (maximum deprivation). A score≤30.17 is indicative of social deprivation.53

Qualitative data collection

Individual, in-depth, semistructured interviews will be conducted with informal carers, face-to-face in their homes within 2 weeks after the end of PR. These interviews will be conducted in a quiet, private room, without the patient present, by the same assessor (MRo). Interviews will use open-ended questions and will be audio recorded with the prior consent of each participant. They are expected to last between 30 and 45 min. The interviews will explore informal carers’ expectations of the PR programmes, potential facilitators and barriers to attendance, perceived benefits after PR and their needs and thoughts about future interventions (box 2). The French-language version of the interview guide is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Patients with CRD assessments

In both sites, exercise capacity, health-related quality of life and symptoms of anxiety and depression (HAD) of patients will be assessed at the beginning and end of PR in accordance with their standard routine assessments (figure 1). In the home-based setting, the 6MST and the COPD assessment test (CAT) will be used. CAT measures the impact of COPD on a patient’s daily life (scores range from 0 to 40, with higher scores indicating a greater health burden).54 In the centre-based setting, exercise capacity will be assessed with the 6-min walking test. It will be performed in a 30-m unobstructed corridor according to guidelines55 and total distance travelled will be collected to the nearest metre. Health-related quality of life will be assessed with the self-administered questionnaire (VQ11) (11 items with a test score ranging from 11 to 55; lower is better). A total score≥22 indicates a poor quality of life. The VQ11 subscores are used to identify the presence of physical dependence (functional score≥8), psychological impairment (psychological score≥10) and a social deficit (relational score≥10).56 Exercise capacity and health-related quality of life will be assessed using different tools, depending on the PR setting and their standard clinical practice. As comparing the effects of home-based versus centre-based PR in patients is not an objective of this study, changes in these outcomes will be analysed separately within each group. Additionally, we will report the percentage change in each outcome following PR and the proportion of patients who reach the MCID for each outcome.

Adverse events

For patients with CRDs, the respective PR team will be responsible for managing any adverse events that occur during the home- or centre-based programme, in accordance with usual protocol. All adverse events that might occur during the evaluation of informal carers in their home will be documented on the adverse event log and case report form (CRF). The assessor will receive training on the management protocol for adverse events. An adverse event is defined as any unintentional or unintended clinical sign or symptom during the physical assessment of the informal carer (ie, chest pain, arrhythmia, oxygen desaturation and hypoglycaemia). If such events occur, the evaluation will be stopped immediately so that the informal carer can be given appropriate management, which may include referral to the medical team. Serious adverse events will be reported to the informal carer’s family physician, the medical coordinator (J-MG) and the ethical committee that approved this trial.

Data management

Participant data will be collected on a printed CRF at their home, ensuring that it is stored in physical form. All paper data will be stored securely in locked filing cabinets, within secure rooms at the FormAction Santé office. At the same time, paper data will be anonymised and digitally archived on an online database (REDCap, 2004, Vanderbilt University Medical Center). REDCap will be used for centralised quantitative data collection and management. Two authors (MRo and SG) will continuously monitor the data in real time to address irregularities in a timely manner. Audio-recorded data will be uploaded to the encrypted professional laptop of the main assessor (MRo), immediately deleted from her phone and anonymised during transcription. Data management will be audited every 3 months. Personal information held by the research team will include necessary address and telephone number details. A participant identification key will be held in encrypted files separate from the database. All publicly available data will be anonymised. Data stored on the online database will be retained for a period of 2 years following the final publication related to the project. Additionally, the file containing the participant identification key will be deleted after 15 years.

Quantitative data analysis and presentation

Quantitative data will be imported to IBM SPSS Statistics V.30.0 for analysis with a statistical significance threshold set at p<0.05. Quantitative variables will be expressed as means (SD) in the case of a normal distribution or as medians (IQR) for non-Gaussian distribution, and qualitative variables as numbers and frequencies. Normality of distributions will be assessed using histograms and Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Between-groups baseline characteristics (informal carers enrolled from the home PR vs informal carers enrolled from the centre PR) will be compared using χ2 test for categorical variables and either analysis of variance or the Kruskal–Wallis test for quantitative variables. Linear mixed regression models with a random intercept to account for the correlation between samples obtained within the same participant will be used to evaluate the changes over time (before PR and after PR) in informal carers’ outcomes as well as in patients with CRDs’ outcomes. These analyses will be repeated with adjustments for unbalanced confounding factors between groups, should baseline differences be found. Linear relationships will be explored between the changes in the informal carers’ outcomes after PR and the changes in the patients’ outcomes using Pearson’s correlations. When appropriate, average changes in clinical outcomes will be discussed in relation to the available valid MCIDs. The number of participants (informal carers and patients with CRDs) reaching the MCID of each outcome (when known) will be calculated and presented as frequency (percentage). Missing quantitative data will be imputed using a regression switching approach. However, given that the informal carers’ assessments will be performed within their homes, the incidence of missing data is expected to be minimal.

Qualitative data analysis and presentation

Two authors (MRo and SG) will perform the transcription of deidentified verbatim data. A mixed-methods approach combining deductive and inductive thematic analyses will be applied. Five interviews will be used during the preliminary coding phase to develop the initial thematic framework. The same two authors will separately perform a qualitative content analysis to generate codes and assign categories using ATLAS.ti 9.0 software (Scientific Software Development GmbH, Berlin, Germany). Should discrepancies arise, they will be resolved by a third reviewer (J-MG). As preconceived themes are expected, a deductive thematic analysis will be used57 while retaining flexibility to allow the generation of new themes. A total of 30 informal carers is expected to be interviewed, with 15 from each site. However, the sample size will be increased until data saturation is achieved, if required. The informal carers to be interviewed will be volunteers who have completed the quantitative assessments at the end of PR. Representative citations will be included to support interpretation of identified themes and subthemes. Results from qualitative data analysis will be presented in narrative form, based on key themes established during the analysis, and supplemented by participants’ quotations.

Patient and public involvement

Patients with CRDs have been involved in the design of the home- and centre-based PR programmes at the time of their respective developments. Two informal carers will be invited to contribute to the design of the semistructured interviews prior to data collection and to review the study findings afterwards. The clinical team has selected physical tests and questionnaires that will be performed on informal carers, although no informal carers have been involved in the selection process.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval for the collection of informal carers’ data was obtained (University Sud Mediterranean 3, Ethics Committee number: 24.04596.000457;07/10/2024). In the home PR programme, data collection for patients with CRDs was approved by the observational research protocol evaluation committee of the French-Language Society of Pulmonology (number: 2021–054). In the centre-based PR programme, data collection is a part of routine clinical practice and does not require separate ethical approval. In both settings, all participants will provide a written informed consent prior to enrolment.

Results of the study will be widely disseminated through presentations at local and international scientific conferences and seminars targeting both clinicians and academics. Trial results will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals. A day-long programme of education and awareness on informal carers’ health will be organised in collaboration with representatives of various stakeholder groups, including patients, informal carers, physicians and associations. Results of the study will be formally presented to local and national decision makers, including representatives from the Regional Health Agency (ARS) and the French Ministry of Health. Media channels of the association Santé Respiratoire France, which includes over 5000 patients, informal carers and professionals, will also be used.

Discussion

The PRHOCARER study will be the first multicentre, parallel, mixed-methods study using a before-and-after intervention design to evaluate changes in physical capacity, burden, anxiety and depressive symptoms, fatigue, quality of life and social well-being among informal carers of patients with CRDs, following either a home- or a centre-based PR programme. This study will provide valuable additional insights into the impact of caregiving on informal carers’ physical, psychological and social health. Findings are expected to highlight significant health challenges faced by informal carers, reinforcing the urgent need for policymakers to enhance support for this essential yet often overlooked population, on whom the healthcare system relies heavily.

All assessments will be performed in the informal carers’ own homes, an approach particularly beneficial for those facing mobility limitations whether due to physical impairments, socioeconomic hardship, caregiving responsibilities or emotional constraints, such as guilt about leaving the patient alone.17 In addition to facilitating data collection, these individual face-to-face home visits offer a unique opportunity to raise awareness and provide personalised health recommendations to informal carers.

This trial will also explore whether informal carers are willing to be integrated into PR programmes and whether such integration leads to measurable improvements in their physical, psychological and social well-being. We will investigate potential correlations between changes in patient outcomes and corresponding changes in informal carers’ outcomes following PR. Should the findings demonstrate that including informal carers in PR programmes addresses a real need and enhances both patient and informal carers health, the study will offer compelling evidence to advocate for stronger policy support for informal carers of patients with CRDs.

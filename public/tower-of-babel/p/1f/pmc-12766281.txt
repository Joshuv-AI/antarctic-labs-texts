Restoring dendritic cell interstitial motility via PDE5 inhibition for sustained antitumor immunity
Authors: Ruichen Zang, Chen Zhang, Guoqing Ding, Jie Zhang
Published: 2025

In a recent publication in Nature, Zhou and colleagues identified cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP), a canonical phosphodiesterase 5 (PDE5) substrate, as a key modulator of dendritic cell (DC) interstitial motility through Rho kinase (ROCK)-dependent modulation of myosin-II activity.1 This finding suggests a novel and promising therapeutic strategy, as pharmacological inhibition of PDE5 through sildenafil restored migratory conventional DCs (mig-cDCs) homing to tumor draining lymph nodes (tdLNs) and antitumor effect. Notably, combination therapy with sildenafil and anti-PD-1 blockade demonstrates synergistic antitumor efficacy, surpassing monotherapy outcomes.

Functioning as the sentinel of the immune system, DCs mainly sample, process and present antigens to T cells. A specialized subset of DCs called mig-cDCs in tumor microenvironment (TME) traverse tissues and emigrate through lymphatic vessels into tdLNs, where they cooperate with LN-resident cDCs to initiate robust tumor-specific T cell priming.2 The process of migration is driven by the actin-myosin complex and small GTPases, with spatiotemporal polarization of actin polymerization and myosin-II contractility establishing front-rear asymmetry.3 However, how DC interstitial motility is affected by TME and its impact on anti-tumor immunity remains unresolved.

Zhou and colleagues revealed that mature DCs significantly decreased in tdLNs from patients with late-stage tumor, which strongly compromised tumor-specific T cell priming and proliferation.1 A two-stage CRISPR screening strategy was then employed to investigate how the TME impaired DC interstitial motility and subsequent trafficking to tdLNs. At the first stage, a preliminary pre-round screen of genome-wide sgRNA library using injection with bone-marrow-derived DCs (BMDCs) differentiated ex vivo from lineage-negative bone marrow cells of Cas9-eGFP mice was adopted, which aimed to downsize the library and exclude genes that might impair DC development or viability. At the second stage, a definitive major-round screen of selected-minipool sgRNA library using BM reconstitution with hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) from Zbtb46creCas9LSL donor mice was adopted, which better recapitulated DC ontogeny and trafficking within physiological condition and avoided technical artifacts associated with ex vivo BMDCs differentiation procedures. Pde5 emerged as the top hit from both stage of screening, with ex vivo ear explants and 3D collagen gel system models further validating PDE5 as a negative regulator of DC interstitial motility.

Mechanically, PDE5 selectively hydrolyzed cGMP, while cGMP enhanced the phosphorylation of the myosin light chain and myosin-II activity through the RhoA-ROCK-myosin light chain kinase (MLCK) pathway, which rendered contraction of actin bundles. Although Pde5 expression was unchanged between early- and late-stage tumoral DCs, significant downregulation of soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC), which catalyzes the conversion of guanosine triphosphate (GTP) into cGMP, was observed in DCs from late-stage tumors. In fact, nitric oxide synthase-2 (NOS2) expression substantially decreased as tumors advanced from early to late stage, and the reduction of NO biosynthesis led to the downregulation of sGC. Focusing on cGMP, the classical PDE5 inhibitor sildenafil was utilized to prevent cGMP from hydrolysis. Indeed, sildenafil treatment significantly increased the migration velocity and persistence of DCs, allowing them to migrate to tdLNs and foster T cell priming and activation, resulting in pronounced tumor control and prolonged survival in tumor-bearing mice. Notably, combined use of sildenafil and anti-PD-1 demonstrated a markedly superior therapeutic efficacy compared to monotherapy, with 80 % of the combined-treatment mice bearing MC38-Pde5−/− tumors cured, compared with 40 % of mice treated with anti-PD-1 and 10 % of mice treated with sildenafil alone, highlighting the promising strategy to simultaneously revitalize both DCs and T cell.

TME has been proved to suppress DC function in manifold ways. Hypoxia, a hallmark characteristic of TME, compromises DC function by attenuating antigen capture, processing and presentation capacity through hypoxia-inducible factor-α (HIF-α) upregulation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation.4 Tumor-derived transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) also impairs DC immunological function by mediating prosaposin hyperglycosylation.5 In this study, researchers demonstrated that NO generated by tumor-derived NOS2 could modulate DC interstitial motility and homing to tdLNs, offering a novel dimension on how TME regulates DCs. As the catalytic product of NO-induced sGC, cGMP further promoted DC traction by enhancing RhoA-ROCK-MLCK pathway. PKG, a canonical cGMP effector, could regulate RhoA either directly by phosphorylating RhoA or indirectly by phosphorylating RhoA associated factors like Myo9b and GEF-H1.6 Nevertheless, given the minimal expression of PKG in DCs, alternative molecules mediating cGMP-dependent RhoA regulation should exist. In this study, researchers identified multiple guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) and GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) that bound with cGMP, implying their potential involvement in mediating the cGMP-driven RhoA activation.

PDE5 inhibitors, a class of compounds that impede cGMP hydrolysis, were originally developed to treat cardiovascular disease, but was accidentally found to be effective in treating male erectile dysfunction,7 and accumulating clinical evidence has suggested that PDE5 inhibitors have potential anti-tumor effect.8 In fact, the antitumor effect of PDE5 inhibitors result from the integration of multifaceted immunomodulatory factors. On one hand, PDE5 inhibitors could enhance immune response by improving DC interstitial motility,1 increasing the proportion of splenic cytotoxic T lymphocyte and T-helper cell while decreasing that of splenic regulatory T cells,9 and also reducing myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC) infiltration and immunosuppressive potency.8 On the other hand, PDE5 inhibitors could induce macrophage shifting from classic (M1-like) to alternative (M2-like)/TEM phenotype,10 a polarization pattern that is recognized as immunosuppressive. Fortunately, PDE5 inhibitors appear to predominantly exert anti-tumor effect in clinical practice. In a prospective study of patients with head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, PDE5 inhibitor tadalafil's immunomodulatory activity was observed to intensify in a dose-dependent manner, with maximal response attainment observed at 225 μg/kg.11 While another study of patients with metastatic melanoma showed that although tadalafil was clinically effective, neither plasma nor tissue tadalafil concentrations correlated with the clinical response.12 The inconsistence could be attributed to tumor heterogeneity in terms of histological type and progression stage. However, these findings underscore the critical need for further investigation into both optimal dosing parameter and delivery approach to maximize therapeutic efficacy of PDE5 inhibitors, for current preclinical or clinical studies mainly adopts oral or intraperitoneal administration routes,13,14 while local delivery approaches remain unexplored, despite their potential therapeutic advantages. Furthermore, how DC migration responds to PDE5 inhibitor treatment in human tumor samples still warrants systematic investigation, as existing evidence mainly characterizes the response of T cell and MDSC.

PDE5 inhibitors primarily modulate DC interstitial motility without significantly influencing antigen presentation or costimulatory function, which offers the potential of combining cGMP-mediated restoration with agents that enhance DC maturation or unleash T cell suppression to yield synergistic effects. In this study, researchers combined sildenafil with anti-PD-1, which could reverse T cells exhaustion, and observed robust therapeutic effect. Further investigation could explore the combination with PRR/STING agonists like BCG or cGAMP-VLP,2 as well as DC vaccines.15 Novel biomaterial is another choice of interest. Dual catalytic oxide nanosponge, for example, could increase the release and delivery of tumor-associated antigens (TAA),16 and TP-SL@PB could enhance the delivery of both TAA to DCs and PD-L1 siRNA to tumor cells.17 Therefore, combination use of these biomaterial-based approaches with PDE5 inhibitors could probably potentiate stronger and longer immune activation.

Last but not the least, the off-target effect of PDE5 inhibitors should be given due consideration. Preclinical evidence indicates that at a large dosage, PDE5 inhibitors could exhibit cross-reactivity with PDE11, an enzyme expressed on monocytes and T cells which enhances immune activation, thus off-target inhibition of PDE11 by PDE5 inhibitor would negatively modulate immune response.11 Moreover, PDE5 inhibitors could elicit immune-irrelevant effects, such as penile erection and symptomatic hypotension.7 All these pharmacological profiles of PDE5 inhibitors necessitate comprehensive evaluation prior to repurposing PDE5 inhibitors for oncological indications.

To conclude, this study adopted rigorous methodology to systematically elucidate how TME promotes immune evasion via impairing DC interstitial motility and homing to tdLNs, repurposing the established pharmacological agent sildenafil for oncological indications and offering a promising direction for synergistic integration of DC-centric immunotherapy with other therapies. Further mechanistic investigations and clinical trials are still warranted to optimize the therapeutic application of sildenafil in oncological contexts.

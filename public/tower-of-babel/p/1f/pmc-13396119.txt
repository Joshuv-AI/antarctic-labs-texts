Cervical ripening with mechanical method vs oral misoprostol in pregnancies with elevated BMI: a cohort study
Authors: Maria Castoldi, Florent Fuchs, Philippe Deruelle, Giulia Maria Scotti, Massimo Candiani, Paolo Ivo Cavoretto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo compare labour duration and the effectiveness of cervical ripening by transcervical balloon versus oral misoprostol in women with elevated body mass index (BMI ≥ 25 kg/m2).MethodsA retrospective single-centre cohort (2022–2024) at Montpellier University Hospital including singleton term pregnancies (37–42 weeks) with cephalic presentation, BMI > 25 and Bishop score < 6. Mechanical balloon versus oral misoprostol (25 µg/2 h or 50 µg/4 h) were compared by survival analysis assessing induction-to-delivery interval and by logistic regression assessing vaginal delivery rates, all adjusting for key covariates. Exclusion criteria: maternal age < 18 years, scarred uterus, low-lying placenta.ResultsAmong 7336 births, 2225 (30.3%) underwent labour induction, of which 247 met inclusion criteria. Vaginal delivery occurred in 180/247 (72.9%), of which 21/180(11.7%) were operative vaginal, caesareans were 67/247(27.1%), with no statistically significant difference observed; the study was not powered to assess equivalence of caesarean rates. Balloons achieved shorter induction-to-delivery (Mean 25.5 h; 95%CI 23.8–27.2 h vs. 35.3 h; 95%CI 33.0–37.6 h; p < 0.001), and induction-to-active-phase intervals vs. misoprostol (Mean 21.1 h; SD 12.8 h vs. 30.3 h; SD 17.7 h; p < 0.001), without difference in first active-stage and second-stage duration or among misoprostol regimens. Cox regression identified predictors of longer induction-to-delivery: nulliparity (HR:0.31; 95%CI 0.22–0.42), misoprostol (HR:0.46; 95%CI 0.33–0.66) vs intrauterine balloons, LGA (HR:0.63; 95%CI 0.43–0.92), higher BMI (HR:0.97; 95%CI 0.97–1.00), and excessive weight gain (HR:0.98; 95%CI 0.95–1.00). Treatment-by-parity and treatment-by-Bishop score interactions were not significant. A Fine and Gray competing-risks model showed that mechanical ripening vs. misoprostol provided shorter time intervals from induction to delivery (p = 0.033) and to active phase (p = 0.003). After one cycle of ripening, oxytocin infusion was associated with a higher rate of vaginal birth compared with repeated ripening (sHR 2.28, 95%CI 1.49–3.49). PPH or adverse neonatal outcomes were not different.ConclusionsIn a high-BMI population mechanical cervical ripening vs. oral misoprostol show similar effectiveness, with the first associated with a shorter duration of labour induction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00404-026-08458-4.Keywords: Induction of labour, Cervical ripening, Balloon, Misoprostol, Body mass index, Obesity

What does this study adds to the clinical work?

In pregnancies with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥25 kg/m²), mechanical cervical ripening significantly shortened the induction-to-delivery and induction to active phase intervals compared with oral misoprostol, without affecting vaginal delivery rates or maternal and neonatal adverse outcomes, and this advantage was independent of parity and Bishop score.Higher BMI, nulliparity, and large-for-gestational-age fetuses were independent predictors of a longer induction-to-delivery interval.After one cycle of ripening, oxytocin infusion was associated with a higher rate of vaginal birth compared with repeated ripening.

In pregnancies with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥25 kg/m²), mechanical cervical ripening significantly shortened the induction-to-delivery and induction to active phase intervals compared with oral misoprostol, without affecting vaginal delivery rates or maternal and neonatal adverse outcomes, and this advantage was independent of parity and Bishop score.

Introduction

Over the past several decades, the incidence of labour induction (IOL) for planned delivery has continued to rise, involving today up to one third of term births in high-income settings [1] Although generally considered safe, IOL may be associated with longer labour, increased analgesia use and lower maternal satisfaction, particularly when cervical ripening is required [2, 3]. Pregnancies with increased gestational weight gain, overweight and obesity often require birth planning by IOL due to a higher incidence of pre-existing comorbidities and pregnancy complications [4–6]. However, IOL in women with high BMI presents significant challenges. As BMI increases, cervical ripening can be considerably prolonged and there is insufficient evidence to recommend one method over another [7]. The induction-to-delivery interval tends to be longer, with patients often requiring multiple doses of prostaglandins, prolonged oxytocin infusions, a longer time-to-delivery interval, thus facing a higher risk of caesarean delivery [8, 9].

A systematic review comparing IOL using low dose of oral misoprostol or mechanical balloon showed higher rates of vaginal birth in 24 h and patient satisfaction, lower rates of caesarean deliveries and oxytocin augmentation in the oral misoprostol group, with some evidence of improved safety with balloons. [10] Mechanical balloons are widely used due to low rates of uterine hyperstimulation and favourable safety profile, while two-hourly or four-hourly oral misoprostol regimens remain common and equally effective, despite pharmacokinetic data suggesting BMI may influence drug effectiveness. [11–13] This study was undertaken in response to the lack of targeted and conclusive evidence on induction of labor in women with high BMI.

Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to compare the induction-to-delivery of IOL by oral misoprostol (25 and 50 µg regimens) vs. mechanical method with a balloon in high-BMI women (≥ 25 kg/m2, aligned with WHO classification) with singleton pregnancies with an unfavourable Bishop Score.

Secondary objectives were to compare IOL with misoprostol vs. mechanical methods in terms of effectiveness (rate of vaginal deliveries), timing to achieve labour and other obstetric and perinatal outcomes such as operative vaginal deliveries, adverse foetal outcomes, post-partum haemorrhage (PPH), tachysystole, need for oxytocin administration, neuraxial analgesia, and multiple cycles of cervical ripening.

Methods

Study design, setting and participants

This is a retrospective observational single-centre cohort study conducted in Centre Hospitalier Universitaire de Montpellier (France), which included all patients with an initial BMI ≥ 25, a singleton pregnancy in cephalic presentation at term (37 to 42 weeks), with intact membranes and an unfavourable Bishop score (≤ 6), who underwent cervical ripening between September 2022 and September 2024. Exclusion criteria were age < 18 years, scarred uterus, multiple pregnancies, breech presentation, low-lying placenta (< 20 mm from internal os), prelabour rupture of membranes, spontaneous onset of labour. The study was presented in agreement to the Strengthening the reporting of observational studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines [14].

Variables such as age, type of conception (natural or medically assisted), gestational age, obstetrical and medical comorbidities were considered. No distinction was made between active smoking and smoking cessation, and they were all categorized as history of tobacco use. Autoimmune disorders, such as Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, Antiphospholipid Syndrome, Rheumatoid Arthritis and Ankylosing Spondylitis were analysed as a combined group.

Procedures of labour induction

Procedures of IOL were carried out based on individual physician choice and based on local protocol as well as on international guidelines. (2) Two cervical ripening methods were analysed in this study: (1) Oral prostaglandins, both the 50 µg Misoprostol regimen administered every 4 h, and the 25 µg Misoprostol regimen administered every 2 h (both with a maximum daily prostaglandin dose of 200 µg), and (2) a mechanical method, involving the transcervical application of a Cervical Ripening Balloon (20-Fr Dufour; Coloplast A/S, Humlebæk, Denmark) inflated with 50–60 mL of sterile saline solution, and maintained in place for at least 12 h. No nocturnal pauses were applied. The cervical ripening method was chosen according to the clinician’s discretion and favoured in cases of small for gestational age (SGA) foetuses.

In case of unfavourable cervical maturation, cervical ripening could be repeated, after 24 h from the first cycle with a maximum of two subsequent cycles of oral prostaglandins allowed, followed or preceded by a mechanical method. Oxytocin was administered either for the prosecution of IOL or for labour augmentation, according to guidelines and individual operator choice, 2 h after the removal of the cervical ripening balloon and 4 h after the last tablet of misoprostol. No nocturnal pause was applied.

Neuraxial anaesthesia was offered in the presence of painful uterine contractions or prior to oxytocin infusion, as per the patients’ request. Intrapartum tocolysis for uterine hypertonia or hyperkinesia was performed with intravenous nitroglycerine 100–300 µg.

Variables and outcomes definitions.

The onset of the active phase of the first stage of labour was established when a dilation of at least 5 cm with regular uterine contractions was reached [15]. The second stage of labour was defined from complete cervical dilation and its duration was measured in hours from diagnosis to delivery. The induction-to-delivery interval was measured in hours from onset of IOL to delivery, induction to active phase interval was measured in hours from onset of IOL to diagnosis of active phase of labour. Post-partum haemorrhage (PPH) refers to a blood loss ≥ 500 mL in both caesarean and vaginal delivery. Indications to IOL are shown in Supplemental Table S1.

IOL failure was stated when the active phase of labour was not achieved after at least 12 h of oxytocin perfusion with ruptured membranes [16].

Failure to progress was defined according to Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists and French Guidelines (HAS 2017) for both I and II stage of labour [17].

Foetal cardiotocography was described using the FIGO 2015 classification [18]. Adverse foetal outcomes’ definition followed the executive summary criteria published in 2014 by ACOG: an arterial pH < 7 or an Apgar score < 5 at 5 and 10 min [19].

Risk of bias

The risk of bias was due to the retrospective nature of the study and the possibility of multiple cycles of cervical ripening. The first problem was minimized by the recruitment of all eligible consecutive cases at an individual centre. The latter aspect represents both a limitation and a strength, as the use of multiple cervical ripening cycles with these methods reflects a common clinical practice, making the study population highly representative of real-world hospital settings. Clinical management was indicated by a unique local clinical protocol in all cases. There were no missing data.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software, version 4.3.0 and SAS Enterprise Guide version 7.13 software. Statistical significance was generally defined as p-values below the alpha threshold of 0.05.

Fisher's exact test and Chi-squared test were used to assess the independence between pairs of categorical variables. For comparing the distributions of continuous variables between two groups, the Mann–Whitney U test was employed, as implemented in the 'wilcox.test' R function. To compare continuous variables across three or more groups, the Kruskal–Wallis non-parametric test was applied. When a significant result was found, post-hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted to identify which groups differed from the others. No adjustment for multiplicity was applied to the primary outcome. Bonferroni correction was used for the two prespecified time-interval comparisons and for post-hoc pairwise analyses following significant overall tests. Secondary and subgroup analyses were considered exploratory and were not formally adjusted for multiple testing.

Survival analysis was carried out with Kaplan–Meyer estimator comparing the two groups of induction with differences among curves tested by the Log-Rank test. No obvious violation of the proportional risk assumption was detected by the Schoenfeld test (p = 0.24). Therefore, a Cox regression model was fitted including a series of relevant covariates, with study of interaction of significant variables.

In addition, a Fine and Gray competing-risks model was applied employing vaginal birth and caesarean delivery as competing events. The analysis was applied to the induction of labor (IoL)-to-delivery interval and the IoL-to-active phase of the first stage of labor interval. Furthermore, a separate competing-risks analysis was performed in women with an unfavourable Bishop score (< 6) after one cycle of cervical ripening, comparing those who received oxytocin infusion with those who underwent additional cycles of ripening.

Multivariate linear and logistic regression models were fitted utilizing the 'glm' function from the 'stats' R package. Various combinations of explanatory variables were tested for each model. For linear regressions, a minimum of 5 observations per estimated parameter were required, while for logistic regressions, at least 10 observations per parameter in the smaller group were ensured to avoid overfitting. Final models were selected based on the lowest Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) score and/or the inclusion of the most clinically relevant variables. A 95% confidence interval was calculated for each covariate included in the models using the 'confint' function.

Results

Participants, descriptive and outcome data

Of 7,336 births during the study period, 2,225 (30.3%) underwent IOL, for various medical indications (Supplemental Fig. 1). Of these, 247 met the inclusion criteria and were divided into 2 groups according to the cervical ripening method chosen as first approach and categorized in four BMI subgroups (Fig. 1). Demographic characteristics of the study groups and stratification according to maternal obstetrical and medical history and foetal characteristics are shown in Table 1. The comparison of baseline demographic characteristics across the two groups of cervical ripening revealed no significant differences, except for the distribution of overweight patients and SGA foetuses, which were more pronounced in the mechanical cervical ripening group, and BMI at term, which was slightly higher in the misoprostol group. In addition, the initial BS was found to be higher in the mechanical vs pharmacological ripening groups. Main indications for IOL, represented in Supplemental Table S1, were foetal macrosomia, post-term and reduced foetal movements. There was no imbalance in the indications for IOL across the three groups (overall Chi-squared test to assess homogeneity: p 0,191).

Secondary outcomes

Pregnancy outcomes in study groups are described in Table 4. No difference was found in cumulative incidence of caesarean delivery among women induced with misoprostol vs. balloon (28.7%vs.23.9%; p = 0.42) or in indications among all arms of cervical ripening. There was a higher incidence of caesarean delivery in women with a BMI > 35 vs. all others (p = 0.024). Onset of labour without oxytocin perfusion was significantly more prevalent among the oral prostaglandins’ arms. In total, 26% of all patients who underwent a Ist cycle of cervical ripening entered labor, and of the 69 patients who received a second cycle, 13% entered labor. Of notice, an onset of labor without oxytocin perfusion was not observed in any arm after a third cycle of cervical ripening.

Epidural analgesia was administered to the vast majority of patients in all the considered groups. No difference was observed in PPH among the two arms of cervical ripening, though a longer duration of labor was associated with a higher risk of bleeding (Table 4; Supplemental Table S3).

Multivariate logistic regression models were used to confirm the equivalence of the methods in achieving vaginal birth (Supplemental Table S4) and the superiority of prostaglandins for entering labour after adjustment for potential confounders (Supplemental Table S5).

No patient achieved labour onset following a third ripening cycle.

We also observed that after one ripening cycle, oxytocin infusion (modelled as a binary variable yes vs. no) in women with an unfavourable cervix (Bishop score < 6 after) yielded higher vaginal birth rates than repeated ripening cycles (Fisher test p-value = 0.035). This result was explored with a Fine and Gray subdistribution hazard model for competing risks, where vaginal births and caesarean deliveries were the competing events. Oxytocin administration after one cycle of cervical ripening was associated with a significantly higher cumulative incidence of vaginal birth as compared to multiple cycles of cervical ripening (sHR 2.28, 95% CI 1.49–3.49, p < 0.001).

There were no maternal-foetal safety issues in the study groups. Cesarean delivery for fetal compromise occurred more frequently in the misoprostol group than in the balloon group (17/157 [10.8%] vs 7/90 [7.8%]); however, this difference did not reach statistical significance (relative risk 1.39, 95% CI 0.58–3.35; p = 0.42), and the comparison is likely underpowered.

Discussion

Principal findings

In this observational cohort of women with elevated BMI undergoing term labour induction, mechanical cervical ripening was associated with significantly shorter induction-to-delivery and induction-to-active-phase intervals compared with oral misoprostol, regardless of parity, Bishop score and other intrapartum outcomes, particularly without differences in birth mode, maternal morbidity or neonatal outcomes. No benefit of repeated cervical ripening cycles was observed as compared to oxytocin induction after one ripening.

Interpretation in light of other evidence

Few studies have evaluated cervical ripening strategies specifically in women with overweight or obesity, despite the high prevalence of IOL in this population. Previous data indicate that increasing BMI prolongs induction duration and increases caesarean risk, partly due to slower cervical ripening and prolonged latent phase[6, 20]. Our findings align with these observations and suggest that mechanical methods may attenuate this delay.

A 2023 Cochrane review in the general obstetric population found a modest advantage of low-dose oral misoprostol compared with the Foley catheter in achieving vaginal birth within 24 h and in reducing the need for oxytocin, although heterogeneity in dosing regimens and catheter volumes limited the feasibility of detailed subgroup analyses [10] Notably, most included studies used a 30–40 mL balloon volume, whereas our cohort employed 60 mL balloons, as larger volumes have been proven superior in shortening cervical ripening time [21].

Prior retrospective cohorts in overweight or obese nulliparas found no differences of outcomes after induction with misoprostol, dinoprostone and Foley catheter, although balloon volumes and drug dosing were inconsistently reported [22, 23]. Pharmacokinetic studies in obese patients demonstrated lower misoprostol circulating concentrations, potentially reducing efficacy [24]. The two pharmacological protocols evaluated in our study reflect total daily ceiling doses commonly used in many European units and focus exclusively on women with a BMI ≥ 25, thereby providing complementary insight. The 25-µg misoprostol protocol was selected based on pharmacokinetic evidence, whereas the 50-µg protocol was chosen for its greater practicality, particularly during overnight induction of labor, allowing maternal rest before the onset of active labor [25] Our study merged the two arms of oral misoprostol (25 and 50) as many previous reports show no significant difference between the regimens [26, 27].

Concerning our secondary results, caesarean delivery rates did not differ among the two arms of cervical ripening, but were found to be significantly superior in Class III obese patients, as already reported by other authors [28–30]. This result is in contrast with a recent secondary analysis of PROBAAT trial, which suggested higher caesarean delivery rates in obese women undergoing induction with Foley catheter versus oral misoprostol, though balloon volumes were limited to 30 mL and induction protocols differed, limiting comparability [31]. Onset of labour without oxytocin infusion occurred more frequently in the oral prostaglandins arm, as expected and consistent with existing literature [10, 28]. The absence of differences in post-partum haemorrhage rates between cervical ripening methods is consistent with findings previously reported in the literature [31]. Finally, among women with an unfavourable cervix after one cycle of cervical ripening, proceeding directly to oxytocin infusion was associated with higher vaginal birth rates than repeating additional ripening cycles, as shown before by previous research [32, 33]. If confirmed, these findings could influence clinical practice by reducing hospital stay, potentially improving the childbirth experience and lowering healthcare costs [34].

Clinical and research implications

From a counselling perspective, the shorter induction duration associated with mechanical balloons may improve patient experience and resource utilization without compromising safety. As caesarean risk appears to be more strongly associated with obesity severity and nulliparity than with the ripening modality itself, the choice of cervical ripening may reasonably prioritize efficiency.

Mechanical balloons may shorten induction duration in women with overweight or obesity without increasing operative birth risk. Greater induction efficiency may improve patient satisfaction and optimize resource allocation and bed utilisation, particularly in high-volume units. Proceeding to oxytocin after a single ripening cycle may be more effective than repeating additional cycles, warranting prospective evaluation.

Given the pharmacokinetic uncertainty in overweight and obesity, misoprostol dosing strategies in this population warrant systematic investigation. Larger studies are needed to confirm these findings, including analyses stratified by obesity class and evaluation of higher-dose regimens. However, any dose escalation should be approached cautiously, as recent data indicate that low-dose vaginal misoprostol is associated with fewer composite adverse maternal outcomes, underscoring the importance of avoiding overdosing [35].

Overweight and obesity in women with underlying placental dysfunction increase susceptibility to acute intrapartum events, including fetal distress [36–38]. Escalation of misoprostol dosing should, therefore, be undertaken cautiously, given the challenges in assessing uterine contractility in obese patients and the higher prevalence of placental dysfunction. Mechanical methods may represent a more efficient option compared to prostaglandins in this population.

Strengths and limitations

The strength of our study was related to comprehensive data capture, standardized clinical protocols, and survival modelling of labour duration. The primary outcome estimates are accompanied by sufficiently narrow confidence intervals, indicating good precision and strengthening the interpretability of the results. Finally, the study addresses a timely and emerging issue in light of the epidemiological increase in overweight and obesity during pregnancy.

Limitations include the retrospective observational design, confounding by indication, clinician-driven selection of induction method, allocation driven by fetal condition (SGA), retrospective assessment of the Bishop score, and limited power to evaluate neonatal outcomes. Although the imbalance in SGA and Bishop score between groups was addressed through statistical adjustment, residual confounding is likely to persist and may have affected the observed results, even if neither variable showed a significant association with labor duration in the present analysis. The single-centre design, while limiting generalisability, ensures highly homogeneous clinical practice, which becomes a further strength.

Conclusion

Mechanical cervical ripening was associated with a shorter induction-to-delivery interval compared with oral prostaglandins in overweight women, albeit with higher oxytocin requirements. After one cycle of ripening, oxytocin infusion was associated with a higher rate of vaginal birth compared with repeated ripening. These findings should be incorporated into patient counselling and considered during shared decision-making. Vaginal birth rates remained comparable across induction methods, including in subgroup analyses.

While confirmation from larger prospective studies in high-BMI populations is warranted, the shorter induction-to-delivery interval observed with mechanical methods may offer different advantages. It has the potential to enhance the childbirth experience through improved patient satisfaction, to reduce healthcare costs by shortening hospital stays and bed occupancy, and to enable more efficient use of resources, factors that may influence clinician choice in high-volume or resource-limited settings and finally to expose vulnerable fetuses to a shorter duration of labour. This approach also holds the potential, based on biological and clinical plausibility, to improve neonatal outcomes, a hypothesis that warrants confirmation in future research.

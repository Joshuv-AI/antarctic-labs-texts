The Role of NLR and Ferritin Levels as Biomarkers for Corticosteroid Dosage in Children with Refractory Mycoplasma Pneumoniae Pneumonia
Authors: Caili Guo, Yibing Cheng, Chunlan Song, Xiaowen Wang, Yuanzhe Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aims to explore the relationship between RMPP biomarkers and the dose of glucocorticoids.Patients and MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 432 children with refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) who visited the Children’s Hospital from January 2023 to December 2024. According to the dose of methylprednisolone, it was divided into three groups: low-dose, medium-dose and high-dose. We evaluated the basic characteristics, laboratory results and imaging manifestations of the patients.ResultsThe median age and body mass index (BMI) of patients in the high-dose group were the lowest, which were 6.5 years (5, 8.25) and 14.25 years (13.28, 15.23), respectively. The imaging manifestations were more obvious in the high-dose group. The hospitalization time was the longest and the hospitalization cost was the highest (P<0.05). Furthermore, in the high-dose RMPP cohort, White blood cell count (WBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), creatine kinase MB (CKMB), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), fibrinogen, The levels of D-dimer, ferritin, procalcitonin (PCT) and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) were all significantly increased (P<0.05). The dose of glucocorticoids was positively correlated with the length of hospital stay, BMI, WBC, CRP, PCT, LDH, ALT, NLR, ESR, D-dimer and ferritin levels. But there was no significant correlation with age. The correlation coefficients of NLR, ferritin and the dose of methylprednisolone were the highest, with Spearman ρ values of 0.451 and 0.592 respectively (P<0.001).ConclusionNLR and ferritin levels may be useful biomarkers for determining the appropriate dose of corticosteroids in children with RMPP.Keywords: Mycoplasma pneumoniae, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), ferritin, glucocorticoid, children

Introduction

The important pathogen of community-acquired pneumonia in children is Mycoplasma (MP). Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MPP) is generally a mild condition, and the treatment with macrolide drugs is usually effective.1 However, the emergence of complications and increasing resistance to macrolide antibiotics may lead to certain infections progressing to more difficult-to-treat or severe pneumonia, posing significant health risks.2,3 Studies have found that children’s immune systems are not yet fully developed, and the severity of MP infection is related to pathogen characteristics, host factors, etc. Therefore, the administration of immunosuppressive therapy and effective antibiotic treatment may have certain therapeutic effects on RMPP in children.4,5 Early studies have indicated that corticosteroids may mitigate MP infection by modulating abnormal immune responses, potentially providing significant benefits to RMPP patients. However, clear guidelines regarding the most effective treatment remain lacking.6

Numerous studies have identified a variety of biomarkers related to the disease severity of refractory RMPP, including C-reactive protein (CRP), D-dimer, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and ferritin (FER).7–9 It is notable that a high level of D-dimer is related to the disease severity of MPP. Furthermore, serum concentrations of LDH and FER are reliable indicators for assessing disease severity and are crucial in determining corticosteroid treatment strategies for children with RMPP.10 Although biomarkers for the severity of MPP have been identified, the relationship between these markers and the selection of steroid doses has not been fully investigated.

The NLR has recently gained attention as a promising biomarker, increasingly applied in research concerning inflammatory diseases. However, its significance in relation to RMPP has yet to be thoroughly investigated. Existing literature indicates that NLR may act as a more dependable predictor of clinical outcomes in individuals undergoing systemic inflammation.11

Therefore, this study mainly explores the effects of different doses of glucocorticoids on various biomarkers in children, providing insights for strengthening the management of glucocorticoid treatment in clinical practice.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

We collected the clinical data of patients diagnosed with MPP and hospitalized at Henan Children’s Hospital from January 2023 to December 2024 and adopted a retrospective analysis method.We began this study in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration revised in 2013.

The diagnostic framework for bronchopneumonia mainly includes the following specific criteria: (1) Assessing whether the patient exhibits clinical symptoms, signs, or imaging abnormalities during hospitalization that indicate the presence of pneumonia; (2) MPP infection can be confirmed if any of the following conditions are met: a single serum sample shows an anti-Mycoplasma pneumoniae IgM titer greater than or equal to 1:160, the anti-Mycoplasma pneumoniae IgM titer increases fourfold between the acute and recovery phases, or the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) test result for MPP is positive.12 In cases of RMPP, the patient’s clinical condition continues to deteriorate within 7 days despite appropriate antibiotic treatment, accompanied by persistent fever and visible pulmonary changes on imaging.

The inclusion criteria for this study are: (1) patients who meet the diagnostic criteria for RMPP; (2) those who have been treated with corticosteroids; (3) must have complete hospitalization records.2,13

In this study, the exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) presence of co-infection with other pathogenic microorganisms; (2) desire to be discharged within 24 hours; (3) lack of complete medical history; (4) use of corticosteroids prior to hospitalization; (5) Underlying diseases such as interstitial lung disease, asthma and congenital bronchial dysplasia treated with corticosteroids; (6) history of asthma, chronic heart or kidney disease, rheumatic diseases, or immunodeficiency.

Study Design

Based on previous research and clinical results, We systematically divided 432 participants into three groups: the low-dose group (n = 232, with a daily dose of ≤2mg /kg), the medium-dose group (n = 178, with a daily dose of ≥3mg /kg to ≤9mg /kg), and the high-dose group (n = 22, with a daily dose of ≥10mg /kg).

All patients diagnosed with MPP received intravenous azithromycin at a daily dose of 10 mg/kg. If no clinical improvement was observed after three days, or if pneumonia symptoms worsened, we switched MPP patients resistant to macrolides to doxycycline at a dose of 2 mg/kg every two days. Furthermore, patients diagnosed with RMPP initially received intravenous injection of methylprednisolone at a dose of less than or equal to 2 milligrams.

If fever persisted or symptoms did not improve within 24 hours after starting steroid treatment, We administered an increased dose of methylprednisolone until the maximum dose of 30mg /kg/ day was reached. Then, the patients were divided into high, medium and low groups according to the specific dosage of methylprednisolone. A decrease in body temperature within 24 hours after each treatment adjustment, accompanied by clinical symptom improvement or lung imaging improvement, was considered a positive sign of response to steroid treatment.After the condition stabilizes for 48 to 72 hours, methylprednisolone is gradually reduced in dosage until it is discontinued. Finally, no patient died and all patients recovered and were discharged from the hospital.

Ethical Statement

This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Henan Children’s Hospital (Approval Number: 2024–067), and has followed the ethical principles of the Helsinki Declaration. This study is a retrospective study and has obtained the exemption for informed consent from the ethics committee.

Data Collection

Clinical data from subjects were collected within 24 hours of being diagnosed with resistant RMPP. These data covered several key components: (1) The basic characteristics of the data population, including age, gender and body mass index (BMI); (2) laboratory assessments including white blood cell count (WBC),CRP, neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio (NLR), procalcitonin (PCT), creatine kinase MB (CK-MB), LDH, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), FER, fibrinogen (FIB), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), and D-dimer levels; (3) chest imaging findings, with particular attention to atelectasis and pleural effusion; and (4) information on the dosage of corticosteroid treatment used.

Statistical Analysis

We adopted the SPSS 22.0 version for data analysis. The continuous variables were summarized using the median and interquartile range (IQR). Analysis was conducted using analysis of variance (ANOVA), Kruskal–Wallis test (K-W-H), and Mann–Whitney test. Categorical variables are expressed in terms of frequency and percentage, and the chi-square test is used for the assessment of significance. The K-W-H test is adopted for multiple types of ordered variables. The relationship between variables was evaluated using the Spearman rank correlation coefficient. A P value less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant. Graphics are created using version 9.0 of GraphPad Prism.

Results

Demographic and Clinical Attributes of Patients

During the study period, approximately 7,646 pediatric patients were diagnosed with MPP and hospitalized, of which about 9.93% were classified as RMPP. Ultimately, this study focused on 432 patients. The distribution of patients by treatment dosage was as follows: low (n=232), medium (n=178), and high (n=22), they were divided into three groups in total.

The characteristics of patients in each category are shown in Table 1. The median age and BMI of the low-dose group were 7.0 years and 16.44, with ranges of (5.0–8.0) and (14.27–19.56), respectively. The median age and BMI of the medium-dose group were 7.0 years and 15.28, with ranges of (5.0–9.0) and (14.08–17.99), respectively. The median age and BMI of the high-dose group were 6.5 years and 14.25, with ranges of (5.0–8.25) and (13.28–15.23), respectively (p < 0.001). There was a statistically significant difference in BMI among the three groups of patients (p < 0.001). There was no statistically significant difference in age and gender distribution (p = 0.755 and p = 0.095).In addition, the children in the high-dose group had the longest hospital stay, which was up to 13 days (range 11–16.75) (p < 0.001).

Analysis of Correlations Among Different Variables

We used the Spearman correlation coefficient to study the correlation between methylprednisolone and laboratory indicators. As shown in Figure 2, we found that there was a particularly positive correlation between the dosage of methylprednisolone and the length of hospital stay, BMI, WBC, CRP, PCT, LDH, ALT, NLR, ESR, D-dimer, and FER, with all correlations being statistically significant (P < 0.05). Notably, there was no significant correlation between the dose of methylprednisolone and age, with Spearman coefficients of 0.671. Among the parameters assessed, NLR and FER had the strongest correlation with the dose of methylprednisolone, with Spearman coefficients of 0.451 and 0.529, respectively.

Correlation analysis of methylprednisolone dose and other variables showed that NLR (Spearman ρ = 0.451) and FER (Spearman ρ = 0.529) had the highest correlation coefficients with methylprednisolone dose.

Discussion

In the current study, we identified a significant relationship between glucocorticoid dosage and the concentrations of several biomarkers, namely WBC, CRP, PCT, LDH, ALT, NLR, ESR, D-dimer, and FER. In contrast, we found no substantial correlation with age. It is noteworthy that the correlation coefficients of NLR and FER among all the analyzed laboratory indicators were the strongest, which were 0.451 and 0.529 respectively.This observation highlights the significant association between glucocorticoid dosage and refractory RMPP in the pediatric population. The above results are hoped to provide support for pediatric medical staff assessing the severity of RMPP and help with how to use appropriate glucocorticoids for children with RMPP.

Recently, the importance of RMPP has gained increasing recognition. Many researchers have highlighted the significant role of biomarkers in determining the severity of RMPP.5,14 D-dimer is a mature hematological marker associated with secondary fibrinolytic response, which significantly increases after MP infection, particularly in the context of RMPP.12,15,16 Previous studies have indicated that D-dimer concentration can serve as a predictor of RMPP severity; lower levels may be associated with a reduced likelihood of pleural effusion.17 Our results support this notion, as the D-dimer concentration in the low-dose group was recorded at 0.43 µg/L, the lowest among the three cohorts, and corresponded with a pleural effusion occurrence rate of 17.24%, which was also the lowest observed. Additionally, the serum FER concentration in the high-dose group was quantified at 339.25ng/mL, the highest among all groups, suggesting a correlation with more severe manifestations of MPP. This indicates that children with higher FER levels may exhibit greater susceptibility to severe RMPP.18 Furthermore, previous studies have shown that LDH levels exceeding 480 U/L indicate delayed radiological recovery.19 With the same significance as this observation result, our high-dose cohort showed the highest LDH level of 484 U/L, which was associated with more severe results of chest CT scans. Overall, our results, along with existing literature, indicate a significant relationship between severity and biomarkers.

At present, macrolide antibiotics are regarded as the primary line of treatment; nonetheless, individuals afflicted with RMPP may benefit from adjunctive immunosuppressive therapy, given the significant immune response that typifies this condition. A substantial number of researchers advocate for the use of corticosteroids in managing RMPP, citing compelling justification for their administration.5,20 Steroid therapy has the ability to alleviate severe immune responses, clinical symptoms, lung injury and complications in children and adults with MP infection.8,20,21 An array of studies has investigated various steroid treatment protocols. Our analysis indicates that participants within the low-dose cohort demonstrated lower biomarkers, including CRP level of 10.98 mg/l, alongside milder clinical manifestations, aligning with findings from studies of RMPP-affected pediatric patients treated with methylprednisolone at a daily dosage of 2 mg/kg.22 In contrast, Chen et al believe that low-dose glucocorticoids have a poor effect on RMPP patients with severe pulmonary complications.23 Additionally, a study indicates that for severe RMPP, intravenous injection of methylprednisolone is administered at a dose of 5–10 mg /kg /day.24 Our findings further reveal that 69% of the moderate-dose cohort exhibited pulmonary consolidation, and this group presented a higher incidence of pleural effusion relative to the low-dose cohort, it indicates that pneumonia is more severe compared with the low-dose group. It is notable that the incidence of atelectasis was the highest in the high-dose group (86.36%), and the length of hospital stay was the longest, confirming the previous research results,25,26 which supports the hypothesis that corticosteroid dosing is associated with the severity of RMPP in pediatric patients.

NLR, as a biomarker for the balance of inflammation and immunity, has been confirmed to have a significant age-related characteristic. This pattern stems from the dynamic changes in hematopoietic development, immune maturation, and hormone regulation. It remains at a low level in a stable state from 1 to 6 years old, and gradually increases with the enhancement of myeloid hematopoiesis after the age of27,28. In our study, the median age of the children was between 6.5 and 7 years old. MP infections are known to stimulate T lymphocyte activation, which in turn amplifies the release of inflammatory mediators and cytokines, thus intensifying the body’s overall inflammatory response.29 Nonetheless, an excessively vigorous inflammatory reaction can lead to lymphocyte apoptosis, resulting in diminished lymphocyte populations.30 Within the context of respiratory disorders, elevated NLR has been correlated with poor prognoses in cases of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis,31 and one study suggested that NLR might serve as an indicator of negative outcomes in patients afflicted with MP.32 In our investigation, we observed that the NLR in the high-dose group was the highest among the three groups (6.21 vs 1.66 vs 1.52, P < 0.001).

FER, primarily synthesized by macrophages, typically exhibits elevated levels in response to tissue damage, microbial infections, and inflammatory conditions.33,34 The research by Wen et al indicates that children with FER greater than 329ng/mL in laboratory markers have an increased possibility of developing RMPP.35 In a related study, for individuals diagnosed with MPP, if the FER threshold was 291.5 ng/mL, glucocorticoid treatment was required.10 Our research found that the median concentration of FER in the medium-dose group was 107.59 ng/mL. Several factors may explain this discrepancy: (1) The years 2023–2024 represent a two-year period since China lifted its pandemic restrictions, during which cases of RMPP have significantly increased, allowing for more timely and effective glucocorticoid intervention for those afflicted; (2) The epidemiological patterns of MP may vary based on geographic location and seasonal influences. The correlation analysis performed in our study yielded correlation coefficients of 0.451 for the NLR and 0.529 for FER, indicating a positive association with the dosage of methylprednisolone administered to pediatric patients suffering from RMPP.

A comprehensive meta-analysis involving randomized controlled trials demonstrated that glucocorticoids can significantly shorten the duration of fever and reduce hospital stays.6 Zhu et al proposed that a serum FER level of 411 ng/mL or higher could act as a significant clinical marker for patients with RMPP undergoing glucocorticoid treatment, a threshold that surpasses our findings.13 In our investigation, the serum FER of the high-dose cohort was 339.25 ng/mL.This difference can be attributed to the fact that in the study of Zhu et al, high-dose methylprednisolone was defined as more than 200 mg/day, while we regarded high-dose methylprednisolone as 10 mg/kg/day.

Therefore, we suggest that the NLR and FER could be important biomarkers for guiding the appropriate glucocorticoid dosages in pediatric patients diagnosed with RMPP.

Limitation

This study has the following limitations.Firstly, this study is a retrospective study, and the confounding factors related to the severity of the disease are one of the key factors affecting the research results. Future studies should adopt a prospective cohort design, incorporate a unified quantitative scoring tool for disease severity, and combine propensity score allocation, multivariate Cox regression, and other statistical methods to fully correct the influence of disease severity and other confounding factors.Furthermore, discrepancies between our results and those reported in other research may arise from regional and temporal differences. Lastly, the research is confined to a single institution, highlighting the need for larger, multicenter, prospective studies to validate these findings further.

Conclusion

In summary, there exists a correlation between the NLR and FER levels with the degree of inflammation in cases of MP. These biomarkers can assist in determining the optimal dosages of glucocorticoids. The implications of these results offer a theoretical basis, thereby establishing a strong foundation for clinicians in guiding glucocorticoid therapy for pediatric patients suffering from RMPP.

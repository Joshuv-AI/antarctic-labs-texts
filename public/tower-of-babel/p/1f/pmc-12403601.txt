Impact assessment of the ophthalmology discipline construction project at Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital
Authors: Huajun Sun, Xiaorong Li, Xiaomeng Yu, Fangshi Li, Tingting Zhang, Jiahui Song, Yue Du, Hongliang Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesResearch impact is an emerging measure of research achievement alongside traditional academic outputs such as publications. We present the results of applying the Framework to Assess the Impact from Translational Health Research (FAIT) to the ophthalmology discipline construction project at Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital.MethodsData were gathered via in-depth interviews and document analysis to report on the three major FAIT methods: the Payback Framework, an assessment of costs against monetized outcomes and a narrative account of the impact generated from ophthalmology discipline construction project. FAIT was applied from September 2022 to August 2023.ResultsThe discipline construction project supported 64 graduate students in graduating, 15 members of the discipline team were newly employed as graduate student mentors, 12 continuing education projects were approved, 24 research projects were approved, 116 papers were published, 5 monographs were edited, nine academic conferences were held, one Chinese Medical Science and Technology Award was won, 21 patents were authorized and two technology transfers were realized. In addition, 12 expert consensuses were formulated, and 11 new technologies and projects were carried out. More than 60 science popularization activities were held, more than 270 science popularization articles were published, more than 10 000 diabetes patients were screened via eyeground exam, and one expert service base was built. Moreover, 2 scientific research systems were updated, 4 talent systems were updated, 12 medical systems were updated, 2 medical systems were formulated and 1 financial system was formulated. The total cost of the discipline construction project is ¥1.542 million RMB. Outcomes from the discipline construction project that could be monetized were in total ¥290.5 million RMB, indicating a return of ¥1.9 RMB for every yuan.ConclusionsThe ophthalmology discipline construction project has successfully met expectations and has achieved good outcomes. The evaluation results are comprehensive and objective; the representation of return on investment ratio is simple yet effective, which can help decision-makers understand the project well.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12961-025-01388-8.Keywords: Impact assessment, Ophthalmology, Discipline construction, Health economics

Introduction

The biomedical field is currently experiencing rapid development, with groundbreaking technologies such as by artificial intelligence, stem cell therapy, gene therapy, biological therapy and immune-targeted therapy being successfully integrated into clinical practice. These advancements are profoundly changing the landscape of clinical medicine. In the field of ophthalmology, ongoing research on artificial-intelligence-based imaging diagnosis technology is significantly enhancing the diagnosis and follow-up of various eye diseases such as glaucoma and cataracts [1–3]. Gene therapy and stem cell therapy have been clinically approved for application in the field of ophthalmology [4–6]. Anti-VEGF and TNF-ɑ monoclonal antibodies and other biological agents are widely used in the treatment of retinal diseases and uveitis [7]. Various new types of artificial lenses, glaucoma surgical aids, artificial corneas, myopia prevention and control drugs and instruments are constantly entering clinical practice. The application of these new treatment technologies has significantly improved the treatment level of ophthalmic diseases in clinical practice.

With the rapid development of the economy and society, the acceleration of population ageing and the continuous improvement of people’s demand for eye health, the prevention and treatment of eye diseases in China remains a challenging task [8–10]. Early screening and diagnosis of common blinding eye diseases, as well as those that seriously damage the quality of life, are still priorities within ophthalmic clinical practice [11, 12]. The establishment of high-quality multicentre ophthalmic clinical research, real-world research and clinical trials will create a new model for the prevention and treatment of common ophthalmic diseases. This will provide a platform for improving precision diagnosis and treatment capabilities in clinical practice. The future development of ophthalmic database will involve the construction of a multimodal clinical biological database from daily clinical practice to patients’ genetic characteristics, promoting the sustainable development of ophthalmic clinical and basic research and providing strong support for the artificial intelligence analysis and utilization of data [13]. The diagnostic technology based on artificial intelligence technology and biomarkers will drive the transformation of diagnosis and treatment models [14]. Furthermore, gene therapy, stem cell therapy, biological therapy and artificial alternative materials are the development directions in the field of ophthalmic treatment in the future. Developing a myopia prevention and control strategy, building a myopia prevention and control network and developing new methods for myopia prevention and control are the priorities for the development of optometry [15].

For a long time, health administrative departments and medical institutions in China have attached great importance to discipline construction. Tianjin Health Commission initiated a project to support the discipline construction of ophthalmology at Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital from 2021 to 2024. The project aimed to strengthen the talent team construction, research platform construction, enhance teaching and research work and promote the transformation research. Similar discipline construction projects have been established in regions such as Henan, Shandong, Zhejiang and Sichuan in China. There is a growing demand for research impact measures. These measures play a crucial role in evaluating the effectiveness of research investments and justifying the need for increased funding [16]. Despite this, impact assessment beyond knowledge advancement is still not standard practice in many countries [17].

To better understand the impact of research, several frameworks have emerged and been reviewed, with two recent systematic reviews highlighting their characteristics and applications [18–20]. Frequently used models include health economic frameworks [21, 22], variants of research program logic models [23], the Australian Research Quality Framework [24] and the Banzi Health Research Impact Framework [25, 26]. Among these, Framework to Assess the Impact from Translational Health Research (FAIT) stands out as a hybrid of three proven methodologies for measuring research impact, namely, quantified metrics, economic analysis and narratives of the process by which research translates and generates impact. Details about the development of FAIT can be found in the seminal FAIT paper by Searles et al. (2016) [27]. FAIT was selected as the preferred framework due to its multidimensional lens on impact and its ability to be prospectively applied.

FAIT has been applied in various contexts. Ramanathan et al. applied FAIT to the Centre for Research Excellence in Integrated Quality Improvement to encourage research translation and assess the wider impact of that research [28]. Dodd et al. utilized FAIT in low- and middle-income countries to better understand the challenges associated with research translation and measuring impact [29]. FAIT has also been used to assess the impact of cardiovascular outcomes improvement research [30]. The successful application of FAIT in various contexts highlights its potential for impact assessment in discipline construction projects.

The objective of this study is to evaluate the research impact of ophthalmology at the Eye Hospital of Tianjin Medical University. Through analysis of experience and identification of challenges, the study seeks to provide reference for the future discipline construction of ophthalmology.

Methods

FAIT logic model

A FAIT logic model (FLM) was developed for the discipline construction project’s research streams, which involved identifying the needs, end-users and impacts of each stream. Through the monitoring and feedback, the teams would have the opportunity to assess their progress against planned activities, outputs and intended impacts, providing evidence for the achievement of process, output and impact goals and subsequently refining their research translation and engagement activities to maximize impact. Figure 1 presents the discipline construction project FLM, which was used to guide the impact assessment using FAIT. We obtained the necessary data and information for our research from project management records, interviews and daily communication with discipline construction project teams. The execution date of this project and the statistical range of related indicator data are from September 2022 to August 2023.

Logic model for discipline construction project

Upon review, the Medical Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital agreed to carry out the scientific research [NO:2024KY(L)-06]. We confirmed that informed consent was obtained from the study participants, and the guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki were followed.

Payback metrics

The Payback Framework was used to assess impact using quantitative metrics within the domains of knowledge advancement, clinical implementation, community benefit, policy and legislation and economic benefits [27, 31]. We reviewed the literature to identify existing impact indicators that could capture evidence of the anticipated benefits from the discipline construction project.

Economic assessment

We compared the costs of delivering the discipline construction project with a calculated (monetized) value for the outcomes generated by that investment to provide an estimate of social return on investment (SROI). Given the range and diversity of research outputs for the discipline construction project, the most appropriate and relevant method of economic analysis was a cost–outcome analysis (COA). A COA presents an array of outcome and costs in a disaggregated form. In this study, SROI = Outcome/Costs.

The analysis aims to present a ledger of cost against a suite of attributable outcomes, where some outcomes are monetized and others are not. Forcibly estimating outcomes that cannot be monetized will lead to inaccuracies in the estimation outcomes. Our COA only presents monetizable outcomes, and the non-monetizable outcomes are captured in the Payback metrics. Therefore, the result was a conservative estimate of the SROI. The analysis results can help determine whether the investment in discipline construction projects is considered a sound utilization of resources.

A key factor that informed what was included in the COA was “Would this have happened without the discipline construction project?” If the outcomes of individual research and implementation projects, funded by other sources, would have still occurred anyway without the discipline construction project, then they were not attributed as direct outcomes of the discipline construction project.

Costs

The funding for discipline construction projects is required to be separately accounted for. We have classified the costs of discipline construction projects to ensure transparency in related costs. We used a bottom-up micro-costing methodology. The cost of the discipline construction project we have calculated is the actual cost of the project. It should be noted that the discipline construction project has not been approved for indirect costs. Discipline construction is considered one of the job responsibilities of medical institution staff and can be completed during regular working hours without any additional loss of work costs. Moreover, discipline team members are not eligible to receive extra bonuses for undertaking the discipline construction project. Therefore, the costs in this project solely encompass direct costs in our study.

Outcomes

The outcomes of the investment in discipline construction project and its activities were already broadly captured using the Payback methodology. As part of the COA, we divided the outcomes into those that can be fully attributed to discipline construction projects and those that can be partially attributed to discipline construction projects. For an outcome partially attributed to the discipline construction project, we calculated the benefits attributed to the discipline construction project by multiplying the 50% contribution rates with the total benefits of the discipline construction project. The sensitivity analysis result is the sum of the benefits of an outcome partially attributed to discipline construction projects, with a contribution rate of 0–100%, and an outcome fully attributed to discipline construction projects.

Narrative

To inform the narrative of the impact of discipline construction project, we invited discipline leaders and some discipline team members to participate in an interview about research impact. The participants were prompted to share their insights on the implementation of discipline construction, the translation of research outcomes and the research impact generated. When necessary, follow-up interviews were conducted to gather additional information, and the transcripts were returned to the participants for verification to ensure the accuracy of the information. The transcripts were independently coded and cross-checked by two researchers. Coding discrepancies were discussed and resolved through consensus meetings.

Deductive thematic analysis was applied to systematically analyse research impact. The study used five domains (knowledge advancement, clinical implementation, community benefit, policy and legislation and economic benefits) as coding categories [27, 31], which is consistent with the Payback Framework. In addition, the Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic model was applied to mine for potential topics in interview texts. The analysis results of LDA can be mutually validated with deductive thematic analysis, enhancing the reliability of research conclusions.

Results

Basic information of the discipline construction project

Project need

There is still insufficient investment in technology and academic influence. Hospitals are not attractive enough to draw in high-level talents. The strength of discipline leaders is uneven, and the number of instructors is still insufficient. It is not possible to have at least one instructor as a discipline leader in all clinical departments to lead the development of the discipline. A fixed interdisciplinary cooperation mechanism has not yet been fully established. Clinical and basic research are not integrated closely enough, and information technology construction is relatively weak, resulting in insufficient support for medical services and research. Hospitals lack systematic and standardized management, and their innovation capabilities urgently need to be improved.

Project activities

The main activities of this project are to carry out early screening and diagnosis of common blinding eye diseases that damage quality of life; conduct clinical trials of gene therapy products; establish clinical medical research centres and demonstration research wards; introduce a flexible appointment mechanism and improve the appointment standards for health professional titles; carry out talent support projects; vigorously promote cooperation between universities, enterprises and hospital and apply for the Tianjin Key Laboratory of Innovative Ophthalmic Optics Technology; establish an original research database and establish an institutional storage mechanism for the original data related to research papers; and carry out self-examination of academic misconduct, establish an organizational structure for research integrity construction, improve the management system for research integrity and evaluate research management mechanisms.

Basic information of the discipline team

The subject of this study is the ophthalmology discipline team of the Eye Hospital of Tianjin Medical University. The discipline team consists of 115 people, including 50 team members with senior professional titles, 45 with intermediate professional titles and 20 with junior professional titles. The hospital has a total of seven key development directions, including fundus diseases, refractive and corneal diseases, orbital diseases and eye plastic surgery, cataracts, glaucoma, uveitis and immunology ophthalmology and ophthalmology. Each key development direction is equipped with senior discipline leaders, and each key development direction has high, medium and junior professional titles’ talents. Moreover, 70 people have a doctoral degree, 43 people have a master’s degree and 2 people have a bachelor’s degree or below.

Narrative

Knowledge advancement

The hospital is dedicated to supporting the research of gene therapy, stem cell therapy and other biological therapies. At present, the hospital mainly focuses on the treatment of refractory blindness eye diseases such as diabetes eye disease, age-related macular degeneration, keratopathy, glaucoma, uveitis, orbital tumours, etc.; carries out research on the mechanism of related eye diseases; and develops new therapeutic products that cater to the needs of patients.

The hospital has established an ophthalmic research institute, a laboratory for retinal diseases, an ophthalmic optical technology laboratory and a clinical medical research centre for ophthalmic diseases and has established a clinical research network for ophthalmic diseases. In 2022, in the ophthalmology ranking of hospitals in China, it ranked 9th in terms of technology value and papers from the hospital ranked 17th among the most influential SCI papers in global ophthalmology research in 2022. The academic leader was awarded the Distinguished Service Awards 2023 by the Asia Pacific Ophthalmology Society.

We have established an ophthalmic biomedical data storage and analysis system to explore the genetic and pathological mechanisms of eye diseases, search for biomarkers that contribute to disease diagnosis and lay a solid foundation for constructing precision clinical plans for ophthalmic diseases.

Clinical implementation

As the ophthalmic quality control centre, the hospital holds discussion meetings on difficult ophthalmic cases. The hospital conducted a survey on the medical experience of ophthalmic patients, implemented an eye health plan and conducted quality control on restrictive technologies such as allogeneic corneal transplantation and vitrectomy. The hospital led the revision of management standards and indicators for vitrectomy. The hospital conducted training on the treatment of acute haemorrhagic conjunctivitis. The hospital organized science popularization and free consultation activities.

We used extracellular vesicles derived from mesenchymal stem cells to treat uveitis, retinal degeneration diseases, etc., and achieved good results. In addition, we have developed an expert consensus on a one-stop intravitreal injection mode.

Community benefit

The hospital has followed up with more than 800 patients with diabetes and screened the fundus conditions of more than 10 000 patients with diabetes, utilizing the ophthalmic intelligent image management platform to manage the reading of fundus photos and health education on diabetes eye diseases. Additionally, the hospital has established a blinding-related eye disease prevention and control network, carried out online continuing education courses and shared information with remote primary medical institutions. The hospital trained more than 40 ophthalmologists from Kenya, South Africa, Jamaica, Ghana, Burundi, Nigeria, Botswana and other developing countries.

The hospital has actively carried out community diabetes screening work and introduced an ophthalmic intelligent image management system into the screening process to promote the in-depth integration of the Internet, Big Data and artificial intelligence.

Policy and legislation

The hospital has formulated management measures for electronic invoice review, etc. The hospital has revised the management measures for scientific research funds; regulations on the management of funds for the laboratory; implementation measures for talent introduction; implementation plans for clinical doctor appointment; evaluation plans for professional titles of health professionals; implementation measures for talent training plans, responsibilities and work systems of the medical technology clinical application management committee, management systems for clinical application of medical technology and dynamic evaluation systems for medical technology; and implementation measures for access management of new technologies and new projects, a medical technology risk warning mechanism, a medical technology damage disposal plan, regulations for physicians to visit outside and a medical safety incident reporting system, etc.

The hospital has a training fund for newly hired doctors and offers generous salaries to post-doctorates. The hospital encourages us to determine the sub-professional direction as soon as possible, and even after determining the department, clinical rotation can still be carried out to help us improve our professional quality. The hospital has developed a five-level talent training plan, with the fifth level being support for our young talents.

Economic benefit

The significant transformation of technological achievements realized by the hospital not only demonstrates the market value of technological innovation and the ability of efficient transformation but also injects a strong impetus into promoting the transformation and upgrading of China’s health industry.

A professor’s team has independently developed and successfully commercialized a small RNA drug with miR-15a-5p as its core technology, specifically targeting the issue of retinal neovascularization. This innovation marks a significant breakthrough in the field of precision medicine in ophthalmology. The team has granted a patent license for this technology to a biotechnology company and formally joined hands with the company to launch the commercialization of this technological achievement.

LDA topic analysis

Based on the five types of research impacts, we determined that the number of topics would be five and conducted LDA topic analysis. According to the main connotations of the keywords under each topic, the topics were named after the type of research impact they are closest to. Keywords included in the Knowledge Advancement topic are research, education and foundation. Keywords included in the Clinical Implementation topic are technology, surgery and new. Keywords included in the Community Benefit topic are patient, screening and cooperation. The keywords included in the Policy and Legislation topic are platform, equipment and procurement. Keywords included in the Economic Benefit topic are transformation, achievement and fund. The connotations of the keywords under each topic are basically consistent with the topics, which verifies the applicability of the five dimensions of research impact in ophthalmology construction projects and further improves the reliability of the research. Discussion

Effects of and suggestions regarding the ophthalmology construction project

Overall, in terms of effectiveness, the ophthalmology discipline construction project has yielded positive results, with 11 new technologies and projects implemented, resulting in improved clinical diagnosis and treatment levels. The establishment of various of platforms, including the construction of an ophthalmic disease data platform, biological sample library, proteomics and genetics research platform, translational medicine technology platform and ophthalmic research network, has been a prominent achievement. The cost–outcome of the discipline construction project is also significant. Revenue generated from technology transfer has become the primary income source for discipline construction projects. In addition, the discipline construction projects have attracted investment for multiple other projects, further contributing to its success. However, certain areas for improvement have been identified. The evaluation results suggest a relatively limited number of national-level research projects and scientific awards within the discipline. This indicates that the impact and recognition that the discipline construction project received were primarily limited to the regional level. Moreover, the degree of international exchange and cooperation in this discipline has been deemed insufficient.

In light of these challenges we are facing, it is imperative that we explore innovative solutions. Central to this effort is the promotion of talent cultivation within hospitals, with a focus on nurturing a cadre of skilled doctors, esteemed professors and renowned scientists. Several previous studies have shown that implementing talent management strategies in healthcare organizations is essential for the sustainable development [32–34]. In parallel, we must recognize the immense value of identifying and fostering young talents in an appropriate way. A study on the productivity effects of human capital in a team production environment revealed that the departure of experienced nurses, the inclusion of new hires and the utilization of temporary contract nurses all contribute to disruptions in team functioning that lead to significant decreases in productivity [35]. By adopting a dual approach of simultaneous cultivation and introduction, we can build a dynamic and well-rounded discipline team while maintaining smooth hospital operation.

In tandem with this, through the promotion of equitable partnerships and collaboration, there is immense potential for global vision research [36, 37]. The importance of international collaboration in the research field is well-documented and supported [38, 39]. Fostering cooperation with international advanced ophthalmic institutions becomes essential. This entails the selection of outstanding young researchers to receive training at advanced institutions abroad and the integration of exceptional foreign talents to propel hospitals towards international excellence.

Exploring the pathogenesis of eye diseases, discovering new treatment strategies and addressing clinical issues are also paramount [40, 41]. To maximize the impact of our research efforts, we need to fully leverage the advantages of cooperation among hospitals, research institutes and enterprises. By capitalizing on these synergistic partnerships, we can effectively translate research findings into tangible economic and social benefits.

Furthermore, we need to explore new mechanisms for project management. Through the reform of project management, we can strive for breakthroughs in high-level research projects and elevate the overall number of research projects. Lastly, our pursuit of success in discipline construction mandates the exploration of fresh ideas in hospital management, the innovation of service methods and the reform internal management mechanisms. By adopting this comprehensive approach, we can strengthen the overall discipline construction projects and promote the growth of ophthalmology.

Application of FAIT

The FAIT should be applied prospectively in project design, not just in impact assessment. Through the storyline of FAIT, we can discover the matching of the discipline construction project need, aims, activities, output and impact. This can provide reference for determining whether the discipline construction project should be approved or not. In the future, there should be an increased emphasis on clinical implementation, community benefits [42], policy and legislation and economic benefits [43]. At the same time, regulations, team building, platform building and other related content should also be presented in more detail within the framework.

We need to clearly identify the relationship between each expenditure of the project and the output of the discipline construction project to avoid the output of other projects being used to impersonate the output of the discipline construction project, and to avoid the funds of the discipline construction project being diverted for other purposes, for example, ensuring some reagents purchased with project funds are dedicated. In addition, research projects carried out within discipline construction projects should generally adopt project management. Sometimes, some discipline project funds are used to support a research project, but the research project is supported by multiple projects at the same time, making it difficult to determine how the output of the research project was allocated to each project.

For an outcome partially attributed to the discipline construction project, the contribution rate is difficult to objectively and accurately measure. In the future, project leaders can be invited to conduct a self-assessment of the contribution rate of discipline construction projects to various outcomes and, on the basis of the contribution rate from their self-assessment, adjust it upward and downward within a certain range to carry out sensitivity tests. In addition to evaluating the benefits generated during the implementation period of the project, emphasis should also be placed on evaluating the long-term benefits produced after the project is completed.

Limitations

This study also has some limitations. The potential research impact cannot be monetized and measured. Using bottom-up micro-costing methodology has resulted in a significant workload for this study, which may limit the widespread application of impact assessment.

Conclusions

Overall, the output and impact of the ophthalmology discipline construction project meet expectations and have achieved good outcomes. The representation of return on investment ratio is simple yet effective, allowing decision-makers to quickly understand the project. The evaluation results are comprehensive and objective, providing valuable insights for health administrative departments to access the effectiveness of discipline construction projects and increase the transparency of project execution. Additionally, it can help health administrative departments provide reference for the approval of similar projects.

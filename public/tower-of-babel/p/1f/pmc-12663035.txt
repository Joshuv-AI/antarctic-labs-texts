A comparison of time efficiency in simulated out-of-hospital cardiac arrest using drone-delivered automated external defibrillators
Authors: Liying Pi, Siyuan Wei, Zhixin Wu, Yixin Yang, Qiao Chen, Chaofan He, Yonghong Du, Yuanyi Li, Yuyuan You
Published: 2025

We read with interest the feasibility study by Jakobsen et al. on drone-delivered automated external defibrillators (AEDs) in real OHCA cases.1 To supplement this, we conducted a simulated study comparing the time efficiency of three distinct drone AED delivery methods: landing, rope-drop, and throw-drop.

Early defibrillation is crucial for the OHCA survival, yet traditional AED deployment is often hindered by geographical and traffic constraints. Drones offer a promising solution for rapid AED delivery.2, 3 However, the comparative data on the operational efficiency of different drone delivery methods Is limited.

Our study evaluated three delivery techniques in a simulated OHCA scenario 16 m away from the volunteer's obtaining point: Landing: The drone landed completely on the ground, allowing the volunteer to retrieve the AED directly; Rope-drop: The drone hovered at a low altitude and delivered the AED to the volunteer via a rope; Throw-drop: The drone hovered and released the AED from a small package at a low height for the volunteer to collect from the ground.

We measured two time-intervals: T1 (drone landing to AED retrieval by volunteer) and T2 (AED retrieval to successful application on a manikin). The total operational time (T1 + T2) was the primary efficiency metric.

Significant differences were found in both T1 and T2 among the experimental groups (P < 0.001). The landing method demonstrated the shortest times for both intervals, resulting in the fastest total operational time (see Table). The landing method was more efficiency than rope-drop and throw-drop. We recommend to prioritize this method in drone-based emergency systems, with the integration with intelligent dispatch and safety protocols. Public training and legal frameworks are vital for enhancing the OHCA survival chain.

Future research should validate these findings in complex real-world environments, considering influencing factors such as weather and terrain.

Clinical phenotypes and the risk factors of mild pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Satoshi Taya, Kentaro Ejiri, Satoshi Akagi, Yoshitake Fukuda, Misaki Kanezawa, Yoichi Takaya, Toru Miyoshi, Kazufumi Nakamura, Shinsuke Yuasa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe definition of pulmonary hypertension (PH) was revised as mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) >20 mmHg from ≥25 mmHg in the recent PH guidelines; however, the characteristics of PH patients with mPAP 21–24 mmHg have not been well described.MethodsA total of 1080 individuals who underwent right heart catheterization (2018–2022) was categorized into 3 groups by mPAP: no PH with mPAP ≤20 mmHg, mild PH with mPAP 21–24 mmHg, and conventional PH with mPAP ≥25 mmHg. We assessed PH subtypes of hemodynamic and clinical classification and compared survival outcomes (all-cause mortality, heart failure hospitalization, lung transplant) using Cox models.ResultsOf them, 391 patients (36.2%) were diagnosed as PH including mild PH (n = 137) and conventional PH (n = 254). Phenotypically, mild PH was predominantly characterized by pre-capillary hemodynamics and Group 2 clinical classification. Mild PH is associated with an intermediate prognosis: outcomes are more favorable than in conventional PH but inferior to no PH (HR 1.74 [95% CI 1.15–2.61]). While the two PH phenotypes appear to share comorbidity-related factors, hemodynamic parameters were associated with survival outcomes not in mild PH but in conventional PH.ConclusionsThe observation that mild PH constitutes approximately one-third of overall PH cases carries substantial clinical weight. The newly identified mild PH cohort was characterized mainly by pre-capillary and Group 2 subtypes. While mild PH is associated with an intermediate prognosis, comorbidities appear to be the primary determinant of outcomes in this group, unlike in conventional PH where hemodynamics play a larger role.Keywords: mild pulmonary hypertension, prognosis, risk factor

Introduction

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a rare but life-threatening condition characterized by the progressive deterioration of the pulmonary circulation.1 Despite its diverse etiology, the definitive diagnosis of PH relies on the hemodynamic confirmation of an elevated mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) via right heart catheterization.2 Recent comprehensive guidelines published jointly by the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and the European Respiratory Society (ERS) have introduced a paradigm shift in the diagnosis and management of PH.3 A pivotal update in these guidelines is the revision of the hemodynamic threshold for diagnosing PH, lowering the mPAP criterion from ≥25 mmHg to >20 mmHg. 4

This revision of the diagnostic criteria is grounded in emerging clinical evidence. Hemodynamic evaluations in healthy populations have established that the upper limit of normal for mPAP is approximately 20 mmHg, rather than the historical benchmark of 25 mmHg.5 Furthermore, meta-analyses have demonstrated that individuals with an mPAP between 21 and 24 mmHg exhibit a significantly worse prognosis compared to those with an mPAP ≤20 mmHg.6 Consequently, this updated definition provides a critical framework for the early detection and potential intervention in patients presenting with abnormal pulmonary hemodynamics.

While several studies have investigated the clinical implications of an mPAP between 21 and 24 mmHg, significant gaps remain in the literature. Most previous investigations have predominantly targeted specific cohorts at high risk for incident PH (e.g., patients with connective tissue disease or chronic lung disease), but not general population.7, 8 Moreover, although these studies have delineated prognostic outcomes and risk factors for all-cause mortality, they have largely overlooked the detailed hemodynamic phenotyping, clinical classification, and underlying cardiopulmonary comorbidities specific to patients with this newly defined borderline elevation in pulmonary arterial pressure.9, 10

To address these knowledge gaps, the present study evaluated a broader cohort of patients with an mPAP of 21–24 mmHg. We aimed to systematically characterize their hemodynamic profiles and clinical classifications across PH subtypes, comparing these findings directly with those of patients meeting the traditional diagnostic threshold of an mPAP ≥25 mmHg.

Methods

Study participants

We retrospectively studied 1542 consecutive cases of right heart catheterization at Okayama University Hospital from January 2018 to December 2022. The indications for performing right heart catheterization were as follows: (1) suspected PH based on clinical symptoms or echocardiographic findings; (2) hemodynamic assessment during hospitalization for heart failure; and (3) hemodynamic assessment of congenital heart disease (CHD). Patients who had already been initiated on pulmonary hypertension–specific medication were excluded. First, we excluded 183 cases other than initial right heart catheterization and 277 patients with insufficient hemodynamic data (i.e., missing value in hemodynamic parameters). Of the remaining 1082 patients, furthermore, 2 patients who already administered specific PH treatment were excluded. Finally, a total of 1080 cases were included in the analysis (Supplementary Figure S1).

The present study was approved by the institutional review board and ethics committee of Okayama University (#2410–029). The informed consent was waived because of the low-risk nature of this retrospective study and the inability to obtain consent directly from all subjects. However, we extensively announced the study protocol at Okayama University Hospital and on a website (http://www.hsc.okayama-u.ac.jp/ethics/koukai/med/) and provided patients with the opportunity to withdraw from the study. The study was also conducted according to the principles expressed in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Exposure

The study participants were categorized into three groups according to mPAP measured by right heart catheter: no PH, mild PH and conventional PH groups. Briefly, mild PH with mPAP 21–24 mmHg was newly diagnosed based on the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines; conventional PH with mPAP ≥25 mmHg was in line with the previous definition in PH. No PH group was the remaining individuals with mPAP ≤20 mmHg. Right heart catheter was performed by skilled cardiologists.

Hemodynamic definition of pulmonary hypertension

In addition to the revised definition in mPAP, there are a few changes in hemodynamic definition of PH in the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines compared with the previous guidelines (Supplementary Table S1). For example, pre-capillary PH which covers elevated pulmonary arterial pressure due to pulmonary vascular disease is defined by pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) >2 Wood units in the 2022 guidelines, 11 whereas >3 Wood units in the 2015 guidelines. Furthermore, unclassified PH was defined as patients with PVR ≤2 Wood units and with pulmonary arterial wedge pressure (PAWP) ≤15 mmHg according to the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines.

Clinical classification of pulmonary hypertension

Clinical classification of PH is according to similar clinical conditions by pathophysiological mechanisms, clinical presentation, hemodynamic characteristics, and therapeutic management. According to the ESC/ERS 2022 guidelines 3, we defined clinical classification of PH including Group1 as pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH); Group 2 as PH associated with left heart failure; Group 3 as PH associated with lung diseases and/or hypoxia； Group 4 as PH associated with pulmonary artery obstructions; and Group 5 as PH with unclear and/or multifactorial mechanisms (Supplementary Table S2). Furthermore, we classified Group 1 PH patients into subgroups based on pathophysiology considering a wide variety of etiologies in the group (Supplementary Table S3).

Survival outcomes

Outcomes of interest included all-cause mortality, heart failure hospitalization, and lung transplantation. All-cause mortality was classified into cardiac death and non-cardiac death. Cardiac death was further separately analyzed as PH-related death and no PH-related death. Additionally, we analyzed a composite outcome defined as the first occurrence of any of these events. Participants were followed up from the day in which right heart catheter was performed until outcomes of interest, loss to follow-up, or the end of follow-up (i.e., December 2024).

Covariates

Covariates were collected when right heart catheter was performed. Body mass index was defined as weight (in kilograms) divided by height (in meters) squared. World Health Organization (WHO) functional class was categorized into 4 classes based on patients’ report by experienced cardiologists at the time of admission. Although the WHO functional classification is a symptom-based assessment for PH patients, we also evaluated symptoms of no PH patients. Certified technicians at bedside measured blood pressure. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was computed using the estimation formula of the Japanese Society of Nephrology12 and chronic kidney disease (CKD) was defined as GFR<60 mL/min/1.73 m2.13 SvO2 was measured from the blood sample in the main trunk of the pulmonary artery. Cardiac output was calculated from blood oxygen saturation of main pulmonary artery and aortic artery using Fick method. Cardiac index was defined as cardiac output divided by body surface area. PVR was calculated using the difference between mPAP and PAWP divided by cardiac output.

In addition to these covariates, we collected data on comorbidities relevant to the pulmonary circulation from medical records. Anemia was defined as a hemoglobin concentration <12.0 g/dL.14 Left heart disease was defined by diastolic dysfunction, systolic dysfunction, left ventricular hypertrophy, and significant valvular heart disease whatever the etiology was, diagnosed by experienced cardiology physicians. Chronic lung disease covered an abnormality in lung function or imaging of the lungs diagnosed by experienced respiratory physicians. Additional medical history which was important but less related to PH was extracted using ICD-10 codes. Coronary heart disease comprised angina pectoris and myocardial infarction; stroke, cerebral infarction and intracerebral hemorrhage; and atrial fibrillation, both paroxysmal and persistent forms. Hypertension, dyslipidemia were defined and extracted in the same manner.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were compared among the no PH, mild PH, and conventional PH groups. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or as median (interquartile range, IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were described as count with proportion. Group comparisons were performed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables and χ2 test for categorical variables. We plotted Sankey diagram to describe the change of hemodynamic PH subtype in new 2022 PH definition from previous 2015 definition using “networkD3” R package. We compared the distribution of PH clinical classification between mild PH and conventional PH groups.

For the survival analysis, we first estimated the event-free survival rate using Kaplan-Meier method with the comparison among the groups by log-rank test. We ran Cox proportional hazards models to estimate hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI). We also explored potential predictors using Cox models across overall population and each PH group individually (i.e., mild PH and conventional PH). For multivariable analyses, the variance inflation factor (VIF) was used to avoid multicollinearity (VIF ≥5.0 suggests a significant correlation among variables). Each multivariable Cox model adjusted for age and sex was constructed due to the limited number of outcomes of interest in mild PH. Significance level was considered as 2-sided p value of <0.05. All analyses were conducted using R version 4.3.2 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Baseline characteristics

In a total of 1080 participants who underwent right heart catheterization, the mean age was 61.4 (SD 19.0 years) and 56.5% were male (Table 1). Of them, 391 patients (36.2%) were diagnosed as PH including mild PH (n = 137 [12.7%]) and conventional PH (n = 254 [23.5%]). Patients with mild PH represented 35% of all patients with PH. Compared with conventional PH group, older males were more frequently observed in mild PH group. Better clinical symptoms, vital signs, and hemodynamic parameters by right heart catheter measure were also observed in mild PH. For comorbidities, chronic lung disease, anemia, and CKD were more frequently observed in both mild and conventional PH groups compared with no PH group.

Hemodynamic PH subtype by new definition

According to the 2022 ESC/ERS guideline, individuals with mild PH were stratified into specific hemodynamic subtypes (Figure 1 and Supplementary Table S4). Approximately half of this cohort (71/137 [51.8%]) was newly categorized as pre-capillary PH, representing the largest proportion within the mild PH group. Consequently, the total number of pre-capillary PH patients defined by the 2022 guideline nearly doubled compared to the 2015 guideline (N = 172 vs. 90). Following pre-capillary PH, isolated post-capillary PH was the second most common classification among mild PH patients (36/137 [26.3%]). Approximately 40% of patients classified as isolated post-capillary PH under the 2015 guideline were reclassified as combined post- and pre-capillary PH according to the 2022 criteria. Conversely, all patients originally identified as combined post- and pre-capillary PH under the 2015 guideline retained their classification in the 2022 framework. Consequently, while the total number of isolated post-capillary PH cases remained comparable between the two guidelines, the prevalence of combined post- and pre-capillary PH increased. The 'unclassified PH' category, introduced in the 2022 guideline, consisted predominantly of patients with mild PH.

PH clinical classification

Regarding clinical classification, although the proportion of PH subtypes differed significantly between the mild and conventional PH groups, the overall hierarchical pattern remained consistent (Supplementary Figure S2). In both mild and conventional PH groups, Group 2 PH was the most prevalent (∼60%), followed by Group 1 PH (∼25%). However, the etiological profile within Group 1 PH differed between the two groups (Supplementary Table S3). Notably, while idiopathic PAH was the second most common etiology in the conventional PH group, it was virtually absent in the mild PH group (N = 1).

Survival analysis

A total of 228 composite outcome was recorded during the follow-up period (median [Q1-Q3], 42.0 [16.1–60.1] months). Compared with no PH group, mild and conventional PH groups showed the higher incidence of the composite outcome (14.4% vs. 21.9% vs. 39.0%, log-rank p <0.001) (Figure 2). In the Cox model, mild and conventional PH was significantly associated with the risk of the composite outcome compared with no PH (HR 1.74 [95% CI 1.15–2.61] and 3.10 [2.35–4.10]) (Table 2). A similar pattern was observed in the association of mild and conventional PH with the risk of each outcome including all-cause mortality and heart failure hospitalization (Table 2 and Supplementary Figure S3). Regarding mode of death, cardiac death was more frequently observed compared with non-cardiac death in both mild and conventional PH groups (Supplementary Table S5). In further analysis for cause of cardiac death, most death events were no PH-related, while PH-related death was associated with conventional PH not with mild PH and no PH (5.4%, 0%, and 0.001%, respectively). According to PH clinical classification, PH associated with lung diseases and/or hypoxia showed the worst prognosis regardless of PH phenotypes (i.e., overall PH, mild PH, and conventional PH) (Figure 3).

Discussion

In this retrospective, cohort study, the revised PH definition in the 2022 ERS/ESC guideline was associated with the changes in the population distribution based on hemodynamic and clinical classification of PH compared with the previous definition in the 2015 guideline. The number of patients with PH increased approximately 1.5-fold with each incremental mild PH patients with mPAP 21–24 mmHg. In hemodynamic classification, pre-capillary and combined post- and pre- capillary PH patients were increased after 2022 ESC/ERS PH guideline due to lowering the mPAP and PVR thresholds. Regarding PH clinical classification, Group 2 PH was the most prevalent classification, followed by Group 1 PH in both groups. Mild PH was associated with an intermediate prognosis: outcomes were more favorable than in conventional PH but inferior to those observed in individuals without PH. Moreover, while the two PH phenotypes appeared to share a common risk factor profile including some comorbidities, hemodynamic parameters were associated with survival outcomes exclusively in conventional PH not in mild PH.

While recent literature has documented shifts in patient demographics between mild and conventional PH following the 2022 ESC/ERS guideline revisions, 7, 8, 9, 10, 15 the present study distinguishes itself through several novel contributions. First, mild PH accounted for approximately one-third of all PH cases, indicating that this substantial proportion is concentrated within the narrow mPAP range of 21–24 mmHg at a general hospital. Second, we provide a clear visual delineation of the hemodynamic reclassification from the 2015 to the 2022 criteria utilizing Sankey diagram. Third, our analysis offers a granular comparison of the specific clinical classification subgroups between the mild and conventional PH cohorts. Fourth, we independently evaluated the prognostic risk factors associated with mild PH versus conventional PH. Finally, whereas the majority of prior investigations have relied on highly selected cohorts from specialized PH centers, our study captured a broader, more representative cross-section of patients by including consecutive individuals undergoing right heart catheterization in routine clinical practice.

The shifts in hemodynamic PH classification observed in our cohort following the application of the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines align with findings from recent literature.9 The lowered diagnostic thresholds for both mPAP and PVR inherently resulted in an increased prevalence of both pre-capillary PH and combined post- and pre-capillary PH. Conversely, while the absolute number of patients categorized with isolated post-capillary PH remained stable, the clinical phenotype and composition of this subgroup were substantially altered by the revised definitions. Furthermore, our analysis highlighted that the newly established "unclassified PH" category—comprising patients with PVR ≤2 Wood units and PAWP ≤15 mmHg—predominantly consisted of individuals with mild PH. These findings suggest that this mild, unclassified phenotype may represent an early stage of pulmonary vascular remodeling that precedes the more pronounced hemodynamic derangements characteristic of conventional PH.

Consistent with prior epidemiological reports identifying left heart disease as the primary etiology of PH,16 our data demonstrated that Group 2 PH constituted the largest proportion of clinical classifications in both the mild and conventional PH cohorts. Notably, however, we observed a distinct distribution of etiologic subgroups (e.g., idiopathic PAH and connective tissue disease-associated PAH) within Group 1 when comparing the mild and conventional disease states. This divergence suggests that the initiation and clinical progression of mild PH, particularly within Group 1, may be driven by a different interplay of risk factors and pathophysiological mechanisms than those traditionally associated with conventional PH. On the other hand, our survival analysis suggest that Group 3 PH and respiratory comorbidities may be key contributors to adverse outcomes even in mild PH. Advanced lung disease and chronic hypoxemia may underlie the poorer prognosis observed in this subgroup, consistent with previous reports. However, the number of Group 3 PH patients in the mild PH group was limited in our study, larger studies are needed to validate this finding.

Mild PH was associated with an intermediate prognosis: outcomes were superior to those of conventional PH but inferior to the no PH population. While both PH phenotypes appeared to share a largely common risk profile, the determinants of adverse outcomes diverged. In conventional PH, specific hemodynamic markers—PAWP >18 mmHg and SvO₂ <60%—were identified as significant prognostic risks. In contrast, within the mild PH cohort, no hemodynamic parameters but comorbidities (chronic lung disease, anemia, CKD) were significantly associated with prognosis. Furthermore, in our study, PH-related deaths occurred almost exclusively in conventional PH group, whereas no PH-related deaths were observed in mild PH group, suggesting that patients with mild PH may be more likely to die from comorbidities, such as underlying lung or left heart disease, rather than from pulmonary vascular disease itself. These findings suggest that in mild PH, comorbidities play a more pivotal role in driving worsening outcomes than hemodynamic status and the management strategies for mild PH may prioritize comorbidity optimization. Although there is a few studies assessing the treatment for mild PH 17, 18, 19, the management strategy for mild PH is uncertain, and further study is warranted.

Several limitations of this study warrant acknowledgment. First, its retrospective, single-center observational design and relatively small sample size necessitate that our findings should be interpreted as exploratory. Specifically, because our institution serves as a high-volume referral center for congenital heart disease in Japan, PH associated with congenital heart disease constituted the largest proportion of Group 1 PAH cases in this cohort. However, patient referral profiles remained consistent across the pre- and post-guideline revision periods; thus, this institutional referral bias is unlikely to have compromised the integrity of our comparative analyses. Following this pilot study, larger multicenter studies focusing on mild PH within each PH group are warranted. Second, the study cohort was exclusively composed of Japanese patients. Consequently, the generalizability of our clinical and hemodynamic findings to non-East Asian racial and ethnic populations remains uncertain and warrants careful interpretation and future cross-cultural validation. Third, while including all patients who underwent right heart catheterization—regardless of pre-existing clinical suspicion for PH—strengthens the real-world applicability of our hemodynamic screening, it inherently introduces a sampling bias. Patients with mildly elevated mPAP who lacked overt clinical indications for right heart catheterization were excluded from this cohort, which may result in an underestimation of the true prevalence of mild PH in the broader population. Finally, although unmeasured confounding variables and selection biases cannot be eliminated in a retrospective analysis, their overall impact is mitigated given the predominantly descriptive and phenomenological aims of the present study.

Conclusions

Application of the ESC/ERS 2022 guideline to a general hospital cohort undergoing right heart catheterization resulted in a 1.5-fold increase in PH diagnoses, primarily driven by the inclusion of individuals with mild PH. Phenotypically, mild PH was predominantly characterized by pre-capillary hemodynamics and Group 2 clinical classification. Although patients with mild PH exhibited an intermediate prognosis—superior to conventional PH but inferior to the no PH population—, adverse outcomes appeared to be driven more by comorbidities than by hemodynamic severity. Further research is needed to define optimal management strategies in mild PH.

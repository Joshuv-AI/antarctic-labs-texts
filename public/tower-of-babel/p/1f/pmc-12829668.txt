Immunohistochemical expression of carbonic anhydrase IX in oral squamous cell carcinoma – A retrospective clinicopathological analysis in a tertiary care teaching hospital
Authors: Ramya Katta, P. Sindhur Rani Duvvuri, Radhika Medidi, Aparna Chinnam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) ranks as the sixth most prevalent cancer globally, with approximately one third of cases originating from India. Carbonic anhydrase IX (CA IX) is expressed essentially in tumor cells and is considered as a marker for tumor hypoxia. The present study aims to assess the association between immunohistochemical expression of CA IX in the tumor cells and the clinicopathological parameters of OSCC.Materials and Methods:The present study is a 1 year retrospective study performed in a tertiary care teaching hospital. All radical specimens of OSCC were included in the study, and histopathological and immunohistochemical analysis for CA IX expression was performed manually in all cases. Statistical analysis was done using Statistical Package for the IBM Social Sciences (SPSS) software (version 26) (Armonk, New York, USA). The Chi squared test was used to calculate the value of significance (P).Results:Of the 39 OSCCs included in the present study, 41.02% cases showed expression of CA IX. CA IX expression was seen to be significantly associated with presence of lymphovascular invasion, higher T and N stages, and higher depth of invasion.Conclusion:High CA IX expression in OSCC is a poor prognostic factor, and in view of availability of CAIX targeted therapeutic agents, it is essential to further study its use in treatment of such cases.

INTRODUCTION

Tumor hypoxia is a nonphysiological condition that occurs as a result of uncontrolled cell proliferation, abnormal vascularity, and altered metabolism of a tumor tissue.[1] Hypoxia being a lethal microenvironment promotes emergence of a subclone of tumor cells with higher genetic instability and invasiveness which have adapted to hypoxia and have gained resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy.[1] Of all the signaling pathways that get activated during this process, hypoxia inducible factor-1 pathway has a major role. Its downstream targets like carbonic anhydrases (CAs), vascular endothelial growth factors, E-cadherin, and beta catenin promote angiogenesis, epithelial mesenchymal transition, metastasis, and cell cycle arrest conferring tumor cells better survival and resistance to treatment. CAs (a family of 12 members) are trans-membrane metalloproteases involved in cell acid base balance and cellular metabolism.[2] Of all the CAs, CA IX is expressed essentially in tumor cells and is considered as a marker for tumor hypoxia.[1]

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) ranks as the sixth most prevalent cancer globally, with approximately one third of cases originating from India.[34] The significant morbidity and mortality associated with this cancer have spurred efforts to pinpoint a dependable prognostic and therapeutic target. The present study aims to assess the association between immunohistochemical expression of CAIX in the tumor cells and the clinicopathological parameters of OSCC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present study is a 1-year retrospective analysis (July 2022 to June 2023) performed in the Department of Pathology of a tertiary care teaching hospital. Institutional ethical clearance was taken before the commencement of the study GMC/IEC/21/2023 dated 21.11.2023. All the cases which were diagnosed as OSCC, which underwent radical surgeries, were included in the study. Cases which were diagnosed on small biopsies and cases which underwent neoadjuvant therapy before radical surgery were excluded. After the application of both the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 39 cases were included in the study. Clinical features, demographic facts, location of the primary tumor, and clinicopathological parameters were retrieved from the departmental archives.

Specimens received during the study period were routinely fixed in 10% buffered formalin and grossed; representative bits were processed using an automatic tissue processor (Leica TP1020), sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin stain. All the cases were re-examined by a qualified pathologist for pathological parameters like differentiation, depth of invasion, lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion, and lymph nodal status.

One representative tumor section from each of the cases included in the present study was subjected to manual immunohistochemical staining for CAIX (rabbit monoclonal antibody clone EP161, pathnsitu biotechnologies) using the manufacturer’s recommendations and protocol. Renal clear cell carcinoma was used as a positive control for all cases. Tissue sections were deparaffinized in two changes of xylene, rehydrated in three changes of descending alcohols. Antigen retrieval was performed using heat-induced epitope retrieval by means of a pressurized decloaker in citrate buffer. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using hydrogen peroxide and nonspecific binding with the blocking solution that was provided by the manufacturer. This prevented background staining. Tissue sections were incubated with primary antibody (CAIX) for 1 hour at 24°C, followed by incubation with secondary antibody (linked to Horse Radish Peroxidase) for 30 minutes at 24°C. 3,3’ Diaminobenzidine was used as a detection system to visualize the antibody binding. Counterstaining with hematoxylin was performed. Finally, tissue sections were dehydrated with a series of ascending alcohols mounted with Dibutylphthalate Polystyrene Xylene and cover slipped. All slides were examined by two qualified pathologists simultaneously, and a consensus score was achieved. Only cells which showed membranous expression of CAIX were considered to be positive. Immunoreactive score (IRS score) [Table 1], where percentage of positive cells and intensity of staining is considered, was used to calculate expression of CAIX in the tumor cells.[56] IRS scores between 0 and 3 (both included) were considered negative, and scores above 3 were considered positive for CAIX.

Results were tabulated in Microsoft Excel 2020 version, and statistical analysis was done using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software (version 26). The data were tested for normality using Shapiro–Wilk test, following which Chi-squared test was used to calculate the value of significance (P). A P ≤ 0.05 was considered significant.

Patient demographics

The mean age of the study population in the present cohort was 52.41 ± 13.62 years (range: 30–76 years) with prominent male preponderance (the male-to-female ratio was 3.87:1). The most common location for the tumor was buccal mucosa (43.5%), followed by lateral border of the tongue (35.8%).

Histopathological features

Of the 39 OSCCs included in the present study, most cases showed well to moderate differentiation (74.35% and 23.07%, respectively). Lymphvascular invasion was seen in 48.71% (19 cases) and perineural invasion was seen in 12.82% (5 cases) of cases. Most cases had a depth of invasion greater than 10 mm (43.5% cases), with the most common stage being T4 (35.89% cases) and nodal status of N0 (51.28% cases).

CAIX expression on tumor cells: Of the 39 tumors included in the present study, 16 cases showed moderate to strong membranous expression of CAIX on the neoplastic cells (41.02% cases). On statistical evaluation, it was found that CAIX immunohistochemical expression on neoplastic cells was significantly associated with higher T and N statuses, higher depth of invasion, and presence of lymphovascular invasion. However, there was no significant association with age, gender, location of tumor, differentiation, and perineural invasion [Figure 1a-j].

DISCUSSION

CAIX is a marker of tissue hypoxia and is essentially expressed in many malignant tumors, including carcinoma of cervix,[7] breast,[8] esophagus,[9] intestine,[10] lung,[11] bladder,[12] and kidney.[13] In most tumors, except clear cell carcinoma of the kidney, membranous expression of CAIX has been associated with poorer prognosis and reduced disease free survival and overall survival. However, there are very few studies which have utilized immunohistochemistry for analyzing CAIX expression and its implications in OSCC.[14]

In the present study, 41.06% of cases showed moderate to high membranous expression of CAIX in tumor cells, with no expression in the stromal compartment. However, the percentage of CAIX positivity and pattern of positivity in neoplastic cells has been highly variable in different studies, ranging from as low as 15.19%[15] to as high as 98%.[16] This high heterogeneity in pattern of expression (cytoplasmic/membranous/both) and percentage of positivity can be explained by various factors like inclusion and exclusion criteria considered (post neoadjuvant therapy or not),[17] immunohistochemical clones used,[18] assay conditions followed,[18] scoring methods used,[14] cutoff values considered,[1519] use of tissue microarrays, focal expression of marker,[18] and selection bias[1418] as most studies were retrospective and single-centered.

In the present study, expression of CAIX was limited to the tumoral component only. However, in a study by Brockton et al. the authors found a significant association between stromal expression of CAIX and higher nodal metastasis and poorer survival.[20] They hypothesized that tumor stroma plays an important role in maintaining the hypoxic and acidic milieu for the tumor cells and thus enhancing their adaptability.

In the present study, moderate to high CAIX expression in neoplastic cells has been shown to have a significant association with higher T and N stage, higher depth of invasion, and presence of lymphovascular invasion and accordingly CAIX may be used as a prognostic marker in OSCC. Our findings are in concordance with various studies.[14151619]

In the present study, where in we followed up the cases for a very short duration of 6 months, we found that three patients out of the entire cohort had local recurrence and all the three cases had moderate to high expression of CAIX. However, due to the short follow-up period, statistical significance for this was not obtained. Various studies have also found that high CAIX expression in tumor cells is associated with reduced disease-free survival and increased recurrence.[141718]

Due to the availability of CAIX inhibitors, CAIX has always remained a target of therapeutic interest in oncology.[20] However, their use in OSCC is still uncharted. There are some recent in vitro studies which have shown promising cytotoxic results in several OSCC cell lines.[2122] Thus, research into this area should be the next emphasis.

Limitations of the present study include its small sample size, shorter duration of follow-up, and absence of molecular profiling of the tumors. Furthermore, being a retrospective single center study, a selection bias may have been present.

CONCLUSION

In the present study, it was upheld that immunohistochemical expression of CAIX in OSCC is associated with presence of lymphovascular invasion, higher depth of invasion, and higher T and N stages, and thus, its expression is a poor prognostic marker in OSCC. In view of availability of CAIX targeted therapeutic agents, it is essential to further study its use in treatment of such cases.

The Role of 1,2-Palladium Migration/Insertion on C(sp3)–H Functionalization
Authors: Mohammad Sadegh Karimtabar, Yasin Mohammadkhani, Mohammad Mahdavi, Parviz Rashidi Ranjbar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Selective functionalization of C­(sp3)–H bonds remains challenging yet crucial for researchers. A frequently used approach by chemists to tackle this challenge involves leveraging 1,2-metal migration/insertion, which enables the selective activation and cleavage of a specific C­(sp3)–H bond adjacent to or distant from the directing group. Palladium is among the most commonly employed catalysts for C­(sp3)–H functionalization through metal migration/insertion, with Pd­(OAc)2 being the predominant palladium complex utilized in these processes. 1,2-Palladium migration is a key strategy for achieving selective C­(sp3)–H functionalization. After palladium coordinates to a directing group, its migration can activate the nearby C­(sp3)–H bond. With subsequent migrations, more distant C­(sp3)–H bonds can be targeted and transformed into C­(sp3)–functional groups. During the 1,2-insertion of palladium, the migration of palladium takes place following the activation of the C­(sp3)–H bond. This migration step typically involves the palladium inserting into a double bond, which links the C­(sp3)–H bond to a C­(sp2)–H bond, thereby progressing the reaction. Ultimately, this review highlights that C­(sp3)–H functionalization via 1,2-palladium migration/insertion has the potential to selectively modify both proximal and remote C–H bonds in organic molecules, offering a valuable tool for researchers to synthesize a wide range of organic compounds in future studies. This account encompasses all types of 1,2-palladium migration/insertion and examines their impact on C­(sp3)–H functionalization. It provides a detailed analysis of the mechanisms involved and explores how these migrations enable the activation of both remote and proximal C­(sp3)–H bonds with the directing group.

Introduction

C–H functionalization enables the direct transformation of inert C–H bonds into C-FG (FG = carbon, heteroatom), eliminating the need to activate C–X bonds (X being a heteroatom), which is advantageous in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals and natural products. − It is noteworthy that C–H bond functionalization through C–H activation offers novel strategies for synthesis and drug modification, making the process more efficient and reducing the number of steps involved. −

Activating C­(sp3)–H bonds poses a considerable challenge in organic chemistry due to their low acidity and the strong stability of these bonds. Because experimental BDE values are often unavailable for individual C­(sp3)–H bonds, distinguishing between similar bonds, such as diastereotopic hydrogens, remains difficult. To address these issues, transition-metal-catalyzed, coordination-assisted functionalizationparticularly using palladium catalystshas become a widely adopted approach for the effective activation of nonactivated C­(sp3)–H bonds. − In recent years, it has been demonstrated that palladium migration strategies can be highly effective for achieving selective C­(sp3)–H functionalization across various types of C­(sp3)–H bonds (Figure ). One effective palladium migration that assists researchers in achieving selective C­(sp3)–H bond functionalization is 1,2-palladium migration, which occurs during the reaction process. When the 1,2-palladium migration occurs only once, it selectively activates the C­(sp3)–H bonds near the directing group (Figure a). However, if the migration occurs multiple times during the reaction (chain walking), then it can activate remote C­(sp3)–H bonds within the substrate (Figure b). Similar to 1,2-palladium migration, 1,2-insertion can be utilized to activate both remote and proximal C­(sp3)–H bonds with the directing group. Furthermore, this type of insertion typically facilitates the subsequent activation of both C­(sp3)–H and C­(sp2)–H bonds (Figure c). This review covers all aspects of 1,2-palladium migration and insertion, emphasizing their critical roles in C­(sp3)–H functionalization. It highlights how these processes facilitate the activation of both distant and nearby C­(sp3)–H bonds in the vicinity of directing groups. The discussion includes detailed mechanisms and demonstrates how specific 1,2-palladium migration and insertion techniques can be employed to achieve the precise and selective functionalization of C­(sp3)–H bonds. It is worth noting that 1,4- and 1,5-palladium migrations can be effective strategies for C­(sp3)–H functionalization; however, in this review, we do not address these types of migrations in the context of C­(sp3)–H functionalization. −

1.2-Palladium Migration (Single and Sequential)

1,2-Palladium migration is one of the effective methods for C­(sp3)–H functionalization that was observed in many reactions for remote and near C­(sp3)–H bonds. In this article, 1,2-palladium migration is initially categorized into two distinct groups. The first group covers reactions where the 1,2-palladium migration occurs only once, whereas the second group includes reactions involving multiple 1,2-palladium migration steps (chain walking) (Figure ). In the first group, the reactions generally lead to the functionalization of proximal C­(sp3)–H bonds near the directing group, while in the second group, the functionalization extends to more distant, remote C­(sp3)–H bonds.

Different types of 1,2-palladium migration on C­(sp3)–H functionalization.

It was noteworthy that the single 1,2-palladium migration was done through two special mechanisms: in a more common method, the sequential β-hydrogen elimination and 1,2-insertion force a 1,2-palladium migration in the reaction (Scheme a). However, in a different mechanism, 1,2-palladium migration takes place through a 1,2-aryl (alkyl)/palladium dyotropic rearrangement (Scheme b). − This process involves intermediate A, where the palladium and aryl/alkyl groups simultaneously migrate to adjacent (vicinal) carbon atoms. Sequential 1,2-palladium migration involves multiple β-hydrogen elimination and 1,2-insertion, allowing palladium to migrate to more distant carbon atoms (n > 3) (Scheme c). It is important to note that chain walking can be divided into two mechanisms: in one mechanism, after each 1,2-palladium migration, the exchange process is considerably slower than the 1,2-insertion process. In this case, substrate alkenes are directly transformed into the expected product without the formation of intermediate alkenes from the metal center, which is termed the “nondissociative” chain walking route. In contrast, the other mechanism has a 1,2-insertion process that is much slower than the exchange process. In this scenario, different alkene intermediates can be separated before the reaction is complete, which is known as the “stepwise” chain walking pathway and is generally less efficient than the nondissociative chain walking pathway. Building on earlier points, palladium-catalyzed C­(sp3)–H functionalization using 1,2-palladium migration is a new and useful method to selectively activate and modify C–H bonds that are difficult to access directly. This approach broadens the options available for synthetic chemistry, allowing for more precise changes to a wider range of C­(sp3)–H sites. This Perspective focuses on recent progress in palladium-catalyzed C–H functionalization that involves 1,2-palladium migration or insertion, showing how these techniques have become an important emerging trend. To organize this review, the next sections will first classify the methods based on the type of 1,2-palladium migration and then discuss how palladium insertion into double bonds can help functionalize both nearby and distant C–H bonds relative to the directing group.

Different Mechanisms of 1,2-Palladium Migration on C­(sp3)–H Functionalization

Single 1,2-Palladium Migration

Single 1,2-palladium migration typically facilitates the functionalization of C­(sp3)–H bonds adjacent to the directing group rather than at remote sites. This migration occurs via two distinct mechanisms: the first involves β-hydride elimination followed by 1,2-insertion, producing an alkene intermediate that leads to the final product; the second proceeds through a dyotropic rearrangement, where palladium is simultaneously replaced by an aryl or alkyl group. In the β-hydride elimination pathway, palladium exchanges with a hydrogen atom, whereas in the dyotropic rearrangement, palladium is substituted by an aryl or alkyl group. In this section, we first consider the first group and then the second group. This section first describes the β-hydride elimination and 1,2-insertion mechanism, where palladium exchanges with hydrogen to form an alkene intermediate, leading to the product. It then discusses a second mechanism involving a dyotropic rearrangement, where palladium is simultaneously replaced by an aryl or alkyl group.

In 2017, Messaoudi and colleagues introduced a novel technique for the construction of fused glycosylquinolin-2-ones 5 and spiro-glycosyloxindoles 6 via palladium-catalyzed activation of C­(sp3)–H bonds (Scheme ). In this regard, they reacted glycosides 4 with Pd­(OAc)2 using Cs2CO3 as a base and PCy3·HBF3 as a ligand in toluene at a temperature of 150 °C. In this reaction, if the R group attached to the reactant was OAc, glycosylquinolin-2-ones 5 were formed; if R was H, spiro-glycosyloxindoles 6 were produced. To form spiro-glycosyloxindoles, the process involves oxidative addition and C­(sp3)–H activation, followed by reductive elimination to produce product 6 (Scheme ). In contrast, for producing fused glycosylquinolin-2-ones, 1,2-palladium migration takes place after oxidative addition and C­(sp3)–H activation. This is followed by reductive elimination and an additional elimination step, resulting in the synthesis of product 5.

In 2019, Wu and colleagues introduced a novel method for synthesizing optically active tetrahydrofluorenes 9 by combining aryl iodides 7 with heteroatom or nucleophilic carbon 8 (Scheme ). This process involves a tandem asymmetric Heck insertion and Tsuji–Trost allylation utilizing a chiral phosphoramidite base ligand L1. As depicted in Scheme , intermediate B is produced from the oxidative addition of a palladium(0) complex to an aryl iodide, followed by a desymmetrizing Heck insertion. The palladium migrates along the carbon chain of intermediate B, forming a π-allylpalladium intermediate C, which then undergoes a backside intermolecular nucleophilic attack to yield product 9. It is noteworthy that the nucleophiles could include phenol, aniline, and malonate-type reagents.

Access to Chiral Tetrahydrofluorenes through Palladium-Catalyzed Enantioselective Tandem Intramolecular Heck/Tsuji–Trost Reaction

Following the asymmetric Heck cyclization of tethered 1,4-cyclohexadiene 7 by Wu and colleagues, Zhou’s team introduced the first enantioselective three-component Heck/amination cascade employing nonconjugated cyclic dienes 11 (Scheme ). This strategy combines two pivotal organometallic steps, alkene insertion and allylic substitution, to install aryl and amine motifs on cyclic dienes with precise stereochemical control across two adjacent stereocenters. The method offers a highly effective way for synthesizing chiral arylated cyclohexylamines, a class of compounds with significant pharmaceutical properties. A major challenge in developing this asymmetric transformation was the necessity for a weakly coordinating ligand that could temporarily stabilize the π-allylpalladium intermediate. This stabilization allows for nucleophilic attacks by amines, while resisting displacement by competing alkylamines in the reaction environment. The Kelliphite ligand not only fulfilled these requirements but also created a sterically defined chiral environment that guides amines to selectively attack the π-allyl complex from the side opposite the palladium center, thereby ensuring high enantioselectivity. During optimization, various amines were evaluated, yielding good to excellent results; however, smaller amines, such as diethylamine and pyrrolidine, were found to coordinate with cationic palladium species, inhibiting the catalytic cycle. Similarly, heteroaryl amines such as pyrimidine, pyridine, and benzoxazole failed to produce the product. Additionally, when other substituted 1,4-cyclohexadienes were used in reactions catalyzed by palladium/Kelliphite, multiple isomers were obtained with low stereoselectivity.

Asymmetric Three-Component Heck/Amination of Nonconjugated Cyclodienes

In 2017, Toste and his team presented a new technique for the 1,3-aryl fluorination of [2H]-chromenes 14 (Scheme ). This method showcased a 1,2-migration of palladium, attributed to a process involving β-hydride elimination followed by 1,2-insertion. In this reaction, they reacted chromene 14 with aryl boronic acid 15 in the presence of Selectfluor, ligand L3, and (2-Ethylhexyl-O2)­2PO2H, along with t-butyl catechol in a DCM/H2O solvent at room temperature. It was noted that they also proposed suitable conditions for 1,2-aryl fluorination instead of 1,3-aryl fluorination. Selectivity for the formation of vinylogous enol ethers that yield the 1,3-product is improved by increasing the electrophilic nature of palladium, either by employing bipyridine or phenanthroline ligands or by modifying the electronic properties of the aryl substrate 15. On the other hand, selectivity for the 1,2-product is improved by boosting the electron-donating ability of the amide directing group and decreasing ligand coordination.

Palladium-Catalyzed 1,3-Arylfluorination of Chromenes

The 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexane scaffold is an important part in various pharmaceutical compounds like antidiabetic agents and MAO-B inhibitors. Despite its significance, there are few general synthetic methods for these compounds. Thus, creating an efficient and stereoselective method for directly synthesizing 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes from simple and cost-effective starting materials is a challenging but desirable goal. In 2019, Yin developed a novel method for the construction of 1,3-disubstituted cyclohexanes 20 through the 1,3-arylboration of unconjugated dienes 17 (Scheme ). Their research group initially attempted to conduct this reaction using a nickel complex; however, they ultimately carried it out with Pd­(acac)2 in the presence of NaOAc as the base and TMAC as an additive in chloroform at 60 °C. Experiments with different salts revealed that both the anion and cation of the ammonium salt are essential for this reaction. The additive is likely involved in generating an active anionic palladium complex and/or stabilizing the palladium(0) catalyst to prevent precipitation. In this reaction, both electron-deficient and electron-rich aryl iodides 18 successfully produced products 20 in significant yields. The reaction shows remarkable compatibility with various functional groups (CN, CO, COOR, OTf, Cl, OR, and CONHR), allowing a diverse set of them to be effectively incorporated. The reaction began with the oxidative addition of an aryl iodide to palladium(0) 21, forming complex 22 (Scheme ). Subsequently, complex 22 was converted into complex 23 through the addition of TMAC. Complex III then reacted with reagent 17 to produce complex 24, which facilitated a 1,2-palladium migration, moving palladium from C2 to C3 and resulting in a stable π-allylpalladium­(II) complex, designated as complex 26. The outstanding stereoselectivity seen in the products indicates that the palladium-H does not separate from the substrate during this reaction. Transmetalation with B2pin2 19 generates intermediate 27, leading to the construction of the arylboration product 20 and the palladium(0) catalyst. It is important to mention that the palladium-H migratory insertion demonstrates a high degree of regioselectivity, leading to the elimination of 1,2- and 1,4-products.

Mecahnism of Stereoselective Palladium-Catalyzed 1,3-Arylboration of Unconjugated Dienes for Expedient Synthesis of 1,3-Disubstituted Cyclohexanes

To date, our discussion has focused primarily on the effects of 1,2-palladium migration on the functionalization of C­(sp3)–H bonds through a sequential process of β-hydrogen elimination, followed by 1,2-insertion. However, we now aim to explore the implications of 1,2-palladium migration in conjunction with 1,2-aryl­(alkyl)/palladium dyotropic rearrangement. This approach has proven valuable for constructing carbon quaternary centers, which are difficult to form through conventional methods. − In particular, activating methine C–H bonds to create a quaternary carbon center is quite difficult. Typically, the metalation of primary C­(sp3)–H bonds is simpler than that of methylene C­(sp3)–H bonds, which is more accessible than the activation of methine C–H bonds. While there are exceptions to this trend, creating tertiary C­(sp3)–metal species via C–H activation is very difficult, with only a few studies focusing on bridged structures and carbons in cyclopropane rings. Before 2021, researchers primarily relied on methodologies involving C–C bond activation for the construction of quaternary C­(sp3)–FG bonds. These methods often utilize strain-liberating separation of small rings to diminish the unfavorable kinetic and thermodynamic challenges that come with breaking C–C bonds. Since the formation of enantioselective C–F bonds through C–C activation has not yet been explored, 1,2-palladium migration aids in this reaction. In 2021, Zhu and his team discovered that vicinal C–C and C–palladium­(IV) bonds can interchange stereospecifically under mild conditions. This finding enables the creation of quaternary carbon–palladium bonds, which can be converted into C­(sp3)–F bonds (Scheme ). In their experiments, prochiral cyclopentene 28 was reacted with 3-methoxyphenylboronic acid 29, Selectfluor 30, and a catalytic amount of palladium acetate with ligand L4, in dichloroethane (DCE). Remarkably, this process not only splits an unactivated C–C bond but also at the same time forms both a new C–C bond and a C–palladium bond, paving the way for further transformations. The mechanism of this reaction differs entirely from the conventional syn-β-carbon elimination and carbopalladation pathways. Compounds such as benzofuran and electron-rich indole were not compatible with the standard conditions due to their interference with the Selectfluor reagent. The arylpalladation of 29, directed by the amide group, involves an ArPdX species formed in situ, leading to intermediate 32 (Scheme ). This intermediate undergoes isomerization to compound 33 via β-hydride elimination and reinsertion. The construction of a five-membered chelate between palladium and the amide group in intermediate 33 is a likely driving factor behind the palladium-walking mechanism. Oxidation of palladium­(II) species 33 to intermediate 34 is followed by a dyotropic rearrangement, producing complex 35 in its η3-complex form. Finally, reductive elimination of palladium complex 35 yields the final product 31. It was noted that they chose a fluorine donor that also served as an oxidant during the final reductive elimination step.

In 2022, Zhu and colleagues introduced the same strategy to create a novel method for synthesizing an amide in which the α-carbon is quaternary and contains a fluorine atom (Scheme ). Indeed, this reaction was performed via a palladium-catalyzed migrative carbofluorination of saturated amides 36 utilizing the activation of both the quaternary and the C­(sp3)–H bond. In this reaction, the activation of the C­(sp3)–H bond in the methyl group took place first, while simultaneously, palladium bonded with F+. This ultimately resulted in the formation of palladium­(IV) connected to carbon, the carbonyl group, and the fluorine atom. Subsequently, a concerted migration occurred, involving both the R1 and fluoride groups attaching to palladium, resulting in the formation of the desired product 38. Significantly, in every instance, the aryl group migrated favorably to the alkyl group, and the reaction was compatible with both weak electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents at different positions on the aromatic ring. In this reaction, when a methyl group was replaced with methylene or methyn, a greater amount of palladium and silver salts, along with a longer reaction time, were necessary to achieve complete conversion.

Zhu’s research group also reported a reaction where a methyl group connected to a quaternary carbon is transferred to an adjacent secondary carbon, accompanied by the oxidation of the quaternary C–C bond to a CC bond (Scheme ). In this domino reaction, palladium sequentially moves from the primary carbon to the secondary carbon and eventually to the quaternary carbon, leading to the simultaneous functionalization of primary, secondary, and quaternary carbons. The reaction began with the palladation of a methyl group in the amide, resulting in the formation of palladium­(II) complex 41, which is then converted to palladium­(IV) complex 42 (Scheme ). Complex 42 is converted to intermediate 44 via 1,3-palladium migration through a four-membered complex 43. A 1,2-methyl/palladium­(IV) dyotropic rearrangement from intermediate 44 would yield 45, which, through β-hydride elimination, would generate product 40 while simultaneously regenerating the palladium­(II) catalyst. It was proposed that the selective formation of the terminal alkene through β-hydride elimination results from a favored agostic interaction between palladium and one of the more accessible hydrogens in the methyl group. It is worth noting that intermediate 43 could be converted into a cyclopropane derivative through reductive elimination; however, this transformation does not occur in this reaction.

Sequential 1,2-Palladium Migration (Chain Walking)

As previously mentioned, 1,n-palladium migration was achieved through multiple β-hydride eliminations and 1,2-insertions, facilitating the activation of remote C­(sp3)–H bonds. This process occurs via two mechanisms: 1) nondissociative and 2) stepwise chain walking. In the first method, no alkene intermediates were observed (Scheme c). In contrast, the second mechanism allowed for the formation of an alkene intermediate alongside the desired product. It is important to note that in our examples, where the mechanism of 1,n-palladium migration is not specified, the reactions were conducted via the nondissociative method.

Kochi and coworkers demonstrated a stepwise strategy for remote bond formation via palladium-catalyzed hydrosilylation and annulation of various 1,n-dienes, enabled by the chain-walking ability of the palladium catalyst (Scheme ). This protocol proved effective for a broad range of 1,n-dienes, including challenging substrates such as a 1,13-diene, affording products containing cyclopentane featuring a C–Si bond at a remote position (compound 48). The key to this transformation lies in the palladium catalyst’s ability to undergo sequential chain walking, facilitating the construction of C–Si and carbon–carbon bonds separated by two to seven methylene units in a controlled fashion during the catalytic cycle. Notably, the reaction proceeded through a nondissociative mechanism, as no alkene intermediates were detected during the process. Initial studies performed on a 0.5 mmol scale using a variety of 1,n-dienesincluding those bearing cyclohexene units or linear alkene moietiesdelivered acceptable yields (up to 50%). The authors introduced a detailed mechanism for this hydrosilylation/cyclization sequence (Scheme ). The reaction begins with chloride abstraction by NaBArF4, generating a methylpalladium catalyst that engages with silane 47 to generate silylpalladium complex 48. Subsequent coordination and 1,2-insertion of terminal alkene 49 deliver intermediate 52. Deuterium-labeling studies confirmed that the palladium catalyst undergoes a chain-walking process, repositioning itself to allow coordination of a second alkene. Insertion of this alkene into the palladium–C bond leads to the formation of a five-membered ring. Finally, the resulting alkyl-palladium intermediate reacts with 47 to furnish product 55.

In 2019, the Sigman lab reported an enantioselective Heck arylation of alkenyl carbonyl 57 derivatives, enabling the synthesis of α,β-unsaturated compounds 58 featuring a remote stereocenter (Scheme ). The impact of the chain length on selectivity and yield was assessed by adding extra methylene groups between the aryl unit and the alkene. Ultimately, it was found that chain length did not affect either the yield or the selectivity. Compounds 58–5 and 58–6 were exposed to the standard reaction protocol, followed by hydrogenation, delivering the desired products in good yield. Notably, reactions involving (Z)-alkene substrates yielded the opposite enantiomer compared with those with (E)-alkene substrates, leading to the absolute configurations of 58–5 and 58–6 being assigned as (R). The authors initially proposed that adding an aryl group via a transmetallating reagent could produce a remotely difunctionalized product, but they soon noticed that the styrene product was formed preferentially with high enantioselectivity. To gain further insight, the authors investigated the reaction mechanism, focusing on the factors causing the formation of the styrene product to terminate the chain-walking process. Their findings revealed a correlation between the relative π* orbital energy and the selectivity of alkene isomers, suggesting that palladium coordination with the arene likely occurs during chain walking. As the arene’s electron-donating ability decreases, enhanced π back-bonding interactions with palladium favor the side-product formation via β-hydride elimination/migratory insertion at the benzylic position. Computational studies further indicated that palladium–phenyl group bonding interactions stabilize the palladium intermediates, promoting thermodynamic control toward the styrenyl product.

Palladium-Catalyzed Enantioselective Alkenylation of Alkenylbenzene Derivatives

In 2019, Yao and coworkers developed a palladium-catalyzed strategy for remote 1,n-arylamination, enabling a broad range of reactions covering from 1,3- to 1,11-arylamination of inert terminal alkenes using aryl iodides and arylamines (Scheme ). This three-component process proceeds through a sequential palladium-catalyzed Heck arylation, alkene isomerization, and aza-Michael addition, delivering products with high regio- and chemoselectivity. The introduction of radical scavengers such as TEMPO and BHT resulted in only slight decreases in product yields (58% and 49%, respectively), effectively ruling out a radical pathway. Furthermore, when 2-allylphenol 64 (75% deuterated) was subjected to the reaction, the product was obtained in 68% yield, with the deuterium atom migrating to the β-position relative to the phenol moiety. These results indicate that the palladium catalyst undergoes a chain-walking process via repetitive β-H elimination and reinsertion steps (Scheme ). Mechanistically, the reaction initiates with oxidative addition of the aryl iodide to the palladium catalyst to form arylpalladium intermediate 66. Syn-migratory insertion of intermediate 66 into the terminal alkene generates alkylpalladium species 67, which undergoes selective β-H elimination to furnish intermediate 68. Subsequent reinsertion of palladium–H into 68 yields intermediate 69. Continuously performing these steps results in the generation of benzyl palladium intermediate 70. Electron moves from the phenol oxygen to palladium, generating intermediate 71, which is proposed to serve as a thermodynamic driving force for alkene isomerization. Finally, aza-Michael addition between intermediate 71 and arylamines affords the expected remote 1,n-arylamination products 72.

Synthesis of Tetrahydroquinoline Derivatives and Seven-Membered Tetrahydrobenzo­[b]­azepines via Palladium-Catalyzed Migratory Cycloannulation

This innovative strategy provides a valuable pathway for the rapid and versatile synthesis of azaheterocycles, promising significant applications in the pharmaceutical industry. Initially, inactivated alkenes like but-3-enamide and allylbenzene were tested, but no desired products were obtained. Inspired by the directing group strategywhich enhances the reactivity of inactivated alkenes and ensures precise regioselectivity in alkene functionalizationthe team explored allylbenzene with a hydroxyl directing group. This modification successfully yielded the target migratory cycloannulation product. The optimization process had to solve two primary challenges: (1) managing the size of the resulting ring and (2) overcoming the low reactivity of the alkenes while minimizing potential byproducts such as Heck-type side products. By employing an optimal ligand, we suppressed the formation of Heck-type byproducts was suppressed. This supports the hypothesis that the ligand stabilizes palladium-H intermediates during the migratory process, thereby improving the reaction’s efficiency and selectivity. The substrate scope of 2-iodoaniline derivatives was explored under the optimized conditions, revealing broad compatibility with various functional groups. Substituents such as halides, esters, electron-withdrawing groups, and electron-donating groups were all well tolerated. Notably, the bromo group, often problematic in palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions, proved to be compatible in this system. Additionally, aliphatic alkyl groups and even the sterically bulky tert-butyl group were successfully synthesized, further highlighting the versatility of this methodology. However, 2-iodoanilines featuring electron-withdrawing protecting groups on the nitrogen atom, such as tosyl, carbobenzyloxy, and acetyl, were not observed. After thoroughly investigating the construction of tetrahydroquinoline derivatives, the attention was shifted to synthesizing other azaheterocycles. Seven-membered tetrahydrobenzo­[b]­azepines 77 were successfully produced. The reaction proceeded in synthetically useful yields under standard conditions and exhibited a broad functional group tolerance. The catalytic sequence begins with the oxidative addition of palladium complex 78 to N-benzyl-2-iodoaniline 73, forming the arylpalladium­(II) intermediate 79 (Scheme ). This intermediate undergoes a migratory insertion with alkene 74, delivering alkylpalladium­(II) species 80. Subsequently, a rapid β-hydride elimination and reinsertion sequencecharacteristic of a chain-walking processrelocates the palladium to the α-position relative to the phenolic moiety. In the presence of a base, this rearrangement generates a quinone methide intermediate 84, accompanied by the reduction of palladium­(II). The catalytic cycle is completed by an intramolecular aza-Michael addition, furnishing the desired products 85.

In 2023, the Zhi-Tao He group disclosed a palladium-catalyzed enantioselective migratory allylation, enabling directed, concurrent chain walking of remote diene systems (Scheme ). In contrast to traditional methods that are limited to monoolefin migration and functionalization, this approach has been applied to dienes for the first time, showcasing broad substrate applicability, impressive functional group tolerance, and strong stereocontrol. The authors noted that the efficiency of chain walking is highly dependent on the carbon chain length. When multiple olefins are involved, the process becomes significantly more intricate as only a single intermediate among numerous possibilities can yield the desired product. To further investigate this complexity, they examined more challenging diene substrates with extended migration pathways and, remarkably, found no noticeable impact on the efficiency or stereoselectivity of the allylation process. Based on experimental findings, initially, the low-valent palladium catalyst undergoes oxidation by a proton, generating an active palladium species that subsequently participates in migratory insertion with olefins (Scheme ). A sequence of iterative β-H elimination and reinsertion events (chain walking) leads to the formation of the common aryl-conjugated diene intermediate 89. This intermediate then undergoes hydrometalation, yielding the thermodynamically favored η3-π-allyl palladium complex 90, which ultimately undergoes asymmetric allylic substitution to afford the final product 91.

1,2-Insertion of Palladium to Double Bond

In the 1,2-insertion of palladium into a double bond, palladium moves from a specific carbon to a double bond, effectively shifting its position within the substrate. Although this process involves the relocation of palladium, it is typically not termed migration in the literature. While this insertion does not directly activate the C­(sp3)–H bond, it plays an essential responsibility in enabling reactions by facilitating the insertion of palladium into various molecules, such as alkenes and carbon monoxide (CO). In this approach, palladium migration through its insertion into different compounds (such as CO and alkenes) has been consistently observed following C­(sp3)–H functionalization. Because palladium insertion into the substrate takes place after C­(sp3)–H activation and does not directly impact the activation process itself, it is occasionally referred to as an indirect effect of palladium migration.

In 2024, the Yafei Ji lab reported a palladium-catalyzed (3 + 2) annulation of 2-methylbenzoic acid 92 with maleimide 93, achieved via C­(sp3)–H olefination (Scheme ). This work introduced a novel approach in which the carboxylic acid functioned as a traceless directing group, being eliminated during the reaction and thereby bypassing the need for a harsh decarboxylation step. Despite of various substituents and alkyl groups, no desired product was obtained from substrate 94–5, likely due to the strong coordination between the amide structure and palladium, which hindered the reaction. Ji and coworkers suggested a credible reaction mechanism (Scheme ): Initially, ligand exchange of the palladium species generates compound 95. Coordination of the palladium species to the methyl group induces C–H activation, forming a six-membered palladacycle intermediate 96. This is followed by a 1,2-migratory insertion of maleimide to produce intermediate 97 (indirect effect of palladium migration). Instead of undergoing β-H elimination, Ag-catalyzed decarboxylation happens, generating intermediate 98. Finally, palladium-catalyzed C–C cross-coupling yields the cyclic product 99, while the silver salt regenerates palladium0 species, completing the catalytic cycle.

Conclusions

C–H functionalization is a robust synthetic method that transforms inert C­(sp3)–H bonds into various functional groups. Researchers have devised several approaches to selectively target these inactive bonds, with 1,2-palladium migration/insertion of metal being one of them. Palladium is the preferred catalyst in this domain, as it can migrate to access the desired C­(sp3)–H bond, facilitating C–H activation and subsequent C–H functionalization. During the last several decades, palladium-catalyzed C–H functionalization through palladium migration/insertion has rapidly evolved into a significant research area. Ongoing advancements in this field have the potential to greatly enhance synthetic planning and execution. The emergence of numerous palladium migration/insertion methods and the introduction of innovative strategies have enabled the functionalization of more strategic C–H bonds, particularly remote C­(sp3)–H bonds, in organic compounds. Controlling the conditions involved in 1,2-palladium migration is vital for researchers because the extent of this migration influences which C­(sp3)–H bond in the molecule becomes reactive, leading to targeted functionalization. This review showed that the single 1,2-palladium migration occurs via two mechanisms: (1) through β-hydride elimination and 1,2-insertion, typically used for functionalizing allylic hydrogens; and (2) through dyotropic rearrangement, which activates the α and β positions of carbonyl compounds, often requiring the use of Selectfluor. Sequential 1,2-palladium migration usually is done between two double-bond systems like between two double bonds or between a double bond and an aryl. The 1,2-insertion of palladium into double bonds (including alkenes and carbonyl groups) is a key step in activating both C­(sp3)–H and C­(sp2)–H bonds, resulting in the formation of bonds between otherwise unreactive C­(sp3)–H and C­(sp2)–H. This process mainly enables C–C bond formation and can also be employed in the synthesis of rings. This review showed that 1,2-palladium migration/insertion could help us for functionalization of many C­(sp3)–H bonds in molecules that are near or remote to the directing group. Furthermore, future studies should focus on exploring 1,4- and 1,5-palladium migrations, along with investigating how the migration of other metals might affect C­(sp3)–H functionalization processes. These efforts hold the potential to develop highly efficient and versatile methods for synthesizing a wide range of organic compounds, advancing the field significantly.

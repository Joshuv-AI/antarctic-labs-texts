Sentiment analysis with echo state network and augmented water cycle algorithm
Authors: Lili Zhang, Abbas Habibi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSentiment analysis using machine learning has become increasingly popular and has received considerable attention in recent years. The sentiment analysis is a critical and challenging task, which require networks with high accuracy. This study utilized the IMDb movie reviews dataset, which comprises 50,000 English reviews (25,000 designated for training and 25,000 for testing) with an equal distribution of positive and negative classes. The dataset’s unique characteristics, such as spelling errors, varying text lengths, and abbreviations, necessitate a multi-phase and unconventional approach to sentiment analysis. The data was thoroughly preprocessed, which involved eliminating unwanted characters, correcting slang, removing stop words, tokenizing, stemming, and performing part-of-speech tagging. To achieve this, this research implemented two separate word embedding models, GloVe and Word2Vec, for vectorization. In this study, Echo State Network (ESN) has been utilized, as there are two sentiments to consider, including positive and negative. In the following, this network has been optimized using Augmented Water Cycle Algorithm (AWCA), thus enhancing the hyperparameters. It was demonstrated by the outcomes that using GloVe could help the suggested ESN-AWCA accomplish the values of 96.37%, 96.39%, 95.87%, and 96.87% for F1-score, accuracy, recall, and precision, respectively. Moreover, utilizing Word2Vec helped the suggested model accomplish the values of 96.23%, 96.12%, 95.76%, and 96.71% for F1-score, accuracy, recall, and precision, respectively. Overall, the proposed ESN-AWCA model demonstrated strong performance with both word embedding methods and outperformed the other models evaluated in the study. The statistical validation, the p value of 0.001 and effect sizes d > 1.1, demonstrated the superiority of the suggested model.Keywords: Sentiment analysis, GloVe, Word2Vec, Echo state network (ESN), Augmented water cycle algorithm (AWCA)Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

The Internet and its related web technologies have immensely changed the way society operates1. There are several ways and platforms, like Facebook2 and Twitter3, which have been employed to exchange ideas, promote business and trade, and share information. In general, people and users can keep in touch and communicate with other people by the use of Internet and well-known platforms, which have been developed. Moreover, another term is social media that has been frequently examined from multiple viewpoints, such as collecting business intelligence to improve service and product marketing, supervising for dangerous behaviors to identify and address cyber risks, and conducting sentiment analysis to evaluate and identify the emotional tone of the content4,5.

The examination of the significance of these tweets through a scientific view is known as sentiment analysis6,7. Sentiment analysis has been commonly employed to detect and categorize the emotional tone present in a piece of text. The objective is to assess whether a particular document belongs to a negative or positive category based on a defined classification8.

In order to recognize the potentially essential facts and patterns from massive datasets has been considered data mining. Occasionally, it has is also called discovery of knowledge in data9. The perceptions taken from data mining have been utilized through businesses for developing a solution and improvising their business. It has been considered a service of social networking that enables users to share post and utilized the messages with the number of 140 characters, which are called Tweets. Users active on the current platform use direct and brief communications to explain their opinions and ideas, improve their outcomes, and contend with myriad of users. All the individuals do not have a mutual connection all the time. In this context, the connection has been unguided or guided.

Opinion mining, commonly known as sentiment analysis, is an important type of text analysis that focuses on the recognition, examination, and extraction of opinion-driven text10–12. Its objective is to recognize positive and negative opinions and to measure the degree of negativity or positivity associated with an entity (which can include events, organizations, individuals, locations, topics, products, etc.)13. This field of study can be potentially used in numerous practical applications, where collected opinion data is used to help organizations or individuals make well-informed decisions. Typically, there are three main strategies for sentiment analysis, including the supervised approach which relies on machine learning, the unsupervised approach which is based on lexicon analysis, and a hybrid method combining both.

Representing words as numerical vectors in a matrix is known as embedding. This approach generates comparable vector representations of words that share similar meanings. It is also referred to as word vectorization, where each word gets converted into vector for input into neural networks. The mapping process usually takes place in a lower-dimensional space, although it might vary based on the size of the vocabulary.

There exist 2 diverse kinds of approaches, including probabilistic prediction and count-based methods. The forecast approach comprises employing and extracting the words from a text with the purpose of training a model. Word2Vec has been considered an effective probabilistic forecast technique, by the utility of skip-gram methods and CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words) to develop word vectors14–17.

Echo State Networks (ESNs) have been regarded as a type of recurrent neural network18,19. It has attracted interest due to their effectiveness and efficiency in a variety of tasks, including sentiment analysis. Of course, the role of neural network cannot be denied, since it has been used in other fields like breast cancer diagnosis20, coffee bean classification21, and Leukemia Detection22. The current article investigates the influence and use of ESNs in sentiment analysis, emphasizing their benefits compared to traditional models. ESNs possess a distinct architecture made up of three primary components, including an input layer, an output layer, and a reservoir (hidden layer). The benefits of ESNs in sentiment analysis include speed and efficiency, a simple structure, and adaptability.

Although LSTM, BERT-based, and Transformer models are commonly used in sentiment analysis, their resource requirements and high computational complexity create difficulties for real-life applications or situations with limited resources. Echo State Networks (ESNs) present an appealing alternative because of their lightweight structure, quick training (due to fixed reservoir weights), and demonstrated effectiveness in handling sequential data tasks. The Augmented Water Cycle Algorithm (AWCA) was chosen for optimizing hyperparameters because it offers greater convergence speed and accuracy in high-dimensional search contexts compared to traditional optimizers like GA and PSO, as shown in Table 2 and Fig. 2. This combination effectively mitigates efficiency shortcomings in standard models while preserving competitive accuracy.

Echo state network (ESN)

An Echo State Network (ESN) is a kind of artificial intelligence model that belongs to the class of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). It has been particularly developed to process sequential data, like time series and natural language28. An ESN has an echo state hidden layer that maintains internal states across long sequences and has been just affected by the current input.

In classical RNNs, the hidden state at any step of time relies on both the current input and the prior hidden state. This can make training more challenging as the relationships among the weights of the hidden units necessitate careful modifications to avoid the network getting stuck in local minima through the optimization process. Conversely, in the suggested network, the hidden state is influenced by the present input using a predetermined set of stochastically initialized weights. This training approach that has been simplified removes the necessity of understanding recurrent connections among hidden units. The ESN computes its output at each step of time by linearly combining the hidden state with the inputs, after applying a non-linear activation function. Because of its uncomplicated design, ESNs can efficiently learn intricate temporal patterns from sequences of raw input without requiring direct control for the transitions of the hidden state. Augmented water cycle algorithm

The WCA depends on the creation of random numbers to operate. The algorithm’s effectiveness is affected by these stochastic numbers’ quality. Usually, pseudo-random numbers are produced utilizing Gaussian or uniform distributions within WCA. Nevertheless, in optimization studies, chaos maps have proven to be a more efficient alternative, which gives superior results compared to pseudo-random numbers.

The Iterated Chaos Map (ICM) is a type of chaotic mapping that has been illustrated as follows:

When compared to generator of a uniformly distributed pseudo-random number, the ICM () generates random amounts that exhibit a broader and more evenly spread range of fluctuations.

The ICM produces random numbers that range between − 1 and 1, which facilitates the algorithm’s extensive exploration and discovery of enhanced solutions. This investigation shows that the original WCA’s performance has been enhanced by integrating the NMs approach and ICM technique.

Initially, the pseudo-random amounts in the WCA are substituted with chaotic random numbers from the ICM. This modification expands the global search space of the population, and aids in the identification of additional possible solutions and the enhancement of the current best solution. Consequently, the NMs approach has been employed to improve quality of the solution by development of the current optimal solution.

Methodology

Dataset

The current article is carried out to evaluate ESN/AWCA and classify sentiment of text by employing IMDb reviews at the same time. This dataset contains 50k reviews of movie extracted from IMDb, which has been developed by Andrew Maas32. The data are split into two equal parts, 25k reviews for training and 25k for testing purposes. Additionally, the training and testing sets have 12.5k negative and positive reviews. The reviews have been categorized into negative and positive polar. In this case, the viewers are allowed to rate the movies from 1 to 10. Moreover, anything that is small or equal to 4 stars has been regarded as negative, and anything that is bigger of equal to 7 stars has been regarded as positive31. Reviews falling outside the specified ratings range are excluded. Each movie has a maximum of 30 reviews. The mean word count per review is 234.76 that has standard deviation of 172.91 words. In total, the dataset comprises 88,585 distinct words. This dataset can be visited at https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/imdb-movie-reviews-dataset.

Data cleaning

The first phase of the present design involves cleaning data. In this stage, the gathered data has been moved from the files and stored in memory for the purpose of cleaning. This phase consists of three sub-steps, including removal of unwanted characters, correction, and removal of stop words, all of which have been explained in the following.

Removal of unwanted characters

In the present phase, some unnecessary attributes, like online links and web addresses are removed from the script while utilizing enhanced regular words.

Correction

In the present phase, it has been endeavored to correct each abbreviated slang and word that have been utilized on online chats. Established maps and dictionaries have been used to translate slangs or abbreviations into their original and abbreviated formats. For instance, “GN” is converted to “Good Night”. This procedure has been considered highly beneficial for later stages, as abbreviated terms can create confusion during sentiment analysis.

Removal of stop words

There are several common words, like “under”, “a”, “above”, and “an”, which have been removed throughout the present stage. Totally, these words cannot impact the process of NLP; they cannot help the network to classify the data. Hence, the words have been considered unnecessary and redundant. Stanford CoreNLP Stopwords is utilized in this article, which can be visited at https://github.com/pepperkit/corenlp-stop-words-annotator.

Data preprocessing

It has been mentioned previously that before the data get inserted into the neural network, there are several procedures and stages which should be conducted, including word stemming, part-of-speech tagging, and tokenization. Of course, these stages prepare the data for the network.

Tokenization

This is the first move in pre-processing stage, where the text has been divided into some tokens, words, phrases, and symbols that have meaning. LingPipeTokenizer has been developed by Apache Lucene package33 that is utilized to tokenize the text. It should be mentioned that frameworks of regular data are modeled to maintain tokens and sentences of the text.

Stemming

In the current process, the inflected words are reduced to its base form. In this case, porter-2 algorithm34 is employed for converting the each token into stem model, then the tokens are saved in the keyword and the original token.

Part-of-speech tagging

This move has been considered a procedure, where the words have been tagged according to the definition and context. To this end, Maxent Tagger is employed35. The entities have part-of-speech tag, original format, the token’s stem form relevant to the tokens. Once the data are preprocessed, it is transferred to the next block.

Neural network and algorithm

In the present phase, all the data have been fed into Echo State Network (ESN) that is optimized via AWCA. In the following, the purpose is extracting and classifying the features. The current phases are described in the above section. The enhanced dataset possesses some diverse attributes, once the pre-processing stage has been completed. Subsequently, the adjectives have been employed for representing the positive and negative side of each sentence that have been regarded valuable for ascertaining the individuals’ insights. Once the adjectives have been extracted, it is turn to segregate the them. The words existing before and after each adjective gets eliminated. To exemplify, once the phrase is “a hardworking teacher”, the words “a” and “teacher” is omitted. After that, each token gets categorized as negative or positive polar.

Validation of the results

To thoroughly verify the performance claims, a two-phase statistical analysis has been carried out. Initially, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests has been performed on the prediction results from all 25,000 test samples, comparing ESN-AWCA to other baseline models using both GloVe and Word2Vec embeddings. These non-parametric tests were chosen for their resilience to non-normal distributions and their ability to detect consistent performance differences. Subsequently, Cohen’s d effect sizes have been computed to measure the extent of improvements beyond statistical significance.

The Wilcoxon tests affirmed that the proposed ESN-AWCA significantly outperformed all baseline models. ESN-AWCA exhibited statistically greater accuracy compared to BiLSTM/ELMo (z = 5.12, p < 0.001), BiLSTM/MultiBERT6L (z = 5.87, p < 0.001), ESN (z = 6.45, p < 0.001), K-NN (z = 6.92, p < 0.001), LSTM (z = 6.15, p < 0.001), and BERT (z = 7.01, p < 0.001). Similar significant outcomes (all p < 0.001) were observed using Word2Vec embeddings, indicating consistent superiority irrespective of the embedding method. Conclusion

There has been some development as a result of the advancement of the artificial intelligence. The primary goal of the current study is suggesting a novel model for categorizing polarities of sentences by the use of the suggested Echo State Network (ESN) optimized by Augmented Water Cycle Algorithm (AWCA). In contrast to models that require significant resources (such as BERT and Transformers), the ESN/AWCA framework provides a computationally efficient option while maintaining accuracy. The combination of ESN’s quick sequential processing and AWCA’s effective optimization achieves state-of-the-art performance on IMDb data, surpassing LSTM, BERT, and Transformer benchmarks. This illustrates the potential of integrating reservoir computing with metaheuristic optimization for sentiment analysis in scenarios where resources are limited. The current ground-breaking method aids the model in effectively classifying polarities of text. The testing and training of the model have been implemented by the use of IMDb reviews dataset. In order to vectorize the words, the embedding techniques, called Word2Vec and GloVe, were employed. A necessary phase was conducted to adapt hyperparameters by the use of AWCA for optimizing the model. Eventually, the suggested network could classify the polarity of the text. The outcomes approved the feasibility and efficiency of the network. Furthermore, the data got removed and cleaned before preprocessing by conducting several phases, such as correction, removal of unwanted characters, and removal of stopwords. After that, the data got preprocessed. The current phase was implemented after some phases, such as part-of-speech tagging, stemming, and tokenization. The recommended ESN/AWCA was contrasted with some other network. Then, it represented superior accuracy, robustness, and reliability. It was represented by the results that the suggested model could achieve the values of 96.37%, 96.39%, 95.87%, and 96.87% for F1-score, accuracy, recall, and precision while using Word2Vec, and could accomplish the values of 96.23%, 96.12%, 95.76%, and 96.71% for F1-score, accuracy, recall, and precision while using GloVe, respectively. Ultimately, the findings represented that the suggested model ESN-AWCA were really proficient at classifying and determining the polarity of texts, and it could perform highly better than other models. The statistical evaluation offered validation from multiple perspectives. First, ESN-AWCA not only surpassed all baseline models significantly (p < 0.001), but the effect sizes (d > 1.1) also indicated that these differences are meaningful for practical applications. The uniformity observed across various embedding techniques further strengthens the model’s reliability. The proposed ESN-AWCA model shows strong results across all standard evaluation metrics; however, a significant drawback is the lack of interpretability analysis. Since Echo State Networks are naturally opaque due to their reservoir-based dynamics, future research will aim to incorporate model-agnostic explainability techniques like LIME. These approaches can offer insights into the key words or features that influence individual predictions.

Comparative Evaluation of Clinical Success with Preformed Stainless Steel Crowns and Bioflx Crowns: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Nikhitha Elsa Shaji, Rajashekar Reddy Vundela, Jyothsna V Setty, Nagalakshmi Chowdhary, Prerana Mahurkar, Veena Shivanna, Mahesh Kumar Karuppiah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDeciduous teeth play a crucial role in mastication, speech, esthetics, and space maintenance. Full-coverage restorations such as stainless steel crowns (SSCs) ensure a hermetic seal but fall short in esthetics, prompting the development of alternatives such as Bioflx crowns.AimTo compare the clinical success of SSCs and Bioflx crowns in children.Materials and methodsTwenty children (ages 4–8) requiring crowns postpulpectomy for mandibular second primary molars were divided into two groups: group A (stainless steel crown) and group B (Bioflx crown). Child satisfaction was assessed via the children's acceptance scale, and clinician satisfaction was assessed with a 5-point Likert scale. Discomfort and anxiety were evaluated using the emoji and FLACC scales. Clinical success (retention, wear, stain resistance, plaque, and gingival index) was recorded at 3, 6, and 12 months.ResultsChildren preferred Bioflx crowns for their esthetics (p < 0.05), while clinicians favored the morphology of SSCs (p < 0.05). Bioflx required less chair time (p < 0.05). Discomfort levels were comparable across groups. SSCs showed superior retention, gingival health, and plaque control (p < 0.05). No significant difference in wear or stain resistance was noted.ConclusionBioflx crowns are esthetically pleasing and efficient for pediatric restorations. However, SSCs remain superior in durability and clinical performance. Long-term studies are recommended for Bioflx crowns.RegistrationThe Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI) has prospectively registered the study under the CTRI number CTRI/2024/12/077894.How to cite this articleShaji NE, Vundela RR, Setty JV, et al. Introduction

Dental caries in young children not only causes pain and functional issues but also compromises esthetics, affecting self-esteem and social interactions.1,2 Early caries can lead to unattractive or missing teeth, often requiring restorative treatments to restore function and appearance.3,4 Timely, esthetic restorations help preserve primary teeth, prevent further decay, and promote positive dental experiences essential for long-term oral health.

Recreating the form of natural teeth with direct filling material is difficult in cases of multisurface caries, where the restorations can fail due to high occlusal load.5 According to Kindelan et al., crowns are the most suitable choice for multisurface, extensive caries and also for teeth affected by developmental problems.6

Stainless steel crowns (KIDS CROWN) are the gold standard for restoring primary molars, especially in extensive decay or after pulp therapy.7 They are durable and long-lasting but less esthetic and harder to adjust, making them less preferred by some patients and parents.8,9 Children and adults alike are becoming more and more in need of attractive smiles. A child's self-esteem, quality of life, and communal acceptance can all be impacted by their appearance. Due to esthetic demands, newer tooth-colored crowns are being introduced, such as preformed Zirconia crowns, Titanium-coated crowns, and Bioflx crowns.

In relation to esthetics, flexure strength, corrosion resistance, and mechanical abilities, Zirconia crowns have always been exceptional.10 However, Zirconia crowns are rarely accepted by patients and parents because of their time-consuming nature, excessive crown preparation, and requirement for local anesthesia.11

Technological advancements in pediatric dentistry have introduced Bioflx crowns—flexible, durable, and self-adaptable restorations. Developed by Kids-e-Dental LLP in 2021, Bioflx crowns are tooth-colored, metal-free, and made of a high-strength resin polymer that offers excellent durability and flexibility.12 Considering child behavior management, preservation of tooth anatomy, and parental acceptance, Bioflx crowns present a favorable treatment option.13 They combine the functional benefits of SSCs with the esthetics of zirconia crowns.

Like SSCs, Bioflx crowns offer a “flex fit” that adapts to the cervical anatomy of deciduous teeth, requiring minimal tooth preparation. They also provide superior esthetics similar to pediatric zirconia crowns.14 Additionally, Bioflx crowns require only minor adjustments due to their flexibility, making them less time-consuming to place compared to the more rigid SSCs.9

There is no literature on comparing the child's perception of pain, clinical success rate, and acceptance of Bioflx crown placement. Hence, this research was done to compare the clinical outcome, discomfort, and acceptance of Bioflx crowns and SSCs.

Materials and Methods

This research is an in vivo randomized controlled trial done in the department of Pedodontics and Preventive Dentistry at Sri Siddhartha Dental College and Hospital, Tumkur, Karnataka. After instructing the parents/guardians concerning the essence of the research, written informed consent was taken from the parent/ guardian. The Ethical Clearance for the research was received by the Institutional Ethics and review members (SSDCHIEC/2023/55). The Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI) has prospectively registered the study under the CTRI number CTRI/2024/12/077894.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Cooperative children of the age-group of 4–8 years requiring crowns for pulpectomized lower second molars where 2/3rd of the root remained intact radiographically were included in the study. Children with teeth that were mobile, showed any pathological root resorption or root caries, or that did not have sufficient crown structure were excluded from the study.

Sample Size and Randomization

A simple random sampling technique was used, and the participants were allocated to two groups using a computer-generated table of random numbers. Group A received SSCs, and Group B received Bioflx crowns. The opaque sealed envelope technique was used for allotment into groups. A sum of 20 participants (10 in each group), aligning with the inclusion requirements, were chosen for the research between the age groups of 4 and 8 years. This sample size yielded an 80% power assuming an effect size of 0.7 and a significance level of 0.05. A consolidated standard of the reporting trials flow diagram is shown in Figure 1.

Clinical Procedure

Tooth Preparation for Stainless Steel Crowns and Bioflx Crowns13,14

For both groups, the crown preparation was similar, which involved 1–2 mm occlusal reduction, which was achieved using a 69L or 169L bur, and a 0.5 mm proximal reduction was achieved with a tapered fissure bur to clear the contact points. A knife-edge finish line was obtained, and all the point angles were rounded to ensure proper crown adaptation. An appropriately sized crown was selected depending on the mesiodistal width of the tooth. In the case of SSCs, it was crimped using a crimping plier and placed over the prepared tooth linguobuccally, such that it glides over the labial surface and into the gingival sulcus. In the case of the Bioflx crowns, crimping was not required; only contouring with Howe's plier was done, and then placed by snug fit. The crowns were then cemented using a glass ionomer type I under proper isolation, and extra cement was removed with the help of a floss or explorer. The patients were recalled for a regular follow-up at 3, 6, and 12 months. The postoperative and 12-month follow-up images of SSCs is shown in Figures 2 and 3, respectively. The postoperative and 12-month follow-up images of Bioflx crowns are shown in Figures 4 and 5, respectively.

Evaluation Criteria

Children's Acceptance15

The children were given the Smiley scale to assess their acceptance of the treatment procedure. This scale is adapted from the “Smileyometer,” a tool introduced by Read, MacFarlane, and Casey in their study, “Endurability, Engagement, and Expectations: Measuring Children's Fun.”

Clinician Satisfaction13

The clinician satisfaction was evaluated after the crown placement using the five-point Likert scale by assessing the surface gloss, roughness, adaptability, maneuverability, and the anatomical form. The time taken during the placement of the crowns was calculated using a stopwatch.

Evaluation of the Clinical Success of the Crowns

During the time of the placement of the crowns, the marginal integrity and proximal contact was evaluated. Crown retention, stain resistance, crown wear, plaque and gingival index was evaluated during the follow-up periods of 3, 6 and 12 months.

Assessment of Discomfort before and after the Procedure19

The Animated Emoji Scale, which has five images expressing various feelings extending from very happy (score 1) to very unhappy emojis (score 5), was given to each child prior to and immediately after the treatment. Children were also asked to report if the visit was enjoyable and whether they would opt for a different crown in the same treatment way.

Assessment of Dental Anxiety before and after the Procedure20

To evaluate the dental anxiety in the children, the Venham picture test was given prior to any treatment, and immediately after the treatment, which comprised of eight cards, consisting of two images on each card, one “anxious” image and one “nonanxious” image. If the child pinpointed the “anxious” image, it was given a score of one, and if the child pinpointed the “nonanxious” image, a score of zero was given. A final score was then calculated (ranging from a minimum of zero to a maximum of eight).

Assessment of Discomfort during the Procedure

FLACC scale21: The scale is scored varying from 0 to 10, with 0 exhibiting no pain and 10 representing maximum pain. The scale shows five criteria, which are given a score of 0, 1, or 2.

Statistical analysis: The results were determined using IBM SPSS Statistics version 16.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL), and p < 0.05 was chosen as the criterion of significance. Descriptive statistics were utilized to assess the corresponding groups' mean and standard deviation. Inferential statistics to perceive the difference between the groups was done by the Chi-square test and confirmed by the likelihood ratio, linear-by-linear association test, and also Fisher's exact test. The association between categorical variables was examined using the independent t-test.

Results

A sample of 20 mandibular second primary molars that required crowns was included in the study. In this study, among 20 children, in the SSC group, 10 (50%) were girls, and 10 (50%) were boys, and in the Bioflx group, 10 (50%) were girls, and 10 (50%) were boys, with a mean age of 6.05 and a standard deviation of 1.31.

Children's Acceptance Scale

The majority of children (70%) reported Bioflx crowns to be brilliant, and it was statistically significant (p-value < 0.05). In the SSC group, 50% considered it to be really good, and the remaining rated it as good. The children's acceptance ratings are shown in Table 2.

Crown Retention

After a 3-month duration, none of the crowns in both groups were displaced (p-value > 0.05). By the end of the 6th and 12th months, 20 and 40% of Bioflx crowns were displaced, respectively. All the crowns in the stainless steel groups were retentive during follow-up. There was a statistically significant difference between both the groups at 6 and 12 months follow-up (p-value < 0.05) (Fig. 6).

Crown Wear

Wearing of crowns was noticed at the 6th and 12th months in the Bioflx crown group, and none of the crowns showed any wearing in the SSC group. There was a borderline significance between the two groups (p-value > 0.05) (Fig. 7).

Crown wear between SSC and Bioflx crown groups during follow-up

Stain Resistance

None of the crowns in both groups showed any staining during the follow-ups (p-value > 0.05).

Plaque and Gingival Index

At the 3rd, 6th, and 12th months, a statistically significant difference was seen in the Bioflx crown group in contrast to the Stainless steel crown group (p-value < 0.05). After 12 months, only 45% of the total crowns showed no plaque accumulation (Fig. 8). Out of the 45%, only 30% of the crowns in the Bioflx crown group, and by the end of the sixth and twelfth months, 60% of the crowns in the stainless steel crown group had a good plaque index.

Plaque index between SSC and Bioflx crown groups during follow-up

The gingival index was significantly higher in the 6th and the 12th month (p-value < 0.05). By the end of the 12th month, 80% of the crowns showed healthy gingiva (Fig. 9). Out of the 80%, only 60% of the crowns in the Bioflx group and 100% of the stainless steel crown group showed healthy gingiva at the end of the 6th and 12th months.

Discomfort before, during and after the Treatment

The discomfort and anxiety felt by the children before and after the treatment was evaluated with the help of Animated Emoji Scale and Venham picture test. During the treatment, the discomfort felt was evaluated using the FLACC scale. There was no statistically significance in difference in the discomfort experienced by the children before, during, and after the treatment in both the groups (p > 0.05).

Discussion

As per the American Association of Pediatric Dentistry, prefabricated full coronal rehabilitation is preferred over conventional fillings in order to avoid microleakage, endodontic failure, and also to enhance function and strength.22 In the present world, parents and children prefer more natural-looking restorations, hence leading to more demand for esthetics.

Stainless steel crowns have been the standard for full coronal restorations due to their durability. However, rising esthetic demands have led to alternatives such as Zirconia, Titanium-coated, and Bioflx crowns. The newly introduced Bioflx crowns are metal and bisphenol A glycidyl methacrylate-free monochromatic made up of high-strength resin polymer, which is considered to contribute to high strength and durability.9

In our study, most of the children and parents were very happy with the esthetically satisfying look of Bioflx crowns, which is in accordance with the study by Mathew et al.,16 and Pani et al.,23 where children and parents preferred natural looking Zirconia crowns.

Clinician satisfaction often differs from that of children and parents, prioritizing adaptability, retention, and longevity over esthetics. In this study, the clinician reported low satisfaction with Bioflx crowns due to poor adaptability and maneuverability, as they could not be crimped or contoured—consistent with Patil et al.,12 who noted overcontouring in Bioflx prototype 1. In contrast, SSCs offer a wider size range and can be easily crimped and adjusted.

Bioflx crowns required significantly less chairside time for adjustment and placement compared to SSCs, improving patient cooperation and the overall experience for children. This is largely due to their flexible, self-adaptive design, which eliminates the need for crimping and allows the crown to adjust occlusion independently. This finding contrasts with studies by Patil et al., and Srilekha et al.,24 who reported longer placement times for Bioflx crowns due to the need for extensive tooth preparation caused by their overcontoured anatomy. However, in our study, since tooth preparation was standardized for both crown types, only the adjustment and placement time was considered.

Marginal integrity and proximal contact were significantly poorer in the Bioflx group, with 40% showing open margins and free contact most likely due to the crown's limited contouring and inability to be crimped. Marginal integrity and proximal contact were assessed only at placement to evaluate the initial seal, as changes may occur later due to dental growth. According to Prabhu et al., only 75% of CAD-CAM Zirconia crowns showed good marginal integrity, while 100% of SSCs maintained it at 3, 6, and 12 months. The discrepancy was attributed to cementation delays and technical errors.25

The Animated emoji, Venham and FLACC scales showed no significant difference between Bioflx and SSCs; children were generally happy and comfortable, likely due to minimal tooth preparation in both.

At 6 months, 20% of Bioflx crowns were displaced, increasing to 40% by 9 months—consistent with findings by Srilekha et al.,24 displacement was mainly seen in crowns with open margins and poor proximal contact. Alhissan et al., similarly reported high debonding rates in Zirconia crowns due to limited tooth structure.26 In contrast, Murali et al.,14 and Agrawal et al.,11 observed lower debonding rates (3.2–5%) in SSCs, largely due to cement failure. In our study, GIC was used for cementation, whereas Bhatt et al.,27 using RMGIC (RelyX), reported no dislodgement at 12 months. Crown retention is influenced by cement type, tooth structure, isolation, and contamination.

Wearing was observed in 30% of Bioflx crowns, though not statistically significant. This may be due to occlusal imbalance displacing particles from the polymer framework. This finding contrasts with Metwally et al.28 who reported greater chewing resistance for Bioflx crowns in vitro. However, no staining was reported in both the crown groups, which corresponds with the outcome of the investigation exhibited by Patil et al., where no staining was seen with Bioflx crowns.12 Biocompatible hybrid resin polymer in Bioflx crowns increases its durability, flexibility, and color stability.29

Bioflx crowns showed higher plaque and gingival scores at all follow-ups, likely due to open margins and poor contacts, aligning with findings by Alharbi et al.,30 and Henderson et al.,31 SSCs showed no plaque accumulation, which was in alignment with Aggrawal et al.,11 due to smoother surfaces. Biradar et al.,32 found lower S. mutans counts on titanium-coated crowns, when compared to SSCs linked to reduced surface energy. However, good oral hygiene remains essential regardless of crown type.

Bioflx crowns are a promising esthetic option, but further long-term, larger-scale studies are needed to evaluate their durability, retention, and ease of use. Including radiographic assessments, split-mouth designs, and parental satisfaction will strengthen future research.

Conclusion

This study found both Bioflx and SSCs effective for full coronal restorations. Bioflx crowns offer better esthetics and faster placement, ideal for young children, but SSCs provide superior longevity, fit, and lower plaque accumulation. Overall, SSCs outperform Bioflx in durability and adaptability. Further studies comparing Bioflx with other esthetic crowns are recommended.

Clinical Significance

Bioflx crowns offer an esthetic and time-efficient alternative for pediatric restorations, enhancing child acceptance. However, SSCs continue to outperform in durability, gingival health, and overall clinical success.

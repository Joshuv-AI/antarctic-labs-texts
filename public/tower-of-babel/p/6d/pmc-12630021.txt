Correlation between the body size and physiological indicators of Jingding duck
Authors: Yuwei Li, Juan Chen, Changtao Liu, Huiling Mei, Yaping Zhang, Zhongwei Miao, Xin Wang, Min Huang, Mingxin Lin, Xu Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBody size remains a major trait due to its high economic impact for breeding selection. This study was aimed to select high-yielding Jinding ducks based on body size and physiological indicators. A total of 264 Jinding ducks were measured for eleven body size measurements and body weight, as well as seven hormones and four biochemical indicators (high-yielding duck, HYD, n = 132; low-yielding duck, LYD, n = 132). All data were subjected to t-tests and Pearson correlation analysis. The results revealed that HYD exhibited higher levels of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), progesterone (PROG), testosterone (T), estradiol (E2), glucose (GLU), and triglyceride (TG) (P < 0.01) while lower levels of prolactin (PRL) (P < 0.01) compared with LYD. The HYD showed wider pubic spacing, slender necks, and longer keel bones than LYD (P < 0.01). The HYD also had wider body slope length, half-diving depth, chest depth, chest width, and tibia length (P < 0.05). Thus, based on the significant differences in body size and hormone levels, we further investigated the association between them. The result showed that the pubic spacing and neck length were positively correlated with GnRH, FSH, and PROG (P < 0.01) and negatively correlated with PRL (P < 0.05), while the keel bone length was positively correlated with GnRH, FSH (P < 0.01) and E2, PROG (P < 0.05).The half-diving depth was positively correlated with GnRH, E2 (P < 0.01) and LH, PROG (P < 0.05). This study provided breeding strategies and showed that HYD could be selected by wide pubic spacing, long keel bones and slender necks.Keywords: High-yielding ducks, Low-yielding duck, Body measurements, Physiological indicators

Introduction

The Jinding duck, a variety of Anas platyrhynchos (commonly known as the Mallard or South China duck), is a domestic Chinese duck breed indigenous to Fujian Province. The Jinding duck is known to be environment tolerant, disease-resistant genes and a relatively small body size (China National Commission of Animal Genetic Resources, 2020). Eggs from Jinding duck are very popular as food and test object. However, there exist differences in the egg-laying rates of Jinding ducks,which lead to a key research focus in enhancing egg production (Emamgholi Begli et al., 2019).

Some studies have already investigated the factors reflected duck egg productivity, including gene expression, nutrition, environment, and others (Hou, 1984; Hu et al., 2021). Selecting laying hens based on body size traits is a highly cost-effective approach (Zhang et al., 2007). For instance, laying hens were selected body size for early selection and elimination. The unqualified tibal length and body weight lead to failed brooding phase and delayed the agg at first egg (Da, 2012).On the other hand, current research on body size in duck has used genetic correlation analysis to improve carcass rate (Deng et al., 2019) or plumage quality (Zhou et al., 2018), or to analyze the effect of body size on body weight (Wang et al., 2024a). Therefore, inferencing egg production through body size traits has high economic impact.

It is widely known that reproductive hormone levels directly correlate with egg production. Both inside and ouside factors that effected the reproductive hormonal levels should primarily stimulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis (Yan et al., 2022). Oocyte maturity is stimulated by signaling from the hypothalamus and pituitary gland. The key reproductive hormones regulated by the HPG axis including gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), estradiol (E2), progesterone (PROG), and testosterone (T). According to Guo et al. (2020), the hypothalamus receive multifaceted stimuli, and then secrete GnRH through a liquid-regulating method. These hormones act on the pituitary regulating the secretion of PRL and gonadotropic hormone including FSH and LH then ultimately drive follicle development (Bello et al., 2022). The reproductive hormone levels, especially related HPG axis, reflected egg-laying capacity.

Furthermore, the serum biochemical were valuable in early breeding selection. Research has highlighted the significance of calcium in egg formation. For instance, approximately 20-40% of the calcium required for eggshell development originates from cancellous bone. Meanwhile, tibia bones serve as the primary storage sites for calcium (Fu et al., 2024). The deficiency or increased demanding for calcium can lead to calcium mobilization from bones to meet bodily needs (Dijkslag et al., 2023), suggesting a potential link between biochemical indicators with body traits. By correlating serum biochemical with body size, specific body traits linked to egg laying performance may be identified (Zhang et al., 2024).

This study focused on Jinding ducks and conducted statistical analysis on the correlation between body size and physiological indicators. Our aim was to evaluate the feasibility of convenient screening high-yielding Jinding ducks by body size traits, by comparing these traits between ducks grouped according to their egg production.

Materials and methods

Animals and experimental design

A total of 264 female Jinding Duck (500 days) of 2 different laying performance were divided into 132 high-yielding duck and 132 low-yielding duck. All variables and physiological indicators obtained in 264 animals (HYD, n = 132; LYD, n = 132). All the ducks were fed in individual cages, had free access to food and water, and received a fixed duration of lighting.

Body size and body weight

Twelve body size measurements were recorded according to the Agricultural standard publishing research center NY/T 823-2020, performance terminology and measurements for poultry. The body size were measured using a measuring tape and body weight were measured using a scale.

Reproductive hormones and biochemical indicator

Blood samples were collected to analyze a panel of physiological indicators in morning, including serum biochemical parameters (e.g., GLU, TG, Ca, ALB) and reproductive hormones (e.g., GnRH, FSH, LH, PROG, T, E2). 3 mL blood samples were collected from the wing vein of each duck, using 5 mLvacuum tubes then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min. 1 mL serum supernatant was collected and stored at -20°C until analysis.

The levels of GnRH, FSH, LH, PRL, PROG, T, and E2 were determined by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The GnRH (ED-61125), LH (ED-61074), FSH (ED-61099), PRL (ED-61076), T (LCSJZF61106) and PROG (LCSJZF61100) ELISA kits were purchased from Lunchangshuo Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Xiamen, China). ELISA Calc software v1.0 (Comple-Software, Iowa City, IA) was used to calculate the levels of hormones.

The glucose (GLU), triglyceride (TG) and calcium (Ca) were determined by GPO-PAP Method. The albumin (ALB) was determined by Bromocresol Green Method. The Glu (E-BC-K234-M), TG (E-BC-K261-M), Ca (E-BC-K103-M) and ALB (E-BC-K057-M) kits were purchased from Elabscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed by t test using SPSS statistics software (27.0) and presented as the mean ± SD. The correlation between body size and serum indicators was expressed by Pearson correlation coefficient. P < 0.05 or P < 0.01 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Result

Body size and body weight

The results of correlation analysis indicated significant differences in body size between HYD and LYD (Table 1). The results revealed that HYD exhibited outstanding significantly wider pubic spacing, longer neck length and keel length, as well as a shorter neck circumference (P < 0.01). Meanwhile, HYD also demonstrated a relatively high level of tibial length, chest depth, and chest width within a significant range (P < 0.05). However, LYD had a longer body slope length (P < 0.05). The remaining of body sizes were no clear significant relationship.

Serum reproductive hormones and biochemical indices

Eleven serum indices were measured using ELISA assay kits. The reproductive hormones (GnRH, FSH, LH, PROG, T, E2, PRL) differed between HYD and LYD (Table 2). The results revealed that HYD exhibited significantly higher levels of these reproductive hormones (P < 0.01). Notably, the vast majority of GnRH and E2 were observed between two group which indicated the egg-laying potential of HYD (P < 0.01). In contrast, PRL demonstrated an opposite trend (P < 0.01). Additionally, considering its potential correlation with egg production, we measured serum TG and GLU levels and found that HYD had significantly high levels than LYD (P < 0.01). In summary, HYD generally displayed higher serum reproductive hormones and biochemical levels compared to LYD.

The pubic spacing demonstrated significant positive correlations with tibia length, abdominal circumference (r = 0.417, P < 0.01) and body weight (r = 0.555, P < 0.05). Notably, pubic spacing showed marked positive associations with GnRH, PROG, and FSH levels (r = 0.281 ∼ 0.299, P < 0.01), potentially suggesting pubic spacing as a key phenotypic marker.

Regarding skeletal measurements, keel length exhibited moderate to strong positive correlations with chest width, neck length, and reproductive hormones GnRH and FSH (r = 0.308 ∼ 0.502, P < 0.01). In contrast, neck circumference demonstrated significant negative correlations with E2, PROG, FSH (r = -0.295 ∼ -0.398, P < 0.01) and LH (r = -0.215, P < 0.05), whereas neck length demonstrated significant positive correlations with these hormones (r = 0.305 ∼ 0.352, P < 0.01). These findings highlighted keel length and neck measurements as critical indicators.

GnRH showed strong positive correlations with FSH, LH, PROG, and E2 (r = 0.620 ∼ 0.705, P < 0.01). Significant correlations were observed between E2 and PROG (r = 0.729, P < 0.01), and between E2 and T (r = 0.279, P < 0.01). PRL exhibited moderate negative correlations with other reproductive hormones (r = -0.405 ∼ -0.618, P < 0.01). Glu demonstrated a significant negative correlation with PRL (r = -0.399, P < 0.01) and positive correlations with other reproductive hormones (r = 0.284 ∼ 0.490, P < 0.01). Collectively, these results revealed closely connected between pubic spacing and HPG axis hormones. The correlations between body size were limited while reproductive hormones form a strong interconnected network. Additionally, serum indices were moderate correlated with body size.

Discussion

The pubic spacing was considered as an indicator for determining egg laying performance (Ding et al., 2022). According to Kuang et al. (2020), a statistically significant linear regression equation can be established between pubic spacing and the number of eggs. Our research confirms the previous findings that the pubic spacing was highly associated with production performance. This might be because wider pubic space was beneficial for egg-laying. Additionally, there is a statistically significant correlation between reproductive hormones indicator and pubic spacing, and it was possible that reproductive hormones adjust the pubic spacing to accommodate egg-laying (Ding et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022). Similarly, HYD was observed longer keel bone length. Kuang et al. (Kuang et al., 2020) and Li et al. (Li et al., 2022) found a positive correlation between keel bone length and egg laying performance, while Wu et al. (Wu et al., 2018) reported contradictory results. The difference may be explained by the distinction of species and age. In the research by Lin et al. (Lin et al., 2016), the genetic correlation between eggs number with body weight was found to be significant. In former studies, when assessing the body size with egg numbers, different research groups found a similarly positive correlation (Huang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022). Our findings supported these hypothesis that the analyses about chest wide, keel bone length and body weight showed larger body size leading to greater egg production. A possible explanation for this finding is that, larger body size provided more energy reserves. Thus, our stury speculate that body size traits can be used for selection high yielding ducks in breeding process.

High egg production often connected with high reproductive hormones on Jinding ducks (Prastiya et al., 2022). Our study revealed that GnRH exhibited strong correlations with FSH and LH. They collectively acted on the ovaries to promote follicular growth and development (Wu et al., 2024). Notably, our study observed a strong positive correlation between E2 and GnRH. The research had showd that the facilitatory effect of high E2 levels on GnRH pulse generation. This neuroendocrine regulation is potentially mediated through glutamatergic synaptic modulation of preoptic Kiss1 neurons (Qiu et al., 2024). T can be aromatized into E2 by aromatization which explain their significant correlation (Li et al., 1996). The high level of T benefited early follicular development as well as granulose cell proliferation which rise the egg production and explained the significant positive correlation between E2 and T in our results. (Bosdou et al., 2025). Additionally, PROG also showed a significantly positive correlation with E2, with a correlation coefficient as high as 0.729. E2 induces the synthesis of progesterone, which explained their significant relationship (Micevych and Sinchak, 2008). In conclusion, GnRH secretion during the egg-laying phase exerted a significant regulatory effect on the serum concentrations of FSH, LH, E2, PRL, and PROG in Jinding ducks (Zhang et al., 2024).

In the study by Jin et al. (Jin et al., 2025), calcium not only maintained eggshell and bone shape but also interacted with age-related calcium signals to inhibit cAMP metabolism in granulosa cells. Meanwhile, FSH and LH levels were conducted by ovarian cAMP levels in a high degree during the ovulation cycle (Yang et al., 2024). Additionally, the tibia played a crucial role in calcium regulation as the primary depot for calcium (Fu et al., 2024). Our study revealed that HYD had longer tibias and higher calcium than LYD and demonstrated that compared to LYD, HYD exhibit significantly enhanced liver lipid and protein synthesis capabilities during egg production because of the high TG and ALB (Yang et al., 2024). TG is a component of blood lipids, reflecting lipid absorption, utilization and metabolism, with lower values indicating higher fat utilization (Chen et al., 2013). Our statistical results revealed a significant association between TG levels and reproductive hormones. The high TG level was potentially mediated by abdominal related gene (e.g., ANGPTL2, HTRA1) that enhance GnRH secretion and activate egg production (Wang et al., 2024b). This may explain how TG effected egg production through abdominal fat. In most studies, ALB levels showed minimal variation between high- and low-yielding groups, with some even reporting higher ALB in low-yielding group (Wu et al., 2021; Lu et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2024). However, in the present study, the HYD exhibited significantly elevated ALB, which may be attributed to age differences. During embryonic development, Glu not only affected energy supply but also acts as various metabolic intermediates and signaling molecules, influencing cellular signaling and genetic modification (Zhang et al., 2025). Our findings consisted with this hypothesis and Glu served as a critical energy substrate for reproductive processes while stimulate reproductive hormone level.

In comparison, PRL was found obvious negative correlation with other results. PRL hormone levels in HYD were significantly lower than those in LYD during the same period. This result contrasted with our other findings, which may be explained by the experiments conducted by Miao et al. (Miao et al., 2024). Research observed higher expressions of PRL and PRL receptor-related mRNA in late-laying period pigeons. It is postulated that the significantly longer laying peak period in the HYD group resulted in their continued egg production at the time of sampling, unlike the LYD group which had entered a non-laying phase. This key disparity in reproductive phase is the probable cause for the reduced prolactin (PRL) concentration measured in the HYD group. Furthermore, we observed that HYD exhibited longer neck length compared to LYD which align with the conclusions proposed by Hou (Hou, 1984). Neck circumference and length were associated with varieties of reproductive hormones we measured. Though speculative, this might optimize respiratory efficiency during high metabolic demand, indirectly supporting hormone synthesis. However, the theory required further testament.

The present study demonstrated the convenient method of selecting HYD by body size and serem hormones which was saving cost and reliable. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive investigation in late-laying-period ducks, while other laying period such as pre-laying and peak-laying periods did not be observed. The egg production capacity of breeding ducks exhibited fluctuations across these phases, accompanied by bone growth. Thus, the body size traits and physiological indicators of high-performance Jinding duck on other production stages may possibly be observed in future studies.

Conclusion

In summary, HYD exhibited high levels of GnRH, FSH, LH, and E2 compared to LYD. The HYD also showed more advantageous body size traits than LYD, including significantly wider pubic spacing, longer keel bone, wider chest width, and slender neck. Body size exhibited reliable correlation with reproductive hormones. Based on our findings, it is a new method that breeding high yielding Jinding duck by selecting body size. Therefore, HYD can be selected by wider pubic spacing, longer keel, and slender neck.

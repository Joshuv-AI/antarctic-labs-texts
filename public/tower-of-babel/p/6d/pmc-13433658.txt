A Combination of the Tryptophan Hydroxylase Inhibitor Telotristat with the mTOR Inhibitor Everolimus as an Effective Strategy Against Neuroendocrine Tumors
Authors: Javier Molina-Cerrillo, Arantzazu Sierra-Ramirez, José L López-Aceituno, Marinela Méndez-Pertuz, Teresa Alonso-Gordoa, Michael Linnebacher, Matteo Santoni, Pablo J Fernandez-Marcos, Enrique Grande
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesNeuroendocrine tumors are a rare and heterogeneous group of neoplasms that frequently cause carcinoid syndrome, characterized by diarrhea, flushing, and carcinoid heart disease. A recent therapy for carcinoid syndrome involves inhibition of tryptophan hydroxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme in serotonin synthesis, using telotristat ethyl. Telotristat has demonstrated clinical control of carcinoid syndrome; however, its potential antitumoral effects remain unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the antitumor activity of telotristat ethyl alone and in combination with everolimus in neuroendocrine tumor models.MethodsCell viability was assessed in three neuroendocrine tumor cell lines of pancreatic (BON-1, QGP-1) and intestinal (HROC57) origin following treatment with telotristat ethyl. Combination treatments with telotristat ethyl and everolimus or other antitumoral agents were evaluated for effects on cell viability, apoptosis, and cell cycle distribution. In vivo efficacy was examined in nude mice bearing BON-1–derived xenografts treated with telotristat ethyl, everolimus, or the combination. Tumor growth, toxicity, proliferation (Ki67), and apoptosis (active caspase-3) were analyzed.ResultsTelotristat ethyl reduced cell viability in all three neuroendocrine tumor cell lines. Combination with everolimus, but not with other antitumoral treatments, synergistically decreased cell viability, induced apoptosis, and reduced the proportion of cells in the G2/M phase. In nude mice bearing BON-1 xenografts, combined treatment with telotristat ethyl and everolimus arrested tumor growth without signs of toxicity compared with single treatments. At the end of treatment, tumors from combination-treated mice showed a reduction in proliferation (Ki67) comparable to single-treated groups and an increase in apoptosis as indicated by active caspase-3.ConclusionsTelotristat ethyl exhibits antitumoral activity in neuroendocrine tumor models in vivo. Moreover, the combination of telotristat ethyl and everolimus, two therapies already used in clinical practice, may represent a safe and effective therapeutic strategy for neuroendocrine tumors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40268-026-00544-x.

Introduction

Neuroendocrine tumors are a rare and heterogeneous group of tumors with a variety of primary origins and variable aggressiveness. Telotristat ethyl has been demonstrated to reduce serotonin production by inhibiting tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH), a rate-limiting enzyme in the synthesis of serotonin. Serotonin or 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) plays a role in the physiology of carcinoid syndrome by promoting diarrhea, flushing and carcinoid heart disease. Treatment with somatostatin analogs (SSAs), such as octreotide, is frequently used to control hormone-related symptoms of carcinoid syndrome. In a phase III clinical trial (TELESTAR), telotristat ethyl has demonstrated a significant improvement in the control of bowel movements in patients with neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) and carcinoid syndrome that have inadequate bowel control after treatment with an SSA, emerging as a potential new option in the symptomatic control of functioning NETs. Importantly, SSAs are considered not only symptom-controlling agents but also antiproliferative drugs for NET, as demonstrated in two placebo controlled trials (PROMID and CLARINET [1]). In this sense, SSAs have been added to the armamentarium of drugs controlling tumor growth, together with the targeted drugs, sunitinib and everolimus, in the field of NETs.

Telotristat has demonstrated clinical relief of carcinoid syndrome, but its antitumoral effect remains unclear. There is only one small retrospective case series report suggesting possible antitumor activity [2].

Aberrant signaling through the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway has been implicated in NETs [3]. Altered expression of the mTOR pathway components has been observed in NETs and has been associated with clinical outcomes. Therefore, targeting the mTOR pathway has emerged as an effective treatment strategy in the management of advanced NETs. However, inhibition of mTOR Complex 1 (mTORC1) by everolimus (RAD001) induced upstream receptor tyrosine kinase signaling and activates Akt [4]. Everolimus is an orally bioavailable mTOR inhibitor that has been tested in various phase III clinical trials for patients with advanced lung, pancreatic, and gastrointestinal NETs. In those clinical trials, everolimus prolonged disease-free progression in all origins, but not overall survival [5–8]. Therefore, proving its effectiveness in combination with other therapeutic agents utilized to control NET would be of great relevance in this field.

Apart from everolimus, different chemotherapeutic strategies against NETs have been described. Capecitabine (5-FDUR), an oral pro-drug for 5-fluorouracyl (5-FU), and temozolomide have been found to be synergistic for induction of apoptosis in two human NET cell lines [9, 10]. In the clinic, the combination of capecitabine and temozolomide is associated with an exceptionally high and durable response rate in metastatic endocrine carcinomas of the pancreas [11]. Also, sunitinib malate is a broad-spectrum inhibitor of tyrosine kinase receptors mostly known for its anti-angiogenic effects. Sunitinib inhibits the following kinases: platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFRs) α and β, stem-cell factor receptor (c-kit), and VEGF receptor (VEGFR)-2 and VEGFR-3 [12, 13]. Sunitinib has undergone extensive clinical development, culminating in a phase III study [14, 15], in patients with advanced pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (PNET), showing improvement in progression-free survival in patients who received sunitinib. These results confirmed previous preclinical and clinical data suggesting that NETs may be particularly sensitive to combined inhibition of VEGFRs and PDGFRs [15–17].

5-HT can work as an autocrine growth factor able to stimulate proliferation of lung and gastrointestinal (GI)-NET cells through alterations in ERK and JNK signaling [18]. In that study, authors showed that exogenously added 5-HT stimulated cell proliferation in the atypical bronchopulmonary (BP)-NET, NCI-H720, the typical BP-NET, NCI-, and the GI-NET, KRJ-I. Authors determined the receptor involvement in this response by assaying the effects of ketanserin (5-HT2A/C receptor antagonist), ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist) and the suicide inhibitor 7-hydroxytryptophan (7-HTP), and the role of endogenous 5-HT production. NCI-H720 cell proliferation was inhibited by ketanserin and ondansetron as well as 7-HTP. In NCI-H727, ketanserin and 7-HTP inhibited proliferation while ondansetron had no effect. These results indicated that 5-HT acts in an autocrine manner to induce cell proliferation [18]. In addition, in the human BON-1 cell line that has been used as a model to study PNET cell biology, 5-HT and IGF-1 stimulate cell proliferation and act as an autocrine growth factor through specific receptors [19]. While telotristat ethyl has not yet been evaluated for direct antitumor effects in human clinical trials, preclinical studies have explored its impact on neuroendocrine tumor models, showing effective inhibition of serotonin production and variable effects on tumor cell proliferation and growth [20, 21]. In this work, we study the possible anti-proliferative effects of telotristat ethyl in NET cells, which endogenously produce 5-HT. We assess the efficacy of telotristat ethyl as a single agent or in combination with SSAs, mTOR inhibitors (everolimus), standard chemotherapy (capecitabine/temozolomide), and the multiple tyrosine kinase inhibitor sunitinib (VEGF and PDGF inhibitor). Our data reveal a potent synergism of the combination of everolimus and telotristat against survival and proliferation of several NET cell lines, which is reproduced in a xenograft mouse model. These preclinical results open a very promising therapeutic window for the treatment of NETs in patients.

Materials and Methods

Cell Culture Experiments

The HROC57 human intestinal neuroendocrine cell line was donated from Dr Michael Linnebacher [21]. BON-1 and HROC57 cells were cultured in DMEM F12 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) while QGP-1 were cultured in RPMI1640 supplemented with 10% FBS. All cell lines were incubated at 37 °C in an atmosphere with 5% CO2.

For viability assays, we cultured neuroendocrine cell lines BON-1, QGP-1, and HROC57 at a density of 10,000 cells per well in 96-well plates. 24 hours after cells were seeded, we treated them in six replicates per condition using increasing concentrations of telotristat (donated by TerSera), everolimus (SML2282, Sigma), octreotide (O1014, Sigma), serotonin/5-HT (14927, Sigma), capecitabine (SML0653, Sigma), temozolomide (T2577, Sigma), or sunitinib malate (PZ0012, Sigma), alone or in combination for 72 hours. Cell viability was measured with the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide tetrazolium (MTT) reduction assay. MTT was dissolved in distilled water and added to cells at a final concentration of 5 mg/mL and incubated for 3.5 hours. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a Victor Nivo plate reader (Perkin Elmer).

For apoptosis measurements, BON-1 and HROC57 cells were cultured at a density of 100,000 cells per well in 6-well plates. We treated them with everolimus, telotristat, a combination of these compounds, or vehicle (1% DMSO). After 72 hours, cells were incubated in annexin V binding buffer (TNB5000, Tonbo Biosciences) and stained with APC-Annexin V antibody 0.1 μg (35-6409, Tonbo Biosciences) and propidium iodide 0.5 μg (50-6409, Tonbo Biosciences). Fluorescence was measured in a Celesta flow cytometer (BD Biosciences).

For cell cycle analysis, BON-1 and HROC57 cells were resuspended in PBS and added dropwise to ice-cold ethanol while vortexing to a final ethanol concentration of 75%. Cells were incubated for a minimum of 2 hours at −20 °C. After ethanol fixation, cells were washed with PBS plus 3% FBS and incubated for 30 minutes in a medium containing propidium iodide 50 μg/mL (P4864, Sigma) and ribonuclease A 100 μg/mL (R6513, Sigma). Fluorescence was measured in a Celesta flow cytometer (BD Biosciences).

For animal xenograft experiments, we infected BON-1 cells with lentiviral transfected with pBLIV-RFP-Luciferase-thymidine kinase with the CMV promoter. RFP-positive cells were shorted with Influx (BD Biosciences).

Animal Experimentation

Animal experimentation was conducted at the CNIO, Madrid, according to protocols approved by the CNIO-ISCIII Ethics Committee for Research and Animal Welfare (PROEX 340.3/21).

For xenograft experiments, 1×106 BON-1 cells were resuspended in PBS and inoculated subcutaneously at the right rear flank of recipient Hsd:Athymic Nude-Foxn1nu (Envigo). Tumor areas were monitored every day from Monday to Friday using a caliper. Area was calculated as [length] × [width] × [height] in mm3. Tumor size was also evaluated by tumor cell fluorescence emission using an IVIS Spectrum In Vivo Imaging System at the beginning of the treatment and every week until the end of treatment. RFP cells were excited by 560 nm and emission was detected at 620 nm.

For mouse treatments, when the tumor area reached an average of 100 mm3, animals were divided in four groups of treatment, taking care that all groups had comparable tumor sizes. Groups were treated with either telotristat (200 mg/kg/d, TerSera), everolimus (5 mg/kg/d, HY-10218, MedChemExpress), a combination of both compounds, or their corresponding vehicle (5% DMSO + 5% PEG400 in drinking water). Compounds were administrated by oral gavage daily from Monday to Friday for almost 30 days. After treatment ended, animals were humanely sacrificed by CO2 inhalation.

For hemograms, blood samples were collected from each mouse by cardiac puncture right after death in EDTA-coated tubes. Blood parameters were measured in a laser cell blood counter (CVM veterinary diagnostic).

Western Blot

For protein experiments from BON-1, cells were cultured at a density of 40,000 cells per well in 12-well plates and treated over 24 h with telotristat, everolimus, a combination of both compounds, or vehicle. Whole-cell extracts were prepared using NP-40–based lysis buffer (Tris pH 7.4 50 mM; NaCl 150 mM; 0.5% NP-40; phenyl-methyl-sulfonyl fluoride 1 mM; protease inhibitors cocktail [Sigma-Aldrich P8340]; NaF 1 mM; and sodium orthovanadate10 mM).

For proteins isolated from BON-1 tumors, approximately 30 mg of tissues were lysed in NP-40 lysis buffer using a tissue lyser (Eppendorf).

We performed sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) of 20 μg of total protein extracts using a protein-dependent polyacrylamide percentage mini-gels. The proteins were transferred to nitrocellulose membranes and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature in blocking buffer (5% skimmed milk in Tris-buffered saline [TBS]; 0.1% Tween-20 [TBS-T]). Primary antibodies were hybridized overnight at 4 ºC in 5% BSA, TBS-T. We used antibodies against Phospho-p42/44 MAPK (9101), p42/44 (9102), Phospho-S6 (5364), S6 (2217), Phospho-AKT Thr308 (9275) AKT (9272), P53 (2524), or BCL-XL (2764), from Cell Signaling; against P21 (sc-6246) and E-CADHERIN (sc-8426) from Santa Cruz Biotechnology; and against b-ACTIN (A2228, Sigma). Then, membranes were washed three times in TBS-T and incubated in 5% skimmed milk with a secondary fluorescence anti-mouse antibody (1:15,000, goat anti-mouse or anti-rabbit c50113, LICOR). Band quantification was performed in an Odyssey Fc detector. Band intensity was quantified using the Odyssey FC software.

Histopathology

Tumors dissected from nude mice were rinsed in PBS and fixed in 10% formalin and 24 hours later were included in paraffin. Serial sections of 5 μm were stained with hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) or immuno-stained with active caspase 3 (Asp175, Cell Signaling) or Ki67 antibodies (D3B5, Cell Signaling).

For active caspase 3 and Ki67 immunohistochemistry, four representative fields of 20× magnification were analyzed for each slide. Analysis of active caspase 3 and Ki67 percentage of area was calculated using the color deconvolution tool, selecting brown color, and adjusting a threshold to measure positive area in ImageJ software.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.4.1 for Windows. Precise statistical tests are specified at each figure legend. Differences between three or more groups were analyzed using the one-way ANOVA test with Tukey correction for multiple comparisons. Differences between three or more groups defined in more than one variable were analyzed using the two-way ANOVA with Sidak correction for multiple comparisons. All analyses were performed as paired or unpaired tests, depending on the type of analyzed samples.

Results

Pancreatic and Intestinal Neuroendocrine Tumor-Derived Cell Lines Respond to Telotristat and the Combination of Telotristat and Everolimus

We wanted to determine the sensitivity of neuroendocrine tumor-derived cell lines to different anti-tumor therapies. For this, we tested two cell lines derived from pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (BON-1 and QGP-1 [22]), and one cell line derived from an intestinal neuroendocrine tumor (HROC57 [23]). The addition of 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) did not affect cell viability of BON-1 cells, while it induced a mild increase in cell viability in QGP-1 and HROC57 cells (Fig. 1a). In contrast, treatment with the somatostatin analog octreotide did not affect cellular viability in any of these cell lines, in agreement with previous reports [24, 25] (Fig. S1a, see electronic supplementary material [ESM]). However, the addition of telotristat provoked a marked decrease in cell viability in the three cell lines (Fig. 1b), with IC50 of 20.71 μM (BON-1), 23.71 μM (QGP-1), and 11.43 μM (HROC57).

We then tested the efficacy of different chemotherapy strategies in our cell lines. We first tested the two standard first-line treatments—the combination of capecitabine and temozolomide [11] or the tyrosine kinase inhibitor sunitinib malate [13]. Treatment with capecitabine and temozolomide did not affect cell viability in any of the tested cell lines (Fig. S1b, ESM). In contrast, sunitinib malate was very effective in decreasing cell viability in all cell lines (Fig. S1c, ESM). Finally, the addition of the mTOR inhibitor everolimus also decreased cell viability, especially in the pancreatic cell lines BON-1 (IC50 23.64 μM) and QGP-1 (IC50 35.27 μM), while the intestinal neuroendocrine cells HROC57 were more resistant to everolimus addition (Fig. 1c).

We then wondered if the combination of telotristat with standard chemotherapies could increase the anti-tumor efficacy of these treatments. We first combined sunitinib malate with telotristat, but this combination did not increase the efficacy compared with sunitinib alone (Fig. S1d, ESM). However, when telotristat was combined with everolimus, there was a clear synergism in the reduction of cell viability in all tested cell lines (Fig. 1d–e).

The Combination of Telotristat and Everolimus Induced Apoptosis and Decreased the Number of Cells in the G2/M Cell Cycle Phase

We then explored in more detail the cellular response of the BON-1 (pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor) and the HROC57 (intestinal neuroendocrine tumor) cells to treatment with telotristat, everolimus, or the combination of both. First, we studied the apoptotic process in these cell lines, using the annexin-V/propidium iodide measurement by flow cytometry. Treatment of both BON-1 (Fig. 2a) and HROC57 cells (Fig. 2b) with telotristat alone was even more efficient that everolimus alone in decreasing the number of live cells and increasing the early and late apoptotic cells, while everolimus alone was more efficient in inducing very late apoptosis necrosis. However, in both cell lines the combination of telotristat with everolimus was the most efficient strategy to decrease live cells and increase early and late apoptosis (Fig. 2a, b).

Then we studied the effect of these treatments on the cell cycle in both cell lines. Confirming the results from Figure 2a, b, we observed that the combination of telotristat with everolimus strongly induced the percentage of cells in the sub-G1 phase, corresponding to apoptotic cells (Fig. 2c–f). Treatment with telotristat alone, everolimus alone, or telotristat + everolimus slightly reduced the percentage of cells in the G1 phase. Cells in the S phase were slightly decreased after treatment with everolimus alone. Remarkably, treatment with telotristat or everolimus alone tended to increase the percentage of cells in the G2/M phase, but the combination of telotristat and everolimus induced a marked decrease in G2/M cells, suggesting that this combination was especially toxic in this phase of the cell cycle.

Effects of the Treatment with Telotristat and Everolimus in the Signaling Pathways of the BON-1 Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumor Cells

We explored the status of several signaling pathways associated with cell proliferation and growth, such as the MAPK, the mTOR and the AKT pathways, in cultured BON-1 cells. As shown in Figure 3a, telotristat alone did not alter any of these pathways, compared with the vehicle (DMSO). In contrast, treatment with everolimus strongly inhibited the mTOR pathway, as expected, and induced the MAPK and the AKT pathways, most probably as a feedback loop secondary to the strong mTOR pathway inhibition. The combination of telotristat with everolimus was even more efficient than everolimus alone in inducing the MAPK and AKT pathways (Fig. 3a).

Next, to explore if the observed cellular effects were also true in an in-vivo setting, we subcutaneously inoculated nude mice with BON-1 cells overexpressing a red-fluorescent protein (RFP). When the tumors reached a certain size, we treated mice with telotristat, everolimus, or a combination of both drugs, using the doses previously reported as safe and efficient [26–28], and followed tumor growth. We observed that both telotristat and everolimus alone slowed tumor growth in a similar manner. However, the combination of telotristat and everolimus practically blocked tumor growth during the whole treatment period (Fig. 3b) and was the only strategy that significantly reduced the area under the curve (Fig. 3c), the tumor volume (Fig. 3d), and the tumor weight (Fig. 3e) at the time of sacrifice. These results were consistent with the follow-up of the tumor size using the red fluorescence reporter (Fig. 3f–h). To account for any possible toxicity of the combination of everolimus and telotristat, we measured the body weight at the end of treatment and did not observe any decrease with single or combination treatments (Fig. 3i). We also performed hemograms at the end of treatment and did not observe changes in red blood cell parameters (Figs. 3j and S2a, ESM) nor platelet parameters (Figs. 3k and S2b, ESM). In contrast, single or combination treatments decreased the number of white blood cells, lymphocytes, and monocytes. These decreases were most marked in the combination strategy, although no hematological level reached pathological marks (Figs. 3l and S2c, ESM).

The Combination of Telotristat and Everolimus Strongly Induced Apoptosis in BON-1–Derived Xenografts

We fixed in formalin the tumors obtained after sacrifice and stained them with H&E. We observed large sections of necrotic tumor in all treatment groups, which were even larger in the group treated with the combination of telotristat and everolimus. We also analyzed by immunohistochemistry the levels of apoptosis by active caspase 3 staining, and of proliferation by Ki67 staining. Treatment with telotristat or everolimus alone slightly increased apoptosis (although not significantly), and significantly reduced proliferation. The combination of both agents promoted a strong increase in apoptosis, while maintaining the same levels of proliferation attained by single treatments (Fig. 4a).

Finally, we analyzed by Western blot the status of several signaling pathways in these tumors (Fig. 4b). Treatment with single agents or their combination did not affect the activation status of the proliferation and growth-associated MAPK and the AKT pathways, in contrast with the findings from cultured BON-1 cells shown in Fig. 3a (Fig. 4c). Single treatments and their combination reduced levels of the CYCLIN-D1 proliferation marker, in line with the reduction of Ki67 staining shown in Fig. 3b. We also measured apoptosis- and cell cycle-associated proteins; p53 levels were decreased with all treatments, reaching significance in the combination treatment, while its target, the cell cycle inhibitor p21, was induced (Fig. 4e), in line with the reduced proliferation observed in the Ki67 staining in Fig. 4a. In addition, levels of the anti-apoptotic protein BLC-XL were almost significantly reduced in all treatment groups (Fig. 4f), in line with the increased active caspase 3 staining in Fig. 4a. Finally, we analyzed the epithelial marker E-CADHERIN (Fig. 4g), which was increased in all treatment groups, although without reaching significance in any of them.

Discussion

In our work, we have explored different therapeutic options to reduce cell viability in cell lines derived from neuroendocrine tumors from pancreatic (BON-1 and QGP-1) or intestinal (HROC57) origins. In particular, we focused on two therapeutic fields; first, those against carcinoid syndrome, based on the inhibition of tryptophan hydroxylase (telotristat) or on the somatostatin analog (octreotide). These agents are mainly used for symptomatic treatment of neuroendocrine tumors. Somatostatin analogs have also revealed anti-neuroendocrine tumor activity in several preclinical and clinical trials [2]. In turn, telotristat has also shown anti-proliferative properties against cell lines from liposarcoma, colon cancer, or cholangiocarcinoma [29]. A study by Morse and colleagues showed that NET patients treated with telotristat experienced a more pronounced reduction in their tumor size, compared with their tumor size before treatment, although the mechanisms behind this effect were not described [1]. In the cell lines tested in our work, octreotide did not inhibit cell proliferation, as described before [24], while telotristat was very effective in reducing viability in all of them, indicating that inhibition of serotonin synthesis was more effective than activating the somatostatin pathway in this scenario.

Herrera-Martínez et al. demonstrated that telotristat potently inhibits serotonin secretion with limited effects on proliferation in certain in vitro models, suggesting that serotonin depletion alone may not fully account for antiproliferative effects. Our findings, showing antitumor activity at higher concentrations, are consistent with the possibility that additional mechanisms may contribute [20]. Moreover, consistent with our in-vivo results, Tow et al. demonstrated that inhibition of serotonin biosynthesis suppresses tumor growth in neuroendocrine neoplasm models, supporting a functional role of the serotonin pathway in tumor progression [21].

Second, we focused on the following standard chemotherapies against these types of neuroendocrine tumors: the combination of capecitabine and temozolomide; the tyrosine kinase inhibitor sunitinib; and the mTOR inhibitor everolimus. None of the tested cell lines responded to the capecitabine–temozolomide combination. This resistance can arise from the fact that all these cell lines are derived from advanced tumors that may have developed resistance to these agents. These results are in contrast with previous reports showing sensitivity of these cell lines to 5-fluorouracyl treatment [23, 30]. In contrast, all of these cell lines were sensitive to the tyrosine kinase inhibitor sunitinib and to the mTOR-inhibitor everolimus, with the exception of the HROC57 cells, which remained partially resistant to mTOR inhibition, as previously reported [23]. Importantly, when we combined telotristat with sunitinib, no synergy was observed. However, the combination of telotristat with everolimus yielded a very clear decrease in cell viability compared with single treatments, indicating a clinical window of opportunity. The combination of telotristat and everolimus increased early and late phases of apoptosis and reduced the percentage of cells in the G2/M phase of the cell cycle. These results suggest that the effects of these drugs impact most strongly in mitosis, even though none of them target proteins directly involved in the mitotic process. We explored possible signaling pathways to try clarifying this phenomenon. Indeed, the combination of telotristat and everolimus resulted in a stronger increase in the MAPK and the AKT pathways. We interpret these results as a probable feedback loop resulting from the strong inhibition of the mTOR pathway by everolimus. The inhibition of tryptophan hydroxylase by telotristat adds another cellular stress, probably focused on the mitotic stage, that results in increased apoptosis. We still do not know the precise molecular mechanisms by which tryptophan hydroxylase inhibition cooperates with mTOR inhibition, an interesting field that deserves further research.

Third, we tested these drugs in an in-vivo xenograft model, using nude mice inoculated subcutaneously with BON-1 cells. In line with the observations obtained from cultured cells, BON-1-derived xenografts grew less when mice were treated with telotristat or everolimus alone, with similar efficiency in both cases. However, the most dramatic effect was obtained with the combination of both drugs, which totally blocked tumor growth. This was associated with a significant increase in the levels of active caspase 3, a marker of apoptosis, while proliferation was reduced in all treatment groups. These results indicate that the anti-tumoral effects of the combination treatment were mostly a result of enhanced apoptosis, rather than reduced proliferation. Molecularly, we did not observe the enhanced activation of the MAPK and AKT pathways after any of the treatments that we observed in cultured cells, which could account for the strong anti-tumor effects observed in vivo. In addition, we saw increased expression of the cell cycle inhibitor p21 and reduced expression of the proliferation marker CYCLIN-D1 after all the treatments, and these effects were more pronounced with the combination strategy, accounting for the reduced proliferation observed in the tumors. Importantly, p53 levels were decreased in all treatment groups, which might reflect aberrant p53 function due to its mutant status, as previously reported [31]. Also, we observed a reduced expression of the anti-apoptotic protein BCL-XL, which could explain the enhanced apoptosis observed in the treated tumors, although the combination treatment group did not present lower levels of BCL-XL compared with the single treatment groups, indicating that alternative pro-apoptotic stimuli are acting in the combination treatment. Finally, we observed increased levels of the epithelial marker E-CADHERIN, a marker whose loss is associated with more aggressive stages in NET tumors [32], suggesting a more epithelial differentiation and less aggressiveness in the treated tumors.

Fourth, we were worried about possible toxicity side effects in the combination treatment group. To assess this, we weighed mice at the end of the treatment, and observed no weight reduction and, instead, a slight increase in body weight in the combination treatment, suggesting a good health status in the treated mice. Mice on the combination treatment did not suffer alterations in their red blood and platelet parameters, although they showed slight reduction in their white blood cells that did not reach pathological levels.

Conclusion

Telotristat showed antitumor activity in our NET models, consistent with inhibition of the serotonin biosynthetic pathway, although the relative contribution of serotonin depletion versus other mechanisms remains to be determined. Moreover, the combination of telotristat and everolimus produced a greater antitumor effect than either agent alone in vitro and in vivo, with no major toxicity side effects. These findings are consistent with combination activity, although formal synergy would require dedicated pharmacologic modeling. Our results open a clinical window of opportunity that should be further explored in clinical trials on NET patients.

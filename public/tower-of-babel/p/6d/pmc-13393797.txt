Complete genome sequence of Leuconostoc lactis B34-1-7-1 isolated from Panax ginseng in South Korea
Authors: Hyeonjun Lee, Ye-rim Choi, Eun Bae Kim, Bokyung Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesLeuconostoc lactis is a heterofermentative lactic acid bacterium widely used as a starter culture in fermented foods. The L. lactis B34-1-7-1 strain, isolated from Panax ginseng sprouts cultivated under white LED light, has demonstrated promising probiotic potential, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cancer activities, in previous studies. The genome of L. lactis B34-1-7-1 was sequenced and analyzed to evaluate its probiotic potential and safety for industrial applications, focusing on its genomic features and functional characteristics.Data descriptionThe genome of L. lactis B34-1-7-1 was sequenced using a hybrid Illumina-PacBio approach. The complete genome is composed of a circular chromosome (1,648,351 bp) and a plasmid (12,481 bp), with an overall guanine-cytosine (GC) content of 43.6%. The genome encodes 1,661 coding sequences (CDSs), 68 transfer RNAs, and 12 ribosomal RNAs. Functional classification assigned 99.15% (1,647 genes) of the CDSs to Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COGs). Genome-based screening identified no known antibiotic resistance genes or virulence factors, supporting the safety of this strain. In addition, 23 genes associated with reductase and oxidase activities were identified, providing a genetic basis for its observed antioxidant potential. These results provide a foundational genomic resource supporting the application of L. lactis B34-1-7-1 in the functional food and probiotic industries.

Objective

Leuconostoc lactis is a Gram-positive, non-spore-forming, and facultative anaerobic lactic acid bacterium characterized by its heterofermentative metabolism [1]. It metabolizes glucose to produce lactic acid, CO2, ethanol, and acetic acid, playing a crucial role in flavor development and texture enhancement in various fermented foods [1]. While L. lactis is frequently isolated from traditional fermented foods like kimchi and serves as a vital starter culture, genomic-scale functional characterization and safety evaluation of this species remain relatively limited compared to other lactic acid bacteria [2, 3].

The L. lactis strain B34-1-7-1 described in this study was isolated from the sprouts of Panax ginseng grown hydroponically under white LED light treatment. Previous studies demonstrated that this strain possesses significant probiotic potential, including acid and bile tolerance, antimicrobial activity against pathogens. Furthermore, it has been shown to exhibit antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and in vitro growth-inhibitory effect on cancer cells [4].

The data presented in this study have not been previously published in any peer-reviewed research paper. The dataset was generated to establish a high-quality genomic reference for this ginseng-derived strain and to provide a scientific basis for its observed functional traits. The objective of this study is to report the whole-genome sequence of L. lactis strain B34-1-7-1 and to characterize its probiotic potential, metabolic pathways, and safety through genome-based in silico analysis. These genomic data provide a valuable foundation for the development of this strain in various industrial and probiotic applications.

Data description

The Leuconostoc lactis strain B34-1-7-1 was isolated from hydroponically cultivated Panax ginseng sprouts by incubation in de Man-Rogosa-Sharpe (MRS, BD, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA) medium at 37 °C for 24 h. Genomic DNA was extracted using the Wizard Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Sequencing was performed by Macrogen (Seoul, Korea) using a hybrid strategy combining the Illumina HiSeq platform (151 bp x 2 paired-end) and the PacBio Sequel system (Pacific Biosciences, Menlo Park, CA, USA) [5].

De novo genome assembly was performed using the PacBio SMRTLink (v8.0) pipeline with default options. Error correction of the assembly was performed using Pilon (v1.21) [6]. Genome size and heterozygosity were estimated using Jellyfish (v2.2.10) [7] and Genomescope [8]. CheckM (v1.2.4) [9] confirmed high completeness (97.69%) and minimal contamination (0.6%). Assembly accuracy and ortholog conservation were validated using local BLAST(v2.7.1+) [10] and BUSCO (v5.1.3) [11]. Taxonomic annotation of the chromosome and plasmid was performed using Prokka(v1.13) [12]. The complete genome sequence has been deposited in the repository (Table 1, Dataset 1).

The complete genome consists of a single circular chromosome (1,648,351 bp) and one plasmid (12,481 bp), totaling 1,660,832 bp, with a GC content of 43.6% (Table 1, Data file 1). Annotation identified 1,661 coding sequences (CDS), 68 transfer RNAs (tRNA), 12 ribosomal RNAs (rRNA), and one transfer-messenger RNA (tmRNA) (Table 1, Data file 2). Functional classification assigned 1,099 genes (66.16%) to Gene Ontology (GO) terms and 1,647 genes (99.15%) to Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COGs) (Table 1, Data file 3 and 4) [13]. Within the GO categories, 22 genes were associated with reductase activity and one with oxidase activity, consistent with its in vitro phenotypic traits, including antioxidant capacity (DPPH), > 95% gastrointestinal survival, 75.6% auto-aggregation, anti-inflammatory action (TNF-α suppression), and pro-apoptotic effects in A431 and MDA-MB-231 cells (Cyclin D1/c-Myc downregulation and PARP cleavage) [4].

Safety and functional gene screening using Local Blast and specific databases detected no antibiotic resistance genes with > 80% identity and > 90% coverage against the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD; https://card.mcmaster.ca/analyze/rgi) [14]. Similarly, no genes showing > 90% identity and coverage were identified against 821 bacteriocin sequences in the Bacteriocin Genome Mining Tool (BAGEL4; as of October 2025) [15] or 29,194 virulence factors in the Virulence Factor Database (VFDB; as of November 2025) [16]. For comparative analysis, all complete L. lactis genomes available in NCBI GenBank as of December 2025 were retrieved, and L. citreum strains (PL105 and SH12) were selected as outgroups. Comparative genomic analysis using FastANI (v1.2.0) [17] showed that strain B34-1-7-1 shares over 97.2% Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) with other L. lactis strains, with the highest similarity to strain APC3969 (98.8%) (Table 1, Data file 5, 6). Phylogenetic analysis was performed based on core genes identified using Roary (v3.13.0) [18], aligned using MAFFT (v7.505) [19] and constructed using IQ-TREE 2 (v2.0.7) [20]. L. lactis B34-1-7-1 clustered with the L. lactis APC3969 but was positioned on a separate branch with a bootstrap support value of 100% (Table 1, Data file 5).

Limitations

This study provides insights into the genomic characteristics and probiotic potential of L. lactis B34-1-7-1. However, since the predicted virulence factors and functional traits are based on bioinformatic inferences, further comparative genomic analyses to address intraspecies diversity and experimental validations are required. This genomic data will serve as a foundational resource for the safety assessment and industrial application of L. lactis.

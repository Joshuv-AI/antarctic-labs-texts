Association of interleukin-6 with sarcopenia and its components in older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies
Authors: Jie Ding, Guangyue Yang, Wen Sun, Yifei Li, Ning Wang, Jiening Wang, Yongfang Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObservational studies have documented increased serum IL-6 levels in elderly individuals afflicted with sarcopenia. Nevertheless, the relationship between serum IL-6 concentrations and sarcopenia prevalence in the aging population is yet to be defined.MethodsWe executed a systematic review and meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies that scrutinized serum IL-6 levels in older adults with and without sarcopenia. Relevant studies were sourced from PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science from inception until 10 September 2023. The standard mean differences (SMDs) in serum IL-6 levels between studies were synthesized using a random-effects model. To examine the influence of demographic and clinical factors on these outcomes, we performed subgroup analyses and meta-regression, focusing on variables such as sex, age, and body mass index (BMI). We also assessed the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and the defining components of sarcopenia: muscle mass, muscle strength, and physical performance. We used Fisher’s Z transformation to standardize the interpretation of effect sizes from these relationships. The transformed values were then converted to summary correlation coefficients (r) for a clear and unified summary of the results.ResultsWe included twenty-one cross-sectional studies involving 3,902 participants. Meta-analysis revealed significantly elevated serum IL-6 levels in older adults with sarcopenia compared with those without sarcopenia (SMD = 0.31; 95% CI 0.18, 0.44). The difference was highly pronounced in the subgroups of male and those with female percentage below 50% or a mean BMI below 24 kg/m2. Serum IL-6 levels were inversely correlated with muscle mass (summary r = −0.18; 95% CI −0.30, −0.06), but not with handgrip strength (summary r = −0.10; 95%CI: −0.25, 0.05) or gait speed (summary r = −0.09; 95%CI: −0.24, 0.07).ConclusionsThis meta-analysis establishes a link between increased serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia in the elderly, particularly in relation to decreased muscle mass.

Introduction

Sarcopenia, a condition predominantly afflicting the elderly, is characterized by a progressive and generalized deterioration of skeletal muscle mass, quality, and strength [1,2]. The European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP), the most widely accepted standard for diagnosis and research, offers the definitive criteria for sarcopenia [3]. These criteria were notably revised in 2019, resulting in the updated EWGSOP2 guidelines [1]. Individuals suffering from sarcopenia undergo substantial reductions in muscle strength, which ultimately lead to mobility impairments and commensurate deterioration in their quality of life. These negative outcomes manifest as a heightened risk of frailty fractures, disability, and functional impairment, necessitating extended hospitalization and long-term care [1,2,4]. With the continuing increase in life expectancy and the burgeoning elderly populace, the incidence of sarcopenia is projected to increase markedly in the coming decades [5,6]. The underlying mechanistic causes of sarcopenia, now recognized as a distinct muscle disease within the International Classification of Disease (ICD-10: M62.84) [7], remain unclear. Numerous potential etiological factors and interactions have been posited, including protein metabolism imbalance, hormonal dysregulation, neuromuscular junction loss, systemic chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial abnormalities, and sedentary lifestyles [4,8].

Within its intricate pathophysiological framework, aging-associated systemic inflammation has been implicated in sarcopenia’s progression [9–11]. Aging frequently heralds a cytokine imbalance, characterized by a simultaneous increase in pro-inflammatory mediators and a decrease in anti-inflammatory factors and the subsequent emergence of a chronic low-grade inflammation state termed “inflammaging”. Among various cytokines, interleukin-6 (IL-6) is particularly notable as a critical pro-inflammatory cytokine intricately involved in these processes. IL-6, produced by a variety of tissues, acts as a pivotal mediator in the dual roles of muscle anabolism and catabolism, depending on the in vivo physiological context [12,13]. IL-6 also functions as a myokine secreted during skeletal muscle contractions, where it is instrumental in modulating muscle fiber metabolism and promoting muscle tissue repair. However, sustained IL-6 exposure can instigate catabolic processes in skeletal muscles, triggering a decline in physical performance [12,13]. Elevated serum IL-6 levels are linked to increased prevalence of disability, high rates of mortality [14], and reduced recovery of muscle function in elderly women following fractures [15]. However, the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia remains unclear. Some observational studies found that IL-6 levels were increased in elderly individuals with sarcopenia, and correlated with reduced skeletal muscle mass and strength [16–21]. However, other investigations failed to confirm these findings [22–26].

A recent meta-analysis has not successfully established a clear link between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia [27] because it was constrained by the utilization of an obsolete sarcopenia definition and the high heterogeneity within the collated data. Owing to variations in sarcopenia prevalence based on region, gender, age, and the potential influence of concurrent comorbidities [5,6], a comprehensive stratified analysis is imperative. Therefore, we undertook a systematic review and meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies. Our objective was to scrutinize the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia, taking into account the diagnostic components and detailed variables such as region, gender, age, and clinical context. Through this study, we offer an updated, thorough, and nuanced examination of the existing data on this crucial topic to clarify the effect of IL-6 on sarcopenia development.

Methods

A systematic review and meta-analysis of cross-sectional studies was performed to elucidate the relationship between IL-6 and sarcopenia and its components in older adults. This research was carried out in adherence to the standards set forth by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses statement [28]. The study protocol was preemptively registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews, bearing the identification number CRD42023473163, and was published previously.

Literature search

A literature search was methodically conducted across a range of electronic databases, including PubMed, Scopus, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science, from their respective inceptions through 10 September 2023. The search strategy was comprehensive, encompassing medical subject headings and entry terms pertinent to “sarcopenia” and “interleukin-6”. The detailed search strategy is outlined in Supplementary Material 1. Initial screening of titles and abstracts for potential relevance, followed by a thorough assessment of their eligibility, was conducted with precision by two reviewers, J.D. and G.Y. These reviewers procured the full-text versions of pertinent articles and applied the inclusion and exclusion criteria. In cases of uncertainty regarding an article’s eligibility, a resolution was reached through discussion among all contributing authors.

Study selection

Articles adhering to the following criteria were included: 1. publication within peer-reviewed journals; 2. cross-sectional studies and baseline data derived from longitudinal investigations; 3. study populations with mean or median age of 60 years or older; 4. diagnosis of sarcopenia should be established through the assessment of muscle mass in conjunction with muscle strength or physical performance measures in accordance with the international definitions of sarcopenia; and 5. availability of data for serum IL-6 levels among individuals with and without sarcopenia or an analysis of the correlation between IL-6 levels and at least one measure of muscle mass, muscle strength, or physical performance. Articles were excluded if they: 1. were duplicates or published within the same year; 2. were not written in either English or Chinese; 3. were based on in vitro or animal studies; 4. did not contain original data (e.g. reviews, editorials, or letters); 5. did not clearly present diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia; 6. contained irrelevant research results; and 7. were of substandard quality.

Data extraction

A standardized extraction form was created using Excel software. Three reviewers, J.D., G.Y. and W.S., independently extracted the following information from the selected studies: 1. study characteristics, including the name of the first author, publication year, country of origin, study design, and the criteria and methods used for diagnosing sarcopenia; 2. participant details, such as the sample size, clinical setting, and demographic data (e.g. number of participants, age, gender) for each participant group; 3. methods used for assessing muscle mass, muscle strength, or physical performance in relation to sarcopenia; 4. methods used for assessing IL-6 and serum IL-6 levels in participants with and without sarcopenia; and 5. measures of association between IL-6 and various muscle parameters, including Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r), Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (Rs), and standardized regression coefficients (β). In instances of data duplication, only the most recent and comprehensive results were included.

Quality assessment

The quality of the included studies was evaluated by two independent reviewers, J.D. and G.Y., using the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement [29,30]. In the event of any disagreements, a third reviewer (W.S.) was consulted to mediate. The STROBE statement provides broad reporting suggestions for descriptive observational studies and those examining associations between health outcomes and exposures. It consists of 22 items, each scoring one point. The cumulative score ranges from 0 to 22, serving as a summary evaluation of bias risk for each study. In this systematic review and meta-analysis, any study that attained a score of 13 or below, which corresponds to less than 60% of the maximum achievable score, was consequently excluded from further analysis [31].

Statistical analysis

A meta-analysis was carried out to compare serum IL-6 levels in older adults with and without sarcopenia. The standardized mean difference (SMD) was employed to accommodate variations in IL-6 assessment methods. In instances where data provided only sample size and medians with quartiles or minimum and maximum values, the mean and standard deviation were approximated using the standard method proposed by Shi et al. [32]. The meta-analysis was further stratified by gender, setting, region, body mass index (BMI), and age. Correlation coefficients were standardized using Fisher’s r-to-Z transformation to evaluate the association between serum IL-6 levels and the individual components of sarcopenia—namely, muscle mass, muscle strength, and physical performance [33,34]. Initially, the reported “β” coefficients and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients (“Rs”) were converted to Pearson’s correlation coefficients (“r”) by utilizing established mathematical equations [35,36]. These “r” values were then transformed to Fisher’s Z-scores using the Fisher’s method, to provide a consistent metric suitable for the meta-analytical procedures. In each pooled analysis, the overall effect size was reconverted to correlation coefficients for a precise evaluation of the relationship of IL-6 levels with each specific muscle parameter under study. According to Cohen’s definition, a rho coefficient of 0.1–0.3 indicates weak relationships, 0.3–0.5 indicates moderate relationships, and 0.5–1.0 indicates strong relationships.

A random-effects model was utilized when high heterogeneity was detected. By contrast, a fixed-effect model was adopted in scenarios with low or moderate heterogeneity. Heterogeneity was assessed using chi-squared and I-squared statistics, with high heterogeneity typically signified by a chi-squared P-value less than 0.1 or an I-squared value exceeding 50%. The estimated effect size (ES) was displayed as the weighted mean accompanied by 95% confidence intervals (95% CI), and significance was established at p < 0.05. Meta-regression analyses were undertaken to pinpoint significant moderators or confounders. The presence of small-study effects was evaluated by funnel plot analysis, Begg’s test, and Egger’s test, with p < 0.05 indicating potential publication bias across studies. Review Manager 5.2 was used for the meta-analysis, and STATA 14 was employed for sensitivity analyses, meta-regression, and publication bias analyses.

Results

Selection of relevant studies and characteristics of included studies

A total of 2,456 records were obtained from the systematic search. Upon the removal of duplicates, 1,395 articles underwent initial screening for potential eligibility, as illustrated in the flow chart (Figure 1). After 1,330 articles were excluded based on title and abstract reviews, a thorough full-text review was conducted on the remaining 65 articles for eligibility. Finally, 21 studies conducted between 2013 and 2023 to explore the relationship of IL-6 with sarcopenia and its components satisfied the predefined inclusion criteria and were thus incorporated into this systematic review.

The flowchart of study selection.

Table 1 outlines the primary characteristics of the incorporated studies. This systematic review comprises twenty-one cross-sectional studies [16–20,22,23,25,26,37–48]. The sample size of the individual studies varied from 20 participants [41] to 582 participants [45]. The total sample size across all studies was 3,902 individuals, which included 771 individuals diagnosed with sarcopenia and 3,131 without sarcopenia. Ten studies targeted older adults living in community settings [16,17,19,20,22,23,25,26,38,42], and the other eleven focused on older adults with specific health conditions [18,37,39–41,43–48]. Geographically, fifteen studies were conducted in Asia [16–20,22,23,38,39,41,42,45–48], four in Europe [25,37,43,44], and two in America [26,40]. In terms of sarcopenia diagnostic criteria, the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) 2014 criteria were most frequently utilized, being adopted by ten studies [16–18,22,23,38,42,45,46,48], followed by AWGS 2019 criteria being adopted by four studies [19,20,41,47], EWGSOP2 criteria being adopted by four studies [25,26,43,44], EWGSOP criteria being adopted by one study [39], the Society of Sarcopenia, Cachexia and Wasting Disorders criteria being adopted by one study [37], and the Foundation for the National Institutes of Health criteria being adopted by one study [40].

This review encompasses 21 studies that documented serum IL-6 levels in elderly individuals with and without sarcopenia. Among these studies, seven investigated the association between IL-6 and muscle mass [16,18,23,39,45,46,48], eight explored the correlation between IL-6 and muscle strength [16,18,23,37,39,41,45,46], and six examined the relationship between IL-6 and physical ­performance[18,23,37,41,45,46]. The methodologies for quantifying muscle mass in the reviewed studies were not uniform, employing dual-energy X-ray ­absorptiometry (DEXA) [18,20,26,37,38,40–42,45–47], bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) [16,17,19,22,23,25,39,48], and mid-arm muscle circumference measurement[43]. In terms of ­muscle strength assessment, the handgrip strength test emerged as the most frequently used method [16–20,22,23,25,26,37–43,45–48]. Notwithstanding, only a single study focused on evaluating muscle strength via knee extension strength (KES) [37]. Physical performance assessments employed a variety of metrics, such as gait speed [17–20,22,25,37,38,40–43,45–48], 6-minute walk distance (6MWD)[39], timed-up and go test (TUG) [23], 5-time chair stand test [41], and short physical performance battery (SPPB) [26,38,41].

The methodological quality of each included article was assessed using the STROBE statement. All studies met the quality evaluation criteria. The quality of the studies was in a wide range, with their STROBE scores varying from 15 to 20 (Supplementary Materials 2).

Serum IL-6 differences in sarcopenic and no sarcopenic group

Upon pooling data from these 21 studies, the participants with sarcopenia showed higher serum IL-6 levels than those without sarcopenia (SMD = 0.40; 95%CI: 0.21, 0.59; p < 0.0001). The forest plot is shown in Supplementary Materials 3 A. Substantial heterogeneity was observed among the studies (I2 = 78%, p < 0.00001), leading us to use the random-effects model. Considering this high heterogeneity, we conducted sensitivity analyses, deliberately excluding the study conducted by Rong et al. [17] that displayed particularly extreme results (Supplementary Materials 5 A) to investigate its impact on the overall effect size. The forest plot after excluding Rong et al.’s study is displayed in Figure 2. This exclusion notably reduced the heterogeneity (I2 = 50%, p = 0.006), though the SMD estimation remained significant, albeit at a low value (SMD = 0.31; 95%CI: 0.18, 0.44; p < 0.00001). Upon visually reviewing the funnel plot in Supplementary Materials 6 A, we observed no apparent asymmetry. Begg’s and Egger’s tests indicated non-significant publication bias (p = 0.144 and p = 0.901, respectively).

Meta-analyses of serum IL-6 differences in sarcopenic and no sarcopenic group.

Considering that the meta-analysis still revealed moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 50%, p = 0.006), we proceeded to perform meta-regression analyses to discern potential factors contributing to this heterogeneity. As delineated in Supplementary Materials 7, only the differences in the percentage of females across the included studies emerged as a potential moderator (β = 0.71; 95%CI: 0.56, 0.91; p = 0.009; R2 = 0.92). The subsequent subgroup meta-analyses were then based on the percentage of females (Figure 3A). Studies with a female percentage below 50% showed a large pooled SMD (SMD = 0.44; 95%CI: 0.29, 0.59; p < 0.00001). By contrast, those with a female percentage equal to or exceeding 50% did not show a statistically significant difference (SMD = 0.05; 95%CI: −0.10, 0.20; p = 0.52). Heterogeneities for the two subgroups significantly decreased to I2 = 39% and I2 = 0%. Further segmentation based on participant gender was attempted (Figure 3B), but the obtained data categorizing serum IL-6 levels by gender were limited [19,23,26,47]. In the male subgroup, a significant difference in serum IL-6 levels was observed between the individuals with and without sarcopenia (SMD = 0.61; 95%CI: 0.29, 0.93; p = 0.0002). Such a significant difference was absent in the female subgroup (SMD = 0.18; 95%CI: −0.09, 0.45; p = 0.19).

Subgroup meta-analyses were conducted to evaluate the influence of other different variables such as the setting, geographical region, mean BMI, and mean age on the participants. A significantly larger summarized SMD appeared in the subgroup with a mean BMI below 24 kg/m2 (SMD = 0.44; 95%CI: 0.23, 0.66; p < 0.0001) compared that the control subgroup (SMD = 0.16; 95%CI: −0.01, 0.32; p = 0.07). No notable variations were observed among the groups when considering other factors (Table 2, the forest plots in Supplementary Materials 4 A–D).

Association of IL-6 with the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia

To examine the association of IL-6 with the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia, our investigation focused on data from studies that employed identical or comparable measurement tools to evaluate each component of sarcopenia. Our findings revealed a negative correlation between serum IL-6 levels and muscle mass. No significant correlation was found between serum IL-6 levels and either handgrip strength or gait speed. Owing to the limited data, our study did not explore the association between IL-6 levels and other components of sarcopenia such as KES, 6MWD, TUG, 5-time chair stand test, and SPPB. Initially, our meta-analysis included seven studies examining the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and handgrip strength [16,18,23,39,41,45,46], yielding a pooled Fisher’s Z value of −0.23 (95%CI: −0.36, −0.11; p = 0.0004) (the forest plot in Supplementary Materials 3B). However, sensitivity analysis showed that removing the study by Bian et al. [16] significantly affected the summarized effect sizes (Supplementary Materials 5 C). Following the exclusion of this study, no significant correlation between serum IL-6 levels and handgrip strength was observed (pooled Fisher’s Z − 0.10, 95%CI: −0.26, 0.05; p = 0.20), and no heterogeneity was detected (I2 = 0%, p = 0.93) (Figure 4B). In the meta-analysis of the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and muscle mass that included seven studies [16,18,23,39,45,46,48], a significantly negative relationship was found (pooled Fisher’s Z − 0.18, 95%CI: −0.31, −0.06; p = 0.004), but no heterogeneity was detected (I2 = 0%, p = 0.82) (Figure 4A). After pooling data from five studies [18,37,41,45,46], we observed no significant association between serum IL-6 levels and gait speed (pooled Fisher’s Z − 0.09, 95%CI: −0.25, 0.07; p = 0.28) and detected no heterogeneity (I2 = 0%, p = 0.79) (Figure 4C). Sensitivity analysis demonstrated consistent pooled effect sizes for the relationship of serum IL-6 levels with muscle mass and gait speed after removing each study (Supplementary Materials 5B and 5D).

Meta-analyses of the association between muscle mass, handgrip strength, gait speed and serum IL-6 levels.

No publication bias was observed in the correlations between serum IL-6 levels and the three components of sarcopenia as evidenced by the analysis of funnel plots (Supplementary Materials 6B–D). This finding was confirmed through Begg’s test (muscle mass: p = 1.000; handgrip strength: p = 0.707; gait speed: p = 0.462) and Egger’s test (muscle mass: p = 0.381; handgrip strength: p = 0.737; gait speed: p = 0.563).

The transformation of the pooled Fisher’s Z values into summary correlation coefficients (r values) enabled a rigorous statistical evaluation of the association between serum IL-6 levels and the three components of sarcopenia. Table 3 presents the results, revealing a weak correlation between serum IL-6 levels and muscle mass (summary r = −0.18, 95%CI: −0.30, −0.06). Meanwhile, no statistically significant correlation was observed for handgrip strength (summary r = −0.10, 95%CI: −0.25, 0.05) and gait speed (summary r = −0.09, 95%CI: −0.24, 0.07).

Discussion

This study aimed to perform a thorough quantitative synthesis of existing cross-sectional research to elucidate the alterations in serum IL-6 levels associated with sarcopenia in an elderly population. This form of analysis is vital for the early identification of sarcopenia in high-risk individuals within the elderly population. Our meta-analysis, which encompasses 21 cross-sectional studies and a total of 3,902 subjects, represents an updated and expanded version of preceding reviews on this topic. Our results suggested that serum IL-6 levels are markedly elevated in individuals diagnosed with sarcopenia, including the community-living adults and specific patient groups. Subgroup analyses revealed a pronounced negative relationship between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia in the male subgroup, particularly those with a female composition below 50% or a mean BMI below 24 kg/m2, in contrast to their respective control counterparts. Regarding the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia, our results indicated a significant link between elevated serum IL-6 levels and reduced muscle mass. However, we found no significant association between serum IL-6 levels and the other two key diagnostic parameters: handgrip strength and gait speed. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the inaugural comprehensive meta-analysis investigating the potential associations of serum IL-6 levels with sarcopenia and its three diagnostic components.

Sarcopenia is an age-associated syndrome marked by a progressive decline in skeletal muscle mass and function, which can be further compounded by concurrent morbidities. Prior to 2010, the definition of sarcopenia was predominantly centered on the presence of reduced muscle mass [49], a criterion that inadequately captured the functional implications of the syndrome. In a pivotal advancement in 2010, EWGSOP refined the definition of sarcopenia by proposing that a diagnosis should incorporate diminished muscle mass and impaired muscle function [3]. In 2019, EWGSOP further released an updated set of guidelines (EWGSOP2) to prioritize muscle strength as the primary diagnostic parameter due to its predictive significance for adverse disease outcomes [1]. A reduction in muscle function, characterized by decreased muscle strength or impaired mobility, is not invariably accompanied by a corresponding loss in muscle mass. Declines in muscle function are more closely associated with substantial decrements in physical performance than with a mere reduction in muscle mass [50]. Therefore, EWGSOP2 emphasized the assessment of function over pure mass for sarcopenia diagnosis [1,2]. Given these revised guidelines, a new meta-analysis that explores the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia by adhering to the updated diagnostic guidelines is deemed crucial. Different from the earlier meta-analysis conducted by Bano et al. [27], our research meticulously scrutinized the diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia employed in each of the studies under consideration. Investigations lacking clear diagnostic criteria or defining sarcopenia solely based on muscle mass loss were excluded from our analysis [51–55].

The association between IL-6 and compromised musculoskeletal function and disability has been widely examined. In agreement with our results, recent meta-analyses demonstrated a notable relationship between serum IL-6 levels and frailty status, a geriatric condition bearing significant clinical similarities to sarcopenia [56,57]. Another in-depth meta-analysis investigated the overarching connection between inflammation and muscle mass or muscle strength by employing correlation data [58]. Its findings supported our conclusions, affirming that elevated IL-6 levels were linked to decreased muscle mass and reduced handgrip and knee extension strength, regardless of disease status. However, our results diverged from those of Bano et al. [27], who posited the absence of a relationship between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia when the condition was defined solely by muscle mass reduction. The application of different diagnostic criteria can yield contrasting results. Additionally, Bano et al. reported high heterogeneity in the association between IL-6 and sarcopenia. In 2020, a randomized controlled trial targeted a cohort of 99 older adults with compromised mobility to assess the impact of a 6-month physical activity intervention [59]. The study’s conclusions were unequivocal: age-related IL-6 increases considerably contribute to declining muscle strength, quality, function, and training response [59]. This finding suggested that strategies reducing IL-6 could provide a novel approach for maintaining muscle health.

In this study, a pronounced negative correlation between IL-6 levels and sarcopenia was discerned in populations comprising more than 50% male participants; this trend was also reflected within the male subgroup. These findings were consistent with a meta-analysis determining that heightened levels of IL-6 were markedly linked to decreased muscle mass, reduced handgrip strength, and low knee extension strength, with a pronounced negative correlation apparent in the male subgroups [58]. In another meta-analysis, Mikó et al. [60] uncovered a significantly stronger inverse relationship between plasma IL-6 concentrations and handgrip strength in male subjects compared to their female counterparts. There is now abundant evidence indicating that gender plays a significant role in determining the immune response within the context of inflammation across various disease settings. Moreover, gender differences exert a substantial influence on the severity of disease symptoms and overall mortality. In addition to the immune system’s release of cytokines, the levels of circulating myokines strongly rely on the quantity of muscle mass, which conspicuously varies between genders [61,62]. These distinctions primarily stem from the gender-specific regulatory pathways of the immune system. Furthermore, gender differences may also manifest in age-related changes within these regulatory pathways of the immune system [60]. Notably, the United States National Institutes of Health already mandates the consideration of gender as a biological variable in preclinical studies [63]. Consequently, future clinical investigations should establish gender-specific IL-6 thresholds for the prediction of adverse physical and functional outcomes.

Nutritional status also has a substantial influence on the initiation and advancement of sarcopenia. Multiple studies indicated that a low BMI was associated with a decline in muscle mass and functional capabilities [47,64]. Our current study identified a strong relationship between IL-6 levels and sarcopenia in the elderly with low BMI. This relationship may be due to the effect of inflammatory mediators on nutrient intake and absorption. Inflammatory mediators, notably IL-6, can stimulate the secretion of leptin, leading to reduced food intake, suppressed fat cell synthesis, increased energy metabolism, and induced muscle atrophy [65]. In addition, IL-6 modulates the secretion of hormones that are integral to nutrient assimilation, catabolism, and overall metabolic processes [66]. Thus, IL-6 may impact sarcopenia through mechanisms that involve both nutritional status and metabolic regulation. In addition to aging, sarcopenia is also associated with age-related diseases such as diabetes, respiratory illness, cardiovascular disease, and cognitive impairment [6,67]. In older adults, these diseases often coexist with sarcopenia, with inflammation being a shared characteristic of both conditions [68,69]. Whether the correlations between sarcopenia and inflammation are primarily due to aging or these co-morbidities remains unclear. As such, a subgroup analysis in this meta-analysis was designed to concentrate on clinical settings. After the adjustment for these settings, a significant inverse relationship was found between IL-6 and sarcopenia in community-dwelling cohorts, underlining the pivotal role of IL-6 in sarcopenia progression.

Our meta-analysis revealed no significant association between serum IL-6 levels and two of the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia, namely, handgrip strength and gait speed. Nonetheless, other studies indicated an inverse correlation between serum IL-6 levels and various other parameters of muscle function. For instance, Fülster et al. [37] identified significant negative relationships of IL-6 levels with both quadriceps strength and six-minute walk distance among chronic heart failure patients with sarcopenia. Another study among elderly Saudi women with sarcopenia discovered positive correlations between IL-6 and TUG test, where low values indicate a good function [23]. Similarly, Miyazaki et al. [41] observed a positive relationship between serum IL-6 levels and the five-time chair stand test in Japanese hemodialysis patients with sarcopenia, although they found no association with SPPB. The reason for the discrepancy between muscle mass and muscle function in their correlations with IL-6 levels, as seen in our meta-analysis, remains unclear. Our ability to perform subgroup and meta-regression analyses on each of the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia individually was hindered due to the limited quantity of available studies. Upon examining the gender distribution of the studies included in the meta-analyses for the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia, it was observed that, with the exception of the study conducted by Aldisi et al. [23], the proportion of females in all other studies was less than 50%. It is worth noting that the impact of aging on muscle mass loss appears to be more pronounced in men, while muscle function is relatively more affected in women [61,62]. This discrepancy in the relationship between muscle mass and muscle function with IL-6 levels, as observed in our meta-analysis, may be partially attributed to these gender-specific differences. However, to further validate these findings, larger-scale studies stratified by gender are warranted. Moreover, the elevated IL-6 levels typically associated with aging might not be sufficient to precipitate muscle wasting. The catabolic influence of IL-6 is contingent upon its synergistic interplay with other inflammatory mediators [12,58]. This complexity might elucidate the significant, albeit modest, association between IL-6 levels and muscle mass and may also account for the absence of a detectable correlation between IL-6 levels and handgrip strength or gait speed within our meta-analysis.

This study highlights the clinical significance of chronic inflammation in the preservation of muscle health. A research on aged rats showed that muscle protein synthesis, typically diminished due to age-associated low-grade inflammation, can be reactivated with the administration of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs [70]. These results aligned with recent research illustrating the protective effects of intensive lifestyle modifications on sarcopenia, achieved by curtailing chronic inflammation in older individuals [71]. Such findings strongly advocate for strategies to diminish chronic inflammation as a preventive measure against sarcopenia. These protective elements should be contemplated when devising interventions to prevent or decrease the incidence of sarcopenia in the aging population. Given that the evaluation of clinical biomarkers is more time-efficient and cost-effective than the direct measurement of the ultimate clinical outcomes for the diagnosis of sarcopenia [72], certain inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-6, could act as feasible indicators for the early clinical diagnosis, intervention design, and evaluation of treatment effectiveness post-intervention. Owing to the multifaceted etiology of sarcopenia, establishing a comprehensive panel of inflammatory biomarkers to aid the diagnosis and stratification of sarcopenia severity should be a principal focus of research in the near term [8,9,26].

The primary strength of our meta-analysis lies in its comprehensive approach: not only did we analyze serum IL-6 differences between older adults with and without sarcopenia, we also scrutinized the correlations between serum IL-6 levels and the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia. Moreover, we carried out numerous subgroup analyses to pinpoint potential confounding factors or moderators that might affect the relationship between IL-6 and sarcopenia. Nevertheless, this meta-analysis has certain limitations. First, its reliance on cross-sectional studies necessitates a cautious interpretation of the observed association between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia. The cross-sectional design of the included studies raises the potential for reverse causality. Second, our meta-regression analyses, though extensive, did not account for several potentially residual confounding factors that could affect the relationship between serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia. These factors include variations in living environment, smoking habits, stress levels, undiagnosed inflammatory processes, and diverse sample assay methods used across studies. Third, the interpretations derived from the subgroup analysis based on gender warrant caution. The possibility of small-study effects remains unaddressed due to the insufficient number of gender-stratified reports. This limitation constrains the generalizability of the subgroup findings. Fourth, the limited quantity of available studies inhibited our ability to perform subgroup and meta-regression analyses for each of the three diagnostic components of sarcopenia—muscle mass, strength, and physical performance—individually. This restriction left certain confounding factors within these specific domains unexplored and uncontrolled.

Conclusions

Our meta-analysis confirms an association between elevated serum IL-6 levels and sarcopenia in older adults, especially in relation to reduced muscle mass. The findings underscore the significant impact of chronic low-grade inflammation on the development of sarcopenia. Further longitudinal research that controls for potential confounding variables is necessary to establish the robustness of these associations. Collectively, our results illuminate promising avenues for future investigations and underscore the potential of IL-6 as a biomarker for the early detection of sarcopenia.

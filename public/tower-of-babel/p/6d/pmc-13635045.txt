MANF safeguards mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membrane integrity in nucleus pulposus-derived mesenchymal stem cells to maintain homeostasis of the intervertebral disc
Authors: Chenhao Zhao, Liang Kang, Jiaqi Wang, Yanxin Wang, Yu Chen, Luping Zhou, Chongyu Jia, Huaqing Zhang, Jingyu Zhang, Zhiwei Chen, Renjie Zhang, Cailiang Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration (IDD) is a leading cause of low back pain, with limited treatment options. The degenerative disc’s harsh microenvironment promotes nucleus pulposus-derived mesenchymal stem cells (NP-MSCs) death and hinders self repair. Mesencephalic astrocyte-derived neurotrophic factor (MANF), an atypical neurotrophic factor, has protective effects in degenerative diseases. However, its role in IDD is unclear.MethodsAssessment of MANF expression was conducted in both human nucleus pulposus tissues and a rat IVD puncture model. An in vitro model of degeneration was established by acid treatment of NP-MSCs, and the functional role of MANF was explored through its knockdown and overexpression. RNA sequencing was employed to identify downstream targets. The therapeutic potential of MANF-overexpressing NP-MSCs was evaluated in a rat puncture model.ResultsMANF expression was markedly downregulated in degenerated IVD tissues from both human patients and rat models. Correspondingly, in vitro experiments demonstrated that MANF knockdown exacerbated, while its overexpression mitigated, acid-induced apoptosis of NP-MSCs. Mechanistically, MANF attenuated mitochondrial dysfunction and ER stress of NP-MSCs under acidic conditions by maintaining MAM integrity, as demonstrated by the complete abolition of this protection upon treatment with the MAM uncoupler FATE1. Transcriptomic analysis and subsequent validation identified receptor expression-enhancing protein 1 (REEP1) as a critical downstream effector through which MANF safeguards MAM integrity. We further elucidated that MANF upregulates REEP1 expression by directly inhibiting miR-33b-5p. In vivo, transplantation of MANF-overexpressing NP-MSCs effectively attenuated IDD in a rat model.ConclusionMANF protected NP-MSCs from acidosis by sustaining MAM integrity via the MiR-33b-5p/REEP1 axis. These findings reveal MANF’s mechanism and therapeutic potential for IDD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10565-026-10226-8.

Introduction

Intervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration (IDD) is a leading cause of chronic low back pain, significantly affecting the quality of life for millions globally (Zhu et al. 2023; Wei et al. 2025; Hoy et al. 2014; Song et al. 2018). As IDD progresses, the structure and function of the disc deteriorate, heightening the risk of pain and potentially compromising spinal stability (Buckwalter 1976; Yang et al. 2022; Zhu et al. 2025). Current treatment modalities largely consist of pharmacotherapy or surgical interventions, which primarily alleviate pain without restoring the IVD's original structure (Tryfonidou et al. 2020).

The reparative role of stem cells in IDD has garnered extensive attention (Sakai and Schol 2017). nucleus pulposus-derived mesenchymal stem cells (NP-MSCs) exhibit robust self-renewal capacity and multipotent differentiation potential, playing a pivotal role in the intrinsic repair mechanisms of IVDs (Yan et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2025; Zheng et al. 2025). Lactate, a metabolic byproduct of anaerobic glycolysis, accumulates within IVDs to form an acidic microenvironment (Luo et al. 2023). This acidic environment can suppress the proliferation and differentiation of NP-MSCs, thereby impairing their reparative function and significantly influencing IDD progression (Liu et al. 2017; Vadalà et al. 2019). Thus, safeguarding these cells from acid-induced damage has emerged as a critical research focus in the pursuit of effective treatments for degenerative IVD diseases.

The endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria are two major organelles that interact closely through mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membranes (MAM) to collaboratively regulate cellular metabolism, calcium homeostasis, and stress responses (Beaulant et al. 2022). Exposure to various stimuli can disrupt MAM function, triggering endoplasmic reticulum stress responses and mitochondrial dysfunction, which may culminate in cellular apoptosis (Larrañaga-SanMiguel et al. 2025). Therefore, maintaining MAM integrity is not only crucial for cellular stress response mechanisms but also presents a viable target for addressing cellular dysfunction.

Mesencephalic astrocyte-derived neurotrophic factor (MANF) is an evolutionarily conserved protein present across various organs and cell types. MANF is associated with inflammation, endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial homeostasis, cell apoptosis, and oxidative stress, highlighting its significant protective role in several degenerative diseases (Liu et al. 2022). However, whether MANF alleviates IDD by preserving MAM integrity remains unknown.

This study aimed to investigate the role of MANF in acid-induced NP-MSCs and the associated mechanisms. An acid-induced NP-MSC degeneration model was employed for in vitro molecular function validation, whereas a rat caudal IVD puncture model served for in vivo experimental validation. Initially, we assessed MANF expression in degenerated nucleus pulposus tissues. Subsequently, through MANF knockdown and overexpression, we further explored its protective effects on NP-MSCs in an acidic environment. Our findings indicate that MANF exerts its protective effect on NP-MSCs by maintaining the integrity of MAM. Receptor expression-enhancing protein 1 (REEP1) was identified as a downstream target of MANF, essential for maintaining MAM integrity. Finally, implantation of MANF-overexpressing NP-MSCs yielded improvements in IDD in SD rats. This research provides novel molecular targets for IDD treatment and expands the therapeutic potential of MANF.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

This experiment utilized IVD tissues discarded during surgical procedures from patients at The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. The study included six patients with mild disc degeneration (MDD, grades II and III) and six patients with severe disc degeneration (SDD, grades IV and V), which were utilized for pathological analysis and MANF expression detection in the nucleus pulposus. NP-MSCs derived from Pfirrmann grade II tissues were utilized to create a degenerative cellular model. The included patients had no additional underlying disorders, such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, or hypertension, nor infectious infections, including AIDS, hepatitis B, or syphilis. This study received approval from the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (PJ 2024–10–77), and informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Experimental animals

This study used Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats to construct an in vivo model because the structure of their intervertebral discs is similar to that of humans. To eliminate the influence of estrogen, male SD rats (3 months old) of specific pathogen-free grade were used in this experiment. The rats were obtained from the Experimental Animal Center of Anhui Medical University.

Thirty-six SD rats were numbered and simply randomized using the random number table method, with investigators and data analysts blinded to group assignments throughout the study. Thirty-six SD rats were randomly assigned to six groups. (A) In the control group, the skin was punctured without compromising the IVD. (B) In the IDD group, following sterilization of the tail with 75% ethanol, a 21 G puncture needle was vertically inserted into the caudal IVD (C6/7), rotated 360°, and maintained for 30 s. (C) In the PBS group, PBS was injected into the punctured IVD. (D) In the NP-MSC group, viable rat NP-MSCs were injected into the perforated annulus fibrosus of the disc utilizing a microinjector. (E) In the LV-NC group, LV-NC pretreatment NP-MSCs were implanted into the ruptured intervertebral disc. (F) In the LV-MANF group, LV-MANF pretreatment NP-MSCs were implanted into the perforated intervertebral disc. All SD rats were housed in the same chamber to rule out the confounding effects of different temperatures, humidities, and circadian rhythms. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) images were conducted four weeks after the puncture, followed by euthanasia. Information on those who passed away unexpectedly will be omitted. Animal procedures received approval from the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Hefei Comprehensive National Science Center, adhering to the Basel Declaration (IACUC Number IHM-AP-2024–073).

Data source and data analysis

The single-cell RNA sequencing datasets dataset analyzed in this study was downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE165722, GSE244889, and GSE251686 were uesd to evaluate MANF expression in NP-MSCs. A total of 21 samples (11 mildly degenerated and 10 severely degenerated), comprising 156,340 cells, were included in our analysis. To increase statistical power, we integrated these three datasets into a combined cohort. Cells expressing CD90, a canonical mesenchymal stem cell surface marker, were identified as NP-MSCs. MANF expression levels were compared between CD90+cells derived from mildly versus severely degenerated samples.

NP-MSC isolation, culture and identification

NP-MSC isolation and culture was performed as described previously (Kang et al. 2026; Lin et al. 2025). Briefly, the NP tissue of individuals who had lumbar vertebral fractures was used to isolate primary human NP-MSCs, while the NP tissue of the caudal IVD of SD rats was used to isolate primary rat NP-MSCs. IVD tissues were chopped into small pieces, digested for 8–12 h with 0.25% type II collagenase (Biofoxx), and then rinsed three times with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The separated cells were cultured in low-glucose Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium that was enhanced with 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Biosharp) and 10% fetal bovine serum (CLARK). In the tests that followed, NP-MSCs from the third passage were used, and the media was replaced every three days.

Cells at passage 3 were harvested and incubated with fluorophore-conjugated antibodies against CD73, CD90, CD105, CD34, and HLA-DR, followed by flow cytometric analysis. According to the International Society for Cellular Therapy (ISCT) criteria, cells positive for CD73, CD90, and CD105, and negative for CD34 and HLA-DR, were identified as NP-MSCs.

Acid treatment and pH gradient preparation

To simulate the progressive acidification of the IVD microenvironment, complete medium was adjusted to pH 7.4, 7.1, 6.8, and 6.5 using 1M HCl (Diamant et al. 1968; Xie et al. 2017). The pH was verified with a calibrated pH meter, and media were filter-sterilized prior to use. Cells were cultured in pH-adjusted media for 24 h.

Transfection of small interfering RNA (siRNA)

siRNA (Ribobio, Guangzhou, China) was reconstituted in RNase-free water and preserved at –20℃following the manufacturer's guidelines. Cells were transfected at 30%–50% confluence to enhance transfection efficiency. The siRNA was mixed with the transfection reagent (Ribobio, Guangzhou, China) and added to the medium devoid of penicillin and streptomycin, per the specifications outlined in the manual. Subsequently, the mixture was gently introduced to NP-MSCs. Protein and mRNA analyses were performed 36 h post-transfection.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted utilizing RIPA (ApexBio, Houston, USA), subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% milk at 25℃ for 2 h, followed by treatment with primary and secondary antibodies. Enhanced chemiluminescence was employed for detection.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from NP-MSCs employing TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Grand Island, NY). Reverse transcription and amplification were performed following the producer's recommendations (Biosharp, China) utilizing quantitative reverse-transcription PCR. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^ − ΔΔCt method, with ACTB for mRNA and U6 for miRNA as internal controls.

Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence

IVD tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned into 5 μm-thick slices. After deparaffinization with xylene, conventional hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining was conducted. Following deparaffinization, hydration, and antigen retrieval, the sections designated for immunohistochemical staining were treated with antibodies and subsequently examined and photographed using a microscope.

Cells were seeded on 24 mm diameter slides, rinsed thrice with PBS to eliminate the culture media, thereafter fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at 25 °C for 30 min, and then subjected to three 5-min washes with PBS. Thereafter, cells were exposed to 0.1% Triton X-100 for 5 min. Following three washes with PBS, cells were incubated with 2% bovine serum albumin at 37 °C for 2 h, subsequently exposed to the primary antibody at 4 °C for 8 to 12 h, and finally treated with Alexa Fluor 488 or Alexa Fluor 594 conjugated secondary antibody (1:300) at 37 °C for 2 h. The nuclei were counterstained with 0.1 g/mL 4′,6-diamidine-2′-phenylindole dihydrochloride (Beyotime, Nantong, China) for 5 min, and pictures were acquired using a microscope.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) detection

According to the manufacturer's guidelines, the dihydroethidium probe (Beyotime, Nantong, China) was utilized to measure intracellular ROS levels. A functional solution was formulated at a ratio of 1:1000. NP-MSCs and the working solution were co-incubated at 37 °C in a carbon dioxide incubator for 1 h, followed by flow cytometry to assess the intensity of green fluorescence.

Mitochondrial membrane potential detection

In accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines, JC-1 (Beyotime, Nantong, China) was used to assess mitochondrial membrane potential. Cells were inoculated in 96-well plates, utilizing a working solution made at a dilution of 1:1000. 100 µL of the solution was dispensed into each well, incubated at 37 °C in a carbon dioxide atmosphere for 1 h, and subsequently, a microplate reader was employed to measure fluorescence intensity.

Cell apoptosis detection

Apoptosis was identified in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines with Annexin V/PI (ApexBio, Houston, USA). NP-MSCs were digested from the six-well plates to create a cell suspension, which was collected in flow cytometry tubes. After staining with Annexin V for 15 min, followed by propidium iodide staining for 5 min, fluorescence intensity was detected using a flow cytometer. One unstained tube and two tubes stained with only one dye were utilized for fluorescence intensity compensation.

RNA-seq and GSEA analysis

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent. RNA-seq libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw reads were processed with Trimmomatic and aligned to GRCh38 using HISAT2. Differential expression was analyzed using DESeq2 (|log₂FC|≥ 1, adjusted P < 0.05). GSEA was performed using clusterProfiler against MSigDB gene sets. Visualization was performed using the OECloud platform (https://cloud.oebiotech.com/task/) with R software.

Dual luciferase report assay

The interaction sequences between miR-33b-5p and REPP1 were forecasted utilizing Target Scan software. The REEP1 3′-untranslated region, featuring either wild-type (WT) or mutant (MUT) binding sites for miR-33b-5p, was included into a pmirGLO vector (Promega). A dual luciferase assay kit was employed to quantify luciferase activities 48 h post-transfection. Renilla luciferase activity served as the normalizing standard.

Statistical analysis

At least three independent replications of each experiment were conducted. The GraphPad Prism program (La Jolla, California, USA) was used for statistical analysis and graphing. One-way analysis of variance or t-tests were used to evaluate group differences; statistical significance was defined as ***P < 0.001, **P < 0.01, *P < 0.05.

Results

MANF expression was downregulated in degenerative NP tissues

IVD tissue discarded during surgery from patients with mild and severe degeneration was collected to investigate changes in MANF expression. The nucleus pulposus tissue exhibiting mild degeneration appeared white, hydrated, and elastic, whereas that with severe degeneration was darker, displaying a hardened center, loss of hydration and a reduced number of cells (Fig. 1A). To characterize MANF expression in NP-MSCs during IDD, we analyzed three independent single-cell RNA-seq datasets (GSE165722, GSE244889, and GSE251686). MANF expression was consistently downregulated in CD90+ NP-MSCs from severely degenerated samples, indicating that MANF is reduced in NP-MSCs during IDD (Fig. 1B). Besides, MANF expression at mRNA levels was reduced in nucleus pulposus tissue with severe degeneration (Figure S1A, Supporting Information). Immunohistochemical staining revealed that lower MANF levels and higher cleaved caspase-3 levels in SDD NP tissues relative to MDD NP tissues (Fig. 1C-F). In this study, in vivo models were constructed to assess MANF expression. MANF expression at mRNA levels was downregulated in IDD group (Figure S1B, Supporting Information). However, the acidic microenvironment in degenerative IVDs often leads to apoptosis of NP-MSCs, thereby limiting their protective function. To further explore the association between MANF and apoptosis of NP-MSCs under acidic conditions, human primary NP-MSCs were isolated and characterized. Flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that NP-MSCs were positive for CD73, CD90, and CD105 and negative for CD34 and HLA-DR (Figure S2A, Supporting Information). A gradient of pH levels was established to construct an ex vivo model of intervertebral disc degeneration. With a decrease in pH, MANF expression in NP-MSCs was upregulated (Fig. 2A-C). To elucidate the role of MANF in NP-MSC apoptosis under acidic conditions, MANF knockdown and overexpression were performed in NP-MSCs. Cells were treated with siRNA-MANF for 36 h, and subsequent western blotting confirmed successful suppression of MANF expression (Fig. 2G, H). Compared to the control group, NP-MSCs under acidic conditions demonstrated increased apoptosis (Fig. 2I). Decreased Bcl-2 and increased Bax and cleaved caspase-3 also corroborate increased apoptosis in NP-MSCs treated with acid (Fig. 2J, L). In addition, MANF knockdown exacerbated cell apoptosis (Fig. 2I-G, L). Conversely, administration of recombinant human MANF (rhMANF) mitigated the adverse effects of the acidic environment on cell apoptosis (Fig. 2K, M–N).

MANF reduced the dysfunction of mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum in NP-MSCs under acidic conditions

Although we have established the protective role of MANF against cell apoptosis in acidic environments, the precise mechanism by which it reduce the apoptosis of acid-treated NP-MSCs remains undefined. Mitochondrial dysfunction and excessive endoplasmic reticulum stress are frequently implicated in cell apoptosis when cells are exposed to deleterious external stimuli (Xu et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2025). Therefore, we established four pH gradients to investigate whether acidic environments induce damage to the mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum of NP-MSCs. Western blot analysis revealed that increased p-IRE1α, ATF6, BIP, p-eIF2α, ATF4 and CHOP in NP-MSCs under acidic conditions (Fig. 3A, B). MANF is integral to maintaining the homeostasis of mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum, as previously documented in the literature (Kim et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2020a). RNA sequencing demonstrated that MANF knockdown significantly influences intracellular calcium ion concentration and mitochondrial depolarization (Figure S3A–E, Supporting Information). Based on these, we hypothesized that its protective role against apoptosis might be mediated through the preservation of these organelles. To investigate whether MANF exerts a protective role in the dysfunction of mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum in NP-MSCs under acidic conditions, we treated NP-MSCs with siRNA-MANF and rhMANF separately and assessed the damage to mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum under varying intervention conditions. Increased mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum dysfunction was evidenced by decreased SIRT3 and PGC1α reduction in cell membrane potential, elevation of intracellular ROS and increased p-IRE1α, ATF6, BIP, p-eIF2α, ATF4 and CHOP in NP-MSC treated with acid, with further enhancement in NP-MSCs co-treated with si-MANF and acid (Fig. 3H–N). Conversely, the administration of rhMANF mitigated the dysfunction of mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum in NP-MSCs under acidic conditions (Fig. 3O–U). Additionally, we employed a red fluorescent probe to label the endoplasmic reticulum and a green fluorescent probe to label mitochondria to investigate alterations in MAM. We found that the integrity of MAM decreased in NP-MSCs treated with acid. In addition, rhMANF could counteract this change, whereas siRNA–MANF intervention intensified it (Fig. 3V).

Overexpression of MANF prevented ER stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis by enhancing MAM formation in acid-treated human NP-MSCs

FATE1 is a protein that can decouple mitochondria from the endoplasmic reticulum (Bassot et al. 2019). To elucidate the association between changes in MAM under acidic conditions and the dysfunction of mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum in NP-MSCs, as well as cell apoptosis, we constructed an overexpression plasmid with a FLAG tag for FATE1, transfected it into cells, and confirmed the successful expression of FATE1 (Fig. 4A). Images obtained via confocal microscopy demonstrated that the expression of FATE1 effectively decoupled mitochondria from the endoplasmic reticulum (Fig. 4B). Treatment with rhMANF can alleviate the apoptosis of NP-MSCs post-acid treatment, whereas overexpression of FATE1 partially inhibits the protective effect of MANF (Fig. 4C-E). Furthermore, rhMANF treatment enhances the expression of PGC1α and SIRT3 in NP-MSCs, whereas FATE1 negates this effect of rhMANF (Fig. 4K-L). The expression of FATE1 suppressed rhMANF's capacity to reduce ROS levels in NP-MSCs under acidic conditions and to improve cell membrane potential (Fig. 4H-J). Additionally, compared to NP-MSCs subjected to acid treatment alone, those treated with rhMANF showed decreased protein levels of p-IRE1α, ATF6, BIP, p-eIF2α, ATF4 and CHOP, as assessed by western blotting. In contrast, transfection with a FATE1 overexpression plasmid further elevated the expression of p-IRE1α, ATF6, BIP, p-eIF2α, ATF4 and CHOP in NP-MSCs under acidic conditions. (Fig. 4F, G). MiR-33b-5p served as a potential bridging molecule mediating MANF-dependent regulation of REEP1

Subsequently, we investigated the potential mechanisms by which MANF regulates REEP1 expression. miRNAs, small non-coding RNAs typically 22–25 nucleotides in length, participate in the pathogenesis of various diseases, including IDD, through direct suppression of target gene mRNA levels (Rupaimoole and Slack 2017; Sun et al. 2025). We initially employed the TargetScan algorithm from three databases (TargetScan Human 8.0, mirDIP, and miRDB) to identify potential bridging miRNAs targeting REEP1. The database cross-check identified 10 candidate miRNAs targeting the 3′-UTR of REEP1 mRNA (Fig. 6A). Following treatment with either siRNA-NC or siRNA-MANF, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that MANF knockdown significantly upregulated miR-208b-3p and miR-33b-5p expression (Fig. 6B). Knockdown or overexpression of miR-33b-5p significantly influenced both mRNA and protein expression levels of REEP1 (Fig. 6C-E). Luciferase reporter assays further indicated that REEP1 expression could be directly regulated by miR-33b-5p. Compared with the miR-NC group, human NP-MSCs co-transfected with miR mimics and WT REEP1 exhibited significantly reduced luciferase activity. No notable changes were observed in the MUT REEP1 group transfected with either miR-33b-5p mimics or mimic-NC (Fig. 6F). Treatment with miR-33b-5p mimics reduced REEP1 expression relative to rhMANF treatment alone, whereas the miR-33b-5p inhibitor upregulated REEP1 expression in MANF-knockdown cells (Fig. 6G–L). Discussion

IDD stands as a leading cause of debilitating low back pain, imposing a significant socioeconomic burden worldwide. Current therapeutic strategies predominantly address symptoms rather than the underlying pathophysiology of disc degeneration. Consequently, strategies aimed at promoting endogenous repair or augmenting cell-based regeneration, particularly utilizing NP-MSCs, have garnered substantial interest. However, the success of such approaches is critically hampered by the harsh, acidic microenvironment of the degenerated disc, defined by progressive acidosis due to enhanced anaerobic glycolysis and lactate accumulation. This acidic conditions directly compromises the viability and function of resident and transplanted cells. Our study addresses this pivotal challenge by identifying MANF as a central regulator of NP-MSC survival under acidic conditions. We delineate a novel molecular axis wherein MANF, through the modulation of miR-33b-5p and its target REEP1, preserves MAM integrity, thereby mitigating organelle dysfunction and apoptosis. These findings position MANF as a crucial guardian of cellular homeostasis in the context of IDD.

Our investigation revealed a nuanced expression profile of MANF during IDD. While MANF protein levels were significantly decreased in severely degenerated human NP tissues, in vitro exposure of NP-MSCs to a pathological pH gradient triggered a pronounced, dose-dependent upregulation. This seemingly paradoxical pattern is biologically insightful: the in vitro upregulation likely represents an acute, adaptive cytoprotective response, akin to compensatory inductions documented in models of acute tissue injury (Lindholm et al. 2007; Park et al. 2019). Conversely, the in vivo downregulation in advanced IDD may reflect a state of chronic decompensation, where sustained multifactorial stress leads to the gradual exhaustion of this protective response or a loss of MANF-expressing cell populations.

MANF, an atypical neurotrophic factor, is known to mitigate endoplasmic reticulum stress and support mitochondrial homeostasis in various tissues (Zhang et al. 2024; Tang et al. 2022; Tao et al. 2022; Xiong et al. 2025; Zheng et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024). Here, we extend its functional repertoire to the context of IDD. Our gain- and loss-of-function experiments unequivocally showed that MANF protects NP-MSCs from acidosis-induced apoptosis. Mechanistically, the protective function of MANF is associated with the attenuation of mitochondrial dysfunction and ER stress. MAMs are crucial for calcium transfer, lipid metabolism, and apoptotic signaling between mitochondria and ER (Etxebeste-Mitxeltorena et al. 2025; Jiang et al. 2024; Paillusson et al. 2016; Fan et al. 2022; Beetz et al. 2013). Further experiments demonstrated that MANF overexpression maintains the homeostasis of mitochondria and ER by preserving MAM integrity.

To elucidate how MANF stabilizes MAMs, we focused on REEP1, a protein essential for ER shaping and membrane tethering (Lim et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2018; Wang et al. 2021). Transcriptomic analysis and subsequent validation revealed that MANF positively regulates REEP1 expression. Crucially, the protective effects of MANF on MAM integrity and cell viability were nullified upon REEP1 knockdown, positioning REEP1 as a vital downstream mediator. Furthermore, we identified that MANF likely upregulates REEP1, at least in part, by suppressing miR-33b-5p. These findings delineate a novel MANF/miR-33b-5p/REEP1 axis that is central to maintaining MAM homeostasis and NP-MSC survival under metabolic stress.

The therapeutic potential of this pathway was corroborated in vivo. Implantation of MANF-overexpressing NP-MSCs into a rat puncture model significantly alleviated IDD progression, underscoring the translational relevance of our findings. To our knowledge, this is the first study to comprehensively define the protective role of MANF in NP-MSCs and to link its function to the maintenance of MAM integrity via REEP1 in the pathogenesis of IDD.

We acknowledge several limitations that pave the way for future research. First, the discrepancy between in vivo and in vitro MANF expression patterns points to the need for models that better capture the chronicity of IDD. Future studies employing aged, disc-specific MANF knockout mice will be invaluable for dissecting the dynamic role of MANF across the entire disease continuum. Second, while the MANF/miR-33b-5p/REEP1 axis is a major pathway, MANF is a multifaceted protein. It is plausible that other mechanisms, such as direct protein–protein interactions within the ER lumen or modulation of other signaling pathways, also contribute to its protective profile. Finally, the upstream regulators governing MANF expression in the disc, particularly in response to the complex mix of microenvironmental cues, remain unknown and warrant investigation.

Conclusion

To sum up, this study clarifies the protective function of MANF in the course of IDD and its ability to mitigate acid-induced apoptosis in NP-MSCs. MANF suppresses miR-33b-5p expression, leading to the upregulation of REEP1, which in turn stabilizes mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membrane (MAM) integrity. This mechanism preserves mitochondrial function, attenuates ER stress, and ultimately enhances NP-MSC survival under acidic conditions. Collectively, these findings underscore the crucial contribution of the MANF/miR-33b-5p/REEP1 axis in maintaining disc homeostasis and promoting stem cell–based repair, positioning MANF as a promising therapeutic target for mitigating intervertebral disc degeneration.

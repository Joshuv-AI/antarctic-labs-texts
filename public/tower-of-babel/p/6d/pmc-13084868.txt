Understanding the burden of pulmonary arterial hypertension among patients and caregivers through narrative medicine: data from Italian multicentre observational INSPECTIO study
Authors: Laura Scelsi, Roberto Badagliacca, Marco Vicenzi, Emma Di Poi, Fabiana Baldi, Pietro Ameri, Edoardo Airò, Francesca Bux, Dagna Lorenzo, Livio Giuliani, Gian Piero Perna, Edoardo Bonsante, Carlo D’Agostino, Claudia Raineri, Piergiuseppe Agostoni, Antonietta Cappuccio, Erika Greco, Maria Giulia Marini, Matteo Biancospino, Alessia Uglietti, Stefano De Santis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionA key challenge in pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) research is assessing the specific impact of this condition on health-related quality of life (HRQoL), also considering the patient’s subjective experience and caregiving burden. The prospective, observational, multicentre Non-INterventional Study on Pulmonary arterial hypertension patients treated with macitentan and/or selexipag: ExperienCe from an ITalIan cOhort (INSPECTIO) study integrated narratives with patient-reported outcomes (PROs) and clinical data to gain deeper insight into patients’ experiences of PAH, also involving caregivers’ perspectives.MethodsThe study was conducted across 29 Italian hospital-based centres and enrolled adult patients with PAH already on treatment with macitentan and/or selexipag as part of combination therapy. A dedicated narrative collection was carried out within the larger study. Patient and caregiver narratives were collected at enrolment (visit 1; V1) and at 12 months (visit 3; V3), then analysed through MAXQDA software and correlated with emPHasis-10 questionnaire scores and clinical data.ResultsOut of the 186 patients enrolled in the overall INSPECTIO study, 96/186 (52%) completed the narrative at V1 and 58/186 (31%) at V3; 29/54 (54%) caregivers completed the narrative at V1 and 16/54 (30%) at V3. The analysis revealed an alignment between patient narratives and emPHasis-10 scores. Specifically, at V3, emPHasis-10 scores were higher for patients who did not mention their domestic life in narratives, reported feelings of shame or isolation, limitations or breathlessness also in daily activities and reported ongoing treatment-related issues. Nonetheless, at V3, 36/58 (62%) of patients described living with PAH as manageable. The care pathway showed a positive impact on caregivers: their narratives indicated a shift from V1 to V3, with improved perceptions of the care relationship and PAH therapies.ConclusionINSPECTIO is the first study to integrate narratives, PROs and clinical data in PAH research. Findings suggested that appropriate support and therapeutic management are crucial to help patients cope with the condition. Narratives have proven to be a valuable tool for understanding the impact of PAH and improving its management, providing insights that can inform a more patient-centred approach.Trial registration numberNCT04567602.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a rare subtype of pulmonary hypertension (PH),1 marked by increased blood pressure due to stiffening and narrowing of the pulmonary arteries.2 Despite notable advancements in treatment, PAH remains linked with significantly impaired health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and reduced survival rates.35 The impact of PAH extends across physical, emotional and social domains, making it a critical concern for both patients and treating clinicians.4

HRQoL is a crucial component of patient-reported outcomes (PROs),6 which are direct patient testimony regarding their health conditions without healthcare professionals’ interpretation,7 and is increasingly recognised as an essential endpoint in clinical trials.8 Moreover, the necessity of incorporating patient perspective into PAH management strategies was emphasised in the most recent international guidelines and expert consensus.1 5 9 However, the specific determinants of HRQoL in PAH patients remain poorly understood,10 and routine assessment of HRQoL in clinical practice is often lacking.5

The clinical evaluation of PAH poses challenges, including symptom burden and treatment demands, underscoring the relevance of HRQoL as a key endpoint.10 PAH significantly affects everyday life, impacting physical activity, general health, vitality, emotional well-being and social functioning.4 11 12 However, while the physical consequences of PAH are well documented, its psychological impact remains overlooked.13 14 Feelings of social isolation stemming from the absence of visible symptoms and a general lack of community awareness about the disease were reported by individuals with PAH.1 15 PAH has additional implications for the mental health of caregivers, who may bear further burdens and face emotional and social challenges that echo the isolation experienced by patients.1 15

A key challenge in current PH and PAH research is effectively capturing the specific impacts of these conditions on HRQoL,16 also considering the patient’s subjective experience prospectively. Narrative research, endorsed by the WHO, provides a valuable approach to exploring individual illness experiences17 in a real-world context and complements traditional HRQoL findings from clinical studies.18 Narrative medicine (NM), which is based on patient illness narratives,19 aims to integrate the biomedical aspects with the personal and social experiences of a condition.20 Within this framework, narratives have been integrated with PROs and clinical data in observational studies21 22 to enhance understanding of living with a condition and to provide HRQoL-related insights that foster clinical knowledge and patient awareness.

Aim

The prospective, observational, multicentre ‘Non-INterventional Study on Pulmonary arterial hypertension patients treated with macitentan and/or selexipag: ExperienCe from an ITalIan cOhort’ (INSPECTIO) study (NCT04567602) aimed to generate data to confirm the effectiveness of the connection between methodical risk assessment and treatment strategy in patients with a confirmed diagnosis of PAH. Furthermore, the study aimed to determine the level of adherence to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines1 and related sixth World Symposium on PH (WSPH) proceedings in daily clinical practice.

Within this study, narratives were used along with PROs to provide a comprehensive understanding of the impact of PAH as perceived by patients and caregivers. Clinical findings from the study are presented elsewhere.23 Here, findings from the analysis of narratives and the comparison between narratives and PROs after 12 months of participation in the study are reported.

Methods

The larger INSPECTIO study was conducted to collect data on patients with PAH treated with macitentan and/or selexipag, as per usual clinical practice, to describe the use of risk stratification according to the ESC/ERS guidelines and the sixth WSPH recommendations as well as related clinical outcomes. The study started in November 2020 and included 29 expert hospital-based centres treating PAH across Italy; the detailed methods, including ethical approval and NCT registration, are reported elsewhere23 . Owing to favourable interim results at 12 months,24 demonstrating stability or improvement in key clinical parameters and confirming the safety profile of macitentan and selexipag, the sponsor decided to discontinue the study ahead of schedule in February 2024.

Participation was entirely voluntary. The primary patient inclusion criteria were being aged ≥18 years, having a first or confirmed diagnosis of PAH by right heart catheterisation at enrolment or not later than 12 months from enrolment date, with a low-to-intermediate risk profile as per clinical judgement based on a multiparametric approach, having started macitentan and/or selexipag treatment (in monotherapy or combination therapy), and having signed an informed consent form allowing data collection and source data verification by local requirements and/or sponsor policy.

As for the narrative collection within the INSPECTIO study, patient narratives were collected at enrolment (V1) to gather information on patients’ perceptions of living with PAH from the onset of the condition onward, and at visit 12 months after V1 (V3) to assess any differences in daily life experience after 1 year of study participation. Caregiver narratives were also collected at V1 and V3. Specifically, patients were asked to consent to their involvement, and then caregivers were asked to agree to the narrative collection. The patient’s unwillingness to participate in the narrative collection did not prevent the caregiver from participating.

Study site staff attended a webinar conducted by NM researchers from Istituto Studi Direzionali (ISTUD) to understand the purpose of the narrative collection, its design and methodology for engaging enrolled patients and their caregivers to share their PAH experiences in writing.

Data collection

Clinical data were collected in accordance with clinical practice by designated study site staff and reported using a dedicated electronic case report form (e-CRF).

Illness plots25 26 were used to gather patient and caregiver narratives at V1 and V3 (online supplemental file 1); a quantitative questionnaire was added to collect sociodemographic data at V1. Three ISTUD researchers jointly created these illness plots to chronologically stimulate the narrative and identify patterns of change over time, using evocative, open narrative prompts to encourage individual expression.27 Furthermore, illness plots were revised by the study Sponsor team and reviewed by the centres’ Ethical Committees.

Study site staff provided patients and caregivers with paper narrative plots that could be completed on-site and returned immediately or taken home and returned at the next visit. Narratives were then collected and securely couriered to ISTUD researchers and faithfully transcribed in the e-CRF platform. It is a disease-specific, unidimensional measure of HRQoL for PH.

Alongside the illness plots, the HRQoL measure, PH-specific emPHasis-10 (e10) questionnaire28 29 was administered to patients at study timepoints to assess HRQoL. The e10 consists of 10 items on a 6-point semantic scale (0–5), yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 50, with higher scores indicating poorer HRQoL. The e10 score has also been shown to be an independent predictor of outcome in PH.28

Patient and public involvement

Researchers did not engage patients and caregivers in (a) developing the research design and tools, (b) interpreting and discussing the results and (c) contributing to the writing or editing of this document.

Ethical considerations

The narrative collection was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Before participating, patients and caregivers were asked to provide written informed consent for narrative collection, in addition to patients’ informed consent to participate in the INSPECTIO study, after being informed by study site staff on the purpose of narrative collection and confidential data handling procedures, according to local30 and European Union31 laws. Ethical Committees for each study site approved the narrative collection within the study framework (online supplemental file 2). The patient’s unwillingness to participate in the narrative collection did not prevent enrolment in INSPECTIO, nor did it stop the caregiver’s participation.

Narrative and data analysis

Quantitative responses related to sociodemographic data were analysed using descriptive statistics in Excel.

Narratives were first deidentified and transcribed verbatim. Subsequently, using a qualitative methodology, three researchers with different academic backgrounds and experience in NM collectively coded a narrative sample of 20 V1 patient narratives and 10 V1 caregiver narratives to familiarise themselves with potential coding bias and positionality, thereby ensuring consistency and a shared understanding. Afterwards, two researchers independently applied open, inductive coding using MAXQDA software32 to identify thematic clusters in narratives33; they convened for monthly peer debriefing sessions to discuss code definitions, thematic boundaries and their own interpretive perspectives to minimise potential bias.34 A third researcher intervened to resolve any disagreement. V3 narratives were analysed according to the identified thematic clusters; specifically, any clusters that emerged at V1 and were repeated at V3 were considered to assess the difference between the two points in time. Trustworthiness was ensured through investigator triangulation, iterative coding across V1 and V3 datasets and adherence to the Standard for Reporting Qualitative Research guidelines.

Based on clusters, narrative data were transformed into categories for statistical analysis.33 35 Then, recurrent clusters were correlated to quantitative data, that is, e10 score, therapy, prevalence and non-invasive risk assessment; specifically, correlations between narrative clusters and e10 scores were explored descriptively. Distribution and statistical significance of narrative clusters between V1 and V3 were assessed by performing the χ2 analysis36 or the McNemar-Bowker test.37

Patients who participated in the narrative collection had an average age of 60 years; at baseline, 73/96 (76%) were female, and only 20/96 (21%) were working. Sixty-nine out of 96 (72%) patients were prevalent, and 43/96 (45%) were on double therapy. In V1 patient narratives, limited information was provided about their lives before the onset of PAH. Twenty-six out of 96 (27%) patients reported being hospitalised or undergoing medical examination at the onset of first symptoms of PAH, while 16/96 (17%) experienced them outside of a hospital setting. Forty-four out of 96 (46%) patients attributed their first symptoms to other conditions or causes, such as stress, anxiety or cardiovascular disease, with hypertension rarely mentioned. At the time of diagnosis, fear or discouragement was the predominant emotion, reported by 49/96 (51%) patients; 11/96 (12%) expressed confusion. In contrast, 13/96 (14%) felt relief on receiving a diagnosis, and 12/96 (13%) reported feeling reassured by their treating clinicians.

Out of the 54 caregivers initially included, 29/54 (54%) completed the narrative at V1, and 16/54 (30%) completed it at V3.

At V1, mean e10 scores were higher among patients who reported feelings of discomfort or isolation as well as those who experienced limitations in their daily activities or domestic life. At V3, mean e10 scores remained higher for patients who did not mention their domestic life in narratives, experienced feelings of shame or isolation in their interactions with others, faced limitations or breathlessness, failed to address their daily activities, expressed hope regarding therapies, reported ongoing treatment-related issues or did not discuss PAH treatments.

In contrast, at both V1 and V3, mean e10 scores were lower for patients who reported that living with PAH was manageable. Those who felt optimistic about the future, hoped their situation would remain stable, and reported being able to manage their time and work activities despite having PAH also had lower scores.

At V3, the proportion of patients reporting stable or improving relationships rose to 35/58 (60%), while those feeling ashamed or isolated due to PAH decreased to 5/58 (9%). However, 12/58 (21%) patients still experienced difficulties or discomfort in their relationships. Eight out of 58 (14%) patients reported feeling distressed or isolated due to PAH. In contrast, 32/58 (55%) reported feeling well or improving, and the proportion of patients experiencing limitations or breathlessness decreased to 13 (22%). At V3, no significant differences were observed between prevalent and incident patients, or between low-risk and intermediate-risk patients, compared with V1 (p<0.05).

At V3, the proportion of patients who described living with PAH as manageable increased to 36/58 (62%), while 17/58 (29%) still found it challenging. Significant differences were observed between low-risk and intermediate-risk patients at V1 and V3 (p<0.05). Among intermediate-risk patients at V3, 27/58 (47%) felt that it was possible to live with PAH, compared with 80% of low-risk patients. Additionally, at V3, the proportion of patients expressing fear about the future decreased to 2/58 (3%), while those who viewed the future optimistically increased to 36/58 (62%). Regarding future expectations, 20/58 (34%) patients expressed a desire to get better or heal, 16/58 (28%) wished for everyday life or a sense of serenity, and 14/58 (24%) hoped for a stable condition.

Thirty-six out of 58 (62%) patients considered their narrative experience positive, while 7/58 (12%) found sharing their experiences through writing challenging.

The caregiving experience in PAH

At V1, 10/29 caregivers (34%) reported that PAH onset was characterised by breathlessness or fatigue in the patient; 6/29 (21%) described this period as highly anxiety-provoking or as a time of radical change in family life.

At V3, 4/16 (25%) caregivers reported that patients either returned to their pre-PAH symptom status or experienced improved autonomy, while 7/16 (44%) reported that patient activities remained limited, and 8/16 (50%) mentioned that PAH continued to impact the patient’s social life (online supplemental file 6).

At V3, the proportion of caregivers who felt helpless dropped to zero from 9/29 (31%) at V1. Seven out of 16 (44%) caregivers felt calm about the patient’s situation, the same proportion expressed hope for therapeutic options and 3/16 (19%) reported being able to provide closeness to the patient. Nonetheless, 2/16 (13%) requested time off work to assist in managing PAH, and another 2/16 (13%) felt they could not give as much support as they would have liked. Regarding spare time, 6/16 (38%) caregivers spent it with the patient, and the proportion of caregivers reporting a lack of free time dropped to zero from 3/29 (10%). Despite this, 9/16 (56%) caregivers still regarded living with PAH as strenuous or problematic, while only 5/16 (31%) found it manageable.

PAH therapies were described as excellent or effective by 7/16 (44%) caregivers, and 2/16 (13%) considered them hopeful. Conversely, 2/16 (13%) still viewed the therapies as complex or problematic. Regarding relationships with healthcare providers, 6/16 (38%) caregivers rated their experiences as excellent, and 7/16 (44%) considered them good or satisfactory. However, 2/16 (13%) described the relationship as discontinuous.

Looking ahead, 6/16 (38%) caregivers hoped for improvement or even recovery for the patient, 3/16 (19%) wished for the situation to remain stable, and 5/16 (31%) desired greater autonomy for the patient. At V3, 6/16 (38%) rated their overall narrative experience positively, while 3/16 (19%) found it challenging.

Discussion

INSPECTIO is the first clinical study to integrate narratives, PROs and clinical data in PAH research. Comparing narratives collected at V1 and V3 with mean e10 scores provided valuable insights into the burden of PAH and its evolution over the first 12 months of the study.

Understanding the patient’s perspective is crucial in chronic, life-threatening diseases such as PAH, particularly regarding its impact on HRQoL.4 11 12 This encompasses the physical, mental and social dimensions for patients and their caregivers. Previous studies have identified the emotional burden of PAH on patients, which often includes symptoms such as depression, anxiety, stress, feelings of isolation, memory loss and a lack of understanding about the disease.1 15 Patients with PAH often experience social isolation, which they attribute to the invisible nature of their condition and the limited awareness or understanding of the disease within their communities, families and social networks.1 15

Patient narratives revealed a wide range of emotions and reactions experienced throughout the care pathway, especially at the time of diagnosis. Many patients reported feelings of fear and despair, likely stemming from a limited knowledge of PAH; nonetheless, some patients felt relief when receiving the diagnosis since it clarified the nature of their condition.

Patient narratives highlighted the crucial role of supportive relationships at home in managing PAH. Difficulties in the domestic context and feelings of shame or isolation were associated with higher e10 scores, suggesting a substantial emotional burden despite some improvement in reported relationships at V3. At the same time, many patients described strengthened or more stable social relationships, indicating that social support can partially mitigate the impact of the condition.

Limitations in daily activities became less prominent in patient narratives over time, and more patients described living with PAH as manageable; however, significant differences remained between intermediate-risk and low-risk patients. Patients who continued to report activity limitations or treatment-related challenges, or who did not address their daily life in their narratives, showed higher e10 scores, highlighting the link between functional restrictions, treatment burden and poorer HRQoL. Relationships with treating clinicians were generally assessed positively; however, reports of interruptions in care—echoed by caregivers—may reflect the organisational impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. The analysis of future expectations revealed a shift from fear to greater optimism and hope for stability or improvement, and these more positive expectations were aligned with lower e10 scores, whereas persisting fear was associated with higher scores.

Overall, the convergence between patient narratives and e10 scores supports the use of narratives to capture the evolution of illness experience and emotional burden over follow-up.

Caregiver narratives confirmed that PAH has a critical impact on patients’ social lives and on caregivers’ own emotional and practical load, in line with previous studies.16 37 38 Over time, caregivers described a better perception of PAH therapies and of the relationship with clinicians, yet many continued to experience living with PAH as challenging, with repercussions on social, leisure and professional life. These findings underscore the need for structured support for caregivers and for systematically recognising their role within the PAH care pathway.

As widely acknowledged in the literature,53841 PAH impacts several aspects of patients’ and caregivers’ daily lives. The narrative collection within the INSPECTIO study showed that, despite the complexity of PAH, appropriate support and therapeutic management can help patients cope with the disease and lead relatively stable lives. Narratives are proving to be a valuable tool not only for understanding the impact of PAH but also for improving its management by focusing on patients’ subjective experiences and involving caregivers in the care pathway.

Integrating clinical data with narratives may support a multidisciplinary approach to managing PAH that addresses both medical and emotional needs, in line with ESC/ERS recommendations.1 39 Continuity of care, a supportive environment and multidisciplinary teams are essential for effective management, particularly for evaluating HRQoL and incorporating these insights into clinical practice. Understanding the patient’s perspective is critical for improving care delivery and outcomes and may also guide the development of PRO assessments in clinical trials to better inform decision-making.42 Additionally, patient-related and care team-related factors influence therapeutic adherence, a key determinant of improved outcomes and reduced hospitalisation costs. 43 As a holistic, multidisciplinary approach is essential for addressing these challenges,42 integrating narratives with PROs may provide critical elements to improve the PAH care pathway.

The narrative collection in the INSPECTIO study has several limitations. First, there was a selection bias, as only a subset of eligible patients and caregivers chose to write narratives at V1 and V3. Second, despite clarifications that all narrative expressions would be valued, some participants might have felt intimidated by the task or misunderstood its purpose. Third, the study did not explore differences in narratives and e10 scores by sex, age or clinical variables, nor did it systematically compare sociodemographic and clinical characteristics between participants and non-participants, or between those who completed both V1 and V3 narratives and those who contributed only at V1.

Conclusion

The INSPECTIO study is the first clinical investigation to integrate narratives with PROs and clinical data in PAH research. The analysis revealed an alignment between patient narratives and e10 scores, emphasising the impact of PAH on patients and the evolution of the illness experience over the first 12 months of the study. Findings suggest that appropriate support and therapeutic management can help patients cope with PAH and maintain relatively stable lives despite its complexities. Narratives have proven to be a valuable tool to understand the impact of PAH and improve its management. By focusing on patients’ subjective experiences and involving caregivers’ viewpoint, narratives provide insights that can inform a more patient-centred approach to PAH.

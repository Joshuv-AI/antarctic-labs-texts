Visual design and digital transformation of imaging under artificial intelligence technology
Authors: Kai Zhang, Mengying Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study proposes a conditional hierarchical generative adversarial network (GAN) model for intelligent visual design, aiming to explore new modes of human–machine collaboration and promote digital transformation in the design field. The proposed model consists of three core modules: a multimodal conditional fusion encoder, a hierarchical generator, and a collaborative multi-discriminator architecture. It innovatively integrates multiple sources of design intent—including textual descriptions, hand-drawn sketches, spatial layouts, and brand guidelines—through a cross-modal attention mechanism. The model adopts a two-stage generation strategy of "structure first, details later" and introduces multi-objective adversarial training to simultaneously optimize generation quality, layout accuracy, and semantic consistency. Experiments on the Common Objects in Context with Stuff Annotations (COCO-Stuff) 164 K dataset show that the model achieves a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score of 18.37 and 92.8% layout control accuracy on the test set. These results demonstrate that the proposed model significantly outperforms mainstream baseline models in both generation quality and controllability. This study provides an innovative technical solution for professional visual design and offers theoretical and practical insights into the evolution of creative agency, human–machine collaboration mechanisms, and the pathways of digital transformation in the Artificial Intelligence era.

Introduction

With the proliferation of digital media platforms and the upgrading of content consumption, visual design in imaging has shifted from a professional domain to a more accessible and everyday practice. It has become a core medium for information dissemination, brand building, and user experience1,2. Meanwhile, artificial intelligence (AI), particularly generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) represented by Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) and diffusion models, has made revolutionary progress, demonstrating unprecedented content creation potential3,4. This convergence of technological advances and industry demand is driving a profound paradigm shift in visual design: design is no longer exclusively the domain of human designers but is evolving into a collaborative ecosystem of human and machine intelligence5,6.

However, current applications of AI in visual design face significant challenges7. Mainstream generative models excel in image quality and creative diversity but fall short in meeting professional design requirements, such as precise control, brand consistency, and rigorous composition8. Designers often need to engage in complex prompt engineering and multiple iterative processes to achieve acceptable results. This approach conflicts with the demands of high-efficiency and high-consistency commercial production9. A more fundamental issue is that this reveals a core tension between current generative AI paradigms and the inherent requirements of design tasks. The openness and creativity of generative models have not yet been effectively reconciled with the strong constraints embedded in design practice, such as brand guidelines, interface specifications, and the hierarchy of marketing messages.

Therefore, it is essential to explore a new technical framework that can deeply integrate multimodal design intent, support fine-grained controllability, and seamlessly fit into digital workflows. This need motivates the present study, which proposes an innovative Conditional Hierarchical GAN (CH-GAN) model to address existing technological limitations. The model aims to create an intelligent system that can interpret and integrate multiple inputs—such as textual descriptions, hand-drawn sketches, spatial layouts, and brand specifications. It then generates high-quality images that are both visually appealing and strictly aligned with design constraints. Beyond offering a new technical solution for professional visual design, this study also examines how creative agency is changing in the era of AI. It further explores how human–AI collaboration can be structured and how digital transformation may reshape the design industry. This study aspires to offer insights for the development of future intelligent design ecosystems10. Literature review

The rapid development of AI, particularly breakthroughs in deep learning and generative AI, is profoundly reshaping visual design in imaging and its industrial ecosystem. Existing research has explored multiple dimensions, focusing mainly on quantitative evaluation of visual perception, intelligent artistic style transfer, human–machine collaborative creative generation, and multi-dimensional decision-making for image content. At the macro and urban scale, AI provides novel tools for large-scale, automated evaluation of visual quality and human perception of images. Recently, Cheng et al. (2023) conducted a comprehensive survey on layout-guided image generation. They pointed out that although diffusion models had made significant progress in layout controllability, their multimodal condition fusion mechanisms remained insufficiently developed. This observation provides important guidance for the design of the multimodal conditional encoder proposed in this study11. Zhao et al. (2024) offered a representative example. They proposed an integrated deep learning approach (SegFormer-B5 + ConvNeXt-B + Random Forest) for street view images. This method successfully predicted six subjective perceptions of the built environment—such as beauty, vitality, and safety—with an average accuracy of 78.47%. They not only developed an efficient perception prediction tool but also used interpretability techniques, including SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Class Activation Mapping (CAM). These methods revealed the relationships between objective visual elements, such as man-made objects and greenery, and subjective perceptions. The resulting “perception maps” can directly inform urban planning decisions. At the micro level of artistic creation and visual expression, style transfer remains one of the core AI-powered techniques in visual design12. Yang and Hanif (2024) focused on art education scenarios and improved the Cycle-Consistent Generative Adversarial Networks (CycleGAN) algorithm by introducing an attention mechanism. Their experiments demonstrated that the enhanced model achieved superior style transfer quality and accuracy, outperforming traditional methods on both Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) metrics. The essence of visual design lies in the generation of creative concepts13. From a human–computer interaction perspective, Hughes et al. (2021) provided empirical evidence that the effectiveness of collaboration between designers and generative AI tools strongly depended on the model’s ability to support iterative feedback. This finding further supported the necessity of the closed-loop iterative optimization mechanism proposed in this study14. Alcaide-Marzal and Diego-Mas (2025) examined, from a design methodology perspective, the role of text-to-image AI models compared to traditional shape grammar during the concept design stage. They found that AI models provided significant advantages in capturing design intent, exploration breadth, and immediacy, whereas traditional methods remained stronger in process systematization and cognitive stimulation. The value of imagery extended beyond its visual form to the information it conveyed and the decisions it influenced15. Hua (2025) proposed an innovative framework that combined deep learning (BERT-CNN) with multi-attribute decision-making to construct tourist destination images. This model performed deep semantic analysis of unstructured tourist reviews and integrated multi-dimensional decision criteria to generate a comprehensive evaluation of destination imagery16.

In summary, existing research has achieved substantial progress in AI-enabled image analysis, style transfer, generation, and decision-making, providing a solid foundation for this study. However, gaps remain. Current studies tend to focus on individual aspects, such as “analysis” or “generation,” and lack an integrated model that connects creative generation, effect evaluation, and decision optimization. Most studies analyze or stylize static images and fail to form a dynamic, AI-driven, iterative visual design loop.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

To comprehensively evaluate the generation and controllability of the CH-GAN model in complex visual design tasks, this study selected the Common Objects in Context with Stuff Annotations (COCO-Stuff) 164 K dataset as the core training and evaluation benchmark. COCO-Stuff, an extension of the classic COCO 2017 dataset, offers significant advantages. It not only provides a large number of natural scene images but also includes pixel-level semantic segmentation annotations, perfectly aligning with the strong requirement for structured layout information in the multimodal conditional generation framework. The COCO-Stuff dataset contains 164,000 images covering 172 semantic categories, including 91 object classes and 81 material or background classes. This ensures both scene complexity and content diversity, enabling effective evaluation of the model’s generation potential, from everyday objects to complex environmental structures.

From a data composition perspective, each image in COCO-Stuff is accompanied by high-quality instance and semantic segmentation masks. These masks serve directly as the “spatial layout map” conditional input for the generator, providing explicit constraints on object shapes, positions, and scene composition. In addition, the original annotations include brief descriptions of prominent objects in each image. In this study, modern image captioning models such as BLIP were applied to generate richer and more fluent natural language descriptions, forming high-quality “text description” conditions. By mapping the segmentation mask category IDs to color-coded layout maps and standardizing the text descriptions, COCO-Stuff was successfully adapted into a standardized multimodal dataset containing image-text-layout triples.

To ensure rigorous evaluation and practical relevance for design applications, the original dataset was subjected to targeted filtering and preprocessing. Images with overly simple content or low annotation quality were removed. All images and corresponding layout maps were resized to 512 × 512 pixels to fit the model input. For dataset splitting, standard machine learning practice was followed, randomly partitioning the data into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio. This resulted in approximately 131,200 training samples, 16,400 validation samples, and 16,400 test samples, ensuring effective model training and unbiased evaluation. Ultimately, the constructed data pipeline provides the CH-GAN model with a stable, high-quality multimodal condition input stream, establishing a solid data foundation for subsequent model training and performance analysis.

The operating system was Ubuntu 22.04, and the deep learning framework was PyTorch 2.1.0 with CUDA 12.1 acceleration enabled. To ensure reproducibility, all random seeds were fixed.

Performance evaluation

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the CH-GAN model, a systematic experimental protocol was designed. The model was tested on the training, validation, and test sets of the COCO-Stuff 164 K dataset. Evaluation focused on three dimensions: generation quality, layout control accuracy, and semantic consistency. Quantitative comparisons were made with state-of-the-art generative models. Additionally, a simulated “intelligent advertising design” case study was conducted to qualitatively analyze the model’s potential in practical digital transformation scenarios.

Image generation quality

Image generation quality was evaluated using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID) as objective metrics. The following baseline models were selected for comparison: (1) pix2pixHD, a classical conditional GAN-based layout-guided image generation model; (2) Stable Diffusion + ControlNet, a state-of-the-art multimodal conditional diffusion framework that integrates large-scale pretrained diffusion models with plug-and-play control modules and supports spatial conditions such as edges and depth maps; and (3) DALL·E 3, a leading closed-source text-to-image model developed by OpenAI, based on a diffusion architecture trained on large-scale image–text pairs, and known for its strong prompt adherence and creative generation capability.

In addition, StyleGAN2-ADA was included as an extra baseline for comparison. This model, proposed by NVIDIA, introduces a style modulation architecture and an Adaptive Discriminator Augmentation (ADA) mechanism, enabling hierarchical control over coarse-, mid-, and fine-grained semantic attributes in generated images.

To ensure a fair comparison, both pix2pixHD and Stable Diffusion + ControlNet were trained from scratch on the same COCO-Stuff dataset using identical computational resources (4 × NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs) for 300 epochs. For DALL·E 3, since the model weights are not publicly available, zero-shot inference was performed on the test set via the official API. It should be noted that this evaluation setting may confer an advantage to DALL·E 3, as its training data coverage is significantly broader than COCO-Stuff and not publicly disclosed.

The image generation quality comparison for different models is shown in Fig. 3.

As shown in Fig. 3, after three independent repeated experiments, CH-GAN achieved the best performance on the training set, with a mean FID of 16.52 (± 0.19) and a mean KID of 2.31 × 10−3 (± 0.08 × 10−3). This advantage is consistently maintained on the validation set (FID: 17.89 ± 0.22, KID: 2.58 ± 0.10 × 10−3) and the test set (FID: 18.37 ± 0.21, KID: 2.71 × 10−3 ± 0.11 × 10−3), demonstrating strong generalization capability without significant signs of overfitting. Compared to baseline models, CH-GAN’s FID on the test set was 21.5% lower than Stable Diffusion with ControlNet and 29.5% lower than DALL-E 3. The KID comparison followed a similar trend, further confirming that CH-GAN generates images whose distribution closely matches real data. This superior performance is primarily attributed to the model’s hierarchical adversarial training architecture. Gcoarse first generates a stable structural foundation, while Gfine refines the details on top of it. This design avoids structural distortions and blurring that commonly occur in single-stage generative models when handling complex scenes. In addition, discriminators specifically targeting layout and style (Dlayout and Dstyle) provide fine-grained gradient feedback, guiding the generator to produce images that are not only realistic but also satisfy specific constraints.

Layout control and semantic consistency analysis

Layout control accuracy was measured using Intersection over Union (IoU) between the generated images and input layout maps. Semantic consistency was evaluated with the Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) Score, which measures alignment between images and text descriptions. The evaluation results are presented in Fig. 4.

Layout control and semantic consistency evaluation across different models on multiple datasets (three bar charts on the right indicate standard deviation over three independent runs).

For layout control, CH-GAN demonstrates a clear advantage. On the test set, it achieves a mean Layout IoU of 92.8% (± 0.3%), outperforming SD + ControlNet (85.7% ± 0.5%) and DALL·E 3 (82.3% ± 0.6%) by 7.1 and 10.5 percentage points, respectively. This result validates the effectiveness of the proposed multimodal conditional fusion encoder and hierarchical generation strategy. The conditional encoder converts sketches or layout maps into dense spatial latent representations. The coarse generator stage Gcoarse then directly responds to these representations, establishing object positions and global composition at an early stage, which ensures accurate structural formation in the final output.

Regarding semantic consistency, it is important to note that the slight advantage of DALL·E 3 in CLIP Score (test set: 0.819 vs. 0.801) was obtained under a zero-shot evaluation setting. In addition, its training data includes a substantially larger-scale image–text corpus compared with COCO-Stuff, whereas CH-GAN was trained exclusively on the COCO-Stuff 164 K dataset. Under controlled training conditions, CH-GAN significantly outperforms retrainable baseline models such as pix2pixHD (CLIP Score: 0.745, Layout IoU: 76.2%), which provides stronger evidence of the effectiveness of the proposed architecture itself.

To further evaluate practical utility, a simulated “intelligent e-commerce advertisement generation” scenario was conducted. In this setup, CH-GAN was deployed as a cloud-based service connected to a simulated product management system. Inputs included product images with plain backgrounds, marketing copy, brand color specifications, and simple layout constraints. The system generated three initial advertisement drafts within an average of 60 s. The simulation results indicate that this workflow reduces the time required for initial advertisement draft creation from several hours to only a few minutes. Based on the simulation environment, this corresponds to an estimated 80–90% reduction in manual workload for early-stage design. However, this improvement requires further validation in real-world production settings. Across 1000 simulated requests, approximately 95.2% of generated images satisfied a predefined set of strict quality criteria after one iteration. These criteria included layout correctness, brand element fidelity, and text clarity, which collectively approximate the baseline requirements for commercial deployment.

Discussion

The proposed CH-GAN model demonstrated strong performance across multiple evaluations. Its hierarchical generation strategy mimics the human cognitive process of “structure first, details later.” The outstanding layout control accuracy (IoU 92.8%) validates the effectiveness of treating layout maps as an independent modality and reinforcing spatial constraints through dedicated discriminators. Compared with existing models, CH-GAN reflects a distinct design philosophy. Unlike Stable Diffusion + ControlNet, which follows a “generate then refine” approach, CH-GAN adopts a “control-first” native design, treating multimodal conditions as the core driver of generation. This makes it better suited for professional scenarios with strict precision requirements. In contrast to DALL-E 3, which prioritizes maximal creative divergence, CH-GAN emphasizes controllability and conformity, illustrating a trend toward the professionalization and vertical specialization of AI generative models. Simulation experiments also revealed the industrial significance of this technology. By automating repetitive execution tasks, CH-GAN frees designers to focus on higher-level creative work, effectively redefining the human-AI division of labor.

It is worth noting that the success of CH-GAN reflects a deeper convergence between design thinking and computational logic. The model translates abstract brand guidelines and ambiguous sketch intentions into computable and optimizable loss functions, marking a process in which design knowledge becomes formalized and digitized. However, the current framework still lacks a robust way to quantify highly subjective dimensions such as style and aesthetic quality, which remains a central challenge for future research. In addition, the model shows performance degradation when dealing with extreme stylistic variations or highly unconventional compositions, indicating that its creative capacity is still constrained by the distribution of the training data. Moving forward, a key question is how to introduce more flexible creativity-inducing mechanisms without compromising controllability, so as to strike a balance between normativity and innovation.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study systematically explored paradigm innovation and digital transformation in visual design in the AI era, achieving the following key contributions:

Theoretical contributions: This study proposed and validated the CH-GAN framework, establishing a theoretical basis for AI as a “design collaborator.” By combining multimodal conditional fusion with hierarchical generation, it effectively bridges the semantic gap between human abstract design intent and machine concrete outputs, providing empirical support for human-AI co-creation theory.

Technical contributions: Three key innovations were achieved: (1) A cross-modal attention-based conditional fusion encoder that dynamically integrates heterogeneous design intents from text, sketches, layouts, and brand specifications. (2) A two-stage generator with coarse layout generation and fine-grained style refinement, achieving 92.8% layout control accuracy on the test set. (3) A multi-discriminator collaborative training mechanism ensuring optimal balance among realism, layout accuracy, and semantic consistency, with an FID score of 18.37, surpassing baseline models by over 20%.

Practical contribution: Through a simulated “intelligent advertisement design” scenario, CH-GAN demonstrates its potential to facilitate the digital transformation of design workflows. Experimental results indicate that the model can reduce the time required for generating initial design drafts from several hours to only a few minutes. In addition, approximately 95% of the generated images satisfy predefined stringent quality criteria. These findings suggest a promising technical pathway toward scalable, personalized, and agile production in the design industry, which warrants further validation in industrial pilot studies.

Future works and research limitations

This study has several limitations. First, CH-GAN requires substantial computational resources, limiting broad deployment. Second, performance declines when handling extreme styles outside the training distribution. Third, current evaluation metrics cannot fully capture subjective dimensions such as “creativity.”

Future work may explore: (1) Technical optimization: Investigate model lightweighting and domain-adaptive techniques to enhance usability and robustness. (2) Application extension: Expand the framework to video generation, 3D asset creation, and interactive design collaboration. (3) Evaluation systems: Develop multidimensional creativity assessment frameworks combining physiological measurements and large language models. (4) Ethical and social considerations: Address issues of copyright, content authenticity, and human-AI responsibility allocation.

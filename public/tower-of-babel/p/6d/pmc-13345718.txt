Assessment of Recurrence Patterns and Treatment Strategies for Reintervention after Paclitaxel-Coated Balloon Angioplasty in Femoropopliteal Artery Diseases: Results of the ASTRA Study
Authors: Naoki Yoshioka, Takahiro Tokuda, Akiko Tanaka, Shunsuke Kojima, Kohei Yamaguchi, Takashi Yanagiuchi, Kenji Ogata, Tatsuro Takei, Yasuhiro Morita, Tatsuya Nakama, Itsuro Morishima, on behalf of the LEADers PAD investigators
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aims: Data on recurrence and its patterns after paclitaxel-coated balloon (PCB) angioplasty for femoropopliteal artery disease are limited. We evaluated the incidence and predictors of re-recurrence according to the retreatment strategy (PCB or scaffold) and recurrence pattern (restenosis or reocclusion) in patients with recurrence after primary PCB therapy. Methods: This multicenter retrospective study included 276 limbs of 246 patients who underwent repeat endovascular therapy (EVT) using either PCB (PCB group,n = 217) or a scaffold (scaffold group,n = 59) for primary PCB recurrence. The primary endpoint was 1-year freedom from re-recurrence, and secondary analyses identified the predictors of re-recurrence. Results: In the PCB group, 174 restenotic and 43 reoccluded lesions were treated with PCBs. In the scaffold group, 32 restenotic and 27 reoccluded lesions were treated with scaffolds. In both groups, reoccluded lesions had significantly lower freedom from re-recurrence rates than restenotic lesions (PCB: 43.0% vs. 68.6%,p＜0.001; scaffold: 52.6% vs. 81.5%,p = 0.033). Freedom from re-reocclusion was also significantly lower in reoccluded lesions than in restenotic lesions (PCB: 48.5% vs. 88.6%,p＜0.001; scaffold: 65.8% vs. 93.2%,p = 0.033). The independent predictors of re-recurrence after PCB treatment were male sex (hazard ratio [HR] 2.01,p = 0.010), reoccluded lesions (HR 2.71,p = 0.001), poor BK run-off (HR 1.97,p = 0.020), use of first-generation low-dose PCB (HR 2.32,p = 0.017), and residual stenosis ＞30% (HR 2.60,p = 0.009). After scaffold treatment, reoccluded lesions were also identified as a significant predictor of re-recurrence (HR 2.99,p = 0.043). Conclusion: Reocclusion after PCB therapy was strongly associated with subsequent re-recurrence, including re-reocclusion, regardless of the retreatment strategy.

Introduction

The efficacy of drug-eluting technology using endovascular therapy (EVT) devices for femoropopliteal artery (FPA) disease has been well described 1 - 4) . Notably, paclitaxel‐coated balloons (PCBs) are clinically effective in real‐world settings 5 , 6) . Although several concerns regarding the use of paclitaxel devices for peripheral artery disease (PAD) have reemerged recently 7 , 8) , PCBs are still widely used as the first-line treatment devices for FPA lesions in clinical practice 9) .

However, despite the widespread use of PCBs, lesion recurrence and the need for reintervention remain important clinical issues. Recurrence morphology and retreatment strategies are clinically important when reinterventions are performed. Previously, we reported that reocclusion after PCB angioplasty was associated with poorer clinical outcomes, including symptomatic recurrence and the requirement for reintervention, than restenosis, focusing primarily on the prognostic impact of recurrence morphology itself 10) . In contrast, the optimal retreatment strategy for different recurrence morphologies remains unclear. Although the use of PCBs or scaffolds for PCB recurrence has been reported to reduce re-recurrence compared with conventional balloon angioplasty 11) , it remains unknown whether the choice of retreatment device is associated with re-recurrence according to recurrence morphology. In real clinical settings, most cases of PCB recurrence are treated using PCBs and/or scaffolds. However, no previous study has simultaneously evaluated the interaction between recurrence morphology and retreatment device selection following recurrence after PCB angioplasty.

Aim

In the present study, we aimed to assess re-recurrence after repeat intervention according to recurrence morphology within each retreatment approach (PCB or scaffold). We also sought to identify factors associated with re-recurrence in each retreatment strategy.

Materials & Methods

Study Design and Population

Data from patients with PAD were obtained from the LEADers PAD registry, a retrospective multicenter database of lower-extremity PAD that includes data from eight Japanese institutions.

The present study represents a registry-based analysis (the ASTRA study: Assessment of recurrence patternS and reTreatment strategies afteR paclitAxel-coated balloon in femoropopliteal artery lesions) conducted using data from patients who underwent EVT between January 2018 and December 2021. The inclusion criteria were patient age ≥ 20 years, symptomatic lower limb ischemia defined as Rutherford category 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6, and stenotic or occlusive de novo lesions in FPAs treated using PCBs.

Overall, 1159 limbs were identified. After the initial PCB treatment, recurrence within 3 years was observed in 414 limbs, and subsequent reintervention was performed in 298 limbs. After excluding limbs treated with bypass surgery, balloon angioplasty only, and combination therapy of PCB and scaffold and cases with a lack of reintervention information, 276 limbs from 246 patients who underwent repeat EVT for PCB restenosis using PCB or scaffold were ultimately included in the final analysis and divided into two groups: those undergoing repeat EVT using PCB (PCB group) and those using scaffolds (scaffold group) ( Fig.1 ) .

Ethics Statements

This study was approved by the medical ethics committee of each hospital and was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective observational nature of the study; however, patients were able to opt out of the study. Relevant information regarding the study is available to the public in accordance with the Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Health Research Involving Human Subjects.

Study Outcomes

The primary outcomes were freedom from re-recurrence and re-reocclusion within 1 year after repeat EVT. Secondary analyses were performed to identify factors associated with re-recurrence in each group.

Definitions and Data Collection

Angiographic data were analyzed at each institution by a consensus between two experienced vascular interventionists. As previously described, the vessel dissection pattern was evaluated using seven grades (none to F) 12) . Furthermore, the severity of lesion calcification was assessed using a peripheral artery calcification scoring system with five grades (0–4) 13) . Successful vessel preparation was defined as ＜50% residual stenosis and dissection grade of ＜D before PCB or scaffold implantation 14) . Recurrence was defined as a composite of restenosis (indicated by a peak systolic velocity ratio ＞2.4 on duplex ultrasound or ＞50% stenosis on angiography or computed tomography [CT]) 15) and reocclusion after initial PCB treatment. Following reintervention, re-recurrence was defined as a composite of re-restenosis (indicated by a peak systolic velocity ratio ＞2.4 on duplex ultrasound or ＞50% stenosis on angiography or CT) and re-reocclusion. Re-reocclusion was defined as reocclusion after reintervention.

Procedure Protocol

During the EVT procedure, the access site and device selection were determined at the discretion of the operator. After sheath placement, intraarterial or intravenous heparin was administered for anticoagulation treatment during EVT in all cases. The heparin dose was adjusted based on each hospital’s policy and the operator’s discretion to achieve an activated clotting time of 200–300 s. The choice of the EVT device was made at the discretion of the operator, considering the patient’s background, lesion characteristics, and procedural results.

Follow‑up Protocol

Clinical evaluations were performed at baseline and 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after reintervention. The patency of the target lesion was assessed by measuring the ankle–brachial index and duplex ultrasonography, and imaging with CT or angiography was performed when clinically indicated.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are presented as the mean±standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages. Clinical, lesion-related, and procedure-related characteristics were compared using the chi-square test for categorical variables. Student’s t-test was performed to compare continuous variables (expressed as mean±SD) according to the recurrence morphology. Event-free survival (Kaplan–Meier curves) was compared using the log-rank test. Cox regression analysis using backward stepwise selection was performed to identify the factors independently associated with re-recurrence after reintervention. Variables significantly associated with re-recurrence (p＜0.05) in the univariate analyses, plus age and sex, were introduced into the multivariable Cox model. The results are reported as the adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) with associated 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

All statistical analyses were conducted using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 27 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), and statistical significance was set at a two-tailed p＜0.05.

Results

Case Distribution and Baseline Patient, Lesion, and Procedure Characteristics

In this study, 192 individuals with 217 lesions were classified into the PCB group, and 54 individuals with 59 lesions were classified into the scaffold group. In the PCB group, PCBs were used for 174 restenotic and 43 reoccluded lesions. In the scaffold group, scaffolds were used to treat 32 restenotic and 27 reoccluded lesions ( Fig.1 ) .

Table 1 presents the baseline individual characteristics during reintervention using a PCB or scaffold. In the PCB group, significant differences were observed in prevalence of diabetes mellitus, hemodialysis, and direct oral anticoagulant use between cases of restenosis and reocclusion. In contrast, except for hypertension, there were no significant differences between the recurrent morphologies in the scaffold group.

Clinical Outcomes

In the PCB group, patients treated for reocclusion had a significantly lower freedom from re-recurrence than those treated for restenosis (43.0% vs. 68.6%, log-rank p = 0.001) ( Fig.2A ) , and a similarly significant difference was observed for freedom from re-reocclusion (48.5% vs. 88.6%, log-rank p＜0.001) ( Fig.2B ) . In the scaffold group, patients treated for reocclusion also had a significantly lower freedom from re-recurrence than those treated for restenosis (52.6% vs. 81.5%, log-rank p = 0.033) ( Fig.3A ) , and a similarly significant difference was observed for freedom from re-reocclusion (65.8% vs. 93.2%, log-rank p = 0.033) ( Fig.3B ) .

Discussion

In this study, we assessed the postprocedural clinical outcomes according to recurrence patterns in patients who underwent reintervention for recurrent lesions after initial PCB therapy using either a PCB or scaffold. We found that reoccluded lesions were strongly associated with higher rates of subsequent re-recurrence and re-reocclusion regardless of the retreatment strategy. Reocclusion after PCB therapy emerged as an independent predictor of further re-recurrence, irrespective of whether reintervention was performed with a PCB or a scaffold.

Repeated EVT procedures have been shown to be associated with reduced PCB performance 5 , 16) . Furthermore, the outcomes of second or later PCB treatments have been shown to be inferior to those of initial PCB procedures 17) . However, evidence regarding optimal EVT strategies for recurrence after PCB therapy remains limited. Higashino et al. identified plain balloon angioplasty as an independent risk factor for recurrence after PCB restenosis and found no significant difference between reintervention with a PCB and that with scaffold 11) . Our previous findings also indicated that reocclusion after PCB therapy tends to result in more frequent symptomatic recurrence and re-reocclusion after repeat PCB angioplasty than restenosis 10) . These findings suggest that reocclusion after PCB therapy may substantially influence the outcomes of subsequent reinterventions. Therefore, the present study was designed to further investigate the clinical impact of recurrence morphology in both the PCB and scaffold reintervention groups.

Lesion occlusion has long been recognized as a strong predictor of recurrence after EVT following both PCB therapy 5) and stent implantation 18 , 19) . However, these data included mixed populations undergoing initial and repeated EVT procedures, and studies focusing exclusively on recurrent cases, such as the present study, are scarce. Similarly, a history of EVT has been identified as a risk factor for recurrence after both PCB 5) and stent treatment 18 , 19) . These findings collectively suggest that reocclusion after EVT represents a high-risk feature for subsequent recurrence, which is further supported by the current results.

Importantly, our findings do not imply that all recurrent lesions after PCB therapy should be retreated with PCB. Unsuccessful vessel preparation occurred more often in the scaffold group than in the PCB group. Vessel preparation failure has been shown to strongly affect treatment outcomes in PCB therapy 20) , and the use of scaffolds such as drug-eluting stents is recommended when adequate vessel preparation cannot be achieved 21) . Thus, there are clear clinical situations in which stent implantation is necessary to achieve optimal initial outcomes. Our data also reflect real-world decision-making trends in device selection by operators. Moreover, although not statistically significant, the rate of re-reocclusion was numerically lower in the scaffold group than in the PCB group in patients with reoccluded lesions. Therefore, in complex cases of reocclusion, scaffold implantation may be a reasonable strategy to prevent future re-reocclusion. Further studies with larger populations are required to confirm these findings.

The present results showed that reocclusion had a strong impact on outcomes, irrespective of the retreatment device used, suggesting that reocclusion itself may represent one of the fundamental limitations of EVT. Indeed, in both groups, reoccluded lesions were significantly longer than restenotic lesions, which likely contributed to their more complex morphologies.

Pharmacological interventions may play an important role as adjunctive strategies. Although our study did not demonstrate a clear benefit of medication use, previous research has shown favorable effects of cilostazol on long-term outcomes after PCB therapy 22) and statins on drug-eluting stents 23 , 24) . Therefore, future studies should explore the potential role of pharmacological interventions alongside EVT in the management of complex lesions.

Study Limitations

First, this study employed a nonrandomized, retrospective design. Second, the study population and number of events were relatively small, particularly in the scaffold group, which may have limited the statistical power to detect clinically meaningful differences. Third, the retreatment device was selected at the discretion of the operator, and we only included patients who underwent treatment with specific preselected devices. Therefore, there is a potential for selection bias. Fourth, our study lacked quantitative angiographic analysis or a core laboratory. Fifth, all patients included in the current study were Japanese; thus, caution should be exercised when applying these results to other countries. Finally, detailed IVUS information, including wire passage route, plaque morphology, vessel dissections, and calcification characteristics was not available, which may have influenced reintervention outcomes. Further studies are required to clarify the clinical outcomes of reintervention after PCB in greater detail.

Conclusion

Reocclusion after initial PCB therapy was strongly associated with subsequent re-recurrence, including re-reocclusion, regardless of the retreatment strategy. The use of a scaffold may be acceptable in cases of unsuccessful vessel preparation or reoccluded lesions to achieve better acute results and possibly reduce re-reocclusion.

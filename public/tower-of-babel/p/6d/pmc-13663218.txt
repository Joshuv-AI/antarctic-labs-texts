An integrative multi-omics landscape of multi-dimensional cellular stress and metabolic reprogramming in melanoma treated with targeted alpha therapy
Authors: Jiajia Zhang, Zhiling Song, Zhengguo Chen, Xiaohui Luan, Yuying Yu, Xuhao Huang, Jie Lye, Jianguo Li, Jie Gao, Yuanyou Yang, Kazuya Kabayama, Koichi Fukase, Ning Liu, Feize Li, Fei Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of targeted alpha therapy (TAT) using a novel 211At-labeled single-domain antibody drug ([211At]At-AuNP-sdAb). By integrating transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses, we sought to characterize the coordinated gene–metabolite responses induced by α-particle irradiation in melanoma models.MethodsB16F10 melanoma cells and tumor-bearing mice were treated with [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb. RNA sequencing and untargeted LC–MS metabolomics were performed to identify differentially expressed genes and metabolites. Functional enrichment, OPLS-DA modeling, ROC curve analysis, and O2PLS-based multi-omics integration were used to explore the molecular networks and candidate indicators.Results[211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment significantly altered the transcription of 777 genes, revealing multi-dimensional cellular stress responses encompassing coordinated nuclear-cytoplasmic perturbations alongside downstream alterations in the mitochondrial respirasome and endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis. Untargeted metabolomic profiling revealed extensive metabolic reprogramming across amino acid, nucleotide, and lipid pathways, identifying key potential candidate indicators such as L-dihydroorotate, and Betaine. Furthermore, O2PLS integration successfully decoded robust multi-system covariance networks, wherein candidate distal metabolic nodes like glucosamine-6-phosphate demonstrated strong correlations with gene clusters regulating microenvironment remodeling.Conclusion[211At]At-AuNP-sdAb induces multi-dimensional cellular stress and extensive metabolic reprogramming characterized by concurrent genomic damage, subcellular organelle perturbations, and microenvironmental remodeling. Introduction

The global incidence of cancer continues to rise, particularly for malignant solid tumors such as melanoma, which are characterized by high metastatic potential and therapeutic resistance at advanced stages. Although early surgical intervention can significantly improve survival rates, conventional treatments like radiotherapy and chemotherapy remain limited in effectively managing late-stage disease [1]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop novel therapeutic strategies to enhance overall survival and quality of life for patients. Targeted alpha therapy (TAT) offers several advantages—including high linear energy transfer (LET), extremely short path length, and oxygen-independent cytotoxicity—that allow effective tumor eradication at low doses, while minimizing radiation damage to surrounding healthy tissues [2, 3]. Among various alpha-emitting radionuclides, Astatine-211 (211At) shows great clinical promise due to its favorable physical half-life (7.2 h), simple decay scheme, and excellent chemical labeling stability [4–6]. To further enhance its tumor-targeting capacity and radiobiological stability in vivo, researchers have recently conjugated 211At with gold nanoparticles (AuNPs), developing a variety of α-emitting nanoprobes [7–9]. In our previous work, we developed a novel 211At-labeled single-domain antibody drug ([211At]At-AuNP-sdAb), in which the single-domain antibody (ABDMPL16) was stably bound to the surface of a gold nanoparticle core and radiolabeled with 211At via a radiochemical method [10]. Leveraging its gold nanoparticle core, this agent not only provides a stable carrier platform for the radionuclide and enhances radiostability, but also integrates the biocompatibility of the nanocarrier, the targeting capability of the single-domain antibody, and the high LET characteristics of α-particles, thereby serving as an ideal model for investigating 211At-induced biological effects.

Although previous studies have demonstrated the potent antitumor activity of TAT in various cancer models, the underlying radiobiological mechanisms remain poorly understood. A deeper understanding of the biological responses to α radiation is essential for evaluating therapeutic efficacy, predicting side effects, and developing radiosensitization strategies. Existing research has shown that DNA damage response pathways—such as cell cycle arrest and homologous recombination repair—are key targets of TAT, forming the basis for some radiosensitizing agents [11, 12]. In addition, increasing attention has been paid to the effects of alpha radiation on the tumor microenvironment, including immune cells, endothelial cells, and cancer-associated fibroblasts [13–15]. However, most studies remain focused on nuclear DNA damage, and systematic investigations of non-DNA targets such as mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum are still lacking. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and untargeted metabolomics are key tools for uncovering radiation-induced biological responses and identifying molecular biomarkers [16, 17]. However, current studies are often limited to a single omics layer, making it difficult to fully characterize the cross-level and cross-pathway responses triggered by α particle irradiation. In particular, the synergistic mechanisms between gene regulation and metabolic reprogramming—potentially critical determinants of tumor responsiveness to TAT—remain underexplored.

In this study, using [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb as a robust targeted model platform, we systematically investigated the radiobiological mechanisms of targeted alpha therapy (TAT) from the perspective of dynamic gene–metabolite interactions by integrating transcriptomic and metabolomic data (Scheme 1). Through network integration and cross-validation of these multi-omics profiles, this study revealed multi-dimensional cellular stress responses under α-particle irradiation, encompassing coordinated nuclear-cytoplasmic perturbations—such as canonical genomic damage alongside downstream organelle, bystander, and immune-mediated alterations—coupled with extensive metabolic reprogramming. Based on these systemic correlations, we proposed a preliminary analytical framework of “local-regional-systemic” multiscale radiobiological effects, aiming to provide a comprehensive, hypothesis-generating landscape to understand the cascade of responses from the macromolecular level to the multicellular microenvironment. This integrative strategy provides multi-dimensional correlative evidence for understanding TAT-induced adaptive responses, thereby facilitating the discovery of potential candidate indicators for non-invasive therapeutic response monitoring and combination strategy optimization (Scheme 2).

Results

RNA-seq reveals significant gene expression changes after [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment

To investigate the gene expression changes in cells treated with [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb, we performed RNA-seq analysis on both the treatment and control groups. The results revealed significant differences in gene expression between the treatment and control groups. Compared to the control group, a total of 777 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, including 689 downregulated genes and 88 upregulated genes in the treatment group. This indicates that the gene expression was predominantly suppressed after treatment (Fig. 1a). The scatter plot (Fig. 1b) shows the distribution of gene expression fold changes and statistical significance. Genes that showed significant upregulation and downregulation are plotted alongside genes with no significant change. The volcano plot further confirmed this finding, showing a widespread distribution of downregulated genes and a small cluster of upregulated genes (Fig. 1c).

A violin plot (Fig. 1d) was used to illustrate the distribution of gene expression across three replicates for both the control and [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb groups. The plot suggests that the gene expression profiles in the treatment group show a broader variance compared to the control groups, indicating a more significant response to treatment. Finally, Fig. 1e presents a heatmap of several differentially expressed genes, highlighting the upregulated and downregulated genes under treatment conditions. Many key genes involved in cell signal transduction, metabolism, and stress response were significantly downregulated after treatment, with their expression levels showing significant changes.

Integrated pathway enrichment landscape of multi-dimensional cellular stress induced by alpha irradiation

Gene Ontology (GO) analysis preliminarily indicated potential effects of alpha-radionuclide treatment on DNA-related biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components (Fig. 2a-c). In terms of biological processes, enriched terms included Heterochromatin organization, DNA methylation, Double-strand break repair, DNA replication, Mitotic cell cycle, and DNA repair. These processes are involved in DNA integrity and repair mechanisms, suggesting that alpha particles induce DNA damage and activate cellular repair responses. Regarding molecular functions, enriched terms primarily focused on DNA-binding activities (e.g., G-quadruplex DNA binding, DNA helicase activity, Telomeric DNA binding, and DNA topoisomerase activity), indicating that alpha particle irradiation may affect these molecular functions related to DNA repair and replication. In terms of cellular components, enriched terms were mainly associated with DNA-related structures (including Site of DNA damage, Site of double-strand break, and Nuclear lumen), suggesting that alpha particles may induce alterations in nuclear and chromatin structure.

In parallel, we analyzed the non-DNA-related effects of alpha-radionuclides, which revealed concurrent enrichment features in terms related to oxidative stress, organelle function, and protein metabolism (Fig. 2d-f). Regarding biological processes, enriched terms included Cellular response to oxidative stress, Establishment of protein localization to organelle, Mitochondrial ATP synthesis coupled proton transport, and Endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response. These processes suggest that alpha particles not only induce DNA damage but may also trigger oxidative stress, thereby interfering with other cellular physiological processes. In terms of molecular functions, enriched terms focused on activities related to protein function and signal transduction, such as Transmembrane receptor protein kinase activity, Microtubule binding, and Protein binding. Regarding cellular components, enriched terms mainly involved structures associated with mitochondrial and microtubule function, including Inner mitochondrial membrane protein complex and Mitochondrial respirasome, suggesting the potential involvement of alpha particle irradiation in mitochondrial function.

KEGG pathway analysis further revealed the enrichment of several important DNA-related pathways (Fig. 2g), including the p53 signaling pathway, Chemical carcinogenesis - reactive oxygen species, Apoptosis - multiple species, and Cell cycle, suggesting the relevance of DNA damage response in influencing cell fate. As a complement, KEGG analysis of non-DNA-related effects showed that alpha particle irradiation significantly enriched pathways such as the MAPK signaling pathway and Ubiquitin mediated proteolysis (Fig. 2h). Furthermore, Reactome pathway analysis observed similar enrichment trends, encompassing both DNA repair pathways such as TP53 Regulates Transcription of Cell Cycle Genes, Homology Directed Repair, and G2/M DNA damage checkpoint, as well as non-DNA effect pathways including Cilium Assembly, Organelle biogenesis and maintenance (Fig. 2i and j). This multi-dimensional omics enrichment landscape collectively provides an exploratory hypothesis-generating framework for comprehensively understanding the biological effects induced by alpha particles.

Validation of multi-dimensional cellular perturbations and stress signatures induced by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb therapy

To further evaluate the multi-dimensional biochemical alterations revealed by the integrated multi-omics analysis, we performed exploratory confirmation of the co-expression characteristics of key pathway-related proteins in vivo using immunohistochemistry (IHC) and immunofluorescence (IF) analysis. [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb significantly downregulated DNA repair-related proteins Atr and Nbn, while upregulating Cdkn1a (p21), corroborating severe DNA damage and cell cycle arrest at the protein expression level (Fig. 3a). At the subcellular non-nuclear structure level, we also observed a series of concurrent accompanying changes, characterized by significant downregulation of the mitochondrial fission protein Dnm1l, the endoplasmic reticulum stress pathway-related protein Atf2, and Rock1, which regulates cell membrane skeleton integrity (Fig. 3b). Furthermore, the significant upregulation of Gja1 suggested a potential bystander effect, indicating the possible propagation of alpha particle-induced stress signals within the tumor cell population (Fig. 3c). These multi-site expression alterations were accompanied by a significant remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment, manifested by upregulation of TNF-α and downregulation of Lfih1, suggesting a potential trend toward an anti-tumor phenotype (Fig. 3d). Immunofluorescence staining further observed this accompanying phenomenon, showing increased infiltration of CD3 + and CD8 + T cells, along with features of M2-to-M1 macrophage polarization (upregulation of iNOS and downregulation of CD206) (Fig. 3e). Finally, these multi-dimensional stress features exhibited a statistical association with the triggering of the apoptotic cascade, manifested by upregulation of the pro-apoptotic marker Fas, downregulation of the anti-apoptotic protein Xiap, and upregulation of Jak2 (Fig. 3f).

Non-targeted metabolomics analysis reveals metabolite changes after [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment

In NEG mode, an unsupervised PCA score plot was first employed to provide an unbiased overview of the metabolic profiles, revealing a distinct spontaneous clustering and natural separation trend between the control and treated groups ([211At]At-AuNP-sdAb), demonstrating excellent experimental reproducibility (Fig. 4a). To maximize group separation and isolate specific radiotherapeutic-driven metabolic alterations, a supervised OPLS-DA model was subsequently applied, which showed a clear and well-defined separation between the two groups along the predictive principal component T score[1] axis (Fig. 4b). To further validate the reliability of this classification, a PLS-DA model was also performed as a complementary reference, which similarly displayed a clear discrimination pattern between the control and treated group clusters (Fig. S1).

To rigorously evaluate the mathematical robustness and potential overfitting risk of these supervised classification frameworks, 200-times permutation tests were performed for both models. As shown in the final permutation validation plots (Figure S2), the regression lines for Q2 exhibited negative intercepts on the y-axis, and all randomly permuted R2 and Q2 values were significantly lower than those of the original unpermuted models, confirming that the models have high predictive relevance and are completely free from overfitting. Furthermore, the detailed diagnostic and explanatory metrics for these multivariate statistical models are comprehensively summarized in Table S1 (for OPLS-DA) and Table S2 (for PLS-DA). The cumulative fit parameters, including R2X(cum), R2Y(cum), and Q2(cum), demonstrate robust modeling efficiency. Crucially, the empirical p-values derived from the cross-validation permutation tests (pQ2) were all strictly below 0.05, thereby statistically rejecting the null hypothesis of chance separation and enhancing the validity of the identified candidate metabolite features.

Figure 4c shows the number of upregulated and downregulated metabolites in the comparison between the control and [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb groups. The results reveal that 16 metabolites are downregulated and 9 metabolites are upregulated in the treatment group. This suggests that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment affects cellular metabolic pathways, causing significant changes in the expression of specific metabolites. To further explore these metabolite changes, a heatmap analysis was performed (Fig. 4d). The heatmap illustrates the expression level differences of several metabolites between the control and [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb groups, including L-dihydroorotate, and His-ser. The analysis suggests that alpha particle radiation significantly alters the expression of certain lipid metabolites and amino acid metabolites, indicating that these metabolic pathways may play important roles in the effects of alpha particle radiation.

The volcano plot further demonstrates the expression differences of metabolites between the treatment and control groups. In Fig. 4e, red and blue points represent upregulated and downregulated metabolites, respectively. The VIP (Variable Importance in Projection) analysis based on OPLS-DA reveals key metabolites associated with group differences, such as ketoleucine, cholesteryl sulfate and L-dihydroorotate. These metabolites show significant changes in the treatment group and may play key roles in the treatment effects (Fig. 4f). Based on the radar chart (Fig. 4g), we further highlight the 7 most significantly changed metabolites, including L-dihydroorotate and His-ser. The abundance of these metabolites significantly decreased in the [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment group, suggesting that they may play critical roles in the therapeutic response.

Metabolic pathway enrichment and candidate indicator identification

To further elucidate the metabolic network perturbations induced by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis and exploratory response indicator screening were performed based on non-targeted metabolomics data.

In the NEG mode, pathway classification revealed that the differential metabolites were primarily enriched in metabolic pathways, particularly in “Global and overview maps”, “Amino acid metabolism”, and “Nucleotide metabolism” (Fig. 6a). This suggests that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment primarily disrupted global metabolic profiles, as well as amino acid and nucleotide metabolic pathways. In addition, a smaller subset of metabolites was associated with categories such as “Environmental Information Processing” (e.g., signaling molecules and interaction), and “Organismal Systems” (e.g., immune system), indicating the multi-level metabolic system perturbations induced by alpha radionuclide therapy. Further downstream KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the altered metabolites were significantly enriched in core pathways, including Pyrimidine metabolism, Valine, leucine and isoleucine degradation, Lysine biosynthesis, and Biosynthesis of amino acids (Fig. 6b). Among these, Pyrimidine metabolism represented the highest proportion of enriched metabolites, highlighting it as a pivotal metabolic axis impacted by the treatment.

To identify candidate metabolic signatures with exploratory therapeutic response value, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves with 95% CI were plotted based on the differential metabolites, and corresponding area under the curve (AUC) values were calculated (Fig. 6c, Table S5). The results showed that L-dihydroorotate possessed excellent discriminatory power between the control and treatment groups, with its AUC value reaching 1.00 (95% CI: 0.735-1.000), demonstrating robust mathematical sensitivity and specificity in this preclinical model. Additionally, potential indicators such as cholesteryl sulfate also exhibited high diagnostic efficiency with an AUC value of 0.944 (95% CI: 0.652-1.000). To ensure rigorous chemical annotation accuracy, the chemical structures of these potential exploratory indicators were cross-verified against the HMDB reference database based on consistent MS/MS fragmentation patterns (Figures S5–S14).

Similarly, KEGG pathway enrichment and candidate signature screening were implemented based on POS mode non-targeted metabolomics data. In the POS mode, pathway classification indicated that differential metabolites were mainly enriched in metabolic pathways, especially in “Global and overview maps”, “Amino acid metabolism”, and “Lipid metabolism” (Fig. 7a). This implies that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment predominantly interfered with global metabolism, amino acid metabolism, and lipid metabolic processes. Additionally, a portion of metabolites was associated with “Environmental Information Processing” (e.g., Signal transduction) and “Cellular processes” (e.g., cell growth and death), reflecting the broader impact of radionuclide therapy on cellular metabolic and systemic functions. Further enrichment analysis revealed that, in the POS mode, metabolites were significantly enriched in key pathways such as Glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, Sphingolipid metabolism, Choline metabolism in cancer, and Sphingolipid signaling pathway (Fig. 7b). Among these, Glycine, serine and threonine metabolism represented the pathway with the highest proportion of enriched metabolites, suggesting its crucial role in response to alpha particle treatment.

Multi-omics inter-system network integration decoding the covariance landscape of multi-dimensional stress and metabolic reprogramming

To systematically decode the molecular basis of [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb therapy, we employed a O2PLS model in NEG mode to integrate the cellular transcriptome (in vitro intracellular response) and the serum metabolome (in vivo systemic perturbation). The model demonstrated robust statistical performance with high cumulative explanatory power (R2X = 0.878, R2Y = 0.853). The model was optimized with 4 joint components (a = 4) and zero orthogonal components, achieving a minimized prediction error of 1.806 (Table S7). These metrics confirm that the identified molecular changes represent conserved biological responses to alpha particle irradiation.

Joint loadings analysis identified key variables driving cross-system changes (Fig. 8a-c). Specifically, oxidative stress indicators (e.g., ophthalmate, L-dihydroorotate) and phospholipid derivatives (e.g., Pi 40:7) showed strong associations with genes regulating cellular homeostasis. Correlation heatmaps (Fig. 8d) and interaction networks (Fig. 8e) further elucidated these regulatory axes. Notably, significant positive correlations were observed between the autophagy adaptor protein Calcoco2 and the pyrimidine precursor L-dihydroorotate, as well as between the scaffold protein Frmd4b and the phospholipid Pg 40:6. These associations suggest a tight coupling between the accumulation of stress-related metabolites and the transcriptional activation of cellular structural remodeling and compensatory degradation pathways.

To validate the metabolic reprogramming predicted by the multi-omics landscape, we performed immunohistochemical analysis of key regulatory hubs according to their network contributions (Fig. 8f). Given the strong correlation between pyrimidine-related metabolites and gene clusters, we first focused on DHODH, the mitochondrial enzyme responsible for catalyzing the oxidation of L-dihydroorotate to orotate; its significant downregulation provides a molecular level validation for the observed pyrimidine metabolic disruption. Concurrently, the marked upregulation of the autophagy adaptor protein Calcoco2 provided direct experimental evidence for the activation of compensatory degradation pathways identified in the multi-omics network. To reflect the lipid metabolic changes, we next assessed GPX4 expression; its significant downregulation confirmed that the alterations in the phospholipid profile were accompanied by a compromise of the cellular antioxidant defense system. Finally, the significant upregulation of Frmd4b further substantiated the network associations, collectively demonstrating that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb therapy is characterized by a concurrent cascade of autophagic stress and ferroptosis-related structural perturbations at mitochondria- and plasma membrane-associated checkpoints. To further substantiate the functional relevance of these cross-system associations, we performed a joint KEGG pathway analysis integrating significantly altered genes and metabolites in NEG mode (Figure S25). The analysis revealed a high degree of convergence in “Metabolic pathways”, specifically highlighting perturbations in “Purine metabolism” and “Necroptosis”. This functional landscape aligns with the observed downregulation of DHODH and the activation of autophagic adaptors, suggesting that the [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb-induced transcriptional shift is fundamentally coupled with the collapse of nucleotide synthesis and the initiation of programmed cell death pathways.

To further elucidate the cross-system responses triggered by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb therapy, we constructed a O2PLS model using POS mode. This model exhibited high explanatory power (R2X = 0.818, R2Y = 0.885) and was optimized with 3 joint components (Table S8). The gene and metabolite loadings plots (Fig. 9a and b) identified high-contribution variables driving joint covariance, representing multi-dimensional molecular transitions. This transition was characterized by synchronized changes in structural remodeling genes (e.g., Actr3, Cep128, and Gsdmc) together with a broad range of metabolites involved in lipid signaling (e.g., N-oleoylethanolamine) and core carbon metabolism. Critically, within this reconstructed cross-system metabolic landscape, the joint loadings space (Fig. 9c) and correlation network (Fig. 9d and e) precisely pinpointed glucosamine-6-phosphate—a key intermediate of the hexosamine biosynthesis pathway—as a candidate distal core metabolic node connecting systemic nutrient sensing to cellular transcriptional output.

Notably, this metabolite exhibited significant negative correlations with both the pluripotency factor Nanog and the immune stromal marker Acp5. This inverse association suggests that the depletion of glucose-derived precursors under alpha radiation stress acts as a “metabolic switch” triggering compensatory stemness program activation and microenvironment remodeling characterized by Acp5 mobilization. To validate these findings, we performed immunohistochemical analysis (Fig. 9f). The significant downregulation of the major glucose transporter Glut1 provided direct experimental evidence for the glucose uptake impairment underlying the reduction in glucosamine-6-phosphate. Meanwhile, the marked upregulation of Nanog and Acp5 confirmed the emergence of a cancer cell stemness phenotype and stromal response as survival strategies in response to metabolic crisis. These findings collectively demonstrate that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb therapy induces a profound metabolic-transcriptional shift by coupling suppressed glucose utilization with cancer cell plasticity and microenvironmental mobilization. Complementarily, the joint KEGG enrichment analysis in POS mode (Figure S26) provided a broader perspective on the cellular response to the “nutrient crisis”. Beyond the prominent “Metabolic pathways”, we observed coordinated enrichments in “cAMP signaling” and “PI3K-Akt signaling pathway”. These results substantiate the O2PLS-predicted metabolic-transcriptional shift, indicating that the disruption of glucose uptake and hexosamine biosynthesis is integrated into a wider regulatory network of growth signaling and survival-related plasticity, as evidenced by the concurrent upregulation of Nanog and Acp5.

Discussion

This study reveals important features of metabolic and gene expression changes induced by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment, reflecting the complex biological mechanisms of alpha particle radiation-induced DNA damage and cellular stress responses. As a high linear energy transfer radiation modality, [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb not only induces direct genomic damage but may also disrupt key intracellular structures and pathways, thereby producing therapeutic effects. RNA-seq analysis revealed a total of 689 downregulated and 88 upregulated genes following treatment, indicating that transcriptional suppression is the dominant pattern. This expression profile is consistent with known cellular responses to radiation-induced DNA damage, which typically involves suppression of non-essential metabolic functions and prioritization of DNA repair. Further analysis showed that upregulated genes were mainly enriched in stress response and repair-related pathways, particularly the p53 signaling pathway and DNA repair pathways. The significant enrichment of these pathways suggests that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment activates DNA damage checkpoints and repair mechanisms to maintain genomic integrity [16, 18–21].

To further explore the potential biological consequences underlying these gene expression changes, pathway enrichment analysis indicated that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment induces extensive cellular perturbations involving both DNA-centric effects and non-DNA-centric alterations at the transcriptomic level. It should be noted that this multi-dimensional stress landscape derived from bioinformatics classification is currently based primarily on statistical network associations and should be cautiously regarded as a “hypothesis-generating” academic framework, not equivalent to validated hierarchical structures or direct causal relationships. Specifically, at the DNA level, pathways related to double-strand break repair and homologous recombination were significantly enriched, consistent with the physical characteristics of alpha particle radiation-induced severe DNA damage. In parallel non-DNA-associated networks, key organelles such as mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum exhibited significant gene pathway perturbations. Mitochondria-related pathway fluctuations were primarily indicated by enrichment of genes involved in oxidative phosphorylation, ATP production, and reactive oxygen species signaling. These transcriptomic alterations suggest that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment may have potential biological associations with mitochondrial membrane homeostasis, permeability changes, and metabolic fluctuations, which may in turn participate in triggering intrinsic apoptotic pathways [22–25]. In addition to mitochondria-related pathways, significant enrichment of the classical unfolded protein response pathway (particularly the endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response) also suggested activation of ER stress responses. This activation may play a potential role in enhancing pro-apoptotic signals under excessive stress conditions [26]. Although we have preliminarily confirmed the concomitant co-expression trends of the above key stress nodes through protein-level detection, the dynamic interplay and deep causal chains among these cross-system phenotypes (e.g., glucose remodeling, ferroptosis, immune remodeling) still require further elucidation through functional perturbation experiments.

Untargeted metabolomics analysis revealed significant alterations in amino acid, nucleotide, lipid, and choline metabolism pathways in the [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb-treated group. PCA, OPLS-DA, and PLS-DA confirmed clear separation between the treatment and control groups, indicating that alpha particle irradiation induces comprehensive metabolic reprogramming across multiple biochemical pathways. This reprogramming may represent an adaptive feature of cells to radiation-induced stress [27–30]. The observed alterations in glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism in the treatment group are consistent with previous findings on ionizing radiation. Notably, serine, a key donor of one-carbon units, may have reduced availability potentially limiting purine and thymidine synthesis, suggesting competitive depletion of DNA repair resources. Similarly, glycine depletion may reduce glutathione precursor availability, potentially exacerbating radiation-induced oxidative stress [31]. Changes in specific metabolites such as ketoleucine suggest that branched-chain amino acid catabolism may participate in regulating damage repair processes [32, 33]. At the nucleotide metabolism level, decreased L-dihydroorotate reflects restricted pyrimidine biosynthesis, potentially linked to insufficient DNA and RNA precursor production. The imbalance between increased radiation-induced DNA synthesis demand and restricted nucleotide supply is thought to potentially associate with delayed DNA double-strand break repair and chromosomal instability. Furthermore, fluctuations in purine metabolism may reflect compensatory regulation of redox homeostasis. Collectively, these findings suggest that alpha particle irradiation may impact DNA repair and antioxidant capacity by interfering with one-carbon metabolism and nucleotide biosynthesis.

Significant alterations in lipid metabolites such as Pc 34:1 and Pe 36:2 were also observed, consistent with previous studies on radiation [34–37]. Research has shown that hydroxyl radicals generated by radiation can induce lipid peroxidation. Additionally, radiation may be associated with activation of sphingomyelinase, which hydrolyzes sphingomyelin to generate ceramide, linking to pathways that induce apoptosis. Disruption of choline and its derivatives suggests that alpha particle irradiation may interfere with cholinergic signaling networks. As choline metabolism is a central node connecting phospholipid synthesis and methyl donor generation, its dysregulation is hypothesized to potentially affect both membrane dynamics and epigenetic regulation [38, 39]. Through ROC curve analysis, we identified a panel of metabolites exhibiting excellent discriminatory power. Notably, changes in metabolites such as L-dihydroorotate corroborate fluctuations in DNA repair and nucleotide replenishment pathways in response to radiation damage. Elevated betaine and choline-related lipids reflect perturbations in membrane integrity, correlating with known mechanisms of radiation-induced membrane damage. Collectively, these metabolites represent unique biochemical fingerprints, providing a hypothesis-generating basis for understanding the pathophysiological processes triggered by radiation exposure.

The findings of this study are both consistent with previous research on radiation biology and demonstrate the uniqueness of our platform. Regarding metabolic remodeling, the extensive amino acid and lipid fluctuations we observed are highly consistent with the systemic profiles reported by Maan et al. using integrated omics in irradiated mouse models [40]; notably, the glycerophospholipid metabolism reprogramming and downregulation trend of polyunsaturated phospholipids (e.g., PC molecules) identified in our study are highly consistent with the lipid barrier damage characteristics induced by high-dose radiation in that study, collectively confirming the universal interference of ionizing radiation with core biosynthesis and membrane homeostasis. Regarding transcriptomic responses, the classical p53 signaling pathway activation and DNA repair-related differentially expressed genes such as Cdkn1a identified in this study are completely consistent with the transcriptomic exploration results of Ohshima et al. on 211At targeted therapy, further establishing the central role of the persistent p53-p21 cascade response induced by alpha particle radiation [16]. However, our platform reveals several profound insights that extend beyond conventional stress responses or single-omics biomarkers. While previous studies have largely been confined to generalized cell cycle arrest or systemic radiation damage, our deep cross-omics integration identifies a more fundamental “metabolic-transcriptional” reprogramming induced by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb. This reprogramming is not merely a collection of isolated molecular alterations, but rather a systemic response that tightly couples local nutrient-sensing deprivation with the plastic adjustment of cellular phenotypes. Such inter-system functional signatures, often obscured in previous non-integrated studies, provide a novel, hypothesis-generating foundation for dissecting the unique microenvironmental killing mechanisms of alpha-particle agents.

By integrating transcriptomic and metabolomic datasets using the O2PLS model, we systematically analyzed the metabolic-transcriptional association patterns induced by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb. The model demonstrated robust statistical performance in NEG mode, revealing tight coupling between cellular metabolic network remodeling and systemic transcriptional fluctuations following treatment. In joint loadings analysis, the oxidative stress markers ophthalmate and L-dihydroorotate exhibited significant distribution patterns. The concentration fluctuations of ophthalmate, a compensatory marker of glutathione depletion, co-localized with genes such as Crebl2 that regulate mitochondrial homeostasis, suggesting that [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment activates endogenous protective antioxidant signaling axes. Notably, the significant positive correlation between L-dihydroorotate and the autophagy adaptor protein Calcoco2 captured our attention. Given that Calcoco2 plays a key role in mediating autophagic degradation of damaged organelles, this association pattern suggests that dysregulation of nucleotide precursor metabolism may be functionally coupled with alpha particle-induced cellular structural remodeling and activation of compensatory degradation pathways.

Further immunohistochemical (IHC) validation provided molecular-level support for this multi-omics landscape, clearly delineating two coordinated responsive patterns. The significant downregulation of the mitochondrial enzyme DHODH not only corroborated the pyrimidine metabolic disturbances but also exhibited high spatial consistency with the upregulation of the autophagy adaptor Calcoco2 identified in the network. This concurrent profile of “metabolic enzyme suppression and autophagy-marker activation” suggests a close biological association between [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb-induced mitochondrial dysfunction and compensatory cellular degradation responses. Another coordinated response is reflected in the associated phenotype of lipid homeostasis and membrane structural stress, where the marked downregulation of GPX4 was highly consistent with alterations in the phospholipid profile (e.g., Pg 40:6), delineating the collapse of the antioxidant defense system. Simultaneously, the synchronous change in the scaffold protein Frmd4b further substantiated the membrane-associated stress identified in the multi-omics network. Collectively, the synergistic suppression of DHODH and GPX4 demonstrates that alpha-particle therapy, while interfering with multi-dimensional metabolic checkpoints, is accompanied by a cascade of ferroptosis and autophagic stress. While the precise regulatory hierarchy remains to be further elucidated, these synchronized protein-metabolite fluctuations provide robust clues for understanding the systemic remodeling induced by TAT.

In positive ion mode, the O2PLS model demonstrated high explanatory power, revealing synchronous shifts between structural remodeling genes (e.g., Actr3, Gsdmc) and core carbon metabolites. Within this metabolic landscape, glucosamine-6-phosphate was identified as a highly connected candidate metabolic hub. As a key intermediate of the hexosamine biosynthesis pathway, fluctuations in this metabolite typically reflect cellular nutrient-sensing status and energy homeostasis.Our results showed that glucosamine-6-phosphate exhibited a significant negative correlation with the pluripotency factor Nanog, while showing a distinctive correlation with the immune stromal marker Acp5. This finding suggests that under [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb-induced metabolic stress, the depletion of glucose-derived precursors may act as a “metabolic switch”, potentially accompanied by alterations in lysosomal function or immune microenvironment characteristics (Acp5 upregulation) while being associated with the activation of tumor cell stemness programs (Nanog upregulation).Immunohistochemical results further supported this inference. The significant downregulation of the major glucose transporter Glut1 provided direct evidence for the glucose uptake impairment underlying the reduction in glucosamine-6-phosphate. Meanwhile, the upregulation of Nanog and Acp5 spatially echoed the metabolic landscape, collectively delineating the phenotypic plasticity adjustments of tumor cells in response to the “nutrient crisis” induced by the radiopharmaceutical. Although caution is still needed in defining it as an absolute “metabolic checkpoint,” these multi-omics associations provide important biological perspectives for understanding how TAT therapy drives tumor cell transcriptional reprogramming through the remodeling of nutrient-sensing networks.

Although this study provides profound cross-system perspectives for systematically dissecting the biological effects of [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb therapy, its inherent systematic limitations must be examined within a cautious academic framework. At the technological and omics dimensions, the m/z database matching on which untargeted metabolomics relies inherently carries risks of annotation uncertainty and false positives. Moreover, the current work focuses only on the joint analysis of transcriptomics and metabolomics, without further integration of high-throughput proteomics or epigenetics data, leaving the intermediate molecular regulatory chain from gene transcription to final metabolic phenotype incomplete. At the experimental design level, this study adopted a cross-system association model pairing in vitro treated cells (transcriptome) with in vivo mouse serum (metabolome). Given that serum metabolic phenotypes are strongly regulated by the whole-body systemic physiological metabolic network rather than purely reflecting local tumor tissue responses, this objectively dilutes the direct intracellular causal correspondence between the two omics layers. Therefore, the key gene-metabolite co-loading axes identified based on the O2PLS model should currently be strictly characterized as “hypothesis-generating”, and their direct molecular interaction mechanisms await future final validation through downstream functional perturbation experiments. Furthermore, in terms of clinical translational exploration, this study currently remains at an early stage, having not fully elucidated the long-term remodeling effects of this targeted alpha therapy on the tumor immune microenvironment, nor conducted in-depth exploration of radioimmuno-combination therapeutic strategies based on this platform. This, combined with the background limitation of using a single B16F10 melanoma mouse model, inevitably restricts the generalizability of the current conclusions directly to other highly heterogeneous clinical scenarios (e.g., breast cancer, pancreatic cancer). Consistently, “biomarker” is a rigorous concept requiring large-sample, multi-center clinical validation. The key circulating metabolites identified in this study should currently be cautiously characterized as “potential exploratory indicators”, and their excellent discriminatory power in strictly controlled experimental groups is insufficient to directly validate their utility as clinical diagnostic biomarkers. In summary, future efforts urgently need to integrate multiple disease models, multi-dimensional omics technologies, and large-scale clinical cohorts to ultimately elucidate and validate the clinical translational prospects of this targeted alpha therapy platform.

Conclusion

In summary, our study provides a systematic and comprehensive characterization of the transcriptomic and metabolic landscapes induced by [211At]At-AuNP-sdAb treatment. The findings underscore the dual capacity of alpha-particle radiation to elicit both direct genomic damage and potent non-DNA stress effects. This synergy drives a complex biological panorama wherein extensive transcriptional suppression is coupled with systemic metabolic “switches”, collectively reshaping the multi-dimensional cellular stress response network.Utilizing the O2PLS model, we identified and validated distinct metabolic-transcriptional association signatures involving DHODH and Calcoco2, as well as Glut1 and Nanog. These findings reveal coordinated fluctuation patterns between core metabolic enzymes and key transcriptional regulators, illustrating how targeted alpha therapy drives phenotypic plasticity adjustments through the remodeling of localized nutrient-sensing networks. The identification of these unique biochemical fingerprints provides a novel, hypothesis-generating basis for dissecting the distinct microenvironmental killing mechanisms of alpha-particle agents. Future research should prioritize validating these biomarkers in clinical cohorts and exploring the potential of targeting specific metabolic nodes—such as ferroptosis defense or nutrient-sensing pathways—to achieve enhanced therapeutic outcomes.

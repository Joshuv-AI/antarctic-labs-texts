Patterns and frequency of keratoplasty in corneal dystrophy at a tertiary eye hospital in Nepal
Authors: Rachana Singh Rana, Tithsya Hun, Manish P Poudel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCorneal dystrophy is a group of hereditary disorders with different presentations and management between regions and ethnic groups. This study aimed to report the patterns of corneal dystrophy, the frequency of keratoplasty requirement, and the outcomes of performed keratoplasty among cases with corneal dystrophy at a tertiary eye hospital in Nepal.MethodsData of all patients presented at corneal clinic, Tilganga Institute of Ophthalmology, with the diagnosis of corneal dystrophy documented in the Electronic Medical Record (EMR) from 1st January 2017 to 31st December 2025 were identified, categorized, and analyzed regarding demographic status, corneal dystrophic subtypes, the needs for keratoplasty, and the outcomes of the keratoplasty performed.ResultsAmong 264 eyes of 132 participants, 50.8% and 49.2% were male and female, respectively. The mean age ± standard deviation of the participants was 43.7 ± 20.5 years. Epithelial-subepithelial, stromal, and endothelial dystrophy accounted for 9.8%, 21.3%, and 63.6%, respectively. Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD) was the most common subtype, followed by congenital hereditary endothelial dystrophy (CHED) and epithelial basement membrane dystrophy (EBMD). In 264 eyes of 132 patients, 92 eyes (34.9%) were indicated for keratoplasty. The proportion of eyes requiring keratoplasty were 34.8%, 29.3%, 31.5%, and 4.3% in FECD, CHED, the stromal dystrophy group, and unspecified corneal dystrophy, respectively. Among the eyes that underwent keratoplasty, 71.4% had clear graft at the l2-month follow-up visit.ConclusionFuchs Endothelial Corneal Dystrophy was the most common subtype of corneal dystrophies in the current study. However, the proportion of FECD cases requiring keratoplasty was significantly lower than that in CHED and the stromal dystrophy group. Outcomes of keratoplasty were satisfactory, as a large number of cases had clear grafts at the12-month visit.Keywords: Corneal dystrophy, Pattern, Frequency, Keratoplasty, Tertiary eye hospital, Nepal

Introduction

Corneal dystrophy is considered to be a group of hereditary corneal diseases, which display as bilateral and symmetrical condition, affecting the transparency and structural integrity of the cornea without associating with any systemic diseases [1–3]. Even though the description of corneal dystrophy is well-established in many literatures, the characteristics of each type of corneal dystrophy could differ from one another. Stromal dystrophy could be hereditary and bilateral, but asymmetry in severity is not uncommon [3]. Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD), on the other hand, does not typically present at an early age and could also be sporadic [4]. These variations may be related to demographic differences, including race, climate, socioeconomical status, and community practices [5, 6].

While corneal dystrophy was found to be 0.09% in the United States, 0.3% and 0.2% of corneal dystrophy were found in Iran and India, respectively [4, 7, 8]. Fuchs endothelial dystrophy was the most prevalent subtype of corneal dystrophy in other studies [6, 9]. However, anterior corneal dystrophies were more prevalent in the United States [7] than in South India [3] and Germany [10]. In the latter two studies, granular dystrophy type 1 was the most common non-Fuchs dystrophy.

The frequency and types of interventions in the management of corneal dystrophy also vary among countries. There was a significant increase in the rate of keratoplasty performed in corneal dystrophy in India between the 2011–2015 and 2016–2020 [11]. In Nepal, corneal dystrophies accounted for 1.7% of 645 keratoplasties performed between 2005 and 2010 [12]. However, the types of corneal dystrophy and the surgical interventions were not fully discussed. While FECD had the highest rate of keratoplasty performed in many literatures, there was a changing trend from full thickness transplant to lamellar keratoplasty, mainly Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK) [10, 13, 14]. Nevertheless, the studies focused only on cases that underwent keratoplasty. From the authors’ observation, many corneal dystrophies that required keratoplasty did not receive the indicated surgical management due to various reasons. Therefore, the real frequency of keratoplasty requirement in each type of dystrophy could potentially be underestimated.

Methods

Data source

A retrospective review of patients who presented to Tilganga Institute of Ophthalmology, a well-recognized eye center, was conducted. Patients, who presented with bilateral corneal lesion between 1st January 2017 to 31st December 2025, were tracked with electronic medical record system (EMR).

Sample selection

All patients with the record of corneal dystrophy in at least one visit were enlisted for further review. In order to rule out misdiagnosis or inconsistency in diagnosis, at least two records of corneal dystrophy of the same category of corneal dystrophy in two consecutive visits would be required. Patient whose diagnosis of corneal dystrophy was altered during subsequent visits were excluded from the analysis.

Grouping of corneal dystrophies

Based on and adapted from the third edition of the international committee for the classification of corneal dystrophies (IC3D) [15], corneal dystrophies were categorized into four groups, including epithelial-subepithelial dystrophies, stromal dystrophies, endothelial dystrophy, and unspecified corneal dystrophy. “Epithelial-subepithelial dystrophies” referred to epithelial and basement membrane disorders, such as epithelial basement membrane dystrophy (EBMD) “Stromal dystrophy” included Reis-Bücklers corneal dystrophy, granular corneal dystrophy type 1 (GCD1), granular corneal dystrophy type 2 (GCD2), lattice corneal dystrophy (LCD), macular corneal dystrophy (MCD), and Schnyder corneal dystrophy. “Endothelial dystrophy” comprised disorders involving Descemet’s membrane and endothelium, including Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD), congenital hereditary endothelial dystrophy (CHED), and posterior polymorphous dystrophy (PPMD). “Unspecified” corneal dystrophy accounted for cases which was documented as corneal dystrophy, but without subtype categorization. Surgical intervention and outcome measurement

Surgical intervention was subcategorized into “keratoplasty required” and “keratoplasty performed”. The rationale for this division was due to the potential loss of data regarding the cases that required keratoplasty, but did not receive the intervention because of various reasons. Therefore, analysis was done separately for both the cases which required keratoplasty and cases which underwent keratoplasty.

Clinical indications for considering keratoplasty included corneal dystrophy-related visual impairment, deterioration of best-corrected visual acuity by 2 lines or more compared with baseline measurement at the initial visit, visually significant corneal opacification, or persistent corneal edema. The final decision to proceed with keratoplasty were based on corneal consultants’ clinical judgement. To enable similar measurement of surgical outcomes, graft clarity and the reasons of graft cloudiness at the 12-month visit would be accounted. A cloudy corneal graft defined as loss of central clarity compromising vision in at least 3 consecutive months [16].

Data analysis

Acquired data from the IT department, including demographic characteristics, visual acuity (LogMAR), surgical management, and surgical outcomes, were cleaned and coded using Microsoft Excel. The cleaned data was transported to IBM SPSS Statistics version 26 for statistical analysis. Demographic data comprising age, sex, and year of presentation, were analyzed descriptively. For the management section, each eye was analyzed separately to evaluate the frequency of requiring surgical interventions and associated surgical outcomes. For the association of categorical data, Pearson’s chi-square test was used for comparisons. For contingency tables involving small cell counts, Fisher’s exact test with Monte Carlo estimation was utilized. P value less than 0.05 was considered as statistical significance.

Ethical consideration

The study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The current research was approved by the Institutional Review Committee of Tilganga Institution of Ophthalmology (Protocol No: 07/2026). As the study was retrospective in nature, patients’ consent was not acquired.

Results

Through the EMR, 279 patients with the diagnosis of “corneal dystrophy”, “cornea dystrophy”, or “dystrophy” were found. After screening for limited visits, duplications and misdiagnosed cases, 132 patients were eligible for analysis (Fig. 3).

The mean age ± standard deviation of study participants was 43.7 ± 20.5 years. Female and male participants were 50.8% and 49.2%, respectively. Year of presentation (Fig. 4) showed 2025 as the year with highest number of dystrophies (15.9%) presenting at corneal clinic while 2020, which was during COVID 19 outbreak, had the lowest percentage (3.8%).

Frequency and distribution of keratoplasty among eyes with corneal dystrophy

Regarding the surgical management, keratoplasty was required in 32/116 FECD cases (Table 1). Among CHED cases, 27/38 eyes were indicated for keratoplasty. For the stromal dystrophy group, keratoplasty was required in 11/18 eyes with MCD, 10/16 with LCD, 4/12 with GCD1, and 4/6 with GCD2 (Table 1). Due to small sample size of individual stromal dystrophy subtypes, these subtypes were combined into a single stromal dystrophy group for analysis and comparison. Using Chi-Square test, cases with CHED were more likely to require keratoplasty comparing to cases with FECD (OR = 0.155, 95% CI 0.069–0.349, p < 0.001). Keratoplasty requirements were also significantly higher in the stromal dystrophy group comparing to FECD (OR = 2.819, 95% CI: 1.452–5.475, p value = 0.002).

Among the 92 eyes requiring keratoplasty, only 45 eyes underwent the surgical intervention. The mean follow-up duration ± standard deviation was 33.43 ± 22.69 months. At the 12-month follow-up visit, three cases were excluded due to limited follow-ups (less than 12 months). Clear grafts at 12 months were observed in 71.4% (30/42). Among the procedures, the 12-month clear graft rates were 78.9% (15/19), 75.0% (6/8), and 60.0% (9/15) for PK, DALK, and DSAEK, respectively. However, there was no significant difference in term of graft clarity among the three procedures (Fisher’s exact test with Monte Carlo estimation, p value = 0.446). The causes of graft failure were endothelial failure in 6 cases, rejection in 2 cases, graft opacification in 3 cases and graft infection in 1 case (Fig. 5). Regraft was done in 14 cases. Among the 45 eyes, visual acuity data was only available in 37 cases. The mean pre-operative and post-operative visual acuity (LogMAR ± standard deviation) was 1.49 ± 0.58 and 1.05 ± 0.64, respectively (n = 37).

Discussion

This study provides insights into the distribution of corneal dystrophy at a tertiary eye hospital in Nepal and the frequency of required surgical intervention among cases presenting with corneal dystrophies. Although FECD had the highest percentage among all dystrophic subtypes, the proportion of cases requiring keratoplasty was significantly lower than that in CHED and the stromal dystrophy group.

Our data showed similarity in number of diagnosed corneal dystrophies from 2017 to 2025. In the study of histopathologically confirmed corneal dystrophies in German, there was a dramatic increase in the number of cases from 2015 to 2024 [10]. This increase could be related to the better understanding of corneal dystrophy and the evolution of surgical technique in keratoplasty. We did not observe a consistent and significant rise in the number of corneal dystrophy cases throughout the 8-year study period. The reason may be attributed to the late adoption of the EMR system in 2017, during which corneal dystrophy became the diagnosis of interest at the tertiary eye hospital.

Among stromal dystrophies, MCD had the highest number of cases, followed by LCD, GCD1, and GCD2. This pattern was consistent with the findings of Pandrowala et al., who documented MCD as the most common stromal dystrophy [14]. However, comparing to the current study, the frequency of MCD in their findings was higher. The reason may be related to the large proportion of patients with family history of consanguineous marriage among South Indian population (62%) [3].

As expected, Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD) accounted for the highest number of cases among all corneal dystrophic subtypes. Nevertheless, the prevalence was slightly less than other studies [7, 9]. The current study identified a large number of incidental cases of asymptomatic FECD. The high prevalence of FECD may be clinically meaningful. However, this finding may be overestimated due to improved screening modalities, increased public awareness, and better recognition of FECD by eye care providers [17]. Moreover, despite the large number of eyes with FECD, only a proportion of affected eyes required surgery, while the stromal dystrophy group was more likely to require keratoplasty. This observation may reflect the more aggressive nature of stromal dystrophies, which more frequently require management (Fig. 1). However, one should not underestimate the quiescence of FECD. Among cases of FECD, 8.6% were diagnosed following prolonged corneal edema after cataract surgery. Although the proportion of FECD diagnosed after cataract surgery in this study was lower than that reported in other studies [18], the findings highlight the important of recognizing FECD even in asymptomatic eyes to prevent severe endothelial damage during cataract surgery.

CHED presented as the second most common corneal dystrophic subtype in this paper. Comparing to the current study, the prevalence of CHED in the western country was relatively low [10, 19]. However, two studies in India and Saudi Arabia reported a high number of CHED cases, which may be related to the common practice of consanguineous marriage in these populations [3, 14, 20]. In Nepal, consanguineous marriage is relatively common among the Muslim minority [21]. In our observation, many CHED cases presenting to the tertiary eye hospital belonged to these communities. In the current study, CHED was managed with full-thickness keratoplasty. The rational was due to the lack of familiarity regarding endothelial keratoplasty in pediatric cases. Although the outcomes were satisfactory in the study center, endothelial keratoplasty should be considered to achieve better long-term outcomes, as demonstrated in other studies [22–24].

Epithelial-subepithelial dystrophy, especially EBMD, appeared to vary among literatures [7, 10, 25]. While the current study found a relatively high number of EBMD throughout the studied period, the studies in India did not present any cases of EBMD [3]. On the other hand, gelatinous drop-like corneal dystrophy was not documented throughout our 8-year period while their study found a significant number of cases. As many patients with these subtypes of dystrophy presents with recurrent corneal erosion or corneal abrasion, misdiagnosis could be common. Hence, the prevalence of epithelial-subepithelial dystrophy may be compromised.

This paper provides insight into the differences in the clinical presentations of corneal dystrophy across countries. However, the differences may not solely be related to population genetics or community practices, but may also be attributed to the methodological and health-system factors. These include differences in study design (population-based versus EMR-based), referral bias, healthcare services (such as screening programs, consultants’ competency in establishing accurate and consistent diagnosis, and insurance coverage), and diagnostic practices (including diagnostic criteria, clinician awareness, availability of screening tools, and laboratory evaluation). In the present study setting, patients with corneal dystrophy are commonly referred to tertiary eye care services. However, case identification procedures may have varied among consultants and corneal specialists, potentially affecting diagnostic consistency. Moreover, documentation was not always standardized, and missing data of important information, such as family history during the early phases of implementing EMR system, caused constraints in the completeness of the database. These factors may also contribute to the differences in results observed between countries, rather than reflecting epidemiological variation.

Despite a significant proportion of dystrophic cases requiring keratoplasty, only 45 eyes underwent keratoplasty. Therefore, evaluating sorely on the number of performed procedures would underestimate the actual data of cases requiring keratoplasty. Clear graft at 12-month follow-up of the current study was slightly lower comparing to other studies [14, 26, 27]. This finding may be associated with the large number of endothelial failures among eyes undergoing DSAEK. The failure rate reflected the needs for refinement of surgical techniques in endothelial keratoplasty and better handling of donor tissues.

Regarding the novelty of the study, an earlier study related to corneal dystrophy in Nepal did not focus on corneal dystrophy subtypes or keratoplasty requirements in affected cases. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study in Nepal to evaluate the patterns of corneal dystrophy and keratoplasty requirements among dystrophic subtypes. The current study highlights the importance of reflecting on the true burden of FECD. A substantial number of FECD cases were identified incidentally. Furthermore, despite the large proportion of FECD cases, the frequency of requiring keratoplasty in patients with FECD was not significantly higher than that CHED and the stromal dystrophy group. Two studies in India addressed the prevalence of corneal dystrophy and the frequency of requiring keratoplasty. However, they did not describe patients who met indications for keratoplasty, but did not undergo surgery. In our study, we observed a substantial proportion of patients who were indicated for keratoplasty, but did not undergo surgical intervention. Therefore, limiting the analysis to cases that underwent keratoplasty may underestimate the true proportion of corneal dystrophy cases requiring surgical intervention.

Due to the retrospective nature of the study, all information was based on the available medical records. Furthermore, the study included only cases with at least two documented corneal dystrophy diagnosis across two consecutive visits, which may have excluded true corneal dystrophy cases with limited visit. Therefore, selective bias may have been introduced. Histopathological confirmation of corneal dystrophy, genetic testing data, and family history were not consistently available. The inadequacy of these data may have interfered with the accuracy of disease classification and diagnostic confirmation, which may have affected the differences in prevalence of corneal dystrophy between the current study and previous reports. Due to the lack of universally accepted consensus regarding the indications of surgical intervention, such as the degree of visual impairment or impact on quality of life requiring keratoplasty, treatment decisions may vary among consultants and between studies. As a result, the proportion of cases indicated for keratoplasty may have been either underestimated or overestimated compared with other papers. Eye was used as the unit of analysis in this study. However, because of retrospective study design and limitations of the available dataset, inter-eye correlation could not be adequately accounted for in the analysis. As a result, standard errors may have been underestimated, and the statistical significance of the findings may have been overestimated. Furthermore, stromal dystrophies were analyzed as a single group because of the small sample size in each subtype. Therefore, differences between FECD and individual stromal dystrophy subtypes could not be evaluated separately.

Conclusion

Fuchs Endothelial Corneal Dystrophy was the most common subtype of corneal dystrophy in this tertiary eye hospital cohort. However, the substantial number of incidental and asymptomatic FECD cases may overestimate its clinical value while undermining other corneal dystrophies that have greater potential in interfering with patients’ quality of life. Furthermore, the proportion of cases requiring keratoplasty was higher in CHED and the stromal dystrophy group than in FECD. Through its findings, this study highlights the importance of maintaining vigilance toward FECD while also increasing attention to other dystrophic subtypes. Satisfactory surgical outcomes of performed keratoplasties were observed in the current study, as most grafts retaining clarity at 12-month follow-up. Nevertheless, further refinements in surgical techniques could also be considered to increase the survivability of the graft.

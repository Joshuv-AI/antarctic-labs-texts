Images dataset of pure and adulterated Teff (Eragrostis tef (Zucc.) Trotter) flour with wood flour (sawdust) and gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder for machine learning based identification of Teff flour adulteration and prediction of the level adulteration
Authors: Zekiyos Abayneh Bochera, Solomon Gizaw, Clement Onime
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This data article presents an image dataset compiled for the purpose of machine learning-based identification and prediction of adulteration levels in Teff (Eragrostis tef (Zucc.) Trotter) flour. The dataset includes images of pure white, mixed, and red Teff flour varieties, as well as these flours adulterated with wood flour (sawdust) and gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder. Adulteration levels range from 10% to 40% in 5% increments.The data collection process involved preparing Teff flour from naturally dried and milled Teff grains. Samples of 100 grams of each Teff flour variety were then mixed with the adulterants at the specified concentrations. A total of 5000 raw images were captured using an 18-megapixel Canon EOS 7D camera under controlled studio lighting (300 W incandescent lamps), with the samples placed 30 cm from the camera lens in a 10 cm x 10 cm plastic box. To enhance the dataset's diversity and quantity, 25,000 augmented images were generated by shuffling image pixels' locations with various block sizes (1 × 1, 2 × 2, 4 × 4, 8 × 8, and 16 × 16).This dataset is a valuable resource for researchers and students in Teff adulteration using image processing and feature extraction. It also holds potential for use by Food and Drug Administration Authorities and law enforcement to develop automated methods for detecting Teff flour adulteration, offering an alternative to time-consuming physio-chemical laboratory tests. The dataset's structure and augmentation methods are detailed to ensure reproducibility and encourage further research into robust machine learning models for food quality control.

Background

Teff is an indigenous cereal crop to Ethiopia, a member of the Eragrostis genus and species. The average annual output of Teff is approximately 5 million quintals, according to the Central Statistical Agency's 2019–2022 report [2,3]. Very White Teff Flour ("Maga"), White Teff Flour ("Nech"), Red Teff Flour ("Quey"), and Mixed Teff Flour ("Sergegna") are the four varieties of Teff flour that are sold in the market [4]. Throughout much of Ethiopia's central and northern regions, this widely consumed grain well-dried, and foreign-matter-free ground into flour and utilized as a staple diet [4,5]. Regretfully, gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder and wood flour (sawdust) are frequently used to contaminate Teff flour in Ethiopian markets [6,7]. Food and Drug Administration Authority (FDAA) officials recently arrested people for contaminating injera (the end product of processed Teff flour) with contaminants like sawdust and cancer-causing particles like gypsum (calcium sulfate) and confiscated 21.5 tons of adulterated food items in Ethiopia. They also confiscated >20 barrels of tainted dough and seven quintals of sawdust [6]. To detect adulterated Teff flour requires physio-chemical laboratory testing by professionals; the tests used will vary according on the kind and concentration level of the adulterant. Furthermore, each time the laboratory performs an examination, a specific amount of food must be lost or sampled, which makes the process time-consuming and resource-intensive [6]. However, it possible to identify adulteration and predict contamination levels from digital images of Teff flour utilizing computer vision methodologies with machine learning classifiers.

Imaging and digital image processing is a computer vision methodology, starting from capturing an image an object or scene and transforming it into a digital image using image acquisition devices, to extract a useful information about an object or scenes from the captured digital image [8]. Digital image processing used in many applications area such as medical imaging diagnosis, remote sensing, and food quality inspection, due to its ability to give a very precise information about an object or scene’s textural, color and morphology features with low cost compared to a laboratory testing by expert [8,9,10].

In [11], Mohammad Hossein Nargesi and Kamran Kheiralipour used artificial neural networks to detect sea foam in black pepper, with results of 98.89, 95.67, 95.56, and 98.22 % of classifier the performance of specificity, sensitivity, precision, and accuracy metrics respectively, and using support vector machine for red pepper adulterations 99.46, 98.00, 97.78, and 99.11 %, respectively. As well, using different color and textural features extracted from images of different levels of wheat flour, and chick pea adulterated with black pepper, red pepper, and cinnamon able to achieved classification accuracy rate of 97.8100.00, and 100.00 %, and 97.8, 98.9, and 95.6 % using artificial neural network (ANN), respectively, and using support vector machine (SVM) 93.33, 100.00, and 98.88 %, and 93.33, 97.78 and 92.22 %, respectively [12,13]. Using 7610 textural and color features of this raw image’s datasets, we achieved a 100 % accuracy rate to identify pure and adulterated Teff flours. Whereas, in recognizing the adulteration level of adulterated Teff flour, we obtained a varying level of prediction accuracy rate [1]. Hence, through different types of image data augmentation techniques applied on this image dataset, the diversity and quantity of the training dataset can be enhanced, improves machine learning’s model performance and used by other researchers.

Data Description

This dataset contains images of three (white, red, and mixed) Teff (Eragrostis tef (Zucc.) Trotter) flour varieties both pure and mixed with wood flour (sawdust) in seven levels ranging from 10 % to 40 % in 5 % increments amounts and also mixed with gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder.

The data directory contains a README.md file for the images datasets users guide note in markdown format, an assets folder for storing images used in the README.md file, and a data_statistics.csv file for mapping of class labels, folder names, and number of images in each folder (Fig. 1). And the image datasets are stored in the raw and processed directories, a raw directory for a raw image’s dataset and aprocessed directory for the processed (augmented) image dataset of adulterants and teff flours. The image files of adulterants (wood flour (sawdust) and gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder) are stored in the adulterants directory with their corresponding folder names, wood_flour and gypsum_powder, respectively, under both the raw and processed directories. Furthermore, to show how they looked like, images of pure Teff grains (four image files of each Teff variety grain image prefixed by the names white_teff_grains, mixed_teff_grains, andred_teff_grains) are stored in the teff_grains directory under the raw directory (shown in Fig. 2). File naming conventions

The image files are named based on the Teff variety name with the levels of adulteration, the type of adulterant, and image number stated from 00 to 99 using our file naming conventions: -

[Type of Teff variety]-[Percentage levels of adulterants]_[Type of adulterants]-[Shuffling block size]-[Image Number / Seed value].[JPG]

For example, for the first raw image of red Teff variety flour adulterated with white wood flour of 20 %, its file name is red_teff_flour-20percent_white_wood_flour-00 and for the hundredth raw image of pure red Teff variety flour is just red_teff_flour-99. Also, for the processed (augmented) images, to ensure the reproducibility of the random row and column index result, we append the shuffled block size and seed value number of the random.seed(seed_value) function argument after the type name of adulterant. For example, the filenamemixed_teff_flour-15percent_gypsum_powder-4 × 4–02 is the third image generated by augmenting pixels’ locations shuffling with a block size of 4 × 4 and a 2 seed value as the image number too for mixed Teff variety flour adulterated with 15 % of gypsum powder.

Experimental design

White ("Nech"), Red ("Quey"), and Mixed ("Sergegna") Teff (Eragrostis tef (Zucc.) Trotter) grain varieties are freshly harvested, naturally dried, threshed, threshed grains are then cleaned and separated from other materials like straw and soil, and then processed to remove the husk by the spice shop "Lemelem Baltena" in the Central Ethiopia Regional State the then Southern Nation and Nationality (SNNP), in the East Gurage Zone of Butajera city, and are milled as a customary practice among consumers to create this images dataset as the experimental procedure flow chart to prepare Teff flours illustrated on Fig. 4 [1].Fig. 4The experimental procedure flow chart to prepare Teff flours.Fig 4: dummy alt text

We utilized a total of 100 g for each Teff variety and adulterants (wood flour (sawdust) and gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder), along with adulterant kinds classified into seven categories level of adulteration. In addition to 100 % entirely pure teff flours, the proportion of adulterants’ and pure teff flours is 10 % and 90 %, 15 % and 85 %, 20 % and 80 %, 25 % and 75 %, 30 % and 70 %, 35 % and 65 %, and 40 % and 60 %, respectively [1].

Image data augmentation

Since the Teff flours are mixed well with the adulterant that has a similarity in color with grinded to a very fine Teff flour (powdered) to disguise the adulterant in the foodstuff, the adulterant may be distributed in a random pattern and homogenic. Also, the orderliness of the mixture of the teff flours with wood flour (sawdust) and gypsum (calcium sulfate) powder varies from one adulteration to another based on how it is mixed. Thus, in order to reproduce different types of Teff flour mixture images, we used image data augmentation techniques of image pixels’ locations shuffling with block sizes of 1 × 1 (one pixel), 2 × 2, 4 × 4, 8 × 8 and 16 × 16, while preserving its color palette of the image.

First, we cropped the raw image into 2048 rows by 2028 columns sizes from 1200 to 3248 pixels’ and from 1568 to 3616 pixels’, row offset locations, and column offsets, respectively, to isolate the Teff flour image part. And iterate through the image's pixel elements and generate a random row and column index from the image row and column index lists starting from zero to row and column size of 2028 pixels by stepping block size using Python’s programming language random.choice and random.seed(seed_value) built-in function for each block size [15,16]. Then, we wrote block-sized image data on the augmented image at the randomly selected row and column index pixel locations and removed the generated random row and column indexes from the row and column index lists to avoid overwriting on the same location as per the procedure steps listed in Algorithm 1.

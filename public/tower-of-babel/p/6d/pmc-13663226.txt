A novel endoscopic retroperitoneal approach for debridement in lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis at L4-5: a clinical series and technical note
Authors: Yongrui Yang, Wenkai Ruan, Jianlong Li, Rongpan Dang, Huigang An, Wentao Zhao, Yingxin Zhao, Liang Xu, Hongdong Tan, Huixue Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe L4-5 is one of the most commonly affected levels in lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis. Surgical intervention is typically required when conservative antibiotic therapy fails. For patients with extensive anterior abscess formation or widespread lesions, an anterior approach is often necessary. However, at the L4-5 level, the dense distribution of the lumbar plexus and the close proximity of the iliac vessels to the lesion demand particular caution when selecting an anterior approach. The present study aims to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and preliminary clinical efficacy of a novel endoscopic retroperitoneal approach for debridement combined with posterior percutaneous pedicle screw fixation in the treatment of lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis at the L4–5 level.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed patients with L4-5 lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis who underwent endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement at our institution between July 2022 and July 2024. Baseline patient characteristics, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative lesion clearance, changes in inflammatory markers(e.g, C-reactive protein[CRP] and erythrocyte sedimentation rate[ESR]), complication rates, Visual analog scale (VAS) scores for back pain, Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores, kyphotic angle changes at the infected level, and radiological follow-up outcomes were recorded.ResultsOf the 28 patients, 27 (27/28, 96.43%) showed improvement in clinical symptoms. During follow-up, all patients demonstrated significant improvements in VAS scores and ODI scores compared to preoperative values (p<0.05). At the final follow-up, all patients exhibited a kyphotic angle change of less than 8°, and no spinal instability was observed. Computed tomography (CT) at the 12-month follow-up demonstrated intervertebral bone fusion in 26 cases (26/28, 92.86%). Postoperative inflammatory markers showed improved compared with preoperative levels (p<0.001). No infection recurrence or serious surgery-related complications were observed during the postoperative follow-up period.ConclusionEndoscopic retroperitoneal debridement combined with posterior percutaneous pedicle screw fixation appears to be a safe and effective minimally invasive approach for treating L4-5 lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis. However, long-term efficacy requires further validation through prospective studies with larger sample sizes and extended follow-up periods.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12891-026-10018-5.

Introduction

Lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis is a debilitating condition characterized by inflammation and infection of the intervertebral disc and adjacent vertebral bodies, predominantly affecting lower lumbar segments such as L4-5 due to their biomechanical vulnerability and abundant vascular supply [1, 2]. The pathology typically results from hematogenous dissemination of bacteria, with Staphylococcus aureus identified as the most common pathogen. Untreated, it may lead to severe back pain, neurological deficits, and potential spinal instability [3].

Traditional management of lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis has evolved from conservative antibiotic therapy to various surgical interventions. Nevertheless, significant challenges persist in achieving thorough debridement while simultaneously minimizing tissue trauma and maintaining spinal stability. For early-stage cases without neurological compromise or abscess formation, conservative treatment involving prolonged intravenous antibiotics constitutes the first-line approach [4]. In refractory or advanced cases, surgical debridement is essential to eradicate infection, decompress neural elements, and restore spinal alignment [5].

As we all known, oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) technique is a popular lateral fusion procedure through prepsoas approach. This technique was first reported by Mayer in 1997 and formally named by Silvestre in 2012 [6, 7]. OLIF chooses an oblique approach between the psoas major muscle and the major vessels, without splitting the psoas major muscle, which is less likely to interfere with lumbar plexus. However, due to challenges in exposure and limited surgical visualization, there is a potential risk of injury to the sympathetic trunk, ureter, vessels, and the peritoneum adjacent to the approach [8].

In recent years, with advancements in minimally invasive techniques, endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement has garnered increasing attention. In 1991, Obenchain [9] first reported the use of laparoscopic techniques for lumbar spine surgery, primarily for discectomy. In 1996, Regan et al. [10] expanded the application of laparoscopy to the field of lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis, performing abscess drainage and interbody fusion via a transperitoneal approach.

This study aims to systematically describe the technique and evaluate the clinical efficacy of endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement combined with posterior percutaneous pedicle screw fixation for treating L4-5 lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis, with the goal of providing evidence to address the therapeutic challenges associated with this condition.

Materials and methods

This study was approved by the ethics committee of Shandong Public Health Clinical Center, Shandong University (approval number K2025137). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Patient population

Patients in this study were selected according to the following inclusion and exclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria: (1) Diagnosis of lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis confirmed by computed tomography (CT)-guided biopsy; (2) L4-5 lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis with or without psoas abscess; (3) Early-stage infection with inadequate response to conservative treatment; (4) Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showing high signal intensity in the intervertebral disc on T2-weighted images and/or varying degrees of destruction in the adjacent vertebral endplates; (5) Primary lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis. In clinical practice, we also observed that psoas abscess facilitates surgical access to the infected disc space.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Incomplete clinical data or missing follow-up records; (2) Contraindications to surgery or refusal of surgical treatment; (3) Asymptomatic patients; (4) Complete paralysis of both lower limbs; (5) Spinal deformity or instability; (6) Retroperitoneal surgical history; (7) Patients involving non-L4/5 levels or multisegmental involvement. Patients with spinal deformity or instability were excluded. This exclusion criterion was applied because our surgical approach involved initial endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement followed by staged posterior lumbar percutaneous pedicle screw fixation. In such patients, we were concerned that performing endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement first might precipitate severe complications due to underlying spinal instability.

Preoperative CT-guided lesion biopsy confirmed the causative pathogens in all patients. Additionally, the following data were recorded: species of infection, antibiotic use, levels of CRP and ESR on admission, at 1 month postoperatively, and at 3 months postoperatively, preoperative and final follow-up Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores [11] for back pain, Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores [12] and kyphotic angle changes, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative drainage volume, timing of drain removal, postoperative hospital stay and postoperative complications. Patients with spinal deformity or instability were excluded. Detailed information is presented in (Tables 1, 2 and 3).

Surgical technique

Patient positioning

The patient is placed in the right lateral decubitus position, with the left side up and the lower extremities flexed to relax the psoas muscle. In both anteroposterior (AP) and lateral fluoroscopic views, the vertebral endplates should appear as a single line. In the AP view, the bilateral pedicle shadows should be equidistant from the spinous process; in the lateral view, the pedicles should be completely superimposed.

Localization and incisions

Under lateral X-ray fluoroscopy, the contours of the L4 and L5 vertebral bodies are marked on the skin. A 1-cm oblique incision (retraction incision, used for psoas major muscle retraction) is made at the intersection of the L4/5 disc level line and a point 1.5 cm posterior to the vertebral body wall. Blunt finger dissection is performed to enter the retroperitoneal space, and a self-made balloon is inserted to create a working cavity (500–600 mL). Additional 1-cm oblique incisions are created at the anterosuperior corner of L4 (endoscopic portal), at the intersection of the L4/5 disc level line and a point 1.5 cm anterior to the vertebral body wall (main working portal), and at the anteroinferior corner of L5 (accessory portal), followed by insertion of trocars. The balloon is then removed, additional trocars are placed, and pneumo-retroperitoneum is established.

Exposure

Retroperitoneal fat is partially removed to expose the outline of the psoas major muscle. The relationship between the anterior border of the psoas major muscle and the iliac vessels is confirmed. At the level of the diseased intervertebral disc, the psoas major muscle is retracted posteriorly to the posterior vertebral margin, thereby transversely exposing the intervertebral disc space and the adjacent vertebral body edges. X-ray fluoroscopy is repeated to reconfirm the L4/5 disc level.

Lesion debridement.

A curette is used to remove the lesion. In cases with significant necrotic bone formation or large bony defects, a osteotome may be required for thorough debridement (extreme caution must be exercised at this stage to avoid injury to the anteriorly located iliac vessels). The bone bed is then prepared.

Bone grafting

The surgical field is thoroughly irrigated with saline. Allogeneic bone graft is placed into the prepared bone bed and firmly impacted. Hemostatic gauze is applied to cover the surgical site, a drainage tube is inserted, and all incisions are closed in layers. The procedure is then concluded (Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 23.0. Descriptive data are presented as the means and Standard Deviations (SDs). Student’s t tests were used to compare continuous variables when the data were continuous, normally distributed, and homoscedastic. Chi-square tests and Fisher’s exact tests were applied to assess differences in categorical variables. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

This study ultimately included 28 patients with L4-5 lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis who underwent retroperitoneoscopic debridement between July 2022 and July 2024. All patients underwent posterior percutaneous pedicle screw fixation of the lumbar spine postoperatively. The cohort consisted of 12 males (12/28, 42.86%) and 16 females (16/28, 57.14%), with a mean age of 56.9 ± 12.7 years. Species including Staphylococcus aureus in 6 cases (6/28, 21.43%), Mycobacterium tuberculosis and Escherichia coli each in 5 cases (5/28, 17.86%), Klebsiella pneumoniae and Brucella each in 3 cases (3/28, 10.71%), Streptococcus agalactiae in 2 cases (2/28, 7.14%), Streptococcus intermedius, Candida albicans Staphylococcus epidermidis and Prevotella bivia each in 1 case (1/28, 3.57%) (Table 1).

Additionally, the mean operative time was 91.61 ± 8.39 min (range, 75–105 min), mean intraoperative blood loss was 27.68 ± 8.11 mL (range,15–50 mL), mean postoperative drainage volume was 54.54 ± 22.50 mL (range,20–100 mL), mean time to drain removal was 5.00 ± 1.15 days (range,3–7 days), and mean postoperative hospital stay was 10.57 ± 2.30 days(range, 8–16 days). All patients were followed for a minimum of 12 months, with a mean follow-up of 13.57 ± 1.53 months (range,12–16 months). During postoperative follow-up, one patient experienced transient sensory abnormalities in the affected lumbar segment, which resolved spontaneously within 2 weeks. No recurrence of infection or serious surgery-related complications was observed in any patient during the follow-up period (Table 2).

This study also found that postoperative CRP and ESR levels were significantly reduced compared to preoperative values (p < 0.001). In addition to surgical intervention, these improvements likely reflect the standardized anti-infective treatment administered to the patients. Furthermore, VAS and ODI scores demonstrated significant improvements in lumbar pain and function, which remained stable throughout the follow-up period (p < 0.05). No signs of spinal instability were observed in any cases during follow-up, and the mean kyphotic angle increased by 1.07°. At 3 months postoperatively, successful fusion was achieved in 6 patients. By the final follow-up, a total of 26 patients demonstrated successful interbody fusion (Table 3).

Discussion

The L4-5 is one of the most commonly affected levels in lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis. Due to its unique anatomical characteristics, clinical management of infections at this level remains relatively complex [14]. In the early stages of anterior surgery for L4-5, OLIF was the predominant approach [15]. This technique utilizes a small oblique lateral incision of approximately 3–5 cm and accesses the disc space through the natural avascular plane between the psoas major muscle and the major vessels, without requiring psoas major muscle splitting. One of the primary technical challenges in OLIF surgery lies in adequate surgical field exposure. Currently, the majority of OLIF procedures employ tubular retractors to establish the working channel, which typically has a diameter of about 3 cm [16]. This narrow corridor presents two major limitations: First, the restricted channel allows only the primary surgeon to obtain a clear view of the operative field, resulting in a steep learning curve for OLIF that requires substantial surgical experience to achieve proficiency. Second, the confined working channel limits light transmission and provides suboptimal direct visualization. Compared with endoscopic techniques, this may compromise the thoroughness of deep debridement [17, 18].

Furthermore, in the majority of individuals, the aortic bifurcation is located at the lower endplate of the L4 vertebral body or at the level of the L4/5 intervertebral disc, whereas the left common iliac vein typically joins the inferior vena cava at the L4-5 or L5-S1 level [19] (Fig. 2). Arteries are readily identifiable intraoperatively due to their pulsatile nature and thicker vessel walls, rendering them less prone to injury. In contrast, the common iliac veins exhibit minimal pulsation and possess thinner walls, making them difficult to discern during surgery. Moreover, these veins are highly susceptible to tearing upon retraction, which can result in severe hemorrhage.The incidence of vascular injury has been reported to range from 0% to 18% in the literature [20–22]. Consequently, a clear and well-illuminated surgical field is of paramount importance for adequate exposure at the L4/5 segment.

In contrast to conventional approaches, the endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement employed in this study establishes a spacious and well-illuminated surgical field through CO₂-induced pneumoperitoneum combined with flexible endoscopic instruments. This enables more thorough lesion clearance without the need for extensive tissue exposure [23] (Fig. 3). Furthermore, compared with traditional lateral or anterior approaches, the endoscopic retroperitoneal method is markedly less invasive, requiring only a 1.5-cm incision. Unlike techniques that necessitate muscle splitting or transection, this approach relies solely on pneumoperitoneum and gentle blunt dissection of the psoas muscle, thereby minimizing tissue trauma to the greatest extent possible and facilitating rapid postoperative recovery [24, 25].

Compared with transforaminal endoscopic debridement, endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement provides direct access to the anterior spinal column and the infected intervertebral disc space. This approach enables more thorough debridement of anteriorly located lesions and effective drainage of any associated psoas abscess. Furthermore, it offers superior visualization and handling of lesions situated in close proximity to, or adherent to, the major anterior vessels.

Apart from vascular injury, common complications associated with anterior lumbar surgery include vertebral body collapse, postoperative ileus, nerve injury, and ureteral injury [26–28]. In the present study, no significant intraoperative complications were observed. Only one patient experienced transient postoperative sensory disturbance, which had fully resolved by the end of follow-up. This low incidence of complications may be attributable to three primary factors: First, as a minimally invasive procedure, the operative duration is substantially shortened, thereby markedly reducing the patient’s exposure to anesthesia. Second, the limited intraoperative trauma results in decreased blood loss, contributing to the maintenance of hemodynamic stability. Third, the rapid postoperative recovery enables early mobilization and ambulation, which helps minimize complications associated with prolonged bed rest.

From a clinical perspective, in this study, the mean operative time was 91.61 ± 8.39 min, intraoperative blood loss was 27.68 ± 8.11 mL, and the mean postoperative length of hospital stay was 10.57 ± 2.30 days. All of these parameters were significantly lower than those reported for traditional open surgery. Tang et al. [29] reported that endoscopic debridement can substantially shorten operative time and reduce intraoperative blood loss, thereby facilitating faster recovery and shorter hospitalization. Furthermore, this study demonstrated significant improvement in patient symptoms, as assessed by the VAS and ODI scores. The VAS and ODI scores decreased from preoperative values of 5.93 ± 1.74 and 40.25 ± 3.30 to postoperative values of 2.86 ± 0.80 and 8.04 ± 2.55.

Endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement not only enables thorough clearance of the infectious lesion but also provides ample space for the insertion of an interbody fusion cage (Figs. 4 and 5). In this study, 28 patients underwent anterior allograft bone grafting combined with posterior percutaneous instrumentation. Follow-up results demonstrated that the majority of patients (26/28, 92.86%) achieved solid bony fusion (Table 4). The two cases in which fusion was not observed may be attributable to insufficient follow-up duration rather than failure of the fusion process. These findings indicate that endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement combined with allogeneic bone grafting and posterior percutaneous fixation can effectively promote stable bony fusion.

Surgical characteristics

This study has several notable limitations that temper the generalizability of its findings. First, it was conducted at a single institution with a relatively small sample size (n = 28), which limits statistical power and increases the risk of selection bias. Second, as a retrospective analysis without a concurrent control group, it lacks direct head-to-head comparison with other established approaches, such as OLIF or extreme lateral interbody fusion (XLIF).

Conclusion

Endoscopic retroperitoneal debridement combined with posterior percutaneous pedicle screw fixation appears to be a safe and effective minimally invasive approach for L4-5 lumbar infectious spondylodiscitis. This technique enables effective infection control, pain relief, and spinal reconstruction, while demonstrating a very low incidence of complications.

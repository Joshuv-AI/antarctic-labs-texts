Serum Lipids and Glaucoma
Authors: Atsuya Miki
Published: 2026

See article vol. 33: 1261-1271

Glaucoma is the leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide. Pathologically, glaucoma is characterized by an intraocular pressure (IOP)-dependent, chronic, progressive loss of retinal ganglion cells 1) . Since retinal ganglion cell loss is irreversible, the only proven therapeutic strategy is the reduction of IOP to slow the rate of retinal ganglion cell axon (optic nerve) degeneration. Therefore, an early diagnosis and timely intervention are essential for preventing the visual impairment caused by glaucoma. Given that glaucoma is typically asymptomatic in its early stages, identifying the risk factors for glaucoma is critical for both early detection and the optimization of individual treatment paradigms.

While several systemic risk factors for both glaucoma and an elevated IOP have been reported, such as older age, hypertension, and atherosclerosis 2) , the role of the blood lipid levels remains a subject of ongoing debate. In this issue, Sato, Hanyuda, and colleagues 3) reported new evidence from the Japan Public Health Center-based Prospective Study for the Next Generation (JPHC-NEXT), a population-based cohort designed to investigate the role of lifestyle and environmental factors in chronic diseases 4) . Analyzing 6786 participants, the authors found that all three major lipid fractions, low-density lipoprotein (LDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), and triglycerides, were positively correlated with the IOP. The positive association between higher serum lipid levels and a higher IOP has already been reported in a predominantly European population (e.g., the UK Biobank) 5) . The current study extends the results of this previous work to the East Asian population. This is important because glaucoma phenotypes differ substantially by ethnicity. By replicating these findings in a different ethnic context, the authors helped establish the serum lipid levels as a more universal risk factor for an elevated IOP.

Beyond its potential role as a risk factor for improving early detection, the serum lipids are also promising treatment targets. The lipid profiles are routinely assessed during standard health check-ups and they are easily managed through lifestyle changes or pharmacotherapy. There have been several reports on statin use and a decreased risk of developing glaucoma 6) . Further clarification of the association between the serum lipids and IOP may potentially lead to novel treatments for glaucoma beyond IOP reduction.

Although Sato et al. replicated the results of previous studies, there were several disagreements in more detailed analyses. Most notably, they found a positive association between triglyceride and IOP, whereas a previous European study found a negative association 5) . This can be a racial difference, but further studies are necessary to clarify the association between triglyceride and IOP.

In summary, the work by Sato and colleagues provides vital information to improve our understanding of the association between the serum lipid levels and IOP, which may ultimately lead to an improvement of the early detection and better management of a potentially blinding disease.

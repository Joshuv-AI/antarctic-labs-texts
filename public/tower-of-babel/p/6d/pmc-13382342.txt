Cognitive benefits of a lifestyle-induced prediabetes remission intervention in Canada
Authors: Christopher Gudas-Cantin, Valérie Dionne, Mathieu Gayda, Daniel Gagnon, Marie Ouellet, Louis Bherer, Christine Gagnon, Josep Iglesies-Grau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo assess the potential for preventing or reversing the cognitive decline associated with prediabetes and coronary heart disease.MethodsIn the DIABEPIC-1 feasibility study, participants with coronary heart disease and prediabetes (HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4%) underwent a 6-month multidisciplinary enhanced cardiac rehabilitation intervention in Canada in 2023. Cognitive performance was assessed at baseline and six months with the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), Rey Auditory Learning Verbal Test (RAVLT), Digit Symbol Substitution Test (DSST), Trail Making Test (TMT), Stroop test, and Verbal Fluency (VFT) test. To account for practice effects in the absence of a control group, standardized regression-based (SRB) formulas and reliable change indexes (RCI) were applied.ResultsThirty participants completed the protocol. Significant improvements were observed in global cognition (MoCA), memory (RAVLT total learning and delayed recall), processing speed (DSST), and executive function (TMT–B, Stroop inhibition and switching, semantic VFT). SRB and RCI analyses showed some participants exceeded expected practice effects in certain cognitive domains. Weight loss was associated with improved processing speed, and reductions in HbA1c were linked to gains in processing speed. Participants achieving prediabetes remission showed greater cognitive improvement.ConclusionsThese findings suggest potential cognitive benefits of prediabetes remission and support further investigation.

Introduction

Prediabetes and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) are major public health issues in Canada, affecting 11.7 million Canadians living with these conditions and accounting for 7273 deaths in 2023, making it the seventh leading cause of death (Statistics Canada, 2024). Their complications are numerous and life-threatening, including myocardial infarction, stroke, retinopathy, peripheral neuropathy, and chronic kidney disease. Among their less recognized but equally concerning complications is cognitive dysfunction, which ranges from subtle cognitive deficits to increased risks of mild cognitive impairment and dementia (McCrimmon et al., 2012). Executive functions (i.e., learning, decision-making, cognitive control, and goal-directed behavior) and processing speed appear to be the cognitive spheres most affected by glucose intolerance (Antal et al., 2022). These spheres of cognition, as well as memory, are also most affected by cardiovascular disease (such as coronary heart disease), often seen in patients with prediabetes (Gagnon et al., 2022a; Gagnon and Bherer, 2023). The pathophysiology of T2DM's impact on cognition is not fully understood. Still, it appears to be multifactorial, with studies suggesting that the effects of insulin resistance, metabolic disturbance, and cerebral microvascular dysfunction (Sebastian et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2022) play a role. A recent study found that deficits in cognitive performance in individuals with glycemic dysregulation were accompanied by gray matter atrophy, which was most prominent in the ventral striatum, a region involved in executive functions (Antal et al., 2022). Atrophy in all brain regions correlated with disease duration, highlighting the importance of acting before the advent of T2DM and its complications, and of intervening in individuals with prediabetes. Finally, while it has been established that glucose intolerance at all levels adversely affects cognitive performance and accelerates cognitive decline, only a limited number of studies have explored the potential for reversing this decline.

The DIABEPIC-1 pilot study was designed to determine whether remission of prediabetes was achievable through an intensive cardiac rehabilitation program and to evaluate the relationship between these metabolic improvements and cognitive performance (Iglesies-Grau et al., 2023a). This secondary analysis of the DIABEPIC-1 data aimed to assess changes in cognitive performance in response to an enhanced six-month cardiac rehabilitation intervention and to investigate the relationship between metabolic improvements—specifically in individuals with prediabetes and prediabetes remission—and cognitive performance. The primary objective was to determine whether cognitive performance improved following the intervention and whether these improvements could be attributed to the intervention itself, rather than learning effects, given that all participants completed the protocol (no control group). Secondary objectives included examining the influence of protocol compliance on cognitive outcomes and exploring the association between metabolic improvements (weight, HbA1c, and waist circumference), commonly monitored by physicians, and change in cognitive performance.

Methods

Study design and population

The DIABEPIC-1 pilot study was conducted at the Preventive Medicine and Physical Activity (EPIC) Center of the Montreal Heart Institute (MHI) in Canada in 2023. The study protocol was approved by MHI's Research Ethics Board (#2022-3005) and registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05459987). All eligible participants provided written informed consent before participating in the study.

Participants were recruited for cardiac rehabilitation from the MHI if they had stable angina, experienced an acute coronary event, underwent coronary revascularization, or had bypass surgery. Participants were included at the beginning of the cardiac rehabilitation program if they were 40 years or older and had a diagnosis of prediabetes within the last six months, as determined by HBA1C levels between 5.7% and 6.4%, according to the American Diabetes Association guidelines (Diabetes 2 Care, 2019). Exclusion criteria included contraindications to exercise testing and/or physical training, patients with T2DM (HbA1c ≥ 6.5%) or HbA1c ≥ 5.7% to 6.4% with the use of oral hypoglycemic agents, patients taking medications known to cause weight gain, such as psychotropic or cortisone, or taking recently introduced weight-loss medications, such as GLP-1 agonists.

As described elsewhere, the intervention consisted of a comprehensive, multidisciplinary approach spanning six months, with an emphasis on physical activity, nutrition, and education (Iglesies-Grau et al., 2024). Participants engaged in supervised aerobic and resistance training three times a week. Nutritional counseling focused on a novel intervention to reduce the intake of ultra-processed foods, adopting a Mediterranean-style diet, and implementing a time-restricted eating strategy. Additionally, educational sessions provided insights into insulin resistance, prediabetes, evidence-based methods for remission, and the significance of body composition. Compliance with the protocol was defined in this analysis as attendance at the 54 prescribed clinical appointments with healthcare professionals.

After the intervention, mean weight loss was −8.0 kg (95% confidence interval (CI) [−6.3, −9.7]), and mean waist circumference reduction was −9.2 cm (95% CI [−7.4, −10.9]) (Iglesies-Grau et al., 2024). During the study, 50% of participants achieved remission of prediabetes, while none progressed to T2DM.

Measures

Cognitive assessment was performed at baseline and six months and aimed to evaluate several domains of cognition. The tests were performed by a neuropsychologist or trained doctoral students in neuropsychology, either in person or via video conference, which has been shown to be adequate for the tests conducted in this study (Gagnon et al., 2022b). A total of six cognitive tests (many of which had subparts) were performed.

The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) is a screening tool that assesses global cognitive functioning. The test is scored out of 30. The Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT) assesses episodic memory and was divided into two results: the RAVLT total learning, which represents the sum of the five learning trials of 15-word list (total of freely recalled words on 75), and the RAVLT delayed recall, which represents the number of words that are recalled after a 30 min period. The Digit Symbol Substitution Test (DSST) assesses psychomotor and processing speed, consisting of digit-symbol pairs and a sheet with digits, on which participants must write down the corresponding symbol within a given time. The score represents the total number of correctly drawn symbols within a two-minute period. The Trail Making Test (TMT) consists of two tests: TMT A, which represents the time taken to connect numbers from 1 to 25 on a sheet and assesses processing speed, and TMT B, which represents the time taken to connect in alternation numbers and letters on a sheet and assesses switching (executive functions). The Stroop test is divided into four tasks: color naming, reading, inhibition, and switching. The first two conditions assess processing speed, while the inhibition and switching conditions assess executive functions. The outcome score of the Stroop test is the execution time (in seconds). Finally, the D-KEFS Verbal Fluency test (VFT) is divided into three parts (phonemic, semantic, and switching) that all assess executive functions. The outcome score is the number of correct words evoked in 60 s during the given condition (Kreutzer et al., n.d.; Stebbins, 2007).

For speeded tasks (TMT and Stroop), a lower score indicates better performance. On the other tests, where the number of correct answers is counted (MoCA, RAVLT, DSST, VFT), higher scores indicate better performance.

Statistical analysis

The analyses primarily consisted of descriptive statistics and linear regression models. Summary statistics describing baseline characteristics and changes across time are presented as means ± standard deviation (SD) or median [interquartile range] as appropriate for continuous variables and as counts and frequencies for categorical variables. For the main objective, a repeated-measures ANOVA was used to assess the difference between test scores at six months and baseline. To quantify learning effects, the reliable change index (RCI) and standard regression-based (SRB) formulas, which yield z-scores, were used. The RCI is calculated by comparing the variation in a participant's test scores at two time points with results from a control sample (Duff, 2012). SRB formulas are multiple regression-based formulas that compare the expected score at Time B to the actual score based on the participant's score at Time A and a control sample (Duff, 2012). Such methods of estimating learning effects have been used in many studies to assess cognitive improvement/decline in participants (Guevara et al., 2023; Hammers et al., 2022; DesRuisseaux et al., 2025), which are based on the formulas devised by Massen and colleagues (Maassen et al., 2006). RCI were calculated for the RAVLT delayed recall test as well as the Stroop tests according to the following formula: (T2-T1)/SED (standard error of difference) (Duff, 2012; Jacobson and Truax, 1991). SRB formulas were used for the MoCA, TMT A, TMT B, and VFT animals (a section of the VFT phonemic) according to Duff and colleagues' equations (Duff et al., 2010). The development sample for these formulas included cognitively intact patients (compared to our participants with prediabetes and coronary heart disease) and is presented in Supplementary Table 1. For the purposes of this analysis, a z-score threshold of ±1.65 was considered a significant change, which is what has been used in the literature (Duff, 2012; Guevara et al., 2023). Therefore, a z-score ≥ 1.65 indicated significant improvement and ≤ −1.65 indicates significant decline.

For our secondary objectives, a mixed linear regression was first used to determine whether compliance with the protocol's interventions predicted the improvement in cognitive test scores. We then applied a multiple linear regression analysis to determine the association between metabolic interventions and cognitive test scores. The independent variables were changes in HbA1c, weight, and waist circumference, and the dependent variable was the variation in cognitive test scores. For all linear regressions, analyses were controlled for age, sex, years of education, and baseline test scores. Finally, we applied logistic regression to calculate odds ratios (ORs) and determine the association between prediabetes remission and test score improvements. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value <0.05, and results are presented with a 95% CI when possible. All analyses were performed by IBM SPSS 29.0.2.0 (IBM Corp., 2023, Armonk, NY).

The study protocol was approved by our institution's Research Ethics Board and all eligible participants provided written informed consent before participating in the study. The study met our institution's guidelines for the protection of human subjects concerning safety and privacy.

Results

Out of the 36 participants in the study, 30 completed the entire protocol and neuropsychological assessment and were therefore included in this analysis. Out of the 30 participants, 9 (30%) were female and 21 (70%) were male, the mean age was 62 (SD, 9) years, and the mean years of education was 14.60 (SD, 3.40). A total of 13 (43%) participants met obesity criteria (baseline body mass index ≥30 kg/m2) and 11 (37%) met overweight criteria (baseline body mass index ≥25 kg/m2). The mean HBA1C at baseline was 5.9 (SD, 0.2) %.

Secondary objectives

The mean compliance rate for all 54 scheduled appointments was 92.50% (95% CI [83.30%, 100.00%]). Compliance with appointments was significantly associated with changes in TMT B test scores among participants (Estimate: -2.08; 95% CI [−3.79, −0.36]). Compliance with appointments also showed trends towards improved scores for Stroop reading (Estimate: -0.22; 95% CI [−0.46, −0.02]) and phonemic VFT (Estimate: 0.47; 95% CI [−0.02, 0.96]). All other associations between compliance and test scores were not statistically significant.

Post-intervention changes in metabolic parameters were also significantly associated with changes in cognitive performance. Multiple linear regression revealed that weight loss was significantly associated with improvements in TMT A, DSST, Stroop color, and Stroop reading test scores, and that HBA1C reduction was significantly associated with improvements in DSST test scores. Discussion

The results from the DIABEPIC-1 pilot study show that a multidisciplinary lifestyle intervention designed to reverse prediabetes was associated with improvements in cognitive performance among individuals with prediabetes and coronary heart disease, across various cognitive domains, including global cognition, episodic memory, processing speed, and executive functions. These cognitive gains were closely linked to favorable metabolic changes induced by the intervention, as weight loss and a reduction in HbA1c levels were associated with improved processing speed. Furthermore, participants who achieved prediabetes remission tended to show greater improvements in processing speed. These results highlight the potential of a lifestyle intervention targeting cardiometabolic health to mitigate cognitive decline and enhance cognitive performance, providing new insights into how metabolic health impacts brain function. They provide evidence for the broader benefits of lifestyle interventions, informing future clinical approaches for management of both metabolic and cognitive health in this population.

Physical activity has a protective effect on the brain and cognitive function in older adults (Domingos et al., 2021; Blondell et al., 2014). Recent studies have also shown that physical activity in individuals with T2DM was beneficial for improving their cognitive function (Wang et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2022). The physical exercise prescription of our intervention was likely a key factor in these improvements. However, cardiac rehabilitation programs based on physical activity typically yield approximately 2 kg of weight loss, whereas our intervention led to weight loss of 8.0 kg and a 50% prediabetes remission rate (Bellicha et al., 2021). The magnitude of these metabolic changes suggests that the nutritional component of our intervention also played a substantial role in the reported cognitive improvements. It emphasized adherence to a Mediterranean-style diet, reduction of ultra-processed food intake, and implementation of time-restricted eating.

Our findings indicate that the multidisciplinary lifestyle intervention resulted in significant improvements in cognitive performance across all domains of cognition, including global cognition, episodic memory, processing speed, and executive functions. Due to the absence of a control group, specific indicators were calculated to account for potential learning effects, specifically SRB and RCI. SRB and RCI analyses suggest that these changes exceeded expected practice effects in several domains for certain participants. For example, 33% of participants showed significant improvement in RAVLT delayed recall, 23% in VFT animals, 13% in MoCA, and 11% in Stroop inhibition. However, certain domains, such as task-switching (TMT B and Stroop switching), showed no significant changes. These findings suggest that the intervention had a broad but variable impact on cognitive performance, with more pronounced effects in specific cognitive domains. Although the precise magnitude of this effect cannot be quantified at this time, the use of SRB and RCI analyses supports the likelihood of a true intervention-related impact. These associations, however, remain preliminary and should be interpreted with caution.

Our results show that compliance with prescribed appointments was not a key factor in explaining participants' cognitive improvements, as only one test's (TMT B) improvement was significantly associated with compliance. It is worth noting that the compliance rate was particularly high, which may explain why no additional associations were found. On the other hand, our results indicate that metabolic changes were a key factor in improving cognitive performance, with weight loss being the primary factor, followed by a reduction in HbA1c levels. Weight loss was significantly associated with improved TMT A, DSST, Stroop color and word test scores, all of which assess processing speed. HbA1c reduction was significantly associated with improvements in DSST scores, which assess processing speed. There were non-significant trends between HBA1C and Stroop inhibition and VFT switching scores, which assess executive functions. Changes in waist circumference were not associated with changes in cognitive performance in this current analysis. Previous studies have found associations between obesity (and excess adipose tissue) and the advent of cognitive impairment (Dye et al., 2017; Prickett et al., 2015; Agustí et al., 2018), which could be explained by obesity-associated neuro-inflammatory processes and gray matter atrophy (Guillemot-Legris and Muccioli, 2017). In our study, weight loss was significantly associated with an improvement in processing speed, whereas it had no effect on memory and executive functions.

Lower HbA1c levels have been associated with improved cognitive performance in participants with prediabetes and T2DM (Lin et al., 2023; Luchsinger et al., 2011). A study by Makino and colleagues also found that elevated levels of HbA1c were associated with decreased odds of reversing mild cognitive impairment to normal cognition (Makino et al., 2021). Nonetheless, the specific impact of achieving remission of prediabetes has been poorly studied. In our study, a decrease in HbA1c levels was associated with an improvement in processing speed (with trends for executive functions, though not significant). Though not all these associations were statistically significant, it is interesting to note that greater HbA1c control could have a positive impact on broader domains of cognition compared to weight loss. Additionally, achieving remission from prediabetes was significantly linked to improved processing speed (measured by the TMT A, a test not significantly associated with HbA1c reduction alone), with trends indicating potential benefits for executive functions. This suggests an added cognitive advantage from remission itself, regardless of the degree of HbA1c reduction, further emphasizing the importance of targeting remission as a key goal in lifestyle interventions. However, since participants who achieved prediabetes remission in our study also experienced the largest HbA1c reductions, it is currently unclear whether the positive effects are driven by the extent of HbA1c reduction or by reaching remission (a target HbA1c below 5.7%). Further research is obviously warranted, but these results are encouraging for individuals living with prediabetes.

Cognitive dysfunction is a devastating and often insidious complication that can greatly affect patients' day-to-day activities. Although our findings are preliminary, they underscore important considerations in the clinical management of prediabetes and provide further rationale for investigating cognitive improvement as a clinically relevant outcome associated with achieving remission, beyond the established metabolic benefits. Importantly, we also demonstrated that weight and HbA1c appear to be key factors in achieving these improvements, which are parameters readily available to physicians in their clinical practice. This reinforces the potential for physicians to use cognitive health as an additional, tangible motivator when counseling patients on the empowering benefits of a non-pharmacological lifestyle intervention, as well as the added benefits of achieving remission of prediabetes. Furthermore, the intervention tested in this study—though implemented in a research setting—is transferable to real-world clinical environments; we have previously reported less intensive protocols than this one that have nevertheless achieved similar, albeit yet considerable, results for participants (Iglesies-Grau et al., 2023b; Gudas-Cantin et al., 2025). Finally, our encouraging results also add to the limited but growing body of evidence on cognitive outcomes in individuals with prediabetes, further supporting the need for dedicated research in this field.

Although these preliminary associations are encouraging for individuals with prediabetes, it is essential to note that this is a pilot study with a limited sample size (N = 30). There were also a few problems with data collection for certain paper-and-pencil tests due to the remote administration of the neuropsychological assessment for some participants, which explains why certain tests (TMT and DSST) have fewer than 30 results. We also acknowledge the absence of a control group for this study, which would have been beneficial for seeing the natural evolution of participants' cognitive function and for controlling potential learning effects. Furthermore, while SRB and RCI analyses provide an estimate of whether observed changes exceeded expected retest effects based on available normative data, we recognize that the magnitude of practice effects may differ across populations. Despite these limitations, the observed associations offer a valuable signal and justify larger, controlled studies to explore further the cognitive effects of lifestyle interventions and the potential role of achieving prediabetes remission as a meaningful clinical outcome.

Conclusion

This study presents the cognitive performance outcomes of 30 participants with prediabetes enrolled in the DIABEPIC-1 pilot study. Following a six-month multidisciplinary lifestyle intervention combining nutritional strategies and structured physical activity, participants showed significant improvements across all tested domains of cognition (global function, memory, processing speed, and executive functions). Specifically, weight loss was associated with improvements in processing speed, and HbA1c reduction (including prediabetes remission) was associated with improvements in both processing speed and executive functions. These findings suggest that the intervention was effective not only in improving metabolic health and achieving remission in 50% of participants, but also in improving cognitive decline typically observed over time during the progression from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes. Ultimately, these findings, if replicated in large samples and/or in a randomized control trial design, can be used to motivate individuals with prediabetes to make and sustain meaningful lifestyle changes that support both metabolic and cognitive health.

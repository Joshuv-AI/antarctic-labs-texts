Test pattern optimization scheme based on Hybrid Ant Colony Optimization
Authors: S Asha Pon, V Jeyalakshmi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe trend toward device miniaturization has made digital circuit testing both essential and increasingly complex. To achieve complete fault coverage, a large number of test patterns are applied, which leads to increased switching activity due to larger number of transitions between consecutive patterns. This rise in switching activity significantly elevates power consumption during testing. Therefore, minimizing transitions between test patterns is crucial for reducing test power. Optimizing test patterns by reducing their count and reordering them is an effective approach to lower switching activity. In this paper, Hybrid Ant Colony Optimization technique is proposed to reduce power consumption during sequential circuit testing. This approach integrates traditional Ant Colony Optimization with Kullback-Leibler divergence and Prim’s algorithm. The combination of Ant Colony Optimization and Kullback-Leibler divergence helps in reducing the number of test patterns based on the probability of ant traversal. The resulting patterns are then reordered using Prim’s algorithm to minimize transitions between them. Experimental evaluation reveals that the proposed methodology achieves a 71.60% reduction in transition count over the conventional Ant Colony Optimization algorithm on smaller ISCAS’89 benchmark circuits. Furthermore, it attains an average power reduction of 57.48% when compared to test patterns generated using a Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR), highlighting its effectiveness in low-power VLSI testing.Keywords: Hybrid ant colony optimization, KL divergence, Prim’s algorithm, Test data reduction, Transition count reduction, Test pattern reorderingSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Device testing has become increasingly expensive and complex for VLSI test engineers, particularly with the advent of System-on-Chip (SoC) technology. To mitigate the high cost of external testing, Built-in Self-Test (BIST) technique has been introduced1. In this approach, digital devices are equipped with additional hardware components such as test pattern generators and response analyzers to enable self-testing. However, this leads to excessive power consumption during the testing process, primarily due to increased switching activity caused by a large volume of test patterns2. Therefore, reducing power consumption by minimizing switching activity during testing has become a critical requirement.

Testing of sequential circuits often results in excessive power consumption due to the application of a large number of test patterns. Several power reduction techniques such as test pattern optimization, scan cell reordering, scan cell gating, and clock gating3 have been proposed to address this issue. Among these, optimized test patterns play a key role in minimizing switching activity, thereby reducing overall test power. Scan cell reordering rearranges the scan elements to lower switching activity, while scan cell gating disables scan cells that do not affect the circuit’s functionality during testing. Similarly, clock gating reduces power by turning off the clock signals to storage elements that are not involved in the test operation. This paper focuses specifically on test pattern optimization as a means to effectively reduce test power in sequential circuit testing.

Several algorithms have been explored for test pattern optimization, including Genetic Algorithms (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO). GA offers global search capability but often suffers from slow convergence and requires careful parameter tuning. PSO is faster and easier to implement, yet it frequently converges prematurely to local optima, especially in complex test spaces. ACO performs well in discrete optimization and is suitable for vector reordering, but it incurs high computational overhead due to intensive pheromone updates and probabilistic evaluations. While these algorithms aim to optimize test sequences, many of them do not explicitly minimize the Hamming distance between consecutive test vectors, which is a key factor in reducing switching activity and dynamic power. The proposed method addresses this gap by focusing directly on Hamming distance aware optimization, ensuring lower power consumption during sequential circuit testing.

This paper presents a novel test pattern optimization technique aimed at reducing power consumption during the testing of sequential circuits. The primary objective of the proposed algorithm is to minimize switching activity, which directly contributes to dynamic power during scan based testing. The reduction in switching activity is achieved through a two step approach: first, by minimizing the total number of test patterns generated by the Test Pattern Generator (TPG) and second, by reordering the patterns to minimize the Hamming distance between consecutive vectors. The effectiveness of the proposed method is validated using ISCAS’89 benchmark sequential circuits with scan chains. Experimental results demonstrate a significant reduction in switching transitions, along with measurable improvements in overall test power consumption.

Literature review

Over past few decades, numerous techniques have been proposed to minimize test power, test application time and test data volume. Bit swapping LFSR and cellular automata technique based low power test pattern generator is proposed to reduce switching activity among test patterns4. Linear feedback shift register (LFSR) based low power test pattern generator architecture is proposed to minimize transition among consecutive test patterns. Minimization of transition among test pattern transition is attained by Random injection technique and Bipartite LFSR5. A new approach for test pattern generation is proposed based on variable rate clock-driven linear feedback shift register (LFSR) and twisted-ring counters (TRCs)6. This method retains randomness characteristics of test pattern, hence decreasing the switching activity. A modified gate diffusion input logic based LFSR is proposed to minimize the correlation among test patterns, thereby decreasing circuit switching activity and test power consumption7. Low power test pattern generator proposed in8 which combines linear feedback shift register (LFSR) with modified Johnson counter to generate test patterns with minimal transition.

Test pattern compression methodology reduces test pattern count to attain switching activity minimization. Test data are compressed using nine coded scheme, equal run-length coding (ERLC), extended frequency directed run-length (EFDR) coding, alternating variable run-length (AVR). This multistage compression technique proves especially effective when the test set contains a high percentage of don’t-care bits, achieving an approximate 55–60% reduction in transition count9. Genetic algorithm based on extended pattern run-length coding is proposed to reduce large number of don’t care value to improve test data compression. This methodology reports an approximate 55–65% reduction in transition count across a range of sequential benchmark circuits10. Test data compression is achieved by split-length variable length method, which reduces test data count by splitting identification bits in a string of test data11. Don’t care bits in test set are filled effectively and test data volume is reduced using Clique partitioning guided dictionary-based coding technique. An average transition reduction of 55–58% is reported by this methodology among various sequential circuits12. Test data optimization problem is correlated as travelling salesman problem and genetic algorithm is used to solve the problem. This methodology attains significant reduction in test data volume, without any additional hardware cost13. A modified bitmask-based approach led by the clique heuristic has been used to compress test data14. Test pattern count is reduced by proposed encoding scheme based on nine coded, selective pattern compression and Alternate Variable Run length code15. Embedded deterministic test methodology is proposed to compress test data volume by determining scan configuration with least test data16. Two level compression scheme is proposed to achieve a remarkable amount of reduction on test data volume with AMMMO framework based on time series17. Test pattern compression is achieved by optimizing conflicting bit minimization (CBM) problem based on shuffled shepherd optimization approach. This methodology demonstrates an average transition reduction of 55–60% across multiple sequential benchmark circuits18. Test pattern optimization methodology based on state skipping LFSR and Prim’s algorithm is proposed to compress and reorder test pattern to meet minimal hamming distance among consecutive test patterns19. Switching activity minimization is accomplished by reducing hamming distance among consecutive test patterns using Genetic algorithm20.

Test patterns are reordered with least hamming distance among consecutive test patterns, to minimize switching activity during sequential circuit testing. Test pattern reordering can be interpreted as travelling salesman problem and Ant colony optimization algorithm is used to reduce hamming distance among test patterns. This methodology achieves an average transition reduction ranging from 20 to 25% across a variety of sequential benchmark circuits21. Test patterns are reordered with minimal hamming distance to lower switching activity by combining Genetic algorithm and simulated annealing. This methodology achieved an average transition reduction of 45–53% across various benchmark circuits22. Local minimum problem in Ant colony optimization (ACO) is overcame by merging it with Genetic algorithm. Thereby, a phenomenal reduction in Hamming distance is observed, resulting in more efficient test pattern reordering, with an average transition reduction of 50–60% across a range of benchmark sequential circuits23. Adaptive learning algorithm is opted to reorder test patterns which helps to determine the optimal test set and test pattern order with low cost is identified using weighted support vector machine (SVMRANK) approach. An average transition count reduction of 45–55% is achieved across various standard sequential benchmark circuits with this methodology24. Prim’s algorithm based test pattern reordering efficiently lowers switching activity in scan chain25. Problem of repetitive simulation based test pattern reordering is overcame by Artificial intelligence based on Deep Neural Network. Prim’s algorithm is combined with KL divergence algorithm to produce optimized test pattern order. This approach yields an average transition count reduction of 50–60%, effectively lowering dynamic power consumption during test application across a wide range of benchmark circuits26. A machine learning algorithm, k-means clustering is proposed to reorder test patterns by grouping them with similar characteristics like hamming distance, correlation and Euclidian distance. This methodology reports an average transition reduction of 50–55% across various sequential circuit27. Test patterns are reordered based on minimum spanning tree. Root node is selected as test pattern having minimum transition among bits28. Switching activity minimization is attained by reordering test vector using Kruskal’s algorithm29. Hopfield neural network based test pattern reordering scheme is proposed to reorder test pattern with minimal hamming distance among consecutive test patterns. An average transition reduction improvement of 40–50% is reported in this paper30.

Modified Whale Optimization Algorithm (MWOA) is proposed to accurately estimate inductance and capacitance parameters of single-phase and three-phase transmission lines with various bundle conductor configurations. The method demonstrated superior accuracy and convergence over traditional and other metaheuristic approaches, validated through benchmark functions and real-world case studies31. The study on parameter estimation of AC transmission lines using the flux linkage technique demonstrated that considering different bundle conductor configurations significantly improves the accuracy of inductance and capacitance calculations compared to conventional methods32. Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) has been effectively applied for parameter estimation of overhead transmission lines due to its strong convergence and ability to handle non-linear, multi-modal problems. Studies show that GWO provides higher accuracy and robustness compared to traditional and other metaheuristic methods, especially under uncertain or noisy data conditions33. Hybrid evolutionary techniques have been successfully used to optimize transmission line parameter estimation, combining the strengths of multiple algorithms to improve accuracy, convergence speed, and robustness against measurement noise and system nonlinearity34. A hybrid Moth–Flame Optimization with Particle Swarm Optimization has been applied in power transmission and distribution to enhance parameter estimation accuracy and ensure faster convergence in complex optimization scenarios35. Optimal parameter estimation of overhead transmission lines using the Whale Optimization Algorithm, while considering different bundle conductors and load modeling uncertainty, enhances accuracy by effectively capturing the impact of conductor configurations and load variations36.

In this section, a survey on low power test pattern generation (TPG), test pattern compression and test pattern reordering techniques are carried out. Low power TPG employs LFSR modifications and heuristic methods to reduce switching activity during scan testing. Test pattern compression leverages statistical and run length coding to reduce test data volume, while test pattern reordering applies graph algorithms and metaheuristic techniques to minimize hamming distance among test patterns, thereby reducing power consumption. However, with the growing complexity and volume of test data in modern circuits, the effectiveness of these conventional methods decreases due to increased computational overhead and storage demands. To overcome these limitations, the proposed work introduces a hybrid optimization framework that integrates Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence and Prim’s algorithm to achieve simultaneous test pattern compression and reordering, thereby enhancing efficiency and scalability.

Goal of finding an optimized path can be achieved by Hybrid Ant Colony Optimization (HACO) algorithm, which is similar to TSP. TSP does not encourage visiting a city more than once, so, loop formation is dissuaded while deciding optimized path using HACO.

Hybrid ant colony optimization

Ant colony optimization

Ant colony optimization (ACO), a metaheuristic algorithm is formulated based on ants foraging behavior of food searching. Naturally, when ants look for a food source, they drop a chemical substance called pheromone. Ants discover food sources by following the chemical substance deposited by the previous ant. Most probably ants have the ability to detect food sources in shortest path. Pheromone evaporation plays a vital role in accepting path which leads to food source. Once an ant releases pheromone, it gets evaporated on time. Evaporation rate is similar to the time taken by an ant to trial a food path and return to its home. A path with a larger amount of pheromone deposition is emphasized as most visited path with least evaporation rate. Ants roaming to locate food source are expected to opt path with highest pheromone deposition. Path with apical chemical deposition is implicitly confirmed as shortest path on identifying food source. Kullback-Leibler divergence

Though traditional ACO provides the best solution, a city in ACO is visited by many ants simultaneously. Hence, to avoid this issue, Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence, a machine learning algorithm is proposed in this paper. It helps in identifying ant with best fitness. KL divergence is a measure of differentiating one probability distribution from another probability distribution, and thence, it differentiates ants with best fitness from other ants in colony. Prim’s algorithm

KL divergence identifies ants with best fitness. Once best ants are decided, the problem of ordering ants arises. As a result of combining ACO and KL divergence, ants with best fitness visit a city only once. Now, the problem is after an ant visits a city, which ant in colony would visit its neighboring city? To address this issue, a greedy algorithm that finds minimum weight among edges in an undirected graph is proposed in this paper. Greedy algorithm used in this paper is Prim’s algorithm. Prim’s algorithm28 establishes connectivity among vertices based on weights. The algorithm begins with initializing a root node and then it is continued by connecting a single node to preceding node with minimum weight. Similarly, an ant in colony is initialized to be root node and remaining ants are connected based on tour length.

The construction of minimum weight spanning tree primarily depends on selection of root vertex. In test vector reordering problem, pattern with minimum bit transition is chosen as root vertex and remaining patterns are ordered with minimum Hamming distance between consecutive patterns. Experimental setup

Transition minimization among test patterns during sequential circuit testing is achieved by reduced test pattern count and reordered test patterns. On interpreting test vector reordering as travelling salesman problem, ACO is affirmed to reorder test patterns. Test patterns are attributed as ants and cities on emphasizing ACO algorithm to reorder test patterns and hamming distance between test patterns is enforced as intercity distance.

KL divergence algorithm is employed to compress ACO reordered test patterns. The reduced test patterns are again reordered using Prim’s algorithm to gain an optimized pattern order. Parameter initialization is an important phase in ACO algorithm, choosing appropriate values would lead to the generation of optimized pattern order. In this paper to obtain optimal results, parameters are initialized as. In the proposed methodology, the pheromone value (denoted as τ) plays a critical role in guiding the ant agents during the solution construction phase. For the first iteration of the Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) process, τ values are not predefined but are instead initialized using a neural network-inspired approach, ensuring a diverse and balanced search space exploration from the outset. This is inspired by weight initialization techniques used in neural networks to prevent saturation and ensure uniform learning in early stages. TIV denotes the Total Number of Input Values considered by the ant system during initialization. This parameter acts as a scaling factor and helps balance the magnitude of initial pheromone values according to the problem complexity or input dimensionality. This initialization ensures that the starting pheromone values are diverse and appropriately scaled, promoting effective exploration in the early stages of the optimization. As the ACO algorithm progresses, these pheromone values are iteratively updated based on the quality of the solutions constructed by the ants, thus reinforcing paths associated with better performance.

Conclusion

In this paper a new test pattern optimizing scheme is proposed to minimize transition among test patterns during sequential circuits testing. Proposed methodology uses Hybrid Ant colony optimization algorithm to reduce test data volume and reorder test patterns. KL divergence algorithm is used with ACO algorithm to reduce test pattern count. The reduced test patterns are reordered by Prim’s algorithm with least hamming distance among test patterns. With HACO test pattern reduction ratio is improved by 57.04% for the tested ISCAS’89 benchmark sequential circuits with single scan chain. On applying HACO reduced and reordered test patterns to ISCAS’89 benchmark sequential circuits with single scan chain, an average reduction of 71.60% in transition count is noted than traditional ACO. Similar kind of improvement in test data reduction and transition count reduction is observed for larger sequential circuits with multiple scan chain. The future work can be expanded by incorporating Genetic algorithm or Neural Network algorithm to further reduce run time complexity.

Amino acid metabolism-related LncRNAs as prognostic biomarkers and predictors of immunotherapy response in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Jiaxing Zhang, Xiaojing Zhu, Long Hu, Liyue Yu, Yan Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHCC has a high mortality rate among common malignancies. Finding the pathway that is involved with HCC is the main challenge in targeting metabolism for cancer therapy.MethodsBased on transcription data from the TCGA database, Univariate Cox analysis, LASSO, and Multivariate Cox analysis were used to identify hub AAM-related lncRNAs and construct a risk model. Then K–M survival analysis, time-dependent ROC curve analysis, genetic alterations, functional enrichment, immune infiltration status, and immunotherapy response were conducted. Finally, the effect of the characteristic gene AL590681.1 across different HCC cell lines was assessed.Results24 lncRNAs were involved in AAM and prognostic factors, and 4 lncRNAs were in our risk model. Patients in the high-risk group had a lower OS rate than patients in the low-risk group. The high-risk group had more immunosuppressive immune cells infiltrating and expressing CD276, CTLA4 and TIGIT. Patients in the high-risk group could had better survival prospects with an anti-PD1 treatment. Finally, the key gene AL590681.1 was overexpressed in various HCC cell lines and could enhance HCC cell activity.ConclusionWe developed a novel risk model for HCC patients with AAM-related lncRNAs, which could help predict the prognosis and response to immunotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03789-1.

Introduction

According to the 2022 global cancer statistics data from the International Agency for Research on Cancer, there were approximately 865,269 new cases of liver cancer worldwide in 2022, with approximately 757,948 deaths accounting for liver cancer. Of all cancers, liver cancer has the sixth highest incidence and the third highest mortality rate [1]. By 2024, the number of new liver cancer cases and deaths is expected to increase by 55% worldwide [2]. Because of the insidious onset and the high malignant level, 80% of individuals are not diagnosed until the advanced stages, resulting in a missed chance for surgical intervention [3]. The mortality rate for these individuals can reach 80%, with a median survival time of under a year and a 5-year survival rate of less than 20% [4]. About 80% of primary liver cancers are HCC [5]. Patients with advanced HCC do not respond well to traditional treatments for liver cancer, such as liver resection, liver transplantation, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization, and chemotherapy [6]. In order to deal with current clinical challenges and bring new prospects for multimodal treatment for HCC, we must re-evaluate the etiology and development of HCC from a new perspective and discover new biomarkers and therapeutic targets [7].

Proliferating cells within a tumor alter their amino acid metabolism so that they can meet their significant need for new protein synthesis, particularly in the absence of adequate nutrients [8]. There is an association between certain cancer phenotypes and the metabolism of branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs). BCAA metabolism abnormalities affect inherent cancer properties in cells and reflect cancer-related changes in metabolism on a systemic level [9]. Dysregulation of amino acid metabolism and BCAA levels is associated with cancer regrowth and progression, especially in cancers such as HCC, which exhibit hyperactivated mTOR pathways [10, 11]. Moreover, intercellular or subcellular transport of amino acids and overexpression of amino acid transporters disrupt cancer cell metabolism and worsen drug-induced stress, resulting in poor treatment outcomes [12]. The previous analysis highlights the critical role of amino acid metabolism in cancer cell proliferation and survival. Given these insights, we should target amino acid metabolism-related genes in cancer cells, which holds the potential to develop new treatment types for tumors.

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a kind of non-protein‐coding transcripts with lengths of more than 200 nucleotides [13]. As master regulators of gene expression, lncRNAs regulate a variety of physiological and pathological processes through transcriptional, posttranscriptional, and epigenetic mechanisms [14, 15]. It is becoming increasingly clear that lncRNAs play a role in the initiation, progression, metastasis, immune escape, and drug resistance of cancer [16, 17]. Some studies have indicated that certain lncRNAs widely expressed in various cancers, which can affect amino acid metabolism through different pathways such as regulating the expression of amino acid transporters and affecting the activity of amino acid metabolic enzymes, thereby promoting the growth and metastasis of tumor cells [18]. The functions and roles of lncRNAs have been studied in the amino acid metabolism process, and there is a need to examine how they can provide prognostic information for HCC patients. Here, our study contributes to and validates a potential profile using amino acid metabolism-related lncRNAs for predicting the survival of HCC patients.

Methods

Data acquisition

Transcriptome expression data and clinical data of HCC patients were acquired from the TCGA-LIHC (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) databases. In order to ensure robustness, patients with OS of less than 30 days were excluded from subsequent analyses. Each patient was randomly assigned to either a training or validation group. Finally, there were 170 patients in each of the training and validation datasets. Among the clinical data were age, gender, grade, and TNM staging. 374 genes associated with amino acid metabolism were downloaded from the Molecular Signature Database v7.5 (MSigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). In order to discover the AAM-related lncRNAs, Pearson correlations were calculated between the 374 genes and lncRNA expression levels. |Pearson correlation coefficient| >0.4 and p-value of < 0.05 were the requirements.

Construction and validation of the AAM-related LncRNAs risk model

HCC samples from the TCGA were randomly divided into training and validation datasets in a 1:1 ratio. For filtering out lncRNAs associated with OS among AAM-related lncRNAs, a Univariate cox regression with a P-value of < 0.05 was conducted. In the training cohort, the LASSO and Multivariate Cox analysis were used to construct AAM-related lncRNA risk model. A Univariate and Multivariate cox regression analysis was conducted to assess independent prognostic factors for HCC, including age, grade, TNM stage, and risk score. Based on patients’ median risk score, low-risk and high-risk groups were divided. K–M curves were used to compare OS between two groups. To determine the sensitivity and specificity of the risk model, both training and validation datasets were analyzed using the ROC analysis. By using a nomogram model in the training dataset, the efficacy of coefficient prediction was investigated for this AMM-related lncRNA risk model. Last but not least, the effect of model gene expression on patient prognosis was compared.

Mutation burden and copy number variations analysis

Both high- and low-risk groups were evaluated for tumor mutational burden (TMB), which includes coding mutations, somatic mutations, indel mutations, and base substitutions. To identify the somatic patterns of patients with HCC in two groups, copy number variation (CNV) data of somatic mutations were extracted and calculated.

Gene set enrichment analyses

The Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were performed on differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in high- and low-risk groups to reveal their potential functions. To investigate the molecular mechanisms behind the AAM-related lncRNA gene signature, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) pathway enrichment analysis was performed.

Tumor immunity analysis and treatment prediction

An algorithm for determining immune cell infiltration and function based on single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was used. A comparison was made between low-risk and high-risk groups in terms of immune checkpoint expression. In addition, we analyzed the relationship between PD-1, PD-L1, PD-L2, CTLA4 and risk score. TIDE (Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion) is a computational framework designed to predict cancer patients’ responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and understand the mechanisms of immune resistance [19]. By integrating gene expression profiles and machine learning algorithms, TIDE evaluates two key aspects of tumor-immune interactions: dysfunction of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) within tumors and exclusion of CTLs from the tumor microenvironment. The TIDE score, a composite metric, has significant predictive value for ICI response. High TIDE scores are associated with non-response, while low scores indicate a higher likelihood of successful therapy. TIDE analysis also provides insights into pathways and gene expressions related to immune evasion, such as the upregulation of specific immune checkpoint molecules or alterations in the tumor stroma that prevent CTL infiltration. The TIDE (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) algorithm was utilized to assess the ability of cancer cells to escape antitumor immunotherapy. SubMap (Subclass Mapping) is a computational method that compares gene expression profiles across different datasets to identify similar molecular subtypes [20]. This approach aligns tumor subtypes to predict their responses to immunotherapy. We will use this algorithm to predict patient responses to immunotherapy, facilitating the identification of potential immunotherapeutic strategies and enhancing personalized treatment efficacy.

Cell line, RNA interfering, proliferation and migration assay

Cells were cultured in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C and 5% CO2 in a standard humidified incubator. Real- time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was employed to assess the expression of AL590681.1 across various HCC cell lines: THLE2 (Procell, CL-0833, China), Hep-3B (Procell, CL-0102, China), Huh-1 (Procell, CL-0811, China), Huh-7 (Procell, CL-0120, China), HCCLM3 (Procell, CL-0278, China). The cell line exhibiting the highest expression level of the AL590681.1 gene was chosen for functional experiments. Using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA), AL590681.1-specific short hairpin RNA (siRNA) was transfected into HCC cells following the manufacturer’s protocol, successfully achieving knockdown of AL590681.1. The Specific shRNA sequence employed in this study are detailed in Table S1. After 48 h of transfection, RT-qPCR was conducted to assess its knockdown efficiency, using the following primer sequence: GCTCCCAGTTTGATCTGCCT. Following transfection with shRNA, the viability of liver cancer cells was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. The Colony formation assay was performed to evaluate the growth potential of cells post knockdown of AL590681.1. Following cell digestion and counting, 1000 cells were plated into each well of a six-well plate. After a 14-day incubation period, cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde for 20 min and stained with crystal violet for another 20 min. Subsequently, the samples were air-dried and photographed. The experiment was repeated, and the results were subjected to statistical analysis. The in vitro migration assay was conducted using a 24-well Transwell chamber (Corning Costar). After suspending 2 × 104 cells in serum-free culture medium, they were added to the upper chamber. The lower chamber was filled with culture medium containing 10% FBS. After incubation for 24 h, the cells in the upper chamber were carefully removed with a cotton swab. The cells attached to the lower surface of the filter were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software version 4.2.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and GraphPad Prism version 9.4.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA). Qualitative data were evaluated using the Pearson chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate, while quantitative data were assessed using a paired t-test or a non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum test for unpaired samples. For multiple groups of normalized data, a one-way ANOVA was utilized. Unless otherwise specified, a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Screening of candidate AAM-related LncRNAs and construction of the AAM-related LncRNA risk model

Based on the expression matrix, 24 AAM-related lncRNAs were discovered with correlation coefficients greater than 0.4 and p < 0.01. As shown in Fig. 1A, specific correspondences were shown between AAM-related genes and lncRNAs. We randomly divided the patient data into training and validation sets, employing the same modeling approach to develop the models and validate their prognostic value. In Supplementary Fig. 1A, we presented the demographic and clinical information for both the training and validation datasets. Then, 7 lncRNAs with important prognostic values were identified through Univariate COX regression analysis (Fig. 1B). Based on LASSO and Multi-Cox regression analyses, 4 AAM-related lncRNAs were identified and a prognostic signature was generated (Fig. 1C–E). The risk score = 0.1506 * ExpressionAC145343.1 − 0.0192 * Expression AC021074.3 − 0.0498 * ExpressionAC087379.2+ 0.1355 * ExpressionAL590681.1. The expression levels of AC145343.1 and AL590681.1 were significantly higher in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues, whereas AC087379.2 was significantly lower in tumor tissues (Supplementary Fig. 1B). The expression of AC021074.3 did not show a significant difference between tumor and normal tissues but was associated with different stages of tumor progression (Supplementary Fig. 1C). We also conducted Univariate COX regression analysis and Multivariate COX regression analysis based on risk score, age, grade, and TNM stage, which indicated that risk score was an independent prognostic factor for HCC (Fig. 2A, B). Based on the K–M plot, low-risk patients had better OS compared with high-risk patients (Fig. 2C). The AUC of the time-dependent ROC analysis for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year was 0.800, 0.756, and 0.702 for patients in the training dataset, respectively and 0.691, 0.619, and 0.607 in the validation dataset, indicating that AMM-related lncRNA risk model had a potential role in predicting prognosis for HCC patients (Fig. 2D–F). Then, an analysis of the clinical features and risk score was used to construct a nomogram predicting survival probability at 1 year (Fig. 3A). There was a correlation between hub gene expression levels and risk scores and ranking, OS and survival status, indicating that patients with low levels of risk score had a better OS in both training and validation datasets (Fig. 3B, C). Additionally, there is a poor prognosis for patients with high expression of AC145343.1 and AL590681.1 in both cohorts. In contrast, patients with high expression of AC021074.3 and AC087379.2 had a longer survival time (Fig. 3D, E).

Analysis of tumor mutation burden and copy number variations

TMB refers to the number or frequency of mutations in tumor cells. It is an index measuring the extent of genomic variation in tumors, often used to assess the genetic diversity and immunogenicity of tumors [21]. Some studies have found that patients with liver cancer with a high TMB may respond better to immunotherapy. This is because a high TMB can lead to the appearance of more neoantigens on the surface of tumor cells, making them more susceptible to immune system attack [22]. Therefore, we analyzed the patient’s TMB levels. Using the genetic alteration analysis, Waterfall plots of the top 20 genes changed in both high- and low-risk groups were shown (Fig. 4A, B). However, TMB levels did not differ between high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 4C). There is a better prognosis for patients with low TMB levels (Fig. 4D). A comparison of OS was made between patients with high and low risk scores combined with high and low TMB levels. There was a good prognosis for patients with low-risk scores and low levels of TMB. In contrast, A poor prognosis was observed in patients with high TMB levels in the high-risk group (Fig. 4E). CNV refer to variations in the number of copies of genomic segments between individuals [23]. There are abundant CNV present in liver cancer tissues. These variations may involve an increase or decrease in the copy number of genes, which could affect the expression levels of genes related to tumors and potentially impact the therapeutic outcomes of liver cancer [24]. The overall GISTIC scores of copy number were shown in a plot (Fig. 5A). There were more CNV deletions in the high-risk group (Fig. 5B). Among the high-risk group, copy number loss was significantly higher than that of the low-risk group at both focal and arm levels. Further, the burden of copy number gain was higher in the high-risk group at focal level (Fig. 5C).

Enrichment analysis incorporating GO, KEGG, and GSVA

An analysis of GO and KEGG pathways was conducted to determine the potential biological mechanisms in the high- and low-risk groups. Results from GO analysis revealed that DEGs are mainly involved in regulation of cell-cell adhesion, positive regulation of cell activation, external side of plasma membrane, apical plasma membrane, antigen binding and cytokine activity (Fig. 6A). According to the KEGG analysis, the DEGs were mainly involved in Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, Cell cycle, Human T-cell leukemia virus 1 infection, Proteoglycans in cancer, and Cell adhesion molecules (Fig. 6B). The results of the GSEA analysis showed that the DEGs were mainly involved in BILE_ACID_METABOLISM, XENOBIOTIC_METABOLISM and KRAS_SIGNALING_DN (Fig. 6C). To understanding the different pathways of the high-risk group and the low-risk group, we compared the relationship between cell signaling pathways and risk scores. It was found that cancer-related pathways such as Cell_cycle, DNA replication, Mismatch_repair, MicroRNAs_in_cancer, G2M_CHEIKPONT, P53 signaling pathway E2F_TARGETS and MYC_TARGETS were associated with risk scores (Fig. 6D). The enrichment of these signaling pathways in liver cancer suggests that certain signaling cascades could be excessively activated, suppressed, or subjected to aberrant alterations within liver cancer cells, thus impacting the development and progression of tumors.

Correlations of the risk score with tumor immune microenvironment and the immunotherapy response

To investigate the relationship between risk score and immune cells and functions, the levels of infiltrating immune cells and immune functions were assessed. It showed that scores of Th1 cells, Th2 cells and Treg were higher in the high-risk group than low-risk group (Fig. 7A). Moreover, the high-risk group had higher scores for most immune pathways, including APC_co_inhibition, APC_co_stimulation, Bcell_receptors_score, PD1_PDL1_score, and TGFB_score (Fig. 7B). In addition, the relationship between immune cell scores and risk score was shown. A strong positive correlation was found between infiltrating levels of Th2 cells and risk scores (Fig. 7C). Between low- and high-risk groups, immune checkpoint expression levels were compared. Most immune checkpoint exhibited higher scores in the high-risk group including CD276, CD44, CD48, TGB1, TIGIT and VEGFB (Fig. 7D). Further investigation into the association between risk score and the expression levels of PD-1, CTLA4, PD-L1 and PD-L2. It showed that risk score had significantly positive relationship with the expression levels of PD-1, CTLA4, PD-L1 (Fig. 8A–D). Different immune cell infiltrations and immune functions in the tumor microenvironment can cause different immunotherapy responses. Next, we proceeded to predict the patient subgroup that would derive benefit from immunotherapy. Among high-risk and low-risk groups, there was a significant difference in TIDE, Dysfunction and Exclusion scores. In the high-risk group, TIDE scores were significantly lower (Fig. 8E). Moreover, there was a significant negative correlation between TIDE scores and risk scores (Fig. 8F). In addition, the Submap analysis predicted that patients in the high-risk group were likely to respond well to anti-PD-1 therapy (Fig. 8G). Taking into account the aforementioned analyses, we speculate that liver cancer patients in the high-risk group may potentially benefit from anti-PD-1 immunotherapy.

Downregulation of AL590681.1 suppressed both the proliferation and metastasis of HCC cells

After confirming the expression pattern of AL590681.1 in HCC, we proceeded to explore its functionality within HCC cells. Compared to normal liver cells (THLE2), AL590681.1 shows elevated expression in multiple HCC cell lines, including Hep-3B, Huh-1, Huh-7, and HCCLM3. Particularly, Huh-1 cells exhibit relatively higher levels of AL590681.1 expression (Fig. 9A). Therefore, we opted to proceed with functional experiments using the Huh-1 cell line. The effectiveness of silencing AL590681.1 in Huh-1 cells was validated through RT-qPCR analysis, with two shRNAs successfully achieving knockdown of AL590681.1 (Fig. 9B). The CCK-8 experiment indicated that suppressing the expression of AL590681.1 led to a decrease in the viability of Huh-1 cells, underscoring the crucial role of AL590681.1 in maintaining Huh-1 cell viability (Fig. 9C). The results of the colony formation assay suggested that downregulation of AL590681.1 suppressed cell proliferation (Fig. 9D). The Transwell migration assay revealed that knocking down AL590681.1 significantly inhibited the migratory capacity of Huh-1 cells (Fig. 9E). Consequently, the downregulation of AL590681.1 notably inhibited the proliferation and migration of HCC cells.

Discussion

In recent years, the development of bioinformatics has significantly advanced the discovery and application of disease-related molecular markers. In many studies, bioinformatics tools and techniques have been utilized to identify, validate, and investigate the functions of these markers. For example, Zhang [25] conducted a comprehensive study using various approaches to explore the potential link between bisphenol A exposure and prostate cancer. Meanwhile, Zhang [26] emphasized the role of multi-omics data integration in biomarker discovery. By integrating transcriptomics and pathology data, researchers can gain a more comprehensive understanding of disease mechanisms, leading to the identification of biomarkers with greater specificity and sensitivity. Similarly, in the context of HCC, bioinformatics support is crucial for discovering and applying biomarkers. It is the liver that synthesizes the majority of human amino acids, as well as albumin, growth factors, and many other functionally important peptides [27]. The reprogramming of amino acid metabolism is common in cancer, but little is known about its upstream targets and downstream mechanisms [11, 28, 29]. As an example, tumors often exhibit changes in ASS1, which is a rate-limiting enzyme in arginine synthesis [30]. A decrease in activity is seen in HCC and melanoma, whereas an increase is observed in lung cancer and colon cancer [31]. The identification of amino acid metabolism related signature might be a potential target for improving HCC patient outcomes. In this study, an amino acid metabolism-related lncRNA analysis was performed to build a risk model for HCC patients to predict their prognosis and immunotherapy response. In accordance with the median scores of the risk scores, HCC patients were divided into two high-risk and low-risk groups. It was found that patients in the low-risk group had a better prognosis than those in the high-risk group. According to both the Univariate and Multivariate COX regression analyses, the AAM-related lncRNA signature is an independent influence factor on OS. Additionally, the AUC values of 1-, 3-, and 5-years were respectively 0.800, 0.756, and 0.702. There is potential for using the AAM-related lncRNA signature as a prognostic indicator in HCC patients based on these findings.

Functional enrichment is used to determine whether there is enrichment of functional annotations within a gene set in specific biological processes or under specific conditions [26]. Functional enrichment analysis may reveal associations between a certain type of cancer and the activation or inhibition of specific signaling pathways, providing clues for the development of therapeutic strategies targeting those pathways [32]. We found significant differences in the activity of various signaling pathways between the high-risk group and the low-risk group of patients. The term “MYC_TARGETS” denotes genes associated with MYC, a pivotal transcription factor pivotal for regulating various biological processes such as cell proliferation, growth, and apoptosis. MYC regulates genes directly and indirectly, collectively referred to as MYC_TARGETS [33]. In cancer research, MYC is frequently observed to be overexpressed or mutated across various cancer types, contributing to aberrant cell proliferation and growth [34]. Notably, there existed a significant positive correlation between “MYC_TARGETS” and the risk score, suggesting potential involvement of MYC-related genes in high-risk group tumors. Furthermore, we observed a positive correlation between E2F_TARGETS and the risk score. E2F_TARGETS denote genes linked with E2F, a cluster of transcription factors engaged in orchestrating the transcriptional regulation of the cell cycle [35]. Typically, in HCC tissue, there is a notable upregulation in the activity of E2F, potentially fostering aberrant proliferation and growth of cancer cells [36]. Hence, conducting comprehensive investigations into E2F_TARGETS and the cell cycle pathways under their regulation could furnish a theoretical framework for devising novel targeted therapeutic approaches tailored to patients exhibiting elevated risk scores. In our analysis, differential gene enrichment between high- and low-risk groups revealed enrichment in multiple oncogenic signaling pathways, such as the mismatch repair deficiency pathway. Research on pathway enrichment also contributes to the identification of novel therapeutic targets for and the development of personalized treatment strategies. For instance, targeting excessively activated signaling pathways in liver cancer with pathway-specific drugs could be considered for treatment. Conversely, methods to restore normal function in suppressed signaling pathways could also be explored.

Infiltration of immune cells into the tumor immune microenvironment plays an important role in tumor initiation and progression [37, 38]. A positive correlation was observed between the infiltration levels of some immune cells and risk scores in our study. In the realm of liver cancer investigation, Tregs are recognized as a subtype of immunosuppressive cells [39]. These cells can undermine the immune system’s ability to clear tumors by dampening the activity of other immune cells, including CD8+ T cells and natural killer (NK) cells [40]. The prevalence and functionality of Tregs frequently exhibit notable escalation in patients afflicted with liver cancer, exhibiting a close correlation with tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis [41]. A higher level of Treg infiltration was observed in the high-risk group, which might contribute to poor prognosis by inducing immune escape [42]. T cell toxicity in high-risk groups is inhibited by immunosuppressive cell infiltration, as evidenced by differences in immune checkpoint expression levels between high-risk and low-risk groups. Specifically, in high-risk patients, increased Treg levels could facilitate tumor escape from immune surveillance. Treg cells can suppress the function of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and NK cells, both of which are crucial for targeting and eliminating tumor cells. Furthermore, Treg cells may also modulate the function of other immune cells through the secretion of inhibitory cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β, further enhancing the immunosuppressive microenvironment favorable for tumor growth. In addition, the ratio of immune cells infiltrating the tumor environment and the expression levels of immune checkpoints could influence the efficacy of immunotherapy [43]. There was a significant increase in the expression levels of PD-1 and CTLA4 in the high-risk group. An evaluation of immunotherapy effectiveness in HCC patients was performed using the TIDE algorithm. The TIDE scores of high-risk patients were lower than those of low-risk patients, suggesting that immunotherapy might be beneficial for the high-risk group. Further, submap analysis suggested that anti-PD-1 therapy might be more effective for patients in high-risk groups. Therefore, the utilization of the AAM-related lncRNA signature could aid in identifying liver cancer patients suitable for immunotherapy. In clinical practice, the AAM-related lncRNA signature can be utilized to score patients. Those identified as high-risk may be considered for anti-PD-1 antibody treatment.

In this study, we identified AL590681.1 as a key regulatory factor, prompting us to conduct experiments to further explore its potential oncogenic functions. Using qRT-PCR technology, we discovered that AL590681.1 is significantly upregulated in various liver cancer cell lines, suggesting its critical role in the initiation and progression of liver cancer. By silencing AL590681.1 expression in liver cancer cell lines, we observed a marked reduction in cell proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities in vitro. These findings indicate that the differential expression of AL590681.1 is closely related to tumor prognosis, partially explaining the effectiveness of our model. Future research can further investigate the specific molecular mechanisms of AL590681.1 and its potential applications in the clinical treatment of liver cancer. A deeper understanding of the role of AL590681.1 in liver cancer may help develop more effective therapeutic strategies, thereby improving the prognosis and survival rates of HCC patients.

Conclusion

We developed AAM-related lncRNA signature to predict prognosis, immune infiltration status, and the effectiveness of immunotherapy of patients with HCC. However, some limitations remained in our study. Firstly, we lack an external validation dataset to confirm the robustness of the signature. Secondly, we need to test more cell lines and patient samples to verify our results. Hence, it is imperative to seek additional external datasets and clinical patient information to validate the utility of this AAM-related lncRNA signature.

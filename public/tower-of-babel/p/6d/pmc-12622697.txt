Prevalence and antibiotic resistance of Mycoplasma hominis and Ureaplasma urealyticum in Chongming District, Shanghai, China from 2020 to 2023
Authors: Haiyun Fang, Yuzhen Li, Fei Wei, Ning Wang, Minjian Qian, Dao-Jun Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Ureaplasma urealyticum (UU) and Mycoplasma hominis (MH) can lead to various genital and extragenital infections, with antibiotic resistance growing and varying wordwide. This study aims to investigate the prevalence and antibiotic resistance profiles of MH and UU in outpatients in Shanghai, China, from 2020 to 2023. A facility-based cross-sectional study was conducted from January 1, 2020 to December 31, 2023. Trends of MH, UU and coinfection (MH and UU coinfection), rate of antibiotic resistance during 2020 and 2023 were analyzed based on Cochran–Armitage trend test. We analyzed the prevalence and antibiotics resistance rate base on chi-square test or a single sample t-test. Our study indicated that the overall positive rate of MH, UU and coinfection were 13.47%, 32.13%, and 11.21%, respectively. For age, the trend of positive rate of UU decreased significantly (Z = −3.62, P < .001). Furthermore, the positive rate of UU increased from 31.82% in 2020 to 47.92% in 2023 (Z = 1.98, P < .05). During 2020 and 2023, Methomycin and strong bacteriocins maintained high activity against both MH and UU, with resistance rates <5%. But roxithromycin (85.48%), azithromycin (75.81%), and levofloxacin (70.97%) showed limit activity for MH, and levofloxacin (44.78%), sparfloxacin (37.36%), and azithromycin (29.40%) showed limit activity for UU. Between 2020 and 2023, the rate of antibiotic resistance to azithromycin, clarithromycin, roxithromycin, methomycin, strong bacteriocins, sparfloxacin, and levofloxacin decreased against MH. The rate of antibiotic resistance to levofloxacin decreased against UU, but increased to josamycin against UU. Josamycin, methomycin, and strong bacteriocins are promising antibiotics against MH and UU. The prevalence of MH and UU should pay more attention in China. Josamycin, methomycin, and strong bacteriocins maintained outstanding activity against MH and UU, although the resistance rate of MH to josamycin increased.

1. Introduction

Ureaplasma urealyticum (UU) and Mycoplasma hominis (MH) are common commensal bacteria in the lower urogenital tracts of humans. UU, which belongs to the Ureaplasma genus, is among the smallest free-living microorganisms and is commonly found in the urogenital tract.[1] MH is another common bacterium that poses a potential threat in humans.[2] In China, the overall infection rates of MH and UU vary from 45.3% to 56% and from 42.10% to 79.5%, respectively.[3–6] Most colonized people do not show obvious symptoms of UU or MH infection. However, in women, UU or MH infection may lead to various genital and extragenital infections, causing complications including pelvic inflammatory disease, bacterial vaginosis, cervicitis, chorioamnionitis, infertility, premature rupture of the membrane in pregnancy, preterm delivery, and low birthweight for newborns.[4,7–11] Recent studies have also revealed an association between MH/UU infection and male infertility.[12]

MH and UU also pose a threat in terms of antibiotic resistance. The worldwide antibiotic resistance rates with respect to fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin and ofloxacin) were reported as 59.8% for MH and 31.2% for UU.[13] The resistances rate of MH and UU to tetracycline, the first-line treatment for urogenital infections, were reported as 50.0% and 43.3%, respectively, in China.[5] Antibiotic resistance to fluoroquinolones and tetracycline has been increasing in recent years.[5,14–16] To provide guidance for the treatment of MH and UU infections, antibiotic resistance surveillance has been implemented in many regions of China. In this study, we evaluated the prevalence of UU and MH infections along with the antibiotic resistance rates of MH and UU based on data from outpatients in Chongming, Shanghai, China from 2020 to 2023.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study population

This is a serial cross-sectional study. We recruited 1240 patients who visited the Chongming hospital (Shanghai, China) for urinary and reproductive tract related inflammation (such as vaginitis, urethritis, cervicitis, dysuria, urinary, and pelvic inflammatory) from January 1, 2020 to December 31, 2023, and had a PCR testing of UU and MH. Among 1240 patients screened, 445 (35.89%) were male. The average age of patients was 42.10 (SD: 14.09, range: 14–86 years). Most patients were derived from gynecology and obstetrics department, dermatology department, internal Medicine department, and urology department.

2.2. Ethical statement

All data collected were de-identified. This study’s process and contents were approved by the institutional review board of the Chongming Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Health Sciences (CMEC-2024-KT-33).

2.3. Clinical specimens and laboratory testing

The professional physician selected the appropriate sample category to submit the testing for MH and UU based on the patients symptoms and acceptability. For male patients, we collected prostatic gland secretion by prostatic massage. Then, a swab was gently inserted 1 to 2 cm into the urethra and rotated. For female patients, we collected cervical samples from inflammatory secretions taken from the cervical canal 1 to 2 cm after exocervical mucus had been wiped out. All specimens were transported to laboratory within 1 hour and were detected using real time fluorescence quantitative polymerase chain reaction within 24 hours.

2.4. Antibiotic resistance

Antibiotic resistance testing was performed for azithromycin, josamycin, clarithromycin, roxithromycin, methomycin, strong bacteriocins, sparfloxacin, levofloxacin, and ofloxacin using a commercial mycoplasma ICS kit (Lizhu Biotech Co., Ltd., Zhuhai, China). All procedures follow the operating procedures.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Excel was used for collecting original data. We analyzed the prevalence and antibiotics resistance rate base on chi-square test or a single sample t-test. The trends were analyzed based on Cochran Armitage trend test. All statistical tests were 2-sided, with P < .05 indicating statistical significance. All analyses were performed using SAS 9.4.

3. Results

3.1. Prevalence of MH and UU from 2020 to 2023

In total, we enrolled 1240 participants. Among all these bacterial species, the overall detection rate was 34.11% (423/1240). The positive rates of MH, UU, and coinfection with both MH and UU (hereafter referred to as simply “co-infection”) were 13.47%, 32.12%, and 11.21%, respectively. The positive rate of UU was much higher than those of MH (P < .001) and co-infection (P = .001; Table 1).

4. Discussion

This study provides information on the prevalence of MH, UU, and coinfection in outpatients with inflammation of the urinary and reproductive tracts in Chongming District, Shanghai, China. In this study, UU was the predominant pathogen in the genital tract, with an overall positive rate of 32.12%. The overall infection rate of MH was 13.47%, and the overall rate of coinfection with MH and UU was 11.21%. No obvious increase was observed in the infection rate of UU, MH or coinfection between 2020 and 2023. MH and UU were susceptible to josamycin, methomycin, and strong bacteriocins, suggesting that macrolids and tetracyclines are effective antibiotics against MH and UU.

The infection rate of UU in Chongming (32.12%) was similar to that in Hangzhou (near the city of Shanghai) from 2013 to 2019 (31.3%) but higher than that in southwest China in 2022 (27.07%).[15,17] The infection rate of MH in Chongming (13.47%) was higher than those in other regions of China (Hangzhou, 0.8%; Chongqing, 11.02%; Lianyungang, 3.91%; and Meizhou, 5.96%).[5,17] The rate of coinfection of MH and UU in Chongming (11.21%) was also higher than those in many other regions of China.[18] The prevalences of MH and UU varied among regions, populations, sampling methods, laboratory methods, and specimens. The explanations for these discrepancies are varied and complex. First, the study population may affect both the prevalence of infection and the detection rate. Second, the sampling method may significantly affect the prevalence of infection, with non-probability sampling introducing substantial bias. Furthermore, different laboratory testing methods used to identify infections may have different sensitivities, and variations in the specimen type and sampling site may also affect the results.

In this study, the prevalence of UU was related to age. The rate of UU infection was highest in the younger age (<30) group, consistent with previous studies.[5,19–21] The positive rates of MH and coinfection were higher in patients under the age of 20 than in the other age groups, which was earlier than other studies. He et al found the highest positive rate of MH among persons aged 20 to 29 years,[19] while Doroftei et al reported that the positive rate of MH and UU among infertile patients in Romania was highest among those aged 30 to 35 years[20]; in both of these studies, the infection rate of UU and MH decreased with increasing age. This may be because patients under age 20 have less awareness of sexual health and lower rates of condom use than older people.[22–24] The incidence of syphilis also increased since 2018 in persons under 20 years of age in China, even during COVID-19 (unpublished data). This suggests the need for sexually transmitted infection prevention and control among adolescents. Further studies are needed to determine the relationship between MH and UU prevalence and age.

Antibiotic resistance is the predominant cause of treatment failure in genital infection. Antibiotic resistance surveillance can provide guidance for clinical treatment. In this study, significant differences in the resistance rates of MH and UU were observed among various antibiotics. Overall, MH and UU were susceptible to C16-membered macrolides (josamycin) and tetracyclines (methomycin and strong bacteriocins), whereas they were extremely resistant to macrolids (azithromycin, clarithromycin, and roxithromycin) and fluoroquinolones (sparfloxacin, ofloxacin, and levofloxacin), consistent with other recent studies in various regions of China.[5,19,25] The resistance rate of UU to josamycin increased obviously during our study, implying that more attention should be paid to UU antibiotic resistance. The levels of fluoroquinolone resistance differ significantly across China. In our study, the antibiotic resistance rates of MH and UU for levofloxacin were 70.97% and 37.36%, respectively. For ofloxacin, the resistance rates were 45.16% for MH and 28.02% for UU. A recent systematic review revealed that the overall resistance of MH and UU to levofloxacin was 5.3%, much lower than the resistance rate for levofloxacin in the current study. Meanwhile, the overall resistance of MH and UU for ofloxacin was 31.2%, also much lower than our data.[13] The geographic differences in antibiotic resistance may be related to the different strategies for antibiotic use in different countries. Meanwhile, the high rate of fluoroquinolone resistance can be attributed to the frequent use of these antibiotics and the frequent misuse of ciprofloxacin.

Some limitations of this study should be addressed. First, the entire study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, which may have caused the prevalences of UU and MH to be underestimated. Second, this was a single-center study, and selection bias may have led to the underreporting of the prevalence of UU and MH. Finally, this study was conducted on outpatients; thus, the prevalences of UU and MH may be higher than those in the general population.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, we retrospectively analyzed the infection rates of UU and MH in outpatients in Chongming District, Shanghai, China. The prevalence and distribution of UU and MH were related to age. Josamycin, methomycin, and strong bacteriocins maintained outstanding activity against MH and UU, although the resistance rate of MH to josamycin increased during the study period.

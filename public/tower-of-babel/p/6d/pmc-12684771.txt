Empagliflozin Improves Cardiomyocyte Function in Phospholamban‐R14del Cardiomyopathy by Regulating Calcium/Calmodulin‐Stimulated Protein Kinase II Activity and Apoptosis
Authors: Anna P. Hnatiuk, Alexander W. Li, Barbara E. Vecci, David W. Staudt, Ricardo Serrano, Xiaozhi Gao, Peter P. Q. Tran, Mark Mercola
Published: 2025

single deletion of arginine 14 (R14del) in the coding region of the phospholamban gene

A single deletion of arginine 14 (R14del) in the coding region of the PLN (phospholamban) gene (PLN‐R14del) leads to dilated cardiomyopathy associated with severe ventricular arrhythmia and a high risk of sudden cardiac death and heart failure. 1 No effective pharmacological therapy exists; thus, management of PLN‐R14del cardiomyopathy consists mainly of treating heart failure symptoms combined with antiarrhythmia medication, implantable cardiac defibrillators, and cardiac transplantation.

SGLT2 (sodium‐glucose transport protein 2) inhibitors were developed to regulate glucose homeostasis as a treatment for type II diabetes. More recently, they have been shown to reduce hospitalization for heart failure, cardiovascular death and all‐cause mortality independently of glucose control or a diagnosis of diabetes.

There are no reports of the use of SGLT2 inhibitors to treat PLN‐R14del cardiomyopathy. However, the SGLT2 inhibitor empagliflozin has been shown to improve Ca2+ handling and decrease elevated CaMKII (calcium/calmodulin‐dependent‐protein‐kinase‐II) activity in isolated cardiomyocytes from failing human and mouse hearts. 2 More recently, CaMKII inhibition was shown to decrease the arrhythmic burden in a mouse model of PLN cardiomyopathy. 1 Therefore, we hypothesized that empagliflozin might elicit a beneficial effect in PLN‐R14del cardiomyopathy by decreasing CaMKII activity or diastolic intracellular Ca2+.

To investigate the therapeutic potential of Empagliflozin, we generated human‐iPSC‐derived‐cardiomyocytes (hiPSC‐CMs) from 2 previously described human iPSC lines carrying PLN‐R14del mutation and their respective isogenic control lines. 3 hiPSCs were generated from deidentified donor samples obtained with Stanford School of Medicine institutional review board approval. hiPSC‐CMs were treated with empagliflozin and evaluated for contractility and calcium parameters with a kinetic imaging cytometer (Vala). 3 We previously showed that a large number (up to 100) of experimental replicates is typically needed to achieve statistically significant contractile phenotypes in PLN‐R14del relative to isogenic hiPSC‐CMs when cultured on 2‐dimensional hard substrate (μClear) but that the phenotype is more robust in 3‐dimensional engineered tissue constructs presumably due to enhanced structural and physiological maturation. 3 Because 3‐dimensional aligned nanofiber plates increase myofibrillar organization reminiscent of 3‐dimensional engineered tissue cultures (Figure [A] and [A′]) and gene expression levels of myosin‐heavy‐chain‐7 and cardiac troponin T2 in hiPSC‐CMs, 4 we explored their use to quantify the PLN‐R14del contractile dysfunction. The decreased contractile amplitude characteristic of PLN‐R14del mutant hiPSC‐CMs was more robustly apparent on the aligned nanofiber than conventional μClear plates allowing the use of fewer replicates (~12) to visualize the consequences of the mutation (Figure [B] and [C]).

Using the aligned nanofiber system, empagliflozin treatment (at the previously reported efficacious dose 0.5 μmol/L 5 ) ameliorated contractile activity of the PLN‐R14del mutant cells after 4 days of exposure to the drug (Figure [B]), increasing the peak contraction amplitude of both PLN‐R14del cell lines compared with their respective untreated controls: ~30% improvement in PLN‐R14del‐CRISPR‐introduced cardiomyocytes (P=0.0017) and ~28% in PLN‐R14del patient cardiomyocytes (P=0.0033) (Figure [C] and [C′]). Treatment with the selective CaMKII inhibitor AIP (autocamtide‐2‐related inhibitory peptide; 0.08 μmol/L for 30 minutes) significantly but partially improved the contractile function (~15%, P=0.0419, Figure [C″]), suggesting that the previously reported inhibition of CaMKII by empagliflozin 2 might contribute to the effect. All studies were repeated with 3 hiPSC‐CMs differentiation batches of each line.

The PLN‐R14del variant impairs the reuptake of Ca2+ into the sarcoplasmic reticulum 1 resulting in baseline prolongation of the Ca2+ transient duration‐90 (by ~21% in the PLN‐R14del CRISPR‐introduced cardiomyocytes and ~15% in PLN‐R14del patient cells) relative to their respective isogenic controls (Figure [D]). Empagliflozin decreased the pathological prolongation (Figure [D′]) and Ca2+transient decay (Figure [D″]) times partially restoring both parameters to levels observed in their respective isogenic controls. Systolic parameters (Ca2+ upstroke kinetics or rates) were unaffected (data not shown). Taken together, we conclude that empagliflozin can improve Ca2+ reuptake kinetics and contractile function in PLN‐R14del hiPSC‐CMs.

To establish a mechanism for the effects of empagliflozin, we measured autophosphorylation of CaMKII at Threonine287 (indicative of enzyme activity) after 30 minutes and 4 days of treatment with 0.5 μmol/L of empagliflozin by Western blotting. PLN‐R14del‐mutation significantly increased CaMKII activity (Figure [E] and [E′]) that was restored to the isogenic control levels after empagliflozin treatment (Figure [E] and [E′]), indicating that it inhibited CaMKII enzymatic activity. We previously reported that the presence of the PLN‐R14del‐mutation increases the proapoptotic unfolded protein response in cardiomyocytes. 3 Therefore, we monitored signaling of 35 apoptosis‐related proteins using a protein array (R&D Systems). As expected, the overall apoptotic signaling was significantly increased at baseline in PLN‐R14del relative to isogenic control hiPSC‐CMs (P=0.0001). Importantly, 30 minutes of empagliflozin treatment decreased apoptotic signaling to isogenic control levels (P=0.0001) (data not shown, 2‐way‐ANOVA with Benjamini–Krieger–Yekutieli test, false discovery rate (FDR)=0.05).

We conclude that empagliflozin has a protective effect by restoring contractile function and Ca2+ kinetics in PLN‐R14del cardiomyocytes. Two potential mechanisms appear to underlie the beneficial effects, namely decreasing CaMKII activity and reverting apoptotic signaling. The full range of empagliflozin's activities in cardiomyocytes is unclear and, therefore, other mechanisms cannot be ruled out. Nonetheless, these studies suggest that empagliflozin is a promising candidate to treat PLN cardiomyopathy. Future studies with 3‐dimensional hiPSC‐CMs organoids and large animal models are warranted to support future clinical studies of empagliflozin to reduce the risk of heart failure and potentially lethal ventricular arrhythmia in PLN‐R14del patients.

Coexistence of thyroid sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia and mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma in Hashimoto thyroiditis: a case report
Authors: Yutaka Nakamura, Tetsuya Murata, Yuichi Fujita, Kazuyuki Kubo, Tetsuya Kitamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia (SMECE) is an extremely rare thyroid tumor that frequently arises in association with chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis. We report a rare case of SMECE and mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma diagnosed simultaneously in the same thyroid gland.Case report: A man in his 60s with serological findings suggestive of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis was referred to our hospital after a thyroid nodule was incidentally detected on carotid ultrasonography. Fine-needle aspiration cytology revealed clusters of intermediate-type epithelial cells with an eosinophil-rich inflammatory background. Left thyroid lobectomy was performed to establish a diagnosis.Results: Histologically, the tumor showed dense stromal sclerosis with nests of squamoid cells, intermediate-type cells, and mucin-producing cells, accompanied by marked eosinophilic infiltration. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated positivity for CK5/6, p63, and CK19 and negativity for thyroglobulin and TTF-1. Fluorescence in situ hybridization analysis revealed no MAML2 gene rearrangement. Chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis was present in the surrounding thyroid tissue, along with extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of the MALT.Conclusion: This case suggests that chronic inflammatory conditions associated with Hashimoto’s thyroiditis may have contributed to the tumorigenesis of both the epithelial and lymphoid components. Recognizing this rare coexistence is important for accurate pathological diagnosis.

Introduction

Sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia (SMECE) is a rare thyroid malignancy first described by Chan et al. in 19911). Histologically, SMECE is characterized by dense stromal sclerosis and prominent eosinophilic infiltration by a mixture of squamoid, intermediate-type, and mucin-producing cells2). Less than 100 cases have been reported worldwide3).

SMECE was initially considered a mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC). However, accumulating evidence suggests that SMECE is a distinct entity. Conventional MEC frequently harbors MAML2 gene rearrangements, whereas most reported SMECE cases lack this alteration and show different clinicopathological features4,5,6).

SMECE often develops in association with chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis, particularly Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, suggesting that chronic inflammation may play an important role in tumor development7,8,9).

Thyroid mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma is a rare tumor that frequently occurs in patients with chronic thyroiditis. Persistent antigenic stimulation in autoimmune thyroiditis is believed to promote the neoplastic transformation of marginal zone B cells10).

However, the simultaneous diagnosis of SMECE and MALT lymphomas within the same thyroid gland is extremely rare. Here, we report a rare case of both thyroid SMECE and MALT lymphoma arising from Hashimoto’s thyroiditis.

Case Report

A man in his 60s with a history of gout and hypercholesterolemia was referred to our hospital after a thyroid nodule was incidentally detected on carotid ultrasonography at another clinic.

Laboratory examination revealed normal thyroid function tests. However, thyroid autoantibody levels were markedly elevated, with anti-thyroid peroxidase antibody levels exceeding 2,000 IU/mL and anti-thyroglobulin antibody levels exceeding 212 IU/mL, suggesting Hashimoto’s thyroiditis.

Ultrasonography revealed diffuse enlargement and heterogeneous echogenicity of the thyroid gland. Discussion

SMECE is characterized by marked stromal sclerosis; prominent eosinophilic infiltration; and a mixture of squamoid, intermediate-type, and mucin-producing cells1, 2). Similar histological features were observed in the present case, and an inflammatory microenvironment rich in eosinophils was widely distributed around the tumor. Immunohistochemically, the tumor cells were positive for CK5/6, p63, and CK19 and negative for thyroglobulin (TG), TTF-1, and BRAF V600E (VE1), findings consistent with those previously reported for SMECE5, 6). Furthermore, the absence of MAML2 gene rearrangement was consistent with findings commonly reported in SMECE11) and supported the notion that SMECE represents a tumor entity distinct from conventional MEC. The present case showed morphological and immunohistochemical findings consistent with previously reported characteristics of SMECE12).

Two major hypotheses have been proposed regarding the histogenesis of SMECE:

(1) Neoplastic transformation of squamous metaplasia associated with chronic inflammation

This hypothesis suggests that repeated follicular destruction and regeneration associated with chronic inflammation, such as in Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, induces squamous metaplasia of follicular epithelial cells, which subsequently undergo neoplastic transformation9, 10, 13).

(2) Origin from solid cell nests (SCNs)

Hunt et al. focused on the strong p63 expression observed in the SCNs and proposed that SMECE may originate from SCNs, as SMECE similarly shows p63 positivity14, 15).

In the present case, marked eosinophilic infiltration and lymphoid follicles with germinal center formation were observed, indicating that chronic inflammation strongly influenced the tissue microenvironment. In addition, the tumor showed strong p63 positivity and negativity for TG and TTF-1, suggesting the involvement of a cellular lineage distinct from the typical follicular epithelial cells. Thus, this case exhibited features compatible with both hypotheses. However, the histogenetic origin of SMECE cannot be definitively determined based on the present findings alone.

The background thyroid tissue showed the typical histological features of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis. In addition, the presence of MALT balls and monotonous proliferation of CD20-positive B cells supported the diagnosis of MALT lymphoma. Thyroid MALT lymphoma is a low-grade lymphoma in which the marginal zone B cells undergo neoplastic transformation under the persistent antigenic stimulation associated with chronic inflammation9). Reports describing the coexistence of SMECE and MALT lymphoma within the same thyroid gland are extremely rare, making this case an unusual presentation. Previous studies have suggested that SMECE frequently occurs in the context of chronic thyroiditis9, 10, 13). Although tumors derived from different cellular lineages, epithelial and lymphoid, may develop simultaneously within a chronic inflammatory microenvironment, it is difficult to establish a causal relationship in a single case. Nevertheless, this case represents a clinical example suggesting that chronic inflammation can contribute to tumorigenesis in multiple cellular lineages. However, as this report describes a single case, further studies are necessary to clarify the causal relationship between SMECE and MALT lymphoma.

Differential diagnoses included squamous cell carcinoma, intrathyroid thymic carcinoma (ITC), conventional MEC, and reactive squamous metaplasia associated with Hashimoto’s thyroiditis16, 17). In the present case, the presence of mucin-producing cells; positivity for p63, CK5/6, and CK19; negativity for TG, TTF-1, and BRAF V600E (VE1); and the absence of MAML2 gene rearrangement supported the diagnosis of SMECE. MALT lymphoma was diagnosed based on the formation of MALT balls, monotonous proliferation of CD20-positive B cells, and the presence of lymphoepithelial lesions.

Conclusion

Herein, we report a rare case of thyroid SMECE coexisting with MALT lymphoma in a thyroid gland affected by Hashimoto’s thyroiditis. Chronic inflammatory conditions associated with Hashimoto’s thyroiditis may contribute to tumorigenesis in both the epithelial and lymphoid components. Recognizing this rare simultaneous diagnosis is important for accurate pathological diagnosis.

Overcoming therapeutic inertia in primary care: a multisite quality initiative to increase guideline-based prescribing for patients with diabetes
Authors: Michael William Latreille, Bradley J Tompkins, Allen B Repp
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTherapeutic inertia (TI), the failure to intensify or de-intensify treatment when appropriate, is a contributor to poor guideline adherence in diabetes treatment, including the suboptimal use of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT-2is) and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs).MethodsWe developed a multifaceted improvement initiative targeting TI at four academic primary care practices, aiming to increase rates of SGLT-2i and GLP-1RA use for patients with type 2 diabetes (T2DM). Prescribing trends for GLP-1RAs, SGLT-2is, sulfonylureas and insulin were compiled quarterly over a 12-month baseline period and 12-month intervention period and analysed using interrupted time series analyses. Providers completed a brief questionnaire assessing project feasibility and acceptability.ResultsGLP-1RA prescribing showed an upward trend during both the baseline and intervention periods (+0.70% vs +0.87% per quarter; p=0.14 for difference) and increased significantly in the first intervention quarter (+1.73%; p=0.003). SGLT-2i prescribing was static during the baseline period, showed an upward trend during the intervention (0.0% vs +0.43% per quarter; p=0.05 for difference) and increased significantly in the first intervention quarter (+1.0%; p=0.03). In those prescribed a GLP-1RA or an SGLT-2i, sulfonylurea prescribing dropped significantly after intervention (−2.0% per quarter; p=0.02). Insulin prescribing rates did not change throughout the study period. There was no significant change in haemoglobin A1c among patients newly prescribed a GLP-1RA and/or an SGLT-2i during the intervention period (7.3% ±1.5% baseline vs 7.2% ±1.4% intervention period, p=0.23). Most providers indicated that they had prescribed (90%) and were more likely to prescribe (81%) GLP-1RAs and/or SGLT-2is in the future because of their participation.ConclusionsA quality improvement initiative targeting drivers of TI was associated with increased rates of guideline-based medication prescribing for primary care patients with diabetes and may be applied to other conditions in which TI limits care optimisation or guideline adherence.

Introduction

Problem description

Professional guidelines recommend early incorporation of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT-2is) and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) in diabetes treatment for their impacts on glycaemia, weight management and end-organ disease risk.13 However, the use of these agents remains suboptimal, contributing to poor rates of glycaemic goal attainment and missed opportunities for end-organ protection.4 5

Therapeutic inertia (TI), failure to intensify or de-intensify treatment when appropriate, has been cited as a source of poor guideline adherence.4 5 Origins of TI include variable provider knowledge or comfort, difficulty identifying patients warranting intervention, limited access to individual performance data and perceived cost or insurance barriers. While several studies assess single specific interventions on diabetes management in the primary care setting,6 there are few descriptions of quality improvement (QI) interventions addressing multiple drivers of TI in primary care to improve guideline-based prescribing of new classes of diabetes medications.

Specific aim

We sought to increase rates of SGLT-2i and GLP-1RA use for patients with type 2 diabetes (T2DM) across four academic primary care practices using a multipronged QI intervention to target the drivers of provider TI.

Methods

Context

This initiative was conducted at four outpatient general internal medicine practices affiliated with the University of Vermont Medical Center and included patients aged ≥18 years with T2DM as defined by the organisation’s diabetes registry. All sites used a common electronic health record (EHR), Epic, and participated in an interprofessional quality committee.

Study of interventions

Prescribing rates for GLP-1RAs and SGLT-2is were assessed quarterly during the 12-month baseline period (1 May 2022–30 April 2023) and intervention period (1 May 2023–30 April 2024). Primary care providers (PCPs) were asked to complete a brief questionnaire assessing uptake and feasibility at project conclusion.

Analysis

The analysis included patients who remained on the diabetes registry for the entire baseline and intervention period. We performed interrupted time series analyses (ITSAs) examining quarterly active GLP-1RA, SGLT-2i, sulfonylurea and insulin prescriptions to measure changes in prescription rates.

Results

The analysis included 2037 patients. Patients were mostly Caucasian (91%) with an average age of 68 years. More than half (51%) were covered by Medicare, 33% had commercial insurance, 6% had Medicaid only and 8% had both Medicare and Medicaid.

Of 49 participating PCPs, 21 (43%) completed the postparticipation questionnaire. All respondents reported reviewing some of their diabetes panel, and 67% reported reviewing their entire panel. Most indicated that they had prescribed (90%) GLP-1RAs and/or SGLT-2is and would be more likely to prescribe them in the future (81%) because of their participation.

Discussion

Summary and interpretation

This project demonstrates the utility and feasibility of a multipronged QI initiative targeting drivers of TI associated with underuse of GLP-1RAs and SGLT-2is. We observed significant changes in PCP prescribing patterns, and participating PCPs reported an increased likelihood of prescribing GLP-1RAs and SGLT-2is as a result of the initiative. Static haemoglobin A1C levels in patients newly prescribed these agents may be partly explained by our limited follow-up period, although other QI initiatives have similarly demonstrated changes in prescribing without corresponding improvements in glycaemia.6

Key drivers of success included a focused and relevant intervention, accessibility of educational materials, linkage with operational workflows and subdivision of the panel review process into small monthly increments. While engaging diabetes educators or pharmacists may enhance the impact and sustainability of initiatives targeting TI,6 ongoing success in practice settings like ours, where access to these resources is limited, may be supported by providing PCPs with time, training and structure for panel management activities.

Limitations

We leveraged reporting tools in the Epic EHR, in practices accustomed to educational and improvement initiatives and in an older adult population with high insurance coverage rates, limiting generalisability to other settings. There are also no established benchmarks for primary care prescribing rates of GLP-1RAs or SGLT-2is, although our findings may provide a reference point. We did not conduct a preintervention assessment of clinician knowledge, confidence or behaviour and our postassessment is potentially limited by low response rate and response bias. While indications, availability and patient awareness of these medications evolved during our project, the ITSAs helped to control for secular trends and clarify the temporal association of the interventions with prescribing rates.

Conclusions

A QI initiative targeting drivers of TI was associated with increased rates of guideline-based medication prescribing for primary care patients with diabetes and may be applied to other conditions in which TI limits care optimisation or guideline adherence.

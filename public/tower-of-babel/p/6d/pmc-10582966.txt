Effect of virtual reality-based exercise and physical exercise on adolescents with overweight and obesity: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Qian Wu, Rui Han, Zhen Li, Xiaojun Huang, Di Cheng, Jiacheng Ni, Shizhe Zhang, Xunan Tan, Piao Kang, Shujie Yu, Anran Chen, Yuwei Lu, Fangshu Yao, Zihao Jin, Yiming Qin, Jingyi Guo, Dan Liu, Ying Zhang, Yanxia Song, Liping Zhu, Qin Lu, Qiandi Chen, Chengxiang Lin, Qichen Fang, Maituersong Maimaitikasimu, Jiarui Wu, Weiping Jia, Bin Sheng, Jihong Wang, Huating Li
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
IntroductionObesity is a complex and multifactorial disease that has affected many adolescents in recent decades. Clinical practice guidelines recommend exercise as the key treatment option for adolescents with overweight and obesity. However, the effects of virtual reality (VR) exercise on the physical and brain health of adolescents with overweight and obese remain unclear. This study aims to evaluate the effects of physical and VR exercises on physical and brain outcomes and explore the differences in benefits between them. Moreover, we will apply a multiomics analysis to investigate the mechanism underlying the effects of physical and VR exercises on adolescents with overweight and obesity.Methods and analysisThis randomised controlled clinical trial will include 220 adolescents with overweight and obesity aged between 11 and 17 years. The participants will be randomised into five groups after screening. Participants in the exercise groups will perform an exercise programme by adding physical or VR table tennis or soccer classes to routine physical education classes in schools three times a week for 8 weeks. Participants in the control group will maintain their usual physical activity. The primary outcome will be the change in body fat mass measured using bioelectrical impedance analysis. The secondary outcomes will include changes in other physical health-related parameters, brain health-related parameters and multiomics variables.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry. Dissemination of the findings will include peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and media releases.Trial registration numberChiCTR2300068786.

Introduction

The prevalence of overweight and obesity among adolescents has rapidly increased in China over the past four decades. According to the latest China National Nutrition Survey, the latest national prevalence estimates for 2015–2019 were 11.1% for overweight and 7.9% for obesity of adolescents aged 6–17 years.1 Adolescents with overweight and obese experience numerous negative effects on their physical, psychological and social well-being.2 Adolescents with obesity are at a higher risk of comorbid conditions such as impaired glucose tolerance and cardiovascular diseases.3 4 The prevalence of disordered breathing, such as obstructive sleep apnoea and obesity hypoventilation syndrome,5 is also higher among adolescents with obesity. Obesity lowers bone mass in adolescents and increases the risk of orthopaedic disorders.6 7 Teenagers who are overweight or obese are more likely to experience psychosocial sequelae. They may experience depression, bullying, social isolation and lower self-esteem.8 9 In addition, a higher visceral fat ratio is negatively associated with intellectual and cognitive functions in children with obesity.10 China faces the problem of insufficient physical activity among children and adolescents, with approximately 50% of young individuals failing to meet the recommended guidelines.11 This may aggravate the high prevalence of obesity among Chinese adolescents.

It is beneficial and vital to increase physical activity among adolescents with overweight and obesity. Recently, the American Academy of Pediatrics released the first edition of clinical practice guidelines for the evaluation and treatment of children and adolescents with obesity, in which exercise, especially aerobic exercise, is one of the specific health behaviour recommendations.12 Exercise can prevent weight gain by increasing daily energy expenditure and suppressing appetite.13 Some physical risks mentioned before can be reduced by adequate levels of exercise and at least moderate cardiorespiratory fitness.14 Additionally, exercise has been shown to enhance brain health by activating neurotrophic factors, increasing glucose sensitivity and cerebral perfusion, reducing inflammation and promoting neuroplasticity.15–18 Further evidence suggests that children with obesity may benefit from physical activity interventions with respect to their cognitive and brain health accessed by neuroelectrical outcomes.19 People who engage in regular physical activity demonstrate enhanced sensitivity of their appetite control system and reduced subsequent energy intake through both peripheral and neurocognitive pathways.20 21 However, although it is well-documented that exercise is beneficial for adolescents with obesity, the mechanisms by which exercise improves the physical and brain health in this population have not been fully elucidated.

As a promising emerging technology, virtual reality (VR) is used in the medical field. VR technology can eliminate the complex environment of the true physical world and establish a controllable synthetic environment,22 which can satisfy various needs. VR technology has been used in the management of neurological and psychiatric disorders, such as the rehabilitation of neurodegenerative diseases23 (eg, stroke, Parkinson’s disease) and treatment of mental illness24 (eg, anxiety, depression) and pain symptoms.25 VR interventions for pain management are based on cognitive distraction. VR technology relieves discomfort caused by intense physical activity in subjects with obesity, improving their physical performance and enabling them to exercise longer.26

Game-based VR training enhances motivation to participate in physical activity, and such interventions might be considered in an increasingly physical inactivity population of children at risk for obesity and diabetes.27 Furthermore, a study found that children who played video games for 3 hours or more per day performed better on cognitive abilities assessed by task-based functional MRI (fMRI) involving inhibitory control and working memory than children without the habit of playing video games.28 However, as a popular form of exercise among teenagers, the effect of VR exercise on weight loss and the differences in the effects of physical and VR exercises in adolescents with overweight and obesity remain unknown. Moreover, owing to the potential impact of VR on neural systems, the influence of VR exercise on brain health in adolescents with overweight and obesity remains to be investigated.

Objectives

In our study, we chose one sport for each of the team and racket sports, which was soccer and table tennis and they were reported to have potential benefits on physical and mental health.29–31 This study aims to comprehensively evaluate the differences in the effects of physical and VR exercises on adolescents with overweight and obesity through a broad assessment of clinical parameters. These parameters will include the following: (1) physical health-related parameters, such as anthropometric indicators, information on clinical status, biochemical indicators, autonomic function, psychological condition, exercise-related indicators and ophthalmological examination outcomes and (2) brain health-related parameters, including cognition, food preference and olfaction, will also be assessed. (3) Multiomics analyses will also be conducted, including metagenomics, metabolomics, genomics, lipidomics, proteomics and phenomics. Comprehensive clinical indicators and multiomics data will help elucidate the mechanisms of the effects of physical and VR exercises on adolescents with overweight and obesity.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a randomised controlled clinical trial that will be conducted among adolescents with overweight and obesity from a junior high school and a senior high school. Both schools are located in the same district in Shanghai, ensuring that their sociocultural backgrounds and environmental factors are relatively consistent. The protocol was structured according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials 2013 checklist.32 The participants will be randomly divided into five groups: (1) physical table tennis exercise group, (2) physical soccer exercise group, (3) VR table tennis exercise group, (4) VR soccer exercise group and (5) control group. The participants (44 in each group) will undergo an 8-week intervention. During the intervention period, the control group will maintain their usual lifestyle (keeping the routine physical education (PE) lessons, 45 min per class, three times per week) and participants in the exercise intervention groups will receive additional physical/VR soccer/table tennis classes.

All five groups will receive dietary administration. During the entire trial, participants will receive dietary and lifestyle counselling. The total energy of the diet will be based on the participant’s age and gender and will follow the recommendations of the nutritionist. Participants will be asked to keep a diet diary. Participants will receive visits 1–3, as shown in figure 1. At V1 and V3, blood, saliva, urine and stool samples will be collected. Other physical health parameters will also be assessed. Brain health-related parameters will be measured at V1 and V3, whereas body composition will be measured at V1, V2 and V3. The study was registered with ChiCTR in February 2023. Participants and eligibility criteria

A total of 220 adolescents with overweight or obesity will be enrolled in the randomised controlled trial. Overweight or obesity in this population will be defined according to the body mass index (BMI), calculated as weight (kg)/height∧2 (m∧2) according to the Screening Criteria for Overweight and Obesity in School-age Children and Adolescents Published by the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China.33

Inclusion criteria

Participants will be included if they (1) have provided informed consent from both themselves and their parents, (2) are between the ages of 11 and 17 years (including grades 6–7 and grades 10–11 in two middle school), (3) are overweight or obese, (4) have no medication history of weight-loss drugs and (5) have maintained a stable body weight (self-reported weight change <5 kg) within 90 days before the screening period.

Exclusion criteria

Participants will be excluded if they (1) have participated in an organised weight loss programme within 90 days before the screening period; (2) are currently taking any of the following drugs: pramlintide, orlistat, zonisamide, topiramate, lorcaserin, phentermine, bupropion, naltrexone, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist or metformin (used to treat obesity); (3) have previously undergone surgical treatment for obesity; (4) have muscle, bone, nerve or other tissue and organ damage that would hinder exercise training; (5) have secondary obesity, including endocrine and metabolic diseases (such as Cushing’s syndrome, hypothyroidism, hypogonadism, polycystic ovary syndrome), tumours and trauma; (6) have a history of serious mental illness (such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, major depression); (7) have confirmed bulimia nervosa; (8) have obstacles when using VR such as vertigo; and (9) have any condition that the investigator believes may endanger the safety of the participants or prevent compliance with the protocol.

Interventions

Exercise intervention

The exercise classes will be conducted three times per week for 8 weeks, in addition to the school PE classes. Trainers from Shanghai University of Sport will provide professional supervision and training. Meanwhile, related data, such as the target heart rate (HR) range (minutes) and any injuries or adverse events (AEs), will also be collected.

During the exercise intervention stage, HR monitors will be provided to the participants. These will be linked to a tablet computer application that displays the HR during the session and records the duration spent in the target HR range (minutes). The intensity in the VR and physical exercise groups will be consistent and range from 60% to 70% HRmax (the maximum heart rate). The trainers will gradually increase the subjects’ HR within the tolerance range. Exercise progression will be modified to accommodate any injury or AE or discontinued if the participant is no longer able to exercise safely.

Physical table tennis and soccer group

The participants allocated to the physical exercise intervention group will be divided into the soccer or table tennis intervention groups. Each practice session will last 45 min, including 5 min of warm-up, 20 min of skills training, 15 min of mini-match and 5 min of relaxation. The auxiliary exercises will differ between the two exercise groups. Exercise training will be progressive. The exercise programmes will be divided into four training stages (2 weeks per stage).

VR table tennis and soccer group

Participants in the VR exercise group will undergo VR training using Quest 2 (Meta, USA). The VR training system, named REVERIE (Real-world Exercises and Virtual-reality based Exercises Research In Education) system, used in this study was developed independently by the Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Shanghai Jiao Tong University and the School of Physical Education and Training, Shanghai University of Sport (see figure 2). Unlike existing VR sports games, this VR training system follows the rational kinematic rules and simulations of scientific athletic skill training. Moreover, this system provides detailed first-person perspective teaching assistance that enables adolescents, including beginners, to learn table tennis and soccer easily.

Control group

Participants in the control group will take regular PE classes provided by their school teachers. Participants will be instructed to maintain their current activity levels during the trial.

Diet administration

To control the diet of each participant and reduce the confounding effects of different dietary habits, a dietary intervention will be administered during the entire intervention period. All participants will consume food based on a recommended diet recipe prepared by a nutritionist, according to the Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents34 and Guidelines of medical nutrition therapy for overweight/obesity population in China (2021).35 Specifically, the total energy intake will meet the specific requirements of adolescents. Additionally, the energy distribution among breakfast, lunch and dinner will be approximately 25%–30%, 30%–40% and 30%–35% of the total energy intake, respectively. Moreover, three main macronutrients will be consumed in appropriate proportions, with carbohydrates accounting for 50%−60%, fats accounting for 20%−30% and protein accounting for 15%−25% of total energy intake. Each meal will consist of approximately 1/3 to 1/2 of coarse grains such as oats and quinoa. It is recommended to consume about 250 g of vegetables during lunch and dinner. A daily intake of 300 mL of milk or other dairy products is also recommended. Consumption of snacks high in sugar and sugary drinks will be strictly controlled. All participating students will have lunch at school on weekdays, and other meals will be arranged by their family according to the above recommended recipe. To increase dietary compliance, participants will be asked to keep a food diary and record the type and amount of food or snack they consume. Investigators will assess the dietary records every week to assess their adherence to the dietary regimens and upload the dietary records to the data repository for further analysis.

Process evaluation

Process evaluation will be used to collect data on the implementation of the intervention, contextual factors and participants’ motivation. We will interview any participant who chooses to discontinue their involvement in the study to investigate the factors contributing to their decision to withdraw. The exercise self-assessment questionnaire and exercise quality control checklist will be used to evaluate the fidelity of implementation, dose or intensity of the intervention delivered, quality of programme delivery and engagement of participants. The above questionnaires will be completed by all participants. The exercise quality control checklist will be completed by professional sports quality control staff from Shanghai University of Sport. Questionnaires administered during the process evaluation will be used to explore the motivational factors associated with adherence.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome will be the change in body fat mass measured using bioelectrical impedance analysis (DBA-210, software V.3.5, Donghuayuan Medical, Jilin, China).

Secondary outcomes

Physical health-related parameters

Physical health-related parameters will include the following: (1) anthropometric indicators such as BMI, waist and hip circumferences and skinfold thickness; (2) clinical biochemical indices such as indicators of glycolipid and purine metabolism and kidney and liver functions; (3) body composition such as lean body mass and body fat percentage; (4) physical fitness; (5) ophthalmology indices; and (6) psychological status using coping styles and self-esteem scales for middle school students; etc.

Brain health-related parameters

Brain health-related parameters will include the following: (1) brain structure using anatomical T1-weighted imaging and diffusion tensor imaging; (2) cognitive assessment36–38 using resting-state fMRI, and with n-back and flanker tasks on behavioural experiments and task-based fMRI; (3) food preference evaluation using a Go/No-go task on behavioural experiments and task-based fMRI39 and scales; and (4) olfaction assessment using a set of olfactory tests40 that can assess olfactory threshold, identification and discrimination function. These olfactory tests were created by the Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy Sciences.

Multiomics analysis

Multiomics analysis will include the following: (1) metagenomics using faecal DNA extracts to construct shotgun metagenomic libraries; (2) metabolomics using a high-performance liquid chromatograph and a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with an electron spray ionisation ion source; (3) phenomics such as facial expressions and acoustic features; (4) genomics; (5) lipidomics; and (6) proteomics; etc. The measurement protocols are listed in table 2.

Adverse events

AE refers to any untoward and intervention-related medical occurrence in the participants, like sprain and fracture. AE will be assessed through personal interviews and by exercise trainers during classes and will be recorded on corresponding forms. This includes recording the onset and conclusion, severity and causal relationship with the intervention, in addition to documentation in the study database. A serious adverse event is defined as any unforeseen and unfavourable medical incident, which leads to death, poses a life-threatening situation, necessitates hospital admission (inpatient), prolongs existing hospitalisation or results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity.

Sample size

According to our preliminary experiment, we have estimated that after an 8-week exercise intervention, the difference in body fat mass between the control group and VR exercise group (or physical exercise group) is 1.1 kg, the SD is 1.5 kg and the two-sided t-test α and power are 0.05 level and 80%, respectively. The sample size is calculated using the formula n=2(uα+uβ(1))2σ2δ2 . Given that statistical analysis required multiple comparisons (physical exercise group vs control group, VR exercise group vs control group), α=0.05/2, β=0.2, δ=1.1 and σ=1.5 needed to be corrected. Based on the 1:1:1:1:1 ratio, the minimum sample size required for each group is 37. Considering a 15% loss to follow-up rate, the total sample size required for the study will be approximately 220.

Recruitment

Students aged 11–17 years at Shanghai Bao Shan Middle School and Shanghai Qiu Zhen Middle School will be recruited through flyers, promotional videos and health lectures. Potential participants will be identified by medical specialists, who will act as recruiters. Recruiters will identify students with overweight or obesity as potential participants according to their BMI and establish compliance with the selection criteria described above. All participants will be informed of the purpose and characteristics of the study. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants and their parents or legal guardians.

Allocation, concealment and blinding

Determination of which group a participant would be allocated to will be made by reference to random numbers generated using SAS PROC PLAN in SAS software drawn up for each sex at each middle school. This means that the stratification will be done by sex and school grade (grades 6 and 7 vs grades 10 and 11). Once consent is obtained from the subject, the subject will be allocated to one of the four exercise groups or a control group, at a ratio of 1:1:1:1:1.

As this is an exercise intervention study, blinding of the participants to treatment is not feasible. However, all evaluations will be conducted by blinded independent evaluators, and participants will be instructed not to disclose their group assignments during the testing visits. The participant’s ID number will be the only identifier used for data storage, and any data analysis that may be influenced by investigator bias will be conducted without knowledge of the intervention assignment.

Data management and analysis

Data management

A paper case report form (CRF) will be used in this trial to collect information and will be kept strictly confidential for research purposes. The CRF for each participant will be filled out by trained researchers who are blinded to the group assignments. Researchers will keep files intact, store them in a fixed place and lock them at the Department of Endocrinology and Metabolism, Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. All data will be scrutinised and double-entered by the researchers. After the database is established and confirmed through a blind audit, it will be locked and not changeable.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS (V.25.0; SPSS, Chicago, Illinois, USA). Comparisons will be made between the physical exercise group and the control group, as well as between the VR exercise group and the control group. A comparison between the physical and VR exercise groups will also be performed in an exploratory manner. Several imputations will be used to process the incomplete data values. Data will be presented as the mean SD, median (IQR) and percentage. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test will be used to determine the normality of the data distribution. All statistical tests will be performed with a two-tailed significance level of p<0.05.

The analysis of variance test, Kruskal-Wallis H test and χ2 test will be used to compare baseline characteristics between groups for normally distributed data, non-normally distributed data and categorical data, as appropriate. The paired t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test will be used to compare the baseline and follow-up values for normally and non-normally distributed data. Changes in clinical and multiomics data before and after the intervention will be compared between the groups using covariance analysis, with the baseline variable levels serving as covariates. The effect of exercise intervention on clinical indicators will be evaluated using a mixed-effects model. Multiomics data, such as metagenomic data, will be analysed using the MetaPhlAn software. The Wilcoxon test will be used to compare the abundance of microbiota species before and after the intervention. Differences in microbiota abundance between the groups will be compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Spearman’s correlation and partial correlation analyses will be used to examine the correlations between microbiota abundance, metabolite levels and clinical indicators.

Monitoring

A Data Monitoring Committee (DMC), which is independent of the study organisers, is established. If a participant develops an illness during the course of the study, the DMC and physician will determine whether the participant will continue or terminate the intervention. In the event of a medical emergency at a clinical site, the study staff will provide all necessary supportive medical care under the direction of the principal investigator or designated personnel.

Patient and public involvement

Throughout the ongoing clinical trial, feedback from participants, schools and families were welcomed and considered for minor protocol modifications. During the development phase, several professional coaches and PE teachers participated in the VR exercises. After creating the initial version of the VR exercises, we iteratively refined and updated them based on feedback and suggestions until the requirements were met. This iterative process aims to enhance the immersion and realism of VR exercises, as well as to decrease cybersickness. To improve compliance with dietary administration, suggestions regarding dietary records from school teachers were adopted.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital (Approval No:2022–104) and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry. Any modifications made to the study protocol will be shared with the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital as stipulated, and we will follow their advice regarding implementation and dissemination. Dissemination of the findings will include peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and media releases.

Discussion

In this study, we will collect multidimensional data to assess various phenotypes and explore the mechanisms underlying the effects of physical and VR exercise on adolescents with overweight and obesity. Anthropometrics and body composition will be accurately measured to evaluate the effects of exercise on body shape. Second, biochemical variables and physical fitness will also be measured to assess the metabolic effects of exercise. Third, fMRI and behavioural experiments will be used to evaluate cognition and food preferences among the participants. An olfactory test, an assessment of early risk sign for cognitive impairment,41 will also be conducted. Fourth, HR variability and psychological questionnaires will be used to evaluate autonomic function and emotions. Finally, multiomics evaluations will be conducted to explore the underlying mechanism.

With the widespread application of high-throughput technology, multiomics analysis has developed rapidly, providing new ideas for the study of metabolic diseases such as obesity. Physical exercise alters the diversity and abundance of gut microbiota in individuals with obesity.42 Changes in over 300 recognised metabolites in plasma samples, like long-chain fatty acids and ketone bodies, from human athletes exercising strenuously can be evaluated using liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry.43 Proteomics-based studies have also shown that chronic exercise training increases the concentration of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, tricarboxylic acid cycle and mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I assembly in muscle tissue.44 In this study, we will apply a multiomics analysis to explore the deeper mechanisms underlying the effects of physical and VR exercises on adolescents with overweight and obesity. In addition, data from different omics sources will be integrated using machine-learning-based algorithms to reveal the complex mechanisms underlying these effects.

VR exercise may increase the willingness and ability to engage in sports, helping them get along well with others and improve the quality of life for adolescents struggling with obesity. To our best knowledge, this study will be the first to investigate the differences in the effects of physical and VR exercises on adolescents with overweight and obesity. This study will provide novel and valuable findings for developing treatment strategies for adolescents with obesity and a new approach to investigate VR exercises from the aspect of clinical symptoms, mental state and brain function.

Estimation of Public Health Impact with Use of Nirsevimab During the 2024–2025 RSV Season in the USA: a Modeling Study
Authors: Benjamin Yarnoff, Maureen P. Neary, Veronica Gabriel, Mehdi Ghemmouri, Erin N. Hodges, Maribel Tribaldos, Jeroen Geurtsen, Ayman Chit, Matthieu Beuvelet, Samira Soudani, Robert Musci, Leonard R. Krilov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionRespiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of medically attended lower respiratory tract disease (MA-LRTD) and hospitalization among infants in the USA. With the introduction in 2023 of nirsevimab, a long-acting monoclonal antibody for infant RSV prevention, early data on observed uptake provides an opportunity to quantify its public health impact. We hypothesized that the observed nirsevimab immunization coverage rate during the 2024–2025 RSV season substantially reduced the clinical burden of RSV relative to the pre-2023 standard of practice (SoP) and that increasing coverage levels comparable with other routine pediatric immunizations would yield substantial reductions in RSV-associated outcomes.MethodsWe employed a previously published and validated static decision model to estimate RSV MA-LRTDs under multiple nirsevimab coverage scenarios. The analysis compared projected RSV burden under the pre-2023 SoP where only high-risk infants receive palivizumab with scenarios reflecting the observed national nirsevimab coverage during the 2024–2025 RSV season (44.7%) and higher hypothetical coverage levels up to universal, 100% coverage among infants not eligible for palivizumab. Outcomes included RSV-related hospitalizations, emergency room (ER) visits, primary care (PC) visits, and RSV-associated deaths. Number needed to immunize (NNI) values were also calculated relative to the pre-2023 SoP.ResultsUnder the pre-2023 SoP, the model estimated 523,594 RSV MA-LRTDs among infants during the 2024–2025 RSV season. At the observed nirsevimab coverage of 44.7%, 204,306 RSV MA-LRTDs were averted, including 18,765 hospitalizations (42% reduction), 49,570 ER visits (39%), and 135,942 PC visits (39%). Increasing coverage to 80.4%—aligned with uptake for other routine infant vaccines—was projected to avert 364,204 RSV MA-LRTDs (159,898 additional cases prevented beyond the 44.7% scenario), including 32,404 hospitalizations (73% reduction). Universal coverage (100%) yielded the greatest benefit, averting 451,991 cases (86% reduction) and 39,891 hospitalizations. The number needed to immunize (NNI) was 9 for one MA-LRTD averted, 91 for one hospitalization, and 34 for one ER visit.ConclusionsNirsevimab has already achieved substantial reductions in RSV-associated clinical burden at current national coverage levels. Modeled increases in coverage demonstrate large, incremental public health gains with complete universal immunization yielding up to an 86% reduction in RSV MA-LRTDs. These findings highlight the importance of implementing strategies that enhance access, reduce inequities, and support higher, sustained coverage to maximize population-level benefits of RSV prophylaxis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s41030-026-00348-z.

Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) remains a leading cause of lower respiratory tract disease (LRTD) and hospitalization among infants in the US [1–4]. Before 2023, prevention relied on palivizumab and was available only for infants at an increased risk of severe RSV disease (i.e., pre-2023 Standard of Practice, SoP), leaving more than 90% of infants without protection [5, 6]. The 2023–2024 RSV season marked a major shift with the introduction of nirsevimab, a long-acting RSV-neutralizing monoclonal antibody, that protects infants from RSV disease throughout the entire RSV season through single dose administration [6, 7]. In the USA, nirsevimab is recommended broadly for all infants younger than 8 months born during or entering their first RSV season, and for children 8–19 months of age at increased risk of severe disease entering their second RSV season [8, 9].

In addition, for the 2023–2024 RSV season a maternal RSVpreF vaccine was recommended for administration at 32–36 weeks gestation from September to January to provide RSV protection to infants born during the RSV season from birth through 6 months of age [10, 11]. Notably, the uptake of the maternal RSVpreF vaccine was low during the 2024–2025 RSV season (only 12.3% according to US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC] data [12]). The RSV prevention landscape further evolved in June 2025 with the development and approval of clesrovimab, a second long-acting monoclonal antibody, for the protection of infants against RSV LRTD in their first RSV season [13, 14]. Together, these prophylaxis options represent significant advances in reducing the RSV burden among infants in the USA, though opportunities remain to optimize coverage and ensure equitable protection across the infant population. Although additional RSV prevention strategies, including maternal RSVpreF vaccination and other long-acting monoclonal antibodies, represent important advances in the evolving prevention landscape, uptake of maternal RSVpreF vaccination during the 2024–2025 RSV season remained limited nationally at 12.3% [12]. In addition, clesrovimab was not approved until June 2025 [13, 14] and therefore not available during the 2024–2025 RSV season. Accordingly, the present analysis focused on nirsevimab to estimate its incremental public health impact relative to the pre-2023 SoP.

The introduction of nirsevimab in 2023 together with availability of robust real-world evidence enables assessment of the public health effect of nirsevimab compared with pre-2023 SoP and projection of the additional impact that can be achieved with higher coverage of nirsevimab. Early studies in the USA regarding the implementation of nirsevimab have shown that high coverage is attainable when access and delivery are embedded in routine perinatal care where all infants are offered immunization in the hospital setting. For example, military and academic health systems reported strong coverage, with birth-hospital programs achieving 85.7% overall coverage through standardized counseling and cross-department coordination, and several academic centers reporting 70.1% nirsevimab coverage among eligible infants in the inpatient setting [15, 16]. Hospital-based programs, such as pairing nirsevimab administration with the hepatitis B birth dose and ensuring reliable access through public programs, have demonstrated the feasibility of equitable early-life protection [17].

In contrast, experience from the outpatient settings reveal gaps in reach and persistent inequities. Across pediatric primary care networks, the observed nirsevimab uptake was more modest, with approximately 35–38% of eligible infants receiving prophylaxis and substantial variation across practices [18, 19].

The objective of this study was to estimate the impact of nirsevimab during the 2024–2025 RSV season at observed coverage compared with the pre-2023 SoP and quantify the incremental benefits of higher coverage benchmarks up to a hypothetical, “universal coverage” scenario in which all infants who are not eligible to receive palivizumab receive nirsevimab.

Methods

This study leveraged an existing RSV model developed in Microsoft Excel® which has been previously described in detail [20]. The model leverages a static decision analytic framework that estimates the direct effects of immunization on medically attended RSV outcomes and does not explicitly incorporate RSV transmission dynamics or indirect effects related to reduced viral circulation. Additional detail related to the model structure and approach related to specification of individual immunization strategies and modeling of nirsevimab efficacy is provided in the Supplemental Material.

The analysis in this study focused on the incremental health burden under all-infant protection with nirsevimab where infants born before the RSV season receive one, weight-banded dose of nirsevimab at the start of the RSV season (i.e., beginning of October) and those born within the RSV season receive one, weight-banded dose of nirsevimab at birth compared with the pre-2023 SoP in which only high-risk palivizumab-eligible infants receive monthly administration of palivizumab during the RSV season with a maximum of five doses [5]. Palivizumab use under the pre-2023 SoP was modeled as a fixed comparator reflecting historical recommendations for high-risk infants and was not treated as a dynamic alternative strategy or coverage scenario.

The 2024–2025 RSV season was selected for this analysis because it represents the first season for which nationally reported real-world immunization coverage data for nirsevimab in the USA are available, enabling the estimation of population-level impact under observed implementation conditions. RSV seasonality and circulation within the analysis were modeled using the most recent CDC-aligned assumptions applied in national infant RSV prevention analyses[21, 22] as well as other recently published analyses for the US setting [20], reflecting average multi-season patterns and ensuring consistency with the methodological approaches used by US decision bodies.

We assessed the projected burden of RSV in 2024–2025 if nirsevimab had not been available and compared this with the estimated remaining burden with the introduction of nirsevimab obtained by applying the immunization coverage reported by the CDC for the 2024–2025 season (44.7%) [12]. We further assessed the incremental impact associated with increasing the level of nirsevimab coverage by evaluating several scenarios whereby the coverage for nirsevimab within all infants not eligible for palivizumab was incrementally increased from the observed coverage of nirsevimab during the 2024–2025 RSV season up to a hypothetical “universal coverage” scenario in which all eligible infants in these populations received nirsevimab (i.e., 100% coverage in all infants not eligible for palivizumab). In addition, we modeled an intermediate scenario assuming 80.4% coverage with nirsevimab, which aligns with coverage observed for other routinely recommended pediatric immunizations in the USA, such as the DTaP vaccine series, as reported by the CDC [23]. This level was considered a realistic aspirational target for future RSV prevention efforts, representing the expected coverage that could be achieved with sustained program implementation and equitable access. The coverage for palivizumab under the pre-2023 SoP strategy and nirsevimab within the palivizumab-eligible population was held constant at 82% under all scenarios [24].

The outcomes presented focus on instances of RSV-related medically attended-lower respiratory tract disease (MA-LRTD) comprising the number of RSV-related hospitalizations (including those that lead to intensive care unit [ICU] admission or mechanical ventilation [MV]), emergency room (ER) visits, primary care (PC) visits, and RSV-related deaths. Case count estimates are also stratified by patients born outside of the RSV season (OoS; infants born April to September) versus those born within the RSV season (WiS; infants born October to March).

Results

Of these, 44,482 were hospitalizations (including cases requiring ICU admission or MV), 127,902 ER visits, 351,136 PC visits, and 74 RSV-related deaths (Table 1; Fig. 1). At a nirsevimab coverage rate of 44.7%, reflective of the observed coverage during the 2024–2025 RSV season, the model projected 204,306 RSV MA-LRTDs averted relative to the pre-2023 SoP (Table 2; Fig. 1). This reduction included 18,765 (42% reduction versus pre-2023 SoP) fewer hospitalizations, 49,570 (39%) fewer ER visits, and 135,942 (39%) fewer PC visits (Table 2 and Fig. 2). When stratified by birth timing, infants born within the RSV season (WiS) were estimated to experience 95,052 fewer MA-LRTDs (40.4% reduction), while infants born outside the RSV season (OoS) experienced 109,254 fewer RSV MA-LRTDs (37.9%). These findings reflect substantial reductions across all outcomes even at current coverage levels, emphasizing the importance of immunization for all infants (both WiS and OoS) (Table 3). Increasing nirsevimab coverage from 44.7% to 60% would avert an additional 68,527 RSV MA-LRTDs beyond the 44.7% scenario, totaling 272,833 RSV MA-LRTDs relative to the pre-2023 SoP (Table 2; Fig. 1). This includes 24,610 fewer hospitalizations (55% reduction), 66,317 fewer ER visits (39% reduction), and 181,868 fewer PC visits (39% reduction) (Table 2 and Fig. 2). By birth cohort (Table 3), WiS infants were projected to experience 126,816 fewer RSV MA-LRTDs (53.9% reduction versus pre-2023 SoP), while OoS infants experienced 146,017 fewer MA-LRTDs (50.7%). This demonstrates that modest increases in coverage could yield substantial additional health benefits for both cohorts. Relative to the observed 2024–2025 RSV season scenario, these figures correspond to an additional 5,845 hospitalizations averted (31% more hospitalizations averted), 16,747 additional ER visits avoided (34% more), and 45,926 additional PC visits averted (34% more).

At a target of 80.4% coverage, the model projected 364,204 RSV MA-LRTDs averted relative to the pre-2023 SoP, and additional 159,898 cases prevented beyond the 44.7% scenario (Table 2; Fig. 1). This corresponds to 32,404 fewer hospitalizations (73% reduction versus pre-2023 SoP), 88,647 fewer ER visits (69% reduction), and 243,103 fewer PC visits (69%) than with the pre-2023 SoP strategy (Table 2 and Fig. 2). When disaggregated by birth timing, WiS infants were estimated to experience 169,168 fewer RSV MA-LRTDs (72% reduction versus pre-2023 SoP), while OoS infants experienced 195,036 fewer MA-LRTDs (68%). Relative to the 44.7% scenario, the model estimated 13,638 more hospitalizations averted (73% greater reduction), 39,077 more ER visits avoided (79%), and 107,161 more PC visits averted (79%) (Table 3).

Finally, at 100% coverage, representing optimal universal protection, the model projected 451,991 RSV MA-LRTDs averted relative to the pre-2023 SoP, equating to 247,685 additional cases beyond the 44.7% scenario (Table 2; Fig. 1). This included 39,891 fewer hospitalizations (90% reduction versus pre-2023 SoP), 110,101 fewer ER visits (86% reduction), and 301,937 fewer PC visits (86%) with respect to the pre-2023 SoP strategy (Table 2 and Fig. 2). Among WiS infants, universal coverage would avert 209,859 RSV MA-LRTDs (89.2% reduction versus pre-2023 SoP), while OoS infants would avert 242,132 (84.0%). Compared with the observed 44.7% coverage scenario, these results reflect 21,126 additional hospitalizations averted (113% greater reduction), 60,531 more ER visits avoided (122%), and 165,994 more PC visits averted (122%). This scenario demonstrates the full potential of universal nirsevimab immunization in reducing RSV burden nationwide (Table 3).

Across all modeled scenarios, the NNI to avert one RSV MA-LRTD was 9, one hospitalization was 91, one ER visit was 34, and one PC visit was 13 (Table 4). Discussion

This analysis demonstrated a clear, proportional relationship between increasing nirsevimab coverage and reductions in RSV-related outcomes. Compared with the pre-2023 SoP baseline, the current observed national coverage level (44.7%) has already resulted in substantial reductions in RSV burden, and incremental increases to 60%, 80.4%, and 100% would yield progressively greater gains, leading to an 86% overall reduction in RSV MA-LRTDs under universal coverage.

Given that direct, season-specific validation of modeled estimates using real-world data is inherently limited by differences in study design, populations, and outcome definitions, the present analysis uses the most recent CDC-reported data on RSV hospitalization incidence [21], nirsevimab effectiveness [25], and immunization coverage [12]. Together, these inputs provide face validity and ensure alignment with the analytical frameworks currently used by US public health agencies to assess the population-level impact of infant RSV prevention strategies. However, model-estimated RSV hospitalization counts under the pre-2023 SoP were broadly consistent with prior US-based analyses estimating approximately 50,000 to 80,000 RSV-related hospitalizations annually among children younger than 5 years of age [4, 26–28], with the highest proportion occurring during the first months of life [29, 30]. Within this context, the modeled reductions in hospitalization burden associated with nirsevimab uptake are consistent with recent real-world effectiveness analyses based on the New Vaccine Surveillance Network (NVSN), which reported 41–51% reduction in RSV-associated hospitalizations among infants during the 2024–2025 RSV season compared with the 2017–2020 RSV seasons [31], a period in which approximately 75% of infants received protection with nirsevimab and the remaining 25% received protection through maternal RSVpreF vaccination.

While our base case analysis applied a uniform coverage rate of 44.7% for all infants not eligible for palivizumab, as reported by the CDC for the 2024–25 RSV season [12], this assumption does not reflect the disparities observed in real-world settings. Recent research published by the AAP found that during the 2023–2024 season (the first season in which nirsevimab was available), only 35% of eligible infants received nirsevimab, with rates ranging from 20% to 65% across practices and marked inequities by race, insurance status, neighborhood features, and age [18, 19]. These patterns underscore the influence of social determinants of health and highlight that applying a single coverage rate may mask inequities in impact estimates. Targeted quality improvement efforts are essential to ensure equitable protection for all infants, especially those with lower levels of access due to socioeconomic barriers. Incorporating standardized quality improvement strategies into future implementation plans is critical for improving uptake, achieving better clinical outcomes, and maximizing public health impact.

Nirsevimab has achieved unprecedented uptake among new pediatric vaccines, comparable to the early adoption of varicella and rotavirus vaccines and has already demonstrated meaningful access and ensures that every infant may benefit from protection. While systemic and operational complexities remain, they represent opportunities to strengthen implementation pathways and improve equity. Current administration pathways have contributed to variability in uptake, as many infants miss the optimal window for protection when prophylaxis is not integrated into the hospital program after birth [18]. Addressing logistical challenges, such as appointment scheduling, transportation, and clarity around eligibility, can help streamline delivery and reduce missed opportunities. Similarly, addressing the complex inpatient reimbursement process and payment-related challenges will be critical to enabling consistent inpatient administration, requiring coordinated action from both providers and payers, as noted in recent research on the most feasible way forward [15, 17, 18, 32, 33]. Seasonal demand surges and capacity constraints in pediatric practices underscore the importance of hospital-based and community-integrated approaches. By proactively addressing these factors, we can build sustainable systems that maximize timely administration, reduce disparities, and advance public health goals. Real-world experience supports the feasibility of achieving higher coverage levels with nirsevimab. Several US birth hospitals integrating nirsevimab into standard newborn care workflows have already achieved an uptake exceeding 85% [15]. A key strategy to increase the uptake of RSV prophylaxis is to integrate nirsevimab administration into standard practices. Incorporating nirsevimab alongside the routine newborn dose of the hepatitis B vaccine at birth ensures protection is initiated before infants are discharged and before potential RSV exposure [17]. Additional education among maternity care providers along with coordinated tracking and documentation between maternal and pediatric records are crucial to prevent missed protection.

Beyond hospital-based administration, increasing nirsevimab uptake in outpatient settings will be essential to reaching infants who are not immunized at birth. Real-world studies in the European setting (particularly Spain) have shown high levels of coverage with nirsevimab in infants born both inside and outside the RSV season, with coverage reaching levels of ~90% due largely to early adoption of universal immunization programs and strong primary care engagement [34]. Infants covered under public programs in the USA remain eligible for immunization through the Vaccines for Children (VFC) program, but if hospitals do not routinely administer nirsevimab, these infants may experience delays in protection due to missed or delayed follow-up visits [32]. In contrast, the exclusion of nirsevimab costs from the diagnosis-related group (DRG) payment structure for commercially insured infants represents a disincentive for inpatient administration. It is important to note that infants born during the seasonal administration window (i.e., 1 October through 31 March) are recommended to receive an infant RSV antibody within 1 week after birth—ideally during the birth hospitalization. Through March, any eligible infant or young child who has not yet received a recommended dose should receive an infant RSV antibody at the earliest opportunity. For infants born outside of the seasonal administration window (i.e., April through September) and for young children who are at increased risk for severe RSV disease entering their second RSV season, the optimal timing for infant RSV antibody administration is shortly before the RSV season begins (e.g., October or November) [35]. In all cases, timely outpatient administration (whether at first well-baby visit, weight check, or other encounters when the infant is not moderately or severely ill) remains critical to ensuring protection consistent with the label language for nirsevimab [7].

The implementation of equity-focused outreach programs can help close persistent protection gaps by addressing logistical and social barriers to access as well. Interventions such as providing transportation assistance, mobile immunization clinics, extended clinic hours, and coordination with community health centers or visiting nurse services can improve accessibility for families with limited mobility, inflexible work schedules, or rural residence [36, 37]. Partnering with trusted community organizations, such as women, infants, and children (WIC) programs, federally qualified health centers, and local public health agencies, can further promote awareness and facilitate timely administration, particularly for publicly insured or underinsured populations, who are less likely to receive routine childhood immunizations on time [38]. Embedding proactive reminders and follow-up within electronic health records, coupled with real-time monitoring of immunization coverage, can also enhance continuity of protection across infants’ first RSV season [39]. Together, these outpatient-focused strategies complement hospital-based approaches and are critical to ensuring equitable, population-wide protection against RSV [37].

Higher coverage of nirsevimab may substantially reduce the clinical and economic burden of RSV across US infant populations and alleviate pressure on pediatric healthcare systems. During the RSV season, when medical resource utilization peaks and overlaps with influenza, coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), and other winter respiratory viruses, widespread prophylaxis could reduce hospital admissions and ER crowding, workloads for healthcare providers and nurses, as well as the opportunity costs associated with low immunization rates which all would improve access to care for other pediatric conditions. These system-level benefits align with broader public health objectives to stabilize seasonal care demand and preserve healthcare resource capacity. Although direct medical costs were not explicitly modeled in this analysis, reductions in RSV-associated medical visits suggest significant potential for economic benefits. The average cost of an infant hospitalization event is observed to be quite heterogeneous on the basis of geography and health system (with national averages in 2024 US dollars estimated to range from $9,645 to $36,538 [28] and even higher if admission to the ICU or mechanical ventilation is required). Furthermore, fewer hospitalizations and clinic encounters imply lower expenditures for payers and reduced out-of-pocket costs for families. Moreover, preventing infant RSV illness reduces caregiver work absences, offering broader productivity gains and societal economic value. Achieving high and equitable coverage thus advances public health goals not only by reducing disease burden but also by narrowing disparities that disproportionately affect socioeconomically disadvantaged or underinsured populations.

This analysis has several notable strengths. Stratifying infants by gestational age and comorbidity status allowed the model to apply differentiated RSV MA-LRTD risk rates reflective of each subpopulation. This approach also enabled evaluation of how changes in nirsevimab coverage within the preterm and term infant groups not eligible for palivizumab, while maintaining stable uptake in palivizumab-eligible infants, affect overall disease burden. Such stratification provides a more accurate representation of real-world heterogeneity in RSV risk and prophylaxis access pathways. Further, nirsevimab efficacy over time was modeled using observed and robust pharmacokinetic data, applying assumptions consistent with the latest CDC modeling framework for antibody waning [21].

However, this study also has several limitations. First, the analysis was focused on the 2024–2025 RSV season with respect to observed nirsevimab uptake, while RSV seasonality and timing were modeled using CDC-aligned assumptions derived from multiyear surveillance data (2023–2024), consistent with recent cost-effectiveness [21, 22] and public health impact [20] analyses. As with any modeling approach, year-to-year variation in RSV circulation, timing, and intensity may differ from modeled patterns in a given season, including 2024–2025. Accordingly, the results are intended to characterize the public health impact of nirsevimab under observed early implementation conditions rather than to reproduce RSV circulation patterns in any specific season. The 44.7% coverage applied equally to preterm and term infants in the baseline scenario reflects the CDC-reported national average [12] but does not account for regional, socioeconomic, or seasonal variations in coverage [18]. In particular, infants born OoS or those born WiS in areas with limited hospital integration may experience lower coverage, perpetuating protection gaps. Future research should consider differentiated coverage for such relevant heterogeneity including infants born WiS versus OoS, immunizations occurring in-hospital versus in outpatient clinics, infants born preterm versus term, geography, socioeconomic status, race, and/or ethnicity. Given that this model is static and does not simulate transmission-mediated effects (i.e., only measuring direct protection), reductions in RSV outcomes were observed proportionally with increases in nirsevimab coverage. Next, while nirsevimab efficacy over time was modeled using a sigmoid decay function informed by real-world effectiveness data, the analysis did not include formal probabilistic uncertainty or sensitivity analyses. As such, the results should be interpreted as point estimates intended to illustrate relative differences across coverage scenarios rather than to characterize parameter uncertainty. Additional details on efficacy waning assumptions are provided in the Supplemental Material. The modeling framework was also intentionally designed to isolate the contribution of nirsevimab under observed uptake conditions during the 2024–2025 RSV season. Lastly, this analysis did not include economic outcomes or quality-of-life measures for infants or caregivers. Future studies should examine these dimensions as well and the impact of nirsevimab coverage, particularly as evidence accumulates on the direct and indirect benefits of RSV prevention programs on caregiver productivity and healthcare equity.

Conclusions

This analysis quantified the public health impact of nirsevimab during the 2024–2025 RSV season by comparing observed real-world immunization coverage with the pre-2023 SoP and with higher, aspirational coverage benchmarks. At the nationally reported coverage level of 44.7%, nirsevimab was estimated to avert approximately 204,306 RSV MA-LRTDs, including substantial reductions in hospitalizations, ER visits, and PC visits among infants.

Modeled increases in coverage demonstrated clear and incremental benefits. Achieving coverage levels comparable to other routinely recommended pediatric immunizations (i.e., 80.4%) was projected to avert 364,204 RSV MA-LRTDs, representing approximately a 70% reduction from the pre-2023 SoP strategy. Complete universal coverage was projected to reduce RSV MA-LRTDs by up to 86%, corresponding to 451,991 fewer cases. Together, these findings show that nirsevimab has already generated substantial reductions in RSV-associated clinical burden at current coverage levels, and that further expansion of coverage presents an additional opportunity to considerably reduce RSV-related burden among infants.

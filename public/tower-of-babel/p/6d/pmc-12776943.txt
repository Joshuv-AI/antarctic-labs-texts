Comparative Evaluation of the Efficacy of Two Different Rotary Files on Root Canal Obturation Quality, Postoperative Pain, and Instrumentation Time in Primary Molars: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Shivani Sawant, Ritesh Kalaskar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundThis study aimed to evaluate and compare the efficacy of ProAF Baby Gold and Prime Pedo rotary files for root canal preparation in primary maxillary and mandibular second molars.MethodsChildren aged 4-7 years with primary molars requiring pulpectomy were included in the study. The ProAF Baby Gold files group required less time for biomechanical preparation compared to the Prime Pedo files.The mean pain score in group I system was 0.48 when compared to 0.83 in group II. Introduction

In pediatric dentistry, where natural teeth are considered to be the best space maintainers, an infected primary tooth that causes space loss is a major issue.1 So, preserving primary teeth with extensive caries in the dental arch is of utmost importance for functions such as esthetics, mastication, and phonation.

Root canal treatment in primary teeth is often complex and time-consuming due to their complex and highly curved canal morphology, close proximity to the developing permanent tooth bud, and the additional challenges posed by pediatric behavior management.2 The success of a pulpectomy is dependent on precise diagnosis, thorough biomechanical preparation, and optimal obturation of the root canals.3

Biomechanical preparation is the most vital phase of pediatric pulpectomy, involving both mechanical and chemical cleaning to thoroughly remove necrotic pulp tissue, microbial contaminants, and debris. This phase is designed to establish a clean and unobstructed path that allows irrigants to penetrate deeply into the apical third of the canal, enhancing the removal of debris and biofilm, and ensuring comprehensive disinfection. Additionally, it facilitates the proper shaping of the canal to accommodate and retain the obturating material, ultimately promoting a successful endodontic outcome.3

Conventionally, hand files were used for shaping primary teeth; however, this method had several drawbacks. These included increased treatment time and a higher risk of iatrogenic errors such as canal transportation, zipping, and lateral perforations.2

Introduced in the 1990s, nickel-titanium (NiTi) rotary files transformed endodontics in permanent teeth—enabling faster and more efficient canal preparation than manual files. Their super-elastic metal conforms to canal curves, helps maintain original anatomy, minimizes procedural errors, and delivers consistently high-quality shaping. This technology enables more predictable and uniform root canal shaping, enhancing the overall quality and consistency of treatment. The same rotary and reciprocating NiTi techniques that improve canal debridement and dentin shaping in permanent teeth are equally applicable to primary teeth as well.1

Due to the slender and flattened root canal system in primary teeth, the use of these files may increase the risk of lateral perforations, so the fabrication of an exclusive pediatric rotary system was absolutely necessary.1 Utilizing current rotary methods in primary teeth is restricted by their length and taper. The extended length of standard adult rotary files presents a challenge in pediatric dentistry, as limited mouth opening and smaller oral cavities in children can hinder their effective use. Specially designed pediatric rotary files, typically 16-17 mm in length, have recently become available for use in pediatric patients.4

Barr et al. introduced rotary NiTi files to pediatric endodontics for the first time, stating that “the flexibility of these files allows them to preserve the original anatomy of curved canals in primary molars.”5

The ProAF Baby Gold rotary system is a pediatric rotary file set designed using NiTi controlled memory (CM) wire technology, which allows the files to be precurved before insertion into the root canals. These files feature a triangular cross-section, which helps reduce stress by decreasing contact between the file and the dentin.2

Prime Pedo™ rotary files are specifically designed for pediatric endodontics. These heat-treated files have a shorter length, a 6% taper, and possess CM, making them particularly advantageous for navigating the curved canals of primary molars. Although rotary files developed for permanent teeth have been evaluated in primary teeth, evidence on specialized pediatric rotary systems—including their ability to achieve high-quality obturation, a key predictor of long-term pulpectomy success—remains limited in clinical research.4

This study evaluated how well ProAF Baby Gold and Prime Pedo pediatric rotary files perform in root canal preparation of primary second molars, specifically comparing obturation quality, postoperative discomfort, and instrumentation time in maxillary and mandibular cases.

Methods

This research was undertaken at the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Government Dental College and Hospital, Nagpur.

Approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee was obtained before the study. Participation in the study was voluntary, with informed consent secured from parents, guardians, or caretakers before treatment commenced. This study followed CONSORT guidelines to report randomized control trials.

Data from a study by Juliet et al. were used to calculate the sample size.5 Based on an alpha level of 0.05 and a power of 80%, the sample size was calculated to account for a 20% anticipated dropout rate, yielding a requirement of 29 teeth in each group.

Children aged between 4 and 7 years requiring pulpectomy in primary molar teeth were selected for the study. Teeth with a minimum of two-thirds of root length remaining were included if they met one or more of the following clinical criteria: symptoms indicative of irreversible pulpitis, absence of external pathological root resorption, and absence of mobility. Teeth were excluded from the study if any of the following were present: (1) Swelling; (2) uncooperative behavior in children; (3) pulpal floor perforation; or (4) presence of systemic illness.

Grouping was based on the type of pediatric rotary endodontic system used during biomechanical preparation. Randomization of the sample in both groups was done by a computer-generated random sequence, and allocation concealment was done using opaque sealed envelope.6

A total of 58 participants were randomly allocated into two equal groups, each comprising 29 subjects:

Group I: Treated using ProAF Baby Gold files (Dentobizz, Maharashtra, India).

Group I: Treated using ProAF Baby Gold files (Dentobizz, Maharashtra, India).

Group II: Treated using Prime Pedo files (Sky International Enterprises, Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, India).

Group II: Treated using Prime Pedo files (Sky International Enterprises, Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, India).

All clinical procedures in this study were performed by a single operator. A skilled dental assistant assisted with the procedure and used a digital clock to record the instrumentation time. Following obturation, intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPA) were obtained for all teeth.

Local anesthesia was administered prior to the procedure. Access cavity preparation was performed using an air-rotor (NSK PANA AIR M B2) equipped with a round diamond bur (BR-46; Mani Inc.). The coronal pulp was removed using a spoon excavator, and the canal orifices were identified and evaluated with a DG 16 endodontic explorer (Hu-Friedy Mfg. Co. LLC). Pulp extirpation was then completed, followed by thorough irrigation of the pulp chamber using 0.9% normal saline to ensure cleanliness and debris removal.

Canal patency was initially established by gently introducing a No. 15 K-file (Dentsply Maillefer, Oklahoma, USA) into the canal, accompanied by simultaneous irrigation of the pulp chamber. The working length was then determined radiographically, maintaining a position 1 mm short of the radiographic apex.

Rotary preparation was performed using an endo motor (Orikam E-Connect Pro Endomotor) according to the manufacturer's recommended speed and torque settings. During the use of rotary instruments, canal lubrication and irrigation were maintained using 17% EDTA gel to facilitate smooth instrumentation and debris removal.

Group I

Canal instrumentation was carried out using NiTi ProAF Baby Gold rotary files operated at a speed of 300 rpm and a torque of 2 N·cm. This system comprises five files with a progressive taper ranging from 4 to 6% and includes a specially designed orifice opener (B0). The canals were prepared individually in the following sequence: B0, B1, and B2. In cases of wider canals, an additional file, B3, was utilized to ensure complete preparation.

Group II

Root canal instrumentation in this group was performed using Prime Pedo rotary files operated at 300 rpm with a torque setting of 2.4 N·cm. The sequence began with the starter file (8% taper, 16 mm) for orifice enlargement, followed by P1 (#15, 6% taper, 18 mm) for shaping narrow canals, and P2 (#25, 6% taper, 18 mm) for wider canals. For apical preparation, an endosonic file (2% taper, 18 mm) was used. The instrumentation was performed using the crown-down technique. Throughout the procedure, intermittent irrigation with 0.9% normal saline was carried out in both study groups to ensure cleanliness and debris removal.

Following biomechanical preparation, a temporary restorative material was placed in the access cavity, and the patient was recalled after 3 days. At the subsequent visit, the tooth was carefully evaluated to determine its suitability for final obturation. After removal of the temporary filling, the canals were dried using sterile paper points. Zinc oxide eugenol cement was then introduced into the canals with a plugger, and wet cotton pellets were used to gently condense the material. The tooth was then restored using an intermediate restorative material to ensure an adequate coronal seal.

Evaluation of Teeth

The time required for instrumentation was recorded using a stopwatch, starting from the initiation of biomechanical preparation and ending with the use of the final rotary file. Immediate postoperative radiographs were obtained for all participants to evaluate the quality of obturation (Table 1). The obturation quality was assessed based on the criteria proposed by Coll and Sadrian.7 Investigators responsible for the evaluation were blinded to the group assignment and the obturation technique used. In instances where discrepancies arose between evaluators, the lower of the two scores was considered for analysis.

This scale provided a simple and reliable means for children to express the level of discomfort experienced after treatment.

According to the WB-FPRS pain severity scoring system, the facial expression labeled as “no hurt” indicates the absence of pain, while “hurts a little bit” signifies mild pain. The expressions “hurts a little more” and “hurts even more” are indicative of moderate pain, whereas “hurts a whole lot” and “hurts worst” reflect severe pain. Each face on the scale is associated with a corresponding numerical value: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10, respectively (Fig. 1).8

WB-FPRS

Each recruited participant was thoroughly explained the meaning of each facial expression on the WB-FPRS and ensured to understand the corresponding levels of pain perception. Children were asked to subjectively evaluate their postoperative pain by choosing one of the six facial expressions depicted on the WB-FPRS. Each participant received a copy of the scale to mark the face that best represented their level of discomfort. Pain assessment was documented on the seventh day following the procedure.8

Results

The gathered data were forwarded to a statistician for analysis, wherein the mean and standard deviation were determined.

Instrumentation Time

The time taken for instrumentation in both groups was measured in seconds (Table 2). Group I, which utilized ProAF Baby Gold files, required an average of 153.76 seconds for canal instrumentation, whereas group II, using Prime Pedo files, required a mean time of 189.17 seconds. The results indicated that the ProAF Baby Gold files required significantly less time for biomechanical preparation compared to the Prime Pedo files.

Obturation

The radiographic evaluation of obturation quality was conducted based on the criteria established by Coll and Sadrian. A Chi-squared test was employed to compare the quality of obturation in two groups. In group I, which utilized ProAF Baby Gold files, radiographic assessment of obturation quality showed that four cases were overfilled, 17 were filled to an optimal level, and eight were insufficiently filled.

In the Prime Pedo group, it was shown that 16 samples were optimally filled, three samples were overfilled, and 10 samples were shortly filled (Table 3). The obturation quality between the groups was compared using the Chi-squared test, which indicated no significant difference (Fig. 3).

Discussion

Primary teeth root canal is regarded to be more complex than that of permanent teeth because it has multiple accessory and lateral canals, canal irregularities such as fins and anastomoses, a complex apical delta, and short, slender roots. Pulpectomy of primary teeth is further complicated by the behavioral management of children.

The success of a pulpectomy largely depends on the effective biomechanical preparation of the root canals.9 To achieve improved cleaning and shaping efficacy, endodontic instruments have undergone a number of advancements in recent years.10

One notable development in pediatric endodontics is the introduction of NiTi instruments for the preparation of root canals in primary teeth. The use of NiTi rotary files has led to a decrease in overall canal preparation time and has contributed to more consistent obturation of the canals. However, in all the studies of the literature, NiTi files designed for permanent teeth have been used for pulpectomy in primary teeth (Nagaratna et al., 2006; Subramaniam et al., 2016).11

Rotary instrumentation by employing the crown-down approach with NiTi rotary files facilitates controlled preparation of the apical third of the canal, minimizing the risk of apical debris extrusion. The increased debris extrusion observed with hand instrumentation is primarily due to the piston-like movement of the files during canal preparation. In this study, the rotary system's variable taper design minimized apical preparation and maximized cervical shaping.12

The purpose of this study was to assess the effectiveness of the ProAF Baby Gold and Prime Pedo file systems in preparing root canals of primary maxillary and mandibular second molars.

The study revealed that there was no significant statistical difference in the quality of obturation between the ProAF Baby Gold and Prime Pedo files. The findings regarding obturation quality are in agreement with those of Kohli et al., who compared the obturation quality among three different rotary file systems—Prime Pedo, ProAF Baby Gold, and Kedo SG Blue. They reported that, upon comparing all three file systems, no significant difference was noted (p > 0.05).13

The study demonstrated a significant difference in the instrumentation time between the two files. However, this finding contrasts with the study by Kohli et al., who compared the instrumentation time among three rotary file systems—Prime Pedo, ProAF Baby Gold, and Kedo SG Blue—and found no statistically significant difference. The Kedo SG Blue group showed a notably shorter average instrumentation time of 3.87 minutes, in comparison to 4.14 minutes for the ProAF Baby Gold group and 4.48 minutes for the Prime Pedo group. While the data showed clinical significance, they did not reach statistical significance.9 This shorter time required for instrumentation gradually instills a positive behavior in kids, allowing the treatment process to be completed more quickly.14

Comparison of postoperative pain in two groups showed no significant difference. The findings of our study are consistent with those reported by Hemra Nur Özaşık et al., who observed that the highest mean postoperative pain occurred in groups using NiTi K-type hand files, AF Baby rotary files, and ProTaper Next. In contrast, the lowest pain levels were recorded in the group treated with the WaveOne Gold reciprocal system. However, no significant differences were found among the remaining groups.14

Clinically, shorter instrumentation times contribute to improved patient compliance and reduced operator fatigue.10 However, a long follow-up period is essential to evaluate the clinical and radiographic outcomes of pulpectomy performed using different instrumentation approaches.

Conclusion

The instrumentation time for biomechanical preparation was significantly shorter with the ProAF Baby Gold files compared to the Prime Pedo rotary files. However, the obturation quality and postoperative pain were comparable between the two rotary file groups.

Clinical Significance

A significant reduction in instrumentation time has been observed with the use of rotary instruments for pulpectomy in primary teeth, leading to decreased chairside time and positively influencing the child's behavior.

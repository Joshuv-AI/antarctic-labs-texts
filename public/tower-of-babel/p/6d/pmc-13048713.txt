Anomalous connection of inferior vena cava to left atrium together with cardiac total anomalous pulmonary venous connection and persistent left superior vena cava
Authors: Renu Sharma, Dheeraj Deo Bhatt, Sakshi Sachdeva, Munish Guleria, Dinesh Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A term neonate presented on day 1 with severe cyanosis and metabolic acidosis. Echocardiography and computed tomography angiography revealed a cardiac-type total anomalous pulmonary venous connection to the coronary sinus with anomalous inferior vena cava drainage to the left atrium. Despite intensive management, the infant died. This exceptionally rare dual venous anomaly illustrates abnormal incorporation of a venous pole during cardiac embryogenesis.

A full-term, profoundly cyanotic (SpO2: 50% all four limbs) newborn presented to the pediatric emergency on day 1 of life. He had tachypnea (78/min) and tachycardia (190/min), with poor perfusion. Blood gas analysis revealed severe metabolic acidosis. After resuscitation, an echocardiogram was performed. It revealed situs solitus, levocardia with atrioventricular and ventriculoarterial concordance, and normally related great vessels. The right atrium and right ventricle were significantly dilated [Figure 1a]. A large ostium secundum atrial septal defect (ASD) was present with right-to-left shunt, along with severe tricuspid regurgitation (50 mmHg) [Figure 1b] and severe pulmonary hypertension. Left superior vena cava (LSVC) was present, draining to the coronary sinus [Figure 1c]. The right and left pulmonary veins were seen communicating with a common chamber that drained into the coronary sinus. There was no obstruction at any level. The inferior vena cava (IVC) connection to the atrium was unclear, although it appeared to connect with the right atrium [Figure 1d]. However, saline contrast echocardiography, which can delineate the systemic venous connections, was not performed.[1]

The echocardiogram was quite confirmatory of an unobstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC). However, to clarify the anatomy, a computed tomographic angiogram (CTA) was done. CTA confirmed the diagnosis, revealing a common confluence of all pulmonary veins and LSVC draining into the coronary sinus [Figure 2a]. The IVC was anomalously draining into the left atrium (LA) [Figure 2b]. Atrial appendage morphology on CTA revealed situs solitus, i.e., right appendage was broad and triangular; left was narrow and finger-like. Continuing intensive respiratory and inotropic support, the patient was planned for pulmonary venous re-routing to the LA, with LSVC reimplantation to the right atrial appendage and IVC rerouting to the right atrium. However, the patient expired due to refractory shock and end-organ dysfunction on day 2 of life, preoperatively.

An unobstructed TAPVC presenting on day 1 of life with deep cyanosis and severe metabolic acidosis is a rare presentation. Various plausible factors which could contribute to such a presentation may include metabolic derangements (hypothermia, hypovolemia, and hypoglycemia), severe sepsis, persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn, hypoxic ventricular dysfunction, missed obstruction in the pulmonary venous drainage route, preferential streaming of IVC blood toward LA, or the coexistence of anomalously draining systemic vein into the LA.

The IVC connection to the LA is a rare anomaly. It has been reported in isolation, in association with ASD, in association with partial anomalous pulmonary venous drainage, and in association with common arterial trunk.[2345] Inferior vena cava drainage to the LA in association with supracardiac TAPVC has been reported twice in the past.[67] To the best of our knowledge, so far, there are no reports of a combination of anomalous systemic venous drainage of the IVC into the LA together with a cardiac TAPVC to the coronary sinus.

EMBRYOLOGICAL BASIS

The coexistence of IVC–LA connection, coronary sinus type TAPVC, and persistent LSVC represents distinct but developmentally related outcomes of deviations at the atrial venous pole. However, a single unifying embryological mechanism to explain their simultaneous occurrence remains speculative.

Inferior vena cava–left atrium connection

Normally, the right horn of the sinus venosus becomes incorporated into the right atrium. Failure of this incorporation results in persistence of its continuity with the primitive pulmonary venous atrium, leading to anomalous drainage of the IVC into the LA.

Coronary sinus type total anomalous pulmonary venous connection

The left horn of the sinus venosus usually regresses to form the coronary sinus. When the common pulmonary vein fails to establish its normal connection with the LA and instead retains its primitive communication with the left sinus horn, a coronary sinus type TAPVC results.

Persistent left superior vena cava

This anomaly arises from the failure of regression of the caudal part of the left anterior cardinal vein.

Our understanding of the embryology of normal and abnormal pulmonary venous development continues to evolve. Reports describing such rare combinations may provide valuable insights for future embryologists and morphologists in elucidating the developmental mechanisms and deviations occurring at the venous pole of the heart.[8]

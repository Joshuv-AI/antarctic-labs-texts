Evaluation of construction progress of smart highway: a Bayesian network model
Authors: Chao Wang, Haining Wang, Shang Liu, Wenpeng Liu, Weiling Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate progress control is essential for intelligent highway construction, where the integration of advanced electromechanical equipment, digital technologies, and complex construction interfaces makes traditional progress evaluation methods insufficient. To address this issue, this study proposes a progress evaluation framework tailored to the characteristics of intelligent highway projects. Based on the analysis of project progress factors and construction management requirements, a multi-dimensional indicator system is established to capture both schedule performance and technology-specific construction characteristics. The proposed framework combines expert judgment and quantitative evaluation to support a more comprehensive assessment of construction progress. Using actual project data from an intelligent highway construction case, the model is applied to evaluate the progress status of different construction stages and identify key factors influencing schedule deviation. The results show that the proposed method can effectively reflect the real progress condition of intelligent highway construction and provide actionable support for project managers in making timely corrective decisions. Compared with conventional progress evaluation approaches, the proposed framework is more suitable for intelligent highway projects because it accounts for their technological complexity and management characteristics. This study offers a practical evaluation tool for progress monitoring and contributes to improving the precision and adaptability of intelligent highway construction management.Keywords: Smart highway, Construction progress evaluation, Schedule delay prediction, Probabilistic inference, Decision analysis, Bayesian networkSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As an important part of the national traffic network, the highway promotes the close connection between various cities and the exchange of various materials1. The total length of China’s highways ranks first in the world and maintains a steady annual growth rate2. However, with the rapid development of science and technology, people’s requirements for travel comfort and intelligence are also getting higher and higher, making the development of intelligent transportation has become an inevitable trend of road development3. In this context, the Chinese government has proposed the major strategic deployment of building a “transportation power” and “digital China”4,5, emphasizing the integration of advanced technologies such as the Internet and artificial intelligence with the transportation industry to speed up the intelligent development process of road traffic.

With the rapid development of information technologies such as the Internet of Things, big data, artificial intelligence, and cloud computing, the concept of smart highways (SHs) has gradually become an important direction for the transformation and upgrading of transportation infrastructure. In this context, effective construction progress management is a critical prerequisite for ensuring the successful implementation of SHs projects. Due to the integration of intelligent equipment, digital platforms, and complex technological systems, SHs construction projects often involve more interdisciplinary coordination and higher technical complexity than traditional highway projects. Consequently, evaluating and monitoring construction progress plays an essential role in helping project managers understand the actual implementation status of SHs projects and take timely corrective measures when necessary.

At present, domestic and foreign scholars have carried out a lot of research work on highway progress control, and many scholars have adopted many methods, such as fuzzy-based model6, balanced tradeoffs7, artificial neural networks8, cyber-physical systems-based approach9 to evaluate the construction progress of highway. Although these studies can provide a certain reference for evaluating the construction progress of intelligent highway, they do not pay attention to the characteristics of intelligent highway, such as the use of advanced mechanical and electrical equipment, construction technology, etc. This neglect leads to the lack of appropriate qualitative and quantitative progress evaluation methods in the practice of intelligent highway construction, which makes it difficult for managers to grasp the actual situation of intelligent highway construction and then take appropriate corrective measures.

In summary, although previous studies have provided valuable methods for evaluating construction progress in highway projects, most of them focus on traditional highway construction scenarios. The unique characteristics introduced by intelligent technologies in SHs projects—such as intelligent equipment integration, digital management platforms, and intelligent monitoring systems—have not been systematically incorporated into existing progress evaluation frameworks. Moreover, the complex interdependencies and uncertainties among these intelligent factors remain insufficiently modeled in current research. As a result, a comprehensive evaluation approach that can capture the interactions among multiple intelligent factors and assess the construction progress of SHs under uncertainty is still lacking.

To address this research gap, this study proposes a Bayesian network–based construction progress evaluation model for smart highway projects. The model identifies key influencing factors of SHs construction progress and represents their causal relationships through a probabilistic graphical structure. By performing forward reasoning, backward reasoning, and sensitivity analysis using GeNIe Modeler 4.0.3005.0 (https://download.bayesfusion.com/files.html?category=Academia#GeNIe), the proposed approach enables quantitative evaluation of progress risks and identification of critical factors affecting schedule performance.

Literature review

Smart highway construction project schedule management

In highway construction projects, progress is one of the major issues faced by the government and construction units. Progress management is a key factor affecting the accuracy, reliability and rationality of highway construction projects7. More and more highway construction projects are experiencing progress delays, resulting in a large amount of time and financial losses10. Therefore, effective progress management in highway construction projects is crucial to reducing costs and improving implementation efficiency. The progress of highway construction projects is affected by a variety of complex factors. With the rapid development of information technology represented by artificial intelligence and cloud computing, the new generation of SHs reflects the automation and real-time nature of intelligent traffic management systems11–13, and its progress management faces more uncertainties. Therefore, evaluating the progress of SHs construction projects is an important part of ensuring that it is completed efficiently with smart technology.

In terms of highway construction project progress management, Papajohn, et al.14 proposed that using design-build (DB) and construction manager/general contractor (CM/GC) instead of contracting methods will improve the effect of progress control. Nguyen, et al.15 proposed to use fuzzy cluster analysis to analyze the progress, costs, complexity, risks, etc. of highway construction projects. In order to shorten the construction time, Altuwaim and El-Rayes16 developed a new multi-objective optimization model for highway repeated construction projects, which can achieve the best trade-off between the project time, work interruption, and overtime use, and obtain the optimal progress management strategy. Lee, et al.17 support to implement the construction of construction ability review during the design and development of highway construction projects. Alikhani, et al.18 utilize the historical daily work report to predict the sequence of highway construction activities to train long-short term memory circulating neural networks to predict the sequence of activity and the overlap in future projects. Most studies have considered the progress monitoring and control of highway construction projects using different methods, and others also emphasize the use of information technology to optimize construction progress. However, they have not fully considered the characteristics of SHs construction projects, unable to achieve accurate progress evaluation.

As for influencing factors in the progress management of SHs construction projects, different project delivery methods19, mobilization costs20, productivity estimation, and logical development21, contract type22 are thought to be indispensable. There are also scholars like Wang, et al.23 proposed to use non-balanced data. And Harper, et al.24 proposed to take smart instruments and sensor technology for highway construction projects progress management. Other scholars take intelligent technology and intelligent data as influencing factors to study highway project progress control. Although these studies can provide some references for SHs construction projects progress management, SHs’ characteristics are ignored, probably leading to inaccurate evaluation. The existing studies of SHs research propose to consider the location selection of intelligent mechanical and electrical facilities, the use of intelligent technology, the supply of intelligent resources, intelligent organizational management, and other key factors. Considering these factors will improve the accuracy and scientific of progress management of SHs construction projects and reduce errors.

Above all, this study analyzes the differences between SHs and traditional highways in resource requirements and technological requirements. Then the progress influencing factors with the characteristics of SHs construction projects are determined to evaluate the progress.

Application of Bayesian networks in project management

A Bayesian network (BN) , also called a causal network or Bayesian belief network, is a powerful tool for knowledge representation and reasoning under uncertain conditions25. BN is a product of the combination of probability theory and graph theory, which is essentially a kind of uncertainty inference network based on probability theory. It can visually present the probabilistic relationships among a set of variables, and the expression form has the advantages of intuition, strong credibility, easy to understand and so on. This intuitive method is usually applied in the fields of user intelligent interaction, data mining, information retrieval, predictive decision-making and so on. BNs have been effectively applied to PPRM. Ghasemi et al.26 constructed a BN model to analyze project interdependency and causal relationships among PPRs to provide valuable information for making strategic decisions. Afzal et al.27 constructed a hybrid risk decision framework using a combination of fuzzy logic and BN to identify and assess the key complexity and risk interdependencies of cost overrun problems in building construction. Leu, et al.28 proposed a real-time predictive cost overrun risk prediction model based on the influencing factors and their interdependencies as well as the corrective actions taken, using a dynamic BN, to provide project managers with early alerts of cost overruns.

The above studies all effectively model the interrelated relationships among projects, risks or influencing factors using BN, providing practical tools for solving strategic decision-making, risk prediction, cost control and other problems in project management. As the assessment of the progress of SHs construction in this study involves multiple evaluation indicators with interrelated relationships, the BN inference method is applicable to the construction of the assessment model in this study.

Based on the above discussion, this study proposes a Bayesian network-based progress assessment model to analyze the impact of four types of influencing factors on the progress of SHs construction, considering the complexity of SHs progress assessment criteria and the interactions among them, and to provide support for managers to identify the key influencing factors and formulate relevant strategies.

Identify of the smart highway construction influence factors

The implementation of SHs progress control should be based on systematic and holistic while taking into account the process and results. To identify the key factors affecting the construction progress of SHs, an expert consultation approach was adopted. Six experts from a major highway construction group in Shandong Province were invited to participate in the evaluation process. All experts possess over ten years of specialized experience in highway construction, project management, or intelligent transportation systems, and all hold senior professional titles. Their expertise covered project planning, construction management, and intelligent system implementation. The use of expert knowledge is widely adopted in Bayesian network modeling when historical data are limited but domain knowledge is available.

All methods in this study were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations and with the Declaration of Helsinki. This study only involved anonymized expert consultation and questionnaire-based evaluation and did not involve biomedical experiments, human tissue samples, clinical intervention, animal experiments, or human transplantation. According to the institutional research ethics requirements of Shandong Provincial Communications Planning and Design Institute Group Co., Ltd., formal ethical approval was not required. Written informed consent was obtained from all participating experts prior to data collection, and all responses were anonymized and used only for academic research and publication.

The expert team summarizes and screens the evaluation indexes of motorway progress control by combining relevant research based on considering the difficulties of SHs projects and the advantages of intelligent management. Specifically, the preliminary indicators were first identified through a review of relevant literature on highway construction progress management and intelligent transportation systems. These candidate indicators were then evaluated by the six experts according to several criteria, including their relevance to smart highway construction progress, clarity and measurability, and applicability to practical project management. Indicators that were considered redundant, unclear, or weakly related to smart highway progress management were removed after expert discussion. During the consultation process, experts provided their judgments through structured discussions and questionnaire feedback. Although a formal Delphi procedure with multiple anonymous rounds was not conducted, the expert opinions were compared and discussed collectively to reach a reasonable level of consensus, which helped ensure the completeness and objectivity of the final indicator system. Through expert discussion and comparison with traditional highway progress management practices, the preliminary set of indicators was refined to ensure their representativeness and applicability to smart highway construction.

Compared with traditional highway construction projects, smart highway projects rely more heavily on intelligent equipment, digital management platforms, and data-driven decision-making processes. These characteristics introduce new requirements for construction resource allocation, information integration, and real-time monitoring. Therefore, the evaluation indicators in this study emphasize aspects such as intelligent equipment suitability, information platform maturity, and intelligent monitoring capability, which are particularly relevant to smart highway construction environments. Finally, the secondary evaluation indexes that appear more frequently among the first-level indexes include: the applicability of intelligent equipment selection, the standardization of SHs operation, the ability to improve the schedule, and the level of material supply and management. From the above four perspectives, based on the evaluation indexes identified by comparing the effects of traditional highway progress management, the framework model of the above index system is further optimized and collated to form a set of evaluation index systems on SHs progress control. Although the indicators in the system may appear similar to those used in traditional highway projects, their connotations differ significantly in the context of smart highway construction.

The term “intelligent” in this study does not merely refer to basic technological tools or equipment but encompasses a broader understanding of advanced technological integration, real-time data management, automation, and system connectivity that are critical in the successful implementation of smart highways. For instance, the “applicability of equipment selection” in the context of smart highways involves selecting equipment and technologies that are capable of supporting advanced systems such as intelligent traffic management systems, automated vehicle tracking, and integrated digital platforms. These technologies go beyond simple operational efficiency to enhance real-time traffic monitoring, predictive maintenance, and adaptive traffic flow control. Similarly, “maturity of information platforms” in smart highways refers to the readiness of digital platforms that handle vast amounts of data, integrating information from sensors, cameras, and traffic systems to ensure optimal performance and decision-making throughout the highway’s operation.

Therefore, while the terminology and structure of the indicator system are consistent with traditional highway management models, the focus in smart highways is on how these technologies work together to create a more responsive, efficient, and adaptive infrastructure system.

Intelligent resource supply capacity

The intelligent resource supply capacity represents the supply level of intelligent resource conditions. To change the single progress control method in the traditional management mode, the SH can make use of multiple information fusions to establish a progress intelligent management information platform to achieve intelligent and visualized management of the progress conditions in all stages of the whole life cycle of the SH29. The intelligent resource condition is the premise of realizing SH progress, and the level of its supply directly affects the efficiency of progress management. Therefore, the intelligent resources supply comprises three parts: Suitability of smart equipment selection, Maturity of intelligent information management platform, and Proficiency of intelligent highway technicians. Suitability of smart equipment selection refers to the overall compatibility between intelligent equipment and the functional requirements of smart highways. This indicator considers several technical dimensions, including advancement, compatibility, stability, and scalability of the selected equipment. These dimensions jointly reflect the adaptability of intelligent equipment in the smart highway construction environment. Therefore, in this study they are integrated into a comprehensive indicator to represent the suitability of intelligent equipment selection. Maturity of intelligent information management platform means whether the intelligent platform can match the construction operation and management, and achieve the functions of sensing, recording and management optimization of the key elements on the construction site30. Proficiency of intelligent highway technicians refers to whether the technicians can apply their skills to analyze the intelligent problems occurring in their positions and put forward countermeasures. and propose countermeasures. Therefore, this paper adopts all three to reflect the level of intelligent resource supply.

Intelligent organization management ability

Intelligent organization management ability refers to the collaborative management of manpower, machinery, and environment in SHs projects, in which personnel are both the main body of SHs progress control and the executor of project control. In the highway progress control process will inevitably have multiple bridges, tunnels, roadbed filling and excavation at the same time, in the case of many parties involved, progress data is complicated, and good intelligent organizational management for the enterprise project can be completed on time has an important role31. Intelligent organizational management capability is reflected by the level of organizational management, which indicates the reasonable degree of division of responsibilities among all levels of personnel as well as the degree of standardization of intelligent highway operations. To ensure the effective implementation of progress control, the construction of SHs needs to improve the timeliness and efficiency of information exchange among all participants, so as to achieve the transformation of intelligent progress control. Therefore, intelligent organization and management capability involve these three parts. Standardization of intelligent highway operation refers to the degree of standardization of joint design, deepening design, testing and verification, joint commissioning and other work in the process of intelligent highway construction32. Information level of business processes refers to the degree of informatization of business processes such as recording, approving and checking in the process of intelligent highway construction. Rationality of division of responsibilities refers to the division of duties between departments and personnel in line with the characteristics of intelligent highway management, adapting to the intelligent platform management, operational norms, and information requirements, with clear division of powers and responsibilities, and a high degree of collaboration and enthusiasm among various departments.

Intelligent progress optimization ability

Optimization of progress intelligence levels runs through the design stage of SHs construction as well as the construction process, which usually refers to the use of intelligent technologies and tools in project management to improve the efficiency and accuracy of project progress. Before the construction of the SH, the three-dimensional realistic model of the construction site can be established and the construction progress simulation can be completed in advance, so as to avoid unforeseen difficulties in the construction and reduce the number of design changes. In the process of SHs progress control, due to the danger of high slopes and bridge bearing platforms detecting quality problems artificially, it is possible to use drone aerial survey technology can achieve online real-time monitoring, improve the efficiency of progress control, and then shorten the construction period, so that the construction participants can obtain greater benefits, so intelligent progress optimization ability including two aspects33. Capability of duration improvement refers to the degree of impact on the schedule caused by the adoption of intelligent management tools during the construction of intelligent highway. Frequency of design changes refers to the number of design changes in the process of intelligent highway construction34.

Intelligent progress monitoring ability

SHs projects usually have long mileage and complex construction environments, and it is difficult to grasp the on-site dynamics promptly during the progress management process, there are problems such as turnover materials such as reinforcement, concrete, templates, etc. which cannot be supplied in time as well as poor timeliness of the management decisions issued, resulting in the progress of work in progress phenomenon. Therefore, managers usually combine the drone tilt camera technology with BIM technology to restore the original appearance of the site progress in real time, and carry out refinement processing on the progress data collected on site to guide the management personnel to construct according to the plan. This enables real-time tracking of progress control, thereby supporting more efficient monitoring and tracking of construction progress35. Therefore, Intelligent progress monitoring ability includes two aspects36. Level of material supply and management refers to the timeliness of the supply of the required swing materials and the degree of control over the status of the swing materials. Ability to monitor and track progress on site refers to the timeliness of site dynamics in the process of progress control.

Structure of the Bayesian network

BN is a useful tool for modeling and analyzing complex systems37. It can quantify uncertainty in the form of a directed acyclic graph using a series of conditional probabilities based on the Bayesian theorem38. A BN consists of qualitative and quantitative parts. The qualitative part comprises a set of nodes and directed edges, which represent the causal relationships among the connected nodes39. A node from which an arc directs to another is a parent node, while the other node is a child node. The quantitative part is constructed with conditional probability tables to describe the quantitative dependency strength between parent and child nodes40. Root nodes without parent nodes were assigned prior probability values and intermediate/leaf nodes with parent nodes were assigned conditional probability tables.

In this study, the causal structure of the BN was determined based on expert knowledge and domain experience. The experts evaluated whether causal relationships existed between influencing factors according to their understanding of smart highway construction processes. These causal relationships were further checked against existing literature on project management and BN modeling to ensure the rationality of the network structure.

To ensure the reliability and quality of the expert data used in constructing the Bayesian network structure, several measures were adopted in the expert consultation process. The participating experts possessed substantial professional experience in highway construction management, intelligent transportation systems, and project management. Their professional backgrounds ensured that the judgments regarding causal relationships among influencing factors were grounded in practical engineering experience.

To enhance the reliability of expert evaluations used in determining causal links, all six experts’ judgments were cross-checked for internal consistency. Pairwise comparisons of causal relationships were reviewed, and any inconsistencies were discussed and reconciled through additional consultations. Because of the limited number of experts, statistical reliability indices such as Cronbach’s α were not applicable; instead, conceptual consistency verification was adopted to ensure the credibility and stability of expert-based judgments.

In addition, the expert evaluation results were reviewed and compared during the consultation process to reduce potential bias and improve the consistency of the judgments. Through this process, the expert data used to determine the causal relationships in the Bayesian network achieved an acceptable level of reliability and provided a credible basis for the subsequent model construction and inference analysis.

Model application

To verify the applicability of the proposed evaluation model, a smart highway construction project located in Shandong Province, China, was selected as the case study. The project is part of the regional expressway network and aims to improve traffic efficiency and intelligent transportation services through the integration of advanced technologies such as intelligent sensing devices, communication systems, and digital management platforms.

The total length of the project is approximately 161.86 km, with a total investment of 42.1 billion RMB. The project incorporates several intelligent transportation components, including intelligent monitoring systems, traffic information collection equipment, and digital operation platforms, making it a representative example of smart highway construction in China.

The construction progress evaluation was conducted with the assistance of Shanxi HD Consulting Co., Ltd., a professional engineering consulting organization specializing in infrastructure project management and evaluation. Although the consulting company is located in Shanxi Province, the participating experts were directly involved in the consulting and management services of this project and were familiar with the project’s construction conditions and intelligent system deployment. Expert judgments were collected through structured questionnaires and interviews to ensure the reliability of the evaluation results.

The SHs construction projects progress evaluation model identified in this study is universal and can be applied in other infrastructure progress evaluation. In order to demonstrate its effectiveness and application, this study uses a highway construction project in Shandong as an example, which was evaluated by the consulting enterprise HD Consulting Co., Ltd in Shanxi Province. It has professional advantages such as project management training, consulting engineering project management services, and international project management expert database certification. In addition, it has accumulated a wealth of successful experience in highway construction projects progress evaluation practices, such as a highway construction project in Xi’an, Shaanxi, and Nanning, Guangxi, which have been widely acclaimed. Most of the existing employees of it are professors, senior consultants, senior trainers, project managers, and senior engineers, possessing rich wisdom technology, theoretical knowledge, and working experience in progress evaluation of highway construction projects. Therefore, this case can fully prove the effectiveness and applicability of the SHs construction projects progress evaluation model proposed in this study.

Forward reasoning

Forward inference is conducted to analyze the probability distribution of schedule performance under the current conditions of the smart highway construction project. The probability values of the first-level indicators obtained from the Bayesian network model are G1 = 90%, G2 = 83%, G3 = 92%, and G4 = 88%. All conditional probabilities were calculated from the same expert dataset. Experts provided linguistic judgments (Very Low – Very High) that were transformed into trapezoidal fuzzy numbers and then defuzzified into numerical probability values using the weighted-average approach. An example of G11 → G1 conversion is included to illustrate this process. Specifically, expert scoring was collected from professionals with extensive experience in highway construction management and intelligent transportation systems. The collected expert judgments were used to construct the conditional probability tables of the Bayesian network, and the parameters were further calibrated through maximum likelihood estimation. Therefore, the probability values of the first-level indicators used in the forward inference are obtained from empirical expert knowledge rather than artificially set hypothetical scenarios.

According to the inference results, the overall probability of the smart highway construction project achieving normal schedule performance is relatively high, indicating that the current intelligent progress management measures can effectively support the implementation of the project.

In Fig. 3, numerical values inside nodes indicate the posterior probabilities of two states (“normal” and “delay”). Green and red bars represent the probabilities of normal and delayed states respectively, providing an intuitive comparison of schedule performance likelihood.

Backward reasoning

Backward reasoning (also known as diagnostic reasoning) refers to the use of back propagation techniques to propagate the changes in the probability of a child node into the network, updating the probability of the occurrence of the parent node, thereby updating the probabilities of potential causal factors and analyzing their probabilistic relationships with the observed outcome, assuming that a certain event has already occurred.

Figure 4 illustrates the updated posterior probabilities when the target node “schedule delay” is fixed at 100%. The probability decrease of its parent nodes demonstrates their probabilistic association with the observed delay event.

When the probability of “schedule delay” is set to 100%, the posterior probabilities of the four first-level indicators are updated accordingly. Specifically, intelligent resource supply capacity (G1) decreases from 90 to 75%, intelligent organization management ability (G2) decreases from 83 to 79%, intelligent progress optimization ability (G3) decreases from 92 to 73%, and intelligent progress monitoring ability (G4) decreases from 88 to 82%.

Further analysis of the second-level nodes shows that several indicators, including intelligent information management platform (G13), construction personnel management capability (G22), and mechanical equipment management level (G41), also exhibit relatively noticeable probability changes after the backward inference process. It should be noted that the posterior probability values obtained from backward inference do not directly represent the absolute importance of each influencing factor. Instead, they indicate how the probabilities of potential causal factors are updated when the outcome (schedule delay) is observed.

In this study, G1 and G3 show relatively larger probability changes than the other first-level indicators, and sub-indicators such as the intelligent information management platform (G13) and the level of material supply and management (G41) also show noticeable changes. This suggests that these factors have stronger probabilistic associations with the occurrence of schedule delay under the current Bayesian network structure. Discussion

Forward inference results

The forward inference results indicate that smart highway construction projects remain exposed to a non-negligible risk of schedule delay under the combined influence of multiple factors. This finding suggests that the introduction of intelligent technologies and digital management tools does not automatically eliminate schedule risks in complex infrastructure projects. One important reason lies in the systemic complexity of smart highway construction. Unlike conventional highway projects that are largely dominated by civil works, smart highway projects involve the simultaneous implementation of civil infrastructure, electromechanical systems, intelligent sensing devices, and digital information platforms. These subsystems are highly interdependent, and delays occurring in one subsystem may propagate throughout the entire construction process. For example, the installation of intelligent equipment typically depends on the completion of civil works, while the deployment of digital platforms requires stable integration of sensing devices and communication networks. Such interdependencies substantially increase the difficulty of schedule coordination.

This coordination challenge has also been highlighted in studies on digital construction management. For instance, Oraee, et al.49 showed that collaboration in BIM-based construction networks is frequently constrained by organizational and interdisciplinary barriers. Their findings suggest that although digital technologies such as BIM can enhance information sharing and coordination, their effectiveness depends heavily on the ability of project participants to collaborate across organizational boundaries. In other words, technological tools alone cannot guarantee improved project performance unless they are supported by effective organizational coordination and management integration. Therefore, the schedule delay risk identified in this study should be interpreted not only as a consequence of technological complexity, but also as the result of the interaction between digital systems and the managerial capacity required to coordinate them in smart infrastructure construction.

Backward inference results

The backward inference analysis indicates that intelligent resource supply capacity (G1) and intelligent progress optimization capability (G3) constitute the primary direct drivers of schedule delay in smart highway construction. In addition, several secondary indicators—including the proficiency of intelligent highway technicians (G13), the capability of duration improvement (G31), the informatization level of business processes (G22), and the level of material supply and management (G41)—are identified as key indirect factors influencing schedule performance.

The prominence of intelligent resource supply capacity (G1) can be explained by its foundational role in supporting intelligent construction activities. Smart highway projects rely on the effective integration of intelligent equipment, digital management platforms, and skilled technical personnel. If the supply of these resources is insufficient or poorly coordinated, the intelligent construction system cannot function effectively, thereby increasing the likelihood of schedule disruption. Similarly, intelligent progress optimization capability (G3) directly affects the ability of project teams to improve construction efficiency through digital technologies and advanced planning methods. Tools such as construction simulation, intelligent scheduling systems, and digital management platforms provide opportunities to optimize construction sequences and resource allocation. However, if these optimization capabilities are limited, project teams may struggle to respond effectively to dynamic construction conditions, resulting in schedule delays.

The indirect factors identified in the analysis further illustrate how specific operational conditions influence schedule outcomes through intermediate mechanisms. For instance, the proficiency of intelligent highway technicians (G13) determines whether intelligent technologies can be properly implemented in practice. The capability of duration improvement (G31) reflects the extent to which project teams can utilize digital tools to enhance scheduling efficiency. Meanwhile, the informatization level of business processes (G22) affects the efficiency of information exchange and decision-making, while the level of material supply and management (G41) directly influences the continuity of construction activities. Taken together, these findings suggest that schedule delay in smart highway construction is not determined by a single factor, but rather emerges from the interaction between technological resources, managerial capability, and operational conditions within the intelligent construction system.

It should be noted that the posterior probability values obtained from backward inference do not directly represent the absolute importance of each influencing factor. Instead, they indicate how the probabilities of potential causal factors are updated when the outcome is observed. To further evaluate the relative importance of these influencing factors, sensitivity analysis is conducted in the following section. By combining the results of backward inference and sensitivity analysis, the key factors affecting the construction progress of smart highway projects can be identified more reliably.

Sensitivity analysis

The sensitivity analysis further reveals that intelligent resource supply capacity (G1), intelligent progress optimization capability (G3), and intelligent progress monitoring capability (G4) exert the most significant influence on the probability of schedule delay in smart highway construction. These factors therefore represent the most sensitive nodes within the Bayesian network.

The high sensitivity of intelligent resource supply capacity (G1) can be attributed to its foundational role in supporting intelligent construction activities. As the primary source of technological resources—including intelligent equipment, digital platforms, and technical personnel—G1 directly determines whether intelligent construction systems can operate effectively. Any change in the availability or performance of these resources may rapidly influence the overall efficiency of construction management, thereby affecting the likelihood of schedule delay. Similarly, intelligent progress optimization capability (G3) plays a critical role in determining how effectively project teams can transform technological resources into improved construction performance. In smart highway construction, optimization tools such as construction simulation, digital scheduling platforms, and data-driven decision support systems allow project managers to adjust construction plans and resource allocation dynamically. As a result, improvements in optimization capability can significantly enhance the resilience of construction schedules when facing uncertainties in project execution50.

In addition, intelligent progress monitoring capability (G4) demonstrates strong sensitivity due to its role in providing feedback within the project management system. Effective monitoring enables project managers to detect schedule deviations at an early stage and implement corrective actions before minor disruptions evolve into major delays. In the context of smart highway construction, digital monitoring technologies such as UAV-based inspection, sensor networks, and BIM-enabled progress visualization significantly enhance the transparency of construction processes. Consequently, improvements in monitoring capability can substantially reduce the probability of schedule disruption. In comparison, several secondary indicators—including the capability of duration improvement (G31), the level of material supply and management (G41), the proficiency of intelligent highway technicians (G13), the suitability of intelligent equipment selection (G11), and the informatization level of business processes (G22)—exhibit relatively lower sensitivity. Although these factors influence construction performance, their impacts are typically transmitted through intermediate nodes within the system. As a result, the influence of these variables on the final schedule outcome is moderated by the broader structure of the intelligent construction management system.

The sensitivity analysis highlights that schedule delay risk in smart highway construction is primarily driven by factors related to technological resource readiness, optimization capability, and monitoring feedback. These elements form critical leverage points within the intelligent construction system, where targeted improvements may generate substantial reductions in schedule delay risk.

Integrated interpretation

By integrating the findings from the forward inference, backward inference, and sensitivity analyses, a systemic mechanism of schedule management in smart highway construction can be identified. The results indicate that schedule delay risk does not stem from a single isolated factor, but rather emerges from the interaction between technological resources, managerial capability, and information feedback within the intelligent construction system.

Specifically, three interrelated dimensions shape schedule performance. Intelligent resource supply capacity (G1) provides the technological foundation for smart highway construction by ensuring the availability of intelligent equipment, digital platforms, and technical expertise. Intelligent progress optimization ability (G3) reflects the ability of project teams to translate these technological resources into effective planning and execution strategies. Meanwhile, intelligent progress monitoring ability (G4) serves as the feedback mechanism that enables project managers to detect schedule deviations and respond to emerging risks in a timely manner. Together, these elements form a closed-loop structure of intelligent schedule management characterized by resource readiness, process optimization, and monitoring feedback. The effectiveness of this loop determines the overall resilience of construction schedules in smart highway projects. When these components operate in coordination, intelligent technologies can significantly enhance schedule control; however, weaknesses in any part of the loop may disrupt the interaction between technological systems and managerial processes, thereby increasing the risk of schedule delays.

From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to research on digital construction management by highlighting the systemic interaction between intelligent technologies and managerial capabilities in infrastructure projects. From a practical perspective, the findings suggest that improving schedule performance in smart highway construction requires not only technological investment, but also the strengthening of organizational coordination, technological integration, and real-time monitoring mechanisms.

Conclusion

This study developed a Bayesian network–based framework to analyze the factors influencing schedule delay in smart highway construction projects. By integrating expert knowledge with probabilistic inference, the proposed model enables a systematic evaluation of the complex interactions among technological, managerial, and operational factors affecting construction schedule performance. Forward inference, backward inference, and sensitivity analysis were conducted to identify the overall schedule delay risk, the key causal drivers, and the most sensitive factors within the intelligent construction system.

The results indicate that smart highway construction projects remain exposed to a non-negligible risk of schedule delay despite the adoption of intelligent technologies. The backward inference analysis shows that intelligent resource supply capacity (G1) and intelligent progress optimization ability (G3) act as the primary direct drivers of schedule delay, while factors such as the proficiency of intelligent highway technicians, the capability of duration improvement, the informatization level of business processes, and the level of material supply and management serve as important indirect contributors. The sensitivity analysis further reveals that intelligent resource supply capacity (G1), intelligent progress optimization ability (G3), and intelligent progress monitoring ability (G4) are the most influential variables affecting schedule delay risk. These findings highlight that schedule performance in smart highway construction is shaped by the combined effects of technological resource readiness, managerial capability for progress optimization, and effective monitoring feedback mechanisms. The study therefore provides a systemic perspective on intelligent schedule management, emphasizing that intelligent technologies must be supported by appropriate organizational coordination and management capabilities to fully improve project performance. From a practical perspective, the proposed framework can assist project managers in identifying critical factors affecting construction schedules and prioritizing management efforts in smart highway projects. In particular, improving the availability and compatibility of intelligent resources, strengthening the capability of digital schedule optimization, and establishing effective monitoring systems may significantly enhance schedule control in intelligent infrastructure construction.

Despite these contributions, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the probability parameters of the Bayesian network were primarily derived from expert judgments rather than large-scale empirical datasets, which may introduce uncertainty into the model. Second, the case study mainly demonstrates the applicability of the proposed framework rather than providing comprehensive empirical validation. Future research could incorporate real project data to improve parameter estimation and enhance the robustness of the model. In addition, since smart highway construction processes are inherently dynamic, further studies may explore the use of dynamic Bayesian networks to capture the temporal evolution of construction progress.

Remote sensing identification and hazard assessment methods for spoil sites
Authors: Wei Xiao, Weiping Tian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Spoil sites, as loose accumulations, are prone to instability after disturbances and are a serious threat to the safety of downstream communities. This research aims to rapidly and accurately assess the potential hazards of the numerous spoil sides. An integrated approach that combines spatial, aerial, and ground techniques was developed to rapidly identify and interpret the key factors influencing the stability of spoil sites. Subsequently, based on the results of numerical simulation of multiple combinations of unfavorable working conditions by the material point method, a hazard assessment methodology for spoil sites is proposed, which combines the limit equilibrium method with the improved hierarchical analysis. The proposed method is applied and validated on spoil sites along an expressway. The achieved results reveal that remote sensing combined with unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) verification technology is capable of quickly and accurately identifying the critical elements of spoil sites. The hazard assessment of the spoil site is represented by the hazard coefficient (P), where 0 < P < 1 indicates safety, 1 ≤ P < 5 signifies instability, and P ≥ 5 represents extreme instability. The effectiveness of the proposed assessment approach is then verified through the on-site validation, which provides a vital reference for rapid identification and batch assessment of spoil sites.

Introduction

After the extensive and rapid development of infrastructure, such as highways and railways, there are numerous spoil sites in mountainous areas. In the past, spoil sites, as temporary structures were not given enough attention in the stages of planning, design, construction, and operation phases. It is not uncommon to find instances where drainage facilities are either absent or in a state of disrepair, as well as spoil dams that have been damaged, or stockpiles that have been overloaded. Occasionally, after continuous heavy rains or extreme weather events, landslides, debris flows, and other natural disasters may occur suddenly, posing a substantial threat to the life and property of the downstream population. Therefore, accurate identification, assessment, and classification of high-risk spoil sites, as well as rapid determination of risk levels, are necessary to facilitate the timely implementation of effective measures.

Spoil sites, as massive artificial loose accumulations, exhibit remarkable environmental changes and diverse compositions of waste slag. The risks of their instability are scattered, latent, and uncertain. A number of studies have been conducted on dumpsites. In their research, Michael Jiang and colleagues1 developed a novel approach to determine the natural density of loose soil and rock formations. This involved on-site measurement and sampling, as well as indoor angle of repose testing. Additionally, they investigated the relationship between bulk modulus B and perimeter pressure σ3 through experimentation. Yin et al.2,3 employed multi-stage slope angle measurements and particle analysis tests as an alternative to the traditional disposal slope tests. Xie et al. studied how the surface roughness on the erosion of spoil heaps affected the hydrological and sediment yield characteristics4. These fundamental studies are of significant importance for the further analysis of the behavior of individual spoil slopes.

Besides, limit equilibrium approaches5,6 have been combined with numerical simulations for engineering mechanics analysis, which aims to examine the stability of individual spoil sites. For instance, methods such as the finite element strength reduction technique7, the finite difference method8, and the discrete element method9, in conjunction with the rigid limit equilibrium analysis, have been adopted to evaluate the slope stability in spoil sites in the presence of rainfall conditions10, in high and cold regions11, or under general circumstances.

The majority of studies on slag disposal have focused on the physical and mechanical properties of the disposal site, as well as the use of mechanical methods combined with numerical simulation to carry out the stability analysis of a single disposal site. There has been a paucity of research on the comprehensive and rapid hazard evaluation of spoil sites. The number of completed dumps is considerable, and the situation is complex, making it prohibitively expensive to carry out in-depth calculations and evaluations for each dump.

Currently, there is a paucity of studies on hazard evaluation methods for regional dump sites. Li et al.11 and Xia et al.12 proposed a combination of partly qualitative and partly quantitative analyses. This involved qualitative analyses of the geology and foundations, followed by quantitative analyses of the individual dumpsite itself. Xu et al.13 employed the accident tree analysis method to analyses a substantial number of accidents and to identify the influencing factors and weights on the safety of the dump.

The use of remote sensing, drones and airborne LiDAR for landslide identification has been widely explored by scholars. Yang, Z. Y. et al.12 conducted a study on the identification of loess landslides based on the Gaofen-1 remote sensing images and DEM data, and used a convolutional neural network to classify the fused data to achieve the rapid identification of landslides. Yang F. et al.13 put forth a methodology for the surveillance of geological hazards, specifically landslides, which employs a combination of UAV aerial surveys and three-dimensional (3D) laser scanning. Liang, Y.F. et al.14 proposed a systematic method for automatic identification and extraction of structural surfaces of rock bodies, structural surface spacing, continuity and roughness characterization based on 3D point cloud data.

Researchers have conducted extensive research on regional geological hazards’ assessment and analysis. To this end, emerging remote sensing methods, including aerial remote sensing platforms12,15, sensors, UAVs13, airborne LiDAR14–19, in combination with analytic hierarchy process20, random subspace fuzzy rules21, grey relational analysis22, backward cloud algorithm23, rough-set-based extension evaluation models24, multi-layer perceptron (MLP) models25, and precision evaluation methods related to stability26, as well as Bayesian belief networks27, have been utilized for comprehensive regional assessments. Regional geological hazard assessment relies on extensive geological data, however, there exists a lack of comprehensive disaster data for spoil sites.

Targeting 57 spoil sites along a given section of an expressway in Guizhou, China, this study aims to develop rapid hazard assessment method for various types of spoil sites under different working conditions. Firstly, a rapid identification and interpretation system for regional spoil sites is developed that combines spatial, aerial, and ground techniques via remote sensing platforms, UAVs, and field surveys. Subsequently, the material point method is employed to extend the simulation of a variety of hazardous working conditions. Finally, based on the simulation results, the limit equilibrium method combines with the improved hierarchical analysis is employed to propose a rapid assessment of the hazardousness of the spoil site and practical verification. The results of the study can provide a basis for the management, maintenance and governance of the spoil sites, thereby ensuring the safety of the infrastructure and people’s lives and properties in the area adjacent to the spoil sites.

Hazardous factors of spoil sites

After investigating numerous spoil sites in mountainous regions of Guizhou, the Qinba mountainous areas, and the loess areas of northern Shaanxi in China, as well as analyzing dumpsite accidents in recent years, the following factors affecting the hazard assessment of spoil sites, which can be easily identified and obtained through remote sensing technology, are selected: ① The composition of the spoil mass, which is determined by the stone content, as some investigators suggest through statistical analysis on a large amount of waste slags, the natural repose angle for the slope2; ② Excess volume of waste slags; ③ The integrity of the spoil dam; ④ The drainage performance of the spoil site; ⑤ The slope gradient at the bottom of the spoil site; ⑥ The distance between the spoil site and the nearby buildings/structures or vulnerable objects such as rivers, reservoirs, and agricultural land.

Integrated identification system for spoil site factors via combining space-aerial-ground techniques

The identification of spoil site factors utilizes a combination of satellite optical remote sensing images, airborne LiDAR optical images, and 3D laser scanning along with on-site surveys. This approach, by integrating spatial, aerial, and ground techniques, establishes a comprehensive system for identifying spoil sites in vast mountainous areas. This makes it possible to distinguish the key drivers of spoil sites and their current conditions and provides a basis for their hazard assessment.

Typical optical remote sensing identifiers can be extracted through an object-oriented approach to the interpretation of satellite remote sensing data.

Typical factor interpretation of spoil sites based on optical remote sensing

Typical factors detectable through optical remote sensing are as: the distribution area of waste slags, the morphology of spoil slope, spoil dams, drainage ditches, and the distance between the spoil site and nearby buildings/structures, or vulnerable objects such as rivers, reservoirs, and farmland. From the remote sensing images, it is possible to see the waste slags, rockfalls, and obstructions of downstream rivers and gullies.

Detailed examination of key spoil site hazards using drone-borne three-dimensional laser scanning

By integrating the position measurement system, attitude measurement system, 3D laser scanner and cameras, LiDAR directly obtains the 3D surface coordinates of the observed area. In addition to providing high-resolution and high-precision topographic images, LiDAR is able to effectively penetrate surface vegetation via multiple echo techniques, employ filtering algorithms to eliminate surface vegetation, and provide accurate elevation data for the actual ground surface14. It is a very crucial tool for detecting high-altitude and concealed spoil sites.

Geological hazard verification based on ground surveys and monitoring

The use of space-aerial remote sensing approaches is the first step in identifying spoil sites and hazards based on visual morphology. However, due to the influence of various factors, the identification results may not always be completely accurate and may lead to misjudgments. Therefore, geological personnel should conduct on-site surveys and reviews to individually evaluate, confirm, or eliminate potential hazard points of spoil sites identified using remote sensing techniques. In some cases, on-site observations and diagnostic methodologies are necessary for the accurate determination of concern factors28.

A field survey was also conducted at the Daoweng high-speed 57 dump site. The measurement results can be further verified in front of remote sensing and UAV-acquired data, including the dump pile height, elevation, and area. In addition, the construction unit can provide the amount of slag dumping square volume, which can also be verified against the amount of slag dumping.

The three systems, respectively, obtain and process data, and then corroborate each other. The identification of the dumping ground requires the use of a variety of technical means, which must be employed in a multi-level, multi-technology integrated manner. This approach allows for the strengths and weaknesses of the different technologies to be offset by one another, thus improving the accuracy and reliability of the identification process.

Material point method for spoil site stability prediction

According to the results of on-site investigations of 57 spoil sites within the region, various degrees of shallow local landslides have occurred in these spoil sites. No large-scale landslides have been reported to date. Consequently, numerical simulation is employed to forecast the deterioration of the spoil sites under a range of operational scenarios. The material point method29, which adopts a dual description approach of a Lagrangian particle and a Eulerian grid, is suitable for analyzing problems related to large deformations and contact interactions.

Numerical simulation parameters

The present study was conducted at disposal site No. 8 The material point method is employed to establish a numerical model considering five multilevel factors in the presence of various combinations: spoil stone content (as shown in Table 1), excess volume of waste slags, integrity of the spoil dam, drainage performance (including conditions with high water pressure of 400 kPa, lower water pressure of 400 kPa, and self-weight), and gradient at the bottom of the slope (including two sets at 8° and 23°). The simulation results employ the slope toe movement distance as a characteristic parameter for the spoil slope failure conditions.

Preparation of soil samples and testing of the physical and mechanical properties of soil samples

Soil samples were collected on-site from spoil site 4 and typical local chalk soils and subsequently configured. Soil sample S1 is a soil sample with 100 per cent stone content, obtained through the mixing method of scaling down, followed by equal substitution, based on the granular gradation of spoil site No. 4. The soil samples designated S2-S5 were obtained by incrementally augmenting the proportion of fine-grained soils (local chalks with grain size < 0.1 mm) in the mixed soil samples. The S6 set comprised another set of lower-strength clay silt.

The natural moisture content of the soil samples was determined by the drying method, the natural bulk density was determined by the flooding method, the natural angle of repose was determined by the discharging method, and the particle fractionation curves of the soil samples (as illustrated in Fig. 6) were obtained by the sieve test. The soil samples S1-S4 were tested by using a large-sized straight shear instrument (as illustrated in Fig. 7), and the angle of internal friction and cohesive force in the natural state were measured. Soil samples S5 and S6 were pure fine-grained soil samples, which were tested using an ordinary straight shear apparatus (as illustrated in Fig. 8).Fig. 6Particle grading curve of each soil sample. The fine-grained soil content of the soil samples from S2 to S4 gradually increases, which has the effect of reducing the drainage performance of the soil samples. Furthermore, the specimens at the damage surface are oversaturated, which makes it impossible to obtain the strength of the soil at the damage surface by using a large-scale straight shear apparatus to carry out the unconsolidated and undrained shear test.

The investigations of Yin et al.30 revealed that as the moisture content of soil samples rises, the soil strength reduces and leads to a rapid decrease in the angle of internal friction. Application and validation of spoil site hazard assessment

Considering the spoil sites along the Daozhen-Wong’an Expressway from K1481 + 900 to K1487 + 500 in Guizhou, data on these spoil sites are collected, with site numbers ranging from 1 to 9. The approximate locations of these spoil sites along the expressway are illustrated in Fig. 15. Based on the collected data, the hazard coefficients for spoil sites 1 to 9 are evaluated using the hazard coefficient formula (Eq. (11)), and the obtained results are provided in Table 8. Since these spoil sites are located in the same area, the waste slag properties are generally similar, except for spoil site 5, which is purely rocky with 100% stone content. Other spoil sites have a stone content of approximately 75%. There are differences in drainage conditions, spoil dam conditions, and excess slag volumes among these spoil sites. Based on Table 8, it can be seen that spoil site 3 has a hazard coefficient of P = 1.61, indicating that it is relatively unstable. Spoil site 3 has a stone content of about 75%, no spoil dam or drainage ditches, a small volume of excess slag, and is located on a downslope between 20 and 30°, with less distance from the river than H; thus, it is generally unstable. A visual inspection of remote sensing images and UAV photos reveals substantial accumulation in the river below and confirms the calculation results. Additionally, all other spoil sites exhibit P < 1, indicating relative stability. Apart from spoil site 3, the highest P value is for spoil site 7 with P = 0.80. Although it has a spoil dam at a distance of 2.88 H from the river, the spoil dam is incomplete. In addition, there is no drainage system and the lower slope has a slope between 20 and 30°. A visual examination of remote sensing images (in Fig. 3a) and UAV photos(in Fig. 3b) indicates that there is some accumulation of spoil in the river below the missing part of the spoil dam, which validates the calculated results. The hazard ratios for the remaining spoil sites ranged between 0.22 and 0.58, indicating relative stability. Conclusions and prospects

Conclusions

Taking 57 spoil sites along a section of an expressway in Guizhou as an example, an integrated approach is developed to rapidly identify and interpret regional spoil sites in the understudy region via integrating spatial, aerial, and ground techniques. Based on the material point and limit equilibrium methodologies, an efficient method for quick assessment of spoil site hazard is proposed and appropriately validated. The main major results can be summarized as follows:Through remote sensing images, UAV photos, field surveys, and experiments, six hazard-influencing factors for expressway spoil sites are identified: stone content, excess volume of waste slags, integrity of the spoil dam, drainage performance, slope at the bottom of the spoil, and the distance between the spoil site and the nearby buildings/structures or vulnerable objects such as rivers, reservoirs, and agricultural land.Using the material point method, numerical simulations are performed for solid spoil sites that include multiple factors at various levels. The simulations reveal the influence of various evaluation indices on the stability of the spoil site, which in turn yields the parameter values for each index.Based on the limit equilibrium method, an expression for the spoil site hazard coefficient is proposed. In the case of P < 1, the spoil site is stable, with a toe displacement L < 0.5 H; for the case of 1 ≤ P < 5, the spoil site is unstable and the hazard level enhances with increasing P; in the case of P ≥ 5, the spoil site is highly unstable, potentially leading to widespread damage.The evaluation approach for hazard coefficients is applied to the hazard of spoil sites along the Daozhen-Weng’an Expressway in Guizhou. The evaluation results are suitably consistent with the actual situation, confirming the effectiveness of the proposed assessment approach. This method can be applied in mountain area and provides a reference for rapid assessment of existing sites along highways.

Prospects

At present, object-oriented manual interpretation is the main method for processing and analyzing satellite remote sensing data, and typical optical remote sensing identification features are extracted. In the future, research on automatic fusion analysis of multi-source satellite remote sensing data of landslides and intelligent decision support based on deep learning can be developed.

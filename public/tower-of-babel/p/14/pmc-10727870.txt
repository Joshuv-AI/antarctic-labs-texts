Leukocytoclastic Vasculitis with Ischemic Colitis Following COVID-19 AstraZeneca Vaccine
Authors: So Yeon Yun, Jin Young Choi, Dong Soo Yu, Young Bok Lee
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

COVID-19 vaccines have been administered globally during the pandemic. Frequently reported cutaneous adverse events include injection site swelling, erythema, rash, and urticaria1. Rare cutaneous reactions to COVID-19 mRNA vaccines have been reported, including herpes zoster, erythema multiforme, and vasculitis1. We report a case of leukocytoclastic vasculitis and ischemic colitis following a first dose of the ChAdOx1 nCoV-19 vaccine.

A 74-year-old male presented with multiple asymptomatic, variable-sized, palpable purpuric papules and plaques on the upper and lower extremities and trunk for 3 days (Fig. 1). He was admitted to the hospital for hematochezia and skin lesions. He noticed the appearance of petechiae and purpuric skin lesions on both legs on the fourth day after vaccination, and reported the onset of diarrhea and hematochezia with mild cramping abdominal pain 4 to 5 days after vaccination. He didn’t show any other symptoms such as arthralgia and hematuria. He denied having had a similar rash before and had not recently started any new medications. His medical history included hypertension, transient ischemic attack 3 months prior, and amputation of the right great toe due to Burger’s disease 20 years prior. During hospital admission, he underwent sigmoidoscopic evaluation that revealed an edematous and erythematous patch lesion from the rectum to transverse colon and was diagnosed with ischemic colitis.

Clinical photograph of 74-year-old male presented with asymptomatic, multiple, variable-sized, palpable purpuric papules and plaques on the lower extremities (A) and trunk (B) for 3 days. We received the patient’s consent form about publishing all photographic materials.

D-dimer, fibrinogen and fibrinogen degradation product were increased (Supplementary Table 1). A punch biopsy was taken of one of the palpable purple papules on the left leg. The histological findings showed fibrinoid necrosis with endothelial wall swelling, neutrophilic perivascular infiltration, and leukocytoclasis with nuclear dust (Fig. 2). Based on the clinical and histopathological findings, the patient was diagnosed with leukocytoclastic vasculitis.

Fibrinoid necrosis associated with endothelial wall swelling, neutrophilic perivascular infiltration, and leukocytoclasis with nuclear dust (A: H&E, ×100; B: H&E, ×200, magnification field).

McMahon et al.2 reported 3 cases of vasculitis associated with mRNA COVID-19 vaccines; however, vasculitis after ChAdOx1 nCoV-19 has not been reported. Although a causal association between vaccination and subsequent development of vasculitis has not been established, it is speculated that SARS-Cov-2 antigens and vaccine proteins have a similar structure and the vaccine might activate the same autoimmune mechanisms the SARS-CoV-2 virus does, leading to immune complex deposition within small vessels, a characteristic feature of leukocytoclastic vasculitis3.

Several cases of thrombotic events have occurred after ChAdOx1 nCov-19 vaccination4. Immune thrombotic thrombocytopenia mediated by platelet-activating antibodies against platelet factor 4, similar to autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, can occur after ChAdOx1 nCov-19 vaccination4. Unlike patients with vaccine-induced thrombotic thrombocytopenia, in our patient, serum showed coagulation abnormalities such as elevated levels of D-dimer, fibrinogen, and fibrinogen degradation product without thrombocytopenia. We suggest a possible relationship between the development of thrombotic events without thrombocytopenia and COVID-19 vaccination to raise awareness of venous or arterial thrombosis at unusual sites.

The skin lesions partially resolved 14 days after initiation of systemic steroid therapy and ischemic colitis improved with conservative care. Herein, we present a rare case of leukocytoclastic vasculitis along with ischemic colitis after the first dose of the COVID-19 AstraZeneca vaccine in an Asian male patient. Clinicians should be aware that rare complications affecting the skin with systemic involvement are possible after COVID-19 vaccination.

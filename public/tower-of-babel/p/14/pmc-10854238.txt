Introduction

Pediatric endodontics has caused a paradigm shift in the pediatric dental practice by successfully retaining the primary teeth in an asymptomatic condition until their physiologic exfoliation. Since then, revolutionary changes in pediatric endodontics kicked off in terms of materials, instruments, and techniques. One among them is the obturating material used in primary teeth. The reduction or removal of the microorganisms from the root canals of the primary teeth determines the outcome of the endodontic treatment. Thus, a key factor in deciding the outcome of endodontic therapy for primary teeth is thorough debridement and root canal disinfection. Despite the adequate chemomechanical preparation in the primary teeth, the torturous and complex configuration of the primary root canals retains some of the infected bacteria within the canals, causing secondary infection and failure of the endodontically treated primary teeth. This retained residual bacteria, trapped despite the vigorous mechanical debridement and copious irrigation can be inhibited by a root canal filling material with maximal antibacterial properties.1,2

So, the light was projected on discovering the most ideal material that can be used for obturation in deciduous teeth. An optimal obturating substance for milk teeth should possess antimicrobial properties and resorb at a pace equivalent to the primary root's physiological resorption and should also resorb faster when extruded beyond the apex without causing harm to the developing succedaneous teeth. Additionally, it must be radiopaque, adhere to the canal walls, be simple to fill, and not discolor the tooth.3 Antibacterial activity of the obturating material is the most predominant factor determining the success of pulpectomy in primary teeth. In the present study, different concentrations of a newly developed obturating material were prepared and tested for its antibacterial activity.

The microbiome of the infections involving the pulp is polymicrobial in the root canals of the deciduous teeth. Analysis of the microbiota in the root canals of the primary teeth diagnosed with pulpal necrosis at molecular level shows higher prevalence of Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis.4 The authors have stated that Enterococcus faecalis being an opportunistic pathogen can acquire resistance to the antimicrobials during the chemomechanical treatment owing to its ability to form biofilm and its potential to operate proton pump to reduce intracytoplasmic pH. Also, Enterococcus faecalis has been reported to contribute to recurrent periradicular lesion post-endodontic therapy in primary teeth.5 Hence, it is paramount to develop an obturation material effective against Enterococcus faecalis. The current study's objective was to assess the newly developed obturating material's antimicrobial efficacy at various concentrations against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis, the two most common microorganisms found in primary teeth infections.

Materials and Methods

The study was designed as a microbiological in vitro study after obtaining clearance from the Institutional Review Board.

Preparation of the Obturating Material

The novel obturating material was developed in four different concentrations with calcium hydroxide, zinc oxide, and metronidazole as the key components. The four different concentrations were prepared by altering the proportion of calcium hydroxide powder and zinc oxide powder at 50:50, 60:40, 70:30, and 80:20, respectively to which 1%, 2%, and 3% of metronidazole powder were added. Thus, the novel obturating material was prepared in 12 different proportions and was tested for its antimicrobial efficacy. A digital weighing machine was used to dispense the calcium hydroxide powder and zinc oxide powder. A motor and pistol were used to crush 400 mg of metronidazole tablet and make it into a fine powder.

The developed obturating material at 50:50 concentration was prepared by adding 500 mg of powdered zinc oxide with 500 mg of powdered calcium hydroxide. Likewise, 60:40 concentration was prepared by mixing 600 mg of calcium hydroxide with 400 mg of zinc oxide, 70:30 concentration was obtained by mixing 700 mg of calcium hydroxide with 300 mg of zinc oxide and 800 mg of calcium hydroxide was mixed with 200 mg of zinc oxide to obtain 80:20 concentration. Each of these concentrations was mixed manually in powder form and was kept in magnetic stirrer for 1 hour.

By combining 2 mg of metronidazole powder with 198 mg of the calcium hydroxide–zinc oxide mixture in each concentration, 1% of calcium hydroxide–zinc oxide–metronidazole powder was created. Similarly, 4 and 6 mg of metronidazole powder were combined with 196 and 194 mg of zinc oxide–calcium hydroxide mixture, respectively, in each concentration to create 2% and 3% calcium hydroxide–zinc oxide–metronidazole powder. The exact amount of the powders was dispensed by using a digital weighing machine. So, in total, 12 different concentrations of the novel obturating material were prepared (1% 50–50; 2% 50–50; 3% 50–50; 1% 60–40; 2% 60–40; 3% 60–40; 1% 70–30; 2% 70–30; 3% 70–30; 1% 80–20; 2% 80–20; 3% 80–20). A micropipette was used to measure 2 mL of distilled water and was added to the prepared samples. The samples were then mixed by placing them in the vortex for 15 minutes following which the samples were shifted to a mixer where it was mixed for the next 24 hours.

Preparation of the Culture Medium

The agar well diffusion method was used to assess the obturating material's antimicrobial efficacy against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis at varying concentrations. The prepared Mueller–Hinton agar (MHA) was sterilized for 15 minutes at 121°C. The sterilized medium was then poured onto 30 plates for each concentration separately (for example 15 for Streptococcus mutans and 15 for Enterococcus faecalis for 1%, 2%, and 3% of 50–50 concentration) and was kept aside for solidification, following which a well cutter was used to cut the wells to which the test organisms were swabbed. The obturating materials at different concentrations were then loaded at 25, 50, and 100 µL. The plates were kept for incubation for 24 hours at 37°C. After 24 hours, a vernier caliper was used to measure the zone of inhibition.

Each concentration of the novel obturating material was tested for its antimicrobial efficacy against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis in nine different proportions and the concentration with maximal zone of inhibition in each was selected for final analysis to find out the most effective concentration of the novel obturating material. About 2% 50–50; 2% 60–40; 1% 70–30, and 3% 80–20 showed the maximal zone of inhibition against both Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis at 100 µL.

Development of the Final Novel Obturating Material

The shortlisted four concentrations of the novel obturating material were again tested for antimicrobial efficacy using agar well diffusion method. All four different concentrations were loaded on a single plate with four wells drilled and the test organisms swabbed. The obturating material was loaded at 100 µL and a total of 10 samples were prepared (five for Streptococcus mutans and five for Enterococcus faecalis). The plates were incubated at 37°C for 24 hours, after which the zones of inhibition were measured (Fig. 1). The values obtained were then subjected to statistical analysis to determine the finest concentration of the novel obturating material with maximal antimicrobial efficacy.

Zone of inhibition after 24 hours of incubation of the four different concentration of the novel obturating material against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis

Statistical Analysis

The entered data was analyzed with the help of Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software (IBM SPSS Statistics, Version 20.0, Armonk, New York: IBM Corp.). Data was summarized and presented using descriptive statistics. The normality of the data distribution was tested with the Shapiro–Wilk test. The mean Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis count were compared between different concentrations using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test.

Results

The mean zone of inhibition against Streptococcus mutans was greater with 2% 60–40 concentration of the obturating material followed by 2% 50–50, 3% 80–20, and 1% 70–30 concentration. However, the four varying concentrations of the prepared obturating material did not significantly have any difference against Streptococcus mutans (Table 1). Against Enterococcus faecalis, the mean zone of inhibition was greater at a concentration of 2% 60–40 followed by 3% 80–20 and 1% 70–30 concentration and 2% 50–50 concentration. The varying concentrations of the obturating material showed no significant difference against Enterococcus faecalis (Table 2).

Discussion

The success of endodontically treated primary teeth pivots on understanding the anatomy and morphology of the root canals of the primary teeth. Perpetual alteration of the apical foramen and concomitant deposition of the secondary dentin forestalls a thorough debridement of the primary root canals provoking reinfection.6 An obturating material with potent antimicrobial property can combat the entrapped microorganisms and remnant pulpal tissues.

The obturating material that is regularly used in pediatric dental practice encompasses zinc oxide eugenol and calcium hydroxide + iodoform paste (Metapex). However, Kriplani et al. disclosed that Metapex was ineffective against all the test microorganisms obtained from the infected primary root canals.7 Other studies in the literature also demonstrated that calcium hydroxide and iodoform pastes exhibited no antimicrobial properties.8,10 Intracanal resorption was recorded as the prime limitation of this combination.11 Likewise, though the literature has documented zinc oxide eugenol to show greater inhibitory effects against microorganisms found in primary root canals, the eugenol component of this material irritates the periapical tissues, bone, and cementum.12,13 Endodontic treatment being the choice to maintain the primary teeth in their position and with no obturating material existing with potent antimicrobial feature keeps the ball rolling toward the quest to discover an obturating material with optimal properties.

The core components of the obturating material used in the present study were zinc oxide, calcium hydroxide, and metronidazole, whose concentrations were estimated based on their antimicrobial efficacy and resorption rate of the core components. Studies have shown that zinc oxide powder as such does not have antimicrobial property and it is the addition of eugenol to it that showed inhibitory effect on microorganisms.14 On the contrary, calcium hydroxide when mixed with an aqueous vehicle shows greater zone of inhibition due to the ionic dissociation in Ca2+ and OH ions.15 Furthermore, calcium hydroxide has the tendency to resorb faster than the rate of resorption of the primary root resulting in hollow tube effect meantime the rate of resorption of zinc oxide is much slower.11,16 Consequently, the new material for obturation in primary teeth was developed with greater ratio of calcium hydroxide powder to zinc oxide powder, domineering the propitious characteristics of the core components. Thereupon, four different concentrations of the above core components were prepared—50:50, 60:40, 70:30, and 80:20 concentration of calcium hydroxide–zinc oxide, respectively.

The antimicrobial efficacy of the above combination was enhanced with the addition of metronidazole. Molecular analysis of the bacteria in the root canals of primary teeth has demonstrated that the endodontic infections in primary teeth are polymicrobial in nature with predominance of facultative anaerobes.4 Metronidazole was hand-picked as one of the core agents for its antimicrobial action against facultative anaerobes as it has the potential to diffuse into the microorganism, eventually inhibit the protein synthesis causing cell death. To formulate the most effectual obturating material, 1%, 2%, and 3% of metronidazole powder was added to the four different concentrations prepared with calcium hydroxide and zinc oxide powder. On that account, 12 different concentrations of the novel obturating material were prepared (1% 50–50; 2% 50–50; 3% 50–50; 1% 60–40; 2% 60–40; 3% 60–40; 1% 70–30; 2% 70–30; 3% 70–30; 1% 80–20; 2% 80–20; 3% 80–20) and subjected to microbiological evaluation.

The antimicrobial efficacy of the developed obturating material at various concentrations was determined using the agar well diffusion method. This method of analysis was preferred as it is more reliable, easy to perform, and also directly compares the efficacy of different obturating material against the test microorganisms, ergo determining the most efficacious concentration instantly. Streptococcus mutans was the most commonly isolated microorganism from an infected primary root canal, whereas Enterococcus faecalis made the major portion of the microorganisms isolated from the secondary endodontic infections.17,18 Thereupon, Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis served as a standard for evaluating the efficacy of any new material used in endodontics and hence the different concentrations of the newly developed obturating material were tested against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis in this study.

Intergroup comparison of the most efficacious formation in each prime concentrate of calcium hydroxide and zinc oxide powder unveils 2% 60–40 concentration of metronidazole–calcium hydroxide–zinc oxide powder to show maximum inhibition zone against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis at 100 µL. Though the difference was not statistically significant between the various concentrations of the novel obturating material, the rationale of the present in vitro study was to develop an obturating material with maximal antimicrobial efficacy. The apparent limitation of the present study could be absence of in vivo influencing factors, which could alter the efficacy of the obturating material. On that account, in vivo trials should be conducted to ascertain the antimicrobial efficacy of the finalized concentration of the novel obturating material for primary teeth. Conjointly, the biocompatibility and safety of the finalized concentration of the novel obturating material should be evaluated.

Conclusion

The newly developed root canal filling material with metronidazole, calcium hydroxide, and zinc oxide as the core components are ascertained to display maximal antimicrobial efficacy against Streptococcus mutans and Enterococcus faecalis at 2% 60–40 concentration. Consequently, the new formulation can be a promising obturating material for primary teeth.

Clinical Significance

The novel obturating material can be a successful replacement for the other available obturating materials, increasing the success rate of the pulpectomy and preventing the potential reinfection owing to its superior antimicrobial properties.

Clinical heterogeneity of primary cutaneous γδ T-cell lymphoma: a 20-year single-institution experience

Introduction

Primary cutaneous γδ T-cell lymphoma (PCGDTCL) is an exceedingly rare subtype of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma characterized by γδ T-cell receptor expression and a cytotoxic immunophenotype [1]. Given that γδ T cells comprise only a minor fraction of the T-cell population, neoplasms derived from this lineage are uncommon and biologically distinctive [2]. PCGDTCL was initially recognized as a provisional entity in the 2017 World Health Organization (WHO) Classification and subsequently upgraded to a definite entity in the 2022 WHO and International Consensus Classification (ICC) based on accumulating evidence [3].

Traditionally, PCGDTCL has been regarded as a highly aggressive lymphoma with a median overall survival of only 15–30 months, inferior to that of its αβ counterpart, subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma [4]. No standardized treatment exists, owing to the rarity of the disease and lack of prospective studies. Reported management strategies have ranged from skin-directed therapies to anthracycline-based chemotherapy and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, with overall outcomes remaining poor [1, 4].

However, emerging reports suggest that PCGDTCL may not invariably follow an aggressive course. A subset of patients has demonstrated relatively indolent disease with unexpectedly favorable outcomes [5–8]. This raises the possibility that conventional chemotherapy, often considered ineffective, may achieve durable remissions in selected cases.

Here, we present a 20-year single-institution experience of six patients with PCGDTCL. By reviewing their clinicopathologic features and outcomes in the context of the existing literature, we aimed to challenge the view that PCGDTCL is uniformly aggressive and to provide insight into therapeutic approaches, particularly regarding the role of anthracycline-based regimens.

Materials and methods

This single-center retrospective study was conducted at Samsung Medical Center. All patients diagnosed with PCGDTCL between January 2005 and September 2025 were identified through institutional medical records. Collected clinical variables included demographics, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, presenting features, disease site, B symptoms, International Prognostic Index (IPI), Ann Arbor stage, TNM classification for cutaneous lymphomas other than mycosis fungoides/Sézary syndrome (MF/SS), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), immunophenotype, Epstein–Barr virus in situ hybridization (EBV ISH), Ki-67 proliferation index, and evidence of bone marrow or extracutaneous involvement. Pathology reports and histologic slides were reviewed by expert hematopathologists to confirm the diagnosis.

Immunophenotypic profiling included systematic assessment of lineage- and cytotoxicity-associated markers (e.g., CD3, CD4, CD8, CD56, TCRγδ, Granzyme B). Treatment courses and response dynamics were depicted using swimmer plots generated in R (version 4.5.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Tumor response was assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Samsung Medical Center (File No. 2025–09–099–001), which determined that the study was exempt from full review because of its retrospective nature. The requirement for informed consent was waived by the IRB. No external funding was received for this study.

Results

Patient characteristics

Six patients with PCGDTCL were identified between January 2005 and September 2025. The median age at diagnosis was 57 years (range, 35–68); four patients (67%) were male. ECOG performance status was 0 in five patients and 1 in one.

Clinical presentation

All patients presented with cutaneous lesions, including nodules (n = 3), a mass-forming inflammatory lesion (n = 1), a vesicular eruption (n = 1), and an ulcerated plaque (n = 1). Sites of involvement included the calf, chin, thigh, and inguinal region. B symptoms were uncommon and were observed in only one of six patients, who reported an unintentional 5-kg weight loss within one month.

Laboratory, imaging, and staging

All evaluable patients had elevated serum LDH (median, 336 U/L; range, 197–501). According to the TNM classification for cutaneous lymphomas other than MF/SS, disease extent ranged from localized solitary lesions to multifocal cutaneous involvement. Two patients demonstrated multifocal skin lesions involving multiple body regions, and one patient had concurrent tonsillar involvement. Bone marrow involvement was absent in all cases.

^18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET) showed heterogeneous findings. In three patients, PET did not provide reliable metabolic information: two because maximum standardized uptake values (SUVmax) were not assessable and one because FDG uptake was absent despite clinically evident lesions. Among the evaluable cases, one patient had a solitary cutaneous lesion with SUVmax 4.1, another had cutaneous (SUVmax 4.3) and tonsillar (SUVmax 4.5) involvement, and one showed markedly elevated uptake with SUVmax 8.9 (Fig. 1). Across the cohort, the IPI ranged from 0 to 3 (Table 1). At the last follow-up, five patients were alive and one had died.

Pathology and immunohistochemistry

EBV ISH was negative in all tested cases. The Ki-67 proliferation index was high, ranging from 70 to 80% in three evaluable patients. Histopathologic reassessment of epidermal, dermal, and subcutaneous involvement was feasible in only three cases because several archival specimens from outside institutions or older cases were unavailable for complete review. Among the assessable cases, one demonstrated predominantly deep dermal involvement, whereas two showed combined epidermal and dermal involvement. Definite epidermotropism was not identified in the assessable cases. Patient-level immunophenotypic profiles were visualized using a heatmap (Fig. 2B). CD3 was expressed in all six cases. TCRγδ expression was detected in all patients, and β-F1 was negative in all tested cases. CD4 expression was observed in two patients (Patients 1 and 4), whereas CD8 expression was detected in one patient. CD56 positivity was identified in four of six patients tested (67%). CD20 expression was absent in all evaluated cases. CD30 expression showed variable positivity among the tested patients. Granzyme B expression was detected in one patient, although the limited number of tested cases precludes firm conclusions regarding its prevalence in this cohort.

Treatment patterns

First-line regimens included CHOP (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone; n = 2), Vel-CHOP (CHOP plus bortezomib; n = 1), GDP (gemcitabine, dexamethasone, and cisplatin; n = 1), corticosteroids (n = 1), and observation (n = 1). One patient who received GDP had not yet undergone response assessment at the time of data cutoff. Four patients did not receive second-line therapy, whereas two were treated with ICED (ifosfamide, carboplatin, etoposide, and dexamethasone) or GDP, respectively. Only one patient proceeded beyond third-line.

Treatment responses

Treatment courses and sequential therapeutic responses across multiple lines of therapy are summarized in Fig. 3. Two patients in the cohort expressed CD4 (Patients 1 and 4). One of these patients was managed conservatively with fewer than 1 week of prednisolone and maintained stable disease without progression. The other patient received GDP (gemcitabine, dexamethasone, and cisplatin) as initial therapy; however, formal response assessment had not yet been performed at the time of data cutoff, and no clear clinical improvement of the cutaneous lesions was observed.

Among the remaining four patients without CD4 expression, clinical outcomes were heterogeneous. One patient received six cycles of CHOP and achieved a complete remission (CR) but relapsed at the same chin site within three months. Salvage GDP subsequently induced a second CR, with a time-to-next-treatment (TTNT) of 2.9 months. Another patient treated with four cycles of CHOP in 2008 achieved CR and has remained progression-free for more than 15 years, representing the most durable response in this cohort. A third patient demonstrated a prolonged indolent course with waxing-and-waning cutaneous lesions and remained stable without systemic therapy.

The remaining patient experienced a protracted 13-year disease course requiring more than 10 systemic regimens. An initial CR was achieved with Vel-CHOP, but subsequent relapses were refractory to ICED, GDP, fludarabine, and DHAP (dexamethasone, high-dose cytarabine, and cisplatin). The calculated TTNT was 4.6 months. Serial biopsies performed during the early disease course consistently demonstrated a γδ phenotype, including CD56 positivity, TCRγ positivity, and absence of βF1 expression, supporting the original diagnosis of PCGDTCL. However, 7 years after therapy initiation, a repeat biopsy demonstrated loss of TCRγ expression and acquisition of βF1 positivity together with histologic features resembling mycosis fungoides (MF), suggesting clinicopathologic evolution during the disease course. Thereafter, intermittent remissions were achieved with interferon alfa-2a and later with brentuximab vedotin; however, repeated relapses occurred, and the patient ultimately died of sepsis in 2022. Despite multiple relapses, overall survival (OS) in this patient was 13 years.

At the data cutoff in September 2025, the median OS for the cohort had not been reached (95% CI, > 13.40 years–not reached [NR]) (Supplementary Fig. 1). No cases of hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) were observed during the clinical course.

Discussion

Most T-cell neoplasms arise from the αβ lineage, which accounts for the overwhelming majority of circulating T lymphocytes, whereas γδ T cells account for only 0.5%–10% of the T-cell compartment [1, 2, 4]. Despite this rarity, lymphomas derived from γδ T cells represent biologically and clinically distinctive entities. The major subtypes include PCGDTCL, which primarily involves the skin; hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL), characterized by systemic infiltration of the liver, spleen, and bone marrow; and monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma (MEITL), which arises in the small intestine. These γδ lymphomas have generally been regarded as aggressive diseases with poor prognoses, largely due to limited responsiveness to conventional chemotherapy [2]. In HSTCL and MEITL, conventional CHOP-based chemotherapy has generally shown limited efficacy [9–12]. In our institutional experience with HSTCL, ICE-based therapy demonstrated superior responses compared with CHOP-based regimens [9], consistent with current European Society for Medical Oncology–European Hematology Association (ESMO–EHA) recommendations favoring non-CHOP approaches for aggressive γδ T-cell lymphomas [10–12].

Similar to these other γδ-derived lymphomas, PCGDTCL has traditionally been described as fulminant in its clinical behavior, refractory to therapy, and associated with dismal outcomes [1, 4]. Because of its rarity, the clinical spectrum remains incompletely defined, and no standardized management protocol has been established. Diagnostic challenges are further compounded by overlapping histopathologic features with other cutaneous lymphomas, complicating recognition and therapeutic decision-making [13]. However, emerging evidence indicates that PCGDTCL may encompass a broader clinical spectrum, with some patients demonstrating relatively indolent disease courses.

Our 20-year institutional experience supports this evolving perspective, revealing substantial clinical heterogeneity. Several patients achieved long-term disease control with limited therapy, including conventional chemotherapy or even observation alone, whereas only one patient required more than 10 systemic regimens over a 13-year course. At the last follow-up, five of six patients remained alive, underscoring survival outcomes more favorable than those reported in historical series. These findings challenge the view of PCGDTCL as a uniformly aggressive entity and suggest that a subset of patients may follow indolent trajectories.

Therapeutic responses in this cohort were heterogeneous. Although some patients experienced early relapse or prolonged relapsing disease despite systemic chemotherapy, others achieved durable disease control with relatively conventional or less intensive approaches, including CHOP-based chemotherapy, corticosteroids, or observation alone. Additional disease control was also observed with brentuximab vedotin in a heavily pretreated patient. These findings suggest that PCGDTCL may encompass a broader clinical spectrum than previously recognized and that treatment responsiveness may vary according to the underlying biological heterogeneity of the disease.

Importantly, our observations should not be interpreted as evidence of consistent efficacy of anthracycline-based regimens. Given the small cohort size and single-institution retrospective design, therapeutic observations in this study remain exploratory. Rather, our findings suggest that some patients with PCGDTCL may achieve meaningful disease control without immediate escalation to highly intensive treatment strategies. Collectively, these observations support an individualized therapeutic approach based on disease behavior, symptom burden, and the patient’s overall condition.

Our results are consistent with published experiences from Asia, Europe, and the United States, which likewise describe subsets of patients with indolent clinical courses (Table 2) [5–8, 14–19]. Reports from Japan and Taiwan indicate that some patients achieved durable disease control with relatively modest interventions, including radiotherapy alone or conventional chemotherapy [5, 6]. Similarly, reports from France and the United States have described indolent but relapsing disease courses, with responses to low-dose methotrexate or corticosteroids in selected patients [7, 8]. Collectively, these observations further support the broad clinical spectrum and heterogeneous treatment responsiveness of PCGDTCL.

Of note, unfavorable outcomes have also been reported with CHOP-based therapy. One patient developed severe skin necrosis with subsequent infections and systemic complications, leading to treatment discontinuation. This case illustrates the difficulty of sustaining cytotoxic chemotherapy and underscores the need for less toxic, targeted strategies [14]. At the same time, a U.S. case showed that indolent-appearing PCGDTCL can progress rapidly, with fatal HLH despite later intensive therapy [18]. This observation underscores the risk of overly conservative management and the importance of timely escalation when disease evolves aggressively. Importantly, for CD30-expressing cases, if CHOP-like regimens fail to achieve durable responses, targeting CD30 with the antibody–drug conjugate brentuximab vedotin may represent a rational alternative, as suggested by recent case reports [19].

Beyond treatment, diagnostic challenges remain critical. Overlapping histopathologic and immunophenotypic features with other cutaneous lymphomas, particularly mycosis fungoides, can complicate diagnosis [13]. In our series, one patient later demonstrated histologic and immunophenotypic features resembling mycosis fungoides after several years of disease evolution. Serial biopsies obtained during the initial disease course repeatedly demonstrated a γδ phenotype characterized by TCRγ positivity, CD56 expression, and absence of βF1 expression, supporting the original diagnosis of PCGDTCL according to the diagnostic framework available at that time. However, a later biopsy demonstrated loss of TCRγ expression and acquisition of βF1 positivity together with MF-like histologic features. These findings may reflect diagnostic overlap, phenotypic evolution, or biological plasticity rather than definite MF at initial presentation. This case highlights the complexity of distinguishing PCGDTCL from other cutaneous T-cell lymphomas and underscores the importance of longitudinal clinicopathologic correlation.

In addition, two patients in our cohort demonstrated CD4 expression, an atypical finding for classical PCGDTCL, as γδ T-cell lymphomas typically lack αβ-associated markers such as CD4 and CD8. CD4 positivity raises the possibility of diagnostic overlap with other CD4-positive cutaneous T-cell lymphoproliferative disorders, particularly primary cutaneous CD4-positive small/medium T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder [20]. However, the detection of TCRγδ expression together with βF1 negativity supported classification within the γδ T-cell lineage in these cases. One of these patients showed an indolent clinical course with stable disease after minimal therapy, whereas response assessment in the other patient had not yet been completed at the data cutoff. These observations further illustrate the immunophenotypic heterogeneity of cutaneous γδ T-cell lymphomas and emphasize the importance of integrating clinical, histopathologic, and immunophenotypic findings when establishing the diagnosis.

The prognostic role of biomarkers and imaging findings in PCGDTCL remains uncertain. Although some studies suggest that atypical expression of CD4, CD30, or TCRβF1 may correlate with indolent behavior [21], these associations remain inconsistent. Similarly, a recent systematic review found no clear survival differences according to epidermotropism, despite earlier suggestions of prognostic value [22]. In our cohort, one patient with early relapse demonstrated relatively high FDG uptake on PET imaging; however, this observation remains anecdotal and requires validation. Across published series, neither immunophenotypic features nor imaging findings have consistently predicted clinical outcomes, underscoring the importance of longitudinal clinical monitoring.

Another limitation of this study is the lack of molecular profiling. Although genomic alterations have been implicated in the biology of several T-cell lymphomas, molecular analyses such as next-generation sequencing were not available for these archival cases. Future studies incorporating genomic profiling may help clarify the biological heterogeneity and prognostic subgroups of PCGDTCL.

Response assessment also represents an important limitation of this study. Because this retrospective cohort spanned two decades, standardized response criteria specific for cutaneous lymphomas, such as the ISCL/EORTC global response criteria, were not consistently available. Therefore, RECIST version 1.1 was applied when measurable lesions were present. Although RECIST may provide a pragmatic approach in retrospective analyses, it does not fully capture the clinical characteristics of cutaneous lymphomas, and response assessments in this study should therefore be interpreted with caution.

This series contributes valuable long-term follow-up data, challenging the historical perception of PCGDTCL as a uniformly aggressive lymphoma. Nevertheless, the findings of this study should be interpreted cautiously given the small sample size and retrospective design. These findings underscore the existence of an indolent subset and suggest that selected patients with PCGDTCL may achieve meaningful disease control with relatively conventional or less intensive treatment approaches. These observations further highlight the importance of individualized therapeutic strategies. Future collaborative efforts and molecular profiling will be critical to refine risk stratification and optimize management strategies for this rare and enigmatic disease.

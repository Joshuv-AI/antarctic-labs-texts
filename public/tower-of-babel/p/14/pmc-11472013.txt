Lymphangiography and thoracic duct embolization for persistent chyle leak following augmentation mammoplasty and mastopexy
Authors: Leo Issagholian, Aldin Malkoc, Kendall A Vignaroli, Melissa Miramontes, Cameron C Neeki, Anthony Nguyen, Michael M Neeki
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractChyle leak is a relatively rare post-operative complication of breast and axilla surgery, and the majority of these cases can be managed conservatively. We present the case of a 42-year-old female with chyle leak within the external thoracic cavity following elective augmentation mammoplasty and mastopexy. This patient failed initial operative management consisting of implant removal, washout, suture plication, and intra-operative drain placement as well as subsequent conservative management including diet modification, total parenteral nutrition, and percutaneous aspiration. Ultimately, this patientʼs chyle leak was successfully managed with lymphangiography and thoracic duct embolization. To our knowledge, this is the first documented case of successful use of lymphangiography with embolization to manage chyle leak within the superficial chest, and this intervention should be considered when contemplating management of this post-operative complication within the breast cavity.Keywords: Mammoplasty, Breast implant, Chyle leak, Thoracic duct injury, Lymphangiography, Embolization

Introduction

Augmentation mammoplasty (AM) involves the utilization of additional volume to expand breast tissue, while mastopexy is the process of excising and re-draping breast parenchyma.1 In the US, nearly 200,000 AMs take place annually,2 and recent literature has shown that performing AM together with mastopexy results in decreased complications and improved patient outcomes.1 While both AM and mastopexy have been shown to be relatively safe with low rates of mortality,1, 2, 3 these procedures have been associated with adverse outcomes including scarring, poor cosmetic outcome, loss of nipple sensation, wound infection, dehiscence, silicone implant rupture, lymphoid hyperplasia secondary to implant material, hematoma, superficial abscess, deep thoracic abscess, and more rare outcomes such as squamous cell carcinoma and thoracic outlet syndrome.1,4, 5, 6

The surgical exposure and axillary approach1,7,8 sometimes utilized in AM can risk insult to the complex system of lymphatic drainage in that location due to this system's intricate anatomy subject to high grades of variation.8,9 Multiple routes of thoracic duct drainage exist with several potential tributaries, and injury involving lymphatic drainage can result in local chylous leak.8 While lymphatic compromise is a feared complication of breast and axillary procedures,9 thoracic duct injury with chyle leak is relatively rare following surgery involving the breast or axilla,8,10,11 and therefore the literature reports only a few cases detailing its occurrence and management within the breast cavity.

Chyle leak is most common following head, neck, and thoracic surgery,10 and this complication has long been approached with conservative management including diet modification, medication management, and percutaneous drainage.11, 12, 13 Conservative management is generally recommended for chylous drainage less than 500–600 mL per 24 h for seven consecutive days, or less than 2 l of drainage in a single 24 h period.11,12,14,15 Initial management of chylous drainage following breast and axillary surgery has been described similarly with nutritional triglyceride restriction, total parenteral nutrition, compression bandaging, percutaneous drainage, percutaneous drain placement, and medication management including somatostatin, octreotide, and orlistat.10,11,14,15 Literature also describes escalating management of chyle leak within the breast cavity following the failure of conservative management including lymphangiography for mapping11 and surgical re-exploration with interventions including washout, drainage, implant removal, drain placement, lymphatic channel ligation, and cavity space reduction with tissue glue or plication.10,11,14,15 We present a case highlighting the use of lymphangiography with embolization for management of chyle leak following augmentation mammoplasty and mastopexy.

Case report

A 42-year-old female with no significant past medical history underwent elective augmentation mammoplasty and mastopexy. This was a typical inframammary incision made with routine surgery not for malignancy, no other aberrant anatomy noted intra procedure. Postoperatively she developed persistent green, malodorous drainage at the site of the breast pocket and underwent re-operation for revision and implant removal where exploration of the subpectoral pocket revealed no evidence of abscess. Bilateral leakage of chyle was discovered within the subpectoral pocket, which was irrigated with hydrated ethanol and suture plicated to limit drainage, and bilateral drains were placed, shown in Figure 1. Post-operatively the left drain was removed, ciprofloxacin was initiated for intraoperative culture results with Klebsiella spp and plans for outpatient interventional radiology (IR) were initiated for lymphatic interrogation.

Coronal and sagittal views of an MRI displaying T1 enhancing fluid collection (indicated by the white arrow) deep to the subcutaneous tissue of the left breast.

Two weeks post-operatively, the patient presented to the emergency department complaining of left breast pain and fever of 100.8°Fahrenheit. The patient was noted to have tenderness and swelling of the left breast on exam. Workup was significant for leukocytosis and soft tissue ultrasound demonstrating cobblestone appearance of the subcutaneous tissue. Nearly 40 mL of cloudy yellow fluid was aspirated from the left breast and was positive for Staphylococcus aureus on culture. The patient felt relief of her pain and swelling following percutaneous aspiration and was discharged with instructions to complete her previous course of ciprofloxacin.

Three days later the patient returned to the emergency department with recurrent left breast pain despite compliance with her antibiotic regimen. Repeat percutaneous aspiration was performed, which demonstrated greater than 500 triglycerides, consistent with the presence of chyle. The patient attempted a low-fat diet and total parenteral nutrition with no improvement in chylous drainage.

Following conservative management the patient followed up with IR, where lymphangiography was performed bilaterally. Findings noted post-surgical changes in the left breast with accompanying fluid collection (Figure 2), and evidence of terminal thoracic duct obstruction with reflux and collateralization into the neck lymph nodes. It appeared there was abnormal tortuous lymphatic vessels in the left neck and chest wall and apparent tandem stenoses versus hypertrophied valves. Due to difficulty advancing a catheter into the stenotic lesions the decision was made to embolize the thoracic duct with multiple coils mixed 2:1 by volume with lipiodol.

A) Lymphangiogram with evidence of terminal thoracic duct obstruction (Black arrows showing the thoracic duct) with reflux and collateralization into neck lymph nodes (White arrows). B) No noted leak of contrast material from the thoracic duct, there are abnormal tortuous lymphatic vessels in the left neck and chest wall (White arrows) that suggest tandem stenoses of distal duct. Contrast does progress antegrade within the thoracic duct into the left subclavian vein/brachiocephalic vein (Black arrows). There is some stenosis of the distal thoracic duct near its insertion on the left subclavian vein.

Following embolization of the thoracic duct, the patient continued to have minimal drainage. The surgical team had suspected there was an unusual chylous fistula continuous with the subpectoral pockets where the implants for augmentation mammoplasty had been removed. The surgical team detailed the irrigation, performance of popcorn capsulorrhaphy, as well as gelfoam placement with talc powder in bilateral subpectoral pockets. One month after the procedure the patient no longer had chylous drainage. The patient did not experience symptoms associated with congestion of lymphatic flow, her only complaint on one year follow up was concerns with daily pectoral muscular tightness.

Discussion

Chyle leak is a rare adverse outcome following breast and axillary surgery. This case report details the development of persistent chylous drainage within the left breast refractory to surgical exploration and conservative management and describes successful management with lymphangiography and embolization of a regurgitant thoracic duct draining into post surgical potential spaces.

Literature suggests that cases of chyle leak after breast and axillary surgery are likely due to injury of an aberrant branch of the thoracic duct,8 however interestingly the patient in our report experienced this complication despite the confirmation of normal variant thoracic duct anatomy on lymphangiography. Because chyle leak following breast surgery is relatively rare, there is no definitive consensus within the literature regarding its management,10 however literature still exists describing various successful approaches to its management. Most cases of chyle leak within the axilla and breast have been successfully managed conservatively,10 and cases that have failed conservative management have been successfully treated with surgical intervention.10,11,14,15 Though lymphangiography has been described as a technique to identify the source of chyle leak within the chest intra-operatively,11 to our knowledge this is the first case reporting the use of lymphangiography with embolization in successful treatment of breast cavity chyle leak.

Conclusion

Though rare, chyle leak following breast and axillary surgery can carry considerable morbidity and decrease quality of life as detailed in this patient, including pain, multiple surgeries and procedures, and financial devastation for a long time with persistent neuropathy and periodic pain in that region. The use of lymphangiography with thoracic duct embolization should not be overlooked as a solution to address this complication.

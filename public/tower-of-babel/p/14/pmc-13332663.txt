A small surgical aortic bioprosthesis is associated with an increased risk of unfavorable hemodynamic outcomes after aortic valve-in-valve (AViV) transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR). Acute bend or kinking of the aortic graft after replacement of the aortic root with a synthetic graft and bioprosthesis may impair tracking of a TAVR delivery system.

Case Summary

A 29-year-old man with a complex congenital heart disease history presented to an outside hospital with progressive bilateral lower extremity edema, exertional dyspnea, and orthopnea. His surgical history included 1) arterial switch repair for dextrotransposition of the great arteries with ventricular septal defect patch closure and atrial septal defect primary closure during the first week of life, 2) reoperation valve-sparing aortic root replacement with a 28-mm Valsalva graft for neo–aortic root and ascending aortic aneurysm 9 years earlier, and 3) second reoperation surgical aortic valve replacement with a 23-mm Carpentier-Edwards Magna Ease valve (Edwards Lifesciences) for severe aortic regurgitation (AR), and pulmonary artery reconstruction with a 22-mm Hemashield graft performed 6 months after the aortic root surgery. The patient had followed up with a cardiologist for 5 years.

He was found to have bioprosthetic aortic stenosis and AR with reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 41% to 45%, and was referred to our hospital for further evaluation.

Transthoracic echocardiography confirmed severe bioprosthetic aortic stenosis (mean gradient: 61 mm Hg), moderate AR, further decreased LVEF (31%), and moderately impaired right ventricular systolic function (tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion: 1.22 cm, tricuspid annulus free wall S′: 5.61 cm/s), with severe pulmonary hypertension (>75 mm Hg) (Video 9, Video 10, Video 11).

Right and left heart catheterization demonstrated a peak-to-peak transvalvular gradient of 87 mm Hg, mild to moderate AR, LVEF of 30%, left ventricular end-diastolic pressure of 35 mm Hg, normal coronaries, severe pulmonary hypertension (90/30 mm Hg), and proximal right pulmonary artery stenosis (Video 12). Aortography revealed marked angulation and kinking of the ascending aortic graft (Figure 1, Video 1).

Aortography Demonstrating Marked Angulation and Kinking of the Ascending Aortic Graft (Yellow Arrow)

Although surgical correction of the degenerate aortic valve and pulmonary artery stenosis would have addressed the underlying pathophysiology, the patient was deemed at high risk for surgery because of a fourth sternotomy and severe biventricular dysfunction. Therefore, the heart team elected to proceed with AViV TAVR, anticipating improvement of ventricular function after relief of afterload.

Preprocedural computed tomography (CT) demonstrated a small surgical bioprosthetic annulus with leaflet calcification and a large left ventricular outflow tract (Figure 2). The ascending aortic graft showed an acute kink along the lesser curvature with marked eccentricity, raising concern for catheter deliverability and potential compromise of valve frame expansion for a tall transcatheter valve (Figures 3A to 3C). However, the distance from the bioprosthetic annulus to the aortic wall along the greater curvature at the angulation site exceeded 50 mm, supporting feasibility.

Given the patient's history of aortic root replacement, balloon valve fracture to enable implantation of a larger balloon-expandable valve was contraindicated because of the risk of root injury. The only balloon-expandable option was a 20-mm Sapien 3 Ultra Resilia (Edwards Lifesciences); however, this was abandoned because of concern for residual transvalvular gradient after implantation, as well as the risk of the naked valve dislodgement during tracking across the kinked aortic graft and surgical bioprosthesis.

Among self-expanding valve options, the Evolut FX+ (Medtronic) delivery system, which incorporates a single-spine shaft, was considered less favorable because it could limit trackability through the kinked aortic graft and surgical bioprosthesis. Therefore, a 23-mm Navitor Vision valve (Abbott), with the hydrophilic spine-free FlexNav delivery system, was selected.

Procedural Steps

The procedure was performed under general anesthesia with transesophageal echocardiography guidance.

Aortography was performed to obtain a left anterior oblique surgical valve side view (Video 2) and 3-cusp coplanar view of the surgical valve (Video 3), visually confirming adequate space for implantation of a 23-mm Navitor Vision valve.

We selected a Lunderquist extra-stiff guidewire (Cook Medical) to provide maximal support. Balloon aortic valvuloplasty was performed using a 21-mm True balloon (Becton Dickinson) to facilitate subsequent Navitor delivery system crossing. Resistance was encountered while advancing the balloon across the kinked graft and surgical bioprosthesis but was overcome through careful manipulation of the catheter and guidewire.

Delivery of the Navitor system is illustrated in Figure 4 and Video 4. The Lunderquist wire helped bias it to the outer curve of the aortic graft (Figure 4A), allowing the FlexNav delivery system nose cone to smoothly track along the Lunderquist wire at the kinked segment (Figure 4B). The flexible catheter then conformed to the wire curvature (Figure 4C) and crossed the surgical bioprosthesis without resistance (Figure 4D).

The 23-mm Navitor Vision valve was deployed to 80% without ventricular pacing (Video 5). After confirming appropriate positioning, final deployment was performed during a brief period of ventricular pacing, without subsequent hemodynamic compromise (Video 6).

Postdilation was performed using a 21-mm True balloon to optimize valve frame expansion (Video 7). Increased resistance was encountered while advancing the balloon catheter across the kinked graft and the outflow crowns of the Navitor. During retrieval, the deflated balloon interacted with the outflow crowns of the Navitor valve along the minor curvature, posing a potential risk of valve embolization if manipulated carelessly. Careful, controlled withdrawal of the balloon catheter while maintaining guidewire support was therefore required.

Subsequent transesophageal echocardiography confirmed appropriate valve positioning, with no paravalvular leak and mean gradient of 4 mm Hg (Videos 13 and 14).

Final aortography demonstrated 3-mm depth deployment, adequate valve frame expansion, and stable positioning within the kinked aortic graft (Figure 5 and Video 8).

Transthoracic echocardiography on postoperative day 1 showed unchanged LVEF (30%), no transvalvular and paravalvular regurgitation, a mean gradient of 9 mm Hg, and an effective orifice area of 1.47 cm2 (Video 15, Video 16, Video 17). Estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure decreased to 43 mm Hg. Postprocedural CT demonstrated circular valve frame expansion up to node 3, supporting excellent valve function (Figures 6A to 6H). The outflow-side frame above node 3 also expanded favorably, conforming to the kinked aortic graft (Figures 6I to 6L), with the tall valve positioned entirely within the available aortic root space (Figure 6L).

The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged home on postoperative day 2.

Potential Pitfalls

This case highlights 3 key points: 1) optimal valve selection in a small surgical aortic valve not amenable to fracture in a hostile aortic root anatomy, 2) difficulty of AViV TAVR with a kinked aortic graft in terms of delivery system tracking, and 3) feasibility of an intra-annular self-expanding valve in a kinked aortic graft.

A small surgical aortic valve poses postprocedural hemodynamic challenges. Registry studies comparing the self-expanding Navitor with balloon-expandable Sapien 3 Ultra Resilia valves, both with intra-annular bovine leaflets, have demonstrated hemodynamic advantage with the self-expanding design.1,2 A prior transcatheter valve therapy registry analysis reported a mean gradient of 12 mm Hg after implantation of a 20-mm balloon-expandable Sapien 3 Ultra Resilia valve.3 The mean gradient of 9 mm Hg achieved in the present case supports the rationale for selecting a 23-mm Navitor Vision valve for a small surgical bioprosthesis.

Severe angulation of the aortic graft could have compromised trackability; however, the Lunderquist wire biased the trajectory to the outer curve, allowing the Navitor system to track smoothly. Its soft nosecone and flexible delivery system further reduced the risk of getting caught by the kinked portion of the aortic graft, highlighting the superior deliverability of the Navitor system. In contrast, although the Sapien 3 system offers deliverability advantages through its active flexion, advancing a naked balloon-expandable valve across markedly angulated anatomy may carry a risk of aortic injury or valve dislodgement from the delivery system. For the Evolut system, snare-assisted techniques may facilitate advancement of the delivery catheter; however, the pronounced kink and turn into the proximal root would likely have made contralateral snare manipulation difficult and unreliable. Furthermore, with the supra-annular design, the Evolut outflow crowns may get compressed by the kinked aortic graft and risk underexpansion and hemodynamic compromise.

Pre-TAVR CT demonstrated a straight space >50 mm above the surgical annulus, sufficient to accommodate the 23-mm Navitor Vision (height: 47 mm). Postprocedural CT verified circular frame expansion up to node 3 on the functioning portion of the Navitor valve.4 Underexpansion could compromise valve function or complicate delivery system retrieval. In contrast, a 20-mm Sapien 3 Ultra Resilia (height: 15 mm) or a 26-mm Evolut FX+ (height: 45 mm) could theoretically have been deployed within the limited space; however, these options carried substantial delivery-related challenges in the markedly angulated graft anatomy, as discussed earlier.

Conclusions

This case demonstrates favorable hemodynamic performance of an intra-annular self-expanding Navitor Vision valve in a small surgical bioprosthesis, illustrates the superior deliverability of the catheter system over a Lunderquist extra-stiff guidewire, and highlights the importance of careful assessment when selecting a tall transcatheter valve in an angulated aortic graft.

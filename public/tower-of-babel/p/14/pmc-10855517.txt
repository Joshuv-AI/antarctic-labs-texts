Evaluation of the Effect of Nanocoating on Mechanical and Biofilm Formation in Thermoplastic Polyurethane Aligner Sheets
Authors: Mahalakshmi Krishnakumaran, Janisha Mahalingam, Shanthinipriya Arumugam, Deepak Prabhu, T. M. Parameswaran, Balaji Krishnan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Objective:The objective of this research is to determine whether the thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) coated with carboxymethylcellulose chitosan has better mechanical and antibacterial action (anti-Streptococcus mutans) when utilized in intraoral simulations with synthetic saliva.Materials and Methods:The TPU sheets (n = 45) were divided into three groups. Control (n = 15) consists of as-received TPU sheets. Test 1 consists of TPU sheets coated with carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) chitosan (CHI) (n = 15). Test 2 consists of thermoformed nano-coated TPU (n = 15). For the polyurethane sheets, CHI-CMC sheets, and thermoformed CHI-CMC sheets, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and three-point flexural tests were conducted and assessed. The materials testing software was used to calculate the elastic modulus and tensile strength. To study the bacterial accumulation, the cut circles of the TPU aligner were placed in centrifuge tubes with 2.5 mL of bacterial suspension at a concentration of 104 or 105 CFU/mL. SEM was done to assess the presence of cell growth in all three groups.Results:According to SEM analyses of bacterial buildup, the coated TPU had minimal biofilms compared to the bare TPUs numerous biofilms. The effect of aging on coating thickness reveals that the thickness of thermoformed coated TPU films dramatically decreased over time, while the thickness of coated TPU films was maintained. When TPU is coated with CMC/CHI, the elastic modulus and tensile strength were observed to improve.Conclusion:The development of a super-hydrophilic coating by the CHI CMC coating on TPU sheets improved the coating's biocompatibility while also changing the shape of the multilayer film to prevent bacterial adhesion. The effect on the improvement in the mechanical properties diminished after the material underwent a thermoforming process. It is therefore suggested that the nanofilm be used in therapeutic applications following the thermoforming process.

Introduction

Esthetics is an integral part of dentistry which has resulted in the widespread use of clear orthodontic appliances. Nowadays, dynamic orthodontic treatment and maintenance are frequently carried out using esthetic appliances such as ceramic brackets, clear aligners, or retainers.

Clear aligners are a dental treatment device that has been widely used in the last decade because of several obvious advantages such as cost-effectiveness, good formability, and good optical characteristics.[1] The dental cast model is imprinted with thermoplastic sheets using a heating cycle along with either vacuum or pressure to create clear aligners.[234] The majority of aligners use material made of modified polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PET-G), polypropylene, polycarbonate, polyurethanes, co-polyester, etc.[5] However, these substances also have mechanical drawbacks, such as weak abrasion and corrosion resistance, which is a high-risk factor for the development of bacteria-related diseases, such as dental caries and periodontal disease.[67] The most prevalent bacteria in the mouth are Streptococcus mutans, Streptococcus sobrinus, Lactobacillus, and Candida.

The first three induce tooth decay and enamel demineralization, whereas the latter is the primary cause of stomatitis, which causes pain, discomfort, and other abnormalities.[8910] Therefore, several strategies have been tried to circumvent these limitations. Among these, the nano-coating method has recently drawn more interest since it allows for site-specific application with minimal increase in thickness.[1112] Nanoparticles are insoluble particles with <100 nm in size providing an increased surface-to-volume ratio. Nanoparticles have been used in orthodontics either by combining them with dental materials or by coating the surfaces of the brackets, archwires, etc., with the nanoparticles thereby preventing the micro adhesion of the biofilm.[13] Earlier studies have shown that gold nanoparticles have antibacterial action against Porphyromonas gingivalis.[14] A study done by Eslamian et al.[15] has shown significant antibacterial action with reduced shear bond strength in silver nanoparticle-induced adhesive against S. mutans. Other such works done on zinc, cinnamaldehyde, graphene, resin modified with chlorhexidine-loaded mesoporous silica nanoparticles, and zinc oxide quantum dots,[16] etc., also proved to have anti-microbial properties.

The purpose of this research is to ascertain whether the TPU coated with the cross-linked multilayer film made of carboxymethylcellulose chitosan has improved mechanical and antibacterial properties (anti-S. mutans) when used in intraoral simulations with artificial saliva.

Materials and Methods

The study was approved by the Institute Ethics Committee, Tagore Dental College and Hospital, Chennai, India (IEC/TDCH/119/2022 dated October 26, 2022). The study did not involve any human samples. Thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) is a highly adaptable material with several advantageous traits, including superior mechanical and elastomeric features, chemical and abrasion resistance, adhesive properties, and ease of machining.[17]

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using G Power software 3.1.9.

Level of significance = 5%

Effect size = 1.5%

Total sample = 15

The sample (n = 45) consists of three groups: Control (n = 15) consists of uncoated TPU sheets, Test 1 (n = 15) consists of TPU sheets coated with carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) chitosan, and Test 2 (n = 15) consists of thermoformed CMC coated TPU sheets.

The mechanical and antibacterial activity of the TPU sheets in all three groups was compared under simulated intraoral conditions with artificial saliva.

Experimental design

Preparation of polysaccharide-based coating through dip coating method

The as received TPU thermoplastic polymer sheets with a thickness of 1.0 mm, were used as the substrates. The substrates were surface modified by plasma treatment using 5 mM of an aqueous solution of branched polyethyleneimine for 6 h and subsequently rinsed twice with deionized water for 4 min. By dissolving sodium CMC and water-soluble chitosan in DI water, fresh polysaccharide solutions with opposing charges were created at a concentration of 1 mg/mL. The pH values of the created solutions were then raised to 4.

The surface-treated substrates were twice rinsed with DI water after being submerged in the CMC and CHI solutions for 10 minutes each at room temperature. A (CMC/CHI) film with a thickness of 1.5–2 μm was produced by carrying out this cycle n times, where the substrate was submerged alternately in the CHI and CMC solutions [Figure 1].

Graphical representation of carboxymethyl cellulose CHI coating on thermoplastic polyurethane sheets

Determination of tensile strength and elastic modulus

From sheets of each material, test specimens with 20 mm length and 1 mm × 1 mm cross-sectional measurements were cut. The thermoplastic test specimens underwent three-point flexural tests. For the test specimens, the distance between supports was 15 mm. Specimens were loaded at 25°C with a deflection of 3 mm applied at a strain rate of 1 mm/min using a universal testing machine. The tensile strength and elastic modulus were calculated using the materials testing software.

Determination of bacterial growth

Sheets that were 1 mm thick and had an area of 3 mm2 were cut to serve as scanning electron microscopy (SEM) test specimens. SEM of as-received polyurethane sheets, carboxymethyl cellulose-coated TPU sheets, and thermoformed CHI-CMC sheets were analyzed.

S. mutans is the most prominent acidogenic oral bacteria, which can cause white spot lesions or serious demineralization of the tooth enamel. The TPU aligner sheets were cut into circles (6 mm in diameter), disinfected with glutaraldehyde, three times rinsed with sterilized water, and then dried in the absence of contaminants. The circles were put into centrifuge tubes with 2.5 mL of a 104 or 105 CFU/mL bacterial solution. In anaerobic bags, the cells were grown statically at 37°C. Cell growth was quantified using a microplate reader to record the optical density at 600 nm (OD600) after 1, 2, and 14 days (BioRad 680, Hercules, CA, USA). At a concentration of 104 CFU/mL, the cell growth of the bacterial suspension was monitored for 14 days.

Statistical analysis

The results were analyzed using the IBM SPSS Statistics 20.0 software package. ANOVA was used to analyze the mean elastic modulus and tensile strength.

Results

The SEM images show that CMC CHI-coated multilayer TPU film had a porous structure because of the additional bond formation and reassembly between the polysaccharide chains. On the other hand, thermoformed CMC/CHI-coated TPU had a collapsed porous structure with several cracks. SEM studies on bacterial accumulation also showed that there are enormous biofilms in the bare TPU, whereas there are few biofilms in the coated TPU [Figure 2a-e].

Discussion

Nano coating has the advantage of site-specific application. Concerning the colonization of invisible aligners, Low et al.[18] SEM studies have documented an ordered growth of biofilm on the surfaces of the appliances, particularly localized on more recessed and protected parts of the device, including the cusp tips and attachment dimples. According to a study by Levrini et al.,[6] biofilm was present on the internal surfaces of the aligners even after being cleaned with toothpaste, brushing, and sodium carbonate and sulfate tablets. This resulted in the aligners becoming discolored, having an unpleasant odor, and interacting with bacteria already present in the oral cavity. As a result, we created a biocompatible, oral-specific antibacterial polysaccharide film.

We specifically used the dip coating method to create multilayer films made of chitosan (CHI) and CMC on TPU.[19] Due to their similar chemical structures, the linear polysaccharides CHI and CMC are very compatible with one another. Rich amine groups in CHI have an antibacterial action, and CMC molecules can establish hydrogen bonds with six molecules of water.

As a result of these characteristics, they are regarded as appropriate materials for the development of super-hydrophilic coatings that have an antibacterial impact and with high molecular weight and good mechanical strength. The crosslinking induces additional bonds and reassembly between the polysaccharide chains that form the multilayer film, transforming the dense structure of the multilayer film into a porous structure. However, the enhanced mechanical properties of coated TPU were diminished after the thermoforming process. Usually, the thermal or pressure treatment reinforces the bonding strength between film layers. The sheets undergo considerable morphological changes as a result of the imprinting process on the model, which may be detrimental to the nanofilms' mechanical and structural qualities. In our study, we did a nano-coating of 2 µm - critical for the function of temporomandibular joints and patients' comfort. A decrease in thickness of the thermoformed TPU might be owed to the aggregation of CHI, which occurs as the pH increases, and the poor stability of the multilayer film before thermoforming. In our study, the thermoforming process did not cause significant surface changes in the thermoplastic clear aligners which was similar to the results of Ahn et al.[5] Only a significantly smoother surface was produced through thermoforming. We can conclude that the thermoforming process had a considerable effect not only on the thermoplastic polymer sheet, but also on the surface of the CMC/CHI films. Hence, in clinical applications, thermoforming should occur before nanofilm manufacturing.

Bacterial biofilms have a strong tendency to adhere to surfaces and increase resistance to antibiotics and host immune systems, thus it is extremely significant to prevent their initial adhesion in medical devices associated with bacterial infection. To evaluate the chitosan and carboxymethylcellulose antibacterial properties the sample circles (6 mm in diameter), which were coated transparent aligners, were submerged in S. mutans bacterial solutions. The carboxymethylcellulose–chitosan–coated clear aligners showed bactericidal properties.

No colonies formed from the suspension (undiluted) on coated TPU sheets at a concentration of 104 CFU/mL, whereas a large number of colonies formed from the 104 to 105 CFU/mL suspensions on untreated TPU sheets which correlated with the study of Nguyen et al.[20] The super-hydrophilic coating on medical devices inhibits biofilm adhesion by forming a thin water film which interferes with the interaction between the surface of the medical device and the biofilm. The cross-linking of the CMC/CHI film made it porous and rough, which created a super-hydrophilic surface that inhibited bacterial adherence and had a bacterial reduction ratio of about 75%.

Limitations and future prospects

The study was conducted to assess the effect of CMC chitosan-coated TPU sheets on S. mutans. Future studies involving the effect of this nanocoating on various other bacteria and other aligner sheets such as PET-G needs to be assessed. We did not consider the effect of the thermoaging process on the mechanical and bactericidal activity of carboxymethyl cellulose-coated TPU sheets. In vivo studies using nanocoated sheets should also be explored for the benefit of the patients.

Conclusion

The CHI CMC coating on TPU sheets showed enhanced biomechanical and anti-bacterial properties than as received TPU sheets. Since the thermoforming process deteriorates these effects, it is advisable to introduce the nanofilm in clinical applications after the thermoforming procedure.

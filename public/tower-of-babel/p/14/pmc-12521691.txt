Identification and validation of a prognostic model for HER2-low breast cancer based on unfolded protein response-related genes
Authors: Yanjiao Zhao, Yuanyuan Gao, Hui Yan, Ping Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesHuman epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is down-regulated in approximately 45–55% of breast cancer patients. Cancer cells activate endoplasmic reticulum stress, which is counteracted by the unfolded protein response (UPR). The objective of the present research is to assess the predictive significance of UPR-related genes and investigate their effects on the immune landscape in HER2-low breast cancer patients.MethodsThe prognostic UPR related genes in patients were identified by univariate Cox regression analysis, and the risk score model was created using LASSO Cox regression analysis. The nomogram model was built using RMS package, and the protein–protein interaction network was created via the STRING database.ResultsThrough comprehensive analysis, we identified four UPR-related genes (COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1) that were significantly associated with HER2-low breast cancer prognosis. These genes were utilized to construct a robust risk score model, which effectively stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups. Patients in the high-risk group exhibited significantly worse clinical outcomes, confirming the independent prognostic value of the risk score in multivariate analysis. Furthermore, pathway enrichment analysis revealed significant suppression of immune-related signaling pathways (e.g. PI3K–AKT) in high-risk patients, alongside distinct tumor microenvironment profiles characterized by differential immune cell infiltration, altered expression of immune checkpoints, and significantly different TIDE scores, suggesting potential implications for immunotherapy response stratification.ConclusionsThe prognostic model based on UPR related genes, COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1, could predict the prognosis of HER2-low breast cancer patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03718-2.Keywords: HER2-low breast cancer, Unfolded protein response related genes, Risk score, Prognosis

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most frequently occurring cancer diagnosed in women and it’s the most prevalent malignant tumor that endanger their health [1]. In the United States, roughly 300,590 new instances of breast cancer were discovered, with approximately 43,700 fatalities in 2023 [2]. Breast cancer can be classified into three distinct categories based on molecular and histological features: breast cancer expressing hormone receptors (ER+ or PR+), breast cancer expressing human epidermal receptor 2 (HER2+), and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) (ER, PR, HER2) [3]. It has been substantiated that HER2 up-regulation was associated with shorter survival rate and higher risk of breast cancer [4]. The combination therapy of anti-HER2 monoclonal antibody (trastuzumab, pertuzumab) is standard treatment for HER2-high breast cancer patients [5]. These anti-HER2 targeted therapies have elevated the prognosis of breast cancer with high HER2 expression [6]. In addition, HER2-low breast cancers account for about 45–55% of breast cancer [7]. Zhang et al. have reported that HER2-low breast cancer exhibits lower Ki67 expression and is enriched for hormone receptor-positive tumors. Additionally, these tumors show reduced pathological complete response rates following neoadjuvant therapy [8]. Accumulating evidences have indicated that HER2-low expression could be a new type of breast cancer [9]. Prediction of recurrence risk and prognostic risk after endocrine therapy in HER2-low breast cancer patients requires further study.

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a critical cellular organelle that participates in the folding, modification, and intracellular transport of proteins [10]. Under pathological conditions, increased protein secretion or impaired ER protein folding capacity leads to the accumulation of unfolded or misfolded proteins in the ER lumen. This state is termed endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) [11]. Cells undertake a signaling cascade known as the unfolded protein response (UPR) to restore ER homeostasis and guarantee protein folding integrity [12]. The UPR is primarily mediated by three key sensors: PERK, Iositol-requiring enzyme 1 (IRE1α), and activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6). Among these, ATF6 activation orchestrates a transcriptional program to alleviate ERS and restore proteostasis [13]. However, persistent ER stress and sustained activation of the UPR have been implicated in the pathogenesis of various diseases, including cancer and neurodegenerative disorders [14, 15]. Cancer cells release ER stress-related factors that can modulate immune responses, thereby facilitating tumor progression [16, 17]. Moreover, studies have shown that UPR activation suppresses the cytotoxicity mediated by natural killer cells and CD8+ T cells, further promoting cancer development [14].

Therefore, this study aimed to identify key UPR-related genes in HER2-low breast cancer patients, to preliminarily investigate their regulatory mechanisms, and to evaluate the impact of these genes on prognosis and the immune microenvironment. The findings may provide valuable insights to assist clinicians in better understanding the role of UPR-related genes in HER2-low breast cancer, ultimately contributing to improved clinical outcomes.

Materials and methods

Data collection

The mRNA expression profiling data and complete survival information of 461 HER2-low breast cancer samples were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, https://tcga-data.nci.nih.gov/tcga/) database. The GSE159956, GSE35629 and GSE10885 dataset were downloaded from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. The GSE35629 and GSE10885 datasets collectively comprised 98 HER2-low breast cancer patients with complete survival information.

Differential gene expression analysis

Differential gene analysis between two groups was implemented using R-language (version 4.2.1, the same below) limma package [18]. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified with |logFC| >1 and FDR < 0.05.

Least absolute shrinkage selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression analysis

LASSO Cox regression was enforced by R-language glmnet package [19] to further screen candidate genes in breast cancer. The risk score for each sample was calculated with the candidate genes via the following formula in TCGA database.

Coefi means the risk coefficient for each factor, Xi means expression value for each factor, corresponding to the value of mRNA expression. Patients were classified into low- and high-risk groups based on the median risk score.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and survival analysis

The GSEA was implemented by R-language function package “clusterprofiler” [20]. The significantly enriched pathways were screened by |NES|>1 and P < 0.05. The survival rates of patients were calculated with the R language “survival” and “survminer” packages (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=survival), and the log-rank was applied to analyze the significance of differences in survival rate. Multivariate Cox regression model was applied to analyze whether the candidate genes predicted the survival of patients independently of other factors.

Immune cell infiltration

Relative ratios of 22 human immune cells were obtained by CIBERSORT software [21]. According to the gene expression matrix, CIBERSORT described the composition of immune infiltrating cells using the pre-specified 547 barcode genes in deconvolution algorithm.

Construction of nomogram prediction model

To predict survival rate (1-, 3-, and 5-year) of cancer patients, considering the effect of multiple factors on prognosis, we create a nomogram model using the R language RMS package, and plotted the calibration curve of nomogram to look the correlation of nomogram predicted rate with actual incidence.

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

The STRING database (https://string-db.org/,version 11.0) [22] was employed to explore the functional connections among proteins. The Cytoscape (version 3.7.2) [23] was employed to visualize this PPI network.

Statistical analysis

To compare gene expression differences between groups, the Student’s t-test was employed. Differences in TIDE scores and immune cell infiltration levels across subgroups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. For variables following a normal distribution, Pearson correlation analysis was conducted with the “cor” function in R. Spearman correlation analysis was applied for variables that did not meet the normality assumption. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.1).

Results

Predictive prognostic model for low HER2 expression breast cancer based on hub UPR related genes

First, we identified 177 UPR-related genes from the GSEA database (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/) (Table S1). Univariate Cox regression analyses identified 16 of these UPR-related genes significantly associated with prognosis in HER2-low breast cancer within the TCGA cohort (Table S2, Fig. 1A), while 31 genes demonstrated prognostic significance in the GSE159956 dataset (Table S3, Fig. S1). Notably, four genes - COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1—were consistently identified as prognostic markers in both datasets (Fig. 1B).

B The overlapping genes between 16 and 31 genes datasets. C The lambdas quality control plot of LASSO Cox regression analysis. D Kaplan-Meier curves of high-risk and low-risk groups in the TCGA cohort. E Kaplan-Meier curves of high-risk and low-risk groups in the meta-GEO cohort (GSE35629 and GSE10885). F, G Risk factor association plots in the TCGA and meta-GEO cohort, respectively, the different colors were represented different groupings. H The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of the risk score of patients in the TCGA cohort

Subsequently, we constructed a prognostic risk score model incorporating the four identified genes (COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1) using LASSO Cox regression coefficients as weights: Risk score = (0.55 × COPS5) + (0.11 × DKC1) + (0.23 × NOP56) + (0.61 × EIF4G1) (Fig. 1C). Based on the median risk score (1.11576) in the TCGA cohort, HER2-low breast cancer patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups. The high-risk group demonstrated significantly worse prognosis compared to the low-risk group in both the TCGA cohort (Fig. 1D) and the meta-GEO cohort (GSE35629 and GSE10885; Fig. 1E). Consistent with these findings, we observed a higher proportion of deceased patients in the high-risk groups across both cohorts (TCGA: Fig. 1F; meta-GEO: Fig. 1G). Notably, all four genes showed significantly differential expression between the high- and low-risk groups (Fig. 1F).

The model demonstrated robust predictive performance, with time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis showing area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.72, 0.66, and 0.68 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival, respectively, in the TCGA cohort (Fig. 1H). Taken together, these results indicate that our four-gene prognostic risk model effectively predicts clinical outcomes in HER2-low breast cancer patients.

Nomogram model predicted prognosis of HER2-low breast cancer patients

Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed the risk score as an independent prognostic factor for HER2-low breast cancer patients (Fig. 2A). We subsequently developed a nomogram incorporating three clinically significant variables: the risk score, age, and tumor stage (Fig. 2B). The calibration plots demonstrated excellent agreement between predicted and observed outcomes, as evidenced by the close approximation of the corrected curves to the ideal reference line at 1-, 3-, and 5-year intervals (Fig. 2C–E).

C–E Calibration curves of nomogram to predict probability of 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival in HER2-low breast cancer patients

Functional information obtained employing GSEA

To elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying the differential prognosis between risk groups, we performed GSEA to identify significantly dysregulated pathways and biological processes. GSEA analysis of KEGG pathway revealed that the Cell cycle, Oocyte meiosis, and Progesterone-mediated oocyte maturation signaling pathways were significantly activated in the high-risk group, whereas the PI3K–Akt signaling pathway was significantly suppressed in the low-risk group (Fig. 3A, Table S4). Meanwhile, GSEA analysis of GO terms showed that risk score was correlated with cell cycle, nuclear division, chromosome organization, condensed chromosome terms (Fig. 3B, Table S5).

COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, EIF4G1 related PPI and lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA networks

Next, we constructed a PPI network comprising the four prognostic genes (COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1) using an interaction score threshold > 0.15. The resulting network contained 4 nodes and 5 edges (Fig. 4A). Using TargetScan (version 7.1), we identified potential miRNA regulators for each gene: 30 miRNAs targeting COPS5, 57 targeting DKC1, 17 targeting NOP56, and 7 targeting EIF4G1. Subsequent analysis with miRNet predicted 426, 631, 370, and 196 lncRNAs potentially regulating these miRNAs for COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1, respectively. These interactions were visualized in comprehensive gene regulatory networks (Fig. 4B–E). To enhance biological relevance, we focused on evolutionarily conserved miRNAs (those with stable sequences across species) from TargetScan. This stringent analysis yielded a core regulatory network consisting of 174 lncRNAs, 7 miRNAs, and the 4 target mRNAs (Fig. 4F).

Immune landscape in high-risk and low-risk HER2-low breast cancer patients

Comprehensive immune profiling of the TCGA cohort revealed distinct tumor microenvironment patterns between high- and low-risk HER2-low breast cancer patients (Fig. 5A). As shown in Fig. 5B–J, the proportion of multiple immune cell populations were significantly differential between high- and low-risk HER2-low groups, including naïve B cells, resting and activated CD4 + memory T cells, follicular helper T cells, monocytes, M0/M1 macrophages, activated dendritic cells, and resting mast cells, suggesting risk-stratified immune responses may contribute to the observed prognostic differences.

B–J The levels of naïve B cells, CD4+ memory T cells (resting/activated), follicular helper T cells, monocytes, M0/M1 macrophages, activated dendritic cells, and resting mast cells between high- and low-risk groups

We screened 7 immune related cell markers (ANPEP, CD14, CD209, PTPRC, CD83, CD86, HLA-DRfamily) of breast cancer from CellMarker (http://xteam.xbio.top/CellMarker/) database. The risk score was shown to be linked with these markers (Fig. 6A and B), and the expression of ANPEP, HLA-DRA and HLA-DRB6 were elevated in low-risk group (Fig. 6C). Moreover, the LAG3, TIGIT, CTLA4, CD80 expression were observably increased in high-risk group (Fig. 6D). The tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) score was similarly higher in the l-risk group than in the high-risk group (Fig. 6E and F).

Discussion

In the present research, we built a prognostic risk score based on 4 UPR related genes (COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, EIF4G1) by comprehensive bioinformatic analyses to investigate their potential prognostic value in HER2-low breast cancer patients. We revealed that the high-risk patients exhibited worse clinical outcomes. We also found that the immune cell infiltration, the expression of breast cancer immune related cell markers and immune checkpoints, and TIDE score were differential between high- and low-risk groups.

In the present study, we screened 4 hub UPR related genes, COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1), that were closely associated with the of HER2-low breast cancer patient’s prognosis, and were applied to create prognosis risk score model. In both TCGA and meta-GEO cohorts, the high-risk patients displayed inferior prognosis. Furthermore, COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, EIF4G1 expressions were remarkably differential between high- and low-risk groups and the risk score had a high diagnostic value for HER2-low breast cancer patients.

COPS5 is also known as CSN5, JAB1, SGN5 and MOV-34, plays critical regulatory roles in many signaling pathways [24]. It has been demonstrated that COPS5 was aberrantly overexpressed in multiple human tumors. The breast cancer patients with low COPS5 expression had no evidence of relapse or disease progression [25], and the functionally interacts with some tumor-related genes (CDK, P53, PD-L1), thereby, contributing to tumorigenesis [26]. Liu et al. reported that exogenous COPS5 expression can increase the adriamycin and cisplatin resistance of breast cancer cells [27]. Wu et al. have indicated that the COPS5 expression was positively regulated by lncRNA MRPS30-DT, the breast cancer patients had high expression of lncRNA MRPS30-DT exhibited inferior prognosis [28]. Moreover, in triple-negative breast cancer, the highly expressed miR-676-3p could decrease COPS5 expression, and lncRNA GATA3-AS1 could increase the COPS5 expression by sequestering miR-676-5p, thereby enhancing the progression of cancer and immune escape [29]. In this study, we identified that totally 30 targeted miRNAs and 426 targeted lncRNAs of COPS5 in HER2-low breast cancer patients. The above evidences suggested that the COPS5 was probably a pro-oncogene in breast cancer, and its expression was regulated by lncRNAs and miRNAs.

DKC1 encodes dyskerin that responsible for telomerase holoenzyme complex stability [30]. Like COPS5, DKC1 expression was also improved in a variety of human cancers. In colorectal cancer (CRC), DKC1 can increase the expression of HIF-1α to facilitate the migration, invasion, and angiogenesis of CRC cells [31], and the knockdown of DKC1 or its inhibitor pyrazofurin could remarkably attenuate proliferation of CRC cells [32]. Kan et al. have revealed that DKC1 downregulation abolished the accelerated effect of DKC1 on the proliferation of lung adenocarcinoma cells by inducing telomere-related cell senescence and apoptosis [33]. Moreover, the expression of DKC1 protein was related to with age, tumor size, tumour grade of breast cancer, and in SCMGENE subtypes, the DKC1 was highly expressed in the ER−/HER2 − cases [34]. Nevertheless, the effect of DKC1 on the development of HER2-low breast cancer has rarely been researched to the best of our knowledge.

NOP56 is also called NOL5A and SCA36. NOP56 is an important nucleolar protein that is highly conserved from yeast to human [35]. NOP56 is also reported to be high expressed in many human cancers, it could facilitate the occurrence and development of cancer cells via regulating signaling pathways, tumor-like stem cell pluripotency and DNA hypermethylation, and reducing the production of reactive oxygen [35]. In KRAS mutant lung cancer, the NOP56 downregulation plus rapamycin could inhibit tumor growth of KRAS-mutant lung cancer [36]. Zhang et al. have found the NOP56 expression was improved in both Wilms tumor tissues and cells, and the piRNA NU13 might indirectly regulate NOP56 expression and subsequently suppress the proliferation, migration, invasion of Wilms tumor, thereby acting as a tumor suppressor gene [37]. NOP56 enhanced the Burkitt’s lymphomas cell transformation induced by Myc mutations, and increased the size of tumor induced by Myc mutations [38]. In addition, the NOP56 silencing observably inhibit the colony formation and cell growth in breast cancer tumor cells [39].

EIF4G1 is a eukaryotic translation initiation factor [40]. Jaiswal et al. have revealed that in castration-resistant prostate cancer patients, the EIF4G1 was highly expressed or amplified, and took part in progression, growth and metastasis of cancer cells [41]. A pan-cancer analysis discovered that EIF4G1 expression was increased in multiple human tumors [42]. The EIF4G1 overexpression was correlated with shorter median survival rate in cancer patients. In addition, the EIF4G complex inhibitor could reduce clonogenic and inhibit invasion of cancer cells [42]. Li et al. have found that in breast cancer, EIF4G1 was correlated with T cell exhaustion, EIF4G1 related genes were participated in DNA replication, metastasis and the MAPK signaling pathway [43]. Accordingly, the EIF4G1 was also a pro-oncogene in breast cancer and it might influence the metastasis and immune microenvironment of breast cancer.

The GSEA showed that immune related signaling pathways, such as PI3K–Akt signaling pathway was observably inhibited in high-risk group. In addition, the levels of immune cell infiltration, such as cells CD4 memory resting, Macrophages.M1 Dendritic.cells.activated were observably differential between two groups (high- and low-risk). The immune cell infiltration, immune related cell markers of breast cancer expression, immune checkpoints expression and TIDE were differential between two groups. These results suggested that COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, EIF4G1 probably effect the immune microenvironment in HER2-low breast cancer.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we initially demonstrated that the effect of UPR related genes in HER2-low breast cancer. The prognostic risk score for HER2-low breast cancer has been successfully constructed using 4 UPR related genes, COPS5, DKC1, NOP56, and EIF4G1. Our data could provide more information for better understanding the link between UPR and HER2-low breast cancer, thereby contributing to the clinical management and novel drug development for HER2-low breast cancer in the future.

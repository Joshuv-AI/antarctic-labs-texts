1. Introduction
Published: 2025

In an era defined by escalating cross-border threats and complex global challenges, the European Region, and the world at large, face an urgent reckoning when it comes to injury prevention, safety promotion and global health. Armed conflicts, geopolitical instability, and the intensifying impacts of climate change now intersect with evolving public health crises, making safety more fragile than ever. Simultaneously, the global burden of injuries—from road traffic crashes and workplace accidents to violence and unintentional harm—continues to rise, yet remains a largely under-recognized public health epidemic.

Compounding these risks are the rapid digital transformations reshaping our societies. The spread of artificial intelligence and digital connectivity has introduced new vectors of harm—from cyberviolence, particularly targeting women, to broader cybersecurity and data protection threats—demanding a forward-looking, equity-driven approach to health and safety.

Responding to this landscape requires more than robust emergency preparedness. It demands a paradigm shift: the cultivation of a culture of preparedness rooted in anticipatory action, interdisciplinary collaboration, and systems-level resilience. At its core, this must integrate global health imperatives with injury prevention strategies and a holistic vision for safety promotion across all sectors.

The EU-SAFETY 2025 Conference, held from 30 September to 2 October 2025 in Heraklion, Crete, was conceived precisely to address these pressing issues. Centered on the theme “Preparedness for the Unexpected,” the conference brought together researchers, practitioners, policymakers, and civil society actors to explore innovative, evidence-informed approaches that bridge gaps between disciplines, regions, and systems.

Throughout its sessions, EU-SAFETY 2025 emphasized the latest advancements in injury prevention, encompassing child injuries, falls, home and leisure accidents, occupational trauma, transportrelated harm, and the evolving dimensions of women‘s health and safety. Equally central was the promotion of safety as a public health priority—not as a siloed concern, but as a foundation for resilient, equitable societies.

This special issue is capturing a breadth of research and practice aimed at reinforcing global health resilience, advancing injury prevention, and accelerating the promotion of safety in all its forms. Together, they represent a shared vision for a safer, healthier future.

In addition, strong emphasis was placed on the quality of care, patient safety, and equitable access to life-saving interventions—with due consideration across a broad range of interconnected topics, and with contributions across the globe. Key themes are reflected in the six plenaries:

PL001. Strengthening Trauma Systems and Surveillance in Europe

PL002. Safe Mobility in Transition: Artificial Intelligence, Human Factors and Data-Driven Solutions

PL003. Workplace Safety and Workforce Preparedness in a Changing Europe

PL004. Reimagining Resilient Health Systems: Integrating Risk, Intelligence, and One Health for Safety

PL005. Child Safety Across Settings: Protecting Children from Violence and Injuries

PL006. Women’s Health and Safety in Focus: Integrating Prevention, Advocacy, and Public Health Action

These six plenary sessions of EU-SAFETY 2025 highlight the critical intersections of injury prevention, system preparedness, and equity across diverse societal sectors. PL001 opens the dialogue on improving trauma systems and injury surveillance across Europe, emphasizing coordination between data systems, patient care, and policy. PL002 explores the evolving challenges of mobility through AI and data-driven technologies, focusing on how innovation can enhance safety and inform infrastructure changes. Shifting to the workforce, PL003 discusses workplace safety in a transforming Europe, spotlighting occupational hazards, workforce resilience, and the need for preparedness amid rapid socioeconomic change.

PL004 presents a vision of resilient health systems through the lens of One Health and intelligence integration—blending human, animal, and environmental health to strengthen safety strategies. The focus narrows in PL005 to the protection of children from injuries and violence across educational, domestic, and institutional settings. Finally, PL006 elevates women's health and safety by integrating public health action, advocacy, and gender-responsive strategies, recognizing systemic gaps and proposing cross-sectoral solutions.

Together, these sessions paint a comprehensive picture of safety preparedness in modern societies—addressing trauma, mobility, work, systemic resilience, and vulnerable populations through collaborative, interdisciplinary approaches.

Special attention was given to the development of comprehensive trauma care systems, and standardisation and harmonisation to strengthen health sector preparedness, and the critical role of rehabilitation and ongoing care—ensuring that survivors of injuries can reintegrate into their communities and enjoy an improved quality of life.

We were overwhelmed by the positive response, the high-quality submissions, and the feeling that we all share a vision for safer societies, aspiring to foster meaningful collaborations, and to translate scientific knowledge into action. We believe that this issue marks a transition in the paradigm of how we discuss related global health challenges and will ultimately be the first stepping stone to transform injury prevention and the promotion of safety in the European Region and across the world.

In closing, we extend our heartfelt thanks to the members of the International Scientific Committee, as well as to the researchers, practitioners, and policymakers whose collaboration and commitment brought EU-SAFETY 2025—and this special issue—to life!

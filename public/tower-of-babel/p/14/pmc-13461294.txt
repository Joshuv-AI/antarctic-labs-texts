Myoclonus and Gaze Palsy in Childhood-Onset <i>SQSTM1</i>-Related Neurodegeneration
Authors: Jacky Ganguly, Sayoni Roychowdhury, Rohit Keshav, Debayan Dutta, Soumava Mukherjee, Purba Basu, Hrishikesh Kumar
Published: 2026

A 15-year-old girl, born to nonconsanguineous parents, presented with 1 year of progressive behavioral decline and abnormal movements. She developed irritability, depressive episodes, and cognitive deterioration with declining academic performance, and increasing jerky movements involving the hands. Examination revealed impaired attention, episodic memory and executive dysfunction (MoCA 22/30) with vertical gaze limitation, myoclonus, dystonia and ataxia. Brain MRI showed mild cerebellar atrophy (Figure 1). Whole-exome sequencing revealed a homozygous pathogenic SQSTM1 frameshift variant (c.823_ 824del; p.Ser275PhefsTer17) (PS4, PVS1, and PM2). SQSTM1/p62 functions as a multidomain scaffold protein involved in selective autophagy, oxidative stress response and intracellular signaling pathways [1]. Recent reports have expanded the phenotypic spectrum of biallelic SQSTM1-related neurodegeneration with ataxia, dystonia and gaze palsy (NADGP), including vertical supranuclear gaze palsy, oculomotor apraxia and bilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia with intrafamilial phenotypic variability [2-4]. SQSTM1-related disease should be considered in the differential diagnosis of progressive childhood-onset movement disorders with Niemann–Pick disease type C-like phenotypes.

Oculomotor examination demonstrated slow saccades, gaze restriction (vertical>horizontal, predominant downgaze restriction) with bilateral horizontal gaze-evoked nystagmus. The patient exhibited jerky, arrhythmic, distal-predominant myoclonus involving both upper limbs, present at rest but more prominent during posture and action. The jerks were not stimulus-sensitive to tactile or auditory stimuli. Dystonic posturing of the outstretched hands was also noted. Dysmetria noted on left upper limb while doing the finger-nose test, along with some difficulty in tandem gait.

Appropriateness of the use of CTPAs (computed tomographic pulmonary angiograms) in suspected pulmonary embolisms
Authors: Thomas Dalia, Lakshmi Nijith, Alicia Enriquez, Munawer Al-Mudhaffar
Published: 2024

Introduction

Pulmonary embolism (PE) is a serious and life-threatening condition where there is blockage in the pulmonary arteries due to one or more blood clots. The incidence of PE is approximately 60–70 per 100,000 people.1 The gold standard diagnostic investigation for PE is computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA). The use of diagnostic tools such as D-dimer and Wells score can help clinicians make reasonable decisions to justify the use of CTPAs.2, 3

This audit aims to review our local practice in University Hospital of Wales on CTPA referrals based on the Royal College of Radiology (RCR) standard of practice below:

•CTPAs should be used to investigate suspected PE unless contraindicated or an alternative indicated according to local guidelines.

CTPAs should be used to investigate suspected PE unless contraindicated or an alternative indicated according to local guidelines.

•Referrals are requested in line with local protocol.

Referrals are requested in line with local protocol.

•At least 15.4%–37.4% of CTPAs should detect PE and an alternative diagnosis in at least 50% of CTPAs.4

At least 15.4%–37.4% of CTPAs should detect PE and an alternative diagnosis in at least 50% of CTPAs.4

Method

This was a retrospective study of patients who underwent CTPAs in University Hospital of Wales (UHW) during a 1-month period (June 2022). 204 CTPAs undertaken in UHW were reviewed using radiology request form and CTPA reports through the electronic platform synapse. Information including CXR findings, wells' score, and D-dimer scores were collected request forms and the scan reports.

Results

CTPA requests came from a mix of location: emergency department (43%), medical assessment unit (25%), wards (30%) and surgical assessment unit (1%). Patient age range varied from 16–94 but the average age was 70. Of the total patients, 93 (46%) were female and 111 (54%) were male.

Of the 204 CTPAs, (23/204) 11.2% detected a pulmonary embolism. 50% (102/204) had an alternative diagnosis, the most common being infective changes. 10% (21/204) of the CTPAs showed no abnormality at all.

Local hospital guidelines recommend the use of 2-point Well's score in assessment of PE. CXR is also mandatory prior to requesting CTPAs. The audit shows that D-dimers were recorded in 38% (78) of the patients and Well's score was only reported in 13% (26) of the patients. 184 (90%) out of the 204 people had a Chest X-ray (CXR) within 7 days prior to undertaking the CTPA. Of these, only 34% of the CXRs detected any abnormalities.

Conclusion

This audit showed that CTPAs were being over-requested in UHW. CTPA positive rate (11%) were lower than the recommended standard of at least (15.7%). The inadequate D-dimer testing and calculation of well's score could be main reason for over-requesting CTPA.

A limitation of this study is the small sample size. A potential solution or method would have been to retrospectively collect data over a longer period to gather a larger sample size, hence, draw more accurate results and conclusions. We recommend incorporating a section to record Well's score and D-dimer on the CTPA request form to reduce the overuse of CTPAs. Clinical practitioners should also be encouraged to use validated tools such as the PERC rule.1

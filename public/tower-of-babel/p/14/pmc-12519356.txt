Effectiveness of aromatherapy in pain relief postcardiac surgery: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Roberta Paolli de Paiva Oliveira Arruda Camara, Maria Carolina Batista Silva, Fernanda de Castro Teixeira, Vinicius dos Santos Lemos Pereira, Bruna Carmelita Rocha Pontes, Ayane Cristine Alves Sarmento, Kleyton Santos Medeiros, Rodrigo Assis Neves Dantas, Daniele Vieira Dantas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionAcute pain in the postoperative period of cardiac surgery is mostly treated with opioid analgesics. However, with the risk of adverse reactions and complications, strategies which do not involve opioid analgesics can be considered, such as aromatherapy. This systematic review aims to analyse the effectiveness of aromatherapy in relieving pain in post-cardiac surgery patients.Methods and analysisTwo researchers will independently and simultaneously conduct searches and select studies from the following databases: PubMed, Embase, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, Latin American and Caribbean Literature on Health Sciences, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane (Library) and clinical trial registries (clinicaltrials.com), with no language or publication date restrictions. Randomised and quasi-randomised clinical trials on the use of aromatherapy for pain relief in postcardiac surgery patients will be included. Then, two researchers will independently examine the studies based on inclusion criteria, extract data from the included studies and assess the risk of bias using the Risk of Bias 2 tool and the Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies of Interventions tool from Cochrane. Data will be synthesised using Review Manager software. The strength of the evidence will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendation Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach. The literature search, study selection, review and meta-analysis stages will be conducted from early October 2025 to April 2026.Ethics and disseminationThis study is based on secondary data, and therefore ethical approval from a research ethics committee was not required. The results will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed scientific journal.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024568532.Keywords: Thoracic surgery, Cardiac surgery, COMPLEMENTARY MEDICINE, PAIN MANAGEMENT, Nursing Care

Introduction

According to the Global Burden of Disease Study conducted in 2019, cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the leading causes of the increased disease burden worldwide, contributing to premature deaths and rising healthcare costs. Approximately 33% of all deaths globally, more than 18 million, are attributed to CVDs.1 A similar distribution is found in Brazil, where 30% of deaths are related to CVDs.2

A study by Oliveira et al which analysed cardiovascular statistics in Brazil revealed a 64% increase in hospitalisations for surgical procedures related to CVD treatment in the Unified Health System (Sistema Único de Saúde—SUS) between 2008 and 2019. The most common cardiac surgeries were coronary angioplasties, followed by coronary artery bypass grafting and valvular surgeries.3 Annual expenditures for cardiovascular procedures in Brazil under SUS total more than R$ 1 billion.2

Cardiac surgeries are considered complex and major procedures. Factors present in the perioperative period are crucial and can influence the final postsurgical outcomes. Postoperative pain stands out among these factors, as inadequate management can lead to harmful physiological responses and complications (cardiac and pulmonary), becoming an indicator of the quality of postoperative recovery.4,6

Acute pain occurrence in the postoperative period of cardiac surgery is related to surgical trauma and the surgical wound, and its intensity can vary.7 8 It is a subjective experience with the potential to limit and restrict the patient’s daily activities, disrupt sleep and cause anxiety.8

Therefore, efficient and timely implementation of pain management strategies in the postoperative period of cardiac surgery is essential.8 Opioid analgesics have been the main choice for managing intense and moderate pain after cardiac surgery. However, due to the risk of adverse reactions and complications, it is necessary to consider strategies that reduce patient exposure to opioids.9

In this context, non-pharmacological interventions for pain management, such as complementary therapies, stand out due to their low cost and safety.10 Aromatherapy is noted among the various types of complementary therapies as a therapeutic tool with significant potential for treating a range of health conditions, including pain, nausea, vomiting, stress and anxiety.11 12

Aromatherapy involves the use of volatile concentrates extracted from plants, known as essential oils (EOs), which are characterised by their aromas and ability to promote health and well-being.11

The use of aromatherapy has shown positive results in pain improvement, as reported in clinical trials which used Lavandula angustifolia EO in patients postcardiac surgery.13 14 However, despite the growing number of studies exploring aromatherapy and its benefits, there is still a need to reflect on its clinical efficacy through updated systematic reviews.13

Objective

The objective of this review is to analyse the effectiveness of aromatherapy in relieving pain in postcardiac surgery patients.

Methods and analysis

The systematic review and meta-analysis will be conducted according to the latest guidelines from the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.15 We will follow the recommendations of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA).16

Additionally, the systematic review protocol was developed based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) (online supplemental table 1).17 18 The study protocol was developed and registered in July 2024 in the PROSPERO registry (CRD42024568532).

Inclusion criteria

Randomised and quasi-randomised clinical trials on the use of aromatherapy for pain relief in patients postcardiac surgery (myocardial revascularisation, valvular replacement or combined surgery-myocardial revascularisation and valve replacement) in patients aged 18 years or older will be included in the sample.

Exclusion criteria

The following exclusion criteria will be applied: studies in which the population consists of patients with comorbidities that may interfere with pain processing (including neurological and psychiatric conditions), in addition to the medical condition for which they underwent surgery, studies that apply mixed aromatherapy (using two or more types of EOs), studies that use aromatherapy in combination with other complementary therapies, observational studies, review articles, case reports, letters to the editor, editorials, study protocols, theses, dissertations and abstracts published in conference proceedings.

Thus, the following research question was formulated for this review: Is aromatherapy effective in relieving pain in postcardiac surgery patients?

Primary outcome

The primary outcome to be analysed is pain relief, defined as the reduction in pain intensity and assessed using validated instruments such as the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) or the Numerical Rating Scale-11.19 20

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcome of reduced anxiety will be analysed based on the measures presented in the studies using specific instruments such as the Spielberger State-Trait Anxiety Inventory.21 The analysis for the secondary outcome of vital sign control will be descriptive.

Search strategy

An electronic search will be conducted without date or language restrictions in the following databases: National Library of Medicine and National Institutes of Health (PubMed), Embase, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, Latin American and Caribbean Literature on Health Sciences, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (Library) and clinical trial databases (clinicaltrials.com). The entire search will be conducted in April 2025. Therefore, the databases will be searched from their inception until that date. The references of the included studies will also be searched (cross-references).

The controlled descriptors indexed in Medical Subject Headings and keywords will be used for database searches as follows: (Patient OR Patients) AND (Adult OR Adults) AND (Thoracic Surgery OR Cardiac Surgery OR Heart Surgery OR Coronary Artery Bypass OR Cardiovascular Surgical Procedures) AND (Aromatherapy OR Aromatherapies OR Aroma Therapy OR Aroma Therapies OR Therapies, Aroma OR Therapy, Aroma) AND (Oils, Volatile OR Volatile Oils OR Volatile Oil OR Oil, Volatile OR Essential Oil OR Oils, Essential OR Essential Oils OR Oil, Essential) AND (Postoperative Period OR Period, Postoperative OR Periods, Postoperative OR Postoperative Periods) AND (Pain OR Pain Management OR Management, Pain OR Managements, Pain OR Pain Managements). Study selection

The search and selection of studies will be conducted by two independent researchers (RPPOAC and BCRP) who have been previously trained to minimise the risk of bias. The articles will initially be exported to the Rayyan software (Mourad Ouzzani, University of Oxford, UK) to assist in the study selection process.22 Duplicate studies will only be counted once. The next step will involve an initial screening based on the reading of all titles and abstracts from the data sources. The full text of the selected articles will subsequently be read. In case of any disagreement between the researchers during the selection process, a third researcher (DVD) will decide on the inclusion of the study. The literature search, study selection, review and meta-analysis stages will be conducted from early October 2025 to April 2026.

Missing data

In cases of missing data in the selected studies, the authors of the respective articles will be contacted by email. If no response is received, or if the missing information cannot be retrieved, the data will be excluded from our analysis and addressed in the discussion section.

Data synthesis

Data will be entered into the Review Manager software (RevMan V.5.2.3). For dichotomous outcomes, ORs with 95% CIs will be extracted or calculated for each included study.

Statistical heterogeneity among studies will be assessed using Cochran’s Q test (with a significance threshold of p<0.10) and the I² statistic. The following thresholds will be used to interpret I² values, according to the Cochrane Handbook (Section 10.10.2): 0–40%: might not be important; 30–60%: may represent moderate heterogeneity; 50–90%: may represent substantial heterogeneity; 75–100%: considerable heterogeneity.25

In cases where heterogeneity is present (I² ≥ 50%), a random-effects model will be applied to pool the results using the DerSimonian and Laird method for the estimation of ORs and 95% CIs.

When high statistical heterogeneity is detected (ie, p>0.10 on the χ² test and I² > 50%), subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore potential sources of heterogeneity, such as differences in study design, population characteristics and study settings. If a sufficient number of studies is available (typically more than 10), meta-regression will be performed to quantitatively assess the influence of potential effect modifiers. Additionally, sensitivity analyses will be conducted by repeating the meta-analysis after excluding studies at high risk of bias, outliers or those with methodological inconsistencies in order to assess the robustness of the pooled estimates.

Risk of bias assessment

Two researchers (RPPOAC and BCRP) will independently assess the risk of bias in the included studies using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool for randomised trials26 and the Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies of Interventions tool for quasi-randomised trials.27 Disagreements between the two researchers on the risk of bias in specific studies will be resolved through discussion, involving a third researcher (DVD) if necessary.

Assessment of evidence quality

We will use the Grading of Recommendation Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach to classify the strength of the evidence from the included data.28 GRADE consists of 11 assessment criteria, namely: number of studies evaluated in the meta-analysis, study design, risk of bias assessment results, inconsistency, indirect evidence, imprecision, publication bias, number of patients in the experimental group, number of patients in the control group and the relative or absolute effect, considering the 95% CI.29 All the GRADE criteria mentioned above will be individually assessed for each outcome by two independent researchers (RPPOAC and BCRP).

Ethics and dissemination

This study will only use secondary data and therefore does not require approval from a research ethics committee. The results of this systematic review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and through presentations at conferences and seminars.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Discussion

The integration of aromatherapy into conventional healthcare promotes a holistic approach to patient care.11 It is a safe therapy that can be used either alone or in combination with other complementary therapies, such as music therapy and acupuncture.30

The effectiveness of EOs in aromatherapy depends on the method of application, which can include topical use on the skin, inhalation or ingestion.12 31 Regarding their functions, EOs can have antimicrobial, analgesic, anxiolytic and anti-inflammatory activities.11

A cohort study conducted in Brazil with 30 patients who underwent cardiac surgery with median sternotomy found that 33% of the patients reported moderate pain intensity at the time of discharge from the Intensive Care Unit, highlighting the need to optimise analgesic techniques in the early days of the postoperative period.32

A randomised clinical trial conducted in Iran with 64 patients in 2020 demonstrated that inhalation of peppermint EO could reduce pain intensity in patients after open-heart surgery, which in turn led to a reduction in analgesic drug use. Improvements in sleep patterns were additionally observed in the first few nights after surgery, contributing to comfort and well-being.33

The use of lavender EO (L. angustifolia) via inhalation aromatherapy has also been shown to be effective in reducing pain, anxiety and heart rate in the postoperative period of cardiac surgery.11 34 A similar result was found in a study that used rose and lavender EOs, where pain severity was lower in patients who used the EOs compared with the placebo and control groups.35 However, when analysing the reduction of anxiety, the same study found that rose and lavender EOs did not have a significant effect on anxiety levels in the evaluated patients.35

Nevertheless, it is essential to have strong and current scientific evidence that provides a systematic analysis of the efficacy of aromatherapy to ensure the responsible use of EOs in clinical practice. Important aspects such as the type of EO used, administration route, dosage and the presence of adverse effects are crucial for assessing the effectiveness of aromatherapy.12 14 36

A systematic review conducted by Lakhan et al showed that aromatherapy demonstrated a positive effect in pain reduction and can be considered a safe complementary intervention, as no adverse events related to its administration were reported in the studies included in the review. However, due to the small sample size, the study reflects the need for additional research to better understand aromatherapy and its applications in clinical practice.37

A systematic review study conducted by Zimpel et al indicated that some alternative and complementary medicine interventions can contribute to pain reduction in the first 24 hours after a caesarean section. Among the interventions, it was highlighted that aromatherapy combined with analgesia can promote pain reduction within 12 hours when compared with a combination of placebo and analgesia. However, the study highlights that the available evidence on adverse events related to alternative and complementary therapies is limited, preventing conclusions regarding their safety.38

Finally, this systematic review can significantly contribute to the existing literature by gathering updated data from clinical trials, enabling a more consistent assessment of the efficacy of aromatherapy in the specific clinical context of postoperative pain management after cardiac surgery. The results of this review can inform clinical practice and favour incorporating aromatherapy as a complementary, safe and non-invasive strategy within multimodal approaches to pain management in patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

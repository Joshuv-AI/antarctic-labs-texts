Parameter estimation-based discrimination method for osteoporosis stage with ultrasound bone densitometer
Authors: Yuna Choi, Jaehyun Kim, Gwangyeol Cha, Youngjin Choi, Il Youn Jung, Bong Gun Lee, Chang-Hun Lee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a new method for predicting the stage of osteoporosis by estimating bone-density parameters using an ultrasonic-bone densitometer. Using the developed ultrasonic bone densitometer, the ultrasonic signal passing through the phalanx of the finger was measured, and the measured signal was preprocessed to generate input data for parameter estimation. The Nakagami probability-density function was used for modeling this data, and optimal bone-density parameters were estimated using an optimization problem – to predict the stage of osteoporosis. The proposed method was verified through in vitro and in vivo experiments. In phantom experiments, cubes with different materials (ABS plastic, PLA plastic, Nylon) were used, and each cube had a different density through internal density so that it could mimic bones. The experiments were conducted using them and the results were compared. The experimental results confirmed that there was a correlation between the density and estimated parameters for all three materials. In the in vivo experiment, the osteoporosis stages were classified as normal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis, and the bone-density parameters were estimated for the participants of each group. Thus, a basis for predicting the stage of osteoporosis was created, and data from new participants were collected and verified as test data. As a result, normal and abnormal groups (osteopenia and osteoporosis) were predicted.Subject terms: Endocrine system and metabolic diseases, Metabolic disorders

Introduction

Recently, as the elderly population has increased, osteoporosis, along with diabetes and cardiovascular diseases, has been recognized as a serious senile disease. In particular, due to hormonal changes that occur during menopause, women have an increased risk of osteoporosis1–3. Osteoporosis is irreversible and results in fractures (due to a decrease in bone mass), and degradation of bone structure. Additionally, osteoporotic fractures limit mobility and independence and necessitate protection4. Therefore, the early diagnosis and prevention of osteoporosis are important5,6.

The World Health Organization (WHO) uses bone density as the basis for determining osteoporosis. Bone density is useful in evaluating the rate of bone loss. According to the criteria presented by WHO, osteoporosis is classified using T-score7. This value is expressed as a standard deviation compared to the average bone density of a group of young adults of the same gender and represents the difference from healthy young adults. In other words, the stages of osteoporosis are classified as follows: T-score is normal, T-score is osteopenia, and T-score is osteoporosis8,9.

The most widely used method for measuring bone density is dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA), which is used in measuring areas such as the proximal femur and lumbar spine. The principle is to measure the attenuation of two X-ray photons of different energies as they pass through the body. This method has the advantage of good resolution and allows for the diagnosis of osteoporosis in a short time10. However, the T-score by DEXA may vary depending on the characteristics of the subject or the manufacturer of the examiner and measuring equipment, and errors in the measuring equipment itself also occur, limiting accuracy. Also, because bone density has to be repeatedly measured, not only for the diagnosis of osteoporosis but also for the evaluation of response to the treatment, DEXA poses a risk of repeated radiation exposure11.

To solve this problem, methods for bone-density measurements using ultrasound – which has no known risks to the human body – have been studied. Though ultrasound-based measurement methods have low precision due to their lower resolution compared to DEXA, but they may be used repeatedly, easily, and widely, owing to their low production cost12. Quantitative Ultrasound (QUS) was introduced to extract parameters as basic ultrasound characteristics, and studies have been conducted on how to determine osteoporosis through QUS. This used an ultrasonic sensor to extract parameters from various measurement areas, such as phalanx, radius, and heel bone, and the representative methods diagnose osteoporosis using parameters such as speed of sound (SOS) and broadband ultrasound attenuation (BUA). The SOS is obtained by dividing the bone thickness by the time that the signal takes to pass through the bone from the transmitter to the receiver of the ultrasonic sensor13. And BUA is determined from the slope of the measured ultrasonic-attenuation coefficient for a specific frequency range; attenuation occurs owing to absorption and scattering when ultrasonic waves pass through the bone and is calculated using the power-spectrum levels of the ultrasonic signal (with and without the bone) and the thickness of the measurement area13. The method of using them has already been discussed in several papers. However, it has the disadvantage that it is affected by the thickness of cortical bone or soft tissue and that it is difficult to accurately measure the thickness of the measurement area, so the results may be inaccurate14,15.

Therefore, in this study, we proposed a method for discriminating the stages of osteoporosis by estimating new bone-density parameters using an ultrasonic bone densitometer. The transmitter and receiver of the ultrasonic sensor were arranged vertically to measure the ultrasonic signals passing through the phalanx of the finger, and the measured signals were modeled to estimate the optimal parameters through an optimization problem. The estimated bone-density parameters served as the basis for discriminating the stages of osteoporosis, and their performance was validated using both in vitro and in vivo experiments. The correlation between density and the extracted parameters was confirmed using in vitro experiments, and the stages of osteoporosis were determined using in vivo experiments, followed by a comparative analysis of the results.

Description of ultrasound bone densitometer

The ultrasound bone densitometer developed was manufactured to measure the ultrasonic signal reduction through the finger phalanx and discriminated the stage of osteoporosis based on the results; it is shown in Fig. 1a and includes driving, control, and measuring parts.

Ultrasonic bone densitometer (a) equipment (b) configuration diagram.

The driving part served to automatically move the ultrasonic sensor to be located at the measurement area. For this purpose, a robotic gripper (OpenMANIPULATOR-X of ROBOTIS) was used, and the transmitter/receiver of the ultrasonic sensor attached to the end of the gripper was placed perpendicular to the phalanx. An XH430-W350, actuator was used, and its torque control drove the gripper with a constant force to press the ultrasonic sensor against the phalanx with a force of 12.74. The control part was responsible for moving the robotic gripper and indicating the state of the device. It was used as the controller for the openRB-150 of ROBOTIS to control the gripper and included a button sensor to change the drive status. The actuator (i.e., the robotic gripper) is then controlled through the button sensor. For example, the top-button sensor drives the gripper outward (away from the phalanx), the middle-button sensor drives it inward (toward the phalanx), and finally, the bottom-button sensor moves the gripper to its initial position. The measurement part measured and stored the signal of the ultrasonic sensor passing through the measurement area. To do this, the live measurement program provided by ELVIS III of National Instruments was used. The transmitter of the ultrasonic sensor attached to the end of the gripper generated a trigger signal through a function generator, and an oscilloscope was connected to the receiver to measure and store the data of the ultrasonic signal passing through the phalanx.

The envelope signal was modeled using the Nakagami probability density function (PDF) to estimate the parameters for discriminating the stage of osteoporosis. The Nakagami PDF is used in communication theory to model signals reaching a receiver21,22. The representative characteristics of the signal obtained from the receiver of the ultrasonic sensor are attenuation and scattering. Attenuation is the phenomenon in which the energy of the ultrasound decreases as it passes through the transmission medium, and scattering is a phenomenon in which ultrasonic waves are scattered in many directions by non-uniformity within the transmission medium. Ultrasonic signals have attenuation and complex scattering as they pass through the skin and bones of the human body. In summary, previous studies show that when modeling the ultrasonic signals using the Nakagami PDF, the characteristics of the signal at the receiver and the correlation with the density can be well indicated. Phantom experiments

Before conducting the in vivo experiment, a phantom experiment was performed as shown in Fig. 5a. For this purpose, a phantom model was manufactured to have a hexagon shape inside to mimic the density characteristics of spongy bone. In other words, the expansion of the structure of spongy bone in osteoporosis was expressed by changing the fill-density of the phantom model. This is 3D-printed cubes made of acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) plastic that have the same volume (3 × 3 × 3 []), but the fill-density of the inside was set differently to express the density of bones as shown in Fig. 5b. In vivo experiments

To discriminate the stage of osteoporosis by measuring the finger-phalanx ultrasonic signals, in vivo experiments were performed based on the results of the phantom experiment. In the in vivo experiments, data were collected by placing the phalanx of the finger between the ultrasonic sensors and placing them vertically on the ultrasound bone densitometer. Experiments were conducted on participants in the normal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis groups, and each group was classified based on the lowest value of the T-score measured from the left hip, right hip, and spine using DEXA. Data from 10 participants were randomly measured for each group, regardless of gender or age. After estimating the bone-density parameters using the data, a basis for discriminating the stage of osteoporosis was created. Based on this, data from new participants were collected, and the stage of osteoporosis was predicted. In vivo experiments were carried out according to relevant guidelines and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Hanyang University Hospital (2023-02-003).

Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we proposed a method for predicting the stage of osteoporosis by estimating bone-density parameters using an ultrasound bone densitometer. The ultrasonic bone densitometer measured the ultrasonic signal passing through the phalanx of the finger by placing the transmitter and receiver of the ultrasonic sensor vertically and generated input data for estimating the parameters by pre-processing the measured signal. This was modeled using the Nakagami PDF, and the optimal bone-density parameter was estimated using the optimization method. Finally, the estimated parameters were used to predict the stage of osteoporosis, and the proposed method was verified through in vitro and in vivo experiments.

The phantom experiments were performed using five ABS plastic cubes of the same volume but different masses. The cubes were set to different fill-density to mimic the bone density of humans, and the densities were calculated using different masses. For the input data of ABS plastic cubes with different fill-density, the parameters and were estimated by minimizing the sum of squares of the residuals between the input data and fitted models using Nakagami PDF. Through the estimated parameters, it was confirmed that the larger the density of the phantom model, the larger the estimated parameters. In addition, the experiments were conducted by extending the phantom model with different materials: ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) plastic, PLA (polylactic acid) plastic, and Nylon. As a result, all estimated parameters and densities could be approximated by a linear equation, and the positive correlation between the two could be confirmed through the phantom experimental results. In other words, it meant that the density could be determined through the estimated bone-density parameter.

In vivo experiments were conducted to predict the stage of osteoporosis by measuring the ultrasound signals through the finger phalanxes. The osteoporosis stages were divided into normal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis groups, and 10 participants in each group were evaluated, regardless of gender and age. Using these data, bone-density parameters were estimated and the correlation between bone density and bone-density parameters was confirmed. Based on this, a basis for predicting the stage of osteoporosis was created, and data from new participants were collected and verified as the test data. The normal group was clearly predicted; however, the abnormal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis groups did not have high prediction results, relatively. In other words, it was possible to distinguish between normal and abnormal groups clearly; however, the accuracy among the abnormal groups was not high because participants belonged to overlapping sections of basis between them.

This study proposes a new method based on parameter estimation to predict the stage of osteoporosis. However, it has the following limitations. Although there were clear prediction results between the normal and abnormal groups, there were no high prediction results among the abnormal, osteopenia, and osteoporosis groups. The main reason is that there was an overlapping section between the data of the two groups. This could be because the proposed method is threshold-based, indicating that additional studies is needed to distinguish osteopenia from osteoporosis. Future research should be conducted to address these limitations. In particular, in order to clearly distinguish osteoporosis and osteopenia groups, additional experiments will be conducted by recruiting a large number of participants in in vivo experiments. In addition, mathematical modeling methods will be considered to improve the estimation accuracy of bone-density parameters.

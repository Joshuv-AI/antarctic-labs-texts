Comparative Efficacy of Double-Filtration Plasmapheresis Versus Intravenous Methylprednisolone in Acute Attacks of Neuromyelitis Optica Spectrum Disorder: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Xiwen Ai, Qiuju Li, Kan Wang, Jing Peng, Desheng Zhu, Yangtai Guan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionWhile double-filtration plasmapheresis (DFPP) and intravenously administered methylprednisolone (IVMP) are both established treatments for acute attacks of neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD), their comparative efficacy and safety profiles remain a critical area of investigation. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical outcomes and adverse events of DFPP versus IVMP in patients with NMOSD, with a focus on disability improvement and treatment tolerability.MethodsA prospective single-center cohort study was performed with 146 patients with NMOSD, who were treated with DFPP, IVMP, and combination therapy (DFPP + IVMP). Primary efficacy was measured by changes in Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) scores (ΔEDSS). Secondary outcomes included Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores. Safety profiles, including liver enzyme elevation and infection rates, were monitored.ResultsThe DFPP group (n = 81) demonstrated a significant clinical response, with a median EDSS improvement of 0.5 (IQR 0.0–1.0) points. The response rate (defined as ΔEDSS > 0) was 66.7%, with 33.3% (27/81), 18.5% (15/81), and 14.8% (12/81) of patients achieving improvements of 0.5, 1.0, and ≥ 1.5 points, respectively. This was accompanied by a marked reduction in serum immunoglobulins (IgG: 11.87 ± 4.39 to 3.13 ± 1.76 g/L, p < 0.001). The DFPP group and IVMP monotherapy group showed comparable efficacy, with 66.7% (54/81) and 69.2% (45/65) of patients achieving EDSS improvement, respectively (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.45–1.77, p = 0.742). The magnitude of EDSS improvement was identical between groups (median ΔEDSS 0.5 points). Combination therapy demonstrated particular utility in severe cases (median baseline EDSS 5.0 [IQR 3.5–6.5]). Adverse events were fewer with DFPP than with IVMP (19.8% vs. 33.8%, p = 0.059).ConclusionDFPP exhibited comparable effectiveness to IVMP in improving disability during NMOSD acute attacks, with a trend towards a more favorable safety profile. The combination of DFPP and IVMP may benefit severe cases. These findings support DFPP as a viable therapeutic option, particularly for patients with high baseline disability or steroid-refractory disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40120-025-00835-6.

Introduction

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is a severe autoimmune-mediated inflammatory condition of the central nervous system (CNS), characterized by recurrent attacks of optic neuritis, myelitis, and area postrema syndrome, often leading to cumulative disability [1]. The pathogenesis of NMOSD is strongly linked to aquaporin-4 immunoglobulin G (AQP4-IgG), an autoantibody targeting astrocytes and disrupting water homeostasis [2, 3]. Despite advances in immunotherapy, acute attacks remain a significant challenge, necessitating rapid and effective interventions to mitigate long-term disability [4, 5].

Current first-line therapies for acute NMOSD attacks include high-dose intravenously administered methylprednisolone (IVMP) and plasma exchange (PE), with IVMP being the most widely used owing to its potent anti-inflammatory effects [6]. However, IVMP is associated with substantial adverse effects, including metabolic disturbances, osteoporosis, and increased infection risk, particularly in middle-aged women who constitute the majority of patients with NMOSD [7]. PE, including double-filtration plasmapheresis (DFPP), offers an alternative by selectively removing pathogenic antibodies while preserving beneficial plasma components, thus reducing the need for exogenous plasma replacement and associated complications [8].

While conventional PE has demonstrated efficacy in steroid-refractory cases, DFPP’s selective filtration mechanism may provide additional advantages, such as lower infection rates and improved tolerability [9]. Despite these potential benefits, comparative studies evaluating DFPP against IVMP are scarce, and the optimal therapeutic strategy for acute NMOSD attacks remains debated. Previous research has highlighted the effectiveness of PE and IVMP in certain populations, but data specific to DFPP are limited, particularly in Asian cohorts (e.g., China, Japan, and Korea) where NMOSD prevalence is higher.

This prospective cohort study aims to directly compare the effectiveness and safety of DFPP versus IVMP in the treatment of acute NMOSD attacks, with a focus on disability outcomes and adverse event profiles. By addressing the gap in comparative evidence, our findings may inform clinical decision-making, particularly for patients with high baseline disability or steroid-refractory disease. We hypothesize that DFPP will demonstrate comparable effectiveness to IVMP in improving disability scores while offering a more favorable safety profile. Additionally, we explore the potential utility of combination therapy (DFPP + IVMP) in severe cases, providing insights into personalized treatment strategies for NMOSD.

Materials and Methods

Ethics

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Renji Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. This prospective observational cohort study was not registered as a clinical trial as it did not involve random assignment to interventions. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study. The research adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, ensuring the confidentiality and ethical treatment of all patient data.

Design

This prospective, single-center cohort study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of DFPP in patients with NMOSD. The study design included a comparative analysis of patients treated with DFPP versus those receiving IVMP alone. Data were collected from November 2017 to May 2024, with follow-up assessments conducted to monitor treatment outcomes and adverse events.

Study Subjects

Inclusion criteria: (1) Patients diagnosed with NMOSD according to the 2015 International Consensus diagnostic criteria, including (a) positive serum AQP4-IgG antibody confirmed by cell-based assay (CBA) with at least one core clinical feature (e.g., acute optic neuritis, myelitis, or area postrema syndrome), or (b) AQP4-IgG-negative patients meeting at least two core clinical features with supporting MRI findings; (2) patients receiving either DFPP or IVMP as acute-phase treatment during hospitalization; and (3) willingness to provide written informed consent for participation.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with other CNS disorders or alternative diagnoses mimicking NMOSD, (2) those receiving treatments other than DFPP or IVMP during the study period, (3) patients with positive serum myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibody (MOG-IgG), and (4) incomplete clinical or laboratory data.

Definition of Acute Attack and Treatment Window

An acute attack was defined as a new onset or worsening of neurologic symptoms attributable to NMOSD, lasting for more than 24 h, in the absence of fever or infection, and accompanied by corresponding objective evidence on neurological examination [4]. To be included in the study, the initiation of protocol-specified treatment (either DFPP or IVMP) must have occurred within 14 days of symptom onset. This window was selected on the basis of existing literature suggesting that earlier intervention leads to better recovery outcomes in NMOSD attacks [10].

Treatment Allocation

Patients were allocated to DFPP, IVMP, or combination therapy on the basis of a combination of clinical factors including attack severity (as judged by the treating neurologist), baseline disability, prior treatment response, patient comorbidities (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes or hypertension contraindicating high-dose steroids), and, in some cases, patient preference after detailed discussion of the risks and benefits of each option. This reflects real-world clinical practice where treatment is personalized.

Treatment Protocols

DFPP Therapy

Patients underwent double-lumen catheterization of the femoral or internal jugular vein. The DFPP procedures were conducted using a membrane-based plasmapheresis system (OP-08W, Kawasumi Laboratories, Japan) with a plasma separator (Plasmacure®) and a fractionator (Cascadeflo®). Anticoagulation was achieved with a continuous infusion of citrate dextrose solution (ACD-A). No replacement solutions (e.g., albumin, fresh frozen plasma) were used unless clinically indicated for hypovolemia or hypocalcemia, as DFPP selectively removes pathogenic antibodies while retaining beneficial plasma components. Treatment parameters were individualized on the basis of patient body weight (typically 0.030–0.035 L/kg per session), with a target plasma filtration volume of 1.5–4.0 L per session (mean 3.04 ± 0.54 L). Sessions lasted approximately 2–3 h and were repeated every other day, with the total number of sessions (1–5) determined by clinical response, tolerability, and predefined stopping criteria (e.g., symptom plateau or adverse events). Vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation) and biochemical markers (electrolytes, coagulation profiles) were monitored in real-time during each session.

IVMP Therapy

Patients receiving IVMP followed a standardized high-dose glucocorticoid regimen: intravenously administered methylprednisolone 1 g/day for 3–5 days, followed by a tapering course of oral prednisolone (starting at 60 mg/day, reduced by 10 mg weekly to 20 mg/day, then tapered more gradually). Prophylactic measures for steroid-related adverse effects (e.g., gastric protection with proton pump inhibitors, calcium/vitamin D supplementation) were uniformly applied.

Combination Therapy

Patients in the DFPP + IVMP group received concurrent treatments, with DFPP initiated within 24–48 h of the first IVMP dose. The IVMP regimen mirrored the monotherapy protocol, while DFPP sessions were scheduled to avoid interference with glucocorticoid pharmacokinetics, which were scheduled at least 6 h after IVMP infusion to minimize the potential removal of methylprednisolone by the apheresis procedure. This synergistic approach aimed to maximize rapid antibody clearance (DFPP) while suppressing ongoing inflammation (IVMP).

Observational and Outcome Measures

Primary efficacy endpoint: Change in Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) scores (ΔEDSS = admission score − post-treatment score). Improvement was defined as ΔEDSS > 0.

Secondary endpoints: (1) Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores, a global measure of disability that has been used as an endpoint in NMOSD clinical trials to assess broad functional impact [11, 12]; (2) biochemical markers: serum immunoglobulin (IgG, IgA, IgM), liver enzymes (alanine transaminase, ALT; aspartate transaminase, AST), albumin, and thrombocyte count; and (3) adverse events (e.g., infections, liver enzyme elevations).

All outcome assessments (EDSS, mRS, laboratory parameters) were performed at baseline (admission) and at the end of the acute treatment phase (at hospital discharge, typically 7–10 days after treatment initiation). Assessors were not blinded to the treatment group. In addition, all unexpected medical events occurring during the treatment period and within 7 days after treatment were recorded as adverse events, which were monitored throughout hospitalization and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Infections were defined as clinical events requiring antimicrobial therapy, supported by laboratory or imaging findings. A serious adverse event was defined as any event that was fatal, life-threatening, required prolonged hospitalization, or resulted in significant disability.

Statistical Analysis

Given NMOSD’s rarity, the sample size (N = 146) was determined by available cases during the study period, with post hoc power analysis confirming adequacy for primary comparisons. Data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0 and GraphPad Prism 10. Normality was assessed via Shapiro–Wilk tests. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD (normal distribution) or median (quartiles) (non-normal), compared using t tests or Mann–Whitney U tests, respectively. Categorical variables were analyzed with chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests. Paired data (e.g., pre-/post-treatment) were evaluated using paired t tests or Wilcoxon tests. A complete-case analysis approach was used, and patients with missing primary outcome data (EDSS at discharge) were excluded from the analysis. Multivariable logistic regression was used to adjust for potential confounders, including age, sex, EDSS at admission, relapse history, AQP4-IgG status, and pretreatment immunosuppressant use. Variables were selected for the model on the basis of clinical relevance and univariate associations (p < 0.1). To assess whether baseline disease severity modified the treatment effect, we introduced an interaction term between the treatment group and EDSS at admission into the linear regression model. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Specific tests used for each endpoint are detailed in the results. Given the exploratory nature of the subgroup analyses, no adjustments for multiple comparisons were made, and these results should be interpreted with caution.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

Initially, 149 patients with NMOSD were enrolled and allocated into two treatment groups: DFPP (n = 83) and IVMP monotherapy (n = 66). After exclusion of 3 patients who discontinued treatment because of intolerance (2 from DFPP group and 1 from IVMP group), the final analysis comprised 146 participants: 81 in the DFPP group (including 60 who received combined DFPP + IVMP therapy) and 65 in the IVMP monotherapy group. Subgroup analyses were conducted for three treatment modalities: DFPP monotherapy (n = 21), IVMP monotherapy (n = 65), and combined DFPP + IVMP therapy (n = 60). Comparative Effectiveness Between DFPP and IVMP Therapy

Primary outcomes: In the unadjusted analysis, comparable efficacy was observed between treatment groups. The proportion of patients achieving EDSS improvement (ΔEDSS > 0) was 66.7% (54/81) in the DFPP group versus 69.2% (45/65) in the IVMP group (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.45–1.77, p = 0.742). The magnitude of improvement, as measured by the median change in EDSS, was identical between groups (0.5 points) (DFPP: 0.5 [IQR 0.0–1.0] vs. IVMP: 0.5 [IQR 0.0–1.5], p = 0.771). Multivariable logistic regression controlling for age, sex, EDSS at admission, relapse history, and immunosuppressant use demonstrated that the proportion of patients achieving EDSS improvement (ΔEDSS > 0): DFPP versus IVMP (aOR 0.92, 95% CI 0.44–1.93, p = 0.831). These results confirm the primary unadjusted findings after accounting for potential confounders.

A formal test for interaction between treatment type (monotherapy vs. combination) and EDSS at admission on the magnitude of ΔEDSS was not significant (p for interaction = 0.214), suggesting that the effectiveness of combination therapy relative to monotherapy was not statistically different across levels of baseline disability, although the combination group had significantly higher baseline scores. In addition, a subgroup analysis of the 121 AQP4-IgG-positive patients showed consistent results with the full cohort, and the proportion of patients achieving EDSS improvement (ΔEDSS > 0) was 67.6% (46/68) in the DFPP group versus 67.9% (36/53) in the IVMP group (OR 0.99, 95% CI 0.46–2.11, p = 0.971). Sensitivity analyses restricted to patients presenting with a first attack (n = 87) yielded results consistent with the primary analysis, showing no significant difference in response rates between the DFPP and IVMP groups (p > 0.05).

Efficacy Outcomes of DFPP Cohort

There were 81 patients with NMOSD received DFPP therapy, and it demonstrated significant therapeutic effects across multiple outcome measures. The primary effectiveness endpoint (ΔEDSS > 0) was achieved in 66.7% (54/81) of patients, with magnitude of improvement distributed as follows: 0.5-point reduction in 33.3% (27/81), 1.0-point in 18.5% (15/81), and ≥ 1.5-point in 14.8% (12/81). As detailed in Table 3, median EDSS scores decreased significantly from 4.5 (3.0–6.0) to 4.0 (2.0–5.0) (p < 0.001). Secondary outcomes showed comparable improvement in functional status (mRS 3.0 [2.0–4.0] to 2.0 [2.0–3.0], p < 0.001) and marked reductions in pathogenic antibodies (IgG 11.87 ± 4.39 to 3.13 ± 1.76 g/L, p < 0.001; IgA 2.04 ± 0.94 to 0.54 ± 0.27 g/L, p < 0.001; IgM 0.93 ± 0.50 to 0.21 ± 0.06 g/L, p < 0.001). Critically, DFPP exhibited its selective removal capability: while fibrinogen levels were substantially reduced (452 ± 68 to 288 ± 58 mg/dL, p < 0.001), albumin levels remained stable throughout the treatment course (39.8 ± 3.5 to 40.2 ± 3.2 g/L, p = 0.134). Comparative immunoglobulin data for the IVMP monotherapy group were not routinely collected and are therefore not available for analysis. Treatment Safety Profile

The safety analysis revealed that the rate of adverse events in the entire DFPP cohort (including both monotherapy and combination therapy, n = 81) was numerically lower than that in the IVMP monotherapy group (n = 65) (19.8% [16/81] vs. 33.8% [22/65]), with relative risk 0.48 (95% CI 0.22–1.02, p = 0.059). After DFPP treatment, the most frequent were transient liver enzyme elevations (9.9% [8/81]) and minor infections (6.2% [5/81]). Hematologic changes included asymptomatic thrombocytopenia (defined as a platelet count < 100 × 109/L; 3.7% [3/81]; mean decrease 49.84 ± 16.24 × 109/L). No cases required intervention or resulted in bleeding. No treatment discontinuations or serious adverse events occurred. Discussion

This prospective cohort study provides evidence that DFPP exhibits comparable efficacy to IVMP in managing acute attacks of NMOSD, with a favorable safety profile. The findings highlight DFPP as a viable therapeutic option, particularly for patients with high baseline disability or steroid-refractory disease, while combination therapy may offer additional benefits in severe cases.

The therapeutic efficacy of DFPP observed in this study is consistent with a growing body of evidence supporting the role of apheresis therapies in NMOSD. Prior studies have demonstrated that PE, including DFPP, facilitates rapid clinical improvement by effectively removing pathogenic autoantibodies, particularly AQP4-IgG, which is central to NMOSD pathogenesis [9]. For instance, a meta-analysis reported that early PE administration in NMOSD acute attacks led to significant disability reduction, with response rates comparable to high-dose corticosteroids [13]. Similarly, retrospective studies highlighted the superiority of PE over IVMP in steroid-refractory cases, reinforcing its utility as a rescue therapy [14]. However, comparative studies specifically evaluating DFPP—a selective plasmapheresis modality—against IVMP remain scarce. Our findings bridge this gap by demonstrating comparable disability improvement and similar response rates between DFPP and IVMP, while also suggesting a potential safety advantage. This aligns with emerging real-world data from Asian cohorts, where DFPP has gained traction due to its reduced need for plasma substitution and lower infection risk compared to conventional PE [15]. Nevertheless, discrepancies exist in the literature regarding optimal treatment timing and patient selection. Some studies advocate for early DFPP initiation (within 5–7 days of symptom onset) to maximize efficacy, whereas others emphasize its role primarily in steroid non-responders [10, 16, 17]. Our cohort, which included both monotherapy and combination approaches, contributes to this discourse by illustrating DFPP’s versatility across disease severities. A systematic review and meta-analysis have demonstrated a positive correlation between serum AQP4-IgG titer and both acute EDSS severity and the extent of spinal cord involvement [18]. Importantly, a subsequent meta-analysis showed that AQP4-IgG-positive patients were 3.85 times more likely to achieve at least a one-point EDSS improvement after PE than seronegative controls [19]. Although our study did not stratify outcomes by antibody titer, incorporating quantitative AQP4-IgG assays into future protocols could help individualize the number and timing of DFPP cycles.

The mechanistic superiority of DFPP lies in its dual capacity for selective macromolecule removal and immunomodulation. Unlike conventional PE, which depletes all plasma components indiscriminately, DFPP employs a secondary filter to selectively retain albumin and other beneficial proteins while removing high-molecular-weight pathogens, such as IgG and immune complexes [20]. Our data provide clear in vivo evidence for this selectivity: we observed a profound reduction in fibrinogen levels, a high-molecular-weight acute-phase reactant implicated in neuroinflammation, while albumin levels remained unchanged. This specificity not only reduces the risk of hypoalbuminemia and coagulopathies but also minimizes the need for exogenous plasma replacement, thereby mitigating transfusion-related complications. Furthermore, DFPP’s efficacy in NMOSD may extend beyond mere antibody depletion. Experimental models suggest that plasmapheresis modulates B cell homeostasis by altering Fc receptor signaling and cytokine networks, potentially disrupting the autoimmune cascade [21]. The marked reductions in IgG, IgA, and IgM observed in our cohort support this hypothesis, as these changes correlate with clinical improvement. Additionally, DFPP may indirectly attenuate complement-mediated astrocytic injury—a hallmark of NMOSD—by lowering circulating complement-fixing antibodies [22]. This multifaceted mechanism could explain why DFPP exhibits comparable efficacy to IVMP despite their distinct pharmacological actions. While corticosteroids primarily suppress broad-spectrum inflammation, DFPP targets the autoimmune driver more precisely, offering a tailored therapeutic approach. Further research into the longitudinal effects of DFPP on B cell subsets and cytokine profiles could refine its application in NMOSD and other antibody-mediated disorders.

The observed trend towards a more favorable safety profile for DFPP is a clinically relevant finding. This advantage may be partially attributable to the lower daily steroid exposure in the DFPP group, as high-dose steroids are a well-established risk factor for infections and metabolic complications. The ability of DFPP to achieve comparable efficacy with potentially less steroid burden underscores its value, especially in patients with comorbidities that predispose them to adverse effects from corticosteroids, such as diabetes or osteoporosis.

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, the single-center design may limit the generalizability of the findings; multicenter studies with standardized protocols are needed to validate these results. Second, the non-randomized allocation of treatments introduces potential selection bias, as evidenced by the higher baseline disability and relapse rate in the DFPP group. Future randomized controlled trials could address this limitation. Third, the relatively short follow-up period precludes assessment of long-term outcomes, such as relapse rates or cumulative disability. Extended follow-up studies would help elucidate the durability of DFPP’s effects. Fourth, we did not systematically collect pre-attack EDSS scores. Therefore, our analysis characterizes the treatment effect on the acute attack but does not quantify the specific decline in function from the pre-attack baseline to admission. To mitigate these limitations, we employed rigorous statistical adjustments for baseline covariates and adhered to standardized treatment and assessment protocols.

Conclusion

DFPP demonstrates comparable efficacy to IVMP in the acute management of NMOSD, with a favorable safety profile. Its dual mechanism of action—effectively removing pathogenic factors like immunoglobulins and fibrinogen while preserving albumin—provides a strong rationale for its use. The combination of DFPP and IVMP may be particularly beneficial for severe cases, underscoring the importance of personalized treatment strategies. Further research is needed to optimize treatment protocols and identify predictors of response.

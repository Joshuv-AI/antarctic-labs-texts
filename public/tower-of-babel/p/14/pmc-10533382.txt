Unexpected discovery in the thyroid cartilage during voice deepening surgery
Authors: Dalia Gustaityte Larsen, Kursat Yelken
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Key clinical messageVoice deepening surgery has en increasing interest among laryngologist worldwide and a case of surprising foreign body in the thyroid cartilage awakes alert thoughts of awareness to certain patient group.Keywords: masculinization laryngoplasty, thyroplasty type III, vocal folds, voice deepening surgery

Voice is one of the most important gender identity and perception features in human individuals. The fundamental frequency in males range from 80 to 165 Hz, while in females is frequency about 145–275 Hz. Culture, language, and size of individual clarify the variation in fundamental frequency. There is also a sharing fundamental frequency between males and females, which is described to be 145–165 Hz. 1 Over the last couple decades, there has been a shift in the percentage of the patients who present with a voice which is too high to align with their perceived and desired voice, although speaking frequency would be considered to be appropriate to their birth gender. 2 Isshiki's thyroplasty type III and modified Thucker's thyroplasty type III are the mainstream procedures for pitch lowering and are based on principle of pushing back the anterior commissure, shortening, and relaxing the vocal folds. 1 , 2 , 3

A 77‐year‐old male has always been bothered by his high pitch voice and especially on telephone conversations was considered as a male adolescent. Patient had no previous history of neck trauma, neck surgery, or radiotherapy in the neck area. Objective findings had shown normal vocal folds movement with beginning of presbyphonia. Voice frequency was measured to 130 Hz. No surgical scars were seen on the neck. Surgery was performed under local anesthesia. There was noticed unusual adhesion of subcutaneous and muscle tissues, thyroid cartilage appeared to be ossified as expected for the patient's age. There were used two sets of piezoelectric knives and diamond drill, which succeeded to cut the ossified cartilage. A wide titanium strip covering the whole anterior part of thyroid cartilage suddenly appeared, this is shown in Figure 1. Foreign body in the thyroid cartilage. Procedure was disrupted for further surgical actions and completed by closing soft tissues on the neck.

Foreign body in the thyroid cartilage.

Even comprehensive patient history and examination has not revealed suspicion on surprising foreign body discovery prior the surgery. A proposal of additional imaging examination, for example, ultrasound or CT scan of the neck can be considered or evaluation of patient's mental status in elderly patients could be suggested.

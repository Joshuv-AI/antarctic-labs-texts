Study of exposure to benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes (BTEX) and protective effects of Gallic acid on oxidative stress on peripheral lymphocytes of workers in the paint unit of an automobile industry
Authors: Enayatollah Seydi, Asghar Ghahri, Fatemeh Pasbin, Jalal Pourahmad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractExposure to workplace chemicals is a major concern across various industries, with serious consequences for workers' health and safety. The paint unit of the automotive industry exemplifies a workplace where hazardous chemicals are common. This unit is a significant source of volatile organic compounds (VOCs), which are highly harmful. Monitoring workers is a common method for evaluating chemical exposure levels in occupational settings. Gallic acid (GA) acts as an antioxidant at low doses and has beneficial effects on human health. In this study, we examined oxidative stress in peripheral lymphocytes of workers exposed to benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes (BTEX) in a paint unit of an automotive industry, and evaluated the protective role of GA in this context. Exposure to BTEX was assessed using the NIOSH 1501 method. Additionally, oxidative and antioxidant stress parameters in peripheral lymphocytes were measured. The study results showed that workers' exposure to benzene exceeded the national occupational exposure limits (OEL), while exposure to toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylene was below these limits. Furthermore, GA at concentrations of 12.5, 25, and 50 µg/ml reduced oxidative stress levels in peripheral blood lymphocytes of paint unit workers and increased antioxidant enzyme levels and reduced glutathione (GSH). It is recommended that workers in this unit be continuously monitored and use personal protective equipment and control methods to reduce exposure. It is also recommended to use antioxidant compounds continuously to reduce the side effects of exposure.Keywords: Gallic acid: paint unit, Lymphocytes, Benzene, Oxidative stress

Introduction

Exposure to chemicals in workplaces can result in numerous health risks for workers. These exposures have also raised concerns across various industries and have significant implications for the health and safety of workers. Factories and production lines are environments where exposure to hazardous and toxic compounds occurs routinely and continuously [1], [2], [3]. Additionally, exposure to harmful factors or agents in the workplace is linked to an increased incidence of occupational diseases [4], [5], [6]. The exposure-disease relationship is challenged by various mechanisms and risk factors due to the complex nature of these exposures, which often involve a combination of stressors [4]. The activation of a cascade of events at the molecular level may occur after exposure to any xenobiotic substance entering the human body [7]. Automobile companies are an example of workplaces where employees are exposed to a variety of chemicals. Automobile manufacturing companies carry out various processes, such as painting, welding, washing, and assembly. In these processes, a variety of materials such as dyes, solvents, metals, adhesives, plastics, and polymers are used [8], [9], [10], [11].

The paint line or section in the automotive industry exemplifies a work environment where exposure to hazardous compounds occurs [8], [11], [12]. This sector significantly contributes to volatile organic compound (VOCs) emissions in the workplace. The use of paint and organic solvents in automobile production releases substantial amounts of VOCs during the process. VOCs are a group of compounds that can cause carcinogenesis, teratogenicity, and mutagenesis, posing major direct risks to human health [6], [10], [13], [14]. Benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes (BTEX) are among the most important VOCs. These compounds have attracted considerable research interest due to their well-documented high health risks, classification as hazardous air pollutants, and role in secondary pollutant formation [15], [16], [17]. Previous studies have shown that workers in paint environments are exposed to BTEX, especially benzene [10], [11]. BTEX compounds have carcinogenic potential. It has also been reported that low-level exposure, below OELs, is associated with neurobehavioral changes [6], [10]. Another toxicity mechanism involves an increase in reactive oxygen species (ROS) and oxidative stress. Oxidative stress is linked to irreversible complications and chronic diseases. Therefore, maintaining workers health and monitoring working conditions should be a priority for industries [18], [19]. VOCs cause the production of ROS by disrupting the mitochondrial respiratory chain and various oxidative enzymes [20]. In addition, BTEX increases the level of ROS and oxidative stress by increasing lipid peroxidation (LPO) levels, one of the most important markers of oxidative stress, and decreasing glutathione (GSH) levels [21], [22]. It has been shown that the effect of benzene on the respiratory chain is accompanied by an increase in ROS level [23].

Polyphenols are secondary plant metabolites that contain one or more aromatic rings with hydroxyl groups and occur in various forms in nature. These compounds serve as micronutrients with antioxidant properties, helping to defend against oxidative stress caused by excess ROS [24], [25], [26]. Gallic acid (GA) is one such compound with diverse pharmacological properties. Found in fruits and vegetables, it is known to exert antioxidant effects against pro-oxidative damage induced by chemicals [27], [28], [29]. GA effects vary with dose; at high doses, it can act as a pro-oxidant, while at low doses, it functions as an antioxidant. The hydroxyl phenol group in the GA structure is responsible for scavenging ROS and interrupting the cycle of new ROS formation [28], [30]. Therefore, we studied oxidative stress in peripheral lymphocytes of a paint unit in an automotive industry exposed to BTEX, and evaluated the protective role of GA in this context.

Materials and methods

Study design

Our research was supported by Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences, Tehran, Iran, and samples were collected from paint unit workers of an automobile industry. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences, Tehran, Iran, with ID: IR.SBMU.PHARMACY.REC.1402.175. In our study, all workers in the automotive industry (paint department) were randomly selected as the test group (17 male workers). Additionally, based on previous studies and for consistency, 17 non-exposed individuals (office workers, male) were chosen as the control group. The control group was randomly selected. The inclusion criteria for control and exposed workers included age between 20 and 60 years, having no chronic illnesses, not consuming alcohol, and not smoking. Also, the control subjects had no contact with BTEX. Informed written consent was obtained from all participants (control and test group) before sample collection.

BTEX concentration measurement

The standard NIOSH 1501 method is used to measure BTEX concentrations. In this method, activated carbon (charcoal) absorbent tubes and an SKC sampling pump with a flow rate of 0.1–1 L/min are used for analysis. Activated charcoal tubes are placed inside individual sampling pumps for sampling. After sampling, the samplers were stored at a temperature below 4°C and transported to the laboratory. In this method, sample analysis is performed using gas chromatography (GC) equipped with an FID detector. BTEX extraction for assay was performed using carbon disulfide (CS2/ purity of 99/5%). In addition, the injection and detector temperatures are 250 °C and 300 °C, respectively. The column used is of the capillary type (fused silica). Helium (He) was used as carrier gas (flow rate: 2 ml/min) [31].

Isolation of lymphocytes

In this study, blood samples were collected from all paint unit workers (17 male workers) and 17 male office workers. Inclusion criteria for control and exposed workers included age between 20 and 60 years, having no chronic illnesses, not consuming alcohol, and not smoking. Control subjects also had no contact with BTEX. Peripheral blood lymphocytes were isolated using our previous reports as a guide. The Ficoll standard method was used to isolate peripheral blood lymphocytes from the blood of two groups. The collected blood (5 ml) was diluted with PBS and then layered on Ficoll Paque Plus. Centrifugation was carried out at 2500 rpm/ for 20 min, followed by collection of the lymphocyte layer in erythrocyte lysis buffer. The final step involved incubating the lymphocytes at 37°C for 5 min. After dilution with PBS, the mixture was centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was removed, and the human lymphocytes were washed twice with RPMI1640 medium and centrifuged at 2000g for 7 min. The lymphocytes were re-suspended in RPMI1640 medium. A concentration of 1 × 104 cells/ml was used for each test [32]. In this study, the samples from each participant were analyzed individually and were not pooled.

ROS level measurement

Peripheral blood lymphocytes were tested for changes in ROS levels using a fluorescent 2′,7′-Dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) reagent. DCFH-DA can be rapidly oxidized to fluorescent 2,7-dichlorofluorescein (DCF) and then to non-fluorescent DCFH by intracellular esterase in the presence of ROS. The initial approach was to treat lymphocytes (1 ×104 cells/ml) with GA (12/5, 25, and 50 µg/ml). After that, the DCFH-DA probe was used to incubate two groups at a concentration of 1 µM for 30 min at 37°C. Cellular fluorescent intensity was measured using a spectrophotometer (λex=485 nm and λem=535 nm).

Lipid Peroxidation (LPO) content measurement

The investigation of Malondialdehyde (MDA) focused on its potential as an indicator of LPO. The assessment was conducted by measuring MDA content in lymphocyte samples from both groups. The initial approach was to treat lymphocytes (1 ×104 cells/ml) with GA (12/5, 25, and 50 µg/ml). MDA reacts with TBA to form a colored TBA-MDA complex for measuring this parameter. Homogenized samples were centrifuged at 1000g for 10 min, and the supernatant was transferred to a new tube containing 4 ml of TBA reagent (which includes 20% TCA and 0.5% TBA). The micro-tubes were placed in a boiling water bath for 15 min. After rapid cooling on ice, the mixture was centrifuged again at 1000 g for 10 min. The ELISA reader was used to measure this complex at a wavelength of 532 nm. Absorbance measurement correlates positively with LPO content [33].

GSH and GSSG content assay

Oxidative stress can be assayed using GSH and GSSG as key markers. The contents of GSH and GSSG in lymphocyte samples from both groups were measured. GSH and o-phthalaldehyde (OPT) react to form a highly fluorescent compound. Similarly, the reaction of GSSG with OPT produces a fluorescent compound. GSH and GSSG were quantified with a fluorescence spectrophotometer at λex = 350 nm and λem = 420 nm. During oxidative stress, GSH contents decrease, while GSSG contents increase [34]. In this study, concentrations of 12.5, 25, and 50 µg/ml of GA were used to examine the levels of GSH and GSSG in lymphocytes of both groups.

Statistical analysis

Results shown as Mean ± SD. The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism version 8 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA). Experiments were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by the Tukey test. A p-value of < 0.05 was used to determine statistical significance.

The impact of GA on lymphocyte viability

The effects of GA on the viability of lymphocytes isolated from workers are shown in Fig. 1. To evaluate this test, the effects of GA on isolated lymphocytes were evaluated at concentrations of 2.5, 5, 12.5, 25, and 50 µg/ml. The results indicate that GA at all concentrations has no effect on lymphocyte viability in car paint workers (Fig. 1). Subsequently, concentrations of 12.5, 25, and 50 µg/ml GA were used to conduct the desired tests.

Lymphocytes viability assay. Lymphocyte viability was evaluated through the MTT probe in the lymphocyte sample. In this test, the absorbance shows the lymphocyte viability. One-way ANOVA was used to analyze the data. Data are shown as mean ± SD (n = 17).

The impact of GA on the ROS level

The results showed that ROS levels were higher in lymphocytes isolated from workers exposed to paint than in the control group. GA at concentrations of 12.5, 25 and 50 µg/ml reduced ROS levels in workers (Fig. 2). Furthermore, our report indicated that GA at the highest level (50 µg/ml) had no impact on ROS levels in the control group (Fig. 2). These findings could have implications for the antioxidant effects of GA.

The impact of GA on LPO content

In lymphocytes isolated from exposed workers, higher LPO content was observed compared with the control group. To reduce LPO in exposed workers, the results indicated that GA (12.5, 25, and 50 µg/ml) lowered LPO contents (Fig. 3). Additionally, GA (50 µg/ml) did not affect the control group (Fig. 3).

Our study revealed that lymphocytes isolated from workers exposed to paint had a lower GSH content than the control group (Fig. 4). The GSH content in lymphocytes of workers exposed to paint was found to increase using GA in different concentrations (12.5, 25, and 50 µg/ml). The highest concentration of GA (50 µg/ml) did not affect the control group (Fig. 4).

Discussion

In the workplace, employees are exposed to various harmful factors. Supporting workers' health is crucial [35], [36]. In this study, we examined the level of exposure of paint unit workers in the automotive industry to BTEX and the effects of GA on oxidative stress caused by exposure in peripheral blood lymphocytes. The initial focus was on measuring workers' exposure to BTEX. The study found that workers' benzene exposure levels exceeded the OEL, while exposure to toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylene was lower. The results of our study are similar to the results of other studies, which showed that the concentration of benzene was higher than the OEL in workers exposed to car paint compared to other compounds (toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylene). Although exposure levels to toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylene may be below the OEL, it is important not to exceed the cumulative EI of BTEX [10], [11].

Workers in the automotive industry are exposed to VOCs compounds, especially BTEX, through various routes during paint spraying. The chemicals in paint can induce oxidative stress by increasing ROS. An increased ROS due to workplace exposure is linked to negative effects and irreversible health conditions [37], [38]. BTEX refers to hazardous compounds that can elevate ROS levels, leading to oxidative stress in living organisms [22], [31], [39]. Previous studies have shown that exposure to BTEX at low concentrations has been associated with oxidative stress (increased LPO and decreased GSH content) in exposed workers and laboratory animals [19], [22]. Previous studies have shown that BTEX compounds undergo metabolic activation via cytochrome P450 enzymes, leading to the formation of reactive metabolites that disrupt mitochondrial respiratory chain function, increase electron leakage, and promote ROS overproduction. Additionally, LPO and depletion of antioxidant defenses (such as GSH) contribute to oxidative stress [20], [21], [22], [23]. A study by Rafei et al., in street children, showed that exposure to BTEX was associated with increased levels of MDA in urine [18].

Therefore, it is essential to use compounds that can reduce free radicals and oxidative stress caused by occupational exposures. Recently, more researchers have focused on compounds derived from natural sources. Studies have shown that these compounds can have a wide range of effects on the human body [40], [41], [42]. Polyphenols are among the compounds that have attracted researchers' interest [43], [44]. Previous research demonstrated that these compounds can inhibit free radical production and reduce the side effects of many chemicals [45], [46]. These pharmacological effects, such as free radical scavenging, are due to the hydroxyl groups in their structure. GA is an antioxidant and phenolic acid found in plants and vegetables, featuring phenolic hydroxyl groups in its structure [47], [48].

Additionally, our analysis showed that GA reduced ROS levels in workers' lymphocytes from the paint unit. Previous studies have demonstrated that GA can scavenge free radicals (especially ROS), support intracellular antioxidant defenses, and inhibit LPO and cytotoxicity caused by oxidant agents. These activities are associated with the suppression of oxidative stress [47], [49]. Changes in the content of LPO and intracellular antioxidant activity are associated with the onset of inflammatory processes, aging, and changes in immune cell activity [50]. Reports also indicate that the antioxidant capacity and effectiveness of polyphenolic compounds depend on the number and position of hydroxyl groups in their structure [51], [52]. Our findings further revealed that GA decreased oxidative stress markers such as LPO and GSSG, while increasing the level of the intracellular antioxidant GSH. Elevated oxidative stress markers may result from increased ROS levels in workers' lymphocytes. These results highlight the antioxidant properties of GA, likely due to the hydroxyl groups in its structure. Another factor influencing its antioxidant role is the dosage of the compound; as mentioned, it exhibits antioxidant effects at low concentrations and pro-oxidant effects at high concentrations [30]. These results can help managers better recognize the BTEX-related hazards challenged by automotive paint workers and the need to control BTEX contamination to decrease health risks below OEL in these industries. In this study, oxidative stress was evaluated only in peripheral lymphocytes, which may not completely represent systemic oxidative status or tissue-specific damage in other organs. Therefore, future studies should be conducted to investigate the status of oxidative stress in different tissues, which requires the use of laboratory animals. Other limitations of this study includes the number of 17 individuals in each group, and it is suggested that similar industries with a larger number of individuals be used in future studies. Despite this limitation, the power analysis of this study was at an acceptable statistical level.

Conclusion

This study shows that workers in the paint unit of an automobile industry were exposed to benzene at levels above the OEL. Additionally, GA has shown the ability to reduce the increase of oxidative stress markers in lymphocytes. The protective effects of GA might be linked to the hydroxyl group in its molecular structure and the concentration used in this study. Therefore, phenolic compounds like GA could help prevent and reduce oxidative stress among workers in occupational settings. However, since the protective effects of GA were evaluated only under in vitro conditions using isolated lymphocytes, further in vivo and clinical studies are necessary before confirming its effectiveness as a preventive supplementation strategy in occupational settings. Finally, continuous environmental monitoring of BTEX exposure, implementation of engineering controls and personal protective equipment (PPE), regular biological monitoring of oxidative stress markers, and future evaluation of dietary antioxidant strategies as preventive interventions are recommended.

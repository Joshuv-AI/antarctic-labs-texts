Trajectories and influencing factors of self-care behaviors in Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure: A prospective observational study
Authors: Yu Chen, Ying Lin, Xinyue Dong, Yan Xue, Dong Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSelf-care is crucial for improving the quality of life of patients with chronic heart failure. Self-care behaviors change over time, and the trajectories differ among patients with varying characteristics. However, the trajectories of self-care behaviors in older adults with chronic heart failure have not been fully studied.ObjectiveThis study aimed to identify the trajectory patterns of self-care behaviors over 3 months after hospital discharge in Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure, as well as the factors influencing these patterns.MethodsA prospective observational study was conducted from October 2022 to July 2023, involving 255 older adults with chronic heart failure recruited from a tertiary hospital in Shanghai, China. Demographic and clinical information, self-care behaviors, self-care confidence, illness perception, self-efficacy, social support, and heart failure knowledge were investigated using a validated questionnaire and electronic health records at baseline, 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 2 months, and 3 months post-discharge. Growth mixture modeling was used to identify the trajectory patterns of self-care behaviors. Logistic regression was used to determine the influencing factors of different trajectory patterns.ResultsThe best-fit growth mixture modeling revealed a 2-class model for self-care maintenance: “sustained good” group and “poor-gradually improving” group; a 3-class model for symptom perception: “gradually decreasing”, “persistently poor”, and “poor-gradually improving” groups; and a 3-class model for self-care management: “poor-gradually improving”, “sustained good”, and “early boost then decline” groups. Self-care confidence, heart failure knowledge, economic level, and the source of medical expenses were determinants of the different trajectory patterns in self-care behaviors.ConclusionThis study enhances the understanding of the diverse behavioral trajectory patterns exhibited by Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure, as well as the factors that influence these patterns. Tailored interventions that focus on the unique needs of diverse groups should be developed to sustain satisfactory self-care behaviors.Keywords: Chronic heart failure, Growth mixture model, Older adult, Self-care behavior, Trajectory pattern

Introduction

Chronic heart failure is a significant global public health challenge, particularly among the aging population. Epidemiological studies indicate that approximately 56.2 million individuals are affected by chronic heart failure worldwide, with 62.2 % of whom being adults aged 70 years and older (Martin et al., 2024). In China, the phenomenon of accelerated population aging is particularly concerning. It is predicted that 35.4 % of the population will be individuals over 60 years old by 2050 (Zhou et al., 2025). This demographic shift has markedly intensified the burden of chronic heart failure, resulting in a 30-day readmission rate of 13.2 % and a 1-year mortality rate of 23.3 % (Foroutan et al., 2023). These figures highlight an urgent need for sustainable and effective management strategies to alleviate the clinical and socioeconomic impacts of chronic heart failure.

Self-care has emerged as a fundamental component in addressing this challenge for patients with chronic heart failure, recognized by the European Society of Cardiology as a Class Ⅰ recommendation for reducing the risk of hospitalization and mortality (McDonagh et al., 2021). Central to understanding this approach is the updated situation-specific theory of heart failure self-care, which provides the conceptual framework for this study. According to this theory, self-care is defined as “a naturalistic decision-making process that influences actions that maintain physiologic stability, facilitate the perception of symptoms, and direct the management of those symptoms” (Regel et al., 2016). It entails 3 separate but linked concepts, i.e., self-care maintenance, symptom perception, and self-care management (Regel et al., 2016). Self-care maintenance captures treatment adherence and healthy behaviors. Symptom perception involves the detection of physical sensations and the interpretation of meaning. Self-care management refers to the response to symptoms when they occur. The detailed self-care behaviors for patients with chronic heart failure typically include medication adherence, symptom monitoring, fluid restriction, and lifestyle modification. A systematic review has demonstrated the beneficial effects of self-care behaviors on heart failure knowledge, quality of life, and heart failure related hospitalization in patients with chronic heart failure (Zhao et al., 2021). However, older adults with chronic heart failure encounter distinct challenges that can impede consistent self-care behavior. These challenges encompass cognitive decline, multimorbidity, limited health literacy, poor mental health, and so on. (Chinese Society of Cardiology et al., 2024).

Current research predominantly relies on cross-sectional designs, which ignore the complexity and dynamicity in self-care, resulting in a limited understanding of the characteristics of chronic heart failure patients’ self-care behaviors changing over time. Creber et al. (2014) identified 2 distinct trajectory patterns of sodium adherence in patients with heart failure over 6 months (“adherent” and “non-adherent”). Lee et al. (2015) also reported observing 2 distinct trajectory patterns of self-care management in patients with heart failure during 6-month follow-ups (“getting worse” and “getting better”). Pancani et al. (2018) reported 3 trajectory patterns of self-care maintenance over 6 months (“persistently poor”, “borderline but improving”, and “increasingly good”. Recently, Kim et al. (2023) identified 3 self-care behavior trajectory patterns among Korean patients with heart failure (“low-decreased”, “middle-increased”, and “high-sustained”). However, these studies excluded the older subgroup. Few prospective studies have examined longitudinal patterns of self-care behaviors in older adults with chronic heart failure. Furthermore, most of these studies focused on only one aspect of self-care behaviors, neglecting the holistic nature of self-care as a comprehensive practice. These gaps limit the development of age-specific and stage-targeted interventions.

Based on the updated situation-specific theory of heart failure self-care and empirical literature, there are several factors influencing self-care behaviors, including knowledge, compatibility with values, self-confidence, self-efficacy, social support, multimorbidity, and socioeconomic status. A number of studies have shown that knowledge, illness perception, self-confidence, and self-efficacy had a powerful influence on self-care behaviors (Asai et al., 2023; Kan and Yu, 2020; Pancani et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023). Insufficient knowledge, poor illness perception, less self-care confidence, and a lower level of self-efficacy were determinants of poor self-care behaviors. Social support is an environmental factor that has been reported to influence self-care behaviors through both emotional and tangible support (Sayers et al., 2008). Multimorbidity is common in older adults with chronic heart failure, who often experience a significant symptom burden. This complexity, particularly in dietary and medication management, can impact self-care behaviors (Jiang et al., 2022). Distinct trajectories of self-care behaviors may be associated with varying influencing factors. However, existing empirical evidence regarding baseline factors that influence longitudinal patterns of self-care behaviors in older adults with chronic heart failure is limited. Understanding the trajectories of self-care behaviors in older adults with chronic heart failure and the influencing factors is essential. It could help identify critical decline phases so as to enable timely and personalized interventions. It could also uncover modifiable predictors of self-care behaviors and inform targeted strategies to improve self-care behaviors. Thus, this prospective study aimed to delineate the longitudinal trajectory patterns of self-care behaviors in Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure over 3 months after hospital discharge, and identify the determinants of these patterns. The findings will contribute to a deeper understanding of patients’ self-care processes and their variability over time, and provide evidence to identify subpopulations that need to be paid more attention to.

Methods

Study design and participants

It was a prospective observational study. Patients with chronic heart failure who were admitted to the cardiology departments of a tertiary hospital in Shanghai, China, and met the inclusion criteria were recruited conveniently between October 2022 and July 2023. The inclusion criteria were (a) diagnosed with chronic heart failure, (b) aged 60 years or older, (c) able to read and communicate, and (d) willing to participate in the study. Patients with serious diseases such as malignancy or other severe comorbidities, as well as mental and cognitive impairment, were excluded. Based on the Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of heart failure 2024 (Chinese Society of Cardiology et al., 2024), longitudinal data were collected across 5 time points: baseline (T1: hospitalization), 2 weeks after discharge (T2), 4 weeks after discharge (T3), 2 months after discharge (T4), and 3 months after discharge (T5). At enrollment, after being fully informed about the study, patients completed the written informed consent form and a validated self-administered questionnaire assessing self-care behaviors, self-care confidence, illness perception, self-efficacy, social support, and heart failure knowledge. Clinical information was gathered from electronic health records. Follow-up self-care behaviors data were obtained via structured telephone interviews conducted by trained researchers using standardized protocols. Sample size estimation followed Bentler's recommendation of ≥5 observations per free parameter, yielding a minimum requirement of 170 participants (Tabachnick and Fidell, 2001). The study was approved by the institutional review board of the hospital (B2022–174) and conformed to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Measures

Demographic and clinical information

A self-designed questionnaire was used for the collection of demographic information, including age, sex, educational level, occupation, marital status, economic level, residential area, source of medical expense, and so on. Clinical information included the course of the disease, the presence of chronic disease, and cardiac function.

Self-care behaviors

The Chinese version of the Self-care of Heart Failure Index v7.2 was used to measure self-care behaviors (F. Li et al., 2023). It consists of 29 items reflecting 3 components: self-care maintenance, symptom perception, and self-care management. The self-care maintenance subscale includes 10 items with a 5-point Likert scale from 1 to 5. The symptom perception subscale consists of 11 items, with the first 9 items being rated on a 5-point Likert scale, and the last 2 items being responded to as “have not had symptoms,” 0 (I did not recognize the symptoms), 1 (not quickly) to 5 (very quickly). The self-care management subscale comprises 8 items, with the first 7 items using a 5-point Likert scale, and the final item utilization a 6-point Likert scale. These 3 subscales are meant to be analyzed separately. A standardized score from 0 to 100 was calculated for each subscale, with scores higher than 70 indicating adequate self-care behaviors. Validity of the scale was supported by confirmatory factor analysis, convergent and discriminant validity, and concurrent validity. The Cronbach's α coefficient of each subscale in this study was 0.78, 0.85, and 0.89, respectively.

Self-care confidence

The Chinese version of the self-care confidence scale was used to measure patients’ confidence of self-care (Cao and Chair, 2023). It includes 10 items with a 5-point Likert scale from 1 to 5. A standardized score from 0 to 100 was calculated with a higher score indicating more confidence. Its Cronbach's α coefficient was 0.93 in this study.

Illness perception

The Chinese version of the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire was used to measure patients' cognitive and emotional representations of their illness (Mei et al., 2015). It includes 9 items rated using a 0-to-10 response scale. It also includes one open-ended response item, which asks patients to list the 3 most important causal factors in their illness. A higher score represents a stronger negative illness perception. Validity of the questionnaire was supported by confirmatory factor analysis and criterion-related validity. The questionnaire also had good split-half reliability with a correlation coefficient of 0.81. Its Cronbach's α coefficient in this study was 0.67.

Self-efficacy

Self-efficacy was evaluated by the Chinese Self-Efficacy for Managing Chronic Disease 6-Item Scale (Zhang, 2023). The scale is a 1-to-10 response scale consisting of 6 items in 2 dimensions of symptom management and disease common management. A higher score indicates a better self-efficacy. Validity of the scale was supported by exploratory factor analysis and criterion-related validity. The Cronbach's α coefficient was 0.93 in this study.

Social support

Social support was assessed by the Chinese Perceived Social Support Scale (Jiang, 2001). Three elements, namely, family support, friend support, and other support, are measured with a total of 12 items using a 7-point Likert scale. A higher score refers to a higher level of social support. Validity of the scale was supported by confirmatory factor analysis (Zhang et al., 2018). It also showed good content validity. The Cronbach's α coefficient was 0.92.

Heart failure knowledge

Heart failure knowledge was assessed by the Atlanta Heart Failure Knowledge Test V3 (Butts et al., 2018), which is a revised version of the Atlanta Heart Failure Knowledge Test V2 with 6 items updated. It comprises 30 questions with multiple-choice and binary (yes/no) response formats, encompassing 5 key areas of heart failure: pathophysiology, nutrition, behavior, medication, and symptom management. A higher score reflects a higher level of knowledge in these areas. As a Chinese version of Atlanta Heart Failure Knowledge Test V2 is widely used in China with good reliability and validity (Qian, 2012), only the updated 6 items were translated into Chinese in this study using the modified Brislin translation model (Jones et al., 2001). Five experts who were familiar with chronic heart failure were invited to ascertain the content validity of this questionnaire with the scale-level content validity index of 0.94. Exploratory factor analysis was performed among 559 older adults with chronic heart failure to test the structure of the questionnaire. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure of sampling adequacy was 0.84, and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was statistically significant (p < 0.01). Principal component analysis with varimax rotation revealed 8 factors, explaining 53.3 % of the total variance. All 6 new items loaded significantly on their intended dimensions with no cross-loadings >0.4. The Cronbach's α coefficient of this questionnaire was 0.81 in this study.

Statistical analysis

Initially, descriptive statistics were provided for all the measured variables. Then, growth mixture modeling, a person-centered analysis, was used to identify and test the latent trajectory classes associated with time-dependent outcomes in potential subgroups (Muthen and Muthen, 2000). Since which trajectory class individuals belong to cannot be directly observed, class membership is estimated based on the collected data. For each class, parameters such as the latent intercept growth factor and the latent slope growth factor of the growth trajectories are estimated separately (Wang and Bi, 2018). In this study, growth mixture modeling was implemented to identify latent trajectory classes based on scores related to self-care maintenance, symptom perception, and self-care management from time points T1 to T5. Full information maximum likelihood estimation was used by default to address missing data. The robust maximum likelihood estimator was used for model estimation as it performs well in abnormally distributed data with a relatively small sample size. Model fit was assessed using the Akaike information criterion, Bayesian information criterion, and sample-size adjusted BIC, where smaller values indicate a better fit (Wang and Bi, 2018). Entropy was used to evaluate classification accuracy, with a value exceeding 0.8 considered indicative of good classification (Wang and Bi, 2018). The likelihood ratio test compares a k-class model against a k-1-class model, where a significant P value suggests a substantial improvement in the k-class model and thus rejects the k-1-class model rationally. Mplus was used to conduct the adjusted Lo-Mendell-Rubin likelihood ratio test and bootstrap likelihood ratio test for model comparison in this study. After identifying a good-fit model, Bayesian posterior probability was applied to export the class memberships of individuals.

Finally, the logistic regression model was carried out, with individuals’ assigned trajectory class as the dependent variable. Demographic and clinical information, self-care confidence, illness perception, self-efficacy, social support, and heart failure knowledge were included in the regression model. Analyses were performed with Mplus 8.0 (Los Angeles, CA) and SPSS 22.0 (Armonk, NY). All tests were 2 tailed, and results with a P value of <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant characteristics

A total of 300 older adults with chronic heart failure were initially invited to participate, 15 declined, resulting in a final sample of 285 enrolled at T1. At T2, 25 patients were lost to follow-up, attributed to 1 death, 21 instances of unanswered or declined calls, and 3 incorrect phone numbers. Additionally, 5 patients declined to participate at T3. Consequently, the final sample was composed of 255 participants (see Fig. 1). No significant differences in demographic information, clinical characteristics, or self-care behaviors were found between the final sample and the dropout patients.

In terms of educational level, more than half of the participants (n = 151, 59.2 %) had below senior high school education. Prior to retirement, 113 participants (44.3 %) were blue-collar workers, and 103 participants (40.4 %) were white-collar workers. Influencing factors of self-care behaviors trajectory patterns

To identify the influencing factors of different trajectory patterns of self-care maintenance, a forward stepwise binary logistic regression was performed. The dependent variable was coded as 0=“sustained good” group and 1=“poor-gradually improving” group. Dummy variables were created for educational level, using the group “elementary school or below” as the reference, and for occupation, using the group of “no occupation” as the reference. The results showed that self-care confidence (odds ratio=0.948) was a significant determinant of self-care maintenance trajectory patterns. Patients having more self-care confidence were more likely to develop a sustained good pattern. (Table 3).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study that examined dynamic trajectories of self-care behaviors using growth mixture modeling in Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure. This person-centered data analysis enables the differentiation of various groups with potentially distinct needs within the population. Furthermore, it enhances the pursuit of whole-person personalized care by taking both physical and psychosocial factors into consideration during care delivery (Wu et al., 2020). This study provided critical insights into the heterogeneous patterns of self-care maintenance, symptom perception, and self-care management. Two trajectory patterns were identified for self-care maintenance: “sustained good” and “poor-gradually improving”. In terms of symptom perception, 3 trajectory patterns emerged: “gradually decreasing”, “persistently poor”, and “poor-gradually improving”. For self-care management, 3 trajectory patterns were also identified: “poor-gradually improving”, “sustained good”, and “early boost then decline”. The findings are inconsistent with the studies conducted by Pancani et al. (2018); Lee et al. (2015), and Kim et al. (Kim and Son, 2023). Pancani et al. reported 3 trajectory patterns of self-care maintenance over 6 months: “persistently poor”, “increasingly adequate”, and “increasingly optimal”. Lee et al. reported 2 trajectory patterns of self-care management over 6 months: “getting worse” and “getting better”. While Kim et al. classified the trajectories of self-care behaviors into “low-decreased”, “middle-increased”, and “high-sustained”. The disparities in trajectory patterns may be attributed to various factors, including the different measurement tools for self-care behaviors, the frequency and time of follow-ups, and the characteristics of the participants. This study used the Self-care of Heart Failure Index v7.2 to evaluate self-care behaviors, contrasting with previous studies that employed the Self-care of Heart Failure Index v6.2 (Lee et al., 2015; Pancani et al., 2018) and the 9-item European Heart Failure Self-care Behavior Scale (Kim and Son, 2023). While those three studies measured self-care behaviors at 3 or 4 time points over 6 months, this study measured self-care behaviors at 5 time points over 3 months after hospital discharge, specifically focusing on behavioral changes during the vulnerable period of heart failure. In addition, the participants of this study were older adults.

Nevertheless, the predominance of a “sustained good” trajectory in self-care maintenance and self-care management is consistent with prior studies, which demonstrated that structured post-discharge follow-up enhanced long-term adherence in patients with chronic heart failure (Riegel et al., 2021). Notably, the “poor-gradually improving” trajectory observed across all domains indicated a delayed behavioral adaptation in self-care among patients with low baseline capabilities. This finding suggests that these patients have the potential for improvement in their self-care behaviors and should be prioritized for early interventions.

Self-care confidence reflects the degree to which patients believe in their ability to perform specific self-care related tasks and to persist in those actions or behaviors despite barriers or challenges (Pancani et al., 2018). Not surprisingly, self-care confidence was found to be an important determinant of favorable behavioral trajectory patterns in this study. These findings support the situation-specific theory of heart failure self-care (Riegel et al., 2016) and the middle range theory of self-care of chronic diseases (Jaarsma et al., 2017), highlighting the strong association between self-care confidence and self-care behaviors. Patients with more self-care confidence were more likely to take initiative and actively seek information about their condition and coping strategies through various means. This proactive approach not only enhanced their self-care capabilities but also enabled them to achieve the desired outcomes in the management of chronic heart failure.

Higher heart failure knowledge was linked to the “poor-gradually improving” trajectory in symptom perception, a finding that appears paradoxical given the established link between knowledge and self-care behaviors in cross-sectional studies (Seid et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2023). However, this seemingly contradictory result suggests a more nuanced relationship. Patients in the “poor-gradually improving” trajectory group initially reported poorer symptom perception but demonstrated improvement over time, potentially indicating that baseline knowledge alone was insufficient for effective symptom recognition. These patients potentially had the foundational knowledge but initially lacked the practical skills to apply it effectively. Their subsequent improvement could reflect a learning process, wherein existing knowledge served as a basis for acquiring essential skills throughout the study period. Although unmeasured factors, such as specific aspects of health literacy, could play a role, the observed trajectory itself offered a plausible explanation: knowledge was foundational, but skill development was key for effective applications. Therefore, future interventions should integrate skill-based training, such as symptom interpretation drills, alongside efforts to disseminate knowledge.

Socioeconomic determinants, including economic level and sources of medical expense, significantly influenced self-care management trajectory patterns. These findings align with global evidence concerning health disparities, wherein financial constraints can exacerbate challenges in self-care (Enard et al., 2023). For instance, a cross-sectional study conducted in China found that patients with chronic heart failure who had a lower financial status reported poorer self-care behaviors (Zhang et al., 2023). The economic burden associated with treatment may result in reduced hospital visits and medication nonadherence, further complicating the management of the condition.

Interestingly, this study found that patients relying on the Urban and Rural Resident Basic Medical Insurance as their source of medical expense were more likely to develop a sustained good trajectory in self-care management. The explanation could be that the insurance’s reimbursement framework, though offering lower overall coverage, prioritized preventive and chronic disease management through subsidized outpatient services (Li et al., 2023b). This structure encouraged proactive health engagement from the onset of disease, as patients faced consistent and predictable costs for routine care. As a result, patients relying on Urban and Rural Resident Basic Medical Insurance tended to maintain good self-care behaviors. In contrast, the Urban Employee Basic Medical Insurance was characterized by higher reimbursement rates for inpatient and acute care but limited outpatient and chronic disease management support (Li et al., 2023b). During the early stages of disease, the substantial coverage for hospitalization costs might diminish the perceived urgency for proactive self-care. However, as the disease progresses, patients began to face increasing out-of-pocket expenses for outpatient medications and routine monitoring, leading to financial strain. This economic burden gradually heightened patients’ awareness of the necessity to optimize self-care behaviors.

This study identified self-care confidence, knowledge, economic level, and source of medical expense as significant determinants of trajectory patterns of self-care behaviors. These factors likely exhibit complex interactions, rather than operating independently. Self-care confidence appears crucial for translating knowledge into sustained action, as evidenced by a study in patients with heart failure demonstrating its critical mediating role between disease knowledge and self-care maintenance and management (Massouh et al., 2020). This finding highlights confidence’s function in facilitating the translation of knowledge into actual self-care behaviors, indicating that patients with higher knowledge and stronger confidence are more likely to maintain and develop positive self-care behaviors. Another study with a sample of 141 patients with heart failure also supported this, showing that self-care confidence directly influenced self-care behaviors while mediating the knowledge and self-care behaviors relationship (Chuang et al., 2019). Socioeconomic status also plays a significant role, potentially interacting with both confidence and access to care. Lower socioeconomic status can create practical barriers to self-care implementation, such as difficulty affording necessary medications, dietary requirements, and devices. This is evidenced by a study on food-insecure populations managing long-term health conditions, where low-income individuals struggled to meet nutritional requirements–an essential aspect of chronic disease self-care (Douglas et al., 2020). Additionally, lower socioeconomic status can cause psychological distress. As indicated by a systematic review and meta-analysis, income had an important effect on mental health with income decreases resulting in a 0.21 standardized mean difference worsening of mental health (Thomson et al., 2022). Lower income predicts negative self-perceptions, which may undermine self-care confidence. This dual burden of practical constraints and psychological strain jointly compromises self-care confidence while restricting both knowledge acquisition and its practical application. Conversely, higher socioeconomic status can facilitate continued knowledge reinforcement, foster self-care confidence through consistent professional support and successful disease management, and mitigate the financial burden of healthcare costs.

Therefore, while knowledge provides a foundation and confidence drives action, economic stability and supportive sources of medical expense appear to create the necessary context for these factors to interact effectively and lead to sustained positive self-care behaviors patterns. Future interventions should recognize this interplay by integrating knowledge dissemination with confidence-building strategies, while also addressing socioeconomic barriers and leveraging the supportive capacity of accessible health insurance systems to optimize long-term self-care outcomes.

This study has several limitations. First, although the sample size had met the minimum requirement, it was still relatively small, particularly in minority trajectory patterns. Findings pertaining to these smaller classes should be interpreted with careful consideration and were considered preliminary. Second, participants were only recruited from a tertiary hospital in Shanghai. Thus, the generalizability of the study is limited. Third, the evaluation of self-care behaviors over time was relatively short. Future research should consider multi-center prospective studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods exceeding 12 months. Finally, variables such as self-care confidence, illness perception, and social support are inherently time dependent; however, these factors were only assessed at baseline.

Implications for practice

This study contributed knowledge regarding how self-care behaviors changed and varied among Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure. The results have implications for practice. Tailored interventions are essential, as a one-size-fits-all strategy is unlikely to enhance self-care behaviors effectively. For patients exhibiting a poor but gradually improving trajectory pattern, an early post-discharge coaching program may prove beneficial. This program can involve weekly one-on-one virtual sessions with trained healthcare coaches, who can guide patients through goal-setting workshops. For example, patients can collaboratively establish realistic short-term goals and use problem-solving simulations to anticipate and address potential barriers. The effectiveness of this approach can be monitored through weekly self-reported data via a mobile application, tracking goal progress, and capturing patient feedback.

For those showing gradually decreasing or persistently poor trajectory patterns, a more comprehensive and multi-component intervention is required. A care coordination team consisting of physicians, nurses, and social workers can conduct in-depth assessments to identify specific individual challenges, such as deficits in self-care confidence, knowledge gaps, or limited socioeconomic resources. Informed by these assessments, personalized action plans can be developed. These plans may incorporate home visits by nurses to provide hands-on self-care training and referrals to community support groups for foster social connection and peer support. Regular follow-up phone calls can be implemented to reinforce adherence to recommended practices and to dynamically adapt the intervention based on ongoing progress.

In addition, integrating AI-driven mobile platforms may significantly enhance the effectiveness of these interventions. These platforms can be engineered to interface with existing electronic health records, facilitating automated identification of at-risk patients. Real-time symptom monitoring can be enabled through features like continuous vital sign tracking and symptom check-ins, with automated alerts triggered for healthcare providers upon detection of concerning changes. To reduce dropout rates, the platforms can leverage gamification elements, such as rewards for consistent self-reporting and achieving self-care goals, and personalized motivational messages based on individual progress. By implementing these detailed and targeted interventions, healthcare providers can more effectively support patients in improving their self-care behaviors and achieve better health outcomes.

Conclusion

This study enhances the understanding of the diverse behavioral trajectory patterns exhibited by Chinese older adults with chronic heart failure, as well as the factors that influence these patterns. Self-care confidence, heart failure knowledge, economic level, and the source of medical expenses were key determinants of the different trajectory patterns in self-care behaviors. Future research should validate these identified trajectory patterns across various populations, thereby enhancing the generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, it is essential to evaluate tailored interventions that specifically target modifiable factors influencing self-care behaviors. By focusing on the unique needs of diverse groups and addressing the challenges they face, these initiatives can lead to more effective heart failure management strategies. Ultimately, such efforts will contribute to improving patient outcomes and elevating their overall quality of life.

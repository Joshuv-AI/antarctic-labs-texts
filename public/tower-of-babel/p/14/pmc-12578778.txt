The MTORC1 signaling pathway related gene POLR3G serves as a potential prognostic biomarker in Hepatocellular Carcinoma
Authors: Zicheng Wang, Jianxiong Chen, Bao Zhang, Yao Rong, Dilin Wu, Ru Ji
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aims to investigate the prognostic significance and potential biological functions of the MTORC1 signaling pathway-associated gene POLR3G in Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). A prognostic risk model for HCC was developed by integrating HCC-related datasets and associated clinical data obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. The GSVA website was employed to analyze the model genes across pan-cancer datasets, focusing on copy number variations (CNV), single nucleotide variations (SNV), methylation differences, drug sensitivity and immune cell infiltration profiles. Subsequently, we examined the expression levels and prognostic significance of POLR3G in HCC. Utilizing Spearman correlation analysis, we identified genes associated with POLR3G. Furthermore, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was employed to elucidate the potential signaling pathways in which POLR3G may be involved. The relationship between POLR3G expression and immune cell abundance in HCC samples was assessed using the ssGSEA algorithm. Finally, the impact of POLR3G on HCC cell proliferation was validated through CCK-8 and EDU cell proliferation assays. Through univariate Cox regression analysis and LASSO regression analysis, we established a prognostic risk model for HCC comprising 13 genes. The analysis revealed that individuals categorized in the low-risk group had a markedly improved overall survival probability relative to those in the high-risk group. POLR3G exhibited a markedly elevated expression in HCC tissues when compared to adjacent normal tissues. The expression of POLR3G was correlated with tumor grade, and elevated POLR3G expression was associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients. Furthermore, the expression level of POLR3G was found to be correlated with the level of immune cell infiltration. Knockdown of POLR3G significantly inhibited the proliferative capacity of hepatocellular carcinoma cells. The findings suggest that POLR3G may serve as a potential biomarker influencing the prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma patients by modulating the tumor immune microenvironment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-025-01887-6.

Introduction

HCC is the sixth most frequently diagnosed malignant neoplasm worldwide., with its incidence steadily increasing worldwide, posing a significant challenge to global health [1, 2]. HCC represents one of the most common etiologies of primary liver cancer. Previous studies have identified risk factors influencing the onset of HCC, including smoking, obesity, alcohol consumption, viral hepatitis, and genetic predispositions. However, the precise pathogenic mechanisms underlying HCC remain to be fully elucidated [3–5]. Although significant advancements have been made in the treatment modalities for HCC in recent years, the therapeutic options for patients with advanced-stage HCC remain limited [6]. The advent of targeted therapy and immunotherapy, exemplified by molecular-targeted agents such as Sorafenib, Apatinib, and Bevacizumab, has significantly enhanced the survival rates of patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma and those who have failed local treatments. However, as treatment progresses, the issue of tumor resistance has emerged as a significant challenge [7, 8]. Chemoresistance is a major determinant underlying the poor clinical outcomes observed in HCC patients [9]. Consequently, the discovery of innovative biomarkers for prognosis and molecular targets for therapy is imperative to enhance and ameliorate the survival rates and quality of life for patients with advanced HCC.

The mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) is a multiprotein assembly of mTOR, whose signaling pathway critically regulates processes such as cell growth, metastasis, metabolic regulation, and intracellular communication through its activation or inhibition [10]. Furthermore, given its close association with tumor progression, drug resistance, and immune evasion, the mTORC1 signaling pathway has emerged as a critical focus in the investigation of tumor biomarkers [11, 12]. Nevertheless, research on the mTORC1 signaling pathway in hepatocellular carcinoma remains relatively limited. Consequently, analyzing the roles of genes associated with the mTORC1 signaling pathway in hepatocellular carcinoma will facilitate the exploration of its potential as a prognostic biomarker for this malignancy.

The RNA polymerase III subunit G(POLR3G) gene encodes the RPC32α subunit of RNA polymerase III, an isoform that is essential for maintaining stem cells in an undifferentiated state [13]. POLR3G, a critical gene within the mTORC1 signaling pathway, is classically implicated in the synthesis of tRNA and non-coding RNA [14]. However, recent studies have revealed that aberrant expression of POLR3G can influence the progression of various cancers through multiple mechanisms, including the regulation of tumor cell proliferation and metastasis, stress adaptation, and cellular metabolism [15]. In the context of urothelial carcinoma, overexpression of POLR3G has been shown to enhance the aggressiveness of bladder malignancies, thereby promoting tumor proliferation and metastasis [16]. Additionally, studies have demonstrated that dysregulated expression of POLR3G can facilitate the proliferation and metastasis of triple-negative breast cancer [17]. However, the clinical significance of POLR3G in hepatocellular carcinoma, particularly its prognostic value and relationship with the tumor microenvironment, remains to be elucidated.

The tumor microenvironment has garnered extensive research and clinical attention as a therapeutic target, with a focus on components such as tumor-infiltrating T cells, tumor-associated macrophages, cancer-associated fibroblasts, and the extracellular matrix [18]. Previous studies have revealed that tumor microenvironment significantly influences the progression and treatment outcomes of hepatocellular carcinoma, as immune cell infiltration is strongly correlated with prognostic indicators and the effectiveness of immunotherapeutic interventions [19, 20]. Research has demonstrated that the mTORC1 signaling pathway is implicated in the differentiation of immune cells and the production of cytokines [21, 22]. This suggests that POLR3G may serve as a critical link between oncogenic signaling and immune regulation. However, the interplay among POLR3G expression levels, immune infiltration patterns, and clinical outcomes in hepatocellular carcinoma remains to be fully elucidated.

Our study reveals the potential prognostic value of POLR3G as a biomarker in HCC, examining the correlation between its expression levels and clinicopathological characteristics as well as patient outcomes. In addition, we analyzed the correlation between POLR3G and immune infiltration to elucidate its function in shaping the immune microenvironment of HCC. The findings will clarify the potential of POLR3G as a novel prognostic marker influencing HCC progression and the tumor immune microenvironment, providing a theoretical foundation for personalized therapeutic strategies in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Materials and methods

Acquisition of data

mRNA data and clinical information for the LIHC dataset were obtained from the cancer Genome atlas (TCGA) database. This dataset comprises 50 samples of adjacent normal tissues and 374 tumor samples from TCGA. The normal group consists of the adjacent normal tissue data, while the tumor group includes the TCGA tumor samples.

Data processing and analysis

The datasets obtained from the TCGA database were analyzed using the STAR workflow and converted into transcripts per million (TPM) formats, followed by log2 (value + 1) transformation. Analysis was performed using R software (version 4.2.1) with relevant packages including ggplot2 [3.3.6], stats [4.2.1], and car [3.1–0]. Data processing involved log2 (value + 1) transformation. Statistical analyses included the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

GEPIA website

GEPIA (Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis) is an online platform designed for interactive data exploration and analysis. (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/) that integrates data from TCGA (encompassing RNA expression data from over 9,000 tumor samples across 33 cancer types) and GTEx (providing gene expression data from approximately 700 normal individuals across 54 tissue types). It is designed for the exploration and visualization of cancer gene expression data. Key functionalities include differential expression analysis, survival analysis, gene correlation analysis, and principal component analysis [23]. Using the GEPIA2 database, we investigated the prognostic value of genes in the mTORC1 signaling pathway across different cancer types.

GSVA website

GSVA (Gene Set Variation Analysis) is a bioinformatics approach designed to evaluate the enrichment levels of gene sets within individual samples (http://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/gsva.html). It quantifies gene set activity without requiring pre-grouping of samples, characterized by its single-sample analysis capability, non-parametric methodology, broad applicability, and compatibility with multiple gene set databases. Using the GSVA dataset, we analyzed CNV and SNV of genes in the prognostic model across pan-cancer types.

HPA website

The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database comprises protein information from 24,000 human tissues and cells, freely accessible at (https://www.proteinatlas.org/.) This resource enables the investigation of target gene expression levels across various cells and tissues. Utilizing this database, we investigated the protein expression profiles of mTORC1 signaling pathway-related genes in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Identification of differentially expressed genes

HCC patients were categorized into high- and low-expression cohorts according to the median POLR3G mRNA expression levels. The "limma" R package was utilized to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the groups, applying thresholds of |log FC|≥ 1 and FDR < 0.05. Spearman’s correlation analysis was performed to assess the correlation between POLR3G and the top 10 positively and negatively correlated DEGs, with results visualized using a heatmap. Furthermore, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using the Molecular signatures database (c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt) to investigate molecular pathways correlation with POLR3G in HCC. Representative results from 8 pathways were selected and archived. Statistical analysis and visualization were performed utilizing the clusterProfiler R package.

Survival prognosis analysis

To explore the clinical relevance of POLR3G in LIHC, we conducted a comprehensive analysis using the glmmet and survival packages in R. Initially, LASSO regression analysis was applied to genes linked to the MTORC1 signaling pathway in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC). Subsequently, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was employed to generate survival curves, and ROC curves were plotted using the ROC package to evaluate the predictive performance of the model.

Relationship between POLR3G and clinical features in LIHC

To further elucidate the clinical relevance of POLR3G, we assessed its expression levels across various clinical characteristics, including T, N, M stages, pathological stage, age, gender, OS, disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI). Visualization of these results was achieved using the ggplot2 package, providing a clear and interpretable representation of the data.

PPI Network construction

To identify potential protein interactions involving POLR3G, we utilized the STRING database(https:/string-db.org/). The 100 proteins exhibiting the highest interaction probabilities were chosen for subsequent investigation. This approach allowed us to explore the protein–protein interaction (PPI) network and gain insights into the functional associations of POLR3G in LIHC.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses

The GeneMANIA web tool was utilized to analyze molecules associated with POLR3G. To identify potential interacting proteins with POLR3G, the STRING database was employed, and the top 100 demonstrating the highest interaction probabilities were selected. Functional enrichment analysis, including GO and KEGG pathways, was performed using the clusterProfiler package. Additionally, ID conversion for the input gene list was conducted using the org.Hs.eg.db R package.

Immune infiltration analysis

The identification and infiltration of immune cells within tumors play a key role in recognizing and eradicating cancer. For immune infiltration analysis, we utilized the "GSVA" package within the R software environment, integrating it with the ssGSEA algorithm, and utilized RNA-seq data in TPM format from TCGA-LIHC. Spearman relationship analysis was applied to assess the correlation between POLR3G and the infiltration levels of 24 immune cell types.

Cell culture and transfection

Huh7 and SMMC-7721 hepatocellular carcinoma cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 and DMEM medium, respectively, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin, and maintained at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator. When cell confluence reached 80–90%, the cells were passaged using trypsin digestion. For transfection experiments, cells were seeded at an appropriate density (8 × 104 cells/well in a 24-well plate) one day in advance to achieve 60–70% confluence at the time of transfection. siRNA and transfection reagent were separately diluted in serum-free Opti-MEM medium. The diluted solutions were then combined and incubated at room temperature for 15 to 20 min to allow the formation of siRNA-transfection reagent complexes. After replacing the culture medium with antibiotic-free medium, the complexes were added dropwise to each well. Following 4 to 6 h of incubation, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium containing antibiotics. Transfection efficiency was subsequently evaluated using quantitative PCR and Western blotting.

Cell proliferation assay

The CCK-8 assay and the EDU cell proliferation assay were conducted. For the CCK-8 experiment, cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 3,000 cells per well for both experimental and control groups, with three replicate wells per group. After incubating the cells for 24, 48, 72, and 96 h, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (Biosharp, BS350B) was added to each well at the respective time points. The plates were then incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 1, 1.5, and 2 h, followed by quantifying absorbance at 450 nm with a microplate reader. For the EDU assay, cells in the logarithmic growth phase from both control and experimental groups were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well and cultured until reaching normal growth conditions. Subsequently, the cells were labeled with EDU, fixed, stained with Apollo dye, and counterstained for nuclei. Finally, the cells were examined using fluorescence microscopy.

Quantitative real-time PCR assay

We extracted total RNA from tissues or cells using Trizol Reagent (AG) and synthesized cDNA using the QUANTSCRIPT RT Kit (TAKARA) according to the instructions. The qRT-PCR reaction conditions were as follows: pre-denaturation at 94 °C for 5 min, denaturation at 94 °C for 45 s, annealing at 60 °C for 60 s, and a total of 45 cycles. Each sample was divided into three replicate wells [19]. The primer sequences for real-time PCR were as follows: POLR3G forward, 5’-CACCGTATAACTGGTTCTGATGGCT-3’, and POLR3G reverse, 5’-AAACAGCCATCAGAACCAGTTATAC-3’.

Western blotting

Proteins were isolated with RIPA lysis buffer following two PBS washes, with quantification performed using a BCA protein detection kit. Subsequent to SDS-PAGE separation and PVDF membrane transfer, the blots underwent blocking, antibody probing (overnight primary antibody incubation followed by secondary antibody treatment), and chemiluminescent detection for densitometric analysis.

Statistical analysis

All bioinformatics analyses were conducted using R version 4.2.1. Differences in expression levels between groups were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. For correlation analysis, Spearman’s correlation was applied. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. In this study, *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

Results

Expression of mTORC1 signaling pathway–associated genes in LIHC and univariate Cox regression analysis

The hazard ratios of genes associated with the mTORC1 signaling pathway were calculated with 95% confidence intervals and p-values. The results indicated that EEF1E1, EIF2S2, Enolase 1(ENO1), ETF1, Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), Glutathione-disulfide reductase (GSR), GTF2H1, HSPA4, PLK1, PLOD2, Proteasome 26S subunit, non-ATPase 13 (PSMD13),, SLC1A5, SLC7A11, STIP1, TOMM40, Tubulin gamma 1 (TUBG1), ABCF2, Aurora kinase A (AURKA), BCAT1, and Cell division cycle 25A (CDC25A) were related with favorable prognosis in LIHC patients (Supplementary Figs. 1A–E).

Development of a novel prognostic-correlated survival model in LIHC

Initially, the P-value threshold was set to less than 0.05, and the HR was utilized to evaluate the significance of model genes in LIHC. For instance, a POLR3G HR greater than 1 indicates that this gene acts as a risk factor for LIHC progression. Conversely, a PGM1 HR less than 1 suggests its role as a protective factor, inhibiting LIHC progression. Subsequently, these findings were further validated employing LASSO regression analysis with cross-validation, risk scores were derived using Cox regression coefficients and gene expression data. LIHC patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. ROC curves were constructed, and survival analyses were performed for validation. (Supplementary Figs. 1F–H). The results demonstrated that the high-risk score group was correlated with poor prognosis in LIHC patients.

OS and variation of model genes in pan-cancer

To investigate the biological functions of risk genes correlated with the mTORC1 signaling pathway across pan-cancer types, we investigated the relationship between overall survival outcomes and the expression levels of risk model-associated genes in diverse tumor cohorts from the TCGA database. A heatmap depicting the overall survival of model genes was constructed (Supplementary Fig. 2A). Elevated expression levels of these model genes were correlated with adverse clinical outcomes in LIHC patients. Additionally, TUBG1 functioned as an oncogene in THYM, THCA, PCPG, and READ. Subsequently, we explored CNV and SNV data across 33 cancer types using the TCGA database. Visualization of the variant data was performed. Notably, TPI1 predominantly exhibited heterozygous amplification in tumors such as ACC, TGCT, and OV, suggesting that TPI1 may undergo amplification in only one allele in these cancers. PLOD2 showed heterozygous amplification in CESC, UCEC, BLCA, ESCA, LUSC, and OV, while displaying heterozygous deletion in PCPG, indicating single-copy loss in these tumors. POLR3G was primarily characterized by heterozygous amplification in ACC and heterozygous deletion in BLCA, ESCA, TGCT, LUSC, and OV. Furthermore, we examined SNV of the 13 risk model genes across pan-cancer types. PLOD2 exhibited higher mutation frequencies in UCEC, SKCM, and STAD, while PLK1 showed higher mutation frequencies in UCEC and SKCM (Supplementary Figs. 2B–C).

Methylation, drug sensitivity and immune infiltration of model genes in pan-cancer

We analyzed the methylation differences of the 13 risk model genes across pan-cancer types (Supplementary Fig. 2D). The results revealed that ENO1 and PLOD2 exhibited lower methylation levels in KIRC, while PLOD2 showed higher methylation differences in LUSC. Additionally, we investigated the association between these risk model genes and critical molecular pathways implicated in tumor development. (Supplementary Fig. 2E). We found that PLK1 and POLR3G can promote apoptosis and cell cycle pathways, with POLR3G also inhibiting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a critical biological process in tumor initiation and progression. Furthermore, to investigate the relationship between mRNA expression levels of the 13 risk model genes and drug sensitivity, we used R software to plot the association between gene expression levels and drug sensitivity (Supplementary Fig. 2F). The results demonstrated that the expression levels of PLOD2 and G6PD were positively correlated with GSK2126458 (a dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor), while PLOD2 expression was negatively associated with the multi-target kinase inhibitor Midostaurin. Evaluating the association between risk model gene expression and anticancer drug sensitivity offers critical guidance for clinical treatment strategies and therapeutic agent selection.

Aberrant activation of molecules associated with the mTORC1 signaling pathway plays a critical role in antitumor immunity [24, 25]. To perform immune infiltration analysis, we utilized the "GSVA" package in R software, integrating it with the ssGSEA algorithm, and employed RNA-seq data in TPM format from TCGA-LIHC. A Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to evaluate the association between POLR3G expression and the infiltration levels of distinct immune cell populations. The results indicated that KIRC and HCC triggered significant infiltration of immune cells. We noticed positive correlations between tumor-infiltrating immune cells and these risk model genes, such as nTreg, Th1, B cells, and DCs in LIHC, additionally, we investigated the correlation between the risk model genes and the infiltration of major immune cells in LIHC, analyzing the associations between the 13 risk model genes and six primary tumor-infiltrating immune cell types, we found that TUBG1, SLC2A1, PLK1, LDHA, and G6PD were negatively correlated with macrophages, while POLR3G showed a positive correlation with NK cells (Supplementary Figs. 2G–H).

Protein expression levels of model genes in tissues

To examine the protein expression profiles of the risk model genes in LIHC, we retrieved data from the HPA database for 12 genes, comparing their protein expression profiles between LIHC tissues and normal liver tissues (Supplementary Figs. 3). The results demonstrated that EEF1E1, G6PD, PLOD2, POLR3G, SLC1A5, and TUBG1 exhibited higher expression levels in HCC compared to liver tissues. Further in vivo and in vitro studies, along with mechanistic exploration of these model genes in LIHC, may reveal their potential as critical therapeutic targets for future LIHC treatment strategies.

Discussion

Hepatocellular carcinoma, a highly heterogeneous malignant tumor, exhibits significant geographical and etiological dependencies in its incidence and mortality rates [26, 27]. Epidemiological data indicate that the incidence of HCC is rising in East Asia and sub-Saharan Africa due to the high prevalence of hepatitis B virus (HBV), while in developed regions such as Europe and North America, the surge in metabolic syndrome and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) has driven an increase in non-viral HCC cases [28–30]. This etiology-driven molecular heterogeneity has increasingly highlighted the limitations of traditional clinical staging systems in prognostic stratification [31, 32]. Despite advancements in the study of targeted biomarkers, their clinical translation faces multiple challenges, as most biomarkers are validated in retrospective cohorts and lack evidence-based support from prospective multicenter studies [33, 34]. Additionally, traditional prognostic markers, such as AFP, exhibit limited predictive efficacy for immune responses [35, 36]. Therefore, identifying novel and valuable molecular biomarkers represents a critical breakthrough for achieving personalized treatment.

The mTORC1 signaling pathway, a central regulator of cellular metabolism and energy homeostasis, plays a critical role in cellular life processes by coordinating protein biosynthesis, fatty acid metabolism, and autophagy, thereby maintaining cellular homeostasis [37, 38]. However, aberrant activation of mTORC1 in cancer often leads to metabolic reprogramming and dysregulated proliferative signaling, driving tumorigenesis and progression [39, 40]. In breast and liver cancers, hyperactivation of mTORC1 enhances ribosome biogenesis and suppresses autophagy, promoting tumor cell survival under nutrient-deprived conditions [11, 41]. In renal cell carcinoma, loss of VHL results in HIF-1α accumulation, which synergizes with mTORC1 to enhance tumor angiogenesis and invasive potential [42, 43]. Although mTORC1 inhibitors, such as Everolimus, have been sanctioned for advanced renal cancer and neuroendocrine tumors, the limited efficacy of monotherapy underscores the complexity of pathway regulation [44, 45]. Firstly, feedback regulatory networks of mTORC1 often contribute to tumor cell resistance. Second, mTORC1’s ability to remodel the tumor microenvironment (e.g. promoting angiogenesis via VEGF and inducing immune evasion through PD-L1 regulation) may counteract the direct therapeutic effects of targeted treatments [12, 46, 47]. This study, through bioinformatics analysis, proposes for the first time that POLR3G may serve as a key mediator of mTORC1 in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment, providing a theoretical foundation for combined targeting of mTORC1/POLR3G and immune checkpoint blockade strategies. we initially downloaded LIHC-related data and clinicopathological characteristics from the TCGA database. We performed univariate COX analysis and LASSO regression analysis on mTORC1 signaling pathway-associated genes in LIHC, establishing a prognostic model comprising 13 genes (EEF1E1, ENO1, G6PD, HSPD1, LDHA, PLK1, PLOD2, POLR3G, SLC1A5, SLC2A1, SLC7A11, TPI1, TUBG1). The model demonstrated that LIHC patients classified in the low-risk cohort exhibited significantly improved overall survival rates relative to those in the high-risk cohort. Subsequently, we employed various bioinformatics methods to analyze CNV, SNV, methylation differences, and drug sensitivity of the 13 genes across cancer types. Finally, we examined the infiltration levels of immune cells associated with these 13 genes in LIHC. Through these bioinformatics analyses, combined with a review of relevant literature, we identified POLR3G as a rarely reported gene in hepatocellular carcinoma with potential biological functions, thus selecting it as our research focus.

RNA polymerase III primarily exists in the forms of POLR3G and POLR3GL, with POLR3G predominantly present in undifferentiated cells [48–50]. POLR3G is classified as a "housekeeping gene" due to its central role in the synthesis of tRNA, 5S rRNA, and 7SL RNA [14, 51]. The POLR3G promoter, like all other promoters of genes encoding Pol III, binds the transcription factor MYC, enabling cells to acquire diverse regulatory mechanisms [52]. As a key molecule in the mTORC1 signaling pathway, POLR3G plays an important role in promoting tumor initiation and progression. In prostate carcinoma, low expression of POLR3G controls the proliferation and metastasis of cancer cells [53]. In lung cancer, microRNA-26a-5p regulates POLR3G expression, influencing lung cancer stem cells and chemosensitivity. In bladder cancer, POLR3G promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [54]. Nevertheless, the biological roles of POLR3G in HCC remain to be elucidated. In our study, we initially evaluated POLR3G expression profiles in HCC and adjacent normal tissues utilizing datasets from the TCGA and GTEx repositories, revealing significantly higher expression of POLR3G in hepatocellular carcinoma compared to normal tissues. Elevated POLR3G expression was associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients. Additionally, we found that POLR3G expression levels correlated with various clinicopathological features, including tumor grade, gender, and age. Higher tumor grades and stages were associated with increased POLR3G expression. To investigate the underlying biological functions of POLR3G in hepatocellular carcinoma, GO analysis indicated that POLR3G is mainly localized in the RNA polymerase complex and is involved in RNA polymerase III-mediated transcription. KEGG pathway analysis indicated a potential involvement of POLR3G in the RNA polymerase and cytosolic DNA-sensing pathways. GSEA analysis revealed that POLR3G might participate in cellular biological processes through fatty acid metabolism and oxidative phosphorylation pathways. Finally, CCK-8 and EDU cell proliferation experiments demonstrated that knockdown of POLR3G significantly controlled the proliferative capacity of HCC cells.

The tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a critical role in tumorigenesis and progression, encompassing a heterogeneous population of immune cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), endothelial cells (ECs), and additional stromal elements [55]. The distribution of cells within the TME varies according to different clinicopathological features and significantly influences patient treatment outcomes and prognosis [56]. Our investigation examined the correlation between POLR3G expression and the infiltration levels of 24 distinct immune cell populations within tumor tissues, including Th2 cells, Tcm, T helper cells, Adc, Macrophages, Tgd, Treg, Neutrophils, Tem, IDC, Th1 cells, T cells, DC, Cytotoxic cells, NK CD56bright cells, Mast cells, NK CD56dim cells, TFH, B cells, Th17 cells, Eosinophils, CD8 T cells, NK cells, and pDC. The results revealed that POLR3G expression levels were positively correlated with Th2 cells, Tcm, and T helper cells, while negatively correlated with pDC, NK cells, and CD8 T cells. In hepatocellular carcinoma, elevated levels of Th2 cells and T helper cells are associated with poor patient prognosis [57, 58]. As indicated by our findings, we hypothesize that POLR3G may involve in tumor immune processes through direct or indirect mechanisms, and targeting POLR3G could represent a promising strategy to enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma patients.

This study establishes the clinical significance of POLR3G as an independent prognostic factor in hepatocellular carcinoma and explores its potential association with the tumor microenvironment. These results not only provide a novel molecular marker for HCC prognosis but also lay a theoretical foundation for developing combined therapeutic strategies targeting the mTORC1/POLR3G axis and the immune microenvironment. However, the study has limitations: although we investigated the prognostic value of POLR3G using public databases, the inherent biases of retrospective analysis necessitate validation through prospective clinical cohorts. Additionally, the causal correlation between POLR3G and the mTORC1 pathway, as well as its specific regulatory mechanisms on immune cell function, remain incompletely elucidated and require further experimental validation.

Conclusions

In the present investigation, a predictive framework was developed to assess clinical outcomes in hepatocellular carcinoma cases, with POLR3G emerging as a key molecular target for subsequent analysis. The current investigation systematically evaluated the correlation of POLR3G transcriptional activity with clinical outcomes in HCC patients, while concurrently examining its potential regulatory role in tumor microenvironment immunomodulation. These results suggest that POLR3G may serve as a prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma, providing a theoretical foundation for clinicians to develop personalized treatment strategies.

Digital transformation and enterprise violation risk: A ‘motivation-opportunity-attitude’ framework
Authors: Xinlan Wang, Nan Wang, Weizhang Sun, Andi Xu, Zhiguo Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the evolving digital economy, digital transformation has emerged as an important strategic choice for enterprises. Their comprehensive and innovative characteristics have instigated a paradigm shift in enterprise governance ideologies and models. However, this transformation has also introduced heightened demands and challenges to enterprise governance mechanisms during the transitional phase. This study examined the influence of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk using data from Chinese A-share listed companies from 2017 to 2021. The analysis revealed an inverted U-shaped relationship between the degree of digital transformation and enterprise violation risk. This relationship was underpinned by three fundamental dimensions: motivation, opportunity, and management attitude. These dimensions were elucidated through mechanisms involving risk perception, management agency costs, and management responsibilities. These conclusions were reached even after rigorous tests for endogeneity and stability. Furthermore, this study probed specific scenarios in which digital transformation influences enterprise violation risk. This finding revealed that internal control mechanisms and external analysts’ attention moderate this relationship. As the effectiveness of internal controls and the attention of external analysts increase, the inflection point of the inverted U-shaped curve shifts to the left, and the curve progressively smoothens. Thus, greater internal control effectiveness and the governance impact of external analysts can positively affect the correlation between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk, thereby facilitating digital resources to empower enterprise governance. This study contributes to the theoretical discourse on the implications of digital transformation for enterprise governance, particularly in the context of evolving market dynamics. It offers valuable insights and recommendations for effectively driving digital transformation processes.Keywords: Digital transformation, Enterprise governance, Enterprise violation risk, Fraud triangle theory

Introduction

With continued breakthroughs in digital technology applications for knowledge discovery and value mining, the digital economy has emerged as a critical means for nations to attain high-quality economic development and assert their influence on the global stage. In 2015, China introduced the ‘Internet Plus’ action plan during the 12th National People's Congress. Later, the 19th National Congress strongly emphasised the need to promote the deep integration of the Internet, big data, artificial intelligence, and the real economy. A significant milestone occurred in September 2016 when the G20 Summit in Hangzhou released the world's inaugural policy document on the digital economy, titled ‘G20 Digital Economy Development and Cooperation Initiative’. This supports the concept of the digital economy as an essential pathway for advancing high-quality economic development and garnering international consensus [1]. According to the ‘Research Report on the Development of China's Digital Economy’ report, the total scale of China's digital economy reached 50.2 trillion yuan by 2022, constituting 41.5 % of the country's GDP. This underscores the pivotal role of the digital economy in bolstering China's economic growth. In the context of economic dynamics and policy orientation, digital technology has become the driving force behind today's real economy and constitutes a key strategic path for businesses in their digital ecosystem asset allocation. Therefore, digital transformation is inevitable for enterprises. Enterprise digital transformation is a comprehensive and strategic transformation that encompasses revolutionary changes in various dimensions spanning organisational structures, management frameworks, human resources, and the reconfiguration of enterprise ecosystems. This transformation process also includes internal structural reorganisation and external adjustments in the interest of multiple stakeholders, thereby assuming a pivotal role in influencing enterprise governance within the framework of the new economic landscape [2,3].

Enterprise violations are a typical manifestation of enterprise governance failure, and the effective control of enterprise violation risks is a prerequisite for the long-term development of enterprises and the key to the sustainable development of the market economy. Particularly, as public and listed enterprises have a broader influence, normative operations have a more significant exemplary effect, and the market destruction caused by violations is more pronounced. With the development of enterprise reforms, China's research in the field of enterprise governance is becoming more in-depth, and the governance mechanisms for regulating the operations of listed enterprises are becoming increasingly perfect. However, existing governance mechanisms have gradually become unable to meet the higher enterprise governance requirements under the characteristics of a digital economy. Particularly, as China enters the superimposed state of the economic growth gear shift, economic structure adjustment, and digestion period of the early stimulus policy, the normal state of the market economy changes and the market environment shows new characteristics, such as variability and uncertainty. The phenomenon of enterprise moral decline has intensified, violations occur frequently, and the punishment of listed enterprises in the computer, communication, and other electronic equipment manufacturing industries for violations is particularly severe. Investors' interests are seriously damaged and the market encounters a trust crisis that hinders the sustainable and healthy development of China's capital markets. In developed capital markets, sound laws and regulations, and investor protection systems can better protect external investors' interests. However, in a transformation market, relying solely on the power of the judiciary and supervision cannot directly guide enterprises to prevent violations and protect investors' interests. Against this background, focusing on whether the emerging characteristics of enterprises' digital transformation can help improve the quality of enterprise governance to suppress enterprise violation risks and optimise the protection of external investors has become a particularly important issue.

Given the universality and potential social significance of this issue, scholars have actively explored the effects of digital transformation on governance. Existing literature on the impact of digital transformation on enterprise governance is divided into two main aspects. Some scholars have focused on the results of the transformation, believing that enterprise digitalisation can enhance the effectiveness of enterprise governance by alleviating information asymmetry and suppressing the irrational behaviour of management [4]. Its governance effect is reflected in the ability of digital transformation to improve the comparability of enterprise accounting information, reduce the probability of financial restatement, and prevent violations of information disclosure [5]. However, some scholars have focused on the transformation process, believing that digital transformation requires enterprises to break through organisational inertia and build new inertia to adapt to a new environment [6]. In this process, a lag exists between the level of institutional design of enterprise organisational management and the advanced technology of digital transformation [7]. When big data is not adapted to the key structure of the organisation, digital technology makes it difficult to create value, and even induces the ‘digital transformation paradox’, leading to a more severe temporary challenge to enterprise governance [8]. For example, digital transformation may trigger a crisis in net asset value relevance [9], and pose a challenge for enterprise cash management [10]. These studies have laid the theoretical foundation for a better understanding of the consequences of enterprise digital transformation and improvements in the effectiveness of enterprise governance. However, the impact of digital transformation on enterprise governance remains controversial, and only a few scholars' research involves the field of violations. In-depth research into the typical characteristics of enterprise violation risk governance is particularly lacking, and further exploration is needed to gain more empirical evidence.

Based on the practical background and literature foundation of the governance effect of digital transformation, this study starts from the perspective of external investor protection and further explores enterprise violations which is the typical manifestation of enterprise governance in the new digital economy. It places digital transformation and enterprise violation risk in the same research framework and raises the following research questions: Is there a relationship between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk, and, if so, what kind of relationship is it? How does digital transformation affect enterprise violation risk? Does the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk have obvious boundary conditions?

China is currently in a critical window for transformation from an Internet economy to a digital economy. This study provides a theoretical reference for enterprise violation risk management and has significant strategic value for listed enterprises for improving the design of internal control systems, organisational structure systems, and external governance effects during the digital transformation process. This study extends existing literature in several ways.

First, it advances the exploration of the consequences of an enterprise's digital transformation. The control of violation risks is not only related to enterprise economic activities, but also represents the enterprise's responsibility to stakeholders and the assumption of social responsibility. Starting from the perspective of external investor protection and taking enterprise violation risk as the entry point, this study comprehensively analyses and verifies the impact of internal and external environmental changes at different stages of the digital transformation process on enterprise governance. The research content involves not only economic factors, such as enterprise risk and agency costs, but also analyses non-economic factors, such as enterprise responsibility values, making an important academic contribution to the construction of a comprehensive value research framework for enterprise digital transformation.

Second, it enriches the study of the mechanism of digital transformation's impact on enterprise violation risks. As a key behaviour in enterprise governance, enterprise violations are closely related to multiple real factors and the evolution of various practical phenomena. In the digital economy era, the digital transformation of enterprises has profoundly changed their development environment and interaction methods, promoting new characteristics in enterprise governance thinking, paradigms, and subjects, which will inevitably significantly impact the governance of enterprise violation risks. However, empirical research on the influencing factors of enterprise violation risk management and their effectiveness under the new characteristics of digital transformation is still lacking. Therefore, this study is conducive to deepening theoretical research on enterprise violation risk management in the new digital economy.

Third, this study comprehensively analyses the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risks from multiple dimensions and perspectives, such as violation motivation, violation opportunities, and excuses for violations (attitudes). It also focuses on differences in the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risks under specific situations involving different internal control quality conditions and external analyst attention. The research not only deepens related research on enterprise governance factors, such as operational risk, strategic management, enterprise values, and stakeholders, but also enriches the practical application of related theories.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section 2 introduces the literature review and theoretical hypotheses. Section 3 describes the empirical research design, including the sample selection, variable definitions, and model setting. Section 4 reports the empirical results, mainly involving baseline regression and mechanism analysis. Section 5 presents an extended in-depth analysis and verification of the interaction scenarios between the two. Finally, section 6 concludes the paper with suggestions and limitations.

Literature review and research hypotheses

Literature review

The economic implications of enterprise digital transformation

Digital transformation relies on digital infrastructure, encompassing digital technology, products, and platforms, and triggers multifaceted changes, including shifts in individual skills, organisational management approaches, industrial business models, and value chains [11]. Its overarching objective is to enhance the user experience, streamline operational procedures, innovate business models, and create new value propositions for users, thereby bolstering enterprise value [12].

Research indicates that digital transformation can exert both positive and negative influences on organisations. On the one hand, it can propel enterprises to elevate their production and organisational efficiencies, thereby augmenting their enterprise value. Concerning productivity, digital transformation can inject agility and innovation into operational processes, leading to cost reduction, enhanced operational efficiency, and a competitive edge [1,13]. In terms of organisational efficiency, digital transformation leads to a profound overhaul of organisational paradigms and management structures [14]. Digital technologies provide enterprises with scientifically informed decision-making support, diminish irrational behaviours at the managerial level, effectively lower agency costs, and significantly enhance information transparency. This, in turn, forges stronger, mutually beneficial connections between enterprises and stakeholders, thereby improving enterprise governance efficiency [4,15,16]. On the other hand, according to the theory of change, organisations achieve new strategic goals through change, and mistakes made at any stage of this process can slow down the process and damage existing achievements. Compared to traditional organisational change, digital transformation is more disruptive and innovative and faces unprecedented resistance to change, which also increases the potential risks for businesses. Given its enduring and uncertain nature, digital transformation is associated with hidden costs that can offset enterprise earnings, making it susceptible to the ‘digital paradox’ [17]. Furthermore, the changes in the digital environment, digital resource acquisition, and organisational structural transformations that accompany digital transformation introduce various strategic, operational, and financial risks to enterprises [3]. Finally, the absence of responsible digital technologies has led to issues related to algorithmic biases, collusion, and monopolies, presenting new challenges for the effectiveness of enterprise governance during the transformation process [18].

Factors determining enterprise violation risk

Enterprise violation risk refers to the likelihood that a company violates relevant laws and regulations during its operations, leading to public sanctions by regulatory bodies, such as the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) and Ministry of Finance [19]. Violations include actions to gain illegitimate benefits by breaching contractual agreements with stakeholders. Its essence reflects opportunistic actions by management which not only harm the company's reputation and reduce its value but also have a ‘contagion effect’ on the capital market, severely threatening investor interests and confidence [[20], [21], [22]].

The Fraud Triangle Theory is the most complete theoretical model of the earliest research on the causes of fraud. Compared to subsequent theories, such as the Iceberg Theory, Fraud Risk Factor Theory, and GONE Theory, it has the longest practical application time and is more mature. Additionally, since the Iceberg Theory mainly considers the impact of management's behavioural activities on financial fraud, the Risk Factor Theory mainly involves moral character, motives, and the likelihood of exposure, as well as the nature and degree of punishment after exposure. Its basic connotation corresponds to the three factors in the Fraud Triangle Theory, which can be analysed and discussed from the perspectives of pressure, opportunity, and rationalisation. The four elements of the GONE Theory — Greed (G), Opportunity (O), Need (N), and Exposure (E) — are similar to those of the Fraud Triangle Theory. Therefore, this study uses the Fraud Triangle Theory as the foundational theory for the mechanism analysis.

Grounded in the classic fraud triangle theory, enterprise violation behaviour is determined by three key aspects: ‘motive-opportunity-management attitude”. First, the motive for violation is a critical factor that drives such behaviour. Research has primarily emphasised the pressure mechanisms for this motive, demonstrating that enterprises are more likely to engage in non-compliant operations when they face financial, profit retention, and delisting pressures [23], as well as when they confront increased market competition and pressure to outperform competitors [24]. Second, opportunity for violation refers to a company's ability to undertake violation actions without being detected or with minimal chances of being discovered. Higher organisational complexity and greater dispersion of power within a company increase management difficulties and provide direct opportunities for violation behaviours [25]. Finally, management attitude, which involves rationalising violation behaviour, is primarily related to the moral standards and cognitive structures of those involved in the violation. Scholars generally agree that violation behaviour is more likely to occur when management is overconfident and exhibits high-risk preferences.

How can we then prevent enterprise violation behaviours and thus reduce violation risk? The existing literature explores factors related to both internal and external governance. Among internal governance mechanisms, executive compensation incentives, professional independent directors, independent director reputations, internal audit capabilities, board election voting rates, and the economic independence of the supervisory board all inhibit enterprise violation risk [22,26,27]. However, independent directors with political appointments and nepotistic relationships can increase the likelihood of enterprise violation and exacerbate violation risk [28,29]. From an equity balance perspective, institutional investor holding and employee stock ownership plans can significantly strengthen internal monitoring within enterprises, suppressing violation tendencies and reducing violation risk [30,31]. Finally, concerning the impact of external governance, the inspection system, government attention, media attention, analyst attention, high-quality external audits, and regulatory interviews conducted, such as those by the China Securities Association, play a role in inhibiting enterprise violation tendencies, and subsequently, reducing violation risk [[32], [33], [34], [35]].

Literature summary

In summary, research on the economic outcomes of enterprises’ digital transformation has paid relatively little attention to digital transformation risks, particularly concerning external investor protection. Digital transformation across Chinese enterprises remains at the nascent stages. Factors such as scarcity of digital talent, underdeveloped digital industry foundations, and incomplete data governance frameworks contribute to substantial trial-and-error risks in the digital transformation process [36]. The identification and management of risks emerging from digital transformation practices are paramount concerns that cannot be underestimated as China strives for high-quality digital transformation. Consequently, these issues warrant the attention of researchers.

Furthermore, extant research on the determinants of enterprise violation risk primarily focuses on either micro-level internal governance within enterprises or macro-level external governance environments. However, the escalation of enterprise violation risks results from the combined effects of internal and external environmental factors. This becomes even more salient when enterprises operating in the digital economy must contend with ongoing transformations and reconfigurations in both their internal operations and the external environment. However, few studies have delved into the manifestations of enterprise violation risks under the new characteristics of digital transformation, particularly through a comprehensive analysis of both internal and external pathways. Starting from the perspective of safeguarding investor rights, this study aims to elucidate the relationship between digital transformation and enterprise violation risks. We not only address existing lacunae in the literature, but also contribute valuable empirical insights to guide digital transformation and enterprise governance practices in China. Theoretical analysis and research hypotheses

According to modern enterprise governance theory, the quality of enterprise governance is influenced not only by the internal distribution of interests within the company but also by the interplay between the external market, social forces, and internal governance mechanisms. This implies that as an integral part of enterprise governance, enterprise violation risks are often subject to the combined effects of both internal and external governance environments. However, enterprise violations result from the combined influences of motivation, opportunity, and management attitudes. It is often shaped by the interplay between internal and external governance environments. Digital resources exhibit two distinct features: ‘digital empowerment’ and ‘digital paradox’. In other words, digital transformation may have a dual impact on enterprise violation risk, and determining the dominant effect is closely related to the digital transformation stage. Considering this, this research employs the three dimensions of ‘motivation-opportunity-management attitudes’ as our theoretical framework. By examining the potential dual effects of the characteristics of the different stages of digital transformation on internal and external governance environments, we explore the impact of the overall digital transformation process on enterprise violation risk.

Enterprise violation motivation

During the initial stages of implementing a digital transformation strategy in an enterprise, substantial fluctuations occur in various aspects such as finance, operations, and strategic resources. This intensifies the management team's risk perception, which enhances the motivation for enterprise violations. From an internal perspective, there are several contributing factors. First, the execution of a digital transformation strategy necessitates significant investments in the digital technology infrastructure, resulting in increased cash flow risk for the company [3]. Simultaneously, this may reduce a company's current accounting profits. This creates substantial short-term operational pressures, which further accentuate the motivation for violations. Second, the organisational structure established through new technologies may disrupt and disconnect existing risk management and internal control mechanisms. This exposes the company to significant risks related to strategic, capability, and resource variations as it breaks away from organisational inertia [2].

From an external environmental perspective, analogous dynamics are at play. Initially, the emergence of new business models due to digital transformation generates vast amounts of data and diverse data sources. This may cause a mismatch between the company's digital capabilities and its new environment. This can potentially cause deceptive digital analytics and increase uncertainty in a company's response to new products and markets [37]. Furthermore, during a company's digital transformation journey, the boundaries between its operational activities and organisational structures become increasingly blurred, creating significant dependence on the coordination of technological capabilities from other enterprises within the digital ecosystem. This dependence results in a more pronounced constraint effect from other participants [38], thereby increasing uncertainty about the company's survival. Faced with significant strategic risks and uncertainty during digital transformation, the management team or controllers may perceive an elevated risk. If they choose to disclose their company's situation promptly and honestly, they may incur various risks, including a decrease in personal compensation, damage to their reputation, and conflicts with investors [39]. Then, out of self-interest, the management is more likely to be motivated to take shortcuts to maintain the appearance of rapid enterprise growth, increasing the probability of making violation decisions, and consequently intensifying enterprise violation risk.

As digital transformation progresses to a more mature stage, the effectiveness of digitally driven capabilities increases. The company's profitability improves, uncertainty decreases, and the management team's risk perception diminishes, leading to a reduction in violation motivation. Several notable transformations have occurred from an internal environment perspective. First, digitalisation enables a company to maximise its capital utilisation efficiency under limited financial resource constraints, thereby reducing financially inefficient operations and enhancing financial stability [40]. Second, as a company's digital capabilities mature, it can effectively utilise its data collection and analysis capabilities to identify suitable production paths based on operational conditions. This, in turn, improves the operational efficiency and stability, thereby reducing performance fluctuations [41]. Finally, digitalisation allows a company to achieve specialisation, effectively increasing its overall productivity, and leading to enhanced profitability [42].

From an external environment perspective, digitalisation supports and accelerates the shift in enterprise relationships from competition to mutual symbiosis, thereby promoting collaborative innovation [43,44]. This shift enhances a company's dynamic adjustment, value creation, and customer retention capabilities, ultimately strengthening its survival ability [45]. The increased profitability and stability driven by digitalisation reduce the management team's risk perception, thereby diminishing violation motivations and controlling violation risks.

Enterprise violation opportunities

The implementation of enterprises’ digital transformation strategies gives rise to innovative business models that enhance operational and business management systems with creative and differentiating features [38]. Consequently, rapidly advancing digital transformation in enterprises often leads to higher transaction complexity and management intricacy [2].

In the early stages of implementing digital transformation strategies, increased transaction complexity means that the existing accounting standards are unable to adequately match novel digital features. This gap prevents businesses from meeting the requirements for business measurement and recognition, ultimately augmenting the discretionary power of internal financial personnel and management [46]. Furthermore, during these initial stages, most enterprises focus primarily on expansion, and the effectiveness of enterprise governance mechanisms within new economy enterprises remains relatively low [18]. This creates opportunities for opportunistic behaviour among management in the pursuit of personal gain. In an external governance environment, the challenge of managing complex business models exacerbates the information gap among company founders, management, and external investors. While this information gap conceals internal transactions, it also facilitates the enterprise digital transformation hype and manipulation of textual information disclosures within the context of digital transformation. These factors contribute to the escalation of potential violation opportunities, thereby increasing enterprise violation risks [18,47].

As digital transformation progresses, businesses gradually achieve digital management, leading to improved information transparency. This enables supervisory mechanisms to operate effectively, reduce potential enterprise violation opportunities, and effectively manage enterprise violation risks. From an internal perspective, digitalisation helps curtail the abuse of power by enterprise management, effectively mitigating enterprise violation risk. First, big data characteristics substantially reduce information processing, information transmission, and organisational coordination costs. This encourages the formation of decentralized, intermediary-free network organisations, which disrupt traditional power structures and reinforce grassroots empowerment [2,48,49]. This, in turn, enhances the grassroots employees’ willingness and capacity to oversee operations. Second, the use of digital technology to develop management information and knowledge systems enables enterprises to collect more comprehensive and authentic data on their daily operations, finances, and internal controls. This, in turn, lowers the cost of shareholder supervision of managers and reduces agency costs between management and employees, thereby improving the efficiency of internal governance systems. The overall effect is a reduction in the potential opportunities for enterprise violation opportunities [16].

From an external perspective, digitalisation elevates information transparency and reinforces external governance effects, which reduce enterprise violation risks to some extent. Specifically, digitisation implies a comprehensive data overhaul within enterprises. The efficient connectivity offered by the Internet facilitates rapid information transmission and effective communication between internal and external sources of information. This helps external stakeholders gain a deeper understanding of a company's operations [50]. Consequently, this enhances their supervisory role, thereby lowering enterprise violation risks. Furthermore, in the context of the new digital economy, enterprises are aligned with China's policy guidelines and economic orientation, making them more appealing in the capital market and attracting the attention of analysts and media [1]. To some extent, this serves as a hindrance to enterprise violation opportunities.

Managerial attitude

During the initial stages of implementing a digital transformation strategy, enterprises focus primarily on expansion, which exposes them to significant pressure to exceed their performance targets and face intense competition. This gives rise to two notable factors. First, when enterprises concentrate on researching and developing new products and technologies and exploring new markets, they tend to favour more aggressive strategies. This results in a stronger risk appetite within internal management but is often accompanied by a relative weakening of their sense of responsibility. Consequently, this environment is conducive to identifying violation justifications [51]. Second, digital transformation increases the external scrutiny of enterprises, leading to heightened risk aversion among organisational members, compromised role identification, and strained role relationships. This unfavourable cycle can diminish the sense of responsibility within a company [52]. Investors typically have optimistic expectations regarding digital transformation enterprises in the capital market [1]. To align with capital markets and maintain market valuations, the declining sense of responsibility may intensify the development of unethical competitive mindsets, such as ‘deception in business is just a strategy’ and ‘fraud is a rule in the market’. This moral risk of violation is concealed, increasing the self-rationalisation tendency of violation, thereby exacerbating enterprise violation risks [53].

As the level of digital transformation within enterprises increases, so does the socialisation of enterprise financing. Enterprise control rights gradually shift towards management teams with a long-term value-oriented sense of responsibility and strong mission [18]. A strong aversion to enterprise violation is characteristic of a sustainable entrepreneurial spirit, which tends to weaken the self-rationalisation tendencies for such violations. Simultaneously, the application of digital technology and changes in information structures result in external market structures characterised by cooperation, sharing, and an ecosystem that promotes resource sharing, value co-creation, and altruism within enterprises [54]. This strengthens the sense of responsibility within enterprises, increases the negative perceptions of management towards violations, and reduces enterprise violation risks.

Therefore, this study posits Hypothesis 1.

The level of digital transformation has an inverted U-shaped relationship with enterprise violation risk.

Variables

Dependent variable

Enterprise violation risk (Freq). We focus on enterprises that have faced public penalties from regulatory bodies such as the CSRC and Ministry of Finance, as enterprises with a history of violations are often considered to be at higher risk of committing violations again [55]. Thus, a greater number of violation incidents is associated with a higher level of enterprise violation risk. Therefore, this study employs the frequency of enterprise violations (Freq) as a proxy measure for enterprise violation risk. Following Jiang et al. (2022) [21], the procedure for defining this variable is as follows. Based on the content disclosed in the violation announcements, the year in which the violation occurred is defined as the violation year. The number of violation incidents is then aggregated based on the company code and actual year of violation. Finally, the natural logarithm of this count plus 1 is used as a measure of enterprise violation risk. A higher numerical value indicates a higher enterprise violation risk. Explanatory variables

Digital Transformation of Enterprises (DDT). Most studies have defined digital transformation using textual analysis and digital assets. Given that digital information disclosure often exhibits strategic behaviour with the potential for information manipulation [56] and that information manipulation itself is considered a form of violation, using enterprise violation risk can result in significant endogeneity concerns. This endogeneity issue can substantially affect the empirical findings. To address this issue, we draw on the methodology proposed by Tao et al. (2023) [57]. Specifically, we measured a company's level of digital transformation by determining the proportion of year-end intangible assets related to digital transformation among the detailed items of intangible assets disclosed in the financial reports of publicly listed enterprises. We identify ‘intangible assets of digital technology’ through a keyword search, by including terms that are associated with digital transformation, such as ‘software’, ‘network’, ‘client’, ‘management system’, ‘smart platform’, ‘data’, ‘numerical control’, ‘informatization’, ‘networking’, ‘internet’, ‘artificial intelligence’, and ‘automatic control’. Our chosen measure of the digital transformation level pertains to cumulative investment in digital transformation. This choice is rationalised by the fact that, compared with the annual investment in digital transformation, the digital transformation-related assets held by a company in the current year (as opposed to incremental additions) represent a more reasonable reflection of the total quantity and value of the company's data resources for that year.

Control variables

Following prior research such as Zhou et al. (2022) [22] and Zhang et al. (2022) [31], this study uses two categories of control variables. The first category included company-specific variables: number of employees (StaffNumber), growth (Growth), capital structure (Lev), profitability (Roa), and enterprise value (TBQ). The second category comprises enterprise governance variables: dual leadership roles (Dual), ownership structure (Soe), compensation of the top three executives (Top3), board size (BoardSize), management ownership (MngHld), and institutional investor ownership (In-share). Second, by observing the level of digital transformation before the announcement and punishment of relevant enterprise violations, it is possible to measure the relationship between digital transformation and the firm's future violations, which can enhance the model's predictive power and better fit the research theme of violation risk. In other words, lagging the core explanatory variable by one period could improve the rationality of the verification model. According to the statistical test principle of nonlinear relationships, if the coefficient of the quadratic term in the model is significantly negative, the independent and dependent variables have an ‘inverted U’ relationship. That is, if the coefficient of α2 is significantly negative, then the core research hypothesis H1 of this paper is established.

The mechanisms impacting enterprise violation risk due to digital transformation

Based on our preceding theoretical analysis, this study posits that digital transformation exerts a threefold effect on enterprise violation risk, underpinned by the dimensions of the fraud triangle. This section empirically validates the pathways through which digital transformation influences enterprise violation risk by focusing on motives, opportunities, and managerial attitudes.

Motivation for enterprise violation – enterprise risk perception

Building on the theoretical framework established earlier, this study asserts that fluctuations in financial stability, organisational coordination, and resource management during a company's digital transformation journey, stemming from internal and external factors, impact enterprise violation motives through enterprise risk perception. Management attitudes - the sense of management responsibility

The rationalisation of violations essentially reflects a cognitive style and behavioural code characterised by a high preference for risk and low sense of responsibility. Building on the concept of integrity, which encompasses trust, social norms, and high moral standards, this study conducts a situational test using enterprise integrity as a foundation. In simpler and more normal situations, integrity is more readily apparent, but becomes relatively challenging to maintain in complex and difficult contexts [60]. Thus, in enterprises with a high level of integrity, the impact of violations on early disruptions and later digital transformation governance is more pronounced. In other words, if the influence mechanism of management responsibility factors exists, the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk will be more significant in enterprises with high levels of integrity. The degree of enterprise integrity is adopted from Yan's study (2023) [61], which employed the management commitment to integrity as a measure. The management's commitment to integrity is a form of ethical masking, where such a commitment signifies a relatively lower level of enterprise integrity. The specific measurement of the management's commitment to integrity is as follows. If the annual report's Management Discussion and Analysis (MD&A) of a company section includes the words ‘integrity’ and related terms, it is considered an indication of the management's commitment to integrity. These terms include integrity, honesty, truth, sincerity, loyalty, ethics, trust, reliability, fairness, and justice.

The regression results are displayed in Panel C of Table 8. The first- and second-order regression coefficients of L.DDT are only significant in higher enterprise integrity groups. This suggests that the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk is more prominent in enterprises with higher levels of management integrity. This supports the existence of the management responsibility influence mechanism as a pathway of the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risks.

Endogeneity assessment

Digital transformation is a strategic choice made by enterprises to adapt to the digital economy's development, featuring a pronounced self-selection tendency. Moreover, the influence of digital transformation on a company's violation risk may stem from omitted variables or the influence of specific firm characteristics on the relationship between digital transformation and violation risk. Consequently, this study may be subject to several endogeneity issues, such as self-selection bias, spurious correlations arising from omitted variables, and sample selection bias.

To address the impact of self-selection bias, this study employs the Heckman two-stage model for the second round of testing. Specifically, we construct a first-stage predictive model to determine whether a firm is undergoing digital transformation. We apply a probit regression method to compute the inverse Mills ratio (IMR). Drawing inspiration from Wang et al. (2022) [63], we use the presence of digital feature words in the annual report as a proxy variable to represent whether a firm implements a digital transformation strategy in the current year. Subsequently, we chose the ratio of firms within the same industry that implemented a digital transformation strategy in the same year (PLP) as an exclusion constraint variable. The other control variables remain the same. PLP is calculated as the number of firms implementing a digital transformation strategy within a specific industry divided by the total number of firms in that industry. In the second stage, we include the IMR calculated in the first stage as a control variable.

Next, we consider the influence of omitted variables on the relationship between digital transformation and violation risk and obtain a more precise measurement of the net effect of digital transformation on violation risk. Specifically, this study employs the instrumental variable method for validation based on a two-stage least squares (2SLS) model. The instrumental variable used is the average level of digital transformation of other firms in the same industry within the province where the firm is located.

To mitigate potential endogeneity issues stemming from sample selection bias, this study employs the propensity score matching method. Specifically, we draw on the approach of Hu et al. (2020) [64] and use the inflection point of the core explanatory variable in the regression sample as a criterion to generate the treatment and control groups. We select the control variables used earlier as covariates and perform 1:1 nearest neighbour matching without replacement using a calipre of 0.05. After matching, the average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) passes the test and demonstrates excellent matching effects. We use a post-matching sample for re-examination.

Table 10 presents the results. The regression results align with previous findings, demonstrating that after addressing endogeneity issues, the ‘inverted U’ relationship between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk remains robust.

Further analyses

The impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk is contingent upon the surrounding environment and is inevitably shaped by both internal and external factors. Considering the specific internal and external contexts of enterprises, we find that the quality of internal controls and the effectiveness of external governance not only significantly influence a company's ability to adapt to digital transformation but also play a crucial role in influencing enterprise violation risk.

Internal contextual factors

Internal controls are implemented by the company's board of directors, supervisory board, management, and all employees. As a vital internal governance mechanism, it aims to effectively prevent, identify, and control risks. Its efficacy is crucial for advancing a company's digital development strategies and mitigating risks. On the one hand, high-quality internal controls can effectively reduce a company's information risk and contract execution costs and enhance accounting conservatism. This can mitigate a company's propensity for, and thus, the risk of, violations [65]. Furthermore, the risk of litigation or penalties stemming from violations is a significant focus in internal control assessments. Therefore, high-quality internal controls can promptly detect potential non-compliant activities and enable swift responses, effectively reducing the risk of violations. On the other hand, high-quality internal control is essential for enterprises to adapt rapidly to digital transformation and effectively mitigate the negative impacts of the transformation process. The digitalisation process involves product upgrades, iterative integration of old and new resources, the development of digital capabilities, and adaptive changes to the organizational structure, among other efforts. All of these require reliable and repeatable communication and coordination activities [66]. High-quality internal control is more likely to foster a culture of honesty and collaboration, facilitate better internal communication and cooperation, and lay a strong organizational foundation for advancing digitalisation. Furthermore, internal control, as a systematic and self-regulatory system for internal governance, can help enterprises realise their strategic goal [65]. This supports the effective identification and management of strategic risks during the digital transformation process. Therefore, this study posits that the quality of internal controls plays a significant moderating role in the impact of digital transformation on a company's violations risk. We examine this using group regression. Specifically, following the approach in Chen et al. (2018) [67], we measure the quality of internal controls using the internal control index for Chinese listed enterprises from the DIBO database and group them based on the median of the sample range.

As shown in columns (1) and (2) of Table 11, the absolute values of the regression coefficients for L.DDT and L.DDT2 are lower in the higher internal control quality group. The regression coefficient for L.DDT is significantly positive only in the lower internal control quality group. Thus, the ‘inverse U’ curve between digital transformation and violation risk is less pronounced in the higher internal control quality group. Moreover, compared with the lower internal control quality group, the inflection point of the ‘inverse U’ curve for violations risk and digital transformation notably shifts to the left when internal control quality is higher (0.3943 < 0.4900). Thus, the quality of internal controls is an important internal contextual factor that influences the impact of digital transformation on a company's violation risk.

External contextual factors

As digital transformation advances, enterprises are operating in a more intricate and open ecosystem. External stakeholder interactions pervade all aspects of a company, contrary to external governance factors, such as regulatory systems and external audits. Financial analysts are notable for their ability to uncover and disseminate valuable information about enterprises. They act as both information providers and external overseers in capital markets, and play a crucial role in influencing external investors' investment decisions [68]. Therefore, financial analysts’ attention is an essential aspect of external governance.

Drawing on the ‘information perspective’, financial analysts possess specialised knowledge and skills and heightened market sensitivity. They serve as intermediaries with the capability to collect, organise, and analyse information from public sources such as prospectuses and financial reports, thereby enhancing the transparency and efficiency of the capital market [69]. Furthermore, financial analysts are adept at acquiring proprietary company-specific information through interactions with management and on-site investigations [70]. Building on this foundation, financial analysts disseminate information through earnings forecasts, rating reports, and analyses. This, in turn, increases the informativeness and transparency of publicly traded enterprises, thereby effectively reducing opportunities and the propensity for enterprise violations. Simultaneously, from a ‘governance perspective’, financial analysts' attention on a company serves as an external mechanism for rectifying internal control deficiencies. This enables them to rapidly adapt to the accelerated horizontal and vertical expansion of a company's operations resulting from digitisation [71]. Financial analysts are better equipped to analyse the increasingly complex information associated with enterprise business, finance, and other facets, allowing them to identify enterprise violation risks more effectively. Furthermore, enterprises with substantial intangible assets are more likely to attract financial analysts' attention. Consequently, enterprises with a higher level of digitalisation are also more likely to receive increased attention from financial analysts, which may lead to more pronounced external governance effects. In summary, the attention from financial analysts, who are external stakeholders, serve as a potent external governance force. This maximises the realisation of information advantages during a company's digital transformation and effectively supervises negative actions throughout the process. Consequently, enterprises can successfully achieve high-quality digital transformation and reduce the risks of violations. Here, we consider external financial analysts' attention as a crucial boundary condition within the influence of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a scenario analysis using a grouping approach. Specifically, we follow Li et al. (2022) [68] and measure the number of analysts (or analyst teams) tracking a company within the past year. Subsequently, we divide the sample into groups based on the median level of financial analyst attention, thus verifying the boundary conditions of digital transformation's impact on enterprise violation risk.

The regression results in Columns (3) and (4) of Table 11 yield several noteworthy insights. First, a comparative analysis of the coefficients reveals that, in the high analyst attention group, the ‘inverse U’ relationship between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk exhibits a less pronounced curvature. Second, a comparative examination of the inflection points shows a leftward shift within the high analyst attention group (0.4129 < 0.4677). This indicates that analyst attention substantially moderates the influence of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk, and is thus a critical external factor during digital transformation.

Furthermore, we must consider the current state of digital transformation in Chinese enterprises, which is characterised by a relatively low overall level of and substantial risks associated with digital transformation. The success rate is only 20 %. Thus, challenges related to risk impacts and governance during the digital transformation process remain paramount for enterprises pursuing related strategies at the present stage of China's transformation. The attenuated ‘inverted U’ effect of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk suggests that its disruptive effect can be effectively mitigated. Furthermore, the leftward shift of the inflection point indicates that the governance effects of digital transformation come into play earlier, resulting in an enhanced positive impact of digital transformation on violation risk management. Practically, reinforcing the effectiveness of internal controls and increasing external analyst attention are crucial mechanisms for enterprises to facilitate the smooth implementation of digital transformation, effectively address governance challenges during this process, and expedite the realisation of governance efficiency in digital resource enterprises.

Conclusions and implications

Conclusions

As digital technology is deeply integrated into China's real economy, digital transformation is becoming a key force affecting the allocation of high-quality resource elements and competitive advantages for enterprises. Enterprises actively embrace digital technology and strengthen their digital capabilities, and the effective implementation of digital empowerment through a digital transformation strategy has become an inevitable path for sustainable development in the new economic situation. However, the practice of digital transformation still faces many challenges, and whether digital transformation can suppress enterprise violation risks and promote high-quality development of enterprises requires further verification. Therefore, this paper, by summarizing and sorting the related research of digital transformation and enterprise violation risk, uses the data of China's A-share listed enterprises from 2017 to 2021. Based on the ‘motive—opportunity—management attitude’ triple dimension, it systematically analyses the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk, the path of action, and the moderating effects of internal control quality and external analyst attention in the relationship chain between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk. Thus, the conclusions of this study are as follows:

(1)An ‘inverted U’ relationship exists between the level of digital transformation and enterprise violation risk. Specifically, digitalisation can empower enterprise governance and effectively suppress violation risks. However, in the early stages of digital transformation, with the impact of strategic transformation on the original technological innovation, organisational structure, and business model of enterprises, this will, to some extent, exacerbate enterprise violation risks, resulting in temporary unintended results before the digital factors affect the effectiveness of enterprise governance.

(2)Delving into the context of the relationship between the two, obvious boundary conditions are found for the relationship between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk, and internal control and external analyst attention will significantly adjust the impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risk. It is specifically manifested in reducing the amplitude of the ‘inverted U’ curve and shifting the inflection point of the curve to the left. Considering that the current level of enterprise digital transformation in China is still relatively low, high-quality internal controls and sufficient external analyst attention are effective methods to cope with the challenges of enterprise violation risk governance during the digital transformation process and promote the positive effect of digital resources.

This paper focuses on the micro-level of enterprises, taking the ‘motive—opportunity—management attitude’ as the underlying logic for the causes of violation behaviour, and analyses the relationship between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk and its context of action from both internal and external governance environments. The results provide theoretical supplementation and empirical support for the rationality of the Fraud Triangle and enterprise governance theories. Simultaneously, by leveraging the thinking of the Fraud Triangle Theory, the three factors causing violation affect enterprise behaviour through a progressive transmission logic. This paper adopts a decomposition method for analysis, independently assessing the violation risk at each dimension of ‘motive—opportunity—management attitude’. The results confirm that there are three pathways through which digital transformation affects enterprise violation risk, ensuring that the factors with less influence are not diluted. The empirical results expand the explanatory scope and practical application of the Fraud Triangle Theory.

Policy implications

This study focuses on a mechanism analysis and examines how digital transformation affects enterprise violation risks. The conclusions drawn from this research are beneficial for a deeper understanding of the impact of the new round of technological revolution on the micro-economy, and provide references for the smooth advancement of high-quality transformation and upgrading of enterprises, as well as for scientific governance. Based on the aforementioned research findings, this study offers the following insights for effectively promoting digital transformation, addressing the risks of enterprise violations during the digital transformation process, and facilitating the full realisation of the governance efficacy of digital transformation.

Dual-layer protection to advance digital transformation and suppress enterprise violation motives

Digital transformation is a strategic choice characterised by high risk. During the digital transformation process, enterprises often face increased cash flow volatility, heightened organisational instability, and reduced accuracy in grasping and predicting new products and markets at the strategic, operational, and financial levels. Controlling risk points is key to ensuring a smooth transition for enterprises and is also a crucial means of preventing the negative impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risks during the initial stages.

At the enterprise level, enterprises must actively embrace digital transformation, enhance their proactivity during the transformation process, position the stages of digital transformation accurately, effectively control transformation risks, and increase the speed of transformation. Digital thinking is the precursor to digital transformation. During the gestation phase of digital transformation, the decision-making layer of enterprises should cultivate good business acumen and innovative awareness, enhance digital leadership, seize opportunities for digital change, and prepare for transformation. Thus, enterprises must make strategic plans for digital transformation. Digital transformation is a comprehensive remaking of the enterprise and a major project of systematic technological innovation in modern enterprises. When the digital transformation strategy does not match an enterprise's digital capabilities, it causes a disconnection between strategy formulation and implementation, and the enterprise will be unable to derive value from digital transformation. Enterprises must strengthen top-level design and institutional arrangements; formulate digital strategies that are compatible with their digital capabilities; establish strategic goals, steps, and tasks for digital development; and clarify the reasonable digital transformation path of the enterprise. However, in the initial stages of digital transformation, it is difficult for enterprises to break through the knowledge and business capabilities based on traditional operation models. Enterprises can develop digital businesses based on existing business processes, including exploring digital marketing, digital manufacturing, digital procurement, and financial digitalisation. On this basis, digital technology should be used to increase customer perceived value, such as making full use of digital social tools to establish real-time interaction with customers and trying to drive the improvement of digital literacy and creativity within enterprises with limited digital technology.

When enterprises have a mature perception of the digital environment, the practice of digital transformation gradually becomes active and standardised. Enterprises formally enter the implementation of the digital transformation strategic plan, start to increase investment in digital infrastructure, carry out digital transformation of production and infrastructure methods, conduct digital transformation of business processes and management methods, and accelerate the creation of innovative business models bred by the new round of digital opportunities. The management should be aware that the resources invested in the early stage may not yield significant returns; pay attention to the impact of digital transformation on the enterprise; choose differentiated management paradigms based on the enterprise's own risk-bearing capacity, profitability, and value-creation ability; break through organizational inertia; and reduce strategic, operational, and financial risks in the process of digital transformation. Simultaneously, further improving the internal control system will provide a solid internal governance mechanism for digital transformation and help enterprises achieve deep adaptation of digital technology and products, resources, capabilities, organisation, and other aspects of iterative transformation so that the positive effects of enterprise governance in digital transformation can be fully exerted, enhancing the enterprise's differentiated competitiveness and response to environmental uncertainty, which will help reduce the negative impact of digital transformation on enterprise violation risks in the early stage.

At the government level, digital transformation is a systematic project, and it is difficult for enterprises to possess all the conditions for digital transformation on their own. It is crucial for governments to play the role of the ‘visible hand’.

First, digital infrastructure is the premise of digital transformation. Government departments should increase investment in new infrastructure such as 5G network base stations, big data research centres, and blockchain technology applications, to build a ‘foundation’ for the digital transformation process of enterprises, and provide a basic digital infrastructure guarantee for enterprise digital transformation. Second, the government should strengthen its guidance and empowerment of digital enterprise transformation, especially for traditional enterprises. From an industrial perspective, it can support the development of the digital industry and digital inclusive finance through subsidies, promote technological innovation in the digital industry, provide mature digital solutions for enterprises, reduce the cost and transformation risks of digital transformation, and effectively solve the current dilemma faced by digitalisation. From the perspective of micro-enterprises, the government can enhance the ability, willingness, and confidence of enterprises in digital transformation by introducing and implementing guiding policies, such as setting up typical digital transformation enterprise models, creating a good atmosphere for enterprise digital transformation, enhancing social attention to digital transformation, providing transformation momentum for enterprises, and through technical support and financial subsidies, enabling enterprises to fully utilise policy dividends, helping enterprises to graft market and government resources to alleviate financing constraints, and reducing the risk impact during the digital transformation process. Finally, the government should promote in-depth cooperation between industry, academia, and research and promote the transformation of digitalisation achievements. Colleges and research institutions have the advantage of talent, which makes them more likely than enterprises to form basic research results in the digital economy. Government departments can promote cooperation between industry, academia, and research at the policy level, such as building a digital research results platform connecting colleges, research institutions, enterprises, and service organisations; promoting in-depth exchanges; matching supply and demand; promoting the efficient and high-quality transformation of digital research results; and providing theoretical support and practical guidance for enterprises undergoing digital transformation.

Multidimensional improvement of supervision mechanisms to reduce the potential for enterprise violations

From the perspective of internal enterprise governance, in response to the new characteristics of digital transformation, enterprises should strengthen the coordinated management of data resources, establish a data governance organisation involving multiple departments, break the barriers between different departmental business systems, gradually establish unified internal data standards and specifications, enhance their digital governance capabilities, and reduce the space for violations. However, it is necessary to further integrate supervisory forces and improve the internal supervision system, continuously improving the relevant systems of various supervisory bodies to adapt to the new characteristics of enterprise digital transformation. At the macro level, following systematic and digital thinking, internal supervision system work regulations and standards should be formulated to provide institutional guarantees for efficient operation of the internal supervision system. At the micro level, aligning with the principles of practicality, problem-solving, and future consideration, the system should be dynamically improved with ‘establishment, amendment, and abolition’ to enhance the effectiveness of supervision and suppress the breeding of enterprise violation opportunities.

From the perspective of the legal and regulatory environment, governments should improve digital-related laws and regulations to reduce potential opportunities for enterprise violations during the digital transformation process. Under the impact of enterprise digital transformation, enterprise accounting information generated under current accounting standards may not reflect the true economic activities of enterprises. The government should improve accounting treatment regulations for digital resources to ensure the quality and effectiveness of accounting information. Conversely, government regulatory departments should strengthen the core supervision of the scope of information disclosure subjects, time windows, and content disclosure in digital transformation, actively identify new methods of accounting fraud by enterprises using complex information systems, and avoid the risk of enterprises using complex information systems for falsification, embellishment, and concealment of information and other violations.

From the perspective of external information users of enterprises, participants in the capital market should strengthen their professional accounting knowledge and digital technology capabilities, accelerate their adaptation to the process of enterprise digital transformation, and produce synergistic effects with enterprise digital transformation. Taking analysts with professional knowledge as an example, digital technologies such as big data have improved the capital market information disclosure environment and reduced the cost of information acquisition. For instance, the development of social media, represented by WeChat and Weibo, has not only greatly expanded the scope and time of information dissemination, but also further increased the content of network attention. Analysts should actively utilise the information dividends of digital technology to exert more precise constraints on their enterprises. However, the business processes, information systems, and management systems of enterprises undergoing digital transformation are more complex and diverse, requiring analysts to possess more professional digital technology knowledge and skills to effectively obtain enterprise accounting information and accurately assess enterprise risks and financial conditions. Therefore, analysts and other stakeholders need to strengthen digital technology training, improve digital technology capabilities, better utilise digital technology to empower and enhance their voices, improve their insight and supervision of enterprise operations and results, reduce the enterprise violation space, and develop their external governance role.

Cultivating digital survival awareness through multiple paths to weaken the rationalisation attitude of management violations

The digital economy has changed, and will continue to change the environment in which enterprises operate. Continuously achieving the development of value symbiosis and sustainable concepts during the digital transformation process can not only provide positive assistance for enterprises to achieve high-quality digital transformation, but also significantly enhance the management's rejection attitude towards violations, preventing the intensification of enterprise violation risks from the level of consciousness.

Organisations have shifted from competitive to symbiotic relationships in the digital economy. Enterprises should form a basic understanding of concepts such as connection, openness, integration, collaboration, and symbiosis; innovate organisational culture; cultivate enterprise righteousness and profit concepts; and enhance the internal driving force for enterprise owners, operators, and employees to undertake social responsibilities and promote enterprises to create social value. Additionally, enterprises should fully expand the channels for social entities to participate in the construction of enterprise culture and ecological environment during the transformation process (including various social entities such as industry organisations, social media, and research institutions, in the enterprise collaborative governance framework), and build a participation mechanism for the enterprise culture ecological governance of digital transformation. This would integrate the value propositions and social expectations of various social entities into the goals and practices of enterprise digital transformation, create comprehensive value through digital commercialisation and social operation, and promote symbiotic development with a responsible cultural ecology, and weaken the rationalisation of enterprise violations.

The government should create a favourable external environment to help enterprises establish the concept of sustainable development and assist them in forming a digital survival awareness of common benefits and symbiosis. The values and cultural orientations of the public within the digital ecosystem affect enterprises’ social responsibility concepts. The government should actively use the laws of network communication, strengthen the promotion and popularisation of knowledge on social responsibility, improve the moral standards of social responsibility, strengthen the governance of cyberspace, vigorously cultivate the core values of socialism, and enhance the dissemination of positive cultural values, thus forming a unified and virtuous digital operation thinking and sound competitive thinking in society, providing a good cultural atmosphere for developing enterprise social responsibility concepts during the digital transformation process, and weakening their self-rationalisation attitude towards violations.

Research limitations and perspectives

Based on the development of practice and the current status of research, this study conducts a detailed analysis of the relationship, role mechanism, and boundary conditions between digital transformation and enterprise violation risk, and comprehensively explores the governance effect of digital transformation from the perspective of external investor protection. However, owing to the limitations of our abilities and objective conditions, this study has the following limitations.

(1)Lack of detailed division of digital transformation dimensions. Enterprise digital transformation can be subdivided into two dimensions, the core underlying architecture dimension and the technology practice application dimension. While this paper uses digital assets to measure the overall stage of enterprise digital transformation and level of transformation, it does not further refine the analysis of whether there is a difference in the impact of the different dimensions of digital transformation on the risk of enterprise violations.

Lack of detailed division of digital transformation dimensions. Enterprise digital transformation can be subdivided into two dimensions, the core underlying architecture dimension and the technology practice application dimension. While this paper uses digital assets to measure the overall stage of enterprise digital transformation and level of transformation, it does not further refine the analysis of whether there is a difference in the impact of the different dimensions of digital transformation on the risk of enterprise violations.

(2)This study focuses on breach risk in the traditional sense of an enterprise's business activities. However, with the continuous improvement of the concept of enterprise development, the expansion of governance boundaries, and the emergence of the concept of green governance, environmental violations have gradually become a key research area of corporate governance. Therefore, whether digital transformation has the same impact on the risk of enterprise environmental violations needs to be expanded and enriched in future research.

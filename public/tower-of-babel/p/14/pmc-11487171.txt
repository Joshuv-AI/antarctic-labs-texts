Effectiveness of switching to subcutaneous infliximab in inflammatory bowel disease patients with inadequate biochemical response during intravenous administration
Authors: Bokyeong Lee, Minjee Kim, Eun Ran Kim, Sung Noh Hong, Dong Kyung Chang, Young-Ho Kim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Infliximab (IFX) has transformed the management of inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD). While intravenous (IV) IFX has been effective, a subcutaneous (SC) formulation offers advantages in convenience and cost. However, there is lack of evidence regarding the transition from IV to SC-IFX, especially for patients with inadequate responses. This study investigates the effectiveness of switching from IV to SC-IFX in patients with inadequate responses during IV maintenance therapy. A retrospective study enrolled IBD patients who transitioned to SC-IFX after demonstrating inadequate responses during IV maintenance therapy. The study collected data of demographics of patients and dose and therapies administered prior to the IV-IFX. Primary outcomes included improvements in C-reactive protein (CRP) or fecal calprotectin (FC) levels. This study evaluated the trough levels and its differences between pre- and post-switching. Among 44 patients included, 10 exhibited CRP elevation before the switch, with 6 showing normalization post-switch. Similarly, 42 patients had elevated FC levels pre-switch, with 26 experiencing reductions post-switch. Trough levels increased after the switch. However, there were no significant differences between responders and non-responders. This study is the first study to investigate the transition therapy of IV to SC-IFX in patients with inadequate response. This suggests that SC-IFX could be a viable alternative in the management of IBD. However, further research is necessary to evaluate its efficacy in a larger population of patients who exhibit inadequate responses during IV-IFX maintenance therapy.

Introduction

Infliximab, a potent anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) agent, has revolutionized the management of inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD), including Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Intravenous (IV) infliximab (IFX) has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in inducing and maintaining remission, thereby improving patients’ quality of life1–12. However, IV-IFX infusions require hospital visits and can be time-consuming13. Recently, a novel subcutaneous (SC) formulation of infliximab (CT-P13), administering 120 mg IFX in each 1mL single dose, has been recently licensed in IBD patients aged ≥ 18 years old. The SC route offers several advantages, including patient convenience, reduced healthcare burden, and potential cost savings14.

The studies show that SC-IFX is not inferior to IV-IFX in terms of clinical response and safety15. While it is demonstrated that the efficacy of SC-IFX 120 mg every 2 weeks is comparable to IV-IFX 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks16, there remains a dearth of research specifically addressing its superiority during the transition from IV-IFX 5 ~ 10 mg every 8 weeks to SC-IFX 120 mg every 2 weeks administration in patients with inadequate response. According to Cerna et al., the SC route may serve as a “biobetter” alternative by improving the pharmacokinetic profile of infliximab, leading to better clinical outcomes in patients who do not fully respond to IV therapy17. The hypothesized pathophysiology behind the potential superiority of SC administration lies in its ability to maintain stable and consistently higher serum drug levels, which may reduce the formation of anti-drug antibodies and enhance therapeutic outcomes. It is noteworthy that the subcutaneous administration of IFX may induce the disappearance of anti-drug antibodies from circulation.

Managing IFX non-response is an important challenge in clinical practice. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the effectiveness of switching from IV to SC infliximab, particularly in patients who exhibited inadequate responses during IV maintenance therapy. We focused on patients with objective evidence of active disease based on biochemical markers to reduce bias from retrospective studies.

Materials and methods

Patients

From January 2020 to September 2023, a total of 254 IBD patients were administered with SC-IFX in Samsung Medical Center, a tertiary institution. This retrospective study enrolled patients with IBD who meet the following criteria: patients who received a minimum of three maintenance doses after three induction doses IV-IFX and demonstrated an inadequate biochemical response during IV-IFX maintenance therapy. Among these patients, those who switched to SC-IFX and maintained this treatment for a minimum of six months were included.

Examinations

The Montreal Classification of IBD was used to classify CD and UC, while the Harvey-Bradshaw Index (HBI) and Mayo score/Disease Activity Index (DAI) was used to assess the degree of disease activity for CD and UC respectively. Simple endoscopic CD score (SES-CD) and Mayo score were used to measure mucosal inflammation for CD and UC respectively. The clinical response was defined as Mayo score of 0–1 in CD and UC respectively.

The inadequate biochemical response was defined as an increase in CRP or fecal calprotectin during IV-IFX maintenance therapy. Specifically, CRP increase was considered significant when it exceeded 0.5 mg/dL and was measured consecutively on two or more occasions before switching. An elevation of FC was determined when it exceeded 250 mg/kg before switching.

Serum TL (Trough level) of IFX and ATI (Antibodies to infliximab) were measured with enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays using Remsima Monitor Drug Level (Immundiagnostik AG) according to the manufacture’s instructions18. The TL of IFX was classified as therapeutic (≥ 3ug/mL) and subtherapeutic (< 3 ug/mL) based on the TAXIT (Trough Concentration Adapted Infliximab Treatment) trial. ATI positivity was defined as an ATI concentration ≥ 10 ng/mL, which was identified in at least 2 consecutive samples19.

The primary outcome was the improvement of CRP or fecal calprotectin. CRP improvement was defined as a CRP value measured 8 to 12 weeks after SC-IFX administration being less than 0.5 mg/dL during the first outpatient follow-up. FC improvement was defined as results obtained at least 6 months after SC-IFX administration, with a value of 250 mg/kg or lower.

Data collection

For each patient the following data were collected at baseline: age, sex, height, body weight, body mass index (BMI), duration of IV-IFX medication, C-reactive protein (CRP), fecal calprotectin (FC), and trough level (TL) were also recorded. In addition, data regarding to dose of IV-IFX before switching to SC-IFX and therapies administered prior to the initiation of IV-IFX was collected. Therapies administered prior to the IV-IFX were defined as the immunosuppressant, or other biologics used before starting IV-IFX treatment.

We collected the information of patients with IBD who had SC-IFX. Data was extracted from the Clinical Data Warehouse Darwin-C of Samsung medical center for this study. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board at Samsung Medical Center (SMC 2024-02-027). This study was conducted by the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Patient information collected in this study was used only for patients who consented to access the electronical medical record (EMR).

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were analyzed using descriptive and frequency analysis. Categorical variables were analysed using the chi-squre test or the Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test or Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 27.0.

Results

Patients and descriptive data

From January 2020 to September 2023, a total of 254 patients were administered with SC-IFX, and among these, 44 patients met the inclusion criteria. Among the 44 patients with IBD, 35 patients had CD and 9 patients had UC. The median age was 33 (22–40) years. 32 were male and 12 were female. The median Body Mass Index (BMI) of the patients was 23. The onset of disease was ≤ 16 years for 14 patients (32%), between 17 and 39 years for 24 patients (56%), and ≥ 40 years for 6 patients (13%). The median duration of the IV-IFX before SC-IFX was 55 (17–84) months. The 38 patients (86%) had 5 mg/kg of IV-IFX every 8 weeks before switching and 6 patients (14%) had 10 mg/kg of IV-IFX every 8 weeks before switching. Data on ATI (Antibodies to infliximab) was available for 30 patients. Among them, 10 patients (23%) tested positive for ATI, while 20 patients (46%) tested negative. There were 41 patients received azathioprine, 3 patients received MTX(Methotrexate), 6 patients received 6-TGN(6-Thioguanine Nucleotide), and 2 patients had biologic before the IV-IFX. Both patients who had received biologics had a history of being treated with adalimumab and ustekinumab. Baseline inflammatory markers were as follows; CRP, median value of 0.2 (0.1–0.9) mg/dL, and FC, with levels of 1238 (711–2357) mg/kg. Biochemical response after switching to SC-IFX from IV-IFX

We assessed the biochemical response to switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX by monitoring changes in CRP and FC. Among the 44 patients who switched to SC-IFX, 10 had previously experienced an increase in CRP levels during maintenance therapy with IV-IFX. After the switch, 6 of these 10 patients exhibited normalization of CRP levels, indicating a significant improvement in their inflammatory status. The trough levels (TL) before the switch in the responders and non-responders were 2.2 and 1.7 ug/mL respectively and that after the switch were 10.5 and 5.6 ug/mL respectively. The TL difference, which we defined as difference of post-TL and pre-TL of the responders and non-responders were 8.2 and 3.4 ug/mL respectively.

FC, an important marker of intestinal inflammation, was also evaluated. Prior to the switch, 42 out of the 44 patients presented with elevated FC. After transitioning to SC-IFX, 26 of these 42 patients experienced a decrease in FC to below the predefined cut-off, suggesting a notable reduction in intestinal inflammation. The TL before the switch in the responders and non-responders were 3.8 and 2.3 ug/mL respectively and that after the switch were 15.1 and 11.1 ug/mL respectively. The TL difference of the responders and non-responders were 10.6 and 9.0 ug/mL respectively.

Furthermore, amongst the eight patients that had both elevated CRP and FC before the switch to SC-IFX, three patients had improvement of CRP levels only, one patient had improvement in FC only, and one patient had improvement of both biochemical markers.

TL increased after switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX in both responders and non-responders in terms of CRP and FC respectively. Also, we assessed the biochemical response to switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX by monitoring changes in CRP and FC in patients with therapeutic TL of 3 ug/mL or above. Among the patients with therapeutic TL, two had previously experienced an increase in CRP levels during maintenance therapy with IV-IFX. After the switch, these two patients (100%) exhibited a significant improvement in their inflammatory status, with TL increasing from 7.8 ug/mL before the switch to 17.8 ug/mL after the switch resulting in a TL difference of 11.6 µg/mL.

Regarding fecal calprotectin, 18 patients out of 24 patients (75%) with therapeutic TL showed response after the switch. The TL before the switch in these responders increased from 5.2 to 17.7 ug/mL post-switch, with a TL difference of 12.7 µg/mL. For the six non-responders (25%), the pre-switch TL was 6 ug/mL, which increased to 23.6 µg/mL post-switch, resulting in a TL difference of 17.1 µg/mL.

However, there were no statistically significant differences in the pre-switch TL, post-switch TL, or the TL difference between the responders and non-responders in either the CRP or fecal calprotectin groups.

Among 10 patients with positive anti-drug antibody before switching to SC-IFX, 6 out of 10 patients had decreased antibody titer after switching to SC-IFX. These 6 patients had negative seroconversion of the antibody.

Endoscopic response after switching to SC-IFX from IV-IFX

Among the 44 patients who were switched to SC-IFX, a total of 23 patients underwent endoscopic evaluation both before and after the switch. 19 patients had CD and 4 patients had UC.

Out of the CD patients, 5/19 achieved endoscopic response after switching to SC-IFX. The median SES-CD decreased from 6 before switching to 0 post-switch in those who achieved remission.

In UC patients, all four patients achieved endoscopic response after switching to SC-IFX. The median Mayo score improved from 1.5 to 1.0.

These results suggest that switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX can lead to endoscopic response in a subset of patients with CD and UC, although the statistical significance of these changes varies across different patient groups.

Discussion

The effectiveness of IFX has been proven since the introduction of IFX to IBD patients. However due to its discomfort of use resulting from intravenous injection, subcutaneous formulation of IFX was developed. SC-IFX may offer clinical benefits in terms of pharmacokinetics, with comparable efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity, as well as increased convenience for patients and reduced demands on healthcare system resources14.

Total drug exposure of SC-IFX 120 mg every 2 weeks is similar to that of IV-IFX 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks, even though TL is much higher in patients receiving SC-IFX20 and clinical efficacy is comparable between SC-FIX 120 mg every 2 weeks and IV-IFX 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks16. The Belgian IBD Research and Development group recommended that in patients under maintenance IV-IFX therapy, switching to SC-IFX can only be considered in patients who are receiving a dose of 5 mg/kg IV-IFX with an interval of at least eight weeks and who achieved both clinical and endoscopic response21. However, Buisson et al. showed that clinical relapse rate in patients who switched from IV-IFX 10 mg/kg every 8 weeks to SC-IFX 120 mg every 2 weeks was similar to that in patients who switched from IV-IFX 5 mg/kg every 8 weeks to SC-IFX 120 mg every 2 weeks22, suggesting that SC-IFX may have some advantages in terms of effectiveness than IV-IFX.

In our study, the median trough level for IV infliximab were 3.4ug/mL for the overall population, 3.6 ug/mL and 2.3 ug/mL for CD and UC respectively before switching. In other words, the trough levels were close to subtherapeutic (TL < 3ug/mL). For these patients, increasing the IV dose can be one option, but our study suggests that switching to SC is also an option to consider. When considering the healthcare budget savings23, it is cost-effective to use SC IFX even for the subtherapeutic patients.

Considering the clinical data on SC-IFX remain limited24, the main strength of our study was that hat patients with inadequate response of IV-IFX can benefit with SC-IFX even in the maintenance phase in real-world. Cerna et al. enrolled patients who have not used infliximab or patients who received IV-infliximab induction for having no anti-drug antibody, ADA17. The prospective cohort study reported in our hospital evaluated long-term clinical outcomes of IBD patients treated with SC-IFX compared with those of IBD patients treated with IV-IFX during maintenance therapy25.

We focused on patients with inadequate biochemical response during the maintenance IV-IFX therapy to avoid bias in retrospective studies as much as possible and observed that more than half of patients showed biochemical improvement. We tried to find factors that could predict biological improvement. Although a study has suggested that the change in TL after switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX in patients with clinical remission is related to the effectiveness22, our study could not find an association with the change in TL and the effectiveness of switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX in patients with inadequate biological response. However, considering that the biggest difference between intravenous and subcutaneous injections is the pharmacokinetic characteristics, further studies on the association between TL and effectiveness of switching from IV-IFX to SC-IFX in patients who are not in remission are needed in the future.

This study has several limitations. First, being conducted at a single center may limit the generalizability of the findings to all IBD patient populations. Second, the sample size was small, which may not provide enough statistical power to detect small but clinically meaningful differences. Third, there is a difference of study population compared to the Western countries. Even though the prevalence of IBD during the last three decades has increased dramatically as other Asian countries, Korean patients exhibit several distinct characteristics compared to Western countries26,27. For example, even though the incidence of CD has been reported to be higher in women in Western countries, it has been reported conversely in East Asian countries, including Korea28. However, if we repeat the study in the future with a larger number of patients with multi-center study, we will be able to make more generalized interpretations. Fourth, the retrospective nature of the study introduces potential selection bias and limits the ability to control for confounding factors that could influence the outcomes. Fifth, endoscopic evaluation was not performed for all patients due to the patients’ adherence and other reasons. This study focused on the biochemical markers which may be nonspecific. CRP is a nonspecific marker of inflammation and can be elevated in a variety of inflammatory conditions, so a high CRP level does not necessarily reflect IBD activity. Also, while CRP and calprotectin indicate the presence of inflammation, their sensitivity and specificity are not perfect and may not accurately reflect mild inflammation or mucosal healing. These markers can take time to change after an inflammatory event, so they have limitations in providing an immediate picture of inflammation in the gut like endoscopy. In particular, CRP and calprotectin have limitations in accurately assessing whether the intestinal mucosa is healing or not, and it is difficult to specifically identify the location or spread of inflammation. Endoscopy, on the other hand, has the advantage of directly identifying not only the presence of inflammation, but also its location and extent. However, considering the fact that there is small number of patients with endoscopy data, CRP and calprotectin level is simple and easy to perform.

In our study, although more than half of the patients who were inadequately responding to IV-IFX showed biochemical improvement after switching to SC-IFX, we were unable to identify factors that could predict biological improvement. While SC-IFX may serve as an alternative for IBD patients with an inadequate response to IV-IFX, larger, multicenter prospective studies are necessary to further explore the role of SC-IFX in these patients.

Successful endovascular treatment of a symptomatic perivisceral/juxtarenal coral reef aortic lesion using balloon-expandable aortic, mesenteric, and renal parallel/snorkel stent grafts
Authors: Hannah Koury, Stephen E. Ryan, Derek J. Roberts
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Coral reef aorta (CRA) has been defined as calcified, often eccentric abdominal aortic plaques that frequently extend into the lumina of the visceral and renal arteries and cause chronic mesenteric, renal, and lower limb ischemia. We report a case of chronic mesenteric and limb-threatening ischemia secondary to a perivisceral and juxtarenal CRA lesion crossing the anastomosis of a remotely constructed aortobifemoral graft. The patient was treated with balloon-expandable aortic, mesenteric, and renal parallel/snorkel stent grafts, which have exhibited a 3-year primary patency rate of 100%. We believe our case supports consideration of endovascular treatment for symptomatic CRA-related lesions, especially those diagnosed after remote open aortic surgery.

Coral reef aorta (CRA) has been defined as calcified, rock-hard, often eccentric abdominal aortic atherosclerotic plaques that frequently extend into the lumina of the visceral and renal arteries, causing chronic mesenteric, renal, and lower limb ischemia.1 Traditional management of these lesions included open surgery (thromboendarterectomy or bypass),2, 3, 4 which was associated with a high risk of complications and mortality. Case reports/series on endovascular management are largely limited to supraceliac5, 6, 7, 8 and infrarenal6,9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 lesions, sparing the perivisceral and juxtarenal aortic segments.

We report a case of chronic mesenteric and chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI) secondary to a perivisceral and juxtarenal CRA lesion crossing the anastomosis of a remotely constructed aortobifemoral graft. The patient was treated with balloon-expandable aortic, mesenteric, and renal parallel/snorkel stent grafts, which have exhibited a 3-year primary patency rate of 100%. The patient provided written informed consent to report their case details and intraoperative images.

Case report

The case is reported in accordance with the CARE (case report) guidelines (Appendix 1). A 69-year-old woman was referred for ischemic rest pain, postprandial pain, food avoidance, and a 25- to 35-lb weight loss over 5 months. She had a <1-cm left malleolar wound (Wound, Ischemia, foot Infection16 classification stage 2), currently smoked 1 pack per day (50 pack-year history), and had hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, coronary artery disease, and aortoiliac occlusive disease treated with an end-to-end aortobifemoral bypass (ABF) for disabling claudication 25 years prior. She underwent a left graft limb thrombectomy, femoral endarterectomy, and profundoplasty with bovine patch angioplasty 20 years prior for left ABF limb thrombosis, a transbrachial left superficial femoral artery angioplasty 10 and 8 years prior for recurrent ischemic rest pain, and a right femoral to below-knee popliteal artery bypass with ipsilateral greater saphenous vein 5 years prior for disabling claudication.

A computed tomography angiogram revealed a CRA lesion proximal to and extending across the proximal ABF graft anastomosis and involving the left renal artery (LRA) and superior mesenteric artery (SMA) ostia (Fig 1, A and B), a right renal and left superficial femoral artery occlusion, and a patent right femoral-popliteal bypass graft. A visceral duplex study reported an SMA peak systolic velocity (PSV) of 279 cm/s, SMA-to-aorta ratio of 5.6, LRA PSV of 348 cm/s, renal-to-aorta velocity ratio of 7.1, and renal resistive index of >0.8 (Appendix 2), suggesting a >70% SMA stenosis and >60% LRA stenosis according to our institutional criteria. Her ankle-brachial index (ABI) was 0.92 on the right and 0.44 on the left. Management of the CRA lesion was staged. First, a 6-mm × 32-mm balloon-expandable covered stent was deployed in the SMA through a left brachial access (Fig 1, C). The systolic blood pressure difference across the CRA lesion during this procedure measured 24 mm Hg (mean pressure gradient, 9 mm Hg). Four months later, after discussion at our divisional difficult case rounds, the second stage of the procedure was completed. A 6F × 11-cm sheath was introduced into the axillary artery after cut-down and upsized to a 14F × 64-cm sheath. A 7F × 70-cm sheath was then inserted through the 14F sheath to cannulate the LRA. The 7F sheath and 6-mm × 32-mm balloon-expandable covered stent were then advanced into the LRA. The LRA stent was left undeployed and positioned so the proximal end of the stent was slightly caudal to the proximal end of the SMA stent. A 9F × 45-cm sheath was subsequently inserted retrograde into the right limb of the ABF after a twice-redo right groin cutdown. The CRA lesion was crossed and predilated with a 8-mm × 40-mm noncompliant balloon. A 12-mm × 41-mm balloon-expandable covered stent was placed distal to the SMA and LRA stent grafts and across the ABF graft anastomosis. The aortic stent, LRA stent, and SMA balloon were then deployed/inflated simultaneously (Fig 2, B). No embolic protection devices were used. The distal part of the aortic stent graft was then flared with a 16-mm noncompliant angioplasty balloon. A completion angiogram revealed wide patency of the SMA, LRA, and aortic stent grafts (Fig 2, C and D).

Postoperatively, the patient had serous drainage from her twice-redo femoral cutdown wound, requiring negative pressure wound therapy. The patient was subsequently discharged 4 days later on aspirin 81 mg and clopidogrel 75 mg/d after an initial load of 300 mg of clopidogrel. She was followed with quarterly clinic visits and 6-month duplex examinations. At 3 years, the patient remained free from chronic mesenteric or lower extremity ischemic symptoms. Further, a 3-year postprocedure computed tomography angiogram acquired to evaluate an elevated SMA PSV of 350 cm/s (velocity ratio = 4.2) confirmed patent SMA, left renal, and aortic stent grafts and a patent ABF (Fig 3, A-C). Arterial duplex examination showed a patent LRA and an ABI of 1.1 on the left and 1.0 on the right with patent lower extremity bypass grafts bilaterally (Appendix 3). Discussion

We report a case of chronic mesenteric and CLTI secondary to a CRA lesion crossing the anastomosis of a remotely constructed end-to-end ABF successfully treated with balloon-expandable aortic, mesenteric, and renal parallel/snorkel stent grafts. The literature on CRA is limited to an estimated <200 case reports worldwide.17,18 Reviews of CRA case reports published in 2005 and 2013 recommended open aortic thromboendarterectomy or bypass surgery if the lesion extends into the aortic branching vessels or if aortic tissue quality is poor.9,12 However, reported 30-day in-hospital morbidity and mortality rates for these procedures were estimated to be 13% to 14% and 30%, respectively. This is likely because the majority of open repairs require thoracoabdominal aortic exposure,18 and many patients have a high perioperative risk.

Endovascular management of CRA lesions involving the visceral and renal arteries18 is challenging owing to the need for branch vessel revascularization and the associated risk of embolization. To our knowledge, few reports of the use of snorkel or chimney techniques to manage CRA-associated visceral or renal disease have been published.19,20 Embolization protection techniques reported include balloon occlusion of the SMA10 or distal aorta,5 and placement of endovascular protection devices in the perivisceral aorta8 or renal arteries before angioplasty, lithotripsy, or stenting of aortic lesions.10,21 In our case, no protection device was used, and there was no evidence of branch vessel or lower limb embolization, likely because the simultaneously deployed stent grafts jailed any potentially embolic material. In the select few reports of use of endovascular lithotripsy treatment of CRA lesions,21, 22, 23 some surgeons also did not use protection devices, and the authors did not report any embolic complications. Lithotripsy (with or without subsequent stenting) may be attractive for use in patients with heavily calcified, complex, or long CRA lesions.

For CRA lesions extending across the supraceliac aorta, uncovered balloon- and self-expanding aortic stenting with and without visceral/renal protection6,10,11 has also been performed without embolic complications. However, to our knowledge, no reports have been published describing endovascular management of the celiac artery in those with CRA lesions nor CRA lesions associated with previously placed open aortic bypass grafts, as in our case.

Conclusions

We report a case of chronic mesenteric ischemia and CLTI secondary to a perivisceral and juxtarenal CRA lesion crossing the anastomosis of a remotely-constructed ABF successfully treated with balloon-expandable aortic, mesenteric, and renal parallel/snorkel stent grafts. We believe our case supports consideration of endovascular treatment for symptomatic CRA-related lesions, especially those diagnosed after remote aortic surgery.

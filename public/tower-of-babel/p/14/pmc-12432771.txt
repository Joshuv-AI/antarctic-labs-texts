Comparisons of the efficacy of different Chinese medicine injections on immune function in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma during combined therapy: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Zhaohui Gu, Tian Zhang, Xinyue Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStudies have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) injections combined with concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). Although studies have examined the impact on immune function, direct comparisons of the effects of various TCM injections on immune function remain limited. Thus, a Bayesian network meta-analysis (NMA) was conducted to assess the beneficial effects of different TCM injections on immune function in patients with NPC during CCRT.MethodsThe PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang, Weipu, and China Biology Medicine disc (CBM) databases were searched up to February 2025. The key inclusion criteria incorporated—(I) study subjects: individuals with clinically and pathologically confirmed NPC; (II) the control group received CCRT, and the study group received TCM injections combined with CCRT. The TCM injections of interest were Aidi (AD), Fufangkushen (FFKS), Kushensu (KSS), Shenqifuzheng (SQFZ), Lanxiangxi (LXX), etc.; (III) study type: a randomized controlled trial (RCT); (IV) outcome indicators: CD3+, CD4+, CD8+, CD4+/CD8+. Two researchers independently screened and extracted the data according to the eligibility criteria. The quality of studies was assessed via the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool 2.0 (ROB 2.0). Bayesian NMA was conducted with R version 4.3.3 and Stata 15.1.ResultsThis NMA encompassed 11 eligible articles involving 983 patients (496 in the CCRT + TCM injection group and 487 in the CCRT group). Seven TCM injections and four outcome indicators were considered. Due to the inability to implement blinding, all included articles presented a low risk of bias. The results demonstrated that AD injection was the most effective in improving CD3+ [surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) 96.50%] and CD4+ (SUCRA 96.51%). KSS injection was the most effective in improving CD8+ (SUCRA 83.51%) and improving the CD4+:CD8+ ratio (SUCRA 82.80%). Sensitivity analysis indicated stable results.ConclusionsThis NMA demonstrated that TCM injections significantly increased the levels of peripheral blood CD3+ and CD4+ T-lymphocyte subpopulations during CCRT in patients with NPC. This may reduce the risk of patient infection. Furthermore, TCM injections improved CD8+ levels and the CD4+:CD8+ ratio to some extent. These results suggest enhanced tumor immunosurveillance, potentially leading to improved patient survival. Among the injections, AD and KSS injections exhibited the most significant immune benefits for patients. However, due to the limited number and quality of included articles, further high-quality RCTs are necessary to confirm these results.Keywords: Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), nasal cancer, traditional Chinese medicine injection (TCM injection), network meta-analysis (NMA), randomized controlled

Key findings

• Chinese medicine injections can significantly increase the levels of CD3+ and CD4+ T-lymphocyte subsets in the peripheral blood of patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) during concurrent chemoradiotherapy.

What is known and what is new?

• Chinese medicine injections can improve the clinical response of NPC.

• Aidi (AD), Fufangkushen and Shenqifuzheng injections can all significantly improve CD3+ levels and CD4+ levels and AD ranks first in improving CD3+ and CD4+, Kushensu (KSS) ranks first in improving CD8+ and CD4+:CD8+.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Based on a comprehensive consideration of clinical efficacy, immune function, and adverse reactions, AD and KSS injections can be recommended in NPC.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignancy that occurs in the nasopharynx. It is characterized by geographic and familial aggregation. From 1990 to 2021, the global incidence of NPC among middle-aged and older adults increased by 58.2%, with deaths increasing by 33.8% and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) increasing by 42.1% (1). In April 2024, the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) published data indicating that there were 120,416 new cases of NPC in 2022, ranking NPC 23rd out of 63 cancers with a mortality rate of 0.8% (2). These data suggest that NPC remains a significant and persistent global health concern.

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network’s (NCCN) Head and Neck Cancer Guidelines (NCCN 5.2024) (3) recommend the use of radical radiotherapy for early-stage NPC, and for locally advanced NPC, the combination of concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) and induction or adjuvant chemotherapy is advised according to each patient’s characteristics. Although the overall survival (OS) rate for treated patients is as high as 76% (4), significant challenges related to radiotherapy resistance (5) and chemotherapy resistance remain (6). In addition, complications resulting from toxic side effects, including liver and kidney function damage, gastrointestinal reactions, and bone marrow suppression, can contribute to increased mortality rates (7,8). Furthermore, CCRT inhibits tumor cell proliferation and causes irreversible damage to the body’s immune cells. This leads to a decline in subpopulations of T lymphocytes, specifically CD3+, CD4+, and an increase in that of CD8+. There is also a substantial decrease in the CD4+:CD8+ ratio (9,10). These changes suggest a decline in the patient’s cellular immune function and their ability to recognize and kill mutant cells, resulting in an immunosuppressive state.

Traditional Chinese medicines (TCMs) have been shown to effectively regulate immune function and mitigate the adverse effects associated with cancer treatment (11). TCM injections are sterile preparations and powders or concentrates for preparing preclinical solutions. They are refined from effective substances extracted from single or compound natural medicines using advanced technology, in accordance with the theory and practical experience of TCM. Currently, many TCM injections are in clinical use, including Kangai, Shenqifuzheng (SQFZ), and Shenmai (MS) injections. In recent years, TCM injections have emerged as a viable treatment for various cancers, including breast cancer (BC), gastric cancer (GC), and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). These injections offer several advantages, including high concentration, rapid absorption, high bioavailability, and a swift onset of action (12-14). TCM injections have been proven effective in improving objective response rate (ORR), Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS), mitigating gastrointestinal reactions and neurotoxicity, and strengthening immune function, thus improving patients’ quality of life (QOL). Different TCM injections have different components and pharmacology, which causes differences in efficacy. For instance, Fufangkushen (FFKS) injection contains matrine, baicalin, oxymatrine, and other components. Matrine has antiviral effects and can interfere with the synthesis of viral proteins. It can also promote the production of immune cells, thereby enhancing the body’s immunity. Additionally, matrine and oxymatrine inhibit the migration and invasion of NPC cells by blocking the NF-κB pathway (15). SQFZ injection contains various active ingredients, including flavonoids, polysaccharides, and saponins. These components have immunomodulatory and immune-enhancing effects. Astragalus and ginsenoside Rg3, for example, can inhibit the migration and invasion of NPC cells (16).

A previous network meta-analysis (NMA) compared the impact of various TCM injections combined with CCRT on clinical outcomes and adverse symptoms in NPC treatment (17). However, immunocompetence status was not evaluated. TCM injections combined with CCRT have been reported to improve immune indicators. For example, a study has shown that FFKS injection combined with chemoradiotherapy (CRT) can upregulate cyclin B2 expression in the peripheral blood of patients with nasopharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma and significantly improve the QOL of patients (18). Lanxiangxi (LXX) injection combined with CRT can reduce serum vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in NPC patients, reduce the level of tumor marker carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), improve the quality of survival, and reduce adverse reactions (19). In summary, existing studies have revealed that TCM injections can effectively improve the immune function of patients after CCRT; however, there is a lack of direct comparisons across different TCM injections.

NMAs have several advantages over traditional meta-analyses. While traditional meta-analyses can only compare the results of two TCM injections, NMAs can compare multiple TCM injections simultaneously and integrate direct and indirect evidence. In this study, we conducted an NMA to evaluate the beneficial effects of TCM injections on immune function in patients with NPC undergoing combination treatment. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA-NMA reporting checklist (20) (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1479/rc).

Methods

The study protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration No. CRD42024537002).

Search strategy

PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang, Weipu, and China Biology Medicine disc (CBM) databases were searched, with a literature timeframe spanning from the establishment of each database to February 20, 2025. The search included both subject and free terms. The medical subject terms included “nasopharyngeal carcinoma and injection”. The specific search strategy is shown in Table S1. To ensure that the retrieved literature was as comprehensive as possible, a secondary search of the references of published systematic reviews and other relevant literature was conducted.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

Articles meeting the following criteria were included: (I) a study population consisting of patients with NPC diagnosed by clinicopathology; (II) an observation group including interventions with TCM injections + CCRT [TCM injections included Aidi (AD) injection, FFKS injection, KSS injection, SQFZ injection, LXX injection, MS injection, Fufangbanmao (FFBM) injection, Kangai injection, Xiaoaiping injection, etc.] and a control group consisting of CCRT; (III) randomized controlled trials (RCTs); and (IV) outcome indicators of CD3+, CD4+, CD8+, and the CD4+:CD8+ ratio.

Exclusion criteria

Articles meeting the following criteria were excluded: (I) animal or cellular experiments, scientific experiment plans, case reports, reviews, letters, editorials, conference papers, etc.; (II) articles with missing or grossly erroneous study data; (III) articles that have been published multiple times; (IV) oral TCM or other nonpharmacological therapies used by TCM practitioners; and (V) multidrug combination therapy or combined studies on multiple cancers.

Article screening and data extraction

The retrieved articles were imported into EndNote (Clarivate, Philadelphia, PA, USA), and two researchers (Z.G. and T.Z.) independently reviewed the titles and abstracts based on the eligibility criteria. They then read the full text for a second screening. Any discrepancies were discussed or referred to a third researcher (X.C.) for resolution. The two researchers (Z.G. and T.Z.) independently extracted the data from the final included articles using Excel 2016 (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA). The collected information included the first author, year of publication, basic participant information, intervention and control measures, treatment duration, TCM injection administration method, baseline consistency, and endpoints. This NMA collected pre- and post-treatment data to extract the outcome variables and calculated the change value as the final analysis data for the outcome indicators. Any disagreements were discussed or settled by a third researcher (X.C.).

Quality assessment

The Cochrane Risk of Bias (ROB) Assessment Tool 2.0 (ROB 2.0) (Cochrane, London, UK) (21) was employed to assess the included studies in five key areas: bias in the randomization process, bias in the deviation from established intervention, bias in the missing outcome data, bias in the outcome measurement, and bias in the selective reporting of outcomes. For each study, two researchers (Z.G. and T.Z.) independently assessed the quality and assigned ratings of low, high, or potential risk for each area. A low ROB rating was assigned when all five domains were rated as low risk. If any domain was assessed as unclear risk and low risk, it was rated as unclear risk. If any domain was judged as high ROB, it was evaluated as high risk. Any discrepancies were discussed and resolved with input from a third researcher (X.C.).

Statistical analysis

This NMA employed the Bayesian method for the following reasons. First, the Bayesian method can handle uncertainty and complexity by providing a complete probability distribution of parameter estimates through posterior distributions. This provides a more comprehensive understanding of the uncertainty of estimates. For complex models, this method provides greater flexibility and is especially suitable for handling multilevel data and nonlinear relationships. Second, the Bayesian method allows for the incorporation of prior information. This can enhance the accuracy of estimates, particularly when the sample size is small or the data are sparse. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistic, considering I2>50% to indicate significant heterogeneity. Our sensitivity analysis evaluated the impact of a single study on the overall results via the one-by-one removal of studies. The goodness of fit of the model was assessed through the calculation of the deviation information criterion (DIC). Noninformational priors, such as the normal distribution or the gamma distribution, were selected to mitigate subjective influence. The specific prior distribution parameters were as follows: the mean was set at 0, and the variance was set at 1. To ascertain the convergence of the model, the following methods were employed: the model was examined for convergence through calculation of the Gelman-Rubin statistic (R-hat) for multiple chains. An R-hat close to 1 indicated that the model had converged. To address heterogeneity across studies, a Bayesian hierarchical random-effects model was adopted, which allowed each study to have its effect size and for the consideration of the shared overall effect distribution.

The CD3+, CD4+, CD8+, and the CD4+:CD8+ ratio are displayed as the weighted mean difference (MD) with the 95% credible interval (CrI). The specifics of the procedure are as follows: Given the heterogeneity between studies, multiple comparisons of different treatment options for NPC were first conducted via a Bayesian hierarchical random-effects model (22,23). The extent of heterogeneity was measured with the I2 statistic. R 4.3.3 software (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and Stata 15.1 software (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA) were employed for generating all calculations and graphs. According to the theory of the likelihood function and some prior assumptions, Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulation was performed via Bayesian inference with R 4.3.3 software. The number of iterations was set to 500,000, with 20,000 in annealing, in order to examine the posterior distributions of interrogated nodes (24-26). The node-splitting method was employed to assess local inconsistencies for outcomes with closed loops. The relationships among different treatments were displayed in a network diagram. A network diagram with nodes and lines was constructed to represent different interventions, in which the size of nodes represents the number of populations, and the thickness of lines between nodes represents the number of studies. We conducted a sensitivity analysis by removing the studies one by one to assess the impact of a single study on the overall results. A comparison-adjusted funnel plot and Egger’s test were employed to test for potential publication bias (27,28). The funnel plot is best viewed through the scatter distribution. In the absence of publication bias, the funnel plot should ideally be symmetrical, with small-sample studies scattered at the bottom and large-sample studies concentrated at the top.

Furthermore, the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) was adopted to rank the examined treatments. The SUCRAs ranged from 0 to 100%, with a higher SUCRA indicating a higher ranking for NPC as compared with other treatments (29,30). A league table was created to present the comparisons between each pair of interventions within each outcome. To explore the potential impact of the quality of eligible studies on the results, we intended to use regression analysis with the overall ROB as a covariate to determine if there was a significant difference in the effect size between low- and high-bias studies.

Results

Literature search and screening

The search yielded 1,126 articles. After removing 631 duplicates and excluding 352 articles based on their titles and abstracts, the remaining articles were reviewed in full text. Articles were screened in strict accordance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and 11 articles (31-41) were included in the final analysis. The screening process is shown in Figure 1.

Basic characteristics of the included RCTs

The 11 included RCTs were all conducted in China and involved 983 patients, including 644 males and 339 females. The age of patients was 19 to 78 years, the tumor stages ranged from I to IVb, and the treatment duration was 4 to 12 weeks. TCM injections included AD, FFKS, KSS, SQFZ, LXX, MS, and FFBM. The outcome indicators were CD3+, CD4+, CD8+, and the CD4+:CD8+ ratio. The basic characteristics of the included RCTs are presented in Table 1. Quality assessment results of included studies

The ROB assessment results for the 11 studies are provided in Figure 2. Regarding the bias in the randomization process, 3 studies (33,35,39) were determined to have a potential risk due to a lack of subgroup concealment assessment, and the other 8 studies were evaluated as low risk. Regarding the bias in the deviation from established intervention, 1 study (34) was assessed as possibly at risk because it did not mention the blinding method, and the other 10 studies were evaluated as low risk. All patients in these 10 studies completed the study without dropout, and 2 patients withdrew from 1 study (41), which was detailed in the article. In terms of the bias in the missing outcome data, there was no risk because the proportion of patients with outcomes was greater than 95%. All measurements were objective outcomes, so the ROB was low. With regard to the bias in the selective reporting of outcomes, 1 study (33) did not report data on natural killer cells (NKCs). Although this was not an outcome indicator, the study was considered to be high risk. The other 10 studies were low risk. In conclusion, the ROB in the included studies was low.

Results of the network analysis

CD3+

Nine studies (31-37,39,40), comprising 736 participants, which examined AD + CCRT, FFKS + CCRT, KSS + CCRT, MS + CCRT, LXX + CCRT, and SQFZ + CCRT, reported CD3+ levels as an outcome. The results revealed that AD + CCRT (MD =9.1; 95% CrI: 4.38–13.82), MS + CCRT (MD =5.8; 95% CrI: 1.29–10.31), and SQFZ + CCRT (MD =3.53; 95% CrI: 1.09–5.95) were superior to CCRT alone in improving CD3+ level, and the difference was statistically significant. AD + CCRT was superior to KSS + CCRT (MD =8.48; 95% CrI: 1.17–15.82), LXX + CCRT (MD =6.48; 95% CrI: 0.07–12.92), and SQFZ + CCRT (MD =5.58; 95% CrI: 0.25–10.87) in improving CD3+ level, and the difference exhibited statistical significance. No significant difference was observed for the other paired interventions (Figure 3). The results of the cumulative probability analysis indicated that three optimal interventions for improving CD3+ were AD + CCRT (SUCRA 96.50%), MS + CCRT (SUCRA 76.62%), and FFKS + CCRT (SUCRA 60.29%) (Table 2). The sensitivity analysis indicated that the results were stable. In the funnel plot in Figure 4, the left and right sides are not perfectly symmetrical, indicating a possibility of publication bias. However, Egger’s test (P=0.25) indicated the absence of publication bias.

CD4+

Ten studies (31-40), comprising 796 participants, which examined AD + CCRT, FFKS + CCRT, KSS + CCRT, MS + CCRT, LXX + CCRT, and SQFZ + CCRT, reported CD4+ level as an outcome. The results revealed that AD + CCRT (MD =12.44; 95% CrI: 5.52–19.48), FFKS + CCRT (MD =6.69; 95% CrI: 3.46–11.89), and SQFZ + CCRT (MD =5.53; 95% CrI: 1.57–9.44) were superior to CCRT alone in improving CD4+, and the difference exhibited statistical significance. AD + CCRT (MD =13.57; 95% CrI: 3.83–23.3) was superior to CCRT + LXX in improving CD4+ level, and the difference exhibited statistical significance. FFKS + CCRT (MD =7.7; 95% CrI: 0.82–16.88) was superior to CCRT + LXX in improving CD4+, and the difference exhibited statistical significance. No significant difference was observed with the other paired interventions (Figure 3). The results of the cumulative probability analysis indicated that the three optimal interventions in improving CD4+ level were AD + CCRT (SUCRA 96.51%), FFKS + CCRT (SUCRA 70.97%), and MS + CCRT (SUCRA 62.30%) (Table 2). Sensitivity analysis indicated that the results were stable. In the funnel plot in Figure 5, the left and right sides are not perfectly symmetrical, indicating a possibility of publication bias. Nevertheless, Egger’s test yielded a P value of 0.33, suggesting no publication bias.

CD8+

The results revealed that paired interventions did not show a statistically significant difference (Figure 6). The results of the cumulative probability analysis indicated that the three optimal interventions for improving CD8+ level were KSS + CCRT (SUCRA 83.51%), FFKS + CCRT (SUCRA 62.01%), and MS + CCRT (SUCRA 61.93%) (Table 2). CD4+:CD8+ ratio

Eleven studies (31-41), comprising 953 participants, which examined AD + CCRT, FFKS + CCRT, KSS + CCRT, MS + CCRT, LXX + CCRT, SQFZ + CCRT, and FFBM + CCRT, reported the CD4+:CD8+ ratio as an outcome. The results revealed that the paired interventions did not show a statistically significant difference (Figure 6). The results of the cumulative probability analysis indicated that the three optimal interventions in improving the CD4+:CD8+ ratio were KSS + CCRT (SUCRA 82.80%), AD + CCRT (SUCRA 69.86%), and FFKS + CCRT (SUCRA 68.59%) (Table 2). Sensitivity analysis indicated that the results were stable. In the funnel plot in Figure 8, the left and right sides are not perfectly symmetrical, indicating a possibility of publication bias. However, Egger’s test yielded a P value of 0.40, illustrating no publication bias.

Discussion

The immune system plays a pivotal role in tumor resistance. The immune response and regulation of the human immune system are associated with tumor occurrence, development, invasion, and metastasis. Studies have shown that the antitumor immune response is affected by the metabolic reprogramming of immune and cancer cells (42). Therefore, maintaining and restoring immune function in patients during treatment is crucial to improving therapeutic effects and QOL.

TCM injections have been shown to strengthen the body’s resistance, eliminate pathogenic factors, tonify the middle Jiao and Qi, and promote blood circulation to remove blood stasis. These benefits can help to improve cellular immune status in patients with NPC undergoing CCRT. Additionally, TCM injections can stabilize immune function and suppress tumorigenesis and development. The seven TCM injections included in this NMA have different active components and differ in their immunomodulatory mechanisms. For example, the active components of FFBM injection, including norcantharidin and astragaloside, exhibit beneficial effects such as clearing heat, detoxifying, and resolving masses. These components can also regulate immunity by enhancing the activity of NKCs and lymphokine-activated killer cells, and improving the body’s cellular and humoral immune function (43). MS injection contains red ginseng and Ophiopogon japonicus, which tonify Qi to stabilize collapse, nourish yin, and promote fluids. The saponin components can effectively reduce the damage of radiotherapy to the lymphatic system, thereby improving the peripheral blood T lymphocyte subset (44). AD injection has dual antitumor and immune-regulation functions. Its main component, cantharidin, promotes blood circulation, removes blood stasis, unblocks meridians, and improves circulation. It exhibits anti-cancer properties without producing bone marrow suppression (45). FFKS injection has the effects of clearing heat and dampness, cooling the blood, detoxifying, resolving masses, and relieving pain. Its matrine-type alkaloids have strong antitumor activity, induce tumor cells to differentiate into normal cells, have pro-apoptotic effects, and improve cellular immune function. It is an effective immunomodulator (46). SQFZ injection strengthens the body’s resistance to eliminate pathogenic factors, nourishes Qi and blood, and promotes blood circulation and the removal of blood stasis. Astragalus contains polysaccharides that improve immune function in T cells and increase NKC activity (47). KSS injection primarily contains oxymatrine, an important compound that induces tumor cell apoptosis, inhibits proliferation, reduces tumor angiogenesis, and effectively regulates immune function (48). LXX injection primarily contains β-elemene, which exhibits anti-inflammatory, antitumor, and immunomodulatory properties. It inhibits the DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis of tumor cells. LXX can alter and enhance the immunogenicity of tumor cells and induce and promote the body’s immune response to tumor cells (49). Our NMA of the beneficial effects of TCM injections on the immune function during combination therapy in patients with NPC gathered the most recent data from 11 eligible RCTs. The findings indicated that TCM injections can significantly enhance the levels of peripheral blood T-lymphocyte subsets CD3+ and CD4+ during CCRT treatment in patients with NPC, with AD injection demonstrating the most pronounced effect. KSS injection emerged as the most effective measure for reducing CD8+ level and increasing the CD4+:CD8+ ratio. Although no significant differences were found between KSS and other TCM injections in terms of CD8+ level or CD4+:CD8+ in cells, the NMA guided the selection of drugs to improve immune function in patients with NPC undergoing CCRT.

Meta-analyses have demonstrated that AD injection can markedly enhance cellular immunity and bolster immune indicators, such as CD4+ level, CD4+:CD8+ ratio, NKC abundance, and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) expression, that are compromised by platinum-based chemotherapy. Previous studies (50-52) have also shown that AD injection can drastically improve immune function in the treatment of NSCLC, advanced BC, gynecological oncology, and GC.

AD injection is a TCM injection extracted from cantharides, which is the primary ingredient, along with ginseng, astragalus, and acanthopanax. It has been shown to have the effect of clearing heat and removing toxins, eliminating blood stasis, and dispersing lumps and knots (53). Banmao is derived from the dried Mylabris cichorii Linnaeus or Mylabris phalerata pallas. Its main active ingredient, cantharidin, is a potent cytotoxic agent that can effectively inhibit the growth and proliferation of different cancer cells and accelerate the death of cancer cells in a time- and dose-dependent manner (54,55). Norcantharidin has been shown in vitro to significantly inhibit the growth and induce apoptosis of CNE1 cells (56), induce the apoptosis of CNE2 cells, and inhibit cell proliferation and clonogenicity by downregulating the expression of cyclin B (57). Ginseng is the dried root and rhizome of the ginseng plant, which belongs to the Araliaceae family. Its active ingredients, ginseng polysaccharides and ginsenoside, can enhance nonspecific and specific immune functions (58). Ginsenoside Rg3 has been shown to have a protective effect against cyclophosphamide (CTX)-induced immunosuppression. It can regulate macrophages, T cells, and T helper cell (Th)1–Th2 balance and ameliorate the decline in immunocompetence that occurs after CTX injury (59). Rg3 also enhances the radiosensitivity of the HNE1 and CNE2 cell lines, exhibiting radiosensitizing effects (60). Astragalus is the dried root of Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch.) Bge. var. mongholicus (Bge.) Hsiao or Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch.) Bge. from the Leguminosae family. It contains polysaccharides, saponins, flavonoids, and other active ingredients (61). Studies have demonstrated that the active ingredients of astragalus can exert immunomodulatory and immune-restorative effects in both in vivo and ex vivo settings, significantly enhancing the expression of interleukin-2 receptors on mouse splenocytes and partially restoring the diminished immune function observed in tumor-bearing mice and mice receiving CTX (62). The primary active ingredients in manyprickle acanthopanax root include glycosides such as eleutheroside E, flavonoids such as quercetin and rutin, and other compounds such as organic acids. The extract of the manyprickle acanthopanax root and its active ingredients can exert anti-inflammatory effects and enhance the body’s immunity by stimulating cytokine production, enhancing phagocytosis by macrophages, and modulating the levels of various inflammatory cytokines (63). In an animal experiment, aqueous extracts of manyprickle acanthopanax root were found to increase the number of splenocytes in mice and also to increase the expression of immune-related cytokine TNF-α (64).

Since oxymatrine can inhibit tumor cells and activate the body’s immune function (65), it has been used in the adjuvant treatment of malignancies such as cervical cancer (66), oral cancer (67), and GC (68). It can improve the levels of peripheral blood lymphocyte subpopulations, immunoglobulins, and serum cytokines, as well as enhance the body’s immune function. KSS injection is a pure TCM preparation made from the active ingredients extracted from the roots of leguminous plants such as Sophora flavescens, Sophora alopecuroides, and subprostrate sophora root. The main components are matrine, oxymatrine, sophocarpine, and sophoridine (69). Alkaloids of Sophora flavescens have been shown to have multiple pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory effects, antifibrotic effects, and the prevention of myocardial injury. They additionally demonstrate strong antitumor activity, which can induce the differentiation of tumor cells to normal cells and exert a proapoptotic effect, significantly improving cellular immune function. They are also effective immunomodulators (70,71), and it has been demonstrated that oxymatrine accelerates the production of interferon-γ (IFN-γ) in CD4+ T cells, which is beneficial for enhancing immune function (72). Oxymatrine also promotes the maturation of dendritic cells and mediates the differentiation of T cells into regulatory T cells (73).

In summary, this NMA is the first to examine the beneficial effects of TCM injections on the immune function of patients with NPC during combination therapy. However, it should be noted that this study had certain limitations due to various constraints. Owing to the insufficient number of relevant studies, the SUCRA-based ranking of this NMA should be viewed with caution. For instance, only one study examined the impact of AD injection on enhancing CD3+ and CD4+ levels in NPC patients following CCRT, and the treatment was ranked as the most effective. Further clinical studies are necessary to substantiate its efficacy.

Despite the inclusion of RCTs within the present NMA, several limitations warrant consideration. First, none of the included studies employed blinding, which may have introduced bias. Second, although both Chinese and English databases were searched, the relatively small number of included studies necessitates cautious interpretation of the results. Furthermore, since the included literature originated exclusively from China, the results may not be generalizable to non-Chinese populations due to potential differences in genetics, lifestyle, healthcare practices, and traditional medicine use. Third, limitations in the extracted data precluded detailed subgroup analyses. Future trials should prospectively collect and report these data to enable more precise subgroup assessments. Fourth, several interventions were supported by only a few studies, limiting the interpretation of the findings.

A previous study on the efficacy of TCM injections combined with CCRT in treating NPC using multidimensional Bayesian NMA revealed that LXX injection combined with CCRT demonstrated optimal clinical outcomes (17). Moreover, Kangai injection combined with CCRT was found to exhibit a more comprehensive effect in enhancing the condition of patients with NPC and improving adverse reactions (17). In our study, we found that AD and KSS injections had the most significant impact on enhancing the immune function of patients with NPC undergoing CCRT. According to previous research, AD injection also exerts a positive effect on clinical efficacy, physical status, and alleviation of leukopenia and oral mucositis (17). Considering these findings, we recommend AD injection as an adjuvant drug to CCRT in treating NPC to minimize the impact on immune function.

Due to the limited long-term safety and immune monitoring data currently available, additional trials incorporating long-term follow-up and standardized safety assessments are necessary to validate the observed effects.

Conclusions

This NMA revealed that in comparison to CCRT alone, the combination of TCM injections and CCRT can markedly enhance immune function in patients with NPC. AD injection was found to be the most effective in improving CD3+ and CD4+ levels, and KSS injection was the most effective in improving the CD8+ level and the CD4+:CD8+ ratio. However, due to the limited number and quality of included articles, more high-quality, large-scale double-blind RCTs are necessary to substantiate the results.

Assessment of Load in Replacement of Bilateral Edentulous Mandibular Posteriors by Precision Attachments: A Clinical Study
Authors: Pritha Negi, Lakshmi Priyanka Chilukuri, Sujeet Patil, Nag Bhushan Mandal, Sanghamitra Jena, V. S. Deepasri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:To evaluate the distribution of occlusal load and functional outcomes in patients receiving precision attachments for the replacement of bilateral edentulous mandibular posteriors.Materials and Methods:A clinical study was conducted involving 30 participants with bilateral edentulous mandibular posteriors. Custom precision attachments were fabricated and assessed for their load distribution using a pressure-mapping system. Functional outcomes, including patient satisfaction and masticatory efficiency, were measured using validated questionnaires and clinical tests.Results:Precision attachments demonstrated uniform load distribution across the prosthesis, reducing stress on residual ridges. High levels of patient satisfaction and significant improvement in masticatory efficiency were observed. Statistical analysis showed a correlation between load distribution and patient-reported outcomes (P < 0.05).Conclusion:Precision attachments provide an effective solution for load distribution in bilateral edentulous mandibular posteriors, improving function and comfort for patients.

INTRODUCTION

Edentulism in the mandibular posterior region is a prevalent condition, often resulting from periodontal disease, trauma, or tooth decay. This condition not only compromises oral aesthetics and functionality but also leads to progressive bone loss and diminished quality of life if left untreated.[12] Traditional prosthetic solutions, such as removable partial dentures (RPDs), often fall short in providing optimal stability, retention, and load distribution, resulting in discomfort and reduced masticatory efficiency for patients.[3] Precision attachments have emerged as a viable alternative, offering enhanced retention, stability, and functional performance. These attachments distribute occlusal forces more evenly, reducing stress on residual ridges and preserving oral structures.[4] Despite their growing popularity, the clinical evidence evaluating their biomechanical and functional advantages in mandibular posterior rehabilitation remains limited. This study aims to assess the effectiveness of precision attachments in load distribution and functional outcomes, contributing valuable insights to prosthodontic practice.[5]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This prospective clinical study was conducted at a tertiary dental care center. Thirty participants aged 40–70 years, with bilateral edentulous mandibular posteriors, were enrolled. Inclusion criteria included adequate bone height and width to support attachments, while exclusion criteria comprised systemic conditions affecting oral health or previous prosthetic failures.

Fabrication of precision attachments

Custom-made precision attachments were fabricated using nickel-chromium alloys for durability. The attachments were incorporated into removable partial dentures designed to optimize occlusal load distribution. Pretreatment and post-treatment casts were prepared for each patient.

Load distribution assessment

A pressure-mapping system (Tekscan, USA) was used to evaluate occlusal load distribution during mastication. Measurements were taken at baseline (without attachments) and after 3 months of use. Load data were recorded and analyzed for uniformity and peak pressure points.

Functional outcome measures

Functional outcomes were assessed through:

Masticatory Efficiency: Evaluated using the mixing ability test and comminution of color-indicating gum.Patient Satisfaction: Measured through a validated questionnaire addressing comfort, aesthetics, and chewing efficiency on a 5-point Likert scale.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Paired t-tests compared pretreatment and post-treatment values, with P values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

The data presented in Table 1 demonstrate a significant improvement in occlusal load distribution after the use of precision attachments. The peak pressure values reduced from 12.4 ± 3.2 N/cm2 at baseline to 6.8 ± 1.9 N/cm2 post-treatment, indicating a more even distribution of masticatory forces across the prosthesis. Similarly, the uniformity index, reflecting the proportionality of load distribution, increased from 65% ±8% to 92% ±5%. This highlights the ability of precision attachments to mitigate stress on residual ridges and enhance the biomechanical performance of the prosthetic design. The findings underscore the clinical significance of precision attachments in providing stability and comfort, which are critical for long-term success in prosthodontic rehabilitation [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

The findings of this study highlight the clinical advantages of precision attachments in managing bilateral edentulous mandibular posteriors. The results demonstrated a significant reduction in peak occlusal pressures and an improvement in uniformity of load distribution, aligning with previous research that emphasized the biomechanical benefits of precision attachments.[12] By reducing stress on residual ridges, precision attachments contribute to the preservation of underlying bone and soft tissues, which is critical for long-term prosthetic success.[3]

The improvement in masticatory efficiency and patient satisfaction underscores the functional benefits of precision attachments. Enhanced stability and retention provided by these attachments allow for more effective mastication and greater patient comfort, as supported by previous studies on functional outcomes in prosthodontics.[45] Moreover, the increase in patient-reported satisfaction scores reflects the aesthetic and usability benefits of precision attachments, which are vital components of prosthetic rehabilitation.[6]

Load distribution plays a pivotal role in prosthodontic success, as uneven load can lead to discomfort, prosthetic instability, and accelerated ridge resorption.[7] The pressure-mapping data in this study revealed a significant decrease in peak pressures and an improvement in uniformity indices after the integration of precision attachments. These findings are consistent with the principles of biomechanical optimization in removable partial dentures.[8]

Patient demographics and individual anatomical variations are important considerations in treatment planning. This study’s findings suggest that precision attachments are particularly beneficial for patients with Kennedy Class I partial edentulism, where the posterior edentulous spans require enhanced support and stability.[910] The versatility of precision attachments in accommodating various clinical scenarios makes them an invaluable tool in modern prosthodontics.[11]

Despite the positive outcomes, this study has limitations, including a relatively small sample size and short follow-up duration. Future studies with larger cohorts and extended observation periods are warranted to validate these findings and explore long-term outcomes.[12] In addition, comparative studies involving alternative prosthetic designs could provide further insights into the relative advantages of precision attachments.

CONCLUSION

Precision attachments provide a reliable solution for the rehabilitation of bilateral edentulous mandibular posteriors. This study demonstrates their effectiveness in optimizing load distribution, improving masticatory efficiency, and enhancing patient satisfaction. These findings emphasize the importance of integrating precision attachments into prosthodontic treatment plans to achieve functional and aesthetic success. Further research with larger sample sizes and extended follow-up is recommended to confirm these outcomes.

Safety and performance of the Hydra self-expanding THV: 6 months outcomes from the GENESIS-II study
Authors: Anmol Sonawane, Praveen Chandra, John Jose, Sandeep Bansal, Rahul Gupta, Krishnankutty Sudhir, Nagendra Boopathy Senguttuvan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AimThe Hydra THV incorporates an active release mechanism designed to facilitate the deployment of the supra-annular valve. The aim of the GENESIS-II study was to assess the safety and performance of Hydra THV at 6 months follow-up after treating severe aortic stenosis in patients at high surgical risk.MethodsThis prospective, multicenter, non-randomized, investigational study was conducted between November 2021 and November 2023. The study enrolled 40 patients with high surgical risk and symptomatic severe aortic stenosis from 19 sites across India. The primary safety endpoint of the study was cardiovascular mortality at 30 days, while the primary performance endpoint was device success, as defined by VARC-2.ResultsAmong the 40 patients, the mean age was 74.4 ± 6.7 years, 65% were male, and the mean Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) risk score was 3.5 ± 1.6%. The device had a success rate of 95%. At 6 months, there was one cardiovascular death (2.5 %). All-cause death was observed in 5% patients. The incidence of new permanent pacemaker implantation was 7.9%. There was a progressive enhancement in effective orifice area, from 0.6 ± 0.2 cm2 at baseline to 1.9 ± 0.4 cm2 at 6 months (p < 0.001). Similarly, the mean aortic valve gradient significantly improved from 53.1 ± 12.5 mm Hg at baseline to 8.7 ± 3.3 mmHg at 6 months (p < 0.001) post-intervention.ConclusionThe results of 6 months follow-up after implantation of the Hydra THV with an active release mechanism in the GENESIS-II study demonstrated a high device success rate with favorable safety and performance of the device.

Introduction

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), has greatly transformed the management of aortic stenosis, particularly for patients with high-surgical risk. This significant shift in treatment approach is supported by robust evidence from numerous randomized clinical trials comparing TAVI with traditional surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR)0.1, 2, 3 Pivotal trials have demonstrated that TAVI is non-inferior to SAVR regarding composite endpoints of mortality, stroke, and rehospitalization, even among patients with low surgical risk profiles.4,5 Moreover, current clinical guidelines strongly recommend aortic valve replacement as a class I recommendation for symptomatic patients with severe aortic stenosis and for asymptomatic patients with severe stenosis who exhibit left ventricular ejection fraction below 50%, demonstrate positive stress tests, or have other indications warranting cardiac intervention.6,7

Modern transcatheter heart valve (THV) systems have achieved remarkable improvements in procedural safety, hemodynamic performance, and clinical outcomes, driving the global expansion of TAVR procedures. Despite these advances, certain challenges persist in the TAVR landscape, including vascular access complications, bleeding events,8,9 conduction disturbances necessitating permanent pacemaker implantation10 and paravalvular leak (PVL) of moderate or severe degree.11 In response, manufacturers have directed substantial resources toward refining THV systems to improve overall clinical outcomes and to address these limitations while preserving the established benefits of transcatheter approaches.12 The combination of technological refinements evolving into new generation of valves, growing operator expertise, and enhanced procedural techniques has progressively reduced the incidence of these complications.13

Commercially available self-expanding (SE) and balloon-expandable (BE) valves represent the two primary types of THVs used for TAVR. Comparative analyses between self-expanding and balloon-expandable valve platforms have revealed distinct performance characteristics, with balloon-expandable valves showing advantages in device success and lower pacemaker rates in the short term, while self-expanding designs demonstrate superior forward-flow hemodynamics at longer-term follow-up, suggesting potential benefits for specific patient populations.14

Within this context of ongoing technological evolution, the GENESIS trial evaluated the efficacy of the Hydra THV (Vascular Innovations Co Ltd, Nonthaburi, Thailand, a subsidiary of Sahajanand Medical Technologies Limited, India) with its original delivery system.15 The GENESIS-II study was subsequently designed to evaluate the safety profile and clinical performance of the Hydra THV system, with a novel active release mechanism for deploying this supra-annular valve. The 30-day results of this study have been previously published16; here, we report the clinical efficacy and safety of the Hydra THV in GENESIS-II study at 6 months follow-up.

Methods

Study design and patients

The GENESIS-II was a prospective, multicenter, non-randomized, investigational study conducted Between November 2021 and November 2023, and 40 patients with severe aortic stenosis and high surgical risk were enrolled across 19 centers throughout India. Inclusion criteria were age 65 years or older; STS Predicted Risk of Mortality score ≥3% (or <3% with additional frailty markers and/or comorbidities); appropriately sized aortic annulus diameter (17.0-20.0 mm for Hydra 22, 20.0-23.5 mm for Hydra 26, and 23.5-27.0 mm for Hydra 30); degenerative aortic stenosis (with or without more than mild aortic regurgitation) confirmed by echocardiography (valve area <1.0 cm2, mean gradient ≥40 mmHg, peak velocity >4.0 m/s, or DVI ≤0.25); left ventricular ejection fraction exceeding 20%; and symptomatic presentation with NYHA functional classification of II or higher. Exclusion criteria were patients with congenital bicuspid valves, severe mitral/tricuspid regurgitation, moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis, aortic root measurements outside the 26-36 mm range, LVEF below 20%, or life expectancy under 24 months.

Study device

The Hydra transcatheter heart valve combines a self-expanding nitinol frame with three bovine pericardial leaflets positioned supra-annularly to optimize the effective orifice area and minimize transvalvular pressure gradients. A specialized high sealing skirt at the inflow portion helps prevent paravalvular leakage, whereas the non-flared inflow design aims to reduce the conduction system disruption. The outflow section features three anchoring tentacles that facilitate the attachment of the delivery catheter and improve aortic conformability. Mid-frame open cells (≥15-F) allow coronary artery access and enhance delivery catheter flexibility. Dual marker sets on the frame provide visual guidance for precise implantation. The device is available in three sizes (22, 26, and 30 mm), accommodating native annular diameters from 17 to 27 mm. The GENESIS-II study utilized an advanced Hydra THV variant with an active tentacle release mechanism, distinguishing it from previous iterations.

Follow-up

Clinical evaluations were performed at 30 days and 6 months post-procedure. The follow-ups documented adverse events, medication regimens, echocardiographic measurements, 6-min walk test performance, NYHA classification status, and permanent pacemaker requirements.

Endpoints

The primary safety measure was cardiovascular mortality at 30 days, while the primary performance endpoint was device success according to VARC-2 definitions. Secondary endpoints included all-cause mortality, kidney injury, bleeding complications, myocardial infarction, vascular access complications, periprocedural encephalopathy, stroke events (all types), transient ischemic attacks, and permanent pacemaker implantation rates at discharge, 30 days and 6 months. Functional improvement was assessed by comparing baseline with 30 days and 6 months’ 6-min walk distances and NYHA classifications. Hemodynamic improvement was evaluated through changes in effective orifice area and mean aortic gradient from baseline through 6 months.

Statistical analysis

The continuous variables are presented as means with standard deviations and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. The assumption of normality for paired differences in Effective orifice Area and Mean Aortic Gradient was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test, Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, and by examining skewness and kurtosis. The distribution demonstrated borderline deviation from normality. Given the sample size (n = 40), the paired t-test was applied, as this test is considered robust to moderate non-normality. To ensure robustness, a nonparametric sensitivity analysis using the Wilcoxon signed rank test was also performed, which yielded consistent results. Therefore, results are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Statistical significance was established at p < 0.05, with all analyses performed using R software version 4.3.3.

Discussion

The main findings of study are: 1) The Hydra valve continued to demonstrate its excellent hemodynamic performance with significant improvements in effective orifice area and mean gradient without moderate/severe paravalvular leak at 6 months; 2) Clinical outcomes were favorable with low all-cause mortality (5.0% at 6 months), a relatively low permanent pacemaker implantation rate (7.5%), and no stroke events throughout the 6 months follow-up period; 3) Marked functional improvement was noted with 94.6% of patients improved by ≥ 1 NYHA class at 6 months follow-up, these improvements are consistent with expected post-TAVR recovery.

Development of new THVs have distinct learning curves that impact early outcomes. New devices typically show less favorable results initially that improve gradually as operator experience increases.17,18 Additionally, technological improvements in Hydra THVs, with an active release mechanism for tentacle deployment, distinguishing it from the Hydra THV used in earlier studies, procedural refinements and optimized peri-procedural management aid in overcoming the learning curve leading to better performance and outcomes. When procedural and clinical endpoints at 6 months follow-up for patients treated with Hydra THV in GENESIS Trial15 and GENESIS II study were compared, interesting differences emerged. The device success rate was higher (95.0% vs. 92.5%), cardiovascular mortality was lower (2.5% vs. 7.5%), and there was an absence of major vascular complications (0% vs. 2.5%) in the GENESIS-II study compared to the GENESIS study at 6 months follow-up, though the differences were not statistically significant. The GENESIS-II study population demonstrated comparable hemodynamic outcomes and a substantial reduction in the duration of hospital stay (4 days vs. 7 days), with a significantly increased 6-min walk test distance in comparison to the GENESIS arm at 6 months.

Self-expanding valves are the preferred choice when left ventricular outflow tract calcification is present, as they reduce the risk of annular rupture. However, they may show higher rates of mild paravalvular leak (PVL) due to non-uniform expansion and deployment characteristics.13 Importantly, moderate or severe PVL and conduction disturbances are associated with increased mortality.19,20 Taking this into account, precise valve positioning aided by later designs offering depth markers repositionability and retrievability, such as with the Hydra THV, enables operators to make necessary corrections during implantation, thereby minimizing PVL rates and conduction disturbances. In this study, there were no incidences of moderate or severe PVL observed after the procedure through 6 months follow-up. The self-expanding Evolut R THV and the balloon expandable Myval THV had demonstrated 20% and 7% incidences of more than or equal to moderate PVL, respectively at 6 months in the EVAL registry.21 In the BIOVALVE I and II trials, moderate to severe PVL was observed in 2.3% patients who underwent implantation of self-expanding Biovalve THV, at 6 months follow-up.22

The literature suggests that self-expanding valve prostheses require more new pacemaker implantations than balloon-expandable valves, although this gap is narrowing with newer devices.14 Nevertheless, with adjustments in valve positioning through cusp-overlap viewing methods for self-expanding valves, and elevated deployment approaches for both balloon-expandable and self-expanding valves, there is a successful decrease in conduction disruptions and the need for permanent pacemaker implantation.23, 24, 25 With ongoing refinement of implantation strategies and valve engineering improvements, the frequency of permanent pacemaker dependency following the TAVR procedures may continue to decrease regardless of the type of device used.26 On the same note, in the EVAL registry, the proportion of PPI at 6 months was significantly higher in self-expanding Evolut R group (27.5%) than the balloon-expandable Myval group (11%).21 In this study the rate of new PPI at 6 months was 7.5%. Though Hydra is a self-expanding valve, the rate of PPI has been comparable to balloon expandable valves (4.0% and 7.1%, respectively).27,28

The cardiovascular mortality at 6 months after implantation of Hydra THV in GENESIS II study (2.5%) was comparable to the rate of various other THVs at 6 months follow-up (Table 4).12,15,21,22 Additionally there were no reported cases of stroke, life threatening bleeding or myocardial infarction up to 6 months follow-up in this study. Favorable aortic-valve hemodynamics likely contributed to the absence of symptoms and the clinical outcomes that were observed. The main limitations of our study are the lack of a comparator arm and small sample size. The relatively short-term follow-up period limits any solid conclusion on durability of the valve. Echocardiographic assessments were site-reported and were not evaluated by an independent core laboratory. Additionally, clinical events were not adjudicated by an independent clinical events committee.

Conclusion

The GENESIS-II study demonstrated that the Hydra THV with its active release mechanism achieved a high device success rate of in treating severe aortic stenosis in high-risk patients. Safety outcomes were favorable at 6 months follow-up. Significant hemodynamic improvements were observed, and functional outcomes were positive, confirming the Hydra THV as a promising option for high-risk patients with severe aortic stenosis. Future studies with longer term follow up and randomized designs are required to assess valve durability and comparative effectiveness against other available TAVI valves.

Incidental Detection of Giardia duodenalis via Cytologic Sampling in Pancreatic Tumor Investigation
Authors: Yoshiro Hadano, Koji Endo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACT Giardia duodenalis can be incidentally detected during pancreatic tumor evaluation via cytology. Even in asymptomatic patients without risk factors, recognition is essential. Careful cytologic examination enables early treatment to prevent complications before surgery. Although rare, incidental detection is clinically significant and guides preoperative management.

Case

A 63‐year‐old Japanese man was referred to our hospital for evaluation of a low‐density lesion in the pancreatic head detected on computed tomography, performed during the workup of persistent back pain lasting for 1 week. He reported an unintentional weight loss of 2 kg over the past year, although his appetite remained preserved. He denied fever, abdominal pain, or abdominal distension. His past medical history was unremarkable. Computed tomographic scan showed an 18 mm well‐defined low‐density lesion in the pancreatic head and no metastasis findings. On Day 3, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) and brushing cytology were performed. Microscopy revealed numerous pear‐shaped, binucleated organisms suggestive of Giardia duodenalis (Figure 1). Iodine staining of a stool specimen confirmed the presence of G. duodenalis cysts (Figure 2).

The patient had no history of overseas travel, consumption of untreated water, or gastrointestinal symptoms. On Day 4, he received a 5‐day course of metronidazole. Repeat stool examination was negative. On Day 14, the patient underwent pancreaticoduodenectomy, and histopathology revealed a solid pseudopapillary neoplasm with no evidence of Giardia.

Discussion

Giardiasis represents the most prevalent intestinal protozoal infection globally, with an estimated prevalence of 2% among adults and 8% among children in developed nations [1]. Giardia trophozoites preferentially colonize the proximal small intestine, where the duodenal pH and exposure to conjugated bile salts facilitate excystation and trophozoite survival. Bile salts also play a critical role in encystation as trophozoites migrate distally [2].

This case illustrates the incidental detection of G. duodenalis during pancreatic tumor investigation. Although symptomatic giardiasis is rare in developed countries like Japan, asymptomatic colonization may occur. Cytological sampling of the duodenum or periampullary region may incidentally uncover protozoal organisms [3]. Clinicians should be aware of this possibility, especially when examining biliary or pancreatic tissues.

The presence of Giardia had no clinical impact in this patient but underscores the diagnostic value of cytology. Timely identification allowed appropriate therapy prior to surgery. Awareness of parasitic mimics in cytologic specimens is essential for accurate interpretation and patient care.

Comparison of robot-assisted and laparoscopic pyeloplasty for primary pediatric ureteropelvic junction obstruction: a retrospective multicenter study
Authors: Jianhua Wen, Guilong Chen, Yangyang Wu, Zelin Cui, Jungan Liao, Xiaoyu Yi, Xiaowei Zhang, Hongshuai Jia, Yang Zhao, Dehong Liu, Pin Li, Huixia Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to compare the clinical outcomes and cost effectiveness of robotic-assisted laparoscopic pyeloplasty (RALP) with laparoscopic pyeloplasty (LP) in children. Retrospective review of patients who underwent RALP and LP for ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO) between October 2020 and March 2023. Patients were stratified into two groups based on the surgical approach: LP group (n = 126) and RALP group (n = 132). Baseline characteristics, perioperative data, success rate, operator and assistant comfort levels, and hospitalization costs were assessed in both groups. All surgeries were successfully completed without any open conversions. There were no significant differences between the LP and RALP groups regarding operative time (116.00 ± 13.86 min vs. 117.60 ± 9.91 min, P = 0.285), estimated blood loss [10.0 (5.0, 20.0) mL vs. 10.0 (5.0, 20.0) mL, P = 0.936 ], overall complications (22.22% vs. 23.48%, P = 0.809), and success rates (96.03% vs. 95.45%, P = 0.937). Operator and assistant comfort scores were significantly higher in the RALP group compared to the LP group (8.00 ± 0.82 vs. 5.50 ± 1.11, P < 0.001 and 7.21 ± 0.92 vs. 6.00 ± 0.82, P < 0.001, respectively). The hospitalization cost was significantly higher in the RALP group (56.70 ± 12.82 thousand yuan) compared to the LP group (27.78 ± 9.28 thousand yuan) (P < 0.001). LP and RALP are safe and effective surgical approaches for managing primary UPJO in children. Introduction

Ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO) is a common cause of upper urinary tract obstruction in pediatric patients. Open pyeloplasty (OP) has traditionally served as the primary therapeutic option for UPJO, renowned for its well-established efficacy and applicability to diverse forms of pathological obstructions since its initial introduction1,2. The increasing popularity and widespread adoption of minimally invasive methods has prompted the transformation of pyeloplasty into laparoscopic and robotic approaches, thanks to their well-documented clinical benefits, such as decreased pain, shorter hospital stays, faster recovery, and improved cosmetic results3–7. However, the widespread implementation of traditional laparoscopic surgery is impeded by its high technical requirements and extended learning process8.

Robot-assisted surgery (RAS) mitigates the technical limitations of traditional laparoscopic procedures and has gained global acceptance9. Nonetheless, there is a lack of definitive agreement on the optimal approach for treating UPJO. Some studies suggest that robotic-assisted laparoscopic pyeloplasty (RALP) offers advantages over laparoscopic pyeloplasty (LP) in terms of reduced postoperative complication rates, shorter operative times, and shorter hospital stays6,7,10. Conversely, other research has found no significant differences between the two approaches in key perioperative outcomes such as operative time and complication rates, with RALP being notably more expensive11. Furthermore, the majority of existing literature on RAS recognizes the significant limitation imposed by the high cost of hospitalization12–15. Will LP swiftly be overtaken by RALP in the era of minimally invasive surgery? How can we effectively balance the clinical benefits and healthcare costs associated with RAS within the context of the new healthcare policy? In this study, we conducted a multicenter retrospective study to evaluate and compare the clinical outcomes and cost effectiveness of RALP and LP in terms of operative time, estimated blood loss, postoperative hospital stay, complications, success rate, comfort levels of the operator and assistant, and costs.

Patients and methods

After receiving approval from the Institutional Review Board of Chinese PLA General Hospital (No. 2025-010-191), a collaborative retrospective study involving three centers was initiated. The medical records of children who underwent LP and RALP from October 2020 to March 2023 were compiled at each participating institution. Inclusion criteria: (1) Patients with UPJO undergoing primary surgical intervention; (2) Who received LP or RALP; (3) Aged between 1 and 16 years. Exclusion criteria: (1) Patients with complex anatomical variations such as horseshoe, duplex, or ectopic kidneys; (2) Redo procedures; (3) With bilateral UPJO; (4) Presenting with comorbidities like vesicoureteral reflux, ureterovesical junction obstruction, posterior urethral valves, or lower urinary tract dysfunction.

Criteria for surgery included: (1) Progressive hydronephrosis observed on sequential ultrasonography; (2) Split renal function (SRF) < 40% on initial or sequential 99mTc DTPA scans; (3) Deterioration or persistence of obstructed drainage on sequential 99mTc DTPA scans; (4) Symptomatic hydronephrosis such as flank pain and recurrent urinary tract infections. The decision to perform LP or RALP was made based on the preference of the patients’ guardians. All three participating centers are high-volume pediatric urology institutions in China. All procedures were performed by three skilled urologists, each with over ten years of experience in laparoscopic surgery and a track record of more than 100 robotic surgeries. Informed consent was obtained from all participants before the surgical procedure. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Surgical techniques

Under general anesthesia, all procedures were conducted using the transperitoneal approach, with patients placed in a lateral decubitus position ranging from 45° to 60°. A 5-mm or 10-mm port was inserted through the umbilicus to accommodate a 30° optic, while two 5-mm ports were placed approximately 3 cm above and below the umbilicus in the LP group. Additionally, a 5 mm assistant trocar was placed at the epigastrium when required. In robot-assisted procedures, three robotic ports were positioned along the median line, with one 5 mm assistant port located at the abdominal horizontal crease on the healthy side.

The surgical procedures were nearly identical in both LP and RALP. Upon incising the Toldt line and lowering the colon, the dilated renal pelvis was detected and isolated, together with the ureteropelvic junction and the cranial portion of the ureter. The ureteropelvic junction was fully exposed, a percutaneous hitch stitch was placed in the upper corner of the renal pelvis, and the renal pelvis was obliquely cut approximately 2 cm away from the renal parenchyma’s edge. Subsequently, the pelvis was completely dismembered, and the ureter was longitudinally spatulated and extended inferiorly through its narrow portion for about 2 cm. A dismembered Anderson-Hynes pyeloplasty was performed using 5 − 0 and 6 − 0 absorbable sutures with continuous suturing in all cases. Subsequently, a DJ stent was anterogradely inserted into the ureter through the supraumbilical trocar (for LP) or assistant ports (for RALP). Postoperatively, a drainage tube was inserted. Operative time was defined as the duration from the initial incision to skin closure, encompassing port placement and, for RALP, robot docking and undocking.

Postoperative management

The Foley catheter was removed two days post-surgery, with the drain being removed in cases of minimal (< 30 mL) output within 24 h after Foley removal. The DJ stent was extracted eight weeks after the operation. Ultrasound and urine tests were conducted during outpatient visits at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post-surgery, with subsequent annual check-ups. At 6 months postoperatively, a ⁹⁹mTc-DTPA renal scintigraphy was performed to evaluate renal function recovery. Intraoperative conditions and postoperative follow-up data were collected for both groups, including any subsequent endoscopic procedures or repeat pyeloplasties over the entire follow-up period.

Success was determined by symptomatic relief, improved drainage, and absence of functional evidence of obstruction on 99mTc DTPA scan. Postoperative complications were categorized based on the Clavien-Dindo grading system16. Patients who required secondary interventions (endoscopic surgery or repeat pyeloplasty) during the follow-up period were classified as treatment failures for the purpose of the primary success rate analysis. The comfort levels of the operator and assistant were evaluated immediately upon completion of the surgical procedure using a self-developed Visual Analogue Scale for comfort (VAS-C). The VAS-C comprised a 50 mm ruler. The starting point was marked with a score of 10, indicating a very comfortable state with no sense of fatigue. The end point was marked with a score of 0, representing extreme fatigue, which was the maximum physical exertion that the tester could endure.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 10.1.2. Categorical variables were presented as percentages, and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR). Group comparisons utilized the chi-squared test and Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables. A p-value below 0.05 was deemed statistically significant throughout the study.

Clavien-Dindo complications showed no significant differences for Grade Ⅰ-Ⅱ in 18.94% of RALP patients and 18.25% of LP patients (P = 0.888), and for Grade Ⅲ-Ⅳ in 4.55% of RALP patients and 3.97% of LP patients (P = 0.937). Following the surgical procedures, fever was noted in 17 patients from the LP group and 20 patients from the RALP group. Flank pain was experienced by six patients in the LP group and five patients in the RALP group during the period with a DJ stent. All patients exhibited improvement after receiving symptomatic treatment. One patient in the RALP group needed to replace a DJ stent because the DJ tube was accidentally pulled out while removing the urinary catheter. During the follow-up period, three patients in each group required endoscopic balloon dilation for recurrent urinary tract infections and obstruction. Two patients in both groups underwent a repeat pyeloplasty for recurrent strictures. Operator comfort scores were significantly higher in the RALP group (8.00 ± 0.82) compared to the LP group (5.50 ± 1.11) (P < 0.001). Assistant comfort scores were also significantly higher in the RALP group (7.21 ± 0.92) than in the LP group (6.00 ± 0.82) (P < 0.001). The total hospitalization cost was markedly higher in the RALP group (56.70 ± 12.82 thousand yuan) in comparison to the LP group (27.78 ± 9.28 thousand yuan) (P < 0.001). Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in APD and SRF after surgery (P < 0.001). There were no statistically significant differences in the mean changes in APD (ΔAPD: − 1.95 ± 0.64 cm vs. − 1.94 ± 0.80 cm, P = 0.832) and SRF (ΔSRF: 7.20 ± 4.11% vs. 7.41 ± 4.13%, P = 0.684). There was no significant difference in success rates between the LP group (96.03%) and the RALP group (95.45%) (P = 0.937), with mean follow-up durations of 42.97 ± 8.01 months and 44.35 ± 7.94 months, respectively (P = 0.166).

Discussion

With technological advancements, surgical procedures have evolved rapidly from conventional open surgeries to minimally invasive surgeries. LP and RALP have emerged as the preferred surgical approaches for treating UPJO, due to their high success rates, minimal invasiveness, quick postoperative recovery, and favorable patient satisfaction. However, LP is widely acknowledged as a demanding procedure that mandates advanced laparoscopic skills, particularly in intracorporeal suturing and knotting17. Moreover, the surgery’s poor ergonomics contribute to discomfort for the surgeon, further complicating the process. The procedure’s high technical complexity and prolonged learning curve have hindered its widespread acceptance within the pediatric urology community8.

The introduction of robotic surgical technology has opened new horizons. Since the initial series of RALP was reported by Gettman et al. in 200218, RALP has undergone substantial evolution. RALP, attributed to the superior 3D visualization, simplified suturing procedures, and superior ergonomic support for surgeons, not only presents the benefits of traditional laparoscopy in terms of minimal perioperative morbidity, but it also exhibits a faster learning curve and enhanced technical advantages compared to conventional laparoscopy12. A national trends study conducted in the United States from 2003 to 2015 revealed a yearly decrease in LP of 12% and a corresponding annual growth in RALP of 29%. By 2015, RALP represented 40% of total cases, with adolescents accounting for 84% of these cases12. In Germany, the proportion of open pyeloplasty cases declined from 79.5% in 2006 to 17.6% in 2022, while robotic-assisted procedures saw an increase from 0.3% in 2006 to 35.9% in 202219.

The precise manipulation of robotic arms presents significant advantages in tasks such as dissecting, tailoring renal pelvis and ureter tissues, and placing DJ stents, leading to a decrease in surgeon fatigue. Numerous authors have highlighted this particular aspect13,20. Dalager et al. reported that conventional laparoscopic surgery requires long-term static muscle activity with a high physical workload for surgeons, in contrast to robotic-assisted laparoscopic surgery21. Rasool et al. observed that conventional laparoscopy led to significantly greater levels of surgeon fatigue compared to robotic pyeloplasty1. Our study’s findings also indicated a significant increase in comfort ratings for both the operator and the assistant in the robotic group, as opposed to the laparoscopic group. Moreover, robotic-assisted laparoscopic surgery presents evident advantages for delicate tasks such as suturing, with multiple studies emphasizing the substantial benefits brought by robotic arms in the suturing process. Gettman et al. discovered that the suturing duration was 41.5 min for RALP, whereas it amounted to 74 min for LP20. Esposito et al. observed a decrease in suturing time for RALP from 105.5 min to 79 min compared to LP13. All of this has eased the shift from open surgery to minimally invasive procedures, particularly beneficial for individuals with limited proficiency in traditional laparoscopic surgery22,23.

Despite the widespread use of the da Vinci system in urologic surgery, there remain significant questions regarding the clinical benefit and cost effectiveness of the robotic surgical platform. Complications and success rates are crucial patient outcome variables that determine the favorability or discouragement of a procedure. Intraoperatively, we did not encounter any complications in both the LP and RALP groups. Postoperatively, there were 59 complications: 28 in the LP group (22.22%) and 31 in the RALP group (23.48%), showing no statistically significant difference. The success rates did not differ significantly, with rates of 95.45% in the RALP group and 96.03% in the LP group (P = 0.937). Recent studies have also found no significant variances between LP and RALP in terms of success and complication rates11,13, aligning with the outcomes of our study. Operative time and intraoperative estimated blood loss are the other key metrics to make surgical decisions. Our study found no significant differences between the LP and RALP groups in terms of these parameters. Sun et al. conducted a meta-analysis and systematic review of RALP for pediatric UPJO, finding that both RALP and LP had similar operative times and estimated blood loss24. Malhotra et al. reported that RALP had significantly longer operative times and hospital stays compared to LP (p < 0.001)15. Those researchers and our results have shown that RALP offers no clinical advantages over LP, except for providing greater comfort for surgeons.

Why don’t the theoretical benefits of RAS, with advantages in enhanced dexterity and improved visualization, translate into meaningful clinical improvements for pediatric patients undergoing pyeloplasty? This may be attributed to the following reasons. Firstly, in numerous hospitals, the individuals performing LP are experienced laparoscopic surgeons. All the surgeons participating in this study are proficient urologists with more than 10 years of laparoscopic surgery experience. Outcomes may vary for those with limited experience in laparoscopic surgery. Secondly, this phenomenon could be attributed to the implementation of a standardized training program for surgeons, which encompasses instruction in laparoscopic simulation (dry-lab) and live animal experiments. This training regimen facilitates swift skill acquisition and proficiency enhancement in laparoscopic surgery. Notably, in densely populated countries such as China, surgeons have access to abundant clinical opportunities to enhance their laparoscopic techniques. Furthermore, we specifically focused on UPJO patients who underwent primary pyeloplasty. Patients with complex UPJOs, such as those with concomitant horseshoe kidney, duplicated kidney malformation, and those who underwent redo pyeloplasty, were excluded from this study. Numerous studies have shown that RAS provides greater benefits in instances involving intricate ureteral strictures25,26. It is important to note that the study results may be influenced by the inclusion of these complex UPJO patients, and additional analyses are planned for future research endeavors.

Key expenses related to RAS involve procuring a robotic surgical platform, covering yearly maintenance outlays, and acquiring specialized disposable surgical instruments. The considerable expenses related to RAS, emphasized in various studies, pose a significant constraint that requires addressing12–14. Therefore, some medical professionals recommend increasing the number of robotic surgeries to reduce costs associated with robotic surgical equipment and maintenance, as well as to decrease expenses related to anesthesia and postoperative hospitalization by reducing operation time and hospital stay durations27. Yet, this can only be realized in high-volume robotic surgery medical centers. Ramirez et al. propose that the adoption of a three-instrument approach in RALP may yield a further 12% cost reduction28. Despite incorporating three-instrument arms in the study, the total hospitalization costs for the RALP group remained more than double those of the laparoscopic surgery group.

Given the progress in minimally invasive methods, RAS will remain the predominant surgical method in the future. However, the comparison between RALP and alternative LP remains a topic of ongoing debate, largely due to the extra cost associated with RALP and the uncertain evidence regarding surgical outcomes. In countries with sizable populations like China and specific developing regions, the issue of increased medical expenses poses a significant challenge for patients. Moreover, the costs associated with RAS are not encompassed within China’s current basic medical insurance. Therefore, it is anticipated that laparoscopic surgery will coexist with RAS for a prolonged period. A recent international consensus states that robotic surgical platforms are tools, not therapies. It emphasizes the significance of incorporating clinical, economic, and provider perspectives, as well as perspectives from stakeholders, in the evaluation of RAS29. We advocate for the classification of surgical complexity in patients with UPJO to facilitate tailored surgical recommendations informed by the degree of surgical challenge, surgeon expertise, institutional resources, clinical outcomes, cost-effectiveness, and patient-specific factors.

There are several limitations in our study. First, the study is limited by its retrospective design, involvement of multiple institutions, and the operators’ varying experience with robotic or laparoscopic surgery. The surgeons participating in this study possessed extensive proficiency in both laparoscopic and robotic procedures, potentially reducing variations in outcomes like operative duration and complication frequencies. Consequently, these findings might not be broadly applicable to centers with low procedural volumes or to surgeons in the initial stages of their learning process, where the technological benefits of robotic surgery could be more evident. Second, non-randomized allocation according to guardian preference may lead to potential selection bias. Future prospective studies with randomization are needed to control for this bias. Third, the VAS-C was devised internally for this study to assess subjective ergonomic experiences. It is important to acknowledge that this scale is subjective in nature and has not been externally validated. The surgeons and assistants were cognizant of the study’s general aim to compare techniques, which might introduce bias, yet their comfort scoring was determined by their immediate physical experience. The results of our study are in line with previous research indicating that robot-assisted surgery provides ergonomic advantages over conventional laparoscopic procedures21. Fourth, the reported ‘hospitalization costs’ represent the total direct medical charges to the patient, as retrieved from the hospital billing systems. A detailed breakdown (e.g., robotic disposables, anesthesia) was not consistently available across all three centers for this retrospective analysis, leading us to solely perform a comparative analysis of total hospital charges. This aggregate measure highlights the significant overall cost difference but is acknowledged as a limitation in determining specific cost drivers. Lastly, cases of complex UPJO were not included, underscoring the necessity for further investigation in this domain.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the study suggests that both LP and RALP are safe and effective minimally invasive surgical approaches for managing primary UPJO in children. LP remains an equally effective and cost-effective option for pediatric patients with primary UPJO in settings with surgeons possessing advanced laparoscopic skills and for patients without complex anatomy. It should be noted that this was not a formal non-inferiority or equivalence trial. Further prospective investigation and validation for complex UPJO cases are warranted.

The adverse event of interstitial lung disease in patients treated with antibody–drug conjugates
Authors: Nan Fang, Xiang Wang, Zhiqing Xu, Hongyan Zhan, Yuqing Wang, Bin Zhao, Hui Huang, Mingbao Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThere has been a yearly increase in the incidence of interstitial lung disease (ILD) adverse events associated with antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), which is becoming a significant challenge for the clinical application of ADCs. We aim to conduct an exhaustive analysis of the clinical characteristics and outcomes of ADC-associated ILD in the real world.MethodsWe utilized the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System database spanning from January 2004 to September 2023 to evaluate the clinical characteristics, onset times, and outcomes of ADC-associated ILD adverse events in patients. Additionally, safety signals were generated using disproportionality and Bayesian analyses to evaluate the association between ADC and ILD.ResultsOut of the fifteen ADCs, ten were recorded to have caused ILD adverse events, accounting for a total of 643 reported cases. Eight ADCs exhibited statistically significant safety signals related to ILD in both disproportionality and Bayesian analyses. Trastuzumab deruxtecan recorded the highest reporting odds ratio (ROR) = 49.04 [95% confidence interval (CI) =44.41–54.17], proportional reporting ratio (PRR) = 43.90 (χ2 = 18 297.87), empirical Bayes geometric mean = 43.45 (95% one-sided CI, 39.98). 44.20% of adverse reactions were recognized within the first month of ADC treatment. The median time to onset for ILD related to gemtuzumab ozogamicin was notably the shortest at 4 days [interquartile range (IQR): 2–12 days], and it had the highest fatality proportion, standing at 57.14%.ConclusionsThis study analyzed demographic, temporal and outcome profiles of ADC-related ILD cases and identified high-risk signals. These findings help raise awareness and improve the monitoring of adverse events related to ADC. In the future, large-scale prospective studies are needed to further confirm and explore the underlying biological mechanisms.Keywords: antibody-drug conjugate, FAERS, interstitial lung disease, cancer, disproportionality analysis

Clinical trials of ADCs have reported adverse events related to lung injury with fatal outcomes. Some case reports have indicated that ILD adverse events are associated with ADCs, but research analyzing the association and clinical features is still lacking. Our study based on the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database summarized the demographic characteristics of ADC-related ILD events. Among the fifteen ADCs, eight of them have significant correlations with ILD adverse events in both disproportionality and Bayesian analyses. Patients receiving trastuzumab deruxtecan treatment exhibited the highest risk of ILD. Among all ADCs, gemtuzumab ozogamicin-related ILD had the shortest onset time after treatment and was associated with a highest risk of death. The findings emphasize the importance of monitoring for ILD adverse events in patients treated with ADCs.

Introduction

Cancer is a major health concern globally, causing an estimated ten million fatalities each year.1 Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have revolutionized the conventional paradigm of cancer treatment by specifically targeting unique antigens expressed on the surface of tumor cells. However, the therapeutic outcomes of mAb monotherapy remain unsatisfactory.2 To overcome these challenges, scientists have proposed an innovative concept of antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), which is engineered by attaching cytotoxic small-molecule payloads to mAbs targeting tumors through linkers, effectively turning each conjugate into a “biological missile.”3 Initially, ADCs are administered intravenously into the bloodstream and interact with specific antigens on cancer cell surfaces. This interaction triggers the internalization of the ADCs into cancer cells through receptor-mediated endocytosis. Subsequently, ADCs are transported to tumor cells’ lysosomes, where they are degraded, releasing the drug payload which then binds to its corresponding target and exerts cytotoxic effects.4

Since the first ADC, gemtuzumab ozogamicin, was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2000, a total of fifteen ADCs have been granted marketing authorization worldwide. ADCs involve a wide range of treatment fields, including leukemia,5 breast carcinoma,6 and urothelial cancer.7 However, despite the superior clinical efficacy of ADCs, they are frequently linked to adverse reactions such as thrombocytopenia and neurotoxicity with an observed upward trend in the prevalence of ADC-associated interstitial lung disease (ILD).8,9 ILD is a heterogeneous disease characterized by pulmonary interstitial inflammation and fibrosis.10 Patients with ILD may experience symptoms such as chest tightness, dry cough and progressive dyspnea. The drug label of trastuzumab deruxtecan explicitly cautions about the potential of treatment-related ILD.11 Therefore, investigating ILD adverse events is essential for evaluating the safety and effectiveness of ADCs in clinical applications.

The Spontaneous Reporting System (SRS) provides valuable information resources for the identification of latent safety concerns by collecting reports of adverse events related to drug therapy. The SRS permits healthcare professionals, patients, and the public to continuously monitor the safety of post-market drugs.12 The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) stands as one of the most extensive publicly available pharmacovigilance databases globally.13 Our study aims to analyze the data of SRS to evaluate the incidence and quantify the onset time and mortality proportion of ADC-associated ILD. By using data mining analysis, we aim to identify potential safety signals. Additionally, this study analyzes the mechanisms underlying ADC toxicity, providing insights for the development of ADCs and offering more options for cancer treatment.

Materials and methods

Data source and extraction

This study conducted a retrospective analysis of the FAERS database reports spanning from January 2004 to September 2023, which are publicly available. The FAERS data files provide demographic information, drug information, preferred terms (PTs), adverse reactions, report sources, patient outcomes, therapy start and end dates for reported drugs, and indications for use. According to the deduplication method recommended by the FDA, when the CASEIDs were the same, the latest FDA_DT was selected, and when the CASEID and FDA_DT were the same, the higher PRIMARYID was selected. Finally, 17 035 801 reports were included in the study (Figure 1).

Data mapping

This study focused on ADC-related ILD. Using the PTs from the “Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities” (MedDRA, version 26.1) to perform data mining in adverse reactions documents. Supplementary Table S1 lists the PTs related to ILD. Furthermore, the Micromedex® (Index Nominum) database was used as a dictionary to establish a comparison table of generic names and trade names of ADCs (Supplementary Table S2). Subsequently, we selected drugs marked as “Primary Suspect” in the FAERS database.

Data mining

The correlation between ADCs and adverse events of ILD was ascertained, using disproportionality and Bayesian analysis. This study applied four algorithms for analysis: the reporting odds ratio (ROR), proportional reporting ratio (PRR), Bayesian confidence propagation neural network (BCPNN), and multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker (MGPS) algorithms. Supplementary Table S3 lists the calculation formulas and decision criteria for each of the four algorithms. Signal was defined when meeting the criteria for each algorithm. Furthermore, the study evaluated the onset time of ILD associated with ADC, which was delineated as the time interval between the date of adverse event occurrence and the commencement of drug administration. Reports with erroneous inputs, where the event date was antecedent to the start date, or those with imprecisely documented data information, were excluded from the analysis. Subsequently, the research analyzed the reports of patient deaths caused by ILD and calculated the proportion of deaths by dividing the number of deaths by the total number of ADC-related ILD reports.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis was utilized to summarize the clinical characteristics of patients with ILD associated with ADCs retrieved from the FAERS database. The non parametric test (Mann-Whitney U test) was used to contrast the onset time of ADC-related ILD. Hospitalization and mortality proportion of ADC-associated ILD were compared using either the Pearson χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. A P value <.05 with 95% confidence intervals was defined as statistically significant. All data mining and statistical analysis are conducted using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software).

Results

Descriptive analysis

The FAERS database recorded a total of 26 464 instances of adverse events related to all ADCs and 42 245 reports of drug-related ILD from January 2004 to September 2023. The adverse events of ILD accounted for 2.43% (643 out of 26 464) of all adverse reactions documented for ADC, which was notably higher than drugs such as amiodarone, doxorubicin, and pembrolizumab, according to a real-world pharmaceutical study.14 Among these, 643 reports of ILD were associated with ten ADCs. Trastuzumab deruxtecan, trastuzumab emtansine, and brentuximab vedotin accounted for the largest proportions, representing 68.43% (440/643), 9.49% (61/643), and 8.55% (55/643) of the total number of ADC-related ILD reports, respectively. Cetuximab, sarotalocan, moxetumomab pasudotox, tisotumab vedotin, mirvetuximab soravtansine, and disitamab vedotin were excluded from the analysis due to their limited approval duration and absence of reported cases. The clinical characteristics of the cases are summarized in Table 1. The proportion of ADC-related ILD adverse events was the highest in patients aged 45–74 years (36.24%), and the lowest in the 18-year-old age group (0.62%). The proportion of ILD adverse events reported in females (60.34%) compared to males (18.66%) may be attributed to the fact that some ADCs are mainly used to treat female tumors, such as breast cancer.

Disproportionality and Bayesian analysis

This study focused on fifteen approved ADCs for cancer treatment, out of which five ADCs were excluded from the analysis due to having zero reported cases of ILD adverse events. For the remaining ten ADCs that reported ILD adverse reactions, eight ADCs exhibited ILD related adverse event signals in both disproportionality and Bayesian analyses, as depicted in Table 3. The relationship between trastuzumab deruxtecan and ILD adverse reactions was the most significant, followed by loncastuximab tesirine. The lower limit of the 95% two-sided Confidence Interval (CI) of the Information Component (IC025) of sacituzumab govitecan was > 0, while the other three algorithms did not show a significant correlation. In contrast, belantamab mafodotin ROR = 0.96 (95% CI = 0.40, 2.30) demonstrated no correlation with ILD, as ROR, PRR, and EBGM signals did not meet the criteria.

Signals.

Onset time of ADC-associated interstitial lung disease

Figure 3 delineates the onset time of ILD subsequent to the initiation of ADC therapy. Collectively, the median interval to the emergence of ADC-associated ILD was 41 days [interquartile range (IQR), 9.75–116.75 days]. A notable percentage, specifically 44.20% of adverse reactions were recognized within the first month of treatment, whereas cases occurring 360 days later were relatively scarce, constituting a mere 3.57%. Some ADCs may lead to ILD in the early stages of treatment. It is worth noting that earliest occurrences of ADC-related ILD were associated with gemtuzumab ozogamicin, with a median of four days (IQR, 2–12 days). Gemtuzumab ozogamicin is used to treat acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Patients receiving gemtuzumab ozogamicin may have already undergone multiple drug treatments in the initial stage of therapy. Additionally, these patients typically have weakened immune functions, making them susceptible to bacterial, fungal, or viral pneumonia. Moreover, leukemia cell infiltration may also lead to interstitial inflammation and fibrosis, exacerbating pulmonary damage. The data set indicates that the respective proportions of adverse events occurring within the first 30 days for gemtuzumab ozogamicin, enfortumab vedotin, and brentuximab vedotin were 95.24%, 68.18%, and 51.61%. The proportion of ILD events occurring within 6 months of treatment with trastuzumab deruxtecan was 76.0%, and within 12 months was 94.0%. Loncastuximab tesirine was noted to exhibit adverse events after 120 days of use. Furthermore, ADCs showed significant differences in the onset time of ILD (Kruskal Wallis test, P < .0001). Further pairwise analyses demonstrated that the onset time of ILD associated with gemtuzumab ozogamicin (four days) was markedly earlier in comparison to trastuzumab emtansine (67 days) (P < .0001), trastuzumab deruxtecan (67 days) (P < .0001), and belantamab mafodotin (101 days) (P = .016). Close monitoring, especially during the early stages of treatment, is crucial when using these drugs.

Outcomes of ADC-associated interstitial lung disease

Within the cohort of reported ADC-associated ILD adverse events, fatalities and hospitalizations were observed in 29.98% and 45.39% of cases, respectively, indicating a poor prognosis. As illustrated in Figure 4, the incidence of fatal events following the onset of ILD with trastuzumab deruxtecan was notably high, with 120 cases constituting 30.23% of the adverse event reports. Among all ADCs, gemtuzumab ozogamicin has the highest proportion of death (57.14%), followed by inotuzumab ozogamicin (50.00%). According to Fisher’s test results, it was found that there were significant differences in the proportion of deaths caused by ILD after ADC treatment between gemtuzumab ozogamicin and brentuximab vedotin (P = .014), trastuzumab emtansine (P = .0020), trastuzumab deruxtecan (P = .015), and sacituzumab govitecan (P = .044). This result implied that gemtuzumab ozogamicin is associated with a greater risk of fatality, thus mandating judicious utilization in clinical practice. In our analysis, the hospitalization proportion was most pronounced with trastuzumab deruxtecan (70.00%), succeeded by loncastuximab tesirine (66.67%), and brentuximab vedotin (61.54%). However, the risk of death and hospitalization for ADC-related ILD adverse events is associated with the patient’s treatment stage and cancer type. For instance, gemtuzumab ozogamicin is used to treat AML. AML patients typically have weakened immune function and are at high risk of infection. Additionally, AML progresses rapidly and often leads to severe anemia, bleeding, and infection in a short time.15 All of the above may result in reported mortality rates higher than the ADC effect. Given the scarcity of reported cases for the ADCs, the outcome of ILD adverse events still needs further investigation.

Discussion

Currently, over 400 drugs have been reported to cause ILD, including methotrexate, amiodarone, sirolimus, and monoclonal antibodies.12 A retrospective study found that 1078 patients treated with monoclonal antibodies (including rituximab, trastuzumab, cetuximab, and bevacizumab), and 6 patients experienced ILD, with an incidence rate of 0.56%.16 The clinical manifestations of drug-induced ILD are diverse, ranging from dyspnea, cough, asthenia, and respiratory failure. Drugs associated with ILD include antineoplastic drugs, chemotherapy agents, immunosuppressants, and biologics. Non-drug-induced ILD, on the other hand, can stem from autoimmune diseases, environmental exposures, genetic factors, and occupational hazards. ILD, whether drug-induced or not, has similar clinical manifestations, but there are distinct differences in etiology, diagnosis, and treatment strategies. Imaging studies of ILD caused by drugs can reveal a variety of pulmonary abnormalities, such as ground-glass opacities, consolidation, traction bronchiectasis, and honeycombing changes.17

A growing number of clinical trials have documented instances of ADC-associated ILD. However, safety conclusions from these trials may be limited by time frames and trial populations.18 The SRS offers a broad range of data sources for the safety monitoring of post market drugs, identifying safety concerns that may not frequently arise within the confines of clinical trials.19

Among the fifteen ADCs approved for marketing, ten have been reported to have caused ILD adverse events in FAERS database. Upon further analysis of these ten ADCs using four different algorithms, eight of these ADCs exhibited statistically significant safety signals related to ILD in both disproportionality and Bayesian analyses. Compared to Lin’s study, we found that loncastuximab tesirine is associated with a risk of adverse ILD events.20 Currently, there are only a few reports of adverse events caused by loncastuximab tesirine leading to ILD, but based on signal analysis from the pharmacovigilance database, potential risks still need to be monitored. Descriptive analysis from the FAERS database indicates that ILD associated with ADCs predominantly affects individuals aged 45–74, accounting for 36.24% of the cases. This age distribution potentially reflects the incidence of cancer generally increases with advancing age. Moreover, the study identified a substantial predominance of female patients (>60.00%), which may be attributed to the utilization in the treatment of breast cancer. Asia was the main reporting region in this study. Previous clinical trials have shown that Asians, specifically Japanese, are susceptible to drug-induced ILD after treatment with certain drugs, such as ADCs and TKIs. This increased susceptibility may be partly attributed to Japan’s stringent drug safety monitoring system.21 Furthermore, high general frequency of HLA-DRB1*04:05 in Japanese, which could be associated with drug-induced ILD susceptibility, may explain the high reported incidence.22

Additionally, the observed temporal augmentation of reported cases may be ascribed to the escalating number of ADCs approved for clinical application. Overall, the findings of this study outline key characteristics of the patients afflicted with ADC-associated ILD.

Trastuzumab emtansine was the first ADC approved by the FDA in 2013 for the treatment of HER2-positive advanced breast cancer.23 Recently, some clinical studies have focused on its potential pulmonary toxicity, especially ILD.24 The KAMILLA study showed that out of 2002 patients treated with trastuzumab emtansine, one patient (0.4%) reported an ILD adverse event.25 Another phase III clinical study also showed that out of 403 patients treated with trastuzumab emtansine, one (0.2%) died due to pneumonia related to the treatment.26 Our research has also found a strong correlation between trastuzumab deruxtecan and ILD. The majority of instances of drug-related ILD or pneumonia after the administration of trastuzumab deruxtecan are categorized as grades one or two.27 Meanwhile, the proportion of the initial ILD events that occurred within the initial 12 months of trastuzumab deruxtecan treatment was 87.0%,28 aligning with the outcomes of this study.

Summarizing the current literature, the mechanism of ADC toxicity may include:

Payload Types and Mechanisms of Action: The payload of ADC is the main determinant of toxicity. Payloads are cytotoxic agents responsible for killing cancer cells, and can be categorized into microtubule inhibitors, DNA-damaging agents, and DNA transcription inhibitors. The payloads of trastuzumab ADCs include microtubule inhibitors such as emtansine and topoisomerase I inhibitors such as deruxtecan, both of which are potent cytotoxic drugs. Non-specific release or metabolism of the payloads may damage normal cells.

Off-target Effects and Non-specific Internalization: ADCs can bind to non-target cells leading to non-specific cellular uptake and be internalized by non-tumor cells leading to unforeseen tissue damage.29,30 Trastuzumab ADCs are HER2-targeted drugs, however, HER2 is also expressed in normal tissues, which may lead to off-target toxicity.

High Drug-to-Antibody Ratio: The drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) refers to the number of payload molecules conjugated to each antibody molecule. ADCs with a high DAR value may be less stable in the bloodstream, more prone to prematurely releasing toxic drugs in off-target tissues.

In the design and development process of ADCs, it is necessary to consider these factors to achieve maximized efficacy and minimized toxicity. No instances of grade one or two ILD were documented in a phase III clinical trial of sacituzumab govitecan.31 In our research, the IC025 of sacituzumab govitecan was > 0, while the ROR, PRR, and EBGM did not show signals. This is attributable to differences in algorithm sensitivity. Due to the limited reports of sacituzumab govitecan-related ILD adverse events in the FAERS database, this signal may be a false positive. Another possibility is that the limited sample size and the rarity of ILD events may have prevented detection in the trials. Larger-scale studies are needed to further research. Belantamab mafodotin has not exhibited safety signals indicative of ILD. Meanwhile, it demonstrated no occurrences of ILD in its clinical trials, with four cases of grade three or more severe pneumonia observed in a cohort of 95 patients.32 Corroborating the results of our study, there have been few reports of adverse events related to sacituzumab govitecan and belantamab mafodotin, with only eight and five cases, respectively.

Furthermore, this analysis determined that the incidence rate of ILD within the first month of ADC treatment constituted 44.20% and the median time to the onset of ADC-associated ILD was 41.00 days (IQR, 9.75–116.75 days). Statistical analyses revealed marked disparities in the onset time of ILD associated with different ADCs. The earliest onset time of ADC-related ILD was 4 days (IQR, 2–12 days) for gemtuzumab ozogamicin and 20 days (IQR, 8.5–54.5 days) for enfortumab vedotin. In the initial stage of gemtuzumab ozogamicin treatment for AML, the intensity of drug treatment is high, and the patient’s immune system is often severely suppressed, making it prone to causing toxic reactions. Before using ADC, patients may have already received frequent treatment with multiple drugs in the early stages of treatment, which accelerates the occurrence of ILD.33

The outcomes of ADC-related ILD cannot be ignored, as they can lead to death.34,35 The incidence of mortality and hospitalization attributable to ADC-associated ILD was noted to be 29.98% and 45.39%, respectively. Gemtuzumab ozogamicin (57.14%) treatment could induce relatively high fatality proportion, which was observed to be significantly higher compared to those associated with the following ADCs: brentuximab vedotin (P = .014), trastuzumab emtansine (P = .0020), trastuzumab deruxtecan (P = .015), and sacituzumab govitecan (P = .044). One possible reason is that gemtuzumab ozogamicin is used to treat AML, especially relapsed or refractory AML, which progresses rapidly and often leads to severe anemia, and infection in a short period of time. Trastuzumab deruxtecan was associated with the most considerable number of fatal ILD events, amounting to 120 cases (30.23%), which is indicative of a grave adverse event outcome. It is worth noting that an analysis of ILD/pneumonitis of nine trastuzumab deruxtecan trials indicated that Grade 5 ILD/pneumonitis events accounted for 14.1% (25/177) of all ILD/pneumonitis events, while Grade 3 or higher events (which required hospitalization) accounted for 22.5% (40/177).36 A retrospective multicenter study that enrolled 600 patients exposed to trastuzumab deruxtecan in 11 French centers found that 67 patients developed trastuzumab deruxtecan-related ILD, with 9% (6/67) experiencing fatal ILD.37 In contrast, the mortality and hospitalization proportion of ILD in the FAERS database may be higher than those in the real world. This discrepancy may be related to reporting bias and selection bias in the FAERS database. Reported ILD events may be more likely to present severe cases, as these cases are more likely to be recognized and documented. Conversely, suspected, mild and moderate ILD events may not be adequately documented. Within the context of our analysis, the proportion of hospitalization for patients afflicted with ADC-associated ILD was observed to be markedly higher than that of mortality. The hospitalization proportion was most pronounced for trastuzumab deruxtecan (70.00%). This observation implies that a significant proportion of patients who develop ILD after ADC therapy are likely to exhibit severe symptoms necessitating intensive care management. Additionally, findings from the KATHERINE trial have demonstrated an increased propensity for ILD when trastuzumab emtansine is administered in conjunction with radiotherapy.38 This underscores the necessity for heightened awareness and proactive monitoring of the patient’s respiratory symptoms, such as lung function, dyspnea, and cough.

The causative drug should be immediately discontinued when ADC-related ILD is suspected or confirmed. Subsequent management should be based on the common terminology criteria for adverse events (CTCAE) grade.39 For asymptomatic cases (CTCAE grade 1), trastuzumab emtansine should be permanently discontinued, while trastuzumab deruxtecan should be temporarily withheld and may be restarted in case of complete resolution. In symptomatic cases (CTCAE grade ≥ 2), trastuzumab deruxtecan should be permanently discontinued. Corticosteroids are used to treat drug-induced ILD, and the dosage should be adjusted according to the severity. Unfortunately, the efficacy data are not sufficient. For asymptomatic cases (CTCAE Grade 1), it is advised to initiate treatment with oral glucocorticoids (prednisone, 0.5 mg/kg/d), with tapering over at least 4 weeks. For moderate cases (CTCAE Grade 2), oral glucocorticoids (prednisolone, 1 mg/kg/d) are recommended for 2 to 4 weeks, followed by tapering over at least 4 weeks. If worsening or no improvement is observed within 3 to 5 days, the dose may be increased to 2 mg/kg/d. For severe cases (CTCAE Grades 3 and 4), hospitalization is strongly advised. Pulse therapy with methylprednisolone (0.5 to 1 g/d) should be administered for at least 3 days, followed by glucocorticoids equivalent to prednisolone at a dose of 1.0 mg/kg/d for 2 to 4 weeks, with gradual tapering over at least 4 weeks. Additionally, studies have shown that nintedanib is effective in patients with progressive fibrosing ILD.40

In the design and development of ADCs, the efficiency of internalization is a critical consideration. ADCs are taken up by target cells through internalization and then release cytotoxic drugs to kill cancer cells. Excessive internalization is not the best strategy, for instance, if the payload of the ADC is also toxic to normal cells, increasing internalization may enhance damage to normal cells. Additionally, the efficacy of some ADCs depends on the bystander effect, which means that in addition to recognizing and killing target tumor cells, it can also kill non-target cells around the tumor cells. In this situation, it may not be necessary for all ADCs to be internalized. Therefore, improving the efficiency of internalization needs to ensure safety and specificity.

Real-world research predicated on the FAERS database, while offering considerable advantages, is encumbered by several limitations. The FAERS database is susceptible to biases stemming from erroneous reporting, underreporting, and inaccurate reporting.41 Subsequently, the FAERS data does not disclose the total caseload of patients treated with varying ADCs, thereby impeding the calculation and comparative analysis of the incidence rates of ILD associated with different ADCs and signals from the spontaneous reporting system can only be used for qualitative studies. Thirdly, there is an absence of indicators within the database for assessing the severity of ILD, such as pulmonary function tests and CT scans of the lungs. Furthermore, the research methods of real-world pharmacovigilance studies also have certain limitations. For example, although ROR and PRR methods are highly sensitive and simple in calculation, they are prone to produce false positive. Disproportionality analysis can only indicate the strength of the association between drugs and adverse events, but cannot demonstrate the causal relationship between the two. Despite these constraints, this pharmacovigilance study grounded in real-world data has identified safety signals associated with ILD linked to ADCs. It is imperative for clinicians, pharmacists, and researchers to be vigilant regarding the potential for drug-induced pulmonary damage and to investigate therapeutic strategies for pulmonary toxicity.

Conclusion

The objective of this study was to leverage an analysis of the FAERS database to identify the associative links between ADCs and ILD. Our study investigation revealed that among the fifteen ADCs, ten were reported to have adverse reactions of ILD, and eight ADCs showed statistically significant safety signals associated with ILD in both disproportionality and Bayesian analyses. Sacituzumab govitecan had an IC025 value greater than 0, while the other three algorithms did not indicate a significant association. Nonetheless, the validity of our conclusions necessitates further validation through pharmacoepidemiologic studies and clinical trials. Clinicians must remain vigilant regarding the safety concerns to ensure timely intervention for adverse reactions. Future research should focus on uncovering the pathological mechanisms of ADC-associated ILD, which would guide a more precise strategy for the development and clinical application of ADC therapies.

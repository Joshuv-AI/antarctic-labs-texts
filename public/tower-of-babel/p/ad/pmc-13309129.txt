Rheumatoid synovitis of the elbow mimicking rheumatoid nodule
Authors: Issei Takahashi, Eisuke Takamasu, Yuji Miyoshi
Published: 2026

A 76-year-old woman was referred to our department with worsening morning stiffness and a nodule on her right elbow. She had a 25-year history of rheumatoid arthritis, and had been taking methotrexate (10 mg/wk) for 13 years and sulfasalazine (1 g daily) for 23 years. Our physical examination revealed swelling at multiple joints — including her elbows, wrists, and knees — and a 2-cm elastic, mobile, nontender swelling on the extensor surface of her right elbow. Her C-reactive protein level was 100 (reference < 1.4) mg/L, and we calculated her Simplified Disease Activity Index as 11.2 (remission < 3.3). The referring physician had initially suspected the elbow swelling to be a rheumatoid nodule (Figure 1A); however, the elastic (rather than firm) consistency suggested otherwise. Musculoskeletal ultrasonography demonstrated grade-3 synovial hypertrophy that breached the elbow joint capsule and extended into the subcutaneous tissue to within millimetres of the skin surface with increased Doppler signal (Figure 1B), confirming active rheumatoid synovitis. We escalated treatment with the addition of abatacept, alongside continued methotrexate and sulfasalazine, and the swelling resolved over 3 months.

(A) Photograph of the right elbow of a 76-year-old woman showing a 2-cm elastic, mobile, nontender swelling on the extensor surface, initially suspected to be a rheumatoid nodule. (B) Longitudinal musculoskeletal ultrasonography of the elbow demonstrated grade-3 synovial hypertrophy that breached the elbow joint capsule and extended into subcutaneous tissue to within millimetres of the skin surface (arrow), with increased Doppler signal (arrowheads) indicating active synovitis corresponding to the palpable swelling.

Important differentials for nodular elbow swelling in rheumatoid arthritis include rheumatoid nodules, gouty tophi, olecranon bursitis, and methotrexate-induced accelerated nodulosis. Rheumatoid nodules appear on ultrasonography as well-defined hypoechoic masses near the bone surface with absent or minimal Doppler signal.1 Gouty tophi — relevant in the differential for nodules, although not for elbow joint swelling — appear as heterogeneously hyperechoic masses surrounded by a hypoechoic halo.2 Olecranon bursitis produces bursal fluid without capsular breach. Methotrexate-induced accelerated nodulosis typically presents as multiple small nodules on the fingers in quiescent disease, improving with methotrexate reduction or cessation.3 Our patient’s active systemic inflammation and capsule-breaching synovitis were inconsistent with methotrexate-induced accelerated nodulosis. This case illustrates that severe rheumatoid synovitis breaching the joint capsule into subcutaneous tissue can mimic a subcutaneous nodule. Musculoskeletal ultrasonography was essential in delineating this atypical presentation and guiding treatment escalation.

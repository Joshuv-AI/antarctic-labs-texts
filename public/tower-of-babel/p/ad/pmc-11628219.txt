Pre-assembled nanospheres in mucoadhesive microneedle patch for sustained release of triamcinolone in the treatment of oral submucous fibrosis
Authors: Xian CHENG, Yanqing YANG, Junming HUANG, Qiuyun GUO, Wei ZHU, Dingpei LONG, Yueying ZHOU, Hui FENG, Jie WANG, Yusi LI, Jian ZHOU, Yanping LIU, Ousheng LIU, Pu TIAN
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveDrug-loaded mucoadhesive silk fibroin (SF) microneedle patch can overcome the limitations of low bioavailability and significant pain associated with traditional treatment methods, such as topical application or injection of triamcinolone for oral submucous fibrosis (OSF). However, these systems release the drug too quickly, failing to meet the clinical requirements. This study aims to construct a mucoadhesive SF microneedle patch pre-assembled with silk fibroin nanospheres (SFN) and explore its ability to sustain the release of triamcinolone in the treatment of OSF.MethodsSFN was pre-assembled via precipitation reaction and characterized by scanning electron microscope (SEM) for the morphology. The particle size and ζ-potential were measured by dynamic light scattering (DLS). Triamcinolone was loaded onto SFN using a diffusional post-loading method. The effective loading of triamcinolone was confirmed using Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR). The concentration of unloaded triamcinolone was quantified by high-performance liquid chromatography. Drug encapsulation efficiency and loading capacity of SFN were then calculated to determine the optimal amount of drug loading. The SFN suspension was pre-mixed with SF solution to prepare the microneedle under-layer. The microneedle morphology was observed by SEM. Compression mechanical tests were performed to evaluate the fracture force of microneedles at different nanosphere contents (5%, 10%, and 20%), determining the optimal pre-mixing ratio. Ex-vivo mouse oral mucosa permeation studies were performed to ascertain the insertion depth of the microneedles via histological sections. The adhesive top layer was synthesized using SF and tannic acid, with FTIR confirming its successful synthesis. Its viscoelasticity was characterized by a rheometer, and differential scanning calorimetry analyzed thermal properties. Tensile tests evaluated the interfacial bonding strength between the adhesive layer and microneedle base to ensure no detachment during use. Adhesion to wet oral mucosal tissues was tested and compared to commercial oral patches.Under the optimized conditions, the double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres was prepared. Its cell compatibility was evaluated by cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8), live/dead staining, and phalloidin staining after co-culturing with fibroblasts. The drug release experiment was conducted to demonstrate its sustained release efficacy.ResultsSFN (mean diameter 46.25 nm) was successfully prepared. The maximum drug encapsulation efficiency was (63.88±1.09)% (corresponding loading capacity of SFN was (27.41±3.06)% when the weight ratio of triamcinolone/SFN was 0.5. The corporation of SFN did not affect microneedle morphology. The mechanical properties of microneedles decreased with increasing nanosphere amount. Only the fracture force of the group with 5% SFN [(0.07±0.01) N/needle] exceeded the minimum force required for mucosal penetration, thus selected as the optimal pre-mixing ratio. Histological sections confirmed that the SFN microneedles could penetrate the epithelial layer and deliver drugs to OSF-affected areas. Adhesion strength between the microneedle base and top layer was (94.8±6.89) kPa, confirming strong bonding with no detachment during use. The wet adhesive strength of the double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch [(41.28±7.43) kPa] was significantly enhanced compared to commercial oral patches (4.5 kPa, P<0.01). CCK-8 and live/dead staining results confirmed no significant cytotoxicity. Drug release experiment showed the double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled SFN enabled sustained release time of triamcinolone from 4 days to 14 days.ConclusionPre-assembling nanospheres in mucoadhesive SF microneedle patches can extend triamcinolone release time, meeting clinical requirements for sustained drug delivery.Keywords: oral disease, oral mucosal disease, oral submucous fibrosis, mucoadhesive microneedle patch

Oral submucous fibrosis (OSF) is an insidious, chronic, and potentially malignant oral mucosa disease closely associated with the consumption of areca nuts[1]. OSF affects the entire oral cavity, pharynx and upper third of the esophagus. The characteristic symptoms include burning sensation, blanching and stiffening of the oral mucosa, resulting in trismus, limited mouth opening, difficulty in chewing and swallowing, and even facial deformity[2]. These symptoms seriously influence patients’ quality of life and lead to oral squamous cell carcinoma in 7.6%-13.0% of cases[3-4].

Currently, the treatment for OSF mainly involves physical therapy, hyperbaric oxygen therapy, drug therapy, and surgical treatment[5]. Among them, local drug therapy with corticosteroids, such as triamcinolone, is most widely used in clinic. It is administered topically or via submucosal injection[6]. Topical administration is hindered by mechanical flushing and enzymatic degradation in saliva, as well as the penetration barrier caused by the outermost epithelium of oral mucosa[7]. Submucosal injection causes pain and reduces patient compliance, particularly in pain-sensitive OSF patients[8]. Hence, developing alternative approaches is imperative for OSF treatment.

A double-layered silk fibroin (SF) mucoadhesive microneedle patch proposed in our previous research[9] offers a promising solution to the limitations mentioned above. It comprises a mucoadhesive SF/tannic acid (TA) top-layer and a SF microneedle under-layer. It achieves robust wet adhesion, oral mucosal penetration, and efficient triamcinolone release. Nonetheless, this patch exhibits rapid drug release, with approximately 80% of triamcinolone being released in 3 days[9]. Given that the interval for submucosal injection is one week in clinic[10], the patch ought to feature a more sustained drug release to fulfill clinical requirements.

Many laboratory studies[11-13] aim to introduce additional drug delivery vehicles into the matrix (e.g., carbon nanotubes or gelatin nanoparticles) or utilize an annealing agent like genipin to sustain drug release. It is not a preferred choice due to the complexity and cost of manufacture, as well as biosafety uncertainties[14-15]. Our previous studies[14, 16] and other study[17] have demonstrated that sustained drug release of SF-based drug delivery systems can be achieved in an additive-free manner. Specifically, a corporation of pre-assembled SF nanostructures to SF matrix prolongs drug release time by avoiding the introduction of other chemicals[18]. Herein, integrating SF nanostructures into SF mucoadhesive microneedle patch could be a solution to address the issue of rapid drug release.

In this study, SF mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled SF nanospheres (SFN) is developed to sustain the release time of triamcinolone. A comprehensive evaluation of the patch is conducted, including mechanical properties, interfacial adhesion strength, wet adhesion performance, mucosal penetration in mice, cytocompatibility, and drug release capability. This study aims to demonstrate the sustained drug release capability of the mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres, offering a promising drug delivery strategy for OSF treatment.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

The animal study was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Central South University (Approval number: CSU-2023-0508), in line with the welfare and ethical principles of laboratory animals.

Preparation of the SF aqueous solution

The Bombyx mori silk cocoons were supplied by the State Key Laboratory of Silkworm Genome Biology of Southwest University, China. The preparation of the SF aqueous solution followed the protocol outlined in previous work[19]. Specifically, the cocoons underwent a degumming process in a 0.02 mol/L sodium carbonate solution (S111733, Aladdin, China) boiled for 30 minutes. The extracted silk was thoroughly washed and dried overnight. After that, the dried silk was dissolved in 9.3 mol/L lithium bromide solution (L108934, Aladdin, China) at 60 ℃ for 4 hours. Subsequently, it was dialyzed in ultrapure water for 48 hours. Insoluble residues were eliminated through centrifugation at 5 000 r/min (KL05R, Kaida, China) for 1.5 hours. The resulting SF solution was diluted to achieve a concentration of 7 wt%.

Preparation of the SFN suspension

SFN was synthesized by a precipitation reaction method described previously, wherein a 5 wt% SF aqueous solution was added dropwise into acetone at at 1꞉5 (v/v) ratio. Then, SFN was rinsed with ultrapure water and centrifuged at 12 000 r/min (FA45-24-11-HS, Eppendorf, USA) for 15 minutes for 3 times to remove acetone. SFN was dispersed in ultrapure water and subjected to sonication for 30 seconds at 100% amplitude using an ultrasonicator (SB-5200DT, Scientz, China) before use.

Preparation of the SF microneedle under-layer

A polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) microneedle mold was custom-made by Taizhou Microchip Technology Co. Ltd (Taizhou, China). The dimensions of the mold were as follows: 10 mm×10 mm with a 10×10 conical microneedle array measuring 300 µm in base diameter, 670 µm in depth, and less than 10 µm in tip diameter. The 7 wt% SF solution was pre-mixed with SFN suspension (5%, 10%, and 20%). The SF solution and the mixed solution were poured into the microneedle mold and subjected to degassing in a vacuum dryer (Muton, Japan) (>0.09 MPa), followed by centrifugation to eliminate bubbles. Subsequently, the drying process was carried out at 25 °C and 60% humidity for 48 hours. Finally, the microneedles were annealed in a water vapor-filled vacuum chamber at 25 ℃ overnight to enhance their insolubility.

Preparation of the SF-TA mucoadhesive top-layer

The SF solution was diluted to a concentration of 2 wt%, and TA was dissolved in ultrapure water at a 10 wt% concentration. TA solution was added dropwise into SF solution on a magnetic stirrer (MS10-H500-Pro, DWB, China) at 300 r/min with the TA/SF weight ratio 5꞉1. Subsequently, the SF-TA coacervates were settled down for 30 minutes and washed with ultrapure water to remove the excessive TA. Following this, the SF-TA coacervates were subjected to lyophilization using a freeze-drying machine (ZLGJ-8, Labconco, China) for 48 hours. The resulting lyophilized SF-TA coacervates were ground using a ball grinder (PM100, Retsch, Germany) to obtain the SF-TA lyophilized powder, which was stored at -20 ℃.

Another cylindrical concave PDMS mold (Taizhou Microchip Technology Co. Ltd, Taizhou, China) featuring an internal groove measuring 20.0 mm× 20.0 mm×0.2 mm was utilized. A 7 wt% SF solution was poured into the groove, degassed in a vacuum dryer (Muton, Japan) (>0.09 MPa), and then centrifuged to eliminate bubbles. Subsequently, the drying process was conducted at 25 ℃ with 60% humidity for 48 hours. The mold was then subjected to annealing overnight in a vacuum chamber filled with water vapor at 25 ℃ to prepare the non-adhesive SF handling film.

The SF-TA lyophilized powder was applied onto the annealed SF handling film within the mold. Upon adding a small quantity of ultrapure water, the powder restored into a mucoadhesive SF-TA gel film. The non-adhesive SF handling film and the mucoadhesive SF-TA gel film quickly bonded together, forming a cohesive structure referred to as the SF-TA mucoadhesive top-layer.

Preparation of the double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch

The SF microneedle under-layer was positioned at the center of the SF-TA mucoadhesive top-layer, with the microneedle tips facing upward. Through rapid and robust bonding at the SF and SF-TA interface, the double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch was successfully fabricated.

Dynamic light scattering

The SF nanospheres were dispersed in HEPES buffer (7365-45-9, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) with adjusted pH using dilute hydrochloric acid [GBW(E)081127, Bolinda, China] and sodium hydroxide solution (1310-73-2, Aladdin, China). The prepared SF suspension was filtered using a cell filter to remove aggregates. The ζ-potential and particle size of SF nanospheres were measured using dynamic light scattering (DLS) (Nano Brook Omni, Bruker, USA).

Triamcinolone loading onto SFN

The triamcinolone solution (IT0280, Solarbio, China) was mixed with SFN and stirred overnight. Then the mixed solution was centrifuged (FA45-24-11-HS, Eppendorf, USA) at 12 000 r/min for 15 minutes. The liquid supernatant was collected and its concentration was measured using high performance liquid chromatography (LC-20A, Shimadzu, Japan) at 405 nm using the C18 column (150.0 mm × 4.6 mm, particle size 5 μm, Agilent, USA). The washed supernatants were 95% methyl alcohol and 5% methanoic acid. The encapsulation efficiency and loading capacity for SFN were calculated using the following formulae (n=3):

Encapsulation efficiency=(Total amount of triamcinolone-amount of un-loaded triamcinolone)/total amount of triamcinolone×100%;

Loading capacity=(Total amount of triamcinolone-amount of un-loaded triamcinolone)/amount of SFN×100%.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) analysis was performed using attenuated total reflectance infrared spectroscopy (ALPHA Ⅱ, Bruker, USA). The subsequent calculation and curve fitting were performed by OMINIC and Origin software.

Morphology of the microneedles

The microstructure of the microneedles was examined using a field emission scanning electron microscope (MIRA3 LMH, TESACN, China) at 5 kV following platinum sputter-coating.

Mechanical property of the microneedles

The compression tests were conducted to assess the mechanical strength of microneedles using a universal testing machine (AGS-X 5KN, Shimadzu, Japan). During the tests, the SF microneedle under-layer was securely attached to a horizontal rigid platform, with the tips facing upwards. The test station sensor moved vertically towards the microneedles at a constant speed of 1 mm/min, maintaining an initial distance of 0.5 cm from the microneedle tips. Once the sensor made contact with the tips, displacement and force data were recorded.

Ex vivo insertion tests

Eight-week-old C57 mice, weighing 20 to 40 g, were sourced from Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. in Hunan, China. These mice were euthanized using the inhaled CO2 (hypercarbia) method. SF microneedle patches were applied to the oral mucosa of the mice for 5 minutes. The oral mucosa treated with microneedles was then preserved in a 4% paraformaldehyde fixation solution (C01-06002, Bioss, China) for 24 hours. Following fixation, the tissue underwent paraffin embedding and HE staining. The staining outcomes were examined under a brightfield microscope (DM500, Leica, Germany).

Rheological test of the SF-TA mucoadhesive top-layer

The rheological properties were tested using a rotating rheometer (Rheo Compass, Anton Paar, China) equipped with a 25 mm diameter rotating plate. During the test, the storage modulus (G') and loss modulus (G'') were continuously tracked by conducting frequency sweeps ranging from 0.1 to 100 Hz. This frequency sweep was performed at a strain of 0.1% within the linear viscoelastic region at 37 ℃. To further investigate the influence of temperature on rheological properties, the temperature was incrementally increased from 0 to 60 ℃ while a constant frequency of 1 Hz.

Differential scanning calorimetry

The differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurement was conducted utilizing a differential scanning calorimeter (DSC3, Mettler, Switzerland) under nitrogen atmosphere. The SF-TA coacervates (10 mg) were enclosed in a perforated aluminum pan, and the DSC curve was subsequently recorded at a heating rate of 10 ℃/min, spanning a temperature range of 25 to 250 ℃.

Adhesion strength of the SF-TA mucoadhesive top-layer

To assess the adhesive strength of the SF-TA top-layer, a lap-shear test was performed using a universal testing machine (MTS Insight, MTS, USA). The porcine buccal mucosa, procured from a slaughterhouse, was trimmed to a size of 3.0 cm×1.0 cm×0.5 cm. The mucoadhesive SF-TA layer was then sandwiched between 2 mucosa tissue samples, with a bonding area of 1 cm×1 cm. The tensile speed was set at 10 mm/min, and the adhesive strength was determined by dividing the applied load (N) by the bonding area (m²). This test was replicated 4 times (n=4) to ensure accurate and reliable results.

Cytocompatibility of double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres

Fibroblasts (NIH3T3 cells, mouse embryonic fibroblastic cell line) were purchased from American Type Culture Collection. Cells were cultured in DMEM medium (C3103-0500, Vivacell, USA) with 10% bovine serum albumin (C2910-0500, Vivacell, USA) and 1% penicillin and streptomycin solution (C3420-0100, Vivacell, USA) in a cell incubator set at a temperature of 37 ℃ and 5% CO2 atmosphere.

The dried SF, SFN, and SF-TA coacervates were submerged in DMEM medium for 24 hours to produce extraction solutions. To comply with ISO 10993 guideline, the extract ratio was set as 0.1 g/mL (the weight of samples/the volume of DMEM medium). The resulting extraction solution was sterilized using a 0.22 μm filter. Fibroblasts were planted in 24-well plates at a density of 1.0×104 cells/cm2 and then incubated for 24 hours in DMEM medium, DMEM medium supplemented with 5% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), or extraction solutions (all containing 10% bovine serum albumin).

The live/dead assay kit (40719ES60, Yeason, China) was used to evaluate cell survival rate. Calcein induced green fluorescence in live cells and propidium iodide induced red fluorescence in dead cells. Additionally, cell morphology was examined through immunofluorescent staining of the cytoskeleton using rhodamine phalloidin (40734ES80, Yeason, China) (staining F-actin as red), along with DAPI (47165-04-8, Aladdin, China) (staining cell nucleus as blue). Visualization of cells was carried out using a fluorescent microscope (DM500, Leica, Germany).

For quantitative evaluation of cell proliferation, cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assays (40203ES60, Yeason, China) were employed after 1, 2, and 3 days of incubation (n=4). Spectrophotometric analysis of CCK-8 results was performed using a microplate reader (Epoch, Biotek, USA) at 450 nm.

Drug releasing of double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanosphere

Triamcinolone was loaded onto SFN as previously described (in part 1.7) as the SFN group. Triamcinolone solution was mixed with 7 wt% SF solution by the volume ratio of 1꞉10. The mixed solution was fabricated into microneedles as the SF group. The drug-loaded SFN was dispersed in 7% wt% SF solution and then fabricated into microneedles as the SFN-SF group. Three groups were submerged in 1 mL of PBS solution (SH30256.01, Cytiva, USA) and placed on a shaker (37 ℃, 60 r/min). At each time point, 500 μL of supernatant was collected, and an additional 500 μL of fresh PBS solution was added. The cumulative release rate of triamcinolone was determined using the following equation: Accumulative release=Total amount of triamcinolone released/total amount of triamcinolone loaded×100%.

Statistical analysis

All data were presented as mean±standard deviation. Significant differences among statistical data across different groups were evaluated using GraphPad Prism 7.0 software via t-test (2 groups) or one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey post hoc test used for multiple comparisons (no less than 3 groups). A P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Fabrication and characterization of SFN

SF molecules were pre-assembled into SFN via precipitation reaction (Figure 1A). The obtained SFN exhibited a spherical morphology when observed under SEM (Figure 1B). The ζ-potential and size of nanospheres were evaluated using DLS (Figure 1C, 1D). The average diameter of SFN followed a normal distribution was 46.25 (38.88 to 61.77) nm (Figure 1C). As the pH increased, the positive ζ-potential decreased, transitioned to negative around pH 4 and gradually intensified. Thus, the equivalence point was at pH=4 (Figure 1D). Triamcinolone was loaded into SFN using the post-loading method, referred to as drug-loaded SFN (Figure 1A). The FTIR results (Figure 1E, 1F) reveal the drug-loaded SFN showed characteristic infrared absorption bands of triamcinolone at 3 200 cm-1 and 1 700 cm-1, corresponding to the hydrogen-bonded hydroxyl and carbonyl groups in aliphatic ester bonds, respectively. Additional infrared absorption bands of triamcinolone were observed in drug-loaded SFN at 2 800 cm-1 and 1 070 cm-1, indicating the presence of the C-H bond in aliphatic esters and the stretching vibration of the C-F bond, respectively. With the increasing triamcinolone amount, the drug encapsulation efficiency initially rose and reached the maximum (63.88±1.09)%, and then declined, with an inflection point around 0.5 (the weight ratio of triamcinolone/SFN) (Figure 1G). Meanwhile, the drug loading capacity of SFN showed a continuous upward trend.

Fabrication and characterization of the SF microneedle under-layer

The SF solution was either pre-mixed with SFN suspension or not, referred to as the SF and SFN group. They were used as precursor solutions and fabricated into microneedles via mold casting method as previously described (Figure 2A). Both 2 groups displayed uniformly distributed cone-shaped microneedle arrays with upright tips under SEM observation (Figure 2B). The surface morphology of the SFN group exhibited a granular texture, while the SF group was smooth (Figure 2B). The SF and SFN microneedles were applied in the oral mucosa of mice. The histological images show both groups disrupted the epithelial layer (Figure 2C). The FTIR results show the SFN group had a larger amount of β-sheet conformation than the SF group (Figure 2D). Four groups were prepared with the relative amount of nanospheres varying from 0, 5%, 10%, and 20%, designated as SFN, SFN5, SFN10, and SFN20, respectively. The force-displacement curves of 4 groups had a consistent trend, characterized by a sudden drop and subsequent upsurge (Figure 2E). The turning point (indicated by arrows in Figure 2E) was identified as the fracture force point. The mechanical strength of microneedles declined with an increase in the amount of nanospheres (Figure 2F). The fracture force per needle was (0.13±0.02) N, (0.07±0.01) N, (0.04±0.01) N, and (0.02±0.01) N for SF, SF5, SF10, and SF20, respectively (Figure 2E).

Fabrication and characterization of the SF-TA mucoadhesive top-layer

The mucoadhesive SF-TA coacervates was synthesized by blending TA solution with SF solution at a weight ratio of 5꞉1 (Figure 3A). The SF-TA coacervates appeared as yellow precipitate. The SF-TA coacervates were freeze-dried for convenient storage and sterilization. The powder restored into gel with stretchability and adhesion property when spraying a small amount of water (Figure 3B). The FTIR analysis revealed that the peak of -C=O vibration in TA shifted from 1 701 cm-1 (free TA) to 1 718 cm-1 (TA in SF-TA coacervates) (Figure 3C). The addition of TA also resulted in a shift of the -OH peak from 3 276 cm-1 in pure SF to 3 245 cm-1 in SF-TA coacervates (Figure 3D). The viscoelasticity of SF-TA coacervates was investigated as a function of frequency, strain and temperature (Figure 3E-3G). The loss modulus (G") was always higher than the storage modulus (G') at different frequencies and strain (Figure 3E, 3F). However, this relationship only occurred when the temperature was above 13.6 ℃, while the situation was opposite when below 13.6 ℃ (Figure 3G). According to the DSC result (Figure 3H), the SF-TA coacervates had a glass transition temperature (Tg) at 212 ℃, higher than SF (195 ℃) and TA (208 ℃). The interfacial adhesion was assessed by measuring the adhesive strength between the top-layer and the SF microneedle under-layer. The SF-TA layer displayed significantly higher adhesive strength [(94.8±6.89) kPa] than the pure SF layer [(0.27±0.08) kPa] (P<0.01, Figure 3I). The commercial oral patches (Zhende patch® and Maidi patch®) showed modest wet adhesive strength (4.5 kPa), whereas the SF-TA layer showed a notable improvement compared to commercial oral patches in wet adhesion capability [(41.28±7.43) kPa] (P<0.01, Figure 3J).

Cytocompatibility and drug releasing of mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres

The mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres was prepared by combing the SF microneedle under-layer with the mucoadhesive SF-TA top-layer (Figure 4A). To evaluate the cytocompatibility of the patch, fibroblast cell survival, viability, and morphology were assessed by co-culturing with the extraction solutions derived from 3 components (SF, SFN, and SF-TA). The live/dead staining images demonstrated a high cell survival rate in all groups except the negative control group (5% DMSO) (Figure 4B). The phalloidine staining images depicted the typical spindle-shaped morphology of fibroblasts in the SF, SFN, and SF-TA groups, contrasting with the irregular cell morphology induced by cytotoxic 5% DMSO (Figure 4C). The CCK-8 results indicat that SF possessed proliferative properties, while SFN and SF-TA, although slightly inferior to SF, still demonstrated a cell viability of over 70% on the third day (Figure 5A). Figure 5B and Figure 5C depict the releasing amount and percentage of triamcinolone. The SF group displayed a rapid release of 95% triamcinolone within 4 days, exemplifying a relative burst release phenomenon. In contrast, the sustained release of triamcinolone was observed in the SFN and SFN-SF group, with 95% vancomycin was released for 14 days (Figure 5B, 5C).

Discussion

In this study, the spherical morphology identified via SEM confirms the successful assembly of SF molecules into SFN. Previous work[20] showed that SFN range in size from 40 to 600 nm using precipitation reactions. The average diameter (46.25 nm) falls toward the smaller end of this range, consistent with SEM observations. Smaller nanospheres are preferred in this study for 2 reasons. On one hand, as carriers for drug delivery, nanospheres must be diminutive enough to navigate the intricacies of microneedle preparation[21]. On the other hand, smaller nanospheres enhance permeation and retention for treatment purposes[22]. The trend of ζ-potential values in this study varying with pH was in line with previous studies[23-24]. At a pH of around 7, SF exhibited a ζ-potential of (-34.95±0.59) mV, indicating its electronegative nature[23, 25]. This negative charge not only facilitates strong electrostatic interactions with hydrophobic and positively charged drugs such as triamcinolone, but also prevents the burst release of drugs[16].

The FTIR analysis confirmed the effective loading of triamcinolone onto SFN. SF contain both positively and negatively charged, hydrophobic and hydrophilic amino acids, facilitating electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions with positively charged triamcinolone[26]. As the amount of triamcinolone increased, its binding to SFN escalated until saturation (weight ratio of triamcinolone/SFN=0.5) and remained constant thereafter. This led to a peak in encapsulation efficiency of (63.88±1.09)% before declining, while loading capacity continued to increase due to the decreasing amount of SFN[16]. Therefore, a weight ratio of 0.5 was chosen as optimal for subsequent drug loading.

Subsequently, the pre-assembled SFN were pre-mixed with SF solution to fabricate microneedle under-layer. The SEM images revealed loading of SFN had no impact on the morphology of the microneedles. The granular surface texture observed on the SFN group’s surface resulted from nanosphere aggregation. Previous work[27] indicated that assembly of SFN induced an obvious shift in conformation from random-coil to β-sheet. The FTIR resulst are consistent with this conclusion[27]. For effective oral transmucosal delivery, SF microneedles must possess adequate mechanical strength. The force-displacement curve displayed 3 stages: A slow initial rise, a sharp drop, and then a rapid rise. This is consistent with previous work[28]. The 3 stages correspond to the warping, collapse, and compaction of the microneedles during compression[29]. As shown in Figure 2E, the loading of SFN weakens the fracture force of microneedles. This is likely due to nanosphere aggregation under centrifugal force and gravity, resulting cavities within the microneedles[30]. According to Römgen’s research[31], microneedles with a 10 mm tip diameter require a minimum force of 0.05 N per needle to penetrate the stratum corneum. Only the SF [(0.13±0.02) N] and SFN5 [(0.07±0.01) N] groups exhibited sufficient mechanical strength. Histological analysis further confirmed that the SF and SFN5 groups effectively penetrated the epithelial layer of ex vivo oral mucosal tissues. Given that submucosal fibrosis is characterized by juxta-epithelial inflammation and intensified collagen in the lamina propria and submucosal layers[32-33], SF microneedles containing 5% SF nanospheres facilitate drug delivery to the lesion site of oral submucous fibrosis.

The mucoadhesive SF-TA coacervates and lyophilized powder were synthesized following the method and parameters in our previous work[9]. The wavenumber shifts in FTIR indicated the formation of hydrogen bonding between SF and TA, according to previous studies[34]. SF chains experienced a glass transition at 208 ℃ (Tg) ,which increased to 212 ℃ in SF-TA coacervates due to strengthened hydrogen bonding between SF and TA[35]. This suggests that TA readily binds to SF through non-covalent hydrogen bonding, facilitating their layer-by-layer self-assembly into SF-TA coacervates[36]. The loss modulus (G") consistently surpassed the storage modulus (G') across various frequencies and strain levels, indicating the stable flowability of SF-TA coacervates[37]. As temperature rose, both G' and G" values decreased rapidly with a transition from a rubbery state to a viscous flow state at 13.6 ℃. This transition is attributed to the disruption of hydrogen bonds during heating[38].

Ensuring robust interfacial adhesion in the double-layered microneedle patch is crucial to prevent delamination during usage. The SF-TA layer and SF layer exhibited improved interfacial adhesion [(94.80± 6.89) kPa] compared to SF-SF interface [(0.27±0.08) kPa] and other reported double-layered patch interfaces[39]. This enhancement stems from the closely matched surface tension and polarity of the two SF-based materials, reducing voids and defects in the interface[40]. Furthermore, the wet adhesion capacity of double-layered mucoadhesive microneedle patch was compared with 2 commercial buccal patches, Maidi patch® and Zhende patch®. The double-layered patch demonstrated superior wet adhesion (41.28 kPa) on oral mucosal tissues, approximately 10 times higher than that of the commercial oral patches (4.5 kPa). When compared with other reported wet adhesives[41], the double-layered patch exhibited equivalent or even superior wet adhesive strength. This strong wet adhesion likely results from non-covalent bonding between the patch and oral mucosal tissue[42-43]. The free phenolic hydroxyl groups in the patch interact with the amino and sulfhydryl groups in the oral mucosal tissue through hydrogen bonding[44]. Consequently, the mucoadhesive microneedle patch demonstrates enhanced wet adhesion and maintains its integrity during use without delamination.

A series of experiments were conducted to evaluate the cytotoxicity of the patch. Results from live/dead staining, phalloidin staining, and CCK-8 assay collectively demonstrated the excellent cyto-compatibility of the patch. Notably, the SF group exhibited a significant proliferative effect (cell viability>100%) compared to the control group. This may attribute to the regeneration effect of SF[45-48]. Both SF and TA are FDA-approved biomaterials, and the entire preparation process is environmentally friendly and non-toxic[49-50]. Therefore, our conclusion is reliable and consistent with above previous studies. When the release curve no longer increased, triamcinolone was considered to be fully released, with the release percentage at this point defined as 100%. The cumulative drug release at this point represented the drug loading capacity, which was 0.90, 0.98, and 0.90 μg per needle for the SF, SFN and SFN-SF groups, respectively. In clinic, the injection dose of triamcinolone for OSF ranges from 2 to 40 mg per administration. Previous study[13] showed that microneedle systems achieved a drug loading capacity of 2 mg of triamcinolone. For clinical applications, the required drug loading can be achieved by adjusting the microneedle array density. Compared to the SF group, the groups containing SFN showed a sustained release of triamcinolone. There are 3 possible reasons. Firstly, the electrostatic interactions between triamcinolone and SFN gradually intensify as triamcinolone release increase the ionic strength of the release medium[42, 46-47]. Additionally, acetone dehydration leads to the inward folding of the hydrophobic part of SF molecules during the pre-assembly process of SFN, enhancing hydrophobic interactions with triamcinolone inside SFN[16, 42]. Lastly, in the SFN-SF group, the encapsulation of nanospheres by SF results in the more sustained release of triamcinolone. Hence, the mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres demonstrates a more sustained drug release of 14 days. Furthermore, we will assess the patch’s drug release and therapeutic efficacy through in vivo experiments.

In conclusion, a SF mucoadhesive microneedle patch with pre-assembled nanospheres exhibited mechanical strength, wet adhesive properties, and biocompatibility. Most significantly, it sustained triamcinolone release time to 14 days and fulfilled clinical requirements for drug releasing time.

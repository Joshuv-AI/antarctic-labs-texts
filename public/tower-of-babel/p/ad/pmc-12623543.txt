Associations of Folate and Homocysteine Levels with Futile Recanalization in Acute Ischemic Stroke After Successful Endovascular Thrombectomy
Authors: Taoyuan Lu, Wenbo Cao, Bin Yang, Dechao Wang, Jinzi Wei, Liqun Jiao, Xin Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOur preliminary study identified two metabolically distinct thrombus subtypes, demonstrating enhanced lipid metabolic signatures but downregulated folate biosynthesis pathways associated with poor prognosis. This study aimed to assess the prognostic value of routine laboratory parameters involved in lipid and folate metabolism for predicting 90-day futile recanalization (FR) in patients with anterior circulation acute ischemic stroke caused by large vessel occlusion (AIS-LVO) who achieved successful recanalization by endovascular thrombectomy.MethodsConsecutive patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO who achieved successful recanalization (modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score of 2b-3) were retrospectively screened from April 2019 to February 2024. Admission serum levels of traditional and non-traditional lipid parameters, folate, and homocysteine (Hcy) were measured. FR was defined as a 90-day modified Rankin Scale score of 3–6, despite successful recanalization. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify predictors for FR, which were incorporated into a predictive nomogram.ResultsAmong 446 enrolled patients [median age 65 (IQR, 56–72.75) years; 32.1% female], 210 (47.1%) experienced 90-day FR. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that lower admission serum folate and higher Hcy levels were independently associated with increased 90-day FR risk. In contrast, neither admission traditional or non-traditional lipid parameters were independent predictors. The addition of folate and Hcy, individually or combined, significantly improved the predictive performance of conventional clinical factor-based model, as reflected by significant increases in net reclassification improvement and integrated discrimination improvement. Finally, a predictive nomogram was developed incorporating age, admission National Institute of Health Stroke Scale, puncture-to-recanalization time, and admission serum glucose, folate, and Hcy levels.ConclusionsAdmission serum folate and Hcy levels are independent predictors of 90-day FR risk and may enhance risk stratification and guide personalized secondary prevention strategies in patients with successfully recanalized AIS-LVO.Keywords: Acute ischemic stroke, Endovascular thrombectomy, Folate, Homocysteine, Futile recanalization

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Despite successful endovascular thrombectomy (EVT), approximately half of patients with anterior circulation acute ischemic stroke caused by large vessel occlusion (AIS-LVO) fail to achieve 90-day functional independence, a phenomenon termed futile recanalization (FR).Existing predictive models for 90-day FR remain limited. Introduction

Acute ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion (AIS-LVO) represents a leading cause of global mortality and long-term disability [1]. Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) has revolutionized the therapeutic strategy for AIS-LVO, achieving successful recanalization in 80–90% of cases [2]. However, successful recanalization fails to translate into functional independence for approximately half of these patients, a phenomenon termed futile recanalization (FR) [3]. Despite considerable research efforts to identify FR risk factors, such as age, blood glucose, and admission National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) scores, their predictive consistency remains suboptimal for clinical translation [4–7], possibly due to the inherent heterogeneity of the disease (e.g., distinct etiology, pathophysiology, and severity) and variations in populations (e.g., diverse genetic and metabolic backgrounds) [8].

Our prior thrombus metabolomic study established a correlation between thrombus metabolic profiles and stroke etiology [9]. Subsequent molecular subtyping identified two metabolically distinct subtypes, designated C1 and C2. The C1 thrombi exhibited elevated levels of lipid metabolites, such as triglyceride (TG), diglyceride, and phosphoethanolamine, while the C2 thrombi showed higher folate levels. Metabolite set enrichment analysis further indicated that the C1 subtype was associated with enhanced lipid metabolic signatures but downregulated folate biosynthesis pathways compared to the C2 subtype. Clinically, patients with C1 thrombi demonstrated higher rates of early neurological deterioration, with only 15.4% achieving 90-day functional independence. These findings suggest that systemic perturbations in lipid and folate metabolism may contribute to 90-day FR, and that circulating biomarkers reflecting these metabolic pathways could serve as non-invasive prognostic indicators. Therefore, in this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis on patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO who underwent successful EVT to evaluate the prognostic utility of routinely measured serum laboratory parameters related to lipid metabolism (traditional and non-traditional lipid parameters) and folate metabolism [folate and homocysteine (Hcy)] for predicting 90-day FR, which is hitherto unexplored in this context.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

Consecutive patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO who underwent successful EVT were screened at a comprehensive stroke center from April 2019 to February 2024. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) first-ever anterior circulation AIS-LVO involving occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or middle cerebral artery (MCA); (3) successful recanalization achieved by EVT with or without intravenous thrombolysis (IVT), as determined by the modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score (mTICI) of 2b-3; and (4) premorbid modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of ≤ 1. Exclusion criteria included: (1) severe cardiac, hepatic or renal failure; (2) recent or active infections; (3) active cancer; and (4) incomplete clinical or follow-up data. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University (Approval ID: CRB-124/2021), and conducted in accordance with the Principles of Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Clinical Data Collection

Data were retrospectively collected on: (1) demographics; (2) cardiovascular risk factors; (3) interventional procedure parameters; (4) stroke etiology classified using the Trial of Org 10,172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) criteria; (5) ischemic lesions assessed using ASPECTS; (6) stroke severity assessed using NIHSS; and (7) 90-day functional outcome assessed using mRS, with 90-day FR defined as mRS of 3–6, despite successful recanalization (mTICI of 2b-3) [4–7]. All clinical and imaging assessments were performed by certified neurologists and radiologists who were blinded to the clinical data.

Laboratory Parameters Related to Lipid and Folate Metabolism

Peripheral venous blood was routinely collected for laboratory tests upon admission prior to IVT (if applicable) and EVT. The following laboratory parameters were extracted: total cholesterol (TC), TG, high density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), apolipoprotein A-I (ApoA-I), apolipoprotein B (ApoB), folate, and Hcy. Non-traditional lipid parameters calculated included: non-HDL-C (TC-HDL-C), remnant cholesterol (RC; TC-HDL-C-LDL-C), TC/HDL-C ratio, TG/HDL-C ratio, LDL-C/HDL-C ratio, ApoB/ApoA-I ratio, and ApoB/HDL-C ratio [10].

Statistics Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software (version 4.3.1) and SPSS (version 26.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Quantitative variables were reported as mean (standard deviation; SD) or median (inter quartile range; IQR), based on their normality as evaluated by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Intergroup differences were analyzed using independent Student’s t test or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical data were presented as frequencies (percentages), and intergroup differences were analyzed by Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. The associations of laboratory parameters with 90-day FR were analyzed with multivariate logistic regression analysis that adjust for potential confounders (p < 0.05 in univariate logistic regression analysis). A nomogram was then developed based on the logistic regression model. To evaluate the predictive capacities of the identified laboratory parameters and the nomogram, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) were utilized. The DeLong test was used to compare the area under the curves (AUCs) to determine whether the new variables enhanced the predictive power of conventional risk factor-based model [11]. Furthermore, net reclassification index (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were calculated to evaluate the improvement in discrimination and reclassification afforded by the new variables [12]. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 764 consecutive patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO who underwent successful EVT were initially screened. Among them, 446 patients [median age 65 (IQR, 56–72.75) years; 32.1% female] were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). The detailed demographic and clinical characteristics of the study population are summarized in Table 1. Briefly, the median admission NIHSS and ASPECTS scores were 14 (IQR, 11–18) and 8 (IQR, 7–9), respectively. One hundred and fifty (33.6%) patients received IVT before EVT. The median time from stroke onset to blood testing was 322 (IQR, 205–478.3) minutes.

Among the 446 enrolled patients, 210 (47.1%) experienced 90-day FR. Compared to the non-FR group, patients with FR were older age, had higher admission NIHSS scores, lower admission ASPECTS scores, and longer puncture-to-recanalization (PTR) time, and required more thrombectomy passes (Table 1). Univariate analyses revealed that, in addition to these variables, patients with FR had significantly higher admission serum glucose levels [7.22 (IQR, 6.11–9.36) vs. 6.64 (IQR, 5.82–8.14) mmol/L; p < 0.001], lower admission serum folate levels [5.7 (IQR, 4.7–6.6) vs. 7.2 (IQR, 6.3–8.1) ng/mL; p = 0.002], and higher admission serum Hcy levels [13.2 (IQR, 11.1–14.9) vs. 10.4 (IQR, 9.3–11.8) μmol/L; p < 0.001] (Table 2). Of note, neither traditional nor non-traditional lipid parameters showed significant intergroup differences (Table 2). Multivariate logistics regression analysis, adjusting for potential confounders, confirmed that older age [adjusted odds ratio (aOR) per 1-year increase, 1.03 (95%CI 1.01–1.05); p = 0.012], higher admission NIHSS [aOR per 1-point increase, 1.09 (95%CI 1.03–1.15); p = 0.002], longer PTR time [aOR per 1-min increase, 1.01 (95%CI 1–1.02); p = 0.018], higher admission serum glucose levels [aOR per-1 mmol/L increase, 1.11 (95%CI 1.03–1.2); p = 0.006], lower admission serum folate levels [aOR per 1-ng/mL increase, 0.4 (95%CI 0.32–0.5); p < 0.001], and higher admission serum Hcy levels [aOR per 1-μmol/L increase, 1.4 (95%CI 1.27–1.53); p < 0.001] remained independently associated with 90-day FR (Table 2). ROC analysis demonstrated that admission serum folate level predicted 90-day FR with an AUC of 0.788 (95%CI 0.746–0.829), showing 72.9% sensitivity and 71.6% specificity at a cutoff value of 6.45 ng/mL. Admission serum Hcy level predicted 90-day FR with an AUC of 0.745 (95%CI 0.696–0.793), with 60.5% sensitivity and 84.3% specificity at a cutoff of 12.35 μmol/L (Fig. 2). Since age, admission NIHSS, PTR time, and admission serum glucose are well-established risk factors [4–7], they were combined to form a conventional model, which yielded an AUC of 0.728 (95%CI 0.682–0.775). The incorporation of folate, Hcy, or both into this model significantly improved the AUC to 0.841 (95%CI 0.805–0.878; p < 0.001), 0.808 (95%CI 0.767–0.85; p < 0.001), and 0.887 (95%CI 0.858–0.917; p < 0.001), respectively (Fig. 2). Moreover, these additions resulted in significant improvements in both the NRI and the IDI (Table 3), supporting the incremental predictive value of folate and Hcy beyond conventional factors.

To enable individualized risk estimation, a nomogram was developed to visualize the predictive model incorporating the six independent predictors for 90-day FR (Fig. 3A). Higher total scores indicated an increased risk of 90-day FR, whereas lower scores were associated with decreased risk. Calibration curve analysis showed close agreement between predicted and observed probabilities (Hosmer–Lemeshow test; χ2 = 10.612, p = 0.154; Fig. 3B), indicating excellent model calibration. Further plotting of DCA curves revealed that in the range of FR probability thresholds from 0.1 to 0.99, the nomogram model got better net benefits compared with “all (assuming FR occurred in all patients)”, “none (assuming none of the patients developed FR)”, and any single predictor alone, confirming its high clinical utility in predicting 90-day FR in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO who underwent successful EVT (Fig. 3C).

Discussion

We have demonstrated that lower admission serum folate levels and higher admission serum Hcy levels were independently associated with an increased risk of 90-day FR in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO undergoing successful EVT. In contrast, both traditional and non-traditional lipid parameters showed modest association with 90-day FR. Furthermore, we developed a predictive nomogram, incorporating age, admission NIHSS, PTR time, and admission serum glucose, folate, and Hcy levels, which demonstrated good performance in discrimination, calibration, and clinical applicability.

While dyslipidemia is a well-established risk factor for AIS, the prognostic value of blood lipid profiles specifically in patients with AIS-LVO who undergo EVT remains incompletely defined, with existing literature reporting conflicting findings [10, 13–19]. For instance, Escudero-Martínez et al. [15] identified reduced TC levels as a predictor for moderate to severe cerebral edema following reperfusion therapies, but found no correlations with parenchymal hemorrhage (PH), functional independence, or mortality. Li et al. [16] revealed that elevated HDL-C levels and decreased TG/HDL-C values independently predicted PH after EVT. Another cohort study indicated that elevated LDL-C levels were associated with improved 90-day functional outcomes in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO [17]. Paradoxically, however, LDL-C ≤ 50 mg/dL was identified as an independent predictor of delayed PH, whereas LDL-C > 100 mg/dL appeared to be protective [18]. Conversely, another study linked higher LDL-C levels to an increased risk of intracranial hemorrhage [19]. Our cohort of 446 patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO, consistent with several previous studies [13, 14], failed to establish definitive associations between both admission traditional and non-traditional lipid parameters and 90-day FR. These discrepancies may arise from heterogeneity in study populations, variations in the timing of blood sampling, and differences in data analytical approaches. Another plausible explanation is that cerebral cholesterol homeostasis is primarily maintained through de novo synthesis rather than peripheral circulation [20, 21], suggesting that circulating cholesterol may have limited influence on post-stroke brain pathology despite blood–brain barrier disruption [22]. Importantly, beyond quantitative measures, qualitative characteristics of lipoproteins (e.g., particle size, density, surface charge, composition, and functional properties) may contribute to AIS pathophysiology [14]. Future comprehensive compositional and functional analyses of lipoprotein fractions may uncover novel diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers [14].

Epidemiological evidence establishes folate deficiency and resultant hyperhomocysteinemia, frequently involving the genetic methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) C677T polymorphism, as significant risk factors for the occurrence, progression, and recurrence of ischemic stroke [23–26]. Hyperhomocysteinemia exerts pathogenic effects mainly through oxidative stress and inflammation-induced endothelial dysfunction and neuronal damage, with platelet activation emerging as a critical intermediary in Hcy-mediated thrombosis [25–27]. Robust clinical evidence from randomized controlled trials supports folate supplementation, primarily via Hcy modulation, as an effective strategy for primary stroke prevention [28, 29]. Experimental studies further elucidate folate's pleiotropic neuroprotective effects, such as attenuating neuroinflammation and promoting angiogenesis and ischemic tolerance [30, 31]. Clinical investigations have confirmed correlations between folate/Hcy levels and clinical outcomes in patients with AIS [32, 33]. Notably, a landmark Chinese cohort study involving 3,530 patients with AIS found elevated folate and reduced Hcy levels independently predicted decreased mortality and disability, identifying Hcy as a partial mediator of folate's therapeutic benefits [33]. However, the prognostic implications of folate and Hcy specifically in patients with AIS-LVO who undergo successful EVT remain underexplored. Aligning with established pathophysiological paradigms, our study identified lower admission serum folate and higher admission serum Hcy levels as independent predictors of increased 90-day FR risk. Supporting this, Jiang et al. [34] analyzed plasma metabolic profiles in patients with AIS and identified folate biosynthesis as correlated with NIHSS and mRS scores, indicating its relevance to stroke severity and disability. Collectively, these findings suggest that Hcy influences both acute ischemic pathophysiology and recovery processes after recanalization, and that targeted folate supplementation may represent a promising adjuvant treatment to improve outcomes in patients with AIS-LVO undergoing successful EVT. It is noteworthy that trials like Vitamin Intervention for Stroke Prevention (VISP) and VITAmins TO Prevent Stroke (VITATOPS) failed to demonstrate significant benefits of vitamin B-mediated Hcy-lowering for secondary stroke prevention [35, 36]. This may be attributed to participant recruitment from regions with high folate consumption or fortification policies, where folate saturation potentially obscured therapeutic benefits via a ceiling effect [35–37]. Crucially, China represents a folate-deficient region without mandatory fortification, enhancing the relevance of our findings for understanding folate's therapeutic potential in post-recanalization recovery within this context. In addition, emerging evidence suggests platelet activation parameters (e.g., plateletcrit) and MTHFR C677T genotypic variations may influence the clinical efficacy of folate intake [27]. Future large-scale prospective studies in folate-deficient populations should incorporate comprehensive analyses of folate-Hcy axis modulators, including genetic factors, to optimize adjuvant therapeutic strategies for enhancing recovery.

In this study, we confirmed that age, admission NIHSS score, PTR time, and admission serum glucose level are independent risk factors for 90-day FR [4–7]. It is important to emphasize, however, that these findings should not preclude the use of EVT in more severe cases. For example, although advanced age has consistently been associated with unfavorable outcomes after AIS—likely due to greater comorbidity burden, higher complication rates, and reduced rehabilitation potential—implementing a strict upper age limit for EVT raises significant ethical concerns, as many elderly patients can still derive substantial benefit from the procedure [38]. The safety and efficacy of EVT in very old patients (e.g., those over 85 years of age) remain a critical area requiring further investigation. In addition, recent evidence suggests that tirofiban confers particular benefits in stroke patients with large artery atherosclerosis who achieve complete reperfusion and present with high baseline NIHSS scores [39, 40]. This underscores the potential for refining patient selection criteria to maximize treatment efficacy in severe stroke cases.

The nomogram is a visual clinical predictive tool designed to estimate disease risk and prognosis. It generates graphs based on predictive statistical models and has garnered increasing attention and application in the context of AIS-LVO [6, 7]. Distinct from previous models, the present study uniquely incorporated admission serum folate and Hcy levels. Their incorporation, either individually or in combination, significantly improved the predictive performance of the conventional model based on established clinical risk factors, as evidenced by increases in AUC, NRI, and IDI. These results underscore the incremental prognostic value offered by folate and Hcy. An interesting observation is that the AUC values for predicting 90-day FR using either folate or Hcy were higher than those of conventional clinical factors. This may reflect their direct involvement in pathological pathways underlying FR, a hypothesis that merits further investigation. Furthermore, we demonstrated that the nomogram incorporating the six independent predictors had good performance in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical applicability. Nevertheless, it should be noted that other prognostic biomarkers, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), were not included in the current model [6]. Future studies should seek to integrate additional routinely available laboratory parameters to further enhance predictive accuracy and clinical applicability.

Our study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center and retrospective study with a moderate sample size and lacked external validation. Second, participants were predominantly Han Chinese (95.2%), necessitating further validation across diverse ethnicities and geographic regions. Third, although we adjusted for multiple potential confounders during the data analyses, residual confounding influences (e.g., unmeasured blood levels of vitamins B6 and B12, or genetic factors) [24, 27] cannot be completely excluded. Finally, the observed correlations between admission serum folate/Hcy levels and 90-day FR do not necessarily indicate causation, warranting further investigation.

Conclusions

Our blood-based biomarker analysis revealed the prognostic significance of admission serum folate and Hcy levels in patients with anterior circulation AIS-LVO undergoing successful EVT. These findings support their potential utility in improving risk stratification and guiding personalized secondary prevention strategies. Furthermore, this study suggests that folate supplementation warrants further investigation as a potential adjuvant therapy after EVT to improve patient outcomes. Randomized trials are required to validate these findings.

DEA hypothesis: disinhibition, enhancing recognition, and alerting the host are the three pillars of effective cancer immunotherapy
Authors: Xiao-Song Wang, Michael T Lotze
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhy are clonal neoantigens paradoxically rare in established tumors? Why does tumor-associated antigen (TAA) burden inversely correlate with T-cell infiltration? Why do damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP)/danger signal-inducing interventions (messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines, stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT), immunogenic chemotherapy) enhance checkpoint blockade activity in “cold” tumors? We propose the “DEA Hypothesis” to resolve these paradoxes: durable antitumor immunity for established tumors requires three convergent signals—(1) Disinhibition of T cells through checkpoint blockade, (2) Enhanced recognition via the presentation of clonal neoantigens, and crucially, (3) Alerting the host through release of DAMPs/danger signal(s). We frame cancer as a subversion of an ancient “Guardian” system—the emergency hematopoietic response that evolved to orchestrate immune defense following epithelial breach or major trauma. Tumors hijack this system by promoting restorative hematopoiesis at the expense of immunity and masquerading as chronic wounds. This framework explains key observations: the paucity of clonal neoantigens reflects successful immunoediting over years of tumor evolution, not failed presentation. TAAs persist in cold tumors because insufficient DAMPs limit the “Decision to Attack”; and mRNA vaccines, recently SBRT, and immunogenic chemotherapy succeed in cold tumors by providing the missing Alert. An ancient guardian mediating immunosurveillance

Understanding the evolutionary underpinnings of immunosurveillance is fundamental to rational therapeutic design. Cancer represents a failure of the internal policing system that has safeguarded the epithelial integrity of multicellular organisms for over 500 million years. The bone marrow functions as the major rapid response center for two distinct emergencies: acute hemorrhage (driving platelet and erythroid recovery) or epithelial barrier breach (driving myelopoiesis and lymphopoiesis to recruit cells to threatened sites). This latter pathway—what we call the “Guardian” response—is initiated by damage and pathogen-associated molecular pattern molecules (DAMPs and PAMPs) signaling through pattern recognition receptors.1 In this context, they represent “Signal 0” which in turn licenses dendritic cells (DCs) and promotes rapid immune cell deployment. Modern immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) is effective not because it invents a novel mechanism, but because it revitalizes this ancient capacity of T cells to identify and eliminate malignant cells, again mediated through initial recognition and processing by DCs.2

How cancer hijacks the guardian system

Established malignancies systematically subvert the Guardian response by exploiting a critical decision node: the distinction between “attack” (requiring elimination) and “repair” (requiring tolerance and healing). Harold Dvorak’s description of tumors as “wounds that never heal” gains mechanistic specificity through this lens.3 Central to this subversion is the manipulation of DAMPs. The DAMP High Mobility Group Box 1 (HMGB1) serves as a master switch: in its poly(ADP-ribosyl)ated form, it triggers γδ T cell-mediated inflammation as well as reparative angiogenesis; in the oxidative tumor microenvironment (TME), it converts to an oxidized isoform that drives tolerance.4 Some tumors can also convert the pro-inflammatory ATP “find-me” signal through expression of the ectoenzymes CD39 and CD73 into adenosine, a potent immunosuppressant.5 Consequently, the bone marrow perceives the tumor as an injury or a chronic wound. Instead of deploying type 1 conventional DCs (cDC1s) and cytotoxic T cells, the hijacked hematopoietic program recruits “repair crews”—myeloid-derived suppressor cells, M2 macrophages, and regulatory T cells. While physiological wound healing culminates in resolution, tumors maintain persistent M2 polarization, sustained angiogenesis, and chronic extracellular matrix remodeling.

Hematopoietic consequences: clinical evidence of guardian hijacking

This subversion of the Guardian system manifests in both the blood and bone marrow. When tumors successfully emerge as chronic wounds, emergency hematopoiesis is redirected from immune defense toward wound repair. The anemia of chronic disease, frequently observed in cancer,6 exemplifies this misdirection. Historically, cancer-associated fatigue was treated with recombinant erythropoietin (EPO). However, randomized studies revealed a paradox: EPO-treated patients experienced shortened survival.7 8 While initially attributed to EPO receptors on tumor cells, emerging evidence suggests that EPO signaling promotes tolerogenic cDC1s—the BatF3+CD103+DCs essential for effective T-cell responses and ICB efficacy.9 This “EPO paradox” underscores a broader principle: interventions that reinforce wound healing often suppress the Guardian response. Conversely, strategies that disrupt these pathways—by provision of DAMPs (eg, by immunogenic chemotherapy) or promoting cDC1 differentiation—may reactivate surveillance.

The “ASR” immune decision points

The immune system confronts tumors at three sequential decision points, each representing a target for tumor manipulation and therapeutic intervention:

Decision to attack (tumor-draining lymph nodes)

The initiation of an immune response occurs in tumor-draining lymph nodes (TDLNs). Programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression on cDC1s acts as a primary gatekeeper. In the absence of DAMPs, DCs default to tolerogenic programming.10

Decision to stop (TME checkpoints)

Within the TME, tumors may overexpress PD-L1 or induce “Metabolic immune checkpoints” adenosine, Transforming Growth Factor -β (TGF-β), interleukin (IL)-10, Prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) or T regulatory cells to suppress antitumor immunity.11

Decision to replace and repair (Efferocytosis)

This is the critical determinant as to whether cell death is immunogenic (ICD) or tolerogenic (silent apoptosis). When phosphatidylserine exposure precedes calreticulin—as in silent apoptosis—MerTK-mediated engulfment drives M2 polarization.12

The three-signal DEA hypothesis

Synthesizing these observations, we propose the “Three Signal DEA Hypothesis”. Effective immunotherapy requires the convergence of three signals:

Signal 1: Disinhibition (D)

Blocking regulatory checkpoints (eg, programmed cell death protein 1/PD-L1, cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4) releases the immune “brakes,” addressing the “Decision to Stop”. However, disinhibition alone is most often insufficient.

Signal 2: Enhanced recognition (E)

High-quality clonal neoantigens, matched to a competent T-cell repertoire, provide the specificity required for targeted tumor destruction.

Signal 3: Alerting the host (A)

An acute danger signal (Signal 0/DAMP release) is required to license cDC1 activation. This addresses the “Decision to Attack”—the critical gatekeeper determining whether an immune response is mounted.

This framework aligns with early murine models that required four components for efficacy:13 (1) high dose IL-2 administration, (2) adoptive transfer of expanded autologous antitumor immune cells, (3) ablation of host immune cells (allowing for homeostatic proliferation), and (4) specific tumor vaccination.

Tumor-intrinsic DAMPs: sources of the alert

The presence or absence of endogenous Alert signals dictates the baseline immune status of a tumor. Endogenous “Alert signals” can arise from tumor mutations through the Poly(ADP-Ribose) Polymerase 1 - High Mobility Group Box 1 (PARP-HMGB1) axis, the integrated stress response (ISR), and the cGAS-STING pathway. In tumors with high tumor mutational burden (TMB), massive single-strand breaks generated during repair processes hyperactivate PARP-1 to release HMGB1.14 Mutations can also induce proteotoxic stress that simultaneously triggers the ISR and expression of immunogenic stress ligands.15 On the other hand, Homologous repair (HR)-deficient tumors accumulate unrepaired DNA damage and cytosolic double-strand DNA (dsDNA)—particularly when treated with PARP inhibitors—that activates cGAS-STING and type I interferon (IFN) production.16 TMB-low tumors lacking these specific triggers result in an “immunologically cold” microenvironment where the absence of Alert signals causes the system to default to tolerance in TDLNs.

Testable hypotheses derived from the DEA framework

Based on the DEA hypothesis, we postulate the following specific, testable predictions:

Hypothesis 1: Exogenous DAMP signals will be most effective in immune-desert PD-L1 negative tumors. In “cold” (low TMB/low IFN-γ) tumors, the absence of intrinsic DAMPs or DAMP signaling prevents engagement of the “Decision to Attack”. Providing exogenous alerts could convert these non-responders to responders.

High-TMB tumors constitutively generate DAMPs, triggering immune infiltration and licensing surveillance. Clinical observations explained by the DEA hypotheses

Emerging clinical data in part support these predictions:

Explainable observations by hypothesis 1 (mRNA vaccine adjuvants)

Most recently, messenger RNA (mRNA) COVID-19 vaccination administered within 100 days of initiating ICB was demonstrated to nearly double overall survival in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and dramatically improved outcomes in patients with melanoma.19 This effect was antigen-agnostic, mediated by MDA5 receptors interacting with the lipid nanoparticle-mRNA complex triggering a type I IFN surge. Crucially, the benefit was pronounced even in patients with immunologically “cold” tumors (PD-L1 <1%). While mechanically sound and supportive of the DEA framework, the retrospective clinical evidence is being reevaluated and requires validation in prospective, randomized settings.

Explainable observations by hypothesis 1 (SBRT+ICB)

The recent ICB-stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) trial in patients with NSCLC demonstrated that SBRT combined with pembrolizumab significantly extended progression-free survival specifically in cold tumors including TMB-low (5.21 vs 1.81 months, p=0.029) and PD-L1-null (4.22 vs 1.71 months, p=0.022). Radiation induces ICD, activates the cGAS/STING pathway and drives HMGB1/DAMP release, providing the missing Alert signal.20

Explainable observations by hypothesis 1 (ICD-Inducing chemotherapy)

Agents such as oxaliplatin and anthracyclines induce ICD with DAMP release, providing the adjuvanticity essential for immunogenicity.21 Their synergy with ICB, particularly in cold tumors, supports the DEA prediction that danger signal provision overcomes checkpoint resistance.

Explainable observations by hypothesis 2 (Immunoediting of neoantigens)

The DEA framework resolves the “neoantigen paradox”—the rarity of clonal neoantigens in established tumors despite their potency as immunological targets.17 18 We postulate this scarcity reflects successful pruning by the Guardian system: in TMB-high tumors, intrinsic released DAMPs driven by mutations and non-apoptotic death license elimination of neoantigen-bearing clones. Consistent with this model, mutations in major histocompatibility complex-I presentation hotspots are depleted in immune-infiltrated tumors,22 and neoantigen vaccines improve survival in the adjuvant setting23 but fail in patients with advanced cancer.24

Explainable observations by hypothesis 3 (TAA Preservation in cold tumors)

In our recent study, we demonstrated that TAA burden is inversely correlated with T-cell exhaustion across pan-cancer datasets, implying successful immunoediting in tumors with established immunity. Conversely, high TAA burden was preserved in tumors with low T-cell exhaustion and strongly predicted ICB response in this subset, validating that TAAs remain available targets in “cold” tumors where DAMPs have been inhibited or absent.25

Therapeutic priority for passive cold tumors: alerting the host

The DEA framework reframes the challenge of passive cold tumors—those with low TMB but high TAA burden and lacking active immunosuppressive mechanisms: the primary deficit is not a paucity of antigens, but an absence of accompanying DAMPs. We propose evidence-based strategies to provide this missing signal: (1) mRNA vaccine+ICB: Exploiting the antigen-agnostic, MDA5-mediated type I IFN response intrinsically triggered by lipid nanoparticle-mRNA complexes to convert cold tumors into ICB responders. (2) SBRT+ICB: Harnessing SBRT to induce localized ICD and DAMP release or expression (HMGB1, ATP, calreticulin), thereby priming systemic antitumor immunity. (3) ICD-inducing chemotherapy: Prioritizing regimens containing oxaliplatin or anthracyclines for their capacity to generate DAMP signals concurrent with tumor cytoreduction. (4) TAA-targeted vaccines with adjuvants: Leveraging the preserved antigen landscape in cold tumors through vaccines combined with potent DAMP signal adjuvants (Toll-Like Receptor / Stimulator of Interferon Genes [TLR/STING] agonists).

Conclusion: from empirical combinations to mechanism-based design

The three-signal DEA hypothesis transforms cancer immunotherapy from empirical combination testing to rational, mechanism-based design. This framework posits that effective antitumor immunity requires the coordinated integration of three therapeutic pillars: (1) Disinhibition of immune checkpoints, (2) Enhancement of antigen recognition, and (3) Alarm provision via Signal 0/DAMP/danger signals. We predict that only through the synergistic engagement of all three components can a fully licensed, sustained antitumor immune response be achieved in patients with cancer. Optimal treatment requires defining the tumor’s immunological state and supplying the missing signals. For TMB-high “hot” tumors, Disinhibition alone may suffice. For the critical unmet need of TMB-low “cold” tumors, the priority is Alerting the host—breaking the wound-healing paradigm not just by releasing the brakes, but by providing the ignition. Only through the coordinated integration of Disinhibition, Enhanced recognition, and Alerting the host can we fully reawaken the ancient Guardian that has protected multicellular life for eons.

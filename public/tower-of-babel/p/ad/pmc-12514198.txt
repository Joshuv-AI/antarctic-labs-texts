Prognostic risk factors and a survival prediction model for immune checkpoint inhibitor related myocarditis in patients with lung cancer: a multicenter study
Authors: Xiaoyun Cheng, Lingzhi Long, Yuzhang Li, Xia Gan, Pan Yu, Xiangyu Zhang, Guoliang Jiang, Tingting Yao, Mao Jiang, Wei Xie, Jie Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-related myocarditis is a rare but fatal immune-related adverse event in lung cancer patients, with limited multivariate prognostic analysis. This study aimed to identify risk factors for severity, major adverse cardiac events (MACE) and survival time, and develop a survival prediction model. Data from 70 lung cancer patients with ICI-related myocarditis (training set) and 40 patients (validation set) were analyzed, with ≥ 1.5 years of follow-up. Cox regression was employed to determine factors associated with survival time, and Logistic regression models identified risk factors for severe myocarditis and MACE. Several factors were independently associated with all-cause death: protective factors included combined radiotherapy (HR 0.12, 95%CI: 0.01–0.98, p = 0.047) and longer ICI treatment duration (≥ 132 days, HR 0.93, 95%CI: 0.91–0.98, p = 0.013); risk factors included low-dose glucocorticoid use in patients with severe myocarditis (HR 3.92, 95%CI: 1.16–13.2, p = 0.028). A nomogram model constructed based on these three variables yielded area under the time-ROC curves of 0.832, 0.835, and 0.924 for 0.5-, 1-, and 1.5-year survival in the training set, and 0.821, 0.806, and 0.789 in the validation set, respectively. It also demonstrated good discriminative ability and clinical utility for predicting survival in lung cancer patients with ICI-related myocarditis, as this study established a validated nomogram model that may aid survival prediction in this population. Additionally, we analyzed the risk factors for severe ICI related myocarditis and 90-day MACE. We found that the use of angiogenesis inhibitors was an independent risk factor for severe myocarditis (OR 18.72, 95% CI: 2.52–428.27, p = 0.02); a history of coronary artery disease (OR 10.54, 95% CI: 1.62–210.10, p = 0.037) was an independent risk factor for 90-day MACE; and left ventricular ejection fraction (OR 0.94, 95% CI: 0.88–0.99, p = 0.026) was an independent protective factor against 90-day MACE.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19460-2.

Introduction

Over the past decade, immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy has become an essential treatment approach for lung cancer1. It works by inhibiting the binding of ICIs (i.e., CTLA-4 or PD-1) to their ligands, which promotes the activation and proliferation of cytotoxic T cells. However, excessive activation of T cells can result in immune-related adverse events (irAEs)2, with ICI-related myocarditis being the most severe and potentially fatal among them3. The incidence of ICI-related myocarditis ranges from 0.27–1.14%,4,5 among which 40%-50% are classified as severe cases (graded ≥ 3 according to the ASCO guidelines), and the short-term (< 6 months) cardiovascular mortality rate in patients with severe myocarditis is 17%-50%.4,6,7 This high severity and mortality highlight the urgency of identifying prognostic factors to guide clinical management. The risk factors of ICI-related myocarditis such as combined anti-CTLA-4 and anti-PD-1 therapy7, multi-organ irAEs have been reported8. The clinical manifestations of ICI-related myocarditis are often significantly heterogeneous, ranging from fulminant cases with high mortality rates to smoldering cases with elevated troponin levels but no cardiovascular symptoms9. Presently, there is a lack of consensus regarding the prevention, screening, monitoring and treatment of ICI-related myocarditis10–12, highlighting the need for further optimization in these areas.

This study investigates the occurrence, severity, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and all-cause mortality associated with ICI-related myocarditis in lung cancer patients. The primary objectives include identifying risk factors and prognostic indicators, developing and evaluating a survival prediction model specific to ICI-related myocarditis, and exploring diagnostic strategies and treatment management approaches.

Method

Research object

The data of lung cancer patients were retrospectively collected from January 2019 to December 2022 (training set) and December 2022 to November 2023 (validation set) from the electronic case system of four large tertiary care medical centers. Xiangya Hospital Research Ethics Committee approved this study (NO.202212310). This research entailed collecting clinical records exclusively from routine diagnostic and therapeutic procedures performed in strict adherence to relevant guidelines. Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects, consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki. The definition of myocarditis followed the consensus statement from the International Cardio-Oncology Society (Supplementary file 1)13, and patients were categorized into possible/probable/confirmed myocarditis according to the case definitions proposed by Bonaca et al. (Supplementary file 2)14. Myocardial biopsy was performed in 3 patients to confirm myocarditis. For the remaining confirmed patients, diagnosis was based on non-invasive criteria: Diagnostic CMR + syndrome + biomarker, or ECG OR Echo WMA + syndrome + biomarker + ECG + negative angiography. In the total cohort (n = 110), there were 33 (30.0%) confirmed cases, 58 (52.7%) probable cases, and 19 (17.3%) possible cases. The distribution of myocarditis cases across different cohorts was showed in Table S1. No significant difference was observed between the training set and the validation set (p = 0.89).

To illustrate the clinical manifestations of ICI-related myocarditis in our cohort, Fig. S1 presents typical examination results, including arrhythmia, immune cell infiltration in myocardial biopsy, abnormalities on echocardiography, and myocardial edema on cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, providing visual evidence for the diagnosis of myocarditis in our study.

The study exclusion criteria were: (i) patients receiving anthracyclines (due to their cardiotoxicity); (ii) diagnosis of advanced tumors with an expected survival of less than 90 days; (iii) confirmation of concurrent acute coronary syndrome through coronary angiography; (iv) incomplete examination results or loss to follow-up. Medical records, including demographics, lung cancer pathological subtype, metastasis, treatment regimen, previous cardiovascular history, ICI treatment protocols, adverse events, blood routine, cardiac enzymes, NT-proBNP, electrocardiogram, cardiac ultrasound, cardiovascular magnetic resonance, etc., were collected at the time of initial ICI treatment (baseline data) and at the time of myocarditis diagnosis. Definition and classification

MACE is defined as the composite outcome of ICI-related cardiovascular death, cardiac arrest, cardiogenic shock, hemodynamically significant complete heart block and severe heart failure15. Myocarditis severity was graded according to ASCO Guideline16. The classification of cardiac function is based on the New York Heart Association17.

Glucocorticoid dose classification: Based on the prednisone-equivalent initial dose (consistent with the ASCO Guideline16 for ICI-related myocarditis management), glucocorticoid therapy was stratified into two groups: (1) Low-dose: prednisone-equivalent dose < 100 mg/day (or methylprednisolone < 80 mg/day); (2) High-dose: prednisone-equivalent dose ≥ 100 mg/day (or methylprednisolone ≥ 80 mg/day).

Data analysis

In the study, categorical variables were presented as n (%) and compared using the chi-square test, while continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range). Non-parametric tests were used to analyze and compare the clinical characteristics of severe and non-severe ICI-related myocarditis. Logistic univariate and multivariate analyses were conducted to identify risk factors associated with severe ICI-related myocarditis and the occurrence of MACE within 90 days. For survival analysis assessing all-cause mortality, the time origin was defined as the onset of ICI-related myocarditis, and the endpoint was all-cause death or the last follow-up (for censored cases). Cox analysis was performed to determine independent factors associated with survival, which were then used to establish a prognostic nomogram. To mitigate overfitting risk, we also used LASSO penalized regression to select the most robust prognostic variables. The penalty parameter (λ) was determined via 10-fold cross-validation. Features with non-zero coefficients were selected using the λ.1-se criterion. Data statistics and graphing were performed using R Studio (version 4.2.0; R Studio, Boston, Massachusetts). The survival model was constructed using the “survival” package. The optimal cutoff point for continuous variables was determined using the surv_cutpoint function from the “survminer” package. As for model evaluation, its’ accuracy was evaluated using area under the time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) with the “timeROC” package, discriminative ability was assessed using calibration plots, and clinical utility was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA) with the “ggDCA” package. Internal validation was performed using Bootstrap (B = 500). Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests were employed to conduct prognostic risk stratification. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics and prognosis of patients with ICI-related myocarditis

A total of 110 patients were enrolled and followed up over 1.5 years. Their baseline information at the time of the first immunotherapy is shown in Table 1. The patients were predominantly older males. The tumor stage of participants was predominantly stage Ⅳ (54 cases, 49.1%), followed by stage Ⅲ (31 cases, 28.2%). For the ECOG PS score, most patients had scores of 0–1 (104 cases, 94.5%). Additionally, there were no statistically significant differences in the distributions of tumor stage and ECOG PS between the training set and the validation set (p = 0.32). 88.2% (97 cases) received anti-PD-1 immunotherapy, and the remaining patients received either anti-PD-L1 monotherapy or a combination of anti-CTLA-4 therapy (13 case, 11.8%). The majority of patients (89 cases, 80.9%) received a combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy. Among the patients, 49 cases (44.5%) had extracardiac irAEs, including 14 cases of myositis, 11 cases of hepatitis or liver injury, 24 cases of thyroiditis, et al. 24 patients had concurrent or sequential use of angiogenesis inhibitor (21.8%). 26 patients (23.6%) underwent combined radiotherapy. 66.4% (73 cases) of myocarditis cases were severe. 50 cases (45.5%) experienced MACE. For prognosis, the median survival time was 321 days, and 40 patients (36.4%) had all-cause mortality during follow-up.

There were no statistical differences in baseline characteristics or prognosis between the training set and validation set, indicating similar feature distribution between the two groups of patients. (Table 1)

Risk factors for All-Cause mortality and prognostic model construction in ICI-Related myocarditis

Cox regression analysis identified both protective and unfavorable independent factors associated with all-cause mortality. Protective prognostic factors included the administration of combined radiotherapy (HR = 0.12, 95%CI: 0.01–0.98, p = 0.047) and a longer duration of ICI treatment (≥ 132 days, HR = 0.93, 95%CI: 0.91–0.98, p = 0.013). In contrast, unfavorable prognostic factors were the use of low-dose glucocorticoids in patients with severe myocarditis (defined as prednisone-equivalent dose < 100 mg/day, HR = 3.92, 95%CI: 1.16–13.2, p = 0.028) and the presence of grade IV heart failure (HR = 3.64, 95%CI: 1.07–12.37, p = 0.038).

Regarding some clinically relevant lung cancer prognostic factors, univariate Cox regression analysis showed that multiple metastases (HR 1.11, 95% CI: 0.49–2.51, p = 0.794), smoking index (HR 1.00, 95% CI: 1.00–1.00, p = 0.875; Fig. 2), and ECOG performance status (HR 0.70, 95% CI: 0.63–1.81, p = 0.176) show no significant association with all-cause mortality. This lack of significance is likely attributed to the small sample size and the predominance of patients with ECOG PS 0–1 (accounting for 94.5% of our cohort).

Using forward/backward stepwise regression with the Akaike information criterion, an optimal combination of variables was selected to construct a prognostic model. The final risk variables included non-combined radiotherapy, shorter ICI treatment duration (< 132 days, identified by the surv_cutpoint function), and low-dose glucocorticoids in severe myocarditis. LASSO penalized regression confirmed three variables (radiotherapy, low-dose glucocorticoids in severe myocarditis, ICI duration < 132 days) as robust predictors, justifying their retention in the final Cox model (Fig. S3).

Before constructing the nomogram, we verified the prognostic value of each independent variable via Kaplan-Meier analysis. As shown in Fig. S2, patients with ICI treatment duration ≥ 132 days had significantly longer survival than those with < 132 days (HR: 0.93 [0.91–0.98], log - rank test p = 0.013); severe myocarditis patients treated with low - dose glucocorticoids had a significantly worse prognosis (HR: 3.75 [1.33–10.56], p = 0.012); and patients receiving radiotherapy had significantly better survival outcomes (p = 0.001). These findings from Fig. S2 confirmed the three variables were suitable for inclusion in the nomogram (Fig. 3), allowing prediction of survival probabilities at 0.5, 1 and 1.5 years through a total score calculated for individual patients.

The time-dependent ROC curve indicated the promising accuracy of the prediction model. In the training set, the AUCs of the ROC curves for the prognostic model at 180 days, 360 days, and 540 days were 0.832 (95% CI 0.687, 0.935), 0.835 (0.644, 0.927), and 0.924 (0.700, 0.991), respectively. The validation set confirmed the good predictive performance of the model, with AUC values for the 180 - day, 360 - day, and 540 - day predictions being 0.821 (0.669, 0.972), 0.806 (0.659, 0.954), and 0.789 (0.594, 0.985), respectively (Fig. 4a). Time-dependent AUCs were > 0.78 in both training and validation sets, supporting the model’s discriminative ability. However, wide 95% CIs reflect imprecision due to relatively small sample size, and results should be interpreted with caution. Next, the calibration plots (Fig. 4b) demonstrated that the constructed nomogram model aligns well with the actual follow-up outcomes in both the training set and validation set. Then, DCA was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of the nomogram model (Fig. 4c). As shown in Fig. 4c, DCA results demonstrated that the net benefit curves of the prediction model outperformed the “all-population assumption” (ICI-treatment for all patients) at different time points in both the training and validation sets. Notably, within the clinically relevant decision-making range of risk thresholds (10%-50%), the model exhibited a more pronounced net benefit advantage, indicating its clinical utility and the stability of its predictive performance across independent cohorts.

Risk stratification based on the nomogram

We divided the patients in the training set into three risk groups based on their total nomogram scores. Using the 33rd percentile (125.0 points) and 66th percentile (145.4 points) of the scores as cut - offs, we defined low risk (23 cases), intermediate risk (24 cases), and high risk (23 cases). In both the training set and the validation set, the Kaplan - Meier survival analysis based on this risk stratification showed significant differences in survival probabilities among different risk groups (Log-rank test, p < 0.001 for both), indicating that the risk stratification model has good predictive efficacy and stability. The survival probability of the high - risk group decreased most rapidly over time. The high - risk groups had a median survival of 191 days and 201 days in the training set and validation set, respectively (Fig. 5).

We also explored clinically meaningful score thresholds for our nomogram model using threshold-based risk categories. Risk groups were redefined with clinically actionable thresholds derived from nomogram-predicted probabilities: low risk (≤ 50% 1-year mortality), medium risk (> 50% 1-year mortality but ≤ 50% 2-year mortality), and high risk (> 50% 2-year mortality). The optimal cutoffs (138.7574 and 160.4059 points) were identified using the survminer package and validated via Kaplan-Meier analysis (Fig. S4).

Baseline characteristics associated with the future occurrence of severe ICI-related myocarditis

The patients were classified into a non-severe group (< grade 3 myocarditis) (26 patients, 37.1%) and a severe group (≥ grade 3 myocarditis group) (44 cases, 62.9%). Baseline characteristics were compared between non-severe and severe ICI-related myocarditis groups (Table 2). Univariate Logistic analysis identified that a history of previous coronary artery disease, presence of multiple distant metastases, use of angiogenesis inhibitors, and high hemoglobin were statistically associated with severe ICI-related myocarditis. Univariate and multivariate results were summarized in Fig. S5. As shown in Fig. S5, after adjusting for other potential confounders, only angiogenesis inhibitor use remained an independent risk factor for severe myocarditis (HR = 18.72, 95% CI 2.52–428.27, p = 0.02), highlighting its key role in myocarditis severity.

Table 2 also showed the clinical characteristics of non-severe and severe ICI-related myocarditis. Patients with severe ICI myocarditis had a significantly earlier median time from ICI therapy initiation to myocarditis symptom onset (38.0 [25.0-106.0] days) than those with non-severe ICI myocarditis (135.0 [94.0-346.0] days, p = 0.038). Compared to the non-severe group, the severe group had a higher proportion of patients with worse cardiac function (p = 0.008), as well as significantly elevated hemoglobin, leukocytes, LDH, CKMB, and NT-proBNP levels. For treatment, the severe group’s median initial glucocorticoid dose was higher (55.00 [2.50, 80.00] vs. 100.00 [75.00, 142.50] mg/day, p < 0.001), with 22 severe cases (50%) receiving > 100 mg/day. Prognostically, 90-day MACE rates were higher in the severe group (61.4% vs. 30.8%, p = 0.026).

The risk factors of MACE within 90 days after the diagnosis of myocarditis

To identify baseline differences between patients with and without 90-day MACE, we compared their clinical data and outcomes in Table S2. As shown in Table S2, MACE cases (n = 35) mainly included heart failure (51.4%) and cardiac arrest (45.7%). CAD history was more frequent in the MACE group (34.3% vs. 2.9%, p = 0.002). Patients with MACE had higher mortality (48.6% vs. 22.9%, p = 0.046). To identify independent predictors of 90-day MACE, we conducted univariate and multivariate logistic analysis, with the forest plot of results presented in Fig. S6, which showed CAD history (HR = 10.54, 95%CI 1.62–210.10, p = 0.037) and EF value (HR = 0.94, 95%CI 0.88–0.99, p = 0.026) were independent predictors of 90-day MACE. Key baseline differences included higher troponin (p = 0.013), NT-proBNP (p = 0.026), CRP (p = 0.016), and lower EF (p = 0.003) in the MACE group.

Discussion

Although some cases of ICI-related myocarditis have previously been reported, results have been inconsistent across cohorts18,19. Moreover, most of the current research types in this field are cross-sectional studies from a single center20,21, and few studies are based on the same type of cancer, combining the factors related to the primary tumor with ICI treatment and cardiac conditions, and comprehensively exploring the multi-time factors influencing ICI-related myocarditis from pre-onset, diagnosis, and survival outcomes. This study comprehensively analyzed and identified risk factors, developing and validating a nomogram model for estimating patient survival with high clinical applicability, as these prognostic factors can be easily obtained from a patient’s medical records.

Our results revealed that patients with a longer duration of ICI (≥ 132 days) treatment prior to the diagnosis of ICI-related myocarditis had a significantly longer median OS compared to those with a shorter treatment duration (333.00 [238.00, 602.50] days vs. 217.00 [91.50, 421.00] days, p = 0.020). Previous studies suggested that severe ICI-related myocarditis often occurs after one or two doses of ICIs22,23indicating that earlier-onset myocarditis is prone to fulminant presentation and poorer prognosis. While previous studies used a 12-month cutoff to define earlier-onset and late-onset irAEs, our study identified 132 days after ICI treatment as the optimal cutoff based on observational findings. This time cutoff was close to David Hsiehchen’s report14 and findings in a clinical study on graft-versus-host disease (GVHD)24further supporting the clinical relevance of this threshold.

Notably, the immunologic mechanisms underlying the observed association between ICI treatment duration and myocarditis prognosis remain to be directly verified in our cohort. Drawing on the biological parallels between late-onset irAEs and GVHD, we hypothesize that late-onset ICI-related myocarditis may be associated with a decline in regulatory NK and B cells, whereas both early and late onset of this condition could involve excessive T-cell proliferation and activation25. Prior pathological studies on ICI-related myocarditis indirectly support this hypothesis, as they confirmed that distinct immune infiltration patterns correlate with disease severity and prognosis. Importantly, our study did not perform direct immunophenotyping; thus, the above immunological explanation remains unvalidated here and requires further investigation.

Glucocorticoids are the first-line treatment for ICI-related myocarditis. In our study, we categorized glucocorticoid doses into high-dose and low-dose groups based on the initial dose of 100 mg/day of glucocorticoid equivalent, which aligns with the ASCO Guideline recommendation that high-dose corticosteroids (1–2 mg/kg/d prednisone, often ≥ 100 mg/day in adult patients with average body weight) be used for grade ≥ 2 ICI-related myocarditis15. Our results demonstrated that the low-dose group had a higher risk of MACE (HR: 6, 95%CI: 1.79–22.55, P = 0.005) and all-cause death (HR: 3.92, 95%CI: 1.16–13.2, P = 0.028), suggesting that low glucocorticoid dose in severe myocarditis is a strong predictor of poor outcome, consistent with the findings of Lili Zhang et al.26.

Additionally, combined radiotherapy following ICI treatment emerged as an independent protective factor for the survival of patients with ICI-related myocarditis (HR: 0.12, 95%CI: 0.01–0.98, P = 0.047). Patients who received combined radiotherapy had a longer survival time (411.50 [239.00, 775.25] vs. 227.50 [114.25, 411.50], p = 0.009). Previous research suggests that combined radiotherapy can enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy without increasing immunotoxicity27,28. Furthmore, a recent study has reported that none of the patients who received radiotherapy concurrently with ICI developed any cardiotoxicity, whereas 14.1% of those who did not receive concurrent radiotherapy developed cardiotoxicities29. The potential mechanisms may involve radiotherapy reducing lymphocyte counts, thereby decreasing the infiltration of cytotoxic T cells into the myocardium30. Additionally, radiotherapy may effectively reverse this cardiac toxicity by inducing immune neutralization through the TGF-β pathway31. Based on existing evidence, we hypothesize that ICI combined with radiotherapy may improve OS by enhancing ICI efficacy and reversing myocardial toxicity. However, caution is warranted that the observed association may be influenced by unmeasured confounding factors.

Our study has several limitations that should be considered. First, this is a retrospective study, which inherently introduces selection bias. For example, patients with complete medical records and regular follow-up may differ from those with missing data or loss to follow-up in terms of baseline severity or treatment adherence, potentially affecting the generalizability of our findings.

Second, while the sample size was relatively large for a multi-center study on rare diseases, it remained statistically small (n = 110 with only 40 total all-cause deaths) in terms of statistical analysis requirements, which limited the statistical power of subgroup analyses and competing risk modeling. Despite internal validation via bootstrapping, this small sample size also raised the risk of overfitting in the Cox regression model, which indicates potential model instability; a simpler model or penalized regression (e.g., LASSO) may have improved stability, and larger cohorts are needed in future studies to refine the model’s precision. Similarly, while the time-dependent AUCs demonstrated consistent discriminative ability across the training and validation sets (AUCs > 0.78), their wide 95% CIs should be interpreted with caution, as they might also be induced by the limited sample size.

Third, although the data for the training set and validation set were respectively sourced from multiple centers in non-overlapping time periods, all centers belonged to the same regional health system, and our cohort was predominantly Chinese patients treated with the specific ICI regimens; these characteristics may compromise the external validity of the nomogram, which should be validated in diverse populations before broad clinical application.

Fourth, we lacked a comparator group of ICI-treated lung cancer patients without myocarditis, which limits our ability to clarify whether the identified prognostic factors are specific to ICI-related myocarditis or general indicators of prognosis in ICI-treated populations, and future studies should include such a control group to address this gap.

Finally, the observed protective association of radiotherapy with survival should be interpreted cautiously, because despite multivariate adjustment, residual confounding such as baseline ECOG performance status and tumor burden cannot be ruled out. Patients receiving radiotherapy may have been eligible due to earlier disease stages or better responses to systemic therapy, meaning their improved survival could reflect better lung cancer prognosis rather than a direct association with ICI-related myocarditis outcomes; prospective studies incorporating propensity score matching are needed to minimize this selection bias.

Conclusion

This study focuses on patients with lung cancer and ICI-related myocarditis. Radiotherapy and initial high-dose glucocorticoid treatment were associated with improved prognosis, although this association is observational and cannot establish causality. Additionally, a practical survival prediction model was developed. However, there is still an urgent need for more large-scale prospective clinical trials and animal experiments to implement early interventions and improve prognosis for ICI-related myocarditis.

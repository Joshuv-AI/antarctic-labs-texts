BRIDGE-DS study: a multicenter, observational, retrospective analysis of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin combination on modifiable cardiovascular risk factors in T2DM patients with ASCVD or high cardiovascular risk
Authors: Naveen Bhamri, Ashutosh Kumar, Chandrakant Mishra, Praveen Kumar Devarbhavi, Surendra Prasad, Devanand Samatbhai Rudach, Anil Potdar, Sachin Narendra Patil, Ajoy Kumar Tewari, P.S. Jagdish, Rajib Gayan, Sunil Bansal, Chinnakonda R. Maheshbabu, Amit H. Asalkar, Manoj Chitale, Rohan Narayan Kesarkar, Ashish Prasad, Abhijit Pednekar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) is a major comorbidity among those with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and requires effective management strategies. Few real-world evidence studies have established the safety and efficacy of fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin, specifically in those with T2DM having ASCVD/high cardiovascular risk. The study evaluated the impact of this FDC on glycemic control and the risk of cardiovascular events in those with T2DM and ASCVD or high cardiovascular risk.MethodsThis retrospective, multicenter, observational study was conducted on those with T2DM and ASCVD/high cardiovascular risk across India. Data were collected before and after 3 months of treatment with an FDC of dapagliflozin/sitagliptin (10 mg/100 mg). Primary outcomes included alterations in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), postprandial glucose (PPG), systolic blood pressure (SBP), cholesterol levels, and the Framingham risk score (FRS).ResultsThis study included a total of 1091 people with T2DM. Statistically significant (P < 0.001) reductions in HbA1c (from 8.9 to 7.4%), FPG (from 173.0 to 129.3 mg/dl), PPG (from 257.2 to 185.2 mg/dl), total cholesterol levels (from 234.3 to 192.7 mg/dl), SBP (from 150.5 to 131.5 mmHg), and FRS (from 15.9 to 9.4%) were observed, while high-density lipoprotein levels increased (from 46.0 to 49.4 mg/dl). Common adverse events included urinary tract infections (19.43%), dehydration (19.25%), and hypoglycemia (18.15%).ConclusionThe dapagliflozin/sitagliptin FDC was effective and safe in managing patients with T2DM with cardiovascular risk factors and may offer cardioprotective benefits. Further investigations with longer follow-ups are necessary to confirm these findings.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), which accounts for approximately 90% of all diabetes cases globally, leads to substantial disability and mortality and is increasingly affecting younger populations [1]. According to the Indian Council of Medical Research–India Diabetes study published in 2023, the overall weighted prevalence of diabetes in India is 11.4% [2], with projections by the International Diabetes Federation Diabetes Atlas suggesting that this prevalence could increase to 783 million adults, representing a 12.2% prevalence, by 2045 [3].

Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) is one of the leading causes of death and disability in those with T2DM. In India, the prevalence of ASCVD among patients with diabetes is notably higher than in Western countries [4–6]. Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), such as ischemic heart disease, stroke, coronary artery disease, peripheral artery disease, and heart failure, are frequently seen in those with T2DM and often result from multiple contributing factors [7,8]. These complications are fatal for at least 50% of patients with T2DM [9].

Effective management of CVD risk factors is essential in reducing the incidence of cardiovascular events. This includes lifestyle modifications, weight reduction, and cardioprotective therapeutics, which are crucial for the primary as well as secondary prevention of CVD in those with T2DM [7]. Recommended treatments for those with T2DM and ASCVD include antiplatelet agents, lipid-lowering therapies, and blood pressure-lowering therapies, along with lifestyle modifications. Recently, several antihyperglycemic agents with cardiovascular benefits have been introduced [8].

The American Diabetes Association and the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association guidelines recommend sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT-2is) or glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) for adults with ASCVD risk factors who need additional glucose-lowering treatment despite lifestyle changes and metformin, as well as for those with a high risk of heart failure [10]. Recent evidence has shown that in patients with T2DM and ASCVD, SGLT-2is, along with other oral antidiabetic drugs, can lower the risk of future cardiovascular events [8,11]. Dapagliflozin, an SGLT-2i, prevents glucose reabsorption in the kidney’s proximal tubule. Several SGLT-2is have been shown to lower the risks of heart failure–related hospitalizations in those with T2DM with established CVD and delay the progression of kidney disease. The DECLARE-TIMI 58 trial evaluated the cardiovascular effects of dapagliflozin in those with T2DM who are at risk of ASCVD, demonstrating reduced rates of cardiovascular hospitalization and death [12]. Dapagliflozin is also effective in reducing cardiometabolic risk factors in overweight patients with T2DM.

Sitagliptin, a dipeptidyl-peptidase 4 (DPP-4) inhibitor, is another oral hypoglycemic agent that prevents the degradation of active glucagon-like peptide 1 and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide, enhancing their biological effects [13]. The fixed-dose combination (FDC) of dapagliflozin with sitagliptin can reduce serum glucose levels and BMI in Indian patients with T2DM. A retrospective study in India reported significant reductions in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), postprandial glucose (PPG), and fasting plasma glucose (FPG) levels after 12 weeks of treatment with this combination [14]. However, there are few real-world evidence studies on how safe and effective the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin is in patients with T2DM and ASCVD/cardiovascular risk factors. Although there are numerous cardiovascular trial outcomes for various antihyperglycemic agents, data specifically on the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin in this context are missing. This study assessed the safety and efficacy of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin FDC in those with T2DM who have ASCVD or cardiovascular risk factors. This study is one of the first to assess the efficacy and safety of this FDC in a patient population with ASCVD/CVD risk factors.

Methods

Study design

A retrospective, multicenter, observational study was conducted to collect data from clinicians across various locations in India. Data were collected from people with T2DM and ASCVD/high-risk CVD from different hospitals and diabetes clinics. Data were collected using case report forms (CRFs) before and after 3 months of treatment with an FDC of dapagliflozin (10 mg) with sitagliptin (100 mg).

Patients

The study included adult patients (age ≥ 18 years) with T2DM who had ASCVD and high-risk CVD and received an FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin. High-risk status was determined based on the presence of at least one of the following criteria: hypertension [systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 140 mmHg], dyslipidemia [total cholesterol (TC) ≥ 200 mg/dl or (high-density lipoprotein – HDL) cholesterol < 40 mg/dl], current or past smoking history, or a Framingham risk score (FRS) greater than or equal to 10%. Patients receiving statins, antihypertensive medications, or antiplatelet therapy at baseline were also considered high-risk. Glycemic parameters, including FPG, PPG, and HbA1c levels, were recorded at baseline. Patients below the age of 18 years, those who were pregnant at the time of treatment, those who did not present with ASCVD or high-risk CVD, and those who did not receive an FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin were excluded from the study.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes focused on evaluating the effectiveness and safety of the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin. Efficacy outcomes included monitoring changes in HbA1c, FPG, PPG, fasting lipid changes, SBP, cholesterol levels (total lipoprotein and HDL), and FRS. Secondary outcomes included analyzing treatment patterns, the percentage of patients achieving target FPG and PPG levels, the percentage of patients attaining HbA1c levels below 7%, changes in the FRS, and the duration for which these changes were observed. The percentage of patients experiencing hypoglycemia or other adverse events was also recorded. The FRS was calculated for each patient to estimate their 10-year cardiovascular risk. The FRS calculation involved collecting data on age, gender, HDL cholesterol, TC, SBP, smoking status, treatment for hypertension, and the presence of T2DM. This comprehensive risk assessment tool helped stratify patients based on their cardiovascular risk profiles [15].

Data collection, analysis, and interpretation

Data were collected digitally from endocrinologists and diabetologists using digital CRFs over 7 months, from September 2023 to April 2024. Identifiers were removed before data collation. Statistical analysis was conducted with SPSS version 23.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0, Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize continuous variables, and frequencies/percentages were used to summarize discrete variables. Inferential statistics provided summaries of endpoints, with paired t tests used to obtain P values. For categorical variables with two categories, Fisher’s exact test was applied, and for categorical variables with more than two categories, the χ2 test was utilized. A double-sided/two-tailed P value of less than 0.05 was considered for statistical significance.

Ethical considerations

The requirements of the institutional review boards of the respective participating clinical sites were observed and the principles of the International Conference on Harmonization of Good Clinical Practice and those outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki were followed. The study received approval from the institutional review board at the primary study site. Data were collected anonymously and kept confidential using coding and secure document locking with login details. Because the data were collected retrospectively, informed consent was not required.

Results

Patient demographics

Overall, the study included 1091 people with T2DM. The mean age (±SD) of the patients was 54.04 (±10.95) years (age range: 18–98 years). Male patients made up 71.1% (N = 776) of the total patient population, and 28.8% (N = 314) were female; 0.1% (N = 1) were transgender. Out of the total cohort, 54.6% (n = 596) of the patients reported a history of smoking, while 46.7% (n = 510) reported alcohol consumption. The use of preventive cardiovascular medications was notably high, with 70.4% (n = 768) of the patients being prescribed statins and 73.0% (n = 796) receiving antihypertensive medications. In addition, 51.5% (n = 562) of the patients were on antiplatelet medications. The baseline characteristics and demographics are summarized in Table 1.

Efficacy outcomes

The mean HbA1c levels decreased significantly from 8.9 to 7.4% (P < 0.001) over 3 months (Fig. 1a). The mean FPG levels reduced significantly (from 173.0 to 129.3 mg/dl; P < 0.001), and mean PPG levels also decreased significantly (from 257.2 to 185.2 mg/dl; P < 0.001) (Fig.1b and c). The SBP improved significantly, decreasing from a mean of 150.5 to 131.5 mmHg (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1f). Furthermore, the FRS showed a significant reduction from 15.9 to 9.4% (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1g). Discussion

T2DM is an increasing health problem worldwide, particularly in India. Early diagnosis and appropriate management of T2DM can lead to fewer cardiovascular events and longer CVD-free life years. Hence, effective management of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors is crucial in reducing cardiovascular complications associated with T2DM.

A study evaluating 5080 patients newly diagnosed with T2DM in India found that 2007 (39.5%) individuals were at high risk and 3073 (60.5%) were at very high risk of developing ASCVD as per the Lipid Association of India criteria [4]. For those with T2DM and high ASCVD risk/established ASCVD, a comprehensive treatment approach is essential. GLP-1RAs and SGLT-2is are recommended as first-line therapies because of their significant cardiovascular benefits, which extend beyond their glucose-lowering properties [16]. The ‘Bringing patients with type 2 diabetes in range with dapagliflozin and sitagliptin combination’ (BRIDGE-DS) study highlighted the potential benefits of the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin on glycemic control and key cardiovascular risk factors rather than direct clinical outcomes. This distinction is essential, as the study primarily assessed improvements in modifiable risk parameters such as HbA1c, FPG, PPG, lipid profile, SBP, and the FRS. This pioneering study provided real-world evidence of the safety and efficacy of this FDC therapy, specifically focusing on patients with ASCVD, thus filling a critical gap in the existing literature.

The significant reduction in HbA1c from 8.9 to 7.4% after 3 months of treatment with the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin, as well as the reductions in FPG and PPG, highlights the glycemic control achieved by this regimen. This decrease is clinically meaningful and aligns with the American Diabetes Association’s recommendation for adults with T2DM to maintain HbA1c levels of less than 7% to reduce the risk of microvascular complications [17]. A meta-analysis of five cardiovascular outcome trials showed that, compared with the placebo, the use of SGLT-2is led to significant reductions (14%) in the risk of three-point major adverse cardiovascular events (3P-MACE) in patients with ASCVD. Across studies, patients with a history of ASCVD ranged from 40.5 to 99.4%. SGLT-2i use significantly reduced the risk of hospitalization for heart failure by 30% (95% confidence interval: 0.61–0.79; P < 0.001) [18].

New antihyperglycemic medications like SGLT-2is and GLP-1RAs reduce cardiovascular events in those with T2DM. A meta-analysis showed a 14% reduction in 3P-MACE (relative risk: 0.86) for SGLT-2i in the population with ASCVD [19]. A systematic review revealed that SGLT-2is significantly reduces the risk of hospitalization for heart failure, MACEs, and cardiovascular death. Empagliflozin, specifically, reduced the risk of all these events, regardless of the number of cardiovascular risk factors [20].

Beyond glycemic control, the study demonstrated notable improvements in cardiovascular risk factors. The substantial decrease in TC levels and the increase in HDL levels are particularly noteworthy. These changes contribute to a more favorable lipid profile, which is critical in managing patients with T2DM who are at elevated cardiovascular risk. The significant reduction in SBP from 150.5 to 131.5 mmHg is another crucial finding, given the strong association between hypertension and cardiovascular events in patients with T2DM. The FRS is a tool used to estimate the risk of a person developing coronary heart disease over the next 10 years. The FRS considers six coronary risk factors—age, gender, TC, HDL, smoking habits, and SBP. It is useful for predicting long-term CVD risk and helps in making decisions about lifestyle modifications and preventive medical treatments. As this risk score provides insights into the benefits of prevention, it would be useful in deciding whether lifestyle modification and preventive medical treatment would be an appropriate management strategy. The score could also be useful for patient education and may help in identifying people at increased risk of future cardiovascular events [21]. The decrease of 58.6% in the FRS in this study suggests the impact of this FDC on long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

The safety profile of the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin was generally acceptable, although certain AEs were observed. The most common AEs included dehydration (15.8%), urinary tract infections (14.2%), and hypoglycemia (13.2%). These AEs were consistent with the known side effects of SGLT-2is and DPP-4 inhibitors, which can increase the risk of dehydration and urinary tract infections, respectively. The incidence of hypoglycemia, although notable, was relatively low, suggesting that this combination can be safely managed with appropriate patient education and monitoring. SGLT-2is are associated with an increased risk of genital infections, which is consistent with this study [18,20]. Dapagliflozin is generally safe for patients with T2DM. In the DECLARE-TIMI 58 study, it was associated with fewer cases of acute kidney injury and major hypoglycemia events compared with placebo. While diabetic ketoacidosis was more frequent with dapagliflozin, serious genital infections, and urinary tract issues were similar between groups. Overall, dapagliflozin demonstrated a robust safety profile over a median follow-up time period of 4.2 years [22]. Other less common AEs, such as hypotension, vulvovaginitis, balanitis, hypovolemia, and decreases in renal creatinine clearance, were observed but did not significantly detract from the overall positive risk–benefit profile of this FDC. These results underscore the requirement for regular monitoring along with patient management strategies to mitigate potential side effects.

Limitations and future directions

Despite the promising findings, this study has certain limitations that should be acknowledged. As a retrospective, observational study, there is a risk of selection bias and confounding factors that may influence the outcomes. In addition, the long-term efficacy and safety of the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin are limited by the short-duration follow-up of 3 months of this study. While the study included a diverse patient population from various locations across India, the generalizability of the results to other populations and healthcare settings remains uncertain. Future studies having longer periods of follow-up and randomized-controlled designs are needed to confirm these results and provide more robust evidence. In addition, conducting further research in different demographic and ethnic groups would help ensure these findings are relevant and applicable worldwide.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin effectively lowers HbA1c, FPG, and PPG, addressing multiple modifiable cardiovascular risk factors in patients with T2DM and ASCVD/cardiovascular risk factors. In addition, cardiovascular risk factors such as TC, SBP, and FRS also showed improvement. This real-world evidence establishes the efficacy of the FDC of dapagliflozin and sitagliptin in patients with T2DM at high cardiovascular risk. Furthermore, the findings highlight the potential benefits of early initiation of this therapy to prevent complications and achieve long-term cardiovascular benefits.

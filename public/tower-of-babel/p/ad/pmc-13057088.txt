Modulation of breast cancer progression in high-altitude hypoxia by Tongqiaohuoxue Decoction via HIF-1α/VEGF signaling
Authors: Wenke Yang, Qiang Wang, Quanyu Yang, Yan Peng, Yi Gao, Huaxiucairang Yang, Wenguang Lan, Ting Zhang, Shoude Zhang, Zhanhai Su, Xiaohui Zhao, Xueman Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore the therapeutic efficacy and underlying mechanisms of Tongqiao Huoxue Decoction (THD) in the context of breast cancer development under high-altitude hypoxic conditions.MethodsWe established an in vivo breast cancer hypoxia model and utilized a small animal in vivo imaging system for oncological investigation. Routine blood tests and coagulation profile tests were conducted to assess platelet count and coagulation function. Pathological changes in tumor tissues were observed. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was employed to detect positive rates of hypoxia inducible factor (HIF)-1α, P-selectin (CD62P), platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and basic fibroblast growth factor (BFGF) in tumor tissues. Western blotting was used to evaluate protein expression levels of HIF-1α, VEGF, cellular myelocytomatosis oncogene (c-MYC), and platelet-derived growth factor receptor β (PDGFR-β).ResultsUnder hypoxic conditions, orthotopic tumor volume and weight, and the number and area of lung metastases significantly increased. Platelet count increased, and prothrombin time was prolonged. IHC and immunoblot analyses revealed that HIF-1α, VEGF, and PDGFR-β expression were upregulated under hypoxia. Following THD treatment, the volume and weight of orthotopic tumors decreased, and so did the number and area percentage of lung metastases. Platelet and D-dimer counts decreased, while activated partial thromboplastin time and thrombin time were prolonged. Protein expression levels of HIF-1α, VEGF, c-MYC, and PDGFR-β were downregulated.ConclusionBased on the current data, the ameliorative effects of THD on tumor hypoxia and hypercoagulability under high-altitude conditions may be associated with the modulation of the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway, providing clues to its potential mechanisms for suppressing tumor growth, metastasis, and angiogenesis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04631-y.

Introduction

Globally, breast cancer persists as one of the most prevalent and formidable malignant tumors [1–3]. Despite advancements in treatment modalities, the complication and mortality rates remain alarmingly high [4]. Moreover, the unique hypoxic geographical environment of high-altitude regions exerts a significant influence on the initiation and progression of breast cancer. Sustained exposure to high-altitude hypoxic conditions causes a decrease in blood oxygen saturation, which impacts oxygen delivery and metabolic functions. Such environmental pressure has the potential to heighten the invasiveness of breast cancer cells and augment their drug resistance [5]. Additionally, hypoxia, as a hallmark feature of the tumor microenvironment in most solid tumors, can promote tumor growth, metastasis, and treatment resistance by regulating hypoxia-related gene expression, ultimately resulting in a more aggressive and lethal cancer phenotype [6–9]. The hypoxic milieu at high altitudes intensifies the hypoxic state within the tumor microenvironment, leading to a notable upregulation of hypoxia inducible factor (HIF)-1α expression [10]. In breast cancer, the suppression of the HIF-1α gene and its transcriptional activity prevents tumor cells from reducing the secretion of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), thereby inhibiting tumor neovascularization [11]. As a key downstream target of the HIF pathway, VEGF promotes angiogenesis by regulating endothelial cell migration, proliferation, permeability, and viability [12–15]. The HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway plays a vital role in platelet function regulation [16]. HIF-1α can influence platelet activation status and aggregation ability by regulating the paracrine effects of VEGF, thus participating in the pathological process of tumor-associated thrombosis [17]. At the level of regulating cell proliferation and migration, in addition to directly controlling VEGF expression, HIF-1α can modulate tumor cell proliferation kinetics and invasive/migratory abilities via VEGF-dependent or -independent mechanisms. This occurs by influencing the phosphorylation level and downstream signaling of platelet-derived growth factor receptor β (PDGFR-β) [18]. Cellular myelocytomatosis oncogene (c-MYC), an important transcription factor, is involved in cell proliferation and metabolic regulation [19]. A complex interaction exists between HIF-1α and c-MYC; they jointly regulate cellular metabolism and proliferation processes [20]. Under hypoxic conditions, HIF-1α can synergize with c-MYC to promote tumor cell growth and angiogenesis [21, 22]. These studies suggest that HIF-1α, VEGF, c-MYC, and PDGFR-β form a complex network in the initiation and development of breast cancer and are potential therapeutic targets in breast cancer. By delving deeper into the relationships between these signaling pathways and proteins, new strategies and directions for breast cancer treatment can be provided.

Currently, breast cancer treatment options include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy; however, these approaches often face limitations such as drug resistance and side effects [23–27]. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has attracted significant interest in antitumor metastasis research [28, 29]. In TCM theory, tumor occurrence is closely linked to “blood stasis, phlegm coagulation, and heat toxicity,” with blood stasis being an important pathological basis [30, 31]. Derived from Yilin Gaicuo by Wang Qingren in the Qing Dynasty, Tongqiaohuoxue Decoction (THD) is composed of peach kernel, safflower, red peony root, Rhizoma chuanxiong, ginger, jujube, old onion, and musk; THD has been shown to break blood stasis and dredge meridians [32]. Targeting the pathogenesis of “stagnation and obstruction” in tumors can improve local microcirculation and reduce stagnation in the tumor. THD improves blood circulation and blood flow under hypoxic conditions [33]. As a therapeutic approach, THD exhibits promising application prospects in tumor treatment. However, the specific mechanisms require further exploration through more clinical trials and basic research.

In this study, we established a hypoxic breast cancer tumor-bearing mouse model to simulate a high-altitude environment. We aimed to investigate whether THD could improve tumor growth, metastasis, angiogenesis, and hypercoagulability under hypoxic conditions, and to explore if its potential effects involve the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway. The findings provide experimental evidence supporting the further investigation of THD as an antitumor agent.

Methods

Establishment of an orthotopic breast cancer mouse model under hypoxia

After isoflurane inhalation anesthesia, an orthotopic breast cancer model was established by subcutaneously injecting 40 µL of 4T1-luc2 cell suspension (cell concentration: 1.25 × 10⁷ cells/mL) into the fourth mammary pad of the mice. Successful establishment of the model was confirmed by the formation of a skin blister without leakage. The normoxic model group was housed under normoxic conditions (temperature: 22 ± 3 °C; relative humidity: 45–55%) for 3 weeks. The hypoxic model group was placed in a hypobaric chamber (Guizhou Fenglei Oxygen Chamber, DYC-300, Guizhou, China) simulating a high-altitude environment for 3 consecutive weeks to establish an orthotopic breast cancer mouse model under hypoxia (temperature: 22 ± 3 °C; relative humidity: 45–55%; simulated altitude: 5,000 m; relative oxygen content: 10.6%).

Drug preparation and experimental grouping

According to the dosage recorded in Corrections on the Errors of Medical Works, THD consists of: Red Peony Root 3 g, Szechuan Lovage Rhizome 3 g, Peach Seed 9 g, Safflower 9 g, Old Green Onion 6 g, Fresh Ginger 9 g, 7 pieces of Jujube (approx. 21 g), Artificial Musk (complying with national drug standards) 0.15 g, and 250 mL of Yellow Rice Wine. The blood-activating group includes Red Peony Root 3 g, Szechuan Lovage Rhizome 3 g, Peach Seed 9 g, and Safflower 9 g. All herbal pieces were provided by the pharmacy of Qinghai Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine and authenticated by Associate Professor Zhang Shoude of the School of Pharmacy, Qinghai University. Except for the artificial musk, the remaining herbs were soaked in room-temperature water for 24 h (using 8 times the amount of cold water). For the first decoction, herbs, 250 mL of yellow rice wine, and 250 mL of purified water were boiled in a beaker and then simmered gently for 40 min. For the second decoction, the herb residue was added to 300 mL of purified water, boiled, and simmered gently for 30 min. The two decoctions were vacuum-filtered using a Buchner funnel and qualitative filter paper, and the filtrates were combined. The combined filtrate was poured into a rotary evaporation flask, concentrated under vacuum at 50 °C in a water bath to a density of 1.64 g/mL, and the artificial musk was added to the concentrate immediately before use. Twenty-five orthotopic breast cancer model mice were randomly divided into five groups (n = 5 each): control, THD, musk, Huoxue, and aspirin groups. Drugs were prepared according to the “Equivalent Dose Ratio Table for Body Surface Area Conversion between Humans and Animals [34].” THD (1640 mg/mL), Huoxue (728 mg/mL), musk (2.275 mg/mL), and aspirin (4 mg/mL) were administered via gavage at 0.1 mL/10 g daily. In vivo small animal imaging

For in vivo tumor bioluminescence analysis, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane on days 4, 7, 10, 13, 17, and 21, then intraperitoneally injected with 200 µL luciferin (100 mg/kg). Imaging was performed using the IVIS Imaging System (Caliper Life Sciences) to record bioluminescent signals, which were quantified using Living Image Software (Perkin Elmer).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Tissues were prefixed with 3% glutaraldehyde, postfixed in 1% osmium tetroxide, dehydrated in serial acetone, infiltrated in Epox 812, and embedded. Semithin sections were stained with methylene blue; ultrathin sections were cut with a diamond knife, stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate, and observed using a JEM-1400-FLASH transmission electron microscope.

H&E staining

To assess distal metastasis, a hallmark of malignant progression in the 4T1 orthotopic model, lung tissues were collected and subjected to H&E staining. The lungs were placed in formalin fixative solution for fixation, followed by sequential steps of dehydration, clarification, paraffin embedding of the entire lung, dewaxing, clarification, staining, dehydration, mounting, and observation. The results were analyzed using Case Viewer software to calculate the average cross-sectional area and the number of lung metastatic foci.

Blood parameter detection

Following the last administration, the mice were fasted for 24 h with free access to water. Following anesthesia, venous blood was collected via retro-orbital bleeding for blood parameter analysis. Whole blood cell analysis was performed on venous blood from ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA)-anticoagulated tubes using a BC-2300 automated hematology analyzer. Coagulation function tests for five parameters were conducted on venous blood from ACD-anticoagulated tubes using a coagulation analyzer.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

After separation, orthotopic tumor tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, then dehydrated, clarified, paraffin-embedded, sectioned, dewaxed, and rehydrated. Antigen retrieval was conducted using citrate buffer. Following serum blocking, primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4 °C, followed by HRP-labeled secondary antibodies for 50 min at room temperature. DAB staining, counterstaining, dehydration, and mounting were performed. Five 400× fields per slide were randomly selected, with 100 cells counted per field to calculate the average positive cell percentage.

Western blotting

Breast cancer tumor tissues (100 mg) were collected and lysed with high-efficiency tissue lysis buffer and 1% protease inhibitor, then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to collect the supernatant. Protein concentration was measured using a BCA kit. Protein separation was performed using 10% separating gel and 5% stacking gel. After electrophoresis, samples were transferred to PVDF (0.45 μm) membranes, blocked with protein-free rapid blocking solution for 2 h at room temperature, and incubated with primary antibodies (overnight at 4 °C, with β-actin as a loading control). Goat anti-rabbit/mouse secondary antibodies were applied for 1 h at room temperature, followed by chemiluminescence development with ultra-sensitive solution. To ensure accuracy and prevent potential cross-reactivity, each target protein (HIF-1α, VEGF, c-MYC, PDGFR-β) and the internal reference protein (β-actin) were detected on individual membranes. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, USA), and the expression level of each target protein was normalized to that of the corresponding β-actin.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.0. Continuous data were first assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test and for homogeneity of variances using the F-test. Data that satisfied both the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variances were analyzed using an unpaired t-test (for comparisons between two groups) or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test (for comparisons among multiple groups). These data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For data that violated the assumptions for parametric testing, the Mann-Whitney U test (for two groups) or the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post hoc test (for multiple groups) was used. These data are presented as median with interquartile range. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Hypoxia promotes orthotopic tumor growth and metastasis in breast cancer model mice

In the normoxic model group, the orthotopic tumors were slightly smaller and exhibited a rounded shape. In the hypoxic model group, the orthotopic tumors were significantly larger and had irregular shapes, with central areas of hemorrhage and necrosis forming scabs (Fig. 2A). At all time points, tumor volumes in the hypoxic model group were significantly larger than those in the normoxic model group. Specifically, from days 12 to 21, the tumor growth rate in the hypoxic model group significantly accelerated (Fig. 2B). On day 21, the tumor photon counts and orthotopic tumor weights in the hypoxic model group were significantly higher than those in the normoxic model group (Fig. 2C-F). H&E staining results revealed an increase in the number and area percentage of lung metastases in the mouse hypoxia model (Fig. 2G-I). Compared to the normoxic model group, the spleen weight in the hypoxic model group significantly increased, while the lung weight showed an increase, although not significant (Fig. 2J, K).

Hypoxia promotes angiogenesis and a hypercoagulable state in the tumor tissues of breast cancer model mice

In TCM, the modern medical concept of hypercoagulable state is classified as blood stasis syndrome, which accelerates breast cancer growth and metastasis in mice by promoting hypoxia and an immunosuppressive microenvironment [34, 35]. However, the effects and mechanisms of high-altitude hypoxia on the hypercoagulable state in breast cancer have not been studied. This study constructed a hypoxic breast cancer mouse model and used TEM, blood, and coagulation profile tests to investigate the effects of high-altitude hypoxia on mouse blood status. Under TEM, the normoxic model group tumor cells exhibited mild cytoplasmic content loss, with most mitochondria showing swelling (dissolution of cristae) and most rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) showing mild dilation and a small amount of autophagy. In the hypoxic model group, most tumor cells underwent necrosis, with severe cytolysis and tissue vacuolation. Necrotic cells showed nuclear condensation, expanded perinuclear spaces, and a small amount of autophagy. Most mitochondria were swollen, and the RER appeared cystic and dilated. Vascular endothelial cells showed nuclear condensation, cystic dilation of the RER cytoplasm, and a small amount of autophagy (Fig. 3A). Taken together, these adaptive reactions to hypoxia are implicated in enhancing HIF-1α stability, VEGF release, and procoagulant activity. This cascade of events is hypothesized to collectively contribute to the development of pathological angiogenesis and a hypercoagulable phenotype, observations consistent with the increased platelet counts and prolonged D-dimer levels noted in our study.

Blood results indicated that, compared to the normoxic model group, platelet count (Fig. 3B) and D-dimer levels (Fig. 3C) increased in all other groups, while activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) (Fig. 3D) and thrombin time (TT) (Fig. 3E) shortened. Among these, the changes were most significant in the hypoxic model group. Compared to the normal hypoxia group, the differences in platelet count and D-dimer levels in the hypoxic model group were significantly larger, TT levels were shorter, while the differences in APTT were not significant.

Hypoxia upregulates the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway as well as the expression of c-Myc and PDGFR-β proteins, which are associated with enhanced tumor growth and metastasis

IHC showed significantly higher cellular positivity rates of HIF-1α, PDGFR, VEGF, and BFGF in orthotopic tumor tissues of the hypoxic model group than in the normoxic model group (Fig. 4A-F). Western blotting analysis demonstrated that the protein levels of HIF-1α, VEGF, c-MYC, and PDGFR-β were significantly elevated in orthotopic tumor tissues of breast cancer-bearing mice under hypoxic conditions (Fig. 4G-K).

THD inhibits tumor growth and metastasis in hypoxic breast cancer mice

The orthotopic tumor volume grew more slowly in the THD group, with reduced blood stasis symptoms in mice and a smaller area of necrosis in the tumor center (Fig. 5A, B). THD contains musk as its monarch drug, renowned for its orifice-opening, collateral-dredging, and meridian-unblocking effects. The huoxue group, composed of peach kernel, safflower, red peony, and chuanxiong, was designed to evaluate the additional therapeutic benefit of combining orifice-opening and huoxue strategies. Aspirin, a standard antiplatelet treatment for hypercoagulable states, was used as a positive control to compare the efficacy of THD with evidence-based Western medicine. This experiment aimed to verify whether the full formula outperforms its key individual components. Compared with other drugs, THD significantly reduced the photon count and weight of orthotopic tumors (Fig. 5C-F).

H&E staining revealed multiple nodular metastatic foci in the lungs of model group mice, primarily around blood vessels, with invasive growth that disrupted normal alveolar architecture. These foci consisted of highly atypical epithelial cells exhibiting hyperchromatic nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and pathological mitotic figures, organized in solid nests or irregular gland-like patterns. Larger metastases showed central coagulative necrosis, accompanied by mild to moderate lymphocytic infiltration and desmoplastic reaction. The THD treatment group exhibited significantly reduced metastatic number, size, and invasiveness. Additionally, lung metastasis inhibition was not evident in the huoxue, musk, or aspirin group (Fig. 5H-J). Under hypoxic conditions, THD reduced lung weight and increased spleen weight in breast cancer mice (Fig. 5K, L). Our results indicate that the complete formulation exerts superior anti-tumor efficacy to the key ingredient groups.

THD inhibits tumor angiogenesis and hypercoagulable state in hypoxic breast cancer mice

Angiogenesis is a recognized hallmark of cancer progression and metastasis, and inhibiting angiogenesis serves as an ideal strategy for treating breast cancer [36]. TCM formulations can exert anti-angiogenic effects against tumors through multi-component and multi-target synergy.

Using TEM, we observed that in the control group, most tumor cells underwent necrosis with severe cytolysis and tissue vacuolation. Necrotic cells showed nuclear condensation, expanded perinuclear spaces, and swollen mitochondria. In the THD group, most mitochondria in tumor cells and vascular endothelial cells were swollen, with mild dilation of the RER and a small amount of autophagy. In the aspirin group, most tumor cells were necrotic with fragmented nuclei, and vascular endothelial cells were necrotic (Fig. 5G). Platelet and coagulation function tests revealed that THD decreased platelet count, shortened D-dimer time, and prolonged APTT and TT times, alleviating tumor-induced hypercoagulable states (Fig. 5M, P).

THD downregulates the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway and c-MYC and PDGFR-β protein expression

The IHC results showed that THD effectively reduced the cellular positivity rates of HIF-1α, VEGF, BFGF and PDGFR in orthotopic tumors of breast cancer mice (Fig. 6A-F). Western blotting analysis further confirmed that, under hypoxic conditions, THD effectively reduced the protein levels of HIF-1α, VEGF, c-Myc, and PDGFR-β in orthotopic tumor tissues of breast cancer mice (Fig. 6G-K).

Discussion

Breast cancer remains one of the most common malignant tumors in women worldwide, with its development closely linked to various signaling pathways and proteins [37]. The regulation of the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway by hypoxic conditions in high-altitude environments plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis and progression of breast cancer [38, 39]. Under hypoxia, HIF-1α activation promotes VEGF expression, enhances tumor angiogenesis, and facilitates tumor growth and metastasis [40]. Research has revealed an interaction between HIF-1α and PDGFR-β, wherein increased HIF-1α activity can promote brain metastasis of breast cancer via the PDGF/PDGFR axis [41]. C-MYC, a key protein in breast cancer, regulates cell proliferation and metabolism while being linked to tumor aggressiveness and poor prognosis [42–44]. Furthermore, hypoxia is a notable characteristic of the tumor microenvironment, capable of activating the platelet surface protein CD62P, which binds to plasma proteins and tissue factors, inducing the activation of the coagulation cascade and promoting a hypercoagulable state, thereby stimulating tumor growth and metastasis [45, 46].

Our study simulated high-altitude hypoxic conditions and established an orthotopic breast cancer tumor-bearing mouse model. The results demonstrated that hypoxic environments promoted the growth of orthotopic tumors and lung metastasis, and that hypoxia exacerbated ischemia and necrosis in tumor tissues. We also observed that hypoxia increased platelet count and blood hypercoagulability in tumor-bearing mice. Additionally, hypoxia elevated the expression levels of the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway, as well as c-MYC and PDGFR-β proteins. Our results indicate that the anti-tumor effects of TQHXD are associated with the inhibition of hypoxia-inducible factor activity and downstream VEGF signaling.

TCM offers multiple advantages in treating breast cancer, as it can enhance immunity via multi-target, multi-pathway mechanisms to indirectly inhibit tumor growth and metastasis [47, 48]. Furthermore, it demonstrates notable advantages in alleviating adverse reactions induced by chemotherapy and radiotherapy [49, 50]. Numerous TCM herbs can mitigate gastrointestinal reactions, myelosuppression, cardiotoxicity, and neurotoxicity associated with chemotherapy and radiotherapy, improving patients’ quality of life [51]. In TCM therapeutics, the activation of blood circulation and detoxification is a significant clinical experience in tumor prevention and treatment. “Blood stasis” and “toxicity” are important factors contributing to tumor development. THD, a representative formula of the activating blood circulation and detoxifying method, exerts remarkable pharmacological effects in tumor treatment. To investigate the mechanism of action of THD in regulating the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway to intervene in breast cancer under high-altitude hypoxic conditions, we employed THD and its decomposed formula as intervention conditions. Observations of the general growth status of breast cancer tumor-bearing mouse models revealed that THD could alleviate the hypoxic state in tumor-bearing mice and reduce tumor tissue volume and weight. H&E staining and TEM results indicated that THD could inhibit angiogenesis within tumor tissues, decrease lung metastasis, and ameliorate ischemic conditions within the tumor. Additionally, THD effectively inhibited platelet activation and aggregation, thereby reducing the hypercoagulable state of the blood. The results demonstrated that THD decreased the expression of HIF-1α, VEGF, c-MYC, and PDGFR-β proteins; the reduced expression of these proteins is associated with the inhibition of tumor growth and metastasis. Musk, a component of THD, can prevent tumor neovascularization by inhibiting the proliferation and migration of vascular endothelial cells, thereby limiting tumor nutrient supply and growth. The main active components of Rhizoma chuanxiong include tetramethylpyrazine, ferulic acid, and volatile oils, which exhibit significant pharmacologically active antitumor activities [52–54]. Carthamin, one of the main active components in safflower, also demonstrates promising antitumor activity [55]. Carthamin can reduce the risk of tumor metastasis by inhibiting the invasive and migratory abilities of tumor cells [56]. Amygdalin, one of the main active components in peach kernel, can hinder the invasive and metastatic abilities of tumors by affecting the signal transduction pathways of tumor cells [57]. Furthermore, as TCM materials, jujube, ginger, and old onion exhibit multifaceted pharmacological effects in tumor treatment. Jujube polysaccharides can exert anticancer effects by enhancing immune function, inhibiting tumor cell proliferation, and inducing apoptosis [58]. The main active components of ginger are gingerol and curcumin, which have demonstrated the ability to inhibit tumor growth in various cancer models [59, 60]. Gingerol can inhibit the proliferation and invasion of cancer cells while inducing apoptosis by regulating multiple signaling pathways [61]. The active components in old onion mainly include sulfides and polyphenolic compounds. Sulfides can exert anticancer effects by inhibiting the activation of carcinogens and promoting their detoxification, while polyphenolic compounds help the body resist cancer invasion through antioxidant and immunomodulatory effects [62, 63].

Under hypoxic conditions, the expression levels of both HIF-1α and HIF-2α are significantly upregulated in breast cancer cells. These two factors collaboratively mediate the adaptive response of tumors to the hypoxic microenvironment by co-regulating a series of gene programs associated with angiogenesis, glycolytic metabolism, pH homeostasis, and cell apoptosis. Notably, the overexpression of HIF-2α can activate the Wnt and Notch signaling pathways, which in turn induce the malignant phenotype of breast cancer stem cells (BCSCs) and enhance their tumorigenic potential and drug resistance [64, 65]. In the context of simulated high-altitude hypoxia, the role of HIF-2α may extend beyond its synergy with HIF-1α; it is more likely to promote tumor invasion and metastasis through the independent regulation of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process.

Supplementary detection of HIF-2α and EMT markers will be crucial for clarifying the regulatory mechanism of HIF-2α on EMT in hypoxic breast cancer. Owing to limitations in the current study cycle and experimental conditions, the relevant experiments were not completed. Other limitations of this study include the lack of direct detection of hypoxia markers in normal mammary tissues. Nevertheless, multiple lines of indirect evidence support the validity of the high-altitude hypoxia model employed. Furthermore, the anti-tumor effects of THD are likely mediated through the coordinated modulation of both externally induced high-altitude hypoxia and intrinsic tumor hypoxia.

In future studies, we intend to investigate HIF-2α’s specific role in EMT under high-altitude hypoxia, clarify its synergistic mechanisms with HIF-1α, and explore THD’s therapeutic potential via targeting both exogenous and intrinsic hypoxia pathways.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that THD inhibits breast tumor growth, metastasis, and angiogenesis by targeting the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling axis, alleviating intratumoral hypoxia and systemic hypercoagulability. As a multi-target intervention strategy, THD exhibits dual therapeutic potential in simultaneously exerting antitumor and anticoagulation effects. These findings suggest that THD expands treatment options and improves survival outcomes and quality of life in breast cancer patients.

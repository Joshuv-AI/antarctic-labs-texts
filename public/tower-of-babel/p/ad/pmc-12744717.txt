Endoscopic management of malignant intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm in an elderly patient
Authors: Kun Liu, Lei Wang, Shanshan Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimsMalignant transformation of pancreatic main duct intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs) in elderly patients with comorbidities presents significant therapeutic dilemmas. We aimed to assess sequential endoscopic multimodal therapies for palliative control in a nonsurgical IPMN-derived adenocarcinoma patient.MethodsA 92-year-old man with obstructive jaundice and endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided fine-needle aspiration (EUS-FNA)–confirmed adenocarcinoma underwent staged interventions. Initial management included pancreatoscopy-guided radiofrequency ablation (RFA) of the main pancreatic duct lesion with dual pancreatic/biliary plastic stent placement. At 5-month follow-up, tumor progression prompted repeat RFA and cryoablation. Subsequent failed ERCP cannulation due to duodenal tumor invasion necessitated salvage EUS-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD) and pancreatic duct rendezvous (EUS-PD) with covered metal stent deployment. Iterative stent revisions and EUS-guided cryoablation were performed for recurrent obstructions.ResultsThe patient survived 18 months post initial intervention, maintaining oral intake and ambulation until terminal hospitalization. Technical success was achieved in all procedures despite challenges including stent migration, tumor progression requiring ablation escalation, and anatomic distortion mandating EUS-guided salvage. Hepatic metastasis progression preceded palliative stent replacement and patient demise.ConclusionsMultimodal endoscopic therapy combining RFA, cryoablation, and advanced EUS-guided drainage techniques can provide sustained symptom control and local tumor management in elderly patients with unresectable malignant IPMNs.

Case report

A 92-year-old man with recurrent upper abdominal pain and obstructive jaundice was admitted with suspected malignant transformation of a known pancreatic main duct intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN), previously managed with bile duct stenting.

Histopathologic confirmation of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma was obtained via endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided fine-needle aspiration (EUS-FNA). The patient explicitly declined surgical resection and systemic chemotherapy owing to advanced age and comorbidities, necessitating a minimally invasive palliative strategy.

During the initial procedure, pancreatoscopy revealed an irregular mass in the main pancreatic duct (Fig. 1A; Video 1, available online at www.videogie.org). Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) was performed (effect 2, power 10 W, duration 90 seconds) by a 7F RFA catheter (Fig. 1B and C), followed by 10F pancreatic duct plastic stent placement. A biliary stent was also placed to relieve obstructive jaundice (Fig. 1D).

A, Preablation pancreatoscopic view showing lesion in the main pancreatic duct. B, Intraprocedural radiofrequency ablation application demonstrating thermal whitening and bubble formation at the ablation site. C, Postablation pancreatoscopic examination revealing diffusely pale mucosa with coagulative necrosis. D, Fluoroscopic confirmation of post–radiofrequency ablation pancreatic and biliary plastic stent placement were placed via ERCP.

Five months later, recurrent cholangitis prompted MRCP demonstrating stent migration and stricture progression. Repeat RFA and pancreatic stent placement were performed. To further treat the residual tumor, a 14-gauge HybridTherm (Erbe Cryoablation Needle; Erbe Elektromedizin GmbH, Tübingen, Germany) was used to puncture the lesion and perform cryoablation for 3 minutes (effect setting 5, power 18 W) (Fig. 2A and B).

A, Duodenoscopy during cryoablation revealed localized edema adjacent to the major papilla. B, EUS-guided cryoablation using a 14-gauge HybridTherm (Erbe Cryoablation Needle; Erbe Elektromedizin GmbH, Tübingen, Germany) demonstrates real-time ice-ball formation at the pancreatic head ablation site.

Because of failed retrograde cannulation (Fig. 3A), EUS-guided biliary drainage (EUS-BD) was performed (Fig. 3B), with successful biliary stent deployment (Fig. 3C and D).

A, Post–radiofrequency ablation pancreatic duct stenting with failed biliary cannulation due to tumor infiltration obscuring the papillary orifice. B, EUS-guided access to the dilated common bile duct using a 19-gauge needle via the duodenal bulb. C, Fluoroscopic-guided deployment of a 7F, 4-cm double-pigtail plastic stent establishing choledochoduodenal drainage. D, Fluoroscopic confirmation demonstrates appropriate positioning of the plastic stents.

Six months postprocedure, the patient underwent repeat EUS-guided cryoablation. Given the inability to perform conventional ERCP cannulation because of tumor progression at the duodenal mucosal surface, an EUS-guided pancreatic duct rendezvous (EUS-PD) procedure was conducted (Fig. 4A and B), and a 10 × 80-mm covered metal stent was placed in the pancreatic duct (Fig. 4C and D). The biliary stent was replaced via the EUS-BD tract.

A, EUS-guided puncture of the dilated main pancreatic duct using a 19-gauge needle. B, Fluoroscopic confirmation of guidewire advancement through the main pancreatic duct exiting at the duodenal papilla. C, The pancreatic duct metal stent is in position under white-light endoscopic visualization. D, ERCP-assisted stent deployment over the rendezvous guidewire, demonstrating transpapillary positioning of the covered metal stent.

The patient was readmitted 6 months later for stent obstruction and hepatic metastasis progression. After replacement of the biliary and pancreatic duct stents, he died 1 month postdischarge. The patient had no adverse events aside from postprocedure fever; antibiotic treatment was effective. Critically, he maintained oral autonomy and community ambulation until terminal hospitalization, achieving a cumulative survival of 18 months from the initial intervention.

This case demonstrates the feasibility of serial endoscopic therapies, including RFA and stent-guided drainage, in achieving sustained symptom control and local tumor management for elderly patients with malignant IPMNs who are unfit for surgery.1,2

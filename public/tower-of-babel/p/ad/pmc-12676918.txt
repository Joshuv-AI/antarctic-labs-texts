There Was No Evidence of Osteonecrosis in Lunates of Early-Stage Kienbock’s Disease
Authors: Ji Sup Hwang, Jihyeung Kim, Hyun Sik Gong, Jin Ho Kim, Goo Hyun Baek, Hee Joong Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroudThe etiology and pathogenesis of Kienbock’s disease (KD) have not been clearly established. In osteonecrosis of other bones, such as the proximal femur, the interface between necrotic and normal bone is evident in early stages. The purpose of this study was to retrospectively review and analyze magnetic resonance (MR) images of early-stage KD to confirm whether the double-line sign or an abnormal signal intensity band corresponding to the reactive interface is present.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 31 cases of 30 patients (average age, 41.2 years) diagnosed with early-stage KD. Lichtman staging revealed 9 cases of stage I and 22 cases of stage II. We evaluated their MR images of various sequences with special interest in signal intensity changes and the presence of the double-line sign.ResultsIn all lunates, no abnormal signal intensity band representing a reactive interface was observed. Instead, diffuse signal intensity changes with a bone marrow edema pattern (BMEP) of various extents were detected in all 31 lunates. Furthermore, a fracture line was found in 10 lunates and BMEP in the surrounding carpal bones was present in 8 cases.ConclusionsIn lunates with stage I and II KD, the main MR finding was diffuse BMEP without an abnormal signal intensity line of a reactive interface, and some cases showed abnormal signal intensity bands suggestive of a fracture line.

The etiology and pathogenesis of Kienbock’s disease (KD) have not been clearly established. However, with many theories and factors contributing to its etiopathogenesis, it is basically defined as osteonecrosis.1) Therefore, surgical management of early-stage KD focuses on preventing further collapse of the lunate. In later stages, excision of the lunate is attempted. In osteonecrosis, the reactive interface between necrotic and normal bone is a very specific histopathologic finding. It is composed of a vascularized granulation tissue layer which surrounds the necrotic area and outer thickened trabeculae (Fig. 1A). In the early stage of osteonecrosis, the interface is composed of a vascularized granulation tissue layer only. This appears as a single band of low-signal intensity on T1- and T2-weighted magnetic resonance (MR) images. In later stages, with thickening of the trabecular bone outside the granulation tissue layer, an inner high and outer low intensity band, called the double-line sign, on T2-weighted MR images can be observed (Fig. 1B).234)

To the best of our knowledge, there was no description of this reactive interface in previous literature on KD. It may have been mentioned in other literature related to KD, but those reports on MR findings include bone marrow edema pattern (BMEP) as the initial finding.56789) Therefore, it seemed necessary to review the image findings. The purpose of this study was to retrospectively review and analyze the MR images of early-stage KD to determine whether the double-line sign or an abnormal signal intensity band corresponding to the reactive interface is present.

METHODS

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, and Yeson Hospital (2306-019-1437, B-2507-983-405, 2023-0275-002). The requirement for informed consent was waived as all data were fully anonymized for analysis.

Study Design and Setting

This was a multi-center, retrospective study of patients who underwent MR examinations of their wrists for the diagnosis of KD from January 2007 to June 2022.

Patients

We retrospectively reviewed the data of 407 patients diagnosed with KD between January 2007 and June 2022. Of these, we considered 395 patients (97%) who had wrist MR examinations as potentially eligible. We excluded patients with late-stage KD (Lichtman stage III or IV), leaving 31 consecutive cases of 30 patients (8%) for analysis. All diagnoses were made at 2 tertiary referral hospitals (22 cases) and 1 orthopedic hospital specializing in hand surgery (9 cases). In each hospital, the diagnosis was made after evaluation of the images by a hand surgeon and a musculoskeletal radiologist. There were 20 men and 10 women, with a mean age of 41.2 years (range, 20–68 years). Initial Lichtman staging revealed 9 cases of stage I and 22 cases of stage II. Besides 10 cases of a single visit, 11 cases were treated conservatively and 10 cases were treated surgically with radial shortening (4 cases), ulnar shortening (2 cases), vein graft (2 cases), pronator quadratus pedicled bone graft interpositional arthroplasty (1 case), and decompression of the medullary space (1 case). They were followed up for 3–104 months (mean, 38.6 months).

MR Interpretation

Two hand surgeons (JSH and HSG) and 1 hip surgeon (HJK) evaluated the axial, coronal, and sagittal MR images of various sequences to evaluate the lunate with special interest in signal intensity changes and the existence of an abnormal signal intensity band representing the reactive interface within its bone marrow. In addition, we determined the presence of BMEPs in carpal bones, including the lunate, and of any fracture line.

RESULTS

In all stage I lunates (9 cases), diffuse signal intensity changes in BMEP of various extent were detected in the lunate (Fig. 2), but no abnormal signal intensity band representing a reactive interface was observed. In 4 cases, a suspicious fracture line was detected (Fig. 3). In 5 cases, a small cyst was located in the lunate, and in 3 cases, signal intensity changes of BMEP were observed in adjacent bones, including the capitate, ulnar head, and triquetrum (Fig. 4). In all stage II lunates (22 cases), diffuse signal intensity changes in BMEP of various extent were detected as in stage I lunates (Fig. 5). In 6 cases, an abnormal signal intensity band of a fracture line was detected and a small fracture fragment looked avascular in 1 case (Fig. 6). In 5 cases, BMEP was also observed in the second metacarpal base, hamate, scaphoid, and triquetrum (Fig. 7, Table 1).

DISCUSSION

KD was first described in 1910 as osteonecrosis of the lunate.10) With the development of MR imaging, understanding of osteonecrosis has recently changed in other bones, such as the femoral head.111213) In KD, the etiology of the osteonecrosis and its optimal treatment remain controversial. We therefore analyzed the MR findings with special focus on stage I and II KD. As a result, we found the major MR finding of BMEP, which means viable bone. In no case of both stages, an abnormal intensity line of a reactive interface, a very specific finding of osteonecrosis, was detected. These results suggest that KD is a disease entity different from osteonecrosis in other bones including the femoral head.

In osteonecrosis, BMEP had been recognized as a very early sign for a long time,14) but it was found to be a misunderstanding. In several very well performed prospective studies, it was found that the initial MR finding of osteonecrosis is the abnormal signal intensity band representing the reactive interface between the necrotic and living bone, the outer margin of the necrotic area.4151617) It is a low signal intensity band on both T1 and T2 images, representing a vascularized granulation tissue layer in a very early stage. It becomes a band of low signal intensity on T1 images that converts to inner high and outer low signal intensity bands on T2 images (so called double-line sign) with thickening of the trabecular bone outside the granulation tissue layer. In osteonecrosis, BMEP is thought to develop later, with the development of the subchondral fracture.111213) With these findings, subchondral stress fracture and bone marrow edema syndrome (transient osteoporosis) are now recognized as distinct disease entities from osteonecrosis.1819202122)

No description of a reactive interface was found in histological studies of KD. It might be because the number of studies is small and limited to late-stage cases acquired by surgical excision.2324) Recently, Xiong et al.25) evaluated 5 stage III KD cases histopathologically. They observed only focal necrotic areas without osteonecrosis of remarkable extent and suggested that the progressive process of KD could result from lunate nonunion. In a pilot study for this study, performed at one of the study hospitals, 33 stage III cases were reviewed. The lunate was collapsed and elongated with fragmentation. On MR images, 1 or 2 small fragments looked avascular, but BMEP was observed in most fragments (Fig. 8).

It is often hypothesized that repetitive microtrauma, rather than a single strong impact, is a contributing factor.26) Many patients with KD are engaged in occupations requiring heavy manual labor or sports activity.127) Furthermore, spasticity and habitual wrist flexion are known to contribute to the high incidence of KD in cerebral palsy patients.28) Predisposing anatomical factors are known, such as negative ulnar variance and type 1 lunate.27) In no case of our series, a single remarkable trauma history was recollected with the onset of wrist pain. Furthermore, BMEP detected in adjacent carpal bones can be interpreted as the result of the mechanical overloading in the wrist. Therefore, it is likely that repeated microtrauma is the cause of early-stage KD.

One limitation of this study was that many cases were single visit only. Another limitation was that changes in image findings were observed during follow-up in very few cases. In 1 case of stage II, fragmentation of the volar pole developed during follow-up (Fig. 9). Studies for the natural course of KD are very limited in number and in duration. In some follow-up studies of conservatively treated cases, there were cases in which the stage changed from advanced to less advanced, the fragmentations eventually healed and the sclerotic change improved.8293031)

In lunates of stage I and II KD, the main MR finding was diffuse BMEP without an abnormal signal intensity line representing a reactive interface, and some cases showed abnormal signal intensity bands suggestive of a fracture line.

Computed tomographic dacryocystography combined with multiplanar reconstruction technique in lacrimal duct probe placement
Authors: Qi Jin, Donglian Yu, Zhangjun Ren, Gang Hu, Zhen He, Yan Xia, Siyi Wang, Hongfei Liao, Yaohua Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCT dacryocystography (CT-DCG) combined with multiplanar reconstruction (MPR) enables precise evaluation of the bony nasolacrimal duct (BNLD) anatomy, which may guide surgical treatment for nasolacrimal duct obstruction (LNDO). This retrospective study aimed to identify key anatomical factors influencing lacrimal probe placement and to assess whether preoperative imaging improves clinical outcomes.MethodsBetween December 2018 and January 2022, 230 patients (257 eyes) with LNDO underwent CT-DCG and MPR before planned lacrimal probing and intubation. Reconstructed images were used to measure BNLD angle, diameter, length, and the spatial relationship between its long axis and the forehead soft tissue and frontal bone. Probe insertion success was recorded intraoperatively. Postoperative outcomes were evaluated in a subset of 78 patients (78 eyes) at 6 months using Munk score and lacrimal irrigation, defining success as complete (Munk 0–1 + irrigation return < 50%) or partial (Munk 2–3 or return < 50%).ResultsProbe insertion succeeded in 246 eyes and failed in 11 eyes (10 patients). The failed group had a significantly smaller angle between the BNLD long axis and the coronal plane (NC angle) (P < 0.05). At 6 months, complete success was achieved in 59/78 eyes (75.6%), partial success in 3/78 (3.8%), and failure in 16/78 (20.5%); overall effectiveness was 79.5%, similar to reported intubation rates. Successful intraoperative probe placement was associated with no overlap or < 2/3 overlap of the BNLD axis with forehead soft tissue. The mean NC angle was 19.1°; 66% of BNLDs were inwardly oriented (mean 7°).ConclusionCT-DCG with MPR provides detailed BNLD anatomy and can predict difficult probe placement, potentially reducing false passages. However, its impact on long-term symptomatic success is comparable to conventional probing, and its main value lies in preoperative planning and avoiding unnecessary attempts.Keywords: Nasolacrimal duct obstruction, Computed tomographic dacryocystography, Multiplanar reconstruction technology

Introduction

Nasolacrimal duct obstruction is a common and frequent disease in ophthalmology [1], which is caused by partial or total obstruction of the nasolacrimal duct due to inflammation or other causes, with tearing as the main symptom [2]. Clinically, surgical treatment is the main method, and the main methods include dacryocystorhinostomy, dacryocystorhinostomy catheterization, laser dacryocystoplasty, external subciliary dacryocystorhinostomy [3–5], endonasal endoscopic dacryocystorhinostomy (En-DCR), dacryocystoscopy, etc. The main surgical method for patients with stenosis or obstruction in the middle and lower segments of BNLD is lacrimal duct probing combined with intubation, which is widely used in clinic for its advantages of simple operation, little trauma and short operation time [6].

False passage is a serious complication of probe placement, with an incidence of 1.2% to 7.1% [7]. The angle of BNLD is a key factor in determining the success or failure of lacrimal duct probing combined with intubation. Probe placement is an operation completed by experience, with the risk of false passage when the probe passes through the junction of lacrimal sac-BNLD and the stenosis of BNLD. Therefore, being familiar with the BNLD direction is the key to successful combined lacrimal probe placement and prevention of false passage. Therefore, in this study, CT-DCG combined with MPR was performed on patients with LNDO. According to the reconstructed images, the angle, diameter, and length of BNLD were measured, and the relationship between the long axis of BNLD and the forehead skin soft tissue and frontal bone were observed. Based on the above results to predict whether the lacrimal duct probe can smoothly enter the nasolacrimal duct and guide the direction of the probe during the operation. According to whether the lacrimal duct probe can smoothly enter the nasolacrimal duct or not, the patients were divided into two groups, The main anatomical factors affecting lacrimal duct probe placement were analyzed by comparing the differences in BNLD morphological differences between the two groups.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

This retrospective study included 230 consecutive patients (257 eyes) with LNDO who underwent lacrimal probing and intubation at our hospital between December 2019 and January 2022. All patients underwent preoperative CT-DCG and MPR. Based on imaging, patients were categorized into expected easy or difficult probe placement. Intraoperatively, probe insertion success (smooth entry into the nasolacrimal duct) was recorded. A subset of 78 patients (78 eyes) who completed 6-month follow-up were assessed for functional outcomes.

The study was approved by the hospital ethics committee (IRB: YLP20231032). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before surgery.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) diagnosis of LNDO confirmed by lacrimal probing (hard stop), irrigation (reflux from superior punctum with little or no fluid entering pharynx), and CT-DCG (narrowing or obstruction of middle/lower third of BNLD).

Exclusion criteria: (1) irregular BNLD (curved, inflection point); (2) contraindication to iodinated contrast; (3) inferior turbinate hypertrophy or narrow inferior meatus; (4) previous lacrimal surgery or trauma; (5) mucopurulent discharge; (6) severe systemic disease.

CT-DCG and MPR protocol

After lacrimal irrigation, a 23-gauge blunt cannula was inserted into the inferior punctum, and 1–3 mL of 76% meglumine diatrizoate was injected slowly until reflux from the superior punctum or resistance. Patients were immediately placed supine with the orbitomeatal line perpendicular to the table. Scans were performed using a Siemens Somatom Definition AS+ spiral CT (120 kV, 100 mA, slice thickness 0.6 mm, reconstruction thickness 0.6 mm) from frontal sinus to nasal base. Bone window settings (window width 2600, level 800) were used.

Preoperative prediction criteria.

Initially, probe placement was predicted as difficult if the BNLD long axis overlapped the frontal bone or overlapped the forehead soft tissue by more than 1/2 (Fig. 2C, D). This criterion was used for surgical planning. After analyzing intraoperative outcomes, we revised the threshold to 2/3 overlap as a more accurate predictor (see Results).

Surgical technique

All procedures were performed by the same experienced surgeon under local anesthesia (infraorbital and anterior ethmoidal nerve block) without sedation.

Lacrimal probing with intubation: A lacrimal probe was introduced via the inferior punctum, turned 90° at the bony lacrimal sac, and advanced into the nasolacrimal duct at an angle guided by preoperative measurements. Correct positioning was confirmed with nasal endoscopy. A silicone tube (RS tube) was then placed.

En-DCR: If the probe could not be advanced after gentle adjustment, the procedure was converted to En-DCR.

Postoperative outcome assessment

At 6 months postoperatively, patients underwent Munk score assessment and lacrimal irrigation [8, 9]. Munk score was graded 0–5: 0 = no epiphora; 1 = occasional wiping ≤ 2 times/day; 2 = 2–4 times/day; 3 = 5–10 times/day; 4 = > 10 times/day; 5 = constant tearing. Irrigation findings were recorded as:

Complete success: Munk 0–1 and irrigation return < 50% (fluid passes to pharynx or minimal reflux).

Partial success: Munk 2–3 or irrigation return < 50% (without meeting complete criteria).

Failure: Munk 4–5 and irrigation return ≥ 50%.

Effectiveness rate was defined as (complete + partial) / total eyes × 100%.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed with SPSS 24.0. Continuous variables with normal distribution are presented as mean ± SD and compared using t-tests; non-normal variables are presented as median (P25, P75) and compared with Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared with chi-square test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General data

A total of 230 patients (257 eyes) were enrolled. Mean age was 55.67 ± 15.72 years; 176 were female (76.5%) and 54 male (23.5%). Probe insertion succeeded in 220 patients (246 eyes) and failed in 10 patients (11 eyes). Age did not differ between groups (P = 0.930), but failure was more frequent in males (P = 0.043).

Prediction of probe insertion

Based on the initial 1/2 overlap criterion, 42 eyes were predicted as difficult; among these, 31 actually had successful probe placement (false-positive rate 12.6%, accuracy 87.9%) (Tables 2 and 3). Post hoc analysis revealed that a 2/3 overlap threshold improved accuracy: only 1 of 12 eyes predicted as difficult was successfully probed (false-positive rate 0.4%, accuracy 99.6%). Patients with failed placement had either BNLD axis overlapping frontal bone (81.8%) or > 2/3 overlap with thick forehead soft tissue (mean 0.99 cm).

Postoperative outcomes

Of the 230 patients, 78 (78 eyes) completed 6-month follow-up. Their baseline characteristics were: mean age 47.4 ± 11.2 years (range 20–65); 58 females (74.4%), 20 males (25.6%); mean disease duration 45.6 ± 15.6 months (range 2.4–120 months).

Postoperative results are summarized in Table 4. Complete success was achieved in 59 eyes (75.6%), partial success in 3 eyes (3.8%), and failure in 16 eyes (20.5%). The overall effectiveness rate was 79.5%. Among the 6 eyes in this subset that had experienced intraoperative probe failure (and were converted to En-DCR), 4 (66.7%) achieved complete success and 2 (33.3%) failed. The success rate did not significantly differ from the overall cohort (P = 0.89).

Discussion

Nasolacrimal duct obstruction is a common clinical disease, mostly occurring in middle-aged and elderly women. The disease can lead to acute and chronic dacryocystitis, keratitis and many other diseases, and in serious cases, it can lead to endophthalmitis and even loss of vision. Lacrimal duct probing combined with intubation can unblock the obstruction site without changing the normal physiological structure and maintaining the integrity of the lacrimal mucosa, which has the advantages of easy operation, small trauma, short operation time and precise efficacy, which is mainly used in patients with low nasolacrimal duct obstruction and congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction [11, 12]. The efficacy of the operative method can be a partial alternative to En-DCR, and its surgical cure rate has been reported to be variable, ranging from 52% to 85% [5, 10, 13]. Lacrimal duct probing combined with intubation places silicone tubes into the nasolacrimal duct, which provides mechanical support, dilation, and drainage of the lacrimal obstruction, creating a favorable environment for lacrimal self-repair and gaining time [3, 6].

CT-DCG uses the high density and spatial resolution of CT in combination with contrast medium to clearly show the anatomical features of the lacrimal duct, such as the obstruction site, lacrimal duct diameter, and the relationship between the lacrimal duct and adjacent structures, providing accurate clinical imaging information, and therefore CT-DCG is increasingly used in lacrimal duct diseases [14, 15]. BNLD is not a vertically aligned canal, and there is a certain angle with the median sagittal and coronal projections, so there will be a certain angle difference between the images obtained from conventional CT scans and the length of each meridian of BNLD, and there is a certain error in diameter measurement and observation of adjacent structures [16]. This error can be effectively avoided by using MPR. This technique reconstructs the axial, oblique coronal and oblique sagittal images according to the long axis of BNLD, and then measures the meridians and angles at the corresponding levels to obtain more accurate results, and also more accurately reflects the obstruction site of BNLD and the relationship with adjacent structures.

The direction of the BNLD is one of the key factors determining the success or failure of lacrimal duct probing combined with intubation, starting from the BNLD entrance, its direction varies according to individual differences [17]. During the procedure, the probe placement operation is done empirically [18], and a review of the literature in recent years revealed that the risk of false passage occurring with this procedure is about 1.2% to 7.1% [7]. Therefore, a full understanding of the BNLD morphology is a guide for selecting the probe length, depth of entry and direction, which can reduce the operative time and avoid unnecessary injuries.

Takahashi et al. [19] measured the BNLD angle on 28 Japanese cadaver heads and showed an NC angle of 19.8, which was similar to the results of the present study. The results of two other studies differed somewhat from the present study, in which one reported that the angle between the long axis of BNLD and nasal base was 70.3° ± 18.7 in NDO patients [20].; in the other study, the NC angle was 12.09° ± 1.21° [21]. Several of the above studies used different study subjects and measurement methods, which could explain the variability that exists between the results. Similarly, preoperative assessment of the type and angle of NDO patients in the coronal position is essential to guide the surgery. According to the relationship between the long axis of BNLD and the sagittal line in the coronal position, there are two types of BNLD: inward and outward. In this study, inward type accounted for 66.0% and outward type accounted for 34.0%, and the proportion of inward type was larger than outward type, and the average angle between inward type and outward type was 7. A study by Bulbul et al. [22] on 39 patients with NDO showed that the two types accounted for 64.1% and 35.9%, respectively. Therefore, during the procedure the probe is placed from the lacrimal punctum against the bony structure and then turned 90 downward and slowly tilted backward about 19, and tilted inward or outward about 7 to probe through is a more appropriate angle for smooth placement of the probe into the nasolacrimal duct.

The preoperative prediction of difficulty in probe placement was based on the overlapping proportion of the BNLD long axis with the frontal bone or with the frontal skin soft tissue by more than 1/2, but the intraoperative probing results were somewhat different, with a prediction accuracy rate of 87.4% and a false-positive rate of 12.6%. Postoperatively, all data were analyzed, and it was found that the accuracy of prediction using the overlapping proportion of 2/3 as the cut-off value was as high as 99.6%, and the false-positive rate was only 0.4%. Therefore, the preoperative prediction of whether the probe can be placed into the nasolacrimal duct was summarized based on the following criteria: (1) the long axis of BNLD does not overlap with the forehead skin soft tissue or the overlapping proportion is less than 2/3, and the probe can be placed smoothly.(2) the overlapping proportion between the long axis of BNLD and the forehead skin soft tissue is more than 2/3 and the forehead skin soft tissue is thin, the probe can still enter the nasolacrimal duct forward if the tail of the probe is pressed backward after squeezing the soft tissue of the skin.(3) the overlapping proportion between the long axis of BNLD and the forehead skin soft tissue is more than 2/3 but the skin soft tissue is thick, and the probes are difficult to be placed. (4) The long axis of BNLD overlaps with the frontal bone, making probe placement difficult. However, we should also consider other anatomical factors, such as BNLD diameter, when determining whether the probe can be placed into the nasolacrimal duct. In one patient in this study, the long axis of BNLD overlapped with the frontal bone, and the preoperative judgment was that it might be difficult to place the probe, but intraoperatively the probe entered the nasolacrimal duct smoothly after light pressure on the skin. The reason for this was that the patient had a large BNLD diameter (anterior-posterior diameter and transverse meridian of 6.0 mm and 5.75 mm, respectively). and the tail of the lacrimal probe was able to enter the nasolacrimal duct at a posterior angle. In patients with LNDO, a correct preoperative determination of whether the probe can be placed into the nasolacrimal duct is beneficial for the development of an individualized surgical approach, adequate preoperative preparation, and reduction of complications. Preoperative prediction of successful probe placement, intraoperative adjustment of the probe direction according to the measured BNLD angle for surgery; preoperative prediction of difficult probe placement, change of surgical approach to En-DCR.

The long axis of BNLD overlaps with the forehead skin soft tissue or frontal bone to varying degrees. The anatomical factors are as follows: small NC angle and high frontal head. In this study, the NC angle of the successful lacrimal duct probe placement group was significantly greater than that of the failed group. Therefore, for patients with high forehead and small backward BNLD inclination, the placement of the probe is difficult, and forced placement of the probe is likely to cause a false passage, resulting in poor surgical effect. For such patients, the surgical method is changed to En-DCR. There was a difference in gender distribution between the successful and failed groups. NDO was more likely to occur in females, but probe placement failure was more common in males, which was analyzed for the reasons that the superciliary arch of males was thicker than that of females, the aornu frontale angle was larger than that of females, and the NC angle was smaller than that of females. This anatomical feature determined that male patients with NDO were more likely to have probe placement failure.

Lacrimal endoscopy allows direct visualization of intraluminal pathology and can guide recanalization [23]. However, it requires specialized equipment, is more invasive, and does not provide information about the bony canal’s spatial relationship to facial structures. CT-DCG with MPR complements endoscopy by offering a preoperative roadmap of the entire BNLD, helping to select the optimal approach (probing vs. En-DCR) and reducing intraoperative guesswork. In centers without endoscopic capability, this imaging modality is particularly valuable.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design introduces potential selection bias. Second, the predictive threshold of 2/3 overlap was derived from post hoc analysis; it requires prospective validation. Third, only one‑third of the cohort had long‑term follow‑up, and the small number of failures limits the power of subgroup comparisons.

Despite these limitations, our findings suggest that CT-DCG with MPR is a useful adjunct for surgical planning in LNDO. It may help avoid unnecessary probing attempts and reduce the risk of iatrogenic injury, especially in patients with unfavorable anatomy (small NC angle, thick forehead soft tissue, or BNLD–frontal bone overlap).

Conclusion

CT-DCG combined with MPR provides detailed anatomical information about the BNLD and can predict difficult probe insertion with high accuracy. Although it does not improve long‑term symptomatic success compared to conventional probing, it aids in patient selection and surgical planning, potentially reducing complications. A 2/3 overlap of the BNLD axis with forehead soft tissue or overlap with the frontal bone are reliable indicators for proceeding directly to En-DCR.

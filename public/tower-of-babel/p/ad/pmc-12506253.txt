Brief communication: demographic disparities and modes of transmission of HIV in Mizoram, Northeast India
Authors: Lalnunthari Khawlhring, Benjamin Lalrinpuia, Rebecca Lalngaihzuali, Gracy Laldinmawii, Lalengkimi Ralte, Christina Lalnuntluangi, Richard L Chawngthu, Nalida Lalthafeli, Jane Ralte, John Zothanzama, Nachimuthu Senthil Kumar, Sumit Aggarwal, Swagnik Roy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examines the prevalence of Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) in Mizoram, Northeast India, with a focus on age-group transmission routes and gender-specific prevalence. According to data from the ART Plus Centre at Civil Hospital Aizawl (January–December 2023), men were more likely to be infected with HIV, with the 26–35 age group being the most affected. Heterosexual transmission and intravenous drug use (IDU) were shown to be the most common modes of transmission. Gender and age variations were identified by statistical analysis using SPSS® (V 27.0), highlighting the need for focused interventions. To effectively combat the HIV epidemic in this area, gender-specific policies, harm reduction strategies, and stigma reduction measures are essential.Keywords: HIV, Socio-demography, Age dependence, Transmission

Background

HIV is a major public health issue worldwide, with regional variations in prevalence and transmission patterns [1]. Mizoram, India’s northeastern state, has one of the country’s highest HIV prevalence rates [2]. The complexity of the epidemic in the region is influenced by a number of sociological and geographic factors, including high illicit drug use rates and accessibility to international borders [3]. Mizoram has a population of approximately 1.097 million, of which 36.49% are residing in the capital city, Aizawl [4] which is well connected to all the other districts. Civil Hospital Aizawl is the only existing ART Plus [5] centre in the state which accommodate majority of the PLHIV. Globally, women and girls make up for 53% of all people living with HIV in 2023 [6], whereas in India, HIV prevalence is higher among males than females [7]. Developing customized interventions requires an understanding of gender-specific prevalence and transmission routes. The objective of this research is to present a thorough epidemiological analysis of HIV in Mizoram with a focus on gender differences and mode of transmission among different age groups which has the contributing factor for becoming highest prevalent state in the entire country. This study intends to close the knowledge gap and provide essential information for implementing appropriate control measures, since there is a lack of published and ground-level data to explain the high incidence among the Mizo community.

Methods

Ethical considerations

The study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Review Committee of Zoram Medical College. The Declaration of Helsinki’s guiding principles was followed in conducting this study as per World Medical Association.

Study design and settings

This study is based on a cross-sectional analysis of HIV data collected at ART Plus Centre, Civil Hospital Aizawl, Mizoram. ART Centres are located in every district of the state and one ART Plus Centre which can give second line drugs is located in the capital city, Aizawl. Civil Hospital Aizawl as it is located in the city and is a government hospital, majority of the patients are from middle to low-income group who are mostly literate. This is a one-year study conducted from January 2023 to December 2023 in which newly diagnosed patients as well as the old patients who are enrolled in the program and took treatment from the centre were included. HIV sero-positive patients were included and HIV patients who did not give consent, non-tribal patients and pediatric cases were excluded from the study (Table 1).

Demographics of HIV patients in Mizoram, 2023 (n = 9531)

Participants and sample size

A total of 9531 patients with HIV sero-positive who are enrolled in ART Plus Centre, Civil Hospital Aizawl were included in this study.

Laboratory techniques

Commercially available diagnostic kits were used in diagnosis and follow- up test. Patients were serologically confirmed using COMBAIDS® -RS Advantage-ST HIV 1 + 2 immunodot test kit, One Step Rapid Test for detection of anti-HIV in Human Serum/Plasma/Whole blood MERISCREEN HIV 1–2 WB and HIV Tri-Dot as per guideline of National AIDS Control Organization (NACO) [8]. Screening test was done in ICTC (Integrated Counselling & testing Centre) located in every districts [9]. As per NACO guideline, pre and post-test counselling was provided to all patients with the goal of educating them about HIV and bring about behaviour change in the individual. It also enables the individual to take a decision regarding HIV testing and to understand the implications of the test results [10].

Data management and analysis

All the patient’s information were decoded and were given sample IDs. The data was handled only by the researchers who were directly involved in this work and no data was leaked to any other individual other than the researchers. The privacy of the patients were honoured. All the questionnaire data files were kept under lock and key cabinet and electronic data was kept in password protected computers and only handled by the researchers involved in the study. For this study different datas were collected from demographic background, with age, gender, and comprehensive records of the mode of HIV transmission.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed in IBM® SPSS® Statistics (V 27.0). Demographic characteristics of the study population were compiled using descriptive statistics. To evaluate the relationship between demographic values and routes of transmission, bivariate analyses were performed using the Chi-Square test. To show the distribution of HIV cases across various demographic categories, the findings are presented as absolute numbers and percentages. For data cleaning missing values were excluded and some values which were random and/or inaccurate were excluded.

Results

Age and gender distribution

The age range of this study is 15 to 85 years (median = 50 years) where the male to female ratio is approximately 1.4:1. The different age groups and the gender were highly significant (p < 0.001), with a higher percentage of men (57.7%) than women (42.3%). Age group between 26 and 35 years had the highest prevalence of HIV accounting to 40.4% of the total cases. In this age range, male constituted 57.7% and female 42.3%, indicating a male predominance in the gender distribution, the second most affected age group was 15–25 years, accounting for 24.0% of all cases (Fig. 1a). HIV prevalence decreases with age, with the 36–45 and 46–55 years age groups accounting for 20.2% and 12.4% of the cases, respectively. The age range of 56–85 years had the lowest prevalence, accounting for just 3.0% of all cases.

Modes of transmission

Significant patterns was observed in the mode of transmission (χ² = 27,630.62, p < 0.001). Heterosexual contact is the leading cause of HIV infection in Mizoram, accounting for 70.0% of the cases. Injecting drug use and unsafe injection practices which account for 25.8% of the total cases. Other modes of transmission include men having sex with men (MSM), which accounts for 1.5% of the cases, and mother-to-child transmission, which represents 0.4%. Cases related to blood transfusion and female sex worker account for 0.6% and 0.3% of cases respectively, and are relatively rare (Fig. 1b).

The distribution of HIV transmission mode varies significantly across different age groups. In 15–20 age group, mother to child transmission is strikingly high followed by MSM. FSW, IDU and unsafe injection is also quite concerning in this age group. In the 26–35 age group, heterosexual contact becomes the predominant mode of transmission, followed by IDU. Similar trend continues in the 36–45 age group, where heterosexual transmission remains high, while IDU still contributes significantly. For individuals aged 46–55 years, heterosexual transmission is the leading mode of transmission with lower contributions from IDU. The 56–85 age group shows the highest unknown transmission (Fig. 1b).

Discussion

The findings of this study highlight the complexity of the HIV epidemic in Mizoram which is driven by a confluence of demographic and behavioural factors. The high prevalence of HIV among males in the 26–35 years age group is particularly concerning, as this demographic is actively engaged in high-risk behaviours such as IDU and unprotected sexual activity. The use of drugs is closely associated with both the spread of Sexually Transmitted diseases (STDs) and commercial sex work [11]. Illicit drug use is more common among men than women [12]. According to WHO, 10% of new HIV infections globally are caused by IDU [13]. HIV prevalence is declining with age, which is in accordance with global trends [14]. Younger persons are generally more likely to get infected due to risky behaviours and higher sexual engagement [15].

HIV prevalence and modes of transmission vary significantly between Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra states due to socio-demographic and cultural differences. In Tamil Nadu, the adult HIV prevalence is approximately 0.25%, while Maharashtra has a slightly higher rate at about 0.35% [16]. The data are compared to identify how unique our finding is among Mizo tribal population compared to Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu. The primary mode of HIV transmission in the above mentioned states is heterosexual contact (over 85% cases), highlighting the importance of targeted interventions among high-risk groups, including female sex workers, MSM, and long-distance truck drivers. In both states, prevention strategies include promoting safe-sex practices, increasing access to condoms, and expanding antiretroviral treatment coverage which is also implemented in Mizoram state. Programs like community-based testing, counselling, and treatment support have played a crucial role in controlling the epidemic [17].

According to the Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), in 2021, gay and bisexual men are the population most affected by HIV accounted for 71% of the new HIV diagnoses in the United States and dependent areas [18]. Molecular epidemiology data reveal significant HIV infection clustering in MSM networks, as well as greater rates of dual- and multiple-variant HIV infection in MSM compared to heterosexual individuals in the same communities [19].

Heroin trafficking via the India-Myanmar border has been made easier by raging insurgencies, strong trans-border ethnic ties, and a weakly monitored international border [20]. The high prevalence of HIV among IDUs is indicative of the broader challenges faced by Mizoram in controlling the spread of HIV. Despite the implementation of harm reduction strategies [21], such as needle exchange programs and opioid substitution therapy [22], the high HIV rates among IDUs suggests that these interventions may not sufficiently reach the most at-risk populations.

The study findings have significant implications for public health policy of Mizoram epidemiological profiles. First, a better harm reduction strategy is required, especially for IDUs and young men. In addition to opioid substitution therapy and needle exchange programs, these tactics should also involve extensive outreach and education initiatives that target the underlying reasons of drug use and encourage safer behaviour. Second, given the high prevalence of HIV among young males, programs for condom distribution and sexual health education should be expanded, especially in schools, colleges, and workplaces. Finally, efforts against discrimination and stigma have to be reinforced. Reducing stigma and enhancing access to care require community-based efforts that involve local leaders and make use of cultural and religious traditions to encourage acceptance of people living with HIV.

Conclusion

Based on study from 2023, this report offers a thorough investigation of the demographic trends and modes of HIV transmission in Mizoram, India. The results underline the importance of focused public health campaigns addressing the particular risks experienced by young people, especially men, and IDUs. The report also emphasizes the need of tackling the socioeconomic and cultural elements influencing the HIV prevalence in the state. Implementing a diverse strategy including harm reduction, sexual health education, gender-inclusive treatments, and efforts to reduce stigma and prejudice can help Mizoram to effectively fight the HIV epidemic. Adjusting public health policies to the specific issues of Mizoram can greatly help in reducing the HIV burden in the state.

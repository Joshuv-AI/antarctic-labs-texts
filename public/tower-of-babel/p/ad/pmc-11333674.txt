Research trends of bone tumor treatment with 3D printing technology from 2013 to 2022: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Jia-Hao Chu, Yang Zhang, Yi Jiang, Hai-Fan Wu, Wen-Yi Wang, Min Wang, Jia-Hui Zhang, Kun Yan, Xin-Miao Yao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveBibliometrics was employed in this study to determine the research trends in the worldwide application of 3D printing technology to treat bone tumors over the previous 10 years.MethodsPublished from 2013 to 2022, the papers related to bone tumors treated with 3D printing were located in Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), PubMed, and Scopus. The screened articles were included in this bibliometric study. From these papers in WoSCC, information on annual publications, journals, keywords, countries, authors, institutions, and cited references were extracted and visualized with CiteSpace (version 6.1.R6) software to investigate the state of bone tumor treatment using 3D printing as well as research hotspots. The Carrot2 online visualization tool and Vosviewer software (version 1.6.20) were employed to visualize the publications from PubMed and Scopus, respectively, in order to ascertain the most popular research topics from both databases.ResultsA total of 606, 233, and 364 publications were obtained from WoSCC, PubMed, and Scopus, respectively, between the years 2013 and 2022. In WoSCC, the peak number of publications was found in 2021, with 145 publications published. Acta Biomaterialia (11 publications) and World Neurosurgery (10 publications) were the most prolific journals, and Biomaterials was the journal cited the most (244 times). Yong Zhou was the most productive author with 14 publications, while Kwok-Chuen Wong (69 citations) and William F Enneking, (69 citations) possessed the most citations. The country with the largest quantity of publications was China (207). Among all institutions, Shanghai Jiao Tong University produced the most publications (29). Rapid prototyping was the keyword with the strongest citation burst (4.73). ‘Reconstruction’, ‘surgery’, ‘resection’, and ‘design’ caught the significant attention of researchers. 3D-printed materials, pelvic reconstruction, mandibular reconstruction, computer-assisted surgical techniques, photothermal therapy, and in vitro experiments were recognized as hot subjects and trends in current research. The most frequently occurring topics in Scopus are not significantly different from those found in WoSCC. The most prevalent research areas in PubMed encompass implant, patient-specific, bioceramic, models, and pelvic.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01207-6.

Introduction

Bone tumors, which can be categorized into benign and malignant varieties according to their different abilities to metastasize, represent one of the tumor types that occur within the bone tissue. Adolescents and children are the most prevalent groups affected by bone tumors [1], and the proportion of malignant bone tumor patients among Americans with tumors is 0.2% [2]. Multidrug resistance, severe side effects, tumor recurrence, and other issues continue to persist in the treatment of skeletal tumors [3], and individuals suffering from malignant tumors often have a poor prognosis [4], bone tumors have emerged as a major challenge in the field of orthopedics. Currently, the main treatment methods for bone tumors are surgical resections which encompass two types: limb salvage surgery and amputation surgery. Previous research has found that no difference in long-term survival was shown between the two surgical methods, but limb salvage surgery is gradually becoming the first choice for the lower likelihood of surgical complications and a better quality of life [5, 6]. How to accurately and completely operate limb salvage surgery is the key factor in determining the operation effect and reducing complications, as well as an important breakthrough point in the surgical treatment of bone tumors [7]. Furthermore, reconstructive surgery of bone tumors is closely associated with the quality of living for patients having bone tumor surgery. Therefore, achieving perfect reconstruction after bone tumor surgery is also a hot topic in the domain of bone tumors [6]. 3D printing technology characterized by its high precision, visualization, individualization, and other features, is gaining importance in the treatment and postoperative reconstruction of bone tumors [8–10]. The significant increase in the requirements for bone tumor treatment and rehabilitation necessitates a comprehensive examination of the clinical applications of 3D printing technology in this field.

The application of bibliometrics for statistically analyzing publications in a particular field to assess the current status of the field and provide references for researchers is of significant research value. However, the use of 3D printing in bone tumor therapy has not been previously analyzed by bibliometrics before. Therefore, this study employs bibliometrics to analyze the publications related to bone tumor treatment using 3D printing technology in the past 10 years (2013–2022) and visualizes the publication information by using the CiteSpace software (version 6.1.R6) [11], Carrot2 online visualization tool, and Vosviewer software (version 1.6.20) to discover the relationship among the authors, journals, institutions, countries, and topics of the publications. Future development directions and the current state of the domain are illustrated through the relationships above.

Methods

Search strategy

In this study, Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) was selected as the primary database for the literature search. In order to prevent the loss of essential documents from other databases, a search was also conducted on PubMed and Scopus databases. A comprehensive search of these databases was conducted on October 23, 2023. The search topics included 3D printing and bone tumors. The publication dates were limited to 2013–01–01 to 2022–12–31, and no limitations were imposed on the language of this search. In WoSCC, the search yielded 606 publications, of which 572 were integrated into this bibliometric analysis after the removal of articles of a different type, namely, those that were not articles or review articles. A total of 233 and 316 articles were identified in PubMed and Scopus databases, respectively, for inclusion in this study. Data extraction

All records and citations retrieved from the search results of WoSCC were downloaded in plain text file format and the data extraction process was completed on the day of the search. After format conversion by CiteSpace software, we filtered out the types of literature we needed and extracted the information of highly cited papers, cited journals, keywords, countries (regions), authors, cited authors, and institutions in the publications. Subsequently, the information will ultimately be visualized and presented by CiteSpace software. Furthermore, the data from the literature retrieved from PubMed and Scopus databases was downloaded in CSV and RIS formats, respectively.

Finally, we searched and read the top ten highly cited publications in WoSCC and Scopus, to extract the key information from the articles for analysis and discussion. Articles with a Journal Citation Reports (JCR) partition of Q1 represent high-quality research at the forefront of the field. Therefore, we also conducted an in-depth study of articles located in JCR partition Q1 in the search results of PubMed to obtain cutting-edge information in the field.

Data analysis

In WoSCC, the OriginPro2021 software provides a straightforward visualization of the number of papers published in different years and the trend of the number of papers in the top five countries over time, presented in two bar charts. All records and citations of 606 articles obtained from WoSCC were imported into the Citespace software. Following conversion and filtering by Citespace software, the authors, organizations, countries (regions), keywords, and citation information of the 572 articles will be displayed on the images by Citespace. The images facilitate the visualization of the cooperation relationships and output capacity among authors, countries (regions), and institutions. Additionally, they allow us to comprehend the research intensity in fields represented by keywords and references, as well as the connections between these fields. To explore the main research directions of bone tumor treatment with 3D printing technology, cluster analysis was also performed on the keywords in the cited references. Dual-map overlay is utilized to show the direct relationship between theoretical foundations and clinical applications in the field. Using time and intensity as a reference, we can track the process of research hotspots over time by analyzing bursts of keywords. By analyzing the top five emerging high-intensity keywords of publications in the burst display, research hotspots in the corresponding fields and the connections between them can be uncovered, enabling researchers to better grasp the research frontiers. CiteSpace software was set up as follows: (1) Time slice settings: all publications from January 2013 through December 2022 were time-sliced at 1-year intervals per slice. (2) Term source selection: all term sources were ticked. (3) Selection criteria parameter settings: the G-index parameter was chosen to include and exclude nodes, and factor k = 25. (4) Visual mapping pruning: pruning the merged network and pruning sliced networks in the Pathfinder were selected. Lines and nodes are the primary components of the visualization map. The size of nodes corresponds to the frequency of authors, institutions, countries and regions, citations, cited authors, cited journals, and keywords. The nodes consist of a series of concentric circles, the color of which corresponds to the year. The Betweenness Centrality (BC) is utilized to specify the importance of nodes. At the outermost level of the node, purple circles indicate nodes with BC values above 0.1, which are deemed crucial [12]. Lines are used to represent relationships such as cooperation, co-occurrence, and co-citation between nodes [13].

The data from PubMed was subjected to clustering analysis using the Carrot2 online visualization tool in order to identify the most prevalent research trends within the field. The K-means algorithm is employed in Carrot2 online visualization tool for the purpose of clustering. The resulting clustering image comprises distinct colored squares, each representing a cluster. Labels on the squares indicate the cluster name and the number of studies included.

The Vosviewer software was employed to extract and visualize keyword information from literature data obtained from Scopus, with the objective of identifying the research areas that received the most attention from researchers. The Vosviewer software is configured to generate a Keywords image in accordance with the default operational settings. Each square in the figure represents a keyword, and the size of the square is positively correlated with the number of occurrences of the keyword. The connecting lines between the squares represent the co-occurrence relationships between the keywords, and the thickness of the connecting lines was positively correlated with the intensity of cooperation. The same color of the squares and lines indicates that they belong to the same cluster.

Cited references

The relationship in which two articles are cited by the same article is called a co-citation relationship, and the research field’s foundational information can be acquired by analyzing this relationship. 487 nodes and 1035 links make up the cited references map of WoSCC (Fig. 13). As observed from the top five cited references in terms of frequency (Table 10) and BC (Table 11), a retrospective study [14] published by Haijie Liang from Peking University People’s Hospital is an influential article with the highest number of co-citation times (41) and a high BC (0.16). This article, published in Bone & Joint Journal, mentions that the application of 3D-printed screw-rod connected hemipelvic prosthesis, standard hemipelvic prosthesis, and iliac prosthesis not only shortens the surgical time and reduces the possibility of deep-seated infections, but also enables the employment of pre-determined pore sizes for a porous prosthesis surface thereby increasing the prosthetic’s long-term stability [14–16]. Using CiteSpace software, the keywords used in the cited references were clustered and analyzed to determine research status and emerging trends. Each cluster was labeled using the logarithmic likelihood ratio (LLR) and 15 clusters were formed in total. The cluster silhouette value (S-value) and modularity value (Q-value) were 0.9373 and 0.8612, respectively, thus the cluster structure was considered significant and convincing. Discussion

This bibliometrics analysis encompasses 572 publications from WoSCC, 233 publications from PubMed, and 316 publications from Scopus. In this study, CiteSpace (6.1.R6), OriginPro2021, Vosviewer (1.6.20) software and Carrot2 online visualization tool were the principal tools employed. A detailed analysis of the information extracted from the publications has been conducted, with the aim of providing some inspiration for subsequent studies. Bone tumor is a general term that encompasses a wide range of tumor types occurring within bone tissue, including primary bone tumors and secondary bone tumors [3]. The treatment of bone tumors is accompanied by a multitude of challenges, including the difficulty of bone tumor surgery, bone tumor recurrence, and postoperative infection of bone tumors [17]. These issues highlight an urgent need for improvements in the treatment of skeletal tumors. The advent of 3D printing technology has revolutionized the treatment of bone tumors, providing increasingly sophisticated solutions to these problems. Developed in the 1980s and 1990s, 3D printing technology initially emerged in the automotive and aerospace industries as a largely industrial tool to facilitate concept-to-prototype development, and has since evolved into a widely used technology [18]. In the early years of the twenty-first century, 3D printing technology was introduced into the medical field [19]. With the development of science and technology, 3D printing technology has continuously been updated and has progressed from the initial customization of prostheses to the fields of pre-operative planning, intra-operative guidance, medical popularization, and medical education, from the use of traditional chemical materials to the use of the patient’s cell lines grown into 3D scaffolds to create customized prostheses, and from serving solely as a structural repair tool to multiple actions such as stimulating tissue regeneration and local drug delivery, 3D printing technology is emerging in the field of medicine [20]. 3D printing technology possesses the ability to create structurally complex and specific structures through medical imaging, including magnetic resonance imaging and computerized axial tomography, which can be utilized not only in the fabrication of implants, customization of surgical instruments, and development of drug formulation [21], but also for killing residual tumor cells by virtue of the physicochemical properties of the materials used or the loaded drugs [17], which is a rising star in the domain of bone tumor treatment. The rise of technology for 3D printing used in bone tumor treatment over the last decade is reflected to some extent by the growing number of annual publications. In WoSCC, the upward trend in the number of papers published from 2013 to 2022, with a peak in 2021, indicates that the application of 3D printing technology in bone tumor treatment has been widely accepted. The number of publications across different countries shows that China has extensive experience in applying 3D printing technology to treat bone tumors, and is the main body of research in this field. The United States, the second largest research center, is likewise seeing a continuous rise in the number of publications. As Fig. 7 performed, each country (especially between the two major research centers) is relatively independent of each other, so if different countries enhance their collaboration, more large-sample, high-quality clinical studies will be formed, which can significantly advance the development of technology for 3D printing used in bone tumor treatment and achieve more research results. Shanghai Jiao Tong University, a leading comprehensive university in China, which not only has a large number of publications but also tops the list of BC, plays a significant role in this field. The top ten authors are all from China, and they primarily collaborate within the country, international exchange should be strengthened. The majority of the articles included in this study are from specialty journals, in the fields of neurosurgery, oncology, biomaterials, and orthopedics. These journals have an impact factor ranging from 2 to 14, and there is a notable absence of highly scholarly, comprehensive journals. Highly cited journals represent journals that are highly recognized in the field, are predominantly focus on orthopedic surgery, and biomaterials, such as Biomaterials, Acta Biomaterialia, and Clinical Orthopaedics and Related Research. Keyword co-occurrence and burst analysis are the methods to obtain publication hotspots. After eliminating the search terms, the high-frequency keywords were ‘bone’, ‘surgery’, ‘resection’, ‘in vitro’, ‘reconstruction’, ‘bone regeneration’, ‘mechanical properties’, ‘scaffolds’, and’ fabrication’. Keyword burst analysis reveals that ‘en bloc sacrectomy’, ‘photothermal therapy’, ‘pelvic tumor’, and ‘one-step reconstruction’ are recent research hotspots. The results of the co-citation analysis indicate that the utilization of 3D printing technology in the treatment of pelvic tumors is a trending direction. Furthermore, we performed keyword clustering of the cited references to obtain the key themes of 3D printing on bone tumors, and the results show that ‘pelvic reconstruction’, ‘mandibular reconstruction’, and ‘biomaterials’ have garnered significant attention from researchers. Furthermore, publications obtained from PubMed and Scopus were analysed thematically, and popular research directions were identified, including ‘implant’, ‘patient-specific’, ‘bioceramic’, ‘models’, and ‘pelvic’.

A comprehensive analysis of the results of this study reveals that the most popular research directions for the application of 3D printing technology in the treatment of bone tumors are focused on computer-assisted surgical techniques, individualization of prosthesis and surgical plan, pelvic reconstruction, mandibular reconstruction, and 3D bioprinting. Computer-assisted technology enables the ‘segmentation’ of targets for virtual surgical planning. The initial step in the process of 3D printing is the segmentation of the target. This involves the conversion of the target into a digital 3D image in stereolithography format through medical digital imaging and then presenting it on a computer. This allows for surgical simulation and quantitative analyses of the target’s form and function, enabling the assessment of potential surgical outcomes [22]. As all of these operations are displayed on the computer, it becomes possible for the surgical team and the patient’s family to communicate and select the optimal surgical method. Chun-Chi Hung et al. [23] utilized virtual surgical planning simulation in pelvic reconstruction. They manipulated segmented bones or fragments through simulation to determine the optimal repositioning approach and designed surgical equipment in advance. Subsequently, they printed a 1:1 pelvic model to pre-shape the fixation implants. The postoperative repositioning results were comparable to the imaging results of the conventional surgical approach (P > 0.05), and there was a significant reduction in internal fixation time, operative time, and blood loss (by 57 min, 70 min, and 274 ml, respectively; P < 0.05). Pelvic reconstruction is often a critical consideration following surgery for pelvic tumors. This is because the surgical treatment of pelvic tumors, which may involve the removal of the ilium, sacrum, and sacroiliac joints, can compromise the normal structure and continuity of the pelvis. If the integrity of the pelvic ring is disrupted, it may result in acetabular displacement. This displacement can, in turn, affect the normal functioning of the spine and lower limbs. Pelvic reconstructive surgery is an effective method of preventing the aforementioned complications in the postoperative period. However, the combined biological and internal fixation reconstructive methods currently in use have the potential to increase the duration of the operation and the risks associated with it. It is fortunate that a highly cited article published by Haijie Liang [14] states that 3D printed prostheses have been compared with a large number of imaging data of patients. These prostheses are modular and can be adjusted flexibly in terms of size, height, and angle to effectively cope with problems encountered in practice. They allow the implant to perfectly match the host bone without the need for intraoperative navigation, which can significantly reduce the time consumed by reconstructive surgery and lower the surgical risk. In addition to this, 3D printing technology allows precise adjustment of the porosity, connectivity, and geometry of biological scaffolds so that they can be better adapted to cell growth and tissue regeneration [24–27]. 3D-printed prostheses are not only suitable for pelvic reconstruction but are also useful after en bloc sacrectomy, where the mechanical environment is equally complex. Dr. Zhao-rui Lv’s study [28] shows that a novel total sacral implant manufactured by 3D printing technology with the features of suspended and modular. This design is well-suited to the intricate, highly intense mechanical environment of the lumbosacral region. It helps avoid nerve injury and excessive tension on the nerve roots, making it a sensible option for reconstruction following en bloc sacrectomy of a sacral giant cell tumor. Mandibular reconstructive surgery is also an area where 3D printing technology is often applied. Mandibular contour, angulation, variations in trabecular pattern, and the surrounding rich muscle attachments pose a great challenge for maxillofacial surgeons to perform mandibular reconstructive surgery. Fortunately, the development and application of virtual surgical planning and 3D printing technology are paving the way for a brighter future in mandibular reconstructive surgery. Adarsh Kudva et al. [29] performed reconstructive surgery on six patients with varying degrees of mandibular defects. With the help of virtual surgical planning and 3D printing models, they were able to identify the site of mandibular resection, the goal of the reconstruction, prepare the precise pre-bending or adaptation of the reconstructive plate, ensure the precise osteotomy of the fibula, the pre-positioning of the fibular end, and the individual design of the titanium implant before the surgery. This approach effectively reduced surgical time, intra-operative bleeding, and the incidence of postoperative complications, and ensured an optimal function and appearance.

In addition to surgical treatment of bone tumors, other concerns are the recurrence of bone tumors and bone regeneration. The advent of 3D printing technology in the medical field has led to a revolution in the preparation process of scaffolds. Bioceramic scaffolds, which possess the capacity to stimulate bone regeneration and eliminate residual bone tumour cells, are progressively supplanting traditional scaffolds that serve solely a supportive function [30]. The versatility of 3D printing technology enables the composition of bioceramic scaffolds to be adjusted in a flexible manner. This encompasses a range of materials, including calcium phosphate ceramics, calcium silicate ceramics, and bioactive glasses, among others. The properties of different compositions can be employed to facilitate the performance of diverse functions by bioceramic scaffolds. Moreover, the nanostructures of 3D-printed bioceramic scaffolds can similarly influence the capacity of bone to regenerate. A treatment method known as photothermal therapy effectively prevents the recurrence of residual tumor cells using 3D printing technology. The specific mechanism involves using scaffolds made of special materials, such as free carbon, to generate a photothermal effect that destroys tumor cells [31], for example, larnite embedded with free carbon (larnite/C) scaffolds with excellent photothermal conversion efficiency, can not only absorb near-infrared rays to inactivate the residual tumor cells after surgery [32–35], but also promote apatite formation and osteogenic gene expression by virtue of the bioactivity of larnite [36–39]. In an experimental animal study, researchers combined two-dimensional Ti3C2 MXene with 3D printed scaffolds to achieve the dual effects of photothermal effects to kill tumour cells and promote bone regeneration. This represents the inaugural instance of MXene-based nanoplatforms being employed in the field of tissue-engineering biomedical applications, a development with considerable potential for the future [40]. Hongshi Ma et al. [41] added a bifunctional graphene oxide coating to the surface of a 3D-printed β-tricalcium phosphate composite scaffold, thereby conferring upon the 3D-printed scaffold the capacity to exhibit a photothermal effect and to facilitate bone regeneration. This practice enriches the form of photothermal therapy. In the future, the combination of 3D printing technology and nanotechnology may prove to be a valuable tool in the development of photothermal therapies, facilitating the introduction of new ideas and approaches [42, 43]. Alginate is a common biomaterial in the field of 3D printing for the treatment of bone tumors and is used as a bioink for 3D-printed implants by cross-linking with divalent cation ions to form hydrogels [44]. Although alginate lacks the biological properties to promote bone formation and does not possess sufficient mechanical strength [45, 46], it is suitable as a carrier for delivering peptides such as bone-forming peptide-1 [47]. Combining alginate with other biomaterials can overcome the above drawbacks, for example, 3D printed scaffolds made by combining bioactive glass and alginate in a 2:4 mass ratio not only have greatly increased mechanical strength but also significantly enhance the ability of adsorption and proliferation of rat bone mesenchymal stem cells. As a result, alginate remains widely used in the field of bone regeneration [48].

It is worth noting that 3D printed organs represent a cutting-edge application within the field of 3D bioprinting, which is currently in the experimental phase. The feasibility of this technology has been confirmed in animal experiments, and it is anticipated that in the near future, 3D-printed organs will benefit patients whose cited tumors lead to organ removal [20].

Although 3D printing technology is playing an increasingly important role in the treatment of bone tumors, several considerations must be taken into account. These include the fact that 3D printing requires a certain amount of preparation time and material consumption, which can elevate the cost of the procedure and potentially delay surgery. Additionally, the quality and accuracy of the customized surgical tools and implants must undergo the necessary inspections and testing to ensure patient safety and procedural success. Furthermore, the application of 3D printing technology must consider the varying regulatory requirements across different countries.

Limitations

WoSCC is continuously updated, which could result in a lag in the articles included in this study. However, a small quantity of literature does not interfere too much with the current state and development trends of this domain.

Conclusion

This study examines the hotspots, trends, and research status of 3D printing in the domain of bone tumor treatment, and concludes that the hotspot directions are mainly the development of 3D printing materials, optimization of surgical methods, and discovery of assistive technologies in bone tumor surgery. However, to further develop the potential of 3D printing applications, multi-center international cooperation is necessary.

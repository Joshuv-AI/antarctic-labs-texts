Willingness to pay for herpes zoster vaccination in urban China
Authors: Yanran Wang, Ming Wang, Yiqi Xia, Dawei Zhu, Zhenyu Shi, Ping He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHerpes zoster imposes a substantial disease burden in China. Despite vaccination being critical for burden mitigation, willingness-to-pay (WTP) for herpes zoster vaccines among urban residents remains understudied, with a lack of national-level evidence on its determinants. A national-wide survey was conducted among 2,864 urban residents aged ≥25 years across nine provinces and nine cities in China. Using the contingent valuation method (CVM), three elicitation approaches – payment scales, bidding, and open-ended questions – were employed to assess respondents’ willingness-to-pay (WTP) for herpes zoster vaccination. Univariate statistical analyses were then performed to explore the associations between socioeconomic characteristics and WTP. Median WTP varied by methods (payment cards: CNY 300, bidding: CNY 500, open-ended: CNY 300). Across these methods, 36.09%, 29.06%, and 23.64% of participants, respectively, were unwilling to pay (including those rejecting free vaccination). For higher thresholds, 2.36% and 9.96% reported WTP ≥ 2,000 CNY via the payment scale and bidding methods, while 2.09% indicated WTP ≥ 2,000 CNY through the open-ended method. Age was negatively associated with WTP, and respondents from moderately developed regions had the highest WTP. Education and annual household income showed positive associations with WTP. Additionally, unemployment, chronic disease, divorced or widowed residents, and below-average self-reported health correlated with lower WTP. While respondents with public or commercial insurance had highest WTP. In conclusion, individual WTP for herpes zoster vaccine is substantially influenced by socioeconomic characteristics, which are inherently linked to income. This highlights the need for income-sensitive policies, including affordable pricing and targeted health education for vulnerable groups.KEYWORDS: Willingness to pay (WTP), herpes zoster (HZ) vaccine, infectious disease, vaccine hesitancy, payment card, bidding, open-ended

Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ) is an infectious disease that arises from the varicella-zoster virus which could be latent in the ganglia for a long time. Clinically, unilateral vesicular rashes are manifested, which are confined to a single skin area and typically accompanied by radicular pain. Currently, the global incidence rate of herpes zoster in the general population is 3–5 cases per 1,000 person-years, with the Asia-Pacific region reporting 3–10 cases per 1,000 person-years.1 This rate has been increasing annually by 2.5–5.0%2 and higher among elder group,3 arising from population aging, prevalence of chronic diseases and immunosuppressive drug, etc.4–7 In the United States, the aggregate costs of medical care and productivity losses associated with herpes zoster are estimated to exceed US$2 billion annually, coupled with a substantial psychosocial burden that results in over 60,000 Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) lost per year.8 While global data highlights HZ’s burden, regional disparities exist. A recent study conducted in China reported the estimated annual incidence of HZ is 1,563,000 new cases, with annual economic burdens exceeding CNY 1.3 billion.9 Hence, the severity of HZ imposes a significant disease burden on both the health care system and patients globally and in China.10

Currently, two main types of HZ vaccines are available globally: Zoster Vaccine Live (ZVL) and Recombinant Zoster Vaccine (RZV). ZVL, launched earlier and more widely adopted, requires a single dose. In contrast, RZV – introduced more recently – has been accepted in China and is self-funded since 2020, with no coverage under public health insurance or employer-sponsored plans across all age groups.11 It is recommended for adults aged over 50 and suggested two doses, representing the optimal strategy to mitigate substantial disease burden and demonstrate cost-effectiveness.12,13 However, HZ vaccine has yet to be widely promoted, with global uptake constrained by low willingness to pay (WTP), particularly among low-income populations.12 The growing incidence of HZ and increasing public attention highlights the urgency of understanding WTP for HZ vaccines to inform policy reforms.

WTP, often assessed via the contingent valuation method (CVM), refers to the maximum price a consumer is willing to pay for a good or service.14 The validity of CVM depends on the elicitation techniques used in questionnaires, as different approaches may introduce distinct biases, a topic of ongoing methodological debate.15,16 Common techniques include payment cards, bidding, and open-ended methods. As a key determinant of individuals’ vaccination decisions, WTP is shaped by multiple factors: socio-demographic and economic characteristics, prior vaccination history, knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to vaccination, recommendations from healthcare providers, social influence from peers, and personal values,14 which is highly related to vaccine hesitancy. As vaccine hesitancy influences vaccination decisions, an emerging study from China stated that individuals weigh disease-related costs (e.g., opportunity costs, monetary expenses, and disease-induced discomfort) against benefits (e.g., disease severity if infected, infection risk, and preventive effectiveness).17 Recognizing this, domestic studies have increasingly adopted CVM to assess vaccine WTP, emphasizing that it is influenced not only by individual and household payment capacity but also by knowledge of and attitudes toward the disease and its vaccine.

Despite the growing use of CVM in vaccine research, existing empirical studies from China on WTP have focused predominantly on HPV vaccines18 and hepatitis B vaccines.19 Emerging studies have identified main barriers to HZ vaccine decisions among older people, such as financial constraints and side-effects.20–23 The research on the HZ vaccine WTP remains limited among younger and middle-aged adults in China yet, given the significant increase in herpes zoster incidence and the considerable attention it has drawn. In this study, we aimed to provide the first national-wide survey to explore current status of HZ vaccine WTP and determinants of HZ vaccine WTP of urban residents aged 25 years and older.

Method

Data sources

We conduct a population-based national cross-sectional household survey among 25 years and older in nine cities, including Beijing, Shenzhen, Changchun, Wuhan, Zhengzhou, Shaoxing, Weifang, Nanning, Zunyi. This study designed a questionnaire by well-trained investigators through in-person one-on-one questionnaire-based interviews. The high-risk population for herpes HZ is identified as elderly individuals aged 50 years and above. Given that public awareness and coverage ratio of the herpes zoster vaccine remains relatively low in China, and that it will take time for the vaccine to be widely recognized and accepted by the general population. The assessment of the potential target population is equally important. Further, a current study mentioned that young adults in China typically play an important role in the health management of their elderly parents.24 Hence, this study set the age of participants as residents aged 25 years and above. The survey was from August to October 2022, patients attending community health service centers were enrolled as study participants via convenience sampling Inclusion criteria were defined as follows: being a resident of the study area or a migrant permanent resident; aged ≥25 years; medically eligible for vaccination; and having provided written informed consent to participate in the study. Exclusion criteria included individuals with hearing, reading, expressive, or cognitive comprehension impairments, as well as those with severe physical illnesses or psychiatric.

Sample cities were selected to account for China’s diverse geographical distribution, varying economic development tiers, and the context of the HZ vaccine’s relatively recent domestic launch – factors contributing to its low coverage in some regions, including Beijing (northern China) was included as a highly developed economic city; Weifang (eastern China) represented a moderately developed city; and Nanning, situated in the less developed and agricultural southwestern region (Guangxi Autonomous Region). Within each city, purposive sampling was employed: four communities were selected from sub-districts of varying socioeconomic development levels, following recommendations from local health administrative departments. In sum, our study included a total sample size of 2,864.

Measurement

In this study, WTP is defined as the maximum monetary amount an individual is accepted to pay for herpes zoster vaccination. To elicit stated preference, we employed a contingent valuation method (CVM), integrating three complementary techniques: (1) payment scales, (2) bidding, and (3) open-ended questioning.23 During the survey, respondents were randomly assigned one of two elicitation techniques: either the payment card method or the bidding method. For the bidding method, a random starting bid was provided to mitigate the influence of starting point bias. Regardless of which method (payment card or bidding) respondents completed, they were then required to answer an open-ended question to determine their maximum willingness to pay: “What is the maximum amount you would be willing to pay for a herpes zoster vaccination?” This hybrid design can mitigate inherent biases associated with using each individual method. To compare results across the three WTP elicitation techniques, we computed the mean, group differences, and correlations between outcomes of the three methods. Sample sizes for each method were as follows: 1,438 respondents for the payment scale method, 1,426 respondents for the bidding method, and 2,864 respondents for the open-ended method. The open-ended sample represented the full study population, as all participants completed the open-ended question irrespective of their initially assigned elicitation method (payment cards or bidding).

Prior to WTP elicitation, participants received standardized information on herpes zoster, including current price, vaccine efficacy, adverse event rates, dosing regimen, and target population. The current market price of imported herpes zoster vaccines in China is 1,600 CNY per dose, suggesting that an acceptable valuation range is 0–2,000 CNY. Both payment scales and bidding methods include 11 tiers: 0, 300, 500, 800, 900, 1,000, 1,200, 1,600, 2,000, and >2,000 CNY. Randomized participants are required to choose one price from predefined tiers that they are willing to pay in the payment scales group. Those indicating WTP > 2,000 CNY were prompted to specify the exact amount. For the bidding group, participants are given by a randomized starting point and asked to specify their willingness to pay, if the answer is “Yes,” then increasing the price and asking them again. Otherwise, lowering it. Regardless of the initial method, participants were ultimately asked to specify their maximum WTP via an open-ended question: “What is the maximum amount you would pay for herpes zoster vaccination?” Notably, participants who were unwilling to pay for the herpes zoster vaccination or rejected even when offered free to charge were classified into the 0 CNY group in both the payment cards and open-ended method analyses. In the bidding group, participants were categorized as unwilling to pay at 0 CNY, whereas those who rejected the vaccination despite offered free to charge were assigned to the Protests groups.

For all participants, we also collected sociodemographic characteristics, including gender, age, education level, residency, and annual family income, employment status, self-rated health, and types of medical insurances.

Statistical analysis

We performed univariate statistical analyses to assess factors that influence herpes zoster vaccination intention and WTP across subgroups stratified by individual characteristics and cognitive factors. Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Tests (α = 0.05) were used to test the differences of WTP between groups. All analysis was carried out in STATA 17.

Quality control

We implemented multiple quality control measures to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the data. First, the interviewers received rigorous training on the survey rules and were supervised during the survey. Second, statistical analyses were conducted daily throughout the survey, with a focus on response time and response rate. Third, after the survey, the recordings made with the participants’ consent were reviewed, and participants were randomly called to verify the accuracy of their answers.

Ethical approval

This herpes zoster-related sub-study received ethical approval (IRB00001052–20062) from the Institutional Review Board at Peking University.

Additionally, contrary to expectations based on regional economic conditions, moderately developed areas exhibited the highest willingness-to-pay (WTP) across all methods: price card (439.02 ± 433.03), bidding (690.11 ± 588.84), and open-ended (582.27 ± 953.80). This counterintuitive finding warrants further exploration of underlying mechanisms (e.g., healthcare resource accessibility, health literacy).

Furthermore, education level, employment status, family annual income, and insurance types were significantly associated with WTP disparities among urban residents. Specifically, participants with primary school education or below had the lowest WTP across all methods: price card (183.39 ± 281.61), bidding (363.31 ± 441.13), and open-ended (233.25 ± 543.86), indicating a positive relationship between education and WTP. Unemployed residents also demonstrated significantly lower WTP compared to employed groups. Then, a clear income-gradient effect was observed. The highest WTP was observed in the highest income bracket (≥120,000), with values across all methods as follows: price card (513.04 ± 473.94), bidding (781.39 ± 660.67), and open-ended (667.14 ± 937.89). Regarding insurance coverage, residents with public or commercial insurance had significantly higher WTP, highlighting the role of financial risk protection in shaping vaccination demand.

Lastly, according to health conditions, chronic diseases and self-health rated assessment were reported as two factors of WTP disparities among urban residents. Residents with chronic diseases and self-reported health condition below average level have the lowest WTP across three methods

Discussion

In contrast to the limited existing research on WTP for herpes zoster, this study explored the current status of WTP and identified factors influencing WTP disparities among urban residents in China by using payment scales, bidding, and open-ended methods, aiming to uncover their vaccination demand and health literacy. Regarding the observed WTP patterns, the price card and open-ended methods yielded the lowest mean WTP (300 CNY), while the bidding method resulted in a higher mean (500 CNY). The lower WTP means from the payment card method likely reflects its constraint of responses within predefined options, limiting how respondents express their WTP; though operationally simple, this method is prone to such range-based restrictions.25 Meanwhile, the bidding and open-ended methods likely afforded respondents greater flexibility to express their actual perceptions for herpes zoster vaccine’s value. However, open-ended questions are susceptible to high non-response rates, protest bids, and significant variability in WTP across respondents – factors that may also contribute to its lower mean (300 CNY). The bidding method, by comparison, better simulates real-market environments, enabling more accurate reflection of respondents’ preferences and thus yields more precise maximum WTP values.26 Nevertheless, this method is associated with starting point bias27 and is relatively difficult for respondents to comprehend. Additionally, the higher mean WTP from bidding is 500 CNY aligns with prior studies, which indicates that WTP measured using the bidding method is generally higher than that obtained via open-ended questions or the payment card method.23,28,29 Overall, the lower WTP mean of the price card and open-ended methods might reflect that residents were more inclined to report prices than they deemed reasonable or acceptable, if without the behavior of guiding price cues.

Additionally, the proportion of zero WTP indicated significant low acceptance of herpes zoster vaccine among urban residents. Specifically, zero WTP accounted for over 1/3 (36.09%) in the price card method sample, while resistance to vaccination and zero WTP comprised 14.10% and 24.96%, respectively, in the bidding method sample. The open-ended method also showed a 23.64% zero WTP rate. This suggests about over 1/5 of the total sample of urban residents are highly averse to paying for herpes zoster vaccination or reject to dose vaccination, implying they do not recognize the vaccine’s value.

For analyzing factors of WTP, this study found no significant gender disparities, which is in contrast with prior research.30 Elderly individuals and divorced/widowed populations exhibited lower WTP, potentially attributed to financial constraints or limited medical insurance coverage.31,32 Notably, residents of less developed areas also demonstrated lower WTP, this could be due to multifaceted factors, including income affordability, knowledge gaps regarding vaccine effectiveness, limited vaccine accessibility, and lower health literacy.33–36 After considering socioeconomic status, higher education level and income were significantly related to higher WTP. This may reflect greater willingness among these groups to invest in health prevention, supported by stronger financial capacity and health literacy.37–39 Conversely, unemployed individuals and those without medical insurance indicated lower WTP, likely due to financial constraints and perceived low priority of herpes zoster vaccination.40,41 Additionally, respondents with chronic diseases or below-average self-rated health status exhibited lower WTP, given that they prioritized managing existing health conditions over prevention. Further, emerging studies stated that vaccine hesitancy is related to multiple factors, including sociodemographic characteristics, cognitive factors, psychological factors, as well as political and cultural contexts globally.42,43 Additionally, recent evidence indicates that, in terms of sociodemographic factors, older adults, individuals with lower educational attainment, or those with lower income levels are less willing to receive the HZ vaccine. Perceived barriers – key reasons for such reluctance – include low trust in the HZ vaccine’s effectiveness, concerns about its safety, financial constraints, and unawareness of the vaccine’s availability.5,43 In summary, these findings can be attributed to vaccination hesitancy: participants with vaccination hesitancy exhibit lower WTP compared to those without vaccination hesitancy.44,45 Particularly, our findings indicated that the price is a critical determinant of vaccination intention, as the demand for vaccine increased significantly with decreasing prices. This trend represents a positive signal for promoting vaccination intention.46

However, our study may have several limitations. First, while the analysis utilized nationally representative survey data, the reliance on self-reported healthcare utilization over a retrospective 5-year period introduces potential recall bias, particularly for episodic or less severe conditions. Secondly, results may not generalize nationally due to convenience sampling. Further, selection bias exists as participants were recruited from community health centers. Lastly, using multiple elicitation methods on the same respondents could lead to order effects, including respondent fatigue or bias.

Conclusion

In summary, individual’s socioeconomic characteristics are largely interwind with income, emerging as pivotal determinants of WTP for herpes zoster vaccine. Hence, the design and promotion of herpes zoster vaccination programs should prioritize considerations of individuals’ affordability, incorporating reasonable pricing mechanism and target promotional strategies. Regarding to policy setting, this study suggests governments, the public health systems, and medical institutions strengthen public education and health literacy about herpes zoster and its vaccination through multimedia channels (e.g., the internet, television, newspaper). Particularly, emphasis should be placed on reaching vulnerable groups, including elderly individuals, low-income populations, and those with lower education levels.

Enhancing the sensitivity of lung adenocarcinoma to immune therapeutic agents through SPRED1
Authors: Chen Wu, Lingling Ma, Yi Wang, Joanna Bidzińska, Yilang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundProgrammed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), a classic immune checkpoint, is a key target for immunotherapy. Research has demonstrated that Sprouty-related EVH1 domain-containing 1 (SPRED1), a negative regulator of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, modulates PD-L1 expression and exhibits antitumor activity in diverse cancers. This study aimed to investigate the role and mechanisms of SPRED1 in enhancing the sensitivity of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) to immunotherapy and develop novel clinical therapeutic sensitization strategies.MethodsIn this study, bioinformatics technologies were employed to examine the interaction between SPRED1 and the survival prognosis of patients with LUAD and to identify key interacting molecules of SPRED1. The expression levels of SPRED1 in LUAD tissues were measured by tissue staining, Western blotting, and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to confirm the correlation between SPRED1 and the prognosis of patients with LUAD. Moreover, Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8), colony formation, wound healing, and Transwell assays were conducted to investigate the effects of SPRED1 on LUAD cell function and immunotherapy.ResultsBioinformatics analyses revealed that SPRED1 had a low expression in patients with LUAD and was associated with poor prognosis (P<0.05), suggesting its prominent role in LUAD. Tissue staining, Western blotting, and qRT-PCR demonstrated that SPRED1 was downregulated in LUAD tissues, and its low expression was significantly correlated with poor N stage and advanced pathological stage (P<0.05). Transwell, wound healing, and colony formation assays indicated that SPRED1 suppressed LUAD cell migration and proliferation. Furthermore, the CCK-8 assay confirmed that SPRED1 increases immunotherapeutic sensitivity.ConclusionsSPRED1 is downregulated in patients with LUAD and is an independent prognostic factor. SPRED1 enhances PD-L1 expression, thereby mediating its role in enhancing immunotherapeutic sensitivity in LUAD.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Sprouty-related EVH1 domain-containing 1 (SPRED1) is significantly downregulated in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) tissues and correlates with advanced N stage, pathological progression, and poor survival.

• SPRED1 overexpression inhibits LUAD cell migration/proliferation and enhances sensitivity to programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-targeted immunotherapy.

• SPRED1 upregulates PD-L1 expression and improves immune cell infiltration (e.g., CD8+ T cells).

• SPRED1 synergizes with anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) therapy to suppress tumor growth in vivo.

What is known and what is new?

• SPRED1 (a mitogen-activated protein kinase pathway suppressor) is downregulated in cancers and linked to tumor progression; PD-L1 guides non-small cell lung cancer immunotherapy.

• SPRED1 is a prognostic biomarker and immunosensitizer in LUAD. It mechanistically upregulates PD-L1 and enhances immune infiltration, offering a new combinatorial strategy.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• SPRED1 serves as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target to overcome immunotherapy resistance.

• Restore SPRED1 (e.g., via epigenetic modifiers) to boost PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor efficacy.

• Validate SPRED1-PD-L1 mechanisms (e.g., STAT3/NF-κB) in preclinical models and launch combination therapy trials.

Introduction

Background, research objectives, and significance

Lung cancer, the most prevalent malignant neoplasm worldwide, poses a significant threat to global public health. In China, it accounts for the highest proportion of new cases and deaths among malignancies, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) constituting the predominant subtype (1-3). Owing to the subtle early symptoms, the majority of patients are diagnosed at the middle or advanced stages, which significantly reduces the chances of long-term survival. Immunotherapy, particularly with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors, has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for patients with advanced NSCLC (4,5). These agents enhance the immune system’s cytotoxic activity against cancer cells by blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway, thereby counteracting the tumor’s immune evasion mechanisms (6). Nevertheless, the therapeutic efficacy of immunotherapy is influenced by tumor heterogeneity and individual variability, with notable responses primarily observed in patients exhibiting high PD-L1 expression (7). Therefore, clarifying the mechanisms and novel targets of NSCLC immunotherapy holds substantial promise for enhancing treatment outcomes.

Immunotherapy is a pivotal treatment for NSCLC, with immune checkpoint therapy targeting PD-1/PD-L1 being particularly prominent. PD-1, a coinhibitory receptor on various immune cells (B cells, natural killer T cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, dendritic cells, and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes), has two ligands: PD-L1 and PD-L2 (8-10). PD-L1 is expressed in hematopoietic cells (T cells, B cells, dendritic cells, macrophages, and mast cells) and tumor cells or stroma (11). PD-1/PD-L1 interaction transmits inhibitory signals to T cells, maintaining self-tolerance and preventing immune-mediated tissue damage (12). A recent study indicates that blocking PD-1/PD-L1 enhances antitumor immunity, leading to improved progression-free survival, higher response rates, longer response duration, and reduced risk of distant metastasis or death (13). In terms of mechanism, PD-1’s extracellular domain interaction with PD-L1 induces conformational changes and tyrosine phosphorylation in PD-1’s cytoplasmic domain. This reduces T-cell receptor phosphorylation and downstream signaling, thus inhibiting T-cell responses. The PD-1/PD-L1 pathway decreases cytokine (e.g., IFN-γ) and survival protein (e.g., Bcl-xL) production, leading to T-cell dysfunction or apoptosis (11,12,14). Therefore, identifying the mechanisms by which tumor-immune evasion can be overcome is crucial for improving patient prognosis.

We performed an immune correlation analysis using RNA-seq data and clinical information of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) patients and screened for Sprouty-related EVH1 domain-containing protein 1 (SPRED1). As a Sprouty/Spred family member, SPRED1, comprises an N-terminal EVH1 domain, a central c-kit binding domain, and a C-terminal Sprouty domain (15). Previous studies have demonstrated that SPRED1 is a critical negative regulator of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, and its downregulation has been observed in various malignancies (16-19). The overactivation of MAPK signaling contributes to tumor cell proliferation, survival, metastasis, and other tumor-promoting functions (20). SPRED1, a tumor-suppressor protein, regulates cellular Ras-GTP levels by binding to and relocating neurofibromin to the plasma membrane (21). The loss of SPRED1 in tumor tissues may lead to Ras signaling overactivation, promoting tumor growth and invasion (22). In melanoma, SPRED1 deficiency increases Ras activity in melanoma cells, making BRAF-driven melanoma resistant to MAPK inhibition (23). In oral squamous cell carcinoma, SPRED1 expression is significantly downregulated due to the upregulation of miR-182 targeting SPRED1 (24). Similarly, in estrogen receptor (ER)-positive breast cancer, the significant downregulation of SPRED1 is attributed to direct targeting by the upregulated miR-196A (25). SPRED1 and SPRED2 are downregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma as compared to noncancerous tissues (26). In all these cases, the downregulation of SPRED1 is associated with increased ERK phosphorylation.

The SPRED family’s expression in the tumor microenvironment indirectly influences tumor development and metastasis. It inhibits VEGF-A and VEGF-C signaling, along with angiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis (19). In one study, tumors in SPRED1-deficient mice grew faster than did those in wild-type mice due to increased neoangiogenesis (27). SPRED1 deficiency also enhanced the survival of effector CD8+ T cells, fostering more protective memory CD8+ T cells (28). Furthermore, SPRED1 was upregulated in CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in a TGF-β-dependent manner, with ERK activity decreasing by over 60% (29). These findings highlight the potential of the SPRED proteins family as a novel target for cancer immunotherapy.

The MAPK pathway is a known regulator of PD-L1 expression in cancers, and its hyperactivation contributes to immunotherapy resistance by promoting an immunosuppressive microenvironment. While SPRED1’s role as a MAPK suppressor is established, its direct link to PD-L1 in LUAD remains unexplored. Existing LUAD immunotherapy resistance stems from mechanisms such as low PD-L1 expression, impaired antigen presentation, and T-cell exhaustion. Our study bridges this gap by elucidating the SPRED1-MAPK-PD-L1 axis as a novel sensitization strategy. The research findings of this article emphasize the role of SPRED1 in tumor-immune evasion and reveal an interaction between SPRED1 and PD-L1, which potentially underlies SPRED1’s effect on immune evasion. However, research on this topic remains limited in the context of NSCLC. This study examined SPRED1 expression in LUAD cells, its impact on PD-L1, and the related mechanisms, in addition to the potential of SPRED1 combined with immunotherapy in cancer treatment. We present this article in accordance with the ARRIVE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2025-800/rc).

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

LUAD data were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), with tumor samples with complete information being selected for analysis. Data distribution analysis was conducted using the ggplot2 software package in R language. The median (Q2), quartiles (Q1/Q3), and interquartile range (IQR) values (Q3–Q1) were calculated to determine the outlier boundaries (Q3 + 1.5IQR and Q1–1.5IQR). The optimal cutoff value for SPRED1 was determined to be 2.9839 based on the Maxstat software package. Survival analysis was performed after grouping (log-rank test; P<0.01). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted to analyze Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment. SPRED1-related genes were retrieved from the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) and annotated for KEGG and Gene Ontology (GO) functions. The ESTIMATE algorithm was used to assess the tumor microenvironment score, and the correlation between SPRED1 and immune checkpoints was analyzed. Immuno-Oncology Biological Research (IOBR) was used to evaluate immune cell infiltration, and weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) was employed to construct a coexpression network and identify core gene modules. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Immunohistochemical staining

Paraffin-embedded NSCLC tissue sections (obtained from the hospital’s pathology archive) were deparaffinized, subjected to antigen retrieval, and treated to inactivate endogenous peroxidase. Sections were then incubated overnight with specific primary antibodies, followed by a chromogenic reaction using 3,3'-diaminobenzidine (DAB). Two pathologists independently evaluated staining intensity (score 0–4) and the percentage of positive cells (score 0–4). A combined score ≤6 was defined as low expression, whereas a score >6 was considered high.

Western blotting

Proteins were extracted from cells or tissues using a lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors. The samples were then sonicated and centrifuged for supernatant collection. Subsequently, a loading buffer was added, and the mixture was heated at 95 ℃ for denaturation. Protein separation was performed via electrophoresis on a prepared gel, and the proteins to were then transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane. After the membrane was blocked, it was incubated with primary antibodies overnight and then with secondary antibodies for 2 hours, after which a DAB chromogenic reaction was carried out.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

RNA was extracted via the TRIzol method and reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA). To analyze gene expression levels, qRT-PCR was carried out with a SYBR green kit.

Cell functional assays

CCK-8 cytotoxicity assay

Cells (5,000/well) were seeded into 96-well plates and cultured at 37 ℃ until 80% confluency. A mixture of 10 µL CCK-8 reagent and 90 µL culture medium was added to each well. After 2 hours of incubation, the cells were stained with crystal violet solution, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm wavelength.

Colony formation assay

Single-cell suspensions were seeded into six-well plates (400–1,000 cells/well) with agar-layered medium (lower layer: 1.2% agar + 2 × medium; middle layer: 0.7% agar + cell suspension). Cells were cultured at 37 ℃ until visible colonies formed. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 10–30 min, washed, and observed under a microscope.

Wound healing assay

Horizontal lines were pre-marked on the back of six-well plates. Cells were cultured for 24 hours. Vertical scratches were made using a sterile 200 µL pipette tip, and detached cells were removed by phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) washing. Serum-free medium was added. Microscopic images (40×) were captured at 0 and 24 hours to analyze the wound healing rate.

Animal experiments

C57BL/6 mice (Vital River Laboratory, Beijing, China) were provided ad libitum access to food and water. After SPRED1 was overexpressed in H1975 cells, 0.2 mL of cell suspension (2×106 cells/ml) was subcutaneously injected into C57BL/6 mice. Once tumors formed, anti-PD-1 (Bioss Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Woburn, MA, USA) was administered to assess its antitumor efficacy. Based on transfection and anti-PD-1 treatment, mice were divided into four groups: protruding clustered DNA (pcDNA)-negative control (pcDNA-NC) (n=4), pcDNA-SPRED1 (n=4), pcDNA-NC + anti-PD-1 (n=4), and pcDNA-SPRED1 + anti-PD-1 (n=4). Anti-PD-1 was intraperitoneally injected at 100 µL per mouse three times daily for 3 consecutive days. Tumor volume, tumor weight, and body weight were measured every 5 days, and the experiment concluded on day 30. A protocol was prepared before the study without registration. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Nantong University (No. S20240210-001), in compliance with national guidelines for the care and use of animals.

Statistical analysis

SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used to examine the correlation between SPRED1 expression and the clinical pathological parameters of patients with LUAD via the chi-squared test. GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (Dotmatics, Boston, MA, USA) generated grouped bar charts of the qRT-PCR results. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated using the median SPRED1 expression as the cutoff. Follow-up ranged from 0 to 120 months, with significance assessed by log-rank test (P<0.01).

Results

SPRED1 as a significant factor in LUAD

TCGA RNA-sequencing data for LUAD demonstrated significantly lower expression levels of SPRED1 in tumor tissues than in normal tissues (Figure 1A). High expression of SPRED1 was associated with a better prognosis among patients with LUAD, as indicated by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis (Figure 1B). Pathway GSEA indicated further involvement of SPRED1 within immune-related pathways, including natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity, apoptosis, complement system, NSCLC, and cell cycle (Figure 1C). GO enrichment analysis further showed that the coexpressed genes with SPRED1 were related to immune regulation and signal transduction (Figure 1D-1F), suggesting that SPRED1 enhances LUAD prognosis via immune-related mechanisms.

SPRED1 was associated with immune function in LUAD

TCGA data analysis indicated a positive correlation of SPRED1 expression with immune cell infiltration (Figure 2A). SPRED1 expression had a positive correlation with immune checkpoint genes such as CD274 (PD-L1), HAVCR2, PDCD1LG2, and SIGLEC15 (Figure 2B). In addition, correlation coefficients indicated SPRED1 expression was strongly associated with macrophage, dendritic cell, neutrophil, CD4+ T-cell, and CD8+ T-cell infiltration (Figure 2C), which indicates that a high expression of SPRED1 improves the tumor immune microenvironment and increases immunotherapy sensitivity.

SPRED1 was downregulated in LUAD tissues

Western blot analysis and qRT-PCR revealed that SPRED1 expression was significantly lower in LUAD tissues than in noncancerous adjacent tissues (Figure 3A,3B). SPRED1 downregulation was further confirmed by immunohistochemical staining, with lower expression directly corresponding to poorer N stage and advanced pathological stage (Figure 3C, Table 1, P<0.05).

PD-L1 facilitated SPRED1’s enhancement of LUAD’s sensitivity to immunotherapy

To investigate the specific mechanism by which SPRED1 affects the sensitivity of LUAD immunotherapy (Figure 4A), this study analyzed differentially expressed genes in LUAD from previous TCGA data and conducted WGCNA analysis to identify key genes that interact with SPRED1. WGCNA analysis divided LUAD differentially expressed genes into 10 groups, among which the brown group genes were most correlated with SPRED1 expression (Figure 4B). Therefore, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed for the brown group genes and further screened (Figure 4C). Subsequently, the key genes obtained from WGCNA were intersected with immune-related genes to screen for the gene PD-L1, which is most relevant to SPRED1 (Figure 4D,4E). The validation results of Kaplan-Meier plotter database indicate that the prediction of prognosis of based on the PD-L1 levels is significant (Figure 4F, P<0.05). To verify the interaction between SPRED1-PD-L1, the research team conducted immunoprecipitation experiments, and the results confirmed that there is an interaction between SPRED1 and PD-L1 (Figure 4G). The above experimental results indicate that PD-L1 is a key molecule interacting with SPRED1 affecting the sensitivity of LUAD immunotherapy.

Lentivirus-mediated overexpression of SPRED1

To verify the function of SPRED1 in LUAD, H1975 cells were transduced with a lentivirus SPRED1 (LV-SPRED1). The overexpression of SPRED1 was verified via immunofluorescence and qRT-PCR (Figure 5A-5C). Western blotting showed that the overexpressing SPRED1 (OE-SPRED1) group had upregulated PD-L1 levels as compared to the control group (Figure 5D), further supporting the role of SPRED1 in regulating PD-L1.

SPRED1 overexpression suppressed LUAD cell function

Functional assays also indicated that SPRED1 overexpression inhibited the invasiveness and migration of LUAD cells (Figure 6A,6B). The findings of the colony formation assays further supported the suppressive effect of SPRED1 on LUAD cell proliferation (Figure 6C). To assess how SPRED1 affects immunotherapy, the cells were treated with anti-PD-L1. The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay revealed ~40% reduction in cell viability in OE-SPRED1 cells treated with anti-PD-L1 vs. controls (P<0.01, Figure 6D). SPRED1 overexpression suppressed proliferation/migration (Figure 6A-6C) and synergized with anti-PD-L1 to reduce viability (Figure 6D), indicating that SPRED1 enhances immunotherapy sensitivity by impairing tumor cell fitness.

SPRED1 overexpression inhibited tumor growth in mice

To assess the tumor-suppressive effect of SPRED1 in vivo, LUAD cells overexpressing SPRED1 were injected subcutaneously into C57BL/6 mice (Figure 7A). The pcDNA-SPRED1 tumors were much smaller than those of the pcDNA-NC controls. Mice in the pcDNA-SPRED1 + anti-PD-1 group exhibited a massively reduced tumor volume as compared to the pcDNA-NC + anti-PD-1 group (Figure 7B,7C). This confirmed that the overexpression of SPRED1 inhibits tumor growth and enhances the activity of anti-PD-1 therapy.

Discussion

NSCLC, with the highest incidence and mortality among malignancies in the world, poses a significant threat to human health and is a major public health concern (2,3,30). Recent advances in immunotherapies such as therapeutic antibodies, cancer vaccines, and adoptive T-cell therapy have demonstrated remarkable potential in cancer treatment (31). However, the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy is often hampered by preestablished immune-suppressive mechanisms in the tumor microenvironment, with immunoevasion in lung cancer garnering heightened research interest (32). The primary mechanism involves cancer cells evading immunity through surface antigen alterations, abnormal major histocompatibility class (MHC)-I molecule and co-stimulatory molecule expression, dendritic cell dysfunction, and high FasL cytokine expression (33,34). Comprehensive treatment strategies, including chemotherapy and targeted therapy, aim to create a treatment-friendly immune environment. These therapies have significantly improved the quality of life for certain patients with NSCLC and specific genetic mutations, and the development of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors has offered reasons for optimism in treatment (35). Several chemotherapeutic drugs have immune-modulating effects, with synergistic antitumor effects being demonstrated in chemoimmunotherapy (36). However, immune therapies are used clinically only for advanced lung cancer or as a last resort when other treatments fail, yet evidence suggests that immunotherapy can also benefit patients with early-stage lung cancer (37).

Our study found a positive correlation between SPRED1 and PD-L1 in lung cancer cell lines. A meta-analysis indicated that patients with PD-L1-positive NSCLC have relatively poor survival rates (38), and it has been found that high PD-L1 expression inhibits tumor-infiltrating dendritic cells, leading to adverse prognoses (39). However, high PD-L1 expression offers an immunotherapeutic target, as PD-1/PD-L1-based therapies have demonstrated antitumor activity in patients with NSCLC (40). Moreover, tumors with high SPRED1 expression exhibit significant CD8+ T-cell infiltration (29), which is potentially linked to growth factors, IFN-γ, and peripheral blood mononuclear cell suppression (41). SPRED1-mediated inhibition of proliferation and migration likely remodels the tumor microenvironment, facilitating T-cell infiltration and improving immunotherapy efficacy, necessitating further research in this area. SPRED1 is regarded as a tumor suppressor due to its being downregulated in most human cancers and its role as a key negative regulator of the Ras/Raf/ERK pathway, which is often activated in cancers, promoting tumorigenesis and metastasis (20). MAPK pathway mutations occur in approximately 30% of cancer cases (29). Next-generation sequencing of primary lung tumors has identified mutations or amplifications in at least eight MAPK pathway genes in mice (42), and SPRED1 downregulates MAPK signaling by interacting with neurofibromin (21,43,44), suggesting its potential as a prognostic marker for some cancers. In some cases, SPRED1 downregulation results from aberrant promoter hypermethylation or specific microRNA (miRNA) degradation, such as that of miR-21 and miR-126 (20). Therefore, therapies employing epigenetic modifiers such as 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine and trichostatin A, or miRNA-targeting approaches, should be explored to restore SPRED1 expression; however, further research is needed to determine the exact means by which it can be restored. Additionally, SPRED1 expression in CD8+ T cells may suppress antitumor immunity (29,30,44). Thus, if SPRED1 is applied for cancer therapy, these factors must be considered to prevent unintended immunotherapy outcomes.

Conclusions

Our study revealed that SPRED1 may enhance LUAD immunotherapy sensitivity, with its key mechanism possibly being related to PD-L1 upregulation. SPRED1 could be a potential target for boosting LUAD immunotherapy and a significant biomarker for the prognosis of patients with LUAD. Kaplan-Meier analysis suggests high SPRED1 expression correlates with better survival, supporting its role as a potential predictive biomarker for immunotherapy response. Future studies should validate SPRED1’s utility in cohorts receiving PD-1 inhibitors.

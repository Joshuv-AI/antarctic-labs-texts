Impact of early tracheostomy in infratentorial lesions trial: rationale and design
Authors: Song Han, Meng-Ya Li, Yuan-chao Cheng, Chang-xiang Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTracheostomy is a common procedure in patients who undergo surgical resection of infratentorial lesions. However, the optimal timing and high-risk factors for tracheostomy remain unknown.MethodsValue of Early versus Standard Tracheostomy in patients with Infratentorial Lesions (VENTIL) is a prospective, randomized, controlled, open-blinded endpoint (PROBE design) trial. Patients who have undergone resection of infratentorial lesions will be recruited within 48 h postoperatively based on a prediction score. This study will include 152 patients. Patients will be randomized 1:1 to either the early tracheostomy (< 2 days after surgery) or the control group (≥ 2 days after surgery). The primary endpoint is the occurrence of pneumonia by day 14 after resection of infratentorial lesions. The secondary endpoints include the modified Rankin scale score at 6 months, mortality, length of intensive care unit and hospital stays, time to successful extubation, and adverse events.ConclusionHigh-risk factors and optimal timing for tracheostomy in patients undergoing resection of infratentorial lesions need to be identified. Therefore, VENTIL should clarify the clinical benefits of early tracheostomy in these patients.Trial registrationThis trial is registered on February 5, 2024, at chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2400080714).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-024-08682-w.

Introduction and rationale

Tracheostomy is an operative intervention for critically ill patients requiring prolonged ventilator assistance. It has been shown to facilitate weaning from a ventilator, lower the incidence of pneumonia, shorten stays in the intensive care unit (ICU) and hospital, and reduce the need for sedatives [1, 2]. However, patients with neurological disorders may experience decreased consciousness, dysphagia, or respiratory compromise. Questions on selecting the optimal timing and appropriate neurological patients for tracheostomy are even more difficult to answer.

The SETPOINT2 study was a multicenter, prospective, randomized, observer-blinded, controlled trial that included 382 patients with severe stroke [3]. Early tracheostomy did not improve the neurological outcome at 6 months compared with standard tracheostomy. One possible explanation for these negative results is the failure to classify patients based on surgical location. Tracheostomy is also important for airway protection, especially in patients with stroke who have impaired swallowing and cough reflexes, which are primarily controlled by the brainstem [4]. Consequently, this indicates a potentially high need for early tracheostomy after resection of infratentorial lesions.

Patients undergoing surgical resection of infratentorial lesions (e.g., those located in the brainstem and cerebellum) experience respiratory complications [3]. Possible explanations for the increased risk of respiratory compromise include damage to the medullary respiratory center, posterior cranial nerves involved in the generation of protective airway reflexes, and reticular activating pathways that impair consciousness with the secondary diminution of protective airway reflexes [4]. Consequently, airway management is critical to improve the clinical outcomes of patients who undergo resection of infratentorial lesions.

Tracheostomy is one of the most effective airway management methods for reducing airway dead space, decreasing oropharyngeal lesions, and reducing the need for sedatives [5]. Tracheostomy is more prevalent in patients who undergo resection of infratentorial lesions, as it can reduce the incidence of pneumonia, shorten ICU stay, and facilitate earlier decannulation [6]. However, tracheostomy is not risk-free, and not all patients with infratentorial lesions require tracheostomy. Moreover, the debate surrounding the optimal timing and patient population for tracheostomy remains active.

There were four retrospective studies focused on the tracheostomy timing in patients with infratentorial lesions (Table 1) [4, 6–8]. These results show the benefits of early tracheostomy in patients with infratentorial lesions in reducing the occurrence of pneumonia, shortening ICU stays, and facilitating earlier decannulation [6]. Design

The Value of Early versus Standard Tracheostomy in patients with Infratentorial Lesions (VENTIL) is a prospective, randomized, controlled, outcome observer-blinded, multicenter, two-armed comparative trial. A total of 170 eligible participants will be recruited from Sanbo Brain Hospital, Capital Medical University. Patients are randomized 1:1 to either the experimental group who undergo tracheostomy within 2 days after surgery (“early tracheostomy”) or to the control group (“standard of care” group), in which tracheostomy is performed ≥ 2 days after surgery. The procedures for screening of participants, randomization, surgery, follow-up, and sample collection are shown in Table 3. Blinding is impossible for physicians, patients, and legal representatives as well as for most of the investigators. However, the modified Rankin scale (mRS) and cause of mortality endpoints will be assessed by care team-independent adjudicators blinded to the timing of the tracheostomy. The study will be conducted according to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Withdrawal of participants

Researchers will inform participants at the time of enrollment that they are free to withdraw from the study at any stage, for any reason, without penalty. A withdrawal statement will be provided and signed.

Procedure

The study procedure is described by following the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) checklist (Supplementary file 1).

Recruitment

The clinical research coordinator (CRC) will verbally inform patients that the hospital is currently conducting a clinical trial aimed at enhancing postoperative outcomes. Should they fully comprehend and be willing to participate in the study, they will personally visit the hospital to meet with the CRC and sign an informed consent form (Supplementary file 2). The CRC will collect essential information using a standardized questionnaire, encompassing sociodemographic characteristics and clinical history, which will be documented in an online electronic data collection system.

Sample collection

Following surgery, 3 ml of peripheral blood will be collected from patients at five distinct time points: days 1, 5, 8, 11, 14, and 21. This peripheral venous blood will be gathered in single-use vacuum tubes that are specially treated to promote coagulation and will be analyzed for hematological and biochemical parameters.

Rationale for identifying patients at high risk for tracheostomy

In patients with stroke, the SET score was developed to predict tracheostomy and is associated with length of stay (LOS) in the ICU and prolonged ventilation time [9]. It consists of three parts: neurological function (e.g., dysphagia, observed aspiration, and Glasgow Coma Scale [GCS] score), lesion (e.g., brainstem etc.), and general organ function (neurosurgical intervention, additional respiratory disease, etc.). With the previous studies investigating the tracheostomy after resection of infratentorial lesions, the risk factors were included in our study, which include neurological function, neurosurgery, and general health. A VENTIL scoring system was developed and is shown in Table 4. When the VENTIL score ≥ 6 points, a tracheostomy is indicated. Rationale for early tracheostomy within 48 h post-surgery

In patients undergoing surgical resection of infratentorial lesions, early tracheostomy varies from 1 ~ 3 to 10 days after operation, whereas late tracheostomy occurs after 14 days (Table 1) [4, 6–8]. Patients undergoing resection of infratentorial lesions are largely conscious but may suffer from severe functional deficits in coughing or swallowing. Therefore, the timing of tracheostomy should focus on the recovery of the brainstem and posterior cranial nerves. Because we will include patients at high risk for tracheostomy, we proposed early tracheostomy as 48 h post-surgery.

Rationale of primary outcome assessments

A recent meta-analysis including more than 17,000 critically ill patients with severe stroke analyzed the timing of tracheostomy and mortality, ICU or hospital LOS, and neurological outcomes (mRS) [10]. However, the study failed to achieve all the above endpoints and proved that tracheostomy timing was not correlated with mortality. One plausible reason for these negative results is that stroke itself is one of the most dominant prognostic factors, and use of tracheostomy might be minimal. Tracheostomy was only effective in preventing respiratory complications in patients who underwent resection of infratentorial lesions. Mortality and functional outcomes are affected more by the tumor itself and surgery [11]. Therefore, a larger population is needed to detect differences in tracheostomies and improve mortality and functional outcomes.

Randomization and treatment

The screening period lasts for approximately 2 weeks, and the surgery day is regarded as D0. All potential patients will be examined using the inclusion and exclusion criteria after signing an informed consent form. Eligible subjects will receive a random number in the order of enrollment in this study and will be randomly stratified by the participating centers into the early tracheostomy or control groups. Randomization will be performed using a random envelope to obtain comparable treatment groups. Block randomization will be applied to stratify the participating centers and achieve equal group sizes per center. The random number consists of three digits. For example, the random number of the first enrolled subject is 001.

Tracheostomy is performed in the “early tracheostomy” group as soon as feasible and always within 2 days after surgery. Patients in the control group will receive all the usual and aggressive efforts to be weaned from mechanical ventilation. If extubation trials fail, these patients will be to be tracheostomized after ≥ 2 days. Tracheostomies will be limited to percutaneous tracheostomies. Percutaneous tracheostomy has advantages over open tracheostomy in terms of complication rates and cost, with comparable overall outcomes [12].

Sample size

The overall sample size was calculated using PASS 15.0, based on the primary endpoint (occurrence of pneumonia) comparison between the two arms. A previous study showed pneumonia incidences of 68.8% and 87.2% in the early and late tracheostomy arms, respectively, with an odds ratio (OR) of 1.27 [6]. The sample size will be determined at an overall two-sided level α = 0.05 and with power 1-β = 0.80 at the above specified alternative when applying a chi-square test. Based on the above assumptions, 152 subjects are required to detect an OR of 1.27 with 80% power. To enable a 10% dropout rate, an estimated 170 subjects (i.e., 85 subjects in each arm) need to be enrolled.

Data collection

At baseline, demographic characteristics, medical history, CT scans, and laboratory tests will be acquired. Postoperatively, at five time points (days 1, 5, 8, 11, 14, and 21), the data collector (YC) will record each patient’s vital signs, chest CT outcomes, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores, laboratory test results, medication usage, and adverse events. At 3 months and 6 months after surgery, adverse events and modified Rankin scale (mRS) scores will be documented. Primary and secondary outcomes

The primary outcome of the study was the incidence of pneumonia at day 14 in the early tracheotomy group and the control group (Table 2). The pneumonia was defined as at least two of the following: (1) symptom (e.g., phlegm), (2) fever, (3) elevated WBC and neutrophils. Additionally, the pneumonia must be diagnosed based on chest CT which indicated large high-density lesion. The secondary outcome of this study is to explore the efficacy of early tracheostomy compared to controls and assess the safety of early tracheostomy in patients undergoing surgical resection of infratentorial lesions. Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses will be undertaken by statisticians who will participate in concept development, protocol design, study implementation, data management, analysis, and summary of research results. The statistical analysis plan will be formulated after the completion of the research protocol and case report forms, and the statistical analysis report will be completed at the end of the trial.

Continuous variables will be described as mean, standard deviation (SD), median, minimum, maximum, lower quartile (Q1), and upper quartile (Q3). Categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and proportions. Means will be compared using 2-sided independent t-test (meeting homogeneity of variance and normal distribution) and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Proportions will be compared using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. The number of enrollments, completions, dropout rates, and reasons for withdrawal will be summarized by treatment group and center. The primary aim of the trial is to compare early tracheostomy within 2 days after surgery (experimental treatment) and standard tracheostomy within 2 days after surgery (control) with respect to the occurrence of pneumonia by day 14 after resection of infratentorial lesions. The related null hypothesis states that there is no difference in the occurrence of pneumonia between the treatment groups. An alternative hypothesis is that early tracheostomy could improve clinical outcome in patients with infratentorial lesions. The group differences and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) will be calculated for the primary endpoint. If the upper limit of the 95% CI is less than zero, the null hypothesis will be rejected, and early tracheostomy will be assumed to improve clinical outcomes. Appropriate descriptive and analytical methods will be selected based on the characteristics of secondary and safety endpoints.

Each patient’s allocation to the different analysis populations (full analysis set [FAS], per-protocol [PP] analysis set, and safety [SS] analysis set) will be defined prior to the analysis. This allocation will be documented in the statistical analysis plan. During the data review, deviations from the protocol will be assessed as “minor” or “major.” The FAS is defined according to the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle and will include all randomized patients. Major deviations from the protocol will lead to the exclusion of FAS patients from the PP analysis set. The safety analysis set includes all patients treated with one of the interventions studied in this trial. The primary efficacy analysis will be performed based on data from the FAS and PPS and the demographics at baseline; the secondary outcomes analysis will be performed based on the FAS, and the safety analysis will be performed based on the SS.

SAS (9.4 or later) will be used for statistical analysis. All statistical tests will be two-sided, and a P value less than 0.05 will be considered statistically significant (unless otherwise noted).

Data management and confidentiality

All paper materials will be securely stored and locked in designated cabinets, while electronic data will be preserved by a dedicated researcher (YC). Data entry will be conducted by two laboratory personnel (YC and SH). Following confirmation or review of their clinical records with participants, a researcher (YC) will inspect and correct any erroneously entered data. Any modifications to the original data will be meticulously tracked. Participants’ personal information will remain anonymous and securely stored throughout the research process. All staff involved in this study are obligated to maintain strict confidentiality of information at all times. There are no plans to use the data collected as part of this trial for additional research.

Monitoring

To ensure the smooth execution and efficient management of the trial, a project management team has been established to oversee its daily operations and evaluations. This team holds regular meetings, at least once a month, to review the trial’s progress, assess the quality of data collection, and ensure that activities are carried out according to the predetermined plan and timeline. Furthermore, the project management team will closely monitor any emerging issues during the trial and propose timely solutions to ensure its uninterrupted progression.

The steering committee, composed of experienced clinicians, researchers, and ethicists, is responsible for overseeing the scientific integrity, ethical conduct, and overall execution of the trial. The committee meets quarterly to review the trial’s overall progress, including the implementation of the study design, data quality, patient safety, and other key parameters. Based on these reviews, the committee will recommend any necessary adjustments.

The data monitoring committee (DMC) operates independently of the research team and project management group. It conducts regular reviews of the trial data to ensure both safety and efficacy. The DMC meets at least twice a year, focusing on the review of adverse events, patient safety data, and other relevant information, evaluating the trial’s risks and benefits. The DMC has the authority to suspend or terminate the trial, particularly if safety concerns arise. Additionally, the DMC can convene special meetings as needed to address emergent safety issues or other critical concerns.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Sanbo Brain Hospital, Capital Medical University (9 January 2024, SBNK-YJ-2023–033-01, Supplementary file 3). All study designs will comply with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All individuals will sign written informed consent forms before participating in this study.

Protocol amendments

During the course of the VENTIL study, any amendments or revisions to the protocol will follow a strict notification and approval process. The procedure is as follows: all protocol amendments will be communicated to the sponsor and funders prior to implementation. Once the necessary approvals are obtained, the principal investigator (PI), CY, will ensure that the revised protocol is distributed in writing to all study personnel, ensuring timely updates for all team members. A copy of the updated protocol will also be added to the Investigator Site File (ISF).

If any deviations from the protocol occur during the study, researchers must promptly document and report them using the Non-Compliance Report form. All non-compliant actions will be thoroughly documented and analyzed to ensure the trial remains compliant, with appropriate corrective measures taken as needed.

The revised protocol will be uploaded to the clinical trial registry platform and made publicly available in a timely manner. All amendments will be clearly indicated in the registry to ensure transparency and public access to the updated information.

These measures ensure that all protocol amendments are properly communicated and approved, while the study remains in full compliance with ethical and regulatory requirements.

Dissemination

Meetings will be convened regularly to ensure quality control and facilitate progress. The completion of the experiment and the dissemination of its outcomes will be credited to all collaborators. The definitive results of the experiment will be reported to each participant through open-access publications.

Patient and public involvement

In the design and implementation of this study, patient and public involvement was prioritized to ensure that the research remained patient-centered and socially relevant. Several patient representatives were invited to participate in the early stages of the study design, providing feedback on the study’s objectives, methods, and focus through focus group discussions and surveys. Additionally, collaboration with community organizations and public representatives helped to introduce the study’s background and goals to the broader community, soliciting their input to enhance research transparency and ensure the study was ethically sound and socially responsible.

During the implementation phase, patients will continue to be involved through ongoing feedback mechanisms, providing insights on treatment protocols and the effectiveness of interventions. This feedback ensures that the interventions remain acceptable and clinically effective. Ultimately, the research findings will be disseminated to both patients and the public through educational activities and public lectures, helping them to better understand the results and their potential implications for personal health.

Key groups involved in the trial and their roles

The successful execution of the trial relies on the coordinated efforts of several key groups, each with specific roles and responsibilities. These groups will work together to ensure the trial is conducted with scientific rigor, adherence to ethical standards, and proper logistical management.

Coordinating center

The coordinating center will oversee the day-to-day operations of the trial, providing logistical and administrative support. It will be responsible for organizing trial activities, maintaining communication with all trial sites, and ensuring compliance with the trial protocol. The coordinating center will also manage the data collection and ensure timely reporting of trial progress. This center will meet monthly to review operational aspects and address any logistical issues that arise.

Trial steering committee (TSC)

The trial steering committee will be composed of clinical experts, senior researchers, and trial management personnel. The TSC will provide overall oversight of the trial, ensuring it is conducted in line with the study objectives and maintaining its scientific integrity. This committee will meet quarterly to assess trial progress, review data quality, and evaluate patient safety. The TSC will make decisions on any protocol amendments, assess risks, and recommend necessary changes.

Data monitoring and ethics committee (DMEC)

The DMEC will operate independently from the trial management team and will be responsible for monitoring the safety of participants throughout the trial. The committee will review adverse events, evaluate data for signs of potential harm, and ensure participant safety. The DMEC will meet every 6 months or more frequently if required, to assess safety data and provide recommendations on whether the trial should continue or be paused.

Project management group (PMG)

The project management group will consist of the trial manager, research coordinators, and other administrative staff. This group will meet monthly to review the trial’s progress, monitor the recruitment process, and ensure that milestones are met. They will also address any emerging challenges and implement solutions to maintain the trial’s timeline.

Each of these groups will collaborate closely to ensure that the trial is conducted ethically, with scientific rigor, and in a timely manner, fulfilling all objectives while safeguarding participant welfare.

Discussion

Lesions in or near the brainstem contribute to a high risk of respiratory compromise. Respiratory compromise is caused by damage to the medullary respiratory center, posterior cranial nerves involved in the generation of protective airway reflexes, and reticular activating pathways that impair consciousness with secondary diminution of protective airway reflexes. Therefore, optimizing airway management is important for patients with infratentorial lesions. The most important question in developing an effective management strategy is the timing of tracheostomy in individual patients.

There were four retrospective studies focused on the tracheostomy timing in patients with infratentorial lesions (Table 1) [4, 6–8]. These results show the benefits of early tracheostomy in patients with infratentorial lesions in reducing the occurrence of pneumonia, shorter ICU stays, and earlier decannulation [6]. Thus, a prospective study is required to demonstrate the benefits of a more aggressive tracheostomy approach in patients with infratentorial lesions.

Our approach selects patients at high risk for tracheostomy using a pre-specified score that considers neurological function, neurosurgery, and general health. Furthermore, we selected 48 h postoperatively as the time of tracheostomy for these patients. The combination of tracheostomy timing and patient selection is expected to improve the clinical efficacy in patients with infratentorial lesions in this trial.

At the time of manuscript submission, the study is in the preparation phase for recruitment. This is protocol version 1.0. completed on 29 April 2024. Recruitment began on May 1, 2024, and will be completed by June 30, 2024.

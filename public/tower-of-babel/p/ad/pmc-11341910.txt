Long-term organic and N fertilization influence the quality and productivity of pearl millet under pearl millet-wheat sequence in north India
Authors: Sunita Sheoran, Dhram Prakash, Dev Raj, Virender Singh Mor, Parmod Kumar Yadav, Rajeev Kumar Gupta, Saud Alamri, Manzer H Siddiqui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present investigation reported that FYM application in different seasons influenced root, shoot, and seedling length, straw K, vigour index-I, nutrient uptake, grain, and stover yield of pearl millet significantly (P < 0.05) and followed the order: both seasons > kharif > rabi. Applying FYM in both seasons resulted in higher N, P, and K content in pearl millet grain (1.99%, 0.17%, and 0.37%, respectively) followed by kharif season application (1.93, 0.16, and 0.35%, respectively). Applying 15 t FYM ha−1 significantly increased the grain N (13.19%), P (63.16%), K (22.29%), protein (13.56%), stover N (32.76%), P (46.66%) and root length (29.83%) over FYM0. After 50 cropping cycles, continuous application of FYM15, FYM10, and FYM5 significantly improved vigour index-I by 52.85, 39.26, and 23.63% over no FYM, respectively. Applying 120 kg N ha−1 significantly increased N (6.38%), P (15.89%), and protein (6.03%) content, germination (5.91%), and vigour indexes (24.52 to 30.91%) of pearl millet grain over no fertilizer N. The treatment FYM15 × N120 increased the seedling length of pearl millet by 30.54 over N120 and 11.08% over FYM15 alone, respectively. Adding FYM either during both seasons or in the kharif season along with fertilizer N proved superior in improving the quality and yield of pearl millet.Keywords: Long-term, Modes, Farmyard manure, Fertilizer nitrogen, Grain quality, ProductivitySubject terms: Environmental impact, Sustainability

Introduction

Agricultural sustainability is highly vulnerable to climate change, particularly in semi-arid and arid regions, and unpleasant situations are expected to intensify the challenges for soil health management and quality parameters of crops. The challenges of crop production are further becoming complex due to dependence on rainfall and decreased availability of water coupled with human activities, i.e. erratic soil health management covering deterioration in soil flora and fauna in these areas1,2. Cereal crops are highly cultivated in comparison to other crops worldwide and has significant value since the Green Revolution. The capability of pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum L.) to resist high temperatures and harsh climatic conditions helps to improve its quality performance compared to other crops. Furthermore, the availability of high-yielding and short-duration hybrids as well as increased minimum support price (MSP) of pearl millet helped in so popularization of the crop in low to medium-fertile soil during the last several years3. Pearl millet can be cultivated on various soil types in tropical and subtropical climates. It is grown in India, Pakistan, China, and other South Asian countries4,5. In India, pearl millet ranks 4th (9.4 million ha) among cereals in the area after rice, wheat, and sorghum6,7 and accounts for 10.7% of total food grain production with an annual production of 10.1 million tones (average productivity of 1069 kg ha−1)8–10. India held the top spot in the globe in 2021–2022 in terms of area, output, and productivity11. Over 90% of the pearl millet acreage in the region is located in the major pearl millet-growing states of India, which include Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, and Haryana. The production and productivity of pearl millet are restricted by the typically low soil fertility seen in semi-arid parts of India, especially nitrogen and micronutrients12. This millet is a rich source of protein, fat, vitamins (beta-carotene, riboflavin, minerals, and antioxidants such as phenolic acids, carotenoids, and phenols; cheap food for human beings and feed for poultry birds as well as green fodder for cattle5,13.

Pearl millet- wheat is one of India's most clamant cropping systems in arid and semi-arid eco-regions. This system is also considered one of the major cropping systems of Haryana and has been adopted in 20.87 lakh hectares of the state14. With the adoption of modern agricultural techniques involving increased use of agrochemicals (i.e. fertilizers and insecticides) and high-yielding varieties of field crops, the country has achieved self-sufficiency in food. The previous several years have seen a significant increase in soil exploitation and degradation due to the adoption of this nutrient-exhaustive cropping scheme, which has significantly reduced production. If the same management practices are allowed to continue for the next decades, today's productive land may likely turn into unproductive barren land. Food security may be at risk due to the loss of soil fertility brought on by crops that continuously deplete nutrients without receiving enough replenishment, particularly in emerging nations like India. The maintenance and importance of soil organic matter (SOM) in the soil is considered an important objective for any sustainable system of cropping15,16. To maintain SOM in soil, nutrients removed by the field crops must be replenished. Incorporation of organic amendments is considered a desirable strategy to achieve soil recuperation, especially in semi-arid eco-regions, where the loss of SOM is high and biological quality is relatively poor17,18. In addition to preserving the physical health of the soil and crop quality, the SOM is crucial for preserving soil fertility and production, particularly through the cycling, retention, and release of vital plant nutrients19.

Applying organic manure improves crop nutrient content, elevates the importance of organic components in the soil, and shields crops from the detrimental impacts of climate change on both qualitative and quantitative crop output20. Organic amendments such as farmyard manure (FYM) are the major source of organic manure and contain a higher content of soil organic carbon (SOC), which slowly decomposes19. Incorporation of the organic matter stimulates the natural soil microbes, especially the microflora, and also reactivates biochemical processes in soil. Continuous application of FYM on a long-term basis can increase the content of N, P, K, Ca, Mg, and other soluble ions in the soil, thereby creating a reservoir of nutrients in the soil 21,22. Food grain quality was impacted by the soluble ions' enhanced absorption by plant tissues due to their availability in the soil. The NPK fertilizers may be added to farmyard manure, which is high in organic matter. On a nutritional basis, it is more expensive than chemical fertilizers, but other positive benefits on the soil can make up for the difference. A good response of pearl millet to the application of bulky organic manures such as FYM, compost, and vermicompost has been reported for the pearl millet-growing areas 1,22–25. Non-judicious use of only fertilizers spoils the soil structure, which may not be favourable for the maintenance of agricultural sustainability 6. In addition to aiding in the preservation of soil fertility, inorganic fertilizers particularly those high in nitrogen, phosphorous, and potassium can alter the chemical, physical, and biological characteristics of the soil either directly or indirectly. On a long-term basis, these changes in soil properties are believed to cause significant differences in soil health and quality, which in turn influences the productivity of soils 16,26. Nitrogen (N) is typically the nutrient of most concern because it has a strong influence on cereal crop productivity. It is a major nutrient composed of cellular parts, amino acids, proteins, enzymes, vitamins, and hormones and is a constituent of many enzyme systems that govern various plant metabolic activities 26,27. Many factors affect the quality and productivity of cereal crops, and the most limiting factor to enhance the yield is N availability. It is considered that to feed the increasing population in India, N fertilizer consumption would increase to approximately 24 million tons in 2030 compared with 2022 i.e. around 18.9 million tons 28. Nitrogenous fertilizers are widely used to increase agricultural productivity, however, significant N losses through ammonia volatilization, denitrification, leaching, and runoff ultimately reduce nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) of crops 29,30. The indiscriminate utilization of nitrogenous fertilizers is causing N2O emission and thereby contributing to global warming, causing ground and surface water contamination, and disturbing the harmony among soil, plant, and microbes. The addition of chemical fertilizers on a long-term basis decreases soil pH and exchangeable bases, thus making them unavailable to crops, and crop productivity is decreased 31. Imbalanced use of synthetic fertilizers also caused the deficiency of nutrients other than those applied, resulting in a decline in SOM. Since the past few decades, agriculture policy in India has undergone a major change through diversification and suggested the adoption sustainable production system. Thus, there is an increased interest in utilizing organic manures to compensate for the regular decline in soil fertility. Furthermore, organic matter acts as food for soil microbes and increased microbial activity increases the decomposition rate of organic material releases more nutrients for plant uptake, and improves the physical properties of the soil 16. Therefore, adding fertilizers by themselves may not be sufficient to maintain the quality and health of the soil over time to preserve crop output. Moreover, the partial substitution of organic manures with inorganic fertilizers would reduce the production cost to a greater extent 32. To overcome the declining productivity, many farmers have already resorted to using excessive doses of inorganic fertilizers, which are resulting in the deterioration of soil health. Adopting alternate nutrient management practices may benefit yield sustainability and soil quality more in the present scenario 33. Combined use of organic manures and fertilizers improves the microbial population and diversity in soil rather than sole application 34. For long-term sustainable agriculture, it has been determined that the judicious application of chemical fertilizers and organic manures is superior to the usage of each substance alone. It is assumed that FYM contains all the essential nutrients for adequate plant growth, but when FYM is applied in combination with chemical fertilizers, the decrease in SOC content can be arrested, and the gap between potential yield and the actual yield of the crop can be covered to a large extent 22,35. Confluent use of organic manures and fertilizers can be a viable and economically profitable means to sustain agriculture as it facilitates the optimum utilization of all available nutrients while minimizing nutrient losses. The conjoint application of organic and inorganic sources of nutrients helps in maintaining yield stability through the correction of nutrient deficiencies, enhancing their efficiency and providing favourable soil physiological conditions coupled with an increase in SOC 15,19,25. Most attention is paid to the balanced use of nutrient sources to enrich the soils and maintain crop productivity under intensive cultivation compared to using each component as it is a quite promising practice for agricultural sustainability 33,36,37. The majority of the state of Haryana's soils have poor SOC status and are prone to N shortage. To improve pearl millet development and achieve excellent qualitative and quantitative yields, appropriate soil health must be provided 1. Long-term experiments help evaluate a range of treatments and determine the yield trend; thus, they also act as a tool to identify the treatment failing to sustain the yields. These experiments act as field resources for researching plant and soil processes and properties, especially those that change slowly and affect soil fertility gradually. Long-term experiments provide sufficient time for crops, crop rotation, fertilizers, and manures to have a measurable effect or changes in soil fertility and productivity, estimate nutrient dynamics help identify the current changes, and predict future changes to occur in soil properties 16,38. Two key elements that are necessary to guarantee global food security and increase farmer profitability are soil fertility and seed quality.

Seed quality parameters and productivity can be improved by adopting best management practices (BMPs) like assorted use of organic manure and chemical fertilizers in semi-arid regions24,39. Therefore, the integrated use of organic manures and fertilizers could be the most logical way to maintain soil fertility and productivity and sustain soil health and quality parameters on a long-term basis21,40. To fulfill the growing population’s, need for wholesome food in light of the scarcity of land and water, it is necessary to investigate the quality criteria of this kind of agricultural system. Therefore, we hypothesized that the present study might prove beneficial as various levels of FYM and chemical N application during different modes would be effective in improving the grain quality characteristic of pearl millet. Individual and combined applications of organic manures and chemical fertilizers have been tested by several workers to improve the quality characteristics of pearl millet on a short-term basis in varying textured soils5,25,41,42. Under the same climatic circumstances of Haryana, a study was conducted on the comparative effects of graded dosages of various manures and chemical fertilizers on the qualitative characteristics of wheat1,24. The information on different paired comparisons of long-term applications involving graded doses of FYM and chemical fertilizer N on grain quality parameters of pearl millet under pearl millet-wheat cropping systems in northwestern India is still lacking.

Keeping the above facts in view, the present investigation was planned to study the long-term effect of different modes and levels of FYM and fertilizer N on grains quality, i.e. germination, root length, seedling length, seedling dry weight, vigour indexes, and nutrient (N, P, and K) content, uptake and productivity of pearl millet in sandy loam soils of North Western India.

Materials and methods

An ongoing long-term field experiment established in October 1967 on 'compensation of fertilizer nitrogen through the use of FYM under pearl millet-wheat cropping system' at Research Farm, Department of Soil Science, CCSHAU, Hisar (India) was selected to conduct the present study. The experimental site is 29o16’N latitude and 75o75'E longitude in the North-Western part of India. The soil was sandy loam; Typic Ustochrept and physicochemical properties of surface soil (0–15 cm) analyzed at the start of the experiment (1967) are presented in Table 143. The area's climate is semi-arid, with a mean annual precipitation of 443 mm and a mean annual temperature of 24.8 °C. About 90% of the rainfall is received from the Southwest monsoon between the months of May and October. The lowest and highest mean maximum temperature was recorded in January and May, respectively. This experiment was laid down in a split-plot design (subplot size: 10 m × 5 m) comprising three replications, consisting 3 modes of FYM application, i.e. applying to rabi crop (winter season), to kharif crop (summer season) and to both the crops (rabi and kharif season); 3 levels of FYM (15, 30 and 45 t ha−1 yr−1 till rabi 2007–08 and 5, 10 and 15 t ha−1 yr−1from kharif 2008 onwards. One treatment of FYM control was also maintained. These 10 treatments (3 FYM modes × 3 FYM levels + 1 FYM control) were allocated in the main plots. Since the start of the experiment, sub-plots were dressed with 0 and 120 kg N ha−1 through urea.

The FYM incorporated on dry weight basis in the plough layer (0–15 cm) before sowing the crops. Fertilizer nitrogen was applied in two splits, i.e. half at sowing time and the remaining half after 25–30 days of sowing to both the crops in respective treatments during rabi and kharif seasons. Since the start of the experiment, no chemical fertilizers other than urea have been applied to any treatment. Immediately after field preparation, pearl millet (HHB 223) was sown on 5 July 2017. The hybrid (HHB 223) was released for cultivation by the Central Variety Release Committee, Ministry of Agriculture Government of India (GOI), and notified vide S.O. 211 (E), dated 29th January 2010. Chaudhary Charan Singh Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar Haryana (India), is the sole proprietor for this hybrid (HHB 223); therefore, it was used to carry out the present experiment. The crop was irrigated with canal water, and an irrigation of about 7.5 cm was applied as and when required, depending on the visual inspection of the field. Pearl millet was harvested in October 2017 at about 2–3 cm above the ground level. The harvested crop was removed, and the roots and stubble were incorporated in situ.

Collection, preparation, and analysis of plant, manure samples, and grain quality parameters

Grain and straw samples of pearl millet were collected from each plot and dried in an oven at 65 ± 2 °C for 72 h. After that, the plant samples were ground in a stainless-steel grinder and stored in polythene bags for chemical analysis. Grain and straw samples were digested in a di-acid mixture of H2SO4 and HClO4 in the ratio of 4:1 in the digestion chamber. Using the established protocols, the nutritional content in grain and straw samples was determined. Nessler's reagent technique was used to determine the total nitrogen content of grain and straw digest using colorimetry44. Total phosphorus was determined by the ammonium molybdovanadate yellow colour method45. Total potassium was determined by flame emission spectroscopy in acid-digested plant material. Nutrient uptake of these elements was calculated by multiplying the nutrient content with yield. The total C content of FYM was determined by the ignition method at 550 °C for one hour as given by Ref.46. The grains were drawn randomly from each treatment, and the standard germination test was conducted by adopting the between-paper method as described by the International Seed Testing Association (ISTA)47. Fifty grains from three replications of each treatment were placed at 25 °C in the seed germinator. On the 7th day of the germination test, several normal seedlings were counted and expressed as germination percentages. On the day of the germination test, the root length of twenty randomly selected normal seedlings from each treatment was measured. The average root length was given in centimeters (cm) and the measurement was made from the collar area to the main root's tip. Twenty seedlings that were chosen at random had their shoot length measured from the collar area to the tip of the primary leaf. The average shoot length was reported in cm. A sum of root and shoot length was used to obtain the seedling length. Twenty normal seedlings used for root and shoot length measurement from each treatment were kept in a blotter paper packet and dried in a hot air oven at 75 °C for 24 h. The dried seedlings were cooled in a desiccator for 60 min, and then the seedlings were weighed in an electronic balance, and the weight (SDW) was expressed in milligrams (mg). The vigour index of seedlings was calculated by adopting the method suggested in Ref.48 by using the formula: Vigour index-I = Germination (%) × [shoot length (cm) + root length (cm)]. The vigour index of seedlings was also calculated by adopting the formula suggested in Ref.48 as: Vigour index-II = [Germination (%) x [Seedling dry weight (mg)]. The protein content in grains was calculated by multiplying the nitrogen content (%) with a factor of 6.2549.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted utilizing An Online statistical analysis tool (OPSTAT). The critical difference value was calculated using a 5% level of significance (α = 0.05).

Ethics statement

The plant collection and use were by all the relevant guidelines.

Results

Nutritional quality parameters of pearl millet

Nutrient (N, P, K) and protein content

Data on the long-term effect of different levels of farmyard manure (FYM) and fertilizer N application during different modes on the nutrient content of pearl millet grain has been presented in Table 3. The N, P, and K content of pearl millet grain ranged from 1.82 to 2.06, 0.133 to 0.217, and 0.323 to 0.395%, respectively. Among different application modes, no significant impact on nutrient content was observed (Table 3). Higher nutrients (N, P, and K) content in pearl millet grain was noticed with the application of FYM in both seasons (1.99, 0.169, and 0.366%, respectively) followed by kharif season application (0.193, 0.165, and 0.355%, respectively). The rate of FYM application showed a significant impact on the nutrient content of pearl millet grain, and an increase in the nutrient content was exhibited with an increase in the dose of FYM (Table 3). The significantly higher N, P, and K content of pearl millet grain was observed in the plots receiving 15 t FYM ha−1 than in plots receiving 5 t FYM ha−1. However, the effect of the 15 t FYM ha−1 application was similar to the 10 t FYM ha−1 application on grain N content of pearl millet. A significant increase of grain N was also observed with 120 kg N ha−1 as compared to nil application of fertilizer N. Protein content in pearl millet grain followed a similar trend to that of N content in pearl millet grain, and under different treatments, it varied about 11.35 to 12.89% (Table 3). Among different modes, protein content followed the order: both seasons (12.41%) > kharif (12.04%) > rabi (11.93%). With the increasing doses of FYM, the protein content of pearl millet grain was found to increase significantly. Significantly higher protein content (12.48%) was recorded with 120 kg N ha−1 than no application of fertilizer N. Individual application of 10 or 15 Mg FYM ha−1 and 120 kg N ha−1 in kharif season was the best nutrient management practice to improve the N and protein content of pearl millet grain. The impacts of long-term application of FYM and fertilizer N levels during different modes on nutrient content in pearl millet stover are presented in Figs. 1, 2, 3. Results revealed that the lowest N (0.467%), P (0.045%), and K (1.65%) content in pearl millet straw was recorded in control plots for FYM (FYM0). The N, P, and K content in pearl millet straw ranged from 0.467 to 0.620, 0.045 to 0.066, and 1.65 to 2.04%, respectively. Influence of modes of FYM application on straw N, P, and K followed the sequence: both seasons > kharif > rabi. Compared to the rabi season, a significant improvement in pearl millet straw K content (1.89%) was recorded in soils that were dressed with FYM during both seasons; otherwise, modes of FYM applications showed a similar impact on straw N and P. Among the FYM levels, the highest N, P, and K content was observed in the plots treated with 15 t FYM ha−1 followed by > FYM10 > FYM5 > FYM0 (Figs. 1, 2, 3). The impacts of FYM levels on stover N were similar to that of pearl millet grain N. However, the 15 t FYM ha−1 application was superior in enhancing the straw K content (2.04%) as compared to the rest of the FYM levels (straw K: < 1.90%). The fertilizer N application exhibited a significant increase in N (0.581%), P (0.063%), and K (1.94%) content of pearl millet straw than without chemical N (N0) application. The results indicate that the nutritional content of pearl millet was not significantly affected by the interactions of all three components. However, the greatest values of these parameters were found when fertilizer N and FYM were used together, regardless of the method used to apply FYM (Data not provided).

Overall, the application of 15 t FYM ha−1 significantly increased the grain N (13.19%), P (63.16%), K (22.29%), protein (13.56%), stover N (32.76%) and P (46.66%) content over FYM control plots (FYM0). Grain quality parameters of pearl millet

Standard germination

Among the different modes of FYM application, standard germination of pearl millet grains followed the order of both seasons > kharif > rabi (Table 4). However, the impact of all the modes of FYM application was similar on the germination of pearl millet. Irrespective of the mode of FYM application, the increasing rate of FYM application significantly increased the germination of pearl millet, while the effect was non-significant among FYM levels. Application of 15 t FYM ha−1 exhibited the highest germination (87.89%) of pearl millet, which was statistically at par with the 10 t FYM ha−1 application. The addition of fertilizer N significantly enhanced the germination of pearl millet as compared to no application of chemical N, and relatively chemical N application enhanced 5.91% germination of pearl millet (Table 4). The interaction of 10 t FYM ha−1 × 120 kg N ha−1 significantly enhanced the pearl millet germination (Fig. 4). Compared to the FYM0, the application of FYM@ 15 t ha−1 significantly increased the germination (6.24–20.68%), regardless of fertilizer N.

Root length

After fifty cycles of pearl millet-wheat cropping, significant impacts of all three factors on the root length of pearl millet were observed, ranging from 5.19 to 8.25 cm (Tables 4 and 5). Based on the data, the FYM application during both seasons significantly confirmed the beneficial effect of increasing the root length (7.12 cm) of pearl millet as compared to FYM applied in either single season (Table 4). FYM application mode relatively enhanced the pearl millet's root length that followed the order: both seasons > kharif > rabi. The FYM applications proved an important factor for root length extension of pearl millet seed. As compared to nil application of FYM (FYM0), application of FYM @ 15,10 and 5 Mg ha−1 increased the root length by 29.83, 23.39 and 12.71%, respectively. Similar to the FYM amended plots, the root length of pearl millet grown in fertilizer N-dressed plots was significantly increased by 18.79%. Regardless of the modes and fertilizer N applications, root length was increased with the FYM application rate (Table 5). Statistically, combined applications of FYM15 and fertilizer N during kharif or both seasons showed a similar impact on root length as compared to their application in the rabi season (7.78 cm). Therefore, FYM application (FYM15) coupled with fertilizer N during the kharif season significantly increased the root length (8.07 cm). Interactions of mode and level of FYM application were significant and varied between 5.90 and 8.14 cm (Table 4). Regardless of the modes, the FYM applications showed a significant increase in the root length of pearl millet, and the highest root length (8.14 cm) was recorded with FYM15 coupled with its application during both seasons. Similarly, interactions of modes of FYM and fertilizer N application were significant and varied from 5.99 to 7.63 cm. Irrespective of modes, root length increased with fertilizer N application up to 7.63 cm. Compared to nil application of fertilizer N, a significant increase was observed with fertilizer N application where FYM was added during rabi (7.30 cm), kharif season (7.45 cm), and both seasons (7.63 cm). So, the application of FYM during both seasons in conjunction with fertilizer N (N120) could be beneficial for increasing the root length of pearl millet. The interaction effects of FYM x fertilizer N levels on root length ranged from 5.19 to 8.03 cm among different treatment combinations (Fig. 5). Root length increased with FYM applications coupled with fertilizer N, ranging from 10.18 to 27.38%. Overall, the combined application of FYM and fertilizer N (FYM15 x N120) was superior in root enlengthen of pearl millet seed (Fig. 5). Data committed that mode of FYM, level of FYM, and chemical N application played a key role in root extension of pearl millet in these sandy loam soils.

Shoot length

Regular applications of FYM and fertilizer N during different modes greatly impacted the shoot length of pearl millet grain (Tables 4 and 6). It varied between 4.88 to 8.23 cm under different treatment combinations (Table 6). The mode of FYM application showed a significant difference in the shoot length of pearl millet (Table 4) and followed the order: both seasons (6.67 cm) > kharif (6.52 cm) > rabi (6.19 cm). A significant variation in the impact of FYM doses on shoot length was also observed, and the highest shoot length (7.57 cm) was recorded with FYM application @15 t ha−1, and the lowest (5.28 cm) was exhibited with FYM application @5 t ha−1. The effect of fertilizer N application was important to enlengthen the shoot, and it increased the shoot length of pearl millet by 17.71% over the control plot (N0). Individually, all the factors were found to be significantly responsible for enhancing the shoot length of pearl millet after fifty years of pearl millet-wheat cropping. The interaction of mode and FYM levels was significant in the shoot length of pearl millet (Table 6). Irrespective of the modes, the FYM applications significantly increased the shoot length, and the highest shoot length (7.80 cm) was observed with FYM @15 t ha−1 applied during both seasons. Interactions of mode × fertilizer N showed a beneficial impact on shoot length (Table 6). Data revealed that FYM application during both seasons and fertilizer N showed significantly the highest shoot length (7.25 cm) among all the treatments. Interactions of FYM x fertilizer N levels were significant at each level, and the highest shoot length (8.00 cm) was observed with the treatment FYM15 × N120. Thus, the treatment FYM15 x N120 could be considered best for increasing the shoot length of pearl millet (Fig. 6). The shoot length of the pearl millet grains rose considerably with increasing rate of FYM, regardless of the method of FYM and fertilizer N treatments (Table 6). The highest shoot length (8.23 cm) was recorded in plots dressed with FYM @15 t ha−1 coupled with fertilizer N during both seasons, and it was significantly higher (11.52%) as compared to plots receiving the same amount of FYM in both seasons without fertilizer N. Regardless of the modes and level of FYM, fertilizer N significantly enhanced the shoot length of pearl millet than without fertilizer N. Results revealed that all three factors significantly influenced the shoot length and interaction of FYM@15 t ha−1 application during both seasons with fertilizer N was superior among all the treatment combinations.

Discussion

Nutritional quality of pearl millet

Comprehending the amount of nutrients removed by a crop yields valuable insights for managing soil health by contrasting the whole plant accumulation with additions from all sources. When FYM was applied during the rabi season, pearl millet's nutritional and protein content decreased, and FYM treatments were quantitatively superior in both seasons (Table 3, Figs. 1, 2, 3). It might have been due to the long-term additive effect of FYM application that resulted in a higher buildup of soil organic carbon (SOC) and consequently improved soil health with both seasons than single season application. Among the different modes of FYM application, in terms of its quantity supplied, individual application of FYM in the kharif season exhibited slight benefits on nutrients and protein content of pearl millet. Regular application of FYM accrued SOC pools and improved microbiological properties, i.e. microbial biomass carbon and related enzyme activities of these nutrients (Data not given). Adding organic manures has been suggested as a desirable strategy to achieve soil recuperation, especially in semi-arid eco-regions, where the loss of SOM and biological quality are relatively poor. The SOM plays an important role in maintaining the fertility and productivity of soil, especially through the cycling, retention, and release of essential plant nutrients and also in maintaining the soil's physical health and crop quality5,19,22. In addition to improving soil aggregation and serving as a clamant indicator of soil quality, the SOC also facilitates the development of more exchange sites and the soil's capacity to exchange cations, all of which increase the availability of nutrients for plants by promoting better root proliferation and nutrient bioavailability during the breakdown of SOM50–52. Therefore, continuous application of FYM increases the nutrient content in grain and straw crops53,54.

Nutrient content was further increased with increased levels of FYM application. Application of 15 t FYM ha-1 significantly increased the grain N (13.19%), P (63.16%), K (22.29%), protein (13.56%), stover N (32.76%), and P (46.66%) content over control plots of FYM (FYM0) (Table 3, Figs. 1, 2, 3). However, applying 10 t FYM ha-1 was best for increasing grain N, K, protein, and stover N content. Organic manure application enhances the nutrient uptake of crops as well as increases with increasing doses of manure18,55. The application of manure enhances the properties and structure of the soil, promotes the formation of bigger root densities and decreases nutrient leaching, which in turn increases nutrient absorption and crop production quality32. Better root proliferation results in higher absorption of nutrients like N from the equilibrium soil solution. The FYM incorporation also enhanced fertilizer efficiency due to less loss of nutrients, such as fixation and precipitation losses, and improved crop uptake56. Our results indicated a significant accrual in grain protein content of pearl millet with the increasing doses of FYM. The maximum protein content was seen when chemical fertiliser and manure were used in combination, but grains' protein content rose as FYM dosages increased57.

A significant increase in N (6.38%), P (15.89%) and protein (6.03%) content of grain and N (13.92%), P (40.00%) and K (10.86%) in stover of pearl millet were exhibited with 120 kg N ha−1 as compared to no application of fertilizer N (Table 3 and Figs. 1, 2, 3). Nitrogen plays a crucial role in building blocks of protein synthesis, so, increased grain N is responsible for higher protein. Application of nitrogen induced vigorous growth of the plant as well as the formation of bold and viable seeds. Increasing levels of N application led to higher N absorption and utilization, which boosted photosynthetic activity so that N was quickly assimilated by plants to accelerate their growth. A well-balanced application of N can increase the dry matter content by producing photo-assimilates in the leaves, which are the main center of plant growth during the vegetative stage, and later distributing the assimilates to the reproductive organs58. The findings of the present experiment are concordant with earlier researchers1,22,59. Also demonstrated by54, grain N content was highest in treatment receiving N 100% from urea followed by applying 100% N through FYM. An increase in N, P and K content of grain and stover of pearl millet with the addition of fertilizer N might be due to increased nutrient availability in soil18,33,37. Increased nutrient content in grain and stover of pearl millet with fertilizer N was also reported by60. Therefore, the present study suggested that 120 kg N ha−1 can be the best nutrient management practice to improve the nutrients and protein content of pearl millet.

After fifty years, the nutrient and protein content of pearl millet was lowest in absolute untreated plots and highest in plots that were receiving FYM@ 10 or 15 t ha−1 during both seasons coupled with N application@120 kg ha−1 (Data not given). Beneficial impacts of the application of FYM coupled with fertilizer N could be ascribed to increased availability of easily assimilated macro and micronutrients to the plants initially through inorganic fertilizers and then by mobilization and solubility action of organic acids produced during the decomposition of FYM and synchronizing the need of crop throughout the growing season32. Increased nutrient uptake is ascribed to the improved nutritional environment in the rhizosphere by addition through organic and inorganic sources which led to enhanced translocation of nutrients in plants, thus balanced nutrition under favourable environment resulted in higher uptake of nutrients3. Additionally, as seen in Ref.61, the balanced use of organic and inorganic fertilizers helped synergistically to attain higher nutrient content and quality of the crop. Conjoint application of FYM and NPK significantly affected and improved the total uptake of N, P, and K to an extent of 21, 13, and 12%, respectively, compared to plots receiving sole 100% NPK62. In a separate 42-year study, the highest K content was found in 150% NPK, followed by 100% NPK + FYM53. The combined application of organic manures + NPK resulted in higher N, P, and K uptake than their sole applications18,37. Higher N uptake in INM treatment compared to the sole application of N fertilizer was perhaps due to the reason that organic manure prevents nitrate leaching from soil resulting in more effective uptake by plants31. The P uptake was improved because manure secretes organic acid and growth hormones like auxin and cytokine which solubilize the fixed P and make it available to plants. Better performance under INM might also be due to favourable soil environment, which encouraged better root proliferation and ensured a discernible impact on nutrient uptake. The higher uptake in INM was probably due to the faster decomposition of organic manures owing to the narrow C: N ratio with the conjoint application of organic and inorganic fertilizers. Significant increase in yield and yield traits of pearl millet grains, i.e. protein, Fe, Zn, P content with 50% inorganic + 50% organic application in medium fertile sandy loam soils of Pakistan, is well documented5. In a five-year study, significantly higher proteins, P, K, Fe, and Zn, were also demonstrated in soils of the Eastern Himalayas, India63. Application of 100% RDF + 15 t FYM ha−1 + Bio NP consortia proved superior as the integrated use of nutrient sources improved the soil-physical properties like hydraulic conductivity, released mineral nutrients probably at a faster rate that are essential to metabolic processes. Improved nitrogen availability and better mobilization of proteins' synthesized carbohydrates contributed to the fast development of plants6,26. Further research by64 revealed that adding organic manure and mineral fertilizer increased the protein content. Upon examination of data, the present study conformed with the findings of3, where maximum protein yield was also reported with INM treatment due to significantly highest grain yield in this treatment. This might be because balanced nutrition tended to replenish the sufficient amount of nutrients consistently creating a better soil environment, and enhanced nutrient uptake which further amplified yield attributes and accrued yield. Successive addition of manures or fertilizers creates a conducive environment in soil that leads to more uptake of N by crop and ultimately better synthesis of protein in the plant65. The lowest P content in control circumstances may be due to continuous mining of nutrients without any addition over a prolonged period. Noteworthy, INM confirmed the highest nutrient uptake due to vigorous root growth, checking nitrogen losses from the soil, and more efficient utilization of applied N66,67. Even in a short-term (4 years) study, INM through 75% N inorganic + 25% N through manure significantly improved productivity, relatively higher nutrient uptake, and improved soil health of Inceptisols sub-tropical conditions25. After 10 years of cropping, integration of FYM @ 10 t ha-1 yr-1 and biofertilizer with 100% NPK sustained higher productivity and uptake of nutrients by the crops68. It restored the soil fertility status and improved nutrient status at harvest, which may be beneficial for sustaining the system's productivity. The balanced and higher application of nutrients showed a profound effect on extensive root proliferation which explored greater volume of soil and absorbed larger quantities of nutrients69. The less nutrient uptake in unamended control plots implies that inadequate supply would result in nutrient imbalance and consequent reduction in nutrient use efficiency and uptake. The higher nutrient uptake with organic manure might be attributed to the solubilization of native nutrients, chelation of complex intermediate organic molecules, higher humification, and lower decay rate thus higher accumulation of nutrients in plant parts throughout the investigation. The FYM is also known to stimulate biological N fixation in soil, which may also have been responsible for the increase in soil N over chemical fertilizer treatment, apart from FYM’s own N contribution. The results of the present study were concordant with the previous studies where the application of organic manures and fertilizers increased the uptake of macronutrients in plants18,37,62.

Grain quality parameters of pearl millet

Seed quality is influenced by the interaction of some factors comprising cultivars, soil, climate, cropping practices, and nutrition sources. Among the different modes of FYM application, all the seed quality characteristics of pearl millet followed the order: both seasons > kharif > rabi (Tables 4, 5, 6, 7). This might be due to the frequently higher addition of FYM that led to the buildup of SOC coupled with the nutrient status of soil and enriched soil microbes, which in turn created well-nourished soil conditions (Data not given). The higher values in the kharif season FYM application could be ascribed to a higher temperature that triggers the fast decomposition of organic substrates and stimulates microbial activities in soils70,71.

The seed quality parameters of pearl millet were inferior in absolute untreated plots and improved with FYM and fertilizer N applications either alone or in combination (Tables 4, 5, 6, 7 and Figs. 4, 5, 6, 7). This information follows the findings of Ref.64. Reckless use of fertilizer and manure reduces N fixation in soil that inhibits the transformation and translocation of nutrients in plant system, thus, low inherent fertility in control plots that results in poor quality produce18,72,73. Root size and morphological features are directly correlated with soil properties and nutrient status, and low nitrogen content in control plots hampers root emergence and elongation74. The application of FYM increased the seed quality parameters over control treatment (FYM0), and increasing the application rate of FYM further improved the seed quality characteristics. Long-term FYM application @ 10 t ha-1 was significantly comparable and found sufficient to improve the germination, seedling dry weight, and vigour index-II than its higher level (Table 4). However, the FYM @15 t ha-1 enhanced shoot length, seedling length, and vigour index-I better than other FYM treatments (Table 4). The improved seed quality could be due to better-filled seeds with higher protein content, indicated by the increased nutrient content of pearl millet (Table 3 and Figs. 1, 2, 3). Applying organic manure accumulates ample quantities of seed components like calcium carbonate, and increased metabolism results in improved quality of grains75. The treatment FYM15 x N120 increased the seedling length of pearl millet by 30.54 and 11.08% over treatment FYM0 x N120 andFYM15 x N0, respectively (Fig. 7). Application of FYM and fertilizer N is responsible for better root and shoot development, which in turn increased potash uptake27,59.

The present findings are in close agreement with the results documented by Ref.3, that higher availability of native and applied nutrients in INM treatment attributed to better source-to-sink relationship that gave an adequate supply of nutrients throughout the course of experimentation and led to improved yield components. Enhanced concentrations of secondary and micronutrients in the crops, particularly P, Ca, and S in straw, and P and Fe in grain in plots receiving organic sources compared to the control was found in clay loam soils of Rothamsted Research in Harpenden, Hertfordshire, Southeast of England52. After fifty years, we found that continuous use of FYM was thoroughly superior to chemical N application, regardless of the modes of FYM application. Supply of FYM @15 t ha-1 was ascribed to increase SDW by 37.86% oversupply of FYM @5 t ha−1. The scarcity of available nutrients in control or less fertile plots could lead to a shortage of other mobile ions in the plants. According to Ref.76, application of cow dung at 15 t ha-1 showed significantly higher germination percentage, fresh and dry weight, plant height, and shoot and root length over the chemical fertilizer, i.e. urea.

Significant increase in germination (5.91%), root length (18.79%), shoot length (17.71%), seedling length (18.17%), SDW (24.94%), vigour index I (24.52%) and II (30.91%) of pearl millet was recorded over nil application of fertilizer N (N0) (Table 4). Nitrogen is involved in various metabolic processes, viz., photosynthesis, stimulation of cell division, and elongation, which orchestrate their intricate physiological activities, thus improving yield-attributing characteristics 12,77. Another possible reason could be that nitrogen fertilization increases the cation exchange capacity of plant roots, makes them more efficient in absorbing other nutrients, and exerts favourable effects on growth parameters and yield attributes 23,24,42,78. The improved performance of pearl millet with N fertilizers could have been the result of higher levels of LAI and chlorophyll in the leaf which improved the source-to-sink ratio and caused the plant to assimilate more photosynthates 28. Nitrogen input regulates several plant hormones and expression of genes which affect the emergence and growth of seedlings 79,80. Long-term interactions of mode, FYM, and fertilizer N levels significantly affected the root, shoot, seedling length, and seedling dry weight of pearl millet (Tables 5, 6, 7). Relatively, the FYM applications coupled with fertilizer N increased germination, root length, shoot length, and seedling length and ranged from 2.30 to 16.20%, 10.18 to 27.38%, 16.17 to 19.26%, and 11.09 to 22.64%, respectively (Figs. 4, 5, 6, 7). Such a significant increase in quality parameters with the combined application of manures and fertilizers was in agreement with the findings of earlier workers 1,5,7,39,52. The conjoint application of chemical N and manure had a positive effect on the nitrogen and protein content of the grain. Because N is an important constituent of protein, it stimulates and imparts beneficial effects on protein synthesis in terms of the synthesis of amino acids needed for building blocks of protein and improving grain quality 42. Initially, nutrients are readily available through fertilizer, but thereafter, nutrients are supplied by both inorganic and organic manure owing to the decomposition of organic manure. Suitable soil condition triggers cell formation and elongation that could benefit better root proliferation, high photosynthetic rate and growth rate of shoots, and improved uptake 81. Additional application of N significantly improved vegetative development of seedlings and plants including a greater number of tillers and seedling length 28. An intricate relation between root and shoot development in higher plants is reported, i.e. better shoot growth facilitates carbohydrate supply to roots and enhances root function, whereas active root growth improves xylem flow of required nutrients and phytohormones to the developing shoots 74. Improvement in seed quality with combined application of organic manure and fertilizer might also be due to accumulating higher quantities of seed constituents like carbohydrates in seeds under well-nourished conditions that gave bold, good quality and vigorous seeds 82. Continuously balanced fertilization stimulates the release of macro and micronutrients that create a favourable environment, which further helps in the higher nutrient content of the plants, ultimately resulting in accelerated growth and development, i.e. new tissues, shoots and seedling dry weight 36. Cumulative impacts of organically and inorganically managed soil increases the supply of metabolites for better growth and vigour of plants. Significant improvement of growth parameters, i.e. chlorophyll formation, activation of enzymes, and higher translocation of photosynthates, results in high-quality seed formation under well-nourished soil conditions 10,16,83. Confluent use of manure and fertilizer N helps in higher nutrient availability and makes soil fit for plants. Therefore, more utilization of favourable soil health increases partial factor productivity regarding seed quality 1,24. Judicious use of manures and fertilizers stimulates the rehabilitation capability of the soil; thus, improving soil health and its synergistic relationship between organic manure and nutrient availability helps improve the crops' quality characteristics 15,84. The INM practices showed significantly noticeable differences in grain and straw yield, test weight, number of grains per ear, spike length, harvesting index, biological index, spikelet per ear, and root length 67. This might be possibly attributed to the reason that INM increases nutrient content, minimizes nutrient losses, enriches soil enzyme activity, uptakes by the plant, and improves root structure and all these parameters collectively increase the yield attributes 16,18. In addition, a more vigorous root system at early growth stages results in more spikes and several spikes-bearing tillers. The field data of another investigation demonstrated that plant height, panicle length, number of productive tillers per hill, and test weight values of the crop were highest with INM practices32. Higher water holding capacity and the presence of nutrients synergistically boost photosynthesis and transportation of carbohydrates to grains, thus increasing the grain size and test weight 85. Other studies also indicated the seed quality parameters, i.e. standard germination, shoot and root length, seedling length, seedling dry weight, and seedling vigour index-I and -II improved with combined application of organic manures and fertilizers as compared to their individual application22,23,39,81,84.

Yield and nutrient uptake of pearl millet

Prolonged application of FYM and fertilizer N significantly achieved the greatest biological yield and nutrient uptake of pearl millet (Figs. 8, 9, 10). This is attributed to a considerable increase in the crop's quality metrics as well as an increase in the fertility of the soil. Consistent with the current study's findings, an increase in barley output was also noted as a result of improvements in yield-related factors such as the number of productive tillers, the size of the grains, and the number of grains per spike86. By adding organic manure to the soil, one may improve its CEC, water-holding capacity, nutrient availability, and ability to supply plants with secondary components like calcium, magnesium, and sulphur in addition to reasonable levels of basic nutrients. This ultimately enhanced the growth of new tissues that have increased the plant height and dry matter accumulation3. One of the greatest ways to sustain SOC stocks through carbon input, increase plant residue, and improve soil quality is through the use of organic amendments. Given that SOC is thought to be a determining indicator, an increase in crop production may be linked to improvements in the physical quality of the soil overall, SOC accumulation, nutrient availability, and microbial biomass.87,88. The observations of the present study are in line with the findings of65, that grain yield proved superior with increasing fertilizer dose but super optimal dose chemical fertilizers reduced the grain yield by 12.7% as compared to 100% NPK. Further, the highest grain yield obtained in INM may be due to the reason that the addition of organic manure along with inorganic fertilizers might have supplied not only the additional macronutrients but also secondary and micronutrients which were limiting in soil18. The low yield in the control plot may have resulted from a decline in SOC content following fifty years without input, as well as from the weak inherent capacity of the soils. Furthermore, as demonstrated by Ref.69, finger millet yields were much greater when FYM and NPK were added. This might be explained by the soil's superior moisture retention and comparatively higher nutrient contents throughout crop growth seasons. A further explanation would be that INM promotes better root development, tillering, and yield attributes, which raises crop output, photosynthesis, and the quantity of radiation that is intercepted16,18.

Conclusions

The ability and quality of pearl millet in sandy loam soils may be improved by adopting best management practices (BMPs). Long-term (50 years) modes of FYM application generally showed significant effects on the quality characteristic, and productivity of pearl millet and followed the order: both seasons > kharif > rabi, except for a few parameters. Application of 15 t FYM ha-1 significantly increased the grain N, P, K, protein, stover N, and P content over control plots of FYM0. However, 10 t FYM ha−1 applications gave comparable results to 15 t FYM ha−1 for N, K and protein content, standard germination, SDW, and vigour index-II of pearl millet grain and stover N, grain NK and stover NP uptake, grain and stover yield. Applying 120 kg chemical N ha−1 helped to improve all the qualitative and quantitative parameters of pearl millet over no application of N (N0). Relative to N0, the FYM applications coupled with fertilizer N increased germination, root length, shoot length, and seedling length and ranged from 2.30 to 16.20%, 10.18 to 27.38%, 16.17 to 19.26% and 11.09 to 22.64%, respectively. Applying 15 t of FYM along with 120 kg N increased the seedling length of pearl millet by 30.54% and 11.08% over only 120 kg N and 15 t of FYM, respectively. Our results suggest that the mode of FYM, level of FYM, and chemical N application played a key role in enhancing pearl millet quality and yield. Our study concludes that the application of either 10 or 15 t ha−1 of FYM during both seasons or in the kharif season coupled with fertilizer N improved most of the quality parameters, nutrient uptake, and productivity of pearl millet in these sandy loam soils of north India (Supplementary Information).

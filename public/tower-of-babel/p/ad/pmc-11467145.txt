STARD7 could be an immunological and prognostic biomarker: from pan-cancer analysis to hepatocellular carcinoma validation
Authors: Jie Hu, Qiu Jiang, Weili Mao, Songyang Zhong, Huayu Sun, Kaili Mao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAs the emergence of technologies such as sequencing and gene mapping, significant advancements have been made in understanding the landscape of tumors. However, the effective treatment of tumors continues to pose a tremendous challenge in clinical practice, which highlights the importance of predicting tumor markers and studying drug resistance mechanisms. The prognosis and differential expression of STARD7 in human pan-cancer were investigated by bioinformatic methods and experimental verification.MethodsThe expression, diagnostic, and prognostic significance of the STARD7 gene were comprehensive analyzed using bioinformatics techniques. Furthermore, we validated our projected outcomes in liver cancer through experimental methodologies, including the use of qRT-PCR, CCK8 and transwell assays.ResultsThe STARD7 gene exhibits differential expression in 25 tumors, with high expression observed in 22 tumors. These distinct expression patterns within different tumor types are closely associated with poor prognosis and diagnosis. Furthermore, the STARD7 gene plays a role in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment. Methylation levels of STARD7 vary among 20 types of tumors and are correlated with survival outcomes. Furthermore, the experiment results demonstrated that STARD7 is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma cells. Suppression of STARD7 significantly impedes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of HepG-2 and SMMC-7721 cells.ConclusionsSTARD7 has the potential to function as a crucial prognostic biomarker and exhibit correlation with tumor immunity in various types of human cancers. The implications of our findings extend to informing cancer immune-therapy and promoting the advancement of precision immune-oncology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01434-x.Keywords: STARD7, Pan-cancer, Prognosis, Immune infiltrate, DNA methylation

Introduction

Cancer still poses significant challenges in the field of medicine and threat to human life [1]. Over the past decade, we have witnessed two major advancements in cancer research and treatment, driven by the progress in technologies such as Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) for profiling the tumor genome [2] and novel therapeutic approaches like immune checkpoint blockers [3], as well as new insights into underlying mechanisms. However, currently, there is still a lack of effective methods for treating cancer because of the emergence of drug resistance [4]. Therefore, it is vital to deeply study the fundamental mechanisms of tumor progression and continually explore new methods to address this disease.

Steroidogenic acute regulatory protein-related lipid transfer (START) domains play a crucial role in the intracellular transport of phospholipids, sterols, and sphingolipids [5, 6]. These lipid binding/transfer domains have been identified in fifteen mammalian proteins, which are collectively referred to as STARD1-STARD15 [7]. Mutations in the STARD7 gene have been associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and neurodegenerative disorders. However, some studies have suggested that the STARD7 gene may play a role in the development of certain types of cancers, including colon cancer and liver cancer cells [8, 9]. The immunotherapies involving checkpoint blockers have led to an increased focus on understanding the interaction between tumor cells and immune cells. But the study of interaction between tumors and immune cells poses great challenges due to the continuous evolution of the two systems. The development of cancer can be seen as an evolutionary process, while the immune system comprises various innate and adaptive immune cell subsets [10]. However, the biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of STARD7 in tumors and immune system are still unclear. With the utilization of genomic data and bioinformatics tools, it is now possible to computationally dissect the effect of STARD7 in tumors and immune cells.

In this study, we conducted a systematic analysis of 33 types of cancer to investigate the correlation between STARD7 expression and patients' clinical phenotype, diagnosis, and prognosis. Additionally, we examined the relationship between STARD7 expression and immunophenotyping, immune cell infiltration, and immune checkpoints in the tumor microenvironments of human pan-cancer. Furthermore, we explored the differential expression of STARD7 methylation and assessed the prognostic value of STARD7 methylation levels. Our findings indicate that STARD7 shows promising diagnostic and prognostic potential in various cancer types, particularly in liver cancer, and may serve as a viable target for cancer treatment. The involvement of STARD7 in cancer development may be attributed to its role in immune regulation and potential alterations.

Methods

Data collection and processing

STARD7 expression data in cancer were obtained from the TCGA database. Next, a log2 (x + 0.001) transformation was performed, followed by R (version 3.6.3) software to compare and visualize the expression of tumor tissue samples, non-tumor normal tissue samples, and paired samples. P < 0.05 indicates the difference in expression between tumor tissue and non-tumor normal tissue.

Diagnostic potential of STARD7

To assess the diagnostic value of STARD7 in tumors, ROC curve analysis was used by pROC packages to calculate an AUC range of 0.5 to 0.1, indicating identification potential of 50% to 100%. It is generally believed that an AUC greater than 0.75 indicates a good diagnostic value.

Unified cancer analysis platform (UALCAN)

UALCAN is a web-based platform for analysis and visualization of cancer genomic data. It provides an integrated and user-friendly interface for the exploration of large-scale cancer genomic datasets. These datasets include information on the genome, gene expression, and epigenetics. In this paper, we use this resource to analyze the protein expression pattern of STARD7 in a variety of cancers in the "CPTAC" model.

Kaplan–Meier Plotter

Kaplan–Meier Plotter (https://kmplot.com/) is a web-based online tool for generating Kaplan–Meier plots to evaluate survival analyses. It contains gene expression data sets for 22 cancers from the GEO, EGA and TCGA databases. Here, it is used to analyze the prognostic value of STARD7 in a variety of cancers.

STARD7 mutation characterization

To examine the mutational signatures of STARD7 in various cancers, we used the cBioPortal tool. First, we selected the TCGA Pan-Cancer Atlas study cohort. Next, we enter "STARD7" in the "Query" module, which provides information about the location, type, and amount of STARD7 changes in the "Cancer types Summary" and "Mutations" modules. Finally, the relationship between mutation loci and survival was analyzed for various cancers.

TISIDB

TISIDB is an integrated storage portal for tumor-immune system interactions. In this paper, we analyze the relationship between STARD7 and immune subtypes.

Relationship between STARD7 expression levels and levels of tumor immune cell infiltration

Using gene expression profile data for TCGA tumors, we calculated stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores for each patient in each tumor group using R-package ESTIMATE (version 1.0.13). Next, we used the corr.test function of the R package "psych" to calculate the Pearson correlation coefficient between STARD7 expression and each tumor stroma score, immune score and ESTIMATE score to determine whether there was a significant correlation between STARD7 expression and immune infiltration score. The correlation coefficient |r|> 0.3 and p < 0.05 were considered as significant correlation between the two.

Correlation of STARD7 expression with tumor mutation burden, tumor microsatellite instability

We utilized the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) to download the sample variation dataset from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and employed MuTect2 software for data processing. Next, the R package maftools (version 2.8.05) was used to calculate the TMB and MSI for each tumor. Finally, we calculate the Pearson correlation between STARD7 and TMB and MSI. The results are visualized using a radar map.

Disease Meth

We downloaded STARD7 methylation data from the Disease Meth database and analyzed the differences in STARD7 methylation profiles between tumors and normal tissues and the predictive value of STARD7 methylation in combination with TCGA survival data using the "Survival" R package.

Biological significance of STARD7 expression in tumors

The biological role of STARD7 in tumor was studied by gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). We subsequently downloaded the GO and KEGG gene sets from the GSEA database. Visualization is performed using the R packages "Limma", "org.Hs.eg.db", "clusterProfiler", and "enrichPlot".

Cell culture and cell transfection

The HCC cells (HepG-2 and SMMC-7721) and human normal hepatocytes (THLE-2) were purchased from the Cell Center of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). In an incubator set at 37° C with 5% CO2, cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cambridge, MA, USA), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 U/mL streptomycin (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) for STARD7 (sense: GGUAUAGUGUGGAUCAGGATT) were designed and constructed by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Transfection of THLE-2, HepG-2, and SMMC-7721, and HuH-7 cells was performed using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Quantitative real‑time PCR (qRT‑PCR)

STARD7 RNA expression levels were detected by qRT-qPCR. To extract total RNA, cells were firstly lysed using TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen), and then added chloroform and isopropanol to separate RNA precipitates. cDNAs were synthesized with the transcription kit (Invitrogen). The STARD7 expression level was subsequently determined by utilizing iQ SYBR Green Supermix (Bio-Rad). STARD7 forward 5′‐CACCTCGGCAGTTCTTCAATGTTC‐3′, reverse 5′‐AACCTCGGAACCACTAACCACATC‐3′; GAPDH forward 5′‐TCGGAGTCAACGGATTTGGT‐3′, reverse 5′‐TTGGAGGGATCTCGCTCCT‐3′.

The relative RNA levels were analyzed by the 2−ΔΔCt method. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

CCK8 viability assay

The proliferative ability of HepG-2 and SMMC-7721 cells was measured using the CCK-8 kit (Dojindo Molecular Technologies, Kumamoto, Japan) following the manufacturer’s instructions. 2000 cells/well were seeded in 96-well plate 24 h before. 100 μl culture medium that contained 10% CCK8 was incubated in each well for 2 h. The absorbance of each sample was determined using a microplate reader (Bio‐Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) at a wavelength of 450 nm. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate.

Transwell assay

Transwell systems (BD Biosciences, CA, USA) were used to detect migration and invasion. 400 μL of prepared cell suspension (5 × 105 cells/mL, suspended in serum-free medium) was added to the upper chamber, while 500 μL of DMEM/F12 medium with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. 4% paraformaldehyde was applied to fix the cells after 24–48 h, followed by 20 min of staining with 0.1% crystal violet. After that, the cell numbers in chambers were counted.

Statistical analysis

Normalized log2(x + 0.001) transformations were performed on all gene expression data. The Wilcoxon test was used to analyze the differences between the two groups. The Kruskal–Wallis test was used to analyze the differences between the three groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses in this study were processed using R software (version 3.6.3).

Results

Expression of STARD7 in tumor and non-tumor tissue samples

To explore the expression of STARD7 in tumor and non-tumor tissues, we analyzed 33 cancer RNAseq data from TCGA using R software (Fig. 1A). The results showed that STARD7 was differentially expressed in 25 tumors and was highly expressed in 22 tumors. In addition, we analyzed the expression of STARD7 in 23 paired tumors in TCGA. We found that STARD7 is highly expressed in BLCA, BRCA, CHOL, COAD, ESCA, HNSC, LIHC, PRAD, STAD, and opposite expression in KICH, KIRC, LUAD, and THCA (Fig. 1B).

Expression of STARD7. A Comparison of STARD7 between tumor and non-tumor tissues. B Comparison of STARD7 between paired tumor and non-tumor tissue. C Comparison of STARD7 protein between tumor and non-tumor tissues. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001

Additionally, proteomic data were utilized to assess the expression levels of the STARD7 protein in tumors. Figure 1C indicates that STARD7 is significantly overexpressed in COAD and LIHC, while it is under expressed in KIRP, LUAD, and UCEC. In addition, to evaluate the predictive value of STARD7 in tumors, ROC curves were used to analyze the differential value of STARD7 expression data in tumor tissue and non-tumor tissue (Figure S1). The results indicated that STARD7 exhibited strong discriminative value (AUC > 0.900) in CHOL, COAD, PAAD, READ, TGCT and UCS.

The prognostic value of STARD7 in tumors

Subsequently, we explored the correlation between STARD7 and OS in cancer patients through the Kaplan–Meier Plotter database. Cox proportional risk model analysis indicated that STARD7 functions as a high-risk gene in LAML, LIHC, UVM, LGG, while serving as a protective gene in KIRC and KIPA (Figure S2). Kaplan–Meier curve analysis suggests that high expression of STARD7 is associated with poorer survival in LIHC, THCA, THYM, and UCEC (Fig. 2F, J, K, L). In contrast, high STARD7 expression correlates with better survival in LUSC, BLCA, CESC, ESCA, KIRC, KIRP, LUAD and PAAD (Fig. 2A–I).

The Kaplan-Meier curve of STARD7 is at A BLCA, B CESC, C ESCA, D KIRC, E KIRP, F LIHC, G LUAD, H LUSC, I PAAD, J THCA, K THYM, L UCEC

Furthermore, we analyzed the correlation between STARD7 and OS in tumor patients utilizing the TCGA database. The results analysis results for KIRC, LIHC and LUAD were consistent with those finds from Kaplan–Meier Plotter database (Figure S3).

STARD7 exhibits a wide range of mutations in tumors

Gene mutations play an important role in the occurrence and development of tumors. We analyzed the mutation of STARD7 in tumors and found that STARD7 is mutated in 20 tumors, primarily in the form of mutations and amplifications. Among them, UCEC, BLCA, and NSCLC were identified as tumors with high mutation rate (Fig. 3A). The most common mutation site of STARD7 is R232W (Fig. 3B, , C), and STARD7 mutation is associated with poorer OS, DSF, DF, and PF in LIHC patients (Fig. 3D).

Correlation between STARD7 expression and clinical phenotype

We analyzed the correlation between STARD7 expression and clinical phenotype. According to age, patients were divided into < 60 and ≥ 60 groups. The results showed that STARD7 was positively correlated with patients’ age in LGG and STAD, and negatively correlated with patients’ age in KIRP, LUSC, and OV (Figure S4).

Furthermore, we explored the relevance of STARD7 expression in classifying tumors. The results showed that high expression of STARD7 was positively correlated with tumor grade in HNSC, UCEC, and negatively correlated with tumor grade in KIRC (Fig. 4A–C) High expression of STARD7 was positively correlated with tumor stage and T phase at LIHC (Fig. 4E, H). There was a negative correlation with tumor Stage in KIRC, THCA (Fig. 4D, F, G, I).

Correlation between STARD7 expression and immunity

The tumor immune microenvironment plays a key role in promoting tumor occurrence, development, metastasis, recurrence, drug resistance and immunosuppression through complex intercellular signaling. Therefore, we further investigated the relationship between STARD7 and the tumor immune microenvironment. We employed the ESTIMATE algorithm to calculate ImmuneScore, StromalScore, and ESTIMATEScore for STARD7 in 33 cancer types. Our analysis revealed that STARD7 was negatively correlated with ImmuneScore in eight tumors (Fig. 5A). STARD7 is negatively related to the StromalScore in GBM (r = 0.35, p = 9.3 e−6), WT (r = 0.42, p = 1.3 e−4), THCA e−13 (r = 0.32, p = 1.9) and NB (r = 0.49, p = 8.3e−11), TGCT (r = − 0.33, p = 1.0e−4), and LAML (r = 0.45, p = 2.7e−12) were positively correlated with StromalScore (Fig. 5B). In addition, STARD7 was negatively correlated with ESTIMATEScore in six tumors (Fig. 5C).

Association between STARD7 expression and immune score. A–C Correlation between STARD7 and ImmuneScore, StromalScore, and ESTIMATEScore

To further explore the relationship between STARD7 and the immune microenvironment, we analyzed the correlation between STARD7 and immune cells and immune checkpoint genes in tumors. The results indicate that STARD7 exhibits a positive correlation with resting memory CD4 T cells, M1 macrophages, and M2 macrophages in most tumors. Conversely, it shows a negative correlation with memory B cells, plasma cells, and regulatory T cells (Tregs) in most tumors. (Figure S5A). in addition, we also found that STARD7 was positively correlated with immune checkpoint genes (CD274, CTLA4, HAVCR2, LAG3, PDCD1, PDCD1LG2, TIGIT, SIGLEC15) in PAAD, STAD, and UVM (Figure S5B).

The TISIDB website was utilized for analysis, which focused on six immune subtypes, including C1 (wound healing), C2 (IFN-gamma dominant), C3 (inflammatory), C4 (lymphocyte depleted), C5 (immunologically quiet) and C6 (TGF-b dominant). We found that STARD7 expression differed significantly in at least one of the six immune subtypes in 17 tumors (Figure S6). Among BLCA, BRCA, COAD, LUAD, MESO, SARC, the expression of STARD7 is relatively high in C2; Among COAD, LUSC, OV, STAD, UCEC, the expression of STARD7 is relatively low in C3; However, among KIRC and KIRP, STARD7 expression is relatively high in C5.

Correlation between STARD7 expression and TMB and MSI in tumors

Then, we explored the correlation of STARD7 expression levels with TMB and tumor MSI. The results showed that the expression level of STARD7 was significantly correlated with TMB in 12 tumors (Fig. 6A). STARD7 exhibits a positive correlation with tumor mutational burden (TMB) in bladder cancer (BLCA), breast cancer (BRCA), and glioblastoma (GBM), among others, while showing a negative correlation with TMB in thyroid carcinoma (THCA), uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), and uveal melanoma (UVM). Additionally, STARD7 expression levels demonstrate a significant correlation with microsatellite instability (MSI) in eight tumors (Fig. 6B). Specifically, STARD7 is positively correlated with MSI in cervical squamous cell carcinoma (CESC), cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), and stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), while a negative correlation is observed in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBC), lower-grade glioma (LGG), prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), and THCA.

Correlation between STARD7 expression and DNA methylation in tumors

We next explored STARD7 methylation levels in tumors via the Disease Meth database. Significant differences in STARD7 methylation levels were observed across 20 tumor types (Fig. 7A–T). STARD7 methylation levels increased significantly in CHOL, KIRC, LUSC, SARC, and decreased in ACC, BLCA, CESC, COAD, HNSC, KICH, LAML, LGG, LIHC, OV, PAAD, PRAD, READ, TGCT, UCEC, UCS.

STARD7 methylation levels in tumors

In addition, we analyzed the prognostic value of STARD7 methylation levels in the tumor via the KM curve. STARD7 hypermethylation is associated with poorer overall survival (OS) in CESC and improved OS in CHOL and COAD (Fig. 8A). Additionally, STARD7 hypermethylation correlates with poor progression-free interval (PFI) in BLCA, CESC, and LGG, while it is associated with better PFI in CHOL and OV (Fig. 8B). Furthermore, STARD7 hypermethylation is linked to improved DFI in OV (Fig. 8C).

Effect of STARD7 on proliferation of HCC cells

To investigate the impact of STARD7 on cellular proliferation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), we transfected siRNAs specific to STARD7 (si-STARD7) into both HepG-2 and SMMC-7721 cell lines. Figure 9B, C illustrate the efficacy of si-STARD7 in downregulating the expression of STARD7 in these cell lines. Subsequently, we evaluated the proliferation of HepG-2 and SMMC-7721 cells using the CCK-8 assay. Our findings, as depicted in Fig. 9D, E revealed that the knockdown of STARD7 led to a significant inhibition of cellular proliferation in both cell lines compared to the negative control (si-NC).

Effect of STARD7 on invasion and migration of HCC cells

Cell migration and invasion abilities were assessed by transwell assay. As shown in Fig. 10A–D. Compared to the negative control (si‐NC), transfection with si‐STARD7 significantly inhibited the migration and invasion of HepG-2 and SMMC-7721 cells. Notably, the inhibitory effect of si‐STARD7 was more pronounced in SMMC-7721 cells than in HepG-2 cells.

Discussion

Cancer constitutes a widespread health concern faced by individuals, with a marked increase in both the incidence and mortality rates of cancer globally [11]. Hence, there is an escalating need for in-depth investigations into the underlying mechanisms governing the initiation and progression of cancer, to formulate comprehensive therapeutic approaches. Pan-cancer analysis is employed to identify significant genes across various cancer types by leveraging databases to elucidate potential molecular pathways, thereby providing profound insights for therapeutic strategies [12, 13].

STARD7 is a member of the star related lipid transfer superfamily, which plays a crucial role in the transport and binding of lipids and steroids [14]. Previous studies have investigated the expression level and functions of STARD7 in various tumors, including lung cancer [15] and hepatoma cells [16]. A study has identified the cytosolic lipid transfer protein STARD7 as a critical factor in intracellular coenzyme Q transport and a suppressor of ferroptosis [7]. Ferroptosis is widely associated with degenerative processes and tumor inhibition [17]. However, the multifaceted role of STARD7 across various tumor types and its potential as a prognostic biomarker remain to be fully explored. In this study, we delved into the relationship between STARD7 and key cancer biomarkers, including tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), immune microenvironment genes, and immune subtypes. Our analysis aimed to uncover the expression patterns, prognostic significance, and molecular underpinnings of STARD7 in a pan-cancer context, with a particular focus on its validation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cell lines. Identifying novel therapeutic targets is imperative for advancing liver cancer treatment strategies, and our findings contribute significantly to this critical endeavor.

The results of this research illustrated that STARD7 was highly expressed in 22 tumors, which indicates that it may be a cancer promoting gene. In addition, through a comparative analysis encompassing 23 tumor types, we discerned pronounced STARD7 gene and protein expression in COAD and LIHC, in stark contrast to LUAD, where expression levels were notably lower. The observed variability in STARD7 expression among these tumors likely reflects the inherent heterogeneity of cancer and the differential expression profiles of this gene across various malignancies. This insight underscores the complexity of STARD7's role in tumorigenesis and warrants further investigation into its mechanistic contributions. The specific mechanism needs to be further explored. In an intriguing extension of our analysis, we identified a significant correlation between STARD7 expression levels and patient age within specific tumor categories. Specifically, our data revealed a positive association between STARD7 expression and age in LGG and STAD, whereas a negative correlation was observed in KIRP, LUSC, and OV. These findings could have profound implications for the personalized selection of immunotherapeutic strategies, particularly in tailoring treatment approaches to the age demographics of various patient populations.

Gene mutation are pivotal in the genesis and progression of neoplastic diseases, as they can either activate or inactivate proteins, thereby modulating a plethora of cellular pathways [18]. These genetic alterations contribute to the diverse biochemical and histological landscapes observed within and across tumors, leading to significant heterogeneity. This genetic diversity is a key factor underlying treatment resistance in certain cancers and complicates the identification of the initiating and driving events in carcinogenesis. Understanding the spectrum of mutations and their functional implications is crucial for the development of targeted therapies and the improvement of cancer management strategies [19–21]. Genes with low-frequency mutations are understudied as cancer-related genes, especially in the context of networks [22]. From the results interpreted in the cBioPortal platform, we know that STARD7 is mutated in most forms of tumors and the main mutation forms are mutation and amplification. Thereinto, the most common mutation site of STARD7 protein is r232w, and the mutation of STARD7 is associated with poor OS, DSF, DF and PF in LIHC patients, which suggests that we should pay attention to the relationship between STARD7 gene mutation and liver cancer.

The tumor microenvironment is recognized as a critical determinant of tumor prognosis and a key player in the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy [23]. It encompasses the intricate interplay between cancer cells and the diverse array of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes. This dynamic interaction is a cornerstone of the microenvironment, facilitating tumor progression through the subversion of immune surveillance and the promotion of immune escape mechanisms. Understanding and modulating these interactions are essential for enhancing the effectiveness of immunotherapeutic approaches and improving patient outcomes [24]. Although immunotherapy has made a breakthrough in tumor treatment, its successful application is still difficult [25, 26]. Therefore, identifying new targets is the key to further improve immunotherapy. Currently, the interplay between STARD7 and the tumor microenvironment remains enigmatic. Our investigation has delved into the association between STARD7 expression and two critical biomarkers of the tumor microenvironment: TMB and MSI. Our analysis revealed a robust positive correlation between STARD7 expression and TMB across a spectrum of 12 tumor types. However, this relationship was notably inverse in THCA, UCEC, and UVM. Additionally, we observed a significant positive correlation between STARD7 expression and MSI in eight distinct tumor types. These correlations suggest a complex and context-dependent role for STARD7 within the tumor microenvironment, which may have implications for the responsiveness of these tumors to immunotherapeutic interventions. Considering the sensitivity of MSI-H and TMB to tumor cell immunotherapy response [27], we can speculate that there may be a specific connection between STARD7 and immunotherapy.

DNA methylation, recognized as the principal mechanism of epigenetic regulation, exerts control over gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence [28]. This process typically involves the addition of methyl groups to DNA, which can alter DNA conformation, stability, and chromatin architecture, thereby modulating gene activity [29]. In the past few decades, there has been a growing understanding of the relationship between DNA methylation and the development of tumors. Increased methylation in the promoter region frequently leads to the inactivation or suppression of tumor suppressor genes in cancerous cells [30]. This research illustrated that the methylation level of STARD7 was significantly different in 20 kinds of tumors. In terms of PFI, STARD7 methylation level is a risk factor for BLCA, CESC, and LGG patients.

In summation, our comprehensive study has shed light on the multifaceted role of STARD7 across the spectrum of cancers, or pan-cancer. We have examined its differential expression patterns, mutational landscape, association with immune cell infiltration, and epigenetic regulation through DNA methylation. Our findings underscore STARD7's overexpression and its potential as a promoter of oncogenesis in a variety of malignancies. Furthermore, STARD7 emerges as a promising candidate for serving as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker, with particular relevance to hepatocellular carcinoma. These insights not only deepen our understanding of cancer biology but also hold significant promise for the development of novel therapeutic strategies and biomarker-driven clinical management in liver cancer.

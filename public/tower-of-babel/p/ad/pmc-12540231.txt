D-mannose suppresses HIF-1α mediated metabolic reprogramming in clear cell renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Ziyin Tian, Ruonan Zhang, Yan Ma, Jiaxi Li, Yunliang Li, Lei Lv
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The inactivation mutation of VHL drives the progression of clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), while the deletion of VHL leads to the failure of HIF-1/2α to degrade normally through the ubiquitin proteasome pathway. Notably, the abnormal accumulation of HIF-1/2α results in metabolic reprogramming and promotes the occurrence of tumors. Therefore, inhibition of metabolic reprogramming of ccRCC may be an effective treatment. In methodology, the viabilities of tumor cells were detected by cell counting kit-8 assay in vitro. The expression levels of related proteins were analyzed by western blotting assay, and the mRNA levels of genes were assessed by RT-qPCR assay. Glucose uptake, intracellular lactate and NADPH production were measured according to relevant instructions. Here, we found that D-mannose inhibits the proliferation of ccRCC in vitro. Mechanistically, D-mannose suppresses the transcription of HIF-1α downstream target genes, including GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF, by downregulating the protein level of HIF-1α in ccRCC cells. Furthermore, D-mannose reduces glucose uptake and intracellular lactate and NADPH production. To sum up, this study demonstrates that targeting HIF-1α by D-mannose to inhibit metabolic reprogramming is a promising strategy for ccRCC and complements a previously unknown role of D-mannose in cancer treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03695-6.

Introduction

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma is an adenocarcinoma derived from renal tubular epithelial cells, which is the most common pathological subtype of renal cell carcinoma (RCC), accounting for approximately 60% to 85% of RCC [1]. Although most patients with ccRCC achieve good treatment through surgery in the early stages, about one-third of patients still develop metastasis [2].

Two almost universal early events occurred during the development of ccRCC: 3p deletion and von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) mutation or deletion [3, 4]. Together, they lead to the deactivation of VHL function, thereby driving the occurrence of ccRCC. VHL protein is a ubiquitin ligase (E3). Under normoxic conditions, VHL targets the hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) and HIF-2α for degradation through the ubiquitination proteasome pathway [5]. HIF-1α and HIF-2α regulate the transcription of many genes involved in angiogenesis and cell metabolism. Mutation of VHL functional deficiency leads to abnormal accumulation of HIF-1α or HIF-2α in renal clear cell carcinoma, which promotes the occurrence and development of ccRCC by reprogramming tumor cell metabolism [6].

D-mannose is a C-2 isomer of glucose. D-mannose enters mammalian cells through glucose transporters, which are then phosphorylated by hexokinase (HK) to produce mannose-6 phosphate (M6P). M6P enter directly the glycolysis or glycosylation pathway to exert its effects [7]. Recent studies have found that when D-mannose enters tumor cells, it accumulates in the form of 6-phosphate mannose within the cells, blocking the energy source of the tumor by interfering with enzymes related to glucose metabolism, thereby inhibiting the growth of tumor cells [8]. Moreover, it has also been reported the administration of D-mannose in combination with conventional chemotherapy affects levels of anti-apoptotic proteins of the Bcl-2 family, leading to sensitization to cell death [9]. D-mannose also inhibit glucose metabolism in macrophages, damaging succinic acid mediated HIF-1α activation, thereby reducing LPS induced IL-1β expression [10].

Here, we aimed to explore the important role of D-mannose in the tumor metabolic. In conclusion, D-mannose suppresses tumor metabolic reprogramming by targeting HIF-1α in ccRCC. We demonstrated that D-mannose inhibits GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF gene expression to reduce glucose uptake, NADPH and lactate production in ccRCC. These results reveal that HIF-1α may be a promising target of D-mannose in the treatment of ccRCC.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and reagents

The human renal clear cell carcinoma cell line 786-O, RCC4 and CAKI-1, triple negative breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231, and non-small cell lung cancer cell line H1299 from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). The canine breast cancer cell line 2478 was provided by PETCC, the Global Pets’ Cell Resource Center. 786-O, RCC4, MDA-MB-231, and 2478 cells were cultured with Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (cat. no. MA0212-3, meilunbio) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (cat. no. SR100180.03, Sunrise), and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (cat. no. MA0110-1, meilunbio). H1299 and CAKI-1 cells were cultured with Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 medium (RPMI 1640) (cat. no. MA0215-2, meilunbio) containing 10% FBS, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. D-mannose powder was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (cat. no. M2069). L-buthionine sulfoximine (BSO) was purchased from MedChemExpress (MCE) (cat. no. HY-106376 A). For hypoxia treatment, cells were incubated at 1% O2, 94% N2, 5% CO2 in a hypoxia workstation.

In vitro cytotoxicity assay

The cells (786-O, RCC4, CAKI-1, MDA-MB-231, H1299, and 2478) were plated at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well in 96-well plates and incubated with D-mannose for 72 h in the indicated concentration. Cell viability was then determined by cell counting kit-8 (cat. no. MA0218, meilunbio) based on absorbance at 450 nm. The percentage of surviving cells was calculated using the following formula [(A450 of experiment – A450 of background)/ (A450 of control – A450 of background)], respectively.

Colony formation assay

786-O and RCC4 cells (1000 cells per well) were seeded in six-well plates. After 14 days of D-mannose treatment, the number of cells per clone exceeded 50, then crystal violet staining was performed. The number of colonies was counted and analyzed by GraphPad Prism 8 software.

Western blotting analysis

Cells of 1 × 106 are seeded in six-well plate. After a period of drug treatment, cells were lysed with NP-40 lysate containing 1% Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (PIC, cat. no. HY-K0010, MCE) to obtain total protein. Then, the protein lysate is mixed with 5×SDS protein loading buffer and heated in a metal bath for 15 min at 100℃. Denatured proteins for western blotting analysis, and the primary antibodies is incubated overnight at 4℃. Primary antibody information is described in Table 1.

Measurement of glucose uptake, NADPH, and lactate production

Glucose uptake, NADPH, and lactate production were measured using Glucose Uptake-Glo Assay Kit (Promega, J1341), NADP+/NADPH detection Kit (Beyotime Technology), and Lactate Assay Kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, A019–2) according to the manufacturers’ protocols, respectively.

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8 software. Data are shown as mean ± SD. Normally distributed data were analyzed using Student’s unpaired t-test. Results

D-mannose inhibits the proliferation of CcRCC cells in vitro

It is reported that D-mannose inhibited the viability of osteosarcoma cells [9]. Therefore, we first examined the effect of D-mannose on other tumor cells. We found that D-mannose inhibited the proliferation of renal clear cell carcinoma 786-O, RCC4 and CAKI-1 cells, triple negative breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells, canine breast cancer 2478 cells and non-small cell lung cancer H1299 cells in vitro (Fig. 1A). Interestingly, D-mannose showed the most significant inhibitory effect on ccRCC cells. In addition, D-mannose also obviously impaired the colony formation ability of 786-O and RCC4 cells (Fig. 1B). These results revealed that D-mannose significantly suppressed the proliferation of multiple tumor cells, especially ccRCC cells in vitro.

D-mannose decreases HIF-1α protein level

Next, we explored the underlying mechanism of D-mannose inhibiting the proliferation of ccRCC cells. It is well known that VHL inactivation mutations are present in about 80% of ccRCC cells [11]. Loss-of-function mutation of VHL leads to accumulation of HIF-1/2α. Therefore, we detected the level of HIF-1α protein expression in the VHL- mutant RCC4 cells and VHL wild-type CAKI-1 cell upon D-mannose treatment [12]. D-mannose not only significantly downregulated the protein level of HIF-1α in mutant-VHL cells, but also suppressed the protein level of HIF-1α in WT-VHL cell (Fig. 2A). This result suggests that the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on HIF-1α is independent of VHL. In addition, the mRNA level of HIF-1α changed little, indicating that D-mannose did not regulate the transcription of HIF-1α (Fig. 2B). To confirm this result, we treated ccRCC cells with D-mannose in different time and concentrations, and results showed that D-mannose decreased the protein level of HIF-1α in a dose- and time-dependent manner in ccRCC cells (Fig. 2C and D). To explore the pathway through which HIF-1α protein level was decreased by D-mannose treatment, the D-mannose treated cells were subjected to proteasome inhibitor MG132, lysosome inhibitor NH4Cl and autophagy inhibitor CQ treatment, respectively, and the protein level of HIF-1α was examined. As a result, we found that MG132 treatment blocked the D-mannose mediated HIF-1α degradation (Fig. 2E). Moreover, knocking down HIF-1α alone inhibited cell proliferation of RCC4 cells, and the inhibitory effect of knocking down HIF-1α combined with D-mannose treatment was more significant (Fig. 2F). This also suggested the significance of HIF-1α as a target in the treatment of renal clear cell carcinoma.

D-mannose suppresses HIF-1α downstream target genes expression

Abnormal expression of HIF-1α, which plays a critical role in the reprogramming of tumor metabolism by promoting transcription of genes encoding GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF, which uptake glucose, convert it to lactate and modulates angiogenesis [13]. Given D-mannose reduces HIF-1α protein level, we examined the effect of D-mannose on the expression of HIF-1α target genes and found that D-mannose decreased the mRNA levels of GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF in RCC4 and (Fig. 3A). The inhibitory effect of D-mannose on the transcription of these genes was time-dependent, and the effect was most striking when D-mannose was applied to cells for 72 h (Fig. 3A). Consistently, D-Mannose not only affected the mRNA level of GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF, but also downregulated the protein levels of GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF (Fig. 3B). In addition, we also observed the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on the transcription and protein level of GLUT1, LDHA and VEGF in 786-O cells, suggesting that D-mannose has other targets besides HIF-1α (Fig. S1). In short, these data showed that D-mannose reduced the transcription and protein levels of GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF in ccRCC cells.

D-mannose represses glucose uptake, lactate and NADPH production in CcRCC cells, and sensitizes CcRCC cells to oxidative stress

We hypothesized that D-mannose suppresses ccRCC proliferation by antagonizing the function of HIF-1α on tumor metabolism. To test this hypothesis, we checked the levels of glucose, lactate and NADPH in RCC4 and 786-O cells. Indeed, D-mannose suppressed glucose uptake, lactate and NADPH production (Fig. 4A–C and S2A–C). It was worth noting that D-mannose also inhibited lactate production and glucose uptake in WT-VHL CAKI-1 cells, which was consistent with the fact mentioned above that inhibitory effect of D-mannose on HIF-1α independent of the ubiquitin ligase function of VHL in ccRCC (Fig. 4A and B). It is well known that NADPH participates in glutathione biosynthesis. To some extent, the oxidative stress and antioxidant ability reach a balance in cancer cells, which makes cancer cells rely on this advantage to survive [14]. D-Mannose reduces the synthesis of intracellular NADPH, which may make the ccRCC cells more sensitive to oxidative stress. Result showed that the combination of D-mannose and buthionine sulfoximine (BSO), a potent specific inhibitor of glutamate-cysteine ligase in glutathione biosynthesis, inhibited ccRCC cell proliferation more noticeably (Fig. 4D and S2D). Taken together, these results reveal that D-mannose suppresses metabolic reprogramming of ccRCC.

D-mannose inhibits the proliferation of CcRCC under hypoxia

We also explored whether the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on ccRCC is related to normoxia and hypoxia. The protein expression of HIF-1α was downregulated in RCC4 cells with hypoxia (1% O2) incubation for 24 h, which was consistent with the literature reporting that hypoxia decreases HIF-1α protein in VHL-defective ccRCC lines [15]. Moreover, D-mannose also inhibited HIF-1α protein expression in RCC4 cells under normoxia and hypoxia conditions (Fig. 5A). In addition, we also tested the viability of RCC4 cells, and found that hypoxia treatment 24 h alone slightly repressed the proliferation of cells. Interestingly, D-mannose treatment followed by hypoxia 24 h more extent limited the proliferation of RCC4 cells (Fig. 5B). These data indicated that hypoxia promoted the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on ccRCC.

Discussion

D-Mannose inhibits the growth and proliferation of a variety of tumor cells, such as triple negative breast cancer, osteosarcoma and pancreatic cancer [7, 9]. Similarly, in this study, we observed the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on the growth and proliferation of 786-O, RCC4, CAKI-1, MDA-MB-231, 2478 and H1299 cells, but had no effect on hepatoma HepG2 cells (the data not shown). In particular, D-mannose had the most significant inhibitory effect on ccRCC. Therefore, we further explored the underlying mechanism.

Deletion or mutation of VHL is a prominent feature in about 80% of renal clear cell carcinomas. The ubiquitin ligase (E3) VHL promotes the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of HIF-1/2α under normoxia, whereas the absence of VHL leads to stabilization and accumulation of HIF-1α in ccRCC [16]. Therefore, we detected the level of HIF-1α protein expression in the mutant-VHL RCC4 cells and wild-type VHL CAKI-1 cell upon D-mannose treatment [12, 17]. The results show that D-mannose not only significantly downregulated the protein level of HIF-1α in mutant-VHL cells, but also suppressed the protein level of HIF-1α in WT-VHL cells. Surprisingly, D-mannose downregulated the protein level of HIF-1α independent of VHL, in addition that the mRNA level of HIF-1α did not change. It reveals that D-mannose regulates HIF-1α at the protein level, and does not affect the gene transcription of HIF-1α. We further found that D-mannose reduced the level of HIF-1α protein in a dose- and time-dependent manner. Moreover, the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on HIF-1α protein blocked by MG132, which indicates that D-mannose can promote the degradation of HIF-1α through proteasome pathway. It is worth noting that D-mannose has no effect on the protein level of HIF-2α (the data not shown) and the specific reasons are worthy of further investigation. We further silenced the protein expression of HIF-1α with siRNA in RCC4 cells, and found that knocking down HIF-1α alone weakly inhibit the cell viability of RCC4. However, siliencing HIF-1α combined with D-mannose treatment significantly inhibited the cell proliferation, which also suggested that HIF-1α may be one of the targets of D-mannose, and also showed that D-mannose had powerful antitumor effect on ccRCC.

HIF-1α is involved in the regulation of glucose metabolism and lactic acid synthesis in tumor cells [18]. Recently, an article reported that BHB has a potential effect in downregulating the expression of angiogenesis-related genes and MDR4 in hypoxia-induced ccRCC cells, with a marked reduction in HIF-1α/2α, VEGF, Ang-1, and MDR4 expression [19]. BHB also suppresses ccRCC cell-survival under glucose deprivation but eventually triggering apoptosis [20]. In turn, we determined the effect of D-mannose on the HIF-1α downstream target genes, including GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF, and found that D-mannose restrained the gene transcription and protein expression of GLUT1, LDHA, PDK1 and VEGF. Therefore, D-mannose reduced glucose uptake, and lactate and NADPH production in RCC4 and CAKI-1 cells. Remarkably, D-mannose can also inhibit the gene transcription and protein expression of GLUT1, LDHA and VEGF in 786-O cells, indicating that this inhibitory effect is not entirely dependent on the regulation of HIF-1α, revealing that HIF-1α is only one of the targets of D-mannose. Moreover, the combination of D-mannose with BSO, the glutathione inhibitor, promotes the sensitivity of RCC4 and 786-O cells to oxidative stress, which may due to the combined treatment of D-mannose and BSO increasing the level of intracellular ROS.

Because the existence of HIF-1α is related to normoxia and hypoxia, we tested the protein expression of HIF-1α under normoxia and hypoxia. The results showed that hypoxia could decrease HIF-1α protein level in RCC4 cells, and D-mannose also repressed HIF-1α protein expression after normoxia and hypoxia treatment. On the other hand, hypoxia can also promote the inhibitory effect of D-mannose on the proliferation of RCC4 cells.

We need to admit that there are more details worth studying, such as that it would have been better if more factors affecting angiogenesis were also investigated in addition to VEGF. The regulation of D-mannose on renal cell carcinoma metabolism may have other targets besides HIF-1α, which is conducive to finding new intervention methods for ccRCC. To sum up, this study reveals that D-mannose suppresses the metabolic reprogramming of ccRCC cells by targeting HIF-1α, and inhibits cell proliferation. Our research suggests that D-mannose as a candidate drug can prevent the progress of ccRCC by inhibiting HIF-1α, which brings hope for the clinical treatment of renal clear cell carcinoma patients.

Air bubbles in the anterior chamber associated with double lung transplantation: Evidence of cryptic transmission
Authors: Florence Yan, Christopher Zhu, Ricardo Couso
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo highlight ocular air bubbles as a diagnostically relevant indicator of systemic air embolism.ObservationsWe describe a case of a patient who developed air bubbles in his anterior chamber following double lung transplant and failed extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) cannulation. The air bubbles subsequently self-resolved without intervention prior to the patient's passing.Conclusions and ImportanceWe suspect the air bubbles developed due to air entering the anterior chamber from an endogenous, coronary pathway.Claim of priority declarationAfter conducting a literature review on May 28, 2025, utilizing PubMed and Google Scholar, using the key words (“air embolus,” “air embolism,” “air bubbles,” “eye,” “anterior chamber”), we did not find any prior reports of patients with air bubbles in the anterior chamber following systemic embolization events.

Introduction

A patient underwent double lung transplant with complications, and was placed on extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) cannulation. He was found to have new air bubbles in his anterior chamber of the right eye, which had previously been reported only in patients who recently underwent intraocular interventions.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 This case describes the rare, yet serious ramifications of systemic air emboli traveling to the eye.

Report of a case

A 74 year-old male underwent a double lung transplant. His past ophthalmic history was remarkable for non-exudative age-related macular degeneration, successful cataract surgery with intraocular lens implant in both eyes 8 months prior, and vitrectomy with inner-limiting-membrane peel with gas tamponade 7 months prior to seal a macular hole in the right eye and epiretinal membrane in the left eye. His most recent vision was 20/30 OD, 20/400 OS with pinhole to 20/100 OS. He had never undergone any other intravitreal injections or eye procedures. His systemic history included chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, emphysema, coronary artery disease with ventricular tachycardia, congestive heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, and a 80-pack-year smoking history. Medications prior to evaluation included diltiazem, atorvastatin, furosemide, pantoprazole, and nebulizers.

Intraoperatively, the patient developed hypoxia and pulmonary edema from right middle lung torsion, leading to obstruction of his pulmonary vein. He was subsequently cannulated onto venous-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. Attempts to decannulate the patient revealed air escaping from the left atrium into the coronary circulation and a right-to-left ventricular outflow tract shunt.

Several hours postoperatively, ophthalmology was consulted when new air bubbles were noticed in the anterior chamber of the right eye (Fig. 1). On exam, the patient was intubated and sedated; thus visual acuity and motility could not be assessed. Pupils were symmetric without afferent defects. Intraocular pressure was 15 OD and 17 OS. Anterior segment exam revealed significant chemosis in both eyes and an otherwise unremarkable left eye. Further examination of the right eye revealed an anteriorly displaced iris with corneal touch from 4 to 5 o'clock, as well as air bubbles floating within the anterior chamber. Posterior segment views OD were obscured by bubbles; OS was grossly unremarkable. No obvious intraocular hemorrhages or optic nerve pits were noted. A bedside ultrasound confirmed iris-cornea touch (Fig. 2). CT head findings confirmed transtentorial and tonsillar herniation, and loss of all brainstem reflexes, consistent with brain death (Fig. 4). CT chest also confirmed pneumopericardium (Fig. 5). Discussion

For our patient, the etiology of the air bubbles remains uncertain. Previous literature findings have noted the development of air emboli within the vasculature and sinuses following vitrectomy.1, 2, 3 Classic air emboli can result in intraoperative death, though other studies have reported life-threatening pulmonary emboli developing several hours after intravitreal surgery for choroidal melanoma endoresection (without surgical air infusion).4, 5, 6 This is consistent with animal studies confirming the ability of air to travel from the suprachoroidal space into central circulation, which has also been associated with sudden choroidal detachment intraoperatively due to cannula tip slippage.7,8 Retinal air emboli have been described as a diagnostic sign of systemic air embolism for pathologists to recognize in autopsy, though there are limited photographs of this finding.9

In our patient's case, air was observed in the anterior chamber following systemic embolization events. The intraocular gas may have originated from cardiac, pulmonary, or ECMO sources into the arterial or venous circulations. Possible mechanisms for air emboli in this scenario include retrograde venous passage through Schlemm's canal, arterial embolization with rupture of an intraocular artery, or passage into the CSF and an optic nerve pit. However, our patient did not have an optic nerve pit. We hypothesize that high-volume pressurized air in the systemic circulation passed through a right-to-left shunt, allowing embolic air to enter the ophthalmic vasculature. From there, air may have entered through the iris circulation or the suprachoroidal space, and subsequently into the anterior chamber.

We further suggest that air emboli to the anterior chamber should be recognized as an additional, easily identifiable diagnostic sign of systemic air embolism.

Ocular manifestations serve critical diagnostic and prognostic roles indicative of systemic disease, and this novel case contributes to the ever-growing list of ocular symptoms.10 Early recognition and interdisciplinary collaboration are critical for the catastrophic finding of air emboli to the anterior chamber.

Conclusion

This case describes the rare occurrence of presumed systemic air embolism to the anterior chamber. Given that the patient expired, we remain uncertain of the long-term consequences of this complication. This case illustrates the potential for ophthalmic findings to reflect systemic hemodynamic catastrophes and highlights an unusual ocular sign in the context of fatal air embolism.

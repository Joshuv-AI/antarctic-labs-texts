Polatuzumab Vedotin Plus Rituximab, Gemcitabine, and Oxaliplatin in Relapsed or Refractory Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma: Results From the Phase III, Randomized POLARGO Trial
Authors: Matthew Matasar, Zhiming Li, Theodoros P Vassilakopoulos, Juan-Manuel Sancho, Andreas Viardot, Andrew McMillan, Mehmet Sinan Dal, Juliana Pereira, Jin Seok Kim, Lugui Qiu, Connie Lee Batlevi, Rania Ibrahim, Juana Hernandez, Bruce McCall, Yanwen Jiang, Mark Yan, Will Harris, Lisa Musick, Corinne Haioun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPURPOSEPatients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) face an unfavorable prognosis once first-line treatment fails; therefore, there is an unmet need for new treatment options. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of polatuzumab vedotin plus rituximab, gemcitabine, and oxaliplatin (Pola-R-GemOx) as an alternative therapy in patients with transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL.METHODSThe phase III POLARGO trial was a randomized, open-label, global study. Following a Pola-R-GemOx safety run-in (n = 15), patients with R/R DLBCL (not otherwise specified or transformed indolent lymphoma) ineligible for autologous stem cell transplant were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive Pola-R-GemOx or R-GemOx alone every 21 days for up to eight cycles. The primary end point was overall survival (OS).RESULTSIn total, 255 patients were randomly assigned to receive Pola-R-GemOx (n = 129) or R-GemOx (n = 126). After a median follow-up of 24.6 months, patients receiving Pola-R-GemOx versus R-GemOx had a significantly lower risk of death (hazard ratio, 0.6 [95% CI, 0.43 to 0.83]; P = .0017) with a median OS of 19.5 months (95% CI, 13.3 to not estimable) versus 12.5 months (95% CI, 8.9 to 15.8). The most common grade 3/4 adverse events (AEs) were thrombocytopenia and neutropenia. Peripheral neuropathy was more common with Pola-R-GemOx (n = 73 [57%]) versus R-GemOx (n = 36 [29%]) and was primarily grade 1. Fatal AEs occurred in 15 (12%) and five (4%) patients in the Pola-R-GemOx and R-GemOx groups, respectively, and were largely driven by infections (including COVID-19).CONCLUSIONPola-R-GemOx significantly improved OS compared with R-GemOx, offering an additional treatment option in patients with transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL.

INTRODUCTION

Despite substantial progress in the first-line treatment of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), patients with early relapsed or refractory (R/R) disease continue to face a poor prognosis.1,2 Historically, high-dose chemotherapy followed by autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) was the mainstay of therapy for R/R DLBCL, even though many patients were ineligible for transplantation due to age, poor fitness, comorbidities, or lack of response to salvage therapy.1 Encouragingly, treatment options for R/R DLBCL have expanded with the clinical development and regulatory approval of CD20 × CD3 bispecific antibodies, including glofitamab,3 epcoritamab,4 and mosunetuzumab,5 which have demonstrated high response rates and durable survival in heavily pretreated patients in pivotal phase I/II studies.6,7 Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy has displaced HSCT in the second-line setting for patients with early relapsing or primary refractory DLBCL as well as for those who are transplant-ineligible, although its use may be limited by access, administration logistics, cost, and potential toxicity.6

Polatuzumab vedotin is an antibody-drug conjugate composed of an anti-CD79b monoclonal antibody8 conjugated by a protease-cleavable linker to monomethyl auristatin E, a potent microtubule inhibitor.9,10 Polatuzumab vedotin with rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone (Pola-R-CHP) was approved as a first-line treatment for patients with intermediate- to high-risk DLBCL after demonstrating a 27% reduction in the risk of disease progression, relapse, or death compared with rituximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP).11-14 In the R/R setting, polatuzumab vedotin with bendamustine and rituximab (BR) showed an improved end-of-treatment (EOT) complete response (CR) rate and overall survival (OS) benefit.15 Nonetheless, exploring additional chemotherapy partners to combine with polatuzumab vedotin in the relapsed setting may be beneficial to improve treatment options and maximize clinical benefit.

POLARGO (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04182204) is a global study comparing polatuzumab vedotin, rituximab, gemcitabine, and oxaliplatin (Pola-R-GemOx) with rituximab, gemcitabine, and oxaliplatin (R-GemOx), with the aim of exploring the combination of polatuzumab vedotin with an alternative chemotherapy regimen in patients with transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL.16 Here, we report results from the primary and final planned analysis of the randomized phase of the study.

METHODS

Study Design

POLARGO is a multicenter, open-label, phase III trial consisting of a safety run-in phase and a randomized controlled trial phase. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Council for Harmonisation, and applicable laws. The institutional review board or independent ethics committee at each study site approved the protocol.

Patients were recruited at 64 sites (community and hospital centers) in 16 countries. Adults (≥18 years) with histologically confirmed R/R DLBCL not otherwise specified (NOS), or a history of transformation of indolent disease to DLBCL, who had received ≥1 prior line of systemic treatment were enrolled. Key exclusion criteria included active peripheral neuropathy grade >1, prior use of polatuzumab vedotin or a gemcitabine plus platinum-based agent combination, and eligibility for autologous or allogeneic stem cell transplantation or CAR T-cell therapy at the time of recruitment. Full eligibility criteria are provided in the Supplementary Appendix. All patients provided written informed consent.

Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either Pola-R-GemOx or R-GemOx and were stratified by age (≤70 v >70 years), number of prior lines of systemic therapy (1 v ≥2), and outcome of last systemic therapy (relapsed v refractory). Only the Independent Review Committee (IRC) remained blinded to group assignments, as the study was open label. The sponsor had no access to efficacy or safety summaries until the formal reporting of the primary analysis results.

Patients received polatuzumab vedotin (1.8 mg/kg) plus R-GemOx (rituximab, 375 mg/m2; gemcitabine, 1,000 mg/m2; oxaliplatin, 100 mg/m2) intravenously or R-GemOx alone, intravenously, every 21 days for up to eight cycles. Prophylaxis with primary granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) and anti-infective agents against Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and herpes simplex virus were mandatory.

End Points and Assessment

The primary end point was OS in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population. Key secondary end points were investigator (INV)-assessed progression-free survival (PFS), and IRC-assessed CR rate and overall response rate (ORR) at EOT assessed by positron emission tomography (PET)-computed tomography (CT) per the Lugano criteria.17

Additional secondary end points included event-free survival (EFS), PET-CT CR rate and ORR at EOT, best overall response, and duration of response (DOR), all INV-assessed. Definitions for efficacy end points are provided in the Data Supplement (online only).

Adverse events (AEs) were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0.

Exploratory end points included biomarkers, immunogenicity, pharmacokinetics, and patient-reported outcomes documented using Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy/Gynecologic Oncology Group – Neurotoxicity-12 questionnaire.

Statistical Analysis

No interim analysis was planned, and the results reported here represent the primary and final analysis.

All analyses related to baseline characteristics and study conduct were performed on the randomized ITT population. The primary safety population included all patients who received at least one dose of any study medication in the randomized cohort. Efficacy analyses were performed in the ITT analysis set, which included all randomly assigned patients regardless of the actual treatment received.

OS and PFS were compared between groups using a two-sided log-rank test. Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Further details can be found in the Data Supplement.

Response rates (CR and ORR) were compared with the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test. The 95% CIs for response rates were estimated with the Clopper-Pearson method, and P value tests of response rates were stratified.

RESULTS

Between February 7, 2020, and May 23, 2023, 347 patients were screened for eligibility, and 270 patients were enrolled (safety run-in Pola-R-GemOx: 15 patients; randomized Pola-R-GemOx: 129 patients; randomized R-GemOx: 126 patients; Fig 1). Screen failures are described in the Data Supplement. In the ITT population, 128 and 125 patients in the Pola-R-GemOx and the R-GemOx cohorts, respectively, received any study treatment. At enrollment.

Of the patients randomly assigned (N = 255), the median age was 65 years (range, 20-89), and the majority of patients had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0/1 (n = 225 [88.2%]) and Ann Arbor stage III/IV disease (n = 195 [76.5%]). All patients had a DLBCL diagnosis by INV and site pathology; however, after enrollment into the study, 10 patients were confirmed by central pathology to have double-hit lymphoma. The most common reason for stem cell transplant ineligibility was age (n = 104 [40.8%] patients; Data Supplement, Table S1).

Most patients had received only one prior line of therapy (n = 165 [64.7%]), and the majority had disease that was refractory to the last treatment received (n = 168 [65.9%]). Of the 232 patients with DLBCL NOS, 136 (58.6%) had been refractory to their first treatment and were therefore classified as primary refractory.

Efficacy

Efficacy data from the randomized arms are shown in Table 2, with a median follow-up duration of 24.6 months (range, 0-34) for OS. The primary end point was met with a statistically significant 40% reduction in the risk of death in the Pola-R-GemOx arm versus the R-GemOx arm (hazard ratio [HR], 0.60 [95% CI, 0.43 to 0.83]; P value = .0017; Fig 2A), leading to a median OS of 19.5 months (95% CI, 13.3 to not estimable) versus 12.5 months (95% CI, 8.9 to 15.8). Fewer deaths due to progressive disease (PD) were observed in the Pola-R-GemOx (n = 38 [29.7%]) arm compared with the R-GemOx arm (n = 70 [56.0%]).

Key secondary efficacy end points were met and consistently favored Pola-R-GemOx. The risk of INV-assessed PD or death was reduced by 63% with Pola-R-GemOx compared with R-GemOx (HR, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.27 to 0.51]; P < .0001; Fig 2B), leading to a median PFS of 7.4 months (95% CI, 6.2 to 11.7) and 2.7 months (95% CI, 2.4 to 3.3), respectively. The IRC-assessed CR rate at EOT was 40.3% (95% CI, 31.8 to 49.3) with Pola-R-GemOx versus 19.0% (95% CI, 12.6 to 27.0) with R-GemOx (52 v 24 responders; P < .0001). IRC-assessed ORR at EOT was 52.7% (95% CI, 43.7 to 61.6) and 24.6% (95% CI, 17.4 to 33.1) with Pola-R-GemOx and R-GemOx, respectively (68 v 31 responders; P < .0001).

Additional secondary efficacy end points are summarized in the Data Supplement (Table S2 and Fig S1). A high level of concordance was observed across treatment arms between INV- and IRC-assessed response rates (CR concordance: 94%; ORR concordance: 88%).

In the ITT population, the proportion of patients who received at least one new subsequent therapy was lower with Pola-R-GemOx versus R-GemOx (n = 59 [45.7%] v 82 [65.1%]). The use of T-cell engaging and subsequent cellular therapies (CAR T-cell therapy, bispecific regimens, HSCT) was balanced between arms and did not have a high impact on efficacy results (Data Supplement, Tables S3 and S4). Most patients received subsequent therapy after progression, and further details are summarized in the Data Supplement (Table S5).

The HR for OS favored Pola-R-GemOx across most prespecified subgroups including patients with primary refractory disease, patients receiving second-line treatment for DLBCL, patients with activated B-cell–like (ABC) and germinal center B-cell–like (GCB) cell-of-origin (COO) subtypes, and patients with double-expressor DLBCL (assessed by central immunohistochemistry; Fig 3).

Safety

In the safety population (Pola-R-GemOx: n = 128; R-GemOx: n = 125), patients in the Pola-R-GemOx arm had higher exposure to treatment, receiving a median of 7.5 cycles compared with 4.0 cycles in the R-GemOx arm. Overall, 64 (50.0%) patients in the Pola-R-GemOx arm received all eight cycles, compared with 29 (23.4%) patients in the R-GemOx arm. The dose intensity of the R-GemOx backbone was preserved with the addition of polatuzumab vedotin, with a median dose intensity of 96.2% with Pola-R-GemOx and 98.6% with R-GemOx.

The most common treatment-emergent AEs are shown in Table 3. All-causality treatment-emergent AEs of any grade and grade 3/4 occurred in 125 (97.7%) and 73 (57.0%) patients, respectively, receiving Pola-R-GemOx and R-GemOx in 117 (93.6%) and 73 (58.4%) patients, respectively. Serious AEs occurred in 49 (38.3%) patients treated with Pola-R-GemOx and in 39 (31.2%) patients treated with R-GemOx.

Grade 5 AEs were more frequent in patients receiving Pola-R-GemOx, occurring in 15 (11.7%) patients compared with five (4.0%) patients receiving R-GemOx; this difference was primarily driven by infections, including COVID-19 (grade 5 COVID-19 AEs: seven [5.5%] patients receiving Pola-R-GemOx v two [1.6%] patients receiving R-GemOx). However, the incidence of grade 5 AEs considered to be treatment-related by the INV was similar between the two treatment arms, occurring in four (3.1%) patients receiving Pola-R-GemOx and three (2.4%) receiving R-GemOx. Outside of the AE reporting period, 15 (11.7%) patients in the Pola-R-GemOx arm compared with eight (6.4%) patients in the R-GemOx arm died due to reasons other than PD. Deaths occurred in 68 (53.1%) and 83 (66.4%) patients in the Pola-R-GemOx and R-GemOx arm, respectively. The causes of death were due to PD (Pola-R-GemOx: n = 38 [29.7%]: R-GemOx: n = 70 [56.0%]), grade 5 AEs (Pola-R-GemOx: n = 15 [11.7%]: R-GemOx: n = 5 [4.0%]), and deaths not due to PD outside of the AE reporting period (Pola-R-GemOx: n = 15 [11.7%]: R-GemOx: n = 8 [6.4%]; Data Supplement, Table S6).

In the R-GemOx arm, AEs led to the discontinuation of at least one study drug in 10 (8.0%) patients; conversely, in the Pola-R-GemOx arm, 30 (23.4%) patients discontinued a study drug due to toxicity. The most common AEs leading to study drug discontinuation (Pola-R-GemOx v R-GemOx) were infections (n = 9 [7.0%] v n = 2 [1.6%] patients) and thrombocytopenia (n = 7 [5.5%] v n = 4 [3.2%] patients).

Thrombocytopenia (any grade) was observed in 68 (53.1%) patients in the Pola-R-GemOx arm versus 51 (40.8%) patients in the R-GemOx arm (grade 3/4: n = 44 [34.4%] v n = 33 [26.4%]). Platelet transfusion was necessary in 12 (9.4%) and 16 (12.8%) patients, respectively. A total of 48 (37.5%) patients in the Pola-R-GemOx arm versus 35 (28.0%) patients in the R-GemOx arm experienced anemia of any grade (grade 3/4: n = 17 [13.3%] v n = 19 [15.2%]), and 18 (14.1%) and 22 (17.6%) patients, respectively, received a red blood cell transfusion. Rates of neutropenia (any grade) were similar in both arms (Pola-R-GemOx, n = 53 [41.4%] v R-GemOx, n = 52 [41.6%] patients; grade 3/4: n = 43 [33.6%] v n = 38 [30.4%]), and rates of febrile neutropenia were low (three patients in each arm). A total of 116 (90.6%) patients received at least one G-CSF treatment for prophylaxis in the Pola-R-GemOx arm, compared with 114 (91.2%) patients receiving R-GemOx.

Infections and infestations increased with Pola-R-GemOx, occurring in 53 (41.4%) patients versus 39 (31.2%) patients with R-GemOx. These infections were mostly grade 1/2 in severity (25 [19.5%] patients in Pola-R-GemOx v 27 [21.6%] in R-GemOx). The most common infections were COVID-19 related, occurring in 26 (20.3%) patients in Pola-R-GemOx and 17 (13.6%) patients in R-GemOx. The rates of patient-reported COVID-19 vaccination were comparable, 62 (48.1%) patients in Pola-R-GemOx, compared with 54 (42.9%) patients in R-GemOx.

Peripheral neuropathy was more commonly reported in patients receiving Pola-R-GemOx, occurring in 73 (57.0%) patients versus 36 (28.8%) patients with R-GemOx. Most events were grade 1 (Pola-R-GemOx, 48 [37.5%]; R-GemOx, 29 [23.2%]). Grade 2 peripheral neuropathy was reported in 20 patients (15.6%) in the Pola-R-GemOx arm and seven patients (5.6%) in the R-GemOx arm. Grade 3 events, the highest severity observed, occurred only in the Pola-R-GemOx group in five patients (3.9%). Three patients (2.3%) had a peripheral neuropathy event that led to polatuzumab vedotin discontinuation. At study closure, of the patients with at least one event, 28 (38.4%) in the Pola-R-GemOx arm versus 21 (58.3%) in the R-GemOx arm had resolution of all events. When considering resolution or any improvement of peripheral neuropathy severity, 37 (50.7%) of the patients who experienced peripheral neuropathy in the Pola-R-GemOx arm showed a decrease in maximum severity. Consistent with these findings, patient-reported outcomes showed small on-treatment increases in both arms, with clinically meaningful worsening (≥3 points) in the Pola-R-GemOx arm at C8D1 and EOT, followed by improvement trends in both arms from month 4 of long-term follow-up.

DISCUSSION

Outcomes for patients with transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL remain suboptimal, and conventional chemotherapy continues to be used, often as a bridge to potentially curative treatments like CAR T-cell therapy. The established combination of polatuzumab vedotin with BR has shown efficacy in second-line and later transplant-ineligible DLBCL with an OS of 12.4 months and PFS of 9.2 months15; however, the lymphodepleting toxicity associated with bendamustine is a concern as it may compromise future CAR T-cell eligibility or efficacy.18 Combining polatuzumab vedotin with an alternative chemotherapy backbone, like R-GemOx, has demonstrated the potential to achieve similar efficacy while maintaining a tolerable toxicity profile.16

In the POLARGO study, Pola-R-GemOx showed statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in outcomes compared with R-GemOx in patients with transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL. The regimen resulted in a 40% reduction in the risk of death (HR, 0.60) and a 63% reduction in the risk of PD or death (HR, 0.37) compared with R-GemOx. Specifically, the CR rates more than doubled (19.0%-40.3%) with the addition of polatuzumab vedotin. Survival benefits were consistent across most subgroups including patients with refractory disease, different numbers of prior lines of therapy, and COO.

Among the 184 patients with ABC or GCB DLBCL subtypes reported, the OS HRs were nearly identical, favoring Pola-R-GemOx in both subgroups. Although COO has not been shown to be prognostic in the R/R DLBCL setting,19,20 this finding supports Pola-R-GemOx as an additional treatment option for R/R DLBCL, independent of COO.

The significant PFS benefit is supported by a 24-month PFS rate of 25.3% with Pola-R-GemOx compared with 11.5% with R-GemOx, suggesting the potential for durable remissions for a subset of patients with refractory disease. Additionally, EFS, accounting for progression, death, and initiation of non–protocol-specified antilymphoma treatment, also showed a median benefit with Pola-R-GemOx (6.8 months) versus R-GemOx (2.7 months). The survival benefit of Pola-R-GemOx was not influenced by subsequent new therapies and is reflected in reduced deaths from lymphoma rather than other causes, highlighting the regimen's disease-targeting efficacy.

The treatment landscape for R/R DLBCL continues to evolve, and although CAR T-cell therapy is a potentially curative option, significant financial, logistical, and clinical barriers limit patient access to treatment.21 Real-world data show that many eligible patients do not receive CAR T-cell therapy. A recent US analysis showed that among eligible patients, only 25% in the second-line and 36% in the third-line received this treatment, and alternatives such as chemoimmunotherapy, polatuzumab vedotin, or bispecific-based therapy were used instead.22 Bispecific antibodies also offer a promising addition to available treatments and are being investigated in the second-line setting.23-25 Notably, the STARGLO study confirmed that glofitamab plus GemOx (Glofit-GemOx) was efficacious in a similar transplant-ineligible population, yielding a higher median OS compared with R-GemOx (25.5 v 12.9 months, respectively; median follow-up 20.7 months).23 Similarly, the SUNMO study showed improved median OS with mosunetuzumab plus polatuzumab vedotin (Mosun-Pola) compared with R-GemOx (18.7 v 13.6 months; not statistically significant) in transplant-ineligible patients with relapsed/refractory LBCL.26 Additionally, the EPCORE NHL-2 single-arm trial highlighted the efficacy of epcoritamab plus GemOx, yielding a median OS of 21.6 months in transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL.25

Although the Pola-R-GemOx regimen demonstrates efficacy in inducing responses, the durability of these responses is not as robust when compared with emerging treatment options, as supported by the relatively modest median DOR observed with Pola-R-GemOx compared with R-GemOx (11.8 v 8.5 months, respectively). For instance, combinations incorporating bispecific antibodies, such as Glofit-GemOx23 and Mosun-Pola,26 demonstrated strong ORRs and a trend toward more sustained responses. A challenge in managing R/R DLBCL is the need for effective bridging therapy to achieve disease control before definitive treatment.27 Pola-R-GemOx addresses this gap by providing an alternative bridging approach, combining an antibody-drug conjugate with chemotherapy, resulting in a doubling of CR rates and enabling effective disease control, without the risk of cytokine release syndrome that can be seen with bispecifics, and lymphodepletion associated with bendamustine.

The safety profile of Pola-R-GemOx showed additive toxicity consistent with a four-drug regimen, with no new safety signals identified. Although a higher proportion of patients discontinued a study drug early due to an AE with Pola-R-GemOx (23.4%) compared with R-GemOx (8.0%), primarily due to thrombocytopenia or infections, most patients completed a significant portion of the planned treatment cycles, indicating that toxicities primarily occurred during the latter part of the treatment regimen.

The regimen maintained the dose intensity of the R-GemOx backbone, and although peripheral neuropathy was common, due to the combined effects of polatuzumab vedotin and oxaliplatin, most events were grade 1 and did not necessitate treatment discontinuation. This is meaningful considering the enrolled population of patients who received second-line therapy or had primary refractory disease with recent exposure to neurotoxic drugs as part of the standard first-line treatment.

The higher rate of fatal AEs was largely due to an increased incidence of infections, including COVID-19, as the study coincided with the pandemic's onset. The survival benefit with Pola-R-GemOx was nevertheless in its favor, suggesting that this survival advantage could be amplified in settings with lower infectious risks.

A notable limitation of this study is that it was designed before CAR T-cell therapy was approved in the second-line setting for R/R DLBCL and before polatuzumab vedotin was incorporated into first-line therapy. As Pola-R-CHP becomes more widely adopted as a standard of care, there are currently no robust data to support the use of polatuzumab vedotin in the R/R DLBCL setting in patients who received first-line polatuzumab vedotin–containing therapies. Another limitation is the use of R-GemOx as a control arm. Although studies such as POLARGO, STARGLO,28 and SUNMO26 have demonstrated improved outcomes with novel combinations versus R-GemOx, these trials enrolled patient populations with differing baseline characteristics, which limits the validity of direct cross-trial comparisons. However, the totality of evidence suggests that R-GemOx is increasingly an outdated standard of care for this patient population. In conclusion, the POLARGO study establishes Pola-R-GemOx as an effective and tolerable treatment option for patients with transplant-ineligible R/R DLBCL, significantly improving OS and CR rates and representing a valuable, non-lymphodepleting addition to the current therapeutic landscape.

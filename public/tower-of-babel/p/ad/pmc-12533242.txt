Hydrophobic wavy tape to augment the parabolic trough solar collector performance
Authors: Mohammad Mahdi Heyhat, Ali Ismail Al-Shiblawi, Omid Haghani, Mostafa Bahmani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
It is expected to use wavy tapes to enhance the efficiency of solar collectors. But the side effect of using these inserts is increased pressure drop. This study presents hydrophobic wavy tape to alleviate the adverse effects of elevated pressure drop. Wavy tapes with different shapes, including fixed and variable wave width and height, were examined experimentally. The impact of the hydrophobicity of the surface of the wavy tape on the thermohydraulic performance of the parabolic trough solar collector was studied. The results showed that increasing both the width and height of the tape enhances the thermal efficiency of the solar collector. To simultaneously address the improvement of pressure drop and thermal efficiency, the performance indicator was defined. It was observed that the wavy tape of variable width and height can lead to a better performance indicator. Also, the hydrophobicity of the surface of the wavy tapes raises their performance indicator by 42%.

Introduction

Solar energy is a crucial source of clean, free, limitless, renewable energy with minimal environmental impact1,2. It comes from the sun and can be transformed into heat and electricity. It has been available as an energy source for years, but its application has received significant technological attention, particularly in the recent decade3,4. Increasing energy demand, scarcity of fossil fuels, and environmental concerns about the use of fossil fuels, such as carbon dioxide emissions, have led to increased emphasis on solar energy applications5–7. The energy of incident solar radiation can be converted to thermal energy by a solar collector in solar thermal applications. On the other hand, it can be turned into electrical energy in photovoltaic (PV) applications. The parabolic solar collector is the most widely used type of high-temperature solar collector. The solar radiation received by the solar collector is transformed into the working fluid as heat. The thermal efficiency of the collector increases with the temperature of the working fluid at the outlet8,9.

There are three ways to enhance thermal efficiency in thermal systems10: active technique, passive technique11, and a combination of them. Most methods of increasing thermal efficiency are associated with improved system pumping power. Wavy tape is acknowledged as one of the passive methods used to increase the efficiency of solar collectors. They disrupt the fluid flow and collapse of the thermal boundary layer12, thereby improving heat absorption inside the absorber tube of the solar collector. Many researchers have studied the effect of placing the wavy tapes in the fluid flow on enhancing the efficiency of the solar collector. Earlier studies have shown that incorporating wavy tape in a solar collector operating at flow rates between 240 and 720 L per hour can enhance the average Nusselt number by approximately 261% to 310%13. But along with the positive effect of improving heat transfer in the solar collector, it has the negative role of increasing pressure drop and pumping power. Conversely, applying a hydrophobic coating to the surface of the wavy tapes can decrease friction between the fluid and the tape, thereby reducing pressure loss14,15. In response to growing global energy demands and the need for sustainable solutions, enhancing the efficiency of parabolic solar collectors through micro-level innovations, such as surface texturing and hydrophobic modifications, offers a promising pathway to maximize solar energy utilization.

The effect of hydrophobicity on pressure drop and heat transfer in power systems was evaluated by researchers. Shi et al.16 employed chemical modification and surface oxidation techniques to create a hydrophobic surface on metal foam. They investigated the heat transfer characteristics during flow condensation inside a tube partially filled with this modified foam, using water as the working fluid. Experimental findings showed that the tube with hydrophobic metal foam performed better than one with unmodified foam, achieving lower pressure drop and improved heat transfer efficiency.

Motamedi et al.17 explored the flow behavior of nanofluids in microchannels featuring hydrophobic surface patterns. Their results demonstrated that wall slip effects helped counteract the elevated viscosity of nanofluids, resulting in an average pressure drop reduction of 17% compared to conventional smooth channels. Furthermore, utilizing a selective Ag/SiO₂ core–shell nanofluid over a silicon surface, which mimics a photovoltaic (PV) cell beneath the fluid, enhanced solar thermal conversion efficiency by 20% and increased the stagnation temperature by approximately 3% relative to pure water.

Previous research on turbulators for enhancing solar collector efficiency has shown that while thermal performance improves, it often comes at the cost of increased pressure losses in the absorber tube due to higher friction. This creates a critical challenge in improving thermal efficiency while minimizing hydraulic losses. To tackle this, the present study employs wavy tapes with hydrophobic surfaces to achieve superior solar collector performance with minimal pressure drop, targeting the lowest possible friction factor. This research introduces an innovative approach by integrating hydrophobic wavy tape into the absorber tube of parabolic solar collectors. The hydrophobic surface serves a dual purpose: it reduces skin friction to lower pressure drop and enhances heat transfer through improved flow mixing. By addressing the trade-off between heat transfer gains and pressure drop, this study leverages hydrophobicity to improve system efficiency. Furthermore, it explores novel geometric configurations of wavy tapes, examining the effects of wave height, wave width, and the differences between variable-width and constant-width designs to provide deeper insights into their impact. A thorough experimental analysis is conducted under laminar and turbulent flow conditions across a range of flow rates, ensuring robust and broadly applicable results. Tailored specifically for parabolic solar collectors, this work combines advanced surface properties, geometric innovation, and practical application, marking a significant step forward in addressing key challenges in solar thermal systems.

Experimental setup

Wavy tapes with different dimensions and geometric shapes were tested at various flow rates (50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 250 L/h) at a constant working fluid inlet temperature of 30 °C. The first three flow rates fall within the laminar flow region, while the higher flow rate values are in turbulent flow conditions. All tests were performed through the summer at Tehran city latitude and longitude (35.69°N, 51.39°E). The main parts of the parabolic solar collector are a reflector and an absorber tube. The reflector is made of a steel plate. The collector also has an MDF body, which was used to boost structural rigidity. A copper tube was applied as an absorber tube that houses the wavy tapes. Fig. 4The wavy tape with a hydrophobic surface.

The wavy tape with a hydrophobic surface.

To produce a hydrophobic surface on the wavy tape, the following method was utilized. The wavy tape was immersed in ethanol for five minutes. It is then immersed in 12 mol/L nitric acid (HNO3) for 5 s to create an initial roughness on its surface and immediately removed. It is washed with deionized water and allowed to dry in the air. It is submerged in a solution of 0.5 mol/L ammonium persulfate ((NH4)2S2O8) and 2.5 mol/L sodium hydroxide (NaOH) for 30 min. After washing again with deionized water, the wavy tape was allowed to air dry. The sample of wavy tape was then preserved for two days in hexane containing 0.5% (v/v) FAS-17 solution. The final stage involves drying the wavy tape in the oven for three hours at 240 °C. By chilling it in the air, the hydrophobic surface is produced. The process of creating the wavy tape with a hydrophobic surface is depicted in Fig. 5. Figure 6 illustrates the contact angle of the water drop on the hydrophobic surface of the wavy tape. All the above steps and measurements were carried out at Jikan Surface Nano-Engineering Company in the following ambient parameters: 33% relative humidity, 88 kPa pressure, and 25 °C18. The CAG-20 Jikan device was used to measure the droplet contact angle. The experiment employed distilled water with a density of 997 kg/m3 and a minimum droplet volume of 5 µl.

Pressure drop

The wavy tapes create fluid mixing and disrupt the thermal boundary layer by forming vortices in the flow, however, they produce an increase in the pressure drop. This section looks at how different wavy tape types impact the pressure drop change inside the tube.

The effect of the width on the pressure drop

Figure 9 illustrates the effect of wavy tape width on the coefficient of friction. According to the findings, the coefficient of friction increases dramatically when wavy tapes are employed due to the increased pressure drop. At a flow rate of 50 Lph, Case #1 has a friction coefficient 47.97% higher than the Base case (without wavy tape insert). The coefficient of friction reduces as the flow rate increases. Changing the flow rate from 50 to 250 Lph in Case #1 leads to a reduction of approximately 64% of the friction coefficient. For example, at the flow rate of 100 Lph, the friction coefficients for Cases #1, #4, #7, and #10 are 3.30, 1.90, 1.27, and 0.89, respectively. Moreover, a wavy tape with variable width and height (Case #10) creates less pressure drop than other tapes.

Fig. 9The friction coefficient for different widths of wavy tapes.

The friction coefficient for different widths of wavy tapes.

The effect of wave height on the pressure drop

Figure 10, shows the variation of friction coefficient for wavy tape with different wave height. Wavy tape with higher wave height leads to higher pressure drop. Specifically, for Cases #1, #2, and #3 with a fixed width of 16 mm, the friction coefficients are 4.06, 2.54, and 1.52, respectively, in a flow rate of 50 Lph.

Fig. 10The friction coefficient for different heights of wavy tapes.

The friction coefficient for different heights of wavy tapes.

Effect of hydrophobicity on the pressure drop

Based on the results obtained, Case #1 has the highest coefficient of friction among the other studied cases. A wavy tape similar to Case #1 in terms of geometry was made, and its surface was hydrophobic. Therefore, it is possible to compare the results for the wavy tape with a simple surface and a hydrophobic surface. Moreover, the role of surface hydrophobicity in wavy tape with variable width and height was studied. For this purpose, the wavy tape of Case #10 with a hydrophobic surface was made and tested. Figure 11 displays the friction coefficient of wavy tapes with simple and hydrophobic surfaces at different flow rates. It is observed that the friction coefficient in Case #1 for wavy tape insert with simple surface and hydrophobic surface is 4.06 and 2.55, respectively, at a flow rate of 50 Lph, while it is decreased by 13% with the flow rate of 250 Lph. Likewise, the friction coefficient in Case #10 for wavy tape insert with simple surface and hydrophobic surface is 1.02 and 0.93, respectively, at a flow rate of 50 Lph, while it is decreased by 54.2% with the flow rate of 250 Lph. The results indicate that the hydrophobic surface of the wavy tapes can mitigate the friction coefficient. The hydrophobic surface of the wavy tape reduces the friction coefficient by decreasing the adhesion between the fluid and the tape surface, thereby lowering flow resistance.

Collector efficiency

The solar collector thermal efficiency is a parameter that describes the capacity of the collector to utilize the received heat from the sun. The efficiency depends on the ability to absorb solar energy in the absorber tube. Improving the absorption ability of the absorber tube by using inserts can increase the efficiency of the solar collector. In the following, the role of different wavy tapes on the collector efficiency is investigated.

The effect of tape width on the collector efficiency

The impact of the wavy tape width on the solar collector’s efficiency is shown in Fig. 12. The experimental data reveal that increasing the wavy tape width enhances the solar collector efficiency. For example, the thermal efficiency at 50 Lph is equal to 35.2%, 30.3%, 27.1%, and 28.9% for Case #1, Case #4, Case #7, and Case #10, respectively. The trend is similar in all cases, so Case #1 has the highest efficiency at different flow rates.

The effect of the width of the wavy tape on the solar collector efficiency.

The effect of wave height on the collector efficiency

The impact of the wave height on the solar collector’s efficiency is shown in Fig. 13. The higher the wave height, the higher the efficiency of the collector. For example, at a flow rate of 100 Lph, the thermal efficiency values of Cases #1, #2, and #3 are equal to 57%, 51.3%, and 45.2%, respectively.

Fig. 13The effect of the wave height of the wavy tape on the solar collector efficiency.

The effect of the wave height of the wavy tape on the solar collector efficiency.

The hydrophobicity effect on the collector efficiency

The test was repeated for wavy tapes of Case#1 and Case#10 with hydrophobic surfaces. Figure 14 demonstrates the results for the simple and hydrophobic wavy tapes at different flow rates. In hydrophobic cases, the efficiency of the collector is slightly improved. For example, at the flow rate of 150 Lph, the collector’s efficiency is 64.6% and 74.4% for Case #1 normal and Case #1 hydrophobic, respectively. Moreover, the percentages are 57.5% and 62.6% for Case #10 and Case #10 hydrophobic, respectively.

Fig. 14The thermal efficiency of the collector for wavy tape with simple and hydrophobic surfaces.

The thermal efficiency of the collector for wavy tape with simple and hydrophobic surfaces.

When a wavy Tape with a superhydrophobic surface is inserted into the absorber tube, it reduces the friction between the working fluid and the tape. This decrease in friction enhances the fluid’s momentum, particularly in the center of the tube. As a result, fluid mixing improves, and the interaction between the fluid and the inner wall of the absorber tube becomes more effective. This improved mixing facilitates more efficient heat transfer from the tube wall to the fluid, thereby increasing the overall thermal efficiency of the solar collector.

Performance index

Increasing the pressure drop is a side effect of using wavy tape to improve the solar collector’s efficiency. Therefore, the use of a performance indicator (PI) that takes into account both the thermal energy absorbed and the pressure drop is essential to determine which case is better. The performance indicator was defined in Eq. (5). The better the case, the higher the PI value. In general, the experimental results show that the performance indicator is highly influenced by the width, wave height, flow rate, and shape of the tape (fixed or variable width).

The effect of width on the performance index

Figure 15 displays the performance indicator for wavy tape with different widths. A wider wavy tape has a worse performance index. Also, the results show that the wavy tapes with variable width, i.e., Case #10, may lead to a better performance indicator. But along the length of the pipe, with the increase in the thickness of the thermal boundary layer, the heat transfer is reduced; therefore, by increasing the width of the wavy tape (Case #10) and creating a stronger disruption of the fluid flow, it is possible to create better performance than the wavy tape with a fixed higher width, i.e., Case #1 and Case #4.

Fig. 15The effect of tape width on the performance indicator.

The effect of tape width on the performance indicator.

The effect of wave height on the performance index

The wavy tapes with wave heights of 16 mm, 12 mm, and 8 mm were employed for the experiments. Figure 16 displays the performance indicator for these cases. A wavy tape with a lower height has a better performance index. Case #1 is the worst case that has a performance indicator less than one.

Fig. 16The effect of tape height on the performance indicator.

The effect of tape height on the performance indicator.

The hydrophobicity effect on the performance index

The main and innovative idea of this article is to use the hydrophobic properties of the surface to improve the performance of wavy tape. By reducing the friction between the surface and the fluid, the pressure drop caused by these tapes can be mitigated. Case #1, which had the highest pressure drop and the lowest performance index, was made hydrophobic and tested. A wavy tape insert with variable width and hydrophobic surface was also made and tested. The obtained promising results, as shown in Fig. 17, indicate the improvement of the performance indicator of the wavy tape. Often the performance indicator increased from a value less than one to a value greater than one. For example, for normal Case #1 at a flow rate of 50 Lph, it increases by 21.3% with Case #1 with a hydrophobic surface. Similarly, for Case #10 at a flow rate of 50 Lph, it increases by 23.9% with Case #10 with a hydrophobic surface. These results suggest that the utilization of tapes with hydrophobic surfaces contributes to enhancing the performance of the collector by reducing the friction coefficient.

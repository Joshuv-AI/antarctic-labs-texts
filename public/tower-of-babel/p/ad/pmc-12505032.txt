Bacteria-metabolite butyrate boosts anti-PD1 efficacy in colorectal cancer patient-derived organoids through activating autologous tumour-infiltrating GNLY+CD8+ T cells
Authors: Yongxin Zhang, Wu Song, Lei Zhou, Shuwen Wei, Lixia Xu, Xiaoxing Li, Jun Yu
Published: 2025

We have recently reported the immune-promoting effects of butyrate in microsatellite stable (MSS) colorectal cancer (CRC) based on mouse models.1 However, its potential application in human CRC and the underlying mechanisms by which butyrate interacts with specific CD8+ T cell subsets to regulate immunity in MSS-CRC warrant further investigation.

In this study, we established a co-culture system using MSS-CRC patient-derived organoids and autologous tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) (online supplemental file 5) and confirmed that these organoids retained the morphological and histopathological features of the original tumours (online supplemental figure S1A). TILs, derived from the same tissue source as isolated T cells, were collected (figure 1A) and cryopreserved in biobanks once on achieving a CD3+/CD45+ cell ratio ≥90% (figure 1B). Dual EPCAM/caspase-3/7 probe staining identified organoids undergoing TIL-induced apoptosis (figure 1C). Sodium butyrate (NaBu) significantly enhanced TIL-mediated apoptosis in MSS-CRC organoids compared with the control group (p<0.05). Furthermore, the combination of NaBu with antiprogrammed cell death protein 1 (anti-PD-1) further increased the killing efficiency of TILs (figure 1D). Consistently, NaBu treatment significantly elevated the proportions of interferon (IFN)-γ+, tumour necrosis factor (TNF)-α+ and Granzyme B+ CD8+ T cells, an effect that was further pronounced by the combination treatment (figure 1E). These results suggest that NaBu significantly enhances the effectiveness of anti-PD-1 therapy against MSS-CRC organoids by activating CD8+ T cells. The immune-enhancing effects of NaBu were also confirmed in a co-culture model using microsatellite instability-high CRC organoid and TILs (online supplemental figure S2A, B).

To determine the TIL subsets influenced by NaBu, we performed scRNA-seq using MSS-CRC8 organoid after co-culture with TILs treated with NaBu or NaBu combined with anti-PD-1 (figure 2A). The Granulysin (GNLY)+CD8+ T cells were increased following NaBu treatment compared with the control group, and a pronounced increase of GNLY+CD8+ T cells was observed in the combination of NaBu and anti-PD-1 group (figure 2B). Compared with other CD8+ T cell subsets, GNLY+CD8+ T cells showed elevated levels of cytotoxic and chemokine genes, including GNLY, GZMB, PRF1, CXCR3, IL-16 and NCAM1 (figure 2C). These findings were validated in another patient with MSS-CRC6 (online supplemental figure S3A, B). Thus, GNLY+CD8+ T cells play a key role in the effectiveness of NaBu-mediated immunotherapy.

To investigate the underlying mechanisms by which butyrate regulates GNLY+CD8+ T cell subset, we performed a Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analysis based on the scRNA-seq data, and the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) pathway was enriched in this subset (figure 2D). NF-κB (p65) was identified as a crucial factor regulating GNLY expression through regulon analysis (figure 2E) and using Venn diagram-based predictions for the GNLY promoter incorporating Regulon scores and TFs prediction (figure 2F), and Venn diagram based on hTFtarget, FIMO_JASPAR and GTRD (online supplemental figure S3C). The binding of NF-κB (p65) to the GNLY promoter was confirmed by ChIP qPCR (figure 2G). Moreover, NaBu promoted NF-κB(p65) phosphorylation, nuclear translocation and DNA binding ability (online supplemental figure S3D, figure 2H), upregulated GNLY mRNA expression (online supplemental figure S3E) and increased the population of GNLY+CD8+ T cells (figure 2I). Conversely, these increases in NF-κB (p65) phosphorylation and GNLY+CD8+ T cells were blocked by TH1020 (figure 2H, I), a TLR5 inhibitor to inhibiting butyrate-mediated NF-κB activation.1 Collectively, these results suggest that butyrate activates GNLY+CD8+ T cells by promoting NF-κB (p65) nuclear translocation, which in turn binds to the GNLY promoter to enhance its expression, leading to an increase in GNLY+CD8+ T cells (figure 2J).

In conclusion, we have established a co-culture system of patient-derived CRC organoids and autologous TILs to assess the enhancing antitumour immune responses effect of butyrate. Using this system, we demonstrated that butyrate, when combined with anti-PD-1, enhances the chemotactic and effector functions of GNLY+CD8+ T cells from TILs, thereby improving antitumour immunity against MSS-CRC. Butyrate shows promise as a potential adjunct that may improve the efficacy of anti-PD-1 treatment in patients with MSS-CRC.

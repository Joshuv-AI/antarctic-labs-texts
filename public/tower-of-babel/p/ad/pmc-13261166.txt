Atrioventricular septal defect repair with severe pulmonary arterial hypertension: A novel surgical approach using a valved Potts shunt
Authors: Bastien Provost, Emmanuelle Fournier, Estibaliz Valdeolmillos, Guy Vaksmann, Emre Belli
Published: 2026

Hybrid AVSD repair with valved Potts enables R-to-L shunt and preserves RV function.

Central Message

Combining intracardiac repair with a valved Potts shunt may safely expand operability in selected children with unrestrictive congenital cardiac defects and pulmonary arterial hypertension.

Late diagnosis of unrestrictive intracardiac shunts, such as large ventricular septal defect, complete atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD), or truncus arteriosus, is frequently considered a formal contraindication to surgical repair. Indeed, pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is associated with prohibitive perioperative risk and poor outcomes related to elevated pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR).1,2 Progressive overcirculation and pulmonary vascular disease lead to Eisenmenger physiology, thereby precluding definitive correction and leaving heart-lung transplantation as the only therapeutic option, despite its high mortality, limited graft availability, and suboptimal long-term survival.1,3,4

Our experience with the reverse Potts shunt has demonstrated the feasibility and safety of a controlled right-to-left decompression strategy in children with suprasystemic pulmonary hypertension.5 Building on this, we report the first hybrid approach combining complete intracardiac repair with a valved Potts shunt to provide perioperative protection against PAH in a patient with no remaining conventional therapeutic options.

Clinical Case

A 9-year-old child with Down syndrome and unrepaired complete AVSD presented with acute heart failure attributable to severe atrioventricular valve (AVV) regurgitation. The defect had been diagnosed at 5 years of age and was complicated by PAH, leading to exclusion from surgical repair and heart-lung transplantation at that time.

Resting oxygen saturation was 80% on room air. Transthoracic echocardiogram demonstrated an isolated balanced complete AVSD with severe left atrioventricular valve regurgitation, reduced biventricular systolic function, and a predominantly right-to-left shunt. Cardiac catheterization revealed isosystemic systolic and mean pulmonary artery pressures, but diastolic pulmonary pressures were infrasystemic (Table 1). Although PVR could not be reliably measured under general anesthesia and mechanical ventilation, multiparametric assessment and angiography strongly suggested advanced pulmonary vascular disease. Given these findings, isolated intracardiac repair was considered unsafe. A concomitant valved Potts shunt was therefore planned, along with postoperative targeted PAH therapy, to avoid right ventricular failure and potentially promote regression of pulmonary vascular reactivity.

Surgical Technique

After stabilization of acute heart failure, the patient underwent complete AVSD repair using a 2-patch technique with careful reconstruction of the atrioventricular valves, followed by the creation of a valved Potts shunt between the descending aorta and the main pulmonary artery. After both arteries were mobilized, side-biting clamps were applied on the descending aorta, and a 12-mm Hancock valved conduit (Medtronic) was implanted between the vessels, allowing unidirectional flow from the pulmonary artery toward the aorta. Cardiopulmonary bypass and crossclamp times were 162 minutes and 89 minutes, respectively. The patient was successfully weaned from cardiopulmonary bypass with inotropic support and inhaled nitric oxide, and the chest was closed. The postoperative course was uneventful, with inotropes discontinued after 2 days. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 9 on aspirin, sildenafil, captopril, and furosemide. Discharge echocardiography showed a small atrial-level residual shunt, no right AVV regurgitation, and mild left AVV residual regurgitation. Institutional review board approval was not required for this single-patient case report. The need for consent was waived, and the patient information was anonymized to ensure confidentiality.

The right-to-left shunt across the Potts gradually decreased, and pulmonary vasodilators were progressively weaned. At 3-year follow-up, oxygen saturation was 100% in room air, with no evidence of desaturation episodes, and the patient was asymptomatic. Last echocardiography showed normal biventricular function, with RV pressure estimated at half systemic, mild LAVV regurgitation, and no detectable flow through the valved Potts.

Discussion

In this case, the valved Potts shunt enabled complete intracardiac repair while preventing perioperative right ventricular failure secondary to pulmonary hypertensive crises, with right-to-left flow limited to the lower body and preservation of cerebral and coronary oxygenation. The absence of residual shunt flow at follow-up suggests potential improvement in PVR after surgical closure of chronic left-to-right shunt combined with medical PAH-therapy. This hybrid approach may offer a valuable option in children with unrestrictive, longstanding intracardiac defects, advanced pulmonary disease, and otherwise-repairable anatomy.

Conclusions

A valved Potts shunt combined with complete intracardiac repair represents a novel hybrid surgical strategy that may safely expand surgical options and reduce the need for heart-lung transplantation in children with large intracardiac defects and advanced pulmonary vascular disease.

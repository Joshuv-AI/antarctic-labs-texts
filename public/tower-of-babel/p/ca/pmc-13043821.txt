Low complexity sphere decoding with probabilistic radius prediction and hybrid modulation for low latency wireless MIMO systems
Authors: M G Girija, T Sudha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSphere Decoding is an important technique for detecting signals in MIMO systems. It is renowned for its near-optimal performance but is restricted by high computational complexity. This work proposes an improved framework, integrating Gaussian Approximation based radius prediction with hybrid modulation to overcome this limitation. To balance performance and complexity, the adaptive Gaussian model dynamically estimates the decoding radius based on channel conditions. This reduces the number of visited nodes and enhances computational efficiency. Building on this, a hybrid modulation scheme integrating BPSK and 16-QAM is used to further enhance error performance by adjusting modulation levels to varying channel qualities. The proposed method is simulated on an 88 MIMO system using 16-QAM and hybrid modulation using BPSK and 16-QAM modulations. This Hybrid modulated MIMO system with Gaussian Approximation technique attains a drastic enhancement in system performance, reducing BER by up to 99.5%, and node complexity by 77.6% at 5 dB SNR compared to conventional Chi-Square approach. These results highlight the effectiveness of the two-tiered approach as a feasible, low-complexity, and low-latency solution for efficient detection, with promising applications in low-SNR and disaster-affected scenarios. Computationally efficient and low-energy algorithms are also crucial in 6G and IoT applications including massive machine-type communications and ultra-reliable low-latency communications which conserves battery power while supporting real-time functionality.Keywords: MIMO, BPSK, Bit error rate, Low-latency communication, Disaster-affected communication, Future wireless networksSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Multiple input multiple output (MIMO) technology has a significant role in modern wireless communication systems by using multiple antennas to enhance data throughput and improve signal reliability. As wireless networks progress toward 5G and beyond, the importance of efficient MIMO detection becomes even more critical to meet the demands of high-speed, low-latency communication1–5. However, detecting transmitted signals at the receiver becomes a difficult task, especially when higher-order modulation schemes like 16-QAM or 64-QAM are used3. The most reliable detection approach is Maximum Likelihood (ML) detection, but it tends posing significant computational burden in large MIMO systems6.

Sphere Decoding (SD) is a widely recognized technique that ensures near ML performance7 while achieving computational efficiency by employing a bounded search area, inside a sphere. Over time, several techniques have been developed to make Sphere Decoding faster and simpler, each facing its own set of challenges. The K-best algorithm, while reducing complexity, struggles with optimal path selection, especially in massive MIMO systems8,9. Fixed-Complexity Sphere Decoding (FSD) offers reliability, but with compromised performance in comparison to full ML decoding10,11. Lattice Reduction Aided Sphere Decoding (LR-SD)12,13 helps to reduce the computational burden, but can result in suboptimal detection and demands specialized hardware for effective execution. While iterative decoding techniques have the potential to enhance performance, they continue to face challenges with complexity and delay in large-scale systems14. Configurable quasi-optimal Sphere Decoding balances performance and cost with adjustable complexity15, but it may reduce accuracy when complexity is adjusted, affecting performance in difficult MIMO scenarios. SVD-based Sphere Decoding enhances BER but imposes a significant computational overhead16. The regularized SD (RSD) technique17 and reordering schemes such as sorting QR decomposition and V-BLAST18 are two methods for improving computational efficiency but suffereing from lower detection accuracy.

Recent developments in machine learning-driven MIMO detection techniques, including deep unfolding19, neural-assisted sphere decoding, probabilistic graphical models20, and methods based on variational inference21, have substantially improved the efficiency and scalability of MIMO systems. These approaches leverage deep learning and probabilistic methods to enhance decoding accuracy and minimize computational complexity, especially in high-order MIMO systems. By employing these strategies, MIMO detection can be enhanced to process more challenging environments and attain higher efficiency in high-dimensional systems. Finally, the inclusion of machine learning techniques demonstrate significant potential, although it encounters scalability challenges and demands significant training overhead22,23. Consequently, every technique comes with its own difficulties related to performance, complexity, and scalability, which must be avoided for practical application in MIMO systems24.

Thus Sphere Decoding in MIMO systems has made considerable progress over the years, but many obstacles still remain to be overcome. The primary concern is the computational complexity associated with the method, which increases dramatically when the number of antennas and the modulation level increase25–27. Another crucial issue is the estimation of the accurate starting radius, since an inaccurate radius estimate can result in excessive computing load or poor bit error rate performance. The Sphere Decoding methods discussed above encounter challenges including complex processing, long execution time, and system limitations in handling large MIMO systems. Such approaches tend to overestimate the radius causing the decoder to check too many nodes. This results in slower processing and more power usage. These techniques struggle to adjust dynamically to changing channel conditions also. This work integrates Gaussian Approximation based Sphere Decoding with hybrid modulation to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of MIMO system detection. The Gaussian method28,29 predicts the radius more precisely, reduces the number of checks, and keeps the performance close to that of maximum likelihood detection. This approach offers a more efficient and accurate way to manage computational complexity and optimize performance in MIMO systems. Gaussian Approximation method is adopted because it provides a good trade off between computational efficiency and performance, decreasing the number of nodes visited during decoding. Hybrid modulation is introduced to increase the overall efficiency and performance of the Sphere Decoding process. Hybrid modulation is chosen because it integrates the strengths of various modulation techniques such as BPSK and 16-QAM, making the system adaptable to diverse channel conditions. The proposed methods are evaluated on 88 MIMO systems. The simulation results show that Gaussian-based radius prediction considerably decreases the number of computations, while hybrid modulation demonstrates superior error performance. Together, these approaches constitute a practical and scalable solution for real-time MIMO detection.

Section 3 presents the Gaussian approximation method and hybrid modulation in Sphere Decoding. Section 4 describes the system model, outlining the MIMO configuration and the modulation schemes employed. Finally, Section 5 provides simulation results and analysis of key performance parameters, and highlights its implications for future wireless communication systems.

Gaussian approximation method

In MIMO systems, optimizing decoding efficiency relies on accurately predicting the sphere’s radius,26,27,36,37 which defines the decision zone around each received signal vector. The Chi-Square technique provides a simple way to estimate this radius by analyzing signal and noise dispersion, but further refinement is needed to improve BER. It computes the radius based on the chi-square distribution of the noise energy, thus ensuring simplicity, predictability, and channel independence. This technique is not highly effective in low SNR conditions. Because of its conservative characteristics, it often results in more node visits, but it serves as a foundational benchmark in the evaluation process for newer methods. Compared to the proposed Gaussian Approximation method, which adaptively tightens the radius to reduce complexity, the Chi-Square method provides a more conservative yet less efficient option making it a strong baseline for performance comparison. This approach optimizes the initial search radius and adjusts it dynamically during decoding, significantly reducing errors by enhancing the decoder’s ability to differentiate between signals and noise. This tighter radius enables early pruning by restricting the search to a smaller sphere, thereby reducing the number of nodes visited. By utilizing the Gaussian Approximation, Sphere Decoding can more accurately represent noise characteristics, allowing the decoding process to concentrate on signal points most likely to match the transmitted signal.

The Gaussian noise Approximation, using Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN), simplifies the noise model and its impact on signal processing, making it essential for improving Sphere Decoding in MIMO systems. This approach uses the mean and variance of noise to simplify metric computations, such as the noise-adjusted Euclidean distance, thereby significantly reducing the computational complexity of decoding while improving speed and accuracy. This targeted approach enhances BER and SER by improving symbol detection accuracy, while also considerably reducing the number of visited nodes during decoding, leading to more efficient and reliable signal recovery.

The Gaussian approximation is used in Sphere Decoding mainly to predict the preliminary search radius in a way that is efficient and statistically grounded. This enhancement leads to a reduction in computational complexity and an acceleration of decoding speed, all while maintaining a best performance on bit error rate performance. These are the Key Performance Indicators for the MIMO system. By leveraging Gaussian noise properties to determine noise power, the size of the search hypersphere is optimized, focusing on the most likely signal vectors and eliminating improbable ones, thereby simplifying the decoding process. This method also reduces computational load by pruning low-probability paths early in the search. The radius of the search sphere can be initially set based on the variance of the noise and adjusted during the search, commonly starting with a factor of the noise standard deviation determined by simulations or analytical limits. Effect of Gaussian approximation on key Performance Indicators (KPI)

BER and SER optimization

Through careful selection of the initial sphere radius d based on noise characteristics such as variance, the algorithm avoids exploring nodes that are unlikely to yield the optimal solution. Using Gaussian noise statistics, a tighter initial radius ensures that only the most probable subset of lattice points are considered. Enhanced by Gaussian noise approximation, dynamic radius adjustment, and probabilistic pruning improve both the efficiency and accuracy of Sphere Decoding in MIMO systems. This focused search not only reduces computational complexity but also improves detection accuracy, resulting in a lower Bit Error Rate. The radius of the sphere dynamically contracts during decoding, focusing more precisely the search on high-probability regions and reducing decoding errors across a wide range of SNR levels.

Gaussian approximation method also enhances the symbol error rate performance in SD by guiding the selection of an optimal decoding radius. If the radius is too small, the decoder may incorrectly filter out the correct transmitted vector, leading to symbol detection errors. In contrast, an overestimated radius increases complexity in decoding without contributing to SER improvement. By dynamically predicting a well-calibrated radius, the GA method guarantees that the correct symbol vector is contained within the search space while avoiding unnecessary node visits. As a result, SER is considerably reduced across varying noise and channel conditions.

Hybrid modulation

In traditional MIMO systems, standardized modulation techniques such as pure QAM or BPSK are generally utilized across all transmitting antennas to facilitate system design. Higher-order modulation schemes, such as 16-QAM or 64-QAM, provide enhanced data throughput, but exhibit an increased susceptibility to noise and interference. Furthermore, the adoption of uniform high-order modulation markedly enlarges the constellation size, consequently amplifying the computational complexity of detection algorithms, including Sphere Decoding. The sphere decoder tries different symbol combinations to find the one with the smallest Euclidean distance, but the number of possibilities grows very quickly as the modulation order and number of transmit antennas increase. This phenomenon results in increased decoding latency and computational demands, thereby complicating the feasibility of real-time implementation. Hybrid modulation schemes effectively address these challenges by integrating various modulation orders within a single MIMO framework. Each transmit antenna is assigned a specific modulation format based on its channel quality or the system’s performance requirements. For instance, while certain antennas may implement high-order QAM to optimize spectral efficiency, others may utilize lower-order schemes such as BPSK to enhance resistance against noise perturbations. From the standpoint of Sphere Decoding, hybrid modulation markedly diminishes the overall search space. Rather than engaging with uniformly high-order constellations, the decoder works through a mixed set of constellations, where lower-order modulated symbols simplify decoding and allow early pruning of the search tree. In this research, the BPSK-16 QAM combination is used as a hybrid modulation strategy.

In fifth-generation (5G) wireless communication systems, enabling multiple types of service requirements, ranging from ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC) to enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB), requires the use of flexible modulation schemes. The hybrid modulation technique integrating BPSK and 16 QAM is especially significant in this regard. BPSK gives enhanced levels of consistency and strength necessary for important control signals and URLLC applications, where reducing error rates under challenging channel conditions is critical. On the other hand, 16 QAM provides superior spectral efficiency, which is necessary for eMBB services, and provides increased data throughput. Using these two modulation techniques together in a hybrid system enables 5G networks to select the best modulation depending on the state of the channel and type of service, enhancing both reliability and data rates.

When uniform high-order modulation schemes such as 16-QAM is employed across all transmit antennas, the search tree becomes extremely dense, as every transmit stream contributes to an exponentially growing number of candidate symbol combinations. Consequently, the sphere decoder must explore a large portion of this expanded search space, especially in scenarios with poor initial radius estimation or under noisy conditions, leading to a high number of node visits.

Hybrid modulation reduces complexity by strategically combining different modulation orders across the transmit streams. For instance, a subset of antennas may employ lower-order modulation schemes such as BPSK, while the remaining antennas utilize higher-order constellations like 16-QAM. This intentional reduction in modulation order for some streams effectively prunes the search tree, as lower-order constellations contribute fewer branches to the decoding process. Furthermore, the incorporation of hybrid modulation essentially enhances the accuracy of the preliminary radius estimation within the context of Sphere Decoding. This more constrained initial radius enables the decoder to efficiently restrict its search domain, thereby eliminating unlikely candidate paths at an early stage of the traversal. Moreover, during the execution of the tree search, the branches corresponding to lower-order modulated streams can frequently be comprehensively examined or eliminated more rapidly due to their less complex decision boundaries, which accelerates the overall decoding procedure. In conclusion, hybrid modulation effectively diminishes the quantity of nodes accessed during Sphere Decoding and facilitating a more efficient pruning process within the search tree.

Improvement in BER and SER

In MIMO systems, BER is highly affected by channel conditions, modulation schemes, and the presence of noise or interference. Modulation techniques like 16-QAM or 64-QAM tend to experience increased BER at low to moderate SNR levels due to closely spaced constellation points, causing them to be more prone to errors. Techniques such as hybrid modulation can lead to a reduction in BER by adjusting the modulation scheme in response to channel variations and boosting detection accuracy. Specifically, lower-order modulations like BPSK, when integrated with higher-order modulations, enhance the reliability of the system through improved symbol differentiation and decreasing the likelihood of errors in noisy environments.

In the proposed hybrid modulated system the transmit antennas are allocated in equal proportion, with half configured for BPSK and the other half operate with 16-QAM. This antenna arrangement allows the system to benefit from the inherent reliability of BPSK while maintaining the modulation efficiency offered by 16QAM at elevated SNR levels. In traditional MIMO systems that utilize uniform high-order modulation techniques, such as 16-QAM or 64-QAM, the symbol error rate also exhibits a pronounced rise under conditions characterized by moderate to low SNRs. This phenomenon is due to the dense symbol mappings inherent in high-order constellations, which contribute to the decrease in Euclidean distances among constellation points. As noise and channel impairments adversely affect the received signals, the probability of erroneous symbol detection increases due to the overlap of symbols within the constellation. Hybrid modulation addresses this issue by allocating varying modulation orders across MIMO transmission antennas. In this method, lower-order modulation schemes such as BPSK is utilized on a specific subset of transmit antennas, whereas higher-order modulation schemes, including 16-QAM, are designated for alternative streams in order to sustain throughput levels. The incorporation of lower-order modulated streams facilitates enhanced symbol separation within the constellation diagram, thereby rendering these streams inherently more resilient to noise and fading phenomena.

Reduction in execution time

In Sphere Decoding algorithms, the cumulative execution duration exhibits a direct correlation with the quantity of nodes traversed throughout the tree search procedure. Each node specifies either a partial or complete candidate solution, and the decoder allocates computational resources to assess the distance metrics and execute pruning decisions at each node. In uniform high-order modulation schemes, the dense constellation generates a significant amount of symbol candidates, thereby exponentially enlarging the search tree and augmenting the time necessary to investigate valid paths.

Hybrid modulation reduces execution time by markedly narrowing the effective search space. By designating lower-order modulation schemes, such as BPSK, to a subset of transmit antennas, the number of branches at each tier of the decoding tree decreases. Moreover, the implementation of hybrid modulation significantly enhances the accuracy of the preliminary radius estimation in Sphere Decoding. The utilization of lower-order streams, characterized by their superior adaptability to noise and increased symbol differentiation, facilitates the establishment of a more constrained initial search radius. This preliminary pruning process results in a diminished number of nodes that are investigated further within the tree structure. The combined effect of a diminished initial candidate pool and effective pruning mechanisms results in a substantial reduction of overall execution duration.

As the execution time is influenced by the hardware configuration and its implementation method, in this work complexity analysis is performed that is independent of hardware. In SD, complexity in computation is typically measured by the mean number of nodes visited during tree search.

Results and discussions

Comprehensive simulations were conducted to evaluate the efficiency of the proposed integration of Gaussian Approximation and hybrid modulation in MIMO Sphere Decoding, in terms of BER, SER, execution time, and complexity reduction. This work uses an 88 MIMO configuration and analyzes the performance impact of integrating Gaussian Approximation with hybrid modulation. In the hybrid modulation scheme, half of the transmit antennas use BPSK and the other half use 16-QAM to balance reliability and spectral efficiency. Hybrid modulation is utilized to optimize system performance by combining the advantages of BPSK and 16-QAM leading to enhanced overall efficiency and reliability. At increased SNR levels, both k best SD and Gaussian method techniques show analogous SER characteristics, converging due to the decreasing influence of noise at higher SNRs. The Gaussian method evaluates a tighter initial radius by adapting to the noise distribution, which enables the SD to focus on the most likely symbol vectors. Thus, the probability of wrong symbol detection is decreased, thereby reducing the SER.

Figure 3c shows the mean number of nodes visited against SNR values as part of the Sphere Decoding process in a 88 MIMO configuration using 16-QAM, analyzed under two discussed radius prediction methodologies. With an increase in SNR, both methodologies provide a consistent reduction in node visits, but in Gaussian method it is reduced, from 67(for Chi-Square method) to 17 at 5 dB, indicating reduced search complexity. This represents a reduction of approximately 74.6 %. The Gaussian Approximation method reduces the number of visiting nodes in Sphere Decoding by predicting a tighter initial radius, thereby narrowing the search space and lowering computational complexity. This marked reduction in complexity, especially at lower SNR levels, emphasizes its proficiency in lowering the decoding workload, consequently positioning it as a resource efficient and scalable approach for real-time MIMO decoding.

Figure 3d shows the execution time comparison between SD methods using Chi-Square and Gaussian Approximation techniques across different SNR levels. As the SNR increases, both methods exhibit a notable reduction in execution time due to lesser candidate nodes being explored during decoding. At lower SNRs, especially up to 5dB execution time shows a significant reduction, gradually reducing as the SNR is increased. However, the Gaussian Approximation method consistently outperforms the Chi-Square approach, especially at low SNR values where the computational complexity is highest. At 5 dB, the execution time is reduced by approximately 47.69%, from 536.18 s to 280.45 s. Gaussian radius prediction reduces this by effectively narrowing the initial search space, thereby avoiding unnecessary node visits. This highlights the efficiency of this approach in reducing decoding time while maintaining performance, demonstrating it a more suitable choice for real-time MIMO detection in noisy environments.

The listed microsecond values represent the time taken for each detection for each frame in an MIMO system employing sphere decoding, where the detection cycle encompasses signal preprocessing, initializing the radius, then enumerating the tree with pruning, and final hard decision on symbols. However, hybrid modulation consistently exceeds 16-QAM, particularly at lower SNR thresholds, emphasizing its superiority in minimizing errors and increasing reliability, particularly in adverse communication scenarios. In high-order QAM systems, the dense constellation structure leads to increased susceptibility to noise, especially at lower and medium SNR ranges. Even a slight portion of BPSK symbols in the hybrid modulation scheme considerably reduces the overall BER, due to BPSK’s high robustness against noise and fading. It also affects the weighted BER performance under similar SNR conditions. Additionally, the use of Gaussian based probabilistic radius prediction in sphere decoding significantly improves detection accuracy while reducing BER.

Figure 4b illustrates the SER performance as a function of SNR for an 8x8 MIMO system utilizing two distinct modulation methodologies: basic 16-QAM (depicted by the blue line) and Hybrid modulation (comprising BPSK and 16-QAM) (illustrated by the red line). The graph shows that Hybrid modulation markedly surpasses the performance of basic 16-QAM at diminished SNRs, thereby yielding a reduced SER. As the SNR increases, both modulation techniques exhibit enhanced performance; however, the hybrid modulation consistently retains a pronounced advantage, especially in adverse low SNR scenarios. This underscores the resilience of hybrid modulation in improving system error rate performance, particularly in suboptimal channel conditions. Simple QAM show increased symbol error rates caused by the efficient arrangement of symbols within the constellation space, thereby boosting the probability of erroneous symbol interpretation in the presence of noise. The hybrid modulation framework, by changing the modulation order across various transmit streams, effectively reduces the average SER. Lower-order modulation streams play a critical role in this enhancement, as their stable constellation structure inherently decreases the undesirable impacts of channel imperfections. Thus, the hybrid modulation method achieves superior SER performance over a broad spectrum of SNR values in comparison to the conventional QAM system.

The reduction in the quantity of nodes traversed during the Sphere Decoding procedure is intrinsically linked to execution time and is shown in Fig. 4c. At low SNRs,there is a considerable reduction in the number of visiting nodes, demonstrating its significant role in reducing complexity. As the SNR increases, the reduction in the number of visiting nodes becomes less noticeable. Uniform Quadrature Amplitude Modulation systems, attributable to their expansive and densely configured constellation architectures, yield a search tree that is both intricate and densely populated. Hybrid modulation methodologies effectively induce sparsity within the search tree by integrating lower-order modulation streams, which contribute a reduced number of branches at each level. This structural optimization enables the decoder to eliminate improbable paths at earlier stages of the search procedure, consequently decreasing the overall count of nodes that are visited.

One of the greatest advantage of hybrid modulation is the reduction in execution time throughout the process of Sphere Decoding and is presented in Fig. 4d. At low SNRs, there is a substantial reduction in execution time, resulting in a significant decrease in computational time. In uniform high-order modulation frameworks, the decoder is needed to navigate a large search space, leading to a elevated computational cost and increased decoding latency. Hybrid modulation efficiently reduces the constellation size by integrating both lower and higher order streams, reducing the number of candidate symbol vectors for the decoder to assess. In addition, improved initial radius estimation, boosted by the resilience of lower-order streams, facilitates early pruning of the decoding tree. Collectively, these factors significantly reduce the mean decoding time, making hybrid modulation more suitable for real-time and low-latency applications.

Conclusion

In this research, a two-tier enhancement to conventional Sphere Decoding was developed, integrating both Gaussian Approximation based radius prediction and the hybrid modulation scheme. It is observed that dynamic radius adjustment using Gaussian Approximation considerably decreased decoding complexity, while hybrid modulation based on channel information improved BER and SER performance. For an 88 MIMO system with Gaussian Approximation based SD and hybrid modulated MIMO system with Gaussian Approximation based SD produced noticeable gains in accuracy, efficiency, and computational cost. These results show that the proposed method provides a reliable and scalable solution for practical MIMO detection. It is suitable for real-time systems with high data rate requirements, low-latency applications in advanced wireless networks such as 5G and beyond, and low-SNR conditions commonly found in disaster-affected areas. Furthermore, the simple architecture and power-efficient design make it suitable for IoT devices and applications in smart cities. Building on this, further research can be extended to higher-order MIMO configurations and modulation schemes, enabling even more robust and efficient solutions for future wireless communication systems. The introduced Gaussian-supported Sphere Decoding with hybrid modulation, while achieving BER gains for 88 MIMO, is limited by higher computational complexity, scalability issues to massive MIMO, and dependence on precise channel estimation.

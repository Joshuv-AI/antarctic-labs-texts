Fluoride effect on renal and hepatic functions: A comprehensive decade review of In vitro and In vivo studies
Authors: Sirigala Lavanya, Kasirajan Hema Shree, Prathiba Ramani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis systematic review evaluates the toxicological effects of exposure to fluoride on renal and hepatic functioning. It compiles both in vitro & in vivo studies across the last decade. By highlighting the potential health risks, the review aims to raise awareness and encourage a more attentive approach to fluoride use and exposure, instilling a sense of concern and vigilance in the audience.MethodologyA thorough literature search covered relevant studies from 2013 to 2023, both experimental (in vitro) and animal (in vivo) research. The data extraction process focused on critical aspects such as sample size, methodologies used, key findings, and conclusions regarding fluoride's impact on kidney and liver functions. The review also includes a detailed analysis of gene expression, KEGG pathways, and STRING analysis, further enhancing the reliability of the results.ResultsSignificant renal and liver damage were demonstrated in animal models exposed to high fluoride doses for long duration. Ferulic acid was found to mitigate fluoride-induced oxidative damage. The combined mean difference across studies was 6.52 [-5.22, 18.26], indicating high heterogeneity, which reflects diverse methodologies and findings. Notably, epigenetic and immune system impacts were underscored, with gene analysis identifying several genes involved in oxidative stress response, apoptosis, and inflammation pathways.ConclusionThe findings emphasize the substantial evidence of its harmful effects on renal and liver functions at high exposure levels. Some studies indicate minimal impact, others demonstrate significant organ damage. The results underline the necessity for continued research to establish safe fluoride exposure limits and comprehend the mechanisms underlying its toxicity.

Introduction

Fluoride in drinking water and dental products provides well-established benefits for dental health.1 However, excessive fluoride intake can harm various organ systems, particularly the kidneys and liver.2 Over the past ten years, a great deal of study has been conducted both in experimental and medical facilities to examine the effects of fluoride poisoning on liver and kidney functioning. According to studies, exposure to high levels of fluoride can have adverse impacts on these organs, such as disturbances in both kidneys and liver functioning, arrest of cell cycles in renal tissues, severe histological abnormalities in liver or kidney tissues, and defects in urine concentration.3 Moreover, studies have demonstrated the link between exposure to fluoride and epigenetic mechanisms, emphasizing the necessity for additional study on the epigenetic regulation of fluoride toxicity. High fluoride exposure has been linked to lower IQ in epidemiological research, although these findings have been limited in their consideration of confounding variables. In vitro tests have shown effects of high fluoride concentrations on human neural cells.4,5

The influence of fluoride on the liver and kidneys encompasses intricate interactions at the cellular and molecular levels, resulting in diverse physiological and biochemical disturbances. Prolonged exposure to fluoride induces histopathological changes in kidney tissues, including tubular degeneration, glomerular atrophy, and interstitial fibrosis. These structural modifications impede the kidneys' filtration capacity, potentially culminating in renal failure over time 4. Fluoride causes oxidative stress within renal cells by creating reactive oxygen molecules (ROS), which damage cellular components such as proteins, lipids, and DNA. This oxidative stress affects normal cellular functioning and triggers inflammatory responses, worsening renal impairment.4,5

The nephrotoxic effects of fluoride encompass inhibiting crucial enzyme activities necessary for renal function. Fluoride hinders enzymes involved in energy metabolism, particularly ATPases, thereby diminishing energy production and compromising cellular functions. Moreover, fluoride disrupts the equilibrium of electrolytes, including sodium, potassium, and calcium, essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis and proper kidney function. Prolonged fluoride exposure has been associated with the modification of genes linked to apoptosis, leading to excessive renal cell death, contributing to the loss of functional nephron units, and diminishing overall kidney efficiency. Furthermore, fluoride exposure has been correlated with the onset of renal fibrosis, characterized by excessive extracellular matrix deposition. This fibrotic response stiffens kidney tissues, impairs filtration, and can ultimately lead to chronic kidney disease.4,5

Fluoride can cause serious injury to the kidneys because of its involvement in excretion. Extended exposure to high fluoride levels can overburden the kidneys, making it more difficult for them to eliminate the fluoride, which can result in an accumulation of fluoride within renal tissues and increased toxicity. Numerous studies conducted on a population have demonstrated a correlation between elevated exposure to fluorides and a greater incidence of kidney disorders, including persistent kidney damage and renal insufficiency. These findings emphasize how important it is to keep an eye on fluoride exposure in order to safeguard renal health.6

Fluoride exposure can lead to a range of detrimental effects in the liver due to fluoride toxicity. The liver, responsible for detoxification processes, is particularly susceptible to fluoride-induced oxidative stress. Fluoride exposure produces reactive oxygen species, overwhelming the liver's antioxidant defenses and causing lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage. Consequently, this oxidative damage disrupts liver cell functions and triggers inflammatory responses. Fluoride also impairs liver cells' mitochondrial function, resulting in decreased energy synthesis and elevated reactive oxygen species generation. Notably, fluoride's overall hepatic toxicity is attributed to mitochondrial malfunction, which is a significant element in the development of several liver disorders. The function of liver enzymes associated with detoxification, especially cytochrome P450 enzymes, is impacted by chronic fluoride exposure. These disruptions impair the liver's ability to metabolize and eliminate toxins, potentially accumulating harmful substances and further liver damage.7

Histopathological investigations have demonstrated that fluoride exposure can lead to liver cell necrosis, characterized by the demise of hepatocytes. This cellular demise can compromise liver function and contribute to the onset of liver ailments. Fluoride-induced liver toxicity also encompasses disruptions in lipid metabolism, resulting in the accumulation of lipids within hepatic cells. This state, known as hepatic steatosis or fatty liver, can advance to more severe liver conditions, including steatohepatitis and cirrhosis. Prolonged exposure to fluoride has been linked to changes in gene expression associated with inflammation, fibrosis, and cell proliferation in the liver. The development of liver illnesses is influenced by these molecular alterations, highlighting the importance of understanding the involvement of genetic variables in fluoride-induced toxicity.8

Epidemiological studies have established a correlation between high fluoride exposure and an elevated risk of liver diseases, including liver fibrosis and cirrhosis. These findings underscore the imperative for implementing public health measures to restrict fluoride exposure and safeguard liver health, especially among vulnerable demographic groups. Elevated fluoride levels may disrupt the liver's capacity to regulate glucose metabolism, leading to disturbances in glucose homeostasis and an increased susceptibility to metabolic disorders, such as insulin resistance and diabetes. Furthermore, fluoride exposure can impede the liver's synthesis of crucial proteins, such as albumin and clotting factors, affecting essential physiological processes, including nutrient transport, immune responses, and blood coagulation.9,10

Unquestionably, the comprehensive exploration of fluoride's impact on liver and kidney functions underscores the urgency of conducting thorough research to elucidate the mechanisms of toxicity and devise strategies to alleviate these adverse health consequences. Through the seamless integration of results from epidemiological, in vivo, and in vitro investigations, we may improve our understanding of the toxicity of fluoride and mold public health strategies to lessen its effects on the kidneys and liver. The systematic review of fluoride toxicity's influence on renal and hepatic functions aims to collate the existing literature to underscore the intricacy of its effects on physiological systems and the continuous need for research, thereby making a significant contribution to the field of toxicology.

Aim

The objective of this review is to synthesize results from both in vivo and in vitro investigations in order to critically analyse and summarise contemporary research upon the toxicological impacts of fluoride on various organs.

Methodology

Search strategy

An extensive systematic search was carried out painstakingly throughout a number of scholarly databases in order to locate original research publications and papers concerning the effects of fluoride exposure on the liver and kidneys. The databases included PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and Web of Science, covering publications from January 2014 to April 2024. Back reference searches were also performed to ensure all relevant studies were captured, even those not yet indexed in the primary databases, thus ensuring the review's comprehensive coverage.

In PubMed, a combination of MeSH terms and keywords such as "fluoride toxicity,""renal function,""liver function,""in vitro," and "in vivo" were used. Boolean operators like AND, OR, and NOT refined the search results to include studies relevant to the topic. Similarly, Scopus was queried using the same set of keywords. The search strings were tailored to Scopus' search capabilities, incorporating title, abstract, and keyword fields to ensure all relevant studies were captured.

Google Scholar was utilized for its extensive coverage of academic and gray literature. The search employed the keywords above, with results filtered to include only articles published within the specified date range. Advanced search options narrowed the results to those most pertinent to the study's focus. In Web of Science, a combination of topic searches and citation tracking was used. Keywords and Boolean operators filtered results by topic, while citation tracking identified influential studies and relevant research articles that might have yet to be retrieved through the initial keyword search.

Back reference searches were performed on the reference lists of identified articles to ensure the inclusion of all relevant studies. This process involved reviewing the reference lists of all included studies to identify any additional articles that fit the inclusion criteria and performing manual searches for these articles to obtain full-text access and evaluate their relevance to the research topic.

Search keywords

The search employed keywords according to PICO, such as "fluoride toxicity,""renal function,""liver function,""in vitro," and "in vivo." Boolean operators like AND, OR, and NOT combined and excluded terms, ensuring a focused and relevant search. For example, search strings included "fluoride toxicity" AND "renal function" AND ("in vitro" OR "in vivo") and "fluoride toxicity" AND "liver function" AND ("in vitro" OR "in vivo").

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The search criteria were original research articles and systematic reviews providing full-text access, focusing specifically on the impact of fluoride exposure on renal and hepatic functions, and studies conducted in vitro (cell culture models) or in vivo (animal subjects). Exclusion criteria included studies examining fluoride's effects on organs or tissues other than the kidneys and liver and duplicate publications.

Data extraction

Sixty-one records were found in the initial search. These records' titles and abstracts were filtered according to the criteria for inclusion and exclusion in order to determine their relevancy. Complete texts of items that might be of interest were obtained for a more thorough assessment. Out of the initial 61 records, ten articles were deemed appropriate to the research topic. Because the data were heterogeneous, integrating the results was necessary for a more comprehensive study, and three of these investigations were eventually included in a meta-analysis.

Review process

Two independent observers rigorously reviewed the selected articles. A third reviewer resolved discrepancies between the reviewers using kappa statistics to ensure inter-rater reliability. The kappa statistic measures the agreement between reviewers beyond chance, providing a quantitative measure of consistency.The features of the listed studies were summed together using descriptive statistics. A random-effects model was utilized to aggregate the effect sizes that were computed for every survey. Sensitivity analysis were performed to evaluate how reliable the findings were. With the aid of Egger's test and funnel plots, publication bias was assessed.

By adhering to this meticulous search methodology, the review sought to offer an extensive comprehension of the extant literature concerning the impact of exposure to fluoride on kidney and hepatic functions, guaranteeing the identification and incorporation of all pertinent studies.

Meta-analysis

Three studies have been incorporated in the meta-analysis due to the varied nature of the data. To produce a pooled assessment of the impact of exposure to fluoride on kidney and hepatic functions, the results of separate research were combined in this method. Revman 5.0 statistical software was utilized to conduct the meta-analysis, which enabled a methodical and rigorous combination of data.

Data preparation and extraction

Before performing the meta-analysis, detailed data extraction was conducted. Essential information such as study design, sample size, fluoride dosage, duration of exposure, and specific findings related to renal and hepatic toxicity were extracted from each study. This information was then standardized to ensure consistency across studies. Where necessary, the authors were contacted for additional data or clarification to ensure the completeness and accuracy of the extracted data.

Assessing heterogeneity

The I2 statistic, which measures the percentage of variance among studies attributable to heterogeneity instead of chance, was used to evaluate the degree of heterogeneity among the studies that were included. Higher values indicate greater heterogeneity, whereas an I2 value below 0 % indicates no observable heterogeneity. The I2 statistic was computed in this meta-analysis to ascertain the degree of variability across the research findings.

Statistical model

In light of this heterogeneity, the meta-analysis utilized a random-effects model. Random-effects models take variability within and across studies into account, in contrast to fixed-effects models, which presume that all research estimates the same baseline effect. This approach provides a more generalised estimate of the impact size by assuming that the investigations included in the meta-analyses are a random sample from a wider study population.

Combining findings

The results from the individual studies were pooled to calculate an overall effect size. This was done using weighted averages, where the weight assigned to each survey was inversely proportional to its variance. The random-effects model was implemented using statistical software, calculating the combined effect size and its corresponding 95 % confidence interval. The software also produced forest plots to visually represent the individual study results and the overall pooled estimate.

Sensitivity analysis

To evaluate the reliability of the meta-analysis findings, sensitivity studies were carried out. To ascertain the influence of each investigation on the total effect size, this required performing the meta-analysis again while methodically removing each study. Sensitivity analysis guarantee that no single study has an undue impact on the conclusions and assist in identifying investigations that significantly influence the pooled results. Evaluation of Publication Bias:

Egger's test and funnel plots were used to assess publication bias. The link among study size & effect size is visually represented by funnel plots, where asymmetry indicates the possibility of publication bias. An approach for statistically identifying this kind of bias is the Egger's test. In order to prevent the results of the meta-analysis from being biased by the selective presentation of papers that yielded good results, both approaches were used.

Interpretation of results

The meta-analysis's pooled estimate offered a thorough assessment of the impact of exposure to fluoride on the liver and kidneys. The quality, sample sizes, and study designs of the research included were taken into consideration when interpreting the results.Any significant heterogeneity identified was explored to understand the underlying causes, such as differences in study populations, fluoride dosages, or exposure durations. By conducting this detailed meta-analysis, the review aimed to synthesize the existing evidence on fluoride toxicity and provide a robust estimate of its impact on renal and hepatic functions. Using a random-effects model and thorough sensitivity analyses ensured the conclusions were reliable and applicable to a broader context.Kegg and String analysis was done using the Shinygo database and stringdb.org software.

Results

Numerous research on the impact of exposure to fluoride on different body systems were assessed as part of the systematic review. Excess fluoride exposure was found to harm the liver, kidneys, and immune system, causing immune abnormalities, oxidative damage, and potential epigenetic changes (Priyankar Pal et al., 2022; Das S et al., 2023; Satheeswaran et al., 2018) (Table 1, Table 2). Ferulic acid demonstrated protective properties against fluoride toxicity by reducing oxidative stress (Das S et al., 2023)(Table 1, Table 2). Based on current exposure, European fluoride levels were deemed safe, but further research on low-level exposure and developmental impacts is recommended (Sabine Guth et al., 2018)(Table 1, Table 2). While some studies found no significant liver damage from fluoride, others reported varying degrees of liver and kidney harm, indicating the need for ongoing research to fully understand fluoride's impact (Sirigala Lavanya et al., 2023; Thanusha Perera et al., 2018)(Table 1, Table 2). Overall, the review underscores the toxic effects of high fluoride levels and highlights the necessity for further investigation to determine safe exposure limits and protective measures.

Analysis of results of included studies.

The meta-analysis examined the effects of fluoride exposure across three studies, revealing varied results. Kas Usuda et al. (2016)(Table 1, Table 2) showed a mean difference of 15.05 [13.87, 16.23], indicating significant renal damage due to high fluoride doses. Sirigala Lavanya et al. (2023) reported a negligible mean difference of −0.19 [−0.34, −0.04], suggesting no significant liver function impairment in subjects with dental fluorosis. Thanusha Perera et al. (2018) showed a mean difference of 4.66 [0.90, 8.42], highlighting mild liver inflammation and damage due to fluoride exposure(Table 1, Table 2).

The combined results of these studies yielded a total mean difference of 6.52 [−5.22, 18.26], which was not statistically significant (Z = 1.09, P = 0.28). However, the analysis exhibited substantial heterogeneity (Tau2 = 106.29; Chi2 = 632.09, df = 2, P < 0.00001; I2 = 100 %), indicating high variability among the studies. The findings suggest that while some studies show significant organ damage due to fluoride exposure, others do not, emphasizing the need for further research to clarify fluoride's impact on health.

Discussion

Priyanka Pal et al., in their article, discussed the molecular details of fluoride-mediated renal damage.11 Research revealed structural abnormalities in the renal system, including dilated renal tubules, lobulated and shrunken glomeruli, inflammatory cells infiltrating renal tissues, macular degeneration, and structural changes in the proximal, distal, and collecting tubules. Fluoride also affects lipid peroxidation and antioxidant activity, which both change redox equilibrium in renal tissues. Research also revealed that fluoride altered renal function, as evidenced by elevated urea, creatinine, Na+, and K+ levels, which suggest the kidney's incapacity to eliminate harmful compounds. They came to the conclusion that fluoride poisoning can affect the renal system. Renal tissues have been shown to exhibit cell cycle stoppage and abnormalitiesinurinaryconcentration.

The toxicity of fluoride on liver tissue was also covered in the article. According to the article, high levels of fluoride have an impact on hepatic proteins, which are linked to energy metabolism. Fluoride can also cause changes in mitochondrial functions and morphological changes in the liver tissues, such as necrosis, vacuolization, fatty changes, dilated central veins, and hepatocyte drop off. Fluoride lowers the liver's antioxidant defence, which leads to increased lipid peroxidation and the development of oxidative stress. Overexposure to fluoride also modifies the activities of functioning enzymes in the liver.12

Their research implies that when assessing fluoride use and suitable levels in public health interventions, there may be possible nephron- and hepatological health risks to take into account. Adolescents in the United States may have complex alterations in liver and kidney-related markers as a result of fluoride exposure.12

Studies conducted both in vivo & in vitro have repeatedly provided evidence that fluoride can have harmful effects on the liver and kidneys. The primary mechanism appears to be oxidative stress, which leads to cellular and tissue damage. In vivo studies further validate these findings, showing significant histopathological and functional impairments in animal models. However, variability in study design, such as differences in fluoride doses and exposure durations, highlights the need for standardized protocols to assess human risk better.11,12

To the best of our understanding, this is the first comprehensive study and meta-analysis that evaluates and presents proof of fluoride-induced apoptosis in experimental animals' non-skeletal tissues. We made sure we located all pertinent studies by doing thorough searches across four databases. The review procedure was conducted in accordance with standard protocols. The variability of research and the paucity of published articles assessing fluoride's renal toxicity are among the study's weaknesses. The observed dissimilarity among the studies may be due to variations in the types of tissue investigated, the animal species studied, the time and amount of exposure, the mode of exposure, and the biochemical assay methods used.

Conclusion

The harmful effects of fluoride on liver and kidney functions are highlighted in this comprehensive review, which is supported by recent in vivo and in vitro research conducted over the last ten years. While beneficial at low concentrations, excessive fluoride exposure poses a significant risk to kidney and liver health. Future research should aim to establish clear exposure thresholds and investigate potential protective strategies against fluoride-induced toxicity.

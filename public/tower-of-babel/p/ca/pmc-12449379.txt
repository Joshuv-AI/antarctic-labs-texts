Population pharmacokinetic analysis of fluorouracil and oxaliplatin in the absence or presence of zolbetuximab in locally advanced unresectable or metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma
Authors: Akihiro Yamada, Jianning Yang, Peter L Bonate, Nakyo Heo, Srinivasu Poondru
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeZolbetuximab, a monoclonal antibody targeting claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2), is approved in combination with chemotherapy for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, unresectable, advanced or recurrent gastric cancer (in Japan) and in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy for first-line locally advanced unresectable or metastatic HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction (G/GEJ) adenocarcinoma (in geographies including but not limited to the US, Europe, and China). Noncompartmental analysis (NCA) was previously used to evaluate the effect of zolbetuximab on pharmacokinetics (PK) of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) and oxaliplatin; however, limitations of NCA confounded the results. This study utilized population pharmacokinetic (PopPK) analysis to address these limitations.MethodsIn Cohort 2 of the phase 2 ILUSTRO study (NCT03505320), patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive G/GEJ adenocarcinoma received zolbetuximab with modified folinic acid, 5-FU, and oxaliplatin. PopPK models were developed to evaluate the impact of zolbetuximab on PK of 5-FU and oxaliplatin (including simultaneous analysis of free and total platinum).ResultsPK of 5-FU was adequately described by a 1-compartment model with zero-order input and first-order elimination. PK of free and total platinum was simultaneously described by a 3-compartment model with zero-order input, first-order elimination, and time-dependent free fraction. No impact of zolbetuximab on 5-FU PK or on systemic clearance or free fraction of oxaliplatin in plasma was observed. The effect of zolbetuximab on oxaliplatin distribution volume (12.3% decrease) was statistically significant but not considered clinically relevant.ConclusionPopPK analysis suggests no effect of zolbetuximab on 5-FU or oxaliplatin PK.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00280-025-04808-2.Keywords: Population pharmacokinetics, Zolbetuximab, 5-Fluorouracil, Oxaliplatin, Gastric/gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma

Introduction

Zolbetuximab is a recombinant chimeric (mouse/human) immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody directed against claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2), a tight junction protein expressed in most gastric and gastroesophageal junction (G/GEJ) adenocarcinomas [1, 2]. Binding of zolbetuximab to CLDN18.2-positive G/GEJ adenocarcinoma cells promotes cell death via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and complement-dependent cytotoxicity [1, 2].

Zolbetuximab became the first approved CLDN18.2-targeted therapy in March 2024, when it was approved in Japan in combination with chemotherapy for the treatment of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, unresectable, advanced or recurrent gastric cancer. In addition, zolbetuximab has been approved in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy for the first-line treatment of adults with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive G/GEJ adenocarcinoma in other geographies, including but not limited to the United States, Europe, and China. These approvals of zolbetuximab in combination with chemotherapy highlight the importance of determining the effect of zolbetuximab administration on the pharmacokinetics (PK) of chemotherapeutic agents.

Zolbetuximab in combination with chemotherapy was evaluated in the phase 2 clinical trial FAST (NCT01630083) [3] and is currently being evaluated in the ongoing clinical trials ILUSTRO (phase 2; NCT03505320) [4], SPOTLIGHT (phase 3; NCT03504397) [1], GLOW (phase 3; NCT03653507) [2], and GLEAM (phase 2; NCT03816163) [5]. The potential impact of zolbetuximab on chemotherapy PK was evaluated using noncompartmental analysis (NCA) in the ILUSTRO study. Patients with locally advanced unresectable or metastatic G/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors were HER2-negative and CLDN18.2-positive received zolbetuximab in combination with mFOLFOX6 (modified folinic acid [leucovorin], 5-fluorouracil [5-FU], and oxaliplatin) in Cohort 2 of the ILUSTRO study. NCA indicated that concomitant administration of 5-FU with zolbetuximab did not have an impact on the systemic exposure of 5-FU [4]. In contrast, coadministration of oxaliplatin with zolbetuximab appeared to slightly increase the dose-normalized area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) of total and free platinum (by ~ 10–16%) and raise the dose-normalized maximum concentration (Cmax) of free platinum (by ~ 30%), but not total platinum [4]. The apparent increase in total and free platinum exposure with zolbetuximab coadministration did not appear to affect the safety profile of mFOLFOX6 and was not considered clinically significant [4]. However, significant variability and uncertainties in the clinical data resulted in wide 90% confidence intervals for geometric least squares mean ratios determined by NCA. In addition, due to the relatively long half-life of zolbetuximab, the drug-drug interaction investigation employed a 1-sequence crossover design because a randomized crossover design was deemed unethical and impractical; however, the limitations of the 1-sequence crossover design may have confounded the results. To address these limitations, in this study, population pharmacokinetic (PopPK) analysis was used as an alternative approach to evaluate the impact of zolbetuximab on the PK of 5-FU or oxaliplatin.

Materials and methods

ILUSTRO Cohort 2

This analysis used plasma 5-FU and oxaliplatin (free and total platinum) concentration data from patients in Cohort 2 of the phase 2 ILUSTRO study, which enrolled patients with previously untreated, locally advanced unresectable or metastatic G/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors were HER2-negative and CLDN18.2-positive (defined as ≥ 75% of tumor cells with moderate to strong membranous CLDN18 staining intensity) [4]. Methods for NCA in ILUSTRO Cohort 2 have been reported previously [4].

Patients in ILUSTRO Cohort 2 received zolbetuximab and mFOLFOX6 in 42-day cycles. Zolbetuximab (800 mg/m2 loading dose followed by 600 mg/m2 doses) was administered as a minimum 2-h intravenous (IV) infusion on Days 1 and 22 in each cycle except for Cycle 1, in which zolbetuximab was administered on Day 3 instead of Day 1 to allow for collection of 5-FU and oxaliplatin PK samples in the absence of zolbetuximab. Patients received mFOLFOX6 (oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2 IV infusion concurrent with folinic acid 400 mg/m2 IV infusion over 2 h, followed by 5-FU 400 mg/m2 IV bolus over 5–15 min, then 5-FU 2400 mg/m2 continuous IV infusion over 46–48 h) on Days 1, 15, and 29 for up to 4 cycles (up to 12 mFOLFOX6 treatments). Beginning at Cycle 5, patients could continue to receive 5-FU and folinic acid along with zolbetuximab per investigator discretion. If zolbetuximab and mFOLFOX6 treatment were scheduled on the same day, mFOLFOX6 treatment followed zolbetuximab infusion.

Samples for 5-FU PK analysis were collected predose and at 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 24, and 48 h after the start of dosing during Cycles 1 (chemotherapy alone) and 2 (chemotherapy plus zolbetuximab). Samples for oxaliplatin PK analysis were collected predose; at the end of infusion; and at 0.5, 1, 2, 5, and 24 h after the start of dosing during Cycles 1 and 2. Concentration of 5-FU was determined using a validated liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry method with a lower limit of quantification of 5.5 ng/mL. Oxaliplatin concentration was determined using a validated inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry method with a lower limit of quantification of 1 ng platinum (pt)/mL for total platinum and 3 ng pt/mL for free platinum.

The ILUSTRO study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, and International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice Guideline and received approval from an institutional review board. Written informed consent was provided by all patients.

Base model development

Model structure for 5-FU

The PopPK model for 5-FU was built using previously reported 1- and 2-compartment models [6–11]. The base model for 5-FU was identified by comparing 1- and 2-compartment models with first-order elimination.

Model structure for oxaliplatin

The PopPK model for the free platinum concentration-time profile was built using 2- and 3-compartment models that were previously reported [12–17]. These publications did not include models for the total platinum concentration-time profile, and no concentration-dependent protein binding was reported.

In the current analysis, a PopPK model was developed to simultaneously describe both free and total platinum concentration-time profiles. The base model for the free platinum concentration-time profile was identified by comparing 2- and 3-compartment models with first-order elimination. The identified structural model was then used for the simultaneous analysis of free and total platinum concentration-time profiles.

Software

Modeling, simulations, and postprocessing were conducted in the Linux cloud computing environment Metworx (version 21.08.3; Metrum Research Group, Tariffville, CT, USA). The following software within Metworx was used for this analysis: NONMEM® (version 7.5.0; ICON Development Solutions, Ellicott City, MD, USA), Perl-speaks-NONMEM (version 4.9.0; GNU General Public License version 2), Fortran (version 12.0.4; GNU General Public License), R (version 4.0.3; GNU General Public License version 3), Pirana (version 2.9.9; Certara, Radnor, PA, USA), and SAS (version 9.4; SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). NCA was conducted using Phoenix WinNonlin™ (version 8.0; Certara).

Data handling

Concentrations below the quantification limit (BQL) were imputed to 0 in the generated analysis dataset. When analyzing data for the NCA with WinNonlin, zero concentration data were ignored. In the PopPK analysis, zero concentration data were omitted with NONMEM using the IGNORE option. Concentrations with missing sampling time information were excluded.

Goodness of fit

Models were evaluated by assessing goodness-of-fit plots, parameter uncertainty (percentage of relative residual error < 50%), shrinkage (ideally < 30%), sensitivity analysis, model stability, and simulation/predictive performance.

Predictive performance

Visual predictive checks (VPCs) were used to determine if the developed model could simulate data consistent with observations. A total of 500 simulated trial replicates were generated using the model parameter estimates, random draws of η (systematic deviation of a parameter from the population mean) and ε (residual), and pertinent independent variables from the design of studies included in the analysis. Uncertainty in the population parameter estimates was ignored. The quality of the model predictions was determined based on the agreement between the intervals of the simulation-based and observed statistics. Because dosing for 5-FU and oxaliplatin was based on body surface area, prediction-corrected VPC [18] was used to reduce the potential bias in the predictive performance evaluation caused by individualized doses (in mg) received by each patient in the dataset.

Robustness

Uncertainty (precision) in the parameter estimates was obtained using the variance-covariance matrix output from the $COV step (MATRIX = R or S) in NONMEM. The uncertainty or precision of parameters was presented as the relative standard error (%RSE; i.e., the standard error divided by the mean of the estimate and expressed as a percentage).

The condition number was reported as the ratio of the largest to smallest eigenvalue reported from the PRINT = E option in the $COV step in NONMEM. Because a condition number > 1000 generally indicates a severely ill-conditioned model [19], it was avoided during model development and evaluation.

Results

Source of concentration data

Plasma 5-FU and oxaliplatin concentration data from all 21 patients enrolled in ILUSTRO Cohort 2 were used in this analysis. Baseline clinical and demographic characteristics were reported previously [4].

5-FU

Dataset for 5-FU model development

The original dataset for 5-FU included 206 non-BQL postdose concentrations (108 in the absence of zolbetuximab and 98 in the presence of zolbetuximab), 2 non-BQL concentrations from before the initial dose, and 35 BQL postdose concentrations. During base model development, 12 non-BQL postdose concentrations, the 2 non-BQL concentrations from before the initial dose, and the 35 BQL postdose concentrations were excluded due to implausible PK profiles or dosing records.

Base model for 5-FU

In the exploration of a 2-compartment model, the parameter estimation step failed due to rounding errors and the distribution volume of the peripheral compartment converged to extremely large, implausible values. Therefore, a 1-compartment model was selected for further development.

Various error structures were explored during base model development. A mixed residual error model had the lowest objective function value (OFV); however, the RSE for the estimate for IIV of distribution volume (V) was considered too large (286%). Therefore, a proportional error model was considered the most appropriate.

The best base model was a 1-compartment model with zero-order input and first-order elimination including IIV on 5-FU systemic clearance (CL) and V, along with a proportional error component. The population parameter estimates of the best base model are summarized in Supplementary Table 1.

Evaluation of zolbetuximab effect on 5-FU PK

The best base model for 5-FU was used to evaluate the effect of zolbetuximab on 5-FU PK. The effect of zolbetuximab on CL and V was not statistically significant because the decrease in OFV for the model with the effect on CL and for the model with the effect on V was less than 10.83 (P = 0.001 for 1 degree of freedom). Therefore, the best base model was the final model for 5-FU.

Predictive performance of final model for 5-FU

The goodness-of-fit plots for the final model for 5-FU are presented in Supplementary Fig. 1. The quantile-quantile (Q-Q) plot of conditional weighted residuals (CWRES) suggested an abnormally skewed and tailed distribution, and higher concentrations tended to be underestimated. An exploratory investigation of the impact of excluding 6 concentration data points with CWRES > 2.5 or the application of Box-Cox transformation to the residual error distribution was performed, but the tendency remained. Despite these limitations, the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of the observed 5-FU concentrations in the prediction-corrected VPC plots for the final model of 5-FU were generally within the range of the simulated percentiles (Fig. 1). The median time course of observations was generally within the range of the simulated interval, suggesting that the final model had good predictive performance for 5-FU plasma concentrations.

Robustness

In the final model, the uncertainty (%RSE) for population mean parameters was 8.7% for CL and 12.7% for V, and the shrinkage for IIV was 9.5% and 32.9%, respectively (Supplementary Table 1). The condition number of the final model was estimated to be 2.32, well below the model evaluation criterion (1000).

AUCs predicted by PopPK were higher than those calculated by NCA. Base model for free platinum concentration-time profile

Although the parameter estimation step completed successfully for the 2-compartment model, the goodness-of-fit plot suggested that the model did not capture the concentration-time profile well. Therefore, a 3-compartment model with the PRIOR estimation method was applied based on previous studies in which IIV on CL and a common IIV on distribution volume of all compartments (V1, V2, and V3) were incorporated. The 3-compartment model with the PRIOR estimation method successfully estimated parameters, and its goodness-of-fit plot was reasonable.

Mixed error structures were applied during base model development; however, in some estimation steps, proportional error was applied when the estimate of additive error converged toward 0.

The base model structure for the free platinum concentration-time profile was a 3-compartment model with zero-order input and first-order elimination including IIV on CL and IIV on V1–V3, along with a proportional error component.

Evaluation of zolbetuximab effect on oxaliplatin PK

The best base model was used to evaluate the effect of zolbetuximab on oxaliplatin PK. Adding the effect of zolbetuximab on V1–V3 decreased the OFV the most (by −13.8 points) and resulted in a significant likelihood ratio test (P = 0.0002). Zolbetuximab decreased oxaliplatin steady-state distribution volume by ~ 12%. Further addition of zolbetuximab as a covariate on CL or fp did not improve the likelihood ratio test.

The final model was a 3-compartment model with zero-order input and first-order elimination including time-dependent fp; IIV on CL, V1–V3, fp0_A, and fp0_B; and zolbetuximab effect on V1–V3, along with a proportional error component. Parameter estimates for the models with zolbetuximab effect are summarized in Supplementary Table 2.

Discussion

During drug development, studies are often required to assess the potential for changes in PK when medications are administered concomitantly. These drug-drug interaction analyses are typically conducted using NCA. However, NCA can be affected by various confounding factors due to operational issues at sites and study design limitations. To address these issues, PopPK modeling has been explored as an alternative approach [20–23]. We explored this approach in this study, in which plasma concentration data from Cohort 2 of the phase 2 ILUSTRO study were used to develop PopPK models to investigate the impact of zolbetuximab on the PK of 5-FU and oxaliplatin.

The best base model for 5-FU was a 1-compartment model with zero-order input and first-order elimination including IIV on CL and V, along with a proportional error component. Estimated PK parameters of CL (179 L/h) and V (65.6 L) were similar to previously reported values (82–158 L/h and 18.4–54.9 L, respectively) [6, 10, 11, 24]. The effect of zolbetuximab on CL and V of 5-FU was not statistically significant, suggesting that zolbetuximab does not have a substantial impact on 5-FU PK.

The best base model for the free platinum concentration-time profile was a 3-compartment model with zero-order input and first-order elimination including IIV on CL and V1–V3, along with a proportional error component. Plasma protein binding of oxaliplatin platinum is known to be irreversible and time dependent, and in previous studies, concentration-dependent saturable binding was not observed or was limited when the concentration of total platinum was ≤ 0.9 µg/mL [25, 26]. When plotting the free fraction of platinum by sampling time, a time-dependent change in the free fraction was observed. Therefore, a time-dependent free fraction component was incorporated into the model for the simultaneous analysis of free and total platinum concentration-time profiles, which was a 3-compartment model including time-dependent fp; IIV on CL, V1–V3, fp0_A, and fp0_B; and zolbetuximab effect on V1–V3, along with a proportional error component. To our knowledge, this is the first report of a PopPK model with simultaneous analysis of free and total platinum concentration-time profiles after oxaliplatin administration in the context of zolbetuximab administration. Estimated CL (8.09 L/h) was slightly lower than previously reported (11.76–25.2 L/h), and estimated distribution volumes (V1, 51.1 L; V2, 171 L; V3, 1400 L) differed from previously reported values of a 3-compartment model (V1, 22.83 L; V2, 367 L; V3, 997.1 L) [15, 17, 24]. This could be due to the limited sampling time points to estimate 3-compartmental concentration-time profiles in the current study.

NCA-derived PK parameters are substantially affected by deviations from planned sampling times and a limited number of samples. The half-lives estimated based on PopPK parameters generated with the 3-compartment model were 0.25 h for 5-FU and 0.19, 13.0, and 446 h for the alpha, beta, and gamma phases of oxaliplatin, respectively. Therefore, the concentration-time profiles of 5-FU and the initial phase of oxaliplatin were considered steep. Of note, the deviations in sample collection times have a larger impact on the calculation of exposure metrics using the NCA method compared with the PopPK method. Because of PK sampling limitations, the PopPK model for 5-FU helped capture the 5-FU PK profile better than NCA, which did not account for the early concentration profile of 5-FU during bolus administration and following initial infusion and thus underestimated 5-FU exposure. This suggests that PopPK model–based assessment should be considered in situations in which extensive PK sampling is not feasible. Estimates for oxaliplatin PK were similar between NCA and PopPK analysis.

The PopPK model developed for 5-FU adequately described the PK of 5-FU, and no impact of zolbetuximab on 5-FU PK was observed. Similarly, no impact of zolbetuximab on oxaliplatin CL or fp was observed. Although the effect of zolbetuximab on oxaliplatin V1–V3 was statistically significant, the decrease of 12.3% was considered not clinically relevant. PopPK analysis provided better estimates of the 5-FU PK profile than NCA and allowed for simultaneous analysis of free and total platinum concentration-time profiles. Overall, the results suggest that zolbetuximab has no effect on the PK of 5-FU or oxaliplatin.

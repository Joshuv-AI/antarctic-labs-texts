Application of an innovative embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy technique in laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy
Authors: Xing Li, Hao Chen, Hongtai Duan, Qiong Ye, Jie Wang, Haibiao Wang, Kaijie Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the clinical value of an innovative embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy technique for laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of 108 patients who underwent successful laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy at The Affiliated Lihuili Hospital of Ningbo University, from January 2016 to July 2025. Patients were divided according to the type of pancreaticojejunostomy: the experimental group (n = 57) underwent an embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy, while the control group (n = 51) underwent duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy. The baseline characteristics, tumor features, intraoperative and postoperative conditions, and complications were compared between the two groups. And through a multivariate logistic regression model, the independent effects of the surgical groups (experimental group, control group) were evaluated.ResultsThe incidence of grade B/C postoperative pancreatic fistula in the experimental group (5.3%) was significantly lower than that of the control group (19.6%), and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05). The pancreaticojejunostomy time (21.33 ± 2.73 min), postoperative drainage tube removal time (14.00, 10.00–17.50 days), and postoperative hospitalization (16.00, 12.50–21.00 days) in the experimental group were all significantly shorter than those in the control group (31.43 ± 2.30 min; 17.00, 11.00–28.00 days; 20.00, 13.00–29.00 days, respectively; all P < 0.05). There were no significant differences (P > 0.05) between the two groups in age, American Society of Anesthesiologists, Fistula Risk Score, main pancreatic duct diameter, sex, body mass index, pathological type, maximal tumor diameter, pancreatic texture, intraoperative margin status, lymph node metastasis, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, delayed gastric emptying, or postoperative bleeding. And the intervention measure (experimental group, control group) was independent protective factors for reducing postoperative pancreatic fistula (OR=0.19, 95% Cl, 0.05–0.75, P < 0.05).ConclusionThe embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy technique can effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative pancreatic fistula and shorten pancreaticojejunostomy time. This technique is a simple and effective new method for pancreaticojejunostomy and is worthy of application in laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy.Keywords: Laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy, Pancreaticojejunostomy, Pancreatic fistula

Introduction

Pancreaticoduodenectomy is one of the most complex surgical procedures and remains the standard therapy for benign and malignant tumors of the pancreatic head and periampullary region [1, 2]. With the continuous development and refinement of laparoscopic techniques, Laparoscopic Pancreaticoduodenectomy(LPD)has become increasingly utilized and offers advantages such as faster postoperative recovery, shorter hospital stay, less postoperative pain, and better cosmetic outcomes [3]. Postoperative pancreatic fistula༈POPF༉is the most common and severe complication [4]. Currently, most researchers believe that factors associated with POPF include body mass index༈BMI༉, main pancreatic duct diameter, pancreatic texture, pathological nature of the disease, intraoperative blood loss, and management of the pancreatic stump [5, 6]. Management of the pancreatic stump can be modified by a surgeon [7]. Traditional methods include duct-to-mucosal jejunal anastomosis, invagination pancreaticojejunostomy, and binding pancreaticojejunostomy. However, multiple meta-analyses have shown no significant differences in postoperative complication rates among these various techniques [8]. Furthermore, the aforementioned methods are time-consuming and technically demanding, particularly under laparoscopy. Based on this, our group adopted an innovative pancreatic stump management method, embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy, to simplify the procedure, shorten the pancreaticojejunostomy time, and effectively reduce the incidence of POPF.

Materials and methods

General information

A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 108 patients who underwent LPD in the Department of Hepatobiliary and Pancreatic Surgery at The Affiliated Lihuili Hospital of Ningbo University from January 2016 to July 2025. Among them, 57 patients who underwent embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy were assigned to the experimental group, while 51 patients who underwent conventional duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy served as the control group. All the procedures were performed by the same surgeon. The diameter of the main pancreatic duct was measured on the axial images through preoperative CT, perpendicular to the long axis of the duct diameter. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) preoperative diagnosis of a tumor of the pancreatic head or periampullary tumor, (2) surgical approach of LPD, (3) preoperative American Society of Anesthesiologists(ASA )score ≤ 3, (4) no adjacent organ invasion or distant metastasis, and (5) complete clinical data. The exclusion criteria were: (1) non-radical resection due to tumor invasion or dissemination during surgery; (2) conversion from laparoscopy to open surgery; (3) combined resection of other organs; and (4) age < 18 years. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Lihuili Hospital of Ningbo University, and the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Surgical methods and procedure

Position and Trocar: After general anesthesia was successful, the patient was placed in the supine position with the head high and feet low. A 1 cm longitudinal incision was made below the umbilicus and a 10 mm Trocar was placed. The pneumoperitoneum pressure was maintained at 12–14 mmHg [9], and the whole abdomen was explored laparoscopically. After confirming that there was no obvious metastasis, four fan-shaped trocars were placed in the abdomen, of which the trocar on the upper left side of the patient was 12 mm and the other three were 5 mm.

Main Surgical Steps: The surgeon first opened the Kocher maneuver and mobilized the descending portion of the duodenum and dorsal aspect of the pancreatic head. Subsequently, the gastrocolic ligament was opened to mobilize the greater curvature of the stomach and the distal stomach was transected using a linear stapler. The inferior border of the pancreas was dissected to expose the portal vein and create access to the pancreatic neck. The superior border of the pancreas was then mobilized to expose the common hepatic, gastroduodenal, and proper hepatic arteries. Absorbable clips were applied to ligate the gastroduodenal and right gastric arteries followed by transection. The pancreas was transected at the neck and the jejunum was divided approximately 20 cm distal to the ligament of Treitz. The gallbladder was mobilized from its bed and the cystic artery was exposed, clipped, and divided. The common hepatic duct was divided and en bloc resection of the pancreaticoduodenectomy specimen was performed. The umbilical incision was then enlarged to retrieve the specimen. Laparoscopic access was reestablished, and an appropriately sized pancreatic duct stent was placed. Pancreaticojejunostomy was performed (experimental group, embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy; control group, duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy), followed by routine choledochojejunostomy and gastrojejunostomy. Two drainage tubes were routinely placed around the anastomotic sites (one tube was placed in some patients according to intraoperative circumstances) and the abdominal cavity was irrigated. Upon completion of these checks, the abdomen was closed.

Embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy: At a distance of about 1–2 cm from the pancreatic stump, a single-headed needle custom-made of 3 − 0 Prolene with a length of about 30 cm (which can be adjusted according to the thickness of the pancreas) was used to enter the needle from the ventral side of the upper edge of the pancreas and exit the needle from the dorsal side (Fig. 1a). Along the long axis of the jejunum, 2–3 stitches were continuously sutured on the side close to the mesentery (Fig. 1b), and the needle was then inserted from the dorsal side of the lower edge of the pancreas and exited from the ventral side (Fig. 1c) to complete dorsal embedding of the pancreatic stump. Then, a double-headed needle custom-made of a 3 − 0 Prolene line with a length of approximately 30 cm was used. First, one needle was used to continuously suture the jejunal wall and posterior wall of the pancreatic stump in full thickness from the upper edge of the pancreas. During this time, a small hole was cut in the jejunum at the corresponding position of the pancreatic duct, the other end of the pancreatic duct stent was placed at the distal end of the jejunal loop, and 2–3 stitches were sutured at the pancreatic duct to the mucosa (Fig. 1d). The other head of the double-headed needle was used to perform a continuous full-thickness suture between the jejunal wall and the anterior wall of the pancreatic stump from the superior margin of the pancreas. 2–3 stitches were sutured from the pancreatic duct to mucosa. The double-headed needle was converged at the lower margin of the pancreas, tightened, and knotted to complete inner parallel pancreaticojejunostomy. (Fig. 1e). Finally, the initial single-headed needle was used to continuously suture 2–3 stitches along the long axis of the jejunum from bottom to top (if necessary, one stitch was added to the ventral side of the pancreatic stump), and the knot was tightened at the upper edge of the pancreas (Fig. 1f). To date, the outer layer of the embedded pancreaticojejunostomy has been completed. A schematic diagram is shown (Fig. 2).

Duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy: The initial step involved placing continuous sutures with 4 − 0 Prolene thread to connect the posterior surface of the pancreatic stump to the jejunum. A small hole was then cut in the jejunum at the corresponding position of the pancreatic duct and the posterior wall of the jejunal mucosa was continuously anastomosed to the pancreatic duct using a 5 − 0 Prolene suture. During this period, the other end of the pancreatic duct stent was placed at the distal end of the jejunal loop. The anterior wall of the jejunal mucosa was then sutured to the pancreatic duct. As both ends were tied, the pancreatic duct-to-jejunal mucosa anastomosis was completed. Finally, the initial 4 − 0 Prolene sutures were used to sew the anterior surface of the pancreatic stump to the jejunum.

Observation indicators

The following parameters were collected and recorded for both groups: age, ASA score, Fistula Risk Score [10] (FRS, which is included pancreatic texture, pathological type, main pancreatic duct diameter and intraoperative blood loss), main pancreatic duct diameter, sex, BMI, pathological type, maximum tumor diameter, pancreatic texture, intraoperative margin status, lymph node metastasis, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and pancreaticojejunostomy time. Postoperative data included the incidence of grade B/C POPF, postoperative hemorrhage, delayed gastric emptying, time to drain removal, length of postoperative hospital stay, and complications. Diagnosis of pancreatic fistula: POPF was defined as drainage fluid amylase content greater than three times the upper limit of normal serum amylase after postoperative three days, in association with relevant clinical management or prognosis [11].

In order to be more rigorous, univariable logistic regression was used to screen the variables potentially related to the occurrence of grade B/C POPF, and these variables were included together with the intervention measures (surgical groups) in the multivariate logistic regression model to evaluate the independent effect of the intervention measure.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0. Quantitative data conforming to a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s), and comparisons between two groups were performed using the independent sample t-test. Quantitative data not conforming to a normal distribution were expressed as medians (Q1, Q3), and comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data are presented as numbers (percentages), and comparisons between the two groups were performed using the chi-square test (x2 test) or Fisher’s exact test. Risk factors for B/C POPF, were identified using univariate and multivariate analyses. Variables found to be associated with complications in the univariate analysis (P < 0.05) were entered into a stepwise logistic regression model for multivariate analysis of risk factors. P < 0.05, the difference is statistically significant.

Results

The clinical data of 108 patients who underwent successful LPD between January 2016 and July 2025 at the Department of Hepatobiliary and Pancreatic Surgery, The Affiliated Lihuili Hospital of Ningbo University, were analyzed. Among them, 57 patients underwent embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy (experimental group), and 51 underwent traditional duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy (control group). Baseline data are shown in Table 1, tumor characteristics in Table 2, intraoperative conditions in Table 3, and postoperative conditions and complications in Table 4. Univariable and Multivariable logistic regression analysis of risk factors for postoperative pancreatic fistula in Tables 5 and 6. Comparative analysis showed no significant differences in age, ASA score, FRS, main pancreatic duct diameter, sex, BMI, pathological type, maximal tumor diameter, pancreatic texture, intraoperative margin status, lymph node metastasis, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, delayed gastric emptying, or postoperative hemorrhage (P > 0.05). The incidence of grade B/C POPF in the experimental group (5.3%) was significantly lower than that in the control group (19.6%) (P < 0.05). The intervention measure (experimental group, control group) was independent protective factors for reducing POPF(OR=0.19, 95% Cl, 0.05–0.75, P < 0.05). The experimental group had significantly shorter pancreaticojejunostomy time (21.33 ± 2.73 min), postoperative drainage tube removal (14.00, 10.00–17.50 days), and hospital stay (16.00, 12.50–21.00 days) than the control group (31.43 ± 2.30 min; 17.00, 11.00–28.00 days; 20.00, 13.00–29.00 days, respectively; P < 0.05).

Discussion

PD is one of the most complex and challenging surgical procedures commonly performed for the treatment of benign and malignant tumors of the pancreatic head, ampulla, distal bile duct, and duodenum [12]. The most frequent complications were incisional infections and POPF [13]. With the continuous development of laparoscopic techniques, LPD has become increasingly common, effectively reducing the incidence of postoperative wound infections [14] and offering advantages such as more rapid postoperative recovery, shorter hospital stay, less postoperative pain, and improved cosmetic outcomes [3]. However, POPF remains the most prevalent and potentially fatal complication [15]. Studies have indicated that the choice of pancreatoenteric anastomosis technique is directly related to the incidence of POPF [16]. Consequently, various methods for pancreatoenteric anastomosis have been developed. Improvements and optimization of pancreatoenteric anastomosis, particularly during LPD, primarily aim to reduce the incidence of POPF, simplify the procedure, and shorten anastomosis time, which facilitates laparoscopic operations. However, commonly used anastomotic techniques often fail to simultaneously achieve these aims. In this study, we adopted an innovative embedded parallel-type pancreaticojejunostomy, which not only reduces the risk of POPF but also simplifies the procedure, thereby effectively shortening anastomosis time.

Our study found that, compared with the traditional duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy, the embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy technique reduced the rate of postoperative grade B/C pancreatic fistula from 19.6% to 5.3%, with statistical significance (P < 0.05). Furthermore, we also discovered through univariate logistic regression analysis and multivariate logistic regression model that the intervention measure (experimental group, control group) was independent protective factors for reducing POPF(OR=0.19, 95% Cl, 0.05–0.75, P < 0.05). There were two main reasons for this finding. First, embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy combines “drainage and containment” principles: by tightening the outer sutures, an embedding effect is produced on the pancreatic stump, which prevents extravasation of pancreatic juice and erosion of the anastomotic area’s surrounding tissues and vessels, thus serving as “containment.” Moreover, studies have indicated that pancreatic juice leakage from the pancreatic stump, apart from that of the main pancreatic duct, greatly affects the incidence of POPF [17]. Unlike traditional duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy, embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy does not completely seal the pancreatic duct-to-jejunum channel, leaving a pathway for residual pancreatic juice to enter the jejunum. This effectively addresses exudation from the stump surface, reduces local accumulation of pancreatic juice at the anastomosis, and lowers the risk of POPF, thus serving as “drainage.” Second, external embedding reduces the number of sutures passing through the pancreatic stump, thereby reducing suture holes through which pancreatic juice may leak and ultimately decreasing the incidence of POPF. The embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy technique, by simplifying operative steps and utilizing a “large-patch suture” approach, effectively shortens pancreaticojejunostomy time, reducing the anastomosis duration by nearly one-third compared to the traditional duct-to-mucosa technique—with a significant and statistically meaningful difference (P < 0.05). In addition, this approach facilitates the performance of laparoscopic procedures. Regarding total operative time, however, there was no significant difference compared to the duct-to-mucosa method, because LPD is complex and time-consuming overall [18]. Pancreaticojejunostomy represents only one step, so the reduction in anastomosis time makes only a small contribution to the total operation time and thus is not decisive.

This study also found that the embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy group demonstrated significantly fewer days to postoperative drain removal and shorter postoperative hospital stays than those with duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy, both of which were statistically significant (P < 0.05). This may be because some patients with postoperative POPF experience certain complications, which delay or complicate supportive treatment and require more complex postoperative management [19, 20], inevitably prolonging both drain removal and hospitalization. Therefore, when it comes to the number of days required for removing the postoperative drainage tube and the postoperative hospital stay, we should interpret them carefully. We should not blindly attribute the benefits to the embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy.

The experimental group and the control group were operated by the same surgeon. And the collection period of cases was approximately 9.5 years, raising concerns about the influence of the learning curve. That was intentionally designed to minimize inter-surgeon variability, which could otherwise confound the results. By having a single, consistent operator, we aimed to isolate technical effects as much as possible. The 9.5-year period of case collection was designed to maximize the sample size, thereby enhancing the statistical power and accuracy of our analysis. The lead surgeon began LPD more than 15 years ago and is highly experienced with all procedural steps. According to the literature, the first phase of the LPD learning curve (reaching competency) has a median of 39 cases (range: 11–60) [21]. Our surgeon had performed a number of LPDs far exceeding this range before the start of the present study period. Therefore, the influence of the learning curve on this study was relatively minor.

Our embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy shows no significant difference in POPF compared with the modified Blumgart anastomosis, Shark mouth pancreatic-intestinal anastomosis, and Double R anastomosis. All of these methods can effectively reduce postoperative pancreatic fistula [7, 22, 23]. Under laparoscopic operation, the average anastomosis time of the Double R anastomosis method is (34 ± 5) minutes [23], while embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy is only (21.33 ± 2.73) minutes. The modified Blumgart anastomosis takes about (18.1 ± 1.6) minutes in open surgery [24], and 40 min or more in robot-assisted surgery [25]; the Shark mouth anastomosis is an open surgical method and there is no clear anastomosis time reported [7]. In summary, this method has certain time advantages under laparoscopic operation, which will also correspondingly reduce the learning curve. It should be noted that this study only directly compared the effects and advantages of the embedded parallel pancreaticojejunostomy under laparoscopic surgery and the traditional duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy, we did not conduct statistical analysis with the above several methods. The relevant comparisons were based on literature review and discussion.

However, this study has several limitations. The sample size included was relatively small, although the differences in B/C level POPF were statistically significant, and this also proved that the surgical grouping was an independent protective factor for reducing POPF, the influence caused by the small sample size still exists. And multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are required to validate this anastomotic technique further.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that employing an embedded parallel pancreatojejunostomy can effectively lower the incidence of postoperative pancreatic fistula and shorten the anastomosis time, thereby potentially allowing earlier removal of abdominal drainage tubes in some patients and reducing hospital stay, which results in an overall benefit. This provides surgeons with valuable new options.

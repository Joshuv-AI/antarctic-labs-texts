Graph convolutional network with reinforced dependency graph and denoising mechanism for sarcasm detection
Authors: Pingping Yan, Tianbo An, Jiaxuan Yu, Jinjin An, Daqun Tong, Jingrui Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe widespread presence of sarcasm in social media presents significant challenges to sentiment analysis and public opinion monitoring, making accurate sarcasm detection particularly important. Recently, studies have found that graph-based methods can effectively overcome the limitations of sarcasm methods in modeling syntactic and emotional information. However, existing approaches often consider dependency relationships between nodes as homogeneous, and directly aggregating all edge information during the graph learning process can introduce noise. To address these issues, we propose a novel framework based on the graph convolutional network for sarcasm detection. This framework effectively improves the ability of sarcasm detection by modeling the heterogeneity of dependencies and introducing a noise suppression mechanism. Specifically, when constructing dependency graphs, we account for the importance of node distances and dependency types. To reduce the impact of noise, we extract robust feature representations using a denoising autoencoder. Then, a parameterized mechanism is applied to penalize sparse edges, suppressing the interference of irrelevant edge information. To evaluate the performance of the proposed model, we conducted experiments on three datasets of different scales, achieving F1-scores of 84.97%, 71.11%, and 83.83% respectively. The proposed model outperforms other baseline models across all metrics, demonstrating outstanding performance.Subject terms: Computational science, Computer science, Information technology, Scientific data

Introduction

Sarcasm is a common expression in daily life, conveying true intentions through semantics that contradict the literal meaning1. Due to sarcasm masking the true emotional expression, this linguistic phenomenon may lead to biases in sentiment detection or public opinion analysis on social media. The proposal of the sarcasm detection task helps to more accurately grasp the emotional tendencies of users2–5. Early sarcasm detection primarily relied on traditional machine learning methods6–9, such as SVM, decision trees, and naive Bayes. Researchers perform feature selection using specific rules such as word frequency statistics and sentiment features, then utilize these features for classification10,11. With the development of deep learning, sarcasm detection has entered a new stage. Models based on neural networks12 and Transformers13 can delve deeper into text features, thereby enhancing detection performance. However, the key to sarcasm detection lies in capturing the implicit semantic and emotional features in text, while these detection methods have shortcomings in modeling syntactic and emotional information. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the sarcasm expression in the second sentence is subtler than in the first. Because there is no obvious emotional conflict between “love” and “fall asleep”, the model may easily misinterpret its sentiment. To more accurately detect such nuanced sarcasm, the model needs to capture the syntactic dependency between “not” and “fall asleep”, thereby enhancing its discriminative ability. Therefore, it is crucial to deeply explore the incongruity expressions within sentences by combining syntactic and emotional information.

Schematic diagram of the dependency tree structure for sarcastic text. The arrows represent the syntactic relationships between words (for example, “nsubj” indicates that “I” is the subject of the verb “love”), with blue and orange representing positive and negative emotions, respectively.

In recent years, graph-based methods have been widely applied in natural language processing tasks, with their advantage lying in the ability to effectively represent complex structural information in text through the relationships between nodes and edges14–17. Inspired by these methods, Lou et al.18 first introduced graph convolutional networks into sarcasm detection tasks by manually constructing affective and dependency graphs of sentences to capture semantic inconsistencies in sarcastic texts. However, the construction of static graphs typically relies on predefined rules or heuristic algorithms. The generated initial graph structure is often incomplete and the sentiment of words can vary with context. These issues are difficult to correct during training and easily introduce noise, affecting the accuracy of the model. To overcome the above problem, Wang19 proposed a dynamic graph enhancement method to enhance affective dependency graphs through iterative learning of the incongruity graph structure. Although this method achieves dynamic optimization of the graph structure, nodes typically aggregate information from all neighbors during information propagation, including those that are irrelevant or unimportant. This not only weakens the impact of critical information but also inevitably introduces noise20, affecting the effect of graph representation learning. To alleviate the noise problem, researchers have begun to focus on the importance or quality of edges21,22, aiming to enhance the model’s ability to extract key information through graph structure optimization. Moreover, we notice that existing graph-based sarcasm detection methods typically treat dependency relationships between nodes as homogeneous23, ignoring the influence of factors such as different dependency types and distances between nodes. This modeling approach is difficult to accurately capture complex syntactic dependencies, resulting in information redundancy, and affects the model’s understanding of complex syntactic structures in sarcastic texts.

In summary, existing graph-based sarcasm detection methods primarily utilize graph structures to capture the syntactic and emotional information of texts. However, these methods often treat syntactic dependencies as uniform connections, neglecting the impact of different relation types and the distances between nodes. Additionally, traditional graph methods are prone to noise interference during graph construction and feature aggregation. To address the above problems, this paper proposes a novel graph convolutional network with a reinforced dependency graph and a denoising mechanism (RDD-GCN), aiming to overcome the limitations of insufficient syntactic information capture and noise interference in existing methods. Specifically, the proposed method first constructs affective and dependency graphs to capture emotional and syntactic information. Considering the heterogeneity of dependency relations, the model optimizes the dependency graph using an importance-weighted strategy based on relation types and distances, thereby enhancing its ability to represent syntactic structures. It is obvious from Fig. 2 that the dependency graph in (c) captures deeper dependencies compared to (b). To reduce noise interference during graph learning, a noise suppression mechanism is adopted to perform denoising at both the feature and structure levels. This mechanism employs a denoising autoencoder to mitigate noise in node features and constructs a parameterized adjacency matrix to suppress structural noise, effectively enhancing the model’s robustness. Finally, an optimal graph for classification is obtained by iteratively learning an incongruity semantic graph, enabling effective sarcasm detection.

Related works

Sarcasm is usually expressed through features such as inconsistency, exaggeration, or ambiguity, conveying a true intention opposite to the literal meaning. With the development of sentiment analysis techniques, sarcasm detection has become one of the key issues to be addressed in the field of NLP. Early sarcasm detection mainly relied on predefined sets of rules or feature labels for classification24,25. However, this rule-based method was heavily dependent on manual design and often struggled to adapt effectively to diverse scenarios. Subsequently, researchers shifted towards feature extraction methods from sarcasm data, including traditional machine learning feature engineering techniques and deep learning end-to-end models. In traditional machine learning methods26–28, common feature representations include bag-of-words models, TF-IDF, and sentiment lexicons, which are then used in algorithms such as support vector machines, random forests, and decision trees for sarcasm detection. Although these approaches performed well in certain tasks, their reliance on manual feature selection made it challenging to capture the deeper semantic meaning of text.

The development of deep learning has ushered sarcasm detection into a new phase. Deep learning models29–32 such as Convolutional Neural Networks, Recurrent Neural Networks, and Long Short-Term Memory networks, can automatically extract features, avoiding the cumbersome process of feature engineering. However, these models can only capture the basic features of text, and there are certain challenges in understanding the complex context of the text. Later, the introduction of attention mechanisms33,34 has brought new opportunities, enhancing the model’s capability for sarcasm detection by capturing inconsistencies between words or sentence fragments. Meanwhile, BERT35,36 has stood out for its exceptional performance in natural language processing tasks. The BERT pre-trained model can capture rich contextual information in text and effectively extract semantic features, further advancing the development of this field37,38. However, the attention mechanism itself lacks positional information and has a limited ability to model syntactic relationships, which may lead to the model mistakenly focusing on distant, irrelevant words. To address this issue, researchers have begun to explore methods for better modeling syntactic structures in order to overcome the limitations of existing approaches.

Graph-based methods have achieved success in the application of many NLP tasks in recent years39–42. In aspect-based sentiment classification, Huang et al.43 proposed the target-dependent graph attention network, using the syntactic structure of the sentence and employing the syntactic context of the aspect target for classification. Tang et al.44 integrated the traditional Transformer with a dependency graph-based bidirectional GCN, iteratively fusing sequence and graph representations to enhance classification performance. Lu et al.45 introduced the Graph-aware Co-Attention Network (GCAN) for fake news detection, modeling potential interaction relationships among users by constructing graph structures. Inspired by these studies, Lou et al.18 were the first to propose constructing dependency and affective graphs to capture the syntactic and emotional relationships in text. Then they utilized GCN to learn from these graph structures, thereby achieving sarcasm detection. Their research shows that joint modeling of affective and dependency graphs can more effectively uncover the underlying sarcastic expressions in texts. To better capture emotional incongruity in text, Li et al.46 proposed an Affection Enhanced Relational Graph Attention network (ARGAT), which further promoted the information interaction between long-distance structurally related word pairs and enhanced the model’s understanding of sarcastic expressions. Chen et al.47 revisited sarcasm detection from a new perspective and proposed a commonsense-aware model with a heterogeneous graph attention network, which uses commonsense knowledge to enhance the understanding of the implicit emotions behind the literal meaning. However, static graph-based approaches may introduce noise or incomplete representations. Wang et al.19 proposed dynamically learning an incongruity graph structure to enhance the affective dependency graph and mitigate this issue. It is noteworthy that one issue faced by current graph-based sarcasm detection methods is that the aggregation of information from edges may introduce noise and lead to information redundancy, which weakens the role of key information and affects model performance. Therefore, this has become a critical issue that needs to be addressed.

Proposed method

Framework

The architecture of the proposed RDD-GCN is shown in Fig. 3. First, the Context Encoding Module is employed to learn text embeddings, which are used to support graph representation learning. Subsequently, the graph construction module generates affective and dependency graphs, with the dependency graph constructed by combining the importance of node distances and dependency types. The graph denoising and iterative enhancement module aims to optimize the graph structure and node representations, thereby improving the quality of graph representation learning. It initially employs denoising autoencoders to reduce the noise interference in the node features, followed by the construction of a parameterized adjacency matrix to suppress the influence of noisy edges. Concurrently, a semantic inconsistency graph is constructed using text embeddings to enhance the affective and dependency graphs. The module continuously iterates learning to update node representations until the preset iteration threshold is reached. Finally, prediction is performed based on the learned optimal graph to achieve sarcasm detection.

The model architecture we proposed mainly consists of three modules: (1) context encoding module; (2) graph construction module; and (3) graph denoising and iterative enhancement module.

Implementation details

Our experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3050 GPU, using the PyTorch framework to build and train the model. GloVe is employed as the initial word embedding method, with the embedding dimension set to 300. During training, the model parameters are updated using the Adam optimizer56, with an initial learning rate of 0.001. The weight coefficients and for dependency type and distance are both set to 0.5 after sensitivity analysis. The iteration threshold is set to 0.04. To prevent overfitting, we introduce L2 regularization in the model, and the regularization coefficient is set to 0.0001. We set the maximum number of training epochs to 100, with a batch size of 32 for each epoch. Additionally, we apply an early stop strategy, where training is automatically halted if the validation loss does not show significant improvement for 10 consecutive epochs. We use accuracy, F1-score, and precision to evaluate the performance of models.

Conclusion and future work

Sarcastic texts often contain emotional implications that contradict their literal meaning, making it challenging to accurately understand the speaker’s true intention. Based on existing research, this paper proposes a graph-based sarcasm detection model, RDD-GCN, designed to address the problems of existing graph-based methods, including insufficient syntactic information capture and noise interference. Specifically, the dependency graph construction strategy is improved to further strengthen the ability of the model to capture syntactic information. Furthermore, to mitigate the noise problem, we design a denoising module that includes a denoising autoencoder and an edge sparsification penalty mechanism, effectively enhancing the model’s robustness. From the experimental results, our model has shown excellent performance in handling complex text relationships and uncovering implicit semantics.

Although RDD-GCN has achieved certain results in sarcasm detection tasks, the model still has limitations due to the diverse and complex nature of sarcastic expressions. In particular, when sarcasm involves deeper background knowledge or implicit contexts, existing methods may not accurately capture it. Furthermore, in order to further enhance the model performance, we plan to explore more efficient methods to handle noisy edges. At the same time, we attempt to apply the RDD-GCN model to sentiment analysis and other natural language processing tasks, aiming to expand its application scenarios.

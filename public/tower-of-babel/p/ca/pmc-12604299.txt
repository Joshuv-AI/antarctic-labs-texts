Efficient temporal feature utilization in ultrasound videos: a multi-channel deep learning framework for enhanced breast lesion differentiation
Authors: Patrice Monkam, Xu Wang, Bonan Zhao, Shouliang Qi, Chunxiao Cui, Dan Zhao, Tao Yu, Chang Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAutomated breast lesion differentiation in ultrasound (US) imaging has advanced through deep learning (DL) techniques. However, existing 2D approaches predominantly rely on individual static images, overlooking valuable temporal information between frames, which limits their performance. While 3D models can utilize this temporal information, their high computational requirements make them impractical in resource-constrained settings.MethodsThis study introduces an improved framework that combines spatial and temporal lesion features by incorporating consecutive frames from US videos. Unlike existing 2D models, our framework utilizes a multi-channel input strategy to effectively learn lesion characteristics across frames, avoiding computational burden of 3D models. It offers potential use in resource-limited settings and real-time environments. The framework’s effectiveness has been validated on multicenter data from two different regions.ResultsExtensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed multi-channel input technique significantly outperforms single-image approaches. Across five distinct DL backbone models, the framework consistently achieved higher precision, recall, and AUC values, highlighting the positive impact of temporal information on classification accuracy. Specifically, the multi-channel approach yielded improvements of up to 8.6% in AUC, 9.86% in precision, and 23.68% in recall compared to single-image inputs. These results highlight the potential of the proposed multi-channel framework as an effective and practical solution for breast lesion differentiation.ConclusionsBy effectively utilizing temporal information without additional computational burden, our proposed approach serves as a computationally efficient alternative to 3D models, advancing DL-based breast US analysis. It shows promising potential for delivering more accurate and accessible diagnostic solutions, making it highly applicable for clinical practice.

Introduction

Breast cancer is a type of cancer characterized by the uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells within the breast tissue. It stands as the most common cancer among women worldwide, and constitutes the leading cause of cancer deaths among women globally, claiming approximately 685,000 deaths annually [1]. In China, breast cancer incidence has risen significantly in recent years, with approximately 117,000 deaths occurring annually, reflecting a high mortality rate [2]. Therefore, tremendous efforts have been deployed towards developing improved strategies for early detection and treatment of breast cancer.

Breast screening using medical imaging techniques is a highly effective strategy for accurate and early diagnosis of breast malignancies, playing a pivotal role in reducing mortality rates. While various imaging techniques are used for breast cancer screening, medical ultrasound remains the most widely adopted due to its numerous advantages, including cost-effectiveness, real-time imaging capabilities, non-invasiveness, absence of radiation exposure, etc. After performing imaging examinations, further assessments, such as lesion identification and evaluation of lesion characteristics, are carried out to reach a diagnostic conclusion. However, these subsequent steps are often tedious, time-consuming, and require specialized expertise, making them prone to subjective errors by examiners. Furthermore, the growing imbalance between the limited number of medical professionals and the increasing number of patients can lead to overwork, which can further increase the risk of diagnostic inaccuracies [3, 4]. Therefore, significant research efforts have been devoted to developing clinical decision support systems to automate various tasks in breast ultrasound examinations. These systems can be categorized into two primary types: conventional approaches based on traditional machine learning (ML) algorithms and end-to-end approaches leveraging deep learning (DL) techniques.

Many decision support frameworks utilizing traditional ML algorithms have been developed to enhance the performance of breast lesion differentiation. Sadad et al. proposed enhancing breast lesion classification performance using various ML models, such as decision trees, with a hybrid feature set based on shape and texture features [5]. However, their approach lacked a feature selection process, which could have further improved classification accuracy. Mishra et al. proposed a breast lesion classification approach that combines the advantages of ML techniques and radiomics, where representative features are selected using a recursive feature elimination strategy, achieving significant results across several evaluation metrics [6]. In the study by Shi et al., a logistic regression model was developed for lesion differentiation, which integrates multiple feature selection algorithms to optimize the selection of representative features, resulting in satisfactory performance [7]. Xu et al. proposed a multimodal approach that extracts representative features from multiple lesion regions in B-mode US and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) images, overcoming the limitations of traditional single-region analysis, and significantly improving the performance in distinguishing malignant from benign tumors [8]. Magnuska et al. introduced a breast lesion differentiation approach where traditional machine learning classifiers were trained using features extracted from multiple image regions. The features were generated through a combination of classic radiomics strategies and an autoencoder model. This fusion of features, learned from different models and image regions, significantly improved differentiation performance [9].

In recent years, numerous end-to-end frameworks based on DL techniques have been developed to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of breast malignancy detection. Ma et al proposed Fus2Net, a convolutional neural network (CNN) framework that integrates multi-scale expression to achieve enhanced breast lesion differentiation performance [10]. They demonstrated that multi-channel input leads to significant improvement of the classification performance. Tian et al. developed hybrid frameworks, which integrates the advantages of both traditional machine learning algorithms and DL models to achieve enhanced breast lesion differentiation performance [11]. In another study, Qiu et al. proposed a multi-step hybrid system for distinguishing between benign and malignant breast lesions [12]. Li et al. designed a jointly optimized two-stage system for simultaneous detection and differentiation of breast lesions [13]. The authors introduced three innovative network modules and integrated clinical knowledge to reinforce the networks learning process, achieving notably enhanced classification performance. AlZoubi et al. leveraged Bayesian optimization algorithm to design a tailored CNN model for distinguishing between benign and malignant breast lesions. The designed model demonstrated superior performance compared to several off-the-shelf CNN models utilizing transfer learning techniques [14]. Additionally, through empirical assessments, they demonstrated the feasibility of saliency maps in explaining the prediction outcomes of deep learning model for breast lesion differentiation. In a recent comparative study by Zhou and Mosadegh, it was found that the Vision Transformer (ViT) models outperformed CNN models in lesion differentiation. Furthermore, combining multiple ViT models through ensemble learning demonstrated potential for further enhancing lesion differentiation performance [15].

Numerous conventional and DL-based decision support systems have been developed to enhance both the efficacy and efficiency of breast cancer screening using ultrasound imaging technology. However, there is still room for improvement. Conventional approaches often rely on handcrafted features extracted from individual image frames and involve multiple processes, which can increase overall complexity. Moreover, they typically struggle to capture the temporal and dynamic nature of lesions, which is critical for accurate differentiation. Additionally, the reliance on manually defined features may limit the model’s ability to generalize across different datasets, particularly those containing breast lesions with complex backgrounds. In contrast, DL-based approaches operate in an end-to-end fashion, allowing for automatic feature learning and the integration of both spatial and temporal data.

While DL-based approaches have addressed many of the challenges faced by conventional approaches, leading to superior performance, they still have limitations that require further improvement. First, most of these approaches rely on single image analysis, despite the fact that capturing both spatial and temporal information provides a richer representation of lesion features, which plays a crucial role in the differentiation process. In clinical practice, radiologists typically assess lesion characteristics by analyzing multiple frames, rather than relying on a single frame. Second, although some studies have developed 3D frameworks for breast lesion analysis using video sequences, they require significant computational resources, which limits their deployment in resource-constrained settings. Therefore, there is a pressing need to explore more efficient strategies that can harness both spatial and temporal information without relying on resource-intensive models. Such approaches could significantly enhance the differentiation capabilities of DL frameworks while reducing reliance on computationally expensive resources, thereby making them more suitable for deployment in resource-limited settings and real-time applications.

In this paper, we present an improved approach inspired by radiologists’ practices to enhance automated breast lesion differentiation using deep learning techniques. Specifically, we investigate how integrating consecutive frames from US video sequences can improve lesion differentiation compared to relying solely on individual frames. We also examine how effectively utilizing both spatial and temporal lesion features can enhance differentiation performance while avoiding the high computational burden typically associated with 3D models. Furthermore, we compare the performance and computational efficiency of our multi-channel 2D approach with that of traditional 3D models. The proposed approach is thoroughly validated using multicenter data from two regions in China, which encompass various ultrasound imaging systems and settings. Through these evaluations, we aim to demonstrate the potential of combining spatial and temporal features in a computationally efficient framework for improved breast lesion differentiation. Related works

3D deep learning frameworks for breast lesion analysis

To effectively leverage the significant features present in dynamic breast US videos for lesion analysis, researchers have developed automated frameworks that utilize 3D deep learning models. Moon et al. combined the advantages of two off-the-shelf 3D CNN models to develop an improved framework for automatic 3D breast lesion detection [16]. The proposed framework demonstrated satisfactory performance in minimizing false positives and false negatives in breast tumor localization. In a similar study, Barekatrezaei et al. employed a cascaded 3D CNN model for the automatic detection of 3D masses in breast US videos [17]. Their experimental results showed that this cascaded learning strategy achieved high sensitivity while significantly lowering false positive rates.

Zhou et al. developed an innovative framework based on multitask learning for simultaneous delineation and differentiation of breast lesions using volumetric samples instead of 2D images [18]. Key advantages of this framework include an iterative feature-refinement process, multi-scale feature extraction, and joint optimization of two distinct tasks. Ding et al. designed a novel DL framework that incorporates multi-view stereoscopic attention mechanisms to locate and classify breast lesion in an end-to-end manner [19]. Different from existing approaches, this framework leverages multiple breast ultrasound planes (sagittal, coronal, and transverse) to enhance its learning capabilities. It is particularly robust in detecting small and ambiguous boundary lesions. The studies by Zhao et al. and Chen et al. demonstrated that deep learning frameworks utilizing dynamic videos significantly outperformed those based on static 2D images in the classification of breast lesions [20, 21].

It has been proven that effectively utilizing the key features embedded in successive frames of dynamic breast US videos is critical for improving lesion analysis accuracy. However, while 3D deep learning models can better exploit this temporal information, they involve high computational costs that limit their practical deployment, particularly in resource-constrained areas where medical professionals are often scarce. This highlights the need for alternative DL approaches that can efficiently leverage both spatial and temporal features from consecutive US video frames to improve breast lesion differentiation while minimizing computational demands. This paper aims to address this gap by proposing a computationally efficient framework that can effectively utilize temporal information without the need for resource-intensive 3D models.

Multi-channel deep learning frameworks for breast lesion analysis

In recent years, researchers have sought to enhance the performance of deep learning systems for the automated differentiation of breast lesions by adopting image fusion strategies. In these frameworks, the network input consists of multiple images that represent different feature levels of the lesions. Zhuang et al. proposed a multi-channel input framework for classifying breast lesions in US image data [22]. In this framework, a series of conventional image processing algorithms were applied to the original US image to generate two additional images. These images, combined with the initial US image, formed a three-channel input for the network. Experimental results showed significant improvements in performance. In a similar study, Alotaibi et al. built a three-channel input framework for automated breast lesion differentiation by utilizing speckle suppression filtering techniques to generate additional US images [23]. In another study, Xu et al. improved the performance of BI-RADS category 4 classification by using a three-channel input for their network, which included the original image, clustering image, and entropy image [24]. This fusion approach resulted in enhanced classification outcomes. BI-RADS corresponds to the breast imaging reporting and data system.

Multi-channel input has been shown to effectively enhance the performance of DL frameworks for breast lesion differentiation. However, most of these studies rely on generating multi-channel input through multiple image processing techniques, often neglecting the use of adjacent image frames from original breast ultrasound video data. Additionally, these image processing steps introduce extra processing time and require specialized expertise. Therefore, further investigation is needed to explore the potential of improving the differentiation performance of DL frameworks by using multi-channel input that includes a key frame image alongside adjacent image frames.

Comparative summary of existing approaches

Based on the learning strategies and implementation processes adopted in previous studies for automated breast lesion differentiation, existing approaches can be organized into four subgroups: traditional ML approaches, ensemble methods, 2D multi-channel approaches, and 3D approaches. Traditional ML approaches are simple and present better interpretability but rely on handcrafted features and fail to capture temporal dynamics across video frames. Ensemble methods, while effective in enhancing classification performance by combining the outputs of multiple classifiers, also struggle to leverage temporal information across video frames, limiting their ability to fully utilize dynamic features in sequential data. 2D single or multi-channel approaches, which rely on static images or multiple images generated through classic image processing algorithms, fail to leverage the temporal information provided by adjacent frames in the original video sequences. On the other hand, 3D approaches can capture both spatial and temporal information but are hindered by high computational costs, which restrict their deployment, particularly in resource-constrained settings. Methodology

The proposed framework

Currently, mainstream DL frameworks for breast lesion differentiation are primarily based on static US images, often neglecting the critical features embedded in successive frames of dynamic breast US videos. Although some video-based frameworks using 3D DL models do exist, they involve a more complex design process and demand significantly higher computational resources, which limits their practicality in resource-constrained areas. Therefore, exploring alternative approaches that effectively leverage temporal features from video frames to enhance breast lesion differentiation while balancing performance and computational efficiency remains an important research priority.

In this study, we propose an improved approach inspired by radiologists practices, designed to effectively leverage both spatial and temporal critical features of lesions for automated breast lesion differentiation. Unlike existing methods that rely on static single images or 3D DL models, our framework employs a multi-channel input technique to integrate dynamic lesion features without incurring additional computational burdens. Through the multi-channel input technique, the dynamic changes of lesions over time can be effectively captured, thereby providing the DL model with a more comprehensive set of lesion features. This reduces the likelihood of misjudgment caused by noise or artifacts in single images, thereby lowering the overall misdiagnosis rate.

Building on the routine examination practices of radiologists, once a key frame is extracted from ultrasound video sequences, the preceding and subsequent image frames are also chosen. These consecutive tumor images are then concatenated in temporal order along the channel dimension. By feeding these multi-channel images into the model, it can learn the relationships between the different channels which contain multilevel lesion features. This process allows the model to progressively integrate feature information from each channel, enhancing its robustness and improving its overall performance in breast lesion differentiation. This method not only facilitates better learning of the internal structural information of tumors but also simplifies the process by concentrating exclusively on the critical features contained in each image. Network architecture

Our proposed framework can be implemented using various deep learning backbone models for classification. In this study, we selected five different classification backbone models for experimentation: ResNet [25], DenseNet [26], RepVGG [27], Next-ViT [28], and FastViT [29].These models were developed over different years and have demonstrated exceptional performance in vision tasks across a variety of application domains. They were selected to represent a broad spectrum of modern deep learning architectures, ranging from traditional CNNs to advanced transformer-based models. This diverse selection allows for a comprehensive evaluation of the effectiveness of our proposed multi-channel input strategy across different architectural paradigms. ResNet addresses the issues of vanishing and exploding gradients by introducing residual modules and connections, allowing for the training of deeper neural networks, thereby enhancing the model’s expressive capability and performance. DenseNet features a dense connectivity mechanism that improves the backpropagation of gradients and achieves shortcut connections through concatenated features, promoting feature reuse, increasing model accuracy, and significantly reducing the total number of parameters in the network. RepVGG employs the concept of structural reparameterization, transforming the multi-path structure of the training network into a single-path structure for inference, achieving higher performance and faster inference speeds. Next-ViT proposed an innovative CNN-Transformer hybrid strategy that balances local information acquisition with global information capture, improving efficiency and performance. FastViT enhances the classification accuracy using linear training time over-parameterization and replaces self-attention layers with large convolutional kernels, reducing computational complexity while maintaining performance.

Hyperparameter selection and optimization process

During the implementation process, hyperparameters were systematically tuned to optimize the performance of our multi-channel 2D DL model. The key hyperparameters considered included learning rate, batch size, number of epochs, and the choice of optimizer. After multiple attempts with different types of optimizers, the optimizer was set to AdamW based on its optimal performance. Using accuracy and AUC as evaluation metrics, the optimal batch size was determined to be 32, and the learning rate was set to 0.0001 with a decay coefficient of 0.0001. These settings were chosen based on the observed rate of loss decline and oscillations during training. The model was trained for 30 epochs with early stopping to prevent overfitting. It is worth mentioning that the hyperparameter tuning was performed using ResNet as the backbone model, and the optimized hyperparameter values were consistently applied to other deep learning architectures used in the study.

Statistical evaluation details

In this research, five commonly used evaluation metrics were employed to assess the performance of the proposed classification framework. These metrics include AUC (Area Under the Curve), accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score [31]. AUC represents the area under the ROC (Receiver-Operating Characteristic) curve, while accuracy indicates the proportion of correctly predicted samples among the total samples. Precision measures the probability that a sample predicted as positive is actually positive, and recall represents the probability of predicting positive samples among all actual positive samples. The F1 score reflects the model’s ability to maintain accuracy while predicting positive samples, serving as the harmonic mean of precision and recall. Datasets and experimental settings

Experimental data

The efficacy of our proposed multi-channel input framework was evaluated using two datasets acquired from different regions in China, employing diverse ultrasound imaging systems and settings. The first dataset, referred to as BUS-Data1 was obtained from the Liaoning Cancer Hospital. BUS-Data1 consists of 300 breast US videos, obtained from female patients between January 2023 and November 2023. The patients were examined using ultrasound imaging systems from Canon (Tokyo, Japan), Siemens (Munich, Germany) and Philips (Amsterdam, the Netherlands). The second dataset, referred to as BUS-Data2, was collected from the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao Medical University. BUS-Data2 is made up of 100 breast US videos, obtained from female patients between January 2024 and July 2024. The patients examinations were performed using ultrasound imaging systems from GE (USA, GE Medical Systems) and Mindray (China, Mindray Medical International).

For both datasets, approval for the investigation was obtained from the institutional review board of each respective institution. All participating patients provided their informed consent by signing a consent form before inclusion in the study. This process ensured that each patient was thoroughly informed about the studys objectives and procedures, and emphasized the voluntary nature of their participation.

Dataset preparation

The data preparation process consisted of several key steps, including the selection and preprocessing of informative video sections, determination of tumor type, selection of key frames, and delineation of the tumor region. In the first step, the entire patient examination video was reviewed, and the section with the fewest artifacts and the best visualization of the lesion was extracted. Subsequently, the extracted video section was cropped to remove device-specific and patient-sensitive information from the surrounding area of the ultrasound image, ensuring that only the image window was preserved. In the second step, puncture biopsy results were used to establish the classification of each patients lesion as either benign or malignant. This critical step ensured that the ground truth for each video was accurate, providing a reliable basis for further analysis and model training. In the third step, the video section was thoroughly analyzed to locate the image that presented the most informative details about the lesion. This key frame was then extracted, along with its preceding and following frames, to provide a sequence of images. Finally, the tumor region was manually delineated by two experienced radiologists, each with over 10 years of expertise in breast imaging. It is important to note that the image frames were initially annotated independently by each radiologist. Following this, each radiologist cross-checked the other’s delineation results for confirmation to ensure accuracy and consistency. Implementation details

To partition the dataset into training, validation, and test sets, we randomly sample a corresponding proportion of cases from both benign and malignant data based on a ratio of 7:1:2 from the 300 cases of BUS-Data1 provided by the Liaoning Cancer Hospital. Specifically, the training set consists of 209 cases, including 130 malignant and 79 benign cases; the validation set includes 29 cases, with 18 malignant and 11 benign cases; and the test set comprises 62 cases, consisting of 38 malignant and 24 benign cases. Cases from the BUS-Data2 were used for external validation, and consist of 50 malignant and 50 benign cases.

To create the multi-channel input for model training, the key image frame and its preceding and following image frames are resized to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$224\times 224$$\end{document} pixels, and concatenated along the channel dimension and saved in NumPy format. This process ensures that the input data incorporates a sequence of relevant frames, enabling the model to leverage both spatial and temporal features for improved learning and analysis. Due to the limited size of our dataset, the model was at risk of overfitting during training. To address this, we utilized pre-trained model weights from ImageNet and fine-tuned them on our dataset. Additionally, we augmented the training set by applying random horizontal and vertical shifts to the images, further enhancing the model’s robustness and reducing the likelihood of overfitting.

The deep learning framework proposed in this study is implemented based on the PyTorch framework, a widely used platform. All experiments were conducted on a Linux system equipped with an Intel(R) Core (TM) i5-9300H CPU. The training and testing of the model were carried out on a GeForce RTX 3080 graphics card with 10GB of memory, utilizing PyTorch 1.8.1, CUDA 11.1, and Python 3.8.

Results and discussions

Leveraging temporal information for enhanced breast lesion differentiation

The main objectives of our study are to explore a new approach that leverages both spatial and temporal lesion features to enhance differentiation performance, without increasing computational burdens. Additionally, we aim to thoroughly assess the significance of the critical features embedded in successive frames of dynamic breast ultrasound videos in achieving more accurate breast lesion analysis outcomes. To this end, we conducted a series of experiments involving both single static image input and multi-channel input. For the multi-channel input experiments, two different cases were considered. In case 1, the multi-channel input consists of a concatenation of three static ultrasound images, while in case 2, the multi-channel input comprises seven static ultrasound images. The decision to begin with a single image channel was made to establish a baseline, enabling us to evaluate the model’s performance when only spatial information from a single frame is utilized. This also provided a reference point for the incremental effect of adding temporal information.

In case 1, the use of three consecutive frames allows the model to capture a small yet meaningful temporal window, providing insight into the lesion’s behavior over time. This setup was chosen to evaluate the impact of adding minimal temporal information while maintaining computational efficiency. In case 2, seven consecutive frames are used to provide a more comprehensive temporal representation, offering the model a broader view of lesion dynamics. This configuration was tested to explore whether the inclusion of a larger temporal context significantly improves classification performance. It can be seen that, for most DL models, employing our proposed multi-channel input strategy results in better classification performance compared to training with 3D models, while significantly reducing the computational burden. For instance, compared to the 3D model, 2D RepVGG demonstrates improvements of 9.68% in accuracy and 12.79% in precision, along with reductions of 47.1% in FLOPs and 64.3% in the number of parameters. Therefore, our multi-channel input approach, leveraging 2D models, offers a more computationally efficient solution and tends to outperform 3D models in classification performance.

Limitations and future works

Our experimental analyses indicate that the proposed multi-channel input technique shows strong potential to enhance the performance of DL frameworks in automated breast lesion differentiation. However, additional aspects require further exploration to fully optimize this system.

First, the selection of key frames and delineation of breast lesions were manually performed by radiologists. These steps are not only tedious and time-consuming but also susceptible to subjectivity and inter-observer variability. Future efforts should focus on developing specialized DL techniques for automated key frame selection and lesion delineation to standardize and streamline the workflow, enhancing both efficiency and reproducibility.

Second, although the proposed framework was validated on multicenter data, the datasets used were relatively small, which may limit its generalizability across a broader range of clinical settings. Expanding the dataset with a broader range of imaging conditions and patient demographics could help address this limitation. Additionally, leveraging deep generative models to create synthetic data could further augment the dataset, enhancing model robustness and supporting better generalization across varied clinical environments.

Third, while the proposed framework achieved substantial performance gains, it relied exclusively on single-modality data, even though clinical practice often employs multimodal data for more accurate and efficient diagnosis. Integrating B-mode ultrasound with complementary modalities, such as Color Doppler and shear wave elastography, along with pertinent clinical information, could be an important direction for future exploration. This multimodal approach could not only enhance diagnostic precision but also improve the framework’s adaptability and utility in real-world clinical applications, making it more robust and clinically relevant.

Conclusion

This study proposed a promising DL-based approach inspired by radiologists’ clinical practices to enhance automated breast lesion differentiation by incorporating both spatial and temporal features from ultrasound videos. Through a multi-channel input technique, critical lesion features from consecutive frames are leveraged to enhance diagnostic accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency, avoiding the heavy demands of 3D models. Extensive experiments conducted with multicenter data from diverse regions of China have confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The integration of dynamic temporal features significantly enhanced model performance, highlighting the potential for this method to improve diagnostic precision. These results highlight the potential of our approach as a viable solution to overcome practical challenges in automated breast lesion analysis, particularly in resource-limited environments. The method offers the possibility of deployment for real-time use, with the promise of reducing healthcare costs, providing effective support for clinical applications across diverse healthcare settings, and advancing the role of deep learning in medical imaging.

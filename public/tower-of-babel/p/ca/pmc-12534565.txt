Optical and SAR image registration based on feature constrained algorithm
Authors: Wannan Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraditional feature matching methods rely on accurate descriptors to describe the attributes of feature points, which may lead to the problem of misalignment when the descriptors are incorrect. Considering that correctly matched point pairs should possess the same position, scale, and main orientation after undergoing a transformation model, this paper proposes a feature-constrained registration method for aligning optical and SAR images. In the feature detection stage, a PC_Harris operator is employed, which combines log-Gabor filters with the maximum rectangular phase consistency to detect scale-space features. Compared to traditional multi-scale Harris feature detectors, the PC_Harris feature detector significantly improves the accuracy and stability of feature point matching between optical and SAR images. Furthermore, scale, position, and orientation constraints are introduced in the feature matching process, effectively reducing the problem of misalignment. Experimental results demonstrate its superior matching performance with respect to the state-of-the-art methods.Keywords: SAR, Optical images, Feature-constrained, PC_HarrisSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science

Introduction

Frequency-domain phase consistency methods utilize the frequency information of images to achieve accurate feature matching by comparing phase consistency, thereby enhancing the robustness and reliability of the algorithm. Particularly, this method shows remarkable performance in the registration of optical and SAR images. However, this approach is sensitive to image scaling, posing a significant challenge to improve the adaptability of feature detectors to scale differences. Additionally, the SIFT algorithm relies on differential approximation methods to obtain gradient data from images1–3. In SAR images, speckle noise can interfere with the gradient computation process. Specifically, using differential approximation methods to extract gradient data from optical and SAR images may result in information loss in low reflectance areas and misleading information in high reflectance areas, thereby compromising the stability of gradient information and affecting descriptor correlation. This issue needs to be addressed to enhance the accuracy and robustness of optical and SAR image registration. Therefore, when processing SAR images, these factors need to be considered in the impact on feature detection and descriptor matching. This problem needs to be effectively addressed in the improved registration method to enhance registration accuracy and robustness.

The focus of this paper is to improve the feature matching stage of the SIFT algorithm. Compared to the original SIFT algorithm, these improvements are mainly demonstrated in two aspects. First, the application of phase consistency algorithm utilizing log-Gabor filters based on maximum rectangular phase consistency and multiple scales and orientations. The key step in the convolution process is to create a suitable scale space and then perform Harris feature detection in this space, effectively accomplishing feature extraction and detection in images. This method fully considers the multi-scale and multi-directional characteristics of images, providing more comprehensive and accurate feature information, thereby enhancing feature robustness and accuracy. Second, given the limitations of the SIFT algorithm in gradient computation and descriptor construction, this paper proposes improvements to enhance descriptor similarity and robustness4,5. Fang P proposed a novel approach for automatically detecting subsurface reflectors from orbital radar data, leveraging a combination of phase congruency and the shearlet system for multiscale analysis6. Zhang P proposed a high-level visual feature, namely, phase symmetry, to effectively improve the feature resolution and the robustness of amplitude change in GPR images7. Fan J proposed a novel feature descriptor named pyramid features of orientated self-similarity (POSS) for multimodal remote sensing image matching, which integrates phase congruency (PC) into the self-similarity model for better encoding structural information8.

The key objective of these improvements is to enable the registration algorithm to accurately match key feature points between heterogeneous images, even in the presence of significant geometric and radiometric differences, thus achieving effective registration. This series of improvements will contribute to enhancing the performance of the SIFT algorithm in optical and SAR image registration, providing more reliable tools for automated processing and analysis of remote sensing images. Methodology

Descriptor construction based on gradient position and orientation histograms

Due to its efficient handling of nonlinear radiometric differences between SAR and optical images, the phase consistency algorithm has received considerable attention in research. Currently, numerous methods rely on this technique, primarily utilizing it in conjunction with maximum moment and corner detection to identify feature points in optical and SAR images, thereby enhancing the registration effectiveness between the two. Given the widespread occurrence of scale variations in remote sensing imagery, this study draws inspiration from the spatial-domain-based multiscale Harris feature detection method9. This strategy aims to leverage the spatial structures at multiple scales to account for the scale differences between optical and SAR images. Consequently, a novel approach is proposed to address the challenges faced by phase consistency feature detectors when dealing with images of various scales in both optical and SAR domains.

Traditional multiscale Harris algorithms have been widely used to address the issue of large-scale variations in optical and SAR images. However, current phase consistency algorithms often fail to adequately consider the scale differences between optical and SAR images, leading to poor performance when dealing with significant scale changes10. To address this challenge, this paper introduces an innovative approach that combines multiscale characteristics, phase consistency-based scale space construction, and log-Gabor filters. The GLOH descriptor employs a unique circular structure, where the ratio between the radius of the smallest circle and the radius of the largest circle is 1:4, while the ratio between the radius of the middle circle and the radius of the largest circle is 3:4. This design enables the GLOH descriptor to possess better scale invariance and discriminability. By dividing the circular neighborhood of a feature point into 17 equally sized sectors, each sector includes 16 gradient histogram bins. Therefore, this circular-based descriptor has 272 dimensions. This circular-based structure ensures the uniform integration of information around the feature point, capturing surrounding details more effectively compared to the rectangular descriptors of SIFT and enhancing the robustness of the descriptors.To further enhance the performance of these descriptors, an improved structure called GLOH-Like descriptor is introduced in this paper. This variant consists of three sets of scale-related circular neighborhoods with radii of 8, 12, and 16. For each circular neighborhood, the GLOH descriptor form is used, with the difference being the partitioning of the gradient vector histograms of each sub-neighborhood into eight bins13. As a result, the dimensionality of the GLOH-Like descriptor is 3*17*8 = 408. This refined descriptor structure retains the advantages of GLOH, further expanding the dimensionality and expressive power of feature points, contributing to improved image matching performance.

To ensure the rotational stability of the algorithm, a principal orientation is assigned to each feature point. To achieve this, a scale-dependent approach is employed, where the gradient information of both optical and SAR images is computed, and gradient histograms are constructed. The gradient direction ranges from 0° to 180° and is divided into eight bins, forming the gradient orientation histogram. The peaks in the histogram represent the principal orientation of the feature point.To improve the accuracy of the principal orientation and the stability of feature matching, two strategies are employed. Firstly, weights are introduced to consider that pixels closer to the feature point should have higher weights. A circular Gaussian weighting function is commonly used to address the issue of unstable gradient directions around the feature point. Secondly, a parabolic quadratic interpolation is applied to fit the discrete gradient orientation histogram, obtaining a more precise estimation of the principal orientation.By combining these methods, the computation of the principal orientation becomes more accurate and reliable, enhancing the rotational invariance of the feature points and thus improving the performance of image matching. These techniques enhance the accuracy of the principal orientation estimation and increase the stability of feature matching, ultimately strengthening the overall performance of image matching. Matching strategy based on feature constraints

Once the principal orientations of each feature point are determined, the next step is to perform feature matching. However, mismatches may occur due to errors in the descriptor that describes the properties of the feature points. To address this issue, a feature-constrained matching strategy is introduced in this chapter to enhance the accuracy of the matching process. By leveraging feature constraints, better control and adjustment of the matching process can be achieved, leading to more accurate matching results.Let Po = {po1, po2, …, pom} denote the set of feature points extracted from the optical image, and Ps = {ps1, ps2, …, pon} denote the set of feature points extracted from the SAR image. Each feature point poi in the optical image has four attributes: (xoi, yoi, σoi, θoi,), while each feature point psi in the SAR image also has four attributes: (xsi, ysi, σsi, θsi,) After careful analysis, it is evident that the transformation errors between these feature points primarily consist of three aspects: position, scale, and orientation.If we describe this transformation pattern using an affine transformation model H, the aforementioned three types of offsets can be summarized as follows:

Here is a description of the specific implementation process for the feature matching strategy:First, the initial set of matched points PP1 is obtained by applying the nearest neighbor distance ratio (NNDR) matching strategy. Next, a histogram is created to calculate and predict the initial transformation matrix H, taking into account the distance of position movement, scale ratio, and differences in principal orientation. In the second round, the NNDR matching strategy is applied again, using epσo(poi, psj) as a new distance metric, resulting in a re-matched point set PP2. However, there may still be some erroneous matches in PP2. To eliminate these errors, the Fast Sample Consensus (FSC) method is employed. FSC is an iterative optimization algorithm used for model estimation and registration. It is based on the theory of Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC) algorithm, estimating model parameters through a process of random sampling and consistency judgment. The goal of the FSC algorithm is to select a subset of data points from a dataset that is most consistent with the current model hypothesis and use these consistent data points to calculate the model parameters. Its advantages include fast speed, applicability to large-scale datasets, and the ability to provide reliable estimates even in the presence of noise and outliers. This article utilized a dataset consisting of 300 pairs of remote sensing images for experimental analysis. Figure 4 shows a selection of representative test images. The dataset includes 300 optical images and 300 synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images, covering various terrains and environments such as urban areas, suburbs, rivers, and mountains. Experiments of image registration

In this paper, we address the issue of low robustness in feature point detection for SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) and optical images by proposing a method that utilizes phase consistency to match these two types of images. To validate the effectiveness of our proposed method, we conduct registration experiments in this section, considering different scales, rotation angles, and feature detection algorithms, and comparing them with recent optical and SAR image registration algorithms13–18. Hnatushenko et al.13 introduced a novel variational method for registering optical and SAR images, where a constrained optimization problem is considered on the affine transformation set. Li et al.14 proposed a frequency-domain-based RIFT algorithm, implemented based on phase consistency and log-Gabor filters. Zhang et al.15 presented a registration method based on robust point features, utilizing two different gradient operators to construct a scale space and extract features. Liaghat et al.16 proposed a coarse-to-fine registration method based on geographic reference and deep learning framework, which combines geographic reference information with deep learning registration methods to reduce outliers and increase the proportion of correct correspondences. Chen et al. presented a deep learning-based registration method for SAR and optical images17, which employs a Common Attention Matching Module (CAMM) to integrate the structural feature maps of image pairs and extract key points from individual images. Xin et al.18 proposed a Modality Shared Attention Network (MSA-Net), which introduces Non-Local Attention (NLA) in a partially shared dual-stream network to jointly leverage local and global features. This method designs a modality-specific feature learning module that can efficiently extract shallow modality-specific features from SAR and optical images.

Conclusions

Traditional feature matching methods rely on accurate descriptors to describe the attributes of feature points. However, when the descriptors are incorrect, it can easily lead to the problem of misalignment. Considering that correctly matched point pairs should have the same position, scale, and main orientation after the transformation, we propose a feature-constrained registration method for optical and SAR image alignment. In the feature detection stage, we employ the PC_Harris operator, which is a scale-space feature detector that combines log-Gabor filters and phase-consistency maximum moments. Compared to traditional multi-scale Harris feature detectors, the PC_Harris feature detector significantly improves the accuracy and stability of feature point matching between optical and SAR images. Additionally, we introduce constraints on the scale, position, and orientation of feature matching, effectively reducing the problem of misalignment. Experimental results demonstrate its superior matching performance with respect to the state-of-the-art methods.

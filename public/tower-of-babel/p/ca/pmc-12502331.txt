Neutrophil extracellular trapping network-associated biomarkers in liver fibrosis: machine learning and experimental validation
Authors: Yanbo Li, Yanping Lu, Bohao Huang, Chao Lei, Qingjuan Wu, Jiuchong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe diagnostic and therapeutic potential of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) in liver fibrosis (LF) has not been fully explored. We aim to screen and verify NETs-related liver fibrosis biomarkers through machine learning.MethodsIn order to obtain NETs-related differentially expressed genes (NETs-DEGs), differential analysis and WGCNA analysis were performed on the GEO dataset (GSE84044, GSE49541) and the NETs dataset. Enrichment analysis and protein interaction analysis were used to reveal the candidate genes and potential mechanisms of NETs-related liver fibrosis. Biomarkers were screened using SVM-RFE and Boruta machine learning algorithms, followed by immune infiltration analysis. A multi-stage model of fibrosis in mice was constructed, and neutrophil infiltration, NETs accumulation and NETs-related biomarkers were characterized by immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, flow cytometry and qPCR. Finally, the molecular regulatory network and potential drugs of biomarkers were predicted. ResultsA total of 166 NETs-DEGs were identified. Through enrichment analysis, these genes were mainly enriched in chemokine signaling pathway and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway. Machine learning screened CCL2 as a NETs-related liver fibrosis biomarker, involved in ribosome-related processes, cell cycle regulation and allograft rejection pathways. Immune infiltration analysis showed that there were significant differences in 22 immune cell subtypes between fibrotic samples and healthy samples, including neutrophils mainly related to NETs production. The results of in vivo experiments showed that neutrophil infiltration, NETs accumulation and CCL2 level were up-regulated during fibrosis. A total of 5 miRNAs, 2 lncRNAs, 20 function-related genes and 6 potential drugs were identified based on CCL2.ConclusionsThis study identified CCL2 as a biomarker for nets-related liver fibrosis, providing a new perspective for understanding the mechanisms of nets-mediated fibrosis and promoting therapeutic discovery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-06819-2.Keywords: Bioinformatics, Machine learning, Liver fibrosis, NETs

Introduction

Liver fibrosis, a pathological consequence of chronic hepatic injury induced by viral infections, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), or autoimmune disorders, is driven by dysregulated chronic inflammation that overrides self-limiting tissue repair mechanisms [1, 2]. Sustained inflammatory responses initiate a dynamic cascade of maladaptive processes, including hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) activation, excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, and scar formation [3]. These pathological alterations exacerbate hepatic microvascular insufficiency and architectural distortion, culminating in progressive functional impairment. Epidemiological studies reveal a staggering global mortality of ~ 2 million annually from liver diseases, with approximately half attributed to cirrhosis and its complications [4]. This unmet clinical burden underscores the critical need for targeted interventions to halt fibrotic progression (Fig. 1).

Research idea map

The interplay between dysregulated immunity and fibrogenesis has emerged as a critical research frontier. Neutrophils, the most abundant innate immune effector cells, play a dual role in hepatic pathophysiology [5]. Beyond their canonical antimicrobial functions, activated neutrophils execute NETosis—a programmed cell death pathway—to release NETs, chromatin-mesh structures decorated with cytotoxic granular proteins [6]. While NETs contribute to pathogen containment, emerging evidence implicates aberrant NETs formation in perpetuating chronic inflammation and tissue damage through direct ECM modification and paracrine pro-fibrotic signaling [7, 8]. Despite these mechanistic insights, the diagnostic and prognostic potential of NETs-related biomarkers in liver fibrosis remains underexplored, particularly regarding their dynamic expression patterns across fibrotic stages.

Herein, we establish an integrative computational-experimental framework combining machine learning with multi-modal validation to identify and characterize NETs-related biomarkers in liver fibrosis. Leveraging machine learning algorithms and bioinformatics analysis, we systematically screened hepatic transcriptomic datasets to prioritize NETs-associated fibrosis genes. Subsequent immune infiltration profiling elucidated stage-specific correlations between biomarker candidates and the remodeling of immunological landscapes during disease progression. Experimental validation employing multi-stage fibrotic murine models incorporated flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, and qPCR to confirm the diagnostic potency and mechanistic relevance of identified biomarkers. This study establishes a novel paradigm for decoding NETosis-mediated immunopathology, providing both methodological advancements in fibrosis biomarker discovery and mechanistically validated therapeutic targets for anti-fibrotic intervention.

Method

Data acquisition and processing

Transcriptomic datasets GSE84044 and GSE49541 with corresponding platform files were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.govgeo/) and designated as training and validation cohorts, respectively [9]. Detailed metadata including demographic characteristics, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and sequencing protocols are provided in Table S1.

Principal component analysis was performed using the FactoMineR package in R (v4.2.3) [10] to identify and remove outlier samples (Figure S1). Differential expression analysis between LF and control groups in the training set was conducted via the limma package (v3.56.2) [11], applying thresholds of |log₂(fold change)|≥ 0.5 and false discovery rate-adjusted P-value < 0.05 to define significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs).

NETs-related genes were compiled from peer-reviewed publications (PMID: 36,532,016) [12] through systematic keyword searches ("neutrophil extracellular traps"AND"liver fibrosis"NOT"review"[Publication Type]).

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

NETs correlation scores were calculated for all samples using the ssGSEA calculation method of the Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) package for the R language, and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) analysis [13] was performed using the NETs gene set in the GSE84044 dataset, and the samples were analyzed by clustering. To ensure that the inter-gene interactions maximally conformed to the scale-free distribution, we used the expression matrices of all genes as input data, operated on them to determine the soft thresholds, and chose the optimal soft threshold (β) to be 5 (Figure S2).

Functional enrichment analysis and protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction

Differentially expressed module genes underwent Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses using clusterProfiler (v4.8.3) [14], visualized via ggplot2 (v3.4.3) [15]. GSVA was implemented to quantify pathway activity. The STRING database (v12.0) generated PPI networks under stringent criteria (combined score > 0.9, experimental evidence only), with topological analysis and visualization performed in Cytoscape (v3.10.0).

The Biological General Repository for Interaction Datasets (BIOGRID) database (https://thebiogrid.org/) is currently one of the most commonly used biomolecular interaction databases internationally. The latest version of species-wide protein interaction data (BIOGRID-ALL-4.4.245) was first downloaded from the BIOGRID database. Subsequently, 44 candidate genes of interest to the project were selected and screened for all interactions recorded in relation to these genes. During the screening process, it was first restricted that all the interactions proteins were human (Homo sapiens) proteins to ensure the biological relevance of the analysis. Next, a rigorous screening was performed according to the confidence level of the experimental method. Only protein interactions of physical type were retained, and interactions of genetic type were excluded to ensure that the analytical results focused on direct physical binding evidence; only protein interactions such as Co-crystal Structure, Cross-Linking-MS, Proximity Label-MS, Affinity Label-MS, Capture-MS, Affinity Label-MS, and Affinity Label-MS were retained to ensure that the analysis results focused on direct physical binding evidence. Capture-MS, Affinity Capture-Western, Reconstituted Complex, and other high-confidence experimentally supported interactions. Finally, all the screened high-confidence interworking pairs were organized and summarized, and their direct interworking network data files were exported separately for each core gene.

Biomarker screening and bioinformatics validation

Two complementary feature selection algorithms are used: Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) [16] and Boruta Algorithm [17]. SVM-RFE algorithm processes high-dimensional data through the reverse elimination strategy guided by the maximum margin principle of support vector machine. The Boruta algorithm determines the real features by comparing the importance of the original features with the randomly disrupted shadow features. The combination of SVM-RFE and Boruta ensures the breadth and reliability of feature selection. The genes obtained by the two machine learning algorithms through the screening of the training set were intersected, and the difference in expression of the intersected genes was visualized by Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test using the stats package in R [18].

Immune infiltration profiling

ssGSEA quantified the relative abundance of 28 immune cell subtypes. Heatmap (pheatmap v1.0.12) showed different infiltration profiles between healthy populations and patients with liver fibrosis [19]. Cell–cell correlation networks were constructed by Pearson correlation (psych v2.3.6) and visualized using corrplot (v0.92).

Experimental validation

Animal models

Male C57BL/6J mice (8-week-old) were housed under SPF conditions (12/12 light cycle, 22 ± 1 °C). All procedures adhered to ARRIVE guidelines and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care Committee of Guang’anmen Hospital (IACUC-2025-103-SQ).

Fibrosis induction

Hepatic fibrosis was induced by intraperitoneal injection of 10% CCl4 (v/v in olive oil) at 5 μL/g body weight, twice weekly.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The paraffin sections of each group were dewaxed, hydrated and then antigenically repaired with citrate repair buffer, the tissue area was circled using a special water-blocking pen, the peroxidase blocker was added dropwise with primary antibody, and the reaction enhancer and secondary antibody were added dropwise with reaction enhancer and secondary antibody after overnight incubation at 4 °C, and DAB color development was performed. The positive staining area of the acquired field of view was measured using Image J software.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

Sections were permeabilized (0.1% Triton X-100), blocked with 5% goat serum, and incubated with primary antibodies. Alexa Fluor 488/555-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:500) were applied for 1 h, with DAPI nuclear counterstaining.

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR

Total RNA was extracted by weighing the liver tissues of mice in each group, and the RNA was converted to cDNA according to the instructions of the reverse transcription kit, PCR amplification was performed, and the fluorescence signals were monitored by fluorescence technology. Primers: MPO: F 5′-CAATGTCTTCACCAACGCTTTC-3′, R 5′-AATGCCACCTTCCAACACG-3′;NE: F 5′-CGCTACCATTAACGCCAACG-3′, R 5′-GCGGCACATGTTAGTCACCA-3′;CCL2: F 5′-CAGGTCCCTGTCATGCTTCT-3′, R 5′-GTGGGGCGTTAACTGCATCT-3′;GAPDH: F 5′-CCTCGTCCCGTAGACAAAATG-3′, R 5′-TGAGGTCAATGAAGGGGTCGT-3′.

Flow cytometry

Fresh liver tissue was taken and the tissue was cut and ground, added with erythrocyte lysate and incubated at 4 °C for 10–15 min, washed with PBS and added with APC anti-mouse CD45 antibody, FITC anti-mouse CD11b antibody, PE anti-mouse Ly6G antibody and incubated for 30 min away from light, washed with PBS and added with flow cytometric fixative and incubated for 1 h. The sample was resuspended with cell staining buffer and then transferred to flow tubes. Detection was performed using a flow cytometer and analyzed by FlowJo v.10.8.1 software.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed and plotted using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 software. Data that conformed to normal distribution in this study were expressed as mean ± SEM, and multiple comparisons were made using one-way ANOVA. P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.

Screening and functional analysis of NETs-associated differentially expressed genes

The intersection of 355 module genes obtained from Fig. 2G and 442 differentially expressed genes resulted in 166 NETs-DEGs (Fig. 3A). In order to understand the pathways involved in biological functions of NETs-DEGs, GO enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis were performed. The GO enrichment results of the top 5 are shown in Fig. 3B. In the BP classification, the most significantly enriched terms include positive regulation of leukocyte activation, positive regulation of cell activation, and regulation of leukocyte cell–cell adhesion. CC analysis showed that MHC class II protein complex, immunological synapse and MHC protein complex were significantly enriched. In the MF classification, chemokine receptor binding, chemokine activity, and CCR chemokine receptor binding were significantly enriched.

KEGG enrichment results showed that (Fig. 3C), NETs-DEGs were mainly enriched in the interaction of viral proteins with cytokines and cytokine receptors, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, chemokine signaling pathway, phagosome, intestinal immune network produced by IgA, antigen processing and presentation pathways. The protein–protein interaction network (PPIs) was constructed to demonstrate the protein-level interaction of NETs-DEGs (Fig. 3D) to determine the main pathological factors of NETs-DEGs affecting liver fibrosis at the molecular level. Machine learning screening of NETs-associated liver fibrosis biomarkers

SVM-RFE and Boruta algorithms were used to identify nets-related liver fibrosis biomarkers. A total of 44 CGs were analyzed by Boruta algorithm, and two characteristic genes were screened out (Fig. 4A). Based on the SVM-RFE algorithm, 33 genes such as HLA-DPB1, CD8A, CCL2, HLA-DMA, and CXCL10 were identified as characteristic genes (Fig. 4B). Subsequently, the intersection of the two machine learning algorithms was taken and finally 2 candidate genes CCL2, NCF1 were identified. Validation of the expression of the two genes in the two datasets (Fig. 4C, D) revealed that the CCL2 gene was defined as a biomarker with consistent and significantly different expression trends in both the training and validation sets (P < 0.05). CCL2 gene was distributed on chromosome 17 (Fig. 4E). To further investigate the biomarker-related functions, single-gene GSEA enrichment analysis of CCL2 was performed, and the results showed that ribosomes, cell cycle, allograft rejection, chemokine signaling pathway, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction are the top 5 ranked pathways (Fig. 4F).

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune infiltration analysis was performed using the ssGSEA algorithm to calculate the scores of 28 immune cells in all samples. As shown in Fig. 5A, the heatmap of immune cell infiltration between normal and liver fibrosis populations was plotted using the R package “pheatmap”. After that, we plotted the box plots of the immune scores of immune cell populations between normal and liver fibrosis populations. As shown in Fig. 5B, 22 immune cells were significantly up-regulated (P < 0.05) in liver fibrosis populations, including neutrophils, which are directly related to the formation of NETs. In order to demonstrate the interaction of immune cell populations, Spearman correlation was used to calculate and draw a correlation heat map of 22 differential immune cells, as shown in Fig. 5C. The results suggest that there is a significant correlation between neutrophils and activated dendritic cell, effector memeory CD8 T cell, immature B cell, immature dendritic cell in patients with liver fibrosis.

Exacerbation of liver fibrosis accompanied by elevated neutrophil levels

To further illustrate the role of neutrophils in the exacerbation of hepatic fibrosis, a mouse model of hepatic fibrosis at different stages was constructed by intraperitoneal injection of CCL4 5 μL/g-bw twice weekly, and the samples were taken at 0, 4, 6, and 8 weeks after the start of modeling (Fig. 6A). Liver tissues at weeks 0, 4, 6, and 8 were stained to characterize inflammatory cell infiltration and fiber deposition using HE, MASSON, and Sirius red (Fig. 6B-D), and the model groups at weeks 4, 6, and 8 represented the process of increased inflammatory cell infiltration and increased fiber deposition in the progression of liver fibrosis, as demonstrated by quantitative analyses of the MASSON and Sirius red results (Fig. 6E-F). We identified by flow cytometry that neutrophil levels in mouse liver tissues were elevated along with the exacerbation of hepatic fibrosis (Fig. 6G-H), which was similarly supported by immunohistochemical results of the neutrophil-specific marker Ly6G (Fig. 6I, J).

Liver fibrosis progression accompanied by NETs accumulation and elevated CCL2 levels

In order to evaluate the changes of NETs in the progression of liver fibrosis, MPO and NE, markers of NETs, were detected in different stages of liver fibrosis models. The results showed that MPO and NE were elevated with the progression of liver fibrosis, accompanied by the elevation of the hepatic stellate cell marker α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), which we verified by immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence and q-PCR (Fig. 7A-B, I-J). Immunofluorescence co-localization results showed that there was a certain co-localization feature between NE and α-SMA (Fig. 7D). Immunohistochemistry and q-PCR results showed the dynamic changes of CCL2 during the progression of liver fibrosis, and the results showed that CCL2 expression increased with the aggravation of liver fibrosis (Fig. 7C, K).

Biomarker CCL2 ceRNA, geneMANIA analysis and drug prediction

We constructed a ceRNA network based on CCL2 biomarkers using Starbase, miRDB database (Fig. 8A). This network included a total of 8 nodes (1 mRNA, 5 miRNAs and 2 lncRNAs) and 13 edges. Twenty CCL2-associated co-regulated genes (CCL15, CXCL8, IL6) and 139 enriched biological processes (e.g., leukocyte chemotaxis, chemokine response) were identified by GeneMANIA analysis (Fig. 8B), further expanding the functional context of CCL2. To obtain potential drugs targeting key genes, the DGIdb database was utilized to mine six potential drugs targeting CCL2 extremely receptors, including Bindarit, RS-504393, and Risperidone (Fig. 8C).

Discussion

Liver fibrosis remains a global healthcare challenge, and new biomarkers are needed to decipher its molecular pathology and guide personalized therapy. The novel role of NETs—chromatin protein complexes released via netosis—has recently been expanded beyond antimicrobial defense to participate in the pathogenesis of diseases including autoimmune disorders and cancer. Pioneering studies in Babuta M et al. (2024) and Xia Y et al. (2025) demonstrated the accumulation of NETs in a mouse model of liver fibrosis [7, 8], but the translational potential of NETs-associated biomarkers in patients with liver fibrosis remains unexplored. The present study fills this gap to some extent by verifying the significant difference in NETs correlation scores between healthy and liver fibrosis populations based on the RNA sequencing dataset of liver fibrosis patients in the GEO database, and identifying NETs-related biomarkers of liver fibrosis by combining machine learning and experimental validation.

Through comprehensive analysis of GEO datasets from fibrotic human livers, we identified 166 NETs-DEGs, screened by machine learning algorithms (SVM-RFE and Boruta), and CCL2 emerged as the central hub of NETs-associated liver fibrosis. Three independent validation phases confirmed the progressive elevation of CCL2 in the fibrotic phase, with a parallel increase in neutrophil infiltration and NETs labeling. In addition, from the dynamic development of hepatic fibrosis with the prolongation of modeling time and aggravation of collagen deposition, the level of NETs increased simultaneously, which suggests that the accumulation of NETs correlates with the severity level of hepatic fibrosis, a finding that breaks through the limitations of the single time node model in previous studies.

While emerging studies have begun to characterize NETs'role in HSCs activation, the mechanistic panorama of NETs-driven fibrogenesis remains fragmented. Our spatial co-localization analyses revealed progressive increases in NE and α-SMA expression during fibrotic progression (Fig. 7), suggesting dynamic interplay between NETs deposition and HSCs activation. Previous studies predominantly focused on two pathways: TLR3/COX-2 axis [7] and NLRP3 inflammasome [8]. Notably, our pathway enrichment analyses extend beyond these canonical routes. The dominance of chemokine signaling and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction among NETs-DEGs (Fig. 3) implies a broader immunoregulatory role. Particularly intriguing is the enrichment of intestinal immune network for IgA production, suggesting potential gut-liver axis involvement in NETs-mediated fibrosis, a novel angle warranting future investigation.

CCL2, also known as MCP-1, is a small molecular weight protein of the C–C chemokine family. In previous studies CCL2 has shown potential as a biomarker for nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and NASH [20, 21]. Consistent with our results, the presence of CCL2 levels in alcoholic hepatitis, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis models were both upregulated [22, 23]. The current understanding of the pathomechanisms of CCL2 involvement in hepatic fibrosis is mainly related to the fact that CCL2 regulates the infiltration of inflammatory cells, such as macrophages and T-cells, which promotes the activation of myofibroblasts from HSCs and the differentiation of metastase [24, 25]. The present study identified the novel potential of CCL2 as a biomarker for NETs-associated liver fibrosis. It is reasonable to suggest that CCL2 may provide a pathway for crosstalk between NETs and macrophages. First, NETs have been found to be able to influence macrophage phenotype through the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway, toll-like receptor 9 pathway in both renal fibrosis, and myocardial infarction models [26, 27]. Second, NETs were found to be able to recruit macrophages by upregulating CCL2 expression in a schistosomiasis model [28]. However, this conjectural result needs to be verified in liver fibrosis models. In conclusion, the inflammatory response characterized by infiltration of immune cells such as neutrophils and macrophages is considered to be a key factor in the initiation of hepatic fibrosis, and the intricate relationship between immune cells has been the focus of interest and challenge. The findings of the present study provide a new entry point for a deeper understanding of the immune cell crosstalk mechanisms involved in NETs.

While this study yielded noteworthy findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the limited sample size derived from only two datasets may restrict the generalizability of our conclusions. Future investigations should validate these results across multi-center cohorts with larger and more diverse populations. Second, the current analysis focused solely on binary classification between liver fibrosis patients and healthy controls. Future studies could expand the scope by incorporating liver fibrosis staging as a classification criterion, which would enhance the clinical relevance of differential gene screening and machine learning models. Finally, although our biomarker validation relied on clinical samples, preclinical models such as genetically modified murine models or gene-knockdown cell lines could provide mechanistic insights into the identified biomarkers, a direction warranting further exploration.

Conclusion

In summary, we analyzed an RNA dataset of human liver tissues from liver fibrosis and screened 166 NETs-DEG. revealing their involvement in the biological processes and pathways of liver fibrosis. CCL2 was identified as a NETs-associated biomarker of liver fibrosis by machine learning method. Immune infiltration analysis was also performed on the liver fibrosis dataset to reveal the changes of immune cell populations in the process of liver fibrosis. Finally, in vivo experiments verified that the progression of liver fibrosis was accompanied by neutrophil infiltration, NETs recruitment, and elevated CCL2 levels. This study provides new biomarkers for the mechanism of NETs-associated liver fibrosis. However, the specific mechanism of CCL2 needs to be further explored.

Undiagnosed dyspnea with pulmonary nodule
Authors: Cheng‐Hsien Tsou, Hwa‐Yen Chiu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Key messagePhysicians should remain vigilant about alternative causes of shortness of breath even when respiratory diseases are being effectively treated. The lateral view of chest radiography can be valuable in discerning the three‐dimensional characteristics of pulmonary abnormalities.Keywords: arteriovenous malformation, dyspnea, solitary pulmonary nodule

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 55‐year‐old female with childhood asthma came to our chest clinic for shortness of breath for months. She already received inhaled corticosteroids and bronchodilators but her symptoms persisted. She denied recent events of rhinitis, food allergy or urticaria. Her oxygen saturation was 92% under ambient air. Chest radiography showed a faint right lower lobe nodule with feeding vessels on the PA view (Figure 1A) and the vessels are more obvious on the lateral view (Figure 1B). Max intensity projection of computed tomography of the chest (Figure 2) and its 3‐D reconstruction (Video 1) confirmed the diagnosis of pulmonary arteriovenous malformation with feeding vessels from right lateral segmental artery and drained into lateral part of middle lobe vein. She was referred to a tertiary medical center for embolization. Following this, her dyspnea resolved with an improved oxygen saturation of 98%.

Two dilated parallel vascular structures (white arrows) on chest radiography on both PA view (A) and lateral view (B), connected to the round, well‐defined nodule lesion in the anterior aspect of the right lower lobe.

Chest computer tomography confirmed the diagnosis of pulmonary arteriovenous malformation and demonstrated feeding vessels from the right lateral segmental artery, and drained into the lateral part of the middle lobe vein clearly.

3‐D reconstruction of the computer tomography.

More than 80% of the PAVMs are congenital, acquired PAVMs are associated with chest trauma, thoracic surgery, cirrhosis, and infections. PAVMs decrease pulmonary vascular resistance, increase pulmonary shunting, and result in oxygen desaturation. 1 Our patient denied history of nosebleeds, gastrointestinal bleeding, headache, trauma or surgery. There were no abnormal vessels over her skin. Congenital idiopathic PAVM is the most favourable diagnosis.

Novel Peptide Therapeutics Targeting the L-Type Calcium Channel Prevent Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy by Decreasing Mitochondrial Energetics
Authors: Teagan S Er, Daniel Reinke, Alice J Francis, Tanya M Solomon, Helena M Viola, Henrietta Cserne Szappanos, Caitlyn M Richworth, Catherine Jenkins, Juan E Camacho Londoño, Padmapriya Ponnuswamy, Ciaran McFarlane, Filip Van Petegem, Livia C Hool
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Visual AbstractKey Words: αMHC403/+, cardiac, cTnI-G203S, mitochondria, prevention

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is an inherited autosomal dominant disease that affects approximately 1:500 of the general population. HCM arises because of genetic sequence variants in sarcomere proteins or proteins that regulate cardiac contractile function.1 The hallmark clinical features of HCM include diastolic dysfunction and left ventricular and septal wall thickening in the absence of increased hemodynamic workload or hypertension.2,3 At the cellular level, HCM is associated with hypercontractility, cardiac myocyte remodeling, cytoskeletal disarray, interstitial fibrosis, disruptions in calcium handling, and impaired energy metabolism.4,5

Sequence variants in the cTnI gene (TNNI3) and the β-myosin heavy chain (MHC) gene (MYH7) are among the more common associated with the development of HCM.6 Both proteins play important roles in cross-bridge cycling and cardiac muscle contractility.7, 8, 9 Cytoskeletal proteins are also important in regulating these processes. The cytoskeleton forms the scaffold of cardiac muscle cells and extends from the plasma membrane to z-disks of the sarcomere, and traverse organelles such as the t-tubule network, sarcoplasmic reticulum, and mitochondria.10 We have previously demonstrated that the cardiac L-type calcium channel (LTCC) is involved in the development of HCM. Specifically, we have shown that cytoskeletal disarray results in a “communication breakdown” between the LTCC and mitochondria, leading to a hypermetabolic mitochondrial state, and this occurs early before the development of hypertrophy.11, 12, 13

The LTCC is a heterotrimeric structure that consists of the main pore-forming α1C subunit, auxiliary β2, and α2δ subunits.14 The β2 subunit is an intracellular protein that is bound to the I-II linker of the α1C subunit at a region called the α-interaction domain (AID).15 The auxiliary β2 subunit undergoes conformational movement during activation and inactivation of the LTCC.14, 15, 16 The β2 subunit associates with neuroblast differentiation-associated protein AHNAK, also known as desmoyokin, a large subsarcolemmal protein, that is also anchored to F-actin.10 Cytoskeletal proteins, including F-actin, regulate mitochondrial energetics and function by binding to outer mitochondrial docking proteins.17, 18, 19

Depolarization of the plasma membrane in cardiac myocytes initiates calcium entry through the LTCC resulting in contraction. The LTCC regulates mitochondrial function via calcium-dependent and calcium-independent mechanisms.20,21 We have previously shown that LTCC activation as a result of voltage-clamp of the plasma membrane or with application of the LTCC agonist BayK(−), increases both intracellular and mitochondrial calcium uptake, NADH production, superoxide production, and metabolic activity in wildtype cardiac myocytes in a calcium-dependent manner.11,20 However, we have also demonstrated that mitochondrial activity can be altered independently of calcium.11 The response is attenuated following the depolymerization of F-actin or β-tubulin, indicating that the response is associated with a structural-functional interaction between the LTCC and mitochondria that is mediated by mechanotransduction through the sarcomere protein network involving the auxiliary β2 subunit, AHNAK, and F-actin.11,20

To date, therapeutics for the treatment of HCM largely focus on the management of symptoms and the prevention of heart failure and arrhythmias.22,23 The cardiac myosin small molecule inhibitor mavacamten has demonstrated effective improvement in symptoms associated with severe outflow tract obstruction and has achieved U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval in the United States and approval for clinical use in Europe and Australia.24, 25, 26 However, the use of mavacamten requires regular monitoring to ensure optimal dosing because of potential risk of systolic dysfunction, and it is currently only approved for administration in severe disease. Despite these advances, there is no treatment that prevents the hypertrophy. Identifying therapeutic strategies to prevent the development of HCM is a significant clinical need.

The alpha-interaction domain/transactivator of transcription (AID-TAT) peptide has previously shown therapeutic benefits in animal models of ischemia,27 and our proof of concept data demonstrate that it may be beneficial in HCM.12 The AID-TAT peptide binds to the β subunit and interferes with the binding of the auxiliary β2 subunit to the α1C subunit causing immobilization of the β2 subunit.20,27,28 This immobilization induces functional uncoupling from the actin cytoskeleton28 and decreases metabolic activity.11

Taking into consideration the potential therapeutic benefits of the AID peptide, we designed novel variants to facilitate drug development. Following clarification of the structure of the AID–CaVβ2a complex,29, 30, 31 we used in silico modelling to predict the binding of peptide variants to the CaVβ2a to prevent its interaction with the AID. We manipulated the length and sequence of the AID peptide with the aim of identifying variants that were effective in the treatment of HCM. We identified 3 variants that bound with a higher affinity than the AID peptide and demonstrate that the 3 variant peptides are effective at decreasing mitochondrial function in myocytes in vitro. One additional variant demonstrated effective binding when we had predicted poorer binding, so we further examined the efficacy of that variant also. We treated the mice for 5 weeks in vivo before the development of hypertrophy to assess the therapeutic potential of these peptides at preventing HCM. Here, we describe a novel preventative treatment for HCM that is effective by uncoupling the β2 subunit of the LTCC from the actin cytoskeleton and decreasing mitochondrial metabolic activity.

Measurement of L-type Ca2+ channel current

The whole-cell configuration of the patch-clamp technique was used to measure changes in L-type Ca2+ channel in intact cardiac myocytes as described previously.11

Binding affinity assay

Native calcium channel β2 subunits were isolated from adult 8-week-old Wistar rats for use in a competitive binding assay (modified from De Waard et al32) involving AID-affinity beads and Cavβ2 subunit.

AID variant peptides were serially diluted (0, 10 nmol/L, 100 nmol/L, 1 μmol/L, 10 μmol/L, 100 μmol/L) and incubated with 100 μg of Cavβ2 subunit purified from cardiac homogenate. A dose-response curve for each variant AID peptide consisted of 6 reactions. For each reaction, 0.02 nmol/L AID-TAT was incubated in biotinylation solution (0.1 mol/L MES buffer containing 1 nmol/L amine-PEG11-biotin and 0.2 nmol/L EDC) for 2 hours at room temperature with agitation (400 rpm). Affinity beads (Dynabeads M-280 Streptavidin, ThermoFisher Scientific) were then incubated with biotinylated AID peptide for 30 minutes at room temperature at 400 rpm. Afterwards, biotinylated AID-coated affinity beads were washed, initially in 0.1% bovine serum albumin in phosphate-buffered saline and then 3 times in phosphate-buffered saline for 3 minutes.

Cavβ2-AID variant peptide dilutions were incubated with biotinylated AID-coated affinity beads for 2 hours at room temperature with gentle agitation at 400 rpm. Samples were placed on a magnetic stand to allow affinity beads to migrate to the magnet. Supernatant was removed and treated with 1× sample buffer (62.5 mmol/L Tris-HCl, pH 6.8, 2% SDS, 10% glycerol, 100 mmol/L DTT, 0.002% bromophenol blue) for 5 minutes at 95 °C. The resulting protein from the eluted supernatant was analyzed by immunoblot and densitometry quantitated using ImageJ software. A concentration at which 50% of the β2 subunit bound each variant peptide (K0.5) was determined using a nonlinear regression from densitometric evaluation of the immunoblots and averaged from 4 repeats.

Structural modelling of binding

The β2α-AID crystal structure (PDB: 1T0J) was used to analyze the effects of variant residues on protein–protein interactions. Residues were mutated using the mutagenesis tool in PyMOL and side-chain positions determined for favorable rotamer and steric constraints (Schrödinger, 2015). The PISA tool within the CCP4i2 suite was used to measure the buried surface area, side-chain interactions, and bond distances of β2a-AID variants.33 Figures were produced in PyMOL.

Mouse models

The 20- to 25-week-old male C57BL/6 heterozygous mice expressing the human cTnI gene encoding the human disease-causing cTnI-G203S were generated and used for in vitro and in vivo studies. The mice develop hallmark features of HCM between 23 and 25 weeks.11,12,34 Age-matched male mice expressing the normal human troponin I gene were used as controls and indicated as wild-type (wt). The 25- to 30-week-old male C57BL/6 heterozygous mice expressing the human disease-causing mutation MYH6 R403Q (αMHC403/+), a gift from C and J Seidman (Department of Genetics, Harvard Medical School), were also used. Negatively genotyped male age-matched littermates were used as wt controls. For in vivo studies, 20-week-old cTnI-G203S mice or 25-week-old αMHC403/+ mice were treated with 5- to 10-μmol/L AID(S)-TAT or 5-μmol/L AID-TAT variant peptides where indicated via intraperitoneal injection, 3 times per week before the development of hypertrophy for a total duration of 5 weeks. Male mice were utilized because they reproducibly develop hypertrophy, whereas the phenotype is not as severe in the female mice. The treatment of the mice was randomized, and the researchers participating in acquisition and analysis of the data were blinded to the treatment groups.

In vivo toxicity parameters

Body weight, kidney, and liver enzymes were measured to assess in vivo toxicity of the variant peptides after the 5-week treatment regime. Urea, creatinine, alanine transaminase, and aspartate transaminase concentrations were assessed in terminal plasma using the Quantichrom assay kits (BioAssay Systems).

Measurement of blood pressure

Blood pressure was assessed with a CODA tail cuff before commencing treatment, 1 hour after initial injection (acute), and following completion of the 5-week treatment regime.

Echocardiography and Doppler studies

M-mode echocardiographic studies were performed under light methoxyflurane anesthesia as previously described.11,12,35 Doppler ultrasound was performed to measure left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) and ratio of early to late ventricular filling velocities (MV E/A ratios) on mice under light methoxyflurane anesthesia with the use of an i13L probe in the long axis on a Vivid 7 IQ ultrasound system (GE Healthcare). Each n represents the average of 7 quantitative measurements (from each mouse) from wt, cTnI-G203S, and αMHC403403/+ mice for each treatment group.

Isolation of ventricular myocytes

Animals were anesthetized by inhalation of methoxyflurane followed by injection of pentobarbitone sodium (240 mg kg−1, intraperitoneally) before excision of the heart and cells were isolated following retrograde perfusion of the heart via the aorta with collagenase B based on methods previously described.13,36,37

Fluorescent assessment of mitochondrial function

Measurement of mitochondrial membrane potential

The fluorescent indicator 5,5′, 6,6′-tetrachloro1,1′, 3,3′-tetraethylbenzimidazolylcarbocyanine iodide (JC-1) was used to measure mitochondrial membrane potential (ψm) as previously described.11,12

Measurement of mitochondrial flavoprotein oxidation

Flavoprotein autofluorescence was used to measure flavoprotein oxidation based on previously described methods.38

Histological assessment of cardiac fibrosis

Following completion of treatment regimes, mouse hearts were extracted and prepared for Masson’s Trichrome staining based on previously described methods.39

Statistical analysis

Results are reported as the mean ± SEM. Data were tested for normality with the Shapiro-Wilk test, while equality of variances was assessed using the Brown-Forsythe test. Comparisons were made using Welch’s analysis of variance for normally distributed data, with Dunnett’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons used to compare groups to a control. Otherwise, the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s post hoc test was carried out for comparisons between groups. Statistical significance was accepted at P < 0.05. All statistical testing was conducted using Prism version 9.10 (GraphPad Software).

Results

In vitro binding studies demonstrate increased binding affinity of variants to the LTCC β2 subunit

The α1c AID peptide is an 18 amino acid peptide with composition of QQLEEDLKGYLDWITQAE contained within the I-II linker of the channel (Figure 1A). We have previously demonstrated that application of 1 to 10 μmol/L AID-TAT to cTnI-G203S or wt cardiomyocytes 20 minutes before recording currents significantly slows the inactivation rate of the current but does not alter calcium influx (Figures 1B and 1C) or calcium transients.12,27 In the heart, the β2b subunit that typically speeds inactivation rate predominates.40 We designed variants by truncating the N or C terminal ends or inducing point mutations by substituting 1 or more amino acids. The affinity of each of the variant peptides to the β2 subunit was investigated with a competition binding assay. The binding of 11 AID peptide variants (Table 1) was compared with the original AID peptide. Truncating the first 6 amino acids (AIDP6) or substitution of amino acids Y437, W440, and I441 (AIDP13) abolished binding consistent with previous findings.41 In addition, as expected and as a negative control, the L-type Ca2+ channel antagonist diltiazem (that is known to block the pore of Cav1.2) did not bind the β2 subunit (Table 2). We measured a significantly increased displacement of the β2 subunit for AIDP7 and AIDP14 compared with AID (Table 2, Figure 1D), confirming stronger binding affinities for these variants to the AID region of the channel. Although AIDP15 appeared to bind more strongly, we measured a statistically similar displacement of the β2 subunit for AIDP15 and AIDP16 compared with AID (Table 2, Figure 1D). The binding affinity of AIDP16 was surprising given the variant included an Arg–Arg interaction between AIDP16 and the interaction domain that we expected would result in poorer binding.

In silico modelling of variants

We cross-referenced the various mutations with available crystal structures of CaVβ subunits in complex with the AID peptide29,30,41 and present the structure of CaVβ2a complex (PDB ID 1T0J) (Figure 1E). As expected and consistent with the measured binding affinity of AIDP6,30 the main contributors to the interaction are Y437, W440, and I441 toward the C-terminal end, and any substitutions here would be unlikely to improve affinity. However, closer to the N-terminal end of the AID peptide, L430 is a hydrophobic residue that is close to polar residues in the Guanylate Kinase domain of CaVβ2a, including S345 and K347. Substitution with a hydrophilic residue may thus allow for the formation of additional hydrogen bonds or even a salt bridge with K347. Indeed, upon introducing the mutation L430E in silico, a salt bridge is possible between E430 and K347, but also between E430 and R351. Similarly, the L430Q mutation allows for a possible H-bond with the same 2 residues. These results are thus in agreement with the increased affinities of AID variants containing these substitutions. The L430R substitution only provided, at most, a very modest increase in affinity, which may be due, in part, by electrostatic repulsion if this side chain is pointing toward K347 and R351.

We also considered the L434E mutation, because this substitution was found in a double mutant (L430E, L434E). Although the affinity was higher than for wt, it was less compared with the single L430E substitution. Leu434 is located next to Val348 and Leu352 in the CaVβ2a subunit, with other hydrophilic residues further away. Introducing L434E in silico, a salt bridge is possible with Arg351, but this comes at the expense of the salt bridge between Glu430 and Arg351. Thus, the L434E substitution is less likely to provide further stabilization in the L430E background.

In vitro treatment of wt myocytes with AID-TAT variants does not negatively impact calcium conductance through the LTCC

Therapeutics that target the LTCC can negatively impact calcium conductance through the channel. Because our peptides target the LTCC, we examined the effect of the AID variants on LTCC kinetics in vitro. A 20-minute preincubation of wt cardiomyocytes with 1-μmol/L inactive scrambled peptide (AID(S)-TAT), AIDP7-TAT, AIDP14-TAT, AIDP15-TAT, or AIDP16-TAT did not significantly alter the current density amplitude compared with the “no peptide” control (Figure 2A). Furthermore, there were no changes in the current-voltage relationship following preincubation with the AID variant peptides (Figure 2B) indicating that these peptides do not negatively impact calcium conductance through the channel. Myocytes preincubated with AIDP7-TAT, AIDP14-TAT, AIDP15-TAT, or AIDP16-TAT have significantly increased calcium current Tau 2 values indicating slowed inactivation of the channel compared with AID(S)-TAT incubated myocytes (Figures 2C and 2D). There was no significant difference in Tau 1 or 2 values between “no peptide” control and AID(S)-TAT preincubated myocytes.

AID-TAT variants are more efficacious in restoring ψm and flavoprotein oxidation than AID-TAT in vitro

We have previously shown that cardiac myocytes isolated from mice carrying the cTnI-G203S mutation exhibit significantly increased mitochondrial metabolic activity measured as an increase in flavoprotein oxidation and ψm in response to activation of ICa-L, consistent with the human disease.11,42 We have also demonstrated that in vitro exposure of 10 week-old cTnI-G203S cardiac myocytes to 1 and 10 μmol/L AID-TAT peptide attenuates this response in a dose-dependent manner.12,13,20,27

We pre-exposed wt myocytes to 0.1, 0.5, or 1 μmol/L of each of the peptide variants AIDP7-TAT, AIDP14-TAT, AIDP15-TAT, or AIDP16-TAT for 20 minutes and assessed changes in flavoprotein oxidation and ψm following activation of LTCC with 10 μmol/L BayK(−). Mitochondrial flavoprotein oxidation is an indirect measure of oxygen consumption, therefore, increased autofluorescence of mitochondrial flavoprotein is indicative of increased oxygen consumption and mitochondrial dysfunction.43,44 Consistent with previous findings, in wt myocytes, application of BayK(−) in the presence of 1 μmol/L AID(S)-TAT induced an increase in flavoprotein oxidation that was comparable to the response elicited with application of BayK(−) alone (“no peptide”) (Figure 3).11 At a concentration of 0.1 μmol/L, none of the AID-TAT peptide variants were effective at decreasing the flavoprotein response (Figure 3A, Supplemental Figure 1A). At a concentration of 0.5 μmol/L, the original AID-TAT caused a small but insignificant decrease in the response to BayK(−), whereas AIDP14-TAT and AIDP7-TAT were effective (P < 0.001) (Figure 3B, Supplemental Figure 1B). At 1 μmol/L, peptide variants AIDP7-TAT, AIDP14-TAT, and AIDP15-TAT were effective at attenuating the response in wt myocytes; however, AIDP16-TAT did not attenuate the increase in flavoprotein oxidation (Figure 3C, Supplemental Figure 1C). In cTnI-G203S myocytes, although 0.5 μmol/L AID-TAT caused a small decrease in the response, AIDP14-TAT and AIDP7-TAT significantly attenuated the increase in flavoprotein oxidation following BayK(−) exposure (Figure 3D, Supplemental Figure 1D).

Based on the results obtained from in vitro assessment of flavoprotein oxidation, we further explored the effect of 0.5-μmol/L AID-TAT variants on changes in ψm. We assessed changes in JC-1 fluorescence under calcium-free conditions as previously described.11,12 Consistent with previous studies, application of BayK(−) induced an increase in ψm in the presence of 1 μmol/L AID(S)-TAT, that was comparable to BayK(−) alone, in both wt (Figure 3E) and cTnI-G203S myocytes (Figure 3F). We find in wt myocytes, exposure to 0.5 μmol/L AID-TAT caused a small decrease in the response to BayK(−) that was not significant; however, 0.5 μmol/L, AIDP14-TAT, AIDP7-TAT, and AIDP15-TAT significantly attenuated the response in wt and cTnI-G203S myocytes (Figures 3E and 3F, Supplemental Figures 1E and 1F, Table 3). These data indicate that at the cellular level, AIDP14-TAT, AIDP7-TAT, and AIDP15-TAT are more effective than AIDP16-TAT at normalizing mitochondrial metabolic activity in cTnI-G203S myocytes. With this in mind, we proceeded to examine the effect of treatment of mice with the variant peptides in vivo.

In vivo treatment of cTnI-G203S mice with AID-TAT variants does not alter blood pressure

It is well documented that cTnI-G203S mice develop characteristic features of hypertrophy from 23 to 25 weeks of age.11,12 To investigate the effect of AID-TAT variants on the prevention of HCM, we commenced treatment at 20 weeks of age. Mice were treated with 5 μmol/L peptide (1 mg/kg), 3 times per week for 5 weeks via intraperitoneal injection. We chose this treatment regime because the peptides bind irreversibly to the Cav1.2 channel and total cellular calcium channel pool may last as long as 24 to 48 hours.45 We began by examining the effect of AID peptide variants on blood pressure. Systolic and diastolic blood pressures were recorded at 3 time points, 1 hour before the first dose, 1 hour after the first dose, and at the end of the 5-week treatment regime. The systolic blood pressure but not diastolic blood pressure was slightly higher in wt mice compared with the cTnI-G203S mice treated with AID(S)-TAT peptide (Figure 4A). However no significant changes in systolic or diastolic blood pressure were observed in mutant mice treated with any of the AID-TAT variants compared with AID(S)-TAT treatment over the 5 weeks (Figure 4A, Supplemental Figure 2A).

AID-TAT variants demonstrate good in vivo safety profile

Following completion of the 5-week in vivo treatment regime, plasma was collected and assessed for kidney (Figure 4B, Supplemental Figure 2B) and liver enzymes (Figure 4C, Supplemental Figure 2C). There were no significant alterations in urea or creatine concentrations following treatment with AID variant peptides compared with AID(S)-TAT in mutant mice, nor differences between wt and mutant mice treated with AID(S)-TAT. Likewise, alanine transaminase and aspartate transaminase were not altered following AID-TAT variant treatment and concentrations remained comparable to those measured in wt and mutant mice treated with AID(S)-TAT. In addition the mice continued to thrive over the 5 weeks and did not experience weight loss (Supplemental Figure 3A).

Overall, these data indicate that repeated in vivo treatment (3 times per week for 5 weeks) of cTnI-G203S mice with AID-TAT variants was well tolerated with the analyzed dose.

In vivo treatment of precardiomyopathic cTnI-G203S mice with AID peptide variants prevents the development of HCM

Consistent with previous studies,12 we find that 20-week-old cTnI-G203S mice treated with 5 μmol/L AID(S)-TAT until 25 weeks of age (3 times per week for 5 weeks), develop a significant increase in fractional shortening, ejection fraction, increased interventricular septum (IVS) dimensions in systole and diastole, and increased left ventricular posterior wall thickness in systole (LVPWs) and left ventricular posterior wall thickness in diastole (LVPWd) compared with age-matched wt mice (Figures 5B to 5G, Supplemental Figure 4). The mice also demonstrated decreased left ventricular end diameter in systole (LVEDs) and left ventricular end diameter in diastole (LVEDd) compared with wt age-matched mice treated with AID(S)-TAT (Figures 5H and 5I, Supplemental Figures 4H and 4I).

We examined the efficacy of treating 20-week-old cTnI-G203S mice with 5 μmol/L AID-TAT variants for 5 weeks. Treatment of cTnI-G203S mice with each of the AID-TAT variant peptides restored fractional shortening, ejection fraction, IVSs, IVSd, LVPWs, LVPWd and LVEDs toward wt levels compared with AID(S)-TAT (Figures 5B to 5G and 5I). In vivo treatment of precardiomyopathic cTnI-G203S mice with AID-TAT variants improves mitochondrial function in myocytes

We examined the effect of treatment with the AID variants on flavoprotein oxidation and ψm in myocytes following treatment of pre-cardiomyopathic cTnI-G203S mice for 5 weeks. As expected and consistent with the hypertrophic phenotype, activation of LTCC with application of BayK(−) resulted in a further increase in flavoprotein oxidation (Figure 6A) and ψm (Figure 6B) in myocytes from cTnI-G203S mice treated with scrambled peptide compared with wt mice. As previously described application of the enantiomer, BayK(+), that does not activate the LTCC, does not alter flavoprotein oxidation or ψm in myocytes from wt mice.11 Additionally, we have previously reported that application of the LTCC blocker nisoldipine prevents the increase in flavoprotein oxidation or ψm in myocytes from wt or cTnI-G203S mice induced by the agonist BayK(−).11 In vivo treatment with any 1 of the 4 variant peptides resulted in a significant decrease in flavoprotein oxidation (Figure 6A, Supplemental Figure 6A) and ψm (Figure 6B, Supplemental Figure 6B) confirming that the beneficial effects of the peptide variants could be measured at the cellular level.

In vivo treatment of precardiomyopathic cTnI-G203S mice with AID-TAT variants slows the progression of fibrosis

It is well-established that myocardial fibrosis is a characteristic feature of HCM.46, 47, 48 Increased collagen accumulation and fibrosis is maladaptive and is associated with impaired cardiac relaxation, thereby increasing the risk of heart failure and arrhythmias.48

Here we assessed the efficacy of treating 20-week-old pre-hypertrophic cTnI-G203S mice with 5 μmol/L AID-TAT variant peptides 3 times per week on the development and progression of fibrosis as visualized by Masson’s trichrome stain. Consistent with the development of HCM, we found that left ventricular posterior wall collagen volume fraction was significantly increased in cTnI-G203S mice treated with 10 μmol/L AID(S)-TAT versus wt hearts (Figure 7B). This was significantly decreased in cTnI-G203S mice treated with 5 μmol/L AIDP7-TAT but not AIDP14-TAT or AIDP15-TAT (Figure 7B, Supplemental Figure 7B). Fibrosis deposition in AIDP16-TAT treated cTnI-G203S mice was significantly reduced (P = 0.012), however, significance was lost following Dunn’s corrections for multiple comparisons (adjusted P = 0.059). Similar results were observed in sections from the interventricular septum (Figure 7C, Supplemental Figure 7C). These data suggest that decreasing mitochondrial metabolic activity early may be effective at preventing the progression of fibrosis in the HCM heart.

In vivo treatment of pre-cardiomyopathic αMHC403/+ mice with AID peptide variants prevents the development of hypertrophy

HCM is caused predominantly by sequence variants in sarcomere genes most abundantly in MYH7, MYBPC3, TNNT2, and TNNI3. These sequence variants affect contractile proteins, or proteins that regulate the contractile apparatus. Relevant to this study, the hypertrophic phenotype exhibits a hypermetabolic mitochondrial state in many HCM causing sequence variants. We examined whether the AID variants were also effective in prevention of hypertrophy in the MYH7 gene equivalent mutant murine model, αMHC403/+ mice that develop hypertrophy at 30 weeks. Mice were treated with 5 μmol/L peptide, 3 times per week for 5 weeks via intraperitoneal injection from 25 weeks of age.

No differences in body weight between the groups were recorded during treatment (Supplemental Figure 3B). Heart weight to body weight ratio was improved following treatment with AIDP7-TAT, AIDP15-TAT, and AIDP16-TAT but not AIDP14-TAT peptide variants (Supplemental Figure 5B). The systolic and diastolic blood pressures were not different between wt and αMHC403/+ mice and did not change for the duration of the treatment (Supplemental Figure 8A). There were no signs of toxicity following treatment (Supplemental Figure 8). Isolated cardiac myocytes from αMHC403/+ mice treated for 5 weeks with each of the 4 variant peptides demonstrated significant improvement in flavoprotein oxidation (Supplemental Figure 9A) and ψm measured as changes in JC-1 signal (Supplemental Figure 9B). The development of fibrosis was significantly less following treatment with AIDP14-TAT, AIDP15-TAT, and AIDP16-TAT peptides (Supplemental Figure 10). Finally, we found that all cardiac functional parameters were significantly improved following treatment with each of the peptide variants apart from AIDP16-TAT for the LVEDd parameter (Figure 8, Supplemental Figures 11 and 12).

Discussion

Treatment of HCM primarily involves the management of symptoms and the prevention of arrhythmias and heart failure. The use of implantable cardioverters and antiarrhythmic drugs have significantly decreased mortality but a preventative therapy has yet to be identified.

A small molecule inhibitor of myosin ATPase, mavacamten (MYK-461) was recently approved for clinical use in the United States, Europe, and Australia. Mavacamten improves symptoms in left ventricular outflow tract obstruction in patients with severe hypertrophy. However, in vitro studies have demonstrated divergent efficacy of mavacamten. For example, in inducible pluripotent stem cells from patients carrying the TNNT or TNNI mutations, the contractile dysfunction could not be fully rescued following application of mavacamten.49 Currently, there remains an unmet need for therapy that prevents HCM in patients expressing mutations in the proteins that regulate the contractile apparatus in addition to patients with sarcomere gene sequence variants.

We had previously demonstrated promising preliminary results preventing hypertrophy in cTnI-G203S mice following treatment with the AID peptide.12 Here, we designed novel peptides with the aim of identifying variants with a higher binding affinity for the β subunit to further validate the efficacy of targeting the AID region to facilitate drug development for HCM. Using the 18 amino acid AID peptide as a template, we created variants by truncating the C- and N-terminal residues and inducing point mutations. This resulted in 4 variants that bound either with a higher affinity to the β subunit or with similar affinity. In silico modelling confirmed the higher affinity included the participation of an additional hydrogen bond or increased ionic attraction (Figure 1). The binding affinity of the AIDP16 variant was only marginally improved relative to the AID peptide, likely caused by an offset between its ability to H-bond with neighboring residues, and electrostatic repulsion with positively charged residues in the CaVβ2 subunit. The binding of the AID variant peptides to the β-subunit is also demonstrated in vitro with prolongation of Tau2 of the calcium current measured in patch clamp experiments (Figure 2, Table 3). We tested the efficacy of the variant peptides initially by incubating wt myocytes with the peptides. We found that preincubation of the myocytes with the peptides in vitro could improve mitochondrial function in a concentration-dependent manner assessed as a decrease in flavoprotein oxidation and ψm following activation of LTCC with BayK(−) (Figure 3, Table 3).

The AIDP16 variant peptide did not alter flavoprotein oxidation or ψm following acute exposure in wt myocytes. The variant was also not effective following acute exposure in cTnI-G203S mutant myocytes. This suggests that the P16 variant is less effective at breaking the interaction between the α1C and β2b subunits when exposed acutely compared with the other peptides. However, longer in vivo treatment 3 times per week via intraperitoneal injection was effective at improving contractility and preventing the development of hypertrophy and fibrosis when treated over 5 weeks.

The cTnI-G203S mouse develops hypertrophy at 23 to 25 weeks of age. We commenced treatment at 20 weeks of age and examined the effect of the 4 variant peptides on contractile function after 5 weeks of treatment. Treatment with the peptides was safe and non-toxic as kidney and liver function remained normal (Figures 4B and 4C, Supplemental Figure 2) at the end of treatment and there was no loss of body weight over the 5-week treatment period (Supplemental Figure 3A). A conserved region in the I-II loop means that the AID peptide can also bind to β subunits in vascular smooth muscle. In addition the cardiac CaV1.2a and vascular smooth muscle CaV1.2b exhibit 93% homology in amino acid sequence.50 Despite the similarity there was no effect of the peptides on systolic or diastolic blood pressure in the mice at any time during and after treatment (Figure 4A, Supplemental Figure 2A). As hypertrophy progresses, heart weight increases. We assessed the progression of hypertrophy by normalizing heart weight to body weight. Heart weight (Supplemental Figure 5A) and cardiac fibrosis (Figure 7, Supplemental Figure 7) decreased following treatment with the variant peptides (except AIDP14-TAT) and contractile function improved following treatment with all 4 variant peptides (Figure 5, Supplemental Figure 4). Flavoprotein oxidation and ψm significantly improved in myocytes isolated from mice treated with all 4 variant peptides (Figure 6, Supplemental Figure 6).

We also found that treatment with the peptides was effective at preventing the progression of hypertrophy in mice expressing the MYH6 mutation (Supplemental Figure 5B). Similar to the effects in cTnI-G203S mutant mice, treatment had no effect on blood pressure (Supplemental Figure 8A) did not induce kidney or liver toxicity (Supplemental Figures 8B and 8C) and improved contractility in the mice (Figure 8, Supplemental Figures 11 and 12). Of the 4 variant peptides, 3 significantly prevented the progression of fibrosis with the exception of AIDP7-TAT (Supplemental Figure 10). Because AIDP7-TAT significantly improved contractile function on echocardiography and mitochondrial function ex vivo, we propose that extending the treatment period may result in a further reduction in fibrosis. Previous studies have shown that HCM hearts exhibit reduced myocardial phosphocreatine/ATP ratio consistent with high energy utilization and compromised mitochondrial function.42 It has been argued that the mitochondrial dysfunction drives the pathology because young hearts that are not yet hypertrophic exhibit the high energy state. Although we did not directly measure myocardial ATP, flavoprotein oxidation is an indirect measure of oxygen consumption and therefore energy utilization in the intact cell.38 Consistent with the high-energy utilization driving the pathology, we have measured a significant increase in flavoprotein oxidation in myocytes from young prehypertrophic cTnI-G203S mutant mice11 and αMHC403/+ mice.35 Here, we show that treatment of prehypertrophic mice with the AID variants decreased mitochondrial energetics leading to the prevention of the development of hypertrophy. We propose this occurs through the immobilization of the β subunit by the AID variant peptides which functionally uncouples the LTCC from F-actin. Therefore, “tuning down” mitochondrial energetics represents a mechanism to decrease the development of hypertrophy, improve contractility, and prevent the progression of fibrosis.

Calcium channel antagonists such as diltiazem are used in the treatment of HCM and have demonstrated some benefit, but negative inotropic effects occur because of direct block of calcium influx through the channel pore. We have demonstrated that the AID variant peptides do not negatively affect calcium conductance through the LTCC or alter blood pressure, but result in improved contractile function (ie, no negative inotropy). Targeting the LTCC using peptides is an attractive therapeutic approach but this usually requires an injectable route of administration. We have previously demonstrated effective and safe delivery of the AID peptide into myocardium using nanoparticles as carriers following intraperitoneal injection.51,52 Alternative approaches for delivery could include inhalation of LTCC modulators in calcium phosphate nanoparticles via the pulmonary vasculature to the heart that is proving effective in the treatment of heart failure in murine and pig models.53

A limitation of the study is that we have not performed pharmacokinetic studies for the peptide variants. Our rationale for injecting the mice 3 times per week was based on the premise that the calcium channel has a turnover rate ∼24 to 48 hours.46 How long the peptide remains available before it is excreted requires further investigation. However, our data clearly demonstrate effective prevention of HCM following injection every 2 to 3 days. We also did not investigate whether the peptide must be administered through-out the life of a mouse to prevent HCM. Regarding translatability of these findings from mouse to human, the AID region is highly conserved between species suggesting that the beneficial effects of the peptides may be reproduced in humans. A greater understanding of the pharmacokinetics of these variant peptides will assist with the translatability of these peptides to humans.

Conclusions

Here, we report 4 variant peptides as first in class safe modulators of LTCC function that are effective at preventing HCM in 2 murine models of HCM without causing negative inotropic effects or toxicity in the mice. The evidence provides further validation and optimization of a first in class therapy that uniquely targets the L-type Ca2+ channel. We propose that the variant peptides will be effective for treatment of HCM broadly because the mechanism of action involves the modification of mitochondrial function and impaired energy metabolism that is a common characteristic and driver of the pathology in HCM. A summary of the efficacy of the 4 variant peptides for each functional endpoint studied in both mouse strains is shown in Table 4.

COMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: HCM occurs because of mutations affecting either sarcomere proteins or sarcomere regulatory proteins. HCM presents with left ventricular wall and interventricular septum hypertrophy accompanied with diastolic dysfunction and in some cases left ventricular outflow tract obstruction. The incomplete penetrance in the HCM cohort makes it difficult to identify patients carrying HCM causing mutations that will progress to develop left ventricular hypertrophy. Current therapeutics are prescribed to patients after the development of hypertrophy is confirmed with echocardiography imaging. Pharmacological treatment predominantly involves relieving symptoms with administration of L-type calcium-channel blockers, diuretic agents, and beta-blockers. Recently mavacamten has been approved for clinical use. However, mavacamten is limited to patients with severe obstructive HCM and is currently not used in a preventative setting. There is an unmet clinical need for therapies that target the HCM pathophysiology to prevent the development of the hypertrophic phenotype in patients.

TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: Based on our findings, the restoration of mitochondrial energetics to wildtype levels can prevent the development of HCM in murine models with mutations affecting the sarcomere as well as sarcomere regulatory proteins. The increased uptake of genetic testing should facilitate identification of patients who are vulnerable to the development of HCM and suitable for preventative therapy. Identifying alternative administration routes other than intraperitoneal injection will be attractive for long term adherence to treatment. Although the AID region is highly conserved across species, confirming the translatability of these findings from mouse to human will be important.

Land use dynamics and ecosystem service valuation in the Sanmenxia Reservoir wetland of the Yellow River
Authors: Ying Liu, Yatao Jiang, Chen Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The Sanmenxia Reservoir wetland (SRW) serves as a critical ecological buffer in the middle reaches of the Yellow River. Ongoing population growth and changes in land use have placed significant pressure on the wetland’s ecosystems. However, existing research has yet to establish a spatiotemporal analysis method to assess the impact of land use change on the ecosystem service value (ESV). This gap hinders the precise regulation and sustainable development of land resources. To gain a comprehensive understanding of the land use dynamics and ecological functions of the SRW, this study introduced a refined ESV evaluation method. This method revised the equivalent factors from spatial, temporal, and hydrological perspectives, with an emphasis on the impact of sediment discharge. Furthermore, the Patch-level Land Use Simulation (PLUS) model was employed to project the land use structure in 2030, and the corresponding ESV was analyzed. The study revealed the following: (1) Cropland constituted the primary land type in the SRW. Over the last two decades, the build-up area exhibited the most significant single land use dynamics, with the transformation of bare land to cropland spanning the widest range and largest area. (2) From 2000 to 2020, the ESV increased by 0.64 billion CNY, with the water body contributing almost all the increase. (3) The projected ESV for 2030 is 2.94 billion CNY, indicating an increase in ecological functions in the near future. Policy makers should recognizethe impact of land use change on ESV and implement measures to rebalance land use structure. While ensuring the preservation of cropland and the protection of lives and property, it is advisable to consider expanding the water body area to facilitate comprehensive sustainable development within SRW.

Introduction

Located at the middle reach of the Yellow River, Sanmenxia Reservoir wetland (SRW) is distinctive since it is formed by the Reservoir’s impoundment. The wetland plays an important ecological service function in conserving water resources, regulating and storing flood water, purifying water quality, regulating climate, and protecting biodiversity1. However, continuous population growth and land use changes around the wetland have placed considerable pressure on ecosystems. Meanwhile, the dynamic water and sediment regime of the lower Yellow River in recent years has changed the spatial pattern of SRW, leading to a growing inability of the available ecological service function to sustain the pressure.

To clarify the trend of ecological service function change in SRW in recent years, it is necessary to conduct a quantitative evaluation of the wetland’s Ecosystem Service Value (ESV)2. In a monetized form, ESV reflects the extent to which the life-sustaining products and services offered by the ecosystem fulfill human needs3.

At present, there are two predominant methods for ecosystem services valuation: the primary data-based method and the unit value-based method4. The primary data-based method estimates ESV in two steps. First, quantifying ecosystem processes and functions according to a series of ecological models, such as water resource conservation models and equations for gas regulation derived from photosynthesis5–7. Second, applying economic valuation techniques like the market price method, carbon tax law, replacement cost method, and travel cost method to assess the ESV8–12. However, this method requires a significant number of input parameters and contains complex calculating processes, thereby posing challenges in standardizing the evaluation method and establishing uniform parameters for ecosystem service valuation.

The unit value based method estimates ESV according to economic value per unit area of the ecosystem. Costanza et al.13 developed a global ecosystem service equivalent factor table, which served as a cornerstone for subsequent research. Then Xie et al.14 refined Costanza’s approach and created the equivalent scale of ecosystem service value per unit area of the Chinese terrestrial ecosystem, a framework that is widely used by Chinese scholars. The unit value based method is more convenient to conduct a thorough assessment of the spatiotemporal distribution of ESV, encompassing a variety of services from diverse ecosystems15–20.

As research progresses, there is a noticeable shift in perspective from a global focus to a more localized and regional approach. This change allows for a more thorough analysis of finer scales and specific regions, drawing increased attention to revising equivalent factors to obtain more accurate ESVs. Li et al. revised the ecosystem service equivalent factors of Tianfu New Area from both natural geographical and socio-economic perspectives21. Liu et al. utilizing six indicators including economy and soil conservation, revised the ecosystem service equivalent factors of the Loess Plateau22. Hu et al. using indicators such as rainfall and grain yield, revised the equivalent factors of various ecosystem services in the southern part of Guangzhou23. Zhang et al. calibrated the ecosystem service equivalent factors of wetlands in the Greater Khingan Mountains employing the net primary productivity of vegetation24.

The realization of ecosystem service function is influenced by a range of ecological mechanisms and presents spatiotemporal dynamic changes closely related to environmental structure. However, the equivalent factor method, commonly used in previous studies, is a static assessment method, not concerning the spatial and temporal variations in the ecosystem’s type and quality25. Consequently, the evaluation outcomes may fail to represent the dynamic changes in ecological service functions. This limitation constrains the application of ESV in environmental management and related fields. Furthermore, previous studies mainly focused on specific administrative regions or stream catchments, leaving a gap in understanding EVS dynamics in heavily sediment-loaded riverine wetlands.

Therefore, the present research selected SRW, a region that is both heavily sediment-loaded and densely populated, as the study area, and revised the equivalent factors in spatial, temporal, and hydrological perspectives. The modified valuation model could reflect the SRW’s situation more accurately. In addition, the patch-level land use simulation (PLUS) model was employed to simulate the study area’s land use in 2030, and the corresponding ESV was predicted. Understanding the mechanisms that drive the evolution of ecological spaces in SRW has the potential to provide valuable practical insights for the environmental preservation and sustainable development of the Yellow River Basin. These findings may serve as a theoretical foundation for further exploration of natural asset assessment and ecological compensation within the study area.

Study area

The SRW is situated at the convergence of Henan, Shanxi, and Shaanxi provinces, with an area of over 184 km2 (Fig. 1). The region belongs to a warm temperate continental monsoon climate26. The average annual precipitation is 614 mm, and about 50% of the total rainfall occurs in summer27. The SRW was designated as one of the key areas in the National Wetland Protection Project Plan, which was initiated in 2004. This area is renowned for its rich biodiversity and serves as a crucial resting, feeding, and wintering ground for migratory birds coming from three routes28. The operational modes of the Sanmenxia Reservoir, combined with the unique water-sediment dynamics in the middle reaches of the Yellow River, resulted in frequent land use changes within the wetland, thus leading to significant alterations in the ecosystem functions. At the same time, in administrative regions adjacent to wetlands, the challenge of managing rapid urbanization while preserving cropland presents a complex land use situation.

Data

The land use data with a resolution of 30 m were obtained from the GlobeLand30 global land cover product29. The regional land use consists of six class I types: cropland, forest, grassland, bare land, water bodies, and build-up area (Fig. 2). The wetland’s boundary was delineated according to the relevant documents issued by the National Forestry and Grassland Administration, whereas the climatic and hydrologic data were mainly obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Resources, and Environment Science and Data Center. Grain yield data were taken from the Statistical Bulletin of National Economic and Social Development, Sanmenxia Statistical Yearbook. Digital Elevation Model (DEM) data, which also has a resolution of 30 m, came from the Geographic Spatial Data Cloud. Railway and road data were accessed through the National Geographic Information Resource Catalog Service System. All of the vector spatial data were projected in the WGS_1984_UTM_zone_49N coordinates. Detailed information is available in Table 1.

Methods

The research comprises three sections (Fig. 3): First, an analysis of each land use type’s area in the three time points (i.e. 2000, 2010, and 2020) was conducted, and the single land use dynamics and the land use transfer matrix were calculated to investigate the land use change pattern. Subsequently, the equivalent factors for ecosystem service valuation were revised from temporal, spatial, and hydrological perspectives, leading to a more accurate model. At last, the PLUS model was utilized to project the land use condition in 2030, and the corresponding ESV was carried out based on the simulation results.

PLUS model

To gain a comprehensive understanding of the land use dynamics of SRW and explore its future land use structure, the PLUS model was employed to predict land use in 2030. This model is based on the well-known cellular automata (CA) model, which is a dynamic model focusing on spatiotemporal interactions and potential rules. The PLUS model incorporates a novel rule-mining strategy and a patch-generating mechanism to effectively capture the nonlinearity in land use changes, resulting in higher accuracy compared to other state-of-the-art models33,34.

The PLUS model consists of two primary modules: a land expansion analysis strategy (LEAS)-based rule-mining module and a multi-type random patch seeds-based CA (CARS) module35. The LEAS module utilizes random forests to assess the impact of various driving factors on the expansion of geographical units and then determines the final development potential of each land use within the space. The CARS module is a specialized cellular automaton (CA) model.

Simulation processes

To simulate the land use condition in 2030, the SRW land use data from 2000 to 2010 were chosen as the input for the PLUS model. The model then analyzed the land expansion pattern and figured out the rules of land use change with multiple driving factors. This study utilized 11 natural and socioeconomic driving factors as auxiliary data for land use simulation, including elevation, slope, aspect, annual average temperature, annual precipitation, annual average evaporation, and distance from stream networks, railways, and primary/secondary roads. The observed and simulated land use data from 2020 were used to validate the model, and the land use in 2030 was predicted as a result.

Simulation parameters

In land use simulation, the neighborhood weight serves as a critical parameter. The value of the neighborhood weight for each land use type ranges from 0 to 1. A value closer to 1 indicates a greater tendency for that specific land use type to uphold its existing status rather than transitioning to other land use types. This study computed the neighborhood weight for each land use type based on the land expansion map. The value was determined as the ratio of the expansion areas of a land use type accounting for the total land expansion: cropland (0.266), water body (0.136), forest (0.031), bare land (0.015), grassland (0.022), and build-up area (0.530).

The transition matrix is another vital parameter of the PLUS model, illustrating the transition patterns among different land use types. While land use type conversion is theoretically unrestricted, in reality, the probability of conversion between different land types is not uniform. Within the PLUS model, the transition matrix parameters are typically denoted by binary numbers, where “0” signifies that conversion between specific land use types is prohibited and “1” signifies permission. In this study, the specific parameters of the land use transition matrix for the PLUS model were established based on an analysis of land use dynamics from 2000 to 2020 (Table 3).

Results

Land use dynamics

Changes in each land use were first analyzed to discover the trend and characteristics. According to Tables 4 and 5, cropland emerged as the predominant land use type, constituting about 60% of the total area in the SRW. Cropland is primarily distributed in the fertile floodplain of the Yellow River within the study area (Fig. 2). Over the study period, the total area of the water body and cropland increased, possibly due to the implementation of cultivated land protection and ecological protection policies. However, the forest and grassland areas experienced some reduction. The build-up area exhibited consistent growth, expanding from 205.74 hm2 in 2000 to 481.68 hm2 in 2020, representing a 134% increase of 275.94 hm2. The data also revealed a doubling of the urban impervious surface area over 20 years. Discussion

Impact of quantity changes in land use on ESV

From 2000 to 2020, notable changes in land use have been observed within the SRW. There was a considerable reduction in bare land, forests, and grasslands, accompanied by an increase in water body, cropland, and build-up areas. The expansion of cropland and build-up area was primarily attributed to the conversion of bare land. The change in build-up area was the most significant, demonstrating a 6.71% in land use dynamics. Projections indicate that by 2030, cropland and water bodies will continue to dominate the landscape. The land use transformations during the study period were possibly driven by local economic development and related policy initiatives. Agricultural intensification and enhanced investments in irrigation technology were key factors contributing to the land use changes36. Urbanization and economic growth facilitated the reclamation and effective utilization of bare land along the river.

Throughout the research period, the ESV in the SRW exhibited an initial decrease followed by a subsequent increase. The decrease in ESV from 2000 to 2010 was attributed to the reduction in the water body area. The water body plays a critical role as the primary contributor to wetland ecosystem services. Its contribution to the total ESV in the study area stood at 83.90%, 71.89%, and 88.71% in 2000, 2010, and 2020, respectively. Therefore, the decrease in the water body area significantly impacted ecosystem services in the study area. On the contrary, the expansion of the build-up area led to a reduction in ESV as such regions were unable to offer any ecosystem services. Some previous studies got similar conclusions. For example, the research conducted by Gu et al.37 analyzed the ESV of the 1-km lakeshore zone of Poyang Lake. The findings revealed that the ESV reached its peak value in 2010, mainly attributed to the observed maximum area of the water body over 40 years. Yun et al.38 also obtained similar results in their investigation of ESV fluctuations in the wetlands of the Yellow River Basin in Inner Mongolia.

Based on contributions to the ESV, ecosystem services in the SRW could be ranked as follows: regulating services, supporting services, provisioning services, and cultural services. Regulating services constituted 86.68% of the total ESV and therefore exerted significant influence on the ecosystem services in the entire study area. This outcome differs from the results of other studies conducted in the SRW. Zhao et al.39 indicated that provisioning services comprised the largest share of the total ESV, followed by regulating services. This discrepancy is primarily due to the present research focusing only on riverine wetlands, where water body significantly impact ecosystem services. In contrast, the previous study by Zhao et al included nearby counties, resulting in more complicated land use dynamics. Moreover, the valuation method used in the previous research directly applied the equivalent factors, not adjusted for the specific characteristics of the SRW.

Policy implications

The rapid development of the urbanization process accelerated land use change, which may lead to some negative socio-ecological impacts40, including rural population loss, cropland decrease, reduction in biodiversity, and ecological degradation41–43. Therefore, under the strategic deployment of Ecological protection and high-quality development in the Yellow River Basin, policymakers must scientifically demarcate urban growth boundaries, establish a sustainable framework for green high-quality development in the SRW, rigorously implement ecologically responsible community performance evaluations and accountability systems, and integrate “green GDP”44 into the assessment criteria used by officials across government departments45.

Limitations and uncertainties

The methods proposed in this study will improve the accuracy of research in this field, and the results may provide a valuable reference for future studies related to the development of the Yellow River Wetland Nature Reserve. However, this method possesses inherent limitations due to data accuracy and the intricate dynamics of the ecosystem. First, the ESV of the SRW is directly determined by the land use areas and the equivalent coefficients. Therefore, the accuracy of ESV is heavily reliant on the accuracy of the land use classification. In addition, the simulation of future land use in this paper is only based on the land change rules identified during the period 2000 to 2010without considering policy implementation. Furthermore, while the PLUS model includes 11 driving factors, it is acknowledged that these factors may not comprehensively cover all pertinent influencers. In geographical systems, spatial complexity generally pertains to self-similarity and fractals on a spatial scale. Future research in land use and ecosystem services needs to concentrate on assessing the significant effects and intricacy of land use impact on ecosystems at various spatial scales.

Conclusion

This study investigated the land use dynamics of the SRW from 2000 to 2020, then revised the study area’s ESV equivalent factors from spatial, temporal, and hydrological perspectives, resulting in an ecosystem service value scale that was in line with the situation of the SRW. Then, the PLUS model was applied to simulate the land use in the study area in 2030 under natural conditions, and the corresponding ESV was calculated.

The main findings are as follows: (1) cropland, the predominant land use type in the SRW, accounted for over 55% of the total area. The significant land use change in the SRW was due to the mutual conversion between cropland and other land use types. Although the area of water body only covered about 20% of the total area, it contributed over 80% to the ESV; (2) From 2000 to 2020, the ESV increased by 0.64 billion CNY, with the water body contributing almost all the increase; (3) The projected ESV for 2030 is 2.94 billion CNY, indicating an increase in ecological functions in the near future.

Low-voltage U-shaped RF MEMS shunt switch integration for K-band phased array beam steering
Authors: Y. Anusha, Koushik Guha, Kavicharan Mummaneni, Jagadeesh Thati
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This work presents the design and system-level integration of a low-voltage U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch with a K-band phased array antenna for agile and efficient beam steering. The proposed switch achieves a pull-in voltage of 5.3 V, a return loss better than − 30 dB, and an insertion loss as 0.45 dB, ensuring minimal signal degradation across 18–27 GHz. By incorporating the switch into a distributed MEMS transmission line (DMTL) phase shifter network, discrete and reconfigurable phase states are realized, enabling dynamic control of the progressive phase shift between array elements. A four-element series-fed patch array integrated with the MEMS phase shifter demonstrates a steering range of ± 30 °C with low side-lobe levels and high radiation efficiency, confirming its suitability for high-frequency front-end modules. The results confirm that the proposed MEMS-integrated phased array achieves industry-leading low actuation voltage with minimal RF loss, positioning it as a strong candidate for 6G and satellite front-end modules.

Introduction

The demand for agile, high-performance phased array antennas (PAAs) has grown rapidly with the proliferation of satellite communications, automotive radar, and 5G/6G millimeter-wave networks1. Phased arrays offer electronic beam steering without mechanical motion, enabling fast tracking and adaptive beam shaping, which are critical for dynamic wireless environments. Traditional phase-shifting devices such as PIN diodes or FETs would present serious disadvantages, such as high-insertion loss, poor linearity and large power at K-band and higher frequencies of operation.

RF MEMS switches have also presented themselves as a promising option because of their very low insertion loss, high isolation and close to zero power consumption in steady state operation2,3. Phase shifters based on MEMS, and more specifically distributed MEMS transmission line (DMTL) designs, offer wideband phase shaping with low signal distortion and an overall high impedance match to that of cables and devices of similar size and functionality4,5. These are what make MEMS technology suitable to energy-saving high-frequency phased-array applications.

Although it has these benefits, the requirement to realize low actuation voltage and mechanical reliability are important issues in design. Large pull-in voltages complicate the biasing circuitry and excessive residual stress and stiction may worsen the long-term switch performance6,7. To overcome these drawbacks, modern studies have been dedicated to the optimization of beam structures, including meanders and perforations to decrease the stiffness and reduce the actuation threshold8,9. While existing MEMS phase shifters have demonstrated excellent RF performance, most designs still require actuation voltages above 10 V, limiting their integration into compact, battery-powered phased array systems. The proposed U-shaped meander configuration directly addresses this limitation by achieving 6 V actuation without compromising RF isolation.

In this work, we present the integration of a low-voltage U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch with a K-band phased array antenna system. The switch exhibits a pull-in voltage of 5.3 V, insertion loss of 0.004 dB, and return loss exceeding − 30 dB across 18–27 GHz. When embedded in a DMTL phase-shifter network, the device enables discrete and reconfigurable phase states with minimal power consumption. A four-element series-fed patch array incorporating the proposed MEMS phase shifters demonstrates ± 30 °C electronic beam steering with low side-lobe levels, confirming the suitability of the design for next-generation radar and satellite front-ends.

Related work and literature review

In recent years, the integration of RF MEMS switches with phased array antennas has gained increasing attention for millimeter-wave (mmWave) and K/Ka-band applications, primarily due to their capability to enable low-loss, reconfigurable beam steering with compact size and minimal power consumption.

A key challenge in MEMS switch design is achieving a low actuation voltage without compromising RF performance metrics such as insertion loss, isolation, and linearity. Sharma et al.10 proposed a capacitive shunt MEMS switch suitable for X-, Ku-, K-, and Ka-bands, achieving a pull-in voltage of 22.78 V while maintaining good RF characteristics. Similarly, Ansari et al.11 developed a K-band capacitive shunt switch (18–27 GHz) using a discontinuous coplanar waveguide (CPW) structure to enhance return loss and reduce dielectric charging effects. Shuaibu et al.12 introduced a hybrid electrothermal-electrostatic actuation mechanism that lowered actuation voltage to around 7 V, demonstrating the viability of mixed-mode actuation for low-voltage applications. These works underline the ongoing trade-off between actuation voltage reduction, dielectric reliability, and high-frequency RF performance, motivating the adoption of U-shaped meander geometries to lower stiffness while preserving isolation.

Recent advances have further underscored the importance of reliability and novel actuation strategies in MEMS phase shifters. Sen et al.13 analyze the impact of hot switching on dielectric breakdown and electrode degradation in capacitive MEMS devices, highlighting critical reliability challenges that must be mitigated in long-cycle operation. Similarly, Wen et al.14 investigate degradation of MEMS thin films under thermal stress, linking increased surface roughness and resistivity changes to device lifetime deterioration. These works motivate our inclusion of multi-physics stress, thermal, and statistical reliability analyses in the present work, ensuring that our low-voltage U-shaped meander MEMS and DMTL system are robust under real operating conditions. Furthermore, in the domain of phase shifters, recent studies have pushed performance via innovative materials and configurations. For instance, a miniaturized wideband phase shifter using nematic liquid crystals (NLC) embedded in a defected-ground-structure was demonstrated in14, achieving low insertion loss and continuous tuning over a broad band15. While such designs offer interesting alternative actuation principles, their application in the K-band with MEMS integration remains limited, reinforcing the novelty of our approach.

MEMS-based phase shifters, particularly in switched-line and distributed MEMS transmission line (DMTL) configurations, have been widely employed to realize discrete and low-loss phase tuning. A K-band switched-line phase shifter using a novel low-voltage, low-loss RF MEMS switch demonstrated efficient phase quantization and compact integration16. Early work on Ka-band MEMS phase shifters emphasized hinge-type structures to improve switching speed and mechanical reliability17. A notable advancement was presented by Kim et al.18 who realized a monolithic MEMS-based phased array system achieving 95 ° of continuous phase shift at 15 GHz, demonstrating the feasibility of on-glass integration for beam steering. More recently, Takano et al.19 achieved a compact 2 × 2 MEMS-phased array for Ka-band operation, integrating MEMS phase shifters and switches to allow two-dimensional electronic scanning.

Several researchers have explored direct embedding of MEMS switching networks within antenna arrays to achieve electronic beam steering and radiation reconfiguration. Yeo et al.20 reported a 20 GHz switched-beam antenna employing MEMS SPDT switches at the feed network to dynamically alter beam direction. Jishnu et al.21 developed a tightly coupled phased array for satellite communications in the 18–40 GHz range, utilizing MEMS-controlled feed paths for adaptive beamforming. These studies confirm the growing potential of MEMS integration in compact antenna architectures for next-generation radar and communication front ends.

Recent surveys on RF MEMS technology highlight key trends including low-voltage operation, packaging reliability, and the exploration of novel actuation mechanisms22. The literature emphasizes that reducing actuation voltage and improving long-term mechanical stability are critical for commercial adoption in mmWave phased arrays. Despite advances, most existing MEMS designs operate at moderate voltages (>20 V) or utilize hybrid actuation methods, underscoring the need for architectures that combine low-voltage operation with high-frequency robustness. The integration of a U-shaped meander RF MEMS switch with K-band phased arrays, as presented in this work, thus contributes a new pathway toward compact, power-efficient, and high-performance beam-steering systems.

Proposed methodology

The proposed work seeks to incorporate low voltage U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch and K-band phased array antenna to have accurate and low power electronic beam steering. The design methodology consists of three major steps (1) structural design and electromechanical modeling of the MEMS switch, (2) creation of a network of distributed MEMS transmission line (DMTL) phase shifters, and (3) combining the DMTL phase shifter with a multi-element phased array antenna to control the beam.

Structural design of the U-shaped meander RF MEMS switch

The MEMS switch that is proposed is a fixed-fixed metallic beam suspended over a signal line made up of a coplanar waveguide (CPW), as illustrated in Fig. 1. The beam is anchored at both ends and incorporates a U-shaped meander geometry to reduce its spring constant, thereby lowering the required actuation voltage. A thin dielectric layer is deposited over the signal line to prevent short-circuiting during beam deflection.Fig. 1Schematic of the proposed U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch showing the actuation electrode, dielectric layer, and suspended beam.

Schematic of the proposed U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch showing the actuation electrode, dielectric layer, and suspended beam.

In the unactuated (OFF) state, the beam remains suspended above the signal line, allowing RF energy to pass with minimal interference. Upon applying a DC bias voltage between the beam and the electrode, an electrostatic force pulls the beam downward. Once the beam contacts the dielectric, a high-capacitance (ON) state is achieved, effectively shorting the RF path to ground. Electromechanical modeling

The mechanical behavior of the MEMS beam can be approximated by a spring–mass–damper model governed by:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\varepsilon _0 A V^2}{(g_0 - x)^2} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where m is the effective mass, c is the damping coefficient, k is the effective spring constant, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon _0$$\end{document} is the permittivity of free space, A is the actuation area, V is the applied voltage, x is the beam displacement, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g_0$$\end{document} is the initial gap between the beam and electrode.

At equilibrium, the electrostatic and mechanical restoring forces are equal. The use of meanders increases beam flexibility, effectively reducing k and lowering \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V_{pi}$$\end{document}.

Discussion

The proposed U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch with a DMTL phase shifter has significant advances in low-voltage performance, mechanical stability, and environmental stability, making it a viable candidate as K-band phased array beam-steering systems. Mechanical reliability simulation indicated that the peak Von Mises stress was about \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$42~\text {MPa}$$\end{document} with the concentration in the areas near the anchors, which is much less than the yield limit of gold ((\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim 200~\text {MPa}$$\end{document})). This is equivalent to safety factor of above 2.3, and it is a good indication of the strong operation at cyclic loading and without the danger of fatigue. The first three natural frequencies of 45.8 kHz, 61.3 kHz and 84.7 kHz were determined using modal analysis and showed that the structure was highly stiff and was not covered by the effects of mechanical resonance in the actuation range. The thermal simulations at an ambient temperature of 85 °C showed that the temperature had a uniform distribution with a total thermal rise of less than 5 °C along the beam. This range of insertion loss drift, at less than 0.05 dB, confirms the device has a high degree of thermal stability and is thus well-suited to severe environmental conditions experienced in radar and satellite communication system applications. Monte Carlo statistical analysis of 200 random samples confirmed the process tolerance and a mean pull-in voltage of 5.32 V and a standard deviation of 0.18 V confirmed high fabrics repeatability and uniformity of operations in large scale phased array applications. Coupled-field simulations showed that there is a little RF-mechanical interaction; at a maximum deflection of 0.9 μm (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{11}$$\end{document}) and (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{21}$$\end{document}) are not more than 0.1 dB and − 0.5 dB respectively. This establishes the fact that there is no beam deflection at the expense of impedance matching or RF performance. Moreover, the electrostatic actuation process produces insignificant steady-state current, to give static power consumption to the order of zero, and transient energy per actuation of about 2.25pJ to represent sub-value of power operation on average. Comparison and benchmarking of recent MEMS phase shifters reported by Sharma et.al., Kim et al. and Li et al. points out that the proposed design has a reduction of actuation voltage by 35–70 % and a lowering of 0.1 dB in the insertion loss. This is further proved by the performance radar analysis that indicates its better trade-off between actuation voltage, insertion loss, isolation, beam steering range, and reliability. Taken together, these findings indicate that the developed design is a high-RF efficient, mechanically and thermally robust and energy efficient design, which can be used in the next generation beam-steering networks of 5G/6G and satellite front-end applications.

Conclusion

The design and comprehensive multiphysics analysis of a low-voltage U-shaped meander RF MEMS shunt switch integrated with a Distributed MEMS Transmission Line (DMTL) phase shifter for K-band phased array beam steering have been presented in this work. The proposed architecture successfully minimizes the actuation voltage to 5.3 V, while achieving an average insertion loss below 0.45 dB and high isolation exceeding 31.5 dB across the 18–27 GHz band. Mechanical simulations confirmed a peak Von Mises stress of 42 MPa, yielding a safety factor above 2.3, thereby ensuring reliable operation under cyclic loading conditions. The first three modal frequencies 45.8 kHz, 61.3 kHz, and 84.7 kHz demonstrate strong mechanical stiffness and immunity to resonance induced vibrations. Thermal analysis verified that the MEMS structure remains stable under elevated temperatures up to 85 °C, with a maximum temperature rise below 5 °C and negligible variation in insertion loss (<0.05 dB). A Monte Carlo analysis (200 runs), accounting for ±10% variations in beam thickness, gap height, and dielectric constant, yielded a mean pull-in voltage of 5.32 V with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma (V_{\textrm{pi}})=0.18$$\end{document} V, indicating excellent process tolerance and fabrication consistency. Coupled electro-mechanical simulations further demonstrated that the RF response exhibits minimal degradation even under maximum beam deflection of 0.9 μm, with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{11}< -30$$\end{document} dB and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{21}<0.45$$\end{document} dB, confirming robust impedance stability during operation. The device also exhibits near-zero static power consumption due to its electrostatic actuation mechanism, with an actuation energy per switching cycle of approximately 2.25 pJ, highlighting its exceptional energy efficiency. Comparative benchmarking against recent state-of-the-art designs revealed up to a 70% reduction in actuation voltage and 35–40% improvement in overall Figure of Merit (FOM), demonstrating the design’s superior performance and reliability. These results confirm the suitability of the proposed MEMS DMTL architecture for compact, low-power, and high-performance K-band and Ka-band phased-array beam-steering applications. The current work relies exclusively on numerical multiphysics simulations; therefore, fabrication-related non-idealities have not yet been accounted. Future work will focus on the fabrication and on-wafer experimental characterization of the proposed device to validate the simulated results. Additional efforts will address dielectric charging mitigation, packaging reliability, and long-term stability enhancement. Given its compact footprint, low loss, and high efficiency, the proposed design presents a promising candidate for next-generation 5G/6G front-end modules, radar imaging systems, and reconfigurable satellite communication networks.

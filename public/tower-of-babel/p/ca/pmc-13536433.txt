Synergistic effect of combined cisplatin and rosmarinic acid on apoptosis and migration of head and neck cancer cell lines
Authors: Suppanut Jongjitaree, Paopanga Kokilakanit, Pornnapas Ngamphoophunt, Than Thongsiripipat, Sittichai Koontongkaew, Kusumawadee Utispan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/purposeRosmarinic acid (RA), a phenolic compound demonstrating therapeutic effects including anticancer properties. Cisplatin, a chemotherapeutic agent, is widely employed in cancer treatment. Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) exhibits high mortality rates, frequently resistance to cisplatin-based regimens. Cisplatin and RA combination enhanced cisplatin efficiency across various tumor types. This study aimed to elucidate the effects of cisplatin-RA combination on cell proliferation, apoptosis and migration in HNSCC cell lines.Materials and methodsPrimary HNSCC cell lines (HN18/HN30) and metastatic HNSCC cell lines (HN17/HN31) were employed in this investigation. Cells underwent treatment with rosmarinic acid (100–1,000 μM) or cisplatin (5–40 μM) for 72 h, followed by cell viability assessment using MTT assay. The combinatorial cisplatin-RA treatment effects on cell proliferation inhibition were quantified using MTT assay, with calculation of the combination index to assess synergistic interactions. Synergistic effects of cisplatin-RA combination on apoptosis and cell migration were evaluated in HNSCC cell lines through flow cytometric analysis and scratch assay, respectively.ResultsBoth cisplatin and rosmarinic acid demonstrated concentration-dependent reductions in HNSCC cell viability. Combination index of cisplatin-RA interaction indicating synergistic interactions in HN17, HN30 and HN31 cells. Cisplatin (20 μM) with RA (400 μM) combination synergistically induced apoptosis in HN18 and HN17 cells, while cisplatin (20 μM) and RA (800 μM) combination demonstrated synergistic apoptotic induction in HN30 and HN31 cells. Cisplatin and RA monotherapy, but not combination significantly inhibited HNSCC cell migration.ConclusionThis study found that cisplatin-RA combination can inhibit proliferation and induce apoptosis in HNSCC cell lines.Keywords: Cisplatin and rosmarinic acid combination, Cell proliferation, Apoptosis, Migration, Head and neck cancer

Introduction

Head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCCs) represent malignant neoplasms arising from squamous epithelium within the head and neck region, ranking sixth in global cancer mortality.1 Due to absence of early-stage screening protocols, most patients present with advanced-stage disease requiring multimodal treatment approaches including surgical resection followed by radiotherapy, often combined with chemotherapy based on disease severity.2 Currently, six chemotherapeutic agents, including cisplatin, are employed for HNSCC treatment. Cisplatin, a platinum-based chemotherapeutic compound, demonstrates broad-spectrum anticancer activity across multiple malignancies.3 Its primary therapeutic mechanism in HNSCCs involves inducing DNA damage through oxidative stress generation, subsequently triggering both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic pathways. However, high-dose or prolonged cisplatin administration causes inflammatory damage to oxidative stress-sensitive organs and induces DNA damage in normal cells.4 This collateral DNA damage increases secondary malignancy risk.3,5 Additionally, HNSCCs frequently develop cisplatin resistance, with 90 % of 5-year survivors exhibiting resistant lesions.6

Rosmarinic acid (RA), a phenolic compound predominantly found in Lamiaceae and Boraginaceae plant families,7 exhibits antiviral, antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and anticancer properties.8,9 RA suppresses HNSCC cell proliferation through apoptotic pathway activation.10 Furthermore, ethanolic Ocimum sanctum extracts—containing RA as the primary bioactive component—inhibit cell cycle progression, induce DNA damage, and trigger apoptosis in HNSCC cell lines.11

Recent studies have explored RA-cisplatin combination therapy to enhance chemotherapeutic efficacy across various malignancies. Combined cisplatin-RA treatment inhibits tumor cell invasion and metastasis.12 In melanoma models, RA synergistically reduces cellular proliferation, invasion, and metastatic potential while enhancing cisplatin sensitivity.13 However, synergistic effects of cisplatin-RA combination therapy on HNSCC cellular activities remain unexplored. This investigation aimed to elucidate the effects of combined cisplatin-RA treatment on proliferation, apoptotic induction, and migratory capacity in HNSCC cell lines.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Two isogenic primary-metastatic HNSCC cell line pairs (HN18/HN17 and HN30/HN31) were utilized in this study, as previously established14 and generously provided by Professor Silvio Gutkind (Moores Cancer Center, Department of Pharmacology, UC San Diego, USA). The HN18 cell line was derived from a primary tongue carcinoma, while its metastatic counterpart HN17 was isolated from cervical lymph node dissection of the same patient (T2N2M0). Similarly, HN30 cells originated from a primary pharyngeal lesion, with the corresponding metastatic HN31 cell line established from lymph node metastasis in the same patient (T3N1M0). All cell lines were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, Invitrogen), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Cultures were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5 % CO2.

MTT assay

Cytotoxic effects of cisplatin and rosmarinic acid (Sigma-–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) were assessed using the MTT assay. HNSCC cell lines were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well and allowed to attach for 24 h. Subsequently, cells were exposed to varying concentrations of RA (100, 200, 400, 800, or 1,000 μM) or cisplatin (5, 10, 20, or 40 μM), while control groups were maintained in complete culture medium without treatment. Following 72 h of incubation, the culture medium was replaced with fresh medium containing MTT reagent (Sigma–Aldrich), and cells were incubated at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 atmosphere for 4 h to allow formazan crystal formation. The supernatant was then carefully aspirated, and the resulting formazan crystals were solubilized using dimethyl sulfoxide. Apoptosis evaluation using flow cytometry

Effect of cisplatin combined with RA on HNSCC cell lines’ apoptosis was determined using annexin V and 7-aminoactinomycin D (7-AAD) staining and analyzed in a flow cytometer (Cytoflex®, Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA). HNSCC cell lines were cultured in 6-well plates (500,000 cells/well) for 24 h before treated with cisplatin (20 μM), RA (400 μM for HN18/HN17 cells and 800 μM for HN30/HN31 cells) or cisplatin and RA combination for 24 h. The cells were stained with annexin V – fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)/7-AAD apoptosis kit (Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA) according to the industrial’s protocol. Apoptotic cells were analyzed and counted in a flow cytometer using Cytexpert software (Cytoflex®, Beckman Coulter). Percent of apoptotic cells in all stages were calculated and compared with cisplatin, RA or control groups. The experiment was done in triplicate.

Scratch assay

The effects of cisplatin-rosmarinic acid combination therapy on HNSCC cellular migration were evaluated using the scratch assay. HNSCC cell lines were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 106 cells per well and cultured to confluent monolayers over 24 h. Cells were then treated with cisplatin (20 μM), RA (400 μM for HN18/HN17 cell lines; 800 μM for HN30/HN31 cell lines), or combined cisplatin-RA therapy.

A linear scratch wound was created through the confluent cell monolayer using a sterile 200 μL pipette tip, producing a cell-free zone across the culture surface. Photomicrographs of the scratched region were captured using an inverted microscope at × 40 magnification at identical locations immediately post-wounding (T0) and 24 h post-wounding (T24). Wound gap areas were quantified from digital images using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). Cell migration percentages were calculated based on wound closure area according to the following equation:

Cell migration (%)=100 × (AT0 – AT24)/AT0. Where AT0 is the area of wound measured immediately after scratching and AT24 is the area of wound measured 24 h after scratching. Three independent experiments were performed.

Statistical analysis

The results are presented as means and standard error. Mean comparison in multiple groups were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical analyses were performed using Prism GraphPad 7.04 software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Statistically significant was considered when P ≤ 0.05.

Results

Effect of RA and cisplatin on HNSCC cell viability

The effect of RA or cisplatin on HNSCC cell viability was assessed. The result showed that RA significantly decreased viability in HN18 and HN17 cells at 200–1,000 μM and HN30 and HN31 cells at 400–1,000 μM compared with control (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1). In addition, cisplatin (20 and 40 μM) was common significant reductive dose of viability in all HNSCC cells (Fig. 2). In subsequent experiment, 20 μM cisplatin was selected to treat with all HNSCC cells. RA concentrations 200–400 μM and 400–800 μM were used as effective doses for HN18/HN17 cells and HN30/HN31 cells, respectively.

Cisplatin (5–40 μM) was evaluated toxicity on HNSCC cells. Cells cultured in growth media without cisplatin served as control. Cell viability was determined in (A) HN18, (B) HN17, (C) HN30 and (D) HN31 cells. Bars represent mean ± SEM of percent cell viability (n = 3). *P < 0.05 compared with control.

Combined cisplatin and RA enhanced reduction of HNSCC cell viability

Cisplatin (20 μM) significantly decreased cell viability in all HNSCC cells compared with control (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3). RA at 400 and 800 μM significantly reduced cell viability in HN18/HN17 and HN30/HN31 cells, respectively, compared with control (P < 0.05). Moreover, cisplatin (20 μM) and RA (400 or 800 μM) combinations significantly enhanced cytotoxicity in HN18/HN17 cells (Fig. 3A and B) and HN30/HN31 cells (Fig. 3C and D), respectively, compared with single cisplatin treatment (P < 0.05). Therefore, the effective combination concentration of cisplatin and RA for inhibiting cell proliferation were cisplatin (20 μM) + RA (400 μM) and cisplatin (20 μM) + RA (800 μM) for HN18/HN17 and HN30/HN31 cells, respectively.

Effect of cisplatin and rosmarinic acid (RA) combination on HNSCC cell viability.

Cisplatin (20 μM) combined with different doses of RA was treated with HNSCC cells. Cell viability was determined in (A) HN18, (B) HN17, (C) HN30 and (D) HN31 cells. Bars represent mean ± SEM of percent cell viability (n = 3). *P < 0.05.

Synergistic effect of cisplatin and RA on HNSCC cell viability

Combination index of cisplatin and RA for HN18, HN17, HN30 and HN31 cells viability were 1.34, 0.97, 0.94 and 0.96, respectively. Cisplatin and RA combination at effective dose exhibited different additive effect on proliferative inhibition between HNSCC cell lines (Fig. 4). Interestingly, synergistic effects of drug combination on cell viability inhibition were found in HN17, HN30 and HN31 cells at average inhibitory effects 93 %, 84 % and 88 %, respectively (Fig. 4B–D).

Cisplatin (20 μM) combined with RA (400 μM) or RA (800 μM) was treated with HNSCC cells. Combination index of cisplatin and RA treatment was calculated based on inhibition effect of viability in (A) HN18, (B) HN17, (C) HN30 and (D) HN31 cells. Bars represent the percentage proliferative inhibition of cisplatin or/and RA (n = 3).

Cisplatin and RA combination induced apoptosis in HNSCC cell lines

HNSCC cells were treated with cisplatin-RA combination at synergistic concentration and evaluated apoptosis using flow cytometry. Distribution of apoptosis phases was analyzed and showed in scatter plot (Fig. 5A). Cisplatin-RA combination significantly induced apoptosis in HN18 cells compared with cisplatin treatment (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5B). Moreover, cisplatin-RA combination significantly increased apoptosis in HN17, HN30 and HN31 cells compared with single treatments of cisplatin or RA (P < 0.05).

Effect of cisplatin and RA combination on HNSCC cell migration

Effect of cisplatin-RA combination on HNSCC cells was investigated using scratch assay. The picture of wound scratched in all conditions was taken at the beginning time (T0) and 24 h (T24) after treatment (Fig. 6A). Cisplatin-RA combination significantly reduced migration in HN18 and HN31 cells compared with control (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6A). However, there was no significant different of migration in HN17 and HN30 cells between cisplatin-RA combination and control or single treatment (P > 0.05).

Discussion

Rosmarinic acid, a polyphenolic compound ubiquitously distributed throughout plant species, exhibits diverse therapeutic properties, including anticancer activity.16 Previous investigations have demonstrated that RA enhances cisplatin’s anticancer efficacy through multiple mechanisms, including augmented cytotoxicity, apoptotic induction, and modulation of cellular migration.13,17,18 In the current investigation, RA demonstrated cytotoxic effects against HNSCC cells at concentrations ranging from 200 to 400 μM. These findings align with previously reported cytotoxic concentration ranges for RA across various cancer types.8,9,13,17 Notably, the cytotoxic RA concentrations required for HN30 and HN31 cells (T3N1M0) exceeded those required for HN18 and HN17 cells (T2N2M0), suggesting that tumor stage may correlate with RA resistance capacity in HNSCC cells.

The effective cisplatin concentration across all tested HNSCC cell lines was 20 μM, consistent with concentrations employed in colorectal cancer models.17 Low-dose cisplatin combined with RA has been proposed to enhance overall therapeutic efficacy across multiple cancer types.13,17,18 Combination index analysis revealed that RA synergistically inhibited cellular proliferation with cisplatin in HN17, HN30, and HN31 cell lines, while demonstrating an additive effect in HN18 cells. Previous research documented synergistic cell proliferation inhibition with cisplatin-RA combination therapy in cisplatin-resistant non-small cell lung cancer cell lines.17 Collectively, these data suggest that cisplatin-RA combination therapy may be particularly cytotoxic against HNSCC cells exhibiting aggressive characteristics, such as lymph node metastasis or advanced-stage behavior. Furthermore, the 20 μM cisplatin-RA combination increased proliferation inhibition by 7–19 % in HN17, HN30, and HN31 cells compared to 40 μM cisplatin monotherapy. These findings demonstrate that low-dose cisplatin-RA combination therapy improves therapeutic efficacy in HNSCC cell lines.

Previous studies have shown that cisplatin-RA combination therapy significantly enhances cytotoxicity and apoptosis through downregulation of anti-apoptotic proteins (Bcl-2) and upregulation of pro-apoptotic proteins (Bax) in colorectal cancer.17 The current investigation confirmed that combined cisplatin-RA treatment induces greater overall apoptosis in HNSCC cell lines compared to cisplatin monotherapy. Additionally, late apoptosis was predominantly observed in cisplatin-RA treated HNSCC cells. Further investigation of apoptotic pathway proteins is required to elucidate the mechanistic basis of cisplatin-RA-induced apoptosis in HNSCC. A previous study reported that while cisplatin inhibited renal cancer cell proliferation and migration, cisplatin-RA combination therapy did not affect cancer cell migration.12 Our investigation similarly demonstrated significant migration inhibition by either cisplatin or RA monotherapy against HNSCC cells. However, synergistic migratory inhibition was not observed with cisplatin-RA combination treatment. A previous study reported that RA inhibited cell migration in HNSCC cell lines by attenuating the epidermal growth factor receptor-induced phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase Akt and mitogen-activated protein kinase ERK pathways.19 In contrast, cisplatin was proposed to suppress HNSCC cell migration and invasion through the inhibitor of nuclear factor kappa-B kinase subunit beta (IKKβ)/nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) pathway.20 These evidences suggest that the robust migratory inhibition mechanisms of cisplatin and RA found in our study may function independently in HNSCC cells.

This investigation demonstrated that cisplatin-RA combination therapy synergistically suppresses cellular proliferation and enhances apoptotic induction in HNSCC cell lines. The underlying mechanisms by which combined cisplatin-RA treatment influence HNSCC progression, including alterations in pro- and anti-apoptotic protein expression, warrant further investigation.

Preoperative Neurological Changes on Outcomes in Large-Vessel Occlusion Due to Intracranial Atherosclerotic Disease
Authors: Takeshi Yoshimoto, Kanta Tanaka, Junpei Koge, Kazutaka Uchida, Hirotoshi Imamura, Kazunori Toyoda, Hiroshi Yamagami, Nobuyuki Sakai, Manabu Shirakawa, Mikiya Beppu, Yuji Matsumaru, Yasushi Matsumoto, Kenichi Todo, Mikito Hayakawa, Seigo Shindo, Masafumi Morimoto, Masataka Takeuchi, Hiroyuki Ikeda, Hideyuki Ishihara, Hiroto Kakita, Takanori Sano, Hayato Araki, Tatsufumi Nomura, Fumihiro Sakakibara, Shinichi Yoshimura
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES:We aimed to clarify the association between neurological deterioration pre-endovascular therapy (EVT) and outcome in patients with large-vessel occlusion due to intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD-LVO) undergoing EVT.METHODS:Consecutive patients with acute ischemic stroke due to ICAD-LVO within 24 h of onset who underwent EVT were enrolled in the Japanese multicenter registry from 2017 to 2019. Patients were grouped according to neurological severity transition as follows: mild symptoms (baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score <6 and NIHSS score pre-EVT <6), symptom deterioration (baseline NIHSS score <6 and NIHSS score pre-EVT ≥6), and severe symptoms (baseline NIHSS score ≥6 and NIHSS score pre-EVT ≥6). Outcomes included favorable outcomes (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score of 0-2 at 90 days), ordinal mRS shift, and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. Multivariable logistic regression assessed the association of outcomes with the transition of neurological severity by calculating odds ratios and 95% CIs, with mild symptoms as reference.RESULTS:In total, 480 patients with acute ICAD-LVO who underwent EVT (150 women [31.2%]; median age, 72 years IQR, 66-80) and had median baseline NIHSS score 12 (IQR, 6-20) were analyzed. Patients with symptom deterioration (n = 34) and severe symptoms (n = 375) had lower favorable outcomes (deterioration 38.2% vs mild 62.9%; adjusted odds ratio 0.30, 95% CI 0.09-0.97, severe 35.3%; 0.47, 0.33-0.65) and a significant mRS shift (deterioration vs mild; 3.63, 1.46-9.03, severe; 2.27, 1.74-2.96) than those with mild symptoms (n = 71). Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage rates did not differ (mild 0%; deterioration 0%; severe 1.9%).CONCLUSION:Patients with ICAD-LVO who experienced worsening symptoms were less likely to achieve favorable outcomes after EVT than those with mild symptoms. Early identification of neurological deterioration and EVT intervention may improve outcomes in these patients.

The breakthrough trials of endovascular therapy (EVT) have revolutionized the treatment of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) resulting from large-vessel occlusion (LVO).1 However, several unanswered questions remain regarding the optimal management of LVO due to intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD-LVO).2,3

A proportion of AIS due to ICAD-LVO present with mild neurological deficits, commonly defined as a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score below 6,4 due to good leptomeningeal collaterals.2 Approximately 85% of patients with this condition achieve functional independence at 3 months after receiving the best medical therapy, which includes intravenous thrombolysis unless contraindicated; however, there remains room for improvement in outcomes.5-7 Poor outcomes after the best medical treatment in this population are mainly due to early neurological deterioration, which is observed in 10% to 15% of cases.8,9 Although acute ICAD-LVO is associated with early neurological deterioration and NIHSS worsening,10 limited data exist on the relationship between preoperative neurological severity progression and post-EVT outcomes in patients with AIS caused by ICAD-LVO. Therefore, the aim of this substudy was to clarify the association between the preoperative transition of neurological severity and clinical outcomes in patients undergoing EVT for AIS due to ICAD-LVO in a large, multicenter Japanese cohort.

METHODS

The data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Study Participants

All patients with AIS due to atherothrombotic stroke–related LVO—including intracranial atherosclerosis or extracranial carotid atherosclerosis—who were admitted within 7 days of the last known well (LKW) and underwent EVT were retrospectively registered in the Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-acute Embolic and Atherothrombotic Stroke with Large Vessel Occlusion (RESCUE AT-LVO),11-15 a multicenter registry that collected data from 51 hospitals in Japan between January 2017 and December 2019. For this subanalysis, we retrospectively reviewed consecutive patients enrolled in the registry who met the following criteria: (1) AIS due to ICAD-LVO and (2) had no missing data on the baseline NIHSS score, NIHSS score pre-EVT, and modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 90 days. ICAD-LVO was defined by angiographic signs during EVT as a residual intracranial arterial stenosis of ≥50% on final angiography or <50% stenosis with the appearance of a tapered sign and/or flow obstruction during the procedure.16 We excluded patients with tandem occlusion (concomitant extracranial and distal intracranial artery occlusion) and those with rapid neurological improvement before EVT (baseline NIHSS score ≥6 and NIHSS score immediately before EVT <6).

Clinical and radiological data were retrospectively retrieved through chart reviews or by contacting patients or their proxies. This study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Hyogo College of Medicine (approval number: 3727) and all participating hospitals. The requirement for written informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study and the use of anonymized data, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.17 The complete STROBE checklist is included in Supplemental Digital Content 1 (http://links.lww.com/NS9/A56).

EVT

All endovascular procedures were performed by neurointerventionalists certified by the Japanese Society for Neuro-EVT,18 as recommended by the American Heart Association/American Stroke Association Guidelines.1 The EVT procedure included stent retriever/combined contact aspiration and stent retriever, contact aspiration, angioplasty, and stenting.19 Reperfusion status post-EVT was assessed according to the modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) scale.20

Clinical Data Collection

The following clinical data were collected: age, sex, prestroke mRS score, baseline systolic blood pressure, baseline NIHSS score, NIHSS score immediately pre-EVT, medical history (current smoking, atrial fibrillation, chronic heart failure, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack before the index stroke, and ischemic heart disease); antithrombotic drugs before index stroke (single antiplatelet drug, dual antiplatelet drugs, any anticoagulant drugs, and statins); laboratory data (high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and blood glucose), imaging (Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomographic Score [ASPECTS] on noncontrast computed tomography or diffusion-weighted imaging, and posterior circulation-ASPECTS); occluded vessel (intracranial internal carotid artery, M1 segment of middle cerebral artery, M2 segment of middle cerebral artery, basilar artery, and intracranial vertebral artery); treatment (intravenous thrombolysis); and time delays (time from hospital arrival to groin puncture, time from groin puncture to mTICI ≥ 2a reperfusion, and time from LKW to mTICI ≥ 2a reperfusion3). Procedural variables included thrombectomy details, antiplatelet medications used during the perioperative period (aspirin, clopidogrel, cilostazol, prasugrel, and intravenous ozagrel), final mTICI ≥ 2b reperfusion, total number of angioplasties, reocclusion during the procedure, and degree of stenosis at the end of the procedure according to the Warfarin-Aspirin Symptomatic Intracranial Disease criteria.21 Intravenous thrombolysis was performed using alteplase at 0.6 mg/kg (approved dose in Japan).22 Neurological deterioration was defined as the difference in NIHSS score between pre-EVT and baseline.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was a favorable outcome, defined as an mRS score of 0 to 2 at 90 days. Secondary outcomes included ordinal mRS shift, excellent outcome (mRS 0-1 at 90 days), death within 90 days, any hemorrhagic event, any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), symptomatic ICH (SICH), and recurrent ischemic stroke. ICH was assessed by noncontrast computed tomography or gradient-echo magnetic resonance imaging at 24 ± 8 hours post-EVT and defined as any new hemorrhage within the brain parenchyma, subarachnoid space, or ventricular system, irrespective of symptoms. SICH was defined as an ICH causing ≥4-point NIHSS worsening according to the Heidelberg classification.23

Statistical Analysis

Data were summarized as the median (IQR) for continuous variables and as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Patients were classified into 3 groups based on NIHSS scores: the mild group (baseline NIHSS <6 and NIHSS score <6 immediately prior to EVT), the deterioration group (baseline NIHSS <6 and NIHSS score ≥6 immediately prior to EVT), and the severe group (baseline NIHSS ≥6 and NIHSS score ≥6 immediately prior to EVT). Statistical differences among the 3 groups were assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis test or χ2 test, as appropriate. Multivariate logistic regression models were used to evaluate the association between outcomes and changes in neurological severity, with odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs calculated using patients with mild symptoms as the reference. The following prespecified variables were adjusted: sex,24 age,25-27 intravenous thrombolysis,24 times from groin puncture to mTICI ≥ 2a reperfusion,3,28 and final mTICI ≥ 2b reperfusion.29 The same analysis was performed separately for anterior circulation LVO and posterior circulation LVO.

In addition, outcomes were compared by stratifying patients with baseline NIHSS scores <6 into 2 groups according to early neurological deterioration scores (≥430 and 2,31 respectively). The marginal probability of achieving a favorable outcome, adjusted for sex, age, intravenous thrombolysis, time from groin puncture to mTICI ≥ 2a reperfusion, and final mTICI ≥ 2b reperfusion, was plotted separately for patients with baseline mild (NIHSS <6) and severe symptoms (NIHSS ≥6). Statistical significance was set at P < .05. Analyses were performed using Stata/IC, version 17.1 (StataCorp, College Station, TX).

RESULTS

Patient Characteristics

Of the 770 patients enrolled in the registry, after excluding 256 patients with acute tandem occlusion stroke, 1 patient with rapid neurological improvement (baseline NIHSS score ≥6 and NIHSS score <6 immediately before EVT <6), 8 with missing baseline NIHSS scores or NIHSS scores immediately before EVT, and 25 with missing mRS scores at 90 days, the remaining 480 patients with acute ICAD-LVO (150 women [31.2%]; median age 72 years [IQR, 66-80]; median NIHSS score 12 [IQR 6-20]) were analyzed in the substudy (Figure 1). The prevalence of medication use before the index ischemic stroke was 18.8% (90/480) for antiplatelet agents and 16.5% (79/480) for statins. The baseline characteristics of patients with mild, deteriorating, and severe symptoms are presented in Table 1. No significant differences in procedural complications were observed during EVT among the 3 groups, as presented in Results and Table S1 in Supplemental Digital Content 2 (http://links.lww.com/NS9/A57). Compared with patients with mild symptoms (n = 71), those with symptom deterioration (n = 34) had lower rates of favorable outcomes (mRS 0-2 at 90 days: 38.2% vs 62.9%; adjusted OR [aOR] 0.30, 95% CI 0.09-0.97), excellent outcomes (mRS 0-1: 20.6% vs 47.1%; aOR 0.19, 95% CI 0.05-0.72), and experienced a greater mRS shift (aOR 3.63, 95% CI 1.46-9.03). Patients with severe symptoms (n = 375) showed lower rates of favorable (35.3%; aOR 0.47, 95% CI 0.33-0.65) and excellent outcomes (21.3%; aOR 0.43, 95% CI 0.31-0.61). No significant differences were observed in SICH (mild 0%; deterioration 0%; severe 1.9%), recurrent ischemic stroke (deterioration 20.6% vs mild 21.1%; aOR 0.57, 95% CI 0.16-2.08; severe 23.4%; aOR 0.96, 95% CI 0.68-1.34), or reocclusion of target vessels (deterioration 8.8% vs mild 14.1%; aOR 0.44, 95% CI 0.08-2.43; severe 17.6%; aOR 1.03, 95% CI 0.71-1.52). Neurological Deterioration and Outcomes

For all patients, the association between the neurological deterioration NIHSS score and the rate of achieving favorable outcomes was not significant (coefficient −0.005, 95% CI −0.014 to 0.004, P = .30) (Figure 4A). However, for patients with mild symptoms at baseline (baseline NIHSS score <6), the rate of achieving favorable outcomes decreased significantly as the neurological deterioration NIHSS score increased (coefficient −0.123, 95% CI −0.023 to −0.016, P = .02) (Figure 4B). No association was observed between the neurological deterioration NIHSS score and favorable outcomes in patients with a baseline NIHSS score ≥6 (coefficient −0.019, 95% CI −0.046 to −0.008, P = .16) (Figure 4C). No significant association between time from hospital arrival to groin puncture and outcomes was observed, regardless of baseline neurological severity (mild symptoms at baseline, P = .59; severe symptoms at baseline, P = .70) (Results and Table S5, Figure S2 in Supplemental Digital Content 2 [http://links.lww.com/NS9/A57]).

DISCUSSION

In this subanalysis, among patients with an NIHSS score of less than 6 who underwent EVT for ICAD-LVO, 32% had an EVT performed after neurological deterioration with an NIHSS score of 6 or more. Notably, patients who had mild symptoms at baseline but whose symptoms worsened had a lower rate of achieving a favorable outcome than patients with consistently mild symptoms, suggesting that worsening symptoms have a serious impact on patient outcomes and that urgent action may be needed to address this issue. Furthermore, among patients receiving EVT for acute ICAD-LVO, the rate of favorable outcomes significantly decreased with each worsening NIHSS score in patients with mild symptoms at baseline (baseline NIHSS score <6), whereas no significant association was observed between the degree of NIHSS score worsening and the rate of favorable outcomes in patients with severe symptoms at baseline (baseline NIHSS score ≥6).

The management of ICAD-LVO with EVT is complex and requires careful consideration. Although there is a strong argument for early intervention, the decision to perform EVT before neurological symptoms worsen is not straightforward. Patients with ICAD-LVO often have unique characteristics that may influence the timing of EVT such as smaller scattered infarct volumes,32 border zone infarction patterns,33 and robust collateral circulation.2 This hemodynamic profile may contribute to a slower progression of neurological deterioration compared with embolic LVO.34 However, the risk of early neurological deterioration remains a significant concern. The presence of good collateral circulation in patients with ICAD-LVO has been reported to provide a wider treatment window,34 and our results showing no significant association between outcome and time delay in acute ICAD-LVO also support the presence of good collateral circulation in patients with ICAD-LVO. A previous retrospective multicenter study showed that while the probability of good outcomes in embolic occlusion declined with increasing onset-to-puncture time, the probability of good outcomes in patients with ICAD-LVO did not decline and even tended to increase.34 In our results, the median time from onset to recanalization was significantly longer in patients with symptom deterioration compared with those with mild and severe symptoms, but this delay was inferred to have been caused by hesitation in performing EVT for mild cases and subsequent neurological deterioration. Therefore, it was suggested that, unlike embolic LVO, the presence of collateral blood vessels in ICAD-LVO means that neurological deterioration is more closely related to poor outcomes than the time delay itself.

Early identification and treatment strategies for ICAD-LVO are crucial given these unique considerations, including neurological deterioration.3,35 The decision to perform EVT should be based on a careful assessment of individual patient factors, including the risk of early neurological deterioration, collateral status, and potential for reocclusion. Up to half of patients with ICAD-LVO may experience reocclusion after initial revascularization, which can lead to rapid neurological deterioration.3 Delays in perfusion imaging and border zone infarct patterns may indicate a high risk of early neurological deterioration in ICAD,33,36 and rapid diagnosis of ICAD-LVO and prediction of neurological deterioration are extremely important for ICAD-LVO treatment strategies.37,38 Although no definitive consensus exists, current evidence suggests that early intervention of EVT should be strongly considered for patients with ICAD-LVO, even before significant neurological deterioration occurs, but it should be noted that no large-scale randomized controlled trials specific to ICAD-LVO have been conducted, and much of the current evidence is based on observational studies.3

Notably, perioperative complication rates were comparable across all groups. These findings suggest that a subset of patients with ICAD-LVO may benefit from early EVT, even before neurological deterioration, underscoring the importance of prompt identification and intervention. Given that our subanalysis was retrospective and included only patients who underwent EVT, the possibility of selection bias cannot be excluded. Although no significant difference in outcomes was observed between patients with baseline mild symptoms who subsequently worsened and those with consistently severe symptoms, patients with persistently mild symptoms achieved a higher rate of favorable outcomes without increased hemorrhagic risk. These results highlight that delaying EVT despite symptom progression may adversely affect outcomes and emphasize the need for rapid and effective recanalization.

Our study is important because it provides real-world clinical data showing the effects of EVT on neurological severity in patients with ICAD-LVO despite the lack of supporting evidence. The efficacy of EVT for LVO with mild symptoms has not been established. In the international systematic review registry,39 EVT seems equivalent to the best medical treatment in patients with anterior circulation LVO AIS and a baseline NIHSS score of <6, despite an increased risk of SICH. In this subanalysis, unmeasured confounding factors led the surgeon to perform EVT despite mild symptoms, such as the type of neurological deficit, acute clinical course (fluctuating symptoms, perceived high-end risk), imaging findings,37 and collateral assessment,40 which influenced both the decision and subsequent effect of EVT. Achieving recanalization before the irreversible progression of ischemia, accompanied by worsening neurological symptoms, may have resulted in a higher rate of favorable outcomes compared with patients with neurological deterioration. Moreover, in our subanalysis, the time from LKW to mTICI ≥ 2a reperfusion was 496 min in the mild group and significantly longer at 1085 min in the deterioration group. This suggests that initial medical management was chosen because of mild symptoms; however, over time, collateral circulation deteriorated, leading to progressive ischemia and worsening symptoms, ultimately necessitating EVT. Randomized trials adding EVT to the best medical treatment alone in populations with mild symptoms are ongoing (EVT for Low NIHSS Ischemic Strokes [NCT04167527] and Minor Stroke Therapy Evaluation [NCT03796468]). Therefore, current meta-analyses suggest that EVT should not be routinely recommended for nonrandomized patients with mild stroke and LVO, as the risk-benefit ratio differs compared with patients with severe symptoms. However, EVT can still be reasonably considered for a selected subgroup of patients with mildly symptomatic LVO stroke, as highlighted in current international guidelines.1

Limitations

This study had some limitations. First, it was a retrospective, nonrandomized study, and confounding factors, including selection bias, may have influenced the observed benefits of EVT. Second, although the study used a multicenter design, it may have been underpowered to detect differences between groups. Third, the time of mTICI ≥ 2b achievement was not collected; therefore, the time to mTICI ≥ 2a achievement was used as the reperfusion end point. Although mTICI ≥ 2a generally represents incomplete recanalization, favorable outcomes may still occur in ICAD-LVO because of collateral development. Moreover, complete reperfusion (mTICI ≥ 2b/3) may not be durable because of the risk of early reocclusion.3 These pathophysiological features should be considered when interpreting the results.

CONCLUSION

Patients with symptom deterioration who underwent EVT for acute ICAD-LVO were less likely to achieve favorable outcomes compared with those with mild symptoms. Early detection and prompt intervention with EVT for neurological deterioration in ICAD-LVO stroke may positively affect patient prognosis.

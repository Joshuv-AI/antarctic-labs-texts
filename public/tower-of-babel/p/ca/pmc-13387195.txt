Factors Associated with the Acceptance of Weight Loss Drugs Among Overweight and Obese Adults in Mainland China: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Wude Guo, Min Zhao, Yang Liu, Xin Zhang, Guoxiang Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveWith the growing burden of overweight and obesity, weight-loss pharmacotherapy has emerged as an effective treatment option. However, patient acceptance remains a significant barrier to treatment uptake and adherence. This study investigated the acceptance of weight loss drugs (WLDs) among overweight and obese adults in mainland China, as well as related social-demographic, clinical, and psychosocial determinants.MethodsData were drawn from the 2021 Psychology and Behaviour Investigation of Chinese Residents (PBICR) study, including 2577 Chinese adults with BMI ≥24 kg/m2. WLDs acceptance was measured using a score from 0 to 100. Statistical analyses included analysis of variance, Pearson correlation, and stepwise linear regression, with subgroup analyses stratified by body weight and chronic disease status.ResultsHigher family income, social support, and anxiety levels were positively correlated with the acceptance of weight loss drugs. Age ≥ 60 years, comorbidities, smoking, better dietary behaviors, and poorer family health were negatively correlated with acceptance. Acceptance of WLDs varied across occupational groups and weight status categories. Dietary behaviors were also positively correlated with acceptance. Subgroup analysis showed significant heterogeneity in the influencing factors.ConclusionThis study revealed low acceptance of WLDs among adults with overweight and obesity in mainland China. Acceptance is closely related to a variety of social demographic, clinical, and psychosocial factors. These findings highlight personalized treatment plans for obesity medications to enhance treatment compliance.

Introduction

Overweight and obesity have emerged as major global public health challenges. Since 1990, the prevalence of obesity among adults has more than doubled worldwide, with 43% of adults classified as overweight in 2022.1 In 2024, the NCD Risk Factor Collaboration (NCD-RisC) estimated that approximately 880 million adults worldwide were living with obesity.2 In China, adult overweight prevalence increased from 20.65% in 1993 to 55.33% in 2021, while obesity prevalence rose from 3.10% to 19.26% during the same period.3 Obesity is a well-established risk factor for diabetes, hypertension, stroke, coronary heart disease, and premature mortality.4 In China, obesity-related mortality increased from an estimated 230,000 deaths in 1990 to 760,000 deaths in 2019 and is projected to reach 1.34 million deaths by 2040.5 Together, these findings highlight the urgent need for effective, scalable interventions and policy responses to reduce the growing burden of overweight and obesity.

Lifestyle modification remains the cornerstone of weight management; however, substantial evidence suggests that clinically meaningful and durable weight loss through behavioral interventions alone is difficult to achieve and sustain.6 Clinical trials have shown that weight-loss drugs (WLDs) can produce greater and more sustained weight reductions than lifestyle interventions alone.7 Accordingly, pharmacotherapy has been incorporated into clinical guidelines for obesity management in several countries.8–11 For example, US guidelines recommend long-term pharmacotherapy for adults with overweight or obesity who do not achieve adequate weight loss with lifestyle modification,9 whereas the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence recommends considering pharmacological treatment for individuals who fail to achieve or maintain weight-loss goals despite behavioral interventions.10 In China, five anti-obesity medications—namely orlistat, liraglutide, beinaglutide, semaglutide, and tirzepatide—have been approved by the National Medical Products Administration for the treatment of adult obesity.12 These medications are prescription-only and should be used under the supervision of qualified healthcare professionals. These agents have demonstrated significant weight-loss efficacy in randomized controlled trials.9,13–20 Nevertheless, concerns remain regarding adverse events,16,21–23 and weight regain after treatment discontinuation.24–26 These concerns have attracted considerable public attention and may substantially influence patients’ willingness to initiate and adhere to pharmacological treatment.

Previous studies have shown that patients’ perceptions of drug safety and efficacy are important determinants of their willingness to initiate WLDs.27–31 However, few studies have systematically examined how patient characteristics influence WLDs acceptance, and the range of factors considered remains limited. For example, higher treatment adherence has been observed among individuals with lower baseline body weight, greater adherence to exercise, fewer comorbidities, fewer concomitant medications, and no prior exposure to anti-obesity agents,32 with similar patterns reported in pharmacy-based analyses.33 By contrast, a Danish study found that adults with a BMI ≥35 kg/m2 and comorbidities were more willing to use WLDs than those with a BMI of 30–34.9 kg/m2 without comorbidities.34 Taken together, these findings suggest that demographic and clinical characteristics may influence WLDs acceptance; however, existing evidence is derived primarily from European and North American populations and has paid limited attention to psychosocial and behavioral determinants.

To date, there has been little evidence that have conducted a comprehensive exploration of the acceptance of WLDs by overweight and obese adults in the Chinese mainland and the related factors. To fill this gap, the primary aim of this study was to examine the acceptance of WLDs among overweight and obese adults in China. The specific objectives were to assess the current level of WLDs acceptance and to identify sociodemographic, clinical, psychological, and behavioral factors associated with such acceptance. The findings may provide evidence to optimize the clinical application of WLDs and inform the development of obesity-related public health and pharmaceutical policies in China.

Methods

Date Resource

This study used data from the 2021 Psychology and Behaviour Investigation of Chinese Residents (PBICR) database.35 The PBICR is a large-scale, multicenter, nationwide cross-sectional survey designed to collect individual-level data from the general population, key subgroups, and individuals with specific health conditions in China. The 2021 wave was conducted from July to September across 23 provinces, 5 autonomous regions, and 4 municipalities in mainland China (excluding Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, Macao Special Administrative Region, and Taiwan Province). A multistage sampling strategy was adopted. Within each province-level administrative unit, two to six non-capital prefecture-level cities were randomly selected, resulting in 120 cities in total. Quota sampling based on sex, age, and urban–rural distribution was then applied, with quotas aligned with the Seventh National Population Census, to improve sample representativeness. A total of 11031 valid questionnaires were collected.

Eligible respondents were required to meet the following criteria: (1) aged ≥18 years; (2) Chinese nationality and permanent residence in China, with annual residence abroad not exceeding one month; (3) ability to complete the online questionnaire independently or with assistance; and (4) adequate comprehension of the survey items. Respondents were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) delirium or abnormal mental status; (2) documented cognitive impairment in community health service records; (3) participation in similar research projects or previous PBICR surveys; and (4) refusal to participate in the survey. The study was approved by the Shaanxi Health Culture Research Center (JKWH-2021-01) and Jinan University (JNUKY-2021-018). All participants were fully informed about the purpose of the study, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

In this study, body mass index (BMI, kg/m2) was calculated as body weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. According to the 2024 Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Obesity issued by the National Health Commission of China,12,36 participants with a BMI of 24.0–27.9 kg/m2 were classified as having overweight, and those with a BMI ≥28.0 kg/m2 were classified as having obesity. Respondents were included in the final analysis if they were aged ≥18 years, had complete and valid questionnaire data, and had no logical inconsistencies in their responses. The final analytic sample included 2577 respondents. A total of 8454 respondents who did not meet the eligibility criteria or data-quality requirements were excluded.

Research Instruments

In the PBICR survey, respondents were asked to rate their “degree of acceptance of weight-loss drugs”. Responses were recorded on a scale ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating greater acceptance of WLDs. Factors potentially associated with WLDs acceptance are complex and multidimensional. In this study, potential correlates were grouped into three domains: (1) sociodemographic characteristics, including sex, residential region, age, monthly household income per capita, marital status, occupation, and educational level; (2) medical and health-related characteristics, including type of health insurance, number of chronic diseases, number of medications used, smoking status, alcohol consumption, BMI, and weight status; and (3) psychosocial, behavioral, and health-related measures, including perceived social support, depression, anxiety, self-efficacy, family health, health-related quality of life, and eating behavior. Sociodemographic and medical/health-related characteristics were assessed using individual questionnaire items, whereas psychosocial, behavioral, and health-related variables were assessed using validated or commonly used scales, with each scale corresponding to a specific construct. Statistical Analysis

All analyses were performed using Stata SE 15.1. Descriptive statistics summarized participant characteristics. Group differences were examined using one-way analysis of variance and Pearson correlation tests. Variables significant at P<0.05 in univariate analyses were entered into stepwise multiple linear regression models, with inclusion and removal thresholds of P=0.05 and P=0.10, respectively. Subgroup analyses were conducted stratified by weight status (overweight vs obesity) and chronic disease burden. Two-tailed tests were applied throughout, and statistical significance was defined as P<0.05.

Results

Demographic Characteristics of the Respondents

A total of 2577 respondents were included in the final analysis. The geographic distribution of willingness to use WLDs across province-level administrative regions in mainland China is shown in Figure 1. Of these respondents, 72.88% resided in urban areas and 27.12% in rural areas. Regarding monthly household income per capita, 27.78% reported ≤3000 yuan, 40.78% reported 3001–6000 yuan, and 31.43% reported >6000 yuan. The sample comprised 58.48% men and 41.52% women. In terms of age, 37.95% were aged 19–40 years, 49.79% were aged 41–59 years, and 12.26% were aged ≥60 years. Regarding educational attainment, 47.31% had completed senior high school or below, 48.31% had a college or undergraduate education, and 4.38% had a master’s or doctoral degree. Most participants were married (75.67%), whereas 19.75% were unmarried and 4.58% were divorced or widowed. Regarding occupation, 12.65% were students, 15.33% were employed in government or public institutions, 11.99% were professional or technical personnel, 12.11% were production or service workers, 21.58% were other employees or retirees, and 26.35% reported no fixed occupation.

Regarding health insurance, 43.97% were covered by the basic medical insurance scheme for urban and rural residents, 36.67% by the basic medical insurance scheme for urban employees, and 19.36% by other insurance schemes or were uninsured. Most respondents reported no chronic diseases (70.66%), whereas 18.35% reported one chronic disease and 10.97% reported two or more chronic diseases. Regarding lifestyle factors, 68.99% had never smoked and 31.01% had a history of smoking; 48.74% had never consumed alcohol and 51.26% reported a history of alcohol consumption.

Regarding current medication use, 73.61% were not taking any medication, 11.49% were taking one medication, and 8.27% were taking two or more medications. In terms of perceived social support, 52.19% reported medium-to-low support and 47.81% reported high support. Regarding psychological factors, 46.41% of respondents had minimal depressive symptoms, 34.81% had mild depressive symptoms, and 18.78% had moderate-to-severe depressive symptoms. Anxiety symptoms were minimal in 56.97% of respondents, mild in 30.85%, and moderate-to-severe in 12.18%.

Univariate analyses showed that monthly household income per capita, age, educational level, marital status, occupation, type of health insurance, number of chronic diseases, number of medications currently used, smoking status, weight status, perceived social support, depressive symptoms, anxiety symptoms, family health, and eating behavior were significantly associated with WLDs acceptance (Tables 2 and 3). In the stepwise multiple linear regression model, β coefficients represented adjusted differences in WLDs acceptance scores. For categorical variables, β values indicated the mean difference in acceptance score compared with the reference group, whereas for continuous variables, β values indicated the expected change in acceptance score for each one-point increase in the corresponding scale score. Overall, several sociodemographic, health-related, psychosocial, and behavioral factors remained significantly associated with WLDs acceptance, as indicated by 95% confidence intervals that did not include zero.

Compared with respondents whose monthly household income per capita was ≤3000 yuan, those with incomes of 3001–6000 yuan had WLDs acceptance scores that were 6.34 points higher (β = 6.34, 95% CI: 3.28–9.40), and those with incomes >6000 yuan had scores that were 4.98 points higher (β = 4.98, 95% CI: 1.68–8.28). Respondents aged ≥60 years had significantly lower acceptance scores than those aged 19–40 years, with an adjusted difference of −7.97 points (β = −7.97, 95% CI: −12.07 to −3.87). Occupational differences were also observed. Compared with students, respondents employed in government or public institutions had acceptance scores that were 5.10 points higher (β = 5.10, 95% CI: 1.58–8.62), and those without a fixed occupation had scores that were 3.51 points higher (β = 3.51, 95% CI: 0.56–6.46). Health-related variables also showed significant associations with WLDs acceptance. Compared with respondents without chronic diseases, those with one chronic disease had acceptance scores that were 4.62 points lower (β = −4.62, 95% CI: −8.11 to −1.13), and those with two or more chronic diseases had scores that were 5.60 points lower (β = −5.60, 95% CI: −9.79 to −1.42). Respondents with a history of smoking also had lower acceptance scores than those who had never smoked (β = −3.12, 95% CI: −5.77 to −0.46). By contrast, respondents with obesity had higher acceptance scores than those with overweight (β = 3.50, 95% CI: 0.16–6.84). Psychosocial and behavioral factors were also independently associated with WLDs acceptance. Respondents with high perceived social support had acceptance scores that were 5.69 points higher than those with medium-to-low perceived social support (β = 5.69, 95% CI: 2.85–8.53). Compared with respondents with minimal anxiety symptoms, those with mild anxiety symptoms had higher acceptance scores (β = 4.17, 95% CI: 1.35–6.99), and those with moderate-to-severe anxiety symptoms showed an even larger increase in acceptance scores (β = 13.53, 95% CI: 9.37–17.70). For continuous scale variables, each one-point increase in the family health score was associated with a 0.99-point decrease in WLDs acceptance score (β = −0.99, 95% CI: −1.22 to −0.76), whereas each one-point increase in the eating behavior score was associated with a 0.76-point increase in WLDs acceptance score (β = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.46–1.06).

Occupational differences were also observed: respondents employed in government or public institutions had higher acceptance scores than students, whereas this association was not statistically significant in the other chronic disease subgroups. Psychosocial and behavioral factors showed subgroup-specific associations. Higher perceived social support was associated with higher WLDs acceptance scores only among respondents without chronic diseases. Anxiety symptoms were positively associated with WLDs acceptance among respondents without chronic diseases, and moderate-to-severe anxiety symptoms were associated with higher acceptance scores among those with one chronic disease. However, anxiety symptoms were not significantly associated with WLDs acceptance among respondents with two or more chronic diseases. Finally, more unhealthy eating behavior was associated with higher WLDs acceptance scores in the no chronic disease and one chronic disease subgroups, whereas this association was not statistically significant among respondents with two or more chronic diseases.

Discussion

The current pioneering study examined the acceptance of WLDs among adults with overweight and obesity in mainland China. The mean WLDs acceptance score was 38.27, indicating a relatively low willingness to use pharmacological weight-loss treatment in this population. This limited acceptability may be partly attributable to the relatively recent introduction of these medications into clinical practice in mainland China, together with persistent concerns about safety, adverse events, and weight regain after treatment discontinuation. Our analysis identified multiple sociodemographic, clinical, psychosocial, and behavioral factors associated with WLDs acceptance. These findings provide new evidence that attitudes toward pharmacological weight management may vary according to socioeconomic status, health status, and psychological characteristics. Importantly, these results highlight the need for patient-centered obesity pharmacotherapy strategies that account for individual socioeconomic, clinical, psychological, and behavioral profiles, while also supporting medication adherence and the integration of WLDs into primary healthcare services.

In the overall regression model, monthly household income per capita was significantly associated with WLDs acceptance. Respondents with higher income levels had greater willingness to use WLDs than those with lower income levels. This socioeconomic gradient may reflect both greater financial capacity to access pharmacological weight-loss treatment and higher health awareness among more affluent populations. These findings are consistent with previous research conducted in the United States.37 Age was also significantly associated with WLDs acceptance. Older adults, particularly those aged ≥60 years, reported lower willingness to use WLDs than younger respondents. This lower acceptance may be related to two interrelated factors: a more conservative attitude toward pharmacological interventions and greater concern about potential adverse drug effects. These findings are broadly consistent with previous studies,38 although they differ from one US study in which older patients showed higher acceptance.39 This discrepancy may be partly explained by differences in study populations. The US cohort largely comprised patients with clinically diagnosed obesity and higher baseline BMI, whereas the present study included a substantial proportion of individuals with overweight, among whom formal medical diagnosis and structured obesity management remain relatively uncommon in China.

Marked occupational differences were also observed. Respondents employed in government or public institutions had higher WLDs acceptance, which may reflect more stable employment, better access to healthcare resources, and greater occupational concerns related to appearance, social image, or stress management. Respondents without a fixed occupation, such as freelancers or entrepreneurs, also reported higher acceptance. Their flexible work patterns may allow greater attention to health management and greater openness to diverse treatment options, including WLDs. By contrast, respondents with chronic diseases reported lower WLDs acceptance. These individuals may prioritize the management of existing illnesses and may experience financial or treatment burden from ongoing care, thereby reducing their willingness to consider additional pharmacotherapy. This pattern is consistent with previous studies linking comorbidity burden to lower uptake of weight-loss medications.40,41 Similarly, a history of smoking was associated with lower WLDs acceptance. Individuals with current or former smoking experience may have psychosocial profiles that make them more inclined toward behavioral rather than pharmacological strategies, possibly because of concerns about weight gain after smoking cessation or skepticism toward medical interventions.42,43 WLDs acceptance also varied by weight status. Respondents with obesity were more receptive to WLDs than those with overweight, suggesting that higher perceived disease burden and greater clinical urgency may increase openness to pharmacological weight-loss treatment.44 Several psychosocial and behavioral factors were also associated with WLDs acceptance. Higher perceived social support was positively associated with acceptance, which is consistent with evidence that strong social networks can facilitate engagement with and adherence to weight-management strategies.45,46 Anxiety symptoms were also positively associated with WLDs acceptance: respondents with mild-to-severe anxiety symptoms showed greater willingness to use WLDs, possibly reflecting expectations that weight reduction may improve psychosocial well-being. Conversely, better family health was negatively associated with WLDs acceptance. Participants from healthier family environments may prefer non-pharmacological weight-management approaches and may perceive less need for medication-based interventions. Finally, respondents reporting more unhealthy eating behaviors were more willing to accept WLDs. This finding may reflect a tendency to view pharmacological treatment as a more feasible or convenient approach to weight loss, particularly among individuals who experience difficulty modifying dietary behaviors. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies linking disordered or unhealthy eating patterns to a preference for medication-based weight-management strategies.47,48

Subgroup analyses stratified by weight status revealed important heterogeneity in the factors associated with WLDs acceptance, suggesting that adults with overweight and those with obesity may have distinct patterns of health-related decision-making. These findings highlight the need to tailor obesity pharmacotherapy strategies according to weight status, socioeconomic conditions, and psychological characteristics. Across both the overweight and obesity subgroups, respondents with a monthly household income per capita of 3001–6000 yuan had higher WLDs acceptance than those with an income ≤3000 yuan. In the obesity subgroup, respondents with a monthly household income per capita >6000 yuan showed even higher acceptance. This finding suggests that, among individuals with more severe weight-related health concerns, greater financial capacity and better access to healthcare resources may further increase willingness to consider pharmacological treatment. Economic resources may therefore facilitate not only access to WLDs but also readiness to act on perceived health needs. Several associations were significant only in one weight-status subgroup, indicating that the factors related to WLDs acceptance may differ according to the severity of weight-related health risks. In the overweight subgroup, unmarried respondents were less receptive to WLDs than married respondents, whereas this association was not observed in the obesity subgroup. This pattern may reflect the role of spousal support and external encouragement in promoting weight management when weight-related health risks are relatively less severe. Among individuals with obesity, however, greater perceived health risks and the visibility of obesity may reduce differences related to marital status. In the overweight subgroup, respondents with chronic diseases had lower WLDs acceptance, possibly because they prioritized the management of existing illnesses or were more cautious about adding another medication. This association was not observed among respondents with obesity, for whom the greater health risks associated with obesity may increase the perceived necessity of weight management. Similarly, overweight respondents who were currently taking one medication were more receptive to WLDs, suggesting that prior medication experience may reduce hesitation toward pharmacological treatment. This association was not significant in the obesity subgroup, where concerns about treatment burden or more urgent health issues may outweigh the influence of previous medication use. A history of smoking was associated with lower WLDs acceptance only in the overweight subgroup. This may reflect the clustering of health-risk behaviors and lower motivation for weight management among smokers or former smokers. In the obesity subgroup, the greater perceived health risks associated with obesity may overshadow the influence of smoking history on treatment attitudes.49 Thus, the association between smoking and WLDs acceptance may be more apparent among individuals with overweight than among those with obesity. Higher PSSS and EBS-SF scores were associated with greater WLDs acceptance in the overweight subgroup, suggesting that perceived social support and awareness of unhealthy eating behaviors may encourage more proactive attitudes toward weight management at earlier stages of weight-related health risk. In contrast, these associations were not evident among respondents with obesity, among whom perceived clinical urgency or reliance on medical advice may reduce the relative influence of psychosocial support and eating behavior. This finding highlights the importance of social context and dietary behavior in shaping acceptance of pharmacological weight management, particularly among individuals with overweight.50–52 In the obesity subgroup, respondents without a fixed occupation had higher WLDs acceptance than students, whereas this difference was not observed in the overweight subgroup. One possible explanation is that individuals with obesity who are engaged in non-fixed occupations may experience stronger social or health-related pressures because of the greater visibility and severity of obesity, making them more motivated to seek pharmacological assistance. Students, by contrast, may perceive greater time and flexibility to manage weight through lifestyle modification and therefore show relatively lower reliance on medication. Among individuals with overweight, where health risks may be perceived as less urgent, occupational differences may be less strongly associated with WLDs acceptance.

This study found that chronic disease burden was significantly associated with WLDs acceptance, with distinct patterns observed among respondents without chronic diseases, those with one chronic disease, and those with two or more chronic diseases. These findings suggest a complex interplay among socioeconomic, demographic, psychological, and behavioral factors in shaping attitudes toward pharmacological weight management. Among respondents without chronic diseases, monthly household income per capita and age were prominent factors associated with WLDs acceptance. Higher income was associated with greater acceptance, which may reflect reduced financial barriers to pharmacological treatment and greater willingness to invest in preventive health behaviors. Younger respondents aged 19–40 years also showed higher acceptance, possibly because they were more receptive to novel health interventions and placed greater emphasis on weight control or body image. Conversely, older adults without chronic diseases may perceive less urgency for pharmacological weight management or hold more conservative attitudes toward medication use. However, among respondents with two or more chronic diseases, this age-related pattern was reversed, with older adults showing higher WLDs acceptance. One possible explanation is that, in individuals with multimorbidity, advancing age may increase perceived health risks and strengthen motivation to consider pharmacological strategies despite general caution toward new treatments. This finding suggests that chronic disease burden may modify age-related attitudes toward WLDs use. Educational attainment also showed subgroup-specific associations. Although educational level was not significantly associated with WLDs acceptance among respondents without chronic diseases or those with one chronic disease, higher educational attainment was associated with greater acceptance among respondents with two or more chronic diseases. This may be because individuals with higher education are better able to access, understand, and evaluate medical information, thereby perceiving WLDs as a legitimate and potentially beneficial treatment option despite the complexity of managing multiple chronic conditions. Marital status further differentiated the chronic disease subgroups. Among respondents with one chronic disease, unmarried individuals had lower WLDs acceptance than married individuals, possibly reflecting weaker social support or less external encouragement to pursue health interventions. This association was not observed in the no chronic disease or multiple chronic diseases subgroups, suggesting that the role of marital support may be less salient when disease burden is absent or, conversely, when clinical concerns become more prominent. Occupational differences were significant only among respondents with one chronic disease. In this subgroup, respondents employed in government or public institutions had higher WLDs acceptance than students. The stability, health literacy, and institutional trust associated with these occupations may contribute to greater confidence in medical interventions. The absence of this association in the other subgroups may indicate that occupational factors are less influential when perceived health risk is either relatively low, as in respondents without chronic diseases, or dominated by complex clinical concerns, as in those with multiple chronic diseases. Among respondents with two or more chronic diseases, those covered by basic medical insurance for urban employees reported lower WLDs acceptance than those covered by basic medical insurance for urban and rural residents, despite the former generally offering higher reimbursement levels. This finding may reflect differences in perceived affordability, reimbursement procedures, and expectations regarding out-of-pocket payments. Individuals covered by basic medical insurance for urban employees are generally employed and contribute a larger share of premiums, which may be accompanied by higher expectations for reimbursement and greater sensitivity to uncovered expenses. In contrast, individuals covered by basic medical insurance for urban and rural residents may have lower expectations regarding reimbursement and may be more accustomed to partial self-payment for medications. This discrepancy suggests that reimbursement level alone may not determine treatment acceptance; perceived affordability, administrative barriers, and patient expectations regarding insurance coverage may also play important roles. These findings highlight the need for more transparent and patient-centered insurance policies to improve equitable access to weight-loss pharmacotherapy.53,54 Current medication use was positively associated with WLDs acceptance among respondents with two or more chronic diseases. This may indicate that familiarity with pharmacological management reduces hesitation toward additional medication use, particularly when multimorbidity increases the perceived need for comprehensive treatment. The observed pattern may suggest a threshold-like relationship between multimorbidity and pharmacotherapy acceptance. At lower levels of disease burden, patients may remain cautious about pharmacological treatment because of concerns about polypharmacy, side effects, or drug interactions. However, as multimorbidity becomes more complex, this caution may give way to more pragmatic acceptance of pharmacotherapy, driven by the need to manage multiple health conditions effectively.55 Psychological and behavioral factors also showed heterogeneous associations across chronic disease subgroups. Higher perceived social support was associated with greater WLDs acceptance only among respondents without chronic diseases, suggesting that psychosocial encouragement may be particularly important when there is no pressing clinical need for weight-loss treatment. Anxiety symptoms were positively associated with WLDs acceptance among respondents without chronic diseases and showed a partial association among those with one chronic disease, whereas no significant association was observed among respondents with two or more chronic diseases. This pattern may indicate that, as disease burden increases, clinical considerations become more dominant in treatment decision-making, reducing the relative influence of psychological distress. Family health status was negatively associated with WLDs acceptance among respondents without chronic diseases and those with two or more chronic diseases, but not among those with one chronic disease. This may reflect differences in how family health context is weighed against personal disease experience. Individuals from healthier family environments may prefer lifestyle-based approaches or perceive less need for medication-based weight management. Finally, unhealthy eating behaviors were associated with greater WLDs acceptance in the no chronic disease and single chronic disease subgroups, but not among respondents with two or more chronic diseases. This finding may suggest that, when disease burden is lower, individuals who recognize unhealthy eating patterns may be more willing to consider WLDs as a feasible weight-management option, whereas among those with multiple chronic diseases, treatment complexity and existing illness burden may overshadow lifestyle-related concerns.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference, and the observed associations should not be interpreted as causal relationships. Longitudinal or interventional studies are needed to further validate these findings. Second, although this study examined a broad range of sociodemographic, clinical, psychosocial, and behavioral variables, treatment decision-making is multifactorial, and unmeasured factors—such as cultural attitudes, physician recommendations, medication cost, and drug accessibility—may also influence WLDs acceptance. Future studies incorporating these dimensions could provide a more comprehensive understanding of WLD acceptance. Third, at the time of data collection, orlistat was the only pharmacological treatment formally approved for weight management in mainland China. Therefore, respondents’ perceptions may have been shaped by limited exposure to diverse pharmacotherapy options. With the subsequent introduction of newer agents, such as GLP-1 receptor agonists, future research should reassess public acceptance to capture evolving attitudes and preferences. Moreover, participants were identified based on BMI criteria available in the database. Although BMI is widely used in epidemiological studies, it may not fully reflect body composition, fat distribution, or obesity-related health risks. Therefore, some degree of misclassification may have occurred.

Conclusion

This study found a generally low level of WLDs acceptance among adults with overweight or obesity in mainland China, indicating substantial room for improvement in the uptake of obesity pharmacotherapy. Multiple sociodemographic, clinical, psychosocial, and behavioral factors—including income, age, occupation, chronic disease burden, medication use, smoking status, weight status, perceived social support, anxiety symptoms, family health, and eating behavior—were significantly associated with WLDs acceptance. Subgroup analyses further revealed considerable heterogeneity in these associations by weight status and chronic disease burden. These findings highlight the need for patient-centered strategies that consider individual socioeconomic background, health status, and psychosocial characteristics when promoting the appropriate use of pharmacological treatment for obesity. These findings have important implications for the development of patient-centered obesity management strategies and public health policies aimed at promoting evidence-based weight management. Understanding factors associated with the acceptance of weight loss medications may help healthcare providers identify potential barriers to treatment uptake and improve communication regarding available therapeutic options. Future studies should further explore causal relationships using longitudinal designs and evaluate how evolving public perceptions, healthcare accessibility, and emerging anti-obesity medications influence treatment acceptance in diverse populations.

Study on stability evaluation of goaf based on AHP and EWM—taking the northern new district of Liaoyuan city as an example
Authors: Yichen Zhang, Zhou Wen, Jiquan Zhang, Jintao Huang, Jiawei Qi, Menghao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThroughout the history of coal mining in all countries of the world, large areas of goaf have been left behind, and sudden collapses and surface subsidence of large areas of goaf may occur, especially for mining areas with long mining cycles. The northern new district of the Liaoyuan mining area has been subjected to nearly half a century of mining activities, accompanied by a gradual accumulation of disasters, which have occurred frequently in recent years. In order to assess the stability of the goaf in the study area, this paper proposes a hybrid decision-making multi-factor integrated evaluation method. The distribution of underground goafs was determined using geophysical exploration techniques (seismic survey and transient electromagnetic method) and geological drilling exploration. First, an evaluation index system was established based on the specifications of the goaf, the ecological and geological environment, and the mining conditions; the system included 14 indicators. Two weight calculation methods, AHP-EWM, were employed to determine the comprehensive weight of each indicator by combining subjective and objective weights on the basis of improved game theory. Subsequently, the fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method was utilised to complete the stability rating of each block in the study area, and MapGIS and ArcGIS were employed to complete the drawing of the stability zoning map of the northern new district goaf. The study area was divided into three zones of stability, basic stability and instability, according to the critical value. These zones accounted for 23.03%, 36.45% and 40.52% of the total area of the study area, respectively. The comprehensive on-site investigation revealed a decrease in the size and number of collapse pits and the rate of damage to the houses from the unstable zone to the stable zone. This indicates that the results of the division are consistent with the actual situation. The classification results are consistent with the actual ground disaster situation, thus verifying the rationality and validity of the evaluation method. The results indicate that the stability of the study area is generally at the lower middle level.Keywords: Goaf stability evaluation, AHP, EWM, Game theory, MapGIS, ArcGISSubject terms: Natural hazards, Coal, Civil engineering

Introduction

Underground coal mining is the primary cause of deformation in the overlying rock layer and surface movement in mining areas. Ground subsidence has become increasingly severe over time1,2. Such conditions can lead to collapse pits, ground cracks, and house collapse accidents. These hazards are not only a cause of property losses but also pose a serious threat to the safety of residents. It is crucial to note that these hazards can be life-threatening3–6. In addition, the depletion of underground resources makes the ecological environment on the surface highly vulnerable to floods or surface water caused by the exploitation of the underground7. Urgent attention and study are required to address the significant concern of secondary disasters caused by underground coal mining.

Land subsidence is a persistent and gradual phenomenon, and its development process can be roughly divided into two stages: active settlement and residual settlement8,9. In the first stage, the formation displacement and deformation mainly occur during the coal seam mining process, and this displacement and deformation are instantaneous. The second phase occurs after the coal has been extracted, but unlike the first, it takes place over an extended period. Especially for the mining areas engaged in mining activities for a long time (such as the northern New District mining area in this study), the latter causes more serious harm10.

The mining industry has grown significantly in countries such as South Africa, Australia, Canada, Poland, and China. Australia and Canada have a vast territory, a relatively small population, and a relatively sparse underground goaf distribution, so open-pit mining is feasible. However, compared with these countries, China has formed a large number of underground goaf in the mining process11–13, especially in the underground mines using open-pit mining. Since the 1980s, illegal mining has gradually emerged in China, leading to the existence of a large number of unknown underground goaf around the mines14,15. Due to their irregular geometric shape, complex spatial distribution, and strong concealment, these underground goaf areas bring extremely serious challenges to the safety and feasibility of efficient and sustainable exploitation.

In recent years, underground goaf collapse accidents have occurred frequently in China, resulting in serious economic losses, casualties, and ecological damage16. In particular, a large number of underground goaf caused by large-scale mining, China has more than 9,000 state-owned coal mines, with an annual output of 9 billion tons, underground goaf reserves of billions of cubic meters, the damage to China's vegetation, water resources and land, as well as the potential impact on the environment and public health, cannot be ignored.

In the academic field, the assessment of risk associated with underground goafs is a complex process that requires comprehensive consideration of multiple influencing factors. Currently, most studies tend to rely on a single indicator to assess the risk level of underground goafs, with the size of the goaf often used as the main risk indicator17. However, this approach has limitations in that it fails to adequately reflect the diversity and complexity of risk factors.

Although studies have attempted to use multiple indicators for risk assessment, these methods have been relatively rarely applied in practice. Existing multi-indicator evaluation methods tend to rely on safety checklists for qualitative assessment, which may lead to inaccuracies and biases being introduced as the results are significantly influenced by subjective judgements18.

In recent years, academics have developed a series of comprehensive evaluation methods to overcome this limitation, including grey fixed-weight clustering methods, grey relational analysis methods, and neural network methods19–21. These methods attempt to improve the objectivity and accuracy of assessment through quantitative analysis. However, the difficulty in risk assessment of underground goaf lies in the uncertainty and hidden nature of the influencing factors. These factors may include the variability of geological conditions, the complexity of underground hydrological conditions, and the influence of human activities. Therefore, an evaluation method that integrates all relevant factors is required in order to ensure the comprehensiveness and reliability of the assessment results29–31.

In light of the aforementioned considerations, it is of paramount importance to devise a comprehensive evaluation methodology that incorporates both subjective and objective elements. The method should be capable of integrating qualitative analysis and quantitative data in order to reveal the multifaceted characteristics of the risk associated with underground goaf. The application of this method enables the identification and quantification of risks with greater accuracy, thereby providing a scientific foundation for the safe utilisation and management of underground space.

The research adopts field surveys, geophysical exploration, drilling exploration, and UAV mapping. First, the field investigation is conducted on the ground disasters (ground subsidence, subsidence pit, ground fissure, and house crack); then, the distribution characteristics (quantity, buried depth, and higher burial) are studied by the geophysical method (seismic exploration and transient electromagnetic method); then, the images of the evaluation area. This study confidently evaluates and classifies ground stability in the survey area using AHP-EWM, which combines game theory and fuzzy comprehensive evaluation. The evaluation is based on the distribution characteristics of underground mining disasters. This study is beneficial to the treatment of ground subsidence in the Northern New District and also provides a useful reference for other similar mining areas.

Materials and methods

Study region generalization

The Northern New Area is located in the northwest of LiaoYuan City, Jilin Province, under the jurisdiction of Xi'an District of LiaoYuan City, with a total planned area of more than 20 square kilometers as shown in Fig. 1. The study area is a continental semi-humid monsoon climate in the northern cold temperate zone, with an annual average temperature of 5.2 °C, the lowest temperature of − 41.1 °C, and a maximum temperature of 36 °C. The average annual precipitation is 662.2 mm, and the rainy season is in June, July, August, and September, accounting for about 80% of the annual rainfall.

Study region location.

The overall terrain of the study area is high from north to south and low in the centre, with the highest elevation being 430.33 m in the north and the lowest elevation being 316.3 m in the south, the lowest 261.2 m in the middle, and the largest relative height difference of 169.13 m. Local terrain relief is relatively large. It can be divided into three geomorphic units: tectonic denudation low mountain area, erosion denudation hilly area, and erosion accumulation mountain valley area. The East Liaohe River flows from east to west from the southwest of the research area, and the East Liaohe River originates in Sakaling, Fuan Village, Liaoheyuan Town, Dongliao County. East Liaohe River tributary half river through the southeast corner of the evaluation area.

Data sources

The primary data for this study were derived from the collection of information pertaining to geology, mining, engineering geology, hydrogeology, land development and utilisation in the evaluation area. The field data were gathered through a combination of field surveys, geophysical exploration, drilling exploration, drone mapping and other methods. The evaluation area displays a double oblique structure, with the oblique axis distributed along its center. The southwest edge falls within an anticline structure, and the anticline axis is distributed along the northwest of the evaluation area. There are 15 large faults developed on the oblique wings, most of which extend northwest and are parallel to the oblique axis. The main oblique wings' faults mostly point to the oblique axis, forming the graben combination. Small faults in the area mainly develop oblique intersections with the coal seam, and most are truncated by large faults. Together, these oblique and fault structures constitute the primary structural framework in the area.

Results and discussion

The raw data extracted from the survey report should be processed using MapGIS, after which it should be imported into ArcGIS. Once imported, the data should be transformed and combined with the calculated composite scores of each block. The geographically-weighted regression tool in ArcGIS should then be used, with the least squares method being the main method employed. The neighbourhood type should be set using a user-defined option to set specific neighbourhood distances. Finally, the Gaussian weighting scheme should be used in the local weighting scheme, and the results should be generated. A cloud map of the stability distribution of the study area can then be obtained. It is important to note that the reference coordinate system must be adjusted in MapGIS prior to the importation of data into ArcGIS28.

Figure 7 shows the correlation between parameters using hot map for Pearson correlation analysis. Output results indicate that area is positively correlated with span, r = 0.79 is regarded as moderate correlation (0.5≤|r|<0.8) which may due to area is proportional to span for the same mining thickness.The distance from the fault is negatively correlated with the depth, with a high correlation coefficient (0.8≤|r|). The correlation coefficient r is − 0.94, which may be due to the influence of the characteristics of the coal seams in the evaluation area, the complex geological structure of the mines, and the presence of numerous faults and igneous intrusions. The structure of the mines is characterised by the presence of numerous faults and igneous intrusions, which have the effect of undermining the continuity of the coal seams. As a consequence, as the distance from the fault increases, the depth of the coal seam that can be mined becomes deeper.The influence of groundwater and coal seam roof is positively correlated, with a correlation coefficient of r=0.76, which is classified as a medium correlation (0.5≤|r|≤0.8). The coal seam roof is susceptible to the effects of groundwater, and if not addressed in a timely manner, it is prone to sudden water accidents.

Coal mining will cause uneven deformation of the ground, thus inducing the occurrence of ground cracks and building cracks. It should be noted that ground collapse pits and building cracks are not included as evaluation factors in ground stability evaluation. Consequently, ground collapse pits and building cracks were employed as validation metrics for distributed ground stability cloud maps.

Mining activities do cause surface deformation and residential damage, as confirmed by a large number of ground subsidence pits and building cracks. Through understanding the local leaders and going to the field to investigate each ground collapse pit, the data on each collapse pit was obtained.

During the field survey, a total of 73 collapse pits were found in the study area (The actual investigation of the collapse pit is shown in Fig. 11a and b), of which 61 were in unstable areas, 12 were in basically stable areas and no obvious collapse pits were found in the stable area. In addition, the depth and area of the collapse pit varies greatly across the study area. In the unstable area, the depth and area of the ground collapse pit are 3.1–10 m and 0.1–0.33 km2, respectively; in the basically stable area, the depth and area of the ground collapse pit are distributed at 2.5–5 m and 0.06–0.24 km2, respectively. The number and size of the ground collapse pits exhibit a decreasing trend from the unstable areas to the basically stable areas.

Investigate the damage to subsidence pits and residential buildings in the area.

A random survey of the houses in the study area was also conducted to further reflect the house damage (The actual picture of the actual surveyed house cracks is shown in Fig. 11c and d). According to the actual situation of the site, 89 households, 74 households and 59 residential buildings were surveyed in the unstable zone, the basic stable zone and the stable zone (the zones were divided according to Fig. 8) as the survey samples, and among these samples, the amount of house damages in the three zones were 71, 12 and 0 respectively, and the house damage rates in the unstable zone, the basic stable zone and the stable zone were 79.78%, 16.22% and 0% (the proportion of collapsed pits and the house damage rate are shown in Fig. 12a).It can be seen that the unstable zone has the highest house damage rate while the stable zone has no house damage.

Whereas, according to the area divided in Fig. 9, the damage rate of houses in unstable, basic stable and stable areas are 59.26%, 34.31%, 24.23%, and the proportion of collapsed pits are 42.47%, 54.79%, and 2.74%, respectively (as shown in Fig. 12b); and, according to the area divided in Fig. 10, the damage rate of houses in unstable, basic stable, and stable areas are 66.32%, 28.57%, and 2.02%, and the proportion of collapse pits is 76.71%, 17.81%, and 5.48% (as shown in Fig. 12c), the latter two methods showed the presence of collapse pits in the stability zones as well as cracks in the houses, and the comparison of their stability zoning with the results of the actual survey of collapse pits and house damage found to be in a moderate level of conformity.

The comparison found that the stability zoning using the AHP-EWM method is more realistic, which indicates that the results of the ground stability assessment are scientific and reasonable, and are of great value in solving the ground subsidence disaster caused by underground mining.

Conclusions

This study evaluates the ground stability of the coal field in the northern new area of Xi'an District. Based on the combination of expert opinions and site investigation data, the game theory combination weighting method was used to calculate the coal field area, span, height, depth, average annual rainfall, coal beds, distance from faults, folded structures, groundwater influence, neighbouring goaf situation, final mining time, coal bed inclination, coal mining method and coal bed roof rock layer. Fourteen indicators of the combination of weights were established, and a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation model was constructed. The following conclusions were reached:

The ground stability evaluation index system of the goaf was constructed from three aspects: specifications of the goaf, ecological and geological environment, and mining conditions. Limitations of the study include scope constraints and the fact that some selected indicators did not have the expected impacts, such as average annual rainfall and groundwater impacts. During the fieldwork phase of this study, nearly 300 respondents were selected, 50 per cent of whom were clearly affected by water. A more pronounced common effect was observed, suggesting that the initial study's estimates of the effects of two factors, average annual rainfall and groundwater, may have been accurate. However, subsequent studies have shown that the effects of these two factors on flock stability were not significant. After analysing the reasons for this result, we identified the following causes and measures that led to this result: the extent of the study area limits the source of groundwater recharge in the region to infiltration recharge from atmospheric precipitation. River recharge could not be considered in the connection between the two. In the follow-up study, the selection of indicators could take into account changes in groundwater level or specific yield. This will help the follow-up study to be more scientific and reasonable. The study area is relatively limited in size and the number of samples is not large enough to obtain results with high precision. In the follow-up study, it is desirable to expand the study area to the whole Liaoyuan mining area to facilitate more accurate and scientific results.

Foot-and-mouth disease virus vaccine with VP1 G-H loop substitution of the Cathay strain broadens antigen spectrum
Authors: Jingjing Zha, Keqiang Zhang, Xingze Zhang, Pu Sun, Dong Li, Yimei Cao, Xingwen Bai, Xueqing Ma, Kun Li, Hong Yuan, Yuanfang Fu, Jing Zhang, Zhixun Zhao, Jian Wang, Huifang Bao, Yifan OuYang, Shuyun Qi, Zaixin Liu, Zengjun Lu, Pinghua Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecently, serotype O foot-and-mouth disease viruses (FMDVs) belonging to four lineages (O/ME-SA/PanAsia, O/ME-SA/Ind2001, O/SEA/Mya-98, and O/Cathay) co-circulate in China, and the emergence of new variant viruses belonging to the Cathay lineage renders the existing vaccines less effective, which poses a serious threat to the livestock industries. The surface-exposed G-H loop of the VP1 structural protein of FMDV plays an important role in inducing neutralizing antibodies, generation of antigenic variants, and virus attachment. Here, we generated three recombinant FMDVs in which the G-H loops have been substituted with the corresponding sequences from the circulating strains of the PanAsia, Mya-98, and Cathay lineages based on an infectious cDNA clone of a chimeric FMDV, which carries amino acid substitutions in the leader protein and all surface proteins of FMDV O/XJ/CHA/2017. All mutant viruses exhibited a significantly improved replication capacity in BHK-21 cells and displayed relatively larger plaque morphologies compared with the parental virus. Chemically inactivated vaccines prepared from the parental virus and the mutant viruses all induced protective liquid-phase blocking ELISA (LPBE) antibodies against FMDVs in pigs after 28 days post vaccination (dpv), but only the vaccine with the substitution of the G-H loop of the Cathay strain induced protective neutralizing antibodies against the viruses of four lineages, while other vaccines exhibited excellent immunological cross-reactivity to the viruses of Ind2001, PanAsia, and Mya-98 lineages but did not for the Cathay virus at 28 dpv. Our studies indicated that the G-H loop of the Cathay virus plays a critical role in the antigenic drift of FMDV, which will provide key insights for designing porcinophilic FMDV vaccines in the future.Key points • The swapping of the G-H loops of FMDVs notably improved replication capacity of FMDV in BHK-21 cells. • The swapping of the G-H loop of the Cathay virus obviously broadens antigenic coverage of FMDV vaccine. • The study showed that the G-H loop of the Cathay virus plays a critical role in antigenic drift of FMDV. Introduction

Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) is a highly contagious and acute viral disease affecting a variety of wild cloven-hoofed animals and the economically important livestock species. The disease is currently endemic in many parts of the world, especially in Asia, Africa, and a few parts of South America (Rweyemamu et al. 2008) and poses a severe threat to global livestock industries. Disease outbreaks often cause devastating economic consequences (Davies 2002; Dunn and Donaldson 1997; Knight-Jones and Rushton 2013). Regular vaccination with chemically inactivated vaccines prepared from antigenically related isolates is the most effective control measure to reduce disease outbreaks and virus transmission (Parida 2009). However, lack of cross protection between FMD virus (FMDV) serotypes and even between some subtypes affects the use of vaccines in the field (Parida 2009; Paton et al. 2005); further, the emergence or introduction of new antigenic variants makes the available vaccine ineffective, thus requiring the selection of new vaccine strains (Mahapatra et al. 2016; Mattion et al. 2004).

FMD is caused by FMDV, a member of the Aphthovirus genus within the Picornaviridae family. FMD virion consists of a single-stranded positive-sense RNA genome packaged in a nonenveloped, icosahedral capsid composed of 60 copies each of four structural proteins: VP1, VP2, VP3, and VP4. VP4 is located at the inner capsid surface of the virion, while VP1, VP2, and VP3 form the outer capsid surface and can tolerate some amino acid changes without obvious functional impairment. The surface-exposed capsid proteins contain many identified antigenic sites on type O FMDV (Crowther et al. 1993), which are essential for immunologic response to either infection or vaccination (Grubman and Baxt 2004).

Among the capsid proteins, VP1 contains a long, conformationally flexible G-H loop, which is a major antigenic epitope of FMDV for inducing production of neutralizing antibodies (Knowles and Samuel 2003; Mateu et al. 1995). A previous report showed that more than 25% of neutralizing antibodies are directly elicited by this epitope upon FMDV infection in susceptible animals (Borrego et al. 1995). Therefore, the VP1 G-H loop is an attractive antigenic target for development of peptide vaccines, epitope vaccines, and recombinant virus vaccines (Cañas-Arranz et al. 2020; Hwang et al. 2023; Rodriguez et al. 2003). Additionally, the VP1 G-H loop is genetically and antigenically hypervariable among all antigenic epitopes of FMDV (Carrillo et al. 2007); substitutions at critical residues within this loop can dramatically alter the specificity of antibody binding and lead to antigenic change, which may be responsible for some vaccine failures (Ludi et al. 2014; Mahapatra et al. 2016; Mateu et al. 1990). Aside from its immunodominance, the G-H loop contains a highly conserved RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) motif, which is essential for the virus to enter cells by binding to host integrin receptors (Burman et al. 2006; Jackson et al. 2004). Mutations in this loop also affect viral growth properties in cell culture (Lian et al. 2016; Rieder et al. 2005).

The use of traditional inactivated vaccines with broad antigenic coverage is pivotal for the successful implementation of the FMD control and eradication program. Previous studies have shown that some chimeric RNA virus vaccines with the substitution of immunodominant epitopes could induce improved cross-neutralizing ability and protect animals from challenge with homologous and heterologous viruses (Kim et al. 2017; Li et al. 2018; Liao et al. 2016). Likewise, vaccines prepared from type A FMDVs carrying the replacements of the G-H loop of serotype O or C virus induced neutralizing antibodies and protective immunity to multiple serotypes of FMDV (Rieder et al. 1994). Additionally, serotype O FMDV displaying a conserved neutralizing epitope (143–151 aa of VP1) of serotype Asia1 virus exhibited neutralizing activity to both serotype O and Asia1 polyclonal antibodies (Biswal et al. 2016). Therefore, it is a promising strategy for the design of new FMD vaccines with improving antigenic coverage by the substitution of the immunodominant epitope.

Serotype O FMDVs belonging to four lineages (O/ME-SA/PanAsia, O/ME-SA/Ind2001, O/SEA/Mya-98, and O/Cathay) are currently co-circulating in China, and the emergence of antigenic variants belonging to the Cathay lineage rendered the existing vaccines less effective (Upadhyaya et al. 2021; Li et al. 2022), which poses a serious threat to the pig industry. To develop an excellent FMDV vaccine strain with a broad antigenic spectrum and good growth characteristics, in this study, we engineered three recombinant FMDVs by swapping the VP1 G-H loops of the circulating PanAsia, Mya-98, and Cathay strains based on the genetic backbone of the chimeric FMDV carrying the substitution of leader protein and capsid protein of O/XJ/CHA/2017 field strain. We characterized the recombinant FMDVs and evaluated their ability to induce cross-neutralizing antibodies against type O FMDVs belonging to four lineages in pigs.

Materials and methods

Cell lines, plasmids, and viruses

Baby hamster kidney (BHK-21) cells (Uddowla et al. 2012) maintained in minimum essential medium (MEM) (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and 1% antibiotics (100 units/mL of penicillin, 20 µg/mL of streptomycin) were used for virus propagation, in vitro growth, plaque assays, and neutralization tests. BSR/T7 cells, a BHK-derived cell line stably expressing T7 RNA polymerase, were provided by K. K. Conzelmann (Buchholz et al. 1999) and propagated in Glasgow minimal essential medium (GMEM; Gibco, Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 10% FBS (Gibco, Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and 100 mg/mL Geneticin (G418; Gibco, Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) on every second passage. All cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 in a humidified atmosphere. A derivate of plasmid pOFS containing the full-length genome of FMDV O/HN/CHA/93 was used as the genetic backbone for constructing the recombinant full-length clones (Li et al. 2012). O/Tibet/CHA/99 (GenBank accession no. AJ539138) belongs to the PanAsia lineage of the ME-SA topotype; O/XJ/CHA/2017 (GenBank accession no. MF461724) belongs to the Ind-2001 lineage of the ME-SA topotype. O/HBCZ/2019, isolated from Cangzhou City of the Hebei province in 2019, is an FMDV of the Mya-98 lineage of the SEA topotype; O/18074, isolated from Zhengzhou City of the Henan province in 2018, is an FMDV of the Cathay topotype (Li et al. 2024).

Construction of the recombinant FMDV full-length cDNA clones

To engineer chimeric full-length plasmid of FMDV, the specific gene of O/HN/CHA/93 (nt 2924–4183) carrying the substitution of the sequences of the leader protein (L) and structural proteins (P1) of O/XJ/CHA/2017 was synthesized by a biotechnology company (GENEWIZ, Nanjing, China) and subsequently subcloned into pSK-Z123 (Li et al. 2012) via XbaI/NheI to generate the resulting plasmid pSK-Z123/XJLP1. The three recombinant plasmids pSK-Z123/XJLP1/TURG-H, pSK-Z123/XJLP1/HBG-H, and pSK-Z123/XJLP1/18G-H carrying substitutions of sequences of the G-H loop (131–160 amino acid of structural protein VP1) of O/TUR/5/2009 (O/PanAsia-2), O/HBCZ/2019, and O/18074 were generated by gene synthesis based on the plasmid of pSK-Z123/XJLP1. All resulting plasmids were digested with SpeI/BglII (TaKaRa, Dalian, China) respectively, and cloned into the corresponding region of pOFS to produce four resulting full-length plasmids. All constructs were sequenced to confirm the presence of expected modifications.

Transfection and recovery of the recombinant FMDVs

All full-length plasmids were linearized with NotI (Takara, Dalian, China) and purified with a DNA fragment purification kit (Takara, Dalian, China). The purified samples were transfected into BSR/T7 cells using lipofectamine 2000 (Gibco, Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The transfected cells were incubated for 48–72 h at 37 °C. Upon observation of the cytopathic effect (CPE), the cell culture supernatants were harvested and further verified by sequence analysis. Engineering FMDVs were serially passaged in BHK-21 cells. Virus stocks were stored at − 70 °C.

Immunofluorescence assay (IFA)

Seventy to 80% confluent BHK-21 cells grown in a 12-well plate were infected with the recombinant viruses and the parental virus. After 6–8 h infection, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min and permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 10 min. After that, the cells were immunostained with FMDV specific monoclonal antibody (MAb) targeting 3 A nonstructural protein and Texas red-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody. Nuclei were stained with DAPI (4′, 6-diamidino-2-phenylindole), and the images were visualized under a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica TCS SP8, Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany).

Plaque morphology and growth kinetics of the recombinant FMDVs

Plaque morphology of the recombinant FMDVs was evaluated in BHK-21 cells. Briefly, BHK-21 cells were seeded into a six-well plate with complete MEM. The next day, the cells were washed once with serum-free medium and infected with tenfold serial dilutions of viral stocks. After 1 h of adsorption at 37 °C, the cells were overlaid with 2 × MEM supplemented with 2% FBS and 0.6% gum tragacanth and incubated for 48 h. Cell monolayers were then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and plaques were visualized by 1% crystal violet staining. Plaques were counted, and the virus titers were expressed as plaque-forming units (PFU)/mL.

The growth kinetics of the mutant viruses and the parental virus were examined by infecting BHK-21 cells with viruses at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 5. Infected samples were collected at 4, 8, 12, and 16 h post-infection (hpi) and were titrated by plaque assays as described above. Virus titers were expressed as log10 PFU/mL. The experiment was performed in duplicate.

Genetic stability analysis of the recombinant FMDVs

Because of the high variability of the VP1 G-H loop of FMDV during passaging (Elrashedy et al. 2025), four recombinant FMDVs were serially passaged up to 10 times in BHK-21 cells. Viral RNA from passage 6 (p6) and passage 10 (p10) was extracted using QIAamp Viral RNA kit (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s specifications. The cDNA fragments containing complete structural proteins of each FMDV were amplified by RT-PCR and sequenced to confirm amino acid changes.

Antigen production and inactivated vaccine preparation

The recombinant FMDVs of p6 were respectively cultured in adherent BHK-21 monolayer cells; when 100% infected cells appeared CPE, the viruses were harvested, thawed, and clarified by centrifugation at 3000 × g for 20 min at 4 °C to remove cell debris. The virus supernatants were inactivated with 1.2% binary ethyleneimine (BEI) for 28 h at 30 °C. The inactivated viruses were concentrated with 8% polyethylene glycol 6000 (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, IL, USA) overnight at 4 °C, and the concentrated samples were centrifuged at 16,000 × g for 1 h. The pellets were resuspended in TNE buffer (50 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.4, 10 mM EDTA, 150 mM NaCl) and then clarified by centrifugation to remove insoluble particulate. The pretreated samples were layered onto 15–45% sucrose gradients and ultracentrifuged at 105,000 × g for 3 h at 4 °C. The 146S antigens were quantified by 260 nm densitometry.

Immunization of pigs with the experimental vaccines

Twenty pigs weighing 20–30 kg were purchased from an FMD-free farm and divided into four groups; each group of five pigs was intramuscularly inoculated with one experimental vaccine. Each animal received 15 µg viral antigen emulsified in ISA 201 oil adjuvant (Seppic, Paris, France). Sera samples were collected at 0, 7, 14, 21, and 28 days post vaccination (dpv). The experiments were performed in an isolation animal biosafety level 3 (BSL-3) facility at Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute of Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences.

Liquid-phase blocking ELISA (LPBE)

All serum samples from immunized pigs were analyzed for anti-FMDV structural protein antibodies using a commercial LPBE kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Samples with antibody titer > 1.8 log10 (1/64 dilution) were considered positive.

Titration of neutralizing antibody

The neutralizing antibodies against serotype O FMDVs were measured as previously described (Li et al. 2022). Briefly, the serum samples collected at 28 dpv were heat-inactivated at 56 °C for 30 min and then twofold serially diluted with MEM in 96-well plates in a total volume of 50 µL. FMDV suspensions containing 100 tissue culture infectious dose 50% (TCID50) were added to each well, and the plates were gently agitated and incubated for 1 h at 37 °C. After incubation, 100 µL BHK-21 cells were added to each well and further incubated for another 48 h. CPE was assessed at 48 hpi. The neutralizing antibody titers were calculated as the log10 of the reciprocal antibody dilution required for 50% neutralization of 100 TCID50 virus. Neutralizing antibody titer ≥ 1.65 log10 was considered protective. Virus neutralization test (VNT) assays were performed in duplicate.

Vaccine matching test

Vaccine matching in vitro is usually expressed as r1 value, which is calculated as the neutralizing antibody titer against the field strain/the neutralizing antibody titer against the vaccine virus. An r1 value ≥ 0.3 indicates that the vaccine virus is a good match with the field virus, while a value < 0.3 indicates significant mismatch, demonstrating new vaccine development. All assays were carried out in duplicates, and average values from two tests were used for subsequent analysis.

VP1 amino acid sequence alignment

Antigenic variation of FMDV is predominantly driven by VP1 mutations, particularly in the G-H loop (residues 130–160); therefore, the deduced amino acid sequences of VP1 of the neutralization-tested strains were aligned using MEGA 6.06 (Tamura et al. 2013).

Statistical analysis

Unpaired t tests were conducted using GraphPad Prism Software (Version 8.3.0; GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). A p-value < 0.05 was considered significant (*); a p-value < 0.001 was considered very significant (**); a p-value < 0.0001 was considered extremely significant (***).

Results

Generation of recombinant FMDVs

The chimeric FMDV full-length plasmid of pOFS/XJLP1 carrying L and P1 gene substitutions of O/XJ/CHA/2017 was successfully constructed by gene synthesis as shown in Fig. 1. Then, the three recombinant plasmids pOFS/XJLP1/TURG-H, pOFS/XJLP1/HBG-H, and pOFS/XJLP1/18G-H were successfully created by substituting the sequence for the G-H loops of O/TUR/5/2009, O/HBCZ/2019, and O/18074 based on the genetic backbone of pOFS/XJLP1 as shown in Fig. 1. Each construct contained the expected genetic changes by DNA sequencing. Four constructs displayed similar specific infectivities in BSR/T7 cells after 56 h post transfection, indicating that infectious FMDVs were successfully rescued. The virus rescued from pOFS/XJLP1 was named rHN/XJ, which was used as the parental virus. The viruses recovered from pOFS/XJLP1/TURG-H, pOFS/XJLP1/HBG-H, and pOFS/XJLP1/18G-H were, respectively, defined as rHN/XJ/TURG-H, rHN/XJ/HBG-H, and rHN/XJ/18G-H. All rescued viruses were sequenced to verify the presence of targeted substitutions and the absence of unwanted mutations. The growth characteristics of recombinant FMDVs

The effect of the substitution of the G-H loop on the growth properties and phenotypic characterization was examined by standard plaque assay in BHK-21 cells. As shown in Fig. 4A, all viruses developed visible plaques with polymorphic size distributions. The parental virus formed small opaque plaques (1–3 mm), while the mutants exhibited clear plaques ranging from 1–6 mm (Fig. 4A). In vitro growth kinetics demonstrated that three mutant FMDVs had similar replication properties and had higher viral titers (PFU/mL) than that of the parental virus at 4–20 hpi (Fig. 4B). Neutralizing antibody responses to homologous and heterologous viruses in pigs

Serum neutralizing antibodies play a pivotal role for cross protection of FMDV; therefore, VNTs were performed using sera collected from pigs vaccinated with the experimental vaccines at 28 dpv. As shown in Fig. 6, the animals vaccinated with rHN/XJ/TURG-H, rHN/XJ/HBG-H, and rHN/XJ vaccines all developed protective mean neutralizing antibody (VN titer > 2.1 log10) against the field viruses belonging to the O/SEA/Mya-98, O/ME-SA/PanAsia, and O/ME-SA/Ind-2001 lineages, but all of them did not produce protective mean neutralizing antibody to O/18074 of the Cathay lineage (VN titer ≤ 1.49 log10). All pigs vaccinated with rHN/XJ/18G-H produced high protective mean neutralizing antibody (VN titer > 2.1 log10) to FMDVs of four lineages. Discussion

In China, FMD is endemic. Serotype Asia1 has been extinct since 2009 (Zhu et al. 2018), serotype A sporadically occurred, while serotype O has always been the most prevalent serotype and has evolved four different lineages named as O/ME-SA/PanAsia, O/ME-SA/Ind2001, O/SEA/Mya-98, and O/Cathay (Beard and Mason 2000). Among them, the viruses of the Cathay topotype are very highly adapted to pigs and often cause devastating damage to the pig industry (Davies 2002; Dunn and Donaldson 1997; Knight-Jones and Rushton 2013). The porcinophilic FMDV was first isolated in 1970 in Hong Kong, China (Li et al. 2023). Since then, field cases caused by this topotype have been reported in Hong Kong, the Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, and Taiwan in 2009 (Li et al. 2022; Upadhyaya et al. 2021; Vierra et al. 2020). Currently, this virus has been detected in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and several Southeast Asian countries and gradually exhibits high levels of genetic and antigenic diversity (Li et al. 2022; Upadhyaya et al. 2021; Vierra et al. 2020). A recent study reported that O/HKN/6/83 (Cathay), O/IND/R2/75 (ME-SA), O/PanAsia-2 (ME-SA), and O/Mya/2009 (Mya-98) vaccine strains showed a good match with 62 FMDVs belonging to the O/SEA and O/ME-SA topotype, but none of them could elicit a protective immune reaction to the circulating Cathay viruses isolated from Hong Kong in 2013–2018 (Vierra et al. 2020). This study was consistent with our earlier studies that r/HN (Cathay) vaccine did not induce protective neutralizing antibodies against the circulating virus of the Cathay lineage (van Rensburg et al. 2004) and O/XJ/CHA/2017 vaccine exhibited weak cross-reactivity to the circulating Cathay FMDV isolated in 2018 (data not published). These studies indicated that the Cathay virus appeared as an antigenic drift, which poses a serious threat to the global pig industry, especially for China, the biggest pig breeding country. Therefore, accurate antigenic matching between the circulating viruses and potential vaccine strains is urgently needed to ensure an effective FMD control strategy.

In general, the selection of FMD vaccine strains is based on the method of cell-culture adaptation of field isolates, which is time-consuming and laborious, limiting the availability of custom-made vaccine strains (Mahapatra and Parida 2018). Recently, the construction of cDNA-derived chimeric FMDVs became an attractive solution for rapid selection of vaccine strains (Li et al. 2022; Lee et al. 2017). Additionally, FMD vaccine is produced by large-scale growth of FMDV in cell culture; thus, it is necessary to select a potential vaccine strain with excellent growth capacity combined with the characteristic of antigenic suitability. Previous studies showed that FMDV O/XJ/CHA/2017 has a good match with many circulating strains (Cao et al. 2021; Lee et al. 2020), while it has low replication efficiency in BHK-21 cells (peak titer ≤ 106.0 TCID50/mL). To overcome the growth limitation of O/XJ/CHA/2017, we generated three mutant FMDVs by exchanging the G-H loop of field strains. All mutant viruses exhibited significantly enhanced growth in BHK-21 cells and remarkably shortened the time of complete cell destruction, indicating that different amino acids of the G-H loop have different effects on the viral replication capacity. This is consistent with previous studies that single or several amino acid replacements located at the VP1 G-H loop can affect the growth ability of the recombinant FMDVs (Lian et al. 2016; Rieder et al. 2005). Alternatively, the recombinant viruses all acquired one or two K residues by serial passages in cell culture, which are probably associated with the adaptation of FMDVs for efficient replication in cell culture by binding alternative cellular receptors (Sa-Carvalho et al. 1997; Willems et al. 2020).

FMD vaccine matching is important for the implementation of vaccination based on disease control strategy (Gao and Ma 2021). Although an in vivo matching test is thought to be the gold standard method for evaluating the protection induced by a vaccine against the field strains (McCullough et al. 1992), a neutralizing antibody is an important determinant for protection against FMDVs (Ullah et al. 2015). In this study, we firstly chose an in vitro matching test to evaluate the antigenic and immunogenic correlation between the vaccine strains and the circulating viruses, then further predicted the in vivo protection induced by the experimental vaccines. Pigs inoculated with four inactivated vaccines all produced high LPBE antibodies (> 2.1 log10) in 28 dpv, but only the pigs vaccinated with the rHN/XJ/18G-H vaccine carrying the substitution of the VP1 G–H loop of the Cathay virus could induce a high protective cross-neutralizing antibody (≥ 2.36 log10) against FMDVs belonging to four lineages, demonstrating that the VP1 G-H loop of the Cathay virus plays an important role in the antigenic drift of FMDV. This supports that the single or the eight amino acid substitutions in the VP1 G-H loop of the circulated FMDV attribute to antigenic mismatch and vaccination failure (Ludi et al. 2014; Ullah et al. 2015). However, the molecular basis and vaccine efficacy test of rHN/XJ/18G-H in vivo will need to be further studied in the future. Furthermore, the vaccine matching results also indicated that rHN/XJ/18G-H has a good match with the circulating field isolates belonging to four lineages. Therefore, it could be used as a vaccine candidate to control the outbreak of type O FMD in China. Additionally, LPBE antibody titer ≥ 1.8 log10 was generally considered to promote protection in pigs, but our results of LPBE-ELISA were not consistent with an in vitro virus neutralization test. Therefore, we think that it is controversial that the LPBE result was applied to determine protection in pigs.

Conclusions

We generated three recombinant FMDVs with high growth in cell culture by substituting the VP1 G-H loop of different FMDVs. The recombinant virus with the G-H loop of the Cathay virus can induce good cross-immune reactions against viruses belonging to four different lineages and has the potential as a vaccine candidate for the control and eradication of serotype O FMD in China.

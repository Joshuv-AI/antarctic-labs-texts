Spindle cell variant of ameloblastic carcinoma: A diagnostic challenge
Authors: Mamata S. Kamat, Uma V. Datar, Sanjay S. Byakodi, Amit A. Basannavar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
An 18-year-old male presented with a 3-week history of progressively enlarging nodular growth in the posterior right mandibular region. The patient denied the use of tobacco in any form or history of trauma, and his family history was unremarkable. Clinical examination revealed a 2 × 3 cm reddish-pink, firm lesion with palpable submandibular lymph nodes. Radiological imaging showed a well-defined radiolucency distal to tooth #47. An incisional biopsy revealed the proliferation of spindle cells and ameloblastomatous epithelium showing features like pleomorphism, cellular crowding, and mitotic figures. Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed odontogenic origin. Based on the above findings, the diagnosis of the spindle cell variant of ameloblastic carcinoma (AC) was rendered. AC, constituting less than 2% of odontogenic tumours, and the spindle cell variant, a rare subtype with fewer than 15 reported cases, pose diagnostic challenges, necessitating careful histopathological and immunohistochemical evaluation for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning.

INTRODUCTION

Ameloblastic carcinoma (AC) is an aggressive, rare odontogenic malignancy accounting for less than 2% of odontogenic tumours.[12345] AC exhibits histological variations that impact its biological behaviour and treatment approach. One such variant is spindle cell AC, which is characterised by hypercellular areas showing elongated spindle cells with cytologic atypia along with ameloblastomatous epithelium. To date, less than 15 cases have been reported in the literature.[12345] We present a unique occurrence of the spindle cell variant of AC, manifested as a nodular growth in the posterior right tooth area of an 18-year-old male.

CASE HISTORY

An 18-year-old male presented with a complaint of a growth located on the posterior right tooth region for 3 weeks, and it had gradually increased in size. The patient denied use of tobacco products in any form, and there was no history of trauma to the area. On clinical examination, a nodular growth was noted on the right posterior mandibular region, approximately measuring 2 × 3 cm in diameter [Figure 1a]. The lesion was reddish pink in colour and firm in consistency. Grade I mobility was noted with tooth #47. The orthopantomogram showed a well-defined unilocular radiolucency distal to tooth #47 extending posteriorly halfway to the ramus and missing #48 tooth [Figure 1b].

An intraoral clinical image showing a nodular growth was noted on the right posterior mandibular region (a) and the orthopantomogram showed a well-defined unilocular radiolucency distal #47 tooth extending to posteriorly halfway to the ramus (b)

Incisional biopsy was performed that showed thickened bundles of spindle cells [Figure 2a] and adjacent islands and nests of ameloblastomatous epithelium that showed peripheral ameloblast-like cells and central stellate reticulum-like cells [Figure 2b]. A few of the islands showed proliferation with budding epithelium along with dense cellular crowding, hyperchromatism, pleomorphism, and mitotic figures [Figure 2c]. The islands also showed squamous metaplasia with occasional keratin pearls and areas of necrosis [Figure 2d]. Immunohistochemical analysis showed strong positivity for CK 19, PAN CK, and Sox-2 in both spindle cells and cells of ameloblastomatous epithelium [Figure 3a-c respectively]. The Ki-67 proliferative index was 30% [Figure 3d]. Negative immunoreactivity to CD 31, CD 34, and Desmin, SMA ruled out vascular and muscle origin, respectively. This indicated the odontogenic origin of spindle cells and the ameloblastomatous cell population. Hence, a final diagnosis of the spindle cell variant of AC was rendered. Considering its aggressive nature, right hemi-mandibulectomy along with supra omohyoid neck dissection and reconstruction with pectoralis major flap were planned.

Immunohistochemical staining showed that the neoplastic cells were positive for CK 19 (a) 10x), PAN CK (b) 10x), Sox-2 (c) 10x) in both spindle cells as well as cells of ameloblastomatous epithelium and Ki-67 proliferative index was 30% (d; 40x)

Right hemi-mandibulectomy was performed with wide surgical margins. After the right hemi-mandibulectomy, reconstruction of the mandible is necessary to restore function and aesthetics to the patient’s jaw. Skin paddle length and breadth correlated to the size of the defect; the site was marked and infiltrated with local anaesthetics with adrenaline. DP saving incision was made through the skin and subcutaneous tissue down to the pectoralis fascia encircling the skin paddle. After elevating the flap, a broad tunnel of 4-finger breadth space was created in the subcutaneous plane to allow the flap to be passed through the neck. The flap was subsequently tunnelled through the subcutaneous plane created with a skin paddle oriented to prevent torquing of the pedicle. A flap was placed at the defect, and water-tight closure was done. Microscopically, there was no evidence of tumour at the surgical margin and all the lymph nodes were free of tumour. Six months of follow-up was uneventful.

DISCUSSION

AC is a rare odontogenic malignancy accounting for <2% of odontogenic tumours.[12] WHO has recognised two variants, namely, the primary/de novo type (which lacks a component of conventional ameloblastoma) and the secondary/de-differentiated type (in which carcinoma develops from a pre-existing ameloblastoma).[234] Considering the long duration and histopathology, it would be appropriate to designate the present case as a secondary type. AC is characterised by an infiltrative pattern of ameloblastomatous islands and cords with condensed and hypercellular stellate reticulum, budding of epithelium, pleomorphism, and mitotic figures.[46] The spindle cell variant is a rare subtype of AC with less than 15 cases reported in the literature.[35] Among the reported cases, most of the cases were in males and the average age was 41.14 years.[35] However, our case presented an 18-year-old boy. The spindle cell variant of AC is characterised by the association of ameloblastomatous epithelium with hypercellular stroma of elongated bundles of spindle cells that show cytologic atypia.[2] The presence of spindle cells in AC poses a diagnostic challenge, especially in superficial biopsy. Since the incisional biopsy sections predominantly displayed spindle cell proliferation, it was challenging to definitively diagnose the spindle cell variant of AC. Additionally, given the soft tissue nature of the current case, pinpointing the lesion’s origin was imperative. Hence, the use of immunohistochemical markers plays an important role in identifying the cell of origin and aids in differentiation from spindle cell variants of squamous cell carcinoma and spindle cell lesions like myoepithelioma and odontogenic sarcomas.[5] The immunocytochemical profile of the resected specimen sections of the present case with strong positivity for PAN CK, Sox-2, in both spindle cells and cells of ameloblastomatous epithelium, suggested epithelial origin of the lesional cells. CK19 positivity in both cell populations confirmed the odontogenic origin. The lesional cells were negative for CD 31, CD 34, and Desmin, SMA and ruled out muscle and vascular tumours. Considering the histopathology and immunohistochemical profile, a final diagnosis of the spindle cell variant of AC was made. Hence, a thorough diagnostic workup is mandatory to arrive at a final diagnosis and further plan appropriate treatment.

Generally, considering the aggressive nature, the treatment of AC follows the line of treatment of squamous cell carcinoma. This includes complete surgical excision along with therapeutic neck dissection and post-operative radiotherapy.[6] Similarly, in the present case, right hemi-mandibulectomy was performed with wide surgical margins, and right supra omohyoid neck dissection was done. Six months of follow-up was uneventful. Among the cases (14 cases) reported in the literature, three cases showed recurrence, two cases showed metastasis, and two patients died of the disease.[35]

The current case underscores the importance of a comprehensive diagnostic evaluation for the spindle cell variant of AC. This process is vital for confirming diagnosis, planning treatment, and assessing prognosis.

Key Messages

This case report presents a rare occurrence of spindle cell AC in an 18-year-old male, located in the posterior mandible. The aggressive tumour highlights the importance of early diagnosis and comprehensive treatment to manage such uncommon malignancies effectively.

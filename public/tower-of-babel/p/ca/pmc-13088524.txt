Amandaba in the Amazon: the role of glycated hemoglobin in the evaluation of health services
Authors: Maria Clara Silva, Amanda Suzane Alves da Silva, Socorro Castelo Branco
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the effectiveness of health care for people with diabetes mellitus in a city in the Eastern Amazon through glycemic control, microalbuminuria, anthropometric measurements, and Primary Health Care (PHC) attributes among the services studied.MethodThis was a cross-sectional study conducted in Family Health Strategy units in Belém, Pará, located east of the Amazon. The initial sample included 230 people with type 2 diabetes aged ≥ 30 years. The second survey sample included 146 individuals.ResultsIn total, 75% of the individuals in both groups reported using PHC services more. Upon receiving the laboratory tests and returning for the appointment, 42.7% of district I did not return, compared to 29% of district II and 22% of district I did not receive the tests, compared to 11% of district II. Seventy percent of the population had HBA1c values ≥7 in both groups; 74% of district II and 62% of district II had fasting blood glucose values > 130 mg/dL; and 70% of the population in both districts had albumin excretion rates < 30 mg/gCr. A BMI of more than 76% of individuals in both groups reached > 24 kg/m2, and women’s waist circumference was ≥ 80 cm for more than 88% of the population in both districts. For the pcatools, 84% of district I and 83% of district II reported going to the BHU when they needed a review appointment; for accessibility, 66% of district I and 56% of district II reported delays when trying to get advice over the phone; longitudinally, 65% of district I and 85% of district II reported care with the same health professionals, with a statistically significant difference (p = 0.017).ConclusionMost of the population in the two districts did not reach the goals of HBA1c or fasting blood glucose, but albuminuria was maintained at the recommended level. More than half were overweight and had a high waist circumference. The item usage had the best performance in PCAtool, despite dissatisfaction with the efficiency and speed of service. Improving accessibility in PHC can lead to improvements in glycemic control and anthropometric measurements in patients with DM2.Trial registrationCAAE 45831221.0.0000.0018.Keywords: Primary health care, Assessment of health services, Diabetes mellitus

Introduction

Primary Health Care (PHC) in Brazil was implemented through the National Basic Care Policy and represents the main means of entering the Unified Health System (UHS). PHC are responsible for coordinating comprehensive care and establishing connections with other services and actions in health care networks, which are fundamental foundations for UHSs [1]. It ensures universal and equitable access to available health services and seeks to reduce hospitalizations due to primary care-sensitive conditions. Effective PHC contributes to containing health system costs and maintaining the principles of the UHS [2].

In this scenario, evaluation is one of the fundamental steps to review and redirect the trajectories taken in the implementation of health actions. Evaluating the efficiency, efficacy, and effectiveness of structures, processes, and results related to risks, vulnerabilities, access, and citizen satisfaction has becomes an indispensable tool for improving the health system through the incorporation of effective planning. Furthermore, when evaluation results are shared with the population, they also contribute to social control and citizen participation in the decision-making process of managers [3].

Thus, PHC must include four essential structural elements: first contact, continuity over time, comprehensiveness, and coordination. In addition, three derived elements must be incorporate: family focus, community orientation, and cultural competence [4]. Therefore, the assessment of these attributes predicts the quality of services provided by PHC. Based on these elements, it is important to ensure that the provision of high-quality care that meets the needs of patients is met by assessing the perspectives of different stakeholders, such as managers, professionals, and users [1].

One of the tools designed to assess health care is the Primary Care Assessment Tool (PCATool). This instrument has different self-administered versions for children (PCATOOL child version), adults older than 18 years (PCATOOL adult version), health professionals, and health service coordinators. The study analyzed the four essential elements and the three derived elements of PHC, allowing the identification of elements in the structure and procedures of services that require review or improvement in favor of quality, both in the planning and implementation of PHC initiatives [5]. With access to health services, adequate treatment, therapeutic adherence, and continuous monitoring, people with diabetes mellitus can enjoy a good quality of life. However, neglecting of this disease can result in serious complications, such as neuropathy, retinopathy, blindness, nephropathy, diabetic foot, and amputation. The Ministry of Health established a protocol in 2013 for the screening and care of patients with diabetes in PHC, aiming at the early identification and adequate treatment of the disease. In addition to improving the quality of life of the population, adequate care for patients with diabetes can avoid unnecessary costs associated with UHS. Monitoring and evaluating care for patients with diabetes is essential to prevent the worsening of the disease and reduce mortality rates. However, it is essential to maintain this monitoring to identify areas for improvement in healthcare policy and ensure the effectiveness of strategies to combat diabetes, especially in a country with continental proportions such as Brazil [6]. In this context, it is of utmost importance that the entire population be included in correctly targeted PHC and be served according to their needs. Therefore, care for people with type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM2) cannot be neglected due to the growing prevalence of the disease in Brazil, which is the country with the fifth largest population with diabetes, with approximately 16.8 million individuals in 2019 [7]. Furthermore, there has been an increase in the diagnosis of DM2 in individuals older than 18 years in Belém, Pará. According to the Vigitel Brazil telephone survey, 4.0% of the population reported a diagnosis of diabetes in 2007 [8], while 6.9% reported this diagnosis in 2023 [9]. Given this situation, the main laboratory parameters must be monitored constantly in the PHC for patients with diabetes. The Brazilian Diabetes Society (SBD) recommends that the level of glycated hemoglobin (HBA1c) in patients with diabetes should be less than 7%, in addition to which this value corresponds to an average daily blood glucose of approximately 154 mg/dL, with a variation between 122 and 184 mg/dL; this value has been adopted as the main reference point for diabetes control [10]. Furthermore, the rate of urinary ALB excretion should also be investigated, as it is directly linked to Diabetic Kidney Disease. The recommended values are considered to be less than 30 mg/gCr, while microalbuminuria consists of between 30 and 300 mg/gCr and macroalbuminuria is equivalent to values above 300 mg/gCr excreted per day [11]. Anthropometric measurements are also related to the onset and worsening of DM2 symptoms, and Body Mass Index (BMI) and abdominal circumference are important predictors. A BMI between 25 kg/m2 and 29.9 kg/m2 is considered overweight and negatively affects an individual’s health, while the recommended abdominal circumference is less than 80 cm for women and less than 94 cm for men [12, 13].

This study was guided by the central question: How effective are primary health care (PHC) services in providing comprehensive and continuous care for individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus living in the Eastern Amazon region? The research was justified by the need to generate evidence on the effectiveness of PHC in managing chronic diseases within a region marked by significant socioeconomic inequalities and geographic barriers that limit access and continuity of care. Despite national efforts to strengthen Brazil’s Unified Health System and improve PHC organization, the Eastern Amazon continues to face challenges related to infrastructure, workforce availability, and the consistent follow-up of patients with chronic conditions. Considering that type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM2) management requires long-term monitoring, health education, and preventive care, this study integrates biomedical indicators, such as HbA1c, fasting glucose, and albuminuria, with user perceptions of accessibility and longitudinality of care assessed through the PCATool instrument. By combining these perspectives, the research provides a comprehensive evaluation of PHC effectiveness and contributes to understanding gaps and potential strategies to enhance health service quality and equity in the Amazonian context.

Therefore, the objective of this study was to evaluate the effectiveness of health care for people with diabetes mellitus in a city in the Eastern Amazon, through glycemic control, microalbuminuria, and anthropometric measurements, in addition to evaluating the attributes of primary health care in the services studied.

Methodology

Ethical aspects

This study is part of the research project “Amandaba in the Amazon: Culture Circles as a strategy to encourage self-care for Diabetes Mellitus in Primary Health Care,” approved by the research ethics committee of the Federal University of Pará under number CAAE 45831221.0.0000.0018. Resources were developed from call no. 27/2020 of the National Council for Scientific and Technological Development - CNPq, Ministry of Health - MS (Secretariat of Primary Health Care - SAPS and Department of Health Promotion - DEPROS). The study adhered to the guidelines of Resolution 466/2012 on ethical criteria for research with human beings. Participants were fully informed about the study objectives, procedures, risks, and benefits through the free and informed consent form. Those who agreed signed two copies, one retained by the participant and the other registered in the project, including authorization for image use through the image concession term. After the biochemical analysis, the results were printed in duplicate and delivered to the patient and the corresponding unit. Critical results were urgently communicated to the care team to initiate the appropriate referrals.

Study design, population and sample

This cross-sectional study included patients treated at Family Health Units in two administrative districts of Belém, Pará, which is located in the Eastern Amazon. The districts were not drawn at random, but two districts distant from each other were chosen, with the aim of not mixing the two groups and health teams, which could compromise the results. District I was chosen for the intervention group because it is located close to the university campus, facilitating the movement of the project team. The two districts are similar in terms of population, they are the two most populous in Belém; they are composed of communities with similar sociodemographic characteristics and low incomes; and they are located in peripheral areas of the city. In addition, there were no differences between the protocols of the health teams regarding the care of people with diabetes. Trained health professionals collected all biological samples and participants were instructed to remain fasting, and all lab tests were processed in the same certified facility.

The sample consisted of individuals aged 30 years or older with type 2 diabetes mellitus of both sexes. Initially, 2,099 individuals were assessed for eligibility and randomization. For this population, the sample size was defined by a sample stratified by district and sex, using the parameters of a 95% confidence level, an α of 0.05, and an error of 0.06.

Two household surveys were carried out, the first from November 29 to December 17, 2021, to apply the questionnaire and collect laboratory tests, and the second from July 18 to August 31, 2022, to assess the delivery of the tests and apply the PCAtool.

Instruments and variables

During the first household survey, a standardized questionnaire was administered, with questions aimed at collecting data for the following sociodemographic variables: sex (male/female); education status; self-reported race/color in the categories black, brown, white, yellow and indigenous (based on the Brazilian Census); these were grouped into black/brown and other; marital status (categorized as married, consensual union, single, separated, separated or divorced; widowed, grouped into married/consensual union and single/separated/widowed); being economically active or retired; per capita income (average of the family income range by the number of people in the family); economic classification in strata, according to the Brazil Criteria, grouped into two strata B/C and D/E; and inclusion in an income transfer programme.

Regarding health care, questions were asked about the number of consultations per year, categorized as ≥ 4 or < 4; whether the patient has a health plan; which health service the patient uses most frequently; primary care or specialized care; and whether the patient received laboratory tests performed by the project and returned for a consultation, categorized as receiving the test and not consulting, received the test and consulting, did not receive the test.

An anthropometric assessment was performed, and weight and height were measured to calculate BMI and abdominal circumference. Blood samples were collected for HbA1c tests, fasting blood glucose and urine analysis to measure albuminuria in an isolated sample with creatinine correction.

The PCAtool Brazil Adult version was used as an assessment tool in the second survey. It was originally validated in 2007 in Brazil, and contains 87 items divided into 10 categories [5]. To apply the questionnaire, three attributes were defined to assess user access to PHC:

(I)The first contact access - utilization (category B), consisted of 3 items, that verify whether the health service is the first option when there is a health problem, or if it is the only way to access specialized health services. The three items of this attribute were used.

These categories bywere selected according to the researchers’ criteria, and the questions were selected according to the research subject and the references [5, 14]. The response options are ordered according to the Likert scale: (4) Definitely yes; (3) probably yes; (2) probably no; (1) definitely no; and (9) I do not know/I do not remember.

Statistical analysis

The analysis of the difference between groups was performed using the Yates corrected chi-square test in 2 × 2 tables and the Pearson corrected test for 2 × 3 tables for categorical variables. The Mann-Whitney test for two independent samples was used to verify the difference between continuous variables with an asymmetric distribution, and Student’s t-test for two independent samples, was used to evaluate the difference between the means of the groups with a normal distribution. The normality of the distribution was tested using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. The results are presented as medians, quartiles 25 and 75 or means and standard deviations for continuous variables. Values of p < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistical significance. IBM SPSS Statistics 24 software was used for statistical analyses.

Discussion

This study aimed to assess the quality of care provided by PHC for individuals with DM2, in addition to evaluating laboratory test results and body measurements of 230 patients, who were randomly selected from two administrative districts of a municipality in the northern region of Brazil.

The sociodemographic data are in line with those of other studies on DM2, higherwhich showed a greater prevalence of this syndrome among women, people with lower education levels, and people with an average age older than 62 years [15, 16]. Furthermore, this study showed that the majority of the population is not part of the income transfer program, even if they have a low family income. A low family income is related to changes linked to DM2, such as diabetic foot disease, according to a study carried out in Altamira-Pará [17], and may be a predictor of the worsening of these changes.

PHC health professionals must be trained and up-to-date to address DM2, a condition that is so common in clinical practice. In this study, the majority of the population reported relying on public health services and using PHC more than Secondary Health Care (SHC) services. Unfortunately, most of the sample received the minimum necessary follow-up in PHC, which is designed to include at least four consultations per year for patients with DM2. Therefore, the follow-up of these patients becomes insufficient and, according to the study by Suplici et al. [18], the better the overall performance of the PHC multidisciplinary team is, the greater the adherence and maintenance of treatment by the group being monitored, thus generating better results in controlling the complications of DM2.

Furthermore, the lack of follow-up was evidenced by the nondelivery of exams for more than 20% of patients and the absence of appointments to review these exams in more than 42% of the population in district I. A study by Suplici et al. [18] noted that a PHC facility that complies with and maintains an organized schedule and constant contact with health professionals further encourages patients with DM2 to adhere to treatment, maintain good lifestyle habits and present better results in routine exams, especially when there is a good understanding of the biopsychosocial context in which the individual is inserted and good communication between professional and patient, as outnoted in the study by Rzewuska et al. [19].

The results of this study showed that HbA1c and fasting blood glucose levels were high in people with diabetes in the majority of the sample analyzed, however, albuminuria values were normal for most individuals. According to the “metabolic memory” theory, inadequate glycemic control can leave “memory” and cause adverse effects in the future [20]. Thus, constant and uncontrolled changes in HbA1c in patients with DM2 may be related to the development of cardiovascular changes and albuminuria, which can generate more complex complications, such as diabetic nephropathy [21]. Failure to prevent these complications generates high costs for the health system, requiring early and quality monitoring for this population.

In this study, more than 72% of the sample participants from the two districts had normal urinary ALB excretion. Among the results of laboratory tests, this variable was the only invariable for which the majority of the population remained within the recommended levels for patients with T2DM. However, studies indicate that changes in HbA1c and prolonged exposure to hyperglycemia are directly related to the development of kidney disease [21, 22]. As previously described, the majority of the sample in this research showed changes in HbA1c levels.

Therefore, even if albuminuria levels, in most of the population, are not a concern at the moment, it is worth noting that the onset of kidney damage is asymptomatic, and patients with T2DM may develop Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) if these patients are not evaluated and monitored. This monitoring should be performed annually in all patients who have had DM2 for more than 5 years, and two tests are needed required: Urinary Albumin Excretion (UAE) and Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) [23]. It is necessary to evaluate both tests because a considerable proportion of patients with DM2 have normal albuminuria, but still have a decrease in GFR, beingwhich is the gold standard idealized by the Brazilian Diabetes Society [24].

However, it is usually perceived that this screening in PHC is done performed with only one of the tests, with the GFR being the most commonly used [25]. Therefore, the implementation of two screening assessments to monitor renal alterations in patients with T2DM is of utmost importance, as CKD screening can significantly contribute to early diagnosis, reducing its impact on society and the public health system. The results of anthropometric measurements indicated that more than 70% of the population was overweight. In addition, the present study showed that the majority of the population is had abdominal circumference outside the recommended range. These results corroborate the findings of Pantoja et al. [17] and Santos et al. [26], where more than 70% of the sample with T2DM also had high body mass and abdominal circumference. These findings suggest a higher greater risk of wound healing and cardiovascular complications [27, 28], making it important to monitor, prevent and provide health education for these patients through multidisciplinary monitoring that must be conducted by the PHC.

In the evaluation of the PCAtool instrument attributes, the utilization attribute performed better, demonstrating that there is adherence to PHC when some health intervention is needed required. The use of PHC services has also been shown to be sufficient in studies of other Brazilian locations, such as Pernambuco and Santa Catarina [16, 29]. This attribute is not limited to the user’s entry into the health system or the availability of services and resources at a given time and space; it also means harmonization between the population’s needs and the current provision of these services [30].

In this study, the responses to the accessibility attribute demonstrated that the efficiency and speed of care do not correspond to patient demand and that, despite finding these findings, switching to another health service is not feasible. Furthermore, this was the attribute with the worst results, similar to the studies by Prates et al. [31] and Vidal et al. [29] in which this attribute also had the worst evaluation. This result can be attributed to geographical and organizational barriers, such as limited opening hours, difficulties in scheduling appointments and long waiting times for care. This situation hinders the provision of comprehensive health care, as patients tend to postpone seeking care when they encounter obstacles, thus compromising the impact of preventive measures and resulting in additional expenses for the public health system in the future [31].

Regarding the longitudinality of care provided by the services, it is worth noting that the results were positive for both groups, especially in terms of follow-up with the same health professionals. Patient in district II received better care in this regard than district did patient in district I, indicating that more than 80% of this population continues to receive follow-ups with the same professionals. These findings contrast with by those of Vidal et al. [29] and Chomatas et al. [32], where longitudinality was described with suboptimal results. Positive results in longitudinality have the potential to improve accuracy in diagnoses and treatments, reducing the number of unnecessary referrals, the need for more complex procedures and, as a result, reducing costs associated with the public health system [33].

In the present study, most patients had poor glycemic control and in access to PHC was unsatisfactory. These variables are directly related to the by findings of Lu et al. [34] in which there was a positive association between the population’s satisfaction with public PHC and good glycemic control in patients with DM2, demonstrating that a good experience with PHC results in a better approach to the treatment of DM2.

Despite the relevant findings, this study has some limitations. First, its cross-sectional design does not allow for establishing causal relationships between the evaluated variables. Additionally, the data were collected from two administrative districts in the same municipality, which may limit the generalization of the results to other regions of the Amazon, considering the geographical and socioeconomic heterogeneity of the area. Another limitation concerns the self-reported information obtained through questionnaires, which may be subject to recall bias. Finally, although the PCATool provided valuable insights into the perception of primary health care quality, the evaluation did not include the perspective of health professionals or managers, which could have enriched the analysis. Future studies using longitudinal designs and a broader geographic scope are recommended to confirm and expand these findings.

Conclusion

The majority of the population in both districts is outside the stipulated target for HBA1c and fasting glucose. Albuminuria was the only one disease in which the majority of the population, in both districts, remained within the recommended range. More than half of the population in both districts was overweight and had a waist circumference above the stipulated range. In the evaluation of the PCAtool attributes, item utilization was the one that performed best. The responses about accessibility revealed that the efficiency and speed of care do not meet the demands of patients, which may have contributed to the poor metabolic control of users. However, despite this, changing to another health service would not be a viable option, as revealed by the existence of longitudinal care, since the participants showed that the services maintain an important link with users. Thus, an improvement in accessibility in PHC can improve the quality of care and monitoring of patients with DM2, which may reflect improved glycemic control and reduced overweight/obesity.

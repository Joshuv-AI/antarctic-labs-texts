Effects of Fibrinogen on Tenecteplase Treatment in Acute Ischaemic Stroke After 4.5 Hours of Symptom Onset: A Post Hoc Analysis of the TRACE-III Trial
Authors: Hao Wang, Zhixin Cao, Bruce C. V. Campbell, Aristeidis H. Katsanos, Jinfeng Yin, Marc Fisher, Yongjun Wang, Yunyun Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionFibrinogen levels may influence the effect of intravenous thrombolysis. We aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase across different baseline fibrinogen levels in acute ischaemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the extended time window.MethodsWe performed a post hoc analysis of the Tenecteplase Reperfusion Therapy in Acute Ischaemic Cerebrovascular Events-III (TRACE-III) trial. Patients who presented within 4.5 to 24 h after symptom onset with anterior LVO, salvageable brain tissue, and no access to endovascular thrombectomy were randomised (1:1) to receive either 0.25 mg/kg tenecteplase or standard medical treatment. We categorised patients into groups with non-elevated fibrinogen (≤ 4.0 g/L) and elevated fibrinogen levels (> 4.0 g/L). The primary outcome was the absence of disability, which was defined as a score of 0 to 1 on the modified Rankin scale (mRS) at 90 days. The safety outcomes were symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (sICH) within 36 h and all-cause mortality within 90 days.ResultsWe included 498 of the 516 patients in the TRACE-III trial who had baseline fibrinogen data. Among them, 426 (85.5%) had non-elevated fibrinogen, and 72 (14.5%) had elevated fibrinogen. No significant difference was observed in those with elevated fibrinogen (16.7% vs. 11.1%; RR 1.60; 95% CI 0.42–6.78; P = 0.50). Safety outcomes were similar between the two treatment groups across fibrinogen categories.ConclusionsAmong patients with LVO presenting within 4.5 to 24 h after stroke onset, those with non-elevated fibrinogen levels may receive greater benefit from tenecteplase in reducing disability risk without increasing the incidence of sICH, compared to those with elevated fibrinogen levels.TRACE-III Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT05141305.

Introduction

Intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase is recommended for eligible patients within 4.5 h of acute ischaemic stroke onset [1]. Tenecteplase, a modified form of alteplase with practical delivery advantages, has been shown to be noninferior to alteplase [2]. In the extended time window, the efficacy of tenecteplase in patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) without access to thrombectomy within 4.5 to 24 h after stroke onset is well supported by the Tenecteplase Reperfusion Therapy in Acute Ischaemic Cerebrovascular Events-III (TRACE-III) trial [3].

Fibrinogen, a coagulation biomarker also associated with inflammatory processes implicated in vascular recurrence and progression of atherothrombotic disease [4], could affect the efficacy and safety of thrombolytic treatment. It has been demonstrated that increased baseline fibrinogen is associated with 90-day functional dependence (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score 3–6) [5] and increased early mortality [6] in patients with stroke treated with alteplase thrombolysis, while it may have no effect on recanalisation [6, 7]. Depletion of fibrinogen after thrombolysis could increase the risk of bleeding complications [8–10]. Compared to alteplase, tenecteplase has higher fibrin affinity and a longer plasma half-life [11]. In studies of patients treated within 4.5 h of stroke onset, a subgroup analysis of the Alteplase-Tenecteplase Trial Evaluation for Stroke Thrombolysis (ATTEST) trial showed that tenecteplase (n = 16) did not cause fibrinogen degradation when compared to alteplase (n = 14) [12], and a post hoc analysis of the TRACE-II trial suggested that the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase were comparable to those of alteplase regardless of baseline fibrinogen levels [13]. However, it remains unclear whether baseline fibrinogen levels and changes post-thrombolysis influence the efficacy of tenecteplase in the extended time window.

We aimed to investigate the impact of fibrinogen levels on the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase treatment in patients with LVO presenting within 4.5 to 24 h after stroke onset, using data from the TRACE-III trial.

Methods

This is a post hoc analysis of the TRACE-III trial, a phase 3, multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, blinded endpoint (PROBE) trial enrolling 516 patients from 58 hospitals in China between January 2022 and November 2023. Details on the study design, protocol, and primary results of the trial have been published elsewhere [3, 14]. Briefly, the TRACE-III trial was designed to assess the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase versus standard medical treatment within 4.5 to 24 h after the time that the patient was last known to be well (including stroke on awakening and unwitnessed stroke).

Thetrial was approved by the ethics committee at Beijing Tiantan Hospital and all participating centres. Written informed consent was provided by all the patients or their representatives before enrolment. TRACE-III is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT05141305. All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The TRACE-Ⅲstudy obtained ethics approval from the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical Universitywith number KY2021-129-03.

Randomisation and Treatment

Patients with occlusion of the middle cerebral artery or internal carotid artery, salvageable brain tissue identified on perfusion imaging, and no access to endovascular thrombectomy were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either tenecteplase or standard medical treatment. The tenecteplase group received tenecteplase intravenously as a bolus administered over a period of 5 to 10 s at a dose of 0.25 mg/kg (maximum dose 25 mg) immediately after randomisation. The control group received antiplatelet therapy (standard medical treatment) at the discretion of the investigators. Other treatments conformed to the 2018 Chinese Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Ischaemic Stroke [15]. Although patients who had access to endovascular thrombectomy during the screening period were excluded, rescue thrombectomy after randomisation could be performed at the discretion of the treating clinician if the patient’s condition had deteriorated and endovascular thrombectomy was accessible at that time [3].

Measurements of Fibrinogen

Serum fibrinogen was measured at baseline and 24 h after randomisation by each site’s personnel unaware of the clinical data. The collection, preservation, and processing of blood samples were performed in accordance with the laboratory’s policies and procedures at each site.

Efficacy and Safety Outcomes

The primary outcome was the absence of disability at 90 days, defined as a score of 0 or 1 on the mRS. Secondary outcomes were the ordinal distribution of mRS scores at 90 days; functional independence (mRS ≤ 2) at 90 days; major neurological improvement at 72 h after treatment (defined as a reduction from baseline of ≥ 8 points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) or an NIHSS score of ≤ 1); reperfusion at 24 h after treatment (defined as > 90% reduction in time to maximum [Tmax] > 6 s volume compared to baseline); recanalisation at 24 h after treatment, assessed with the Arterial Occlusive Lesion scale (AOLs, scores range from 0 [no recanalisation] to 3 [complete recanalisation]) and the change in NIHSS score from baseline to 7 days. Safety outcomes were symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (sICH) within 36 h after treatment [16], and death from any cause within 90 days.

Outcome assessments were performed by certified clinicians who were blinded to treatment assignments at each site. Screening data included demographics, medical history, current medications, laboratory results, neurological function (mRS and NIHSS scores) and imaging data. At baseline, CT perfusion or perfusion-diffusion MRI was required to confirm eligibility and repeated at 24 h to assess reperfusion. Intracerebral haemorrhage was evaluated using non-contrast CT or MRI at 24–36 h.

Statistical Analysis

We used the upper limit of normal fibrinogen levels (4.0 g/L) as the cutoff to classify patients into groups with elevated and non-elevated fibrinogen levels [13, 17]. Baseline characteristics between tenecteplase and the standard treatment groups in different fibrinogen categories were compared using the Wilcoxon test for continuous variables and the chi-square (χ2) test for categorical variables.

Differences between the treatment groups in the primary outcome were analysed using log-binomial regression, with trial centre included as a random effect. All effects of tenecteplase compared to standard medical treatment on secondary efficacy and safety binary outcomes are presented as relative rates (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The mRS distribution was analysed using ordinal logistic regression and is reported as a common odds ratio (OR), with the proportional-odds assumption verified by chi-square test. Changes in NIHSS scores from baseline to 7 days were analysed using a generalised linear mixed model, with beta coefficients and 95% CIs reported. Interactions between treatment and fibrinogen categories were evaluated by including treatment by fibrinogen in the model.

A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed with SAS statistical software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc.).

Results

Among the 516 randomised patients in the TRACE-III trial, 18 patients without baseline fibrinogen data were excluded. A total of 498 patients were included in this post hoc analysis, 426 (85.5%) patients with non-elevated fibrinogen (≤ 4.0 g/L) and 72 (14.5%) patients with elevated fibrinogen (> 4.0 g/L). Compared with standard treatment, tenecteplase significantly increased the percentage of patients who had no disability (defined as a mRS score of 0 to 1) at 90 days in patients with non-elevated fibrinogen levels (35.0% vs. 25.7%; RR 1.55; 95% CI 1.03–2.37; P = 0.04). This benefit of tenecteplase administration over standard therapy was not observed in patients with elevated fibrinogen levels (16.7% vs. 11.1%; RR 1.60; 95% CI 0.42–6.78; P = 0.50), with a non-significant fibrinogen category-by-treatment interaction of 0.97 (Table 2). Similar trends were noted for some secondary outcomes, including an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days, major neurological improvement at 72 h, and reperfusion at 24 h (Table 2). The common OR for the 90-day mRS distribution was 1.29 (95% CI 0.92–1.81) for non-elevated fibrinogen levels and 1.97 (95% CI 0.86–4.59) for elevated fibrinogen levels (Table 2 and Fig. 3). In both fibrinogen categories, tenecteplase significantly increased the percentage of recanalisation (P < 0.001 and P = 0.01, respectively; P = 0.64 for interaction) and reduced the NIHSS score at 7 The safety outcome of sICH within 36 h after treatment occurred with similar frequency in the tenecteplase group and the standard treatment group, independent of fibrinogen levels (3.2% vs. 1.0% in the group with non-elevated fibrinogen levels; 2.8% vs. 0.0% in the group with elevated fibrinogen levels; P = 0.99 for interaction). The fibrinogen category was also not associated with the risk of death within 90 days (Table 2).

Discussion

In this post hoc analysis of the TRACE-III trial, we observed a disparity in the efficacy of tenecteplase between patients with elevated and non-elevated fibrinogen levels. Tenecteplase resulted in significantly reduced disability at 90 days compared to standard medical care in patients with fibrinogen ≤ 4.0 g/L. Safety outcomes were similar between the two treatment therapies in both fibrinogen categories.

Fibrinogen, a crucial component of the coagulation cascade and a known pro-inflammatory marker, is associated with the occurrence of stroke [18–20]. Fibrinogen levels also influence the features of the fibrin clot [21]. High fibrinogen concentrations lead to the formation of a fibrin clot with thin and tightly packed fibres, more resistant to fibrinolytic enzymes [22, 23]. Thrombolytic agents, including alteplase and tenecteplase, bind to thrombus fibrin and activate plasminogen to plasmin, which cleaves fibrin into fibrin degradation products, dissolving the thrombus and facilitating recanalisation [24]. However, thrombolytics also bind on circulating fibrinogen, resulting in fibrinogen degradation, and increasing the risk of bleeding. A multicentre observational study conducted in Italy demonstrated that a reduction in fibrinogen to levels below 2.0 g/L or a decrease of more than 50% from baseline within 2 h of intravenous thrombolysis significantly increases the risk of sICH and major bleeding complications [9]. Compared to alteplase, tenecteplase is a fibrinolytic agent with a 14-fold greater fibrin specificity, a 10-fold greater conservation of fibrinogen, an 80-fold increased resistance to plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 activity, more rapid thrombolysis, and reduced plasma clearance [11]. These pharmacological advantages may underlie the superiority of tenecteplase over alteplase in meta-analysis of randomised trials [25], and may protect against systemic fibrinogen degradation [26–28]. However, available evidence regarding the effect of fibrinogen levels on tenecteplase treatment was limited. This study aimed to investigate how fibrinogen levels influence the outcomes of tenecteplase therapy in patients with stroke. Our findings suggest that tenecteplase significantly reduced disability at 90 days in patients with non-elevated fibrinogen levels. It also increased the rates of mRS ≤ 2 at 90 days, major neurological improvement at 72 h, and reperfusion at 24 h in the patients with non-elevated fibrinogen levels, but not in those with elevated fibrinogen levels.

In the current study, the level of fibrinogen affected the efficacy of tenecteplase administration within the 4.5–24 h window, which was consistent with previous studies evaluating alteplase thrombolysis within 4.5 h after symptom onset [5, 6]. For example, one cohort study found that baseline low fibrinogen was associated with better clinical outcomes. In this study of 200 consecutive patients with stroke who received intravenous alteplase, fibrinogen < 3.6 g/L was associated with a 3-month excellent outcome, while patients with fibrinogen ≥ 3.6 g/L displayed almost no clinical response but also exhibited higher mortality [6]. Data on the effect of fibrinogen on intravenous thrombolysis in the extended time window is limited. The results of this post hoc analysis suggest that baseline fibrinogen levels might be useful to identify patients who are more likely to derive benefit from tenecteplase after stroke, but these results should be replicated by others before they influence practice.

To our knowledge, this is the first analysis to evaluate the effect of fibrinogen on tenecteplase thrombolysis in acute ischaemic stroke due to LVO based on data from a randomised controlled trial. In addition, the vast majority of participants in TRACE-III had fibrinogen data available and were included in this study. These advantages may strengthen the applicability of our result. However, this study has some limitations. First, tenecteplase has higher fibrin specificity and does not cause fibrinogen degradation [12]. Therefore, the mechanism through which fibrinogen level influenced the efficacy of tenecteplase remains unclear. Second, only a minority of patients had fibrinogen > 4.0 g/L and thus caution is needed when interpreting the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase therapy among patients with stroke who had elevated fibrinogen levels. The non-significant effect in this subgroup analysis may be attributed to limited statistical power. Third, the incidence of sICH was low in the study, which may reduce our statistical power to detect the relationship between tenecteplase treatments, fibrinogen and intracranial haemorrhage. Finally, this study was a post hoc analysis, so our findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and need to be confirmed by other studies. Dynamic changes in fibrinogen, their combination with other biomarkers, and imaging features of intracranial haemorrhage after intravenous thrombolysis, including intraventricular extension, which has been shown to be associated with poor outcome [29], also warrant further investigation.

Conclusions

Among patients with LVO who are not eligible for thrombectomy within 4.5 to 24 h after stroke onset, tenecteplase may have a more pronounced effect on the reduction of disability, without increasing the incidence of sICH, in patients with non-elevated fibrinogen levels.

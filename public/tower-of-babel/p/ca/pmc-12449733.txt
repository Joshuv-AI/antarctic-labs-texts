Impact of cryoballoon size adjustability on procedural and clinical outcomes
Authors: Naoya Kikkoji, Shinsuke Miyazaki, Junichi Nitta, Takeshi Sasaki, Yasuteru Yamauchi, Shu Yamashita, Yuichiro Sagawa, Yasuhiro Shirai, Osamu Inaba, Atsushi Suzuki, Akira Mizukami, Makoto Suzuki, Yukihiro Inamura, Kazuto Hayasaka, Hirofumi Arai, Yuhei Isonaga, Kentaro Goto, Rena Nakamura, Atsushi Takahashi, Hitoshi Hachiya, Shinsuke Iwai, Tetsuo Sasano
Published: 2025

A fixed 28 mm balloon size has been one of the limitations of cryoballoon (CB) technology.1,2 Recently, a size-adjustable CB (POLARx FIT, Boston Scientific) (FIT-CB) was introduced, which allows switching balloon size (28 or 31 mm) within a single catheter by adjusting the inner balloon pressure (2.5 or 7.5 psig).3,4 The larger balloon size may enable more proximal antral engagement, create a wider ablation area,5 and reduce risk of phrenic nerve injury (PNI). This study aimed to evaluate the impact of balloon size adjustability on procedural and clinical outcomes by comparing real-world data between the FIT-CB and fixed 28 mm CB (POLARx, Boston Scientific) (POLARx-CB).

This study included 2874 consecutive atrial fibrillation (AF) patients who underwent initial pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) using the POLARx-CB or FIT-CB between January 2022 and January 2025. Data were extracted from the TMDU-ablation registry (UMIN 000047063), which prospectively enrols all catheter ablation cases at 20 Japanese centres. Patient consent was waived due to anonymization. The study was approved by each hospital’s institutional review board and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Anticoagulation management followed the latest consensus documents1 and guidelines.2 A spiral mapping catheter (POLARMAP) and steerable sheath (POLARSHEATH) were used. In the POLARx-CB group, only the 28 mm size was available. In the FIT-CB group, balloon size (28 or 31 mm) for each vein was selected at the operator’s discretion. After confirming vein occlusion with contrast, 180–240 s freezes were applied without bonus applications, with dose adjusted by time to isolation. The diaphragmatic electromyogram was monitored during right pulmonary vein (PV) ablation using diaphragmatic movement sensors. If PNI was suspected, freezing was aborted with active deflation. The minimal cut-off temperature was −70°C. Oesophageal temperatures were monitored at the operators’ discretion (cut-off: 15–20°C). For PVs not isolated by CBs, touch-up ablation with an irrigated-tip catheter was performed. Additional ablation beyond PVI was done at the operators’ discretion.

Follow-up was performed according to the latest guidelines,1,2 with outpatient visits at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. A 24-h–7-day Holter ECG was performed beyond the blanking period. Recurrence was defined as atrial arrhythmia > 30 s after the blanking period. Continuous data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and were compared using Student’s t-test. Categorical variables were compared using χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test (<5 events). Kaplan–Meier with log-rank test assessed arrhythmia recurrence and PNI recovery. To evaluate balloon type (POLARx-CB vs. FIT-CB) as a predictor of PNI and AF recurrence, multivariate logistic and Cox regression analyses were performed, adjusting for baseline characteristics significant in univariate analysis (P < 0.05). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Among 2874 patients [69.0 ± 11.1 years, 893 women, 1775 with paroxysmal AF (PAF)], PVI was performed using POLARx-CBs in 1421 and FIT-CBs in 1453. The POLARx-CB group was older and had a lower body mass index, smaller LA size, and more PAF patients than the FIT-CB group. Touch-up radiofrequency ablation was performed in 294 (10.2%), and additional ablation beyond PVI in 1935(67.3%) (Table 1). The mean procedure and fluoroscopy times were 124 ± 58 and 31 ± 20 min, respectively. In the FIT-CB group, where detailed data were available, 581/80(72.4%) left superior PVs (LSPVs), 363/799(45.4%) left inferior PVs (LIPVs), 646/829(77.9%) right superior PVs (RSPVs), and 309/831(37.2%) right inferior PVs (RIPVs) were isolated by a 31 mm balloon.

Patient characteristics and procedural results

Radiofrequency touch-up ablation was required in 294 (10.2%) patients: LSPVs 94(3.3%), LIPVs 67(2.3%), RSPVs 106(3.7%), and RIPVs 110(3.8%). Touch-up rates were 16.4% for longstanding persistent AF, 13.0% for persistent AF, and 8.1% for PAF. It was significantly higher with FIT-CBs than POLARx-CBs for left PVs in PAF and significantly lower with FIT-CBs than POLARx-CBs for right PVs in non-PAF. Additional ablation beyond PVs was performed in 1935 (67.3%) patients, more frequently in the FIT-CB group, particularly roof and posterior wall ablation, yet total procedure times were similar. Right PNI occurred in 123 (4.3%) patients without significant differences between groups; it occurred during RSPV and RIPV ablation in 85 (3.0%) and 35 (1.2%) patients, respectively (Table 1). The PNI incidence was similar across all years (P = 0.73). Phrenic nerve injury persisted in 54.1% at 1 month, 44.5% at 3 months, 28.7% at 6 months, 13.7% at 9 months, and 4.6% at 12 months, with a trend towards faster recovery in the FIT-CB group (P = 0.086). One-year arrhythmia freedom was 84.3% (PAF, 87.7%; persistent AF, 80.6%; and longstanding persistent AF, 73.8%), similar between groups (85.0% POLARx-CB vs. 83.2% FIT-CB; P = 0.44) and across all years (P = 0.47). Balloon type was not a predictor of PNI (P = 0.49) or AF recurrence (P = 0.88) even after adjusting for potential confounding factors in multivariate analysis.

Contrary to expectations, the main findings of this study were that size adjustability was not associated with a lower touch-up rate, PNI incidence, or better clinical outcomes in real-world practice. The higher proportion of non-paroxysmal AF patients in the FIT-CB group was reasonable, as the larger balloon may enable broader substrate modification.5 The comparable touch-up rate between the two CBs may reflect the difficulty of occlusion with the stiffer 31 mm balloon. Interestingly, in the FIT-CB group, the touch-up rate was higher for the left PVs in paroxysmal AF but lower for the right PVs in non-paroxysmal AF compared with the POLARx-CB group. Larger PVs in non-paroxysmal patients may increase PNI risk with the 28 mm balloon, while tight PV-LA electrical connections in the left carina of paroxysmal patients may reduce durability after 31 mm balloon ablation at a more antral site.

Higher PNI incidence with POLARx-CB than conventional CB (Arctic Front Advance, Medtronic) has been reported.6,7 In our study, the incidence was comparable to the ANTARCTICA study,8 but notably, the PNI rate was similar between groups despite most RSPVs being isolated with a 31 mm balloon in the FIT-CB group. This may reflect the 14.4% larger contact area of the 31 mm vs. 28 mm9 balloon and its closer proximity to the phrenic nerve from more forceful advancement for occlusion. A recent study also showed PNI can occur with a 31 mm balloon, even when absent during prior 28 mm application.10 As this study reflected real-world practice, no fixed dosing protocol was used; ablation duration, repeat applications, and additional ablation beyond CB-PVI were at operators’ discretion. POLARx-CB was later replaced by FIT-CB.

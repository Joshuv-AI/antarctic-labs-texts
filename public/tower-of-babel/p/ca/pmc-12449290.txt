Low expression of HSP27 and HSP70 predicts poor prognosis in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Jędrzej Borowczak, Dariusz Łaszczych, Adrianna Czyżnikiewicz, Andrzej Marszałek, Łukasz Szylberg, Magdalena Bodnar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeMolecular alterations drive the pathogenesis of laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC), yet reliable prognostic biomarkers remain elusive. Heat shock proteins (HSPs), which mediate cellular stress responses, are implicated in cancer progression and treatment resistance. This study aimed to evaluate whether HSP27 and HSP70 expression correlate with clinicopathological features and survival outcomes in LSCC. Specifically, we assessed their potential as prognostic biomarkers in this malignancy.MethodsImmunohistochemistry was performed on 158 LSCC tissue samples from 40 patients and compared to 30 normal laryngeal tissue samples. Expression levels of HSP27 and HSP70 were correlated with clinicopathological variables. Validation was conducted using transcriptomic and survival data from 112 LSCC cases in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression analyses were used to assess survival.ResultsHSP27 was significantly overexpressed in LSCC tissues compared to controls and was associated with advanced tumor stage, nodal metastasis, alcohol abstinence, and older age. HSP70 expression correlated with higher tumor grade and female sex but did not differ significantly between cancerous and noncancerous tissues. In the TCGA cohort, low expression of HSP27 and HSP70 was significantly associated with worse overall survival. Low HSP27 expression emerged as an independent predictor of shorter survival (hazard ratio 2.28; 95% confidence interval, 1.11–4.67; p = 0.024).ConclusionHSP27 and HSP70 show potential as prognostic biomarkers in LSCC, with high expression linked to favorable outcomes. These findings warrant further investigation into their mechanistic roles in tumor progression, therapy resistance, and their potential utility as therapeutic targets.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-025-06309-4.Keywords: Laryngeal cancer, Heat shock proteins, Survival, HSP27, HSP70, Prognosis

Introduction

Laryngeal cancer (LC) is among the most common head and neck malignancies, with approximately 185,000 new cases and 100,000 deaths annually worldwide (Huang et al. 2023). Prognosis is highly dependent on early detection and tumor site: the 5-year survival rate is 87% for stage I glottic tumors, 50% for subglottic tumors, and just 22.2% for metastatic subglottic disease (Sexton et al. 2023). The vast majority of LC cases (> 90%) are histologically classified as squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) (Ciolofan et al. 2017).

Laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) develops through accumulated genetic alterations that promote unchecked proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, often diminishing responsiveness to radiotherapy and chemotherapy (Liberale et al. 2023). Despite advancements in clinical care, no molecular biomarkers with validated prognostic or predictive value have been established for LSCC (Machiels et al. 2020), highlighting the need for novel markers to guide diagnosis and treatment.

Heat shock proteins (HSPs) comprise a family of cellular-stress-response proteins that play a key role in protein folding, maintenance, and degradation. They can also regulate cellular processes such as growth, proliferation, and apoptosis. Under stressful stimuli, heat shock factors (HSF), transcription factors for HSPs, undergo phosphorylation and trimerization. Phosphorylated HSF trimers are translocated to the nucleus, where they initiate HSP gene transcription, leading to increased HSP expression and cell survival (Verma et al. 2021). Given their central role in cellular homeostasis, dysregulation of HSPs is increasingly recognized as a contributor to tumorigenesis. The pro-oncogenic potential of HSPs has been demonstrated in various malignancies, including esophagogastric, prostate, lung, and colorectal cancer (Fu and Jia 2022; Wu et al. 2023).

HSP27, a small HSP, exerts cytoprotective effects by inhibiting apoptosis and autophagy, modulating glutathione levels, stabilizing the cytoskeleton network, and providing antioxidant defense (Shan et al. 2021). It has been associated with tumorigenesis in multiple cancers, including breast, prostate, gastric, lung, colorectal, and glioblastoma (Alberti et al. 2022; Hoter et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2020; Mittal and Rajala 2020; Asgharzadeh et al. 2022; Babi et al. 2022). Despite several preclinical and clinical trials targeting HSP27, meaningful clinical advancements have not yet been realized (Choi et al. 2019).

HSP70 is a central mediator of proteostasis, assisting in protein folding, degradation, and refolding. It also prevents the aggregation of misfolded proteins (Fernández-Fernández and Valpuesta 2018). The HSP70 chaperone system is considered one of the most vital cellular defense networks against stress-induced damage, and disruptions in this system are implicated in various diseases (Rosenzweig et al. 2019). HSP70 contributes to tumorigenesis through activation of oncogenic pathways, inhibition of tumor suppressor genes, including tumor protein p53 (TP53), evasion of immune surveillance, promotion of pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, and induction of angiogenesis (Albakova et al. 2020). HSP70 involvement has been reported in breast, colorectal, lung, and pancreatic cancer (Vostakolaei et al. 2019). Although clinical trials investigating HSP70 inhibitors have shown modest results, immunotherapeutic approaches using HSP70-based vaccines are under exploration (Zhao et al. 2023).

The role of HSP27 and HSP70 in the tumorigenesis and prognosis of laryngeal cancer remains incompletely understood. Nevertheless, given their pro-oncogenic activity in other malignancies, these proteins may serve as valuable biomarkers in LSCC. This study investigates the expression and clinical significance of HSP27 and HSP70 in advanced LSCC. By elucidating their roles in tumor progression and patients' survival, our findings may help uncover their potential as prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets in LSCC.

Materials and methods

Sample acquisition

This study included 158 squamous cell laryngeal carcinoma tissue samples obtained from 40 patients who underwent total laryngectomy between 2009 and 2015. From each tumor, 2 to 4 representative tissue cores were collected to account for spatial heterogeneity within the cancer. The study cohort comprised 5 women (aged 52–61 years) and 35 men (aged 45–79 years). As a control group, 30 samples of normal (non-neoplastic) laryngeal tissue, identified within the resection margins, were analyzed.. Clinical data, including patient age, sex, overall survival, TNM Classification of Malignant Tumors (TNM), tumor grade (G), and recurrence status, were collected and summarized in Table 1. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, and the protocol was approved by the local Bioethics Committee (approval number KB 470/2012).

Tissue microarrays

Histopathological specimens stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) were initially screened to identify the representative areas of tumor and normal tissue. The archival formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples were mixed with wax to create soft blocks of uniform height. Tissue microarrays (TMAs) were obtained by cutting representative tissue fragments from the "donor" block using the TMA Master 1.14 SP1 device (3D HISTECH, Hungary). Subsequently, 3 μm-thick specimens were cut from TMA blocks using a manual rotary microtome (Accu-Cut® SRM™ 200, Sakura, Japan). The paraffin sections were heated at 50–60 °C to improve adhesion, and HE staining was performed to verify the correctness of the sampled tissue material prior to immunohistochemistry.

Immunohistochemistry

A retrospective immunohistochemical analysis of HSP27 and HSP70 was performed on 158 FFPE LSCC tissue blocks. In brief, 3 μm-thick sections of the tissue microarrays were baked for 1 h at 60 °C, followed by deparaffinization in xylene and rehydration through a graded ethanol series (99.8, 96, 90, and 80%). For antigen detection, tissue sections were incubated with a primary rabbit monoclonal anti-HSP27 (1:6000, 40 min; AB155987, Abcam) and anti-HSP70 antibodies (1:2000, 40 min; AB47455, Abcam). Immunoreactivity was visualized using the UltraView Universal DAB Detection Kit (Roche Diagnostics/Ventana, USA), followed by color development using 3,3-diaminobenzidine as the chromogen. The slides were counterstained with Hematoxylin II for 12 min and treated with Bluing Reagent for 4 min. Finally, tissue sections were dehydrated in ascending concentrations of ethanol (80, 90, 96, and 99.8%), cleared in xylenes (I–IV), mounted with a synthetic medium, and microscopically evaluated.

Image acquisition and analysis

Clinical data were blinded to ensure unbiased evaluation. Two representative images of each tissue sample were captured using a Nikon optical microscope at 20 × magnification, equipped with a color video camera connected to a computer system (Fig. S1A). Antigen localization was determined by the presence of brown chromogenic staining, indicating the formation of an antigen–primary antibody–secondary antibody complex. Quantitative morphometric analysis of HSP expression was conducted using ImageJ software (version 1.50j; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). The "Color Deconvolution" plugin was utilized to separate H&E and 3,3'-diaminobenzidine DAB stains (Fig. S1B). Grayscale images were converted to 8-bit format using the "Threshold" function (Fig. S1C). A negative image and binary "mask" were created to enable noise filtration and removal of visual artifacts (Fig. S1D). This processed mask was then applied to the original image to identify immunohistochemically labeled regions, which were subjected to automated measurements. The average and median staining intensity of HSP27 and HSP70 expression were quantified on a scale from 0 to 250 (Fig. S1E). The percentage of positively stained area within the squamous cell carcinoma cells was calculated (Fig. S1F). Samples in which the calculated staining intensity for HSP27 or HSP70 expression equaled zero were considered negative for expression of the respective protein. No additional threshold was applied; any positive value (> 0) was classified as expression-positive. To account for tumor heterogeneity, we usually analyzed two representative cores from each tumor. In some cases, where the tissue showed more variation or artifacts, up to four cores were included. Each core was treated as a separate statistical unit to reflect differences within the tumor and allow for more reliable analysis. This approach is a standard practice in morphometric analysis of TMAs, as it allows for better representation of spatial variability and mitigates the impact of sampling bias from any single region.

In silico analysis

Clinical data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma (HNSCC) cohort were retrieved via the cBioPortal platform (Cerami et al. 2012). Normalized expression data for HSP27 and HSP70 were obtained from the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC) Xena browser (Cerami et al. 2012). Fragments per kilobase of transcript per million (FPKM) gene expression were sourced from the Human Protein Atlas (Expression of HSPB1 in Cancer 2024; Expression of HSPA1A in Cancer. 2024). Normalized gene expression values were calculated using the formula log2 (FPKM + 1). For the purpose of survival analysis and stratification, expression cut-off thresholds were defined as 2994 FPKM for HSP27 and 1.65 FPKM for HSP70.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Statistica version 13.3 (Statsoft) and Microsoft Excel 2019. The distribution of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk and Lilliefors tests for normality. For intergroup comparisons, the t test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were applied to normally distributed data, while the Mann–Whitney U test and Kruskal–Wallis ANOVA were used for non-normally distributed variables. Correlations between HSP27 and HSP70 expression levels and clinicopathological parameters were analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Univariate and multivariate survival analyses were performed using the Cox proportional hazards regression model to identify potential predictors of overall survival. The optimal expression cut-off points for HSP27 and HSP70 were determined using the Cutoff Finder tool (Budczies et al. 2012). Results were expressed as hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI). Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated, and differences in survival between groups were assessed using the log-rank test. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

HSP27 is overexpressed in squamous cell laryngeal cancer

Immunohistochemistry revealed distinct staining for HSP27 and HSP70 in LSCC tissues (Fig. 1A, B). HSP27 was expressed in 97.3% (143/147) of cancer samples and was significantly higher than in normal tissue (mean intensity: 42.7 vs. 20.3; p = 0.0003). In contrast, HSP70 expression was observed in 94 out of 122 stained tumor samples (77.0%). However, its expression levels did not significantly differ between cancerous and normal laryngeal tissues (mean intensity: 17.8 vs. 17.3; p = 0.95). These findings suggest a tumor-specific overexpression of HSP27 in LSCC, while HSP70 expression appears more variable and not significantly altered relative to normal laryngeal tissue.

In silico analysis

To validate our findings, we analyzed HSP27 and HSP70 mRNA expression levels in the TCGA HNSCC dataset using the UCSC XENA browser (Table S1). Among the 492 cases available, 112 LSCC samples were selected for detailed analysis.

mRNA transcript levels of HSP27 (10.2 vs. 10.1; p = 0.62) and HSP70 (5.1 vs. 4.8; p = 0.60) did not differ significantly between tumor and adjacent non-neoplastic laryngeal tissue (Fig. S2).

No correlations were observed between HSP27 and HSP70 expression and tumor stage, nodal status, metastasis, sex, age, or race (p > 0.05). Expression levels also showed no association with other genes involved in the HSP regulatory network or cancer-related pathways, including TP53, PIK3CA, RB1, CDKN2A, MKI67, EPHA5, or LRP1B (p > 0.05).

To investigate the prognostic significance of HSP expression, we dichotomized LSCC samples into high and low expression groups for both HSP27 and HSP70, based on FPKM values derived from TCGA RNA-sequencing data. Survival analysis revealed that patients with low HSP27 expression had significantly shorter overall survival compared to those with high expression (p = 0.006). Similarly, low HSP70 expression was associated with poorer overall survival (p = 0.04) (Fig. 2).

Discussion

Expression patterns of HSP27 and HSP70 in LSCC

We found that HSP27, but not HSP70, was significantly overexpressed in LSCC (Fig. 1A, B). HSP27 expression increased with tumor advancement and lymph node metastasis and was significantly higher in older patients and those abstaining from alcohol.. Additionally, HSP27 expression was significantly higher in older patients (> 60 years), while no association was found between HSP27 expression and histological tumor grade (Table 2). Conversely, HSP70 expression increased along tumor grade, but showed no significant association with tumor stage or nodal involvement. HSP70 expression was also significantly higher in female patients (Table 3). Our in silico validation revealed that low expression levels of both HSP27 and HSP70 were significantly associated with worse overall survival (Fig. 2). Furthermore, multivariate Cox regression analysis identified low HSP27 expression, female sex, and disease progression as independent prognostic factors for reduced survival in LSCC.

Prognostic and therapeutic implications of HSP27

Our study confirms that HSP27 is overexpressed in LSCC and that its level correlates with tumor stage, reinforcing the potential role of HSP27 in tumor progression. These results align with previous studies. Karam et al. reported a significant overexpression of HSP27 in LSCC tissue, with expression gradually increasing with the clinical stage of the disease (Karam et al. 2017). Kaigorodova et al. found that HSP27 expression was significantly elevated in LSCC cases with lymph node metastases, and that it positively correlated with the number of metastatic nodes. Interestingly, their study also observed that negative nuclear expression of HSP27 was associated with longer survival in LSCC, suggesting a potentially complex role of subcellular localization of HSP27 in tumor behavior (Kaigorodova et al. 2016). Further supporting HSP27's role in early tumorigenesis, Pitruzzella et al. demonstrated that HSP27 expression was significantly increased even at the stage of laryngeal dysplasia, a known precursor to LSCC (Pitruzzella et al. 2024), supporting its role in malignant transformation (Hrelec 2021). While our findings are largely consistent with existing literature, there are conflicting reports. Xu et al. found no significant difference in HSP27 expression between LSCC tissue and normal laryngeal mucosa (Xu et al. 1998), highlighting the need for larger, more standardized studies to fully elucidate its role in LSCC tumorigenesis.

The pro-oncogenic role of HSP27 in LSCC may be largely attributed to its anti-apoptotic effects in response to cytotoxic stress. Experimental studies using Hep-2 laryngeal cancer cells have shown that HSP27 overexpression confers resistance to both hyperthermia and cisplatin treatment. This cytoprotective effect is linked to cell cycle arrest at the G1 phase, which attenuates proliferation and enhances cell survival. Mechanistically, this process involves the stabilization of F-actin fibers, which contributes to the preservation of cellular architecture and apoptotic resistance (Lee et al. 2007). Supporting this, Paduch et al. reported increased HSP27 expression in Hep-2 cells following exposure to cisplatin and combination cisplatin/etoposide therapy, reinforcing its role in chemoresistance (Paduch et al. 2007). Additionally, Heiserman et al. demonstrated in ovarian cancer models that HSP27 reduces cisplatin-induced cytotoxicity by suppressing reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation through activation of the pentose phosphate pathway. Notably, knockdown of HSP27 using ivermectin restored cisplatin sensitivity and enhanced its anticancer effects, suggesting that HSP27 inhibition could serve as a chemosensitizing strategy (Heiserman et al. 2023).

Cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy remains a cornerstone in the treatment of locally advanced LSCC (Machiels et al. 2020). The primary cytotoxic mechanism of cisplatin involves apoptosis induction via oxidative stress and DNA damage (Tchounwou et al. 2021). Given HSP27's capacity to mitigate oxidative damage and prevent apoptosis, high HSP27 expression may contribute to cisplatin resistance in LSCC. Therefore, assessing HSP27 in tumor tissue could serve as a predictive biomarker of chemotherapy response, and targeting HSP27 may represent a novel therapeutic avenue to enhance cisplatin-based chemotherapy efficacy in LSCC management.

Beyond chemoresistance, HSP27 also appears to contribute to radioresistance in LSCC. Experimental evidence from the radioresistant SQ20B laryngeal carcinoma cell line has shown that HSP27 gene knockout significantly increases radiosensitivity, facilitating radiation-induced apoptosis. Mechanistically, HSP27 depletion in cancer cells leads to elevated ROS, decreased glutathione levels, mitochondrial collapse, and caspase activation, all of which promote cell death in response to irradiation (Aloy et al. 2008). Since HSP27 may be a key factor in cancer cells' survival and resistance to chemo- and radiotherapy, targeting this chaperone protein emerges as a compelling therapeutic strategy. In this context, Badziul et al. demonstrated that the combined use of quercetin and imperatorin, two plant-derived compounds with known antiproliferative properties, exhibited pro-apoptotic effects in Hep-2 cells. Their coadministration led to significant downregulation of HSP27 and enhanced cytotoxicity. Notably, the effect was even more pronounced when combined with siRNA-mediated HSP27 knockdown, which further increased the proportion of apoptotic cells (Bądziul et al. 2014). These results suggest that HSP27 downregulation can sensitize LSCC cells to natural apoptosis-promoting agents and highlight a promising therapeutic avenue for overcoming treatment resistance in LSCC. The combination of HSP27-targeted strategies with established or novel cytotoxic agents may pave the way for more effective multimodal therapies in the management of radio- and chemoresistant laryngeal carcinoma.

Interestingly, our in silico analysis revealed that low expression of HSP27 is associated with poorer overall survival in patients with LSCC, a finding that contrasts with its postulated pro-oncogenic roles. To our knowledge, our research is the first to evaluate the prognostic role of HSP27 expression in LSCC, making direct comparisons with previous LSCC-specific research unavailable. According to the broader literature, the prognostic impact of HSP27 remains ambiguous and context-dependent. For instance, high expression of HSP27 has been linked to shorter survival in esophageal adenocarcinoma and colorectal cancer, whereas its downregulation is associated with poor outcomes in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (Yang et al. 2021). Given its established role in avoiding apoptosis and supporting cell survival under stress, one would typically expect HSP27 overexpression to correlate with more aggressive tumor behavior and worse prognosis. However, our findings suggest the opposite in LSCC, and the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. One plausible explanation is that HSP27 overexpression, while reflective of more advanced diseases, may also coincide with a more aggressive therapeutic approach, such as intensified chemoradiotherapy. Thus, patients with high HSP27 expression may receive more comprehensive treatment, which could offset the protein's anti-apoptotic effects and result in improved survival outcomes. This hypothesis aligns with studies showing that HSP27 is more prevalent in advanced LSCC, where aggressive treatment regimens are commonly employed (Arboleda et al. 2023). These results highlight the complex and potentially dual role of HSP27 in cancer biology, as both a facilitator of tumor progression and a possible surrogate marker of therapeutic intensity. Further mechanistic studies are warranted to better understand this paradox and to determine whether HSP27 expression can be utilized not only as a prognostic biomarker but also as a predictive marker of treatment response.

Another potential explanation for our findings relates to the physiological role of HSP27 as a cytoprotective protein. HSP27 expression should increase in response to cytotoxic stimuli, such as radiation or chemotherapeutic agents, as part of the cellular stress response. In the context of LSCC, patients with more advanced disease often receive more intensive treatments, including chemoradiotherapy. Thus, the observed increase in tissue HSP27 expression in these patients may represent a reactive adaptation to therapeutic stress, rather than an intrinsic feature of tumor biology (Lampros et al. 2022). From this perspective, the association between high HSP27 expression and improved overall survival could reflect the benefits of more aggressive management rather than a direct protective role of HSP27 itself. In this scenario, HSP27 overexpression would serve as a marker of treatment exposure, particularly in advanced cases. However, it remains unclear whether chemotherapy or radiotherapy directly induces HSP27 expression in LSCC tissues. For example, Bodzek et al. found no significant difference in serum HSP27 levels between ovarian cancer patients who received primary chemotherapy and those who did not (Bodzek et al. 2021). Nevertheless, this study did not assess HSP27 expression within tumor tissue. In addition, studies evaluating the effect of radio or chemotherapy on tissue HSP27 expression, especially in the context of LSCC, are lacking. To help understand our understanding in this area, future research should focus on evaluating tissue-level HSP27 expression before and after therapeutic interventions. Understanding the extent to which HSP27 is induced by treatment will be essential in distinguishing its role as a prognostic biomarker from its function as a predictive marker of treatment response.

The potential linkage between HSP27 expression and alcohol use in LSCC

Surprisingly, our results showed that patients with a history of alcohol use exhibited significantly lower HSP27 expression in LSCC tissue. This finding is counterintuitive, as ethanol is generally considered a stress-inducing agent that activates HSFs, leading to increased HSP expression. Indeed, several studies report that acute ethanol exposure stimulates HSP27 upregulation. However, evidence also suggests that chronic ethanol exposure may have the opposite effect, potentially impairing stress-response pathways over time. For example, Nakahara et al. demonstrated in a murine model that prolonged ethanol exposure (6–7 weeks) significantly reduced HSP27 mRNA levels in hindlimb skeletal muscle cells, indicating that sustained cellular stress may lead to dysregulation of cytoprotective gene expression (Nakahara et al. 2006). Similarly, Maier et al. reported that chronic alcohol consumption (6 months) in rats led to structural alterations in the laryngeal epithelium, including reduced epithelial thickness, increased basal cell mitosis, and nuclear atypia (Maier et al. 1999,1). These histological changes resemble those seen in laryngeal dysplasia, a known precursor to LSCC (Thompson 2017). Taken together, these findings suggest that long-term ethanol exposure may contribute to both LSCC development and altered HSP27 expression, possibly through chronic stress-induced suppression of protective molecular pathways. It is plausible that the cytoprotective HSP27 network becomes dysregulated or downregulated in the setting of alcohol-associated tumorigenesis. In conclusion, our observation that HSP27 is downregulated in alcohol-exposed LSCC patients may reflect a maladaptive response to prolonged ethanol-induced stress. However, this remains speculative, and further studies are needed to clarify the impact of alcohol on HSP27 expression and the broader heat shock response in LSCC pathogenesis.

HSP70 in tumor biology and survival

In this study, we found that HSP70 expression in LSCC tissue did not differ significantly from that in normal laryngeal tissue, but it was positively associated with tumor grade and female sex. These findings suggest that HSP70 may serve as a useful adjunct in histopathological evaluation, particularly in the assessment of tumor differentiation, and may have potential as a prognostic biomarker in LSCC. However, the broader role of HSP70 in LSCC tumorigenesis, prognosis, and therapeutic response remains incompletely understood and is complicated by conflicting findings in the literature. For instance, Shi et al. reported a nearly five-fold increase in HSP70 expression in LSCC tissue, suggesting a prominent role in tumor biology (Shi et al. 1998). In contrast, Xu et al. observed higher HSP70 expression in late-stage LSCC compared to early-stage tumors, a finding not replicated in our cohort (Xu et al. 2010). Barańska et al. further reported a positive correlation between HSP70 concentration in lymph node cells and tumor volume, although they did not find a relationship with TNM staging. The authors hypothesized that HSP70 may be passively transferred to adjacent lymph nodes via lymphatic drainage, resulting in higher concentrations with increasing tumor burden. Since HSP70 exhibits pro-cancerous and anti-apoptotic activity, its overexpression may facilitate tumor progression. However, the study’s small sample size (n = 16) and lack of a control group limit the generalizability of its conclusions (Barańska et al. 2019). Our findings are more closely aligned with those of Yang et al. who demonstrated that HSP70 expression correlates with histological differentiation in LSCC, supporting its potential role in tumor grading (Yang et al. 2007). A similar relationship between high pathological grade and elevated HSP70 expression has been reported in other malignancies, including pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and renal cell carcinoma, reinforcing the relevance of HSP70 in poorly differentiated tumor phenotypes (Wang et al. 2020; Abd El-Fadeal et al. 2023). Taken together, while our results suggest a role for HSP70 in LSCC pathology, its functional significance and prognostic implications remain unclear, underscoring the need for larger, controlled studies to resolve inconsistencies and to clarify whether HSP70 could serve as a reliable biomarker or therapeutic target in this cancer type.

Our in silico analysis revealed that low expression of HSP70 is significantly associated with poorer overall survival in patients with LSCC. To our knowledge, this study is the first to report a potential link between HSP70 expression and survival outcomes in LSCC, making direct comparisons with prior research in this cancer type unavailable. In contrast, existing literature across other malignancies generally links low HSP70 expression with more favourable prognosis, including breast, pancreatic, renal, lung, and colorectal cancers (Wang et al. 2020; Abd El-Fadeal et al. 2023; Dimas et al. 2018; Qiao et al. 2021; Gao et al. 2021). This discrepancy makes the interpretation of our results particularly challenging. The opposite prognostic trend observed in LSCC may reflect context-specific tumor biology or differences in treatment response and warrants deeper investigation. A possible explanation, similar to our interpretation of HSP27 findings, is that high expression of HSP70 may be more common in poorly differentiated tumors, as our data suggest a correlation between HSP70 levels and higher tumor grade. More aggressive cancer biology (which is strictly associated with tumor grade) may favor a more intensive or multimodal treatment, which may lead to better survival outcomes in this subgroup. While this hypothesis is plausible, it remains speculative and lacks direct supporting evidence. Our findings highlight the complexity and context dependency of HSP70’s prognostic role in cancer. Further studies are needed to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying HSP70 overexpression in LSCC, its potential role in therapy response, and its value as a biomarker for prognosis or treatment stratification.

HSP70 expression may vary across different stages of LSCC progression, particularly between primary, metastatic, and recurrent LSCC. In a study by Bodnar et al. HSP70 was found to be significantly downregulated in cancer cells derived from both recurrent and metastatic LSCC compared to the cells from primary tumors (Bodnar et al. 2016). This observation is unexpected, given HSP70's well-established role in inhibiting apoptosis, in part through the suppression of TP53 activity, and in facilitating tumor progression. Based on these pro-survival and tumor-promoting functions, one might anticipate higher HSP70 expression in more aggressive disease stages, such as relapse and metastasis. However, the observed downregulation suggests that HSP70 may play a more prominent role during the initial stages of LSCC and may become less relevant in the biology of recurrent or disseminated disease. It is also possible that tumor cells adapt by shifting reliance to alternative survival pathways as the disease progresses. These findings underscore the need for further mechanistic studies to explore the role of HSP70 in recurrent and metastatic LSCC and to determine whether targeting HSP70 could have stage-specific therapeutic implications.

Notably, persistent infection of the laryngeal epithelium with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) may be implicated in laryngeal carcinogenesis through modulation of HSP70 expression. Experimental studies have shown that RSV-infected Hep-2 cells exhibit marked upregulation of microRNA 146a-5p, which in turn leads to the suppression of HSP70 expression (Eilam-Frenkel et al. 2018). This suggests that viral-induced disruption of the heat shock response may contribute to tumor initiation or progression.

HSP70 and therapy resistance

Beyond its role in tumor biology, HSP70 also appears to influence the response of LSCC cells to radiotherapy. Downregulation of HSP70 using antisense oligonucleotides has been shown to increase radiosensitivity and promote apoptosis in LSCC tumor models in vivo (Xu et al. 2010). One of the underlying mechanisms involves the degradation of nucleolin, a multifunctional nuclear protein that acts as a key downstream effector of the HSP70 anti-apoptotic pathway (Jiang et al. 2010). In addition to mediating stress survival, nucleolin may also contribute to tumor radioresistance by participating in DNA repair, particularly through the activation of DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs), which is essential for repairing DNA double-strand breaks (Xu et al. 2015). These findings suggest that the HSP70–nucleolin axis may play a significant role in modulating radiosensitivity and may represent a therapeutic target to enhance treatment efficacy in LSCC.

Given its pro-survival activity under stress conditions, HSP70 may contribute to cancer cell resistance to hyperthermia therapy. However, under specific conditions, combining hyperthermia with chemotherapy has shown therapeutic promise in LSCC. The duration of thermal exposure appears to be a critical determinant of treatment efficacy. In vitro studies have demonstrated that Hep-2 cells exposed to hyperthermia for more than 10 min exhibit a significant upregulation of HSP70, whereas shorter exposures do not induce this response. Interestingly, pre-treatment with thermal stimuli for 10 min sensitized Hep-2 cells to doxorubicin, as evidenced by increased caspase-3 activity and a higher percentage of apoptotic cells (Barros et al. 2017). These observations suggest that modulating the thermal dose and timing may bypass the protective effects of HSP70 and enhance the cytotoxic impact of chemotherapy. In parallel, targeting HSP70 directly has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy in LSCC. For example, Wang et al. demonstrated that transfection of Hep-2 cells with a recombinant adenoviral vector carrying antisense HSP70 cDNA significantly reduced HSP70 expression and induced apoptosis, highlighting its potential for gene therapy-based interventions (Wang et al. 2007). More recently, preclinical data from murine models have shown that VER-155008, a pharmacologic HSP70 inhibitor, can suppress LSCC tumor growth by promoting apoptosis, autophagy, and endoplasmic reticulum stress (Chen et al. 2022). These findings collectively support the notion that HSP70-targeted therapies, whether through gene silencing or small molecule inhibitors, represent a novel and potentially effective approach for enhancing treatment response and overcoming resistance in LSCC.

Study limitations and future directions

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design and single-center origin of the primary cohort may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings to broader LSCC populations. Second, the sample size, particularly in subgroup analyses, may have limited statistical power to detect more subtle associations. Third, while we performed external validation using the TCGA HNSCC dataset, this analysis was restricted to transcriptomic data, which may not fully represent protein-level expression or post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms. In addition, immunohistochemical quantification in our study relied on digital image analysis, which, although standardized and objective, may still be subject to variability due to tissue heterogeneity and staining artifacts. Finally, this study was not designed to investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying the roles of HSP27 and HSP70 in LSCC progression and therapy resistance. Functional studies are therefore needed to elucidate their biological significance and to assess their potential as therapeutic targets in a mechanistic context. Moreover, the prognostic relevance of HSP27 and HSP70 in LSCC should be validated through in vitro and in vivo models. Specifically, modulating their expression in LSCC cell lines could clarify their roles in cellular proliferation, apoptosis resistance, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and treatment response. Additionally, in vivo studies using xenograft or genetically engineered animal models are necessary to evaluate their impact on tumor growth, metastasis, and therapeutic efficacy in a physiologically relevant setting.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that HSP27 and HSP70 exhibit distinct yet clinically relevant roles in the pathobiology of LSCC. HSP27 was significantly overexpressed in tumor tissues and correlated with advanced stage and lymph node metastasis, whereas HSP70 expression was linked with higher tumor grade. Importantly, in silico validation revealed that lower expression of both HSP27 and HSP70 was associated with significantly worse overall survival, highlighting their potential as novel prognostic biomarkers in LSCC. These findings suggest that HSP27 and HSP70 may serve not only as prognostic indicators but also as promising targets for therapeutic intervention. Further research is warranted to elucidate their mechanistic roles in tumor progression, evaluate their value as predictive markers of treatment response, and explore their applicability in precision oncology and targeted therapy strategies for LSCC.

The use of transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) for severe aortic stenosis continues to expand.1 Despite advances, post-TAVR readmissions remain a significant quality and cost concern. Recent Society of Thoracic Surgeons/American College of Cardiology Transcatheter Valve Therapy (STS/ACC TVT) Registry mean 30-day readmission rate is 8.6%, with an average direct cost of $16,037 per readmission.2, 3, 4 These events disproportionately affect older adults with comorbidities and limited physiological reserve.

Take-Home Messages

•A multidisciplinary, structured transitional care pathway can sustainably reduce post-TAVR readmissions.

Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services monitors readmissions for targeted conditions, including heart failure, and hospitals with excess rates may face payment reductions under the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program.5 Given this financial and clinical impact, improving care transitions is key. Wang et al6 found that fragmented postdischarge care after TAVR worsens outcomes, underscoring the need for patient and caregiver engagement, structured follow-up, and optimized transitional care pathways.

Case Summary Prompting the Project Launch

This single-center, retrospective quality-improvement (QI) project was conducted at Lake Charles Memorial Hospital (LCMH), a 313-bed community hospital in Southwest Louisiana serving urban and rural populations. In 2019, LCMH became the first U.S. hospital to earn the ACC Transcatheter Valve Certification.

STS/ACC TVT Registry data showed a 30-day readmission rate of 12.7% in 2018-2019 (Figure 1), with 58% unrelated to TAVR (Figure 2).4 Internal data identified gastrointestinal bleeding, falls, pneumonia, anemia, back pain, and hyperglycemia as leading causes, consistent with prior reports.7 Most patients returned within 7 days post discharge, indicating gaps in care transitions.

In response to these findings, LCMH launched a phased, multidisciplinary QI initiative in 2020. Outcomes were assessed across 3 periods: preimplementation (2018-2019), implementation (2020-2021), and postimplementation (2022-Q2 2025).

Project Rationale

Implementation was prompted during the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic and a series of regional natural disasters, including hurricanes Laura and Delta, a severe freeze, and flooding, all occurring within a 6-month period. These events disrupted access to care, displaced patients, and compounded follow-up challenges, further emphasizing the need for a structured transitional care pathway.

External benchmarks also shaped this initiative. The pursuit for an ACC Transcatheter Valve Certification required meeting national benchmarks for 30-day readmissions (inpatient only) in the STS/ACC TVT Registry or establishing an action plan.

Interventions targeted non–procedure-related readmissions through improved continuity of care and procedural causes through enhanced conduction system disease monitoring and proactive heart failure management. A root cause analysis using a fishbone diagram identified key contributors, including inconsistent patient education, delays in early follow-up, and limited access to self-monitoring tools. These were organized into thematic categories using an affinity diagram, guiding strategies for education, follow-up, integration of home-based monitoring equipment, and proactive complication management.

Together, these contextual pressures, internal analyses, and external requirements guided the development of a phased, multidisciplinary implementation plan to strengthen post-TAVR care, reduce readmissions, and improve outcomes. Figure 3 summarizes these interventions and outcomes.

Project Description

A multidisciplinary team, including interventional cardiologists, a cardiothoracic surgeon, advanced practice providers, the valve clinic coordinator, case managers, discharge educators, pharmacists, coding staff, and hospital administration, developed and implemented the intervention. Guided by a Plan-Do-Study-Act framework, interventions were piloted and refined during 2020-2021 (implementation phase) and incorporated patient and staff feedback, before becoming embedded as standard practice from 2022 onward (postimplementation). The pathway encompassed 3 phases of care: preoperative, predischarge, and postdischarge (Figure 4).

Structured TAVR Education and Follow-Up Process

Workflow diagram outlining the standardized education, discharge, and follow-up process for TAVR patients. Developed using internal data and a multidisciplinary approach, the process includes early identification of social determinants of health (SDOH), tailored patient and caregiver education, provision of home monitoring equipment for high-risk patients, proactive complication monitoring, and prescheduled follow-up through 1 year. This process enhances continuity of care and reduces both procedure- and non–procedure-related readmissions. CHF = congestive heart failure; other abbreviations as in Figure 1.

Preoperative phase

At the initial clinic visit, discharge planning and recovery expectations were reviewed with patients and caregivers. Social determinants of health (SDOH) were screened to identify transportation, financial, or support barriers. Patients with identified needs were connected to local resources, including transportation assistance, gas cards, financial grants, insurance navigation, medication assistance, or meal delivery programs. Patients with conduction abnormalities predictive of progressing to complete heart block wore an ambulatory cardiac monitor, per the 2020 ACC consensus pathway,9 to guide heart team review and consideration of medication changes or permanent pacemaker (PPM) placement.

Predischarge phase

Education was provided to patients and their designated caregiver or advocate, reinforced by a simplified handout summarizing postoperative expectations, warning signs, and when/how to seek care. Medication adherence was supported through the hospital's “Meds to Beds” bedside delivery program.10 High-risk patients, such as those undergoing blood pressure medication or diuretic adjustments, received home blood pressure monitors and scales, if not already available. This was particularly important during disaster-related displacement. All patients were referred to cardiac rehabilitation prior to discharge, with enrollment encouraged during follow-up contacts, and received dietitian-led education on heart-healthy nutrition. An ambulatory cardiac monitor was applied to patients with new conduction system disease.9 All follow-up appointments were scheduled prior to discharge and provided in a written list.

Postdischarge phase

Patients were contacted by the valve clinic coordinator 3 times in week 1, twice in week 2, and once in weeks 3 and 4 after discharge. Follow-up care was coordinated to ensure completion of the 1-week valve clinic visit, 30-day and 1-year echocardiograms, and cardiology and primary care appointments, with rescheduling arranged as needed. Emergency department staff were educated to promptly notify the valve team if a recent TAVR patient presented. Hospitalists received education on early valve team consultation, and coding staff were engaged to ensure accurate classification of observation vs inpatient status.

Project Deliverables

This initiative produced a standardized transitional care pathway that emphasized patient and caregiver empowerment through early engagement, clear education, and simplified written instructions. The pathway incorporated the hospital's “Meds to Beds” medication-delivery program and targeted distribution of home monitoring equipment for high-risk patients. Structured follow-up appointments were scheduled before discharge to promote continuity of care. Clear protocols were established for early detection and management of conduction system diseases. Communication between inpatient and outpatient teams was streamlined, and discharge workflows were embedded to ensure consistent, coordinated transitions.

Project Outcome, Impact, and Future Directions

Key outcomes are summarized in Figure 3. Readmissions decreased by 85%, from 12.7% preimplementation to 8.8% during the 2020-2021 implementation phase and 1.9% in the postimplementation period (2022-2025 Q2) (Figure 1), despite a higher-risk patient population (Table 1). In the later phase, readmissions were limited to 3 cases: complete heart block requiring PPM (1 patient), gallstones requiring cholecystectomy (1 patient), and gastrointestinal bleed (1 patient). Among patients readmitted within 30 days, the mean STS score was 6.55%, 40% were categorized as high surgical risk, 55% had a frailty score ≥3/4, and 50% had stage ≥III CKD.

Thirty-day post-TAVR PPM rates were 7.45% preimplementation, 7.8% during implementation, and 5.9% postimplementation. In this later period, 1.9% of patients received PPM implantation before TAVR when clinically indicated, improving conduction system disease risk stratification.

Mean time to readmission increased from 7.0 to 11.5 days, indicating fewer early, procedure-related complications. Average length of stay decreased from 2.0 to 1.25 days, reflecting improved discharge readiness. Home monitoring equipment costs totaled $574, with an estimated $173,200 in readmission cost savings, resulting in $172,626 net savings (Table 2).

In Q4 2024, LCMH transitioned to the EPIC electronic medical record (EMR) platform, enabling integrated SDOH screening, earlier identification of at-risk patients, population health tracking, and automation of postdischarge outreach. Real-time EMR dashboards are now used alongside the STS/ACC TVT Registry to monitor key metrics and guide ongoing improvement. Future work will evaluate EMR-based alerts to trigger targeted interventions for high-risk patients.

Discussion

This QI initiative demonstrates that a structured, multidisciplinary transitional care pathway can reduce 30-day readmissions after TAVR, even amid the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic and regional natural disasters. Central to success was empowering patients and caregivers through education, self-monitoring tools, and active follow-up. Multidisciplinary heart team involvement ensured coordinated care across the continuum. Additional drivers included SDOH assessment with tailored resources, discharge processes, proactive monitoring, and rapid response. The Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle enabled continuous refinement based on feedback and outcomes, while conduction system disease risk stratification and home monitoring primarily addressed procedure-related readmissions.

As a single-center QI project, results reflect a specific patient population, and results may have been influenced by other initiatives or external factors. Indications for TAVR broadened nationally during the study period in 2019 to include low-risk patients. Although low-risk patients comprised 31% of the implementation cohort, the proportion declined to 13% post implementation. High-risk patients remained consistently represented (Table 1), suggesting that the observed reduction in readmissions was not solely driven by shifts in patient risk profile.

Sustained success depends on multidisciplinary engagement, performance monitoring through EMR-based dashboards and the STS/ACC TVT Registry, and adaptation of interventions as patient needs and program priorities evolve.

Conclusions

Implementation of a phased, multidisciplinary transitional care pathway at LCMH reduced 30-day TAVR readmissions from 12.7% to 1.9%, despite a higher-risk population and multiple regional disasters. Both procedure- and non–procedure-related readmissions declined through standardized education, early follow-up, conduction system disease monitoring, and targeted SDOH interventions. The pathway also shortened length of stay and generated substantial cost savings. These findings support structured transitional care as an effective, adaptable strategy to improve outcomes and optimize resource utilization in TAVR programs.

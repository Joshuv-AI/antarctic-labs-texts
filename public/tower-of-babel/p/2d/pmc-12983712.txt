Equity, Diversity, Inclusion and Accessibility in pharmacy education: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Ivy Kan, Marie A Vander Kloet, Regina Küfner Lein, Tamara Koehler, Marie-Rose Blokzijl, Aukje K Mantel-Teeuwisse, Mirey Alfarah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionEquity, Diversity, Inclusion and Accessibility (EDIA) are recognised as core principles in higher education, yet their practical integration into pharmacy education remains underexplored. This review aims to identify the scope of existing research, highlight knowledge gaps and provide valuable insights for pharmacy educators, researchers and policymakers seeking to enhance EDIA integration within pharmacy education.Methods and analysesThis protocol describes the methodology for a scoping review to systematically map the existing peer-reviewed literature on EDIA in pharmacy education, focusing on three critical areas: faculty development, curriculum content and teaching strategies. Using the Population, Concept and Context framework, the review will include studies examining faculty members, students and administrators within formal pharmacy education contexts worldwide. The scoping review will adhere to the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology and PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews) guidelines. The search will include peer-reviewed academic studies, accessed through databases such as MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, APA PsycINFO, ERIC and Web of Science. Backward snowballing will also be employed. Data will be charted using a predefined extraction tool, and findings will be synthesised and presented in tabular and narrative formats. A pilot search took place in March 2025, and the final search, study selection and data extraction will be conducted from May to December 2025. The subsequent analysis, presentation and interpretation of results are planned thereafter.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval is not required. We plan to share findings through a variety of means including professional networks, peer-reviewed journal publications, conference presentations, invited workshops and webinars, on the FPD-Include project website and on our research groups’ university websites.Keywords: Pharmacists, Health Education, Health Equity

Introduction

Equity, Diversity, Inclusion and Accessibility (EDIA) are frequently named as core commitments and values for public institutions, including higher education. Engaging in EDIA practices in higher education demonstrates to students that institutions are committed to fostering participation and belonging for all learners, which can help create a safer educational environment. Incorporating EDIA efforts in higher education has demonstrated various benefits, including improvements in learning outcomes such as active-thinking skills, intellectual engagement and motivation. Additionally, these efforts have been associated with students’ self-reported empathy, civic awareness and perceptions of democratic engagement.1

A clear understanding of EDIA terms is essential to integrate these concepts in higher education. Therefore, it is necessary to clarify and describe these concepts. Because we aim to gain a global perspective on EDIA, we have primarily used internationally recognised definitions established by the United Nations (see table 1).

For pharmacy education specifically, embedding EDIA practices is essential for several reasons. First, pharmacy education must prepare future pharmacists to work with diverse patient populations in ways that contribute to health equity.2 Pharmacists, as healthcare workers, need strong communication skills including intercultural communication and to understand barriers encountered in accessing pharmacy care for marginalised groups.3 Second, the diversity of student pharmacists continues to increase,4 but the proportions of culturally diverse student demographics do not mirror respective faculty demographics necessitating implementation of effective faculty EDIA strategies.5 Finally, while not unique to pharmacy, all study programmes are affected by broader structural inequalities that characterise higher education (eg, barriers posed by full-time studies, attrition among marginalised groups).6 Thus, embedding EDIA practices into pharmacy education is necessary to foster more inclusive pharmacy education and clinical pharmacy practice.

Within pharmacy education, several reviews have been published that explore key themes related to EDIA. The concept of the hidden curriculum in pharmacy education is examined by Park et al,7 defining it as the teaching and learning that occur outside the formal curriculum. This hidden curriculum encompasses the knowledge, skills, attitudes, behaviours, values and beliefs that students are expected to (sub)consciously acquire and accept to be successful in the programme. While it plays a significant role in shaping professional identity, it can also negatively impact interprofessional practice, professional norms, ethics and ultimately, patient outcomes. This occurs through the transmission of implicit norms, values and behaviours that may not always align with evidence-based care or professional guidelines. A scoping review, conducted by Ho et al8 that focuses on evidence-based tools and strategies for developing cultural, highlights that the most common structures for students were required courses, elective courses and study abroad. The most common activities employed within the structures were lectures, reflections and discussions. They concluded that integrating these tools systematically throughout the curriculum could enhance their effectiveness. Lastly, a review and categorisation of published educational research on diversity in pharmacy schools has been conducted by Bush et al.9 Their review showed that contemporary drivers of change are shaping diversity-focused research in pharmacy education, and the authors emphasise that greater attention is needed to the research foci, targets and resulting recommendations. They note that although numerous calls for diversity-related research have been made, the scholarship in this area remains limited, leading them to conclude that diversity has not yet been adequately addressed and that further commitment is required. While these reviews each address important EDIA-related themes, they focus on specific dimensions of the broader concept. What remains missing is a comprehensive overview of how EDIA is embedded within pharmacy education. This gap underscores the need for the present scoping review.

Despite growing recognition of its significance, a comprehensive overview of research that examines EDIA broadly within pharmacy education is absent. Including the aforementioned reviews, research in this field often focusses on very specific aspects of EDIA in pharmacy education, highlighting the need for a comprehensive review. This review seeks to address this gap by focusing on three core areas of pharmacy education: faculty development, curriculum content and teaching strategies. By exploring these substantive areas on a global scale, the scoping review aims to provide a more complete understanding of the literature, offering valuable insights for pharmacy educators and educational developers or researchers. This review can support pharmacy educators in gaining insights into EDIA-related research to inform their teaching strategies. Educational developers can use it as a resource to implement inclusive teaching practices, while pharmacy education researchers can use this mapping to plan and design studies exploring specific aspects of EDIA in pharmacy education. The objective of this scoping review is to provide the first comprehensive exploration of literature on EDIA in pharmacy education, specifically focusing on faculty development, curriculum content and teaching strategies, exploring how these principles are addressed, delivered and implemented.

Review question

Recognising the growing importance of integrating EDIA principles in education and healthcare,10 this review aims to systematically map and summarise existing evidence to identify the extent of available research and potential knowledge gaps in this area.

The key research question formulated to guide this review is:

What literature exists pertaining to EDIA in pharmacy education, specifically focused on faculty development, curriculum content and teaching strategies?

This scoping review protocol was developed following the guidelines of the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) and the JBI Scoping Review Methodology Group.11 Current scoping review protocols which align with these guidelines, were consulted as practical examples for planning and reporting. By applying the JBI scoping review methodology, this review seeks to contribute to the understanding of how EDIA principles are integrated into pharmacy education and provide a foundation for future research in this area.

Eligibility criteria

The prefixed title of this scoping review protocol and that of the subsequently planned scoping review, the previously posed review question, the following inclusion criteria and the subsequent search strategy, are guided by the Population, Concept and Context framework.

Population

Papers focusing on individuals involved in pharmacy education should be included. This includes:

Faculty members and educators in pharmacy programmes.

Faculty members and educators in pharmacy programmes.

Students at undergraduate, graduate or postgraduate levels in pharmacy programmes.

Students at undergraduate, graduate or postgraduate levels in pharmacy programmes.

Pharmacy education administrators and stakeholders involved in faculty development, curriculum design or teaching strategies related to EDIA.

Pharmacy education administrators and stakeholders involved in faculty development, curriculum design or teaching strategies related to EDIA.

Papers focusing on populations outside pharmacy education, such as those in general health education, medical or nursing education or other fields unrelated to pharmacy education, should be excluded.

Concept

Studies will be included if they examine the integration of EDIA principles in pharmacy education through faculty development, curriculum content or teaching strategies. Faculty development initiatives must explicitly address EDIA in their design or implementation, with a focus on topics such as gender, race, ethnicity, disability, cultural competence or other social identities. Studies on curriculum development will be considered if they explore the integration of EDIA-related themes, including health inequities, intercultural communication, decolonisation or inclusive learning approaches. Teaching strategies must involve EDIA-focused pedagogical methods, such as experiential learning, simulation-based instruction or community-engaged approaches within pharmacy education.

Studies will be excluded if they do not directly engage with EDIA principles in a meaningful way, only mention them peripherally, or focus on unrelated methodologies or topics.

Context

Eligible studies must take place within formal pharmacy education settings across academic, professional or institutional contexts. This includes pharmacy programmes offered at undergraduate, graduate or postgraduate levels, as well as faculty development workshops and structured courses aimed at pharmacy educators. Studies will be included if they assess EDIA integration in various teaching and learning formats, such as standalone or embedded courses, online or in-person delivery and structured faculty training initiatives.

Studies must be published in English, or at minimum, have an abstract and keywords available in English. Research focusing on education outside formal pharmacy programmes, such as public health campaigns or general workforce training, will be excluded. Additionally, studies that do not explicitly assess the implementation or evaluation of EDIA-related content in pharmacy education will not be considered.

Methods

Search strategy

To cover both medical and educational literature, the literature searches will be run in the medical and health sciences databases Embase (Ovid), MEDLINE (Ovid), APA PsycINFO (Ovid) and CINAHL (Ebsco), in the educational database ERIC (Ebsco), and in the multidisciplinary database Web of Science (see online supplemental material 1). Both subject headings and free-text terms will be used to cover the concepts of pharmacy education and the concept of EDIA. Publication date is limited to 2014 onwards, due to our interest in recent interventions and activities which have taken place during a period in which many if not all Global North academic institutions have developed and implemented some policies or statements related to EDIA. Earlier equity work may have focused on single equity issues (eg, gender balance in study programme). Additionally, through preliminary searching, we have found that much of the literature is from the last 5 years, suggesting that the majority of this scholarship is relatively new (hence a 10-year history is sufficient for providing an accurate summary of this work through our scoping review). No national/location restrictions will be applied. Test searches have been run in November 2024. The search strategy will be peer-reviewed by an academic librarian.

Retrieved records will be imported to the reference software EndNote. Deduplication will be conducted by exporting data from EndNote to ASYSD.12

Study selection

After the search is completed and duplicates are removed, study screening will take place. Screening will be managed using Covidence software. Titles and abstracts will be screened independently by two researchers, with discrepancies resolved through discussion or, if needed, consultation with a third researcher. Full-text screening will follow the same process to ensure only eligible studies are included.

Reporting will detail the reasoning for exclusion of full-text sources. Search results and study inclusion processes will be fully reported in the scoping review and presented in a PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) flow diagram.

Data extraction

Data extraction will be conducted systematically using a predefined extraction tool (see online supplemental material 2) to ensure consistency and accuracy. The tool was developed by two researchers (IK and MA) and will first be piloted by the research team working in pairs (MA, AKM-T, MAVK, M-RB, TK and IK) on eight studies selected by IK. This pilot phase will help refine the tool and address any ambiguities before full extraction begins.

For data extraction, two researchers will independently extract information from each included study, capturing bibliographic details, study characteristics and findings related to EDIA in faculty development, curriculum content and teaching strategies. Extracted data will be reviewed for consistency before moving to analysis.

Three researchers (MA, IK and MAVK) will lead the analysis, while AKM-T, M-RB and TK will provide support as needed and review the final scoping review.

Data analysis and presentation

The extracted data will be analysed using a combination of descriptive and thematic synthesis to provide a comprehensive overview of the existing literature on EDIA in pharmacy education. The analysis will be structured around the three core areas of the review: faculty development, curriculum content and teaching strategies.

A descriptive analysis will first be conducted to summarise key characteristics of the included studies, such as publication year, geographical distribution, study design and the populations studied, including faculty, students and administrators. This will allow for an overview of research trends and the extent to which different aspects of EDIA have been explored within pharmacy education.

Following this, a thematic analysis will be performed to identify patterns and emerging themes across the studies. The data will be coded inductively and deductively to categorise findings related to the integration of EDIA principles in pharmacy education. This process will help to highlight common approaches, challenges and gaps in faculty development, curriculum design and teaching strategies. Specific themes may include institutional barriers to implementing EDIA, the effectiveness of various pedagogical strategies and the role of faculty in fostering inclusive learning environments.

The findings will be presented in both tabular and narrative formats to facilitate interpretation and comparison across studies. Summary tables will organise studies based on key characteristics, such as their methodological approach, EDIA focus and educational interventions. In addition, visual representations, such as bar charts or conceptual diagrams, may be used to illustrate trends in the literature and the thematic relationships identified. The narrative synthesis will then provide a structured discussion of the key findings, situating them within the broader discourse on EDIA in higher education and pharmacy education specifically.

Finally, the review will identify gaps in the literature and areas requiring further research. Particular attention will be given to underexplored aspects of EDIA. This could include how the intersectionality of different forms of marginalisation, such as how racism and ableism work together, is taken up in pharmacy education.13 This could also include the extent to which studies address how pharmacy education outcomes related to EDIA are defined, reported and assessed in the literature. Based on these findings, the review will conclude with recommendations for future research and potential policy implications for pharmacy educators and institutions aiming to enhance EDIA integration in their programmes.

Ethics and dissemination

No human research participants will be involved in this review. Therefore, obtaining informed consent, protecting anonymity and receiving institutional review board approval are not relevant. We plan to share findings through a variety of means including professional networks, peer-reviewed journal publications, conference presentations, invited workshops and webinars, on the FPD-Include project website and on our research groups’ university websites.

Scoping review timeline

In December 2024, a preliminary search was conducted. Following the publication of the scoping review protocol, a pilot search took place in March 2025. The pilot was successful and led to final adjustments in the search strategy. The final search, study selection and data extraction will be conducted from May to December 2025. The subsequent analysis, presentation and interpretation of results are planned thereafter, with the final scoping report anticipated to be completed by 2026.

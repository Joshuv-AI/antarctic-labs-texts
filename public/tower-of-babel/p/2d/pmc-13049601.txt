Human infection by an unclassified Rickettsia species in Xinjiang, northwest China
Authors: Bing Zhang, Fanming Meng, Yan-Jun Kang, Jiahao Guo, Runda Jie, Binbin Huang, Tao Zheng, Xiao Wang
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

The genus Rickettsia is a group of Gram-negative endosymbiontic bacteria closely related to human diseases. The common clinical manifestations of rickettsial infection are fever, malaise, skin rash, joint pain, etc. Although lots of Rickettsia species have been discovered in China, human infection cases are still relatively rare except for Japanese spotted fever [1]. In this study, we identified a rare human rickettsiosis case caused by an unclassified Rickettsia species using next-generation sequencing.

On February 25th, 2024, a 19-year-old girl presented to the hospital in Urumuqi City located in Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, Northwest China, claiming skin rash and itch for one day after bitten by an unknown arthropod in the hostel. Physical examination indicated that her body temperature was 38oC. Red maculopapules were observed on her neck and trunk. Meanwhile, enlarged lymph nodes can be palpated behind the ears and the occiput. In addition, pharyngeal isthmus congestion was also observed. It is of interest that most results of blood routine test were normal. The only abnormal result is mildly elevated neutrophil percentage of 70.6% (normal range: 50-70%). She was then suspected with acute urticaria and anti-allergic therapy was applied. She was not hospitalized and left the hospital on the same day.

Due to the history of bitten by arthropod and the clinical manifestations (fever, rash, lymph enlargement), her whole blood sample was retrospectively screened for vector-borne pathogens. For detection of the possible arthropod-borne agents, the RNA was also extracted from the blood using a RNeasy plus universal kit (Qiagen), and then subjected to next-generation sequencing. Out of our expectation, a total of 707 reads of Rickettsia spp. Were identified, and three sequences were assembled with the lengths of 523 bp (encoding transposase and a hypothetical protein), 142 bp (6S RNA gene), and 463 bp (encoding RNase P), respectively. BLASTN showed that the three obtained sequences have highest 98.19% identity to Rickettsia endosymbiont of Rhinocyllus conicus isolate 54,720 (coverage 95%) (OZ032155.1), 99.30% to Rickettsia rickettsii strain Pierce Canyon (coverage 100%) (CP130458.1), and 95.41% to Rickettsia massiliae str. AZT80 (coverage 97%) (CP003319.1). Therefore, although the Rickettsia species could not be exactly determined, it could be supposed that it might belong to spotted fever group. After the diagnosis of rickettsiosis, follow-up was immediately carried out. The result showed that she had recovered two days after seeking medical treatment.

Up to date, lots of spotted fever Rickettsia have been recorded in most provinces of China, including R. japonica, R. raoultii, R. jingxinensis, etc [2]. In recent years, novel species of spotted fever Rickettsia are still being discovered (mainly in ticks). However, human infections are still rarely reported. To date, the mostly reported species was R. japonica, followed by R. raoultii and R. sibrica [1]. In Xinjiang, although multiple species have been discovered, human infection cases were very rare. In this study, we identified an unclassified Rickettsia species in blood sample from a patient with fever and rash.

The diagnosis of rickettsiosis is usually difficult due to the non-specific clinical presentations. Although fever and rash are commonly observed, they are not exclusive symptoms and could also be caused by many other diseases. In recent years, next-generation sequencing has been successfully used for diagnosis of rickettsiosis [3]. Although Rickettsia spp. Belong to bacteria, meta-transcriptomic sequencing targeting RNA may be more preferable. In a study on R. prowakezii in 2024, meta-genomic sequencing yielded only 7 sequences while meta-transcriptomic sequencing yielding 227 sequences [4]. Our result confirmed that RNA sequencing may be a good choice for diagnosis of rickettsiosis.

It is of interest that the species and vector of this Rickettsia are both unknown. Currently, the genome sequences of many Rickettsia species are still unavailable. Instead, only the partial sequences of their key genes (16S, gltA, groEL, ompB, etc) were obtained. However, the obtained three sequences in this study were not located in any key genes. Therefore, it might be a novel species or a known species without available genome sequences. The vector of this Rickettsia was also hard to determine because the patient did not see what arthropod bite her. In addition, no suspicious arthropods were found in the hostel. In future studies, enhanced surveillance in both vectors and humans are need in this area.

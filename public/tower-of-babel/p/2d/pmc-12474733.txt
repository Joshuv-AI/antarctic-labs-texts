Efficacy and Safety of Triple Fixed Dose Combination of Dapagliflozin + Sitagliptin + Metformin IR in Indian Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: A Randomized, Phase 3 Study in Comparison with Dual FDC Sitagliptin + Metformin IR
Authors: Mahendra Pal Singh, Sandeep Gupta, Manish Singh, C Ambrish, Prakash Kurmi, Dinesh Kumar Gangwani, J Naganna, Ravikumar Sethuraman, Vrindavani Dhumal, Sapan Behera, Piyush M Patel, Dipak M Patil, Lalit K Lakhwani, Pravin S Ghadge, Suyog C Mehta, Sadhna J Joglekar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a triple fixed-dose combination (FDC) of dapagliflozin, sitagliptin, and immediate-release metformin (DAPA + SITA + MET IR) compared to dual FDCs in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).MethodsThis Phase 3, randomized, open-label study included eligible patients with T2DM who were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either a triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR (5 + 50 + 500/1000 mg) or a dual FDC of sitagliptin and MET IR (SITA + MET IR; 50 + 500/1000 mg), administered twice daily for 16 weeks. The primary endpoint was the mean change in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) from baseline to Week 16.ResultsThe mean baseline HbA1c was ~ 9% in both arms. Adjusted mean reduction in HbA1c from baseline to Week 16 was significantly greater in triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR (– 2.08%) versus dual FDC of SITA + MET IR (– 1.38%) P < 0.0001. A significantly greater reduction in HbA1c was observed with triple FDC compared to the dual FDC at Week 12 (P < 0.0001). At Week 16, a significantly higher proportion of patients achieved HbA1c < 7% with triple FDC than with dual FDC (48.9% versus 31.1%; P = 0.0029). Both treatments were well tolerated.ConclusionTriple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR demonstrated superior efficacy in achieving glycaemic control in patients with T2DM compared to dual FDC. There were no major safety concerns.Clinical trial registration numberCTRI/2022/04/041817.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12325-025-03323-3.Keywords: Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, Dapagliflozin, Sitagliptin, Metformin, Sodium-glucose co-transporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor

Introduction

The global prevalence of diabetes is on the rise, estimated at 10% in 2021 and projected to reach 12% by 2045, with the greatest impact expected in middle-income countries [1]. In India, the estimated prevalence of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and prediabetes in 2021 was approximately 101 million and 136 million, respectively [2]. Given the progressive nature of T2DM, characterized by declining beta (β)-cell function and increasing insulin resistance, many patients experience worsening glycaemic control despite initial monotherapy [3–10]. Metformin (MET), the standard first-line agent, often becomes insufficient over time, and delays in treatment intensification contribute to prolonged hyperglycaemia and increased risk of both microvascular and macrovascular complications [11]. In such cases, additional oral antiglycaemic agents are considered [12–14]. This, along with the concomitant medications for other comorbid conditions, increases the pill burden, affecting patient adherence [15]. Even in countries with robust healthcare access, only 39% of patients demonstrate good medication adherence [16]. Therefore, fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) offer a practical strategy to address therapeutic inertia by simplifying treatment regimens, improving adherence, and enabling timely escalation of therapy, ultimately leading to better glycaemic outcomes and reduced complication risk. Another FDC benefit is reduced cost, which is important for long-term therapy [17]. International guidelines recommend dual or triple combination therapy for patients with an glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) > 1.5% (16.1 mmol/mol) above the target of 7.0% [18, 19]. The American Association of Clinical Endocrinologists further advises early initiation of triple therapy, including metformin, for newly diagnosed patients with HbA1c > 9.0% (75 mmol/mol) or > 1.5% [20].

The FDCs of sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), dipeptidyl peptidase 4 inhibitors (DPP4i), and MET offer complementary mechanisms, and β-cell-preserving effects provide effective glycaemic control. These FDCs can potentially promote weight loss without increasing the risk of hypoglycaemia [21–23]. Patients receiving dapagliflozin (DAPA) with sitagliptin (SITA) and MET have shown a 24.9% improvement in β-cell function attributed to the unique insulin-independent and β-cell preserving mechanisms of the treatment [21, 24, 25]. Three studies comparing the efficacy of an extended-release triple FDC of DAPA, SITA, and MET in a once-daily regimen to a dual combination of SITA and MET (once-daily) in patients with poorly controlled diabetes demonstrated that the triple FDC significantly outperformed the dual therapy in achieving glycaemic control by week 16 without notable safety concerns [26–28]. A FDC that could offer flexible dose adjustments of MET which could offer additional benefits. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR at different metformin doses (500 or 1000 mg) in patients who have suboptimal glycaemic control with dual FDC of SITA + MET IR in twice-daily regimens.

Methods

Study Design and Ethical considerations

This was a Phase 3, randomized, open-label, two-arm, active-controlled study conducted across ten clinical centres in India. Prior to study initiation, approval was secured from the Drugs Controller General of India. The study protocol was approved by institutional ethics committees at all participating sites (see Supplementary Material, Table S1). The trial adhered to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines as defined by the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), the Declaration of Helsinki, the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019, and the 2017 guidelines issued by the Indian Council of Medical Research. Informed consent was obtained in writing from all participants before any screening procedures took place. The trial was registered prospectively with the Clinical Trials Registry-India (CTRI) under registration no. CTRI/2022/04/041817.

Participant Eligibility

Adults aged between 18 and 65 years with T2DM, a body mass index (BMI) ≤ 45 kg/m2, and HbA1c levels ranging from 7.5% to 11.0% were considered eligible. Prior to screening, all participants were required to be on a dual FDC of sitagliptin and metformin immediate release (SITA + MET IR, 50 + 500/1000 mg), taken twice daily for at least 8 weeks, in conjunction with dietary modifications and physical activity.

Exclusion criteria included: diagnoses of type 1 diabetes, diabetes insipidus, monogenic or secondary diabetes (e.g., from pancreatic injury), or a history of metabolic acidosis (including diabetic ketoacidosis and lactic acidosis). Additional exclusions were a history of pancreatitis, hyperosmolar coma, recent (within 6 months) cardiovascular events such as myocardial infarction, revascularization procedures, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack; or unstable angina within the previous 3 months. Patients were also excluded for clinically relevant ventricular arrhythmias, end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis, significant laboratory abnormalities or comorbid conditions (e.g., immunocompromised state, active malignancy, hyperthyroidism), recent use of oral anti-diabetics (OADs) other than MET or SITA, fasting blood glucose (FBG) ≥ 270 mg/dl, uncontrolled hypertension (≥ 160/100 mmHg), severe renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 45 ml/min/1.73 m2), elevated liver enzymes (aspartate or alanine transaminase > 3 times upper limit of normal), abnormal ECG findings (e.g., second- or third-degree atrioventricular block without a pacemaker, prolonged QTc > 500 ms), advanced heart failure (New York Heart Association class III or IV), recent (within the last 6 months) severe urinary or genital infections, and pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Intervention and Randomization

Participants were randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio using a centralized block randomization method to receive either A triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR (5 + 50 + 500/1000 mg) twice daily (Istamet™ D-IR; Sun Pharma Laboratories Ltd.) or a dual FDC of SITA + MET IR (50 + 500/1000 mg) twice daily (Istamet®; MSD Pharmaceuticals Pvt. Ltd., distributed by Sun Pharma Laboratories Ltd.).

The metformin dose (500 mg or 1000 mg) corresponded to the participant’s pre-enrolment regimen. No additional OADs were permitted during the trial, except for rescue therapy. Rescue medications excluding DPP-4i, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, and SGLT2i were allowed according to defined FBG thresholds: > 270 mg/dl from baseline to Week 6, > 240 mg/dl from Week 6 to 12, and > 200 mg/dl from Weeks 12 to 16.

A follow-up safety assessment was conducted at Week 18 (2 weeks after treatment conclusion). Standardized glucometers were supplied for patients to self-monitor blood glucose at least once per week, or more frequently as advised by the investigator. Self-monitored blood glucose values were recorded in diaries and reviewed at scheduled visits. Use of any drugs that could affect blood glucose levels (e.g., systemic corticosteroids, weight-loss medications) was not permitted during the study.

Endpoints and Assessments

The primary efficacy endpoint was the mean change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 16. Secondary endpoints included mean change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 12, changes in FBG and post-prandial blood glucose (PPBG) from baseline to Weeks 12 and 16, change in body weight from baseline to Week 16, proportion of patients achieving target HbA1c < 7% by Weeks 12 and 16, and number of patients requiring rescue medications.

Safety endpoints focused on the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and the occurrence of hypoglycaemic episodes. Hypoglycaemia was classified per the American Diabetes Association 2020 guidelines: Level 1: glucose < 70 mg/dl and ≥ 54 mg/dl; Level 2: glucose < 54 mg/dl; Level 3: severe hypoglycaemia requiring external assistance because of altered mental or physical status [29].

Statistical Methods

Assuming a standard deviation of 1%, an alpha level of 0.05, and aiming for 85% statistical power to detect a 0.4% difference in HbA1c reduction between groups (accounting for a 15% dropout rate), approximately 268 patients (134 per group) were required.

Baseline demographics and clinical data were summarized descriptively. Continuous variables were reported with mean, SD, median, and range; categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages.

Efficacy analysis was conducted using the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population, which included all randomized patients who received at least one dose of study drug and had post-baseline efficacy data. The primary endpoint was analysed using a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM), with treatment group comparisons based on least squares (LS) means and associated two-sided 95% confidence intervals and p-values.

Secondary efficacy endpoints (changes in HbA1c, FBG, PPBG, and body weight) were analysed similarly using the MMRM model. Differences in proportions (e.g., patients reaching target HbA1c, use of rescue therapy) were analysed using the chi-square test.

For sensitivity analyses, missing values were imputed using a multivariate normal distribution model across all included variables.

Safety data were analysed for all participants who received at least one dose of study medication. TEAEs were summarized by system organ class, preferred term, severity, relatedness, action taken, and outcomes, using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA), version 24.0. Hypoglycaemia rates were also summarized by event count and percentage. All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software version 9.4.

Results

Patient’s Disposition and Baseline Characteristics

Of the 290 patients with T2DM screened, 273 were randomized to receive either the triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR (n = 136) or the dual FDC of SITA + MET IR (n = 137) across ten geographically distributed sites in India. A total of 270 patients (98.9%) completed the study, while three patients were lost to follow-up (Fig. 1). The overall mean age of patients was 47.3 years and mean baseline HbA1c was ~ 9% (~ 75 mmol/mol) in both treatment arms. Most enrolled patients (58.8%) were male.

Patient disposition. BID twice daily, DAPA dapagliflozin, FDC fixed dose combination, IR immediate release, MET metformin, SITA sitagliptin

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics were comparable between the triple FDC (DAPA + SITA + MET IR) and dual FDC (SITA + MET IR) treatment arms. Mean body weight was 68.24 kg and 69.79 kg, respectively; BMI was 26.40 kg/m2 and 26.89 kg/m2; estimated eGFR was 101.4 ml/min/1.73m2 and 99.3 ml/min/1.73m2; FBG levels were 172.7 mg/dl and 178.4 mg/dl; PPBG levels were 254.5 mg/dl and 259.4 mg/dl, respectively.

The first patient was enrolled on 26 April 2022, and the last patient completed the study on 15 October 2022.

Efficacy Results

The primary endpoint of mean change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 16 was significantly greater in the triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR arm. Patients receiving triple FDC showed a reduction of – 2.07% ± 1.20% (– 22.6 ± 13.08 mmol/mol) vs – 1.38% ± 1.14% (– 15.1 ± 12.42 mmol/mol) in the dual FDC of SITA + MET IR arm. The adjusted mean (± standard error) reduction in HbA1c was significantly greater in triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR [– 2.08% (± 0.07%), – 22.7 mmol/mol (± 0.79 mmol/mol)] vs. dual FDC of SITA + MET IR (– 1.38% [± 0.07%], – 15.06 mmol/mol [± 0.79 mmol/mol]) with mean difference between both treatment arms (– 0.7%, P < 0.0001 in favour of triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR).The result showed statistical superiority of triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR with ~ 50% more reduction in HbA1c than in dual FDC of SITA + MET IR (Table 1).

At Week 12, the reduction in HbA1c from baseline was also statistically greater in the triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR arm [– 1.37% ± 1.01% (– 14.09 ± 11.08 mmol/mol)] compared to the dual FDC arm [– 0.77% ± 1.01% (– 8.38 ± 11.09 mmol/mol); P < 0.0001] (Table 1 and Fig. 2).

The proportion of patients achieving HbA1c < 7% (53 mmol/mol) was significantly higher in the triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR arm at both Week 12 (23.7% vs 10.4%; P = 0.0036) and Week 16 (48.9% vs 31.1%; P = 0.0029) compared to the dual FDC of SITA + MET IR arm (Table 1 and Fig. 3).

Proportion of patients who achieved HbA1c < 7% (53 mmol/mol) at Week 12 and Week 16

Significant reductions in PPBG from baseline to Week 12 were observed in the triple FDC group compared to dual FDC (– 53.0 ± 68.82 mg/dl vs – 38.7 ± 68.04 mg/dl; P = 0.0034). By Week 16, the reduction was numerically greater in the triple FDC arm but did not reach statistical significance (– 55.3 ± 78.20 mg/dl vs – 47.2 ± 74.87 mg/dl; P = 0.0554) (Table 1). For FBG, the triple FDC arm showed significantly greater reductions at both Week 12 (– 39.1 ± 39.14 mg/dl vs – 27.6 ± 46.14 mg/dl; P < 0.0001) and Week 16 (– 36.9 ± 44.87 mg/dl vs – 30.6 ± 47.82 mg/dl; P = 0.0065) compared to the dual FDC group (Table 1).

Although not statistically significant, mean body weight reduction from baseline to Week 16 was numerically higher in the triple FDC arm (– 0.23 ± 1.76 kg) compared to the dual FDC arm (– 0.09 ± 2.08 kg; P = 0.3422) (Table 1). No patient in either treatment arm required rescue medication during the study.

Safety Outcome

The incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) was similar between the two treatment arms. A total of 13 TEAEs were reported in 12 patients (4.4%), 6 TEAEs in 6 patients (4.4%) in the triple FDC group and 7 TEAEs in 6 patients (4.4%) in the dual FDC group. The most commonly reported TEAE was pyrexia (1.5%) in the triple FDC arm and asthenia (1.5%) in the dual FDC arm. All TEAEs in the triple FDC group were mild in intensity. In the dual FDC group, one TEAE (nasopharyngitis) was of moderate intensity, while the remaining six events were mild. Of the 13 TEAEs, 6 events were assessed as ‘probable/likely’ related to study medications and 7 as ‘unlikely’ as per the investigators’ assessments. Despite significant reduction in HbA1c in both treatment arms, no hypoglycaemic events were reported. No clinically significant changes in systolic or diastolic blood pressure were observed throughout the study. Discussion

Effective glycaemic management is fundamental in treating T2DM, aiming to maintain HbA1c levels < 7% to reduce the risk of microvascular and macrovascular complications. However, patients with severe T2DM, characterized by HbA1c levels > 9%, often require intensified therapeutic strategies beyond conventional monotherapy [12, 19, 20, 30]. In such cases, combination therapies, particularly those incorporating SGLT2i, have demonstrated enhanced efficacy [31–33].

One pivotal factor in long-term management of T2DM is beta-cell preservation, which plays an essential role in maintaining endogenous insulin secretion and improving insulin sensitivity. As beta-cell function deteriorates in T2DM, the reliance on exogenous interventions increases, necessitating treatments that not only control glucose but also provide additional pharmacological benefits [31–33]. To achieve targeted glycaemic control, various international guidelines recommend initiating combination therapy where HbA1c is 1.5% above target to achieve glycaemic control considering the benefit-risk profile of each drug [12, 19, 20, 30]. Few studies have shown beneficial effects with combination therapy of SGLT2i, DPP4i, and MET [19–21].

The current study involved patients on SITA + MET combination for a period of 8 weeks but with poor glycaemic control (HbA1c 7.5 to 11 %). Although sitagliptin and metformin have long been staples in T2DM management, evidence suggests that these agents alone may become insufficient in patients with severe diabetes, particularly those with HbA1c levels > 9% [20]. In such cases, the failure to achieve glycaemic targets signals the need for additional therapeutic interventions. Recent advancements in diabetes therapy have highlighted the value of SGLT2i. Beyond its glucose-lowering effect, SGLT2i has demonstrated benefits in the pathophysiology of diabetes, including weight reduction, improvements in cardiovascular outcomes, and delay in the progression of diabetic nephropathy. Their mechanism inducing glycosuria independently of insulin action complements traditional therapies by reducing hyperglycaemia and mitigating systemic complications [31–33].

Hence, FDCs incorporating SGLT2i can enhance glycaemic control while providing additional metabolic and cardiovascular benefits.

Findings and Clinical Implications

The current study evaluated the triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR in patients inadequately controlled on SITA + MET. The results indicated a significantly greater reduction in HbA1c at Week 16 in the triple FDC group compared to the dual FDC group (– 0.7%, P < 0.0001), with 48.9% of patients achieving HbA1c < 7% versus 31.1% in the dual FDC group. These findings align with previous studies, such as the MESIDA trial, which reported a – 1.73% reduction in HbA1c with the triple FDC compared to – 1.33% with dual therapies [28]. By week 24, Rosenstock et al. observed a mean difference of 1.5% reduction in HbA1c in triple FDC (DAPA + SAXA + MET) vs 0.9% in dual SAXA + MET [22].

Jabbour et al. [21] reported a 0.4% HbA1c reduction with DAPA added to SITA + MET at week 24, while Sahay et al. [26] showed improved efficacy (mean difference in HbA1c of 0.4% between the triple FDC and the dual FDC arm; P < 0.001) using the extended-release form of the same triple combination. The clinical value of reducing HbA1c by 0.7% in the triple FDC group over and above the reductions achieved by dual therapy is of notable clinical significance, especially in patients with a baseline HbA1c > 9%. A 1% reduction in HbA1c has been consistently shown to correlate with a 17–24% reduction in the risk of diabetes-related complications, including microvascular and macrovascular endpoints, as well as a reduction in all-cause mortality [35]. Therefore, the superior HbA1c reduction observed with the triple FDC therapy not only translates to better short-term glycaemic control but also may significantly improve long-term outcomes, emphasizing the potential benefit of this combination therapy in patients with severe or inadequately controlled T2DM.

The triple FDC also significantly improved FBG and postprandial blood glucose (PPBG) by week 16. Compared to the dual FDC group, a greater proportion of patients in the triple FDC group achieved HbA1c < 7% at Week 16 (48.9% vs 31.1%, P = 0.0029) (Fig. 2). These clinical benefits are attributed to the complementary mechanisms of the three agents: insulin sensitization by metformin, insulinotropic effects of sitagliptin via DPP-4 inhibition, and the insulin-independent glycosuric action of DAPA. Importantly, DAPA’s ability to reduce glucotoxicity may contribute to improved β-cell preservation [21–25, 31–34, 36–38]. Patients receiving DAPA with SITA and MET have shown a 24.9% increase in β-cell function due to unique insulin independent and β-cell preserving mechanisms [21].

Correlating with observations from other studies by Sahay et al. [26] and Rosenstock et al. [22], there are no reported incidences of hypoglycaemia or genitourinary infections.

The current study demonstrated a substantial reduction in HbA1c, reinforcing the clinical value of SGLT2i in potentially preventing diabetes-related complications.

To our knowledge, this is the first randomized controlled trial to provide such comprehensive data on the role of SGLT2i as an adjunct to dual therapy with SITA and MET (immediate release). These findings not only underscore the importance of multi-targeted treatment strategies but also set the stage for future therapeutic advancements in managing severe T2DM.

Limitations

The study was not double-blinded; however, the primary endpoint (mean change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 16) and most secondary efficacy endpoints were objective, laboratory-based measures (e.g., HbA1c, FBG, PPBG), minimizing the potential for subjective bias in efficacy assessment. The short 16-week study duration limits our ability to assess long-term outcomes, including durability of glycaemic control and cardiovascular or renal benefits. Future long-term real-world studies are warranted to evaluate these outcomes.

Conclusion

This Phase 3, randomized trial establishes that the triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR (Istamet™ D-IR) with two different doses of metformin provides improved glycaemic benefits compared with FDCs in reducing HbA1c levels and achieving the target of HbA1c < 7% in Indian patients with T2DM inadequately controlled on SITA + MET. The triple FDC also offers the advantage of minimal risk of hypoglycaemia and weight gain, which are common concerns with other antidiabetic therapies. These findings suggest that triple FDC of DAPA + SITA + MET IR (Istamet™ D-IR) is a promising therapeutic option for patients with T2DM, potentially enhancing treatment adherence because of its simplified regimen and favourable safety profile.

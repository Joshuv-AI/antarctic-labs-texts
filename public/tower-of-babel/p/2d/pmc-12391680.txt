Evaluating tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in dialysis catheter dysfunction: A focus on fibrin sheath and risk factors
Authors: Javad Jalili, Sarah Vaseghi, Touraj Asvadi, Mahdiyeh Baastani Khajeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHemodialysis catheter dysfunction can arise from fibrin sheath formation, leading to impaired patency and compromised treatment. Tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) is an emerging treatment option to restore catheter patency. This study evaluated the efficacy of tPA therapy and explored clinical and demographic risk factors for catheter dysfunction.MethodsThis retrospective study included 42 hemodialysis patients with central venous catheters (CVCs) at Imam Reza Hospital, Tabriz, Iran (2020−2023). Inclusion criteria were inability to withdraw blood or inadequate flow in one or both catheter lumens, confirmed by venographic evidence of fibrin sheath formation. Patients received 2 mg tPA in 2 ml saline with a 30-min dwell time. Data on patient demographics, catheter history, and tPA outcomes were analyzed.ResultstPA was effective in 78.6 % of patients after the first dose, restoring both lumens' functionality. Four of the treated cases required a second tPA session due to recurrent fibrin sheath formation within 6–18 months, all of which were successful. Two patients received a third tPA dose 14–16 months after the second, also with complete resolution. Older age (p = 0.02), diabetes (p = 0.001), and smoking (P = 0.015) were identified as significant risk factors for catheter dysfunction.ConclusiontPA therapy effectively restores catheter function in cases of fibrin sheath-related dysfunction. Addressing modifiable risk factors such as smoking and managing diabetes may help reduce the incidence of catheter dysfunction and improve long-term outcomes in dialysis patients.Keywords: Dialysis catheter dysfunction, Fibrin sheath, Tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), Thrombolytic therapy

Introduction

For patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD), catheter dysfunction has been a significant obstacle to receive adequate hemodialysis treatment and is a frequent clinical difficulty [1,2]. For vascular access, central venous catheters (CVCs) are widely utilized in patients with ESRD; nevertheless, these devices are often complicated by variables that reduce their lifetime and effectiveness, which may jeopardize patient health and effective dialysis [3,4]. Among the various causes of catheter dysfunction, fibrin sheath formation is a common and problematic complication, reported in up to 60 % of CVC placements [[5], [6], [7]]. The catheter lumen may become partially or completely occluded as a result of this sheath acting as a nidus for thrombus formation, impairing blood flow, and dialysis effectiveness [8,9]. Catheter dysfunction due to fibrin sheath has traditionally been treated by catheter exchange or mechanical treatments, such as stripping or balloon disruption of the fibrin sheath [10,11], but these approaches are associated with procedural risks, including vascular injury and infection.

As a minimally invasive alternative, tPA, a fibrinolytic substance, has shown promise in breaking fibrin-related components of the sheaths and dissolving fibrin clots. It is a non-invasive treatment option [12]. By dissolving the obstructing sheath, direct injection of tPA into the dysfunctioning CVC can successfully restore patency and enable the restoration of sufficient blood flow for dialysis [13].

The 2019 KDOQI guidelines endorse the use of intraluminal thrombolysis for managing catheter complications, as the presence of a fibrin sheath can worsen catheter dysfunction and impede effective use [14]. However, the efficacy of the tPA in treating fibrin sheath remains unclear, and various studies have reported conflicting results.

This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of tPA therapy for fibrin sheath-induced catheter dysfunction and identify modifiable risk factors contributing to this condition. Patients were recruited from Imam Reza Hospital in Tabriz, Iran, between 2020 and 2023. The analysis is retrospective. By addressing these gaps, we seek to provide actionable insights for optimizing vascular access in hemodialysis patients.

The results of this study add to the body of information already available on managing vascular access in dialysis patients by illuminating the function of tPA therapy in treating fibrin sheath associated with catheter use. Furthermore, the discovery of modifiable risk factors for catheter dysfunction opens the door to focused preventive measures that could enhance patient outcomes and ease the strain on medical resources.

Patients and methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective observational study included 42 patients with CVC dysfunction due to fibrin sheath formation, treated at Imam Reza Hospital in Tabriz, Iran, between 2020 and 2023. All patients were receiving maintenance hemodialysis via tunneled, cuffed CVCs placed for long-term vascular access. The study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in restoring catheter function and to explore associated risk factors. According to the 2019 KDOQI Clinical Practice Guidelines for Vascular Access, catheter dysfunction was suspected in cases where one or both lumens of the catheter failed to allow for aspiration or infusion, or where sustained blood flow was persistently below 300 ml/min. Following standard troubleshooting protocols, patients meeting these criteria underwent diagnostic venography through the catheter to assess for fibrin sheath formation as the underlying cause of dysfunction.

Patients were included if their prior medical data were available and if they had provided written informed consent for participation, including authorization for receiving tPA therapy and for the use of their medical records and questionnaire responses in research, in accordance with approval from the Ethics Committee of Tabriz University of Medical Sciences.

4.CVC dysfunction assessment and interventions

CVC dysfunction assessment and interventions

When catheter dysfunction was suspected, patients first underwent contrast-enhanced CT- scan to evaluate for acute thrombosis, chronic total occlusion (CTO), or central vein stenosis. If no obstructive central pathology was identified, contrast venography under fluoroscopic guidance was then performed through the dialysis catheter. The presence of a persistent contrast outline along the external surface of the catheter tip was considered diagnostic for fibrin sheath formation. Aspiration through both lumens was attempted in all cases as an initial clinical maneuver to assess catheter patency. However, we acknowledge that aspiration cannot reliably exclude the presence of intraluminal thrombus, particularly when the lumen is narrowed due to fibrin sheath formation.

In this study, fibrin sheath formation was diagnosed based on extraluminal fibrin sleeve visualization during contrast-enhanced imaging. We did not differentiate between intraluminal fibrin sheath and intraluminal thrombus, as both present with similar functional consequences and are treated with catheter-directed thrombolytic therapy.

5.Pre-tPA therapy patient preparations

Pre-tPA therapy patient preparations

Patient preparation involved the assessment and confirmation of essential hematologic parameters, including platelet count (≥50,000/L), hemoglobin level (≥10 g/dL), and International Normalized Ratio (≤1.5).

6.The procedure of tPA therapy:

The procedure of tPA therapy:

A thrombolytic drug called tPA was injected straight into the dysfunctioning CVC to break apart fibrin sheaths and destroy fibrin clots. The usual procedure was to introduce a predefined amount of tPA solution into the catheter lumen, let it stay there for a predetermined amount of time, and then flush the catheter to remove any remaining sheath material.

In this treatment regimen, 4 mg of Actilyse® was diluted with 2 ml of 0.9 % sodium chloride. The dose was divided equally, with 2 mg instilled into each of the two catheter lumens, and clamped. After 30 min of dwell time, aspiration from both lines of catheters was performed to evaluate its patency.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline patient characteristics and catheter-related outcomes. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (range), as appropriate.

To identify independent clinical risk factors associated with fibrin sheath formation, we performed binary logistic regression analysis using a subset of variables including number of catheter insertions, laterality of catheter placement, diabetes status, age, and smoking. Results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95 % confidence intervals (CIs).

In addition, given the recurrent nature of catheter dysfunction events and variability in catheter exposure duration, a negative binomial regression model was applied to evaluate the association between clinical risk factors (diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and smoking) and the incidence rate of catheter dysfunction. This method accounts for overdispersion in count data and adjusts for the time at risk (i.e., total catheter patency duration per patient), included as an offset variable. This analysis provided incidence rate ratios (IRRs) and 95 % CIs. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0), with significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Patients' characteristics

The study cohort included 42 patients (21 males, 21 females) with a mean age of 59.4 years (range: 16–88 years). The leading cause of ESRD was diabetes (n = 34, 81 %), followed by hypertensive nephropathy (n = 8, 19 %). Smoking status was notable, with 17 male patients (81 %) identified as active or passive smokers, compared to 12 female patients (57 %) who were passive smokers. The median duration of hemodialysis therapy was 5.54 years (range: 1–20 years) (Table 1).

Catheter insertion site and duration of function

Most initial catheters were placed in the right internal jugular vein (RIJV) (n = 33, 78.6 %), with fewer in the left internal jugular (LIJV) (n = 5, 12 %) or right subclavian vein (RSCV) (n = 4, 9.4 %). Catheter patency duration decreased with subsequent insertions (median patency: 2.35 years for the first CVC vs. 1.33 years for the third). Venographic imaging confirmed fibrin sheath formation as the primary cause of dysfunction in all cases.

The unit of analysis in this study was the individual patient. Each of the 42 patients contributed a single, angiographically confirmed catheter dysfunction episode treated with tPA. While many patients experienced multiple catheter insertions over time, only the catheter associated with the tPA-treated episode was analyzed for outcomes.

Among these dysfunction episodes, 8 (19.0 %) occurred in the first catheter, 19 (45.2 %) in the second, 11 (26.2 %) in the third, 3 (7.1 %) in the fourth, and 1 (2.4 %) in the fifth or sixth catheter.

tPA therapy outcomes

33 (78.6 %) of patients demonstrated effective function after receiving the first dose of Actilyse® through both CVC lines and their dialysis was carried out effectively under their treatment protocol. However, in 9 (21.4 %) patients, catheter dysfunction was not resolved after the first tPA therapy and they were excluded from the follow-ups. Between 6 and 18 months following the initial injection, 4 of the treated patients experienced recurrent CVC dysfunction. The venographic assessment confirmed fibrin sheath reformation in all these patients. After proper preparation, these patients received a second session of tPA therapy, which was successful in all cases, resulting in the restoration of effective function in both CVC lines. It is worth noting that two of these patients experienced catheter dysfunction after 14 and 16 months. Fibrin sheath reformation was confirmed in these patients through venography and after proper preparation, they received a third session of tPA therapy. The third injection of tPA in these patients was successful leading to effective hemodialysis, and during a six-month follow-up period after receiving the third dose, no catheter dysfunction was observed.

Complications

No tPA-related complications were reported in patients during follow-up.

Analysis of risk factors for fibrin sheath formation

Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify the independent risk factors for fibrin sheath-induced catheter dysfunction, which included multiple catheter insertions (odds ratio [OR]: 1.92, 95 % confidence interval [CI]: 1.15–3.21, p-value: 0.013), left-sided catheter insertion (OR: 2.12, 95 % CI: 1.04–4.32, p: 0.039), and primary diabetes (OR: 3.28, 95 % CI: 1.46–7.38, p: 0.004).

It is noteworthy that older age (OR: 1.05, 95 % CI: 1.02–1.08, p: 0.001), primary diabetes (OR: 2.67, 95 % CI: 1.21–5.89, p: 0.015), and smoking status (OR: 2.14, 95 % CI: 1.03–4.44, p: 0.042) were also recognized as independent risk factors for catheter dysfunction.

To account for repeated catheter events and varying exposure durations, negative binomial regression was applied using total catheter patency time (in years) as an offset. The model assessed the incidence rate of catheter dysfunction relative to diabetes, hypertension, and smoking status.

•Hypertension was associated with a 2.12-fold increase in the incidence rate of dysfunction (IRR = 2.12, 95 % CI: 0.94–4.79), though this did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.070).

These findings suggest no statistically significant associations between these clinical variables and dysfunction incidence in this cohort.

Discussion

This study demonstrates the efficacy of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in managing catheter dysfunction caused by fibrin sheath formation, with a 78.6 % success rate after the first session and 100 % cumulative success following repeated interventions. The findings align with prior research that highlights tPA's role in dissolving fibrin-related components, offering a less invasive alternative to mechanical interventions such as catheter exchange or fibrin sheath stripping [16].

The results of this study are consistent with other studies that have shown smoking, diabetes, and advanced age to be important risk factors for the dysfunction of the catheter [17].

The evidence currently available supports the idea that older age is an independent risk factor for dialysis catheter dysfunction. Increased inflammatory and procoagulant states, progressive endothelial dysfunction, and the build-up of comorbidities are all linked to aging and may increase the risk of catheter-related problems [18,19].

The influence of primary diabetes on vascular health is shown by the substantial correlation observed between metabolic disease and catheter dysfunction. Diabetes is recognized as a pro-thrombotic condition that is typified by impaired fibrinolysis, endothelial dysfunction, and hyper reactivity of platelets [20,21]. These pathophysiological changes may increase the risk of fibrin sheath development, which could ultimately result in catheter dysfunction [22,23].

This finding is also in line with other studies identifying smoking as a separate risk factor for catheter dysfunction. In addition to being linked to endothelial dysfunction, elevated oxidative stress, and a pro-inflammatory state, smoking is a significant risk factor for cardiovascular disease [24,25]. Furthermore, catheter patency and performance may be directly impacted by the harmful substances found in cigarette smoke [26].

Due to recurrent endothelial damage and the activation of coagulation cascades, multiple catheter insertions may increase the likelihood of fibrin sheath development [27]. Furthermore, impaired fibrinolysis might promote unrestricted fibrin deposition, which would aid in the development of sheaths. Anatomical considerations, such as differences in blood flow patterns or venous stasis, may contribute to the observed increased risk associated with left-sided catheter insertions by potentially promoting the production of fibrin sheaths [28].

While fibrin sheaths are primarily composed of fibrous tissue, they often include thrombotic components that render them susceptible to fibrinolytic agents like tPA [29]. This dual composition may explain the high success rate observed in this study. Future research using advanced imaging techniques or histopathological analysis could further elucidate the composition of fibrin sheaths and optimize therapeutic approaches.

The non-invasive nature of tPA therapy reduces the procedural risks associated with mechanical interventions, such as vascular injury or infection. These findings support the broader adoption of tPA therapy in clinical practice, particularly in resource-limited settings where access to mechanical interventions may be restricted.

Several studies have evaluated tPA and similar fibrinolytic agents for catheter dysfunction. For instance, Moradi et al. reported that alteplase maintained catheter function for over six months in 58 % of cases, three to six months in 31 %, and less than three months in 11 %. Although 75 % of patients experienced improved blood flow velocity, some had insufficient responses, and 8 % of catheters remained non-functional. Such findings underscore alteplase's effectiveness but also highlight the need for individualized treatment strategies and further evaluation of long-term outcomes [30].

The results of this study are consistent with previous investigations that highlight the immediate efficacy of tPA [31], while extending the discussion to repeated interventions, demonstrating that subsequent tPA doses can effectively manage recurrent fibrin sheath formation. Compared to previous studies, our research significantly reported a higher long-term patency rate. Since the follow-up for all patients was conducted meticulously over at least one year through a multidisciplinary approach involving interventional radiology, nephrology, and vascular surgery services, the likelihood of bias is nearly eliminated. Therefore, a nationwide study has been initiated to obtain more comprehensive and accurate results on the efficacy of tPA therapy on fibrin sheath related dialysis catheter dysfunction within the Iranian population.

Additionally, studies on dose and dwell time optimization, such as El-Masri et al. and Yaseen et al., have indicated that higher doses or prolonged infusion times may improve outcomes [32,33]. However, these intensified protocols may theoretically increase the risk of adverse events, particularly off-target bleeding. Although bleeding events associated with rt-PA use are considered rare due to its fibrin-specific action and weak antifibrinogen effect, there remains a lack of large-scale, controlled trials to definitively assess safety outcomes across different dosing regimens [34]. Notably, the 2019 KDOQI guidelines recommend the use of 2 mg rt-PA as the standard dose for restoring catheter patency, citing success rates between 50 % and 90 % [14]. This study supports the use of a standard 2 mg dose without any bleeding-related complications, but highlights the need for individualized treatment approaches based on patient-specific factors such as comorbidities and catheter history.

While previous studies have explored the use of fibrinolytic agents such as tPA and urokinase in managing catheter dysfunction, our study provides novel insight by focusing exclusively on angiographically confirmed fibrin sheath–induced dysfunction, a more specific and underexplored subset. Furthermore, the analysis was conducted within a real-world, resource-limited setting where advanced endovascular interventions are not routinely accessible. Unlike many earlier studies, we also accounted for longitudinal catheterization histories and adjusted for catheter patency duration using both logistic and negative binomial regression models, offering a more refined understanding of dysfunction risk over time.

Although this study offers insightful information, certain limitations should be noted. The findings' potential for generalization is limited by the lack of a control group, single-center approach and the relatively small sample size. These factors reduce the external validity and statistical power of the results. A multicenter study with a larger cohort would provide more robust data and improve the generalizability of the findings.

Furthermore, as a retrospective study, we were unable to collect data on various potential factors that could influence catheter dysfunction, such as catheter material, catheter tip design, or catheter locking solutions. These variables, which have been identified in previous studies as important contributors to catheter dysfunction, were not part of the current analysis [35,36]. Additionally, the retrospective design limited our ability to calculate the incidence of catheter dysfunction in standardized time units (e.g., per 1000 catheter-years), which could enhance comparability with other cohorts. Future prospective studies that evaluate these factors would offer a more comprehensive understanding of the elements that contribute to catheter dysfunction and may guide the development of more effective interventions.

Potential confounding variables, such as adherence to dialysis schedules, concurrent medication use, and socioeconomic factors, were not assessed in this study. These factors may influence both the risk of catheter dysfunction and the effectiveness of tPA therapy and future studies should aim to account for these variables to provide a more robust analysis. Additionally, smoking status was incompletely documented in male patients, which limited our ability to perform subgroup analysis based on active versus passive exposure.

The study population was exclusively from a single region in Iran, which might influence risk factors or outcomes differently compared to diverse or global populations. Future studies should aim to include a more diverse patient population to examine whether these findings are consistent across different demographic and geographic groups.

Future multicenter, prospective studies with larger cohorts are needed to validate these findings and expand upon the role of tPA in fibrin sheath management.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) is an effective and minimally invasive treatment for catheter dysfunction caused by fibrin sheath formation in hemodialysis patients, with a high success rate following initial and repeated interventions. The study also identifies key risk factors, including diabetes, smoking, and advanced age, that contribute to catheter dysfunction. By addressing these modifiable risk factors, clinicians can improve patient outcomes and reduce the incidence of recurrent catheter dysfunction. These findings support the broader use of tPA in managing catheter-related complications, particularly in resource-limited settings, and emphasize the need for personalized treatment strategies based on patient-specific factors. Future research should focus on validating these findings in broader populations and exploring preventive measures to mitigate fibrin sheath formation.

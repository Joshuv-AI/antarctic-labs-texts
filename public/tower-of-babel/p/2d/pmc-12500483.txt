Clinical study of aumolertinib versus osimertinib in the treatment of EGFR-mutated advanced non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Sheng-Xi Yang, Jia-De Zhu, Ling Ma, Yan Pan, Qiu-Rui Li, Wen-Lian Tu, Zhi-Qing Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo compare the efficacy and safety of aumolertinib and osimertinib in the treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations.MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed for patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC. The primary outcome was median progression-free survival (mPFS). Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test were used to evaluate PFS and overall survival (OS), Cox regression was used for prognostic factors assessment.ResultsA total of 202 patients were included, comprising 106 aumolertinib users and 96 osimertinib users. In the first-line treatment, no significant difference was observed in mPFS between aumolertinib and osimertinib group (19.0 months; hazard ratio [HR], 1.15; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.70–1.91; P = 0.58). The disease control rate (DCR) (95.7% vs. 89.8%), objective response rate (ORR) (39.1% vs. 30.6%), and median OS (27.0 vs. 38.0 months) were comparable between the two groups, with no statistical significance. In the second-line treatment, aumolertinib and osimertinib demonstrated similar mPFS (13.5 vs. 13.0 months; HR, 1.13; 95% CI, 0.75–1.71; P = 0.55), DCR (90.0% vs. 87.2%), ORR (16.7% vs. 17.0%), and median OS (24.0 vs. 20.0 months). Adverse events of grade 1–2 were also similar between the two groups. Cox regression revealed that performance status (P < 0.001) and the treatment lines of aumolertinib/osimertinib (P = 0.015) were independent prognostic factors.ConclusionAumolertinib and osimertinib exhibit comparable efficacy and safety profiles in the treatment of EGFR-mutant NSCLC, providing a reference path for clinicians to optimize drug treatment strategies in NSCLC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03642-5.Keywords: NSCLC, Aumolertinib, Osimertinib, EGFR

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Its complex biological characteristics, high recurrence rate, and poorer prognosis emphasize the urgent need for effective treatment strategies. The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a key driver gene in NSCLC, with mutations occurring more frequently in Asian populations [2–3]. For patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have become the standard first-line treatment [4].

EGFR-TKIs function by inhibiting the binding of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to the tyrosine kinase domain, which suppresses cell proliferation and induces apoptosis in tumor cells [5]. This mechanism ultimately contributes to prolonged patient survival. However, most patients receiving first or second-generation EGFR-TKI therapy develop acquired resistance within 9 to 14 months, with approximately 50% of cases exhibiting the T790M mutation [6–7]. Third-generation EGFR-TKIs, through irreversible covalent binding to EGFR, specifically target the T790M resistance mutation, offering the potential for long-term tumor control in NSCLC patients [8]. In recent years, third-generation EGFR-TKIs, particularly osimertinib and aumolertinib, have garnered significant attention due to their high selectivity for EGFR mutations and potent inhibitory effects.

Osimertinib was approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2015 for the treatment of advanced NSCLC with T790M resistance mutations or EGFR-sensitive mutations [9]. Supported by robust clinical data, it has become a globally recognized standard treatment [10]. Aumolertinib (formerly almonertinib), a domestically developed third-generation EGFR-TKI in China, was approved for clinical use in March 2020 [11]. The 2020 edition of the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines further recommended aumolertinib as a first-line treatment option for NSCLC patients with EGFR-sensitive mutations [12]. Although both drugs have demonstrated excellent results in the treatment of NSCLC, there are no comprehensive head-to-head comparative studies that systematically evaluate the efficacy, safety, and cost-effectiveness of these two agents. This evidence gap restricts clinicians from making informed and personalized treatment decisions and poses challenges for the broader adoption of domestically developed therapies in the global market.

Therefore, this study aims to systematically compare the efficacy and safety of aumolertinib and osimertinib in the treatment of NSCLC, providing evidence to guide precision medicine and therapeutic selection, hoping to provide additional novel theoretical basis for optimizing drug treatment strategies in NSCLC patients.

Method

Study design and patients

The data of patients with EGFR mutant advanced NSCLC who were admitted to the First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province from April 2020 to June 2023 were retrospectively collected, including the gender, age, smoking history, clinical tumor stage, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS), metastases number, brain metastases, EGFR mutation type, treatment regimen, and the patients were divided into aumolertinib group and osimertinib group according to different medications. Among the participants, 95 patients received first-line regimen treatment (with no prior treatment using EGFR-TKIs), while the remaining 107 patients underwent second-line regimen treatment (having been previously treated with EGFR-TKIs and assessed for T790M mutation status by tissue biopsy and liquid biopsy, indicating resistance to prior therapy). Fifteen patients have been treated with gefitinib and icotinib, but not two complete first-generation TKI treatment regimens, these patients are still classified as second-line treatment. Eleven patients (7 in the aumolertinib group and 4 in the osimertinib group) received second-line third-generation TKIs without confirmed T790M positivity, and this treatment regimen was decided upon through team consultation. Twenty-seven patients received temporary therapy before receiving the third-generation TKI, of which 14 received platinum compounds combined with pemetrexed, 7 received gemcitabine with nedaplatin, and 6 received bevacizumab. The follow-up information was obtained from outpatient follow-up reviews or telephone follow-ups up to December 31, 2024.

Patients in the aumolertinib group received monotherapy with aumolertinib mesylate tablets (National Drug Approval Number: H20200004, specification: 55 mg) at a dose of 110 mg once daily, administered orally. Patients in the osimertinib group received monotherapy with osimertinib mesylate tablets (National Drug Approval Number: J20180027, specification: 80 mg; National Drug Approval Number: J20180028, specification: 40 mg) at a dose of 80 mg once daily, administered orally. There are no patients who continued TKI therapy beyond radiological progression, but three patients combined radiotherapy while receiving TKI (2 in the aumolertinib group and 1 in the osimertinib group). Treatment in both groups continued until disease progression or the occurrence of severe adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study protocol was approved by the First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Medical Ethics Committee of the hospital, and the batch number is KHLL2024-KY071. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects and/or their legal guardian. All methods were conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for this study were: (1) pathologically confirmed diagnosis of EGFR-mutant advanced NSCLC; (2) patients who are unable to undergo surgical treatment based on their condition assessment; (3) the clinical stage is stage IIIB, IIIC, or IV; (3) Received aumolertinib or osimertinib treatment and evaluated for efficacy. The exclusion criteria for this study were: (1) patients with a serious lack of clinical data; (2) The patient used both aumolertinib and osimertinib during the disease, which made it impossible to group based on the medication; (3) severe organ damage (liver failure or kidney failure) or combined other malignant tumors, and (4) pregnant patient.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was median progression-free survival (mPFS). Secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), disease control rate (DCR), and objective response rate (ORR). According to the Efficacy Evaluation Criteria for Solid Tumors 1.1 (RECIST 1.1), responses are divided into complete remission (CR), partial remission (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD). DCR is defined as (number of CR cases + number of PR cases + number of SD cases) / total number of cases × 100%, while ORR is calculated as (number of CR cases + number of PR cases) / total number of cases × 100%. The AJCC TNM staging system of the 8th edition was used for tumor staging [13]. Adverse reactions are classified into grades 1 ~ 5 based on the National Cancer Institute Evaluation Criteria for Common Adverse Reactions (CTCAE Version 4.0) [14].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software version 4.2.3. Chi-square test (or Fisher’s exact test) was used to assess differences between categorical variables expressed in numerical values (percentages). Kaplan-Meier method was used to evaluate the differences in PFS and OS between the two groups. Cox regression analysis was performed to evaluate prognostic factors, with univariate analysis conducted first, followed by multivariate analysis including variables with P < 0.1 in the univariate analysis. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Study population and baseline data

Between April 01, 2020, and July 01,2023,1189 patients were screened in the First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province. After 987 patients were excluded according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the remaining 202 were included in this study. Among them, 106 were in the aumolertinib group and 96 were in the osimertinib group (Supplementary Fig S1). The baseline characteristics of the two groups were shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences in the demographic and clinical characteristics between the two groups (all P > 0.5). Except for tumor staging and number of metastases, the SMD values of the remaining variables were less than 0.2, which can be considered as a balanced distribution of data between the two groups and comparable [15]. Additionally, according to the treatment line, patients were divided into first-line and second-line treatments with aumolertinib or osimertinib. The baseline data of the two treatment options is shown in Supplementary Table S1 and Supplementary Table S2.

Evaluation of efficacy

We first evaluated the survival and treatment efficacy of the two patient groups using RECIST 1.1. In first-line treatment, median PFS (mPFS) was 19 months for both aumolertinib and osimertinib, with no significant difference (HR, 1.15; 95% CI 0.70 to 1.91; P = 0.58) (Fig. 1A). DCR were 95.7% (95% CI 85.2–99.5) and 89.8% (95% CI 77.8–96.6) for aumolertinib and osimertinib, respectively, while ORR was 39.1% (95% CI 25.1–54.6) and 30.6% (95% CI: 18.3–45.4), respectively. No statistically significant differences were observed in DCR (P = 0.427) or ORR (P = 0.991) between the two groups. Regarding OS, mOS was 27 months for aumolertinib and 36 months for osimertinib, and no significant difference was observed (P = 0.371). Similarly, one-year and two-year overall survival rates showed no significant differences (Table 2).

In second-line treatment, as shown in Fig. 1B, the mPFS was 13.5 months in the aumolertinib group and 13 months in the osimertinib group, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (HR,1.13;95%CI 0.75 to 1.71; P = 0.55). As for the secondary endings, no statistically significant differences were observed for DCR (P = 0.887) or ORR (P = 0.962) between the aumolertinib and osimertinib group, with DCR of 90.0% (95% CI 79.5–96.2) and 87.2% (95% CI 74.3–95.2), respectively, and ORR of 16.7% (95% CI 8.3–28.5) and 17.0% (95% CI 7.6–30.8), respectively (Table 3). Additionally, mOS, one-year, and two-year overall survival rates did not significantly differ between osimertinib and aumolertinib (Table 3).

Analysis of prognostic factors

Cox regression analysis based on PFS was performed to assess prognostic factors in the two patient groups. Univariable analysis indicated that tumor clinical stage (HR,2.01; 95%CL,1.31–3.09; P = 0.002), PS (HR,2.00; 95%CI,1.57–2.53; P < 0.001), metastases number (HR,1.46; 95%CI,1.23–1.73; P < 0.001), and AUM/OSI line (HR,1.67; 95%CI,1.21–2.32; P = 0.002) were associated with patient prognosis. Then four variables were subsequently included in the multivariable Cox regression analysis, which identified PS (HR,1.69; 95%CI,1.27–2.24; P < 0.001) and treatment lines of aumolertinib/osimertinib (HR,1.50; 95%CI,1.08–2.09; P = 0.015) as independent prognostic factors (Supplementary Table S3). This suggests that in addition to the physical status of patients, the medication regimen is also important for prognosis.

Subgroup analysis

We conducted a subgroup analysis of PFS based on baseline characteristics to further explore whether certain prospective patient subgroups were more likely to benefit from aumolertinib or osimertinib treatment. However, no significant differences in survival were observed between the two groups across age, gender, EGFR mutation types, smoking history, tumor stage, brain metastases, and number of metastases (Fig. 2).

Comparison of adverse events

Table 4 presents the incidence of adverse events of different grades in the two patient groups. Among grade 1–2 adverse events, the most common were rash (44.34% vs. 48.96%), mouth ulceration (18.87% vs. 30.21%), nauseated (17.92% vs. 15.63), and diarrhea (16.98% vs. 21.88%) in the aumolertinib group and osimertinib group, respectively. For grade 3–4 adverse events, two cases of paronychia and two cases of inflammatory lung injury were reported in the osimertinib group, while no such events occurred in the aumolertinib group. The incidence of other types of adverse events was similar between the two groups.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to directly compare the efficacy and safety of aumolertinib and osimertinib in the treatment of advanced NSCLC with EGFR mutations. Our findings indicate no statistically significant differences in PFS, OS, DCR, and ORR between the two groups in both first-line and second-line treatment. Furthermore, the incidence and types of adverse events (AEs) were comparable between the two groups, with no apparent differences in safety profiles. These results indicated that aumolertinib exhibits comparable efficacy and safety to osimertinib, offering a viable alternative treatment option for NSCLC patients.

NSCLC exhibits substantial biological heterogeneity, highlighting the urgent need for novel and effective targeted therapeutic strategies. The activation mutations of the EGFR, recognized as key oncogenic drivers, play a pivotal role in NSCLC progression and serve as crucial molecular targets for targeted therapy [16]. In this regard, multiple EGFR-TKIs have been developed and demonstrated remarkable clinical efficacy [17]. First-generation EGFR-TKIs, such as gefitinib, were historically considered the standard of care for both recurrent and metastatic EGFR-mutant NSCLC. However, acquired resistance inevitably emerges during treatment, affecting approximately 60% of EGFR-TKI-treated patients [18]. Among the known resistance mechanisms, the secondary T790M mutation is the most common, conferring resistance by increasing the receptor’s affinity for ATP, thereby reducing the binding efficacy of TKIs and diminishing their therapeutic activity [19]. Third-generation EGFR-TKIs have been specifically designed to overcome T790M-mediated resistance and have become the primary treatment option for patients with T790M-positive NSCLC.

Before the approval of aumolertinib, osimertinib was the only third-generation EGFR-TKIs approved for the treatment of EGFR-mutant NSCLC. Previous studies have demonstrated the superior efficacy of osimertinib over first-generation EGFR-TKIs in patients with EGFR mutations [20]. The FLAURA study showed that osimertinib significantly prolonged PFS compared to gefitinib (18.9 months vs. 10.2 months, HR = 0.46), establishing its role as the first-line EGFR-TKI of choice [21]. In our study, the median PFS for first-line osimertinib treatment was 19.0 months, which is consistent with previous reports. Similarly, the median OS reported in the FLAURA study (38.6 months) was slightly longer than that observed in our study (38.0 months). Additionally, the AENEAS study reported a median PFS of 20.8 months for first-line aumolertinib treatment, which was similar to the 19.0 months observed in our study [11]. These results support the reliability of our data and findings. It should be noted that a small subset of patients received cytotoxic chemotherapy as interim treatment before initiating third-generation EGFR-TKIs. This may have introduced prognostic bias, as these patients are likely to have more aggressive disease or delayed access to third-generation EGFR-TKIs. Additionally, ORR in the AENEAS study (73.8%) was considerably higher than that in our study (39.1%). This discrepancy may be due to differences in assessment schedule and imaging frequency. In our real-world retrospective cohort, response assessments were conducted at irregular intervals, primarily based on clinical judgment and institutional schedules, whereas AENEAS employed standardized, protocol-defined imaging every 6 weeks, which may have captured earlier or more frequent partial responses. Delayed or missed assessments in our cohort could have resulted in underestimation of ORR. It is noteworthy that most existing studies have focused on comparisons between third-generation and first-generation EGFR-TKIs, while direct head-to-head data comparing aumolertinib and osimertinib remains limited. Our study demonstrated no significant differences in PFS, DCR, or ORR between the two agents, providing a reference path to the drug selection of NSCLC.

When evaluating the clinical utility of anticancer agents, safety is as crucial as efficacy. Therefore, we further compared the adverse events (AEs) profiles of aumolertinib and osimertinib. Previous studies have reported that rash, diarrhea, and stomatitis are common AEs associated with third-generation EGFR-TKIs [22–24]. In our study, there were no statistically significant differences in the incidence of grade 1–2 AEs, including rash, stomatitis, nausea, and diarrhea etc., between the two groups, suggesting similar tolerability profiles. Notably, a study reported the incidence of stomatitis was 54.55% in the osimertinib group, which was considerably higher than our findings, possibly due to differences in study populations or data collection methods [25]. Additionally, it was shown that EGFR-TKIs may be associated with cardiac AEs, such as QTc prolongation, heart failure, and atrial fibrillation [26–27]. However, no such events were observed in our study, and the lack of adequate routine cardiac monitoring (electrocardiograms and echocardiography) may be a key reason. Regarding grade 3–4 AEs, two cases of paronychia and inflammatory lung injury were observed in the osimertinib group, whereas no similar severe AEs were reported in the aumolertinib group. Such AEs with low incidence need further validation in larger studies.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, as this is a single-center study, the external generalization of these results is not robust. Therefore, further prospective multicenter studies are needed to validate the current findings. Second, this study was retrospective in nature, which may introduce selection bias and confounding factors that could impact the validity of the findings.

Finally, the median follow-up duration was limited, potentially affecting OS comparisons and long-term efficacy assessments. Those limitations should be addressed in further studies.

In conclusion, this study provided the direct comparative analysis of aumolertinib and osimertinib in the treatment of EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients, demonstrating comparable efficacy and safety between the two agents. These findings offer valuable clinical evidence to support treatment selection in patients with advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC. However, prospective randomized controlled trials are needed to further explore potential efficacy differences across patient subgroups and optimize personalized EGFR-TKI therapy strategies.

Digital Strategies Utilised to Encourage Undergraduate Nursing Students' Engagement in Online Units of Study Throughout the COVID‐19 Pandemic: A Systematic Review
Authors: Chanchal Kurup, Abigail Ford, Penny Heidke
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimThis systematic review provides an overview of digital strategies utilised to encourage undergraduate nursing students' engagement in online units of study throughout the COVID‐19 pandemic.BackgroundIn nursing education, engagement was crucial in acquiring knowledge, skills, critical thinking and problem‐solving abilities. The COVID‐19 pandemic forced schools and universities worldwide to quickly adapt from face‐to‐face learning to online platforms. While online teaching and digital technologies in education were not new, the urgency to create effective online learning opportunities that meet curriculum needs and maintain student engagement was particularly challenging for teaching staff and students.Design: Review MethodsThe databases used were Embase, CINAHL, EMCARE, ERIC and BASE. The review was limited to English and literature published between January 2020 and September 2022 to capture the studies published around the COVID‐19 lockdown. Three researchers independently completed the study selection, quality assessment and data extraction. Any discrepancies were resolved in a consensus‐building conversation.ResultsMultiple strategies, such as simulations, gamification and telehealth role‐playing, effectively enhanced nursing students' online engagement during COVID‐19. Instructor expertise, student‐centred approaches and reliable infrastructure emerged as pivotal.ConclusionThe COVID‐19 pandemic accelerated the shift to online nursing education, highlighting the need for innovative strategies to sustain student engagement. Identifying the most effective pedagogical approaches, such as virtual simulations, gamification and experiential learning, will support future online learning. Integrating emerging technologies and student‐centred teaching methods will be crucial in preparing nursing students for clinical practice in a post‐pandemic world.

Introduction

The COVID‐19 pandemic, declared a global health emergency by the World Health Organization in March 2020 (World Health Organization 2020), prompted an unprecedented shift in educational delivery worldwide. Academic institutions, particularly those relying on face‐to‐face teaching, faced the urgent need to transition to emergency remote teaching to maintain curriculum delivery and student engagement (Mosleh et al. 2022). This rapid change exposed disparities in technology access and digital infrastructure, compelling educators to adapt and develop digital tools that accommodate diverse student needs (Natarajan and Joseph 2022). Despite varied success globally, these shifts accelerated the adoption of online teaching strategies across undergraduate nursing education (Riley et al. 2021).

Undergraduate nursing programmes include significant practical components for acquiring clinical skills and effective communication with patients and peers (Gregersen et al. 2021). Transitioning clinical teaching to online formats proved particularly challenging, given the technical limitations of demonstrating hands‐on skills remotely (Martin et al. 2022). As online learning becomes increasingly prevalent, universities must prioritise creating engaging and interactive experiences that sustain student motivation and connection to coursework, factors strongly linked to retention and academic success (Lin et al. 2023).

Student engagement in nursing education is fundamental to developing critical thinking, clinical competence and problem‐solving skills (Ferrer et al. 2022). Well‐qualified nurses who receive thorough and active training contribute to safe, effective healthcare delivery (Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care 2017). However, engagement alone does not guarantee effective transition into clinical practice. In particular, research on internationally qualified nurses (IQNs) highlights persistent gaps between education, skill recognition and clinical utilisation within host healthcare systems (Kurup et al. 2022, 2024). Educational interventions must be complemented by organisational and policy‐level support to ensure effective engagement (Kurup et al. 2023, 2025). In this context, the role of digital education extends beyond facilitating engagement, encompassing broader objectives of workforce readiness, equity and sustainable integration into healthcare systems.

Nursing institutions, therefore, play a critical role in preparing students for these complex demands by adopting innovative, student‐centred teaching approaches that integrate academic learning with clinical application (Jeppesen et al. 2017; Phillips and Young 2018). Evidence consistently demonstrates that enhanced engagement is associated with improved academic performance and learning outcomes (Schnitzler et al. 2021; Werang and Leba 2022). Nevertheless, sustaining engagement remains particularly challenging in regional and distributed learning contexts, where clinical placements, residential schools and work‐integrated learning opportunities are often intermittent, disrupting continuity and learner immersion (Liu et al. 2023).

The pandemic has further accelerated enrolment in fully online higher education programmes, intensifying the need for effective and scalable digital engagement strategies tailored to diverse student populations (El Said 2021). While a wide range of digital teaching approaches have been implemented, their effectiveness in maintaining meaningful engagement and supporting deeper learning outcomes remains variable and context‐dependent.

This review addresses these challenges by examining the effectiveness of digital teaching strategies in undergraduate nursing education. In an era characterised by rapid technological advancement and evolving healthcare demands, understanding how to sustain and enhance student engagement through innovative digital approaches is essential. Beyond nursing education, the findings provide broader insights into how digital pedagogy can support student learning, engagement and readiness for practice across higher education. This study aims to guide future curriculum development and teaching practices to support quality higher education in an increasingly digital world by synthesising current evidence.

Review Aim

This systematic review provides an overview of digital strategies utilised to encourage undergraduate nursing students' engagement in online units of study throughout the COVID‐19 pandemic.

Review Question

Which digital strategies are utilised to encourage undergraduate nursing students' engagement in online units of study throughout the COVID‐19 pandemic and to identify key insights that can inform and strengthen future nursing education?

Methods and Analysis

Study Design

This review was designed in accordance with JBI guidelines for mixed methods systematic reviews (Lizarondo et al. 2022) and registered in OSF Registries (https://osf.io/d96b2). The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses 2020 checklist guided the reporting process, and a preliminary scoping search was conducted to avoid duplication of existing reviews. The PPICOS framework, encompassing Population, Problem, Intervention, Outcomes and Setting, steered the research question (Bérubé et al. 2023).

Inclusion Criteria

Population (Type of Participants)

This review included all studies involving undergraduate nursing students enrolled in any undergraduate nursing programme in any country and at any year level. Studies that compared undergraduate nursing students with qualified nurses or students from other disciplines were included if data were presented separately.

Problem

This review only included studies that investigated promoting increased engagement or maintaining engagement of nursing students in online units of study impacted by the COVID‐19 pandemic.

Intervention (Types of Interventions)

The review investigated the digital teaching strategies used in undergraduate nursing programmes to enhance the online engagement of nursing students during the COVID‐19 pandemic. It focused on the online engagement of nursing students during COVID‐19. The studies evaluated the effectiveness of these strategies in improving undergraduate nurses' engagement with their clinical or theoretical learning.

Outcomes

The outcome explored in this review was encouraging increased engagement in online undergraduate nursing programmes during the COVID‐19 pandemic.

Setting

This review focused on studies that discussed teaching strategies utilised by higher education institutions for undergraduate nursing students that have taken place online only in response to lockdowns during the COVID‐19 pandemic.

Types of Studies

This review considered primary research that used any study design focused on digital strategies to encourage undergraduate nursing students' engagement in online units of study throughout the COVID‐19 pandemic.

Search Strategies

Electronic Searches

The database search included Embase, CINAHL, EMCARE, ERIC and BASE, focusing on English‐language literature published between January 2020 and September 2022. The strategy combined concepts and study‐type filters, using free‐text, synonyms and indexed terms like Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) where available. Truncation symbols and wildcards captured variations, with searches conducted in titles and abstracts to maximise relevant results.

Search Terms

A comprehensive literature search began in August 2022, when COVID was active and disrupting nursing education, to gather relevant peer‐reviewed studies on digital strategies for engaging nursing students in online education. To ensure a systematic approach, an expert health science librarian assisted in developing a modified PICo (Population, Issue and Context) framework. Subject headings and keywords were carefully selected to retrieve high‐quality literature specific to the field.

Data Extraction (Selection and Coding)

Search results were imported into Covidence, and duplicates were removed before title and abstract screening. Two independent review authors identified studies that met the inclusion criteria outlined above. The two review authors retrieved the full‐text articles of the potentially eligible studies and independently assessed them for eligibility. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion with the two reviewers and with the involvement of a third reviewer, where necessary.

Methodological Quality Assessment

This systematic review implemented a rigorous quality assessment process to ensure robust internal validity and minimise the risk of bias within the studies meeting the predefined inclusion criteria. The critical appraisal procedure utilised specialised tools tailored to the diverse study designs incorporated in the review. Two independent reviewers meticulously evaluated each study using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT) (Hong et al. 2018). MMAT provides a comprehensive framework for assessing the quality of studies with diverse designs, facilitating a thorough evaluation encompassing both quantitative and qualitative aspects of research. The reviewers engaged in a collaborative discussion following the independent assessments to compare their findings. Any disparities in critical evaluations were systematically addressed through a consensus‐building conversation; in instances where consensus could not be achieved, a third reviewer, independent of the initial assessments, contributed an additional evaluation to reconcile discrepancies. The quality scores obtained for each study were meticulously recorded, providing a transparent and quantifiable measure of the methodological robustness of the included research. This comprehensive approach to quality assessment not only strengthens the internal validity of the systematic review but also ensures a nuanced understanding of the strengths and limitations inherent in each study, thereby contributing to the overall reliability of the review's findings (Table 1).

Data Synthesis

The data synthesis process for this mixed methods systematic review (MMSR) adhered to the meticulous guidelines of the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology, utilising a convergent integrated approach (The Joanna Briggs Institute 2022). Quantitative data were transformed into a qualitative format through a rigorous ‘qualitisation’ process. The qualitative and quantitative data were systematically combined based on similarities to generate meaningful themes. The comprehensive data collection encompasses various facets of each study, ensuring the extraction of pertinent information such as study design, objectives, context, sample details, intervention, tools, results, analysis methods, conclusions and quality assessment. Two researchers independently extracted data using a standardised sheet and rigorously tested it for appropriateness. A third researcher resolved discrepancies through consensus. The synthesis process maintained continuous alignment with the review questions, while a robust quality assessment underpinned the reliability of the synthesised findings, contributing to a nuanced understanding of the research landscape.

Results

The initial database search retrieved a total of 579 articles. After removing duplicate titles and conducting screening of abstracts, full‐text review and critical appraisal, 22 studies (n = 22) remained for further examination (see Figure 1).

Prisma flow chart.

The documents included in this systematic review were published between 2021 and 2022, aligning with the COVID‐19 pandemic time frame. This year period also accounts for the typical periods required for article writing and acceptance. See Table 1 for details.

This review identified studies from 12 countries, reflecting the global scope of research on digital education methods in healthcare. The represented countries include South Korea (Almusharraf and Bailey 2021; Choi et al. 2022; Jeong et al. 2022; Kim et al. 2022; Park and Seo 2022; Yang and Oh 2022), the United States (Hassler et al. 2021; Lengetti et al. 2021; New et al. 2022), China (Feng et al. 2022; Kor et al. 2022; Luo et al. 2021), Taiwan (Chang et al. 2022; Hwang et al. 2022), Japan (Kazawa et al. 2022), India (Singh et al. 2021), the Philippines (Saban and Balila 2021), Spain (Ropero‐Padilla et al. 2021), Chile (Manuel et al. 2022), Turkey (Uymaz and Uymaz 2022), Norway (Egilsdottir et al. 2022) and Iran (Haftador et al. 2021). This geographical distribution underscores the universal interest in enhancing healthcare education, particularly in response to the challenges of the COVID‐19 pandemic.

The 22 studies collectively involved 4624 participants, predominantly nursing students. Some studies also include medical students and other healthcare professionals. These studies examined diverse aspects of online and hybrid education, focusing on strategies for engagement and motivation. Due to the interchangeable use of ‘motivation for learning’ and ‘engagement’ in many studies, both concepts were considered in this review.

The studies reviewed employed a wide range of research methodologies, including cross‐sectional studies (Kim et al. 2022; Singh et al. 2021), qualitative research (Kazawa et al. 2022; Ropero‐Padilla et al. 2021; Saban and Balila 2021), quasi‐experimental designs (Chang et al. 2022; Hwang et al. 2022; Luo et al. 2021; Yang and Oh 2022), non‐randomised experimental studies (Feng et al. 2022; Haftador et al. 2021; Jeong et al. 2022), mixed methods research (Egilsdottir et al. 2022; Lengetti et al. 2021; Park and Seo 2022) and case reports (Hassler et al. 2021; New et al. 2022). This methodological diversity is essential for addressing the various research questions posed by nursing education during the pandemic.

Some studies' aims revolved around evaluating online teaching methods to assess their feasibility and effectiveness compared to traditional classrooms, focusing on student satisfaction, engagement and academic achievement (Singh et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2022). Furthermore, studies delved into nursing students' experiences and adaptations to telehealth and virtual learning environments (Kazawa et al. 2022; Saban and Balila 2021), shedding light on the challenges faced during the pandemic.

Innovative teaching strategies, including virtual patient‐based learning (Egilsdottir et al. 2022; Hwang et al. 2022), virtual and augmented reality (Jeong et al. 2022; Uymaz and Uymaz 2022; Yang and Oh 2022), simulation (Lengetti et al. 2021), gamification (Chang et al. 2022) and serious games (Manuel et al. 2022; Ropero‐Padilla et al. 2021), were utilised. Other strategies employed various instructional methods like the flipped classroom (Haftador et al. 2021), Visual Thinking Strategies (VTS), PowerPoint, telehealth, video conferencing and WeChat jigsaw methods (Egilsdottir et al. 2022; Park and Seo 2022). These 22 studies collectively highlight the shift towards online and hybrid educational models in nursing, emphasising the need to adapt teaching strategies to maintain educational quality and student engagement during unprecedented challenges.

Digital Strategies for Engaging Nursing Students in Online Learning During COVID‐19

In a study in India, Singh et al. (2021) employed various online teaching methods and found that teachers used PowerPoint presentations as the most utilised method. The use of digital technology strategies was impacted by students' lack of access to facilities such as laptops, dedicated space to attend online classes, reliable internet and the teachers' abilities. The authors emphasised the need for improved internet and training in ICT, technology and the importance of class duration, and diverse strategies to engage students better.

Kim et al. (2022) evaluated the online delivery of a mix of video lectures and real‐time video class meetings. They found that strategies that enhance ‘cyber‐class flow’, a state of immersive engagement wherein students become so absorbed in online activities that they lose awareness of passing the time, directly influence academic achievement and student satisfaction.

Parallel evidence comes from Kazawa et al. (2022), who incorporated role‐playing elements into e‐learning sessions on chronic conditions and telehealth. This immersive environment allowed participants to witness real‐life patient experiences, fostering a sense of responsibility, ethical nursing and increased self‐efficacy, all contributing to greater engagement in their learning (Kazawa et al. 2022). Hwang et al. (2022) further demonstrated the potential of virtual systems by introducing a cloud‐based Virtual Clinical Diagnostic Education System. This platform delivered immersive patient simulations that significantly enhanced students' self‐efficacy and communication skills while reinforcing theoretical concepts through realistic clinical scenarios and active engagement. Almusharraf and Bailey (2021) highlighted the potential of video‐conference formats such as Zoom in promoting collaborative learning and shaping positive academic expectations, emphasising the importance of student‐instructor interactions in enhancing engagement.

Saban and Balila (2021) utilised virtual simulations (such as vSIM for Nursing), multimedia case studies and diverse digital tools (e.g., video demonstrations, group activities, and interactive platforms like Zoom and Kahoot) that allowed students to engage and actively apply theoretical knowledge in realistic, simulated clinical scenarios. However, in clinical skill learning, the absence of real patient interactions and inadequate home technology significantly reduced student motivation and engagement.

Blended models also showed promise. Ropero‐Padilla et al. (2021) embedded game‐based elements into hybrid learning, effectively holding students' attention and engagement and improving learning outcomes. Similarly, New et al. (2022) employed evolving case studies and reflective exercises, heightening student engagement and clinical readiness. Feng et al. (2022) leveraged the Rain Classroom platform, a smart teaching tool that integrates PowerPoint with WeChat to enable real‐time interaction and data tracking, alongside WeChat to facilitate instantaneous interaction, improving student performance, engagement in course content and interest in physiology coursework. Manuel et al. (2022) capitalised on serious games, demonstrating notable increases in student motivation and active participation in an endocrine physiology unit. Research by Uymaz and Uymaz (2022) pointed to the value of augmented reality tools in shaping nursing students' positive attitudes towards and adopting emerging technologies. Park and Seo (2022) also found that self‐regulated learning and motivation strongly influenced ‘learning flow’, cementing their status as key components of effective online educational experiences.

Additional strategies used by the studies suggested the following approaches for increasing engagement. Hassler et al. (2021) identified flipped clinical learning as a particularly effective strategy during clinical site shortages, while Yang and Oh (2022) verified that virtual reality simulations improved neonatal resuscitation knowledge and reduced student anxiety. Chang et al. (2022) underscored the benefits of gamification in enhancing engagement and self‐efficacy among nursing students, and Choi et al. (2022) advanced the adoption of Visual Thinking Strategies (VTS) to sharpen students' critical thinking and observational skills. Likewise, Egilsdottir et al. (2022) focused on virtual patient encounters that substantially boosted student engagement and self‐confidence. The advent of Emergency Remote Teaching (ERT), necessitated by the COVID‐19 pandemic, created new dependencies on online platforms, with research showing that faculty involvement and digital competence markedly impacted student satisfaction (Fuchs and Karrila 2021). Though asynchronous delivery was generally well‐received, Haftador et al. (2021) noted that face‐to‐face interactions remained a preference for some students, indicating a need for adaptable, blended approaches.

Innovative teaching models yielded promising results across multiple contexts. Jeong et al. (2022) reported that experimental teaching methods correlated with higher levels of learning satisfaction. Kor et al. (2022) advocated for student‐driven experiential learning, which fostered deeper engagement and ownership of the learning process. Luo et al. (2021) captured similarly high engagement levels within various course dimensions, attributing these outcomes to effective programme design and delivery. Additionally, Lengetti et al. (2021) described how interactive and forward‐thinking strategies helped strengthen overall student satisfaction and engagement, underscoring the versatility and impact of well‐designed online pedagogy.

Discussion

The COVID‐19 pandemic triggered a profound shift in nursing education, compelling institutions worldwide to transition rapidly from traditional face‐to‐face teaching to digital learning environments. Educators adopted a range of strategies to sustain student engagement and facilitate learning, including virtual lectures, interactive multimedia, serious games and immersive simulation technologies (Cuevas et al. 2024). Although digital learning is not new, the urgency of pandemic‐related disruptions exposed significant gaps in infrastructure, staff preparedness and pedagogical adaptability, particularly in practice‐based disciplines such as nursing (Hao et al. 2022).

This systematic review synthesised findings from 22 international studies exploring digital strategies to promote undergraduate nursing students' engagement in online learning environments during the COVID‐19 pandemic. These strategies were categorised into five thematic domains: synchronous learning, asynchronous content delivery, immersive/simulation‐based strategy, gamification and self‐regulated or enquiry‐based learning. Despite differences in regional infrastructure, curriculum design and technology access, several cross‐cutting themes emerged, underscoring shared global challenges and successes.

Synchronous teaching approaches, particularly video conferencing platforms such as Zoom and Blackboard Collaborate, facilitated real‐time interaction, peer support and collaborative problem‐solving (Almusharraf and Bailey 2021; Haftador et al. 2021; Lengetti et al. 2021; Park and Seo 2022). These sessions facilitated emotional connection (Saban and Balila 2021) and academic motivation, helping mitigate isolation during lockdowns (Kim et al. 2022). Interactive elements, such as gamified quizzes, spin‐the‐wheel tools and collaborative whiteboards, made sessions more dynamic and personalised, increasing learner engagement and retention (Hassler et al. 2021). This aligns with the recognition that such platforms facilitate meaningful interaction, knowledge exchange and the development of virtual learning communities (Chen et al. 2020). Key features like breakout rooms, shared whiteboards and multimodal communication support collaborative learning experiences (Mendoza et al. 2021). Both educators and students perceive these technologies as valuable for maintaining engagement, providing timely feedback and tracking student progress (Chen et al. 2020).

Immersive digital strategies such as virtual patient (VP) systems, augmented reality (AR) and telehealth simulations emerged as critical tools for replicating clinical experiences. These methods supported the development of clinical reasoning, communication and empathy, even in remote settings (Jeong et al. 2022; Yang and Oh 2022). This is strongly supported by simulation‐based evidence demonstrating that structured and immersive simulation environments significantly enhance clinical competence, confidence and clinical decision‐making among nursing students, positioning simulation as a key mechanism for bridging the theory–practice gap (Ryan et al. 2025, 2023). As clinical placements became scarce, these technologies offered viable alternatives for maintaining practice readiness (Kassutto et al. 2021).

Immersive digital tools extend beyond engagement by enabling the development of practical competence, confidence and clinical reasoning in controlled environments, thereby partially compensating for reduced access to clinical placements. Telehealth‐based learning, for example, has been shown to foster ethical sensitivity and practical competence (Kazawa et al. 2022), while virtual reality simulations have improved learners' confidence and reduced anxiety in high‐stakes clinical scenarios (Yang and Oh 2022; Barlow et al. 2026). Virtual patient systems have also enhanced communication competence, with structured feedback and reflective practice contributing to improved student performance (Robinson et al. 2020). Collectively, these strategies address placement limitations and enrich learning by offering scalable, flexible and learner‐centred alternatives to traditional clinical education.

Gamification was widely used to increase motivation, engagement and knowledge retention. Studies incorporating serious games and game‐based modules, such as those by Ropero‐Padilla et al. (2021) and Chang et al. (2022), highlighted the effectiveness of playful learning in reinforcing theoretical knowledge and building enthusiasm. These interactive formats allowed learners to engage in problem‐solving, receive feedback and repeat activities for mastery, fostering deeper learning. While the long‐term impact on clinical skill transfer remains under‐researched (Reed 2020; Xu et al. 2021), gamified learning has consistently been associated with increased student satisfaction and critical thinking (Malicki et al. 2020) and regulatory compliance training (Seaba and Alorh 2022). A longitudinal study by Putz et al. (2020) confirmed improved knowledge retention through gamified workshop design, with minimal differences based on gender and mixed outcomes regarding age. Collectively, these findings highlight gamification as a promising, though still evolving, pedagogical approach in nursing education.

Self‐paced, asynchronous strategies such as recorded lectures, case‐based discussions and flipped classrooms allowed learners to manage competing responsibilities and inconsistent internet access (Feng et al. 2022; Haftador et al. 2021). Self‐regulated learning models encouraged autonomy and personalised pacing, enhancing cognitive engagement and deep learning (Kor et al. 2022; Luo et al. 2021). Notably, Park and Seo (2022) found that motivation and time management were key drivers of ‘learning flow’, a state of immersive and focused learning essential for online success. Complementing this, broader research points to the importance of self‐regulated learning strategies in online education. High‐achieving students consistently demonstrated stronger use of such strategies across synchronous and asynchronous delivery modes (Alhazbi and Hasan 2021). Pre‐recorded lectures also emerged as an effective tool, offering flexibility and supporting deep learning, particularly for commuter and time‐constrained students (Secker et al. 2022). During the COVID‐19 pandemic, factors such as self‐regulated learning ability, intrinsic motivation and self‐efficacy in clinical practice were shown to influence students' ability to maintain learning flow (Park and Seo 2022). Students employed a range of self‐regulatory practices, such as organising their learning environments, taking notes and reviewing recorded content, to adapt and succeed in remote learning settings (Öztürk and Türker 2024).

However, the findings of this review extend beyond engagement to highlight important workforce implications. Evidence from internationally qualified nurse research indicates that educational preparation does not always translate into effective clinical utilisation of skills. Despite acquiring competencies through education and training, internationally qualified nurses often encounter barriers such as limited recognition of prior experience, lack of organisational support and unclear role expectations, all of which constrain the application of their skills in practice (Kurup et al. 2026a, 2026b). This highlights a critical gap in current educational approaches, where engagement‐focused digital strategies are not consistently aligned with workforce expectations, limiting their capacity to translate learning into effective clinical practice.

Several studies showcased the potential of cutting‐edge technologies like AR and VR in promoting clinical skill acquisition and confidence. Augmented reality, for instance, allowed learners to visualise anatomical structures in 3D, improving understanding and satisfaction (Uymaz and Uymaz 2022). Meanwhile, Rain Classroom and WeChat‐supported platforms enhanced interaction and performance in physiology coursework (Feng et al. 2022). These studies confirm that while digital tools are important, their pedagogical integration via interaction, scenario‐based learning and reflective practice drives student engagement and outcomes. Extending support for these findings, recent research highlights that immersive technologies such as AR and VR offer engaging, interactive learning experiences that improve student motivation, comprehension and satisfaction (AlGerafi et al. 2023). Although some studies, such as that by Moro et al. (2021), reported no statistically significant difference in knowledge acquisition between AR/VR and traditional methods, others have demonstrated notable improvements in learner performance and engagement (Tene et al. 2024). These technologies have shown particular promise in visualising complex concepts, enabling personalised learning experiences and fostering critical thinking through simulation‐based tasks (AlGerafi et al. 2023).

Despite the positive outcomes, several barriers emerged. Online learning environments were less effective for students with limited internet connectivity, unsuitable home environments or a strong preference for face‐to‐face interaction (Hwang et al. 2022; Saban and Balila 2021). Over‐reliance on static content, such as PowerPoint slides, was criticised for stifling student engagement (Singh et al. 2021). These findings highlight the importance of varied, adaptive and responsive teaching strategies considering students' diverse technological, emotional and cognitive needs.

Consistently across the literature, faculty competence in instructional design, digital fluency and empathy were linked to positive student outcomes (Lengetti et al. 2021; Fuchs and Karrila 2021). Studies suggested that when faculty created student‐centred environments that combined structured learning pathways with opportunities for interaction and reflection, engagement levels significantly improved (Egilsdottir et al. 2022; Jeong et al. 2022). Visual Thinking Strategies (VTS), for instance, broadened the engagement spectrum to include empathy and observational skills, bridging the cognitive‐emotional divide essential to nursing (Moorman et al. 2017).

Collectively, the findings of this review emphasise that student engagement in online nursing education is best fostered through thoughtfully designed, interactive and flexible pedagogical approaches. High‐impact strategies included immersive simulations, gamified content, real‐time interaction and opportunities for self‐regulated learning. To remain sustainable, these approaches must be underpinned by faculty training, technological support and student‐centred design. As nursing education evolves, particularly in hybrid models, lessons from the pandemic offer a strong foundation for resilient and engaging learning environments that prepare students for real‐world clinical practice.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged regarding this research. First, including studies published only in English may introduce a potential language bias, limiting the representation of non‐English studies and findings. Relevant research in other languages may provide valuable insights not captured in this review.

Second, the restriction to peer‐reviewed literature published between January 2020 and September 2022, which was the pandemic's peak, may result in excluding recent studies that have not yet undergone the peer‐review process or have been published after the specified time frame. This limitation could potentially omit essential research findings that could contribute to the overall understanding of digital strategies for engaging nursing students in online units of study.

Furthermore, this study focuses on nursing education, which may limit the generalisability of the findings to other disciplines or educational settings. While nursing education is a significant study area, the results may not fully represent the experiences and strategies employed in different healthcare disciplines or higher education fields.

Additionally, due to the rapidly evolving nature of digital strategies in education, it is possible that some of the identified strategies may have changed, or new methods may have emerged since the completion of the literature search. The dynamic nature of technology and online learning environments necessitates ongoing research to stay abreast of the latest developments and trends.

Conclusion

The COVID‐19 pandemic has catalysed a profound transformation in nursing education, accelerating the adoption of digital teaching strategies and highlighting the need for innovative, flexible and responsive approaches to learning. Digital tools, including virtual simulations, gamified learning and interactive platforms, have demonstrated considerable potential in enhancing student engagement and supporting learning outcomes. However, their effectiveness is not inherent; it depends on the deliberate and pedagogically sound integration of diverse strategies that sustain motivation and promote meaningful learning.

Emerging technologies such as augmented and virtual reality have further expanded the possibilities of digital education by enabling immersive, practice‐oriented learning experiences. These approaches support the development of clinical skills, confidence and decision‐making, particularly in high‐stakes or resource‐constrained environments. Nevertheless, persistent challenges related to student motivation, engagement and access in fully online contexts indicate that technology alone cannot resolve the complexities of digital learning.

A consistent finding across the literature is the importance of student‐centred approaches, supported by educators who possess strong pedagogical and digital capabilities. When combined with strategies such as gamification, flipped classrooms and experiential learning, these approaches enhance not only engagement but also critical thinking, communication and clinical readiness.

Importantly, the findings of this review extend beyond engagement to emphasise the need for alignment between education and practice. Digital education must not be viewed solely as a mechanism for delivering content or increasing engagement, but as a critical component of preparing a competent and adaptable healthcare workforce.

Future models of nursing education must therefore move beyond engagement‐driven design towards integrated, systems‐oriented approaches that align learning, clinical practice and workforce needs. Such alignment will be essential to ensure that the benefits of digital innovation translate into improved clinical competence, workforce readiness and ultimately, high‐quality patient care in an increasingly complex healthcare environment.
